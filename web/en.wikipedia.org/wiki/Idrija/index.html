Idrija (German: Idria) is a town in western Slovenia. It is the seat of the Municipality of Idrija and is best known for its historic mercury mine, which operated for roughly 500 years and was among the largest mercury mines in the world.

History

Mercury was discovered at Idrija around 1490, according to legend by a tub maker who noticed his vessel had grown heavy with a shiny liquid. Mining, long administered by the Habsburg state, made the town prosperous; ore was extracted from workings that reached hundreds of metres deep. The mine was gradually closed in the late twentieth century because of falling mercury prices and environmental concerns.

In 2012 the "Heritage of Mercury" — comprising Idrija and Almadén in Spain — was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, recognising the two largest mercury mines and their associated industrial and cultural heritage.

Culture

Idrija is also renowned for Idrija lace, a bobbin lace tradition taught at the town's lace school since 1876, and for žlikrofi, small filled dumplings that are a signature dish of the region and bear protected status in Slovenia.
