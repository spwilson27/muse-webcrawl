Life

Jahanara Begum (23 March 1614 – 16 September 1681) was a Mughal princess, eldest daughter of the emperor Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal. On her mother's death in childbirth in 1631, the seventeen-year-old Jahanara became Padshah Begum, first lady of the empire, exercising the influence of an empress: she received envoys, interceded with her father, and amassed a fortune from trade concessions, owning ships that traded with the Dutch and English from Surat. She never married, remaining the empire's most powerful woman for three decades.

Patronage and architecture

Jahanara was one of the great builders of Shahjahanabad (old Delhi): she laid out Chandni Chowk, the city's great bazaar avenue (1650), with its caravanserai and gardens; built mosques, including the Jama Masjid of Agra (1648); and endowed gardens, baths, and wells across the empire. Her patronage, like that of her aunt-by-marriage Nur Jahan, demonstrated the architectural agency of Mughal royal women.

Sufi writings

A devout Sufi of the Qadiri and Chishti orders, initiated by Mullah Shah Badakhshi, Jahanara composed a Persian biography of the Chishti saint Mu'in al-Din Chishti, the Munis al-Arvah — the first work of its kind by a Mughal princess — and wrote on her own spiritual path in the Risala-i Sahibiya. She chose burial in an open-air tomb beside the Nizamuddin shrine in Delhi, with the famous humility inscription: "Let nothing cover my grave save this green grass, for grass suffices well as a covering for the grave of the lowly."

The war of succession

Jahanara supported her brother Dara Shikoh in the war of succession of 1657–59 against Aurangzeb; after Dara's defeat and execution, she was sidelined, though Aurangzeb later restored her title and stipend. She died in Delhi in 1681.
