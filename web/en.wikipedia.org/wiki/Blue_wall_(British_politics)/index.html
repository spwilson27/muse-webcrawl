Blue wall (British politics)

The blue wall is a term in British politics for the set of parliamentary constituencies, mainly in southern England, that had traditionally voted Conservative ("blue") for generations but became increasingly contested — chiefly by the Liberal Democrats — in the late 2010s and 2020s. It is the counterpart to the "red wall" of Labour seats in the Midlands and northern England that fell to the Conservatives in 2019.

Characteristics

Blue wall seats tend to be affluent, home-owning commuter areas with high proportions of graduates and voters who supported Remain in the 2016 EU referendum. Analysts, notably pollster James Kanagasooriam who popularised both "wall" terms, argued these voters were drifting from the Conservatives over Brexit, cultural issues, planning and housebuilding, and perceptions of government competence.

Electoral developments

The Liberal Democrats' shock victory in the 2021 Chesham and Amersham by-election, overturning a large Conservative majority in a campaign focused partly on planning reform and HS2, was taken as evidence of blue wall vulnerability, as were further by-election losses at North Shropshire (2021), Tiverton and Honiton (2022), and Somerton and Frome (2023). At the 2024 general election the Conservatives lost many southern seats to both the Liberal Democrats and Labour, and the party recorded its worst-ever result nationally.
