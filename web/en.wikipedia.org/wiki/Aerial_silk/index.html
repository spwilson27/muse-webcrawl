Aerial silk

Aerial silk, also known as aerial contortion, aerial ribbons, or tissu, is a type of aerial performance in which one or more artists perform aerial acrobatics while hanging from a specialist fabric. Performers climb the suspended fabric without the use of safety lines, relying only on strength, flexibility, and secure wraps of the fabric around the body ("locks") to hold poses and execute drops.

Technique

The fabric is usually a two-way stretch polyester tricot hung as two strands or a single loop from a ceiling point. Performers use foot locks, hip keys, and wraps around the waist or limbs to create friction that supports their weight. Repertoire includes climbs, inversions, drops — controlled falls unwinding the fabric — and poses demanding extreme flexibility. Aerial silk is practised in circus schools and fitness studios worldwide and is a staple of contemporary circus companies such as Cirque du Soleil.

History

Aerial fabric work developed within modern circus in the late 20th century, drawing on older rope and web acts. It gained wide popularity in the 1990s and 2000s as circus arts moved into theatres and recreational training.

Safety

Because performers work without safety harnesses, proper rigging, crash mats during training, and instruction in wrap technique are essential to prevent falls and friction burns.
