Yann Tiersen

Yann Tiersen (born 23 June 1970) is a French musician and composer from Brittany. His music combines classical training with elements of French folk, chanson, musette, and rock, and is noted for its use of piano, violin, accordion, toy piano, and found sounds. He achieved worldwide fame with his score for the 2001 film Amélie.

Career

Tiersen studied violin, piano, and conducting at music academies in Rennes, Nantes, and Boulogne, and played in post-punk bands before turning to solo composition. His early albums La Valse des monstres (1995) and Rue des cascades (1996) built a cult following in France. Director Jean-Pierre Jeunet used music largely drawn from these albums for Amélie, whose soundtrack sold millions of copies and made pieces such as "Comptine d'un autre été : L'après-midi" internationally famous.

Later work

Subsequent albums include L'Absente (2001), Les Retrouvailles (2005), Dust Lane (2010), Skyline (2011), ∞ (Infinity) (2014), and the piano album EUSA (2016), inspired by the island of Ushant where he lives. He also scored the film Good Bye, Lenin! (2003). Tiersen's later work has increasingly incorporated electronic textures and environmental recordings.
