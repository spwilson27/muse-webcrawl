Count Hans Axel von Fersen (4 September 1755 – 20 June 1810), known as Axel von Fersen the Younger, was a Swedish count, a Marshal of the Realm of Sweden, and a soldier and diplomat. He is famous for his close friendship with Queen Marie Antoinette of France and for organising the French royal family's attempted escape, the Flight to Varennes, in 1791.

Life

Son of the statesman Axel von Fersen the Elder, he travelled widely in Europe as a young man, met Marie Antoinette at a masked ball in Paris in 1774, and served as a volunteer with the French forces in the American War of Independence, acting as interpreter between Rochambeau and Washington. His intimacy with the French queen has been the subject of speculation ever since; their surviving correspondence, partly redacted, was published in the 19th century.

During the French Revolution, Fersen planned and personally drove the carriage for part of the royal family's flight in June 1791, which ended with their arrest at Varennes. He later worked in Brussels and Vienna to rally support for the captive royal family and kept a celebrated diary. Back in Sweden he became one of the leading figures of the Gustavian establishment, serving as Marshal of the Realm.

Death

After the death of the popular crown prince Christian August in 1810, rumours—unfounded—blamed the Fersen family for poisoning him. At the prince's funeral procession in Stockholm on 20 June 1810, a mob turned on Fersen, dragged him from his carriage and beat and stamped him to death while troops failed to intervene, in one of the most notorious episodes in Swedish history. He was later officially exonerated and given a state funeral.
