Hasekura Tsunenaga

Hasekura Rokuemon Tsunenaga (1571–1622) was a Japanese samurai and retainer of Date Masamune, the daimyō of Sendai. He led the Keichō Embassy (1613–1620), a diplomatic mission to New Spain (Mexico) and Europe, making him the first Japanese official ambassador to the Americas and to several European courts.

Hasekura Tsunenaga, the portrait painted in Rome in 1615.

The embassy

In September 1613 Hasekura sailed from Tsukinoura aboard the Japanese-built galleon San Juan Bautista, accompanied by the Franciscan friar Luis Sotelo and around 180 Japanese merchants, sailors, and converts. Crossing the Pacific, the embassy reached Acapulco in 1614 and travelled on to Mexico City, where Hasekura was baptised. The mission then crossed the Atlantic to Spain, where Hasekura met King Philip III and was baptised again as Felipe Francisco Hasekura. In Italy he was received by Pope Paul V in Rome in 1615, presenting a letter from Date Masamune proposing trade and requesting missionaries.

Return and aftermath

The embassy returned to Japan in 1620 to find the political climate transformed: Christianity was being suppressed and Japan was moving toward its policy of national seclusion (sakoku). Date Masamune declined to champion the mission's results. Hasekura died in 1622. His journey remained little known until Japan reopened in the 19th century; documents and gifts from the embassy are preserved in Sendai, and a replica of the San Juan Bautista was built in Ishinomaki.
