Savannasaurus

Savannasaurus is a genus of titanosaurian sauropod dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous (Cenomanian–Turonian, about 98–93 million years ago) Winton Formation of Queensland, Australia. The type and only species, Savannasaurus elliottorum, was described in 2016 by Stephen Poropat and colleagues from a well-preserved partial skeleton discovered near Winton.

Discovery

The holotype specimen (AODF 660) was found in 2005 by David Elliott, co-founder of the Australian Age of Dinosaurs museum, on Belmont Station near Winton. Nicknamed "Wade", the skeleton took a decade to prepare and is one of the most complete Australian sauropod specimens, preserving much of the trunk, pelvis, limbs and feet.

Description

Savannasaurus was a medium-sized titanosaur, estimated at about 14 metres long. It was unusually wide-bodied, with a barrel-shaped ribcage and broad hips — its pelvic width exceeded a metre — adaptations interpreted as weight distribution for walking on soft, seasonally flooded ground. As a member of the Diamantinasauria (with Diamantinasaurus and Sarmientosaurus), its discovery helped show that early titanosaurs reached Australia from South America across Antarctica while the continents remained connected.

Palaeoecology

In the Winton Formation ecosystem, Savannasaurus browsed alongside the sauropods Wintonotitan and Diamantinasaurus and was potentially preyed upon by the large theropod Australovenator.
