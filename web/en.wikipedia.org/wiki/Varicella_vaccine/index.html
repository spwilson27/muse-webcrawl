Varicella vaccine

The varicella vaccine, also known as the chickenpox vaccine, is a vaccine that protects against chickenpox. One dose prevails in preventing most severe disease, and two doses are recommended for higher effectiveness. It is a live attenuated vaccine developed from the Oka strain of varicella zoster virus, originally developed by Michiaki Takahashi in Japan in the 1970s.

The vaccine is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. In the United States it was licensed in 1995 and is routinely given to children at 12 to 15 months and again at 4 to 6 years, often combined with the MMR vaccine as MMRV. Widespread vaccination has greatly reduced chickenpox cases, hospitalizations, and deaths.

Safety

Side effects are generally mild, including soreness at the injection site and a mild rash. The vaccine is not recommended for people with severely weakened immune systems or during pregnancy.
