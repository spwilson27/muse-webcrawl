Yakov Mikhailovich Yurovsky (19 June 1878 – 2 August 1938) was a Russian Bolshevik revolutionary and Cheka officer who served as commandant of the Ipatiev House in Yekaterinburg and directed the execution of the former Emperor Nicholas II and his family in July 1918.

Early life

Born into a large working-class Jewish family in Tomsk, Siberia, Yurovsky trained and worked as a watchmaker. He spent time in Germany, converted to Lutheranism for a period, and on returning to Russia became involved in revolutionary activity, joining the Bolsheviks and participating in underground work in the Urals.

The Romanov execution

In early July 1918 Yurovsky was placed in charge of the "House of Special Purpose" — the Ipatiev House, where Nicholas II, his wife Alexandra, their five children and four retainers were held. As White forces approached Yekaterinburg during the Russian Civil War, the Ural Soviet ordered the prisoners killed. In the early hours of 17 July 1918 Yurovsky read out the sentence and led the firing squad in the basement room; the bodies were subsequently concealed in the Koptyaki forest, a fact he documented in a detailed report, often called the "Yurovsky Note".

Later life

After the Civil War Yurovsky worked in state economic and museum administration in Moscow. He died in 1938 of a perforated ulcer. The remains of the imperial family were located decades later and formally identified in the 1990s, broadly confirming Yurovsky's account of the burial.
