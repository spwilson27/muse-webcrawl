Material Design is a design language developed by Google, announced at the Google I/O conference on June 25, 2014. It expanded on the "card" motifs introduced in Google Now, combining principles of good design with shadows, responsive animations and transitions, padding, and depth effects to create a visual language intended to feel like a tactile, paper-like material existing in three-dimensional space.

Principles

The design system is based on the metaphor of material: surfaces and edges provide visual cues grounded in touch and physics, with shadows expressing the hierarchy between elements. It specifies colour palettes, typography (using the Roboto font), iconography, grid layouts based on 8dp spacing, and motion guidelines in which animation is meaningful and responsive to user input. Components such as app bars, buttons, cards, chips, dialogs, floating action buttons, navigation drawers, and snackbars are standardised.

Versions

Material Design 2 (2018) allowed brands more customisation of shapes, colours, and typography while keeping the system coherent. Material You, formally Material 3, launched with Android 12 in 2021, introduced dynamic colour, in which interface palettes are generated from the user's wallpaper, along with larger, more personal components and updated accessibility guidance. Material is implemented across Android, Google's web properties, and frameworks such as Flutter and Angular Material.
