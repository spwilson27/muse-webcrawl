Churchill Falls

Churchill Falls is a waterfall named after former British Prime Minister Winston Churchill, located on the Churchill River in Labrador, Canada. The falls are 75 metres (245 ft) high, and the river drops a total of about 316 metres through a series of rapids above and below the main falls. Churchill Falls, along with the nearby community of the same name, is best known as the site of the Churchill Falls Generating Station, one of the largest underground hydroelectric power stations in the world.

History

The falls were known to the indigenous Innu people as Patamueshikau. The first European to describe them was John McLean of the Hudson's Bay Company in 1839. In 1915 Wilfred Grenfell surveyed the site, and the falls were renamed in 1965 after Churchill, shortly after his death; the river, formerly the Hamilton River, was renamed at the same time.

Hydroelectric development

Between 1966 and 1974 the Churchill Falls (Labrador) Corporation built a massive hydroelectric project, diverting most of the river's flow into the Smallwood Reservoir and through an underground powerhouse with 11 turbines and a capacity of 5,428 megawatts. The works largely dried up the falls themselves, except during spills. Most of the power is sold to Hydro-Québec under a controversial 65-year contract signed in 1969, which has been a long-standing source of dispute between Newfoundland and Labrador and Quebec. A second, smaller station, the Muskrat Falls project on the lower Churchill, was completed in the 2020s.
