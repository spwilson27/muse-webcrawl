"Fischia il vento" ("The Wind Blows") is an Italian partisan song from the Second World War. Its lyrics were written in 1943 by Felice Cascione, a doctor and partisan commander from Liguria, and set to the melody of the popular Soviet song "Katyusha". Together with "Bella ciao", it is one of the two best-known anthems of the Italian resistance movement against Nazi-fascism.


Italian partisans in the Apennines, 1944

Origins

Felice Cascione, known by his nom de guerre "u Megu" ("the Doctor" in Ligurian dialect), composed the verses in the autumn of 1943, shortly after the Italian armistice of 8 September, as he organised one of the first partisan formations in the province of Imperia. The song spread rapidly among the brigades of Liguria and Piedmont and then throughout the resistance. Cascione was killed in action in January 1944, aged 25.

Lyrics and meaning

The song opens with the wind whistling and the storm raging ("Fischia il vento e infuria la bufera") while the partisans march regardless of the weather, determined to conquer "the red spring" in which the sun of the future shines. Its imagery of worn shoes, steadfast hearts and the coming dawn made it a marching song that combined hardship with confidence in liberation. Unlike "Bella ciao", which is elegiac in tone, "Fischia il vento" is explicitly fighting in character.

Legacy

After the war the song remained a staple of anti-fascist commemorations, especially around the 25 April Liberation Day celebrations in Italy, and has been recorded by many folk and rock artists. Annual gatherings at the Passo del Faiallo and other Ligurian resistance sites traditionally open with the song.
