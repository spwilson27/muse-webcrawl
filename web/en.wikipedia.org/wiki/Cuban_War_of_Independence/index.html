The Cuban War of Independence (Spanish: Guerra de Independencia cubana), fought from 1895 to 1898, was the last of three liberation wars fought by Cuba against Spain, following the Ten Years' War (1868–1878) and the Little War (1879–1880). In Cuba it is also known as the Guerra Necesaria, the "Necessary War".

Background and outbreak

The war was organised by the exiled writer and revolutionary José Martí, who founded the Cuban Revolutionary Party in 1892. The uprising began with the Grito de Baire on 24 February 1895. Martí was killed at Dos Ríos in May 1895, but military commanders Máximo Gómez and Antonio Maceo carried the insurrection westward across the island, waging guerrilla war against Spanish forces.

Spanish response

General Valeriano Weyler, appointed captain-general in 1896, adopted a policy of reconcentración, forcing hundreds of thousands of rural Cubans into fortified towns to isolate the rebels. Disease and starvation in the camps killed vast numbers — estimates run from roughly 170,000 upward — and reports in the American press helped turn US opinion against Spain.

American intervention

The explosion of the USS Maine in Havana harbour on 15 February 1898 precipitated the Spanish–American War. US forces defeated Spain in Cuba and elsewhere, and by the Treaty of Paris (1898) Spain relinquished Cuba. After a period of United States military occupation, the Republic of Cuba was formally established on 20 May 1902, though constrained by the Platt Amendment.
