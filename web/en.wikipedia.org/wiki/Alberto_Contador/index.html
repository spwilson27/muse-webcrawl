Alberto Contador Velasco (born 6 December 1982) is a Spanish former professional road racing cyclist, regarded as one of the finest stage racers of his generation. He is one of only a handful of riders to have won all three Grand Tours — the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia and Vuelta a España — and one of only two (with Chris Froome) to have won each of them more than once.

Rise and Grand Tour success

Contador turned professional in 2003 and survived a life-threatening brain haemorrhage caused by a cerebral cavernoma in 2004, returning to win the Tour de France in 2007. In 2008 he won the Giro and the Vuelta within months of each other, then added a second Tour title in 2009, famous for his attacking climbing style and his pistol-shot victory celebration.

Clenbuterol case

Contador tested positive for a minute quantity of clenbuterol during the 2010 Tour, which he had won on the road. After a lengthy legal process, the Court of Arbitration for Sport banned him in February 2012 and stripped his 2010 Tour and 2011 Giro titles. Contador maintained the positive test came from contaminated meat; he returned from suspension to win the Vuelta in 2012.

Later career

He won the Vuelta again in 2014 and the Giro in 2015, and retired after the 2017 Vuelta, where he won the stage atop the Angliru in his final week of racing. He later co-founded the Eolo-Kometa development team with Ivan Basso.
