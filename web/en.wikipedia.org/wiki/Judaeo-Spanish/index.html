Judaeo-Spanish

Judaeo-Spanish or Judeo-Spanish, commonly known as Ladino, is a Romance language derived from Old Spanish. It was spoken by Sephardic Jews, the descendants of Jews expelled from Spain in 1492, across the Ottoman Empire and North Africa.

History

After the Alhambra Decree expelled Jews from Spain in 1492, Sephardic communities carried their Spanish to cities such as Salonica, Constantinople, Sarajevo, and Izmir. Isolated from later developments in Spain, the language preserved features of fifteenth-century Castilian while borrowing vocabulary from Hebrew, Turkish, Greek, Arabic, and other local languages.

Characteristics

Ladino was traditionally written in Hebrew script, especially the Rashi script, and more recently in the Latin alphabet. It retains archaic Spanish sounds and words, such as fijo for "son," and has a rich tradition of ballads (romansas), proverbs, and folk tales.

Present status

The Holocaust destroyed many of the great Ladino-speaking communities of the Balkans, and assimilation further reduced the speaker population. Today Ladino is an endangered language, with most speakers elderly and concentrated in Israel and Turkey, though revival efforts, university programs, and digital publications continue.
