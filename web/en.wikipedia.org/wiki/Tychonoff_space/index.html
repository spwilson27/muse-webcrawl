Tychonoff space

In topology and related branches of mathematics, a Tychonoff space (or completely regular Hausdorff space) is a topological space in which points can be separated from closed sets by continuous real-valued functions, and which also satisfies the Hausdorff separation axiom.

Definition

A space X is completely regular if, whenever C is a closed subset and x a point not in C, there exists a continuous function f from X to the interval [0, 1] with f(x) = 0 and f identically 1 on C. A Tychonoff space is a completely regular T1 space. The class is named after Andrey Nikolayevich Tikhonov (whose name is often transliterated "Tychonoff").

Properties

Every Tychonoff space can be embedded in a compact Hausdorff space — its Stone–Čech compactification — and the Tychonoff cube, a product of unit intervals, is a universal space for the class: Tychonoff's theorem guarantees that arbitrary products of compact spaces are compact. Examples include all metric spaces, all locally compact Hausdorff spaces and all topological manifolds.
