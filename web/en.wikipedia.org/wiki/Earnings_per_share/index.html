Earnings per share

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

EPS is a headline measure of a company's profitability per outstanding share

Earnings per share (EPS) is the portion of a company's profit allocated to each outstanding share of common stock. It serves as an indicator of a company's profitability and is one of the most widely followed figures in financial reporting and equity valuation; dividing the share price by EPS gives the price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio.

Calculation

Basic EPS = (Net income − Preferred dividends) ÷ Weighted average shares outstanding

Basic EPS uses the weighted average number of common shares outstanding during the period. Diluted EPS additionally accounts for the shares that would be created if dilutive securities — stock options, warrants, convertible bonds and convertible preferred shares — were exercised or converted, and is therefore the more conservative figure. Accounting standards (IAS 33 internationally and ASC 260 under US GAAP) require companies with complex capital structures to report both.

Uses and limitations

Investors use EPS to compare companies, track profit growth, and estimate value, and analyst consensus forecasts of EPS drive earnings-season market reactions. Critics note that EPS can be increased by share buybacks without any underlying improvement in profit, ignores the capital required to generate earnings, and can be influenced by accounting choices; it is therefore usually considered alongside cash flow and return on capital measures.
