Delaware Water Gap

The Delaware Water Gap is a water gap on the border of New Jersey and Pennsylvania where the Delaware River cuts through a large ridge of the Appalachian Mountains, the Kittatinny Ridge. The gap is roughly a mile wide and more than 1,200 feet (370 m) deep, flanked by Mount Tammany (1,527 ft) on the New Jersey side and Mount Minsi (1,461 ft) on the Pennsylvania side.

Geology

The gap was formed as the river eroded downward through resistant Silurian Shawangunk conglomerate and quartzite over millions of years, maintaining its course as the surrounding landscape evolved. It is a classic example of a river antecedent to or superimposed upon the ridge it crosses.

Recreation and preservation

The gap is the centrepiece of the Delaware Water Gap National Recreation Area, established in 1965, which preserves some 70,000 acres of parkland along 40 miles of the Middle Delaware River. The park — created after the controversial Tocks Island Dam project was abandoned — is one of the most visited units of the U.S. National Park Service, offering hiking on the Appalachian Trail, canoeing, fishing, and scenic drives. Interstate 80 and the Old Mine Road pass through or alongside the gap.
