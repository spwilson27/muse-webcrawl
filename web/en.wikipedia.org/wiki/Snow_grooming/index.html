Snow grooming

Snow grooming is the process of manipulating snow for recreational purposes with a machine, called a snow groomer or snowcat, to pack, smooth, and redistribute it. Grooming is performed at ski resorts to prepare pistes after snowfall and daily use, and to build and maintain terrain parks, cross-country ski trails, snowmobile trails, and winter sports competition courses.

Process and equipment

A snow groomer is a tracked vehicle fitted with a front blade for pushing and levelling snow and a rear tiller that mills the surface layer and leaves behind a finely textured finish — the evenly ridged pattern known as "corduroy," prized by skiers for its consistent, grippy surface. Grooming typically takes place overnight, after slopes close, so the tilled snow can set into a firm surface before morning. Operators work in coordinated fleets, often using GPS and snow-depth measurement systems to distribute snow evenly and cover thin areas; steep slopes may require winch cats anchored from above.

Other forms

Cross-country trail grooming uses specialized implements to set classic tracks and a smooth skating lane. Snowmobile clubs groom trail networks with smaller tractor-drawn drags. Grooming is also used to form the walls of halfpipes and the take-offs of ski jumps, shaped with specially designed tillers and hand finishing.

History

Ski areas originally packed snow by "boot packing" and sidestepping skiers. Purpose-built grooming machines emerged in the 1950s and 1960s with the development of snowcats by manufacturers such as Bombardier, and modern fleets — led by manufacturers like Prinoth and Kässbohrer (PistenBully) — became standard at resorts worldwide, fundamentally shaping the groomed-piste skiing experience.
