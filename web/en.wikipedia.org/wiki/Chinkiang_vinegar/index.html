Chinkiang vinegar

Chinkiang vinegar (镇江香醋, Zhènjiāng xiāngcù), also known as Zhenjiang aromatic vinegar, is a black rice vinegar produced in Zhenjiang (formerly romanised "Chinkiang"), Jiangsu province, China. Dark, complex, and mildly sweet, it is one of the most famous Chinese vinegars and a staple of Jiangsu cuisine, used in dipping sauces for dumplings and xiaolongbao, in cold dishes, and in braised dishes such as Zhenjiang pork.

Production

The vinegar is made from glutinous rice (often with wheat bran) through solid-state fermentation, followed by ageing — much of it produced by the Hengshun company, founded in 1840. Legend attributes its invention to Hei Ta, son of the mythical Yellow Emperor's minister Du Kang, supposed inventor of wine. Chinkiang vinegar holds protected geographical indication status in China and the European Union, and its traditional brewing technique is listed as national intangible cultural heritage.
