Granola

Granola is a food consisting of rolled oats, nuts, seeds, honey or other sweeteners such as brown sugar, and sometimes puffed rice, that is usually baked until crisp, toasted and golden brown. During the baking process, the mixture is stirred to maintain a loose breakfast cereal consistency. Dried fruit, such as raisins and dates, and confections such as chocolate are sometimes added.

History

Granola was invented in Dansville, New York, by Dr. James Caleb Jackson at the Jackson Sanitarium in 1863. Jackson's granola, which he called "Granula", was made of Graham flour baked into sheets, crumbled, and baked again. A similar cereal was developed by John Harvey Kellogg. The name "granola" was trademarked in the late 19th century.

The food fell out of favour but was revived in the 1960s, when it became associated with the hippie movement and natural foods. Fruits and nuts were added to make it a health food popular with the health-conscious counterculture. Granola bars, a portable snack form developed in the 1970s, further popularised the food.

Granola bars

Granola bars consist of granola pressed and baked into a bar shape, resulting in a convenient snack food. The bars are usually coated in honey or other sweeteners as a binder. See also muesli, a similar unbaked preparation, and oat.
