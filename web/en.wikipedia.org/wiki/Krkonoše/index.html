The Krkonoše (Czech: [ˈkr̩konoʃɛ]; Polish: Karkonosze; German: Riesengebirge, "Giant Mountains") are a mountain range located in the north of the Czech Republic and the south-west of Poland, part of the Sudetes mountain system. The Czech-Polish border, largely following the main ridge, divides the range. The Krkonoše are the highest and most distinctive range of the Sudetes.

Geography

The range extends roughly 40 km from the Szklarska Poręba area in the west to the Lubawka pass in the east. Its main ridge reaches its highest point at Sněžka (1,603 m), the highest peak in the Czech Republic, known for its pyramidal summit, a high-altitude weather station, and a chapel. Glacial cirques, raised bogs, subarctic tundra plateaus, and waterfalls such as the Pančava and Labský falls characterise the landscape. The Elbe (Labe) River rises on the Czech side of the range.

Nature protection

Both countries protect the range as national parks — Krkonoše National Park (Czech Republic, established 1963) and Karkonosze National Park (Poland, 1959) — which together form a UNESCO transboundary biosphere reserve. The harsh summit climate supports arctic-alpine species found nowhere else in Central Europe, including endemic plants.

Tourism and culture

The Krkonoše are a major centre for hiking and winter sports. Resorts such as Špindlerův Mlýn, Pec pod Sněžkou, Harrachov, and Karpacz line the foothills; Harrachov's ski jumps are a landmark. In folklore the mountains are guarded by the mountain spirit Krakonoš (Rübezahl), celebrated in tales and in the herb liqueur named after the range.
