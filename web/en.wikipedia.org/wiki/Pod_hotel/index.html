Pod hotel

Rows of sleeping capsules in a pod hotel

A pod hotel, also known as a capsule hotel, is a type of economical hotel accommodation, developed in Japan, that features many small, bed-sized rooms known as capsules or pods. Each capsule provides enough space for a guest to sleep, typically containing a mattress, pillow, reading light, and sometimes a small television, radio, and alarm clock, but little or no room to stand.

History

The first capsule hotel is generally considered to be the Capsule Inn Osaka, designed by architect Kisho Kurokawa and opened in 1979 in the Umeda district of Osaka. The concept grew out of Japan's need for inexpensive lodging for salarymen who had missed the last train home, and it built on Kurokawa's earlier Nakagin Capsule Tower (1972) in Tokyo.

Facilities and spread

Guests typically store luggage in lockers, share communal bathing facilities — in Japan often modelled on the sento — and may have access to lounges, restaurants, and vending machines. Capsules are usually stacked two units high and arranged along corridors, with privacy provided by a curtain or a fibreglass door. The concept has spread beyond Japan to airports and city centres in China, Singapore, Europe, and North America, sometimes in more upmarket "pod" forms aimed at budget travellers.
