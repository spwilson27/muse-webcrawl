Performance appraisal

A performance appraisal, also referred to as a performance review, performance evaluation, or employee appraisal, is a regular process in which an employee's job performance and contributions are evaluated, usually by their manager, and discussed with the employee. Appraisals are commonly used to inform decisions about pay, promotion, training, and dismissal, and to set goals for the coming period.

Methods

Traditional appraisals use rating scales against defined competencies or objectives, as in management by objectives. Other methods include 360-degree feedback, which gathers input from peers, subordinates, and customers; behaviourally anchored rating scales; self-assessment; and continuous-feedback models that replace the annual review with frequent check-ins. Forced ranking systems, which require managers to distribute staff along a curve, have been controversial and widely abandoned.

Criticism

Appraisals are criticized for rater biases such as halo effects, recency bias, and leniency, for consuming large amounts of managerial time, and for sometimes damaging motivation when poorly conducted. Research in industrial and organizational psychology focuses on improving validity through clearer criteria, rater training, and separation of developmental feedback from pay decisions.
