Bellur Krishnamachar Sundararaja Iyengar (14 December 1918 – 20 August 2014) was an Indian yoga teacher widely regarded as one of the foremost yoga teachers in the world. He founded the style known as Iyengar Yoga, which emphasises precision, alignment and the therapeutic use of props such as belts, blocks and blankets.

Life

Born into a poor family in Bellur, Karnataka, Iyengar suffered childhood illnesses including tuberculosis and typhoid. At 15 he went to Mysore to live with his brother-in-law, the yogi Tirumalai Krishnamacharya, from whom he learned asana practice. He moved to Pune in 1937 to teach yoga. A chance meeting with violinist Yehudi Menuhin in 1952 led to international invitations, and his 1966 book Light on Yoga became a classic translated worldwide.

Teaching

In 1975 he opened the Ramamani Iyengar Memorial Yoga Institute in Pune, named after his wife. Iyengar Yoga is taught through a rigorous teacher-certification system; its methodical sequencing and attention to detail made yoga accessible to older and less flexible students.

Honours

India awarded him the Padma Shri (1991), Padma Bhushan (2002) and Padma Vibhushan (2014). Time magazine named him one of the 100 most influential people in the world in 2004.
