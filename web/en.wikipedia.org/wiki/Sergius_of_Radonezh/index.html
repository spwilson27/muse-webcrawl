Sergius of Radonezh (Russian: Сергий Радонежский, Sergiy Radonezhsky; c. 1314 – 25 September 1392) was a Russian Orthodox monk and spiritual leader, regarded as one of the greatest saints of Russia and the founder of the Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius near Moscow, the spiritual centre of the Russian Orthodox Church.

Life

Born Bartholomew near Rostov to a boyar family impoverished by Mongol taxation, he withdrew with his brother Stephen to the forest near Radonezh around 1337 to live as a hermit. There he built a small church dedicated to the Holy Trinity and was tonsured with the name Sergius. Disciples gradually gathered around him, and the community became the Trinity Monastery, where Sergius introduced the communal (cenobitic) rule in place of solitary hermit life.

Sergius is traditionally credited with blessing Prince Dmitry Donskoy before the Battle of Kulikovo in 1380, a turning point in Moscow's resistance to Mongol rule. His monastery fostered a network of daughter houses across northern Russia, and his disciples were central to the monastic colonization of the Russian North.

Veneration

Sergius was canonized in the mid-fifteenth century. His relics rest in the Trinity Cathedral of the Lavra. He is honoured as the patron saint of Russia and of students, and his feast days are kept on 25 September and 18 July.
