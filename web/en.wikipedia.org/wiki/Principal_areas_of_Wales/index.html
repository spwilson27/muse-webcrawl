Principal areas of Wales

Principal areas of Wales

Country: Wales, United Kingdom

Created: 1 April 1996

Number: 22 (9 counties, 3 cities and counties, 10 county boroughs)

The principal areas of Wales (Welsh: prif ardaloedd Cymru) are the 22 unitary authorities that form the single tier of local government in Wales. They were established on 1 April 1996 under the Local Government (Wales) Act 1994, replacing the two-tier system of counties and districts created in 1974.

Types

For ceremonial and naming purposes the principal areas are styled in three ways: nine are designated counties (including Powys, Gwynedd and Carmarthenshire), three are cities and counties (Cardiff, Swansea and Newport), and ten are county boroughs (including Wrexham, Blaenau Gwent and Merthyr Tydfil). Legally they are all principal areas with identical powers, each governed by a principal council responsible for education, social services, highways, housing, planning and other local services.

List

The 22 principal areas are: Blaenau Gwent, Bridgend, Caerphilly, Cardiff, Carmarthenshire, Ceredigion, Conwy, Denbighshire, Flintshire, Gwynedd, Isle of Anglesey, Merthyr Tydfil, Monmouthshire, Neath Port Talbot, Newport, Pembrokeshire, Powys, Rhondda Cynon Taf, Swansea, Torfaen, Vale of Glamorgan, and Wrexham.

Regional working

Since the 2010s, principal councils have cooperated through regional bodies, and corporate joint committees established under the Local Government and Elections (Wales) Act 2021 now exercise functions in areas such as transport, land-use planning and economic development.
