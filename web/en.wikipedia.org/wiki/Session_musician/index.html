Occupation typeFreelance / contract musician
Activity sectorsRecording studios, film and television scores, advertising, touring
Also calledStudio musician, backing musician, sideman

A session musician, also known as a studio musician or backing musician, is an instrumental or vocal performer who is hired to perform in a recording session or a live performance. Session musicians are usually not permanent members of a musical ensemble or band with the solo artist with whom they are recording or touring. They play behind the scenes and rarely achieve individual fame in their own right as soloists, although notable exceptions exist. The average session musician is highly skilled and typically plays in a wide range of genres.

Roles and skills

Session musicians are prized for their versatility, sight-reading ability, reliability, and capacity to perform in many styles with little rehearsal. They work on albums, film and television soundtracks, jingles, and video game scores, and may be hired for single songs or extended engagements. Many are multi-instrumentalists.

Famous session groups

Certain ensembles of session players became famous for shaping the sound of entire eras: the Wrecking Crew in 1960s Los Angeles (including drummer Hal Blaine and guitarist Carol Kaye), the Funk Brothers at Motown in Detroit, the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section in Alabama, and the Nashville A-Team. Individual session players such as guitarist Steve Lukather and drummer Jeff Porcaro (later of Toto) appeared on hundreds of hit records.

Modern era

Since the 1990s, changes in recording technology and budgets have reduced large-scale studio band work, with many session musicians now recording parts remotely from home studios. Union scale payments and residual arrangements for commercial work remain governed in the United States by the American Federation of Musicians.
