Michelangelo Pistoletto

Born: 23 June 1933, Biella, Italy
Movement: Arte Povera
Known for: Mirror paintings; Venus of the Rags; Cittadellarte

Michelangelo Pistoletto (born 23 June 1933 in Biella) is an Italian artist, action and object artist, and art theorist, regarded as one of the founders and leading figures of the Arte Povera movement.

Mirror paintings

Pistoletto worked in his father's Turin restoration workshop and in advertising before turning to painting. In 1961–62 he developed his signature "mirror paintings" (Quadri specchianti): life-size photographic images, silkscreened onto tissue paper, applied to polished stainless steel panels. Because the viewer's own reflection enters the work, the paintings constantly change with their surroundings, a device Pistoletto used to explore identity, time and the relation between art and life.

Arte Povera

From the mid-1960s he made objects and performances from humble materials — rags, neon, old furniture, vegetables — that the critic Germano Celant grouped in 1967 under the label Arte Povera. His best-known work, Venus of the Rags (Venere degli stracci, 1967), sets a classical statue of Venus before a mound of colourful discarded clothes. Other key works include the Minus Objects, the Zoo performances in Turin, and the giant newspaper sphere rolled through city streets. In July 2023 a monumental version of the Venus in Naples was destroyed by fire in a suspected arson attack; Pistoletto responded by pledging its recreation.

Cittadellarte and the Third Paradise

In 1998 he founded Cittadellarte – Fondazione Pistoletto in a former industrial complex in Biella, a "laboratory" applying art to social transformation across fields such as politics, economics and education. His symbol of the Third Paradise (Terzo Paradiso, 2003) — a reworked infinity sign with three circles — represents a hoped-for reconciliation of nature and technology.
