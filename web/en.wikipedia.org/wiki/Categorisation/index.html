Categorisation

Categorisation (also spelled categorization) is the process by which ideas and objects are recognised, differentiated, and understood as belonging to groups or categories. It is a fundamental cognitive process studied in psychology, cognitive science, linguistics, and philosophy.

Approaches

The classical view, descending from Aristotle, holds that categories are defined by necessary and sufficient conditions. Prototype theory, developed by Eleanor Rosch in the 1970s, showed that human categories often have graded membership around a central prototype. Exemplar theory and theory-based views offer further accounts.

Development and applications

Infants form perceptual categories early in life. Categorisation underlies language, reasoning, and decision-making, and is applied in library classification, taxonomy, and machine learning classification tasks.
