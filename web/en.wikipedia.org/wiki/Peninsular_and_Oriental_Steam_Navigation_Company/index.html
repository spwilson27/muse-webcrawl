Peninsular and Oriental Steam Navigation Company


Common name: P&O
Founded: 1837 (as the Peninsular Steam Navigation Company); royal charter 1840
Fate: Acquired by DP World, 2006
Headquarters: London, United Kingdom
Industry: Shipping, transport, ferries, cruises

The Peninsular and Oriental Steam Navigation Company, universally known as P&O, was a British shipping and logistics company founded in the 1830s and for over a century and a half one of the world's great shipping lines. Its mail contracts and liner services tied Britain to Iberia, the Mediterranean, India, the Far East, and Australia, and its house flag and funnel colours became emblems of the age of steam.

Origins and growth

The company began as the Peninsular Steam Navigation Company, offering services from London to Spain and Portugal, and received a royal charter in 1840. Winning Admiralty mail contracts, it extended services through the Mediterranean to Egypt (with an overland link across the isthmus of Suez before the canal opened in 1869) and onward to India, Ceylon, Singapore, China, and eventually Japan and Australia. "Oriental" was added to its name as eastern routes developed.

Twentieth century

P&O absorbed numerous rivals, including the Orient Line (1960), and diversified into passenger cruising, ferries, container shipping (through Overseas Containers Ltd and later P&O Nedlloyd), ports, and property. Its cruise operations became P&O Cruises. The 1987 capsizing of the cross-Channel ferry Herald of Free Enterprise off Zeebrugge, with the loss of 193 lives, was the company's greatest disaster and a landmark in maritime safety law.

Break-up and legacy

In 2000 the group demerged its cruise business into P&O Princess Cruises (later acquired by Carnival Corporation). Its ports and ferries interests were acquired by Dubai's DP World in 2006 for about £3.9 billion, ending P&O's independence. The P&O name survives in brands including P&O Ferries and P&O Cruises, now under different ownership.
