Hull–White model

In financial mathematics, the Hull–White model is a model of future interest rates. In its most generic formulation, it belongs to the class of no-arbitrage models that are able to fit today's term structure of interest rates precisely. It is a single-factor model in which the short rate follows a mean-reverting Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with a time-dependent drift chosen to match the initial yield curve.

Formulation

The model specifies dr(t) = [θ(t) − a·r(t)]dt + σ dW(t), where a is the speed of mean reversion, σ the volatility, and θ(t) is calibrated so that model bond prices agree with the market's initial term structure. Because the process is Gaussian, bond prices and many interest-rate derivatives have closed-form solutions.

Use

Developed by John C. Hull and Alan White in papers from 1990 onward, the model is widely used to price interest-rate derivatives such as caps, swaptions, and callable bonds, and admits efficient implementation with trinomial trees. A two-factor extension allows a richer correlation structure.
