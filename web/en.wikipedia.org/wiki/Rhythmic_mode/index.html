Rhythmic mode

The rhythmic modes were a system of rhythmic notation used in European music of the late 12th and 13th centuries, particularly by composers of the Notre Dame school such as Léonin and Pérotin. They are generally regarded as the first system in Western music to indicate measured rhythm consistently.

The six modes

Medieval theorists described six modes, each a repeating pattern of long and short values analogous to classical poetic metres: trochaic, iambic, dactylic, anapaestic, spondaic, and tribrachic. Notes were grouped in ligatures, and the pattern of the ligatures told the singer which mode applied; individual note shapes alone did not yet specify duration.

Legacy

The modal system governed the rhythm of organum, conductus, and the early motet. By the late 13th century it was superseded by Franconian and then mensural notation, in which each note shape carried its own rhythmic value, freeing composers from repeating modal patterns.
