Alice Springs

Alice Springs (Arrernte: Mparntwe) is a town in the Northern Territory, Australia, situated almost exactly at the country's geographic centre. With a population of about 25,000, it is the third-largest settlement in the territory and the principal service centre for the vast Central Australian outback. The town lies on the Todd River, between the East and West MacDonnell Ranges, and is known for its desert landscapes, Aboriginal art and culture, and events such as the Henley-on-Todd Regatta.

History

The Arrernte people have lived in the area for tens of thousands of years; Mparntwe is rich in sacred sites connected with Dreaming stories. Europeans arrived with the Overland Telegraph Line: a repeater station was built in 1872 beside a permanent waterhole in the Todd River, named for Alice Todd, wife of telegraph superintendent Charles Todd. The settlement was originally called Stuart, after explorer John McDouall Stuart, and was renamed Alice Springs in 1933. A gold rush at Arltunga in 1887 and the arrival of the railway from Adelaide in 1929 ("The Ghan") spurred growth, as did the Second World War, when the town served as a transport hub.

Geography and climate

Alice Springs sits in a valley of the MacDonnell Ranges on the edge of the Simpson Desert. Summers are extremely hot, regularly exceeding 40 °C, while winter nights can fall below freezing. The Todd River is usually a dry sandy bed and flows only after rare heavy rains. Nearby attractions include Standley Chasm, Simpsons Gap, and Ormiston Gorge in the West MacDonnell National Park; the town is also a staging point for Uluru (Ayers Rock), about 450 km to the southwest.

Economy and services

The economy rests on tourism, government services, health, and education for the surrounding region. The Royal Flying Doctor Service operates a famous base and museum in the town, and the School of the Air — founded in 1951 to teach outback children by radio, now by internet — broadcasts from Alice Springs. The joint US-Australian intelligence facility at Pine Gap lies about 18 km southwest.

Culture

Alice Springs is a centre of Aboriginal art, home to the Araluen Arts Centre and the Papunya Tula artists' movement that launched the Western Desert art phenomenon. Annual events include the Henley-on-Todd Regatta — a boat race held in the dry riverbed with bottomless boats — the Camel Cup, and the Finke Desert Race, one of the world's toughest off-road events.
