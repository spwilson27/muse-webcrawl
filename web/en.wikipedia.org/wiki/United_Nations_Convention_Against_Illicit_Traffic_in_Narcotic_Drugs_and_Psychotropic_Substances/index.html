Vienna Convention (1988)
Signed20 December 1988, Vienna
Effective11 November 1990
Parties191 (near-universal)
DepositarySecretary-General of the United Nations

The convention was adopted in Vienna in December 1988.

The United Nations Convention Against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances of 1988 — often called the Vienna Convention — is one of the three major international drug control treaties, alongside the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs (1961) and the Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971). Adopted in response to the growth of transnational drug trafficking in the 1970s and 1980s, it requires states to criminalise the production, trafficking and laundering of proceeds of illicit drugs.

Provisions

Article 3 obliges parties to establish as criminal offences the production, manufacture, transport, sale and possession for trafficking of controlled drugs, the organisation and financing of trafficking, and the laundering of drug proceeds; it also addresses (subject to constitutional principles) possession for personal consumption. Other articles provide for confiscation of proceeds and instrumentalities, extradition of traffickers, mutual legal assistance, controlled delivery operations, and the monitoring of precursor chemicals — listed in Table I and Table II of the convention — used to manufacture drugs such as cocaine, heroin and amphetamines. The International Narcotics Control Board (INCB) monitors precursor control.

History and influence

The convention entered into force on 11 November 1990 and has 191 parties, making it one of the most widely ratified UN treaties. It shaped national legislation worldwide, including anti-money-laundering regimes later extended beyond drugs by the 2000 Palermo Convention against transnational organised crime. Debates continue over its compatibility with policies such as cannabis legalisation, which critics — and the INCB — regard as in tension with the treaty framework, while reform advocates argue the conventions allow more flexibility than is commonly claimed.
