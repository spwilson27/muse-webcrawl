Demetrius Zvonimir of Croatia

The Baška tablet (c. 1100), an early Glagolitic inscription that names King Zvonimir

Demetrius Zvonimir (Croatian: Dmitar Zvonimir; died 20 April 1089) was King of Croatia from 1075 until his death in 1089. A member of the Trpimirović dynasty, he was previously Ban (viceroy) of Croatia under King Peter Krešimir IV.

Reign

Zvonimir was crowned on 8 October 1075 at Solin by a representative of Pope Gregory VII, to whom he pledged fealty and gave the monastery of St. Gregory in Vrana. His reign is generally regarded as a peaceful and prosperous period for the medieval Croatian kingdom. He was married to Helen of Hungary, sister of King Ladislaus I of Hungary.

Death and succession

Zvonimir died in 1089 without a surviving heir; later legends held that he was murdered by his own nobles after calling them to join a crusade, and that he cursed the Croats to be ruled by foreign kings — a myth associated with the subsequent union of Croatia with Hungary. He was succeeded briefly by Stephen II, the last Trpimirović king, after whose death the Croatian crown passed into the Hungarian sphere.
