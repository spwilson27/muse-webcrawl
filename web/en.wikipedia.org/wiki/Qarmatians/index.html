Qarmatians


Eastern Arabia, heartland of the Qarmatian state

The Qarmatians (Arabic: قرامطة, Qarāmiṭa) were a militant Isma'ili Shia Muslim movement that established a state centred in Eastern Arabia (Bahrayn, in modern terms al-Ahsa) in 899 CE. They are remembered for their egalitarian communal organisation, their raids across Iraq and Arabia, and above all for their sack of Mecca in 930, during which they carried off the Black Stone of the Kaaba.

Origins

The movement takes its name from Hamdan Qarmat, an Isma'ili missionary active in the Sawad of Kufa in the 870s–880s. When the Isma'ili leadership at Salamiyya claimed the imamate for itself in 899, Hamdan's followers in Iraq and Bahrayn rejected the claim and kept to the expectation of the return of Muhammad ibn Isma'il as the Mahdi. Abu Sa'id al-Jannabi founded the Qarmatian state in Bahrayn in 899, and his son Abu Tahir al-Jannabi led its most famous campaigns.

Sack of Mecca

In January 930, during the Hajj season, Abu Tahir's forces stormed Mecca, massacred pilgrims and removed the Black Stone to their capital at al-Ahsa. The stone was returned only in 951, reportedly ransomed and restored to the Kaaba after being broken into fragments.

Society

The Qarmatian state at al-Ahsa was noted by travellers such as Nasir Khusraw for communal property arrangements, state mills and the absence of taxes, alongside a reputation, in hostile Sunni sources, for antinomianism. The state survived until 1077, when it was overthrown by the Uyunids with Seljuk support.
