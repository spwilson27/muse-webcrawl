Fetus in fetu

Fetus in fetu (FIF) is a rare congenital anomaly in which a malformed, parasitic fetus is found inside the body of its normally developed twin. The enclosed fetus typically lacks a functional brain and heart and depends entirely on its host twin for survival. It is most often discovered as an abdominal mass in infancy or early childhood.

Causes

Two main theories explain the condition: the parasitic twin theory, in which one twin becomes enclosed within the other early in development, and the theory that FIF is a highly organised form of teratoma. The presence of a vertebral column and organised limbs is generally used to distinguish fetus in fetu from a teratoma. Reported incidence is on the order of 1 in 500,000 live births, with fewer than 200 cases described in the literature.

Diagnosis and treatment

The condition is usually detected by imaging — ultrasound, CT or MRI — showing a fetiform mass, often in the retroperitoneum. Treatment is surgical removal of the mass, with an excellent prognosis in most cases. Rare cases have been reported in unusual locations, including intracranial sites.
