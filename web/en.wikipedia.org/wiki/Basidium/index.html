Basidium

A basidium (plural basidia) is a microscopic, spore-producing structure found on the hymenophore of fruiting bodies of basidiomycete fungi. It is the defining feature of the phylum Basidiomycota, which includes mushrooms, puffballs, rusts, and smuts. A mature basidium typically bears four sexual spores called basidiospores.

Structure and development

The basidium develops from a binucleate (dikaryotic) hyphal tip cell. Within it, the two nuclei fuse (karyogamy) to form a diploid nucleus, which immediately undergoes meiosis to produce four haploid nuclei. Slender outgrowths called sterigmata grow from the top of the basidium, and a basidiospore forms at the tip of each, into which a nucleus migrates. At maturity the spores are typically discharged forcibly — a mechanism called ballistochory — aided by the formation of Buller's drop at the spore's base.

Types

Basidia may be single-celled (holobasidia), as in most gilled mushrooms, or divided by septa into four cells (phragmobasidia), as in jelly fungi and rusts. In rusts and smuts the basidium (often called a promycelium) germinates from a thick-walled resting spore, the teliospore.
