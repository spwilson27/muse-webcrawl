Old Uyghur (simplified Chinese: 回鹘语; traditional Chinese: 回鶻語; pinyin: Huíhú yǔ) is an extinct Turkic language spoken in the Uyghur Khaganate and its successor states between roughly the 8th and 14th centuries. It developed from Old Turkic and is the direct ancestor, through the Western Yugur language, of one branch of the modern Uyghur people's linguistic heritage, though modern Uyghur itself descends mainly from the Karluk branch.

The language is known from a large body of manuscripts recovered from Turfan, Dunhuang and other Silk Road sites, covering Buddhist, Manichaean and secular texts including contracts, letters and medical treatises.

History

After the collapse of the Uyghur Khaganate in 840, Uyghur groups migrated southwest and founded the kingdom of Qocho (Gaochang) around Turfan and the Ganzhou Uyghur Kingdom in Gansu. In these states Old Uyghur flourished as a literary language. Under Mongol rule Uyghurs served as administrators and scribes, and the Uyghur script was adapted to write Mongolian. The language gradually died out as a spoken vernacular by about the 14th–15th centuries.

Writing system

Old Uyghur was most commonly written in the Old Uyghur alphabet, derived from the Sogdian alphabet and written vertically in columns after the Mongol period. Buddhist texts also used Brahmi and Tibetan scripts, while Manichaean texts sometimes employed the Manichaean script and Christian texts the Syriac Estrangela script.

Literature

The corpus includes translations of Buddhist sutras such as the Altun Yaruk (Golden Light Sutra), the Maitreya cycle Maitrisimit, Manichaean hymns and confessional texts such as the Khuastuanift, and secular documents that illuminate the social and economic life of the Turfan oasis.

Phonology and grammar

Old Uyghur preserved vowel harmony and an agglutinative morphology typical of Turkic languages. Compared with the Orkhon inscriptions it shows a reduction of primary long vowels in some dialects and characteristic sound changes, including the development of initial y- in certain words.
