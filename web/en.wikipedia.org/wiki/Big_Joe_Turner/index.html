Big Joe Turner

Joseph Vernon "Big Joe" Turner Jr. (May 18, 1911 – November 24, 1985) was an American blues shouter from Kansas City, Missouri. One of the pioneers of rock and roll, Turner was famous for his powerful voice and hits such as "Shake, Rattle and Roll" (1954), which preceded Bill Haley's famous cover version.

Career

Turner began singing in Kansas City nightclubs as a teenager, partnering with boogie-woogie pianist Pete Johnson. Their 1938 performance at Carnegie Hall's "From Spirituals to Swing" concert and the recording "Roll 'Em Pete" helped launch the boogie-woogie craze. Through the 1940s he recorded jump blues hits such as "Corrine, Corrina" and "Chains of Love".

Rock and roll era and legacy

Signing with Atlantic Records in 1951, Turner recorded a string of rhythm and blues hits that crossed into early rock and roll, most famously "Shake, Rattle and Roll". He was posthumously inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987 and is remembered as a bridge between blues shouting, jump blues, and rock and roll.
