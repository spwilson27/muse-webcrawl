Sediment transport

Placeholder image: a sediment-laden river in flood.

Sediment transport is the movement of solid particles (sediment), typically due to the action of gravity and the flow of a fluid such as water, wind, or ice. Sediment transport occurs in rivers, oceans, lakes, and deserts, and is fundamental to geomorphology, the study of how landscapes are shaped. The particles range in size from clay and silt through sand and gravel to boulders.

Modes of transport

Grains are transported in several modes. Bed load particles roll, slide, and saltate (bounce) along the bed. Finer material is carried in suspension by turbulence in the water column, while the finest clay may travel as wash load, passing through a reach without depositing. Dissolved material transported in solution is called the dissolved load. The ability of a flow to move sediment is described by concepts such as the Shields parameter and the Hjulström curve, which relate grain size to the flow velocity needed for erosion, transport, and deposition.

Importance

Sediment transport builds deltas, beaches, floodplains, and dunes, and its interruption by dams can cause downstream erosion and coastal land loss. Engineers must predict sediment movement to design bridges, harbours, reservoirs, and irrigation channels that neither silt up nor scour away.
