Central Japan Railway Company

Central Japan Railway Company
Native name: 東海旅客鉄道株式会社
Trade name: JR Central
Founded: 1 April 1987
Headquarters: Nagoya, Aichi, Japan
Services: Tokaido Shinkansen, conventional lines

The Central Japan Railway Company (東海旅客鉄道株式会社, Tōkai Ryokaku Tetsudō Kabushiki-gaisha), known as JR Central, is one of the seven companies of the Japan Railways Group. It was created in 1987 when the Japanese National Railways was privatised and split up.

Operations

JR Central's flagship service is the Tokaido Shinkansen, the busiest high-speed rail line in the world, linking Tokyo, Nagoya, Kyoto and Osaka (Shin-Osaka) and carrying some 150 million passengers a year. The company also operates conventional lines in the Tokai region, including the Chuo, Kansai, Taita and Gotemba lines, centred on Nagoya.

Maglev development

JR Central has developed the SCMaglev superconducting magnetic levitation system and is building the Chuo Shinkansen, a maglev line from Tokyo to Nagoya (later Osaka) designed for speeds of 500 km/h, largely in deep tunnels. Test runs on the Yamanashi test track have reached 603 km/h, a world record for rail vehicles.

Business

Profits from the Tokaido Shinkansen make JR Central one of the most profitable railway companies in the world. It is listed on the Tokyo and Nagoya stock exchanges and is a constituent of the Nikkei 225 index.
