Accademia delle Arti del Disegno

Formation13 January 1563
FoundersCosimo I de' Medici, Giorgio Vasari, Vincenzo Borghini
LocationFlorence, Italy
TypeAcademy of arts

The Accademia delle Arti del Disegno (Academy of the Arts of Drawing) in Florence, Italy, is the oldest art academy in the modern world, founded on 13 January 1563 by Cosimo I de' Medici on the initiative of Giorgio Vasari and Vincenzo Borghini. It elevated the status of artists from craftsmen in guilds to practitioners of a liberal art centred on disegno — drawing and design.

History

The Academy's first members included Michelangelo, who was elected a member in absentia and honoured as its symbolic head; after his death in 1564 his body lay in state under the Academy's auspices before burial in Santa Croce. Members taught anatomy, geometry, and drawing from life, and the Academy supervised much of Florence's artistic life. Galileo Galilei was admitted as a member in 1613 for his skill in drawing and perspective.

Modern academy

Reorganised several times over the centuries, the Academy today is an honorary body of artists, architects, art historians, and writers, organised in classes of painting, sculpture, architecture, art history, and related disciplines. It should not be confused with the Accademia di Belle Arti di Firenze, the teaching institution founded in 1784 from which Michelangelo's David is displayed in the Galleria dell'Accademia.
