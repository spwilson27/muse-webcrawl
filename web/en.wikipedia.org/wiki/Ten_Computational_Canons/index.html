Ten Computational Canons

The Ten Computational Canons (Chinese: 算經十書; pinyin: Suànjīng Shíshū) are a collection of ten ancient and medieval Chinese mathematical works compiled in the early Tang dynasty by the mathematician Li Chunfeng (602–670) and colleagues. Completed around 656 CE, the collection was prescribed as the official textbook for the imperial examinations in mathematics at the Imperial Academy (Guozijian).

Contents

The ten works are: (1) Zhou Bi Suan Jing (Arithmetical Classic of the Gnomon), (2) Jiu Zhang Suan Shu (Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art), (3) Haidao Suanjing (Sea Island Mathematical Manual) by Liu Hui, (4) Sunzi Suanjing (Master Sun's Mathematical Manual), (5) Wu Cao Suanjing (Computational Canon of the Five Administrative Sections), (6) Xiahou Yang Suanjing, (7) Zhang Qiujian Suanjing, (8) Wu Jing Suan Shu (Arithmetic in Five Classics), (9) Jigu Suanjing by Wang Xiaotong, and (10) Zhui Shu by Zu Chongzhi — the last was lost by the Northern Song dynasty and is absent from surviving editions.

Transmission

The canons were printed in 1084 during the Northern Song, among the earliest printed mathematical books in the world. Surviving editions descend from Ming-dynasty copies preserved in the Yongle Encyclopedia and collected by Dai Zhen in the 18th century.
