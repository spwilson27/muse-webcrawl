Neutropenia

SpecialtyHaematology
DefinitionAbsolute neutrophil count below 1.5×109/L
SevereBelow 0.5×109/L
ComplicationsFebrile neutropenia, sepsis

Neutropenia is an abnormally low concentration of neutrophils (a type of white blood cell) in the blood. Neutrophils make up the majority of circulating white blood cells and serve as the primary defence against infections by destroying bacteria and fungal cells. Neutropenia is defined by an absolute neutrophil count (ANC) below 1.5×109/L in adults, and is graded as mild (1.0–1.5), moderate (0.5–1.0), or severe (below 0.5×109/L); complete absence of neutrophils is called agranulocytosis.

Causes

Causes fall into decreased production, increased destruction, and redistribution. The most common cause in practice is chemotherapy, which suppresses the bone marrow; other drugs including clozapine and antithyroid agents can cause idiosyncratic agranulocytosis. Other causes include aplastic anaemia, bone marrow infiltration by malignancy, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, viral infections such as HIV and hepatitis, autoimmune disorders, congenital syndromes such as severe congenital neutropenia (Kostmann syndrome) and cyclic neutropenia, and the benign ethnic neutropenia seen in people of African and Middle Eastern ancestry, associated with the Duffy-null red cell phenotype.

Clinical features

Neutropenia itself produces no symptoms, but it predisposes to recurrent and severe bacterial and fungal infections, often with muted inflammatory signs because neutrophils are needed to form pus and many localising signs. Febrile neutropenia — fever with severe neutropenia, usually in a chemotherapy patient — is a medical emergency treated with immediate broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics.

Management

Management addresses the underlying cause, stops offending drugs, and may include granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF, filgrastim) to speed marrow recovery or prophylactically after chemotherapy. Patients are counselled on infection precautions during periods of severe neutropenia.
