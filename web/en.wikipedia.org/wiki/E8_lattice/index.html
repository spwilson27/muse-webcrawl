E8 lattice

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the E8 lattice is a special lattice in R8. It can be characterised as the unique positive-definite, even, unimodular lattice of rank 8. Its name derives from the fact that it is the root lattice of the E8 root system.

Construction

The E8 lattice can be constructed by taking all vectors in R8 whose coordinates are either all integers or all half-integers, and whose coordinates sum to an even integer. Equivalently, it can be built from two copies of the D8 lattice together with a "glue vector".

Properties

The lattice is even (every vector has even squared norm) and unimodular (its determinant is 1), and such lattices can exist only in dimensions divisible by 8. Its minimal non-zero vectors have squared length 2; there are exactly 240 of them, and these are precisely the roots of the E8 root system. The symmetry group of the lattice is the Weyl group of E8, of order 696,729,600. The E8 lattice gives the densest sphere packing and the highest possible kissing number in eight dimensions — facts proved rigorously by Maryna Viazovska in 2016 for sphere packing, work that contributed to her Fields Medal in 2022.

Applications

The E8 lattice appears in string theory, where the product E8 × E8 is the gauge structure of one form of heterotic string, and in coding theory and modular forms, where its theta function is an Eisenstein series. It also features in Garrett Lisi's proposed "exceptionally simple theory of everything", which drew on the E8 Lie group.
