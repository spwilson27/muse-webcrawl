Women's empowerment

Women's empowerment (or female empowerment) is the process by which women gain power and control over their own lives and acquire the ability to make strategic choices. It is commonly analysed in terms of economic, political, social, educational, and health dimensions, and is a central concept in international development policy, embodied in Sustainable Development Goal 5: achieving gender equality and empowering all women and girls.

Concepts and measurement

Development scholar Naila Kabeer defines empowerment as the expansion in people's ability to make strategic life choices in a context where this ability was previously denied, emphasising three interrelated dimensions: resources (material, human, and social), agency (the ability to define goals and act on them), and achievements (outcomes). Indices such as the Gender Development Index, the Gender Empowerment Measure, and the Women's Empowerment in Agriculture Index attempt to quantify progress across countries.

Economic empowerment

Economic approaches include expanding women's access to education, credit, land and property rights, decent work, and digital services. Microfinance programmes — from the Grameen Bank model onward — have targeted women on the grounds that income under women's control is more likely to be spent on children's health and schooling, though scholars debate the depth of empowerment microcredit alone produces. Legal reforms on inheritance, workplace discrimination, and parental leave are also widely promoted.

Political and social empowerment

Political empowerment encompasses women's suffrage, representation in legislatures (in some countries advanced by reserved seats and quotas), and participation in local government. Social dimensions include freedom from violence and early marriage, bodily autonomy, and shifts in norms about women's roles. Critics within feminism have warned that "empowerment" is sometimes reduced in corporate and development discourse to individual advancement, stripping it of its original collective and transformative meaning.
