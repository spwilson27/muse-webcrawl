Grothendieck spectral sequence

In mathematics, especially homological algebra, the Grothendieck spectral sequence is a tool for computing derived functors.

The Grothendieck spectral sequence, introduced by Alexander Grothendieck in his celebrated 1957 paper (the "Tôhoku paper"), is a spectral sequence that computes the derived functor of a composition of two functors from the derived functors of the two individual functors. It is one of the fundamental computational tools of modern homological algebra and algebraic geometry.

Statement

Let F and G be functors between abelian categories with enough injectives, where F maps injective objects of the first category to G-acyclic objects. Then the derived functors of the composite GF form a spectral sequence whose second page is built from the derived functors of G applied to the derived functors of F, converging to the derived functors of the composite.

Examples and applications

The most famous instances include the Leray spectral sequence for sheaf cohomology, the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence in group cohomology, and the local-to-global Ext spectral sequence. The Grothendieck spectral sequence also underlies the standard construction of higher direct image sheaves and the Leray spectral sequence in étale cohomology.

Construction

The standard construction uses a Cartan–Eilenberg resolution: an injective resolution that is at the same time a resolution by objects adapted to both functors. The resulting double complex yields two spectral sequences, one of which degenerates and identifies the target, while the other is the Grothendieck spectral sequence.
