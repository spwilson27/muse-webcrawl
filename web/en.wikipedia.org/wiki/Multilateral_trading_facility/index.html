Multilateral trading facility

A multilateral trading facility (MTF) is a European regulatory term for a self-regulated financial trading venue, defined under the Markets in Financial Instruments Directive (MiFID) as a multilateral system, operated by an investment firm or a market operator, which brings together multiple third-party buying and selling interests in financial instruments in accordance with non-discretionary rules.

Background

The MTF category was introduced by MiFID in 2007, which abolished the "concentration rule" that had required trades to be executed on national stock exchanges in many member states. This allowed new electronic venues to compete directly with incumbent exchanges for order flow in shares and other instruments. Prominent MTFs included Chi-X Europe, BATS Europe, and Turquoise; several later acquired regulated-market (exchange) status or merged with exchanges.

Regulation

MTFs are subject to requirements concerning fair and orderly trading, transparent rules for access, pre- and post-trade transparency, and systems resilience, but they are not subject to the full listing regime of a regulated market: an MTF operator cannot itself decide to admit instruments to official listing, and companies whose shares trade only on an MTF are not subject to the same disclosure obligations as listed companies. Under MiFID II (effective 2018), a new category, the organised trading facility (OTF), was introduced for non-equity instruments, and MTFs may be registered as SME growth markets.

Comparison

The nearest United States equivalent of an MTF is an alternative trading system (ATS), regulated by the Securities and Exchange Commission, which includes dark pools and electronic communication networks.
