The Diocese of London is a Church of England diocese covering much of Greater London north of the River Thames, along with a small part of Surrey. Its cathedral is St Paul's Cathedral in the City of London, and its bishop — the Bishop of London — ranks third in precedence in the Church of England, after the archbishops of Canterbury and York.

History

London was one of the earliest British sees: a Bishop of London is recorded attending the Council of Arles in 314. After the Anglo-Saxon settlement the see was refounded in 604, when Mellitus was consecrated bishop and St Paul's was established under the patronage of King Æthelberht of Kent. The medieval diocese covered London, Middlesex, Essex, and part of Hertfordshire; Essex was transferred to the new Diocese of Chelmsford in 1914.

Organization

Since 1979 the diocese has operated an area scheme, under which suffragan area bishops oversee five episcopal areas: Edmonton, Kensington, Stepney, Willesden, and the Two Cities (the cities of London and Westminster). The diocese contains several hundred parishes and is notable as one of the Church of England's largest and most demographically diverse, with congregations worshipping in many languages.
