The Bishop of London is the ordinary of the Church of England Diocese of London in the Province of Canterbury. The bishop is one of the most senior clerics in the Church of England, ranking third in precedence after the Archbishops of Canterbury and York, and is one of the five senior bishops who sit ex officio in the House of Lords as Lords Spiritual.

History

A Bishop of London named Restitutus is recorded as attending the Council of Arles in 314 AD, indicating an early Christian presence in Roman Londinium. The see was re-established by Augustine's mission when Mellitus was consecrated bishop in 604 and founded St Paul's Cathedral. The diocese historically covered London, Middlesex, Essex and parts of Hertfordshire; in the 19th century Essex and most of Hertfordshire were transferred to other dioceses, leaving the modern diocese covering Greater London north of the River Thames and a small part of Surrey.

The bishop's cathedral is St Paul's Cathedral, and the official residence is The Old Deanery near the cathedral. Since 2018 the Bishop of London has been Sarah Mullally, the first woman to hold the office; she was previously Bishop of Crediton and Chief Nursing Officer for England.

Role

The Bishop of London oversees some 400 parishes and is assisted by area bishops for Edmonton, Kensington, Stepney, Willesden and Islington. The bishop has also historically held responsibility as Dean of the Chapels Royal and often serves as a Privy Counsellor.
