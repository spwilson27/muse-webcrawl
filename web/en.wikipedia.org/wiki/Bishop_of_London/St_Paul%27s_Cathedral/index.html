St Paul's Cathedral is an Anglican cathedral in London, England, the seat of the Bishop of London and the mother church of the Diocese of London. Designed by Sir Christopher Wren after the Great Fire of London destroyed the medieval cathedral, it is one of the most famous landmarks in the United Kingdom.

History

A cathedral dedicated to St Paul has stood on Ludgate Hill — the highest point in the City of London — since 604 AD. The present building, Wren's masterpiece in English Baroque style, was constructed between 1675 and 1710; its dome, rising 111 metres, dominated the London skyline for centuries. The famous photograph of the dome surrounded by smoke during the Blitz made St Paul's a symbol of British endurance in the Second World War.

Features and events

Inside are the Whispering Gallery, the tombs of Nelson and Wellington, and the national memorials of many notable figures. The cathedral has hosted the funerals of Churchill and Thatcher, the wedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer in 1981, and services of national thanksgiving.
