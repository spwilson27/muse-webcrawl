Bishop of Oxford

The Bishop of Oxford is the diocesan bishop of the Church of England Diocese of Oxford, which covers Oxfordshire, Berkshire and Buckinghamshire. Considered alongside the Bishop of London, the Bishop of Oxford is among the senior bishops of the Church of England: after the Archbishops of Canterbury and York and the Bishops of London, Durham and Winchester, the Bishop of Oxford is one of the first to take a seat in the House of Lords as a Lord Spiritual once seniority permits.

Role and history

The see was founded in 1542 by Henry VIII, with its cathedral at Osney Abbey before moving to Christ Church, Oxford. The diocese expanded in the 19th century to include Berkshire and Buckinghamshire, making it one of the largest Church of England dioceses by area. Because of its size, the bishop is assisted by area bishops of Dorchester, Buckingham and Reading.

Relations with London

The bishops of Oxford and London frequently feature together in lists of precedence, royal commissions and national church bodies, and both are among the small group of diocesan bishops with guaranteed early access to the Lords Spiritual. Historically, the see of Oxford has often been a stepping stone: several bishops have translated between Oxford and other senior sees.
