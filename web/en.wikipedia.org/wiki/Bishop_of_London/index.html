The Bishop of London is the ordinary of the Church of England's Diocese of London in the Province of Canterbury. The office is one of the most senior in the Church of England: after the archbishops of Canterbury and York, the Bishop of London ranks third and is one of the five senior bishops who sit in the House of Lords as Lords Spiritual regardless of seniority. The bishop's cathedral is St Paul's Cathedral, and the diocese covers most of Greater London north of the Thames.

History

A bishop named Restitutus is recorded as attending the Council of Arles in 314, suggesting an organized church in Roman London. The see was refounded in 604 when Mellitus was consecrated by Augustine of Canterbury as part of the Gregorian mission, and St Paul's was founded as its cathedral. Medieval and early modern holders included figures of national importance, such as William Laud (1628–1633), later Archbishop of Canterbury. Since the Victorian era the bishop has delegated much day-to-day oversight to area (suffragan) bishops within the diocese.

Modern role

The Bishop of London serves as Dean of the Chapels Royal and is traditionally the bishop who officiates at many royal ceremonies at St Paul's. Sarah Mullally, previously Chief Nursing Officer for England, became the 133rd Bishop of London in 2018—the first woman to hold the office—and was subsequently named Archbishop of Canterbury-designate following Justin Welby's resignation.
