Supreme Leader of Iran

The Supreme Leader of Iran (Persian: رهبر معظم ایران, rahbar-e mo'azzam-e irān), also referred to as the Supreme Leader of the Islamic Revolution or simply the Leader, is the head of state and the highest political and religious authority of the Islamic Republic of Iran — above the president. The office was established by the Constitution of Iran in 1979 under Ruhollah Khomeini's doctrine of the Guardianship of the Islamic Jurist (Velayat-e faqih).

Powers

The Supreme Leader is commander-in-chief of the armed forces, including the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps; appoints the head of the judiciary, the commanders of the armed forces, and the clerical members of the Guardian Council; appoints the head of state broadcasting; declares war and peace; and can dismiss the president after a court conviction or parliamentary vote. He sets the general policies of the state, supervises their execution, and issues decrees for national referendums. The Leader is chosen — and in theory can be dismissed — by the Assembly of Experts, an elected body of Islamic scholars.

History

Khomeini, leader of the 1979 revolution, held the office until his death on 3 June 1989. The Assembly of Experts then selected Ali Khamenei, then president, despite his relatively junior clerical rank; a constitutional revision the same year removed the requirement that the Leader be a marja' (source of emulation) and strengthened the office's administrative powers. The succession question — and the extent of the Leader's authority relative to elected institutions — remains a central issue of Iranian politics.
