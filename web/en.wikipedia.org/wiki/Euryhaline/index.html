Euryhaline

Euryhaline organisms are able to tolerate a wide range of salinities in their environment. The word derives from Greek roots meaning "wide salt". Euryhaline species include many fish that move between fresh water and the sea, such as salmon, eels, and bull sharks, as well as estuarine invertebrates and plants. The opposite condition, tolerance of only a narrow salinity range, is called stenohaline.

Mechanisms

Euryhaline animals regulate the salt and water balance of their body fluids through osmoregulation. In fresh water they excrete excess water and retain salts; in seawater they drink, excrete excess salt through specialised chloride cells in the gills, and conserve water. These adjustments involve changes in gill ion transporters and hormones such as prolactin and cortisol, and switching between environments can take hours to days.

Examples

Notable euryhaline organisms include Atlantic salmon and other anadromous fish, the European eel, Mozambique tilapia, molly fish, many crabs, and mangrove plants. Their tolerance lets them exploit productive estuarine and intertidal habitats that stenohaline competitors cannot enter.
