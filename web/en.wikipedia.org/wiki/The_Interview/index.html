The Interview is a 2014 American political satire comedy film directed by Seth Rogen and Evan Goldberg, starring James Franco and Rogen as a tabloid television host and his producer who are recruited by the CIA to assassinate North Korean leader Kim Jong-un during a scheduled interview.

Sony Pictures hack and release controversy

In November 2014, Sony Pictures suffered a massive cyberattack by a group calling itself the "Guardians of Peace," which leaked internal documents and emails; threats against cinemas showing the film led major chains to drop it and Sony initially cancelled the theatrical release. The U.S. government attributed the hack to North Korea, which denied responsibility. The decision to pull the film drew criticism from figures including President Barack Obama, and Sony released the film on digital platforms on December 24, 2014, with a limited theatrical run beginning Christmas Day — making it a landmark case for day-and-date digital releasing.

Reception

The film received mixed reviews but earned over $40 million in digital rentals and sales in its first weeks, becoming Sony's most successful online release at the time.
