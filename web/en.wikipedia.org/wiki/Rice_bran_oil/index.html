Rice bran oil

SourceBran and germ of rice (Oryza sativa)

Rice bran oil is the oil extracted from the hard outer brown layer of rice (the bran) together with the rice germ. It is widely used as a cooking oil in Japan, India, China and other Asian countries, and is valued for its mild flavour, high smoke point and balanced fatty acid profile.

Production

Rice bran is a by-product of milling brown rice into white rice. Because the bran contains active lipase enzymes that rapidly hydrolyse the oil into free fatty acids, it must be stabilised soon after milling — typically by heating — before the oil is extracted with solvents such as hexane and then refined. Global production is on the order of a million tonnes per year, with India the largest producer.

Composition and properties

Rice bran oil is unusual among vegetable oils for its content of γ-oryzanol, a mixture of ferulic acid esters of sterols and triterpene alcohols found in few other foods, along with vitamin E compounds (tocopherols and tocotrienols) and plant sterols. Its relatively high smoke point makes it suitable for stir-frying and deep-frying, and its oxidative stability is considered good for a vegetable oil.

Health aspects

Studies have associated rice bran oil and its oryzanol fraction with modest reductions in LDL cholesterol, attributed in part to phytosterols reducing cholesterol absorption. It has a more balanced ratio of saturated, monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids than many common cooking oils.

Other uses

Beyond cooking, rice bran oil is used in cosmetics and skin-care products, in horse and pet feeds as a source of fat and shine-promoting compounds, and industrially as a source of waxes (rice bran wax) obtained during refining.
