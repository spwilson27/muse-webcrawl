Port of Ningbo-Zhoushan

The Port of Ningbo-Zhoushan is a merged deep-water port complex in Zhejiang Province, China, formed in 2006 from the ports of Ningbo and Zhoushan on the coast of the East China Sea, south of Shanghai. It is the busiest port in the world by cargo tonnage, handling well over one billion tonnes of cargo a year, and ranks among the top three container ports globally, with throughput exceeding 35 million TEU annually.

Geography and terminals

The port's terminals spread across the Ningbo waterfront and the Zhoushan archipelago, including the container terminals at Beilun, Chuanshan and Meishan and the major crude-oil and ore facilities at Daxie and Jintang. A network of cross-sea bridges, including the Hangzhou Bay Bridge, links the port to its Yangtze Delta hinterland.

History

Ningbo has been a trading port since the Tang dynasty, when it was a terminus of the maritime Silk Road. The modern harbour at Beilun was developed from the late 1970s to relieve Shanghai and serve Zhejiang's export industries; administrative merger with Zhoushan created a single giant operator under the Zhejiang Seaport Group.
