Ghent

Ghent (Dutch: Gent) is a city and municipality in the Flemish Region of Belgium. It is the capital and largest city of East Flanders province, with a population of roughly 265,000, and a major port at the confluence of the Scheldt and Leie rivers.

History

Ghent grew around two abbeys founded in the 7th century. In the Middle Ages it became one of the largest and richest cities in northern Europe thanks to the cloth trade, famously resisting its counts and Burgundian and Habsburg rulers. Charles V was born there in 1500. The Ghent Altarpiece (1432) by the Van Eyck brothers hangs in Saint Bavo Cathedral.

Today

Ghent University (founded 1817) and the port sustain the modern city. Landmarks include Gravensteen castle, the medieval belfry (a UNESCO site), and the Graslei waterfront. The city is known for the Gentse Feesten festival and pioneering car-free centre policies.
