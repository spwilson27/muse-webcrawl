Anointing of the Sick

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anointing of the Sick, known in the Roman Catholic Church as a sacrament for the seriously ill, is a ritual of healing appropriate not only for physical illness but also for mental and spiritual suffering. In the Catholic Church it is administered by a priest, who anoints the sick person with oil blessed by a bishop, praying for their recovery and forgiveness. The sacrament was formerly known as Extreme Unction, one of the "last rites".

Biblical basis

The practice rests chiefly on the Epistle of James (5:14–15): "Is anyone among you sick? Let them call the elders of the church to pray over them and anoint them with oil in the name of the Lord." Mark 6:13 also describes the apostles anointing the sick with oil.

Catholic practice

Following the reforms of the Second Vatican Council, the 1972 apostolic constitution Sacram Unctionem Infirmorum renamed the sacrament from Extreme Unction to Anointing of the Sick, emphasising that it is not reserved for those at the point of death but for anyone in danger due to sickness or old age. Its effects are held to include the forgiveness of sins, spiritual strengthening, and sometimes physical recovery.

Other traditions

Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox churches celebrate the mystery of Holy Unction, often communally during Holy Week. Some Lutheran, Anglican, and other Protestant churches also practise anointing of the sick as a rite of healing, though usually not counted among the sacraments.
