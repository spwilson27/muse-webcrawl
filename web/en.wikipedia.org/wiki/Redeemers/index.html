Redeemers

Redeemers
Era: Reconstruction era, United States (1865–1877 and after)
Affiliation: Democratic Party (Southern conservatives)
Goal: End Reconstruction; restore white Democratic rule in the South

The Redeemers were a political coalition in the Southern United States during the Reconstruction era who sought to overthrow the Republican governments established after the American Civil War, ostensibly to "redeem" the South from alleged corruption and misrule. They were the Southern wing of the Democratic Party, largely former planters, businessmen, and professionals.

Rise

After 1870, Redeemer Democrats regained control of state governments one by one — Tennessee, Georgia, Virginia, and Texas first — through a combination of electoral organising, economic pressure on black and white Republicans, and outright violence and intimidation by paramilitary groups such as the Ku Klux Klan, the White League, and the Red Shirts. The process culminated in the Compromise of 1877, when the last federal troops were withdrawn from the South and the remaining Republican governments in South Carolina, Louisiana, and Florida fell.

Rule

In power, Redeemer governments cut taxes and public spending, reduced funding for public schools and social services, rewrote state constitutions, favoured railroads and corporations, and relied on convict leasing. Though they presented themselves as reformers, their lasting legacy was the consolidation of white supremacy: from the 1890s their successors erected the Jim Crow system of segregation and disenfranchisement that endured until the civil rights movement of the 1960s.

Historiography

Early 20th-century historians of the Dunning School portrayed the Redeemers sympathetically as rescuers from corrupt "carpetbag" rule; modern scholarship emphasises the central role of racial violence and the destruction of interracial democracy.
