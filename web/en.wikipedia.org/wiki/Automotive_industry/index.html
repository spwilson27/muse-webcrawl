The automotive industry comprises a wide range of companies and organisations involved in the design, development, manufacturing, marketing, selling, repairing and modification of motor vehicles. It is one of the world's largest economic sectors by revenue and a major employer, directly and through its supply chains for steel, glass, rubber, electronics and energy.

History

The industry began in the late 19th century with pioneers such as Benz, Daimler and Peugeot. Henry Ford's moving assembly line (1913) cut the cost of the Model T and made car ownership mass-market, while Alfred Sloan at General Motors introduced annual model changes and a brand ladder. After 1945 Japanese makers, led by Toyota, developed lean production (just-in-time manufacturing), which reshaped factories worldwide. Since the 2010s the industry has been transformed by electrification, with battery-electric vehicles taking a growing share of sales led by Tesla and Chinese manufacturers such as BYD, alongside software-defined vehicles and advanced driver assistance.

Structure

Original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) assemble vehicles from components supplied by tiers of parts makers (Bosch, Denso, Magna, ZF and others). Dealerships handle most new-vehicle retail in North America, while direct sales models have grown with electric vehicles. Closely linked sectors include automotive finance, insurance, fuel retail and the aftermarket for parts and repair.
