Diabetes mellitus type 2

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Type 2 diabetes is the common form of diabetes — about 90% of cases — in which the body both responds poorly to Insulin (insulin resistance) and gradually produces too little of it. It develops over years, usually in adults and strongly linked to excess body weight, inactivity, age and genetic background, and it is a major cause of heart disease, kidney failure, blindness and amputation.

Course and management

Because onset is gradual, many people are diagnosed by routine blood tests (HbA1c or fasting glucose) before symptoms. Management begins with diet, activity and weight loss — substantial weight loss can put early type 2 diabetes into remission — and proceeds through medicines: metformin first-line, then classes including sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists (the last also used against obesity), and insulin when needed.

Scale and prevention

Diabetes overall affects well over 500 million adults worldwide (IDF estimates), most with type 2, and numbers are rising fastest in low- and middle-income countries. Prevention trials show lifestyle programmes cut progression from prediabetes by more than half. Screening and tight control of glucose, blood pressure and lipids reduce complications.
