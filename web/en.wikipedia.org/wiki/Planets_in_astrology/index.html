Planets in astrology

In astrology, the planets represent fundamental drives, energies, and functions in human experience. Astrological usage differs from the astronomical one: the Sun and Moon (the "lights") are counted among the planets, as are the modern outer planets Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto, while the Earth is not counted, since charts are cast from the native's viewpoint on Earth.

The planets and their meanings

Traditionally each planet rules one or two zodiac signs and is associated with a sphere of life: the Sun with vitality and self-expression, the Moon with emotion and habit, Mercury with communication and the mind, Venus with love and values, Mars with drive and conflict, Jupiter with growth and belief, and Saturn with discipline, limitation, and time. The modern planets are associated with collective and generational themes: Uranus with change, Neptune with dreams and dissolution, and Pluto with power and transformation.

Dignities and aspects

A planet's condition is judged by its sign placement, house placement, aspects to other planets, and essential dignities such as domicile, exaltation, detriment, and fall. Retrograde motion is also given interpretive significance.
