Kevin David Mitnick (August 6, 1963 – July 16, 2023) was an American computer security consultant, author and convicted hacker. One of the most famous hackers of the 20th century, he was arrested in 1995 after a highly publicised FBI manhunt and served five years in prison for computer and wire fraud. After his release he became a prominent security consultant and author, best known for popularising the art of social engineering.

Early hacking

Mitnick began manipulating systems as a teenager in Los Angeles, bypassing the punch-card system on city buses and exploring phone phreaking. In 1979 he gained unauthorised access to Digital Equipment Corporation's network, an offence for which he was convicted in 1988 and sentenced to a year in prison followed by supervised release. He famously relied less on technical exploits than on social engineering — persuading employees to reveal passwords and access codes.

Fugitive years and arrest

After allegedly violating his supervised release in 1992, Mitnick became a fugitive. During more than two years on the run he cloned cellular phones and accessed systems belonging to companies including Motorola, Nokia and Sun Microsystems. The pursuit intensified after he broke into the computer of security researcher Tsutomu Shimomura, who helped the FBI track him to an apartment in Raleigh, North Carolina, where he was arrested on February 15, 1995.

Imprisonment and release

Mitnick pleaded guilty to computer fraud and related charges and was released in January 2000, having spent about five years in custody, including eight months in solitary confinement — a restriction he later said stemmed from a prosecutor's claim that he could "start a nuclear war by whistling into a pay phone". His case became a cause célèbre for the hacker community, with a "Free Kevin" campaign criticising the severity of his treatment.

Later career

Barred from computers initially, Mitnick rebuilt his career as a "white hat" consultant, founding Mitnick Security Consulting and serving as chief hacking officer of the security awareness firm KnowBe4. His books include The Art of Deception (2002), The Art of Intrusion (2005) and the memoir Ghost in the Wires (2011). He died in 2023 of pancreatic cancer.
