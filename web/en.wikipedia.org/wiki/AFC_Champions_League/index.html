AFC Champions League

The AFC Champions League, known since 2024 as the AFC Champions League Elite, is an annual club football competition organised by the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) and contested by the top clubs from Asia's highest-ranked leagues. It is the premier club tournament in Asian football, comparable to the UEFA Champions League in Europe.

History

The competition began in 1967 as the Asian Champion Club Tournament. It was relaunched in 2002–03 under the AFC Champions League name after a merger with the Asian Cup Winners' Cup and Asian Super Cup. In 2024 the AFC restructured its club competitions into three tiers, renaming the top tier the AFC Champions League Elite, with 24 teams in a league-stage format divided into West and East regions. Al-Hilal of Saudi Arabia is the most successful club, with four titles. The winner qualifies for the FIFA Intercontinental Cup and the FIFA Club World Cup.

Format

Under the Elite format, teams play a single-leg league phase within their region before knockout rounds; the final stages from the quarter-finals onward have been hosted in a centralised location in Saudi Arabia in the first editions of the new format.
