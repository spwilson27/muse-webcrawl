Speedrunning

Speedrunning
ActivityCompleting video games (or parts of them) as fast as possible
Common categoriesAny%, 100%, glitchless, low%

Speedrunning is the act of playing a video game, or a section of a video game, with the goal of completing it as fast as possible. Speedruns are often done to improve personal times, compete on leaderboards, or entertain audiences, and they frequently exploit the game's programming, including glitches, sequence breaks, and unintended mechanics, unless the category forbids them. Speedrunners may also use tool-assisted speedruns (TAS), in which emulation tools such as frame-by-frame input and savestates are used to construct a theoretically perfect run.

History

Competitive fast play dates to early video game high-score culture and magazines such as Nintendo Power. The practice took shape in the 1990s around Doom, whose demo-recording feature enabled players to share runs; competitions were hosted by the COMPET-N site from 1994. Quake runs led to the famous Quake done Quick series (1997), and the Speed Demos Archive (founded 1998) centralized records. The 2003 Super Mario 64 and Mario Kart video compilations, and the 2004 Metroid-inspired growth of forums, broadened the hobby. In the 2010s, live streaming on Twitch and the rise of the Games Done Quick charity marathons brought speedrunning to a mass audience; the community leaderboard site speedrun.com launched in 2013.

Categories and methods

Common categories include any% (finish by any means), 100% (complete all content), glitchless, and low% (collect as little as possible). Timing may be measured in real time (RTA) or in-game time, and runs must follow community rules, often requiring video proof and specific hardware or emulator standards. Random-number manipulation, wrong warps, out-of-bounds movement, and arbitrary code execution are among the advanced techniques discovered by communities through painstaking research.

Events

Games Done Quick (GDQ), held twice yearly as Awesome Games Done Quick and Summer Games Done Quick, has raised tens of millions of dollars for charities such as the Prevent Cancer Foundation and Doctors Without Borders. Other events include the European Speedrunner Assembly and countless online races.
