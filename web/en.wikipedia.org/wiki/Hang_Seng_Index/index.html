Hang Seng Index

The Hang Seng Index (HSI) is a freefloat-adjusted market-capitalization-weighted stock market index in Hong Kong. It is used to record and monitor daily changes of the largest companies of the Hong Kong stock market and is the main indicator of overall market performance in Hong Kong. Its constituent companies are drawn from sectors such as finance, utilities, property and commerce and industry.

History

The index was created by banker Stanley Kwan of Hang Seng Bank, after which it is named ("Hang Seng" means "ever-growing" in Cantonese). It was launched on 24 November 1969, with a base of 100 points dated to 31 July 1964. It is maintained by Hang Seng Indexes Company Limited, a subsidiary of Hang Seng Bank.

Composition and review

Constituent stocks are reviewed quarterly. The index expanded over time from its original 33 companies to include mainland Chinese enterprises listed in Hong Kong, such as H shares and red chips; a 2021 reform set a target of expanding the index to 100 constituents and capped individual stock weightings. Well-known constituents have included HSBC, Tencent, Alibaba and China Mobile.
