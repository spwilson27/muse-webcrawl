Bharatanatyam

Bharatanatyam
OriginTemples of Tamil Nadu, India
MusicCarnatic music
InstrumentsMridangam, violin, veena, nattuvangam (cymbals)
RelatedSadir, Natya Shastra

Bharatanatyam is a major form of Indian classical dance that originated in the temples of Tamil Nadu. It is the oldest classical dance form of India and is described in the ancient Sanskrit text Natya Shastra. Traditionally performed by women in Hindu temples, the dance combines fixed upper torso, bent legs and turned-out feet with intricate hand gestures (mudras), facial expressions, and footwork.

History

The dance's roots lie in the temple tradition of Sadir, performed by devadasis. Under colonial-era reform movements and stigmatization, the practice declined and was banned in the Madras Presidency in 1947. In the early 20th century, figures such as E. Krishna Iyer and Rukmini Devi Arundale — who founded the Kalakshetra Foundation in 1936 — revived and popularized the form on the public stage, while hereditary teachers such as the members of the Tanjore Quartet lineage codified its repertoire.

Repertoire and technique

A traditional recital (margam) proceeds through items such as alarippu, jatiswaram, shabdam, varnam (the centrepiece), padams and javalis, and concludes with a tillana. Dancers wear a silk sari in a distinctive style with pleated fans, temple jewellery, and ankle bells (ghungroo). The dance is accompanied by Carnatic music, with the teacher (nattuvanar) conducting with hand cymbals.

Recognition

Bharatanatyam is one of the eight major Indian classical dance forms recognized by the Sangeet Natak Akademi. It is taught and performed worldwide, and a dancer's formal debut is called an arangetram.
