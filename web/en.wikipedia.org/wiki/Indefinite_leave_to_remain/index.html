Indefinite leave to remain

Indefinite leave to remain (ILR) is an immigration status granted to a person who does not hold the right of abode in the United Kingdom, but who has been admitted to the UK without any time limit on their stay and who is free to take up employment or study without restriction. It is the principal form of permanent settlement under UK immigration law and is broadly analogous to permanent residency in other countries.

Eligibility and conditions

ILR is typically granted after a qualifying period of lawful residence — commonly five years on work or family routes, or ten years under the long-residence rule — subject to requirements including the Life in the UK test and English language proficiency. Holders lose ILR after a continuous absence from the UK of more than two years, and the status may be revoked in cases of serious criminality. ILR holders are generally eligible to apply for British citizenship after a further period, usually 12 months.

Related statuses

European Union citizens and their families formerly obtained analogous rights under EU law; after Brexit, the EU Settlement Scheme grants "settled status", which is similar in effect. Indefinite leave to enter (ILE) is the equivalent status granted to applicants applying from outside the UK.
