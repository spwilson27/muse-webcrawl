Murder hole

A murder hole or murder-hole (sometimes called a meurtrière) is a hole in the ceiling of a gateway or passageway in a fortification through which the defenders could fire, throw or pour harmful substances on attackers trapped below. Murder holes are commonly found above castle gatehouses, in the vaults of entrance passages between an outer and inner portcullis, and in ceiling chambers above doorways.

Use

An entrance passage fitted with portcullises at both ends could trap a party of attackers in a confined space, whereupon defenders above would drop stones and rubble, thrust spears, or pour water to douse fires set against the gates. Popular tradition associates murder holes with pouring boiling oil or molten lead, but such substances were expensive and scarce; heated sand, quicklime, stones and water were far more practical.

Related features

Murder holes are related to the machicolation, an opening in the floor of a projecting parapet, and to arrow slits and loopholes through which defenders could shoot while remaining under cover.
