Copeland–Erdős constant

The Copeland–Erdős constant is the concatenation of "0." with the base-10 representations of the prime numbers in order. Its value is approximately 0.235711131719232931374143677109… It is named after Arthur Herbert Copeland and Paul Erdős, who proved in 1946 that it is a normal number in base 10.

Normality

A number is normal in base 10 if every finite string of digits occurs in its decimal expansion with the expected limiting frequency. Copeland and Erdős showed that concatenating the terms of any sufficiently dense increasing sequence of integers — including the primes — yields a normal number, generalizing the earlier result for Champernowne's constant, formed from all positive integers.

Related results

Because every normal number is irrational, the constant is irrational. Whether it is transcendental is unknown. Analogous constants formed from other sequences, and in other bases, have also been studied; normality of familiar constants such as π and e remains unproved.
