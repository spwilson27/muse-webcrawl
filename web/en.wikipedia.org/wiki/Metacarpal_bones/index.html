Metacarpal bones

The metacarpal bones or metacarpus, also known as the palm bones, are the five long bones in the hand between the carpal bones of the wrist and the phalanges of the fingers. They form the skeletal framework of the palm. Each metacarpal consists of a base (proximally), a shaft (body) and a rounded head (distally) that articulates with a proximal phalanx, forming the knuckles.

Numbering

The metacarpals are numbered I to V from the thumb side (radial) to the little finger side (ulnar). The first metacarpal, of the thumb, is the shortest and stoutest and sits rotated relative to the others, enabling opposition. The fifth metacarpal is notable clinically for the "boxer's fracture" of its neck, usually sustained by punching.

Articulations and attachments

Proximally the metacarpals articulate with the distal row of carpal bones at the carpometacarpal joints; the interosseous muscles arise between their shafts, and the heads are joined by the deep transverse metacarpal ligament.
