Carlos I (28 September 1863 – 1 February 1908), nicknamed "the Diplomat" and "the Oceanographer", was King of Portugal from 1889 until his assassination in 1908. His reign saw mounting political instability, the humiliation of the 1890 British Ultimatum, and the growth of republican opposition.

Reign

Carlos succeeded his father Luís I in 1889. Within months, Britain's 1890 Ultimatum forced Portugal to abandon its claim to the territory between Angola and Mozambique (the "Pink Map"), a national humiliation that badly damaged the monarchy's prestige. Chronic government instability, financial crisis and corruption scandals followed. In 1906 Carlos appointed João Franco as prime minister, who governed increasingly dictatorially, dissolving parliament and suppressing dissent.

Oceanography and art

An accomplished painter and a pioneering oceanographer, Carlos conducted a series of scientific campaigns aboard the royal yachts Amélia from 1896, studying deep-sea fauna off the Portuguese coast. His oceanographic work was exhibited internationally, and his watercolours and pastels of Portuguese life remain admired.

Assassination

On 1 February 1908, returning to Lisbon, the royal family's open carriage was attacked in the Terreiro do Paço by republican gunmen. Carlos and his elder son, Crown Prince Luís Filipe, were killed; Prince Manuel was wounded and succeeded as Manuel II, the last king of Portugal. The monarchy fell two years later in the revolution of 5 October 1910.
