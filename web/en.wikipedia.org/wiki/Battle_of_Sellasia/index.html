Battle of Sellasia

Date222 BC
LocationSellasia, Laconia, Greece
ResultDecisive Achaean–Macedonian victory; end of the Cleomenean War
BelligerentsAchaean League and Macedon vs. Sparta
CommandersAntigonus III Doson; Philopoemen vs. Cleomenes III

The Battle of Sellasia was fought in the summer of 222 BC between the forces of the Achaean League and its ally Antigonus III Doson of Macedon, and the army of Sparta under King Cleomenes III. The decisive allied victory ended the Cleomenean War and extinguished the social revolution Cleomenes had carried through in Sparta.

Background

Cleomenes had reformed Sparta — abolishing debts, redistributing land, restoring the agoge — and built an army that defeated the Achaeans repeatedly. The Achaean leader Aratus, preferring Macedonian domination to Spartan-led revolution, called in Antigonus Doson, who invaded the Peloponnese in 223 BC and methodically stripped away Cleomenes' allies and conquests.

The battle

Cleomenes took a strong position at Sellasia, guarding the road to Sparta, fortifying the hills (Olympus and Euas) flanking the Eurotas valley road. Antigonus attacked both hills simultaneously: after hard fighting, the Achaean cavalry under the young Philopoemen distinguished itself on one wing, while a flank attack by Illyrian troops dislodged the Spartans opposite them. The Spartan phalanx in the valley, attacked front and flank, was destroyed. Polybius reports that of 6,000 Spartans, only 200 survived.

Aftermath

Cleomenes fled to Egypt, where he died in 219 BC. Antigonus entered Sparta — the first foreign conqueror to do so — but treated the city with restraint, abolishing Cleomenes' reforms and restoring the old order under Macedonian supremacy. The battle ended Sparta's last bid for power in Greece.
