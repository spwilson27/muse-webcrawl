Benedictines

The Benedictines, officially the Order of Saint Benedict (Latin: Ordo Sancti Benedicti, OSB), are a mainly contemplative monastic order of the Catholic Church for men and for women who follow the Rule of Saint Benedict.

Founded6th century, Monte Cassino
FounderBenedict of Nursia
MottoOra et labora — pray and work

Rule and life

Benedict's Rule (c. 530) balances prayer, work and study, with vows of stability, obedience and conversion of life. Monasteries became centres of learning, agriculture and manuscript preservation in medieval Europe.

History

Benedictine houses spread across Europe, gave rise to reforms such as Cluny and the Cistercians, survived dissolutions and revived in the 19th century. Today Benedictines run schools, universities and parishes worldwide. See Monasticism.
