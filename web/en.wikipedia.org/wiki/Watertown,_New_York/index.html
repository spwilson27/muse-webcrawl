Watertown, New York

Watertown is a city in and the county seat of Jefferson County, New York, United States. Located in the North Country region near Lake Ontario and the Thousand Islands, it had a population of roughly 25,000 at the 2020 census. It is the principal city of a small metropolitan area and lies close to Fort Drum, a major U.S. Army installation.

History

Settlement began around 1800, with pioneers attracted by the water power of the Black River, whose falls drove mills and factories. Watertown grew into a manufacturing centre producing paper, machinery, and the portable typewriter carriage return mechanisms; industrialists such as Roswell P. Flower, a future governor of New York, had local ties. The city's Public Square was a notable early example of American town planning, and the Paddock Arcade (1850) is among the oldest continuously operating enclosed shopping arcades in the United States.

Geography and climate

Watertown lies on the Black River southeast of Lake Ontario. It experiences heavy lake-effect snow and is frequently cited among the snowiest small cities in the eastern United States.

Fort Drum connection

The economy is closely tied to nearby Fort Drum, home of the 10th Mountain Division, one of the most deployed divisions in the U.S. Army; the post's expansion in the 1980s transformed the local housing and retail landscape.
