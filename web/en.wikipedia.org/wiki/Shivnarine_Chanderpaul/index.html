Shivnarine Chanderpaul

Shivnarine Chanderpaul (born 16 August 1974) is a Guyanese cricket coach and former captain of the West Indies cricket team. Widely regarded as one of the greatest batsmen of his generation, he is the second-highest run-scorer for the West Indies in Test cricket, behind only Brian Lara, and one of a small number of players to score more than 10,000 runs in both Tests and One Day Internationals.

Career

Chanderpaul made his Test debut against England in Georgetown in March 1994 and his ODI debut later that year. Over a career spanning more than two decades, he played 164 Tests, scoring 11,867 runs at an average of about 51, with 30 centuries, and 268 ODIs, scoring 8,778 runs. He captained the West Indies in 14 Tests and 16 ODIs between 2005 and 2006.

He was named ICC Player of the Year in 2008, a year in which his obdurate batting repeatedly rescued the West Indies, and he was among Wisden's Cricketers of the Year. He retired from international cricket in 2016 and was inducted into the ICC Cricket Hall of Fame in 2022.

Style

Chanderpaul was a left-handed batsman famous for his distinctive crab-like, open-chested stance, in which he faced the bowler with both feet pointing down the pitch before shuffling into a more orthodox position as the ball was bowled. His batting was characterized by patience, concentration, and defensive resilience, making him exceptionally difficult to dismiss. He also bowled occasional leg spin.

Personal life

Born in Unity Village, Guyana, to Indo-Guyanese parents, Chanderpaul came from a fishing family. His son Tagenarine Chanderpaul has also played Test cricket for the West Indies, and father and son both appeared in first-class cricket.
