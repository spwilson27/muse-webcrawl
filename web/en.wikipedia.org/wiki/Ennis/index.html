Ennis

Ennis (Irish: Inis, "island") is the county town of County Clare in the mid-west of Ireland, on the River Fergus just north of where it enters the Shannon estuary. With a population of around 27,000, it is the largest town in County Clare and one of the larger towns in Ireland. Ennis is well known as a centre for traditional Irish music, and in 2021 it was ranked among Ireland's friendliest places in national surveys; it has also repeatedly won Ireland's Tidy Towns competition.

History

The town grew around a Franciscan friary founded in the 1240s under the patronage of the O'Brien kings of Thomond, on an island site in the River Fergus (hence the name). Ennis Friary became one of the richest and most important Franciscan houses in Ireland, famed for its carved tombs and windows. The town developed as a market and administrative centre, and was chosen as the county town of Clare. In 1828 Ennis was the constituency in which Daniel O'Connell won the famous election that led directly to Catholic Emancipation in 1829; his monument stands in the town centre. The Great Famine of the 1840s hit the surrounding county hard, and the town later featured in the War of Independence.

Culture and economy

Ennis hosts the Fleadh Nua traditional music festival each spring and has pubs renowned for nightly sessions; County Clare as a whole is considered a stronghold of Irish traditional music. De Valera Park and Cusack Park host GAA sports. The town's economy rests on retail, public services, tourism for the west of Ireland (including the Burren and the Cliffs of Moher) and light industry. Shannon Airport lies about 24 km to the south.

Transport

Ennis railway station is on the Limerick–Galway line, restored as part of the Western Rail Corridor in 2010, with services to Limerick, Galway and connections to Dublin. The M18 motorway links the town to Limerick, Shannon and Galway.
