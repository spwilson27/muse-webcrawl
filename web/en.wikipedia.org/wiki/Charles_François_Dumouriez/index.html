Charles-François du Périer Dumouriez (25 January 1739 – 14 March 1823) was a French general who won the Battle of Valmy (1792) alongside François Christophe de Kellermann and the Battle of Jemappes (1792), securing the early survival of the French Revolution. A Girondin sympathiser, he fell out with the Jacobin government after his defeat at Neerwinden in 1793 and defected to the Austrians, spending the rest of his life in exile.

Early career

Dumouriez entered the army during the Seven Years' War and was wounded at the Battle of Rossbach. He afterwards served in Corsica and travelled widely in Spain, Portugal, Sweden and Poland, often acting as an unofficial agent of the French foreign ministry. Under the old regime he was imprisoned in the Bastille for a time on suspicion arising from his Polish missions, but was released and later pensioned.

Revolutionary general

At the Revolution he attached himself to the Girondins and briefly served as foreign minister in 1792. Taking command of the Army of the North, he checked the Prussian invasion at Valmy in September 1792, then conquered the Austrian Netherlands after Jemappes. His ambitions for a client Belgian state and a march on the Dutch Republic diverged from Paris's aims.

Defection and exile

Defeated at Neerwinden in March 1793 and threatened with arrest by commissioners sent from Paris, Dumouriez arrested the commissioners and handed them to the Austrians, then deserted to the enemy. He wandered Europe for years as an adviser and intriguer, finally settling in England in 1804, where he advised the British government during the Napoleonic Wars and died in 1823.
