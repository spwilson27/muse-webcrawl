Operation Shingle (22 January 1944), also known as the Battle of Anzio, was an Allied amphibious landing by the US VI Corps on the west coast of Italy at Anzio and Nettuno, some 50 kilometres south of Rome. Championed by Winston Churchill, the landing was intended to outflank the German Winter Line defences around Monte Cassino and draw German forces away from them, opening the road to Rome.

The landing and stalemate

The landing achieved complete surprise and met almost no opposition on the first day. The corps commander, Major General John P. Lucas, chose to consolidate the beachhead rather than dash inland, and German forces under Field Marshal Albert Kesselring reacted quickly, ringing the beachhead with the Fourteenth Army. Four months of bitter fighting followed, with the Allies confined to a shallow, shell-swept perimeter around Anzio; Lucas was relieved and replaced by Major General Lucian Truscott.

Breakout

The beachhead finally broke out in May 1944 as part of Operation Diadem, the Allied offensive against the Gustav Line, and Rome fell on 4 June 1944 — two days before the Normandy landings pushed it from the headlines.
