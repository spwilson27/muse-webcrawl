Beat 'em up

A beat 'em up (also known as a brawler) is a video game genre featuring hand-to-hand combat against large numbers of enemies, typically in side-scrolling stages. The player character advances through levels fighting waves of opponents, often with simple combos, throws, and improvised weapons, and the genre is strongly associated with cooperative multiplayer.

History

The genre traces to kung fu films and games such as Irem's Kung-Fu Master (1984) and Technōs Japan's Renegade (1986), which introduced scrolling multi-directional movement. Technōs's Double Dragon (1987) defined the belt-scroll brawler and two-player co-op. Capcom's Final Fight (1989) and Sega's Streets of Rage series (1991–1994), along with licensed hits like Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles arcade games, marked the genre's arcade golden age.

Decline and revival

With the rise of 3D games in the mid-1990s, classic 2D brawlers declined, and the term "beat 'em up" broadened to include 3D "hack and slash" action games such as Devil May Cry and God of War. Independent titles in the 2010s and 2020s — including Streets of Rage 4 (2020) and Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Shredder's Revenge (2022) — revived the classic form to commercial and critical success.

Characteristics

Common features include scrolling arenas, health bars for enemies, special moves that cost health, environmental weapons, boss fights at the end of stages, and scoring systems. In Japan the genre is called "belt scroll action" for its typical sideways advancement.
