The tesla (symbol: T) is the unit of magnetic flux density (also called magnetic B-field strength) in the International System of Units (SI). One tesla is equal to one weber per square metre. The unit was announced at the Conférence Générale des Poids et Mesures in 1960 and is named in honour of Serbian-American inventor and electrical engineer Nikola Tesla, on the proposal of Slovenian electrical engineer Franc Avčin.

Magnetic field of a bar magnet

Definition

A particle carrying a charge of one coulomb passing through a magnetic field of one tesla at a speed of one metre per second perpendicular to the field experiences a force of one newton, according to the Lorentz force law. Equivalently, 1 T = 1 Wb/m² = 1 N/(A·m) = 1 kg/(s²·A). In the older CGS system, one tesla equals 10,000 gauss, the unit still common in some applications.

Examples

  • The Earth's magnetic field at the surface: about 25–65 microtesla (µT).
  • A typical refrigerator magnet: about 5 millitesla (mT).
  • Clinical MRI scanners: 1.5 T or 3 T.
  • The strongest continuous fields produced by superconducting and hybrid magnets: tens of tesla (over 45 T in hybrid systems).
  • A neutron star's magnetic field: millions to billions of tesla.
