United Airlines, Inc. is a major airline in the United States headquartered at the Willis Tower in Chicago, Illinois. United operates a large domestic and international route network spanning numerous destinations across the United States and six continents, and is among the largest airlines in the world by fleet size and destinations served. Measured by fleet size, it is the largest airline in the world, operating well over 1,000 mainline aircraft.

History

United traces its roots to Varney Air Lines, founded in Boise, Idaho, in 1926, which flew the first contract airmail route in the United States. Varney was acquired by Boeing executive William Boeing's holding company, United Aircraft and Transport Corporation, and merged with other carriers to form United Air Lines in 1931. In 2010, United merged with Continental Airlines in a $3.2 billion deal; the combined carrier retained the United name while adopting Continental's globe livery, and the two airlines began operating under a single certificate in 2011. United was a founding member of the Star Alliance in 1997.

Operations

The airline's major hubs include Chicago–O'Hare, its largest, along with Denver, Houston–Intercontinental, Los Angeles, Newark Liberty, San Francisco, Washington–Dulles, and Guam. Its frequent-flyer program is MileagePlus. United's fleet includes Airbus A320-family aircraft and Boeing 737, 757, 767, 777, and 787 types.
