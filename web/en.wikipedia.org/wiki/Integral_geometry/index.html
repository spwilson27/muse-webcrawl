Integral geometry

Integral geometry is a branch of mathematics concerned with integrals over spaces of geometric objects — such as lines, planes, or rigid motions — that are invariant under the symmetry group of the underlying space. It originated in geometric probability, notably in problems such as Buffon's needle.

Classical results

The Crofton formula expresses the length of a plane curve in terms of the measure of lines intersecting it. Poincaré's formula, Blaschke's work, and the kinematic formulas developed by Chern and others relate integrals of curvature and intersection counts of moving geometric figures. Such results underlie the field of stereology, which estimates properties of three-dimensional structures from two-dimensional sections.

Modern developments

Integral geometry is closely connected with the Radon transform, which reconstructs a function from its integrals over lines or planes — the mathematical basis of computed tomography. Work by Gelfand and others extended these ideas to homogeneous spaces and representation theory.
