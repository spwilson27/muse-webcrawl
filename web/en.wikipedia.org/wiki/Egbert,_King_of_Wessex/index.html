Egbert (also spelled Ecgberht; died 839) was King of Wessex from 802 until his death in 839. His reign marks a turning point in early English history: he defeated Mercia, ended its supremacy, and brought most of southern England under West Saxon overlordship, laying foundations later built on by his grandson Alfred the Great.

Early life and exile

Egbert was a member of the West Saxon royal house. After the death of King Cynewulf in 786, the throne passed to Beorhtric, who was supported by Offa of Mercia. Egbert went into exile, spending time at the court of Charlemagne in Francia, where he gained military and political experience.

Reign

On Beorhtric's death in 802, Egbert returned and took the throne. In 825 he won the decisive Battle of Ellandun against Beornwulf of Mercia, after which Kent, Surrey, Sussex, and Essex submitted to him. In 829 he conquered Mercia itself and received the submission of Northumbria, leading the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle to list him as the eighth bretwalda (overlord of Britain). Although Mercia regained independence in 830, Wessex retained control of the south-east.

Death and succession

Egbert died in 839 and was succeeded by his son Æthelwulf. He was buried at Winchester, the royal centre of Wessex.
