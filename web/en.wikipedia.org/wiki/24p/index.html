24p

Definition: Video format with 24 progressive frames per second

Purpose: Emulating film's frame rate in digital video

Related: 23.976 fps (24000/1001), 2:3 pulldown

24p refers to a video format that operates at 24 progressive frames per second, with a resolution and scanning method analogous to film. The "p" stands for progressive scan, meaning each frame is drawn as a complete image rather than as two interlaced fields. The format became popular for digital cinematography because it closely emulates the motion characteristics and look of 24 fps motion-picture film at far lower cost.

History

The term gained currency in the early 2000s as digital video cameras began offering 24-frame progressive modes, allowing independent filmmakers and television productions to achieve a "film look" without shooting on film stock. Because NTSC video historically ran at 29.97 interlaced frames per second, 24p material was commonly recorded at 23.976 fps and transferred using pulldown techniques so that it could be displayed on broadcast equipment.

24p in broadcasting

Converting 24p material for 59.94 Hz television involved the 2:3 pulldown process, spreading the 24 frames across 60 fields by alternately repeating frames two and three times. Modern flat-panel displays and streaming services increasingly support native 24p playback. Blu-ray discs store films at 1080p24. Some viewers dislike the judder produced when 24p content is shown with pulldown on 60 Hz displays, a problem addressed by 120 Hz displays that can show each frame exactly five times.
