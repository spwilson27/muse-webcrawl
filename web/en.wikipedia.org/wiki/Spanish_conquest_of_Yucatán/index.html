Spanish conquest of Yucatán

The Spanish conquest of Yucatán was the campaign undertaken by Spanish conquistadores against the Late Postclassic Maya states of the Yucatán Peninsula, lasting from 1517 to 1546 in its main phase, with Maya resistance continuing far longer.

Background

Expeditions by Hernández de Córdoba (1517) and Grijalva (1518) first contacted the Maya. Unlike central Mexico, Yucatán had no single empire to seize: it was divided into rival provinces (kuchkabal), which both hindered and prolonged conquest.

Campaigns of the Montejos

Francisco de Montejo received permission in 1526. Two campaigns (1527–28, 1531–35) failed amid fierce resistance and disease. His son, Montejo 'the Younger', finally founded Campeche (1540) and Mérida (1542) on the ruins of T'Hó, and broke organised resistance by 1546 — though a major eastern revolt followed in 1546–47.

Aftermath

The encomienda system was imposed; Franciscans under Diego de Landa evangelised, notoriously burning Maya codices at Maní in 1562. The southern Itza of Petén remained independent until 1697.
