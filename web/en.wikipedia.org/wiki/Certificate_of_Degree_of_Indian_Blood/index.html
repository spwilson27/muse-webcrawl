Certificate of Degree of Indian Blood

A CDIB card issued by the Bureau of Indian Affairs.

A Certificate of Degree of Indian Blood or Certificate of Degree of Alaska Native Blood (abbreviated CDIB) is an official U.S. document issued by the Bureau of Indian Affairs (BIA) that certifies an individual possesses a specific degree of Native American blood of a federally recognized Indian tribe, band, nation, pueblo, village, community, or Alaska Native village.

Purpose and issuance

The CDIB is issued on the basis of documented descent from an enrolled member of a federally recognized tribe, traced through records such as tribal rolls and census records. The card states the holder's calculated blood quantum — for example one-half or one-quarter — but it does not itself confer tribal membership or enrollment, which remains the sovereign determination of each tribe. A CDIB is often required to access federal programs and benefits reserved for American Indians and Alaska Natives, including Indian Health Service care, educational assistance, and certain hiring preferences.

Blood quantum

The blood quantum system derives from federal policies of the allotment era, including the Dawes Act of 1887 and Indian census rolls. The use of blood quantum is controversial: critics argue it is a colonial construct that statistically diminishes tribal populations over generations, while tribes retain the sovereign right to set their own citizenship criteria, which may or may not use blood quantum.
