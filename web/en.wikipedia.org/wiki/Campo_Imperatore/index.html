Campo Imperatore is a mountain grassland plateau in the province of L'Aquila, Abruzzo, Italy, formed by a glacial basin at about 1,800–2,100 metres above sea level on the southern slopes of the Gran Sasso massif. Some 27 kilometres long and up to 11 kilometres wide, it is among the largest high plateaus of the Apennines and is sometimes called the "Little Tibet" of Italy. Its stark landscape has served as a filming location for numerous Westerns and adventure films.

Geography and use

The plateau lies beneath the Corno Grande (2,912 m), the highest peak of the Apennines. It has been used for centuries as summer pasture for sheep and cattle, reached by transhumance routes from Apulia. Today it is protected within the Gran Sasso e Monti della Laga National Park and is used for hiking, cycling (it is a famous Giro d'Italia summit finish), skiing at the Campo Imperatore station, and astronomy at the Campo Imperatore Astronomical Observatory, which hosts the AZT-24 infrared telescope used for supernova research.

The Mussolini rescue

Campo Imperatore is best known internationally as the site of Benito Mussolini's imprisonment and rescue in 1943. After the fall of his regime, Mussolini was held at the Hotel Campo Imperatore (Albergo Rifugio) on the plateau. On 12 September 1943, German commandos led by Otto Skorzeny, with paratroopers under Harald Mors, landed in gliders in Operation Eiche ("Oak") and freed him without a shot being fired, flying him out in a Fieseler Storch light aircraft — one of the most daring special operations of the Second World War.

Film location

The plateau's treeless, windswept scenery doubled for the American West and Central Asia in films including Ladyhawke (1985), The Name of the Rose (1986, exteriors in the area), Red Sonja, and various spaghetti westerns.
