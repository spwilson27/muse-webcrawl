Battle of Tricamarum

The Battle of Tricamarum was fought on 15 December 533 between the East Roman (Byzantine) forces of General Belisarius and the Vandal kingdom of North Africa under King Gelimer and his brother Tzazon. The Byzantine victory, following the earlier triumph at Ad Decimum, effectively destroyed the Vandal kingdom and completed Justinian I's reconquest of North Africa.

Background

As part of Justinian's renovatio imperii, Belisarius landed in Africa in 533 and defeated the Vandals at Ad Decimum in September, capturing Carthage. Gelimer regrouped at Bulla Regia, recalled his brother Tzazon from Sardinia, and advanced to Tricamarum, about 30 km from Carthage, cutting the aqueduct and hoping to induce defections among Belisarius's Hunnic auxiliaries.

The battle

Belisarius marched out and attacked in mid-December. After skirmishing by mounted archers, John the Armenian led repeated cavalry charges against the Vandal centre, where Tzazon was killed. The Byzantine infantry then advanced and the Vandal line broke. Gelimer fled to Numidia when his camp was overrun; he surrendered the following spring on Mount Papua after a blockade.

Aftermath

The Vandal kingdom, founded by Genseric a century earlier, ceased to exist; North Africa was reorganised as the praetorian prefecture of Africa. Gelimer was pensioned off in Galatia after being led in Justinian's triumph at Constantinople. Our main source is Procopius, Belisarius's secretary, in his History of the Wars.
