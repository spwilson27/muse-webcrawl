Nanyue Huairang

Nanyue Huairang (677–744) was a Chinese Chan (Zen) master of the Tang dynasty and a leading disciple of the Sixth Patriarch, Huineng. Through his student Mazu Daoyi, Huairang became the ancestor of one of the two great lines of later Chinese Chan, and thus of the Linji (Rinzai) school.

Life

Huairang was born in Jinzhou and ordained as a young monk, first studying the Vinaya and the teachings of the Northern School before travelling to Caoxi to study under Huineng, under whom he is said to have attained awakening. He later settled on Mount Heng (Nanyue, the Southern Sacred Mountain) in Hunan, from which his name derives, and taught there for many years. Later Chan genealogies, constructed in the Song dynasty, elevated Huairang alongside Qingyuan Xingsi as the two principal heirs of Huineng.

The tile-polishing dialogue

Huairang is famous for his encounter with the young Mazu Daoyi, who was sitting in meditation on Mount Heng. Finding Mazu absorbed in seated meditation, Huairang picked up a roof tile and began polishing it on a rock. When Mazu asked what he was doing, Huairang replied that he was making a mirror; when Mazu objected that polishing a tile could never produce a mirror, Huairang answered that sitting in meditation could likewise never by itself produce a Buddha. The exchange became a celebrated koan illustrating that awakening is not manufactured by technique.
