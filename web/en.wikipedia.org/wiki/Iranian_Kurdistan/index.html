Iranian Kurdistan

Iranian Kurdistan, also known as Eastern Kurdistan (Sorani Kurdish: Rojhilatê Kurdistanê), is the unofficial name for the parts of northwestern Iran inhabited by Kurds. It borders Iraq and Turkey and includes Iran's Kurdistan Province, along with Kurdish-majority areas of Kermanshah, Ilam, and West Azerbaijan provinces.

Geography and demography

The region is mountainous, dominated by the Zagros range. Its principal cities include Sanandaj (the capital of Kurdistan Province), Kermanshah, Mahabad, Bukan, Saqqez, and Marivan. Iranian Kurds number several million — estimates commonly run from about 8 to 12 percent of Iran's population. Most speak Sorani or Southern Kurdish dialects, and most are Sunni Muslims, unlike the Shia majority in Iran, though Kermanshah and Ilam have large Shia Kurdish populations.

History

Kurdish principalities in the region, such as Ardalan centred on Sanandaj, long enjoyed semi-autonomy under Persian suzerainty. In January 1946, with Soviet backing, the short-lived Republic of Mahabad was proclaimed under Qazi Muhammad; it collapsed within the year after Soviet withdrawal, and its leaders were executed. After the 1979 Iranian Revolution, Kurdish movements demanding autonomy came into armed conflict with the new Islamic Republic, and Kurdish parties such as the KDPI and Komala have alternated between exile, insurgency, and ceasefire ever since. The PJAK movement emerged in the 2000s as an offshoot aligned with the PKK.

Culture

The region shares the wider Kurdish cultural repertoire of Newroz celebrations, distinctive dress, music, and dance, and has produced prominent figures such as the master musician and tanbur player, and the celebrated singer Shahram Nazeri from Kermanshah.
