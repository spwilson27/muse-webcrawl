The Kop is the famous single-tier stand behind the goal at the Walton Breck Road end of Anfield, the home of Liverpool F.C. Built in 1906 as a vast open terrace, it was named after Spion Kop, the South African hill where many Liverpool soldiers had died in the Boer War; at its peak it held some 28,000 standing supporters, swaying as one body. "Kop" has become a generic term in British football for a steep home end, but Liverpool's is the original and by far the most celebrated, synonymous with the wall of noise generated on European nights.

Culture

From the 1960s the Kop became the engine of Anfield's atmosphere, popularising terrace chants and adopting "You'll Never Walk Alone" as its anthem. Standing on the Kop was a rite of passage for generations of Liverpudlians. Following the Hillsborough disaster of 1989 and the Taylor Report, the terrace was rebuilt as an all-seater stand in 1994; its last standing match, against Norwich City, was an occasion of city-wide mourning and celebration. The modern Kop, though seated and smaller, retains its flags, banners and reputation as the twelfth man of Liverpool's great comebacks, including the 2019 defeat of Barcelona.
