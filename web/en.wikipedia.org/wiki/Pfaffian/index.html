Pfaffian

In mathematics, the Pfaffian of a skew-symmetric matrix is a polynomial in its entries, most important for the fact that it is the square root of the determinant of the matrix. It is named after the German mathematician Johann Friedrich Pfaff. The term was introduced by Arthur Cayley, who remarked on the association with Pfaff's work on differential equations (Pfaffian systems).

Definition

Let A be a 2n × 2n skew-symmetric matrix. The Pfaffian of A, written pf(A), can be defined as a sum over all perfect matchings (pair partitions) of the set {1, 2, …, 2n}:

pf(A) = Σπ sgn(π) Aπ(1),π(2) Aπ(3),π(4) ⋯ Aπ(2n−1),π(2n)

where the sum runs over all ways of partitioning the indices into ordered pairs, and sgn(π) is the sign of the corresponding permutation. For example, for a 4 × 4 matrix, pf(A) = A12A34 − A13A24 + A14A23.

Properties

The defining relation with the determinant is det(A) = pf(A)². Other key properties include pf(BABT) = det(B) pf(A) for any matrix B of the same size, and pf(cA) = cn pf(A) for a scalar c. For a block-diagonal matrix, the Pfaffian is the product of the Pfaffians of the blocks.

Applications

Pfaffians appear in combinatorics, where the Kasteleyn–Percus method uses them to count dimer coverings of planar graphs; in physics, where the partition function of the two-dimensional Ising model can be expressed via Pfaffians; and in differential geometry and representation theory, including the Euler class of an oriented vector bundle and Grassmann integral formulations of fermionic systems.
