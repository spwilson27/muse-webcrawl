Barbara Radziwiłł

Barbara Radziwiłł (Polish: Barbara Radziwiłłówna; 1520/23 – 1551) was Queen of Poland and Grand Duchess of Lithuania as the second wife of King Sigismund II Augustus. Her secret marriage to the king, contracted in 1547 for love, caused a political scandal in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, and she died only months after her coronation in 1550.

Early life and marriages

Barbara was the daughter of Jerzy Radziwiłł, castellan of Vilnius. Her first husband, Stanislovas Goštautas, died in 1542, leaving her a wealthy widow at the Vilnius court, where she became known for her beauty and education.

Marriage to Sigismund Augustus

Her secret marriage to the future king outraged his parents, Sigismund I and Bona Sforza, and much of the Polish nobility, who had hoped for a politically advantageous match abroad. The Sejm eventually accepted the marriage, and Barbara was crowned in Kraków in December 1550, but her health collapsed and she died the following May, amid rumours — never proven — of poisoning. Her story became one of Poland's most enduring romantic legends, inspiring plays and paintings.
