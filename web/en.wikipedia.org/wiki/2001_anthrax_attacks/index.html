The 2001 anthrax attacks, also known as Amerithrax, occurred in the United States over several weeks beginning in September 2001, shortly after the September 11 attacks. Letters containing anthrax spores were mailed to news media offices and to Democratic Senators Tom Daschle and Patrick Leahy.

Attacks and victims

Five people died of inhalational anthrax: photo editor Robert Stevens in Florida, two Washington, D.C. postal workers, a New York hospital worker, and an elderly Connecticut woman. Seventeen others were infected. The attacks sparked widespread fear, shut down postal facilities and Senate offices, and prompted mass antibiotic prophylaxis.

Investigation

The FBI's investigation was one of the largest in its history. After initially focusing on scientist Steven Hatfill, who was later exonerated and compensated, investigators concluded that Bruce Edwards Ivins, a biodefense researcher at Fort Detrick, was the perpetrator. Ivins died by suicide in 2008 before charges were filed, and the case was closed in 2010, though some scientists have questioned the strength of the evidence.
