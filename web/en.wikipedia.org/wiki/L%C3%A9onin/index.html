Léonin

Léonin (fl. 1150s – c. 1201), also called Leoninus, was a French composer active in Paris in the late 12th century and a leading figure of the Notre Dame school of polyphony.

Life and works

Almost nothing is known of Léonin's life beyond the testimony of the English theorist known as Anonymous IV, who called him the "best composer of organum". He is credited with compiling the Magnus liber organi ("Great Book of Organum"), a collection of two-voice organa for the liturgical year at Notre Dame Cathedral, later revised and supplemented by his younger colleague Pérotin.

Style

Léonin's organa alternate sections in free-flowing organum purum, where the upper voice sings melismas over sustained chant notes, with discant passages in which both voices move in measured rhythm.
