The SMU Mustangs football programme represents Southern Methodist University in Dallas, Texas, in NCAA Division I FBS college football. The Mustangs, named for the wild horses of the American plains, have played since 1915 and are known both for periods of national prominence and for receiving the most severe sanction in NCAA history.

History

SMU rose to prominence in the 1930s under coach Matty Bell, winning a national championship in 1935 and playing in the 1936 Rose Bowl. Running back Doak Walker won the Heisman Trophy in 1948 and remains the school's most famous player. In the early 1980s, the "Pony Express" backfield of Eric Dickerson and Craig James powered SMU to Southwest Conference titles and top national rankings.

The death penalty

In 1987, after repeated violations involving payments to players from a slush fund maintained with the knowledge of school officials, the NCAA imposed the "death penalty", cancelling SMU's entire 1987 season. The school voluntarily cancelled 1988 as well. SMU is the only football programme to receive the sanction since it was authorised in its modern form, and the programme did not reach another bowl game until 2009.

Recent years

After decades in the Western Athletic Conference, Conference USA and the American Athletic Conference, SMU joined the Atlantic Coast Conference in 2024. In its first ACC season the team reached the conference championship game and earned a place in the College Football Playoff.
