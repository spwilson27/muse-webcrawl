Jean Nouvel

Jean Nouvel (born 12 August 1945) is a French architect, founder of Ateliers Jean Nouvel, known for buildings that respond theatrically to light, context, and culture rather than repeating a single signature style. He received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2008, the jury commending his "pursuit of new ideas and [pushing] the boundaries of the field" over some two hundred projects.

Career

Nouvel studied at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris and worked for Claude Parent before founding his first practice in 1970. His breakthrough came with the Institut du Monde Arabe in Paris (1987), built with Architecture-Studio: its south facade of 240 photosensitive apertures — metal diaphragms echoing Arabic mashrabiya screens that open and close with the light — became an icon of high-tech, culturally allusive architecture.

Major works

  • Fondation Cartier, Paris (1994) — a glass screen building that blurs interior, garden, and street.
  • Torre Glòries (formerly Agbar Tower), Barcelona (2005) — a geyser-shaped, colour-shifting office tower.
  • Musée du quai Branly, Paris (2006) — museum of non-Western arts set in a lush garden, guarded by a green wall.
  • One New Change and residential tower One Central Park, Sydney, with its hanging gardens.
  • Louvre Abu Dhabi (2017) — a "museum city" under a vast 180-metre dome whose layered geometry casts a shifting "rain of light".
  • National Museum of Qatar, Doha (2019) — interlocking discs inspired by the desert rose.

Approach

Nouvel insists that "each project is an adventure" and that architecture must invent itself anew for each site, programme, and culture. His work mixes cinematic effects of light and reflection with references to local tradition, from Arab latticework to the desert rose, and ranges from museums and concert halls (the Philharmonie de Paris, 2015) to skyscrapers such as the MoMA Tower (53W53) in New York.
