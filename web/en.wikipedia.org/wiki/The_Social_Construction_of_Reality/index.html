The Social Construction of Reality

Authors: Peter L. Berger, Thomas Luckmann
Published: 1966

The Social Construction of Reality: A Treatise in the Sociology of Knowledge is a 1966 book by sociologists Peter L. Berger and Thomas Luckmann. Drawing on the phenomenology of Alfred Schütz, it became one of the most influential works in the sociology of knowledge and a foundational text for social constructionism.

Argument

Berger and Luckmann argue that society is both an objective and a subjective reality. Human beings externalize meanings through habitualized actions; these become objectivated as institutions that confront individuals as external facts; and new generations internalize them through socialization, so that the humanly produced world is experienced as reality itself. Key concepts include habitualization, institutionalization, legitimation, roles, and reification — treating human creations as if they were natural or divine givens.

Influence

The book helped refound the sociology of knowledge around everyday life rather than elite ideas, and its title popularized the phrase "social construction," later extended — sometimes beyond the authors' intent — across the humanities and sciences. The International Sociological Association named it among the most important sociology books of the twentieth century.
