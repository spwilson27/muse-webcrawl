Seven chieftains of the Magyars

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The seven chieftains of the Magyars (Hungarian: a hét vezér) were the leaders of the seven tribes of the Magyars at the time of their arrival in the Carpathian Basin around 895 AD. According to tradition, the chieftains entered into a blood oath (vérszerződés), swearing eternal allegiance to Álpad, who became the Grand Prince of the Hungarians.

The seven chieftains

The names of the chieftains are preserved in medieval chronicles, most notably the Gesta Hungarorum of Anonymus and the chronicle of Simon of Kéza. The traditional list is:

OndKond

Álmos, the father of Árpád, is often counted as the foremost of the seven, though some lists substitute Árpád himself or include a slightly different set of names. The seven tribes themselves are generally given as Nyék, Megyer, Kürt-Gyarmat, Tarján, Jenő, Kér and Keszi.

The blood oath

According to Anonymus, the chieftains sealed their alliance by mixing their blood in a vessel and drinking from it, swearing that the supreme leader of the Magyars would always be chosen from the line of Álmos, that their common goods would be shared, and that traitors and their descendants would be cursed. The oath is traditionally located at Pusztaszer, where a large memorial park now commemorates the conquest of the Carpathian Basin.

Legacy

The seven chieftains occupy a central place in Hungarian national mythology. The monumental Feszty Panorama (Arrival of the Hungarians), painted by Árpád Feszty and his associates for the Hungarian millennium celebrations of 1896, depicts the chieftains at the head of the conquering host. Statues of the chieftains appear on the colonnades of Heroes' Square in Budapest, flanking the figure of Árpád on the Millennium Monument.
