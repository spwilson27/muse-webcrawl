Richard Wagner

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Richard Wagner in a beret: sharp profile, intense eyes, the composer of the Ring at Bayreuth]

Wilhelm Richard Wagner (22 May 1813 – 13 February 1883) was a German composer, theatre director, essayist and conductor who is chiefly known for his operas (or 'music dramas'). Unlike most opera composers, Wagner wrote both the libretti and the music for all of his stage works, and revolutionised opera through his concept of the Gesamtkunstwerk ('total work of art') and the leitmotif technique.

His mature works — Tristan und Isolde, Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg, the four-opera cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen and his final opera Parsifal — pushed harmony toward the edge of tonality and reshaped Western music. With the patronage of Ludwig II of Bavaria he built his own festival theatre at Bayreuth (opened 1876), which still stages his works annually. His voluminous writings include notorious antisemitic essays, which continue to shadow his reputation.
