The Mahabat Khan Mosque (Pashto/Urdu: مہابت خان مسجد), also spelled Mohabbat Khan Mosque, is a 17th-century Mughal-era mosque in Peshawar, in Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province. Located in the old city near the Chowk Yadgar and the Jewellers' Bazaar (Andar Shehr), it is Peshawar's most famous mosque and a major example of Mughal architecture in the region.

History

The mosque was built in the 1670s (the foundation is generally dated to around 1630–1670 in differing sources; construction is commonly placed in 1670) under Mahabat Khan, the Mughal governor of Peshawar under the emperors Shah Jahan and Aurangzeb, after whom it is named. During the Sikh period in the early 19th century the mosque was desecrated — its minarets were used as gallows by the Italian governor Paolo Avitabile — and it suffered further in the Anglo-Sikh wars before being restored to use as a mosque under British rule. The building was damaged by the earthquake of 2005 and has since undergone conservation work.

Architecture

The mosque is built of brick with a facade of white marble in classic Mughal style. Its prayer hall is surmounted by three fluted domes and flanked by two tall minarets, with a third pair of smaller turrets; the open courtyard contains an ablution pool. The interior is richly decorated with frescoes, floral motifs, and calligraphy typical of later Mughal ornament. The prayer hall is divided into chambers, and the western hall is covered by three low domes decorated with paintings.
