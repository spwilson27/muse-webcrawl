Star of Life

The Star of Life is a blue, six-pointed star, outlined with a white border, which features the Rod of Asclepius in the centre. It is a symbol of emergency medical services and is used internationally to identify ambulances, emergency medical personnel, and related facilities.

History

The symbol was designed by Leo R. Schwartz, chief of the EMS branch of the United States National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, in 1973. It was created after the American Red Cross complained that the previously used orange cross on a white background too closely resembled its own emblem. The Star of Life was registered as a trademark and promoted by the NHTSA as the universal symbol for emergency medical services.

Symbolism

The six points of the star represent the six functions of the emergency medical chain: detection, reporting, response, on-scene care, care in transit, and transfer to definitive care.

Usage

The Star of Life appears on ambulances, on the uniforms of EMTs and paramedics, on emergency department signage, and on medical alert jewellery. It may not be used commercially to imply certification in some jurisdictions, where its use is regulated.
