Attack aircraft
RoleTactical air-to-surface attack
Primary missionClose air support, air interdiction, anti-armour
Notable examplesFairchild Republic A-10 Thunderbolt II; Sukhoi Su-25; Ilyushin Il-2

An attack aircraft, or strike aircraft, is a tactical military aircraft that has a primary role of carrying out airstrikes with greater precision than bombers, and is prepared to encounter strong low-level air defences. Unlike strategic bombers, attack aircraft are designed to operate close to the battlefield, providing close air support to friendly ground forces and attacking tanks, fortifications and other tactical targets.

Characteristics

Attack aircraft typically emphasise armour protection for the pilot and critical systems, heavy cannon armament, large payloads of guided and unguided weapons, and good low-speed, low-altitude handling, trading high top speed for survivability and accuracy over the battlefield.

History

The role emerged in the First World War with armoured ground-attack machines such as the German Junkers J.I. In the Second World War, the Soviet Ilyushin Il-2 Shturmovik — the most produced military aircraft in history — and the German Junkers Ju 87 Stuka exemplified the type. During the Cold War the United States developed the A-10 Thunderbolt II around its 30 mm GAU-8 cannon for the anti-tank role, while the Soviet Union fielded the comparable Sukhoi Su-25. In many air forces, dedicated attack aircraft have since been supplemented or replaced by multirole fighters and armed helicopters, though the type persists worldwide.
