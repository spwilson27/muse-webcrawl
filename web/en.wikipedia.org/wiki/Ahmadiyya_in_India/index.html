Ahmadiyya in India is the Indian branch of the Ahmadiyya Muslim Community, the movement founded in 1889 by Mirza Ghulam Ahmad in Qadian, Punjab, British India. India is therefore the birthplace of the Ahmadiyya movement, and Qadian remains one of its holiest sites, hosting the annual Jalsa Salana gathering.

History

Mirza Ghulam Ahmad claimed to be the promised Messiah and Mahdi and took the first bai'at (pledge of allegiance) at Ludhiana in 1889 before moving to Qadian. After his death in 1908 the community was led by caliphs. The 1914 split produced two branches: the Ahmadiyya Muslim Community (Qadiani), which regards Ghulam Ahmad as a prophet subordinate to Muhammad, and the Lahore Ahmadiyya Movement, which regards him as a reformer. At the Partition of India in 1947, the fourth caliph moved the community's headquarters to Rabwah, Pakistan, but Qadian retained its spiritual importance and an Indian headquarters.

Community and status

Indian Ahmadis are a small minority — estimates range from tens of thousands to around a million adherents — concentrated in Punjab, Kerala, and other states. Unlike in Pakistan, where Ahmadis were declared non-Muslim by constitutional amendment in 1974, in India they are legally recognized as Muslims and counted within Islam in the census, though some mainstream Muslim organizations dispute their status and Ahmadis have faced occasional boycotts and violence.

Activities

The community in India is active in publishing translations of the Quran, interfaith dialogue, education, and charitable work, organized under its Indian caliphal representative structure in Qadian.
