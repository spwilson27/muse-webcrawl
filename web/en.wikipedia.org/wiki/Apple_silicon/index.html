Apple silicon

DesignerApple Inc.
ArchitectureARM (AArch64)
SeriesA series (iPhone/iPad), M series (Mac/iPad), S series (Watch), T, H, U series
Introduced2010 (Apple A4)
FabricationTSMC

Apple silicon refers to a series of system on a chip (SoC) and system-in-package (SiP) processors designed by Apple Inc., mainly using the ARM architecture. They are the basis of most iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch devices, and of all Mac computers since 2020, replacing Intel processors in the Mac.

History

Apple began designing its own chips after acquiring the semiconductor firms P.A. Semi (2008) and Intrinsity (2010). The first, the Apple A4, debuted in the original iPad and the iPhone 4 in 2010. Successive A-series chips added 64-bit processing (the A7 in 2013, the first 64-bit ARM chip in a smartphone), custom GPUs, the Neural Engine for machine learning (A11, 2017), and increasingly integrated memory.

Mac transition

At WWDC in June 2020, Apple announced a two-year transition of the Mac from Intel processors to Apple silicon. The first M-series chip, the Apple M1, launched in November 2020 in the MacBook Air, MacBook Pro, and Mac mini, and was widely praised for its performance per watt. Successors include the M1 Pro/Max/Ultra, and the M2, M3, and M4 families built on successively smaller TSMC processes. The transition completed with the Mac Pro in 2023. Rosetta 2 translation allows software built for Intel Macs to run on the new chips.

Other series

  • S series — systems in package for the Apple Watch.
  • T series — security/controller chips in late Intel Macs (e.g. T2).
  • H series — audio chips in AirPods.
  • U series — ultra-wideband chips (U1, U2) for spatial awareness.
  • R1 — co-processor for the Apple Vision Pro headset.
  • C1 — Apple's first cellular modem chip, introduced in 2025.

Design approach

Apple licenses the ARM instruction set and designs its own CPU and GPU cores. M-series chips use a unified memory architecture in which CPU, GPU, and Neural Engine share high-bandwidth memory on the package, a design credited with much of their efficiency advantage.
