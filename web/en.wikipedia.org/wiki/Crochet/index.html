Crochet

Crochet is a process of creating textiles by using a crochet hook to interlock loops of yarn, thread, or strands of other materials. The name comes from the French term crochet, meaning 'hook'.

Crochet

Tool: Crochet hook
Materials: Yarn, thread

History

Crochet developed in the 19th century, though earlier forms such as shepherd's knitting existed. It became popular in Ireland during the Great Famine, when crochet lace provided income, and spread widely. Unlike knitting, crochet uses a single hook and each stitch is completed before the next begins.

Techniques

Basic stitches include chain, slip stitch, single (UK double), half double, double (UK treble) and treble crochet. Work may be flat, in the round, or in motifs such as granny squares joined together. Amigurumi is crocheted stuffed figures.

Materials

Hooks range from fine steel hooks for lace thread to large plastic or wooden hooks for chunky yarn. Fibres include cotton, wool and acrylic.
