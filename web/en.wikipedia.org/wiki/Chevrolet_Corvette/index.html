Chevrolet Corvette

The Chevrolet Corvette is a two-seat sports car manufactured and marketed by Chevrolet across eight generations since 1953. Often called "America's sports car," it is known for high performance at a lower price than European rivals, for its pioneering use of fibreglass bodywork, and — since the eighth generation (C8, 2020) — for a mid-engine layout.

Generations

The C1 (1953–1962) debuted at the 1953 Motorama as a fibreglass-bodied roadster. The C2 "Sting Ray" (1963–1967) added a coupe and independent rear suspension. The long C3 (1968–1982) spanned the muscle-car and emissions eras, followed by the angular, high-tech C4 (1984–1996). The C5 (1997–2004) introduced the LS V8 and a transaxle; the C6 (2005–2013) brought the 505 hp Z06 and the 638 hp ZR1; the C7 (2014–2019) revived the Stingray name. The C8 moved the engine behind the seats, and the range has since expanded to the all-wheel-drive hybrid E-Ray and the twin-turbo Z06 and ZR1 variants.

Racing

Corvette Racing has been a leading factory team in endurance racing since 1999, winning its class multiple times at the 24 Hours of Le Mans, Daytona and Sebring with the C5-R through C8.R programmes.
