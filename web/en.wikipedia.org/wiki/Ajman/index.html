Ajman

Ajman (Arabic: عجمان, ʿAǧmān) is the capital of the Emirate of Ajman, the smallest of the seven emirates of the United Arab Emirates, and the fifth-largest city in the country. Located on the western coast along the Persian Gulf, it forms part of the continuous urban area with neighbouring Sharjah and Dubai. The emirate also includes the inland exclaves of Manama and Masfout. Ajman is ruled by Sheikh Humaid bin Rashid Al Nuaimi III, of the Al Nuaimi tribe, whose family has governed the emirate since the early nineteenth century.

The Ajman Corniche along the Gulf coast.

History

Ajman emerged as a distinct sheikhdom around 1810, when the Al Nuaimi established themselves at the creek-side settlement, building the fort that now houses the Ajman Museum. Like its neighbours it signed the General Maritime Treaty of 1820 with Britain and became one of the Trucial States, joining the United Arab Emirates on its formation in December 1971. Historically dependent on fishing and pearling, the emirate later developed shipbuilding (it hosts one of the region's largest dhow-building yards), a free zone founded in 1988, and real-estate and tourism sectors.

Geography and economy

The city sits on a creek (khor) that provides a natural harbour used by dhow traffic. Ajman Free Zone and the port serve light industry and trade, while the Corniche beachfront has become a focus for hotels and residential towers housing commuters to Sharjah and Dubai. Ajman University, founded in 1988, was among the first private universities in the UAE. See also Emirate of Ajman, Sharjah and United Arab Emirates.
