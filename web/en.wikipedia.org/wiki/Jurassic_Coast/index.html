The Jurassic Coast is a World Heritage Site on the English Channel coast of southern England. It stretches from Exmouth in East Devon to Studland Bay in Dorset, a distance of about 154 kilometres (96 miles), and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2001. The site's cliffs and beaches expose an almost continuous sequence of rock formations spanning the Triassic, Jurassic and Cretaceous periods — approximately 185 million years of Earth's history.

Geology

Walking the coast from west to east is like walking forward through geological time. The oldest rocks, red Triassic sandstones and mudstones from desert environments, are exposed around Exmouth and Budleigh Salterton. They are succeeded by the Jurassic rocks of west Dorset: the fossil-rich Blue Lias and Charmouth Mudstone around Lyme Regis, the golden Bridport Sands at West Bay, and the limestones of the Isle of Portland. Eastward lie the Cretaceous chalk cliffs of the Purbeck coast. Tilting of the strata during the Alpine mountain-building episode means progressively younger rocks are met as one travels east.

Fossils

The coast is one of the world's classic fossil localities. In the early nineteenth century the Lyme Regis collector Mary Anning discovered the first scientifically recognised ichthyosaur and plesiosaur skeletons in its cliffs. Fossil collecting continues today, with remains of marine reptiles, ammonites, belemnites and dinosaur footprints regularly exposed by landslides and winter storms.

Landforms and places

Famous coastal landforms include Durdle Door, a natural limestone arch near Lulworth Cove; the shingle tombolo of Chesil Beach, 29 kilometres long, enclosing the Fleet lagoon; the Isle of Portland, source of the Portland stone used in St Paul's Cathedral and other great buildings; and the towering chalk stacks of Old Harry Rocks at Studland. Towns along the coast include Lyme Regis, Weymouth, Swanage and Sidmouth. Much of the coast is crossed by the South West Coast Path National Trail.

Management

The site is managed through a partnership of local authorities, the National Trust and Dorset and Devon county councils, balancing conservation of an actively eroding coastline — where natural processes are part of its outstanding value — with tourism and public safety.
