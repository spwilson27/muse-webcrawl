Elliptic curve
FieldAlgebraic geometry, number theory
Equationy² = x³ + ax + b (Weierstrass form)

In mathematics, an elliptic curve is a smooth, projective, algebraic curve of genus one with a specified point O. Over the complex numbers it is a torus; in Weierstrass form it is given by y² = x³ + ax + b with discriminant 4a³ + 27b² ≠ 0.

Group law

The points of an elliptic curve form an abelian group: a line through two points meets the curve in a third, whose reflection defines the sum (chord-and-tangent rule), with O as identity. Over finite fields this discrete-logarithm-hard group underlies elliptic-curve cryptography (ECC).

Number theory

Rational points, rank and torsion are governed by the Mordell–Weil theorem; the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture links rank to the curve's L-function. Elliptic curves were central to Andrew Wiles's proof of Fermat's Last Theorem via modularity, and their point-counting led to major algorithms (Schoof, Lenstra factorisation).
