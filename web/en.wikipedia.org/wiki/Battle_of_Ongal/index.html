Battle of Ongal

The Battle of Ongal was fought in 680 in the Ongal area of the Danube Delta between the Bulgars of Asparuh and the Byzantine Empire under Constantine IV. The Bulgar victory led directly to the founding of the First Bulgarian Empire, recognised by treaty in 681.

Background

Asparuh's Bulgars, part of the disintegrating Old Great Bulgaria of Kubrat, had settled in the marshy Ongal north of the Danube. Constantine IV, freed from the Arab siege of Constantinople, launched a combined land and sea expedition to expel them in 680.

Battle

The Byzantine army hesitated before the Bulgar fortified camp in the marshes. Constantine, suffering from gout, left for the baths at Mesembria; his departure — interpreted by the troops as flight — triggered a disorderly retreat. The Bulgars sallied out, routed the Byzantines at the river crossings, and pursued them south of the Danube into Moesia.

Aftermath

Asparuh occupied territory down to the Balkan Mountains. In 681 Constantinople signed a peace treaty recognising the new Bulgarian state and paying it tribute — conventionally the founding date of Bulgaria, one of Europe's oldest surviving states.
