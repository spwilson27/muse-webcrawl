Roomba

Type: Autonomous robotic vacuum cleaner

Manufacturer: iRobot

Introduced: September 17, 2002

The Roomba is a series of autonomous robotic vacuum cleaners developed and sold by iRobot. First introduced on September 17, 2002, the Roomba was one of the first commercially successful domestic robots, and it is credited with bringing robot vacuum cleaners into mainstream households. The disc-shaped devices navigate autonomously around obstacles using a suite of sensors, cleaning floors without direct human control.

History

iRobot, founded in 1990 by MIT roboticists Rodney Brooks, Colin Angle, and Helen Greiner, launched the original Roomba in 2002 at a price of about US$200. Early models used a randomized "bump and turn" navigation strategy, changing direction when they struck an obstacle. The product line grew rapidly: the second generation added a "Dirt Detect" feature and larger dust bins, and later series introduced room mapping, self-emptying docks, and camera-based navigation.

In 2015 iRobot introduced the Roomba 980, the first model with systematic room mapping using vSLAM (visual simultaneous localization and mapping). The Roomba j7, released in 2021, added obstacle avoidance capable of identifying and steering around objects such as pet waste and charging cords. Higher-end models such as the Roomba Combo j9+ combine vacuuming with mopping and dock at stations that empty the dust bin and refill the mop reservoir.

Technology

Roombas move on two independently driven wheels with a front caster wheel, and carry sensors including cliff sensors that detect drops such as stairs, wall-following sensors, and optical encoders that estimate distance travelled. Cleaning is performed by counter-rotating brushes or a dual rubber-roller extractor system combined with suction and a side brush that sweeps debris from edges. Dirt is collected in a removable bin, in later models fitted with HEPA-style filtration.

Battery power comes from rechargeable packs; most models return automatically to a charging dock when their battery runs low. Since the 900 series, mapping data can be viewed in the iRobot Home app, and users can define "keep out zones" and room-specific cleaning schedules.

Reception and cultural impact

Roombas have sold in the tens of millions and became a cultural fixture, inspiring numerous videos of cats riding the devices and the practice of "Roomba art" using long-exposure photography of the robot's light trails. In 2022 Amazon announced plans to acquire iRobot for about US$1.7 billion; the deal was abandoned in January 2024 following regulatory opposition in the European Union, and Amazon paid a termination fee.
