James A. Garfield National Historic Site

Location: Mentor, Ohio, United States
Managed by: National Park Service

The James A. Garfield National Historic Site is a United States National Historic Site in Mentor, Ohio, preserving the home of James A. Garfield, the 20th president of the United States. The estate, nicknamed Lawnfield, was the site of Garfield's 1880 "front porch campaign" for the presidency and, after his assassination in 1881, became the home of his widow, Lucretia Garfield.

History

Garfield purchased the property in 1876 and enlarged the farmhouse substantially. During the 1880 campaign, delegations of supporters visited him at Lawnfield, where he spoke from his front porch — a model of campaigning later imitated by other candidates. After the president's death, Lucretia Garfield built the Memorial Library wing (1885–86), creating what is considered the first presidential library, containing the president's papers and books. The Garfield family donated the property to the Western Reserve Historical Society in the 1930s, and it later passed to the National Park Service, becoming a National Historic Site in 1980.

The site today

Visitors can tour the furnished main house, which contains about 80 percent original Garfield family furnishings, the Memorial Library, a carriage house, a windmill, and the small campaign office building. Exhibits interpret Garfield's life, his Civil War service, his presidency, and his assassination. The site is administered by the National Park Service and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
