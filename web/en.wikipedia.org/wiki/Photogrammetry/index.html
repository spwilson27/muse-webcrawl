Photogrammetry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A 3D model reconstructed from aerial photographs.

Photogrammetry is the science and technology of obtaining reliable information about physical objects and the environment through the process of recording, measuring and interpreting photographic images and patterns of electromagnetic radiant imagery and other phenomena.

Photogrammetry emerged from developments in optics and projective geometry in the nineteenth century; the French officer Aimé Laussedat developed the first applications using terrestrial photographs for topographic mapping in the 1850s. Aerial photogrammetry, using photographs from aircraft, became the principal method of producing topographic maps during the twentieth century, using stereoscopic plotting instruments to measure elevations from overlapping image pairs.

Methods

Modern digital photogrammetry uses algorithms such as structure from motion (SfM) and bundle adjustment to identify matching features across many overlapping photographs, reconstruct camera positions, and generate a dense point cloud, mesh, and texture of the subject. Outputs include orthophotos, digital elevation models, and textured 3D models. Ground control points measured by GNSS improve absolute accuracy.

Applications

Photogrammetry is used in surveying and mapping, architecture and heritage documentation, archaeology, geology, film and video-game production, forensics, and medicine. Inexpensive drone aircraft and consumer software have made close-range photogrammetry widely accessible, and it is integrated into many smartphones for 3D scanning.
