Skimmer (bird)

The skimmers, genus Rynchops, are three tern-like seabirds notable for their unusual bill: the lower mandible is much longer than the upper. They feed by flying low over water with the lower mandible slicing the surface to snap up fish.

Genus: Rynchops
Species: Black, African and Indian skimmers
Family: Laridae (gulls and allies)

Species

The black skimmer (Rynchops niger) lives in the Americas; the African skimmer (R. flavirostris) along African rivers and lakes; and the Indian skimmer (R. albicollis) in South Asia, now considered vulnerable due to habitat disturbance.

Feeding

Skimming is usually done at dusk or at night, when fish are near the surface. The protruding lower mandible, when it strikes prey, triggers a rapid head snap that closes the bill. Skimmers' pupils can contract to vertical slits, an adaptation unique among birds, helping reduce glare over water.
