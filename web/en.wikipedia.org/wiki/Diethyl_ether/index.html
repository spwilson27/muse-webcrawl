Diethyl ether

Formula(C2H5)2O
Boiling point34.6 °C
AppearanceColourless, highly volatile liquid

Diethyl ether, or simply ether, is an organic compound in the ether class with the formula (C2H5)2O. It is a colourless, highly volatile, sweet-smelling, extremely flammable liquid, commonly used as a solvent in laboratories and as a starting fluid for engines.

History

Ether was prepared as early as the 16th century, traditionally by distilling ethanol with sulfuric acid. Its anaesthetic properties were famously demonstrated in 1846 by William T. G. Morton at Massachusetts General Hospital, an event that launched modern surgical anaesthesia. Ether dominated anaesthesia for a century before being replaced by less flammable agents.

Properties and safety

Ether has low viscosity and a wide flammable range in air; stored ether can form explosive peroxides. It is a mild respiratory irritant and was once widely abused as a recreational inhalant.
