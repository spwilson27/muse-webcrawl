Stephen F. Austin

Born: November 3, 1793, Wythe County, Virginia, U.S.

Died: December 27, 1836 (aged 43), West Columbia, Republic of Texas

Known for: "Father of Texas"

Stephen Fuller Austin (November 3, 1793 – December 27, 1836) was an American-born empresario. Known as the "Father of Texas" and the founder of Anglo Texas, he led the second and, ultimately, the successful colonization of the region by bringing 300 families and their slaves from the United States to the tejanos region in 1825.

Born in southwestern Virginia, Austin's family moved to Missouri. He served in the state legislature and later studied law in New Orleans. After his father, Moses Austin, died in 1821, Stephen Austin inherited the empresario grant his father had obtained from Spain in 1820 to settle 300 families in Mexican Texas.

Early life

Stephen Fuller Austin was born in Wythe County, in southwestern Virginia, in 1793 to Mary Brown and Moses Austin. In 1798, his family moved to Missouri. Austin was sent to school in Connecticut and spent two years at Transylvania University in Lexington, Kentucky.

Colonization of Texas

Austin traveled to San Antonio de Béxar and gained approval of the plan from the Spanish governor. After the death of his father, Austin inherited the grant and proceeded with recruiting colonists, largely from the southern United States. The settlers, known as the "Old Three Hundred", arrived beginning in December 1821. Austin established his headquarters at San Felipe de Austin in 1824, from where he administered the colony.

During the early 1830s, Austin advocated for greater autonomy for Texas within Mexico, but tensions mounted. He was arrested in 1834 and imprisoned in Mexico City for much of that year and 1835. After his release, Austin initially supported negotiations with the Mexican government but eventually backed the Texas Revolution in late 1835.

Later life and death

Austin served briefly as Secretary of State of the Republic of Texas in 1836, but his health declined and he died of pneumonia on December 27, 1836, at West Columbia. The city of Austin, Texas, was later named in his honor.
