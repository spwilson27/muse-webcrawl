United States commemorative coins are special coins issued by the United States Mint to honour people, places, events, and institutions. Each programme must be authorised by an Act of Congress, and the coins are typically sold at a premium, with surcharges often benefiting a designated organisation or project.

The first U.S. commemorative coin was the Columbian half dollar of 1892-1893, issued for the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago. Early commemorative issues (1892-1954) were frequently criticised for speculative abuses, and no programmes were authorised for decades afterward.

Modern era

Commemorative coinage resumed in 1982 with the George Washington 250th Anniversary half dollar. Modern programmes, limited by law in number per year, have marked events such as the Olympic Games, the Statue of Liberty centennial, and the bicentennial of the U.S. Constitution. Coins are struck in proof and uncirculated finishes, in denominations including half dollars, silver dollars, and five-dollar gold pieces.
