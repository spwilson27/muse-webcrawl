Philotas (c. 365 – 330 BC) was a Macedonian nobleman and general in the army of Alexander the Great. The eldest son of Alexander's senior general Parmenion, Philotas commanded the Companion cavalry — the elite heavy cavalry of the Macedonian army — in the great battles of the Persian campaign, including Issus (333 BC) and Gaugamela (331 BC), and distinguished himself at the Persian Gate.

Fall and execution

Philotas was proud, outspoken and critical of Alexander's adoption of Persian customs and claims to divine sonship, and he made powerful enemies at court. In 330 BC, while the army was at Phrada in Drangiana, a conspiracy against Alexander's life (the plot of Dimnus) came to light. Philotas had been told of the plot and failed to report it to the king. He was arrested, tried before the army assembly, tortured, and executed — stoned or speared to death — together with the other alleged conspirators.

Alexander then sent orders east for the assassination of Parmenion at Ecbatana before news of his son's death could reach him, eliminating the whole family. Whether Philotas was actually guilty of treason, or merely of dangerous silence and aristocratic resentment, remains debated by historians; the affair is generally seen as a turning point in Alexander's reign, marking the destruction of the old Macedonian guard of Philip II.
