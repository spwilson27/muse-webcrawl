Data quality

Data quality refers to the condition of a set of values of qualitative or quantitative variables. Data is generally considered high quality if it is "fit for its intended uses in operations, decision making and planning". Poor data quality is a major cause of failed analytics, reporting, and system-integration projects.

Dimensions

  • Accuracy — the data correctly represents the real-world object or event.
  • Completeness — all required values are present; records are not missing.
  • Consistency — the same fact is represented the same way across systems.
  • Timeliness — data is available when needed and reflects the current state.
  • Validity — values conform to defined formats, ranges, and business rules.
  • Uniqueness — each real-world entity is recorded only once; no duplicates.

Management

Data quality management encompasses profiling (measuring the current state of data), cleansing and standardisation, matching and deduplication, and continuous monitoring with defined quality rules and thresholds. Governance frameworks assign ownership of critical data elements to data stewards, who are accountable for their quality. Industry bodies such as DAMA have codified these practices, and regulators in sectors such as banking (for example under BCBS 239) impose explicit data quality requirements for risk reporting.
