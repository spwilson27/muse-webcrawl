Santiago Calatrava

Santiago Calatrava Valls (born 28 July 1951) is a Spanish architect, structural engineer, sculptor, and painter known for his sculptural, white, skeletal structures inspired by natural and anatomical forms. He is among the most prominent — and most controversial, for cost overruns — architects of his generation.

Early life and education

Calatrava was born in Benimàmet, near Valencia, Spain. He studied architecture at the Polytechnic University of Valencia and civil engineering at ETH Zurich, where he earned a doctorate in 1981 with a thesis on the foldability of space frames.

Major works

His projects include the City of Arts and Sciences in Valencia, the Milwaukee Art Museum's Quadracci Pavilion with its movable brise-soleil, the Turning Torso skyscraper in Malmö, the Samuel Beckett Bridge in Dublin, the Athens Olympic Sports Complex, the Margaret Hunt Hill Bridge in Dallas, and the Oculus transportation hub at the World Trade Center in New York. His bridges, such as the Puente del Alamillo in Seville, frequently use inclined pylons and dramatic cable arrangements. The Margaret McDermott Bridge and other works have also drawn criticism for engineering problems and high costs.
