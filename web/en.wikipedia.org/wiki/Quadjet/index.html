Quadjet

Quadjet
RoleFour-engine jet aircraft
ExamplesBoeing 747, Airbus A340, Ilyushin Il-96, Avro RJ

A quadjet is an aircraft powered by four jet engines. Four-engine designs were common in the early decades of the jet age, when individual engines were less powerful and less reliable, and they long dominated long-haul flying because rules such as ETOPS restricted twin-engine aircraft from routes far from diversion airports.

History

The first quadjets included the de Havilland Comet and the Boeing 707, which established the template for the jet airliner. The wide-body Boeing 747 (1969) became the most famous quadjet, joined by the McDonnell Douglas DC-8 and later the Airbus A340 and A380 — the latter a four-engine double-deck aircraft. Smaller quadjets such as the BAe 146/Avro RJ were valued for steep approaches and short runways.

Decline

From the 1990s, improvements in engine reliability and the extension of ETOPS allowed twinjets such as the Boeing 777 and Airbus A330 to fly almost all long-haul routes at lower fuel and maintenance cost. Production of the A340 ended in 2011, the 747 in 2023 and the A380 in 2021, leaving quadjets largely in cargo, military and head-of-state roles, where four engines still offer payload, range or redundancy advantages.
