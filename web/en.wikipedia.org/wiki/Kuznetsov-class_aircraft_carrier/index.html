The Kuznetsov class, Soviet designation Project 1143.5, is a class of aircraft carriers built in the Soviet Union and operated by the Russian Navy. The lead ship, Admiral Flota Sovetskogo Soyuza Kuznetsov, is the Russian Navy's only aircraft carrier. China completed a second, unfinished hull as Liaoning and later built a closely derived ship, Shandong. Officially classed by Russia as a "heavy aircraft-carrying cruiser", the ships launch aircraft using a ski-jump ramp with arrestor wires for recovery (STOBAR), rather than catapults.

Design

Unlike Western carriers designed purely around their air groups, the Kuznetsov class was built with a heavy defensive and offensive missile battery, including twelve P-700 Granit (SS-N-19) anti-ship cruise missile launchers beneath the forward flight deck, extensive anti-aircraft missile and gun systems, and anti-submarine rocket launchers. The rationale included operating under the protective umbrella of land-based aviation and escorting forces in the North Atlantic and Barents Sea. Aviation facilities support Su-33 fighters (and, later, MiG-29K) plus Ka-27 and Ka-31 helicopters.

Ships and service

Admiral Kuznetsov was launched in 1985 and commissioned in 1991, just as the Soviet Union dissolved. Her career was marked by deployments to the Mediterranean, including combat operations off Syria in 2016–17, and by long and troubled refits; a floating dock sank during a 2018 refit, damaging the ship, and a fire broke out aboard in 2019. The second hull, Varyag, was sold incomplete to China in 1998 and, after a long refit, entered Chinese service as Liaoning in 2012, becoming the PLAN's first carrier and the basis for its subsequent carrier programme.
