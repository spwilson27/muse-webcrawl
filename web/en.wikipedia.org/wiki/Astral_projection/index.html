Astral projection (also called astral travel) is a term used in esotericism to describe an intentional out-of-body experience in which a subtle body, often called the "astral body," is said to separate from the physical body and travel through an "astral plane." The concept became popular through Theosophy in the late 19th century and authors such as Sylvan Muldoon, Hereward Carrington and Robert Monroe.

Beliefs and practices

Ideas of a travelling soul or double appear in ancient Egyptian, Greek, Hindu, Daoist and shamanic traditions. Modern occult systems teach induction techniques — relaxation, trance, visualisation, and the "rope technique" of imagining climbing out of the body — often accompanied by reported vibrations and sleep paralysis. Practitioners claim to visit physical places or non-physical realms and beings.

Scientific view

There is no scientific evidence that consciousness can leave the body or perceive distant places; controlled tests of claimed perception during OBEs have failed to confirm it. Mainstream science treats such experiences as dissociative phenomena of the brain, related to lucid dreaming, sleep paralysis and disturbances of bodily self-awareness, and they can be induced by stimulation of the temporoparietal junction.
