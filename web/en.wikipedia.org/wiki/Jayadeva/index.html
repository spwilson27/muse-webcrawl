Jayadeva, the 12th-century Sanskrit poet of the Gita Govinda.

Jayadeva (c. 1170 – c. 1245 CE) was a Sanskrit poet best known for the Gita Govinda, a lyric poem describing the love of the Hindu god Krishna for the gopi Radha. The work occupies a central place in the bhakti devotional tradition and has been enormously influential on Indian poetry, music, dance, and painting. Jayadeva's two hymns are also included in the Guru Granth Sahib, the holy scripture of Sikhism, making him one of only a handful of Hindu poets so honored.

Life

Little is known with certainty about Jayadeva's life. Tradition holds that he was born in the village of Kenduli Sasan near Puri in present-day Odisha — though villages in West Bengal and Mithila also claim him — and that he served as a court poet to Lakshmanasena, the last Hindu ruler of Bengal. He is said to have married Padmavati, often described as a temple dancer, and to have spent his later years at Puri in devotion to Jagannath. His biography is known largely through later devotional texts such as the Bhaktamala, which recount miracles associated with the composition of the Gita Govinda.

The Gita Govinda

The Gita Govinda ("Song of Govinda") is organized into twelve chapters (sargas), further divided into twenty-four songs (prabandhas) of eight couplets each. The ashtapadi songs trace the separation, longing, and reunion of Radha and Krishna, and are interpreted on several levels: as erotic love poetry, as courtly lyric, and as an allegory of the soul's yearning for God. The poem's synthesis of classical Sanskrit form with intense devotional feeling made it a model for later Vaishnava literature, and its songs are still sung in temples at Puri and performed in the Odissi dance tradition.

Influence

Jayadeva's work shaped the development of regional devotional literatures across eastern India, and inspired generations of miniature painters, who illustrated the Radha–Krishna story in the Pahari, Rajasthani, and Odia schools. The first English translation, by Sir William Jones, appeared in 1792, and the poem later influenced European writers including Goethe. His birth anniversary, Jayadeva Jayanti, is celebrated in Odisha and by devotees of the Jagannath cult.
