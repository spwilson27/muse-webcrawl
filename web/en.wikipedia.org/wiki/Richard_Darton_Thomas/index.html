Richard Darton Thomas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Richard Darton Thomas (17 May 1879 – 16 October 1954), usually cited as R. D. Thomas, was a British composer, organist, musicologist and bibliographer, best known for his pioneering study of the composer Thomas Tomkins and for his work on Welsh church music.

Life and career

Thomas was born in Cardiff, Wales. He studied music and worked as a schoolmaster and organist, settling in London where he taught at the Mary Datchelor School and became a specialist in the cataloguing and bibliography of early printed music. He contributed articles to musical dictionaries and periodicals and was involved in the editorial work of early-music societies dedicated to Tudor and Jacobean composers.

Work on Tomkins

Thomas's most lasting contribution is his study of the English composer Thomas Tomkins (1572–1656). From the 1920s he located, copied and edited a large quantity of Tomkins's keyboard and consort music, much of it surviving only in manuscript, and published in 1925 the first substantial modern study of the composer's life and works. His transcripts and notes materially advanced the revival of Tomkins's music; later editors built on the sources he had identified.

Other work

Thomas also wrote about Welsh musical history and song, composed church music and part-songs, and assembled an extensive private library of early music books and manuscripts. His papers and scores are preserved in Welsh and London collections.
