Golden is a city in and the county seat of Jefferson County, Colorado, United States. Founded during the Pike's Peak Gold Rush on 16 June 1859, the town — originally called Golden City — was named after prospector Thomas L. Golden. It served as the capital of the provisional Territory of Jefferson and, from 1862 to 1867, as the capital of the Colorado Territory.

Golden lies at the mouth of Clear Creek Canyon at the foothills of the Rocky Mountains, about 25 miles west of Denver. It is home to the Colorado School of Mines, founded in 1874, and to the Coors Brewing Company, whose brewery in Golden, established by Adolph Coors in 1873, is among the largest single-site breweries in the world. Other major presences include the National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL).

Landmarks

The city is known for its "Welcome to Golden" arch over Washington Avenue, the Clear Creek History Park, the Colorado Railroad Museum, and Buffalo Bill's grave and museum on Lookout Mountain, which overlooks the city. Clear Creek, running through downtown, is popular for kayaking and tubing.
