Grain growth

Grain growth is the increase in size of grains (crystallites) in a material at high temperature. In materials science, it occurs in polycrystalline materials when recovery and recrystallisation are complete and further reduction in the internal energy can only be achieved by reducing the total area of grain boundaries.

Mechanism

Grain growth proceeds by the migration of grain boundaries. Because boundaries are regions of higher energy, the system lowers its total energy as larger grains consume smaller ones, reducing total boundary area. The process is driven by boundary curvature and strongly accelerated by temperature.

Normal and abnormal growth

In normal grain growth, the grain size distribution remains roughly self-similar as the average size increases. In abnormal grain growth (secondary recrystallisation), a few grains grow dramatically at the expense of others, producing a bimodal microstructure, often due to second-phase particles, texture effects or surface energy differences.

Importance

Grain size strongly influences mechanical properties: through the Hall–Petch relation, finer grains generally increase strength and hardness. Controlling grain growth during sintering and heat treatment is therefore central to ceramics and metallurgy.
