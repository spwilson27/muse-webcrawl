3G Capital is a Brazilian-American investment firm founded in 2004 by the partners behind the Brazilian investment group that built Ambev and later AB InBev, including Jorge Paulo Lemann, Carlos Alberto Sicupira and Marcel Herrmann Telles. The firm is known for very large consumer-sector buyouts executed with aggressive cost-cutting, often in partnership with Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway.

Major deals

3G acquired Burger King in 2010 for about $4 billion, then merged it with Canada's Tim Hortons in 2014 to form Restaurant Brands International, later adding Popeyes (2017) and Firehouse Subs. In 2013 it teamed with Berkshire Hathaway to buy H. J. Heinz for $28 billion, and in 2015 engineered the merger of Heinz with Kraft Foods to create Kraft Heinz, one of the world's largest food companies.

Management style

The firm is associated with zero-based budgeting — requiring managers to justify every expense each year from scratch — and a culture of meritocracy, lean staffing and tight cost control imported from its brewing businesses. These methods delivered large margin gains but also drew criticism that underinvestment hurt brand growth, notably at Kraft Heinz, which took a major writedown in 2019.
