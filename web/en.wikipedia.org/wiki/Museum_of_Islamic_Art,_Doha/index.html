Overview

The Museum of Islamic Art (MIA) in Doha, Qatar, is a museum of Islamic art from three continents over 1,400 years, opened in 2008. Designed by I. M. Pei — who came out of retirement and travelled the Islamic world for inspiration, drawing on the ablutions fountain of Cairo's Ibn Tulun Mosque — the museum stands on a purpose-built island off Doha's Corniche, its stacked limestone geometry rising from the water against the West Bay skyline.

Collection

The collection spans ceramics, metalwork, jewellery, woodwork, textiles and glass from Spain to India and Central Asia, including a celebrated figure of a seated prince, Mughal jades and carpets, and one of the world's finest holdings of Islamic manuscripts and scientific instruments. MIA Park beside it hosts public art including Richard Serra's 7. The museum anchors Qatar's cultural quarter with the National Museum of Qatar (2019) and the future Lusail museum projects.
