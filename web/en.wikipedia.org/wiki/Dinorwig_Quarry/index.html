Dinorwig Quarry was a slate quarry near Llanberis in Gwynedd, north-west Wales. At its height in the late 19th century it was, with the neighbouring Penrhyn Quarry, one of the two largest slate quarries in the world, employing more than 3,000 men and producing roofing slate for the rapidly growing industrial cities of Britain and beyond.

History

Slate was worked on the site from the late 18th century; the quarry was developed from 1787 and expanded rapidly after the opening of the Dinorwig Railway and later the Padarn Railway, which carried finished slates down to Port Dinorwic on the Menai Strait. The workings climbed the flank of Elidir Fawr in a series of great terraced galleries, each served by inclines and by locomotive-worked levels.

The quarrymen worked in small partnerships called bargains, splitting and dressing the slate by hand in open sheds. Conditions were dangerous and dusty, and disputes over pay and conditions — notably the lockout of 1885–86 — left a lasting mark on the community.

Closure and legacy

Trade declined through the 20th century as cheaper roofing materials displaced slate, and the quarry closed abruptly in August 1969. Part of the site is now occupied by the Dinorwig power station, a pumped-storage hydroelectric scheme. The quarry workshops at Gilfach Ddu house the National Slate Museum. In 2021 the quarry was inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "The Slate Landscape of Northwest Wales".
