Lake Wakatipu

Lake Wakatipu (Māori: Whakatipu Waimāori) is an inland lake in the Otago region of New Zealand's South Island. With a length of about 80 kilometres, it is the country's longest lake, and at 291 square kilometres its third largest. It lies at an altitude of 310 metres at the southern edge of the Southern Alps.

Geography

The lake has a distinctive zigzag shape formed by glacial action. It is fed by the Dart and Rees rivers at its northern end near Glenorchy and is drained by the Kawarau River near Frankton. The resort town of Queenstown sits on its northeastern shore, beneath the mountain range known as The Remarkables. The lake exhibits a standing wave (seiche) that causes its level to rise and fall by about 12 centimetres every 27 minutes.

Māori legend

In Māori tradition the lake's shape is explained as the burned-out hollow of the giant Matau, whose heart is said to still beat beneath the water, causing the seiche.

Tourism

The vintage steamship TSS Earnslaw has cruised the lake since 1912. The lake is used for boating, kayaking, trout fishing, and lakeside trails.
