Jake Burton Carpenter

Jake Burton Carpenter (April 29, 1954 – November 20, 2019), known as Jake Burton, was an American snowboarder and founder of Burton Snowboards, the company widely credited with building snowboarding into a mainstream global sport. He is often called the "godfather of snowboarding."

Life and career

Carpenter grew up in New York City and Cedarhurst, Long Island. After graduating from the Brooks School and briefly attending the University of Colorado, he earned an economics degree from New York University. Inspired by Sherman Poppen's Snurfer, he founded Burton Snowboards in a barn in Londonderry, Vermont, in 1977, convinced he could improve the board's design with bindings and better construction.

Early years were lean — he sold only a few hundred boards in his first year — but Carpenter evangelized the sport tirelessly, persuading ski resorts to allow snowboarders on their lifts, sponsoring riders, and helping organize the first National Snowboard Championships in 1982. Snowboarding grew rapidly, becoming an Olympic sport in 1998, with Burton as its dominant equipment brand.

Later life

Carpenter survived testicular cancer in 2011 and was later diagnosed with Miller Fisher syndrome, a variant of Guillain–Barré syndrome, in 2018. He died in November 2019 from complications related to his cancer. His contributions were recognized with induction into the U.S. Ski and Snowboard Hall of Fame.
