Herrenchiemsee

Herrenchiemsee is a complex of royal buildings on Herreninsel, the largest island in the Chiemsee lake in southern Bavaria, Germany. Its centrepiece is the New Palace (Neues Schloss), built by King Ludwig II of Bavaria from 1878 as a homage to Versailles and to Louis XIV of France.

The New Palace

Ludwig bought Herreninsel in 1873 to prevent its forests being felled. Construction of the palace, designed by Georg von Dollmann, began in 1878; although modelled on Versailles, the palace was never finished — work stopped with the king's death in 1886, and only the central portion of the planned three wings was completed. Ludwig spent only about ten nights there in total, in September 1885.

Interiors

The completed state rooms surpass Versailles in opulence: the Great Hall of Mirrors, illuminated by 33 chandeliers and 44 candelabra, is longer than its French model, and the staircase, state bedroom and dining room (with a mechanically rising "wishing table") display the finest craftsmanship of the period. The unfinished sections were demolished in the 20th century or left as bare brick shells.

Later history

In August 1948, the constitutional convention that drafted the Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany met in the Old Palace (the former Augustinian monastery) on the island. Today the complex is administered by the Bavarian Palace Department and is a major tourist attraction; the island also preserves the apartment where Ludwig lived during construction and a museum devoted to his life.
