Black Elk Peak, formerly known as Harney Peak, is the highest natural point in South Dakota and the highest summit in the United States east of the Rocky Mountains. It lies in the Black Elk Wilderness area, in the Black Hills of Pennington County, within Black Hills National Forest. A stone fire lookout tower, built in the 1930s, stands on the summit.

Name

The peak was long named for U.S. Army general William S. Harney. The Lakota name for the mountain is Hiŋháŋ Káǧa ("owl maker"). In 2016, the U.S. Board on Geographic Names renamed it Black Elk Peak in honour of Black Elk, the noted Oglala Lakota medicine man, following a campaign arguing that Harney's record — including an 1855 attack on a Lakota village — made his name inappropriate for a mountain sacred to Native peoples.

Geology and recreation

The summit is composed of ancient Precambrian granite, part of the core of the Black Hills uplift. It is a popular hiking destination, reached by trails from Sylvan Lake in Custer State Park and from the Black Elk Wilderness. On clear days, parts of four states can be seen from the top.
