Three Jewels

The Three Jewels, also called the Three Treasures, Three Refuges or Triple Gem (Pali: tiratana; Sanskrit: triratna), are the three things in which Buddhists take refuge and which distinguish a Buddhist from a non-Buddhist: the Buddha (the Awakened One), the Dharma (the teaching), and the Sangha (the community). Taking refuge in the Three Jewels is traditionally the ceremony by which one becomes a Buddhist.

The refuge formula

The refuge is traditionally taken by reciting, in Pali:

Buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi — I go to the Buddha for refuge.
Dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi — I go to the Dhamma for refuge.
Saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi — I go to the Saṅgha for refuge.

The formula is recited three times. In most traditions it is combined with taking the Five Precepts.

The three jewels

  • The Buddha — Siddhartha Gautama, who discovered the path to awakening, and by extension the potential for Buddhahood and, in Mahayana traditions, the Buddha-nature in all beings.
  • The Dharma — the teachings of the Buddha, including the Four Noble Truths and the scriptures, as well as the truth itself to which they point.
  • The Sangha — in the narrow sense the monastic order of monks and nuns; more broadly, the community of noble disciples and of all practitioners.

Symbolism

The Three Jewels are represented by the triratna symbol, a three-pronged emblem found on early Buddhist art and coinage, often combined with the dharmachakra (wheel of the Dharma) and the lotus. In Jainism, a distinct "three jewels" (ratnatraya) — right faith, right knowledge and right conduct — denotes the path to liberation.
