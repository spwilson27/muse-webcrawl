Financial Ombudsman Service

The service resolves disputes between consumers and financial firms
Formed2001 (under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000)
JurisdictionUnited Kingdom
HeadquartersLondon
TypeStatutory ombudsman scheme

The Financial Ombudsman Service is an ombudsman service in the United Kingdom that resolves disputes between consumers and businesses providing financial services, such as banks, insurers, investment firms, and lenders. Established under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000, it began operating in 2001, unifying several predecessor schemes.

Role and process

The service is free to consumers. A consumer must first complain to the business and allow it time to respond (normally up to eight weeks); if the dispute remains unresolved, the ombudsman service can investigate. It decides cases on the basis of what is "fair and reasonable in all the circumstances", taking into account the law, regulation, and good industry practice. If an ombudsman's final decision is accepted by the consumer, it is legally binding on the business; if rejected, the consumer may still go to court.

Powers

The service can direct a business to put things right and to pay compensation, including money awards up to a limit set in rules (which has risen over time), along with interest and compensation for distress and inconvenience.

Workload

Caseloads have at times surged into the hundreds of thousands per year, most notably during the payment protection insurance (PPI) mis-selling scandal, which generated millions of complaints across the industry before the August 2019 deadline for PPI complaints.
