Air Inuit

Air Inuit (Inuktitut: ᐃᓄᐃᑦ ᖃᖓᑦᑕᔩᑦ) is an airline based in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. It operates domestic scheduled services and charter flights in the Nunavik region of northern Quebec, as well as to southern gateways including Montreal. The airline is wholly owned by the Inuit of Nunavik through the Makivik Corporation, and was established to provide an essential air link for the region's remote communities, most of which have no road access to the south.

History

Air Inuit was founded in 1978 by the Inuit of northern Quebec, growing out of the cooperative movement that followed the signing of the James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement in 1975. From the outset the company was conceived as an instrument of Inuit economic self-determination: profits remain in the North and the airline trains and employs Inuit pilots, agents, and maintenance staff. The carrier began with small bush aircraft suited to gravel strips and grew steadily, adding de Havilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otters and later Dash 8 turboprops and Boeing 737 jets for the trunk routes to Montreal.

Operations

The airline serves the fourteen Inuit communities of Nunavik along the Hudson Bay, Hudson Strait, and Ungava Bay coasts, together with destinations in southern Quebec. Its services combine passenger, cargo, and combi configurations, carrying food, building materials, vehicles, and medical evacuees in addition to travellers. Air Inuit also operates charter flights for mining companies, government agencies, and tourism, and provides medevac support in the region.

Fleet

The fleet has included Boeing 737-200 and 737 Next Generation aircraft equipped for gravel-kit operations on northern runways, de Havilland Canada Dash 8-100 and Q300 turboprops, DHC-6 Twin Otters for the shortest strips, and Beechcraft King Air aircraft for charter and medevac work. In 2023 Air Inuit announced the acquisition of Boeing 737-800 aircraft, including freighter conversions, to renew its jet fleet.
