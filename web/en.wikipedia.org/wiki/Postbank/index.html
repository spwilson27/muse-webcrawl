Postbank

The Post Tower in Bonn, long associated with Postbank's parent group.

Postbank is a German retail bank that originated in the postal savings and giro services of the German postal system. Its roots lie in the cheque and savings services offered through post offices from the early twentieth century, which made basic banking available across the country, including in rural areas. The modern bank emerged from the restructuring of the Deutsche Bundespost: in 1990 the postal services were divided, and the banking arm became Deutsche Postbank AG, which was formally established in 1995 when the Bundespost was broken up into Deutsche Post, Deutsche Telekom and Postbank.

Development and ownership

Postbank was partly privatised through an initial public offering in 2004, at the time one of the largest in German history. Deutsche Post gradually reduced its stake, and in 2008 it agreed to sell the bank to Deutsche Bank, which acquired a majority holding in stages and completed full ownership by 2012. Postbank was integrated into Deutsche Bank's retail division, while the Postbank brand continued in use for a large network of branches, many of them combined with Deutsche Post outlets, so that banking services remained available in post offices across Germany.

Business

With around twelve million customers, Postbank was one of Germany's largest retail banks, offering current accounts, savings products, consumer credit, mortgages (partly through its BHW building society subsidiary), and services for small businesses. In the 2020s Deutsche Bank undertook a controversial migration of Postbank customers onto its own IT platform, which caused widespread service problems in 2023 and drew regulatory criticism, and announced the phased replacement of the Postbank brand in some channels.

Similar "postbanks" exist or have existed in many countries, where postal networks were used to deliver savings and payment services, including in France, Italy, Japan and the Netherlands.
