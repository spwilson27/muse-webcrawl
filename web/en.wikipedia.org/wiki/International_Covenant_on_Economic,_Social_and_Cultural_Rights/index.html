International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR) is a multilateral treaty adopted by the United Nations General Assembly on 16 December 1966 and in force from 3 January 1976. Together with the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights it forms the International Bill of Human Rights.

The Covenant recognises rights to work and just conditions of work, social security, an adequate standard of living (food, clothing, housing), the highest attainable standard of health, education, and participation in cultural life. Distinctively, states undertake to realise these rights “progressively”, to the maximum of available resources (Article 2(1)) — a flexibility that has fuelled debate about whether economic and social rights are fully justiciable; the supervisory Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (established 1985) insists that some obligations (non-discrimination, minimum core provision) are immediate. An Optional Protocol (2008, in force 2013) allows individual complaints. Most states are parties; the United States signed (1977) but has not ratified.
