Cognitive deficit

Cognitive deficit is an inclusive term describing impairment in one or more domains of cognitive functioning, such as memory, attention, perception, language, executive function and processing speed. Cognitive deficits may be congenital or acquired, temporary or permanent, mild or severe, and they occur in a wide range of neurological, psychiatric and developmental conditions. The term is broader than intellectual disability and includes deficits that arise after illness or injury in persons of previously normal intelligence.

Causes

Acquired causes include stroke, traumatic brain injury, dementia (such as Alzheimer's disease), infections, tumours, substance abuse and the cognitive side effects of some medications. Cognitive dysfunction is also a core feature of schizophrenia and is common in depression, multiple sclerosis and Parkinson's disease. Congenital and developmental causes include genetic syndromes and prenatal exposures such as fetal alcohol spectrum disorders.

Assessment and treatment

Deficits are characterised with neuropsychological testing; brief screening tools include the Mini–Mental State Examination and the Montreal Cognitive Assessment. Management depends on the underlying cause and may involve treating the primary condition, cognitive rehabilitation, occupational therapy, and compensatory strategies such as external memory aids.
