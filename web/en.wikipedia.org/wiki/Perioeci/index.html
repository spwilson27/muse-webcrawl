Perioeci

The perioeci or perioikoi (Greek: περίοικοι, "dwellers around") were a class of free but politically subordinate inhabitants in several ancient Greek states, most notably in Sparta's territories of Laconia and Messenia. They lived in their own communities surrounding the citizen centre, enjoyed personal freedom and managed their local affairs, but had no vote in the Spartan assembly and were subject to Spartan authority, especially in foreign policy and war.

Status in Sparta

Perioeci occupied an intermediate position between the full Spartan citizens (the Spartiates) and the enslaved helots. They were free men who farmed, traded, and practised crafts — activities from which Spartiates were barred by their military way of life — and they paid taxes or tribute. In wartime the perioeci served in the Spartan army as hoplites, and by the fifth century BC they formed a large part of it, serving in mixed units alongside Spartiates.

Their towns, of which dozens are known, lay in the hill country of the Eurotas valley and along the Laconian coast. Perioecic communities were internally self-governing but had no say in the decisions of the Spartan state, and perioeci could not marry into the citizen body or hold Spartan office.

Origins

The origins of the perioeci are debated. They may have been descendants of earlier inhabitants of Laconia who submitted to the Dorian Spartans without being enslaved, or communities subjugated by Spartan expansion that retained a measure of autonomy. Similar groups existed in other Dorian states, such as the communities subject to Argos.
