Differential (mechanical device)

A differential is a gear train with three drive shafts that has the property that the rotational speed of one shaft is the average of the speeds of the others. A common use of differentials is in motor vehicles, to allow the wheels at each end of an axle to rotate at different speeds while cornering.

An automotive differential in cutaway view

Operation

In a car, the engine drives a pinion and ring gear that turn a carrier; planet (spider) bevel gears inside the carrier mesh with side gears on the two axle shafts. When driving straight, the planet gears do not spin on their own axis and both wheels turn together. In a turn, the planet gears rotate, letting the outer wheel spin faster than the inner one while torque is still delivered to both.

History

Differential mechanisms appear in the ancient Chinese south-pointing chariot and in geared devices of the 19th century; the automotive form was developed as cars became common in the early 20th century.

Limitations and variants

An open differential sends equal torque to both wheels, so a wheel on ice can spin uselessly. Limited-slip, locking and torque-vectoring differentials, and electronic traction control, counter this. Epicyclic (planetary) gear arrangements provide differential action in automatic transmissions and hybrid drivetrains.
