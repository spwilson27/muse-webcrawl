Piperales is an order of flowering plants (4,170 recognized species). It includes the family Piperaceae, the pepper family, which contains black pepper and kava, as well as many other commercially important and medicinally useful species. The order belongs to the magnoliid clade, a group of early-diverging angiosperms distinct from both the monocots and the eudicots.

Classification

In the APG IV system (2016), the order Piperales comprises three families:

Aristolochiaceae — the birthwort family, including Aristolochia and relatives; Piperaceae — the pepper family, including Piper (peppers), Peperomia, and Macropiper; and Saururaceae — the lizard's tail family, a small group of rhizomatous herbs of East Asia and North America.

Older classifications, such as the Cronquist system, sometimes placed the families Chloranthaceae and Lactoridaceae in the order as well; molecular phylogenetic studies have since moved those families elsewhere, and Hydnoraceae has been subsumed into Aristolochiaceae.

Characteristics

Members of Piperales are mostly herbs, shrubs, or lianas with swollen nodes. The leaves are typically simple and alternate, often with sheathing bases. The flowers are small, densely packed in spikes or racemes, and usually lack a distinct perianth. Many species accumulate alkaloids and other pungent compounds — the source of the heat of piperine in black pepper.

Economic importance

Black pepper (Piper nigrum) is among the world's most traded spices. Kava (Piper methysticum) is a traditional Pacific ceremonial beverage plant, and Peperomia species are popular houseplants. Wild ginger (Asarum, in Aristolochiaceae) is grown as an ornamental ground cover.
