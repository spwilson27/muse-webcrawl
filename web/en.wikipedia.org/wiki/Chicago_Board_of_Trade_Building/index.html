Overview

The Chicago Board of Trade Building is a 44-story Art Deco skyscraper at 141 West Jackson Boulevard in the Loop community area of Chicago, Illinois. Completed in 1930, it has been the home of the Chicago Board of Trade (now part of CME Group) and was the tallest building in Chicago until 1965.

Architecture

Designed by the firm of Holabird & Root, the building is clad in grey Indiana limestone and rises without setbacks to a pyramidal roof crowned by a 9.5-metre aluminium statue of Ceres, the Roman goddess of agriculture, a reference to the exchange's grain markets. Sculptures by John W. Norton and Alvin Meyer include figures representing industry and agriculture, and a three-story marble trading hall interior (removed in later renovations) was a noted Art Deco space.

History

The building replaced an earlier 1885 Board of Trade headquarters designed by William W. Boyington, whose clock tower was a Chicago landmark. Designated a Chicago Landmark in 1978 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places, the building and its trading floors — including the open-outcry pits depicted in popular culture — long symbolised Chicago's role in global commodities markets.
