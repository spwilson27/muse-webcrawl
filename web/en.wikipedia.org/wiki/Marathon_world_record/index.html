Marathon world record

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The marathon world record is the fastest time over the marathon distance of 42.195 kilometres (26 miles 385 yards) recognised by World Athletics. Marathon world records are currently held by Kelvin Kiptum of Kenya, who ran 2:00:35 at the 2023 Chicago Marathon, and Tigst Assefa of Ethiopia, who ran 2:11:53 at the 2023 Berlin Marathon in the women's category. In a mixed-gender race, the women's record was later improved by Ruth Chepngetich of Kenya, who ran 2:09:56 at the 2024 Chicago Marathon, becoming the first woman to break 2:10.

Recognition

World Athletics (formerly the IAAF) began recognising marathon world records, rather than "world bests", from 1 January 2004, after standardising criteria for record-eligible courses. Courses must be point-to-point limited in net elevation drop and start-finish separation, which means races such as the Boston Marathon are not eligible for world record purposes.

Women's progression

The women's record progressed from Tegla Loroupe's 2:20:47 in 1998, through Catherine Ndereba and Paula Radcliffe, whose 2:15:25 at the 2003 London Marathon stood for sixteen years, to Brigid Kosgei's 2:14:04 in 2019, Tigst Assefa's 2:11:53 in 2023 and Ruth Chepngetich's 2:09:56 in 2024.

Sub-2-hour marathon

Eliud Kipchoge ran 1:59:40.2 at the INEOS 1:59 Challenge in Vienna in October 2019, but the performance was not eligible for record purposes because of rotating pacemakers, drinks delivery and other assistance.
