Customer acquisition cost

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is the cost of winning a customer to purchase a product or service. It is a key business metric used together with customer lifetime value (LTV) to assess the profitability and scalability of a company's business model.

Calculation

CAC is typically calculated by dividing the total sales and marketing expenses over a period by the number of new customers acquired in that period. Expenses may include advertising spend, sales staff salaries, commissions, and marketing overhead.

Use

Investors in subscription and software-as-a-service businesses commonly compare the ratio of LTV to CAC; a ratio comfortably above 1 indicates that each customer generates more value than the cost of acquiring them. Rising CAC can signal intensifying competition or market saturation.
