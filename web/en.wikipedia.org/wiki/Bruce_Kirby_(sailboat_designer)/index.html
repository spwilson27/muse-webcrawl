Bruce Kirby (sailboat designer)

The Laser (now ILCA) dinghy is Bruce Kirby's most famous design.

Bruce Kirby (February 2, 1929 – July 18, 2021) was a Canadian sailboat designer, sailor and journalist, best known as the designer of the Laser dinghy, the most popular racing sailboat class in the world and an Olympic class for many years. Born in Ottawa, Ontario and raised in Montreal, he worked as editor of the magazine One Design Yachtsman (later Sailing World) in the United States.

The Laser

In 1970 Kirby sketched the design for a simple, inexpensive car-top dinghy during a telephone conversation; the prototype, built by Ian Bruce, debuted as the "Weekender" and was renamed the Laser. More than 200,000 have been built. A long-running dispute over builder licensing and the class name led to the design being marketed as the ILCA Dinghy from 2019.

Other designs

Kirby designed dozens of boats, including the Kirby 25, the Sonar keelboat used in Paralympic sailing, the Ideal 18, and the America's Cup challenger Canada One-era designs through his involvement with Canadian campaigns (he designed the 12-metre yachts Canada I and Canada II). He was inducted into Canada's Sports Hall of Fame and the U.S. National Sailing Hall of Fame.
