The American Red Cross (ARC), also known as the American National Red Cross, is a non-profit humanitarian organisation that provides emergency assistance, disaster relief, and disaster-preparedness education in the United States. It is the designated national affiliate of the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies and part of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement.

History

Clara Barton, who had nursed soldiers in the American Civil War and worked with the International Committee of the Red Cross during the Franco-Prussian War, campaigned for an American society and founded it in 1881, serving as its first president until 1904. The organisation received its first congressional charter in 1900; under its federal charter the President of the United States is its honorary chairman.

Services

The Red Cross responds to tens of thousands of disasters each year — mainly house fires, plus hurricanes, floods, and wildfires — providing shelter, food, and health services. It collects and distributes about 40 per cent of the nation's blood supply through its Biomedical Services, trains millions in first aid, CPR, and swimming, supports military families through Service to the Armed Forces, and participates in international relief and measles-and-rubella vaccination campaigns. Its workforce is overwhelmingly volunteer.
