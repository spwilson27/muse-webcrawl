The Texas City Refinery explosion occurred on March 23, 2005, at the BP refinery in Texas City, Texas, one of the largest oil refineries in the United States. A hydrocarbon vapor cloud formed during the startup of an isomerization unit ignited, killing 15 workers — most of them contractors in trailers near the unit — and injuring 180 others.

Causes

Investigations by the U.S. Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board (CSB) and an independent panel chaired by former Secretary of State James Baker found that a raffinate splitter tower was overfilled, overwhelming a blowdown drum that vented flammable liquid to the atmosphere instead of to a flare. The CSB cited organizational and safety culture failures at BP, inadequate instrumentation and alarms, and the unsafe siting of temporary trailers.

Consequences

BP faced record OSHA fines, later increased to over $80 million, pleaded guilty to a felony violation of the Clean Air Act, and paid billions in settlements and penalties. The disaster drove industry-wide reforms in process safety, including API guidance on trailer siting and fatigue management, and became a landmark case study in process safety management.
