Marks and Spencer

Marks and Spencer (M&S) is a British retailer founded in Leeds in 1884 by Michael Marks, a Polish-born immigrant who ran "penny bazaar" stalls, in partnership from 1894 with Thomas Spencer. By the early 20th century the chain sold only British-made goods under the St Michael brand, registered in 1928.

Development

The flagship Marble Arch store in London opened in 1930. M&S became famous for quality clothing, lingerie, and innovative food retailing, introducing items such as chilled ready meals and melt-in-the-middle puddings. After a profit peak in the late 1990s and a failed takeover bid by Philip Green in 2004, the company pursued repeated turnarounds, closing smaller high-street stores, growing M&S Food, and joining the FTSE 100 again in 2023 after a period outside it.
