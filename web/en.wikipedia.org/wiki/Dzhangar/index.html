Dzhangar (Jangar)
OriginKalmyk (Oirat Mongol) oral tradition
FormEpic poem, sung by jangarchi bards
HeroKhan Jangar of the land of Bumba

The Dzhangar (also spelled Jangar or Djangar) is the heroic epic of the Kalmyk people, the Oirat Mongols of the lower Volga region. It recounts the deeds of Khan Jangar and his twelve (in some versions more) loyal heroes defending their idyllic homeland of Bumba against invading enemies.

Tradition

The epic belongs to the great oral epic tradition of Central Asia, alongside the Kyrgyz Manas and the Mongol Geser. It was preserved by professional bards called jangarchi, who performed chapters (songs) of the cycle from memory, accompanied by the two-stringed dombra or the tovshuur lute. The best-known cycles comprise 12 to 15 chapters, though more have been recorded. The epic's roots are often dated to the 15th–17th centuries, the era of the Oirat khanates.

Recording and legacy

Chapters were first written down in the 19th century; a major version was recorded from the celebrated jangarchi Eelyan Ovla in the early 20th century. In Kalmykia the epic is a central symbol of national identity, celebrated in festivals, monuments in the capital Elista, and the republic's cultural life; Jangar performance was disrupted during the Kalmyk deportation of 1943–57 but revived after the people's return.
