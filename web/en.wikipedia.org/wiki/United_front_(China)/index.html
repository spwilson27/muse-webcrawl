United front (China)

The united front in China is a political strategy and network of groups and key individuals that are influenced or controlled by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and used to advance its interests. It is used to bring non-CCP organizations and individuals under the party's influence and to promote the party's goals among groups that are not formal members of the party.

The united front traces its origins to the alliance between the Kuomintang (Nationalist Party) and the CCP in the 1920s, known as the First United Front, formed to defeat the warlords of northern China. A Second United Front followed in 1937 to resist the Japanese invasion. After the founding of the People's Republic of China in 1949, the united front became institutionalized through bodies such as the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC), which includes representatives of the eight "democratic parties" licensed to operate under CCP leadership.

United Front Work Department

The strategy is coordinated by the United Front Work Department (UFWD) of the Central Committee of the CCP. The department works with overseas Chinese communities, ethnic and religious groups, private entrepreneurs, intellectuals, and organizations in Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan. Its activities include cultivating relationships with influential figures, managing religious affairs, and promoting the party's narrative abroad.

Contemporary role

Under Xi Jinping, the united front has been described as one of the party's "magic weapons" — alongside party building and the People's Liberation Army. Critics in several countries have raised concerns that united front organizations can serve as channels for foreign interference, while Beijing maintains that the work promotes friendship and national unity.
