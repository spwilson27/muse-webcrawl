Shane (film)

Directed by: George Stevens
Screenplay: A. B. Guthrie Jr., based on the 1949 novel by Jack Schaefer
Starring: Alan Ladd, Jean Arthur, Van Heflin, Brandon deWilde, Jack Palance
Released: 1953 (Paramount Pictures)

Shane is a 1953 American Western film produced and directed by George Stevens, starring Alan Ladd in the title role. Adapted from Jack Schaefer's 1949 novel, it tells the story of a weary gunfighter who comes to the aid of a homesteading family in Wyoming Territory in 1889 and is drawn into a conflict with a ruthless cattle baron.

Plot

Shane, a mysterious former gunslinger hoping to leave violence behind, takes work with homesteader Joe Starrett, his wife Marian, and their young son Joey, who idolizes him. Cattle rancher Rufus Ryker, determined to drive the homesteaders off the open range, hires the cold-blooded gunman Jack Wilson. After Wilson murders another settler, Shane — despite his wish for peace and his unspoken love for Marian — takes up his gun again, kills Wilson and the Ryker brothers in a saloon showdown, and, wounded, rides away into the mountains as Joey calls after him: "Shane! Come back!"

Reception and legacy

Shane was a critical and commercial success, nominated for six Academy Awards and winning for Loyal Griggs's color cinematography. It is routinely ranked among the greatest Westerns ever made, was selected for the U.S. National Film Registry in 1993, and appears on the American Film Institute's lists of the best American films and heroes. Its themes — the passing of the gunfighter's West and the cost of violence — influenced generations of later Westerns.
