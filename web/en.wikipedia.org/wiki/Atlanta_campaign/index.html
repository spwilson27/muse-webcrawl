Atlanta campaign

Date: May 7 – September 2, 1864
Location: Northern Georgia, U.S.
Result: Union victory; capture of Atlanta
Union commander: William Tecumseh Sherman
Confederate commanders: Joseph E. Johnston, then John Bell Hood

Sherman's manoeuvre campaign from Chattanooga to Atlanta, May–September 1864.

The Atlanta campaign was a series of battles fought in the Western Theater of the American Civil War throughout northwest Georgia and the area around Atlanta during the summer of 1864. Union Major General William Tecumseh Sherman, commanding some 110,000 men, advanced from Chattanooga, Tennessee, against the Confederate Army of Tennessee under Joseph E. Johnston, and ultimately John Bell Hood, capturing the vital rail and industrial centre of Atlanta on September 2, 1864.

Course of the campaign

Rather than assault Johnston's strong positions frontally, Sherman repeatedly used flanking manoeuvres to force Confederate withdrawals: from Dalton through Resaca, the Dallas–New Hope Church line, and Kennesaw Mountain — where on June 27 Sherman's one major frontal assault was bloodily repulsed. After crossing the Chattahoochee River, Union forces closed on Atlanta. Richmond, dissatisfied with Johnston's retreats, replaced him with the aggressive Hood on July 17; Hood's attacks at Peachtree Creek, Atlanta (Ezra Church) and Jonesborough cost the Confederates heavily. When Union troops cut the last rail line into the city at Jonesborough on August 31–September 1, Hood evacuated Atlanta.

Significance

The fall of Atlanta was a turning point of the war. Coming alongside Admiral Farragut's victory at Mobile Bay, it revived Northern morale at a moment when war weariness threatened Abraham Lincoln's re-election; Lincoln won comfortably in November 1864. Sherman's occupation of the city set the stage for his March to the Sea in November–December 1864. Total casualties in the campaign were roughly 67,000 combined, and much of Atlanta was destroyed by fire as the Confederates destroyed supplies during their evacuation and Sherman later razed military-industrial facilities.
