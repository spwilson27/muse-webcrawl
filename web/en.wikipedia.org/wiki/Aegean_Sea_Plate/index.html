The Aegean Sea Plate (also called the Aegean or Hellenic plate) is a small tectonic plate located in the eastern Mediterranean beneath the Aegean Sea, southern Greece and the far west of Turkey. It is one of several microplates in the complex collision zone between the African and Eurasian plates.

Boundaries

To the south, the plate is bounded by the Hellenic subduction zone, where the African Plate slides beneath the Aegean along the Hellenic arc south of Crete. To the north and east it meets the Anatolian Plate along the North Anatolian Fault system and the extending grabens of western Turkey. The plate's interior is extending in a north–south direction, producing the sunken basin of the Aegean Sea, its many islands, and active volcanism along the Hellenic volcanic arc (including Santorini and Nisyros).

Motion and seismicity

The Aegean Sea Plate moves south-westward at roughly 3–4 cm per year relative to Eurasia, driven by subduction rollback at the Hellenic trench and the westward extrusion of Anatolia. The resulting crustal extension and faulting make Greece and the Aegean among the most seismically active regions of Europe, with frequent moderate earthquakes and occasional destructive events and tsunamis.
