Lenormand

The name Lenormand refers principally to Marie Anne Adelaide Lenormand (1772–1843), the most celebrated French fortune-teller of the Napoleonic era, and by extension to the popular 36-card cartomancy deck named in her honour, the Petit Lenormand.

BornMarie Anne Adelaide Lenormand, May 27, 1772, Alençon, France
DiedJune 25, 1843 (aged 71), Paris, France
OccupationFortune-teller, cartomancer, author
Known forCartomancy; the Lenormand oracle deck

Life

Born in Alençon, Lenormand moved to Paris before the Revolution and established a fashionable salon in the Rue de Tournon, where she read fortunes using cards, astrology, and numerology. She was consulted — by her own later account and by wide contemporary report — by figures including Joséphine de Beauharnais, Maximilien Robespierre, Jean-Paul Marat, and Louis Antoine de Saint-Just; she was imprisoned more than once, including during the Reign of Terror. After Napoleon's divorce from Joséphine, whom she had advised, she was briefly exiled from Paris. Her published memoirs and prophecies made her a European celebrity, and she died wealthy in 1843.

The Lenormand deck

The 36-card Petit Lenormand deck appeared in Nuremberg around 1845, shortly after her death, derived from the German "Game of Hope" (c. 1799). Each card bears a simple emblem — Rider, Clover, Ship, House, Tree, Clouds, Snake, and so on — combined with a playing-card inset. Read in pairs and spreads such as the Grand Tableau (using all 36 cards), Lenormand remains, alongside tarot, one of the two dominant cartomantic systems in the world today.
