Auditory cortex

Auditory cortex
Part of: Temporal lobe
System: Auditory system
Location: Superior temporal gyrus

The auditory cortex is the part of the temporal lobe of the brain that processes auditory information in humans and other vertebrates. It is a part of the auditory system, performing basic and higher functions in hearing, such as the perception of pitch, loudness and sound location, and in humans, the comprehension of language.

Structure

The primary auditory cortex (Brodmann areas 41 and 42) lies in the superior temporal gyrus, largely within the lateral sulcus, and includes Heschl's gyri. It receives point-to-point input from the medial geniculate nucleus of the thalamus and is organised tonotopically: neurons are arranged in an orderly map of sound frequencies. Surrounding it are the secondary auditory areas (including Brodmann area 22 and, in humans, Wernicke's area in the posterior superior temporal gyrus), which process more complex features such as speech and music.

Function

Cells in the primary auditory cortex respond selectively to specific frequencies or combinations of frequencies. Higher auditory areas integrate this information to identify sound sources and meaning. Damage to the auditory cortex can cause cortical deafness or deficits in speech perception.
