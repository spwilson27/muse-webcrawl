Limewater

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Limewater is the common name for a saturated aqueous solution of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)2. It is prepared by mixing excess slaked lime with pure water and filtering off the undissolved solid. At room temperature the solution holds only about 1.5 grams of calcium hydroxide per litre, so limewater is a moderately alkaline solution (pH about 12.4) despite its dilute appearance.

Test for carbon dioxide

Limewater is best known as the standard laboratory test for carbon dioxide. When CO2 is bubbled through it, the solution turns milky white as a precipitate of insoluble calcium carbonate forms:

Ca(OH)2(aq) + CO2(g) → CaCO3(s) + H2O(l)

If carbon dioxide is passed for a longer time, the precipitate redissolves, forming soluble calcium hydrogencarbonate, so the milkiness disappears.

Uses

Limewater has been used in water treatment (lime softening), in the nixtamalisation of maize in Mexican and Central American cooking — where it improves the nutritional value of tortillas — in the preparation of betel nut, in tanning and sugar refining, and historically as an antacid. Because it absorbs carbon dioxide from the air, stored limewater gradually develops a carbonate film and loses strength, and it is kept in stoppered bottles.
