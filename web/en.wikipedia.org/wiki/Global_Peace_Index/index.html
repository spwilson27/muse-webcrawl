Global Peace Index

The Global Peace Index (GPI) is an annual report produced by the Institute for Economics & Peace (IEP) that measures the relative peacefulness of countries and regions. First launched in 2007, the index ranks 163 independent states and territories — covering about 99.7% of the world's population — according to their levels of negative peace, defined as the absence of violence or fear of violence.

Methodology

The index is built from 23 qualitative and quantitative indicators grouped into three domains: ongoing domestic and international conflict, societal safety and security, and militarisation. Indicators include homicide rates, incarceration, political instability, deaths from internal and external conflict, military expenditure as a share of GDP, weapons exports and the size of armed services. Data come from sources including the Economist Intelligence Unit, the United Nations, and the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI).

Findings

Iceland has ranked as the world's most peaceful country in every edition of the index since 2008, typically followed by countries such as Ireland, Austria, New Zealand and Singapore. Afghanistan was ranked the least peaceful country for several years; more recent editions have placed countries such as Yemen, Sudan, Russia and Ukraine at or near the bottom amid ongoing wars. The reports also estimate the economic cost of violence, which the IEP has placed at more than $17 trillion annually in purchasing-power-parity terms in recent editions.

Criticism

The index has been criticised for its weighting choices, for treating indicators such as military expenditure as inherently unpeaceful, and for measuring "negative peace" rather than the broader "positive peace" the IEP assesses separately in a companion index.
