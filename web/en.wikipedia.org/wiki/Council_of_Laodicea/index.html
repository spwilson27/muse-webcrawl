Council of Laodicea

Date: c. 363–364 AD
Location: Laodicea, Phrygia (modern Turkey)
Accepted by: Eastern Orthodox Church (via the Council in Trullo); canons widely cited in the West

The Council of Laodicea was a regional synod of approximately thirty clerics from Asia Minor that assembled about 363–364 AD at Laodicea ad Lycum in Phrygia Pacatiana. The council issued sixty canons dealing with church order, clerical discipline, liturgical practice and the conduct of the laity.

Canons

The canons regulate matters ranging from the ordination of clergy and restrictions on heretical practices to rules for fasting, prayer and the celebration of Lent. Canon 29 decreed that Christians should not Judaize by resting on the Sabbath, but should work on that day and honour the Lord's Day instead. Canon 59 forbade the reading in church of "private psalms" and uncanonical books.

Canon 60 and the biblical canon

The sixtieth canon, whose authenticity is debated, contains a list of the books of the Bible permitted to be read in church. The list includes most of the books of the later canon but omits the Book of Revelation — reflecting continuing Eastern hesitation about that book — making the council a frequently cited witness in the history of the development of the biblical canon.
