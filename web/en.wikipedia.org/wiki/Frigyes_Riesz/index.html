Frigyes Riesz

Born: 22 January 1880, Győr, Austria-Hungary
Died: 28 February 1956 (aged 76), Budapest, Hungary
Fields: Mathematics
Known for: Functional analysis; Riesz representation theorem; Riesz–Fischer theorem

Frigyes Riesz (Hungarian: Riesz Frigyes, 22 January 1880 – 28 February 1956) was a Hungarian mathematician who made fundamental contributions to functional analysis, working alongside his younger brother Marcel Riesz. He is regarded as one of the founders of modern functional analysis.

Life and career

Riesz studied in Zurich, Göttingen and Budapest, taking his doctorate in Budapest in 1902. After teaching in secondary schools, he became a lecturer and then professor at the University of Kolozsvár (Cluj). After the First World War the university moved to Szeged, where Riesz built a celebrated mathematical school together with Alfréd Haar, founding the journal Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum. In 1945 he moved to Budapest, where he remained until his death.

Work

Riesz's contributions include the Riesz–Fischer theorem (1907), which established that the space of square-integrable functions is complete and isomorphic to a sequence space — a decisive step toward the abstract concept of Hilbert space. His Riesz representation theorem characterises the continuous linear functionals on spaces of continuous functions and on Hilbert spaces. He also introduced the classes of Lp spaces, developed the theory of compact operators (the Riesz–Schauder theory), and contributed to ergodic theory, Fourier series and the theory of Hardy spaces. His 1952 monograph with Béla Sz.-Nagy, Functional Analysis, became a classic textbook.
