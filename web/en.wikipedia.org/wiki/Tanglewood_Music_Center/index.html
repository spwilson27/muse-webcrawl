The Tanglewood Music Center is an annual summer music academy in the Berkshire Hills of western Massachusetts, operated by the Boston Symphony Orchestra (BSO) as part of its Tanglewood festival. Founded in 1940 by BSO music director Serge Koussevitzky as the Berkshire Music Center, it brings together advanced students and young professionals — the TMC Fellows — for eight weeks of intensive study, masterclasses, and performance alongside the orchestra.

Programmes

Each summer roughly 150 fellows are selected by audition in instrumental, vocal, conducting, and composition programmes, attending tuition-free on fellowship. Fellows perform as the Tanglewood Music Center Orchestra, give chamber and vocal concerts, and work with the BSO's music director, guest conductors, and visiting artists. The Festival of Contemporary Music, founded in 1964, is a centrepiece of the composition programme. The related Tanglewood Institute serves younger students.

History and alumni

Koussevitzky opened the school in the summer of 1940, famously teaching conducting there; his students included Leonard Bernstein, who returned throughout his life and conducted his last concert at Tanglewood in August 1990. Other notable teachers and alumni include Aaron Copland, who co-directed the school, and generations of orchestral musicians, conductors, and composers; it is often noted that a substantial share of players in major American orchestras are TMC alumni. Performances are centred on the Koussevitzky Music Shed (opened 1938) and Seiji Ozawa Hall (opened 1994).
