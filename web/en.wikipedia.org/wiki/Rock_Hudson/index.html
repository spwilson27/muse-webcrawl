Rock Hudson

Rock Hudson (born Roy Harold Scherer Jr.; November 17, 1925 – October 2, 1985) was an American actor. One of the most popular movie stars of his time, he had a screen career spanning more than three decades, and was a prominent figure in the Golden Age of Hollywood.

Early life

Hudson was born in Winnetka, Illinois, the only child of Katherine Wood, a telephone operator, and Roy Harold Scherer, an auto mechanic. His father left the family during the Great Depression, and his mother later married Wallace Fitzgerald, whose surname the young Roy took for a time.

Career

After serving in the United States Navy during World War II, Hudson moved to Los Angeles to pursue acting. Talent scout Henry Willson renamed him "Rock Hudson" and secured him a contract with Universal Pictures. His breakthrough came with Magnificent Obsession (1954), and he received an Academy Award nomination for Giant (1956), alongside Elizabeth Taylor and James Dean. He later starred in a series of hugely successful romantic comedies with Doris Day, including Pillow Talk (1959), Lover Come Back (1961), and Send Me No Flowers (1964). In the 1970s he starred in the television series McMillan & Wife.

Personal life and death

Hudson was one of the first major celebrities to disclose an AIDS diagnosis. His announcement in July 1985 that he was suffering from the disease drew worldwide attention to the epidemic. He died on October 2, 1985, in Beverly Hills, California, at the age of 59.
