Evens

The Evens (Russian: Эвены, Eveny; Even: Эвэн, plural Эвэсил) are a Tungusic people of eastern Siberia, closely related to the Evenks. They traditionally lived as nomadic reindeer herders, hunters and fishers across a vast territory stretching from the Lena River to the Pacific coast, including parts of the present-day Sakha Republic (Yakutia), Magadan Oblast, Kamchatka Krai, Chukotka and Khabarovsk Krai in the Russian Far East.

Population and language

According to the 2002 Russian census, about 19,000 Evens lived in Russia, with a similar figure recorded in the 2010 census (around 22,000). The Even language belongs to the northern branch of the Tungusic language family and is closely related to Evenki. It is considered endangered: only a minority of ethnic Evens, mostly of the older generation, still speak it fluently, as Russian has become dominant in most communities.

History

The ancestors of the Evens separated from the broader Evenki population and migrated eastward toward the Sea of Okhotsk coast, settling in their present homelands by roughly the 17th century, when they first encountered Russian explorers and tribute collectors. During the Soviet period, Evens were subjected to collectivisation: their reindeer herds were consolidated into state and collective farms, nomadic families were settled in villages, and children were sent to boarding schools, all of which eroded traditional culture and language use. Shamanism, once central to Even spiritual life, was suppressed, though elements of traditional belief — reverence for nature spirits, fire rituals and bear ceremonialism — persisted.

Traditional culture

Traditional Even society was organised around reindeer husbandry. Unlike the Evenks, who used reindeer primarily for riding and transport, Evens developed large-scale herd breeding influenced by neighbouring peoples. Coastal groups combined herding with sea-mammal hunting and fishing. Dwellings included the conical tent (chorama-dyu) and cylindrical bark or hide tents. Clothing, richly decorated with beadwork and appliqué, is a noted feature of Even material culture, as are bone carving and embroidery.

Present day

Since the collapse of the Soviet Union, reindeer herding has declined with the loss of state subsidies, though it remains the economic and cultural mainstay for many Even communities. Cultural revival movements promote the language, festivals such as the Reindeer Herders' Day, and traditional crafts. The Evens are officially recognised in Russia as one of the Indigenous small-numbered peoples of the North, Siberia and the Far East.
