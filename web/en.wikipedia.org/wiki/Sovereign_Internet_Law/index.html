Sovereign Internet Law

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Sovereign Internet Law, formally amendments to Russian federal laws 'On Communications' and 'On Information, Information Technologies and Information Protection', is a 2019 Russian law intended to allow the Russian segment of the internet (RuNet) to function in isolation from the global internet. It was signed by President Vladimir Putin on 1 May 2019 and most provisions entered into force on 1 November 2019.

Provisions

The law requires internet service providers to install deep packet inspection equipment supplied and overseen by the regulator Roskomnadzor, enabling centralised traffic control; creates a national domain name system; and mandates traffic exchange through approved points, with periodic drills of disconnection from the worldwide network.

Criticism

Free-expression and digital-rights groups criticised the law as a step toward a Chinese-style system of censorship and surveillance, and thousands protested against it in Moscow in 2019. Officials defended it as protection against foreign cyber threats and disconnection.
