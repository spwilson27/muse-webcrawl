Qualla Boundary

The Qualla Boundary or The Qualla is the territory held as a land trust by the United States government for the federally recognized Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians in western North Carolina. It covers about 33,000 hectares (82,000 acres) in Swain, Jackson, Cherokee, Graham and Haywood counties, with the town of Cherokee as its main community.

History

Unlike a reservation created by treaty, the Qualla lands were purchased in the 19th century by and for the Cherokee who remained in North Carolina after the forced removal of the Trail of Tears era, notably through the efforts of William Holland Thomas and Chief Yonaguska. The land was placed in federal trust in 1924, and the Eastern Band adopted a charter in the 20th century.

Today

The Qualla Boundary is not technically a reservation but functions similarly, with tribal government, schools, a hospital and the Oconaluftee Indian Village and Unto These Hills drama as cultural attractions. Harrah's Cherokee Casino Resort, opened in 1997, is a major employer.
