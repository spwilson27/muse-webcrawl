La Sonora Matancera is a Cuban band founded in 1924 in the city of Matanzas, from which it takes its name (sonora refers to the sound of its trumpet-led ensemble format). It became one of the most famous and longest-lived orchestras in Latin American music, popularising Cuban rhythms such as son, bolero, guaracha, mambo, and cha-cha-chá across the Americas.

History

The group was founded on January 12, 1924, by guitarist Valentín Cané as the Tuna Liberal, later renamed Sexteto Soprano and finally Sonora Matancera. In 1932 the group settled in Havana, and from 1944 guitarist Rogelio Martínez directed the band for more than half a century, guiding it through its golden age of radio broadcasts, recordings, and film appearances.

Golden age

The Sonora's distinctive sound — two or more trumpets in harmony, piano, bass, and Afro-Cuban percussion supporting a featured vocalist — backed many of Latin music's greatest singers. Celia Cruz joined in 1950 and remained the band's signature voice for 15 years; other celebrated vocalists included Bienvenido Granda ("El Bigote de Oro"), Daniel Santos, Celio González, and Nelson Pinedo. Classic recordings include "Yerbero moderno", "Cao Cao maní picao", and "Burundanga".

Exile and later years

After the Cuban Revolution, the band left Cuba in 1960 and settled in the United States, performing from New York and continuing to record and tour for decades. The Sonora Matancera celebrated its 65th anniversary with a celebrated concert in Central Park in 1989. Its alumni and recordings remain central to the history of salsa and Afro-Cuban popular music.
