Second Battle of El Alamein

The Second Battle of El Alamein (23 October – 11 November 1942) was a battle of the Second World War that took place near the Egyptian railway halt of El Alamein. The First Battle of El Alamein and the Battle of Alam el Halfa had prevented the Axis from advancing further into Egypt. In August 1942, General Claude Auchinleck was relieved as Commander-in-Chief Middle East and replaced by Lieutenant-General Harold Alexander, while Lieutenant-General Bernard Montgomery took command of the Eighth Army.

Background

By the autumn of 1942 the Axis forces under Field Marshal Erwin Rommel had been halted at El Alamein, only 106 km (66 mi) west of Alexandria. The Eighth Army had been rebuilt with fresh divisions, American Sherman tanks and abundant supplies landed through the Suez Canal, while Rommel's Panzerarmee Afrika suffered chronic fuel shortages because Allied air and naval forces interdicted the supply routes across the Mediterranean.

The battle

Operation Lightfoot

The battle opened on the night of 23 October 1942 with a massive artillery barrage from nearly 900 guns, followed by infantry assaults in the north (XXX Corps) and south (XIII Corps). Engineers cleared lanes through the Axis minefields — the "Devil's gardens" — so that the armour of X Corps could pass through. Progress was slower than planned and the fighting became a battle of attrition, which Montgomery, with his superiority in men and matériel, was content to fight.

Operation Supercharge

On the night of 1–2 November, Montgomery launched Operation Supercharge, a concentrated attack near the coast by the 2nd New Zealand Division, supported by British infantry and armour and a heavy artillery barrage. The Axis line was broken; on 4 November Rommel, acting against Hitler's order to stand fast, began a general retreat westward.

Aftermath

The victory at El Alamein was the turning point of the North African campaign. Winston Churchill wrote: "Before Alamein we never had a victory. After Alamein we never had a defeat." The Eighth Army pursued the Axis across Libya, capturing Tripoli in January 1943, and the Axis forces in North Africa finally surrendered in Tunisia in May 1943. The battle, fought alongside the Soviet victory at Stalingrad and the American landing in Operation Torch, marked a decisive shift in the war.
