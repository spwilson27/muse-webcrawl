Cambridge is a city in Middlesex County, Massachusetts, United States, lying immediately across the Charles River from Boston. As of the 2020 census it had a population of 118,403. Named in honour of the University of Cambridge in England, the city is home to Harvard University and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), two of the world's most prestigious universities.

History

Cambridge was founded in 1630 as "Newe Towne" by Puritan settlers from the Massachusetts Bay Colony and was renamed in 1638 when Harvard College — the oldest institution of higher learning in the United States — was established there. In 1775 George Washington took command of the Continental Army on Cambridge Common. Industrialisation in the 19th century brought factories and large immigrant communities, while the arrival of MIT from Boston in 1916 cemented the city's academic character.

Geography and neighbourhoods

The city covers about 7 square miles and is organised around squares — Harvard Square, Central Square, Kendall Square, Porter Square, and Inman Square among them — each a local commercial and cultural centre. Kendall Square, adjacent to MIT, has been called "the most innovative square mile on the planet" for its concentration of biotechnology and technology firms.

Economy and culture

Education, biotechnology, and software dominate the Cambridge economy; companies founded or based in the area include Biogen, Moderna, Akamai, and HubSpot. The city is known for its liberal politics, large student population, bookstores, and cultural institutions such as the American Repertory Theater and the Harvard Art Museums.
