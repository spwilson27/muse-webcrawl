2 Tone Records was a British record label established in Coventry in 1979 by Jerry Dammers of the ska revival band the Specials. The label became the centre of the 2 Tone movement, which fused Jamaican ska and rocksteady with punk and new wave energy, and presented its multiracial bands with a distinctive black-and-white visual identity.

History

Dammers founded the label to release the Specials' debut single "Gangsters" (1979), a reworking of Prince Buster's "Al Capone". He secured a distribution and financing deal with Chrysalis Records while retaining creative control, an arrangement that let 2 Tone sign further acts. Early releases followed quickly: Madness's "The Prince", the Selecter's "On My Radio" and records by the Beat (known in North America as the English Beat). The label's first double A-side release paired the Specials with Rico Rodriguez, and the Specials' "Too Much Too Young" live EP reached number one in the UK in 1980.

The label's mascot, "Walt Jabsco", was a cartoon rude boy in a black suit, white shirt, black tie and pork pie hat, drawn from a photograph of Peter Tosh. The black-and-white checkerboard motif became emblematic of the movement's message of racial unity during a period of heightened racial tension in Britain.

Decline and legacy

By 1981 the ska revival was ebbing; Madness and the Beat had moved to other labels, and the Specials fractured after the chart-topping "Ghost Town". Dammers continued with the Special AKA, whose "Free Nelson Mandela" (1984) became an anthem of the anti-apartheid movement. The label effectively wound down by 1985. 2 Tone's influence has endured: it helped popularise ska worldwide, seeded later ska-punk scenes, and its catalogue continues to be reissued. In 2019 the label's 40th anniversary was marked with extensive reissues and exhibitions in Coventry.
