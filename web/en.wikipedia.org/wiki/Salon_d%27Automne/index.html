Salon d'Automne

Salon d'Automne
Location: Paris, France
First held: 31 October 1903
Venue (original): Petit Palais
Known for: Launching Fauvism (1905)

The Salon d'Automne (Autumn Salon) is an annual art exhibition held in Paris, France, since 1903. It was founded by a group including the architect Frantz Jourdain, with artists such as Eugène Carrière, Paul Desvalloires and Georges Rouault, as a liberal alternative to the conservative official Salon.

The 1905 scandal

The Salon of 1905 caused a sensation when a room of violently coloured paintings by Henri Matisse, André Derain, Maurice de Vlaminck and others prompted the critic Louis Vauxcelles to dub the artists fauves ("wild beasts"), giving Fauvism its name. The Salon also gave early exposure to Cubism, Orphism and many modern movements.

Today

The Salon d'Automne continues to be held each autumn in Paris, exhibiting painting, sculpture, photography and other media.
