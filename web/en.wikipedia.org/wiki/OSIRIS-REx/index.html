OSIRIS-REx

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

OSIRIS-REx
OperatorNASA / Goddard / Lockheed Martin
Launch8 September 2016
TargetAsteroid 101955 Bennu
Sample return24 September 2023 (~250 g)
Sampling Bennu (artist's concept).

OSIRIS-REx was a NASA mission to the carbon-rich asteroid Bennu, which collected a sample of its surface and returned it to Earth on 24 September 2023 — the largest asteroid sample ever delivered, about 250 grams of black rubble from the early solar system. The spacecraft arrived at Bennu in December 2018 and spent two years mapping a world far more rugged than expected, its surface strewn with house-sized boulders and occasionally spitting particles into space.

In October 2020 the spacecraft descended and "high-fived" the surface with its TAGSAM sampling arm, firing nitrogen gas to stir material into the collector — it gathered so much that the lid was wedged open by a rock and samples leaked, prompting an early stow.

Aftermath

Analysis of the returned sample found abundant carbon, water-bearing clays, and amino acids and nucleobases relevant to the chemistry of life — pristine material unaltered by Earth's environment. The spacecraft bus, healthy after capsule release, was retargeted as OSIRIS-APEX to the asteroid Apophis, which it will meet after Apophis's close Earth flyby in 2029.
