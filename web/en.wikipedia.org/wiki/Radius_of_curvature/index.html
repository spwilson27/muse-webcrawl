Radius of curvature

In differential geometry, the radius of curvature, R, is the reciprocal of the curvature κ. For a curve, it equals the radius of the circular arc which best approximates the curve at that point, the osculating circle. For surfaces, the radius of curvature is the radius of a circle that best fits a normal section.

Definition

If κ is the curvature of a curve at a point, the radius of curvature is ρ = 1/κ. The centre of curvature lies along the normal line at distance ρ from the curve. A straight line has zero curvature and therefore an infinite radius of curvature.

Applications

The concept is used in optics (lens and mirror design), road and railway engineering (minimum curve radii for safety), beam bending, and the physics of charged particles moving in magnetic fields (Larmor radius).
