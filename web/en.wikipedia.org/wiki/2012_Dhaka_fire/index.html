The 2012 Dhaka fire broke out on 24 November 2012 at the Tazreen Fashions garment factory in the Ashulia district on the outskirts of Dhaka, Bangladesh. At least 117 people were killed and around 200 injured, making it the deadliest factory fire in Bangladesh's history at that time.

Fire

The fire began on the ground floor of the nine-storey factory, where fabric and yarn were stored. Workers on upper floors were trapped: there were no emergency exits to the exterior, windows were barred or covered, and survivors reported that supervisors initially dismissed the alarm and that some exits were locked. Many died of burns or smoke inhalation; others were killed jumping from upper floors. More than 1,000 workers were in the building on a Saturday evening shift producing clothing for Western retailers, including Walmart suppliers and other international brands.

Aftermath

Protests by garment workers erupted in Dhaka in the days that followed, and the fire focused international attention on safety in Bangladesh's garment industry, the world's second largest. The factory owner, Delowar Hossain, and others were later charged with culpable homicide. Just five months later, the Rana Plaza factory building collapsed near Dhaka killing 1,134 people, producing the binding Accord on Fire and Building Safety in Bangladesh between brands and trade unions.
