Richard Burton

Richard Burton
Born: Richard Walter Jenkins, 10 November 1925, Pontrhydyfen, Wales
Died: 5 August 1984, Céligny, Switzerland
Occupation: Actor
Spouses included: Elizabeth Taylor (twice)

Richard Burton (born Richard Walter Jenkins Jr.; 10 November 1925 – 5 August 1984) was a Welsh actor, famed for his mellifluous baritone voice and his performances on stage and screen. He was nominated for seven Academy Awards without winning, and was one of the highest-paid and most celebrated actors of the 1960s.

Early life and stage career

Born into a poor Welsh mining family in Pontrhydyfen, Jenkins was taken under the wing of his schoolteacher Philip Burton, whose surname he adopted. After Oxford and wartime service in the RAF, he rose rapidly in the theatre, winning acclaim at the Old Vic as Hamlet and Henry V, and on Broadway in Camelot (1960).

Film career and Elizabeth Taylor

On screen Burton starred in The Robe (1953), Look Back in Anger (1959), Becket (1964), The Spy Who Came In from the Cold (1965) and Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf? (1966). While filming Cleopatra (1963) he began his tempestuous romance with Elizabeth Taylor; they married twice (1964–74 and 1975–76) and made eleven films together, becoming the most famous celebrity couple of their era.

Later years

Burton's later career was uneven, marked by heavy drinking, though he won praise for Equus (1977) and his final film, 1984 (1984). He died of a cerebral haemorrhage at his home in Céligny, Switzerland, aged 58, and is buried there. His diaries, published in 2012, revealed a formidable literary intelligence behind the legend.
