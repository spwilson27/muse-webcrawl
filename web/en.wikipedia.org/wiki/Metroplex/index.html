Metroplex

A metroplex is a large metropolitan area containing several cities and their suburbs, with no single clearly dominant centre. The term is a portmanteau of "metropolitan" and "complex." It is most famously applied to the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex in North Texas, one of the largest metropolitan areas in the United States, and the term itself was popularised in the 1970s by the North Texas Commission's regional marketing.

Dallas–Fort Worth

The Dallas–Fort Worth–Arlington metropolitan statistical area encompasses the two anchor cities of Dallas and Fort Worth plus numerous suburbs such as Arlington, Plano, Irving, and Frisco. With a population exceeding 7.5 million, it is the largest metropolitan area in Texas and the Southern United States, and a major centre for technology, finance, logistics, and aviation (including Dallas Fort Worth International Airport and the headquarters of American Airlines).

Other uses

The term is sometimes applied to other polycentric regions, and "Metroplex" also appears as a proper name for various businesses, venues, and — in popular culture — a Transformers character. The concept overlaps with terms such as conurbation and combined statistical area.
