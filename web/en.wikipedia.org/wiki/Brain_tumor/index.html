Brain tumor

Brain tumor
Specialty: Neuro-oncology, neurosurgery
Types: Primary, secondary (metastatic)
Examples: Glioma, meningioma, medulloblastoma
MRI showing a brain tumor

A brain tumor occurs when abnormal cells form within the brain. There are two main types: primary tumors, which start in the brain, and secondary (metastatic) tumors, which spread to the brain from cancers elsewhere in the body. Primary brain tumors can be benign or malignant; the most common malignant type in adults is glioblastoma, while meningiomas are the most common primary tumors overall and are usually benign.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms depend on the tumor's size and location and on raised intracranial pressure. Common features include headaches that are often worse in the morning, seizures, nausea and vomiting, and focal neurological deficits such as weakness, speech difficulty or visual changes. Tumors in children most often arise in the posterior fossa and may cause unsteadiness and hydrocephalus.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis typically involves neurological examination and imaging, usually magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), with a biopsy or surgical specimen providing the definitive classification. The World Health Organization classifies central nervous system tumors by histology and molecular markers into grades I–IV. Treatment may include surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy, chosen according to type, grade and location; for many benign tumors complete surgical removal is curative.
