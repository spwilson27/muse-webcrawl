Procession

TypeCeremonial or ritual organised march
ContextsReligious festivals, funerals, coronations, civic parades, protests

A procession is an organized body of people walking in a formal or ceremonial manner. Processions occur in many cultures and are often associated with religious festivals, state ceremonies, funerals, and public celebrations. Participants may carry banners, icons, candles, relics, or other symbolic objects, and the route is frequently lined with spectators.

Religious processions

Processions are a feature of many religions. In Roman Catholicism and Eastern Orthodoxy, processions accompany feasts such as Corpus Christi, Palm Sunday, and Holy Week, when images of Christ, the Virgin Mary, or saints are carried through the streets; the famous Semana Santa processions in Spain and Latin America draw large crowds of penitents (nazarenos). Hindu festivals such as Rath Yatra involve pulling the chariots of deities through the streets. Shia Muslims hold processions during Ashura commemorating the martyrdom of Husayn ibn Ali.

Funeral and state processions

Funeral processions, in which mourners follow the coffin or hearse to the place of burial or cremation, are common in many traditions. State and military processions mark coronations, inaugurations, and national commemorations. In academic life, processions of faculty in regalia open graduation ceremonies.

History

Processions are ancient: Egyptian festivals moved divine statues by barge and on foot, Greek religion featured the Panathenaic procession depicted on the Parthenon frieze, and Roman triumphs paraded victorious generals and their captives through Rome. See also Parade, Ritual, and Roman triumph.
