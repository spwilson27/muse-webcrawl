The 7.62×39mm (also called 7.62 Soviet, formerly .30 Russian Short) is a rimless bottlenecked intermediate cartridge of Soviet origin. Designed during World War II, it powered the SKS carbine, the RPD light machine gun, and most famously the AK-47 and AKM assault rifles, becoming one of the most widely produced military cartridges of all time.

History

Impressed by the German 7.92×33mm Kurz intermediate round and the StG 44, Soviet designers N. M. Elizarov and B. V. Semin produced a comparable cartridge in 1943, designated M43. It reached service at the very end of the war and was standardised after 1945 as the Soviet bloc's universal rifle round.

Design

The cartridge has a strongly tapered steel case 38.7 mm long — the taper aids reliable extraction in adverse conditions — firing a 7.9 g (122 gr) bullet at about 715 m/s from an AKM. Its ballistics trade long-range precision for controllability in automatic fire, with an effective range around 300–400 metres.

Weapons

Beyond Kalashnikov-pattern rifles used worldwide, the round chambers the SKS, the vz. 58, the RPK light machine gun, and numerous civilian sporting rifles; it remains in front-line service in many countries even though Russia itself largely superseded it with the 5.45×39mm from 1974.

Variants

Loadings include the standard M43 ball (PS), the M67 improved ball projectile, armour-piercing incendiary (BZ), armour-piercing (BP), tracer (T-45), and subsonic variants, plus extensive commercial production for hunting and sport shooting.
