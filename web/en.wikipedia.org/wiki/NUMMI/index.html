NUMMI


New United Motor Manufacturing, Inc.
Industry: Automotive manufacturing
Founded: 1984
Defunct: 2010
Location: Fremont, California, US
Owners: General Motors and Toyota (joint venture)

New United Motor Manufacturing, Inc. (NUMMI) was an American automobile manufacturing company in Fremont, California, jointly owned by General Motors (GM) and Toyota. Opened in 1984 and closed in 2010, it was the first joint venture between an American and a Japanese automaker and became a landmark case study in lean manufacturing.

Background

GM had closed its Fremont Assembly plant in 1982 amid poor quality, low productivity, and adversarial labour relations. Under the NUMMI venture, Toyota managed the plant and rehired much of the same workforce, training them in the Toyota Production System, which emphasises teamwork, kaizen (continuous improvement), standardized work, and stopping the line to fix problems.

Results

Within a year the plant was producing cars with quality comparable to Toyota's Japanese plants, with dramatically improved productivity and morale. Vehicles produced included the Chevrolet Nova, Geo/Chevrolet Prizm, Toyota Corolla, Toyota Tacoma, and Pontiac Vibe.

Closure and after

Following GM's 2009 bankruptcy and withdrawal, Toyota closed the plant on 1 April 2010. Tesla Motors purchased the site later that year; it became the Tesla Fremont Factory. NUMMI's story was famously chronicled in a 2010 episode of the radio program This American Life.
