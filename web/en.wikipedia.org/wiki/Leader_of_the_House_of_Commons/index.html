Leader of the House of Commons

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Leader of the House of Commons
Appointer: The Prime Minister
Formation: 1721 (informal)
Member of: Cabinet of the United Kingdom

The Leader of the House of Commons is a minister of the Crown of the Government of the United Kingdom whose main role is organising government business in the House of Commons. The Leader is a member or attendee of the Cabinet and is usually also Lord President of the Council.

Responsibilities

The Leader of the House is responsible for arranging the government's legislative programme, scheduling debates, and announcing the business of the House each week, traditionally at Business Questions on Thursdays. The office works closely with the government's chief whip to ensure the passage of legislation, and chairs the Cabinet committee on parliamentary business and legislation.

The Leader also has responsibilities relating to the standards and procedures of the House, and represents the government's view in debates on parliamentary reform. The Office of the Leader of the House of Commons, within the Cabinet Office, supports the role.

History

The role developed informally in the 18th century, when the prime minister or a senior minister managed government business in the Commons. For much of the 19th and early 20th centuries the office was frequently held by the prime minister personally. Since the mid-20th century it has usually been a separate appointment, often combined with the posts of Lord President of the Council or Lord Privy Seal.
