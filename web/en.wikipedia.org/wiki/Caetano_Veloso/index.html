Caetano Veloso

Caetano Emanuel Viana Teles Veloso (born 7 August 1942) is a Brazilian composer, singer, guitarist, writer, and political activist. He was a leading figure in the Tropicália movement of the late 1960s and remains one of Brazil's most celebrated and influential musicians.

Career

Born in Santo Amaro, Bahia, Veloso moved to Rio de Janeiro in the 1960s and rose to prominence through televised song festivals. With Gilberto Gil, Gal Costa, Tom Zé, and the band Os Mutantes, he launched Tropicália — a provocative fusion of Brazilian traditions with rock, psychedelia, and avant-garde art — on the 1968 album Tropicália: ou Panis et Circencis. His performances and outspokenness angered Brazil's military dictatorship; in 1969 he and Gil were imprisoned and then exiled to London, returning in 1972.

Later work

Veloso's extensive discography ranges from the intimate Transa (1972) to the international success of Circuladô (1991) and Livro (1997). He won multiple Latin Grammy Awards, including Album of the Year honours, and his memoir Tropical Truth introduced his ideas to English-language readers. In 2012 he was named Latin Recording Academy Person of the Year.
