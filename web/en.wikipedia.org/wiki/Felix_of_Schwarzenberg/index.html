Felix of Schwarzenberg

Prince Felix of Schwarzenberg (German: Felix Ludwig Johann Friedrich Prinz zu Schwarzenberg; 2 October 1800 – 5 April 1852) was an Austrian nobleman, diplomat and statesman who, as minister-president of the Austrian Empire from 1848 to 1852, suppressed the revolutions of 1848 and restored imperial authority, laying the foundations of the neo-absolutist system that governed Austria through the 1850s.

Career

Born at Český Krumlov in Bohemia into the great Schwarzenberg family, Felix served as a cavalry officer and then as a diplomat in St Petersburg, London, Paris, Berlin and Naples. During the revolutionary crisis of 1848 he joined Field Marshal Radetzky's army in Italy before being recalled to Vienna. Appointed minister-president and foreign minister in November 1848, he engineered the abdication of Emperor Ferdinand I in favour of the young Franz Joseph I, and worked with the army commanders Windisch-Grätz and Radetzky to crush the revolutions in Vienna, Hungary and Italy — the Hungarian war being finished with Russian military assistance in 1849.

Policy and legacy

Schwarzenberg imposed the Stadion constitution of March 1849 and, after it was revoked, directed a centralising, authoritarian reconstruction of the monarchy. In foreign policy he sought to restore Austrian primacy in Germany, humiliating Prussia in the Punctation of Olmütz (1850), and entertained the "Greater Austria" project of bringing all Habsburg lands into the German Confederation. His sudden death from a stroke in 1852 left the neo-absolutist system to be administered by his successors, notably Alexander von Bach.
