Zoo

A zoo (short for zoological garden; also called an animal park or menagerie) is a facility in which animals are housed within enclosures, cared for, displayed to the public, and in some cases bred for conservation purposes. The term "zoological garden" refers to zoology, the study of animals. The term is derived from the Greek ζῶον (zoon, "animal") and λογία (-logia, "study").

History

Collections of wild animals were kept by rulers in ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, China and Rome, largely as symbols of power and for entertainment such as venationes (beast hunts). The modern zoo developed in the late 18th and 19th centuries with a growing emphasis on scientific study: the Vienna Zoo (Tiergarten Schönbrunn, founded 1752) is the world's oldest continuously operating zoo, while London Zoo (1828), founded by the Zoological Society of London, opened initially for scientific research before admitting the public in 1847. The Hagenbeck revolution of the early 20th century, pioneered by Carl Hagenbeck in Hamburg, replaced cages with moated, landscape-style enclosures.

Modern zoos

Contemporary accredited zoos emphasise animal welfare, environmental enrichment, education and participation in coordinated breeding programmes for endangered species, such as the European Endangered Species Programme (EEP) and the Species Survival Plan (SSP) in North America, coordinated by bodies including the World Association of Zoos and Aquariums (WAZA). Many zoos fund in-situ conservation projects and conduct research in fields such as nutrition, reproduction and veterinary medicine. At the same time, zoos remain subjects of ethical debate, with critics questioning the keeping of wide-ranging species such as elephants and cetaceans in captivity, and "zoochosis" — stereotypic behaviours associated with confinement.

Types

  • Safari parks — drive-through enclosures over large areas.
  • Aquariums — facilities for aquatic animals.
  • Petting zoos — docile domestic animals for close contact, especially for children.
  • Animal theme parks — combinations of zoos with amusement attractions.
  • Roadside zoos — small, often unaccredited menageries, frequently criticised on welfare grounds.
