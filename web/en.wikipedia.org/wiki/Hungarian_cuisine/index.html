Hungarian cuisine

Hungarian cuisine is the cuisine characteristic of Hungary and its primary ethnic group, the Magyars. Traditional Hungarian dishes are primarily based on meats, seasonal vegetables, fruits, fresh bread, dairy products, and cheeses. The cuisine is famous for its generous use of paprika — both sweet and hot — which arrived from the Americas via the Ottoman Turks and by the 19th century had become the defining spice of Hungarian cooking, replacing black pepper in many dishes.

Gulyás (goulash), the best-known Hungarian dish.

Characteristics

Most dishes are cooked with chopped onion, paprika, and lard or oil as a base (pörkölt base). Sour cream (tejföl) is a frequent finishing ingredient. Meats most commonly used are pork, beef, veal, chicken, and goose; freshwater fish such as carp and zander come from Lake Balaton and the Danube and Tisza rivers.

Notable dishes

  • Gulyás — a soup or stew of meat and vegetables seasoned with paprika; originally the herdsmen's dish of the Great Plain.
  • Pörkölt — meat stewed in a paprika-onion sauce; what is often called "goulash" abroad is usually pörkölt.
  • Halászlé — fisherman's soup of river fish and hot paprika.
  • Chicken paprikash (csirkepaprikás) — chicken in a creamy paprika sauce, served with nokedli dumplings.
  • Töltött káposzta — stuffed cabbage; lángos — deep-fried flatbread; lecsó — pepper and tomato stew.
  • Desserts: Dobos torte, chimney cake (kürtőskalács), rétes (strudel), and somlói galuska.

Drinks

Hungary is noted for wines — Tokaji aszú, Egri Bikavér ("Bull's Blood"), and whites from Lake Balaton — as well as pálinka, a fruit brandy, and the herbal liqueur Unicum.
