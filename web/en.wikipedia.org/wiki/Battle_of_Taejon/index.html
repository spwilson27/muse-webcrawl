The Battle of Taejon (14–21 July 1950) was an early battle of the Korean War, between United States and North Korean forces. The U.S. 24th Infantry Division fought a delaying action around the city of Taejon (modern Daejeon) against the Korean People's Army's 3rd and 4th Divisions. Although the city fell, the week-long stand bought time for United Nations forces to establish the Pusan Perimeter.

U.S. troops fighting in Taejon, July 1950.

Background

After defeats at Osan, Pyongtaek, and Chonan, the 24th Infantry Division under Major General William F. Dean fell back to the Kum River line north of Taejon, a major road and rail hub. Dean was ordered to hold the city long enough for the 1st Cavalry and 25th Infantry Divisions to land and deploy.

The battle

North Korean forces crossed the Kum River from 14 July, outflanked the American positions, and by 19–20 July had infiltrated Taejon itself with tanks and infantry, cutting the roads out of the city. Street fighting raged as the Americans tried to withdraw south. General Dean, fighting with bazooka teams against T-34 tanks, was separated from his men, wounded, and captured; he remained a prisoner until 1953. The division suffered roughly a third of its strength as casualties, but its stand delayed the North Korean advance by about a week.

Aftermath

The delay at Taejon allowed UN forces to build up within the Pusan Perimeter, which held through the summer until the Inchon landing in September. Dean received the Medal of Honor for his conduct in the battle.
