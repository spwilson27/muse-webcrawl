The 1994–95 FA Premier League (known as the FA Carling Premiership for sponsorship reasons) was the third season of the Premier League, the top division of English football. It was won on the final day of the season by Blackburn Rovers, managed by Kenny Dalglish and bankrolled by steel magnate Jack Walker — the club's first league title in 81 years. Blackburn remains one of only seven clubs to have won the Premier League.

Season summary

Blackburn's strike partnership of Alan Shearer and Chris Sutton — the "SAS" — scored 49 league goals between them, with Shearer winning the Golden Boot with 34. The title race went to the last day, 14 May 1995: Blackburn lost 2–1 at Liverpool, but defending champions Manchester United could only draw 1–1 at West Ham United, handing Blackburn the championship by a single point.

The season is also remembered for Manchester United forward Eric Cantona's "kung-fu kick" on a Crystal Palace supporter at Selhurst Park on 25 January 1995, for which he received an eight-month ban. Four clubs were relegated instead of the usual three, as the league was reduced from 22 to 20 teams for the following season; Ipswich Town's 9–0 defeat at Manchester United in March 1995 stood as the joint-largest margin of victory in Premier League history for decades.
