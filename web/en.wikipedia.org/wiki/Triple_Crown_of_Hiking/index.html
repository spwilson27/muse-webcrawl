Triple Crown of Hiking

The Triple Crown of Hiking is an informal honour in the American long-distance hiking community, awarded to hikers who have completed the three great long trails of the United States: the Appalachian Trail (AT), the Pacific Crest Trail (PCT), and the Continental Divide Trail (CDT). Together the three trails total roughly 7,875 miles (about 12,675 km) through 22 states. Hikers who finish all three are called "Triple Crowners".

The three trails

  • Appalachian Trail — approximately 2,200 miles (3,540 km) from Springer Mountain, Georgia, to Mount Katahdin, Maine, the oldest and most heavily used of the three.
  • Pacific Crest Trail — approximately 2,650 miles (4,265 km) from the Mexican border near Campo, California, to the Canadian border in Washington, passing through the Sierra Nevada and Cascade Range.
  • Continental Divide Trail — approximately 3,100 miles (5,000 km) from Mexico to Canada along the Rocky Mountains; the longest, most remote, and least completely marked of the three.

Recognition

The Triple Crown Award is presented by ALDHA-West at its annual gathering. The award operates on the honour system: hikers report their completions rather than proving them. The first person recognised as a Triple Crowner was Eric Ryback, who hiked all three trails by the early 1970s. As of the 2020s, several hundred hikers have been recognised — a small fraction of the thousands who have completed any single trail, though numbers have grown rapidly with the popularity of thru-hiking. Completing all three within a single calendar year — the "Calendar-Year Triple Crown" — is an exceptionally rare feat first achieved in 2001 and repeated only a handful of times.
