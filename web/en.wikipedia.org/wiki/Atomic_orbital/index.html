Atomic orbital

Atomic orbital
Concept: Quantum mechanics of atoms
Quantum numbers: n, ℓ, mℓ, ms
Shapes: s (spherical), p, d, f

An atomic orbital is a function describing the location and wave-like behaviour of an electron in an atom. In quantum mechanics, this function — a solution of the Schrödinger equation for the atom — gives the probability of finding the electron in any particular region around the nucleus. The term may also refer to the physical region or space where the electron can be calculated to be present, as defined by the mathematical form of the orbital.

Quantum numbers

Each orbital is specified by three quantum numbers: the principal quantum number n, which sets the energy level and size; the azimuthal quantum number ℓ, which determines the shape (s, p, d, f for ℓ = 0, 1, 2, 3); and the magnetic quantum number mℓ, which gives the orientation. With electron spin (ms), the Pauli exclusion principle limits each orbital to two electrons of opposite spin.

Shapes and filling

s orbitals are spherical; p orbitals are dumbbell-shaped and come in sets of three; d and f orbitals have more complex forms. Electrons fill orbitals according to the Aufbau principle, Hund's rules, and the Pauli principle, producing the electron configurations that explain the structure of the periodic table and the chemical behaviour of the elements.

Orbitals and bonding

In molecules, atomic orbitals combine into molecular orbitals; simplified models use hybridisation (such as sp³ in methane) to rationalise molecular geometry. Orbital theory thus underpins modern quantum chemistry.
