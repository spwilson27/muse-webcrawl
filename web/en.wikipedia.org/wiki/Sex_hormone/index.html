Sex hormone

Sex hormones, also known as sex steroids, are steroid hormones that interact with vertebrate androgen or estrogen receptors. They regulate sexual development, the reproductive cycle and secondary sexual characteristics. The principal classes are androgens (such as testosterone), estrogens (such as estradiol) and progestogens (such as progesterone).

Production

Sex steroids are synthesised mainly in the gonads — the testes in males and the ovaries in females — with smaller amounts produced by the adrenal glands and, during pregnancy, the placenta. All are derived from cholesterol through the precursor pregnenolone. Their secretion is controlled by the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis: gonadotropin-releasing hormone stimulates release of luteinising hormone and follicle-stimulating hormone, which in turn drive steroid production.

Functions

Androgens drive the development of the male reproductive system and male secondary characteristics such as facial hair and a deepened voice. Estrogens regulate the female reproductive system, the menstrual cycle and female secondary characteristics. Progesterone prepares the uterus for pregnancy and maintains gestation. All three classes are present in both sexes in differing amounts and also affect bone density, muscle mass, mood and cardiovascular health.

Synthetic analogues

Synthetic sex steroids underpin hormonal contraception, hormone replacement therapy, anabolic steroid drugs and gender-affirming hormone therapy. Antiandrogens and aromatase inhibitors are used in treating prostate cancer, breast cancer and endocrine disorders.
