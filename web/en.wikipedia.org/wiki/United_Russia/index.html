United Russia

United Russia (Russian: Единая Россия, romanized: Yedinaya Rossiya) is the ruling political party of Russia. Founded on 1 December 2001 through the merger of the Unity party and the Fatherland – All Russia alliance, it is the largest party in the country and has held a majority — often a constitutional supermajority — in the State Duma since 2007. United Russia supports the policies of President Vladimir Putin, who led the party from 2008 to 2012 and remains closely associated with it, although Putin has formally been an independent since 2012.

History

Unity was created in 1999 to support Putin's presidential bid and performed strongly in that year's Duma elections. The 2001 merger with Fatherland – All Russia unified the pro-Kremlin factions. United Russia won a plurality in 2003 and a constitutional majority in 2007, when Putin headed its federal party list. Dmitry Medvedev became party chairman in 2012. Its share of the vote fell in the disputed 2011 election, which sparked mass protests; the party nevertheless retained a majority and again won a supermajority in 2016 and 2021.

Ideology and criticism

The party describes its ideology as "Russian conservatism" — a synthesis of statism, economic pragmatism, social stability and Russian national interests — and it styles itself a centrist "party of power". Critics, including the late opposition leader Boris Nemtsov and Alexei Navalny — who popularised the epithet "the party of crooks and thieves" — accuse it of corruption, electoral manipulation and serving as an administrative vehicle for the Kremlin rather than an independent political organisation.
