Kyansittha (Burmese: ကျန်စစ်သား, pronounced [tɕàɰ̃sɪʔθá]; c. 1030 – 1112/13) was a king of the Pagan dynasty of Burma (Myanmar) from 1084 to 1112/13. He is considered one of the greatest Burmese monarchs, alongside his father Anawrahta and his grandson Alaungsithu, and his 28-year reign consolidated the Pagan Empire founded by Anawrahta.

Early life and accession

Kyansittha was a son of King Anawrahta, the founder of the Pagan Empire, and served as a general in his father's campaigns, including the conquest of Thaton in 1057. Exiled for a love affair with Manisanda, a Mon princess, he returned to Pagan after Anawrahta's death in 1077. When King Saw Lu was killed in a rebellion in 1084, Kyansittha suppressed the revolt and took the throne.

Reign

Kyansittha strengthened Pagan's administration, promoted agriculture and trade, and patronised Mon culture; many inscriptions of his reign are in the Mon language. His reign is famous for its temple architecture, above all the Ananda Temple (completed c. 1105), one of the largest and best-preserved temples at Bagan, and the Shwegugyi Temple. The Myazedi inscription, erected by his son Yazakumar in 1113, is a key source for Old Burmese, Mon, Pali, and Pyu.

Legacy

Kyansittha remains a folk hero in Myanmar, celebrated in chronicles and popular culture as a spear-throwing warrior king — his very name is said to mean "the soldier who remained."
