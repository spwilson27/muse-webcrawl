Alan Eglin Heathcote Emery (4 March 1928 – 2023) was a British medical geneticist, widely regarded as one of the founding figures of modern medical genetics in the United Kingdom. He is best known for his work on the muscular dystrophies, and the condition Emery–Dreifuss muscular dystrophy is named in part after him.

Early life and education

Emery was born in Oldham, Lancashire. He studied medicine at the University of Manchester, where he also developed an interest in genetics. He later undertook research in the United States before returning to Britain to pursue a career combining clinical medicine and genetic research.

Career

Emery held posts at the University of Manchester before moving to the University of Edinburgh, where he became Professor of Human Genetics and helped to establish medical genetics as a distinct clinical and academic discipline. In the 1960s, together with the American neurologist Fritz Dreifuss, he described the form of muscular dystrophy that now bears their names, characterised by early joint contractures, slowly progressive muscle weakness and cardiac conduction defects.

He founded the journal Clinical Genetics and wrote extensively on the history and practice of medical genetics, including textbooks that became standard references. Emery was also an accomplished musician and wrote on the relationship between genetics, disease and creativity, including studies of composers and their illnesses.

Legacy

Emery's work laid foundations for genetic counselling services in Britain and influenced generations of clinical geneticists. The European Neuromuscular Centre and other bodies have recognised his contributions to the study of neuromuscular disease.
