Rhododendron

Family: Ericaceae
Genus: Rhododendron L.
Species: over 1,000

Rhododendron is a very large genus of about 1,024 species of woody plants in the heath family (Ericaceae). They can be either evergreen or deciduous. Most species are native to eastern Asia and the Himalayan region, with additional centres of diversity in North America, Europe, and tropical Southeast Asia and northern Australia.

Description

Rhododendrons range from ground-hugging alpine shrubs to small trees. Their leaves are often leathery and, in many species, clustered at the ends of branches. The flowers, borne in showy trusses, are usually tubular or bell-shaped and range across almost the entire colour spectrum. The genus also includes the plants commonly called azaleas, treated as subgenera.

Cultivation and symbolism

Rhododendrons are popular ornamental garden plants, preferring acidic, well-drained soils in partial shade. The rhododendron is the national flower of Nepal and the state flower of Washington and West Virginia in the United States. Many species contain grayanotoxins in their leaves and nectar and are poisonous to grazing animals; honey made from them ("mad honey") is toxic.
