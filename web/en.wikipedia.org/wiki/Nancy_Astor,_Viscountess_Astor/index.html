Nancy Astor, Viscountess Astor

Nancy Witcher Astor, Viscountess Astor (1879–1964), born Nancy Langhorne in Virginia, was an American-born British politician and the first woman to sit as a Member of Parliament in the House of Commons.

Political career

Astor was elected for Plymouth Sutton in a 1919 by-election, succeeding her husband Waldorf Astor on his elevation to the House of Lords; Constance Markievicz had been elected earlier but did not take her seat. She served until 1945, campaigning on temperance, women's rights and education.

Legacy

Famed for her wit and sharp exchanges in the Commons, Astor was a pioneering — and sometimes controversial — figure; her home at Cliveden was associated with the "Cliveden set" of the 1930s.
