Augustus Pablo (born Horace Michael Swaby; June 21, 1953 – May 18, 1999) was a Jamaican roots reggae and dub record producer and musician. He popularised the use of the melodica — previously a children's toy — in reggae music, and his spacious, minor-key sound became one of the foundations of dub and modern electronic music production.

Career

Swaby learned piano at the Alpha Boys School in Kingston and acquired a melodica from a visiting girl; he was playing it in session bands while still in his teens. His recording debut, "Iggy Iggy" (1971), was followed by hits including "Java" (1972), a breakthrough that showcased the melodica's eerie, "Far East" sound — the nickname for his signature style, built on minor scales and reverb-drenched melodies.

Frustrated with studio politics, Pablo founded his own Rockers label and Rockers International organization, recording at Channel One and King Tubby's studios and working with engineer King Jammy. His 1976 collaboration with King Tubby, King Tubbys Meets Rockers Uptown, is routinely ranked among the greatest dub albums ever made. Other key works include This Is Augustus Pablo (1974), Ital Dub (1974/75), and Original Rockers (1979).

Legacy

A devout Rastafarian, Pablo produced and mentored singers such as Hugh Mundell and Jacob Miller. He suffered from myasthenia gravis and died in 1999 of a collapsed lung. His influence runs from dub pioneers like Scientist and Mad Professor to dubstep and ambient producers who adopted his sense of space and echo.
