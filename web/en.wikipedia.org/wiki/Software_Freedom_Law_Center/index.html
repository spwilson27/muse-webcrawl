Software Freedom Law Center


Abbreviation: SFLC
Founded: 2005
Founder/Chairman: Eben Moglen
Location: New York City
Type: Nonprofit law firm

The Software Freedom Law Center (SFLC) is an organization that provides pro bono legal representation and related services to not-for-profit developers of free software and open source software. It was launched in February 2005 with initial funding of US$4.25 million pledged by the Open Source Development Labs.

Activities

The SFLC, chaired by Columbia law professor Eben Moglen, advises free software projects on licensing, copyright, trademark, and organisational matters. It played a central role in drafting the GNU General Public License version 3 (GPLv3) in 2006–2007 on behalf of the Free Software Foundation, and through its associated Software Freedom Conservancy and enforcement work it has pursued GPL compliance actions, including the BusyBox litigation against companies that distributed GPL-licensed software without source code.

Related organisations

SFLC staff helped establish the Software Freedom Conservancy (2006) as a fiscal sponsor for free software projects, and SFLC.in, an independent sister organisation in India founded in 2010, works on digital rights and software freedom there.
