The Trawniki training camp was an SS facility established in 1941 at the village of Trawniki, southeast of Lublin in German-occupied Poland. It is principally known as the place where the SS trained auxiliary guards recruited largely from Soviet prisoners of war — the men known to history as the "Trawniki men" (Trawnikimänner) — who served as guards and execution auxiliaries in the extermination camps of Operation Reinhard.

History

The camp was set up on the grounds of a former sugar factory. From the autumn of 1941, the SS brought in thousands of Soviet POWs, particularly ethnic Germans, Ukrainians, Russians, Lithuanians and Latvians, who volunteered or were coerced out of starvation conditions in POW camps. Under commandant Karl Streibel they received basic military and guard training and were issued uniforms and identity cards. In total some 5,000 men passed through Trawniki.

Role in the Holocaust

Trawniki-trained guards were deployed to the Bełżec, Sobibór and Treblinka extermination camps, to the Majdanek and Auschwitz camps, and to units that liquidated ghettos and carried out mass shootings, including the "Harvest Festival" massacre (Aktion Erntefest) of November 1943, in which around 42,000 Jews were murdered in the Lublin district in two days — the Trawniki labour camp's own Jewish prisoners among them. After the war, a number of Trawniki men were prosecuted in the Soviet Union and the West; the most famous case was that of John Demjanjuk. A revolt by Jewish prisoners and the dismantling of the camp preceded the arrival of the Red Army in 1944.
