Dioptrics

Dioptrics is the branch of optics concerned with refraction — the bending of light as it passes through transparent media, especially lenses. It is traditionally contrasted with catoptrics, the study of reflection from mirrors. The dioptre, the unit of optical power of a lens, takes its name from the same Greek root.

History

The study of refraction dates to antiquity; Ptolemy measured angles of refraction for several media. In the early 17th century, Johannes Kepler's Dioptrice (1611) gave the first systematic account of lenses, total internal reflection and the theory of the telescope, while Willebrord Snellius discovered the law of refraction now known as Snell's law. René Descartes published La Dioptrique in 1637 as one of the essays accompanying his Discourse on Method, presenting a derivation of the refraction law and designs for lenses.

Applications

Dioptric theory underlies the design of eyeglasses, microscopes, telescopes, cameras and other instruments whose function depends on refracting optical elements. The power of a lens in dioptres equals the reciprocal of its focal length in metres.
