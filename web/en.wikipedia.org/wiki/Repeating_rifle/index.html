Repeating rifle

A repeating rifle is a single-barrel rifle capable of repeated discharges between ammunition reloads. It stores cartridges in a magazine and uses a manually operated action — lever, bolt, pump, or rotating — to chamber each round, giving a much higher rate of fire than earlier single-shot and muzzle-loading arms.

History

Early repeaters included the Kalthoff repeater of the 17th century and the Cookson rifle. Practical repeating rifles arrived in the mid-19th century: the volcanic and Henry rifles, the Spencer rifle used in the American Civil War, and the Winchester lever-actions (Models 1866, 1873, and 1894) that became icons of the American West.

The introduction of smokeless powder and the box magazine made the bolt-action repeater the standard military rifle: the Mauser Gewehr 98, Lee–Enfield, and Mosin–Nagant served through both World Wars before semi-automatic and automatic rifles displaced them.

Mechanisms

Repeating actions include lever-action, bolt-action, pump-action, and straight-pull designs, fed from tubular, internal box, or detachable magazines.
