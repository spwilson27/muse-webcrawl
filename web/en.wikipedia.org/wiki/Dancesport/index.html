Dancesport

Dancesport is competitive ballroom dancing, contrasted with social or exhibition dancing. It is governed internationally by the World DanceSport Federation (WDSF) and by the World Dance Council (WDC), whose rivalry has shaped the sport since the 1990s.

Styles

Competition is divided into two main styles. International Standard comprises the Waltz, Tango, Viennese Waltz, Slow Foxtrot and Quickstep; International Latin comprises the Cha-cha-cha, Samba, Rumba, Paso Doble and Jive. The American Smooth and American Rhythm styles, danced mainly in the United States, permit partners to break hold. Formation dancing (synchronised teams) and wheelchair dancesport are also contested.

Competitions

Couples are judged on technique, posture, musicality, timing, choreography and presentation. Competitions are graded by age and proficiency, from Juvenile and Junior through Youth, Adult, Senior and Professional categories. Major events include the Blackpool Dance Festival in England, widely considered the sport's most prestigious competition.

Olympic recognition

The WDSF is recognised by the International Olympic Committee, and dancesport appeared at the World Games. A related dance form, breaking (breakdancing), made its Olympic debut at Paris 2024 under dancesport's broader umbrella of advocacy. Dancesport itself has been included in the Asian Games since 2010.
