Battle of Sepeia

The Battle of Sepeia (c. 494 BC) was fought between Sparta, led by King Cleomenes I, and Argos. It was a decisive Spartan victory in their long rivalry for dominance of the Peloponnese and ranks among the most notorious battles of archaic Greek history because of the massacre and deception associated with it.

Background

Cleomenes had already humiliated Argos by campaigning in the Argolid and, according to Herodotus, by acting on an oracle he misunderstood. Around 494 BC he invaded again with the Spartan army and encamped opposite the Argives at Sepeia, near Tiryns.

The battle and massacre

Herodotus relates that the Argives, having learned the Spartan herald's signals, took their meals and rest at the same times as the Spartans. Cleomenes exploited this by ordering his herald to give the signal for breakfast while the army actually marched to attack, catching the Argives unprepared. The Argive army was routed and the survivors fled into a sacred grove of the hero Argus. Cleomenes first picked off men by luring them out with false promises of ransom, then, learning what he had done, had the grove set on fire. Herodotus gives the Argive dead as 6,000 — a catastrophic loss for a single city.

Aftermath

The loss of so many citizens left Argos so short of men that, according to Herodotus, slaves took over the running of the city until the sons of the fallen came of age. Cleomenes was prosecuted at Sparta for failing to capture Argos itself — allegedly because he had been bribed — but was acquitted after pleading that an omen in the temple of Hera had warned him off. The disaster entered Argive memory as a defining trauma, and an oracle quoted by Herodotus was said to have foretold it.
