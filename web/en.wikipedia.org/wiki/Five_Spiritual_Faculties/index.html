Five Spiritual Faculties

The Five Spiritual Faculties (Pali: pañca indriyāni) are a set of qualities in Buddhist teaching that, when developed and balanced, lead toward enlightenment. They are:

  1. Faith or conviction (saddhā)
  2. Energy or effort (viriya)
  3. Mindfulness (sati)
  4. Concentration (samādhi)
  5. Wisdom (paññā)

When these same faculties are strengthened and fully developed, they are known as the Five Strengths (pañca bala). Both sets are included among the thirty-seven bodhipakkhiyā dhammā, the qualities conducive to awakening.

Balance

Commentarial tradition emphasizes balancing the faculties in pairs: faith should be balanced with wisdom, and energy with concentration, while mindfulness is always useful and cannot be in excess. Faith without wisdom may become blind devotion; wisdom without faith may become cunning. Energy unbalanced by concentration leads to restlessness, while concentration unbalanced by energy leads to sloth. In the canonical texts, developing the five faculties is associated with attaining stream-entry and higher stages of awakening.
