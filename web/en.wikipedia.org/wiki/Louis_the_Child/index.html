Louis the Child (893 – 911), sometimes called Louis IV, was the king of East Francia from 900 until his death in 911 and was the last ruler of the Carolingian dynasty in the East Frankish kingdom. He succeeded his father, the emperor Arnulf of Carinthia, at the age of six.

Reign

Louis was crowned at Forchheim in February 900. Because of his youth, real power lay with regents and counsellors, notably Archbishop Hatto I of Mainz and Bishop Adalbero of Augsburg. His reign was troubled by feuds among the rising stem duchies — including the Conradine–Babenberg feud in Franconia — and by devastating invasions of the Magyars, who destroyed a Bavarian army at Pressburg in 907 and raided deep into Saxony, Franconia and Swabia.

Death and succession

Louis died in September 911, aged about eighteen, without children. The East Frankish magnates declined to elect the West Frankish Carolingian king Charles the Simple and instead chose the Franconian duke Conrad I, ending the Carolingian line in the east.
