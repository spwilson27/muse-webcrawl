Sim Wong Hoo (1955 – 4 January 2023) was a Singaporean inventor and billionaire entrepreneur who founded Creative Technology, the company behind the Sound Blaster sound card, which set the standard for PC audio in the 1990s. He was one of Singapore's best-known technologists and was often described as the country's first technology entrepreneur of global scale.

Career

Sim graduated from Ngee Ann Technical College (now Ngee Ann Polytechnic) with a diploma in electronics engineering. After a stint in private engineering work, he founded Creative Technology in 1981 with a small shop in Singapore selling a memory-expansion add-on for the Apple II. Creative's Cubic CT computer (1986) added Chinese-language and audio features; the Creative Music System board followed, and in 1989 the company launched the Sound Blaster, which became the de facto standard sound card, selling over 400 million units across its product lines over the following decades. Creative listed on NASDAQ in 1992 — the first Singapore company to do so — and later on the Singapore Exchange.

Later ventures and legal disputes

As integrated audio eroded the sound-card market, Sim pushed Creative into MP3 players (the Nomad and Zen lines, which preceded the iPod) and later audio technologies such as Super X-Fi holographic audio. Creative won a patent lawsuit over the iPod's user interface in 2006, with Apple paying US$100 million in a settlement and licensing agreement. Sim promoted the Singaporean philosophy of "No U-Turn Syndrome" (NUTS) — a critique of over-reliance on authority — in a 1999 book, Chaotic Thoughts from the Old Millennium.

Death

Sim died on January 4, 2023, at the age of 67. He remained chairman and CEO of Creative until his death and controlled roughly a third of the company's shares.
