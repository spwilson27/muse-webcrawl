Walpurgis Night

Walpurgis Night, an abbreviation of Saint Walpurgis Night (German: Walpurgisnacht), is the eve of the Christian feast day of Saint Walpurga on 1 May. It is widely celebrated in Germany, Finland, Sweden and other parts of Northern and Central Europe with bonfires, dancing and fireworks. In German folklore, Walpurgisnacht is the night when witches were reputed to gather on the Brocken, the highest peak of the Harz mountains — a scene dramatised in Goethe's Faust.

Bonfires are lit on hilltops across northern Europe on Walpurgis Night.

Origins and customs

Saint Walpurga (c. 710–777/9) was an Anglo-Saxon missionary to the Frankish Empire, canonised on 1 May. Her feast coincided with older spring and May Day customs, and the eve acquired associations with witches' sabbaths. In Sweden, Valborg is a public festivity marked by student traditions and bonfires; in Finland, Vappu is one of the biggest annual celebrations, with picnics and balloons.

In culture

Beyond Faust, Walpurgis Night appears in Bram Stoker's short story "Dracula's Guest", in music such as Mendelssohn's cantata Die erste Walpurgisnacht, and in Mussorgsky's Night on Bald Mountain in spirit.
