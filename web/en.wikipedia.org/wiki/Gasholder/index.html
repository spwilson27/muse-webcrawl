Gasholder


A frame-guided gasholder, once a common industrial landmark.

A gasholder or gasometer is a large container in which natural gas or town (coal) gas is stored near atmospheric pressure at ambient temperatures. Gasholders buffer the difference between the steady output of gasworks and fluctuating demand: the container rises as gas is pumped in and falls as gas is drawn out, its weight maintaining pressure in the mains. The telescoping, water-sealed design was patented by William Murdoch in 1817, and the familiar spiral-guided and column-guided frames became landmarks of industrial cities.

Types

The two main historic types are the water-sealed telescoping holder, whose bell rises from a water tank guided by spiral rails or columns, and the dry-seal holders (MAN and Klonne types) that use a piston sealed by tar or a membrane. Capacities ranged up to several million cubic feet; London's Bromley-by-Bow "Seven Sisters" holders were among the largest.

Decline and reuse

With the shift to high-pressure pipeline storage and the end of town gas, most gasholders became redundant from the late 20th century and many were demolished. Some listed examples have been preserved and adapted: the holders at King's Cross in London were re-erected as a park (Gasholder Park) and as frames for apartment buildings.
