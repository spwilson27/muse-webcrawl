Ion implantation

Ion implantation is a low-temperature process by which ions of one element are accelerated into a solid target, thereby changing the target's physical, chemical, or electrical properties. Ion implantation is used in semiconductor device fabrication, in metal finishing, and in materials science research.

Principles

An ion implanter ionises the dopant or treatment material, accelerates the ions in an electric field to energies typically between a few kiloelectronvolts and several megaelectronvolts, selects the desired mass with a magnet, and scans the beam across the target. The ions penetrate the surface and come to rest at a depth determined by their energy, producing a roughly Gaussian dopant profile. The total dose is precisely controlled by measuring the accumulated beam charge.

Semiconductor applications

Implantation is the standard method of introducing dopants such as boron, phosphorus, and arsenic into silicon wafers to form transistors' source, drain, and well regions. Its advantages are exact dose control, reproducibility, and the ability to dope through thin layers with patterned resist masks. Implantation damages the crystal lattice, so wafers are subsequently annealed to repair the damage and activate the dopants.

Other applications

Nitrogen or carbon implantation hardens tool steels, bearings, and orthopaedic implants and improves corrosion resistance, without changing the part's dimensions. Ion implantation is also used to synthesise nanoparticles, modify optical materials, and simulate radiation damage in nuclear materials.
