Darcy's law
Formulated by: Henry Darcy (1856)
Field: Hydrogeology, fluid mechanics
Describes: Flow of fluid through porous media

Darcy's law is an equation that describes the flow of a fluid through a porous medium. The law was formulated by Henry Darcy based on results of experiments on the flow of water through beds of sand, forming the basis of hydrogeology, a branch of earth sciences. It is analogous to Ohm's law in electrostatics and linearly relates the volume flow rate of the fluid to the hydraulic head difference and hydraulic conductivity.

Equation

In its simplest form, the total discharge Q is given by Q = −KA (dh/dl), where K is the hydraulic conductivity of the medium, A the cross-sectional area, and dh/dl the hydraulic gradient. The negative sign indicates that flow is from high head to low head. The specific discharge, or Darcy flux, is proportional to the hydraulic gradient with the proportionality constant being the hydraulic conductivity.

Applications

Darcy's law is fundamental to groundwater hydrology, petroleum engineering and soil science. It applies to laminar flow through saturated porous media; for high velocities or coarse media, corrections such as the Forchheimer equation are used.
