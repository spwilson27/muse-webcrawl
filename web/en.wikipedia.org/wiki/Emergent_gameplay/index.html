Emergent gameplay

Emergent gameplay refers to complex situations in video games, board games, or tabletop role-playing games that emerge from the interaction of relatively simple game mechanics. Designers have attempted to encourage emergent play by providing tools for players within the game, such as physics engines that simulate gravity and collisions, extensive construction systems, or complex artificial intelligence with reactive behaviors.

More recently, game designers, such as Peter Molyneux, have looked to emergent gameplay as the future of video games, arguing that simple systems interacting in combination can produce depth that scripted content alone cannot match.

Intentional emergence

Intentional emergence occurs when some creative uses of the game are intended by the game designers. Since the 1970s and 1980s, board games and tabletop role-playing games such as Cosmic Encounter and Dungeons & Dragons have featured intentional emergence as a central design goal. In video games, the immersive sim genre — including titles such as Deus Ex, Thief, and Dishonored — is built around layered systems of stealth, physics, and AI that allow players to solve problems in unscripted ways.

Unintentional emergence

Unintentional emergence occurs when creative uses of a game were not intended by the designers. A famous example is rocket jumping in first-person shooters, in which players exploit weapon knockback to reach otherwise inaccessible areas; what began as an exploit became a deliberate feature in later games. Combos in fighting games such as Street Fighter II likewise began as a programming bug that players discovered and turned into a core technique. Speedrunning communities are built largely on finding unintended interactions between game systems.

Examples

The simulation games Dwarf Fortress and The Sims are widely cited for the volume of emergent narrative they generate: characters with simulated needs, memories, and relationships produce stories the designers never wrote. The physics systems of The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild allow fire, electricity, magnetism, and weather to interact so that players routinely discover solutions the designers did not anticipate. Multiplayer games add a social layer of emergence, with economies, alliances, and wars arising spontaneously in games such as EVE Online.
