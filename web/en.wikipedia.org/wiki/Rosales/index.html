Rosales

Kingdom: Plantae

Clade: Angiosperms, eudicots, rosids, fabids

Order: Rosales

Families: 9 (APG IV)

Rosales is an order of flowering plants comprising about 7,700 species in roughly 260 genera and nine families. It belongs to the fabid clade of the rosid eudicots and is notable for the great economic importance of its members, which include many fruit crops and timber trees. The order takes its name from the rose family, Rosaceae.

Families

Under the APG IV classification, the order includes:

  • Rosaceae — the rose family: roses, apples, pears, peaches, plums, cherries, strawberries, raspberries, almonds, and hawthorns.
  • Rhamnaceae — the buckthorn family.
  • Elaeagnaceae — oleaster and sea-buckthorn.
  • Ulmaceae — elms and zelkovas.
  • Cannabaceae — cannabis, hops, and hackberries.
  • Moraceae — mulberries, figs, breadfruit, and rubber trees.
  • Urticaceae — nettles and their relatives.
  • Barbeyaceae and Dirachmaceae — two small families of the Horn of Africa and Socotra region.

Characteristics

Members of Rosales are morphologically diverse, ranging from herbs such as strawberry to large forest trees such as elm and fig. Many have stipulate leaves, flowers with a hypanthium (a cup formed by fused floral bases), and reduced, wind-pollinated flowers in the elm, nettle, and mulberry lineages, in contrast to the showy, insect-pollinated flowers of the rose family. A number of families in the order — including Rosaceae, Rhamnaceae, Elaeagnaceae, and Ulmaceae — form nitrogen-fixing root nodules in symbiosis with Frankia bacteria, making them actinorhizal plants.

Evolution and phylogeny

Molecular studies place Rosales as sister to the order Fagales within the nitrogen-fixing clade of fabids, together with Cucurbitales and Fabales. The crown group is estimated to be around 90–100 million years old, diversifying during the Late Cretaceous.
