Sveriges Television

Sveriges Television AB (SVT, "Sweden's Television") is the Swedish national public broadcasting television company. Launched in 1956, it operates several free-to-air channels funded — since 2019 — through a public service tax collected with income tax, replacing the former household licence fee. SVT is independent of political and commercial interests and is owned by a foundation.

History

Television in Sweden began officially on 4 September 1956 as Radiotjänst TV, under the same organisation as public radio. In 1979 the broadcasting corporation was split, and Sveriges Television became a separate company. For four decades SVT held a monopoly on terrestrial television in Sweden; TV3 (broadcast from London) broke it in 1987, and TV4 became the first domestic commercial terrestrial channel in 1992. SVT was late to face competition but remains Sweden's most-watched broadcaster overall.

Channels and services

SVT's main channels are SVT1 and SVT2, plus the children's channel SVT Barn, the SVT24 news channel, and the jointly operated Kunskapskanalen (the Knowledge Channel). Its streaming service, SVT Play, offers live channels and extensive catch-up and archive programming, and the company provides programming in Swedish, Finnish, Sámi, and other minority languages. Iconic SVT productions include the Eurovision Song Contest broadcasts (Sweden's Melodifestivalen is the national selection and one of the country's biggest annual programmes), the Donald Duck Christmas special Kalle Anka watched by millions every Christmas Eve since 1960, and dramas ranging from Rederiet to internationally exported series.

Organisation and funding

SVT, Sveriges Radio, and UR are owned by the Förvaltningsstiftelsen, a foundation designed to insulate them from political control, and operate under a charter licence from the government. Funding moved from a TV licence fee to a dedicated public service fee levied on individuals' taxes in 2019, a change intended to modernise collection as viewing shifted online.
