Mohamed Morsi

Mohamed Mohamed Morsi Eissa al-Ayyat (8 August 1951 – 17 June 2019) was an Egyptian politician and engineer who served as the fifth president of Egypt from 2012 to 2013, when he was deposed in a military coup d'état led by General Abdel Fattah el-Sisi following mass protests. A leading figure in the Muslim Brotherhood, he was Egypt's first democratically elected head of state, though his year in office was deeply controversial.

Early life and career

Morsi studied engineering at Cairo University and earned a PhD in materials science at the University of Southern California in 1982. He worked with NASA on space shuttle engines and taught at California State University, Northridge, before returning to Egypt, where he became head of the materials engineering department at Zagazig University. He was elected to parliament in 2000 as an independent affiliated with the then-banned Muslim Brotherhood.

Presidency

After the 2011 Egyptian revolution, the Brotherhood's Freedom and Justice Party nominated Morsi, who narrowly won the June 2012 run-off against Ahmed Shafik with 51.7% of the vote. He took office on 30 June 2012. His November 2012 constitutional declaration, which temporarily placed his decisions beyond judicial review, triggered mass protests, as did the rushed drafting of an Islamist-leaning constitution. Economic difficulties and perceptions of Brotherhood monopolization of power fueled the Tamarod petition campaign and huge demonstrations on 30 June 2013; on 3 July the military removed and detained him.

Imprisonment and death

Morsi was tried in several cases and sentenced to death in one (later overturned) and to lengthy prison terms in others. On 17 June 2019 he collapsed in a courtroom cage during a hearing and died shortly afterwards, aged 67. Human rights organizations criticized the conditions of his detention; he was buried in Cairo under tight security.
