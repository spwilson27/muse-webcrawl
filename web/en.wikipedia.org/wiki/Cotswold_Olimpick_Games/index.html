Cotswold Olimpick Games

Held annually on Dover's Hill, near Chipping Campden, Gloucestershire.

The Cotswold Olimpick Games is an annual public celebration of games and sports held in the Cotswolds in the English county of Gloucestershire, originating in the early 17th century. The event is famous for unusual contests such as shin-kicking and is regarded by the British Olympic Association as "the first stirrings of Britain's Olympic beginnings".

Origins

The games were founded by the lawyer Robert Dover, with the approval of King James I, and were first held around 1612 on what became known as Dover's Hill, above the town of Chipping Campden. Events included horse-racing, coursing, running, jumping, dancing, hammer-throwing, wrestling, and contests for men and women. A temporary wooden structure, Dover Castle, was erected each year with small cannon fired to begin the festivities. The games were celebrated in a 1636 collection of poems, the Annalia Dubrensia, with contributions from poets including Ben Jonson.

Suppression and revivals

The games were suppressed during the English Civil War and again after the enclosure of Dover's Hill in the 1850s ended the increasingly rowdy event. They were revived in 1951 for the Festival of Britain and have been held annually since 1966, organized by the Robert Dover's Games Society. The modern games conclude with a torchlit procession into Chipping Campden and dancing in the square.

Events

The best-known event is shin-kicking, in which competitors, holding each other's shoulders, kick at their opponent's shins—traditionally with straw-stuffed trousers and steel-toed boots—until one falls. Other events have included tug-of-war, the "pike" (vaulting), obstacle races, and a championship of rural games.
