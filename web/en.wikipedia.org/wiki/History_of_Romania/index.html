History of Romania

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Transylvania, enclosed by the Carpathians, is one of Romania's historic regions.

The history of Romania covers the territory of modern Romania and the Romanian people from the ancient Dacians and Roman conquest of Dacia under Trajan, through the medieval principalities of Wallachia, Moldavia and Transylvania, Ottoman suzerainty, the union of 1859 and the Kingdom of Romania, the territorial gains and losses of the two world wars, the communist regime of Nicolae Ceaușescu, and the post-1989 transition into NATO (2004) and the European Union (2007).

Antiquity and the Middle Ages

The Dacian kingdom of Burebista and Decebalus was conquered by Trajan in 101–106 CE; Roman Dacia was abandoned under Aurelian in 271. The ethnogenesis of the Romanians — from Romanised Dacians and later contacts with Slavs and others — remains debated. In the 14th century the principalities of Wallachia and Moldavia emerged; their voivodes, including Vlad the Impaler and Stephen the Great, resisted Ottoman expansion, though both principalities became tribute-paying vassals while Transylvania passed between Hungary, the Ottomans and the Habsburgs.

Union and kingdom

The double election of Alexandru Ioan Cuza in 1859 united Wallachia and Moldavia; under Carol I Romania gained independence in the war of 1877–78 and became a kingdom in 1881. Joining the Allies in 1916, Romania emerged from the First World War greatly enlarged — Greater Romania including Transylvania, Bessarabia and Bukovina — but in 1940 lost territory to the USSR, Hungary and Bulgaria, leading King Carol II's abdication and the dictatorship of Ion Antonescu, allied with Nazi Germany.

Communism and after

The monarchy was abolished in 1947. Under Gheorghiu-Dej and then Ceaușescu (1965–1989), Romania pursued an increasingly independent line within the Warsaw Pact while imposing harsh austerity and a personality cult at home. The December 1989 revolution — the only violent overthrow of a communist regime in Eastern Europe that year — executed the Ceaușescus and opened a turbulent transition under Ion Iliescu and his successors.
