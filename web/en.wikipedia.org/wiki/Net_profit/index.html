Net profit

Net profit, also referred to as the bottom line, net income, or net earnings, is a measure of the profitability of a venture after accounting for all costs and taxes. It is the actual profit, and it includes the operating expenses that are excluded from gross profit, as well as depreciation, interest, taxes and other expenses. Net profit is the gross profit minus all other expenses not included in the cost of goods sold, and it appears on a company's income statement.

Net profit is the final line of the income statement.

Definition

Net profit is calculated as:

Net profit = Total revenue − Total expenses

where total expenses include the cost of goods sold, operating expenses such as salaries, rent, and utilities, depreciation and amortization, interest expense, and income taxes. If total expenses exceed revenue, the result is a net loss.

Net profit margin

The net profit margin expresses net profit as a percentage of revenue and is a widely used indicator of a company's overall financial health and pricing and cost control:

Net profit margin = (Net profit / Revenue) × 100

Margins vary greatly by industry; supermarkets operate on margins of a few percent, while software firms may exceed 20 percent.
