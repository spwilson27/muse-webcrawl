Creatine kinase

Creatine kinase (CK), also known as creatine phosphokinase (CPK), is an enzyme expressed by various tissues and cell types. It catalyses the reversible conversion of creatine and adenosine triphosphate (ATP) into phosphocreatine and adenosine diphosphate (ADP), forming a rapidly mobilisable reserve of high-energy phosphates in tissues with fluctuating energy demands, such as skeletal muscle, the heart and the brain.

Isoenzymes

Creatine kinase occurs as combinations of two subunits, M (muscle) and B (brain), giving three cytosolic isoenzymes: CK-MM (predominant in skeletal muscle), CK-BB (brain and smooth muscle) and CK-MB (enriched in heart muscle, historically used to diagnose heart attacks). Separate mitochondrial creatine kinases serve the energy-shuttle system within mitochondria.

Clinical significance

Measuring CK in blood is a routine test for muscle damage. Elevated levels occur after myocardial infarction, in rhabdomyolysis, muscular dystrophies, strenuous exercise, and in response to some drugs such as statins. The CK-MB fraction was long a standard marker of heart attack but has largely been replaced by the more sensitive and specific cardiac troponins. Persistently high CK can indicate myositis or metabolic muscle disease, while modest elevations are common in healthy people after exercise.
