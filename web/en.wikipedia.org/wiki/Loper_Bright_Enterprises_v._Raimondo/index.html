Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo, 603 U.S. 369 (2024), is a landmark decision of the Supreme Court of the United States that overruled the Chevron deference doctrine established in Chevron U.S.A., Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc. (1984). The Court held that the Administrative Procedure Act requires courts to exercise their independent judgment in deciding whether an agency has acted within its statutory authority, and that courts may not defer to an agency's interpretation of a statute merely because it is ambiguous.

Background

The case arose from a rule of the National Marine Fisheries Service under the Magnuson–Stevens Fishery Conservation and Management Act requiring Atlantic herring fishing vessels to pay for federal observers to monitor their catches. Family-owned fishing companies, including Loper Bright Enterprises, challenged the rule, arguing the statute did not authorise charging the industry for the monitors. The lower courts upheld the rule by applying Chevron deference. A companion case, Relentless, Inc. v. Department of Commerce, was decided together with it.

Decision

Chief Justice Roberts, writing for a six-justice majority, held that Chevron was inconsistent with the Administrative Procedure Act and with the traditional judicial role of saying what the law is, tracing that duty to Marbury v. Madison. Justice Kagan, dissenting with Justices Sotomayor and Jackson, warned that the decision amounted to a judicial power grab that would transfer policy-laden interpretive questions from expert agencies to courts. Agency interpretations may still receive respect under the older Skidmore standard, in proportion to their power to persuade.

Impact

The decision is expected to reshape administrative law in the United States, inviting challenges to federal regulations across fields from environmental protection to healthcare and financial regulation, and increasing the influence of the federal judiciary over the regulatory state.
