Falaise Pocket

The Falaise Pocket (German: Kessel von Falaise) was the encirclement of the German Army Group B by the Western Allies during the Battle of Normandy in August 1944. Following the American breakout in Operation Cobra and the failed German counterattack Operation Lüttich, Allied forces — Americans from the south, British and Canadians from the north, and the Polish 1st Armoured Division — closed around some 150,000 German troops west of the Seine near Falaise, Argentan, Vimoutiers and Chambois.

The closing of the pocket

Canadian and Polish forces advancing south in Operations Totalize and Tractable met American and French forces near Chambois on 19 August 1944. Polish troops held Hill 262 (Mont Ormel, "the Mace") against desperate German breakout attempts on 20–21 August, while Allied artillery and air power devastated the congested roads of the pocket — the "Corridor of Death". By 21 August the pocket was sealed.

Outcome

Estimates suggest 10,000–15,000 German soldiers were killed and 40,000–50,000 captured, though a significant portion of Army Group B's personnel — perhaps 20,000–40,000 men, including cadres of divisions that would be rebuilt — escaped across the Seine before the pocket fully closed. The destruction of equipment was near-total. The victory ended the Battle of Normandy, opened the road to Paris (liberated 25 August) and the Seine crossings, and is often described as the Wehrmacht's decisive defeat in the West in 1944.
