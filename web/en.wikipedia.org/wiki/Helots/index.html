Helots

Helots (εἵλωτες, heílōtes)
Status: unfree population of ancient Sparta
Regions: Laconia and Messenia
Role: agricultural labour on Spartiate estates (kleroi)
Decline: liberation of Messenia, 370/369 BC
[Image: Helot farmers working the fields of the Eurotas valley beneath Mount Taygetus]

The helots were an unfree population that formed the majority of inhabitants of the Spartan state in ancient Greece. Neither chattel slaves nor free citizens, they were tied to the land — the estates (kleroi) of the Spartiate citizens — and delivered a fixed share of its produce, freeing the Spartans to devote themselves wholly to military training. Their condition and treatment profoundly shaped Spartan society.

Origins

The helots of Laconia were probably subjugated early in Sparta's expansion; far more numerous were the Messenians, conquered in the Messenian Wars of the 8th–7th centuries BC and reduced wholesale to helotry — a grievance that burned for centuries. The word's etymology is disputed, perhaps from the town of Helos or from a root meaning "captured."

Status and treatment

Helots belonged to the state rather than individual masters, could not be sold abroad, lived in families and communities and kept part of their produce. Yet they were systematically humiliated and terrorised: each year the ephors formally declared war on them so that killing a helot carried no religious pollution; the krypteia, a rite of passage for young Spartans, allegedly involved murdering strong helots by night. Thucydides recounts that during the Peloponnesian War the Spartans invited helots to select those who had served bravely, then made some 2,000 of them disappear. Helots also served the army as attendants and light troops — at Plataea thousands accompanied each Spartiate — and could occasionally win freedom as neodamodeis.

Revolts and end

Fear of helot revolt dictated Sparta's conservatism and reluctance to campaign far from home. Major revolts followed the earthquake of 464 BC, when Messenian helots held Mount Ithome for years. After the Battle of Leuctra, Epaminondas liberated Messenia in 370/369 BC, striking away the economic basis of Spartan power; Laconian helotry faded with Sparta's decline.
