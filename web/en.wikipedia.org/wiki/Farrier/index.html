Farrier
OccupationEquine hoof care specialist
DutiesTrimming hooves, fitting and applying horseshoes
RegulationRegistered trade in the UK (Farriers Registration Act 1975)

A farrier is a specialist in equine hoof care, including the trimming and balancing of horses' hooves and the placement of shoes on their hooves, if necessary. A farrier combines some blacksmithing skills (fabricating, adapting or adjusting metal shoes) with knowledge of equine anatomy and hoof care. The word derives from the Latin ferrarius, "worker in iron".

Work

Horses' hooves grow continuously and typically need attention every four to eight weeks. A farrier's routine visit involves assessing the horse's gait and hoof balance, removing old shoes, trimming excess hoof wall, dressing the foot, and then fitting shoes — either ready-made or shaped at a forge, applied hot or cold — nailed or glued to the hoof. Farriers also apply corrective or therapeutic shoeing for conditions such as laminitis, navicular syndrome and cracked hooves, often working with veterinarians.

Tools

The farrier's tools include hoof nippers, rasps, knives, pull-offs, a hammer and clinchers for the trimming and shoeing process, plus a forge, anvil and shoe stock for making and shaping shoes. Many modern farriers work from a vehicle fitted with a gas forge and travel between yards and stables.

Training and regulation

In the United Kingdom, farriery is a regulated profession: the Farriers (Registration) Act 1975 requires registration with the Farriers Registration Council, normally after a four-year apprenticeship and examinations. In the United States there is no national licensing requirement, but voluntary certification is offered by bodies such as the American Farriers Association. Historically, farriers also acted as horse doctors, and the Worshipful Company of Farriers in London dates from 1356.
