Erving Goffman (11 June 1922 – 19 November 1982) was a Canadian-born American sociologist, social psychologist, and writer, considered by some "the most influential American sociologist of the twentieth century". He was a major figure in symbolic interactionism and the study of micro-sociology, developing the dramaturgical analysis of social interaction.

Work

In The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life (1956/1959) Goffman portrayed social life as theatre: individuals perform roles before audiences, manage impressions, and separate "front stage" from "back stage" behaviour. Asylums (1961), based on fieldwork at a mental hospital, introduced the concept of the "total institution" and influenced the critique of institutional psychiatry. Stigma (1963) analysed how spoiled identities are managed. Later works — Interaction Ritual (1967), Frame Analysis (1974) and Gender Advertisements (1979) — explored the framing of experience and the ritual display of gender. Goffman taught at the University of California, Berkeley and the University of Pennsylvania, and served as president of the American Sociological Association.
