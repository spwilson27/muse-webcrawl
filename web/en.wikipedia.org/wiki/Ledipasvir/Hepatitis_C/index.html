Ledipasvir — Hepatitis C

Ledipasvir is an antiviral drug used in the treatment of hepatitis C, a liver disease caused by the hepatitis C virus (HCV). It acts as an inhibitor of the viral NS5A protein, which is essential for viral replication and assembly.

Use in hepatitis C

Ledipasvir is almost always used in a fixed-dose combination with sofosbuvir, marketed as Harvoni. The combination, taken once daily for 8 to 24 weeks, cures more than 90–95% of treated patients with HCV genotypes 1, 4, 5 and 6, replacing older interferon-based regimens that were longer and far less well tolerated.

Mechanism

NS5A has no enzymatic activity but functions as a scaffold in the viral replication complex. By binding NS5A, ledipasvir blocks both viral RNA replication and the assembly of new virions, and acts synergistically with sofosbuvir's inhibition of the NS5B polymerase.

History

Ledipasvir was developed by Gilead Sciences and approved by the US FDA in October 2014 as part of the first interferon-free, all-oral regimen for hepatitis C. Its launch was also notable for controversy over the high list price of the combination.
