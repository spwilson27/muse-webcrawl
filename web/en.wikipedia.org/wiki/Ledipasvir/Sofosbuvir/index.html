Ledipasvir/sofosbuvir

Ledipasvir/sofosbuvir, sold under the brand name Harvoni, is a fixed-dose combination medication for the treatment of hepatitis C. It contains ledipasvir, an NS5A inhibitor, and sofosbuvir, an NS5B polymerase inhibitor, in a single once-daily tablet.

Medical uses

The combination is used for chronic hepatitis C genotypes 1, 4, 5 and 6 in adults and children. Courses typically last 12 weeks, with 8 weeks sufficient for many treatment-naïve patients without cirrhosis, and cure rates (sustained virologic response) above 95% in clinical trials.

History

Harvoni was approved by the US FDA on 10 October 2014 — the first regimen for hepatitis C requiring neither interferon nor ribavirin for most patients. Its US launch price of about $1,125 per pill, or roughly $94,500 for a 12-week course, triggered a wide public and political debate about drug pricing, and cheaper authorised generics were later licensed for low- and middle-income countries.

Side effects

The regimen is generally well tolerated; the most common adverse effects are fatigue and headache. Serious symptomatic bradycardia has been reported when combined with the antiarrhythmic amiodarone.
