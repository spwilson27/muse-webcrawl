Ledipasvir/sofosbuvir

Trade nameHarvoni
ComponentsLedipasvir (NS5A inhibitor) + Sofosbuvir (NS5B polymerase inhibitor)
TypeFixed-dose combination antiviral
ManufacturerGilead Sciences
ApprovedOctober 2014 (US FDA)

Ledipasvir/sofosbuvir, sold under the brand name Harvoni, is a fixed-dose combination medication for the treatment of chronic hepatitis C. It combines two direct-acting antivirals: ledipasvir, an inhibitor of the viral NS5A protein, and sofosbuvir, a nucleotide analogue inhibitor of the NS5B RNA polymerase. It is taken by mouth, once daily, usually for 8 to 12 weeks depending on the patient's treatment history and whether cirrhosis is present.

Medical use

The combination was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in October 2014 for hepatitis C virus genotype 1, and its indication was later extended to additional genotypes and to patients with decompensated cirrhosis (in combination with ribavirin) and post-transplant patients. Clinical trials (the ION programme) reported sustained virologic response — effectively a cure — in roughly 94–99% of treated patients, a dramatic improvement over older interferon-based regimens, with generally mild side effects such as fatigue and headache.

Cost and access

Harvoni launched in the United States at a price of about $1,125 per tablet, or roughly $94,500 for a 12-week course, prompting widespread debate over pharmaceutical pricing. Manufacturer Gilead licensed generic production for many low- and middle-income countries, where a course became available for a small fraction of the U.S. price. The drug is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
