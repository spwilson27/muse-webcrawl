Ledipasvir is a drug for the treatment of hepatitis C which was developed by Gilead Sciences. It is an inhibitor of the hepatitis C virus (HCV) NS5A protein, a phosphoprotein that plays an important role in viral replication and assembly. After phase III clinical trials, on October 10, 2014, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved the combination product ledipasvir/sofosbuvir (brand name Harvoni) for the treatment of chronic hepatitis C genotype 1 infection.

Medical uses

Ledipasvir is used only in fixed-dose combination with the nucleotide polymerase inhibitor sofosbuvir. The combination produces sustained virological response (cure) rates of over 90% in most patient groups with genotype 1, and is also used for certain other genotypes. Treatment courses are typically 8 to 24 weeks depending on viral load, prior treatment history and the presence of cirrhosis.

Mechanism of action

Ledipasvir binds to HCV NS5A, interfering with its role in the viral replication complex and in virion assembly. Resistance can arise through substitutions in NS5A, which is why the drug is combined with an agent of a different class.

Interactions

Ledipasvir absorption is reduced by acid-reducing agents, and it is a substrate and inhibitor of drug transporters such as P-glycoprotein, so co-medication must be reviewed. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines as part of the combination product.
