Ledipasvir

Ledipasvir is a direct-acting antiviral medication used to treat hepatitis C. It is a potent inhibitor of the hepatitis C virus NS5A protein and is co-formulated with sofosbuvir in the once-daily tablet Harvoni.

Pharmacology

Ledipasvir has a molecular weight of 889.0 g/mol and the formula C49H54F2N8O6. It is active at picomolar concentrations against HCV genotypes 1a and 1b, with somewhat lower activity against other genotypes. The drug is highly protein-bound, has a half-life of about 47 hours, and is eliminated mainly unchanged in the faeces.

Clinical use

The ledipasvir/sofosbuvir combination achieves sustained virologic response — effectively a cure — in the great majority of patients after 8–12 weeks of therapy. Absorption of ledipasvir falls as gastric pH rises, so acid-suppressing drugs such as proton-pump inhibitors must be timed carefully during treatment.

Development

Developed by Gilead Sciences (development code GS-5885), ledipasvir was approved in 2014 in combination with sofosbuvir, becoming a cornerstone of modern interferon-free hepatitis C therapy and appearing on the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines as part of that combination.
