Jim Jones

James Warren Jones (May 13, 1931 – November 18, 1978) was an American cult leader who founded the Peoples Temple, a movement that began as a church in Indiana, grew influential in California, and ended in the mass murder-suicide of more than 900 of its members at Jonestown, Guyana, in 1978 — one of the largest single losses of American civilian life in a deliberate act before the September 11 attacks.

Peoples Temple

Jones founded the Wings of Deliverance church in Indianapolis in the 1950s, renaming it the Peoples Temple. Preaching an "apostolic socialism" that combined Pentecostal-style faith healing with racial integration and social welfare programmes, he attracted a large, racially mixed congregation and political influence, moving the Temple to California in the 1960s and later to San Francisco, where he became chairman of the city's Housing Authority. Behind the public image, former members described authoritarian control, staged healings, financial exploitation and rehearsals for collective suicide ("White Nights").

Jonestown

In 1977, facing press investigations and defections, Jones relocated with nearly a thousand followers to the Peoples Temple Agricultural Project — "Jonestown" — a remote commune in the jungle of Guyana. On 18 November 1978, U.S. Congressman Leo Ryan, who had travelled to investigate abuse claims, was murdered with four others at a nearby airstrip by Temple gunmen as he tried to leave with defectors. That evening Jones ordered his followers to drink a grape-flavoured drink laced with cyanide and sedatives; 918 people died at Jonestown, the airstrip and Georgetown, including more than 300 children. Jones himself died of a gunshot wound to the head. The phrase "drinking the Kool-Aid" derives from the event, although the drink used was largely Flavor Aid.
