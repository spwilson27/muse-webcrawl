Serotonin transporter

The serotonin transporter (SERT) is a membrane protein of the solute carrier family.

The serotonin transporter (SERT or 5-HTT), encoded in humans by the SLC6A4 gene, is a monoamine transporter protein that transports the neurotransmitter serotonin from the synaptic cleft back into the presynaptic neuron (reuptake). This terminates the action of serotonin and recycles it for reuse. SERT belongs to the sodium/chloride-dependent neurotransmitter transporter family.

Pharmacology

SERT is the principal target of widely used antidepressant drugs. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) such as sertraline, fluoxetine and citalopram bind to SERT and block reuptake, as do serotonin–norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors and many tricyclic antidepressants. Cocaine and MDMA also interact with the transporter. Over-activity of serotonin due to combinations of these agents can contribute to serotonin syndrome.

Genetics

A polymorphism in the promoter region of SLC6A4, known as 5-HTTLPR, produces long and short alleles that affect transcription of the transporter. The short allele has been extensively studied in relation to anxiety, stress reactivity and depression, though the size and robustness of these effects remain debated.
