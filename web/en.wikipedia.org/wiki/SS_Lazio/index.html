Società Sportiva Lazio (commonly SS Lazio, or simply Lazio) is an Italian professional football club based in Rome. Founded in 1900 as a multi-sport club, Lazio has spent most of its history in Serie A, the top tier of Italian football, and shares the Stadio Olimpico with its fierce city rivals, AS Roma, against whom it contests the Derby della Capitale. The club's colours are sky blue and white, chosen in honour of Greece, the home of the Olympic Games, and its symbol is the eagle, carried onto the pitch before home matches by the trained eagle Olimpia.

History

Lazio was founded on 9 January 1900 in the Prati district of Rome by Luigi Bigiarelli and friends, and famously refused in 1927 to merge into the new AS Roma, remaining independent. The club won its first major trophy, the Coppa Italia, in 1958. A charismatic, volatile side won the club's first scudetto in 1973–74 under coach Tommaso Maestrelli, but the following years brought relegation amid the Totonero betting scandal.

The club's golden era came under president Sergio Cragnotti in the 1990s. Bankrolled by the Cirio food conglomerate, Lazio signed world-class players and, coached by Sven-Göran Eriksson, won the UEFA Cup Winners' Cup in 1999, the UEFA Super Cup the same year, and a second Serie A title in 1999–2000, plus several Coppa Italia trophies. After Cragnotti's empire collapsed, Claudio Lotito took over in 2004 and stabilised the club, adding further Coppa Italia wins in 2009, 2013 (against Roma in the final) and 2019, and Supercoppa titles including in 2017 and 2019.

Supporters and identity

Lazio's traditional heartland is northern and working-to-middle class Rome. Its ultras' groups, notably the former Irriducibili, have been controversial for far-right symbolism and racist incidents, which the club has repeatedly been sanctioned for. The multi-sport parent society, Polisportiva Lazio, is among the largest in Europe, fielding teams in dozens of sports.
