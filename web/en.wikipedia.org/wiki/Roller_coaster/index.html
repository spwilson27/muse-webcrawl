Roller coaster

A roller coaster is a type of amusement ride that employs a form of elevated railroad track designed with tight turns, steep slopes, and sometimes inversions. Passengers ride in open cars along the track, mostly propelled by gravity after a lift hill or launch.

Precursors include Russian ice slides of the 17th–18th centuries and the switchback railways of 1880s America. LaMarcus Adna Thompson patented an early coaster design in 1885. Coasters are built of wood (with traditional rumble and airtime) or steel (allowing loops and complex elements).

Records and types

Types include hypercoasters, giga- and strata-coasters, inverted, suspended, flying, spinning and launched coasters. Famous examples include Steel Vengeance at Cedar Point and the former Kingda Ka, once the tallest and fastest. Riders experience rapid changes in acceleration and g-forces, the source of the thrill.
