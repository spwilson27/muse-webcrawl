Animal sanctuary

[Image: Rescued farm animals grazing at a sanctuary pasture]

An animal sanctuary is a facility where animals are brought to live and be protected for the rest of their lives. Unlike animal shelters, sanctuaries do not generally seek to place animals with individuals or groups; residents are not bought, sold, traded, bred, or used in experiments or entertainment.

Types

Farm sanctuaries rescue animals from slaughter, abandonment and neglect; the first in the United States, Farm Sanctuary, was founded in 1986. Wildlife sanctuaries house injured or confiscated wild animals, while marine and primate sanctuaries care for specialised groups. Accreditation bodies such as the Global Federation of Animal Sanctuaries set standards for care, housing and governance.

Mission

Sanctuaries typically combine lifetime care with public education about animal agriculture, wildlife trade and responsible guardianship. Notable examples include Best Friends Animal Sanctuary in Utah and The Wild Animal Sanctuary in Colorado.
