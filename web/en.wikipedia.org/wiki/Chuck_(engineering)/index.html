Chuck (engineering)

A chuck is a specialized type of clamp used to hold an object with radial symmetry, especially a cylinder. In a drill, a mill, or a lathe, a chuck holds the rotating tool or workpiece.

Types

Drill chucks typically have three jaws that converge simultaneously when a key or knurled ring is turned (keyed and keyless designs). Lathe chucks may have three jaws (self-centering scroll chucks, for round or hexagonal stock) or four independent jaws (for irregular or offset work). Other types include collet chucks, which use a slotted sleeve squeezed around the workpiece for high accuracy; magnetic and vacuum chucks for holding workpieces on surface grinders; and pin chucks for very small drills.

Operation

Jawed chucks are mounted on the machine spindle, often via a threaded, tapered, or camlock backplate. Jaw geometry and grip determine concentricity and holding force; soft jaws can be machined to fit specific parts.
