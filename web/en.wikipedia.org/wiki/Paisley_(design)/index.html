Paisley, or paisley pattern, is an ornamental textile design using the boteh (Persian: بته) — a droplet-shaped motif with a bent or hooked tip, often described as a stylised mango, pine cone, or teardrop. Although of Persian and Indian origin, the pattern takes its English name from the town of Paisley in western Scotland, whose weavers became famous in the 19th century for imitating the Kashmir shawls on which the design travelled to Europe.

Origins

The boteh motif has deep roots in Persian art, appearing in textiles, tiles, and ornament for many centuries; interpretations of its symbolism include a cypress tree, a flame, a mango, and a floral spray bent by the wind. In India, fine woollen shawls bearing rows of botehs were woven in Kashmir from at least the 17th century, using the labour-intensive twill-tapestry (kani) technique, with a single shawl taking years to complete.

European production

Officials and soldiers of the East India Company brought Kashmir shawls to Britain in the later 18th century, where they became fashionable and expensive luxuries. British and French mills set out to imitate them. Paisley's weavers, working with the Jacquard loom and adding colour looms of increasing capacity, came to dominate the trade and gave the pattern its name. At the height of the trade around the 1850s, Paisley's shawls were a byword for the design, though competition from machine printing and changing fashion collapsed the industry in the 1870s.

Later history

The motif never entirely disappeared: it appeared on bandanas (the word itself from the Hindi bandhna, tie-dye), neckerchiefs, and dress fabrics. Paisley enjoyed a major revival in the psychedelic 1960s, championed by the Beatles' interest in Indian culture and worn by rock musicians. In the United States, the bandana paisley became strongly associated with workwear and, later, with gang colours. In the 1980s Prince named his Paisley Park studio and label after the motif. The pattern remains a staple of menswear ties, scarves, and upholstery, and the town of Paisley preserves its shawl heritage at the Paisley Museum.
