Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee

An Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) is a committee mandated by United States federal regulations to oversee and evaluate an institution's program for the care and use of animals in research, teaching, and testing. IACUCs are required at institutions that use animals covered by the Animal Welfare Act or that receive funding from the Public Health Service, including the National Institutes of Health.

Responsibilities

The IACUC is responsible for reviewing and approving all proposed animal research protocols before work begins, inspecting animal facilities at least once every six months, investigating concerns about animal care and use, and ensuring that personnel are properly trained. Committees must include at least one veterinarian, one practicing scientist experienced in animal research, one nonscientific member, and one member who is not affiliated with the institution.

Protocol review

Protocols submitted to the IACUC must describe the rationale for using animals, justify the number of animals requested, and address the "Three Rs" of replacement, reduction, and refinement. The committee evaluates whether pain and distress are minimized and whether euthanasia methods comply with accepted guidelines.

Regulatory basis

The IACUC system was strengthened by the 1985 amendments to the Animal Welfare Act and the Health Research Extension Act, which required institutions receiving federal funds to establish such committees and follow the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.
