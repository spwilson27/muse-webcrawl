Summit
DefinitionPoint on a surface with elevation higher than all points immediately adjacent to it
Highest on EarthMount Everest (8,848.86 m)

A summit is a point on a surface that is higher in elevation than all points immediately adjacent to it. The topographic terms acme, apex, peak (mountain peak), and zenith are synonymous. The term top (mountain top) is generally used only for a mountain point located at some distance from the nearest point of higher elevation. For example, a big, massive rock next to the main summit of a mountain is not considered a summit.

Summits near a higher peak, with some prominence or isolation, but not reaching a certain threshold value for the criteria, are often considered subsummits (or subpeaks) of the higher peak, and are part of the same mountain. A pyramidal peak is an exaggerated form produced by ice erosion of the summit of a mountain.

Definition and prominence

The International Climbing and Mountaineering Federation (UIAA) defines a summit in the Alps as independent if it has a prominence of at least 30 metres (98 ft). Geographers distinguish summits by their topographic prominence — the minimum drop that must be descended before ascending a higher point — and by isolation, the distance to the nearest higher ground. These criteria are used in "peak bagging" lists such as the Munros of Scotland and the fourteen eight-thousanders of the Himalaya and Karakoram, the highest of which is Mount Everest, the Earth's highest summit above sea level.

Other uses

By extension, "summit" commonly denotes a high-level meeting of heads of state or government (a summit meeting), and the highest point of achievement in various pursuits.
