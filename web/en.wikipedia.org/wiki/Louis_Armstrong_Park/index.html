Louis Armstrong Park

LocationTremé, New Orleans, Louisiana, United States
Area32 acres (13 ha)
Created1970s–1980s (opened in phases)
Named forLouis Armstrong

Louis Armstrong Park is a 32-acre (130,000 m²) park located in the Tremé neighborhood of New Orleans, Louisiana, just across North Rampart Street from the French Quarter. It was created in the 1970s and 1980s on land in a historically significant African-American neighborhood, and is named in honor of the New Orleans-born jazz musician Louis Armstrong.

Features

The park contains the New Orleans Municipal Auditorium, the Mahalia Jackson Theater of the Performing Arts, and several buildings of the New Orleans Jazz National Historical Park. At its heart is Congo Square, the ground where enslaved Africans were permitted to gather on Sundays in the 18th and 19th centuries to make music and dance — a site widely regarded as the birthplace of the African-American musical tradition that gave rise to jazz.

A 12-foot statue of Louis Armstrong by sculptor Elizabeth Catlett stands in the park, along with sculptures of other figures of New Orleans music, including Sidney Bechet, Buddy Bolden and Mahalia Jackson.

Events

The park hosts concerts and festivals, including events associated with the French Quarter Festival, and serves as an assembly point — the "back o' town" starting place — for second line parades organized by the city's social aid and pleasure clubs.
