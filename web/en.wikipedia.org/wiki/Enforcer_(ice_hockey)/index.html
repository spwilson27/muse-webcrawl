Enforcer (ice hockey)

An enforcer is an unofficial role in ice hockey. The term is sometimes used synonymously with "fighter", "tough guy" or "goon". Enforcers are players whose role is to discourage and manage opposing violence, respond to dirty plays, and protect their team's star players by fighting, or threatening to fight, opponents.

Fighting is an accepted, though penalised, part of the enforcer's role.

Role

Under the unwritten rules known as "the Code", an enforcer answers for fouls committed against teammates by challenging the offender to a fight. Fighting is technically penalised — usually with a five-minute major penalty — but is tolerated in North American professional leagues such as the NHL, unlike most other team sports where it leads to ejection.

Decline

The role has declined sharply since the 1990s as leagues emphasised speed and skill, roster spots became more valuable, and concerns grew about head injuries and the health of former fighters. Players such as Dave "Tiger" Williams, Bob Probert and Tie Domi are among the best-known enforcers in NHL history.
