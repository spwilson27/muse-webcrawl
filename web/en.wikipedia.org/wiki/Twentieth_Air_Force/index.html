Twentieth Air Force

The Twentieth Air Force is a numbered air force of the United States Air Force within Air Force Global Strike Command. It was established on 4 April 1944 and operated chiefly in the Pacific Theater of World War II, where its B-29 Superfortress bombers conducted the strategic bombing campaign against Japan, culminating in the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Since the Cold War, the Twentieth Air Force has been responsible for the Air Force's intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) force; its headquarters are at Francis E. Warren Air Force Base, Wyoming.

World War II

The Twentieth was created under the direct control of General Henry H. "Hap" Arnold, commanding general of the US Army Air Forces, an unusual arrangement reflecting the importance placed on the new B-29. Its XX Bomber Command, based first in India and then forward in China, began raids on Japan in June 1944 under Operation Matterhorn, hampered by the enormous logistical burden of supplying bases over "the Hump" of the Himalayas. The XXI Bomber Command, led from November 1944 by Major General Curtis LeMay from late January 1945, operated from the Marianas (Saipan, Tinian, and Guam) and, after switching to low-level night incendiary tactics in March 1945, devastated Japanese cities; the firebombing of Tokyo on 9–10 March 1945 was the most destructive air raid in history. The Twentieth's 509th Composite Group dropped the atomic bombs on Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August 1945).

Postwar and the missile era

After the war the Twentieth served in the Pacific and in Korea before being inactivated in 1955. It was reactivated in 1991 under Strategic Air Command to take charge of all US ICBM forces, and today it commands the three missile wings operating the LGM-30G Minuteman III force — the 90th Missile Wing at F. E. Warren AFB, the 91st at Minot AFB, and the 341st at Malmstrom AFB — along with the 377th Air Base Wing and associated helicopter and support units.
