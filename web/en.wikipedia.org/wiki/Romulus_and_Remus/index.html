Romulus and Remus are twin brothers in Roman mythology whose story recounts the founding of the city of Rome. Sons of the god Mars and the Vestal Virgin Rhea Silvia, daughter of the deposed king Numitor of Alba Longa, they were ordered drowned in the Tiber by the usurper Amulius but survived, washed ashore beneath a fig tree where a she-wolf (lupa) suckled them. The image of the Capitoline Wolf with the twins became the emblem of Rome.

The founding

Raised by the shepherd Faustulus, the twins grew up to restore Numitor, then set out to found their own city. They quarrelled over its site — Romulus favouring the Palatine Hill, Remus the Aventine — and over the interpretation of omens (augury by birds). In the ensuing dispute Remus was killed, in some versions by Romulus himself after Remus mockingly leapt over Romulus's new city wall. Romulus founded Rome, traditionally on 21 April 753 BC, and became its first king.

Legacy

Romulus was later deified as the god Quirinus. The twins' story, told by Livy, Plutarch and Dionysius of Halicarnassus, encoded Roman themes of fraternal conflict and martial destiny, and remained central to Roman identity for over a millennium.
