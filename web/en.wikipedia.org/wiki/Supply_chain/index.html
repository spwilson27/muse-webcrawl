Supply chain

[Image: a vast automated warehouse with shelves of goods and forklift trucks]

A supply chain is the network of organisations, people, activities, information and resources involved in creating a product and delivering it to the final consumer — from raw-material extraction through component manufacture, assembly, warehousing and distribution to retail sale. Supply chain management is the discipline of designing and coordinating that network.

Design and disruption

Modern supply chains are global and tiered: a smartphone may combine minerals from Africa, chips from Taiwan, assembly in China or Vietnam and software from the US, co-ordinated by multinational firms and logistics specialists. The dominant late-20th-century philosophy was lean 'just-in-time' production (from Toyota) minimising inventory; its fragility was exposed by the 2011 Japanese earthquake and tsunami, the COVID-19 pandemic (2020–22), the 2021 Suez Canal blockage and chip shortages, driving a shift toward resilience, dual sourcing and 'friend-shoring'. Supply chains are also where much world trade now happens — in intermediate goods crossing borders repeatedly — and a focus of policy on labour standards, emissions and security.
