The Ottoman dynasty, known in Turkish as the House of Osman (Osmanoğlu), ruled the Ottoman Empire from its founding around 1299 until the abolition of the sultanate in 1922 — one of the longest continuous dynasties in European and Islamic history. Its members were sultans of the empire and, from the early 16th century, also claimed the title of caliph of Islam.

Origins

The dynasty traces its descent from Osman I, a Turkish tribal leader (bey) in north-western Anatolia whose small principality, centred on Söğüt, grew at the expense of the declining Byzantine Empire. Ottoman dynastic tradition later supplied an elaborate genealogy linking Osman to the Kayı tribe of the Oghuz Turks and cultivated foundation legends such as "Osman's Dream," in which a tree growing from the founder's navel shaded the whole world.

Succession

The dynasty never adopted primogeniture. In the classical period sons of the sultan governed provinces and fought for the throne on his death; the victorious prince commonly executed his brothers, a practice of royal fratricide defended by Mehmed II on grounds of public order. From the early 17th century fratricide gave way to the kafes ("cage"), in which princes were confined in the palace, and succession passed by agnatic seniority — to the eldest male dynast. Because the dynasty was always reckoned patrilineal, with mothers of sultans often powerful figures as valide sultans, the era of female influence in the 16th–17th centuries is known as the Sultanate of Women.

End of the dynasty

The last reigning sultan, Mehmed VI, was deposed when the Grand National Assembly of Turkey abolished the sultanate on 1 November 1922; his cousin Abdülmecid II was elected caliph but held no temporal power. When the caliphate too was abolished on 3 March 1924, all members of the dynasty were exiled from Turkey. Female dynasts were permitted to return in 1952 and male members in 1974. Heads of the house in exile included Osman Bayezid Osmanoğlu (d. 2017); after the death of Dündar Ali Osman Osmanoğlu in 2021, the headship passed to Harun Osman Osmanoğlu.
