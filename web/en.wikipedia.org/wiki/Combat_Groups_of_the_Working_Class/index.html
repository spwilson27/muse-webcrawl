Combat Groups of the Working Class

The Combat Groups of the Working Class (German: Kampfgruppen der Arbeiterklasse, KdA) was a paramilitary organization in the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) from 1953 to 1989, made up of volunteer party members drawn from workplaces. It was subordinate to the Central Committee of the ruling Socialist Unity Party (SED).

History

The KdA was founded in the aftermath of the uprising of 17 June 1953, when Soviet troops were required to suppress protests by East German workers. The regime concluded it needed a loyal armed force of workers to defend factories and state institutions against internal unrest. Units were organized by workplace, trained by the People's Police and the National People's Army, and equipped with small arms and light weapons.

In August 1961, Kampfgruppen members participated in securing the sector border during the construction of the Berlin Wall. A famous propaganda photograph showed KdA members at the Brandenburg Gate.

Organization and strength

At their peak, the Combat Groups numbered around 200,000 members organized into "hundreds" (Hundertschaften) and battalions, both local factory units and motorized, more heavily armed formations intended for wartime deployment alongside the NVA. Members trained after work and on weekends, swearing an oath to defend the socialist order.

Dissolution

During the Peaceful Revolution of 1989 the KdA was stood down without being deployed against demonstrators. The organization was formally dissolved in December 1989, and its weapons were collected by the police and army.
