The Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta, or California Delta, is a vast inland river delta and estuary in Northern California formed by the confluence of the Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers just west of Stockton, where their waters flow westward into Suisun Bay and on to San Francisco Bay.

Geography

The Delta covers about 1,153 square miles and consists of some 57 reclaimed islands and tracts surrounded by more than 1,100 miles of levees and waterways. Most of the land lies below sea level and is used for highly productive agriculture.

Water supply

The Delta is the hub of California's water system: the State Water Project and the federal Central Valley Project pump water from the southern Delta into aqueducts supplying farms in the San Joaquin Valley and cities as far south as Los Angeles and San Diego, serving some 27 million people.

Ecology

The Delta supports hundreds of species, including the endangered Delta smelt and Chinook salmon runs. It faces threats from land subsidence, ageing levees, invasive species and increasing salinity.
