Zeno's paradoxes

Author: Zeno of Elea (c. 490–430 BC)

Known through: Aristotle's Physics

Subject: Motion, plurality, infinity

Zeno's paradoxes are a set of philosophical arguments devised by the Greek philosopher Zeno of Elea to support the doctrine of his teacher Parmenides that motion and plurality are illusions. They are known chiefly through the accounts and attempted refutations of Aristotle.

The paradoxes of motion

The most famous are the Dichotomy, in which a traveller must first complete half the journey, then half the remainder, and so on infinitely; Achilles and the tortoise, in which the swift Achilles can never overtake a tortoise given a head start, because he must always first reach where the tortoise has just been; the Arrow, which argues a flying arrow is motionless at every instant; and the Stadium.

Resolutions

Modern treatments resolve the paradoxes using the mathematics of convergent infinite series and the calculus of limits: an infinite sequence of ever-shorter intervals can sum to a finite time and distance.
