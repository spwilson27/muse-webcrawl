The Yongzheng Emperor (1678–1735), personal name Yinzhen, was the fourth emperor of the Qing dynasty and the third Qing emperor to rule over China proper, reigning from 1722 to 1735. The fourth son of the Kangxi Emperor, he was a hardworking, austere and often ruthless ruler whose fiscal and administrative reforms filled the imperial treasury and laid the foundations for the prosperity of his son the Qianlong Emperor's long reign.

Contested succession

Kangxi's final years were poisoned by rivalry among his many sons (the "Nine Lords' War for succession"). When the emperor died in December 1722, the commander of the Beijing gendarmerie, Longkodo, announced that he had named Yinzhen as heir; rumours that Yinzhen forged the will or murdered his father dogged his reign and still colour his popular image. He imprisoned or exiled rival brothers, notably the eighth prince Yinsi and the fourteenth prince Yinti.

Reforms

Yongzheng created the Grand Council (Junjichu), a small privy body that became the empire's effective centre of government, and systematised the secret palace-memorial channel through which trusted officials reported directly to him — he is famous for reading and annotating memorials in vermilion ink late into the night. His "tanding rumu" reform merged the poll tax into the land tax, simplifying taxation and curbing evasion, while the "huohao guigong" reform legalised and centralised local surcharges to pay officials honestly ("silver to nourish incorruptibility"). Ruthless anti-corruption drives raised treasury reserves from under 10 million to some 60 million taels.

Death

Yongzheng died suddenly in 1735, aged 56, and was buried in the Western Qing Tombs. Legend attributes his death to assassination by Lü Siniang, daughter of a persecuted scholar — a story historians regard as folklore; his consumption of Taoist elixirs of immortality, containing lead and mercury, is a more plausible contributor. He was succeeded by his fourth son Hongli, the Qianlong Emperor, whose name he had deposited in a sealed box behind the "Zhengda Guangming" tablet under his own system of secret succession.
