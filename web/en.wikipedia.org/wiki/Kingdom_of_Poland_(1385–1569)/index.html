Kingdom of Poland (1385–1569)

Years: 1385 – 1569

Capital: Kraków

Dynasty: Jagiellonian

Succeeded by: Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth

The Kingdom of Poland of 1385–1569, also called Jagiellonian Poland, was the Polish monarchy from the Union of Krewo (1385) to the Union of Lublin (1569). Under the Krewo settlement, Grand Duke Jogaila of Lithuania married Queen Jadwiga of Poland, was baptised and crowned as Władysław II Jagiełło, uniting the two states in a personal union and beginning the Jagiellonian dynasty.

Wars and expansion

The united Polish–Lithuanian forces crushed the Teutonic Order at the Battle of Grunwald (1410), one of the largest battles of medieval Europe. After the Thirteen Years' War, the Second Peace of Thorn (1466) gave Poland Royal Prussia, including Gdańsk and Toruń, and Warmia, leaving the Order's remaining Prussian territory a Polish fief. In 1525 the last Grand Master, Albrecht of Hohenzollern, secularised the Order's state as the Duchy of Prussia and paid homage to Sigismund I in Kraków.

Golden Age

The reigns of Sigismund I the Old and Sigismund II Augustus saw a cultural golden age: the Renaissance court at Wawel, the astronomy of Nicolaus Copernicus, and the growth of the szlachta's "Golden Liberty", with the Sejm gaining decisive power (Nihil novi, 1505). The Union of Lublin (1569) converted the personal union into the real union of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, closing this period of Polish history.
