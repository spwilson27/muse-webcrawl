Severe thunderstorm watch

A severe thunderstorm watch is a weather watch issued by national weather forecasting agencies — in the United States, the Storm Prediction Center (SPC) of the National Weather Service — when atmospheric conditions are favourable for the development of severe thunderstorms over a defined area during a defined period, typically several hours.

Definition

In the United States, a severe thunderstorm is one that produces winds of at least 58 mph (93 km/h), hail of at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) in diameter, or a tornado. A watch means that conditions are favourable for such storms to develop; it does not mean severe weather is already occurring. A severe thunderstorm warning, by contrast, is issued when severe weather is observed or indicated by radar for a specific smaller area.

Particularly Dangerous Situation

In rare cases the SPC issues a watch designated a Particularly Dangerous Situation (PDS) severe thunderstorm watch, usually when widespread wind damage ("derecho" events) or very large hail is expected. Most PDS watches are tornado watches, but PDS severe thunderstorm watches are issued for expected widespread damaging winds.

Issuance and form

Watches are usually issued as rough parallelograms covering parts of one or more states, often described in terms of counties included, a central axis, and valid times. They are numbered sequentially through the year. Local Weather Forecast Offices may add or remove counties as the threat evolves, and watches are cancelled when the threat ends.
