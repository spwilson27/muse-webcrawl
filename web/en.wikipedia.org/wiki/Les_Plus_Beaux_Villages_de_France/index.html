Les Plus Beaux Villages de France ("The Most Beautiful Villages of France") is an independent association of French villages recognized for the quality of their heritage, architecture, and setting. Founded in 1982 by Charles Ceyrac, then mayor of Collonges-la-Rouge, the association promotes tourism and helps preserve the character of small rural communes, which must have a population of fewer than 2,000 inhabitants.

Selection criteria

Villages applying for the label are evaluated against a charter of roughly thirty criteria, including the presence of at least two protected sites or monuments, a policy for preserving and enhancing built heritage, control of signage and commercial development, upkeep of public spaces, and limits on disfiguring construction. Candidate villages are inspected by the association's quality commission, and membership is reviewed periodically; villages that fail to maintain standards can lose the label.

Members and influence

Member villages are distributed across most French regions and include such noted places as Collonges-la-Rouge, Gordes, Roussillon, Saint-Cirq-Lapopie, Conques, Èze, Montrésor, and Yvoire. The label has become a significant driver of tourism—members report substantial increases in visitor numbers—and inspired similar associations abroad, including "The Most Beautiful Villages in the World", a federation uniting the French association with counterparts in Italy (I Borghi più belli d'Italia), Wallonia, Quebec, Japan, and Spain.

Criticism

The association has periodically faced criticism that the label intensifies tourist pressure on fragile villages, raises property prices, and favors picturesque over living communities; the association responds that its controls on development are designed precisely to keep the villages inhabited and intact.
