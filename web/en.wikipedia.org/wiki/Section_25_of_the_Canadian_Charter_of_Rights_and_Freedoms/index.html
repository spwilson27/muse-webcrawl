Section 25 of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms

Section 25
Part of the Constitution Act, 1982
Part ofCanadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms
Enacted1982
SubjectAboriginal, treaty and other rights of Indigenous peoples

Section 25 of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms is a provision of the Constitution of Canada that protects the rights of Aboriginal peoples in Canada from being abrogated or derogated by other Charter guarantees. It forms part of the "general" provisions of the Charter and functions as a shield for Aboriginal, treaty, and related rights, particularly against claims that collective Indigenous rights violate the individual equality rights guaranteed by section 15.

Text

The section provides:

The guarantee in this Charter of certain rights and freedoms shall not be construed so as to abrogate or derogate from any aboriginal, treaty or other rights or freedoms that pertain to the aboriginal peoples of Canada including (a) any rights or freedoms that have been recognized by the Royal Proclamation of October 7, 1763; and (b) any rights or freedoms that now exist by way of land claims agreements or may be so acquired.

Purpose and interpretation

Section 25 was included because Indigenous leaders feared that the Charter's individual rights — especially equality rights that took effect in 1985 — could be used to attack the special collective rights of Aboriginal peoples, such as those under the Indian Act or in treaties, and the sex-based status rules that some Indigenous women had themselves challenged. Scholars debate whether section 25 is merely an interpretive clause or a substantive shield that can render a conflicting Charter right inapplicable. The Supreme Court of Canada addressed it in R v. Kapp (2008), where a communal fishing licence program for Aboriginal fishers was protected against an equality challenge, and discussed its scope again in Dickson v. Vuntut Gwitchin First Nation (2024), concerning a residency requirement for elected council members. Related constitutional protection for Aboriginal and treaty rights is found in section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982, which lies outside the Charter.
