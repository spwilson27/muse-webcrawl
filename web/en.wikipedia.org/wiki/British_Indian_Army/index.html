British Indian Army

The British Indian Army, commonly referred to as the Indian Army, was the main military force of British India before its dissolution in 1947. It was responsible for the defence of British India and the Indian princely states, which could also have their own armies. Raised originally from the sepoy forces of the East India Company, it became a Crown force after 1858 and served across the world in both World Wars, reaching about 2.5 million men in 1945 — the largest volunteer army in history.

Origins

The East India Company maintained presidency armies for Bengal, Madras and Bombay, officered by Britons and manned largely by Indian sepoys. After the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the Company's forces were transferred to the Crown in 1858, artillery was reserved to European units, and recruitment shifted toward so-called "martial races" — Sikhs, Punjabi Muslims, Pathans, Dogras, Rajputs and Gurkhas recruited from Nepal.

Organisation

In 1895 the presidency armies were merged into a single Indian Army of four commands. Lord Kitchener's reforms (1902–09) reorganised it into divisions deployable in the field. British officers held the King's commission; Indian officers long served as Viceroy's commissioned officers (jemadar, subedar) subordinate to the most junior British officer, until Indianisation of the officer corps began after 1917.

The World Wars

About 1.5 million Indian soldiers served in the First World War, in France, Gallipoli, Mesopotamia, Egypt and East Africa; more than 70,000 died. In the Second World War the army expanded to some 2.5 million men, fighting in North and East Africa, Italy and — most famously — Burma, where the Fourteenth Army under General Slim defeated the Japanese. Indian soldiers won 31 Victoria Crosses in the Second World War.

Partition and legacy

At the partition of India in 1947 the army was divided between the Dominion of India and the Dominion of Pakistan, roughly two-thirds to India and one-third to Pakistan. Its regimental traditions, uniforms and organisation live on in the modern Indian Army and Pakistan Army.
