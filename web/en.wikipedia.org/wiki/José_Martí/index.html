José Martí

[Image placeholder: portrait of José Martí]

José Julián Martí Pérez (28 January 1853 – 19 May 1895) was a Cuban national hero, poet, essayist, journalist, translator, professor, and publisher. He is considered a major figure in Latin American literature and the apostle of Cuban independence from Spain. Through his writings and political activity he became a symbol of Cuba's struggle for freedom and a leading voice of the Spanish American Modernismo literary movement.

Early life and imprisonment

Born in Havana to Spanish parents, Martí was politically active from adolescence. At sixteen he founded a newspaper supporting Cuban independence during the Ten Years' War; he was arrested, sentenced to hard labour, and eventually deported to Spain, where he studied law and philosophy. His essay El presidio político en Cuba (1871) denounced the cruelty of Spanish colonial prisons.

Exile and writing

Martí lived much of his adult life in exile, in Mexico, Guatemala, Venezuela, and above all New York City, where he spent fourteen years writing chronicles for Latin American newspapers, teaching, and organising the independence movement. His poetry collections Ismaelillo (1882) and Versos sencillos (1891) are landmarks of Spanish-language poetry; verses from the latter were later adapted into the song "Guantanamera". His essays, including "Nuestra América" (1891), articulated a vision of a united, independent Latin America wary of both Spanish colonialism and United States expansionism.

War and death

In 1892 Martí founded the Cuban Revolutionary Party to unite exile groups behind a new war of independence. He returned to Cuba in April 1895, and was killed on 19 May 1895 in his first battle, at Dos Ríos, fighting Spanish troops. He is honoured in Cuba as a national hero, and José Martí International Airport in Havana and the José Martí Memorial in Revolution Square bear his name.
