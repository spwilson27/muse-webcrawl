Hong Kong dollar

The Hong Kong dollar (Chinese: 港元; symbol: HK$; ISO code: HKD) is the official currency of Hong Kong. It is divided into 100 cents. Under the Linked Exchange Rate System in place since 1983, it is pegged to the United States dollar at approximately HK$7.80 to US$1, within a convertible band of 7.75–7.85.

Issuance

Uniquely, banknotes are issued by three commercial banks — HSBC, Standard Chartered Bank (Hong Kong) and Bank of China (Hong Kong) — under the supervision of the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), which also issues coins and the HK$10 note. Note designs differ by issuing bank.

History

The dollar was adopted in the 19th century, initially alongside silver trade dollars. Hong Kong left the silver standard in 1935 and floated after 1972 before the 1983 peg was introduced amid uncertainty over the territory's future. The peg has survived the Asian financial crisis of 1997–98 and remains a cornerstone of Hong Kong's financial system.

Denominations

Coins circulate in 10, 20 and 50 cents and $1, $2, $5 and $10; notes in $10, $20, $50, $100, $500 and $1,000. The Hong Kong dollar is among the most traded currencies in the world.
