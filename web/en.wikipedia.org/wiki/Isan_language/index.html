Isan language

Isan
Native to: Thailand (Isan), Laos border regions
Speakers: approx. 22 million (2013)
Family: Kra–Dai → Tai → Southwestern → Lao–Isan
Writing system: Thai script; Tai Noi script (historical)
ISO 639-3: tts

Isan (Thai: ภาษาอีสาน) is the principal language of the Isan region of northeastern Thailand. It is a dialect of the Lao language, classified by most linguists as a variety of Lao rather than of Thai, though it is written with the Thai script and influenced by standard Thai through education and media. Isan has roughly 22 million speakers and a rich oral and musical tradition, including mor lam sung poetry.

Classification and history

Isan belongs to the Southwestern Tai language group. Its separation from the Lao spoken across the Mekong River is largely political: the Isan region was part of the Lao kingdom of Lan Xang before coming under Siamese control, and the division hardened after the Franco-Siamese treaties of 1893–1904 fixed the Mekong as the boundary between Siam and French Indochina. "Thaification" policies in the 20th century promoted standard Thai and discouraged the written use of Lao.

Features

Like Lao, Isan is a tonal language with five to seven tones varying by locality. It shares much vocabulary with Lao but has absorbed many loans and calques from standard Thai. Traditionally it was written in the Tai Noi (old Lao) script for religious texts, but today the Thai alphabet is used informally, since Isan has no official written standard.

Status

Isan is primarily a spoken vernacular used in the home and village; education, government and most media in the region use standard Thai. It features prominently in popular luk thung and mor lam music enjoyed throughout Thailand.
