Kubo formula

The Kubo formula, named for Ryogo Kubo, is an equation in quantum statistical mechanics which expresses the linear response of an observable to a time-dependent perturbation in terms of equilibrium correlation functions of the unperturbed system. It is a central result of linear response theory and underlies microscopic derivations of transport coefficients such as electrical and thermal conductivity.

Statement

Consider a system with Hamiltonian H0 subjected to a perturbation H′(t) = −A F(t), where F(t) is an external force coupled to an operator A. To first order in the perturbation, the change in the expectation value of an observable B is given by a convolution of F with a response function φBA(t − t′), which is proportional to the canonical correlation of the Heisenberg-picture operators Ȧ and B evaluated in the equilibrium ensemble of H0. In particular, the generalized susceptibility is the Fourier transform of this response function.

Applications

The Kubo formula provides first-principles expressions for conductivities, viscosities, diffusion constants and spin susceptibilities. Applied to an electric field perturbation, it yields the Kubo formula for the conductivity tensor in terms of current–current correlation functions, forming the basis of the Kubo–Greenwood approach used in computational materials physics. The formalism is closely related to the fluctuation–dissipation theorem, which relates the dissipative part of the response to equilibrium fluctuations.

History

Kubo presented the general theory in a 1957 paper, "Statistical-mechanical theory of irreversible processes. I. General theory and simple applications to magnetic and conduction problems", in the Journal of the Physical Society of Japan. Analogous formulas for specific transport coefficients had earlier been developed by Melville S. Green and by Herbert B. Callen and Theodore A. Welton in connection with the fluctuation–dissipation theorem.
