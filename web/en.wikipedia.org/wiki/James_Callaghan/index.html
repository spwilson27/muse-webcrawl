James Callaghan

Leonard James Callaghan, Baron Callaghan of Cardiff (27 March 1912 – 26 March 2005), often known as Jim Callaghan, was a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1976 to 1979 and Leader of the Labour Party from 1976 to 1980. He is the only person to have held all four Great Offices of State: Chancellor of the Exchequer (1964–1967), Home Secretary (1967–1970), Foreign Secretary (1974–1976) and Prime Minister.

Early career

Born in Portsmouth, Callaghan left school at 17, worked as a tax clerk, became a trade-union official, and served in the Royal Navy during the Second World War. He entered Parliament in 1945 as MP for Cardiff South, later representing Cardiff South East.

In government, 1964–1970

As Chancellor under Harold Wilson, Callaghan raised taxes to defend the pound but was forced into the 1967 devaluation, after which he became Home Secretary. There he handled the beginning of the Troubles in Northern Ireland, sending in troops in 1969, and piloted the Race Relations Act 1968.

Prime Minister, 1976–1979

Callaghan succeeded Wilson in April 1976. His government negotiated the 1976 IMF loan, pursued pay restraint with the trade unions under the "Social Contract", and oversaw devolution referendums in Scotland and Wales. Inflation fell, but the breakdown of pay policy in the "Winter of Discontent" of 1978–79, a wave of strikes amid severe weather, destroyed the government's standing. Callaghan lost a vote of no confidence by one vote in March 1979 — the first such defeat since 1924 — and Labour lost the ensuing general election to Margaret Thatcher's Conservatives.

The remark "Crisis? What Crisis?", a newspaper headline paraphrase of Callaghan's demeanour on returning from a summit in Guadeloupe in January 1979, became emblematic of his fall, though he never uttered the words.

Later life

Callaghan resigned as Labour leader in 1980, became Father of the House of Commons, and entered the House of Lords in 1987 as Baron Callaghan of Cardiff. He died in 2005, one day short of his 93rd birthday, being then the United Kingdom's longest-lived former prime minister.
