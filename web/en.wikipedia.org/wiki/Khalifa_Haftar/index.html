Khalifa Belqasim Haftar (born 7 November 1943) is a Libyan-American military officer and the de facto leader of much of eastern and southern Libya. He commands the Libyan National Army (LNA), a coalition of military units and militias, and has been the central figure of the eastern-based administration rivaling the internationally recognized government in Tripoli since the Second Libyan Civil War began in 2014.

Early career and exile

Haftar was among the young officers who helped Muammar Gaddafi seize power in 1969. He rose to senior command but was captured by Chadian forces in 1987 during the Toyota War, and Gaddafi disavowed the prisoners. Haftar broke with the regime, accepted American assistance, and eventually settled in Virginia, where he lived for roughly two decades and acquired U.S. citizenship, becoming a figure in the exiled National Front for the Salvation of Libya.

Return and civil war

Haftar returned to Libya during the 2011 revolution that overthrew Gaddafi. In February 2014 he announced the suspension of the elected parliament, and in May launched Operation Dignity against Islamist militias in Benghazi. Appointed commander of the armed forces by the Tobruk-based House of Representatives in 2015 and promoted to field marshal, he captured Benghazi by 2017 and took the oil crescent ports and Derna. In April 2019 he launched an offensive on Tripoli that stalled after Turkey intervened in support of the Tripoli government; a ceasefire followed in October 2020. His forces have been backed at various times by Egypt, the United Arab Emirates, Russia (including Wagner Group mercenaries), and France, while his political role and any presidential bid remain contested within Libya's stalled unification process.
