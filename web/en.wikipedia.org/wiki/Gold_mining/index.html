Gold mining

Gold mining is the extraction of gold resources by mining. Historically, gold was obtained from alluvial deposits through simple methods such as panning, but modern mining relies on large-scale open-pit and underground operations, heap leaching, and chemical processing of low-grade ores.

History

Gold has been mined since prehistoric times; the oldest known gold artifacts date to the fifth millennium BC. Ancient Egyptians mined gold in Nubia and the Eastern Desert, and the Romans used hydraulic mining techniques such as hushing on a large scale in Spain. The nineteenth century saw a series of gold rushes — in California (1848), Australia (1851), South Africa's Witwatersrand (1886), and the Klondike (1896) — that transformed economies and drove mass migration.

Methods

Placer mining recovers gold from stream and beach sediments using gravity separation, sluice boxes, and dredges. Hard-rock (lode) mining extracts gold from veins in solid rock, either in open pits or underground shafts. Ore is crushed and the gold recovered by gravity concentration, flotation, or cyanidation — the MacArthur–Forrest cyanide process, introduced in 1887, made low-grade ores economical and remains dominant. Heap leaching percolates cyanide solution through stacked ore to dissolve the gold.

Production

The largest gold-producing countries in recent decades have included China, Australia, Russia, Canada, and the United States. The Witwatersrand Basin in South Africa has produced roughly a third of all gold ever mined. Major mining companies include Newmont, Barrick Gold, and AngloGold Ashanti.

Environmental and social issues

Gold mining can cause deforestation, water contamination from cyanide and acid mine drainage, and mercury pollution from artisanal and small-scale mining, which employs millions of people worldwide. Tailings dam failures have caused serious disasters. Certification schemes and responsible-mining standards seek to reduce these impacts.
