Voivodeship


Voivodeships of Poland

A voivodeship (Polish: województwo) is the highest-level administrative division in Poland, corresponding to a province in many other countries. The term derives from wojewoda (voivode), historically a military commander and later a royal governor of such a territory.

Modern Poland

Since the administrative reforms of 1999, Poland has been divided into 16 voivodeships, each governed by an elected regional assembly (sejmik), an executive board headed by a marshal, and a centrally appointed voivode representing the national government. They include Masovian, the largest by population, and Opole, the smallest.

Historical usage

Voivodeships existed in the Kingdom of Poland and the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, and the term was revived in the Second Polish Republic. Analogous titles were used historically in Serbia, Wallachia, Moldavia and Transylvania for provinces governed by a voivode.
