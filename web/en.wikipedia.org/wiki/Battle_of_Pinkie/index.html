Battle of Pinkie

The Battle of Pinkie, also known as the Battle of Pinkie Cleugh, was fought on 10 September 1547 on the banks of the River Esk near Musselburgh, Scotland. An English army under Edward Seymour, Duke of Somerset, defeated a larger Scottish army led by James Hamilton, Earl of Arran. It was the last pitched battle between the kingdoms of England and Scotland before the Union of the Crowns, and the first in Britain in which combined naval artillery, field artillery, arquebusiers, and cavalry were used in coordination — sometimes called the first modern battle in the British Isles. The day was remembered in Scotland as "Black Saturday".

Background

The battle formed part of the Rough Wooing: Henry VIII's, and after his death Somerset's, attempt to force a marriage between the infant Mary, Queen of Scots and Edward VI, and to break the Auld Alliance between Scotland and France.

The battle

The Scots held a strong position across the Esk but abandoned it to advance against the English, misreading a cavalry manoeuvre as a retreat. Under fire from English ships offshore, field guns, and archers and arquebusiers, and struck by cavalry, the Scottish pikemen were routed with heavy losses in the pursuit toward Edinburgh.

Aftermath

Though a crushing tactical victory, Pinkie failed strategically: the Scots refused the marriage and instead sent Mary to France in 1548 to marry the Dauphin, and English garrisons in Scotland proved unsustainable. The war ended with the Treaty of Norham in 1551.
