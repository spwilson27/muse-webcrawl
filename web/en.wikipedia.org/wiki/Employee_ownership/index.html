Employee ownership

Employee ownership is a form of ownership in which a company's employees hold shares in the business they work for, either directly, through a trust, or through a co-operative structure. Employee-owned companies operate in many industries, from retail and manufacturing to professional services, and range from small firms to large chains such as the John Lewis Partnership in the United Kingdom and Publix Super Markets in the United States.

Forms

The main models include direct ownership, in which employees hold shares individually; indirect ownership through an employee ownership trust, which holds shares collectively on behalf of all employees; and worker co-operatives, in which the workforce owns and democratically controls the enterprise, usually on a one-member-one-vote basis. In the United States the dominant legal vehicle is the employee stock ownership plan (ESOP), a tax-qualified retirement plan that invests primarily in the employer's stock, promoted from the 1970s onward by the lawyer and banker Louis O. Kelso.

Evidence and arguments

Research reviews generally find that employee-owned firms show modest advantages in productivity, employment stability, and survival rates, particularly when ownership is combined with participative management practices. Advocates argue that ownership aligns incentives, spreads capital wealth more broadly, and anchors businesses in their communities. Critics note the concentration of retirement savings in a single asset, the valuation and financing difficulties of buy-outs, and the possibility that employee owners may be risk-averse about investment decisions.

History

Early examples grew out of the co-operative movement of the nineteenth century. In Britain, John Spedan Lewis transferred his department store business to a trust for employees in 1929, creating the John Lewis Partnership. In the United States, ESOPs grew rapidly after the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 gave them a statutory basis; by the twenty-first century there were several thousand plans covering more than ten million employees. The United Kingdom introduced the employee ownership trust model with tax incentives in 2014, after which conversions of private companies to employee ownership increased sharply.
