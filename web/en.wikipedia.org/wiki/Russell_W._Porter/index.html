Russell W. Porter

Russell W. Porter
Born:13 December 1871, Springfield, Vermont
Died:22 February 1949, Pasadena, California
Known for:Amateur telescope making; Palomar Observatory design

Russell Williams Porter (December 13, 1871 – February 22, 1949) was an American artist, engineer, amateur telescope maker, and explorer. He was a founder of the Stellafane Observatory in Springfield, Vermont, and is considered a pioneer of amateur telescope making. Porter was also the principal designer behind the 200-inch Hale Telescope at Palomar Observatory, producing celebrated cutaway drawings of the major instruments of Mount Wilson and Palomar observatories.

Early life and Arctic exploration

Porter trained as an architect and artist at MIT. He participated in several Arctic expeditions, including Robert Peary's expeditions to Greenland and the ill-fated Ziegler expeditions to Franz Josef Land, during which he produced extensive artwork of polar landscapes.

Telescope making

After returning to Vermont, Porter developed innovative designs of reflecting telescopes, building his "Garden Telescopes" and founding, with James Hartness, the Amateur Telescope Makers of Springfield. Their gathering site, Stellafane (1926), became the center of the amateur telescope making movement in the United States, and Porter's serial articles in Scientific American, later collected in the Amateur Telescope Making books, popularised the craft.

Palomar

In 1928 George Ellery Hale recruited Porter to California, where he contributed designs and detailed drawings for the 200-inch Hale Telescope and its mountings at Palomar Observatory, completed shortly before his death.
