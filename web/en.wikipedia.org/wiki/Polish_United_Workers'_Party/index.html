The Polish United Workers' Party (Polish: Polska Zjednoczona Partia Robotnicza, PZPR) was the communist party that governed the Polish People's Republic from its founding in 1948 until the fall of communism in 1989. Formed by the forced merger of the Soviet-backed Polish Workers' Party with the rump Polish Socialist Party, it held a monopoly of power, controlling the government, the military, and the nomenklatura system of appointments.

The former Central Committee building ("White House") in Warsaw.

History

Under Bolesław Bierut the party imposed Stalinist terror; after 1956 Władysław Gomułka's "Polish October" promised a national road to socialism but hardened into stagnation and the antisemitic campaign of 1968. Edward Gierek (1970–1980) borrowed heavily from the West to fund consumption, ending in debt and the strikes that birthed Solidarity. General Wojciech Jaruzelski imposed martial law in December 1981. Round Table talks with Solidarity in 1989 led to semi-free elections the party lost decisively; in January 1990 the PZPR voted to dissolve itself.

Organisation

At its peak the party claimed around 3 million members. Power was concentrated in the Politburo and the First Secretary, with the Central Committee apparatus overseeing every institution of the state. Its youth wing was the Union of Polish Youth (later ZSMP), and its press organ was Trybuna Ludu.
