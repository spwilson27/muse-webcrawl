Constitutional history of Pakistan

The constitutional history of Pakistan covers the development of governing frameworks from independence in 1947 to the present. Pakistan adopted three constitutions — in 1956, 1962 and 1973 — interspersed with periods of martial law in which constitutional government was suspended or abrogated.

Interim period (1947–1956)

At independence, Pakistan was governed under the Government of India Act 1935, as adapted by the Indian Independence Act 1947, with the Constituent Assembly serving both as legislature and constitution-making body. The Objectives Resolution, moved by Prime Minister Liaquat Ali Khan and adopted in 1949, laid down guiding principles. Disputes over representation between East and West Pakistan, the language question, and the dismissal of Prime Minister Khawaja Nazimuddin by Governor-General Ghulam Muhammad in 1953 — and of the Constituent Assembly itself in 1954 — delayed constitution-making for nine years.

Constitution of 1956

The first constitution, promulgated on 23 March 1956, made Pakistan an Islamic republic with a parliamentary system and a unicameral legislature, with parity between the eastern and western wings. It survived only until 7 October 1958, when President Iskander Mirza abrogated it and imposed martial law; he was himself deposed weeks later by Ayub Khan.

Constitution of 1962

Ayub Khan's constitution, promulgated on 1 March 1962, established a presidential system with an indirectly elected president and a system of "Basic Democracies". Ayub resigned amid mass protests in 1969, handing power to Yahya Khan, who abrogated the constitution. The Legal Framework Order 1970 provided for the first general elections, whose outcome — an Awami League majority based in East Pakistan — was not honoured, leading to the Bangladesh Liberation War and the independence of Bangladesh in 1971.

Constitution of 1973

The Constitution of 1973, enacted under Zulfikar Ali Bhutto and effective 14 August 1973, remains Pakistan's constitution. It establishes a federal parliamentary system with a bicameral legislature (the National Assembly and Senate), Islam as the state religion, and fundamental rights enforceable by the courts. It was suspended during the martial law of Zia-ul-Haq (1977–1985), who amended it extensively through the Eighth Amendment, and again held in abeyance under Pervez Musharraf after 1999. The Eighteenth Amendment (2010) restored much of the original parliamentary design, devolved powers to the provinces, and renamed the North-West Frontier Province as Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
