Imperial election

An imperial election was the process by which the ruler of the Holy Roman Empire — formally styled King of the Romans before coronation as emperor — was chosen by the prince-electors. Although the Empire was never a purely hereditary monarchy, from the 15th century the Habsburg dynasty secured the succession almost continuously, and elections became largely confirmatory, with candidates frequently elected as King of the Romans during their father's lifetime (vivente imperatore).

The prince-electors as fixed by the Golden Bull of 1356.

The electors

The Golden Bull of 1356, issued by Emperor Charles IV, definitively fixed the electoral college at seven: the archbishops of Mainz, Cologne, and Trier; the King of Bohemia; the Count Palatine of the Rhine; the Duke of Saxony; and the Margrave of Brandenburg. Later changes added Bavaria (1623/1648) and Hanover (1692), and the number fluctuated in the Empire's final century. Elections were usually held at Frankfurt am Main, and coronation as king traditionally took place at Aachen before moving to Frankfurt from 1562.

Procedure

On an emperor's death (or to elect a successor in his lifetime), the Archbishop of Mainz, as archchancellor of Germany, summoned the electors to Frankfurt within a set period. After oaths, Mass, and deliberation in the Wahlkapelle of St. Bartholomew's Cathedral, the electors voted in order of rank; a majority decided. The chosen candidate swore to an electoral capitulation (Wahlkapitulation) — a negotiated charter limiting imperial power and guaranteeing the electors' privileges — before being proclaimed. Historically, coronation by the Pope in Rome made a King of the Romans into Emperor; after Charles V (crowned 1530), emperors simply took the title "Elected Roman Emperor" without papal coronation.

Bribery and politics

Elections could be hard-fought and fabulously expensive. The election of 1519, contested between Charles of Spain and Francis I of France, involved enormous loans from the Fugger and other banking houses to buy electors' votes, and is estimated to have cost over a million florins. The last imperial election chose Francis II in 1792; the Empire was dissolved in 1806 amid the Napoleonic Wars.
