Sanghamitta

Sanghamitta (also Sanghamitra) was a Buddhist nun in the 3rd century BCE, the eldest daughter of Emperor Ashoka and sister of Mahinda. She is credited with bringing a sapling of the Bodhi tree to Sri Lanka and establishing the bhikkhuni (nuns') order there.

Sanghamitta
Flourished: 3rd century BCE
Father: Ashoka
Brother: Mahinda
Known for: Bringing the Jaya Sri Maha Bodhi sapling to Sri Lanka

Mission to Sri Lanka

After her brother Mahinda converted King Devanampiya Tissa, Queen Anula and other women wished to be ordained. Sanghamitta travelled by ship to Anuradhapura carrying a branch of the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, planted as the Jaya Sri Maha Bodhi, one of the oldest living human-planted trees with a recorded planting date (288 BCE in tradition).

Legacy

Her arrival is commemorated in Sri Lanka on Sanghamitta Day, on the full moon of Unduvap (December). She founded convents and is revered as a founder of the Sri Lankan bhikkhuni lineage.
