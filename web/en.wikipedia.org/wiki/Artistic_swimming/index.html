Artistic swimming

Highest governing bodyWorld Aquatics
FormerlySynchronised swimming
OlympicSince 1984
CharacteristicsWater sport combining swimming, dance and gymnastics

Artistic swimming (known before 2017 as synchronised swimming) is a hybrid form of swimming, dance and gymnastics in which swimmers perform a synchronised routine of elaborate moves in the water, accompanied by music. Performers — solo, in duets, or in teams — combine strength, endurance, flexibility, grace and precise timing, often while holding their breath upside down under water.

History

The sport developed from early-20th-century "water ballet" and was popularised in the United States by Esther Williams's film performances. It became an Olympic sport at the Los Angeles Games of 1984 (as synchronised swimming, with solo and duet events). Governing body FINA renamed it artistic swimming in 2017 — a change met with resistance from many athletes and federations — and FINA itself became World Aquatics in 2023. Men were admitted to the team event at the Paris 2024 Olympics for the first time.

Competition

Events include solo, duet, mixed duet and team technical and free routines, judged on execution, artistic impression and difficulty. Strong national programmes have historically included Russia, China, the United States, Canada, Spain, Japan and Ukraine.
