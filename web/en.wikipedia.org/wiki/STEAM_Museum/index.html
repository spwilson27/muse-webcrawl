STEAM – Museum of the Great Western Railway, usually called the STEAM Museum, is a museum in Swindon, Wiltshire, England, telling the story of the Great Western Railway (GWR) and of the railway works that once dominated the town. It opened in June 2000 in a restored Grade II listed former engineering building of Swindon Works, next to the McArthur Glen Designer Outlet, which occupies other surviving works buildings.

Displays

The museum follows the story of the GWR from Isambard Kingdom Brunel's broad-gauge railway of the 1830s–40s through the conversion to standard gauge, the great locomotive works at Swindon (once one of the largest railway engineering complexes in the world), and the lives of the workers and communities who built and ran the railway. Interactive exhibits recreate a locomotive erecting shop, a foundry, the works' time office and a GWR signal box.

Star exhibits include locomotives such as Caerphilly Castle (Castle class 4-6-0 No. 4073, built at Swindon in 1923), the City class No. 3717 City of Truro (long celebrated for a claimed 102.3 mph run in 1904, an early world speed record for steam traction), a replica broad-gauge locomotive, and historic carriages including a royal saloon. The museum also hosts changing exhibitions and events, and its stores hold a large archive of GWR photographs, drawings and ephemera.
