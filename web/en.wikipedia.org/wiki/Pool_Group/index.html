Pool Group

An offshore support vessel of the kind operated by pool arrangements.

The Pool Group refers to arrangements in the shipping industry in which shipowners place their vessels into a jointly managed commercial pool. Under such an arrangement, a group of similar vessels — for example offshore support vessels, tankers or bulk carriers — is marketed and operated as a single fleet, with earnings shared among the participants according to an agreed formula, often weighted by each vessel's characteristics and the number of days it contributes to the pool.

Purpose

Shipping pools are formed to improve vessel utilisation, spread commercial risk across a larger fleet, offer charterers greater capacity and flexibility, and achieve economies of scale in marketing, chartering and operations. Pools are common in the offshore support vessel sector serving the oil and gas industry, as well as in tanker and dry bulk trades.

Operation

A pool is typically run by a pool manager or administrator, which may be one of the participating owners or an independent company. The manager negotiates contracts and charters for the whole fleet, schedules vessels, and distributes net revenue to members after deducting operating and administrative costs. Membership rules govern entry, withdrawal, and the technical standards vessels must meet.

Competition concerns

Because pools coordinate the commercial activity of otherwise competing owners, they have periodically attracted the attention of competition authorities, notably in the European Union, which has examined whether particular pools restrict competition in concentrated markets.
