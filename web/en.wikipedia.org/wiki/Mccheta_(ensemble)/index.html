Mccheta (ensemble)

Mccheta
Type: Folk dance and song ensemble
Origin: Mtskheta, Georgia
Named after: Mtskheta (Georgian: მცხეთა), the ancient capital of Georgia

Mccheta is a Georgian folk song and dance ensemble named after Mtskheta, the ancient capital and religious centre of Georgia near Tbilisi. Like the country's celebrated state ensembles, Mccheta performs the traditional music and choreography of Georgia's regions, preserving a repertoire rooted in centuries-old polyphonic singing and virtuoso dance.

Repertoire

The ensemble's programmes typically include dances from across Georgia: the martial Khorumi war dance of Adjara, the leaping Kazbeguri of the high Caucasus, the graceful women's dance Narnari, and the competitive mountain dances performed on the balls of the feet in soft leather boots. Men's dances are marked by jumps, spins, toe work and daggers, while women's movement is gliding and demure, the dancers appearing to float in long dresses. Musical accompaniment is provided by traditional instruments such as the panduri and chonguri (lutes), the doli drum, and the accordion-like garmoni, alongside three-part polyphonic singing of the kind inscribed by UNESCO on its list of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Context

Georgia's folk ensembles — among them the famous Sukhishvili Georgian National Ballet and the Rustavi Ensemble — have served since the Soviet era as ambassadors of Georgian culture, and regional ensembles such as Mccheta carry the same tradition at festival and concert performances at home and abroad, appearing in national costume (including the men's chokha) and performing repertoire collected from village singers and dancers.
