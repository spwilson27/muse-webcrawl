Ahmose I (Ancient Egyptian: jꜥḥ-ms, "born of Iah (the Moon)") was a pharaoh of ancient Egypt and the founder of the Eighteenth Dynasty. A member of the Theban royal house, he was the son of Seqenenre Tao and the brother of Kamose, the last rulers of the Seventeenth Dynasty. Ahmose completed the expulsion of the Hyksos rulers from the Nile Delta, reunified Upper and Lower Egypt, and thereby inaugurated the New Kingdom, the era of Egypt's greatest imperial power.

Early life and accession

Ahmose was a child when his father Seqenenre Tao died, apparently violently, in the war against the Hyksos, and still young when his brother Kamose died after a short reign. His mother, Queen Ahhotep I, likely acted as regent during his minority. During this period the kingdom was divided: the Hyksos ruled the north from Avaris, Nubian rulers held the south, and the Theban dynasty controlled the middle.

Expulsion of the Hyksos

On reaching adulthood, Ahmose resumed the war. He besieged and captured Avaris, the Hyksos capital, driving its population eastward, then pursued them to Sharuhen in southern Canaan, which fell after a siege of several years. Contemporary records include the tomb biography of the soldier Ahmose, son of Ebana, and the account on the Tempest Stele, which describes a catastrophic storm during his reign. With the Delta recovered, Ahmose re-established Egyptian administration throughout the country and campaigned in Nubia to secure the southern frontier.

Reign and legacy

Ahmose reorganized the bureaucracy, reopened quarries and trade routes, and began large building projects, including work at Karnak and at Abydos, where he built a pyramid complex — the last royal pyramid constructed in Egypt. His wife, Ahmose-Nefertari, was highly honored and later deified together with their son Amenhotep I. Ahmose reigned about 25 years. His mummy was discovered in the royal cache at Deir el-Bahari in 1881. Later Egyptians regarded him as a second founder of the kingdom, comparable to Narmer and Mentuhotep II.
