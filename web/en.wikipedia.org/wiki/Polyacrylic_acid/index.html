Polyacrylic acid

Poly(acrylic acid) (PAA; trade name Carbomer) is a polymer with the formula (CH2-CHCO2H)n. It is a derivative of acrylic acid (CH2=CHCO2H). In addition to the homopolymers, a variety of copolymers and crosslinked polymers, and partially deprotonated derivatives thereof, are known and of commercial value. In aqueous solution at neutral pH, PAA is an anionic polymer, i.e., many of the side chains of PAA lose their protons and acquire a negative charge. Partially or wholly deprotonated PAAs are polyelectrolytes, with the ability to absorb and retain water and swell to many times their original volume.

Production and properties

Polyacrylic acid is produced by free-radical polymerisation of acrylic acid, usually in aqueous solution. It is a weak polyacid: in water, the carboxylic acid groups ionise progressively as pH rises, causing the polymer coils to expand through electrostatic repulsion. Crosslinked polyacrylic acid (carbomer) forms highly viscous gels at low concentrations.

Applications

The applications of PAA reflect its ability to absorb water and to modify rheology. It is used in disposable diapers (as part of superabsorbent materials, usually in the form of sodium polyacrylate), ion exchange resins, adhesives, detergents, and as a thickening, dispersing, suspending, and emulsifying agent in pharmaceuticals, cosmetics (gels, creams), and paints.
