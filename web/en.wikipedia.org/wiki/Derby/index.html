Derby is a city and unitary authority area in Derbyshire, in the East Midlands of England. It lies on the banks of the River Derwent in the south of the county, historically within the Hundred of Morleston and Litchurch. Derby was traditionally a county town and gained city status in 1977 as part of the Silver Jubilee celebrations of Elizabeth II.

History

The Roman settlement of Derventio stood at nearby Little Chester. The modern name derives from the Old Norse Djúra-bý, "village of the deer", after Danish settlement in the Danelaw. Derby later became a major centre of the silk industry — its silk mill of 1721, now the Museum of Making, is regarded as one of the first factories — and of railway engineering; Derby Works and the Railway Technical Centre made it a heart of the British railway industry.

Economy

Derby is a centre of advanced transport manufacturing. Rolls-Royce, whose aero-engine division is based in the city, is the largest private-sector employer; Toyota Manufacturing UK has its car plant at nearby Burnaston, and train builder Alstom (formerly Bombardier) maintains the Litchurch Lane works. The University of Derby and Derby Cathedral — notable for its 16th-century tower and the tomb of Bess of Hardwick — are prominent institutions.

Sport

Derby County Football Club, founder members of the Football League in 1888, play at Pride Park Stadium. The city also hosts Derbyshire County Cricket Club.
