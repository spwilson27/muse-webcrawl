The 1958 Asian Games, officially the Third Asian Games, were a multi-sport event held in Tokyo, Japan, from May 24 to June 1, 1958. A total of 1,820 athletes from 20 Asian National Olympic Committees competed in 112 events in 13 sports. The Games were the first to feature a torch relay: the flame was lit in Manila, the previous host city, and carried to Tokyo, where the main stadium flame was lit by Japanese athlete Mikio Oda, the first Asian Olympic gold medallist.

Highlights

New sports added at Tokyo included hockey, table tennis, tennis, and volleyball. Host nation Japan dominated the medal table for the third consecutive Games, winning 67 gold medals and 164 medals in total, far ahead of the Philippines and South Korea. Japanese swimmer Tsuyoshi Yamanaka and the debut of world-class Asian swimming were among the notable performances. The Games helped demonstrate Japan's postwar recovery and prepared Tokyo's successful bid to host the 1964 Summer Olympics.

Medal table

Japan finished first with 67 gold, 41 silver, and 30 bronze medals; the Philippines was second with 8 golds, followed by South Korea with 8 golds. Sixteen of the twenty participating nations won at least one medal.
