National Aeronautics and Space Administration

Formed: July 29, 1958
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Primary spaceport: Kennedy Space Center
Motto: "For the Benefit of All"

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) is an independent agency of the United States federal government responsible for the civilian space programme, as well as aeronautics and space research.

History

NASA was created in 1958 in response to the Soviet Union's launch of Sputnik 1, absorbing the earlier National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA). Under President John F. Kennedy's 1961 challenge, NASA's Apollo programme landed the first humans on the Moon in 1969. Subsequent achievements include the Space Shuttle programme (1981–2011), leadership of the International Space Station partnership, robotic exploration of Mars, and observatories such as Hubble and the James Webb Space Telescope.

Programmes

Current efforts include the Artemis programme to return astronauts to the Moon, science missions across the solar system, Earth observation, and aeronautics research. NASA works with commercial partners such as SpaceX and Boeing for crew and cargo transport to low Earth orbit, and operates centres including Johnson Space Center, the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and Goddard Space Flight Center.
