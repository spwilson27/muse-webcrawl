The Soka Gakkai (Japanese: 創価学会, "Value-Creation Society") is a Japanese Buddhist religious movement based on the teachings of the 13th-century priest Nichiren as taught by its first three presidents: Tsunesaburō Makiguchi, Jōsei Toda, and Daisaku Ikeda. It is one of the largest of Japan's new religious movements, claiming some 8 million member households in Japan, and its international arm, Soka Gakkai International (SGI), founded in 1975, claims members in 192 countries and territories.

Beliefs and practices

Members practise Nichiren Buddhism as lay followers: chanting the mantra Nam-myoho-renge-kyo (called daimoku) before a mandala known as the Gohonzon, reciting portions of the Lotus Sutra (gongyō), studying Nichiren's writings and the presidents' guidance, and engaging in shakubuku — propagation aimed at helping others. Central themes include the "human revolution" of the individual, the creation of value, and the ideal of kōsen-rufu, the widespread propagation of Nichiren's teaching as a basis for peace.

History

Makiguchi, an educator, founded the Sōka Kyōiku Gakkai (Value-Creating Education Society) in 1930. He and Toda were imprisoned during the Second World War for resisting the militarist government's religious controls; Makiguchi died in prison in 1944. Toda rebuilt the organisation after the war, renaming it Soka Gakkai, and it grew rapidly in the 1950s. Ikeda, president from 1960 until 1979 and honorary president thereafter, expanded the movement internationally and founded schools, universities (Soka University in Japan and Soka University of America), cultural institutions, and peace-research bodies.

Politics and controversies

In 1964 members founded the political party Kōmeitō, which has frequently been a coalition partner in Japanese governments, including with the Liberal Democratic Party; the party formally separated from the religious organisation in 1970. The Soka Gakkai's rapid growth, proselytising style, and influence prompted controversy and scholarly debate in Japan, and in 1991 the Nichiren Shōshū priesthood excommunicated the lay organisation, a schism after which the Gakkai continued independently. Ikeda's death in November 2023 closed the era of the founding presidents.
