National symbols of Italy

Emblem of the Italian Republic

The national symbols of Italy are the emblems and symbols that represent the Italian Republic and the Italian people, many of them dating to the Risorgimento, the movement for Italian unification in the 19th century.

Principal symbols

  • Flag: the tricolour (il Tricolore) of green, white and red, first adopted in 1797 by the Cispadane Republic and confirmed as the national flag of the Republic in 1946.
  • Emblem: the Emblem of the Italian Republic, adopted on 5 May 1948, composed of the Star of Italy superimposed on a cogwheel, framed by oak and olive branches and the motto REPVBBLICA ITALIANA.
  • Anthem: "Il Canto degli Italiani" ("The Song of the Italians"), written by Goffredo Mameli in 1847, de facto anthem from 1946 and officially recognised by law in 2017.
  • National colours: green, white and red, together with azure blue (azzurro), the colour of the House of Savoy, retained by Italian national sports teams.
  • Personification: Italia turrita, the turreted female allegory of Italy; the national animal is the Italian wolf, the national bird the Italian sparrow, and the national flower the lily.

Other symbols

The Star of Italy (Stella d'Italia) is one of the oldest national symbols, associated with the country since ancient times. The standard of the President of Italy and the Frecce Tricolori aerobatic display team, whose smoke trails reproduce the tricolour, are also prominent national symbols.
