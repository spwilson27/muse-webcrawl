Marshal General of France

Marshal General of France (French: Maréchal général de France), more fully Marshal General of the King's camps and armies, was a supreme dignity of the French military under the Ancien Régime, ranking above the ordinary Marshals of France and giving its holder command over all the king's armies.

History

The title was created in the 16th century and bestowed only rarely. Holders included the Duke of Lesdiguières, Turenne — created Marshal General in 1660 — the Duke of Villars, Maurice de Saxe, and the Duke of Soubise. Under the Empire, Napoleon awarded comparable distinction through the title of Marshal of the Empire rather than reviving the dignity.

Significance

Because the title conferred precedence over fellow marshals, it was reserved for commanders of exceptional stature, such as Turenne, regarded as one of the greatest generals of the 17th century.
