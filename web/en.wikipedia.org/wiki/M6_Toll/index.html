The M6 Toll, also known as the Birmingham Northern Relief Road, is a 27-mile (43 km) tolled motorway in England, running from junction 4 of the M6 near Coleshill to junction 11a near Cannock. It bypasses Birmingham and the congested West Midlands section of the M6, rejoining the main motorway north of the conurbation.

History

A northern relief road for Birmingham was proposed for decades before the scheme was approved as Britain's first privately financed toll motorway under a 53-year concession. Construction began in 2000 and the road opened on 9 December 2003. It was Britain's first tolled motorway open to general traffic (tolls had previously been confined to bridges and tunnels).

Operation

Tolls are charged by vehicle class, with the main toll plazas at Weeford Park and Great Wyrley and additional tolls at some intermediate junctions. Traffic levels have generally run below the original forecasts, though the road is valued by drivers for its reliability compared with the frequently congested free M6 through Birmingham. It serves Norton Canes services, the only service area on the road.
