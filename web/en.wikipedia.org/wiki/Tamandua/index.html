Tamandua

Tamandua is a genus of anteaters in the family Myrmecophagidae, containing two species: the northern tamandua (Tamandua mexicana) and the southern tamandua (Tamandua tetradactyla). Tamanduas are medium-sized, semi-arboreal anteaters of Central and South America, smaller than the giant anteater and larger than the silky anteater. The word tamandua derives from a Tupi word meaning "ant catcher."

Description

Tamanduas have elongated heads and snouts, small eyes, and a long, sticky tongue that can extend more than 40 cm (16 in) to extract ants and termites. The forefeet bear large curved claws — four toes on the forefeet, the third claw being especially enlarged — used to tear open insect nests and for defence; tamanduas walk on their knuckles to keep the claws sharp. Their coat is typically tan to golden with, in many individuals, a black "vest" pattern across the shoulders and back. A muscular prehensile tail, largely hairless underneath, aids climbing.

Behaviour and ecology

Tamanduas are largely nocturnal and spend much of their time in trees, though they also forage on the ground. They feed almost exclusively on ants and termites, avoiding species with strong chemical defences, and may eat tens of thousands of insects in a day, visiting many nests without destroying them. When threatened, a tamandua rears up on its hind legs and tail in a tripod stance and slashes with its foreclaws; it can also release a powerful skunk-like odour from anal glands. Both species are listed as Least Concern by the IUCN.
