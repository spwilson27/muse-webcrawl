Metropolitan municipality (South Africa)

In South Africa, a metropolitan municipality or Category A municipality is a municipality that executes all the functions of local government for a city or conurbation. This is in contrast to areas composed primarily of rural and smaller urban settlements, which are divided into district municipalities (Category C) and local municipalities (Category B) that share local-government functions.

Structure

The post-apartheid local government system was established by the Municipal Structures Act of 1998 and implemented in the 2000 municipal elections. Metropolitan municipalities are single-tier authorities governed by an elected council, often led by an executive mayor. They are responsible for services including water, electricity reticulation, transport planning, housing and municipal health.

List

South Africa has eight metropolitan municipalities: Buffalo City (East London), City of Cape Town, Ekurhuleni, eThekwini (Durban), City of Johannesburg, Mangaung (Bloemfontein), Nelson Mandela Bay (Gqeberha) and City of Tshwane (Pretoria). The three largest — Johannesburg, Cape Town and eThekwini — are among the country's principal economic centres.
