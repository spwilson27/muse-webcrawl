Battle of Trenton

The Battle of Trenton was a small but pivotal battle of the American Revolutionary War, fought on the morning of 26 December 1776 in Trenton, New Jersey. After crossing the icy Delaware River on Christmas night, General George Washington led the main column of the Continental Army in a surprise attack on the Hessian garrison under Colonel Johann Rall. The victory, following a string of defeats around New York, revived American morale and reinvigorated the revolutionary cause.

Background

After losing New York City in the autumn of 1776, Washington's army retreated across New Jersey into Pennsylvania, its enlistments expiring and morale collapsing. Washington planned a three-pronged attack across the Delaware against the Hessian brigade quartered at Trenton. Only the main force succeeded in crossing on the night of 25–26 December, through a driving nor'easter.

The battle

Washington's columns under Greene and Sullivan converged on Trenton at about 8 a.m., achieving complete surprise. Rall, who had been warned but underestimated the threat, attempted to form his regiments in the streets but was mortally wounded; American artillery under Henry Knox dominated the town's main streets. After about an hour, the Hessians surrendered. Roughly 900 were captured; American losses were minimal — famously, only a handful of wounded and deaths from exposure, though two soldiers froze to death on the march.

Aftermath

The victory at Trenton, followed days later by success at Princeton, reversed the momentum of the war in the north and persuaded many soldiers to re-enlist. Emanuel Leutze's 1851 painting Washington Crossing the Delaware made the battle an icon of American national mythology.
