Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo


Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo
Born: c. 1499, Iberian Peninsula
Died: 3 January 1543, Santa Catalina Island, Alta California
Occupation: Explorer, navigator
Known for: First European to explore the coast of present-day California

Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo (Portuguese: João Rodrigues Cabrilho; c. 1499 – 3 January 1543) was an Iberian explorer in service of the Spanish Empire, best known for leading the first European expedition to explore the coast of what is now California in the United States.

Expedition of 1542–1543

Commissioned by Viceroy Antonio de Mendoza of New Spain, Cabrillo sailed north from Navidad (in modern Jalisco) in June 1542 with two ships, the San Salvador and La Victoria. On 28 September 1542 he entered a sheltered bay he named San Miguel — present-day San Diego Bay — becoming the first European to set foot in Alta California. He continued up the coast, naming capes and islands, reached the area of the Russian River, and wintered in the Channel Islands.

Death and legacy

Cabrillo died on 3 January 1543, reportedly from an infection following an injury sustained in a fall (or in a skirmish) on Santa Catalina Island; command passed to his pilot, Bartolomé Ferrer. The Cabrillo National Monument at Point Loma in San Diego commemorates his landing, and many places in California bear his name.
