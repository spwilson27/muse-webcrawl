The O'Brien dynasty (Irish: Uí Bhriain) is an Irish clan and royal dynasty founded by Brian Boru (died 1014), the celebrated High King of Ireland who broke the power of the Viking towns at the Battle of Clontarf. Descended from the Dál gCais of north Munster, the O'Briens ruled the Kingdom of Thomond (roughly modern County Clare, with parts of Limerick and Tipperary) for five centuries, intermittently held the kingship of Munster, and produced several High Kings of Ireland in the 11th and 12th centuries.

History

After Brian's death, his descendants — taking the surname Ó Briain, "grandson/descendant of Brian" — contested the high kingship with the kings of Leinster, the Uí Néill, and later the O'Connors of Connacht. Tairrdelbach Ua Briain (Turlough O'Brien, d. 1086) and his son Muirchertach (d. 1119) dominated Ireland in their lifetimes; Muirchertach commissioned important church reforms and is a major figure in the annals. Domnall Mór Ua Briain (d. 1194) was the last O'Brien king of Munster; thereafter the dynasty's power contracted to Thomond, northwest of the Shannon, which survived as a largely independent lordship while the family feuded with the Norman de Clares — the O'Briens' victory at the Battle of Dysert O'Dea in 1318 preserved Thomond's independence for over two centuries.

In 1543 Murrough O'Brien, "the Tanist", submitted to Henry VIII under the policy of surrender and regrant, becoming the first Earl of Thomond. The main line later held the marquessate of Thomond (extinct 1855), while a junior branch, the Barons Inchiquin, survives; the present holder of that title is recognised as the senior representative of the dynasty, styled The O'Brien, Prince of Thomond — one of the few Irish chieftainships acknowledged by the Irish state.
