Four Major Rivers Project

The Four Major Rivers Restoration Project was a multi-purpose green growth project on the Han River, Nakdong River, Geum River and Yeongsan River in South Korea. The project was spearheaded by former South Korean president Lee Myung-bak and was declared complete on 21 October 2011. It was first announced as part of the "Green New Deal" policy launched in January 2009 to increase jobs and revive the economy through green investment.

Scope

The project dredged roughly 570 million cubic metres of sediment, built 16 new weirs (bo) across the four rivers, raised smaller dams, reinforced riverbanks, and created some 1,700 km of bicycle paths, riverside parks and leisure facilities. The government stated its goals as securing water resources against drought, improving water quality, preventing floods, restoring river ecosystems, and creating regional development and tourism opportunities. The total cost was about 22.2 trillion won.

Criticism

The project was controversial from the start. Critics — including environmental groups, opposition parties and many academics — argued that the weirs slowed the rivers into stagnant reservoirs, worsening algal blooms and water quality, damaging wetlands and endangered species' habitats, and that the project's true purpose was to revive Lee's earlier Grand Korean Waterway canal plan. Subsequent governments debated removing or permanently opening the weirs, and official audits criticised aspects of the project's planning and maintenance costs.
