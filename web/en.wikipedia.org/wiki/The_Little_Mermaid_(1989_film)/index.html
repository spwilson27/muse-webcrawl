The Little Mermaid is a 1989 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Feature Animation and released by Buena Vista Pictures. Loosely based on the 1837 Danish fairy tale by Hans Christian Andersen, it tells the story of Ariel, a mermaid princess who dreams of becoming human and falls in love with Prince Eric, making a bargain with the sea witch Ursula to trade her voice for legs. Written and directed by Ron Clements and John Musker, with music by Alan Menken and lyrics by Howard Ashman, the film is credited with launching the period known as the Disney Renaissance.

Plot

Sixteen-year-old Ariel, youngest daughter of King Triton, is fascinated by the human world and collects its artefacts in a secret grotto, watched over by the crab composer Sebastian and her friend Flounder. She rescues Prince Eric from a shipwreck and falls in love with him. After Triton destroys her grotto, Ariel bargains with the sea witch Ursula: her voice in exchange for three days as a human, during which she must receive "true love's kiss" or belong to Ursula forever. Ursula sabotages the romance by disguising herself as the human Vanessa and bewitching Eric with Ariel's voice. At the disrupted wedding Ariel's voice is restored; when the sun sets she becomes Ursula's prisoner, and Triton trades himself for her. In the climax Eric kills the gigantic Ursula by impaling her with a ship's bowsprit. Triton, seeing Ariel's love, transforms her permanently into a human, and she marries Eric.

Music

The songs — "Part of Your World", "Under the Sea", "Poor Unfortunate Souls", "Kiss the Girl" and "Les Poissons" — were structured like a Broadway musical, with Ashman serving as producer and shaping the story. "Under the Sea" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song, and Menken won Best Original Score.

Reception and legacy

The film grossed $235 million worldwide and was praised by critics, reviving the animated feature after a decade of commercial disappointments. It is routinely ranked among the greatest animated films ever made and was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 2022. It spawned a prequel, a sequel, an animated series, a Broadway musical, and a 2023 live-action remake starring Halle Bailey.
