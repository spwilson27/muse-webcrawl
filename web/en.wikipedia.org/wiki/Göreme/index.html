Göreme

Göreme is a town in Nevşehir Province in the Cappadocia region of central Turkey, renowned for its "fairy chimney" rock formations and its early Christian cave churches. It lies within the Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985.

History

The soft volcanic tuff of the region, deposited by ancient eruptions of Mount Erciyes and neighbouring volcanoes, was easily carved, and from the Roman and Byzantine periods inhabitants excavated dwellings, stables, monasteries and churches in the rock. During periods of persecution and invasion, Christian communities expanded these into extensive monastic complexes. The Göreme Open Air Museum preserves a cluster of rock-cut churches — including the Dark Church (Karanlık Kilise), the Apple Church (Elmalı Kilise) and the Snake Church (Yılanlı Kilise) — decorated with vivid Byzantine frescoes dating mainly from the 10th to 12th centuries.

Tourism

Today Göreme is the principal visitor base for Cappadocia. Tourists stay in cave hotels carved into the fairy chimneys, explore nearby valleys such as Rose Valley and Pigeon Valley, visit the underground cities of Derinkuyu and Kaymaklı, and take dawn hot-air balloon flights over the landscape. The town was known in antiquity by names such as Korama and Matiana.
