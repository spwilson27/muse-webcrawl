Department of Atomic Energy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Department of Atomic Energy (DAE) is an Indian government department with headquarters in Mumbai, Maharashtra. It was established on 3 August 1954 under the direct charge of the Prime Minister of India, through a Presidential Order. The DAE is responsible for India's nuclear technology, including nuclear power generation and research, and operates under the Atomic Energy Commission.

The department was created under the leadership of physicist Homi J. Bhabha, the founding director of India's atomic energy programme, who envisioned a three-stage nuclear power programme using India's limited uranium and abundant thorium reserves.

Organisation

The DAE encompasses research centres, industrial organisations and public sector undertakings, including:

  • Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC), Trombay — India's premier nuclear research facility
  • Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research (IGCAR), Kalpakkam — fast reactor research
  • Raja Ramanna Centre for Advanced Technology (RRCAT), Indore — lasers and accelerators
  • Nuclear Power Corporation of India (NPCIL) — operates India's nuclear power stations
  • Uranium Corporation of India and Indian Rare Earths — mining and minerals
  • Variable Energy Cyclotron Centre, Kolkata; Atomic Minerals Directorate, Hyderabad; and aided institutes such as the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research and the Tata Memorial Centre

Work

The DAE's mandate spans nuclear power, research reactors, fuel fabrication and reprocessing, heavy water production, radioisotope applications in medicine, agriculture and industry, and advanced research in physics, chemistry, biology and engineering. India operates a fleet of pressurised heavy-water reactors and is developing fast breeder reactors and thorium-based designs as stages of Bhabha's programme.
