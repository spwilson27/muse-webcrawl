Marriage in the Catholic Church

Marriage in the Catholic Church, also called matrimony, is the "covenant by which a man and a woman establish between themselves a partnership of the whole of life and which is ordered by its nature to the good of the spouses and the procreation and education of offspring", and which "has been raised by Christ the Lord to the dignity of a sacrament between the baptised". Catholic teaching holds that marriage is an indissoluble union, mirroring Christ's union with the Church (Ephesians 5:32).

A nuptial Mass: Catholic marriage is one of the seven sacraments.

Sacramental theology

Matrimony is one of the seven sacraments of the Catholic Church. In the Latin Church, the spouses themselves are considered the ministers of the sacrament, conferring it on each other by their mutual consent; the priest or deacon acts as the Church's official witness and imparts the nuptial blessing. The essential properties of marriage are unity (monogamy) and indissolubility — "what God has joined together, let no one separate" (Mark 10:9).

Requirements and impediments

For a valid marriage, both parties must be free to marry, must exchange consent freely and knowingly, and — if both are Catholic — must normally marry in the canonical form before an authorised minister and two witnesses. Canon law lists diriment impediments that render a marriage invalid, including prior bond, consanguinity, holy orders, and lack of due discretion. Preparation courses ("Pre-Cana") are ordinarily required before the wedding.

Annulment and remarriage

Because a valid sacramental marriage is considered permanent, the Church does not recognise divorce as dissolving the bond. A declaration of nullity (commonly called an annulment) states that, due to some defect present from the beginning, a valid marriage never in fact came into existence. Catholics who divorce and remarry without an annulment are considered, under Church teaching, not free to receive Communion while the new union persists — a discipline debated and addressed in Pope Francis's 2016 exhortation Amoris laetitia.

Natural family planning and openness to life

Catholic teaching, reaffirmed in the 1968 encyclical Humanae vitae, holds that each marital act must remain open to the transmission of life, and therefore excludes artificial contraception, while permitting the use of natural family planning for serious reasons.
