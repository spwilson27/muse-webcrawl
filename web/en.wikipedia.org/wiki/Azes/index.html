Azes

Azes (Greek: Αζης Azēs, Kharoshthi: 𐨀𐨩 A-ya) is the name of two related Indo-Scythian kings who ruled in the northwestern Indian subcontinent during the 1st century BCE. The two — Azes I and Azes II — are known principally from their coins, which continued and developed the bilingual Greek–Kharoshthi coinage of their Indo-Greek and Scythian predecessors.

Azes I

Azes I (c. 48/47 – 25 BCE) was a Saka king who ruled over Gandhara and the Punjab. His accession is significant because he is generally credited with founding the Azes era, an era used in later Indian inscriptions, which began in 47/46 BCE and has been identified by some scholars with the Vikrama era commencing in 57 BCE. His coins depict the king on horseback on the obverse and Greek and Indian deities on the reverse.

Azes II

Azes II (c. 35 – 12 BCE) succeeded Azes I and ruled jointly at times with his sons Azilises and others. His later coinage shows declining silver quality, and his kingdom was eventually absorbed by the rising Indo-Parthian king Gondophares. Some scholars have questioned whether Azes I and II were distinct persons, but the two-king hypothesis remains the mainstream view.

Coinage and legacy

The coinage of Azes is notable for its extensive bilingual legends and for symbols such as the city goddess and Zeus. The Bajaur casket, an early Buddhist reliquary, bears an inscription dated to the reign of a King Azes, providing important evidence for the chronology of early Buddhism in Gandhara.
