Kabsch algorithm

The Kabsch algorithm, named after Wolfgang Kabsch, is a method for calculating the optimal rotation matrix that minimizes the RMSD (root mean square deviation) between two paired sets of points. It is useful in bioinformatics to compare the structures of proteins, in computer graphics, and in any field where two point sets must be aligned as rigidly as possible.

Algorithm

Given two sets of corresponding points, the algorithm first translates both sets so that their centroids coincide with the origin. It then computes the cross-covariance matrix of the two point sets and obtains its singular value decomposition. The optimal rotation is formed from the singular value factors, with a correction step that ensures the result is a proper rotation (determinant +1) rather than a reflection.

Applications

The algorithm is widely used for structural alignment of molecules, superimposing predicted and experimental protein structures, and for registering 3D scans in computer vision. Variants handle weighted points, partial correspondences, and the estimation of a translation and uniform scaling in addition to rotation.
