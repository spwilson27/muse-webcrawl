Englert in 2013.

François Baron Englert (born 6 November 1932) is a Belgian theoretical physicist and 2013 Nobel laureate in Physics. In August 1964, with his close collaborator Robert Brout, he published the first of the three 1964 papers showing how gauge bosons can acquire mass through spontaneous symmetry breaking in the presence of a vacuum field — the Brout–Englert–Higgs mechanism (Higgs mechanism) underlying the Standard Model's account of mass.

Englert and Brout's paper preceded Peter Higgs's by weeks; a third group (Guralnik, Hagen, Kibble) followed. When the Higgs boson was discovered in 2012, Brout had died (2011), and Englert and Higgs shared the 2013 Nobel 'for the theoretical discovery of a mechanism that contributes to our understanding of the origin of mass of subatomic particles'.

Life

Englert was born in Brussels to a Jewish family and survived the Holocaust as a hidden child under an assumed identity. He studied engineering and physics at the Université libre de Bruxelles, where he remained a professor; with Brout and others he also worked on cosmology — including the idea that cosmic inflation might arise from a primordial phase transition — and on statistical physics.
