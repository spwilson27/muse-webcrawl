Step sequencer

A step sequencer is a music sequencer that divides a musical pattern into a fixed number of discrete steps — commonly 16 sixteenth-notes — each of which can be switched on or off and assigned values such as pitch, velocity or gate length. The pattern loops as the sequencer steps through it at a set tempo.

History

Step sequencing appeared in early electronic instruments and modular synthesisers of the 1960s and 1970s, where rows of knobs set the pitch of successive steps. Classic analogue sequencers and the Roland TB-303's pattern sequencer shaped the sound of electronic dance music, and drum machines such as the TR-808 and TR-909 made step programming of rhythms standard.

Modern use

Step sequencers are built into many synthesisers, grooveboxes and digital audio workstations, often with features such as per-step probability, ratcheting, parameter locks and polyrhythmic track lengths. Hardware examples include grid controllers whose pads represent steps in real time.
