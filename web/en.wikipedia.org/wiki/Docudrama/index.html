Docudrama

A docudrama (or documentary drama) is a genre of television and film which features dramatized re-enactments of actual events. It combines the techniques of documentary and drama: actors perform scripted scenes based on documented incidents, often with voice-over narration, archival footage, or interviews anchoring the dramatized material.

Characteristics

Docudramas hew to known facts while filling gaps with plausible invented dialogue and compression of timelines or composite characters. The form rose to prominence on radio and then television: examples include Peter Watkins's The War Game (1965), a dramatized depiction of nuclear attack on Britain; Roots (1977); and modern series such as Chernobyl (2019), which dramatized the 1986 disaster and its aftermath.

Criticism

The genre is frequently criticized for blurring the line between fact and invention. Viewers may remember dramatized scenes as established history, and subjects or their families sometimes object to portrayals. Filmmakers respond that labelling, sourcing, and the broader truth of events justify dramatic licence, a debate that recurs whenever a popular docudrama depicts living people or contested events.

Related forms

Related forms include docufiction, mockumentary, and the biographical film (biopic). Unlike docufiction, which blurs the boundary deliberately, conventional docudrama aims for factual accuracy in structure while dramatizing individual moments.
