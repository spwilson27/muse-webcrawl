Hotchkiss Mle 1914 machine gun

TypeHeavy machine gun
Place of originFrance
In service1914–1960s
Calibre8 mm Lebel
Feed24-round strips / 251-round articulated belt

The Hotchkiss Mle 1914 was the standard heavy machine gun of the French Army during the First World War and remained in service into the Second. It was a gas-operated, air-cooled weapon chambered for the 8 mm Lebel cartridge.

Design

Unlike water-cooled rivals, the Mle 1914 relied on five large cooling rings around its barrel to dissipate heat. It was usually fed from rigid 24-round metal strips, later supplemented by a 251-round articulated belt. The gun weighed about 23.6 kg, the tripod a similar amount.

Service

Some 47,000 were made during the First World War. The type was exported widely, used by the American Expeditionary Forces, and produced under licence in Japan in modified form. French guns remained in use in Indochina after 1945.
