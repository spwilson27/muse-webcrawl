Transition matrix

The term transition matrix is used in several related contexts in mathematics and physics:

Stochastic (Markov) transition matrix

In the theory of Markov chains, a transition matrix (also called a probability matrix or stochastic matrix) is a square matrix whose entry pij gives the probability of moving from state i to state j in one step. Entries are non-negative and each row sums to 1. Powers of the matrix give multi-step transition probabilities: the (i, j) entry of Pn is the probability of reaching state j from state i in n steps, as expressed by the Chapman–Kolmogorov equations. Stationary distributions are left eigenvectors of the matrix with eigenvalue 1.

Change of basis

In linear algebra, a transition matrix (or change-of-basis matrix) expresses the coordinates of vectors in one basis in terms of another. If P is the transition matrix from basis B to basis B′, coordinates transform as [v]B′ = P−1[v]B (with conventions varying by author), and the matrix representations of a linear operator in the two bases are related by similarity.

Quantum mechanics

In scattering theory, the transition matrix or T-matrix relates the initial and final states of a scattering process and is connected to the S-matrix by S = I − 2πi δ(E − E′) T.
