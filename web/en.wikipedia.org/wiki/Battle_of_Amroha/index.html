Battle of Amroha

The Battle of Amroha was fought on 20 December 1305 between the army of the Delhi Sultanate, commanded by Malik Kafur's contemporaries under Sultan Alauddin Khalji, and a Mongol invading force of the Chagatai Khanate. It took place near Amroha in present-day Uttar Pradesh, India, and ended in a decisive Delhi victory.

The engagement near Amroha, 1305.

Background

The Chagatai Mongols had repeatedly invaded the Sultanate's northwestern frontier. In 1305 a large force — contemporary chronicles give numbers in the tens of thousands — under the commanders Ali Beg and Tartaq crossed the Indus and advanced toward Delhi, ravaging the countryside as it came. Alauddin Khalji dispatched an army under his generals to intercept the raiders before they could threaten the capital.

The battle

The armies met near Amroha. After hard fighting the Sultanate's forces routed the Mongols, capturing their commanders Ali Beg and Tartaq along with some thousands of prisoners. The prisoners were taken to Delhi, where many were executed — chroniclers record the grim spectacle of captives being trampled by elephants — and the captured leaders were put to death.

Significance

Coming after Alauddin's earlier victories over the Mongols at Jalandhar (1298) and Kili (1299), Amroha formed part of the sequence of defeats that discouraged further large-scale Chagatai invasions of India for a generation, although raiding continued, most seriously in 1306–1308.
