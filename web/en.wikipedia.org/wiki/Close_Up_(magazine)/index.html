Close Up (magazine)

Published1927–1933
EditorKenneth Macpherson
SubjectFilm theory and criticism

Close Up was a film magazine published from 1927 to 1933, regarded as the first periodical devoted to film as an art form. It was produced by the Pool Group, centred on the writer Bryher, the poet H.D. and the filmmaker Kenneth Macpherson, who edited it.

Content

The magazine championed avant-garde and Soviet cinema, published translations and essays on film theory, and featured contributions by figures such as Sergei Eisenstein, Dorothy Richardson and Gertrude Stein. It dealt seriously with psychoanalysis, race and censorship in relation to cinema.

Legacy

Though it had a small circulation, Close Up is now recognised as a pioneering journal of film criticism, anticipating later academic film studies.
