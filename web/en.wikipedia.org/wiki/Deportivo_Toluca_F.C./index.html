Deportivo Toluca Fútbol Club S.A. de C.V., commonly known as Deportivo Toluca or simply Toluca, is a Mexican professional football club based in Toluca, Mexico State. The club plays in Liga MX, the top division of the Mexican football league system, and is nicknamed Los Diablos Rojos (The Red Devils). Founded in 1917, Toluca is one of the most successful clubs in Mexican football, having won the league championship eleven times, most recently in the Clausura 2025 tournament, as well as the CONCACAF Champions Cup.

History

The club was founded on 12 February 1917 by the brothers Manuel and Francisco Henkel and Román Alday, among others. Toluca rose to the professional Primera División in the 1950s and won its first league titles in 1966–67 and 1967–68 under coach Ignacio Trelles. A third championship followed in 1974–75.

The club's golden era came under coach Enrique Meza at the turn of the century, when a spectacular attacking side built around Paraguayan striker José Saturnino Cardozo won the Verano 1998, Verano 1999 and Verano 2000 titles. Further championships arrived in Apertura 2002, Apertura 2005, Apertura 2008 and Bicentenario 2010. Toluca also won the CONCACAF Champions' Cup in 1968 and 2003 and reached the Copa Libertadores quarter-finals.

After a long title drought, Toluca won its eleventh league championship in the Clausura 2025 season, defeating América in the final.

Stadium

Toluca plays its home matches at the Estadio Nemesio Díez, one of Mexico's oldest stadiums, known for the closeness of its stands to the pitch and nicknamed La Bombonera. The venue, extensively remodelled ahead of the club's centenary, holds around 31,000 spectators.
