The NKVD prisoner massacres were a series of mass executions of political prisoners carried out by the NKVD, the Soviet secret police, across Eastern Europe during the summer of 1941. The killings took place in the opening weeks of Operation Barbarossa, Nazi Germany's invasion of the Soviet Union, as NKVD forces retreating eastward murdered prisoners who could not be evacuated rather than allow them to fall into German hands.

Background

Following the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of 1939, the Soviet Union annexed eastern Poland, the Baltic states, Bessarabia, and parts of Finland. NKVD prisons in these territories filled with political prisoners, nationalists, intellectuals, former officials, and others deemed enemies of the Soviet state. When Germany invaded on 22 June 1941, orders were issued to evacuate prisoners eastward; where evacuation proved impossible, prisoners were to be shot.

The massacres

Mass executions occurred in dozens of locations. Among the largest were the killings in Lviv (Lwów), where several thousand prisoners were murdered in the city's prisons; at Lutsk, where around 2,000–4,000 Ukrainian prisoners were shot; at Dubno, Berezhany, Ternopil, and Stanislaviv in western Ukraine; at Tartu in Estonia; at Rainiai in Lithuania, where prisoners were tortured and murdered; and in Belarus at Vileyka, Hlybokaye, and elsewhere. In many places bodies were left in cells, courtyards, and hastily dug mass graves.

Estimates and aftermath

Estimates of the total death toll vary widely, from around 9,000 documented victims to figures approaching 100,000 when killings in Russia proper and during evacuations are included. When German forces and local populations discovered the massacre sites, the atrocities were heavily exploited by Nazi propaganda to justify the invasion and to incite pogroms against Jewish communities, whom the Nazis falsely blamed collectively for NKVD crimes; this contributed to atrocities such as the Lviv pogroms. The prisoner massacres remain a significant subject in the historical memory of Ukraine, Poland, and the Baltic states.
