The Mingachevir Reservoir (Azerbaijani: Mingəçevir su anbarı) is a large reservoir on the Kura River in northwestern Azerbaijan. It is the largest reservoir in Azerbaijan and the largest body of water in the Caucasus by surface area, covering about 605 square kilometres. The reservoir was created by damming the Kura at the city of Mingachevir, with construction carried out between 1945 and 1953 during the Soviet era.

Uses

The reservoir's water is used for hydroelectric power at the Mingachevir Hydro Power Station, for irrigation of more than a million hectares of arid land in the Kura-Aras lowland, and for regulating the flow of the Kura to prevent flooding. Two major irrigation canals — the Upper Karabakh Canal and the Upper Shirvan Canal — draw water from the reservoir. It also supports an important fishery and supplies drinking water.

History

Archaeological work ahead of the flooding uncovered settlements and burial grounds of great antiquity along the Kura. The catastrophic regional flooding the dam was meant to control had historically devastated farms in the lowland. In June 2020, a failure at the associated power plant contributed to a nationwide blackout in Azerbaijan, underscoring the reservoir complex's national importance.
