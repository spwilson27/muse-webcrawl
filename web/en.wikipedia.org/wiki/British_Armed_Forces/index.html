British Armed Forces

HeadquartersMinistry of Defence, London

Active personnelApproximately 140,000 regulars (plus reserves and Gurkhas)

The British Armed Forces are the military forces of the United Kingdom, charged with the defence of the country, its overseas territories and the Crown dependencies, as well as promoting British interests abroad and supporting international peacekeeping. They consist of three professional services: the Royal Navy (including the Royal Marines), the British Army and the Royal Air Force. Since 2021 the force structure also includes UK Space Command and the cross-service Strategic Command.

Organisation

The monarch is the ceremonial commander-in-chief, while practical authority is exercised by the government through the Ministry of Defence and the Defence Council. The professional head of the armed forces is the Chief of the Defence Staff. The United Kingdom maintains one of the world's largest defence budgets and fields a nuclear deterrent in the form of Trident ballistic-missile submarines operated by the Royal Navy.

History

The armed forces trace their origins to the standing forces created after the Restoration of 1660 and the formation of the Royal Navy in the Tudor period. British forces played a central role in the wars of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, both World Wars, the Korean War, the Falklands War of 1982, the Gulf War, and operations in Iraq and Afghanistan. Conscription ended in 1960, and since then the forces have been all-volunteer.

Deployments and bases

British personnel are deployed worldwide, including in the Sovereign Base Areas of Cyprus, Gibraltar, the Falkland Islands, Brunei, and at RAF and naval facilities abroad. The forces also contribute to NATO commitments, United Nations peacekeeping and humanitarian relief operations. Reserve forces — the Royal Naval Reserve, Army Reserve and Royal Auxiliary Air Force — supplement the regular services, alongside more than 3,000 Gurkhas recruited from Nepal serving in the Brigade of Gurkhas.
