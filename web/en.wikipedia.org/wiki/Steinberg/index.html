Steinberg Media Technologies GmbH is a German music software company based in Hamburg, founded in 1984 by Karl Steinberg and Manfred Rürup. It is best known for Cubase, one of the first and most influential digital audio workstation (DAW) programs, and for developing the VST (Virtual Studio Technology) and ASIO standards that shaped computer-based music production. Since 2005 Steinberg has been a wholly owned subsidiary of Yamaha.

History

The company's first product was Pro-16, a MIDI sequencer for the Commodore 64 (1984). Cubase was released in 1989 for the Atari ST, introducing the arrange-window metaphor of tracks laid out along a timeline that became standard in DAWs. In 1996 Cubase VST added real-time audio recording and effects on consumer computers, and the accompanying VST plug-in format let third-party developers create virtual instruments and processors. The ASIO protocol (Audio Stream Input/Output, 1997) reduced latency for audio interfaces. Steinberg also released the mastering editor WaveLab (1995), the post-production DAW Nuendo (2000), the HALion sampler, and the notation application Dorico (2016), developed by the former Sibelius team in London.

Key products and standards

  • Cubase — flagship DAW, sold in Elements, Artist and Pro editions
  • Nuendo — DAW aimed at film, TV and game audio post-production
  • WaveLab — audio editing and mastering suite
  • Dorico — music notation software
  • VST / VST3 — virtual instrument and effect plug-in standard, widely licensed
  • ASIO — low-latency audio driver protocol
