Grand Paris

Grand Paris ("Greater Paris") is the project for the development and governance of the Paris metropolitan area launched by President Nicolas Sarkozy in 2007–2008. It comprises an administrative reform — the Métropole du Grand Paris, an intermunicipal authority created on 1 January 2016 covering Paris and 130 surrounding communes with about seven million inhabitants — and a vast transport programme, the Grand Paris Express, an automated metro network of some 200 km and 68 stations (lines 15, 16, 17, 18 and extensions of 11 and 14) intended to link the suburbs to each other rather than only to the centre.

Origins and consultation

Sarkozy's 2008 international consultation, "Le Grand Pari(s)", invited ten teams of architects and urbanists — including Jean Nouvel, Richard Rogers and Rem Koolhaas — to reimagine the metropolis, with themes such as the "post-Kyoto city" and reconnecting Paris to the sea along the Seine. The 2010 Grand Paris law created the Société du Grand Paris to build the express network.

Grand Paris Express

The fully automated, mostly underground network is among Europe's largest infrastructure projects, with costs estimated above €35 billion. Line 14's extensions to Orly Airport and Saint-Denis Pleyel opened for the 2024 Olympic Games; the orbital Line 15 and Lines 16–18 are phased through the late 2020s and early 2030s. New stations are intended to anchor development and reduce the historic divide between Paris proper and the banlieues.

Governance

The Métropole du Grand Paris handles planning, housing and environment across its territory, working through twelve territorial public establishments. Debate continues over the metropolitan tier's powers relative to the Île-de-France region and the departments.
