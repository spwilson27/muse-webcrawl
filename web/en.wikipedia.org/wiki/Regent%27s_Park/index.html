Regent's Park

Regent's Park is one of the Royal Parks of London. It lies within north-west London, partly in the City of Westminster and partly in the London Borough of Camden. The park covers 395 acres (160 hectares) and is named after George, the Prince Regent, later King George IV, for whom the architect John Nash designed the surrounding terraces in the early 19th century.

LocationLondon, England
Area395 acres (160 ha)
DesignerJohn Nash
Opened1835 (to the public, partially)

History

The land was originally part of the manor of Tyburn and was taken by Henry VIII for a hunting park, becoming known as Marylebone Park. In 1811 the lease expired and the Prince Regent commissioned Nash to develop the area as an elegant residential district framing a landscaped park. Building was slowed, but the grand stucco terraces around the park — including Cumberland Terrace, Chester Terrace and York Terrace — became hallmarks of Regency architecture.

Features

The park contains the Open Air Theatre, London Zoo, and extensive sports facilities including the Hub. Queen Mary's Gardens, in the Inner Circle, contain around 12,000 roses of some 400 varieties. The park is bordered by Regent's Canal to the north and is close to Primrose Hill.
