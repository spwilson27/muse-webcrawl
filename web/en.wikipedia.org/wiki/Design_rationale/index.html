Design rationale

An issue-based representation of a design rationale

A design rationale is an explicit documentation of the reasons behind decisions made when designing a system or artifact. It records not only what was decided, but why — including the alternatives that were considered, the arguments for and against each, the criteria applied, and the assumptions and constraints that shaped the outcome.

Purpose

Design decisions are frequently revisited: systems are maintained, extended and redesigned by people other than their original authors, often years later. Without a record of the reasoning, maintainers may repeat rejected alternatives, undo decisions whose purpose they do not understand, or fail to appreciate constraints that remain in force. Capturing rationale is intended to support maintenance, reuse, learning, and accountability in design work.

Representation

Several formalisms have been proposed for structuring design rationale. The best known is IBIS (Issue-Based Information System), which represents deliberation as a graph of issues, positions (candidate answers) and arguments. Related schemes include QOC (Questions, Options, Criteria) and DRL (Decision Representation Language). Rationale is also commonly captured less formally in design documents, decision logs, meeting minutes and commit messages.

Challenges

Despite its recognised benefits, rationale capture has seen limited routine adoption. Documenting reasoning takes time during design, the resulting records can be voluminous and hard to navigate, and the value is realised mainly in the future, which weakens the incentive to produce it. Research has therefore explored semi-automatic capture, for example by mining design discussions or reconstructing rationale from version histories.
