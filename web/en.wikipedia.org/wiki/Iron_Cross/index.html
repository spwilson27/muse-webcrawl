Iron Cross

The Iron Cross (German: Eisernes Kreuz) was a military decoration in the Kingdom of Prussia and later in the German Empire (1871–1918) and Nazi Germany (1933–1945). It was established by King Frederick William III of Prussia on 17 March 1813 during the Napoleonic Wars and was awarded for bravery in battle as well as other military contributions.

Design

The decoration is a black cross pattée with silver or gold borders, derived from the symbol of the Teutonic Order. The 1813 issue bore the letters "FW" for Frederick William and a crown; subsequent issues of 1870, 1914 and 1939 bore the year of re-institution and, in the Second World War, a swastika.

Classes

The award existed in several grades: the Iron Cross 2nd Class, 1st Class, and the Grand Cross. Under Nazi Germany the Knight's Cross of the Iron Cross was instituted in 1939 as the highest class, with further grades of Oak Leaves, Swords and Diamonds. After 1945, wearing the decoration in the Federal Republic of Germany was permitted only in a denazified 1957 version.

Legacy

A stylised Iron Cross remains the emblem of the modern Bundeswehr.
