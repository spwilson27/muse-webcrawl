Difference equation

In mathematics, a difference equation (also called a recurrence relation when it defines a sequence recursively) is an equation that relates successive values of a sequence or function at discrete steps. Difference equations are the discrete analogue of differential equations and arise wherever a quantity evolves in steps — interest compounding period by period, population generations, or samples of a signal.

Definition

A first-order difference equation has the form

xn+1 = f(xn)

while a linear k-th order equation with constant coefficients is

xn+k + ak−1xn+k−1 + … + a0xn = bn.

Given enough initial values, the whole sequence can be generated step by step. The Fibonacci sequence, defined by Fn+2 = Fn+1 + Fn, is the classic second-order example.

Solution methods

Linear equations with constant coefficients are solved by the characteristic (auxiliary) equation: substituting the trial solution xn = rn yields a polynomial in r whose roots give the general solution as a combination of powers; repeated roots introduce polynomial factors in n. Particular solutions for standard forcing terms are found by undetermined coefficients or by the method of generating functions, and the Z-transform plays the role for difference equations that the Laplace transform plays for differential equations. Nonlinear equations, such as the logistic map xn+1 = rxn(1 − xn), generally have no closed-form solution and are studied qualitatively; the logistic map famously exhibits period-doubling and chaos.

Applications

Difference equations underpin digital signal processing and control (as the description of discrete-time systems), numerical methods for differential equations, economic dynamics such as the cobweb model, and population models in ecology from Fibonacci's rabbits onward.
