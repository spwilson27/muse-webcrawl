Henry Jenkins III (born June 4, 1958) is an American media scholar and Provost Professor of Communication, Journalism, and Cinematic Arts, a joint professorship at the University of Southern California (USC) Annenberg School for Communication and Journalism and the USC School of Cinematic Arts. Previously, Jenkins served as the Peter de Florez Professor of Humanities and co-founder and co-director of the Comparative Media Studies program at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT).

Jenkins is one of the leading scholars of fan culture and participatory media. His work introduced the notion of "textual poaching", emphasised the participatory nature of fandom, and introduced the concept of transmedia storytelling — the practice of unfolding a narrative across multiple media platforms, each contributing distinctly to the whole — which has been widely adopted in the entertainment industry. He coined the term participatory culture and, in a 2006 white paper for the MacArthur Foundation, defined it and outlined the new media literacies young people need in a networked society.

Career

Jenkins earned his bachelor's degree in journalism from Georgia State University, a master's in communication studies from the University of Iowa, and a PhD in communication arts from the University of Wisconsin–Madison. He joined MIT in 1992, where he founded and directed the Comparative Media Studies master's program and the Education Arcade initiative. In 2009 he moved to USC. Jenkins has testified before the U.S. Senate and the Federal Communications Commission on media violence and new media literacies, and he has championed the cultural legitimacy of fan fiction, video games, and comics through his widely read blog, Confessions of an Aca-Fan.

Key works and ideas

  • Textual Poachers: Television Fans and Participatory Culture (1992) — adapting Michel de Certeau's idea of readers as "poachers", Jenkins argued that media fans are active producers of meaning and culture, not passive consumers, and documented the productivity of Star Trek and other fandoms.
  • Convergence Culture: Where Old and New Media Collide (2006) — a landmark study of media convergence, arguing that the flow of content across platforms is driven as much by consumers' participatory practices as by corporate strategies.
  • Spreadable Media (2013, with Sam Ford and Joshua Green) — analysing how and why audiences share media content, and the value created by circulation.
  • By Any Media Necessary (2016) — on youth civic engagement through participatory politics.

Influence

Jenkins's concepts have shaped both academic media studies and industry practice: transmedia storytelling is the term of art for franchise world-building across film, television, games, and comics; his work on fan activism informs studies of political movements; and his advocacy contributed to scholarly and public recognition of fan culture as a significant cultural force. He continues to research comics, transmedia, and civic media at USC's Civic Paths and Annenberg Innovation Lab research groups.
