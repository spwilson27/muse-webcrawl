Barentu, Eritrea

Barentu
Region: Gash-Barka, Eritrea
Elevation: about 1,030 m
Population: tens of thousands (estimates vary)
Known for: regional capital; market town of the Kunama and Nara peoples

Barentu is a town in northwestern Eritrea and the capital of the Gash-Barka Region, one of the country's most fertile and populous areas. It lies southwest of Asmara, in the western lowlands near the Gash River, and serves as a commercial centre for the predominantly agricultural surrounding districts.

Overview

Barentu is home principally to the Kunama and Nara peoples, two of Eritrea's nine recognized ethnic groups, whose homelands centre on the Gash-Barka lowlands. The town's weekly markets draw traders from across the region, dealing in cattle, sorghum, sesame, and other produce from the fertile Gash plains. It is connected by road to Asmara via Keren and to the Sudanese border town of Teseney.

History

The town grew under Italian colonial administration as a district post in Eritrea's western province. During the Eritrean War of Independence, Gash-Barka was a contested zone, and the region changed hands during the Ethiopian offensives of the late 1970s and the EPLF's subsequent operations. Barentu was heavily affected by the Eritrean–Ethiopian War of 1998–2000: Ethiopian forces occupied the town during the May–June 2000 offensive, causing extensive damage and mass displacement before withdrawing under the Algiers Agreement. Reconstruction followed, and Barentu resumed its role as the regional capital.
