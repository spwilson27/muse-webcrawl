Eurystheus

In Greek mythology, Eurystheus was king of Tiryns, and in some accounts also of Mycenae, in the Argolid. He is best known as the taskmaster who imposed the Twelve Labours on his cousin Heracles, at the command of the goddess Hera.

Labours of Heracles

After Heracles, driven mad by Hera, killed his own family, the Delphic oracle ordered him to serve Eurystheus for twelve years and perform the labours imposed on him — originally ten, increased to twelve when Eurystheus refused to count the Lernaean Hydra and the Augean stables. Frightened by Heracles' strength, Eurystheus reportedly hid in a large bronze jar and forbade Heracles from entering the city, communicating tasks through his herald Copreus.

Death

After Heracles' death, Eurystheus persecuted his children, the Heracleidae, who fled to Athens. In the ensuing war Eurystheus was killed — in Euripides' Heracleidae he is captured and slain despite Athenian objections.
