Battle of Prokhorovka

The Battle of Prokhorovka was fought on 12 July 1943 near Prokhorovka, about 87 kilometres southeast of Kursk in the Soviet Union, during the German offensive Operation Citadel in the Second World War. It was one of the largest tank battles in history: the Soviet 5th Guards Tank Army under Pavel Rotmistrov counterattacked the II SS-Panzer Corps. Although the Soviets suffered far heavier armoured losses, the German advance in the southern sector of the Kursk salient was halted, and Citadel was soon abandoned.

Background

Operation Citadel, launched 5 July 1943, aimed to pinch off the Kursk salient. In the south, the 4th Panzer Army and II SS-Panzer Corps (Leibstandarte, Das Reich and Totenkopf divisions) drove toward Prokhorovka. Alarmed, Stavka released the 5th Guards Tank Army from the Steppe Front reserve; it marched some 350 km to meet the Germans.

The battle

On the morning of 12 July, Rotmistrov's T-34s and T-70s charged across open ground to close the range at which German Tiger and Panther tanks and assault guns held the advantage. Fierce fighting raged around the October State Farm and Hill 252.2, with tanks engaging at point-blank range. Soviet losses were severe — several hundred armoured vehicles — while German losses on 12 July were far lower. Nevertheless the SS corps' advance stalled, and with the Allied landing in Sicily and a Soviet counteroffensive at Orel, Hitler cancelled Citadel on 13 July.

Historiography

Soviet-era accounts inflated the scale and German losses, describing over 1,500 tanks in a single melee and claiming hundreds of German tanks destroyed. Post-Cold War archival research (notably by Zamulin, and Glantz and House) reduced both figures, though the battle remains one of the largest armoured engagements ever. A major memorial and museum stand at the Prokhorovka field today.
