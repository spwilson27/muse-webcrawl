Point of sale

The point of sale (POS), also called the point of purchase, is the time and place at which a retail transaction is completed. At the point of sale the merchant calculates the amount owed, indicates that amount, may prepare an invoice, and offers options for the customer to make payment. The POS is also the point at which a customer makes a payment in exchange for goods or after provision of a service.

POS systems

A modern POS system combines hardware — a terminal or tablet, barcode scanner, receipt printer, cash drawer, and card reader — with software that records sales, processes card and mobile payments, manages inventory, and reports analytics. Early electronic cash registers replaced mechanical tills in the 1970s; the spread of barcode scanning (the first retail barcode scan occurred in 1974) integrated inventory control with checkout. Cloud-based systems, such as those offered by Square, Lightspeed, and Shopify, brought POS functionality to small businesses via tablets and smartphones.

Functions

  • Scanning items and applying prices, discounts, and taxes
  • Accepting cash, payment cards, contactless and mobile wallet payments
  • Updating inventory levels in real time
  • Issuing receipts and processing returns and refunds
  • Employee timekeeping, customer loyalty programs, and sales reporting

Security

Because POS systems handle payment card data, they are a target for malware that scrapes card numbers from memory ("RAM scraping"), which featured in several large retail data breaches. Card networks require POS environments to comply with the PCI Data Security Standard, and the adoption of EMV chip cards and point-to-point encryption has reduced counterfeit fraud at the point of sale.
