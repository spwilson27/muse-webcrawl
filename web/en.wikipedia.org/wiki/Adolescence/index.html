Adolescence

Adolescence
Stage: Transitional, between childhood and adulthood
Approximate ages: 10–19 (WHO), often extended into early 20s

Adolescence is a transitional stage of human physical and psychological development that generally occurs during the period from puberty to adulthood (typically adolescence). It is usually associated with the teenage years, but its physical, psychological, and cultural expressions may begin earlier and end later.

Physical development

Puberty brings growth spurts, development of secondary sex characteristics, and hormonal changes. Brain development continues into the mid-20s, particularly in the prefrontal cortex involved in planning and impulse control.

Psychological and social development

Adolescents develop abstract reasoning, identity, greater independence from parents, and strong peer influence. Erik Erikson described the central task as identity versus role confusion. Social contexts including school, family, and media shape outcomes, and risk behaviours including early substance use and addiction vulnerability are heightened.

Definitions

The World Health Organization defines adolescents as aged 10–19. Some researchers propose adolescence spanning 10–24 in modern societies, and legal thresholds such as the age of consent and majority vary by jurisdiction.
