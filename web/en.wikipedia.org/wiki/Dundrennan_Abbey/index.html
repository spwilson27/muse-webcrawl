Dundrennan Abbey

Dundrennan Abbey, in Dundrennan, Kirkcudbrightshire, Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland, was a Cistercian monastery founded in 1142 by King David I of Scotland, with monks from Rievaulx Abbey in Yorkshire. It is famous as the place where Mary, Queen of Scots spent her last night on Scottish soil in 1568 before crossing the Solway Firth into England.

History

The abbey was colonised from Rievaulx, itself founded from Clairvaux. Its most famous abbot was St Aelred's associate; the community enjoyed royal patronage and extensive lands. After the Scottish Reformation the abbey declined, and its buildings were gradually quarried for stone.

The abbey today

The surviving ruins, notable for their fine late-Romanesque and early Gothic transepts, are in the care of Historic Environment Scotland. The tomb of Alan, Lord of Galloway (died 1234) lies in the north transept. Mary Queen of Scots rode from here to Workington in May 1568 after her defeat at the Battle of Langside.
