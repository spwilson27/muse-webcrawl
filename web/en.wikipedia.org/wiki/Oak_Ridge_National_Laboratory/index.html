Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) is a federally funded research and development centre in Oak Ridge, Tennessee, sponsored by the United States Department of Energy. Established in 1943 as part of the Manhattan Project, it is the largest science and energy national laboratory in the DOE system by surface area and among the largest by budget and staff.

History

The laboratory began as the Clinton Engineer Works' X-10 site, where the X-10 Graphite Reactor — the world's first continuously operated nuclear reactor — produced the first plutonium for study and demonstrated the plutonium production process later scaled up at Hanford. After the war the laboratory diversified into nuclear energy, isotope production, materials science, and basic research under directors including Alvin Weinberg (1955–1973).

Research and facilities

ORNL operates major user facilities: the Spallation Neutron Source (SNS), the world's most powerful pulsed neutron source; the High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR); the Center for Nanophase Materials Sciences; and the Oak Ridge Leadership Computing Facility, home to the Frontier supercomputer, which in 2022 became the first system to break the exascale barrier. Research programmes span neutron science, energy storage and grid technology, nuclear science, biological and environmental systems, advanced manufacturing, and national security.
