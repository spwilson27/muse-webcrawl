Martin Shubik

Martin Shubik (March 24, 1926 – August 22, 2018) was an American economist and one of the founders of modern game theory as applied to economics. He was Seymour Knox Professor of Mathematical Institutional Economics (Emeritus) at Yale University and worked for decades with the RAND Corporation and the Cowles Foundation.

Work

Shubik took his doctorate at Princeton, where he was close to John von Neumann, John Nash, and Lloyd Shapley. With Shapley he developed the Shapley–Shubik power index, a measure of voting power in weighted voting games, and the assignment game model of a market with indivisible goods. His three-volume Game Theory in the Social Sciences and his two-volume The Theory of Money and Financial Institutions set out a programme for modelling whole economies as games with explicit rules, money, and institutions.

Other interests

Shubik also wrote on military strategy, arms races, and the economics of defence, and devised the "dollar auction" — a famous paradoxical game in which rational bidding for a dollar leads both bidders to pay more than a dollar — widely used to illustrate escalation of commitment.
