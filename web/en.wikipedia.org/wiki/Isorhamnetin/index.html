Isorhamnetin

IUPAC name3,5,7-trihydroxy-2-(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)chromen-4-one
FormulaC₁₆H₁₂O₇
Molar mass316.26 g/mol
ClassFlavonol (O-methylated flavonoid)

Isorhamnetin is an O-methylated flavonol, a type of flavonoid found in many plants. It is the 3′-methyl ether of quercetin and a metabolite of both quercetin and tamarixetin in humans.

Occurrence

Isorhamnetin and its glycosides occur in sea buckthorn berries, Ginkgo biloba leaves, pears, onions, almonds and the flowers of Hippophae and other plants; the glycoside narcissin (isorhamnetin 3-O-rutinoside) is a characteristic pigment of prickly pear flowers and safflower.

Research

Like other flavonols, isorhamnetin shows antioxidant activity in vitro and has been investigated in laboratory and animal studies for anti-inflammatory, cardioprotective and anti-cancer effects, though clinical evidence in humans remains limited. It inhibits some cytochrome P450 and conjugating enzymes in vitro, which is of interest for possible food–drug interactions.
