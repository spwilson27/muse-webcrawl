Estonian Song Festival

Native nameLaulupidu
First held1869, Tartu
FrequencyEvery five years
VenueTallinn Song Festival Grounds
ParticipantsTens of thousands of singers

The Estonian Song Festival (Estonian: Laulupidu) is one of the largest choral events in the world, held in Tallinn every five years. It is recognised by UNESCO, together with the corresponding Latvian and Lithuanian festivals, as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.

History

The first nationwide song festival was held in Tartu in the summer of 1869, during the Estonian national awakening, and featured 845 performers. The tradition grew steadily; from 1896 the festivals were held in Tallinn, and since 1960 they have taken place at the purpose-built Tallinn Song Festival Grounds (Lauluväljak), whose great arched stage can accommodate some 15,000 singers at once, facing an audience of up to 100,000.

During the Soviet occupation, the festivals were retained but their programmes were censored. In the late 1980s, mass singing gatherings — including spontaneous singing at the festival grounds — became a peaceful vehicle of national protest, giving the movement for restored independence its name: the Singing Revolution.

The festival today

The modern festival brings together hundreds of choirs and orchestras from Estonia and the Estonian diaspora. Repertoire ranges from folk songs and works by Estonian composers such as Veljo Tormis to contemporary pieces. A torch-lit procession of participants in folk costume through Tallinn traditionally opens the event, and a companion dance festival (Tantsupidu) is held during the same weekend.
