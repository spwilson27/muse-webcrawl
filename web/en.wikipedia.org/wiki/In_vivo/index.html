In vivo

In vivo
Latin"within the living"
DomainBiology, medicine, pharmacology
Contrasts withIn vitro, in silico, ex vivo

In vivo (Latin for "within the living"; often not italicised in English) is the experimentation using a whole, living organism as opposed to a partial or dead organism. Animal studies and clinical trials are major forms of in vivo research, used when the complexity of a living system cannot be reproduced in a dish or a computer model.

Usage

In drug development, candidate compounds typically pass from in vitro assays (in test tubes or cell cultures) to in vivo testing in animals, where absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion and toxicity can be assessed in the context of whole-body physiology, before human trials begin. In vivo imaging techniques — from bioluminescence imaging in mice to MRI and PET in humans — allow processes to be followed in living subjects over time, reducing the number of animals needed.

Related terms

In vitro ("in glass") refers to studies in artificial environments outside an organism; ex vivo to experiments on tissue taken from an organism and studied externally; in situ to observation in the original place; and in silico to computational simulation, a term coined by analogy. Ethical frameworks such as the 3Rs (replacement, reduction, refinement) govern when in vivo animal work is justified.
