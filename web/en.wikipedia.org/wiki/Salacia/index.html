Salacia

Salacia was the Roman goddess of the sea, worshipped as the consort of Neptune, god of the waters. Her name derives from the Latin sal, "salt," and she personified the calm, sunlit surface of the sea — in contrast to Venilia, who represented the waves breaking on the shore. The Greeks identified her with Amphitrite, wife of Poseidon.

Mythology

According to Roman tradition, Neptune desired to marry Salacia, but she fled and hid from him in the depths of the ocean. A dolphin sent by Neptune persuaded her to return and accept the marriage; in gratitude the dolphin was placed among the stars as the constellation Delphinus. Salacia was sometimes depicted driving a chariot drawn by hippocamps (sea horses) alongside Neptune.

Legacy

The name Salacia was given to a large trans-Neptunian object in the Kuiper belt, 120347 Salacia, discovered in 2004, which has a moon named Actaea after a Nereid.

Salacia is also a botanical genus name, and the word appears in zoological nomenclature; these usages are treated in separate articles.
