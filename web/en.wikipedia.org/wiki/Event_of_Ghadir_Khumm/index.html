Event of Ghadir Khumm

Event of Ghadir Khumm
Date18 Dhu al-Hijjah 10 AH (16 March 632 CE)
LocationGhadir Khumm, near al-Juhfa, Arabia
ParticipantsMuhammad and his companions returning from the Farewell Pilgrimage

The Event of Ghadir Khumm (Arabic: واقعة الغدير) was an event in early Islamic history in which the Islamic prophet Muhammad, shortly after his Farewell Sermon and near the end of his life, is reported to have made a declaration about his cousin and son-in-law Ali ibn Abi Talib before a large gathering of pilgrims at a pond (ghadir) called Khumm, on the way back from Mecca to Medina.

The declaration

According to the reports, Muhammad took Ali by the hand and said words to the effect of: "Whoever I am his mawla, this Ali is his mawla" (Arabic: من كنت مولاه فهذا علي مولاه). Many accounts add the prayer: "O God, befriend whoever befriends him and oppose whoever opposes him."

Interpretations

The meaning of the Arabic word mawla is the central point of dispute. In Shia Islam, the event is understood as the explicit designation of Ali as Muhammad's successor, and its anniversary is celebrated as the festival of Eid al-Ghadir. In Sunni Islam, the statement is typically interpreted as an affirmation of Ali's high standing and a call to love and respect him, rather than a formal appointment to leadership. The historicity of the gathering and the saying is broadly accepted, on account of its wide transmission in both traditions, while its interpretation remains contested.
