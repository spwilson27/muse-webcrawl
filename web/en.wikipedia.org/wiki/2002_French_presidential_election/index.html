The 2002 French presidential election was held on 21 April and 5 May 2002. It produced one of the great shocks of modern French politics: Jean-Marie Le Pen, leader of the far-right National Front, edged out Socialist prime minister Lionel Jospin in the first round and advanced to the runoff against incumbent president Jacques Chirac, who then won re-election with the largest share of the vote in the history of the Fifth Republic.

First round

Sixteen candidates stood. Chirac led with 19.88 percent, but Le Pen's 16.86 percent narrowly beat Jospin's 16.18 percent, eliminating the prime minister — an outcome driven by a fragmented left vote spread among numerous candidates, high abstention, and a campaign dominated by law-and-order themes. Jospin announced his retirement from politics that night.

Second round

The result triggered mass demonstrations, including May Day marches of over a million people against the far right. Parties across the spectrum urged a vote for Chirac under the slogan of a "republican front." Chirac refused to debate Le Pen and won the runoff 82.21 to 17.79 percent.

Consequences

Chirac appointed Jean-Pierre Raffarin as prime minister, and the right won the subsequent legislative elections, ending the "cohabitation" between a president and prime minister of opposing camps. The shock of 21 April reshaped French party politics and remains a reference point in discussions of protest voting and far-right support in France.
