Lac du Bourget (Lake Bourget) is a lake in the department of Savoie in eastern France, lying at the southern end of the Jura Mountains. With a surface area of about 44.5 square kilometres and a maximum depth of 145 metres, it is the largest entirely natural lake wholly within France by volume, and one of the deepest. The spa town of Aix-les-Bains stands on its eastern shore, and the city of Chambéry lies a short distance to the south.

Geography

The lake is elongated, running roughly north–south for about 18 kilometres, and is fed principally by the river Leysse. Its outflow, the Canal de Savières, connects it to the Rhône. The western shore is dominated by the steep slopes of the Mont du Chat and the Dent du Chat, while the eastern shore is gentler and more developed.

Ecology

Extensive reed beds at the southern end of the lake form an important wetland habitat for birds and fish; a major restoration programme in the late twentieth century improved water quality and reintroduced species such as the lake trout and the European perch populations. The lake is celebrated in French literature, notably in Alphonse de Lamartine's poem "Le Lac" (1820), inspired by the lake and the memory of Julie Charles.
