Model rocket

A model rocket is a small rocket designed to reach low altitudes — typically a few hundred metres — and be recovered by a variety of means, such as a parachute or streamer. Model rockets are constructed from lightweight materials including paper, wood, plastic, and foam, and are powered by commercially made, single-use or reloadable solid-propellant motors.

History

The hobby became widely accessible in 1957 when Orville Carlisle, a licensed pyrotechnics expert, designed the first model rocket motors intended for safe mass production, and his brother Robert and he marketed them through the Model Missiles company. Vernon Estes founded Estes Industries in 1958, making motors with an automated machine and mass-producing kits; the hobby grew rapidly during the Space Race. The National Association of Rocketry, founded in 1957, publishes a safety code followed by hobbyists in the United States.

Motors and flight

Motors are classified by total impulse with letter codes — A, B, C, and so on, each letter doubling the impulse range of the previous one. A typical flight profile consists of powered ascent, a timed delay with a smoke trail, and an ejection charge that deploys the recovery system. Larger and more powerful rockets fall under high-power rocketry, which is regulated and certified separately.
