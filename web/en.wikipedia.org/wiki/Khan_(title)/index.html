Khan (title)

Khan is a historic title of Inner Asian origin for a ruler or military leader among nomadic and Turkic-Mongolic peoples. It originally denoted the chief of a clan or tribe and later came to mean a sovereign ruler, roughly equivalent to king or emperor in successor states of the Mongol Empire.

Etymology and usage

The title is attested among the Rouran, Göktürks and other steppe peoples; its deeper etymology is debated. A supreme ruler could be styled Khagan ("khan of khans"). Genghis Khan united the Mongol tribes in 1206, and his descendants ruled khanates stretching across Eurasia.

Later use

Khan persisted as a noble and royal title in Central and South Asia — among the Uzbek khanates, the Mughal sphere, Pashtun and Baloch chiefs — and survives today as a common surname, especially among Pashtuns and South Asian Muslims, often without implying descent from rulers.
