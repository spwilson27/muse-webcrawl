Chord voicing

In music theory, voicing is the manner in which the notes of a chord are distributed, doubled and spaced among instruments or voices. Two arrangements of the same chord can sound markedly different depending on their voicing.

Characteristics

Voicing encompasses the ordering of pitches from lowest to highest, the spacing between adjacent notes (open versus close position), the doubling of chord tones, and the assignment of notes to particular instruments or registers. In voice leading, voicings are chosen to connect smoothly between successive chords.

In jazz and arranging

Jazz pianists and arrangers use distinctive voicings such as rootless voicings, drop-2 voicings and quartal voicings, which colour harmony while leaving room for the bass and melody.
