British Rail Class 508

The British Rail Class 508 is a type of electric multiple unit (EMU) passenger train built by British Rail Engineering Limited at York Works between 1979 and 1984. Part of the 1972-design family of suburban EMUs that also includes the Classes 313, 314, 315 and 507, the Class 508 was designed for operation on the third-rail network south of London before most of the fleet transferred to Merseyside.

A Class 508 in Merseyrail service.

History

Forty-three four-car units were built for services from London Waterloo to the south-western suburbs. A distinctive feature when new was their automatic sliding doors — unusual for the time — operated by the guard. The trailing car was later removed, reducing the units to three cars, and from the mid-1990s almost the entire fleet was transferred to Merseyrail to work the Northern and Wirral lines alongside the near-identical Class 507.

Service and withdrawal

The units were refurbished in the 2000s with new seating, lighting and passenger information systems. Merseyrail began replacing them with Stadler Class 777 trains from 2023, and the last Class 508s were withdrawn from passenger service in January 2024, ending over four decades of operation; several units have been preserved or used for departmental purposes.
