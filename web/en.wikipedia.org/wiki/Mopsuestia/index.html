Mopsuestia

Mopsuestia (Ancient Greek: Μοψουεστία, Mopsou(h)estia; Syriac: Maṣīṣtā; Arabic: al-Maṣṣīṣah; Armenian: Msis, Mises, Mamistra; Turkish: Yakapınar) is an ancient city and former bishopric, and a present Latin Catholic titular see, located in Cilicia Campestris (in the Çukurova of modern Turkey) on the Pyramus River (now the Ceyhan River).

The city lies some 20 km (12 mi) east of Adana, at the foot of the Taurus Mountains, on the ancient route from Tarsus to Antioch. Its modern successor settlement is Yakapınar, in the Yüreğir district of Adana Province.

History

The founding of Mopsuestia is attributed in legendary accounts to the seer Mopsus, a figure of Greek mythology who lived before the Trojan War. The city was well located for trade, and flourished under Seleucid rule; Pliny the Elder calls it a free city. Under Roman rule it prospered, and it remained important in the Byzantine period as a frontier city of Cilicia.

Mopsuestia was a significant early Christian centre. Its most famous bishop was Theodore of Mopsuestia (c. 350–428), a leading theologian of the Antiochene school and teacher of Nestorius; Theodore's works were posthumously condemned at the Second Council of Constantinople in 553. The city suffered in the Arab–Byzantine wars, changing hands repeatedly; it was retaken by the Byzantines under Nikephoros II Phokas in 965.

In the Crusader period Mopsuestia (Mamistra) formed part of the Principality of Antioch and later of the Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia. It declined after the Mamluk conquests of the 13th–14th centuries.

Remains

Visible remains include a Roman bridge over the Ceyhan River, stretches of city walls, a Roman-era mosaic pavement (the famous "Samson mosaic" and a Noah's Ark mosaic, discovered in 1955), and the ruins of a basilica. The mosaics are now displayed in the Adana Archaeology Museum.
