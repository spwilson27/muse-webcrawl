Andrey Sheptytsky, OSBM (Ukrainian: Андре́й Шепти́цький; 29 July 1865 – 1 November 1944) was a Ukrainian Greek Catholic archbishop who headed the church from 1901 until his death in 1944. Descended from a polonised noble family, he chose to identify with the Ukrainian people and became one of the most influential Ukrainian figures of the first half of the 20th century.

Life and ministry

Born Roman Aleksander Maria Szeptycki, he entered the Basilian monastery in 1888, taking the name Andrey. After studies in Kraków and elsewhere he was ordained priest in 1892, appointed Bishop of Stanislaviv in 1899, and became Metropolitan Archbishop of Lviv and head of the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church in 1901, an office he held for over four decades under Austro-Hungarian, Russian, Polish, Soviet and German rule.

Sheptytsky founded schools, hospitals and museums, supported Ukrainian cultural institutions, and promoted the study of Eastern Christian spirituality and ecumenism. He was arrested and exiled by the Russian authorities during the First World War.

The Holocaust

During the Nazi occupation of Galicia, Sheptytsky publicly condemned the murder of Jews, issued the pastoral letter "Thou Shalt Not Kill" in 1942, and organised the sheltering of Jews in monasteries and his own residence, saving dozens of lives, including the children of Lviv's rabbi. He also protested the persecution of Poles during the ethnic violence in Volhynia. He died in November 1944, shortly after the Soviet return to Lviv, and was succeeded by Josyf Slipyj. His cause for beatification has been advanced by the Catholic Church, and he was declared Venerable in 2015.
