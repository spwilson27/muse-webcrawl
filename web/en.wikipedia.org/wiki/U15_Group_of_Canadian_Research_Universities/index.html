U15 Group of Canadian Research Universities

The U15 Group of Canadian Research Universities (French: U15 – Regroupement des universités de recherche du Canada) is an association of fifteen public research universities in Canada. Based in Ottawa, Ontario, the group acts as an advocacy organization for research-intensive universities, promoting the value of university research to Canadian governments, the private sector, and the public.

U15 members collectively undertake the large majority of university research in Canada. They attract a disproportionate share of the country's research funding, enroll a majority of its doctoral students, and hold most of the patents and spin-off companies generated by Canadian universities.

History

The organization began informally in the 1980s as a grouping of ten research-intensive universities (the "Group of Ten"), whose executive heads met to coordinate on research policy. It expanded to thirteen members in 2006 (the "G13") and became the U15 in 2011 with the admission of the University of Manitoba and the University of Saskatchewan. It was formally incorporated under its present name in 2012.

Members

The fifteen members are: Dalhousie University, Laval University, McGill University, McMaster University, Queen's University at Kingston, the University of Alberta, the University of British Columbia, the University of Calgary, the University of Manitoba, the University of Ottawa, the University of Saskatchewan, the University of Toronto, the University of Waterloo, the University of Western Ontario (Western University), and the University of Montreal.

Role

The U15 advocates for sustained federal investment in fundamental research, research infrastructure through the Canada Foundation for Innovation, and graduate training. It publishes data on the economic impact of its members, noting that they contribute well over $30 billion annually to the Canadian economy and conduct roughly 80 percent of all university research in the country.
