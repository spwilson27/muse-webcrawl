In geometry, a cube is a three-dimensional solid object bounded by six congruent square faces, a type of regular hexahedron. It has eight vertices and twelve edges, and all of its faces meet at right angles. The cube is one of the five Platonic solids and is the only regular hexahedron.

Properties

For a cube with edge length a, the volume is a3 — which is why raising a number to the third power is called "cubing" it — and the surface area is 6a2. The space diagonal has length a√3. The cube has octahedral symmetry: it has 9 planes of symmetry and 13 axes of rotational symmetry. Its dual polyhedron is the regular octahedron, which has one vertex at the center of each face of the cube.

Nets and unfolding

A cube can be unfolded into a net of six squares in exactly 11 distinct arrangements. Because a net of four squares in a row can fold into a band of side faces, cubes appear naturally in packaging and in dice, where opposite faces of standard dice sum to seven.

Related shapes

The cube is the three-dimensional case of the more general concept of a hypercube: the four-dimensional analogue is the tesseract. Cubes tile three-dimensional space exactly, the only Platonic solid that does so, which underlies the cubic crystal system in mineralogy — minerals such as pyrite and halite commonly form cubic crystals. Doubling the cube, the problem of constructing a cube with twice the volume of a given cube using only compass and straightedge, was one of the classical problems of antiquity and was proved impossible in the 19th century.

In culture

The cube's regularity has made it a common symbol of stability and rationality. The Rubik's Cube, invented by Ernő Rubik in 1974, is a 3×3×3 cube puzzle that became the world's best-selling toy. Architecturally, cubic forms recur from the Kaaba in Mecca to modernist buildings and the Cube houses of Rotterdam.
