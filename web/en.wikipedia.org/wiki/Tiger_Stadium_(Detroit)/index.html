Tiger Stadium, previously known as Navin Field and Briggs Stadium, was a multi-purpose stadium in Detroit, Michigan, located in the Corktown neighbourhood at the corner of Michigan and Trumbull avenues, a site used for baseball since 1896. It was the home of the Detroit Tigers of Major League Baseball from 1912 to 1999, and of the Detroit Lions of the NFL from 1938 to 1974.

History

The stadium opened the same day as Boston's Fenway Park, on April 20, 1912. It was expanded in stages to a capacity of more than 52,000, with distinctive features including a right-field overhang, a towering flagpole in centre field — in play — and a roof over most of the seating. It hosted three World Series won by the Tigers (1935, 1945, and 1968) and the famous 1971 All-Star Game home run by Reggie Jackson that struck a light tower.

After baseball

The Tigers moved to Comerica Park in 2000. After years of preservation efforts, the stadium was demolished in 2008–2009, though the playing field was maintained by volunteers and later redeveloped as a youth sports site preserving the historic field and flagpole.
