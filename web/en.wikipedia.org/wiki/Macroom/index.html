Macroom

Macroom (Irish: Maigh Chromtha) is a market town in County Cork, Ireland, located in the valley of the River Sullane about halfway between Cork city and Killarney. It is a gateway to the Gaeltacht region of Muskerry and has a population of roughly 4,000.

History

The town grew around a castle of the McCarthy clan, whose gateway arch still stands at the entrance to the town square; the castle was burned in 1690 during the Williamite War and rebuilt in altered form. The surrounding district saw fighting during the Irish War of Independence, including the Kilmichael ambush of November 1920 nearby. Macroom was also the birthplace of William Penn's father, Admiral Sir William Penn, whose family held lands in the area.

The town today

Macroom lies on the N22 road; a long-planned bypass opened in stages in the 2020s, relieving through traffic. Local features include the Gearagh, a submerged glacial woodland and nature reserve on the River Lee to the east, created when the Lee hydroelectric scheme flooded the valley in the 1950s.
