Aaru (Ancient Egyptian: Sekhet-Aaru, "Field of Reeds") is the heavenly paradise of ancient Egyptian mythology, ruled over by the god Osiris. It was imagined as an idealised version of Egypt — an eternal field of lush reeds and abundant harvests, sometimes described as lying in the east where the sun rises, or as a series of islands.

Judgement and entry

Only souls whose hearts were judged lighter than or equal in weight to the feather of Ma'at (truth and order) in the Hall of Two Truths could enter Aaru. There the deceased were believed to continue a perfected version of their earthly lives: ploughing, harvesting, eating and drinking forever. Vignettes in the Book of the Dead, such as the famous scene in the Papyrus of Ani, depict the deceased and their spouse working happily in the Field of Reeds.

Depictions

Aaru is typically shown divided by waterways and canals, with the blessed dead harvesting tall grain. Ushabti figurines placed in tombs were intended to perform any labour required of the deceased in the fields of Aaru.
