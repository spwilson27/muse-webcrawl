Martin Denny (April 10, 1911 – March 2, 2005) was an American pianist, composer and arranger. Best known as the founder of the exotica genre of music, he popularised a lush, tropical style combining jazz, Latin rhythms, bird calls and unusual percussion that evoked an imagined South Pacific and became a staple of 1950s hi-fi and lounge culture.

Early career

Born in New York and raised in Los Angeles, Denny studied classical piano and toured South America in the early 1930s with the Don Dean Orchestra, collecting instruments and musical impressions. After serving in the US Army Air Forces in the Second World War he studied composition in Los Angeles and worked as an arranger and bandleader.

Exotica

In 1954 Denny settled in Hawaii, performing at the Shell Bar of the Hawaiian Village Hotel in Waikiki with a small combo including vibraphonist Arthur Lyman and percussionist Augie Colón, who supplied the famous bird calls and jungle sounds. Their residency inspired the album Exotica (1957), whose cover featured model Sandy Warner. A single of Les Baxter's "Quiet Village" from the album reached No. 2 on the US charts in 1959, and Denny recorded dozens of albums for Liberty Records, including Forbidden Island, Primitiva and Afro-Desia.

Later life and legacy

Denny continued performing in Hawaii into old age. His records were rediscovered in the 1990s lounge revival, heavily reissued and sampled, and he is regarded as a foundational figure of tiki culture and space-age pop.
