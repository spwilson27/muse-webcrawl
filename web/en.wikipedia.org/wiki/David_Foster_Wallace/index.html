David Foster Wallace

David Foster Wallace (February 21, 1962 – September 12, 2008) was an American writer and essayist known for his novel Infinite Jest (1996), which was widely acclaimed and named one of Time magazine's 100 best English-language novels published between 1923 and 2005. His writing is characterized by long sentences, extensive footnotes and endnotes, irony, and an earnest concern with sincerity and communication.

Life

Wallace was born in Ithaca, New York, and raised in Urbana, Illinois. He graduated from Amherst College with degrees in English and philosophy, and later earned an MFA in creative writing from the University of Arizona. He taught at Illinois State University and Pomona College.

Works

His novels include The Broom of the System (1987) and the posthumously published, unfinished The Pale King (2011), a finalist for the Pulitzer Prize. His essay and short-story collections include A Supposedly Fun Thing I'll Never Do Again (1997), Brief Interviews with Hideous Men (1999), and Consider the Lobster (2005). His 2005 Kenyon College commencement address was published as This Is Water.

Death

Wallace, who had long struggled with depression, died by suicide in Claremont, California, in 2008, at age 46.
