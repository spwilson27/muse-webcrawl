Indo-Mesopotamia relations

Period: c. 2900–1900 BCE

Parties: Indus Valley civilisation and Mesopotamian city-states

Key goods: Carnelian, lapis lazuli, gold, timber, cotton

Indo-Mesopotamia relations were the trade and cultural contacts between the Indus Valley civilisation of South Asia (known to Mesopotamians as Meluhha) and the city-states of Mesopotamia during the third millennium BCE.

Evidence

Akkadian texts mention ships from Meluhha, Magan, and Dilmun bringing goods to Mesopotamian ports. Archaeological evidence includes Indus seals found in Mesopotamia, etched carnelian beads, and standardized Indus weights.

Trade routes

Commerce moved through the Persian Gulf, with Dilmun (modern Bahrain) and Magan (Oman) as intermediaries. Goods included timber, gold, lapis lazuli, carnelian, ivory, and possibly cotton. Trade declined with the weakening of the Indus cities after about 1900 BCE.
