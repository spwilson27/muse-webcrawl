The Great British Beer Festival (GBBF) is an annual beer festival organized by the Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA), nicknamed "the biggest pub in the world". Held over five days, usually in August, it serves several hundred real ales from British breweries alongside ciders, perries and a major international beer bar, and hosts the judging of the Champion Beer of Britain competition.

History

CAMRA held the first national festival at Covent Garden in 1977. The event moved between venues including Alexandra Palace and Leeds before settling for long spells at Olympia in London (and, from 2025, a new home at the NEC in Birmingham was announced). The festival is staffed almost entirely by CAMRA volunteers, who rack, stillage and cellar hundreds of casks, serving beer by gravity and handpump.

Champion Beer of Britain

The festival is the stage for the final judging of the Champion Beer of Britain, one of the most prestigious awards in British brewing; winners gain a large boost in sales. Notable past winners include Timothy Taylor's Landlord and several champion stouts, porters and speciality beers across categories.
