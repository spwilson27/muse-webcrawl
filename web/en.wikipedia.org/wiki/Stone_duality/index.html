Stone duality

Stone duality is a family of theorems in topology and universal algebra describing a dual equivalence (a duality of categories) between certain lattices and certain topological spaces. The archetypal result is Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras, proved by Marshall Harvey Stone in 1936, which states that every Boolean algebra is isomorphic to the Boolean algebra of clopen sets of a totally disconnected, compact Hausdorff space — now called a Stone space.

Stone's theorem

Given a Boolean algebra B, its spectrum or Stone space S(B) is the set of ultrafilters on B, equipped with a topology whose basic open sets are the sets of ultrafilters containing a given element. Stone showed that this space is compact, Hausdorff, and zero-dimensional, and that the assignment B ↦ S(B) is a contravariant functor giving an equivalence between the category of Boolean algebras and the category of Stone spaces.

Generalizations

The duality has been extended in several directions. Priestley duality (1970) relates bounded distributive lattices to ordered Stone spaces carrying a partial order. Stone duality for locales and the theory of frames generalize the correspondence to pointless topology. Related dualities include Gelfand duality for commutative C*-algebras and the duality between algebraic geometry schemes and commutative rings.

Significance

Stone duality underlies Stone's representation theorem for distributive lattices, the theory of profinite groups (which are inverse limits of finite groups and hence Stone spaces), and applications in mathematical logic, where Stone spaces of theories coincide with spaces of complete types in model theory.
