Moment (mathematics)

[Image placeholder: moments of a distribution]

In mathematics, the moments of a function are certain quantitative measures related to the shape of the function's graph. The concept arises in probability theory, statistics, and mathematical physics. If the function represents a probability distribution, then the first moment is the expected value (mean), the second central moment is the variance, and the standardized third and fourth moments describe skewness and kurtosis respectively.

Definition

If f is a probability density function, then the nth moment about a value c is

μ′n = ∫ (x − c)n f(x) dx.

Moments about zero are called raw moments; moments about the mean are called central moments.

Moments of a random variable

For a random variable X, the nth raw moment is E[Xn], provided the expectation exists. The first raw moment is the mean μ. The second central moment is the variance σ², whose positive square root is the standard deviation. Higher standardized moments measure the asymmetry (skewness) and the "tailedness" (kurtosis) of the distribution.

Significance

Moments are used to characterize distributions: under suitable conditions, a distribution is uniquely determined by its sequence of moments, a question related to the classical moment problem. Moment-generating functions, whose derivatives at zero give the raw moments, provide a convenient analytical device. The method of moments — matching sample moments to theoretical moments — is one of the oldest techniques of statistical estimation.

Applications

In mechanics, the concept mirrors physical moments: the zeroth moment is total mass, the first moment relates to the centre of mass, and the second moment gives the moment of inertia. In image processing, image moments are used to compute features such as area, centroid and orientation.
