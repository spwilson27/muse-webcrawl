Mathieu Kassovitz

Mathieu Kassovitz
Born: 3 August 1967, Paris, France
Occupation: Film director, screenwriter, actor, producer
Years active: 1979 – present

Mathieu Kassovitz (born 3 August 1967) is a French film director, screenwriter, film producer and actor. He is best known as the director of La Haine (1995), a black-and-white film about three young men in the Paris suburbs in the aftermath of a riot, for which he won the Best Director award at the Cannes Film Festival and the César Award for Best Film.

Career

Kassovitz, the son of director Peter Kassovitz, began his career as an actor in his father's films. His first feature as director, Métisse (1993), was followed by La Haine (known in English as Hate), which became one of the most acclaimed French films of the 1990s and a landmark of cinéma du look's successor generation. His later French films include the thriller The Crimson Rivers (2000), with Jean Reno and Vincent Cassel.

In Hollywood he directed Gothika (2003), starring Halle Berry, and the science-fiction film Babylon A.D. (2008), a production he publicly criticized after studio interference. He returned to France for Rebellion (2011), about the Ouvéa cave hostage taking in New Caledonia.

Acting

As an actor, Kassovitz played Nino Quincampoix in Jean-Pierre Jeunet's Amélie (2001), and appeared in Munich (2005) and the television series Le Bureau des Légendes (The Bureau), in which he stars as the intelligence officer Malotru.
