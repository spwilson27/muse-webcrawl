Kafi

Kafi is a classical form of Sufi music and poetry originating in the Sindh and Punjab regions of South Asia. The term also refers to the poetic genre itself, composed in Punjabi, Sindhi, and Saraiki, and sung in a distinctive devotional style.

Poetic form

A kafi poem is generally built around a refrain and a series of verses, often drawing on the imagery of the beloved, the desert, and the spinning wheel as metaphors for the soul's longing for God. The form was perfected by the great Sufi poets of the region, including Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai in Sindhi and Bulleh Shah and Khwaja Ghulam Farid in Punjabi and Saraiki.

Musical setting

In musical performance, kafi is sung in specific ragas of Hindustani and Sindhi classical music — there is also a raga named Kafi in Hindustani music, a major melodic framework associated with spring and the Holi festival. Sufi singers traditionally accompany the verses with harmonium, tabla, dholak and other regional instruments.

Celebrated exponents of sung kafi include Abida Parveen, Pathanay Khan, and Allan Fakir, whose renditions carried the form to international audiences in the twentieth century.
