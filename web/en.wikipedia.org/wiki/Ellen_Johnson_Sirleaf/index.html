Ellen Johnson Sirleaf (born 29 October 1938) is a Liberian politician and economist who served as the 24th president of Liberia from 2006 to 2018. Sirleaf was the first elected female head of state in Africa.

Known as the "Iron Lady", she was educated in the United States and worked at Citibank, the World Bank and the United Nations Development Programme before entering Liberian politics. She was imprisoned under the Doe regime and exiled during the Taylor years for her opposition.

Early life and career

Johnson Sirleaf was born in Monrovia to a Gola father and a mother of Kru and German descent. She studied economics and accounting, earning a degree from the University of Colorado Boulder and a Master of Public Administration from Harvard Kennedy School in 1971.

Political career

She served as Minister of Finance under President William Tolbert (1979–1980). After Samuel Doe's 1980 coup she was imprisoned, and later fled into exile. She ran for president in 1997 against Charles Taylor, finishing second, and again in 2005, defeating footballer George Weah in a run-off.

Presidency

Johnson Sirleaf's administration stabilised Liberia after its civil wars, secured extensive debt relief, attracted foreign investment, rebuilt institutions, and managed the country's response to the West African Ebola epidemic of 2014–2016. In 2018 she oversaw Liberia's first peaceful, democratic transfer of power since 1944, handing office to George Weah.

Awards

In 2011 Johnson Sirleaf was jointly awarded the Nobel Peace Prize with Leymah Gbowee and Tawakkol Karman "for their non-violent struggle for the safety of women and for women's rights to full participation in peace-building work".
