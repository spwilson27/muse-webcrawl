A line dance is a choreographed dance in which a group of people dance along to a repeating sequence of steps while arranged in one or more lines or rows. The dancers all face the same direction and execute the steps at the same time; unlike circle dances, line dancers are not in physical contact with each other, and there are no partners. Line dancing is practised and enjoyed in many countries and is strongly associated with country music, though it is also danced to pop, disco, Latin and soul music.

History

Line dancing has roots in folk and contra dance traditions, and in social dances performed in rows for centuries. Its modern form took shape in the United States in the 1970s, when disco hits such as Van McCoy's "The Hustle" (1975) prompted group dances performed in lines; the Electric Slide, choreographed by Ric Silver in 1976 to Marcia Griffiths' "Electric Boogie", became a durable favourite. A country-music boom followed in the early 1990s with dances such as the Boot Scootin' Boogie, choreographed to the Brooks & Dunn song of the same name, and Billy Ray Cyrus's "Achy Breaky Heart". Soul line dancing developed in parallel in African-American social settings, and line dances remain popular at weddings, parties and community events worldwide.

Format and teaching

Dances are described by counts (commonly 32 or 64 beats) and by the number of "walls" — the directions faced during one repetition; a four-wall dance rotates participants through north, east, south and west in a single sequence. Step sheets published by choreographers standardise the sequences, and instructors teach them in classes and social evenings run by organisations and clubs. Competitive line dancing is governed by bodies such as the World Dance Council-affiliated organisations and the United Country Western Dance Council.
