Cant deficiency

Cant deficiency is a railway engineering term describing the difference between the superelevation (cant) actually applied to a curved track and the greater cant that would be needed to fully balance the centrifugal force of a train travelling at its actual speed. When cant deficiency occurs, passengers feel a sideways force pushing them toward the outside of the curve.

Definition

The equilibrium cant for a given curve radius, speed and track gauge is the cant at which the resultant of gravity and centrifugal force is perpendicular to the track plane. If the applied cant is lower, the shortfall — expressed in millimetres of cant — is the cant deficiency. The opposite case, excess cant at low speed, is cant excess.

Limits and tilting trains

Permitted cant deficiency is limited by passenger comfort and safety against derailment or overturning. Conventional trains are typically limited to roughly 100–150 mm of deficiency, while tilting trains compensate by leaning the body into the curve and are allowed much higher values, permitting faster running on existing alignments.
