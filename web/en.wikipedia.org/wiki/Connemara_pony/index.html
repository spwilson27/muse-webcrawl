The Connemara pony is a pony breed originating in the Connemara region of County Galway in western Ireland. It is the only pony breed native to Ireland and is noted for its hardiness, athleticism, and gentle temperament. Connemaras are widely exported and are popular mounts for both children and adults in jumping, eventing, dressage, and driving.

History

The breed developed over centuries in the rugged, rocky terrain of Connemara, where small native ponies were crossed at various times with horses brought by traders and settlers — legend attributes some bloodlines to Andalusian horses from wrecked ships of the Spanish Armada in 1588. Arab, Thoroughbred, Hackney, and Welsh blood were also introduced in the 18th and 19th centuries. By the early 20th century the breed was deteriorating through indiscriminate crossbreeding, and the Connemara Pony Breeders' Society was founded in 1923 to preserve and improve it; a studbook was established, stallions were inspected, and mares were selected for registration.

Characteristics

Breed-standard Connemaras stand between 12.2 and 14.2 hands (though larger individuals occur and are not disqualified). They have a well-proportioned head with a straight or slightly dished profile, a well-laid-back shoulder, a strong body, and hard, sound limbs suited to rough ground. Grey is the most common colour; bay, brown, dun, and black are also typical. Foals are born dark and many grey out with age.

Uses

Connemaras excel as all-round riding and competition ponies: they are noted jumpers, have competed successfully in international eventing and show jumping, and are also used for trekking, hunting, and driving. Crossbreds — particularly Connemara × Thoroughbred — are prized as sport horses. The breed has a known inherited condition, hoof wall separation disease, for which a DNA test is available; responsible breeders test breeding stock.
