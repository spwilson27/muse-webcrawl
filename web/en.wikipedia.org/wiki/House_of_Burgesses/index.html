House of Burgesses

The House of Burgesses was the elected lower house of the Virginia General Assembly, the legislative body of the Colony of Virginia. Established in 1619, it was the first elected legislative assembly in the New World, and met for the first time at Jamestown on 30 July 1619 under Governor Sir George Yeardley.

History

The first session comprised 22 burgesses chosen by the colony's settlements, together with the governor and his council. Initially the whole General Assembly met as a single body; by the 1640s the burgesses sat separately as a lower house, with the Governor's Council acting as the upper house.

The House sat at Jamestown until the capital moved to Williamsburg in 1699. In the eighteenth century it became a training ground for leaders of the American Revolution, including George Washington, Thomas Jefferson and Patrick Henry, whose 1765 Virginia Resolves against the Stamp Act were passed by the House. After independence, the House of Burgesses was succeeded in 1776 by the Virginia House of Delegates.
