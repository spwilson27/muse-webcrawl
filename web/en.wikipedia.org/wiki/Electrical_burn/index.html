Electrical burn

An electrical burn is a burn that results from electricity passing through the body, causing rapid and extensive tissue damage. Unlike ordinary thermal burns, electrical burns often injure deep tissues along the path of the current while leaving relatively small surface wounds at the entry and exit points. Electrical burns are classified among the most severe types of burn injury.

Causes and mechanism

Electrical burns most commonly occur from contact with household or industrial power supplies, high-voltage transmission lines, arc flash, and lightning. As current passes through the body, electrical energy is converted to heat, with damage greatest in tissues of high electrical resistance, such as bone, which can heat surrounding muscle. High-voltage injuries may also produce an arc-flash or flame burn to the skin without current entering the body.

Complications and treatment

Complications include cardiac arrhythmias and cardiac arrest, deep muscle necrosis, compartment syndrome, fractures from tetanic muscle contractions or falls, renal failure from myoglobin release, and cataracts after head injuries. Treatment prioritises safety at the scene, cardiopulmonary resuscitation where needed, cardiac monitoring, aggressive fluid resuscitation, wound care, and surgical removal of dead tissue; severe cases are managed in specialist burn centres.
