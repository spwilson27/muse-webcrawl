Smith's fracture

A Smith's fracture is a fracture of the distal radius of the wrist in which the broken fragment is displaced toward the palm (volar displacement). It is sometimes called a reverse Colles' fracture, because in the more common Colles' fracture the fragment is displaced in the opposite (dorsal) direction.

Named after: Robert William Smith (1847)
Mechanism: Fall onto a flexed wrist or blow to the back of the wrist
Treatment: Reduction and splinting; surgery for unstable injuries

Classification

Smith fractures are divided into three types: Type I, extra-articular; Type II, in which the fracture line crosses into the joint; and Type III, a fracture-dislocation involving the radiocarpal joint (a Barton fracture pattern).

Treatment

Stable fractures are treated by closed reduction and casting with the wrist in extension; unstable or intra-articular fractures often require surgical fixation, commonly with a volar locking plate.
