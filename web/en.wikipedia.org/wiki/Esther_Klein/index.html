Esther Szekeres (née Klein; Hungarian: Klein Eszter; February 5, 1910 – August 28, 2005) was a Hungarian–Australian mathematician. She is best remembered in combinatorial geometry as the young mathematician whose question led to the famous "Happy Ending problem", so named by Paul Erdős because it resulted in the marriage of Klein to fellow mathematician George Szekeres.

The Happy Ending problem

In early 1930s Budapest, Klein met regularly with a circle of young mathematicians including Erdős, Szekeres and Pál Turán. Klein observed that any set of five points in the plane in general position (no three collinear) contains four points forming a convex quadrilateral, and she posed the general problem: for which numbers of points must a convex polygon of a given size appear? Erdős and Szekeres pursued the question, proving in their celebrated 1935 paper that sufficiently large point sets in general position always contain convex polygons of any prescribed size — a foundational result of Ramsey theory and combinatorial geometry. Klein and Szekeres married in 1937.

Later life

The couple escaped wartime Europe for Shanghai, where they lived among the Jewish refugee community; the future mathematician's family story is recounted in accounts of the Shanghai ghetto. After the war they emigrated to Australia, where George took a position at the University of Adelaide and later at the University of New South Wales. Esther taught mathematics and mentored students, and the pair remained central figures in Australian mathematics for decades. Both died in Adelaide in 2005, on the same day, within an hour of one another.
