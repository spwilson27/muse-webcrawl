Shortness of breath

Shortness of breath (SOB), medically known as dyspnea, is the subjective sensation of difficulty or discomfort in breathing — an uncomfortable awareness of one's own breathing effort. It is a common symptom in cardiopulmonary disease and a frequent reason for emergency department visits. Related terms describe breathlessness in specific positions: orthopnea when lying flat, platypnea when upright, and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea waking the patient at night.

Causes

Causes span respiratory diseases (asthma, COPD, pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, pneumothorax, interstitial lung disease), cardiac conditions (heart failure, myocardial ischemia, valvular disease), anaemia, metabolic acidosis, neuromuscular weakness, and anxiety or panic attacks. Acute severe dyspnea is a medical emergency requiring rapid assessment of airway, breathing, and circulation.

Evaluation

Assessment includes history, examination, pulse oximetry, blood tests (including BNP and D-dimer where indicated), electrocardiography, chest imaging, and sometimes arterial blood gas analysis and echocardiography. Severity scales such as the modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) scale grade chronic breathlessness by its effect on daily activities.

Management

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause — bronchodilators for obstructive airway disease, diuretics for heart failure, anticoagulation for pulmonary embolism — together with supplemental oxygen when hypoxemic. In chronic and palliative settings, opioids, breathing techniques, handheld fans, and pulmonary rehabilitation can relieve the sensation of breathlessness.
