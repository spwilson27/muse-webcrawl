Albrecht Altdorfer (c. 1480 – 12 February 1538) was a German painter, engraver and architect of the Renaissance working in Regensburg, Bavaria. Along with Lucas Cranach the Elder and Wolf Huber, he is regarded as a leading figure of the Danube school, celebrated for pioneering pure landscape painting in European art.

Life

Altdorfer became a citizen of Regensburg in 1505 and rose to prominence in civic life, serving on the city council and as city architect from 1526. He declined election as mayor in 1528, reportedly to complete his most famous commission.

Works

His masterpiece, The Battle of Alexander at Issus (1529, Alte Pinakothek, Munich), commissioned by Wilhelm IV of Bavaria, depicts Alexander the Great's victory over Darius III amid a vast cosmic landscape of mountains, sea and sky. Altdorfer also painted some of the earliest independent landscapes in Western art, such as Danube Landscape near Regensburg (c. 1528), and produced innovative etchings and woodcuts. His other major works include the altarpiece for St. Florian's Abbey (1518) and Susanna and the Elders (1526).

Legacy

Altdorfer's dramatic treatment of nature — forests, mountains and atmospheric light dominating human figures — influenced generations of landscape painters and secured his place among the great masters of the Northern Renaissance.
