Ring of Gullion

The Ring of Gullion is a geological formation and region in County Armagh, Northern Ireland, centred on the extinct volcano of Slieve Gullion. It is designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.

Geology

The ring is a ring dyke complex formed about 60 million years ago during the opening of the North Atlantic, when magma intruded into a circular fracture around a collapsing caldera. Slieve Gullion itself, at 573 metres, is the eroded core of the volcano, ringed by hills including Camlough Mountain and the ring peaks.

Heritage

The landscape is rich in prehistoric monuments, including the Clontygora and Ballymacdermot court tombs and the Kilnasaggart Stone, and features in Irish mythology: Slieve Gullion's summit passage tomb and lake are associated with the Cailleach and with Cú Chulainn and Fionn mac Cumhaill. The area is promoted for walking, including the Ring of Gullion Way.
