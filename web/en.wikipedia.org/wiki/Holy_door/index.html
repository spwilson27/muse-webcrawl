Holy door

A holy door (Latin: porta sancta) is a special door in certain Roman Catholic churches — most famously the four papal major basilicas of Rome — that is normally sealed shut and opened only during a Holy Year (Jubilee). Passing through a holy door during a Jubilee is associated with the granting of a plenary indulgence to pilgrims who fulfil the prescribed conditions.

Rome's holy doors

Holy doors are maintained at the basilicas of St Peter's in the Vatican, St John Lateran, St Mary Major, and St Paul Outside the Walls. The tradition of a holy door at St Peter's is documented from the Jubilee of 1500 under Pope Alexander VI. The door at St Peter's, a bronze door by Vico Consorti cast for the Holy Year 1950, bears sixteen panels depicting scenes of sin and redemption.

Rite of opening

Traditionally, the Pope ceremonially began the demolition of the wall sealing the door, striking it with a hammer; since the Great Jubilee of 2000 the wall is dismantled beforehand and the Pope pushes the doors open. At the end of the Holy Year the door is closed and sealed again, with a casket of documents and medals traditionally immured in the wall.

Other holy doors

By papal grant, a small number of other churches possess holy doors, including Santiago de Compostela Cathedral (opened in Jacobean Holy Years) and the Basilica of Sainte-Anne-de-Beaupré in Quebec. For the extraordinary Jubilee of Mercy (2015–16), Pope Francis authorised holy doors in cathedrals worldwide.
