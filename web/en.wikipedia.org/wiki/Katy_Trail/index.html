Katy Trail

The Katy Trail is a recreational rail trail in the US state of Missouri, running 240 miles (390 km) between Machens (near St. Charles) and Clinton. Operated as Katy Trail State Park, it is one of the longest rails-to-trails projects in the United States and follows the former corridor of the Missouri–Kansas–Texas Railroad (the "Katy").

Route

Most of the trail runs beside the Missouri River through farmland, bluffs and small towns, passing through Jefferson City, Boonville, Sedalia and the wine country around Hermann and Augusta. Its surface is crushed limestone, open to cyclists, walkers and (in places) equestrians. Much of the route parallels Lewis and Clark's journey and the Missouri River section of the American Discovery Trail.

History

After the MKT line was abandoned in 1986, the corridor was railbanked under the National Trails System Act; philanthropist Ted Jones and his wife Pat funded much of the initial construction. The first sections opened in 1990, and the trail was completed to Clinton in 1999, with the Machens extension following in 2011.
