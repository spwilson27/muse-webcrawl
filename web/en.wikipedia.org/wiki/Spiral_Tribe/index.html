Spiral Tribe

Spiral Tribe was a British free-party sound system and travelling collective that played a central role in the UK rave scene and the European free tekno movement of the 1990s. Formed in London in 1990, the group organised unlicensed warehouse parties and outdoor festivals, touring with a convoy of vehicles and a large PA system, and released influential breakbeat hardcore and techno records as Spiral Tribe Sound System.

Castlemorton and exile

Spiral Tribe were closely associated with the Castlemorton Common Festival of May 1992, a week-long free festival attended by tens of thousands, which prompted the British government's Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 — legislation famously targeting gatherings featuring music with "repetitive beats". Members faced prosecution (a lengthy trial ended in acquittals and dropped charges), and the collective left Britain for mainland Europe.

Legacy

In Europe, Spiral Tribe helped seed the teknival scene, especially in France, and are credited (through offshoots such as the SP23 network) with spreading freetekno culture across the continent. Their DIY ethos, distinctive spiral logo and mutant vehicles made them emblematic of the traveller-rave culture of the era. The story is recounted in the documentary and memoir projects of former members, including Mark Angelo Harrison.
