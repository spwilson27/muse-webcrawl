The Argentine Football Association (Spanish: Asociación del Fútbol Argentino, AFA) is the governing body of football in Argentina, based in Buenos Aires. It organises the Argentine league system, including the Primera División, the Copa Argentina, and the Argentina national football team. Founded in 1893, it is the eighth-oldest national football association in the world and the oldest outside Europe.

History

The association was founded on 21 February 1893 as the Argentine Association Football League, largely through the efforts of Scotsman Alexander Watson Hutton, regarded as the father of Argentine football. It was the first South American association to affiliate with FIFA, in 1912, and was a founding member of CONMEBOL in 1916. In 1931 the AFA oversaw the introduction of professionalism. Long-serving president Julio Grondona led the association from 1979 until his death in 2014.

Competitions and national team

The AFA runs the multi-division professional and semi-professional league pyramid, the Copa Argentina, and youth and women's competitions. The men's national team has won the FIFA World Cup three times (1978, 1986, 2022), a record 16 Copa América titles, and Olympic gold in 2004 and 2008. The national team's training complex at Ezeiza is named after Lionel Messi.
