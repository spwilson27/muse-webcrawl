Chives, scientific name Allium schoenoprasum, is a species of flowering plant in the onion family Amaryllidaceae. It is a perennial herb grown worldwide for its slender, hollow green leaves, which have a mild onion flavour and are used as a seasoning. Chives are the smallest of the cultivated onion relatives and are unusual in being native across Europe, Asia, and North America simultaneously.

Description

Chives grow in clumps from small bulbs, sending up hollow, tube-like leaves 20–50 cm tall. In late spring and summer the plants produce dense spherical umbels of pale purple, star-shaped flowers, which are also edible and are visited by pollinators. The flavour comes from sulphur-rich compounds (organosulfur chemistry) shared with onions and garlic, though in milder concentration.

Cultivation

Chives are hardy perennials suited to herb gardens and containers; they thrive in rich, well-drained soil in full sun. Leaves are harvested by cutting the clump a few centimetres above the ground; regular cutting and division every few years keeps clumps vigorous. Removing flowers before they set seed prevents self-sowing and prolongs leaf production. Chives are said to repel some insect pests and are a traditional companion plant in gardens.

Culinary use

The leaves are usually snipped with scissors and used fresh, since cooking quickly dulls their flavour. Chives are one of the French fines herbes, along with parsley, tarragon, and chervil. They garnish soups, baked potatoes with sour cream, omelettes, soft cheeses, and potato salads across European, North American, and East Asian cuisines. In Sweden, chives are an essential accompaniment to pickled herring at Midsummer. Closely related garlic chives (Allium tuberosum), with flat leaves and a garlicky taste, are a staple in Chinese and Japanese cooking.

History

Chives have been cultivated in China since at least the third millennium BC and in Europe since the Middle Ages; Marco Polo is sometimes credited with bringing knowledge of them from China, though they were already native to Europe. Romanies traditionally used chives in fortune-telling, and dried bunches were hung in homes to ward off disease and evil.
