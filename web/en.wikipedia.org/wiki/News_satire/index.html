News satire

News satire, also called fake news in its original sense, is a genre of satire and parody presented in the format of genuine journalism, using humour, irony and exaggeration to comment on current events, politics and the media itself.

History

The genre has roots in 18th- and 19th-century satirical writing, and flourished in print with magazines such as Punch. In the modern era, television programmes including Britain's That Was the Week That Was (1962) and Spitting Image, and in the United States Saturday Night Live's "Weekend Update", The Daily Show and The Colbert Report, brought news satire to mass audiences. Print and online outlets such as The Onion (founded 1988) publish wholly invented stories in deadpan news style.

Effects and confusion

Studies suggest satirical programmes can inform audiences about public affairs, and some viewers treat them as genuine news sources. Because satirical stories are sometimes mistaken for real reporting when shared out of context, the genre is often contrasted with deliberately deceptive disinformation, which imitates the same format without satirical intent or labelling.
