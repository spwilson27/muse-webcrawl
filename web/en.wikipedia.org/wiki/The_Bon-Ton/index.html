The Bon-Ton

The Bon-Ton, also billed as The Bon-Ton; or, High Life Above Stairs, is a two-act afterpiece — a comic operetta or "burletta" — by the Scottish playwright and composer David Garrick, first performed at the Theatre Royal, Drury Lane, London, on 18 March 1775. It was one of the last pieces Garrick wrote for the stage before his retirement in 1776.

Plot and characters

The piece satirises fashionable London society — the bon ton of the title. Lord Minikin, having acquired a country estate and wealth, imitates high life while his household turns upside down: his servants ape their betters, playing at being ladies and gentlemen "above stairs" while their masters' authority crumbles. Characters include Lord Minikin, Lady Minikin, Sir John Trotley, Colonel Tivy, Miss Tittup, and the servant Davy. The comedy turns on the period anxiety — and amusement — about social climbing and the blurring of ranks in a commercial age.

Context and reception

Garrick, the dominant English actor-manager of his century, wrote the piece late in his career, drawing on familiar themes of fashion, gambling, and the manners of the beau monde. The Bon-Ton includes one of the early theatrical references to fashionable society's recreations, and its short, light form was typical of the afterpieces that rounded out an evening's bill at the patent theatres. The work was published in 1775 and has been noted by theatre historians as an example of Garrick's late style and of the period's satire on the ton.
