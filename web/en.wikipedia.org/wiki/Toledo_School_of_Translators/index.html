Toledo School of Translators

Toledo School of Translators
Spanish: Escuela de Traductores de Toledo
Active: 12th–13th centuries
Location: Toledo, Spain

The Toledo School of Translators (Spanish: Escuela de Traductores de Toledo) is the name given to the group of scholars who worked together in the city of Toledo during the 12th and 13th centuries to translate many of the great works of Arabic science and philosophy into Latin and Castilian, making them available to the rest of Europe.

History

After the Christian reconquest of Toledo in 1085, the city retained large Arabic-speaking and Jewish communities and rich libraries. Under Archbishop Raymond of Toledo and later under King Alfonso X of Castile, multilingual teams — often a Jewish or Mozarabic scholar rendering Arabic into the vernacular and a Christian cleric rendering it into Latin — translated works of astronomy, mathematics, medicine and philosophy.

Figures and works

Notable translators include Gerard of Cremona, who translated dozens of works including Ptolemy's Almagest, and Dominicus Gundissalinus. The translations transmitted Greek learning preserved in Arabic, together with original Arab scholarship, and were fundamental to the 12th-century Renaissance in Europe.
