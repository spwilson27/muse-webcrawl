Gruyère

Town: Gruyères, canton of Fribourg, Switzerland

Best known for: Gruyère cheese; Château de Gruyères

The name Gruyère (French: Gruyère) refers to a district and region in the canton of Fribourg in western Switzerland, centred on the medieval town of Gruyères and Lake Gruyère (Lac de la Gruyère), and gives its name to the celebrated cheese made in the region.

Geography

The Gruyère district lies in the upper Saane valley in the foothills of the Swiss Alps, with Bulle as its principal modern town. The medieval counts of Gruyère ruled the area from their castle at Gruyères until the line died out in 1554, after which the territory was divided between Fribourg and Bern.

Gruyère cheese

Gruyère cheese (French: Gruyère, German: Greyerzer) is a hard Swiss cheese made from cow's milk, traditionally produced in wheels and aged for six months or more, prized for its slightly salty, nutty flavour. It is a classic ingredient of fondue, French onion soup and croque-monsieur, and holds protected designation of origin (AOP) status in Switzerland.
