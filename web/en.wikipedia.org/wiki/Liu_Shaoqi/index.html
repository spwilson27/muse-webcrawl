Liu Shaoqi (1898–1969)

Liu Shaoqi (24 November 1898 – 12 November 1969) was a Chinese revolutionary and statesman who served as Chairman (President) of the People's Republic of China from 1959 to 1968 and was for many years regarded as Mao Zedong's designated successor. A leading organiser of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), he was purged during the Cultural Revolution, denounced as China's foremost "capitalist roader", and died in custody in 1969 after being denied medical care. He was posthumously rehabilitated in 1980.

Early life and party career

Born into a moderately prosperous peasant family in Ningxiang County, Hunan, Liu studied in Moscow in 1921 and joined the CCP the same year. He became a leading labour organiser, played a central role in the Anyuan coal miners' strike, and worked in the party underground in Kuomintang-controlled cities during the 1930s, building the party's organisation in northern China. By the 1940s he was one of Mao's closest lieutenants and the party's chief theorist of organisation; his 1939 lectures published as How to Be a Good Communist became a standard party text.

Rise to head of state

After 1949, Liu served as vice-chairman of the Central People's Government and then as chairman of the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (1954–1959). In 1959 he succeeded Mao as Chairman of the People's Republic — head of state — while Mao retained leadership of the party and army. Following the failures of the Great Leap Forward, Liu and Deng Xiaoping guided economic retrenchment in the early 1960s, allowing limited market mechanisms and household farming experiments, which restored production but conflicted with Mao's vision.

Fall during the Cultural Revolution

When Mao launched the Cultural Revolution in 1966, Liu became its chief target. At the Eighth Plenary Session of the Eighth Central Committee in August 1966 he was demoted from second to eighth in the party hierarchy. Red Guards denounced him as a "traitor, renegade, and scab", and he was placed under house arrest in 1967. In October 1968 the Twelfth Plenary Session expelled him from the party "forever" and stripped him of all posts. Transferred to Kaifeng, he died there on 12 November 1969, ill with diabetes and pneumonia, after repeated refusals of treatment; his body was cremated under a false name.

Rehabilitation and legacy

In February 1980 the Fifth Plenary Session of the Eleventh Central Committee posthumously rehabilitated Liu, declaring the case against him the "biggest frame-up in the history of the party", and he was given a state funeral. His former residence in Ningxiang is preserved as a memorial.
