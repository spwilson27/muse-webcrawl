Patrick Michael Grundy (16 November 1917 – 4 November 1959) was an English mathematician known for his work in combinatorial game theory. He introduced the concept now known as the Grundy value (or nim-value) in his 1939 paper "Mathematics and games".

Work

Grundy studied at Cambridge and worked on statistics and game theory. Independently of Roland Sprague, and building on the earlier theory of Nim by Charles Bouton, he proved that every impartial game is equivalent to a Nim heap of a certain size — the result now called the Sprague–Grundy theorem. The size of that equivalent heap is the game's Grundy value.

The Sprague–Grundy theorem is a foundational result in combinatorial game theory and underlies the analysis of many impartial games. See also Sprague–Grundy theorem and Nim.
