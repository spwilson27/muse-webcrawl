Venice Film Festival

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Palazzo del Cinema on the Lido at dusk during the festival, the Golden Lion banners lit over the red carpet]

The Venice Film Festival (Mostra Internazionale d'Arte Cinematografica) is the world's oldest film festival, founded in 1932 as part of the Venice Biennale, and held annually in late August or early September on the Lido island in the Venetian lagoon. Its top prize is the Golden Lion (Leone d'Oro).

Born within Mussolini's cultural politics — the early festival's prize was the Coppa Mussolini — Venice relaunched after the war as art cinema's salon: the 1950s here crowned Kurosawa (Rashomon, 1951), Satyajit Ray (Aparajito, 1957) and the European modernists of the coming decade.

The autumn launchpad

Venice's slot at the start of the awards season made it the Oscar race's starting pistol: in the 2010s and 2020s a parade of eventual Best Pictures and directors premiered on the Lido (Gravity, Birdman, Spotlight, The Shape of Water — itself a Golden Lion winner — Roma, Joker, Nomadland, Poor Things). The Biennale's art-festival parentage keeps its selection's intellectual polish above the market's noise.

The place

The festival's glamour is architectural: the white rationalist Palazzo del Cinema (1937), the Excelsior's beach, stars arriving by water taxi — a stage set no purpose-built site can match. As the senior member of the 'Big Five' festivals (with Cannes, Berlin, Toronto and Sundance in the usual listing), Venice opens the film year's second half.
