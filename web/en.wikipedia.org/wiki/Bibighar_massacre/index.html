The Bibighar massacre was a killing of British women and children in Cawnpore (now Kanpur), India, on 15 July 1857, during the Indian Rebellion of 1857. The victims had been held captive in a small residence called the Bibighar ("House of the Ladies") following the surrender of the British garrison under Major General Sir Hugh Wheeler at Satichaura Ghat.

Background

After Wheeler's force surrendered on terms promising safe passage down the Ganges, boats carrying the evacuees were fired upon at Satichaura Ghat on 27 June; the surviving women and children were taken into captivity, first at Savada House and then at the Bibighar. As British relief forces under Henry Havelock approached Cawnpore and defeated rebel forces in engagements near the city, the captives were killed on the orders of Nana Sahib's household; when sepoys refused to fire on the women, others carried out the killings with swords and cleavers. The bodies were thrown into a well in the compound.

Aftermath

The discovery of the massacre site by recapturing British troops provoked intense outrage in Britain and brutal reprisals against Indian soldiers and civilians. "Remember Cawnpore" became a rallying cry. After 1857 the site was made a memorial, with the well covered and an angel statue (the "Angel of the Resurrection" by Carlo Marochetti) erected. After Indian independence the memorials were moved to the grounds of All Souls' Church (Kanpur Memorial Church).
