Vyborg Castle

Vyborg Castle (Russian: Выборгский замок; Finnish: Viipurin linna; Swedish: Viborgs slott) is a medieval fortress in Vyborg, Russia, built on a small island in the Bay of Vyborg at the eastern end of the Gulf of Finland. Founded by the Swedes in 1293, it is one of the best-preserved medieval Swedish castles and the historic nucleus around which the town of Vyborg grew.

History

The castle was established during the Third Swedish Crusade by the Swedish marshal Torkel Knutsson, who chose the site to command trade and defence in Karelia against Novgorod. Its massive keep — the Tower of St. Olaf, named for the Norwegian king and saint Olaf II — became the landmark of the town. The fortress was repeatedly besieged in the Swedish–Novgorodian and later Russo-Swedish wars and was substantially rebuilt in stone over the 15th and 16th centuries. Russian forces under Peter the Great captured Vyborg in 1710 during the Great Northern War, and the castle remained in Russian and then Finnish hands (1917–1940) before Vyborg was ceded to the Soviet Union after the Winter War and again in 1944.

Today

The castle now houses the Vyborg Museum-Reserve, with exhibitions on the town's Swedish, Finnish, and Russian past, and hosts medieval festivals and re-enactments. A major restoration programme of the castle complex has been under way since the 2010s. Its silhouette — whitewashed St. Olaf's tower rising over the water — remains the symbol of Vyborg.
