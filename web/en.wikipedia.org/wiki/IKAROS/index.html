IKAROS (Interplanetary Kite-craft Accelerated by Radiation Of the Sun) is a Japanese experimental spacecraft developed by the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) to test solar sail technology in interplanetary flight. Launched on 20 May 2010 aboard an H-IIA rocket together with the Akatsuki Venus probe, it became the first spacecraft to successfully demonstrate solar-sail propulsion in interplanetary space.

Design

The drum-shaped craft, 1.6 m in diameter and about 315 kg, deployed a square sail 14 metres across the diagonal by spinning — up to about 20 rpm — without rigid booms. The 7.5-micrometre polyimide film carried thin-film solar cells, liquid-crystal attitude-control devices whose reflectivity could be varied electrically, dust detectors and a gamma-ray burst instrument (GAP).

Mission

The sail deployed fully on 10 June 2010; JAXA confirmed photon acceleration days later. In December 2010 IKAROS passed Venus at about 80,000 km, completing its planned mission. It continued operating for years in a hibernation–wake cycle as power from its solar cells waxed and waned, and inspired follow-on sail designs such as NASA's and the Breakthrough Starshot concept studies.
