Mamoudzou

Mamoudzou is the capital and largest commune of Mayotte, a French overseas department and region in the Indian Ocean, located at the northern entrance of the Mozambique Channel. It lies on Grande-Terre, the main island of the archipelago, opposite the island of Petite-Terre, where the former capital Dzaoudzi and the airport are located.

History

Mamoudzou became the functional capital of Mayotte in 1977, taking over from Dzaoudzi, although the prefecture's offices moved across the lagoon gradually. Its growth since then has been rapid, driven by Mayotte's status as a French department (granted in 2011) and by substantial immigration from the Comoros islands.

Geography

The commune is divided into several villages, including the town centre of Mamoudzou proper, Kavani, Kawéni (whose industrial zone hosts much of the island's commerce), Mtsapéré, Passamaïnty, Tsoundzou and Vahibé. Ferries called barges cross the channel between Mamoudzou and Dzaoudzi on Petite-Terre every half hour, carrying pedestrians and vehicles.

Demographics

Mamoudzou is by far the largest urban centre in Mayotte, with a population of roughly 71,000 in the commune at the 2017 census and a young age structure typical of the department, where about half the population is under 18. The main languages spoken are Shimaore and Kibushi, alongside French, the language of administration and schooling.
