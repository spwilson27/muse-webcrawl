Charles Montgomery Plantagenet Schicklgruber "Monty" Burns, usually called Mr. Burns, is a fictional character in the animated television series The Simpsons, voiced by Harry Shearer. He is the owner of the Springfield Nuclear Power Plant, where Homer Simpson works, and serves as the show's principal antagonist: a miserly, frail and fantastically wealthy tycoon of great (and inconsistent, but plainly very advanced) age, attended by his sycophantic assistant Waylon Smithers.

Character

Burns embodies every vice of the robber-baron capitalist: he is greedy, callous, environmentally reckless and nostalgic for the labour practices of the 19th century. His immense age is a running gag — he is variously said to be 81, 104 or far older, with flashbacks showing him as a young man in the early 1900s and jokes implying he has forgotten the password to his own vault, outlived his contemporaries, and once rode in a car with the Wright brothers. Physically he is extraordinarily weak, yet indestructible in the manner of cartoon villains. His signature habits include steepling his fingers while murmuring "Excellent…", mistaking Homer for someone else, and ordering Smithers to "release the hounds" on unwelcome visitors. His backstory, told in episodes such as "Rosebud", presents him as a child who abandoned his loving poor family — and his beloved teddy bear, Bobo — to live with a heartless millionaire.

Creation and reception

Matt Groening modelled Burns partly on figures including oil tycoon John D. Rockefeller and the puppet-like department store magnate of Olsen's; his appearance and manner were also inspired by Groening's high-school teacher and by Lionel Barrymore. The character's name derives from Montgomery Park and Burnside Street in Groening's hometown of Portland, Oregon. Burns is consistently ranked among the greatest television villains; Wizard magazine placed him high on its list of greatest villains of all time, and his two-part mystery storyline "Who Shot Mr. Burns?" (1995) became one of the show's most famous episodes.
