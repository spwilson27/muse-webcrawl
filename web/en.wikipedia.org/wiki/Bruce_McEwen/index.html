Bruce Sherman McEwen (January 17, 1938 – January 2, 2020) was an American neuroendocrinologist at Rockefeller University, known for his pioneering work on the effects of stress hormones on the brain.

Research

In 1968 McEwen's laboratory demonstrated that the hippocampus concentrates the stress hormone corticosterone, the first evidence that steroid hormones act on specific brain structures — a discovery that helped found modern neuroendocrinology. His group went on to show that stress hormones remodel neurons: chronic stress shrinks dendrites in the hippocampus while enlarging them in the amygdala.

Allostatic load

With Eliot Stellar, McEwen developed the concept of allostatic load — the cumulative "wear and tear" on the body from repeated or chronic stress — in a widely cited 1993 paper. The concept became influential in research linking socioeconomic adversity, chronic disease, and health disparities. He authored the popular book The End of Stress as We Know It (2002).
