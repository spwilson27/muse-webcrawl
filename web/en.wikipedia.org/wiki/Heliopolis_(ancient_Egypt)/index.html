Heliopolis (ancient Egypt)

Location: Matariya, Cairo, Egypt
Region: Lower Egypt
Egyptian name: Iunu
Greek name: Ἡλιούπολις ("City of the Sun")
Principal deity: Ra-Atum

Heliopolis (Greek: Ἡλιούπολις, "City of the Sun") was a major city of ancient Egypt. It was the capital of the 13th or Heliopolite Nome of Lower Egypt and a major religious centre. Its native Egyptian name was Iunu (transliterated jwnw), meaning "the Pillars", from which the Hebrew On and Coptic Ōn derive. The site is now located in the northern suburbs of modern Cairo, in the districts of Matariya and Ain Shams.

Religion

Heliopolis was the cult centre of the sun god Ra, syncretised with the creator god Atum. Its theologians developed one of the principal Egyptian creation myths, in which Atum emerged from the primordial waters on the first mound of earth and generated the Ennead, the company of nine gods including Shu, Tefnut, Geb, Nut, Osiris, Isis, Seth and Nephthys. The sacred benben stone, prototype of the obelisk and the pyramidion, was kept in the temple of Ra, and the mythical Bennu bird — associated with the Greek phoenix — belonged to the city.

History

Occupied from predynastic times, Heliopolis flourished through the Old, Middle and New Kingdoms; its priesthood was renowned for learning, and Greek writers such as Herodotus and Strabo described the Egyptians of Heliopolis as the most learned in the country. Pharaohs including Djoser, Senusret I and Ramesses II built temples and obelisks there. The city declined under Persian, Greek and Roman rule, and much of its stone was later reused in medieval Cairo.

Archaeology

Little survives above ground: the most conspicuous monument is the granite obelisk of Senusret I, erected around 1940 BC and the oldest surviving obelisk in Egypt. Excavations by Egyptian and German missions in Matariya have uncovered temple remains, colossal statues, workshops and cemeteries, including the tombs of the high priests of Ra and the burial vaults of the sacred Mnevis bulls.
