Forestry England is an executive agency of the Forestry Commission that manages the nation's forests — the public forest estate in England. The estate covers roughly 250,000 hectares, about 2% of England's land area, and includes well-known forests such as the New Forest, the Forest of Dean, Kielder Forest, Thetford Forest, and Sherwood Pines.

Role

Forestry England manages the estate for timber production, wildlife conservation, carbon storage, and public recreation. Its woods receive tens of millions of visits a year and host visitor centres, cycling and walking trails, campsites, and Forest Live concerts. Much of the estate is designated as Sites of Special Scientific Interest or other protected status.

History

The agency succeeded the Forest Enterprise arm of the Forestry Commission. Plans in 2010–11 to sell off parts of the public forest estate provoked strong public opposition and were abandoned, and the Forestry and Land Management legislation that followed kept the estate in public ownership. It took its present name, Forestry England, on 1 April 2019 as part of the Commission's restructuring.
