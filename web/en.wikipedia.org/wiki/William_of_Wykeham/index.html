William of Wykeham (1323/24 – 27 September 1404) was an English bishop and statesman who served as Bishop of Winchester from 1366 and twice as Chancellor of England, and who founded two of England's most enduring educational institutions: Winchester College and New College, Oxford.

Rise

Wykeham rose from modest Hampshire origins through skill as a builder and administrator. Employed in the works at Windsor Castle under Edward III, he became the king's secretary and chief minister in all but name, accumulating ecclesiastical offices to an extent that drew criticism. Consecrated Bishop of Winchester in 1367 — then England's richest see — he served as chancellor from 1367 to 1371. Political attack during the crisis of 1376 (the Good Parliament) cost him his temporalities for a time, but he returned to office under Richard II, serving a second chancellorship in 1389–1391.

Foundations

Wykeham's lasting monument is educational. In 1382 he founded Winchester College, a grammar school for seventy poor scholars, and in 1379 he had obtained the charter for New College, Oxford, formally the College of St Mary of Winchester in Oxford, whose scholars were to come from Winchester. The two foundations, linked in design, quadrangles, statutes and chapel worship, formed a single educational system — a ladder from school to university — that became a model for later foundations such as Eton and King's College, Cambridge. His motto, "Manners makyth man", is still used by both institutions.
