Rational function

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a rational function is any function that can be defined by a rational fraction — an algebraic fraction whose numerator and denominator are both polynomials. The coefficients of the polynomials need not be rational numbers; they may be taken in any field K. The domain of a rational function is the set of values of the variables for which the denominator is not zero.

f(x) = P(x) / Q(x), where Q(x) ≠ 0.

Definitions

Every polynomial function is a rational function (with denominator the constant polynomial 1). A function that cannot be written in this form, such as f(x) = sin(x), is not a rational function. The formal theory treats rational functions as equivalence classes of formal quotients of polynomials, forming the field of rational functions, which is the field of fractions of the polynomial ring.

Properties

Rational functions are continuous wherever they are defined. Their graphs may have vertical asymptotes at the real zeros of the denominator, horizontal or oblique asymptotes determined by the relative degrees of numerator and denominator, and removable discontinuities ("holes") at points where numerator and denominator share a common factor. The derivative and the integral of a rational function can be computed in closed form; integration typically proceeds by partial fraction decomposition, producing logarithmic and arctangent terms.

Applications

Rational functions appear throughout science and engineering. In physics they describe quantities such as the electric field of distributed charges and rates in enzyme kinetics (the Michaelis–Menten equation). In control engineering, transfer functions of linear systems are rational functions of the Laplace variable. In numerical analysis, Padé approximants — rational approximations to a function — often converge far better than Taylor polynomials. In complex analysis, rational functions on the Riemann sphere are exactly the meromorphic functions taking values in the extended complex plane.
