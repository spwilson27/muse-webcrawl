Aqaba

Aqaba, Jordan's only coastal city, on the northern shore of the Red Sea.

Aqaba (Arabic: ٱلْعَقَبَة, al-ʿAqaba) is the only coastal city in Jordan and the largest and most populous city on the Gulf of Aqaba. Situated in southernmost Jordan, Aqaba is the administrative center of the Aqaba Governorate, with a population of roughly 150,000. The city plays a major role in the development of the Jordanian economy through its port — Jordan's only seaport — and through tourism, owing to its warm climate, beaches, and coral reefs.

History

The site of Aqaba has been inhabited since about 4000 BCE, benefiting from its strategic position at the junction of trade routes between Asia, Africa, and Europe. The ancient city of Ayla was founded around 650 CE during the early Islamic era. In 1917, during the First World War, the Battle of Aqaba saw Arab forces advised by T. E. Lawrence ("Lawrence of Arabia") capture the Ottoman port, an event dramatized in the film Lawrence of Arabia.

Economy and tourism

The Port of Aqaba handles most of Jordan's imports and exports, including phosphates and potash. Since 2001 the city has been part of the Aqaba Special Economic Zone, attracting investment in industry and tourism. Aqaba is a popular destination for scuba diving and snorkeling on the coral reefs of the Gulf of Aqaba, and the nearby resort city of Eilat lies just across the border in Israel.
