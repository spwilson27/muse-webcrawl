Twelve-tone equal temperament

Twelve-tone equal temperament (12-TET, 12-ET, or 12-EDO) is the musical system of tuning in which the octave is divided into twelve equal parts, known as semitones. It is the dominant tuning system of Western music. In 12-TET the ratio between adjacent semitones is the twelfth root of two (21/12 ≈ 1.059463), and each semitone spans exactly 100 cents.

Properties

Because every interval is the same size in every key, music can be transposed freely without retuning. The cost is that no interval except the octave is acoustically pure: the equal-tempered major third, for example, is about 14 cents wider than the just 5:4 third, and the perfect fifth is about 2 cents narrower than the just 3:2 fifth. Precursors of the system were calculated by the Chinese prince Zhu Zaiyu in 1584 and described in Europe by Simon Stevin around 1585; keyboard instruments gradually adopted it during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, displacing meantone and well temperaments.
