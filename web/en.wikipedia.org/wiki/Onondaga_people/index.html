The Onondaga people (Onondaga: Onoñda'gega', "People of the Hills") are a Native American people, one of the five original nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy. Their homeland lies in present-day Onondaga County, New York, south of Syracuse. As the confederacy's capital, Onondaga is the seat of the Grand Council: the Onondaga are Keepers of the Council Fire and keepers of the league's wampum records, and the Tadodaho, the league's principal chief, is an Onondaga title.

History

According to Haudenosaunee tradition, the Peacemaker and Hiawatha completed the founding of the League at Onondaga, where the last resisting leader, Tadodaho, was won over and the Tree of Peace planted. During the colonial era the centrally located Onondaga hosted diplomacy with French and English officials. The Sullivan Expedition of 1779 largely bypassed their main town, but post-war state land purchases reduced their territory to the present reservation of about 7,300 acres.

The Onondaga Nation today

The Onondaga Nation is a federally recognised sovereign nation governed by traditional chiefs selected by clan mothers according to the Great Law of Peace; it is notable for retaining traditional governance and for never accepting U.S. citizenship en masse. The nation issues its own passports, famously used by the Iroquois Nationals lacrosse team — lacrosse ("the Creator's game") holds deep cultural significance.

Onondaga Lake

Sacred Onondaga Lake, at whose shore the Peacemaker is honoured, became one of the most polluted lakes in the United States from industrial waste; a long-running remediation programme, including an Onondaga Nation land-rights action asserting environmental claims, has accompanied its cleanup.
