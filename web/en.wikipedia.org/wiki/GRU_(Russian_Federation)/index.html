The Main Directorate of the General Staff of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation, formerly the Main Intelligence Directorate, and still commonly known by its previous abbreviation GRU (Russian: ГРУ), is the foreign military intelligence agency of the General Staff of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation. Unlike Russia's other security and intelligence agencies — such as the SVR, FSB and FSO — whose heads report directly to the President of Russia, the Director of the GRU is subordinate to the Russian military command, i.e. the Chief of the General Staff and the Minister of Defence.

History

The GRU was created in May 1992 as the successor in the Russian Federation to the Soviet GRU, from which it inherited its personnel, stations and the Spetsnaz special forces brigades. Its headquarters, nicknamed "the Aquarium", is located in Moscow's Khoroshovsky District.

Operations and controversies

The agency has been publicly attributed a wide range of operations by Western governments. GRU units fought in the Chechen wars, the 2008 war in Georgia, the annexation of Crimea in 2014 and the intervention in Syria. Unit 26165 has been identified by the US and Dutch authorities as the cyber unit behind the hacking group known as Fancy Bear (APT28), the 2016 Democratic National Committee hack, and intrusions against the OPCW. Unit 29155, reported by the New York Times and Bellingcat, has been linked to the 2018 poisoning of Sergei and Yulia Skripal in Salisbury, England, and to explosions at ammunition depots in Bulgaria and the Czech Republic. GRU officers were charged in the United States in connection with interference in the 2016 presidential election.

Structure

The GRU is organised into numbered directorates responsible for geographic regions and technical collection, controls military attachés abroad, and commands Spetsnaz GRU brigades. Its director from 2016 until his death in office in November 2018 was Igor Korobov; he was succeeded by Vice Admiral Igor Kostyukov, the first naval officer to hold the post.
