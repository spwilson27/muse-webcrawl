Girolamo Savonarola (21 September 1452 – 23 May 1498) was an Italian Dominican friar and preacher whose fiery denunciations of corruption in church and state made him the effective ruler of Florence from 1494 until his excommunication, arrest and execution in 1498.

Rise to power

Born in Ferrara, Savonarola entered the Dominican order in 1475. After years of preaching across Lombardy and Tuscany, he became prior of San Marco in Florence in 1491. His sermons prophesied a coming scourge that would renew the Church, and many Florentines saw the French invasion of Italy in 1494 and the expulsion of the Medici as their fulfilment. Savonarola helped shape the new Florentine republic, pressing for moral reform and a more popular government under a Great Council.

Bonfire of the Vanities

His campaign against worldliness culminated in the "Bonfire of the Vanities" of 7 February 1497, when followers burned mirrors, cosmetics, fine clothes, books, artworks and musical instruments in the Piazza della Signoria.

Downfall

Pope Alexander VI, a frequent target of Savonarola's preaching, excommunicated him in 1497. When an ordeal by fire arranged in April 1498 collapsed amid rain and disorder, popular support evaporated. Savonarola was arrested, tortured, and on 23 May 1498 hanged and burned in the same piazza; his ashes were thrown into the Arno.
