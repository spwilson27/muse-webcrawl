Lomnický štít

Lomnický štít
Elevation: 2,634 m (8,642 ft)
Range: High Tatras
Country: Slovakia

Lomnický štít (German: Lomnitzer Spitze; Hungarian: Lomnici-csúcs) is one of the highest and most visited peaks of the High Tatra mountains in northern Slovakia, rising to 2,634 metres (8,642 ft) above sea level. It is the second-highest peak in Slovakia and in the whole Carpathian range after Gerlachovský štít.

Access

The peak is connected by cable car from Tatranská Lomnica, reaching the summit via the station at Skalnaté pleso. Completed in 1940, the cable car was for a time among the most modern in Europe, and the summit terminus was one of the highest buildings in Europe. Ascent on foot is possible only with a licensed mountain guide.

Observatory

On the summit stands the Lomnický štít Observatory, built between 1957 and 1962, operated by the Slovak Academy of Sciences. At 2,632 m it is one of the highest astronomical observatories in Central Europe, specialising in observations of the solar corona. A meteorological station on the peak has provided continuous measurements since 1940.

History

The first recorded ascent was made by the shoemaker and amateur miner Jakub Fábry in the 1790s, although local miners had likely visited the summit earlier. Until the early 20th century the peak was mistakenly thought to be the highest in the Tatras.
