Beloved (novel)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The first edition cover of Beloved by Toni Morrison (1987)]
The first edition cover of Beloved by Toni Morrison (1987)

Beloved is a 1987 novel by the American author Toni Morrison. Set in Ohio after the American Civil War, it tells of Sethe, a formerly enslaved woman haunted by the ghost of her dead daughter — whom she killed to prevent her re-enslavement — and by the trauma of slavery itself. The novel won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction in 1988 and is widely considered Morrison's masterpiece and one of the greatest American novels; in 2006 a New York Times survey of writers and critics named it the best American fiction of the previous 25 years.

Plot

In 1873 Cincinnati, Sethe lives at 124 Bluestone Road with her daughter Denver, in a house spiteful with a baby's ghost. Paul D, a fellow survivor of the Kentucky plantation Sweet Home, arrives and exorcises the spirit — which returns embodied as a young woman calling herself Beloved, the word on the dead child's headstone. As Beloved consumes Sethe's life, the community's women finally intervene; the novel interleaves present action with searing memories — the schoolteacher's pseudoscientific cruelty, the theft of Sethe's milk, the escape and the infanticide in the shed as slavecatchers arrived.

Themes and basis

Morrison based Sethe on Margaret Garner, an enslaved woman who in 1856 killed her daughter rather than see her returned to slavery. The novel's concepts — 'rememory', the unspeakability of trauma, motherhood under slavery, the haunting of national history — are worked through in a lyrical, nonlinear prose blending realism, folklore and the Gothic.

Reception

Dedicated to 'Sixty Million and more' (the dead of the Middle Passage and slavery), Beloved anchored Morrison's 1993 Nobel Prize in Literature. It is a staple of American syllabuses and, consequently, a frequent target of book challenges. Oprah Winfrey produced and starred in the 1998 film adaptation.
