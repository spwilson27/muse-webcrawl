Nissen fundoplication

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Schematic of the Nissen wrap

Nissen fundoplication is a surgical procedure to treat gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) and hiatal hernia. In the procedure, the gastric fundus — the upper part of the stomach — is wrapped completely, 360 degrees, around the lower end of the esophagus and sutured in place, reinforcing the closing function of the lower esophageal sphincter. When the stomach contracts, the wrap closes the esophagus instead of allowing acid to reflux into it.

History

The operation was developed by the German-born surgeon Rudolph Nissen, who performed the first procedure in 1955 and published the results in 1956. Originally an open operation, it is now most commonly performed laparoscopically, which reduces recovery time and postoperative pain.

Technique

After reduction of any hiatal hernia and repair of the diaphragmatic crura, the fundus is mobilised — often dividing the short gastric vessels — and passed behind the esophagus. A short, floppy wrap of about 2 cm is then created over a bougie to calibrate the tightness of the repair. Partial-wrap variants, such as the Toupet (posterior 270°) and Dor (anterior) fundoplications, are used in patients with weak esophageal motility, who are at higher risk of postoperative swallowing difficulty with a full wrap.

Outcomes and complications

Laparoscopic Nissen fundoplication controls reflux symptoms in the great majority of appropriately selected patients, and many are able to stop acid-suppression medication. Recognised complications include dysphagia, gas-bloat syndrome (inability to belch or vomit), wrap migration or disruption, and recurrence of reflux; some patients require revisional surgery or resume medication over the long term. It remains the most common antireflux operation worldwide.
