Articles of War

The Articles of War are a set of regulations defining military law, discipline and the conduct of soldiers and sailors. Historically, Britain and the United States each maintained Articles of War governing their armed forces; in the United States they were replaced in 1951 by the Uniform Code of Military Justice.

British Articles of War

English Articles of War date to the medieval period and were issued by the Crown to govern armies on campaign. From 1689 the Mutiny Acts gave statutory authority for military discipline, and naval Articles of War — famously read aloud to ships' companies — prescribed severe penalties, including death for offences such as cowardice or striking an officer.

American Articles of War

The Continental Congress adopted Articles of War in 1775, modelled on British practice, and revised them in 1776 and 1806. The Articles governed courts-martial and offences from desertion to espionage until the Uniform Code of Military Justice took effect in 1951, unifying the law of all U.S. services.
