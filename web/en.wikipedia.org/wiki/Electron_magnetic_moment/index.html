Electron magnetic moment

The electron magnetic moment is the magnetic dipole moment of an electron, arising from its intrinsic spin and its electric charge. Its value is approximately −9.2847647 × 10−24 joules per tesla, close to one Bohr magneton. The electron's magnetic moment is one of the most precisely measured and theoretically calculated quantities in physics.

The g-factor and the anomalous moment

The Dirac equation predicts that the electron's spin g-factor should be exactly 2. Precision measurements instead give g ≈ 2.00231930436. The small difference, the anomalous magnetic moment, arises from quantum electrodynamic (QED) corrections — the electron's interaction with virtual photons and particle-antiparticle pairs. Julian Schwinger calculated the leading correction, α/2π, in 1948.

Test of QED

Agreement between the measured anomalous magnetic moment and QED calculations carried to five loops (involving over 12,000 Feynman diagrams) extends to more than ten significant figures, making it one of the most stringent tests of any physical theory. The measurement is also used to determine the fine-structure constant α with high precision, and discrepancies could signal physics beyond the Standard Model.
