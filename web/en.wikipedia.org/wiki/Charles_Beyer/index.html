Charles Frederick Beyer (14 August 1813 – 2 June 1876), originally Carl Friedrich Beyer, was a celebrated German-born locomotive designer and builder, and co-founder of the locomotive building company Beyer, Peacock and Company in Manchester, England. He is regarded as one of the leading locomotive engineers of the Victorian era, and the firm he founded became one of the world's largest exporters of steam locomotives.

Charles Beyer (1813–1876), locomotive engineer

Early life

Beyer was born in Plauen, Saxony, the son of a weaver. He was apprenticed in the machine works and later studied engineering. His talent for mechanical drawing brought him to the attention of Georg Borsig, the Berlin locomotive builder, but Beyer chose to seek his fortune in England, then the centre of the railway world.

Career in England

Beyer arrived in England in 1834 and found work in Manchester, the heart of the cotton and engineering industries. He became chief draughtsman and then works manager at Sharp, Roberts and Company, one of the leading locomotive builders of the day, where he worked under the influence of Richard Roberts. There he gained the experience in locomotive design that would define his career.

Beyer, Peacock and Company

In 1854 Beyer left Sharp, Roberts to found his own locomotive works in partnership with Richard Peacock, formerly locomotive superintendent of the Manchester, Sheffield and Lincolnshire Railway. The firm, Beyer, Peacock and Company, established the Gorton Foundry in Gorton, Manchester. Under Beyer's technical direction the works produced locomotives noted for their quality, reliability and elegant finish, supplying railways across Britain and throughout the world, including India, Australia, South America and Europe. The company remained a major locomotive builder well into the twentieth century.

Later life and death

Beyer became a naturalised British subject and a wealthy man. He was a member of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers and the Institution of Civil Engineers. He died at his home, Lymm Hall in Cheshire, in 1876. His estate supported charitable and educational causes, and he is commemorated for his contribution to Manchester's engineering heritage.
