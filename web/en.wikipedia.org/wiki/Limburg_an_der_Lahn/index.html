Limburg an der Lahn (officially abbreviated Limburg a. d. Lahn) is the district seat of Limburg-Weilburg in Hesse, Germany. It lies on both banks of the Lahn river between the Taunus and Westerwald hills, roughly halfway between Frankfurt am Main and Koblenz.

The town is best known for its largely intact medieval old town of half-timbered houses and for Limburg Cathedral, the late Romanesque church of St. George with seven spires, which stands on a rock above the river and is one of the most famous church silhouettes in Germany.

History

Limburg was first mentioned in 910, when Konrad Kurzbold founded a collegiate church on the Limburg rock; a castle and market settlement followed at an important crossing of the Lahn on the trade route between Cologne and Frankfurt. The town received city rights around 1214. Its merchants and the chapter of St. George prospered; after fires in the 13th century the town was rebuilt with the half-timbered houses that still characterise it. Limburg passed to Hesse in 1866 with the Prussian annexation of Nassau.

Sights

Limburg Cathedral, consecrated in 1235, is a masterpiece of late Romanesque and early Gothic architecture, painted in its original colour scheme of ochre and white. Below it lie the old bridge over the Lahn (1315), the old town hall, and streets such as Fischmarkt and Plötze lined with timber-framed houses from the 16th and 17th centuries. Limburg Castle stands beside the cathedral.

Catholic diocese

Limburg is the seat of a Roman Catholic diocese founded in 1821. The town came to international attention in 2013–2014 when Bishop Franz-Peter Tebartz-van Elst was removed after controversy over the cost of a new diocesan residence, reported at about €31 million.

Transport

Limburg Süd station on the Cologne–Frankfurt high-speed line is the only ICE stop in Hesse outside Frankfurt, putting the town about 40 minutes from Frankfurt Airport. The A3 motorway passes nearby.
