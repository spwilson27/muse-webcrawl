Cloak

A cloak is a type of loose garment worn over indoor clothing, serving the same purpose as an overcoat: it protects the wearer from cold, rain, or wind, and may form part of a fashionable outfit or uniform. Cloaks have been worn since ancient times and are typically sleeveless, fastening at the neck or shoulders and falling to the knees or below.

[Image: A long woollen travelling cloak with a clasp at the throat]

History

The Greek himation and the Roman paenula and lacerna were cloak-like outer garments. In medieval Europe the cloak was standard outdoor dress for both sexes, often hooded for travellers. Military and ceremonial cloaks survived long after everyday use declined: the Spanish cape, the officer's boat cloak, and academic and chivalric mantles all preserve the form. The word itself derives from Old French cloque, "bell", describing the garment's flared shape.

Types

Styles include the short cape, the hooded travelling cloak, the opera cloak, and waterproof oilskin or waxed-cotton cloaks. In some cultures the cloak carried specific meaning — the woollen aba of the Middle East, the Māori korowai, or the Bhutanese gho's accompanying kabney sash.

In culture

Cloaks are a staple of fantasy and Gothic fiction, from the "cloak of invisibility" of folklore and the Harry Potter books to the black cloak of Count Dracula. The idiom "cloak and dagger" refers to espionage and secrecy, evoking the theatrical villains of earlier melodrama.
