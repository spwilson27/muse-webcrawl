Augmented reality

Augmented reality
DefinitionReal-world environment enhanced by computer-generated perceptual information
Coined1990, Tom Caudell (Boeing)
HardwareSmartphones, smartglasses, head-mounted displays
ContrastVirtual reality (fully immersive)

Augmented reality (AR) is an interactive experience that combines the real world and computer-generated 3D content, so that virtual objects appear to coexist in the same space as real ones. The content can span multiple sensory modalities, including visual, auditory, and haptic. AR is one of the key technologies on the reality–virtuality continuum described by Paul Milgram, sitting between the real environment and fully immersive virtual reality.

Technology

AR systems track the user's viewpoint and register virtual content to physical locations, using cameras, inertial sensors, GPS, and computer vision techniques such as SLAM (simultaneous localisation and mapping). Displays range from handheld phones and tablets to optical see-through glasses and video passthrough headsets. Software platforms such as Apple's ARKit, Google's ARCore, and Microsoft's HoloLens line made markerless tracking widely available from the late 2010s.

History

Precursors include Morton Heilig's Sensorama (1962) and Ivan Sutherland's 1968 head-mounted display, the "Sword of Damocles." Tom Caudell coined the term in 1990 for a Boeing wire-harness assembly aid. AR reached mass audiences with mobile games and apps — most famously Pokémon Go (2016), downloaded over a billion times — and with social-media face filters and camera effects.

Applications

Applications include navigation overlays, retail visualisation (placing furniture or trying on glasses virtually), industrial maintenance and warehouse guidance, surgical planning and medical training, education, military head-up displays, and cultural heritage experiences in museums and tourism.
