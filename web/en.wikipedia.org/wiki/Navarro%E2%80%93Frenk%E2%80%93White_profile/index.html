The Navarro–Frenk–White (NFW) profile is a spatial mass distribution of dark matter fitted to dark matter halos identified in N-body simulations. Proposed by Julio Navarro, Carlos Frenk, and Simon White in papers published in 1996 and 1997, it describes a universal density profile whose form is characterised by two parameters: a characteristic density and a scale radius.

Formulation

The density falls as the inverse of radius in the inner regions (a "cusp") and as the inverse cube of radius in the outer regions. The profile is commonly expressed in terms of the halo's virial radius and concentration parameter, which relates the virial radius to the scale radius. Simulated halos of all masses, from dwarf galaxies to galaxy clusters, were found to be well fit by the profile with concentrations varying systematically with mass and redshift.

Observational status

The NFW profile underlies much of modern cosmological modelling, including halo occupation models and gravitational lensing analyses. However, observations of some dwarf and low-surface-brightness galaxies suggest shallower central densities than the predicted cusp — the so-called core–cusp problem — although baryonic feedback may reconcile simulations with observations. Alternative profiles, such as the Einasto profile, can fit simulated halos still more closely.
