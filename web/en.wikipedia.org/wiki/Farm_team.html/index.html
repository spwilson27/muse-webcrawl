Farm team

A farm team is a team or club whose primary purpose is to provide experience and training for young players, with an agreement that any successful players can move on to a higher level at a given point, usually in the same organization. Such systems are most common in North American professional sports.

Baseball

In Minor League Baseball, farm teams are owned by or affiliated with Major League Baseball clubs. The system, developed in the 1920s and 1930s principally by Branch Rickey of the St. Louis Cardinals, allows parent clubs to develop young talent through successive levels (Rookie, Single-A, Double-A, Triple-A) before promotion to the majors.

Other sports

Analogous arrangements exist in ice hockey (minor leagues affiliated with NHL teams) and basketball (the NBA G League). Historically the term derives from the idea of "growing" players like crops on a farm.
