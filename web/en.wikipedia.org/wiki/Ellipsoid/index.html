Ellipsoid

[Image: An ellipsoid formed by rotating an ellipse about its minor axis — the oblate spheroid of the Earth]

Overview

An ellipsoid is a surface that can be obtained from a sphere by deforming it by means of directional scalings, with quadratic equation x²/a² + y²/b² + z²/c² = 1. It is the three-dimensional analogue of the ellipse. Special cases include the sphere, the oblate spheroid (like the Earth, flattened at the poles) and the prolate spheroid (elongated, like a rugby ball).

Earth ellipsoid

Geodesy models the Earth as a reference ellipsoid — an oblate spheroid whose flattening is about 1/298 (WGS 84: semi-major axis 6,378,137 m, flattening 1/298.257). Local and global ellipsoids (Clarke 1866, GRS 80, WGS 84) underpin map projections, datums and GPS. Ellipsoids also model other rotating planets, and ellipsoidal coordinates and harmonics appear throughout physics and astronomy.
