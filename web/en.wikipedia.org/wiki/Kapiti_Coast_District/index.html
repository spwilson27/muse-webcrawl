Kapiti Coast District

The Kapiti Coast District is a local government district in the lower North Island of New Zealand, administered by the Kapiti Coast District Council. It stretches along the western coast of the Wellington Region, from Paekākāriki in the south to Ōtaki in the north, and takes its name from Kapiti Island, which lies about five kilometres offshore.

Geography

The district occupies a narrow coastal plain between the Tasman Sea and the Tararua Range. Its main population centres, from south to north, are Paekākāriki, Raumati, Paraparaumu, Waikanae and Ōtaki. The Waikanae River, Ōtaki River and numerous smaller streams cross the plain. Kapiti Island is a nature reserve managed by the Department of Conservation and is visible from most of the coast.

History

The coast has a long Māori history, associated with iwi including Ngāti Toa, Te Āti Awa and Ngāti Raukawa. The famous chief Te Rauparaha used Kapiti Island as a base in the early 19th century. European settlement followed, and the area developed as a holiday and retirement destination for Wellingtonians, aided by the North Island Main Trunk railway.

Transport

State Highway 1 and the Kapiti Line commuter railway link the district with Wellington. The Transmission Gully motorway, opened in 2022, provides an inland motorway route between Paekākāriki and Wellington. Kapiti Coast Airport at Paraparaumu has a long aviation history, having once been Wellington's main airport.

Communities

Paraparaumu is the commercial hub, home to the Coastlands shopping centre. Waikanae is known for its beach and gardens, Paekākāriki for its village atmosphere and Queen Elizabeth Park, and Ōtaki for its outlet shopping and Māori heritage, including Rangiātea Church.
