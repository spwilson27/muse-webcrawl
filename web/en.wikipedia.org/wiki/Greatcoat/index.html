Greatcoat

A greatcoat is a large, heavy overcoat typically made of wool, worn for warmth in cold weather. Greatcoats were a standard item of military uniform from the 17th century onwards, and civilian versions were common in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The garment usually extends below the knee, has a deep collar that can be turned up, and may be single- or double-breasted.

History

Civilian greatcoats of the 18th and 19th centuries were often made with one or more short shoulder capes, a style associated with coachmen, who sat exposed on the box of a horse-drawn coach; the multi-caped "carrick" coat is an example. Military greatcoats, by contrast, were plain, heavy and durable garments issued to soldiers for campaign in winter, and doubled as a blanket when rolled and carried over the shoulder or strapped to the pack.

During the First World War and Second World War, greatcoats in khaki, grey or field-grey wool were standard issue to armies fighting in Europe. The Russian shinel became an iconic greatcoat, issued to soldiers of the Imperial and later Soviet armies, famously rolled into a sausage-shaped bundle worn diagonally across the body when not in use.

Modern use

While largely replaced in front-line service by layered synthetic clothing and parkas, greatcoats remain in use for ceremonial duties, by sentries, and as a fashion item derived from military styling, alongside related garments such as the trench coat and the pea coat.
