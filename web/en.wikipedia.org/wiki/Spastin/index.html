Spastin

Spastin is a microtubule-severing protein in humans encoded by the SPAST gene (also known as SPG4). It belongs to the AAA (ATPases associated with diverse cellular activities) family of ATPases and uses ATP hydrolysis to cut microtubules, regulating their length and number in axons and other cells.

Function

Spastin is translated in two isoforms, a longer membrane-associated M1 form and a shorter cytosolic M87 form. Besides microtubule severing, it participates in endosomal trafficking, cytokinesis (as part of the ESCRT machinery partnership), and the shaping of the endoplasmic reticulum.

Clinical significance

Heterozygous loss-of-function mutations in SPAST are the most common cause of autosomal dominant hereditary spastic paraplegia (HSP type SPG4), accounting for roughly 40% of dominant cases. Reduced severing activity leads to degeneration of the long corticospinal tract axons, causing progressive stiffness and weakness of the legs.
