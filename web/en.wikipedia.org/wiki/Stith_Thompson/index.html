Stith Thompson

Stith Thompson (March 7, 1885 – January 10, 1976) was an American folklorist best known for his monumental Motif-Index of Folk-Literature (1932–36; revised 1955–58), the standard classification system for the narrative elements ("motifs") found in folktales, myths and ballads around the world. With Antti Aarne he also developed the Aarne–Thompson tale-type index, later expanded by Hans-Jörg Uther into the ATU Index.

Life and career

Thompson was born in Bloomfield, Kentucky, and educated at the University of Wisconsin, the University of California, Berkeley (MA) and Harvard (PhD, 1914). He taught at the University of Texas and then, from 1921 until his retirement in 1955, at Indiana University, where he founded one of the first folklore programmes in the United States and made Indiana a world centre of the discipline. With the Danish scholar Axel Olrik and others he helped institutionalise the "historic-geographic" (Finnish) method in America.

Works

The Motif-Index organises thousands of motifs — from "Magic object" to "Deceptive bargain" — under 23 capital-letter categories (A: mythological motifs; B: animal motifs; F: magic and marvels; and so on), each subdivided numerically. It remains the first tool consulted by scholars tracing a story element across cultures. Thompson's other works include The Folktale (1946), a classic survey of narrative scholarship, and the anthology One Hundred Favorite Folktales (1968). His translation of Aarne's index, The Types of the Folktale (1928; 2nd revision 1961), gave the classification its modern form.

Legacy

Thompson presided over the American Folklore Society and was central to establishing folklore as an academic discipline in the United States. The classification systems bearing his name continue to structure tale scholarship in the digital era, even as later folklorists have questioned their European narrative bias.
