Prolog

Prolog is a logic programming language associated with artificial intelligence and computational linguistics. Its name comes from the French programmation en logique ("programming in logic"). In Prolog, program logic is expressed as facts and rules describing relations, and computation proceeds by answering queries through unification and backtracking search, based on the resolution principle and Horn clauses.

History

Prolog was created around 1972 by Alain Colmerauer and Philippe Roussel at Aix-Marseille University, in collaboration with Robert Kowalski at the University of Edinburgh, who provided the procedural interpretation of Horn clauses. It gained prominence in the 1980s as the language of Japan's Fifth Generation Computer Systems project. Influential implementations include SWI-Prolog, GNU Prolog, and SICStus Prolog; the language is standardized as ISO/IEC 13211.

Language features

A Prolog program consists of clauses: facts such as parent(tom, bob). and rules such as grandparent(X, Z) :- parent(X, Y), parent(Y, Z). Execution uses depth-first search with backtracking, built-in unification, and features such as the cut operator (!) for controlling search, negation as failure, and (in modern systems) constraint logic programming extensions. Lists and recursive data structures are handled naturally.

Applications

Prolog has been used in expert systems, natural language processing, theorem proving, automated planning, and symbolic AI. An early version of IBM's Watson question-answering system used Prolog, and the language remains influential in teaching logic programming.
