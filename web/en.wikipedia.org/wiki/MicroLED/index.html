MicroLED, also known as micro-LED, mLED or µLED, is an emerging flat-panel display technology consisting of arrays of microscopic LEDs (typically smaller than 100 micrometres) forming the individual picture elements. Each pixel is self-emissive and consists of red, green and blue sub-pixel diodes, so no backlight or colour filters are required.

Advantages

Compared with OLED displays, microLED offers higher peak brightness (potentially exceeding 100,000 nits), better energy efficiency, a wider colour gamut and near-perfect black levels, together with the longer lifetime and resistance to burn-in of inorganic gallium nitride diodes. Response times are in the nanosecond range, far faster than liquid-crystal displays. Modularity allows panels of virtually any size to be assembled from tiles.

Challenges

The principal obstacle to mass adoption is mass transfer: millions of microscopic dies must be moved from their growth wafers to the display backplane with extremely high speed, placement accuracy and yield, and defective pixels repaired economically. Efficiency of very small red LEDs also falls sharply as die size shrinks, and inspection and testing costs remain high. These factors have so far confined microLED mainly to premium products.

History and products

MicroLED arrays were first demonstrated in 2000 by Hongxing Jiang and Jingyu Lin at Texas Tech University. Sony showed a 55-inch "Crystal LED Display" prototype in 2012 and later commercialised modular CLEDIS walls. Samsung introduced its 146-inch modular television "The Wall" in 2018 and has since sold large microLED televisions at prices around $100,000 and above. The technology is also pursued for smartwatches, augmented-reality glasses and automotive displays, where its brightness and efficiency are especially valuable.
