Market research

Market research is an organized effort to gather information about target markets and customers: know about them, starting with who they are. It is an important component of business strategy and a major factor in maintaining competitiveness. Market research helps to identify and analyze the needs of a market, the market size, and the competition. Its techniques encompass both qualitative techniques such as focus groups, in-depth interviews, and ethnography, as well as quantitative techniques such as customer surveys and analysis of secondary data.

Market research, which includes social and opinion research, is the systematic gathering and interpretation of information about individuals or organizations using statistical and analytical methods and techniques of the applied social sciences to gain insight or support decision making.

History

Market research grew alongside advertising in the 1920s. Early pioneers included Daniel Starch and George Gallup, whose polling methods were adapted from political research. Charles Coolidge Parlin of the Curtis Publishing Company is often called the father of commercial research. After the Second World War, techniques such as motivational research, panel data, and computer-assisted analysis expanded the field, and large research firms came to serve multinational clients. The advent of the internet transformed data collection through online panels, web analytics, and social-media listening.

Types

Primary and secondary research

Primary research is collected first-hand for a specific purpose, through surveys, interviews, focus groups, observations, or experiments. Secondary research draws on existing sources such as government statistics, trade publications, company reports, and previously published studies.

Qualitative and quantitative research

Qualitative research explores attitudes and motivations in depth with small samples; quantitative research measures incidence and relationships numerically with larger, structured samples. Market research is also divided by purpose into exploratory, descriptive, and causal studies, and by client use into research for market sizing, segmentation, product and concept testing, brand tracking, pricing, and customer satisfaction.

Process

A typical research project proceeds through defining the problem and objectives, developing a research plan, collecting information, analyzing it, and presenting findings to decision makers. Industry standards for ethics and quality are maintained by bodies such as ESOMAR and the Insights Association, and privacy rules such as the GDPR affect how personal data in research may be used.
