Terrytoons was an American animation studio in New Rochelle, New York, founded by Paul Terry, Frank Moser and Joseph Coffman in 1929. For most of its existence it produced theatrical cartoon shorts for distribution by 20th Century Fox. The studio was one of the most prolific of the Golden Age of American animation, though it worked on notably low budgets, and is best known for characters including Mighty Mouse, Heckle and Jeckle, Gandy Goose, Dinky Duck and Deputy Dawg.

History

Paul Terry had earlier produced the Aesop's Fables series with Amedee J. Van Beuren. After parting with Van Beuren he set up his own studio, releasing its first sound cartoon, Dinner Time, around the time of Disney's Steamboat Willie. Terry was famous for economising — he reportedly boasted of producing cartoons more cheaply than any competitor — and resisted costly innovations, though the studio eventually adopted colour (initially two-strip processes, later Technicolor).

The studio's biggest success, Mighty Mouse, debuted in 1942 as "Super Mouse" in The Mouse of Tomorrow. The magpies Heckle and Jeckle followed in 1946 (The Talking Magpies). Director Gene Deitch briefly modernised the studio's output in the mid-1950s with characters such as John Doormat and Clint Clobber.

CBS era and television

In 1955 Terry sold the studio and its library to CBS. Terrytoons became a division of CBS, producing for television as well as theatres: Mighty Mouse Playhouse was an early Saturday-morning success, and the studio created Deputy Dawg (1960), The Hector Heathcote Show and the Bakshi-era Mighty Heroes. Animator Ralph Bakshi began his career at Terrytoons, rising to creative director before leaving in 1967. Theatrical production ended in 1968, and the studio wound down by 1972. The Terrytoons library is now controlled by Paramount (via CBS's successor companies).
