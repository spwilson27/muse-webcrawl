Festival Internacional Cervantino

The Festival Internacional Cervantino (FIC), popularly known as El Cervantino, is an annual festival of the performing arts held in the city of Guanajuato, Mexico. It is one of the most important cultural festivals in Latin America, presenting theatre, music, opera, dance, film and visual arts from countries around the world.

History

The festival began in 1972, growing out of performances of the Entremeses — short comic plays by Miguel de Cervantes — staged by university students in the plazas of Guanajuato since the 1950s, directed by Enrique Ruelas. In 1972 the Mexican government expanded it into an international festival, keeping the name in honour of Cervantes, author of Don Quixote. Each edition invites a guest country and a guest Mexican state.

Events

Events take place in venues throughout the city, including the Teatro Juárez, the Alhóndiga de Granaditas and numerous open-air plazas. Street performances and informal gatherings, known as callejoneadas accompanied by student musical groups (estudiantinas), fill the city during the festival weeks in October.
