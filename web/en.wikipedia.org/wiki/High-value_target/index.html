High-value target


Iraqi most-wanted playing cards

In United States military terminology, a high-value target (HVT) is the term for a person or resource that an enemy commander requires for the successful completion of a mission. The term has been used widely in news media during the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan to describe senior insurgent and terrorist leaders.

Definition and usage

US Army doctrine defines a high-value target as an asset that the enemy commander requires for the completion of a mission, whose loss would seriously degrade important enemy functions throughout the area of interest. Closely related concepts include the high-payoff target (HPT), whose engagement offers particular benefit to friendly forces. The term "high-value individual" (HVI) is sometimes used for persons, such as senior leaders of al-Qaeda or the Taliban.

Notable examples

Famous HVTs have included Osama bin Laden, killed in Pakistan in 2011 by US Navy SEALs; Abu Musab al-Zarqawi, leader of al-Qaeda in Iraq, killed in 2006; Saddam Hussein, captured in 2003 (he was the "Ace of Spades" in the coalition's most-wanted Iraqi playing cards); and Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi, the Islamic State leader who died during a US raid in Syria in 2019. Operations against HVTs — often conducted by special operations forces such as Joint Special Operations Command — became a central feature of US counterterrorism strategy, though analysts debate the long-term effectiveness of leadership "decapitation" against networked organizations.
