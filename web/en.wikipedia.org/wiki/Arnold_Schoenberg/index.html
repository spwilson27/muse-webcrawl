Arnold Schoenberg

Arnold Schoenberg (13 September 1874 – 13 July 1951) was an Austrian-American composer, music theorist, teacher and writer. He was among the first modernists and a leader of the Second Viennese School.

Arnold Schoenberg
Born13 September 1874, Vienna, Austria
Died13 July 1951, Los Angeles, California
EraModern
Known forTwelve-tone technique, atonality

Works

Early tonal works include Verklärte Nacht (1899) and Gurre-Lieder. He moved to atonality in works such as Pierrot lunaire (1912), then developed the twelve-tone (dodecaphonic) method in the 1920s, used in the Suite for Piano Op. 25 and the opera Moses und Aron.

Life

Largely self-taught, Schoenberg taught Alban Berg and Anton Webern. Fleeing Nazi persecution in 1933 because of his Jewish heritage, he emigrated to the US, taught at UCLA, and became an American citizen. He famously suffered triskaidekaphobia.

Legacy

His emancipation of dissonance profoundly influenced 20th-century music, serialism and film scoring.
