Five-spice powder

TypeSpice blend
Place of originChina
Main ingredientsStar anise, cloves, cinnamon, Sichuan pepper, fennel seeds

Five-spice powder (Chinese: 五香粉; pinyin: wǔxiāng fěn) is a spice mixture of five or more spices used predominantly in Chinese and Taiwanese cuisine, and also appearing in Vietnamese and other Asian cooking. It aims to encompass the five primary flavours — sweet, sour, bitter, pungent and salty.

Ingredients

The most common recipe combines star anise, cloves, Chinese cinnamon (cassia), Sichuan pepper and fennel seeds, ground together. Regional and commercial variations may substitute or add ingredients such as ginger, nutmeg, turmeric, dried orange peel, galangal or liquorice.

Uses

The powder is a hallmark seasoning of Cantonese roast meats such as char siu and siu yuk, and is used in marinades, stir-fries, red-cooked dishes, and as a component of spiced salt served with fried foods. It is potent and generally used sparingly.
