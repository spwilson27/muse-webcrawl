Camming device

A spring-loaded camming device (also SLCD, or generically a "cam" or "friend") is a piece of rock climbing or mountaineering protection equipment. It consists of two, three or four cams mounted on a common axle or two adjacent axles, so that pulling on the axle forces the cams to spread farther apart.

Operation

The climber squeezes a trigger to retract the lobes, inserts the unit into a crack, and releases; springs expand the lobes against the rock. A load on the stem drives the lobes outward with a camming angle — typically about 14 degrees — that multiplies the holding force. This allows protection in parallel-sided and slightly flared cracks where passive nuts cannot hold.

History

Ray Jardine developed the first successful devices, marketed from 1978 as "Friends" by Wild Country, after earlier work with Greg Lowe and others. Modern units such as Black Diamond Camalots use a double axle to increase expansion range. Cams are sized by range and colour-coded, and are a standard part of a traditional climbing rack.
