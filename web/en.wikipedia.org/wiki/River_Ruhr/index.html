The Ruhr is a river in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, a right-bank tributary of the Rhine. It lends its name to the Ruhr area (Ruhrgebiet), one of Europe's largest urban and industrial regions, although the river itself flows along the region's southern edge rather than through the coal-mining cities of the Emscher zone further north.

Course

The Ruhr rises near the town of Winterberg in the upland Sauerland at an elevation of about 670 metres and flows west for 219 km (136 mi) past Meschede, Arnsberg, Wickede, Fröndenberg, Menden, Iserlohn's outskirts, Schwerte, Herdecke, Hagen, Wetter, Witten, Hattingen, Bochum's south, Essen and Mülheim an der Ruhr, before joining the Rhine at Duisburg-Ruhrort, site of Europe's largest inland port.

Water management

A chain of reservoirs in the upper and middle valley — including the Möhne, Henne, Sorpe and Bigge reservoirs on tributaries, and Lake Baldeney and Lake Kettwig on the river itself — regulates flow and supplies drinking water to millions in the Ruhr region. This system is managed by the Ruhrverband, one of Germany's historic water boards founded in 1913. The Möhne dam was breached in the famous "Dambusters" raid of May 1943, causing catastrophic flooding downstream.

History

Coal was mined on the outcrops of the Ruhr valley from the Middle Ages, and the river was made navigable with locks in the 1780s to carry coal to the Rhine. As mining spread north to the Emscher, the valley itself became greener, and today its reservoirs and wooded slopes are a major recreational area; the Ruhr Valley cycle path follows the river for its whole length.
