Amir Kabir Dam

The Amir Kabir Dam (Persian: سد امیرکبیر), also known as the Karaj Dam, is a large arch dam on the Karaj River in the Alborz mountain range of northern Iran, about 63 km northwest of Tehran and a short distance upstream of the city of Karaj. Named after Amir Kabir, the reformist 19th-century chief minister of Persia, it was one of Iran's first major modern multi-purpose dams, supplying water and hydroelectric power to Tehran.

Construction and purpose

Construction began in 1957 and was completed in 1961, with the dam built with American engineering assistance under Iran's development programme of the era. The double-curvature (variable radius) arch dam rises about 180 m from its foundations, making it one of the taller dams in Iran at the time of completion. Its reservoir, with a capacity of roughly 202 million cubic metres, regulates the Karaj River for Tehran's drinking water supply, irrigation in the Karaj plain, flood control, and power generation of about 90 MW.

Recreation

The lake behind the dam, set in steep mountain scenery along the Karaj–Chalus road, is a popular leisure destination for residents of Tehran and Karaj, noted for boating, fishing (including stocked trout) and the village of Varian on its shore, which is reachable only by boat.
