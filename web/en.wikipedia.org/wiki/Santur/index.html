Santur

The santur (also santūr, santoor, Persian: سنتور) is a hammered dulcimer originating from Persia (modern-day Iran). The trapezoid-shaped box is played by striking its metal strings — typically arranged over movable bridges in courses — with two light wooden hammers (mezrab).

Construction and playing

The Persian santur commonly has 72 strings arranged in 18 sets of four, stretched across a trapezoidal soundboard of walnut or other hardwood, giving a range of about three octaves. The right-hand bridges divide the strings so that the same courses produce notes a fifth apart on either side. Players use thin, sometimes felt-tipped mallets, producing a bright, shimmering tone well suited to the Persian dastgah modal system.

History and spread

Instruments of the hammered-dulcimer family appear in ancient Mesopotamian and Persian art. Variants spread widely: the Iraqi santur, the Indian santoor (central to Kashmiri Sufiana music and popularized in Indian classical music by Shivkumar Sharma), the Greek santouri, and related instruments such as the Chinese yangqin and the European cimbalom descend from the same family.

In Persian music

The santur is a core instrument of Persian classical music, used both solo and in ensembles. Notable masters include Abolhasan Saba, Faramarz Payvar, and Parviz Meshkatian, who expanded its technique and repertoire in the 20th century.
