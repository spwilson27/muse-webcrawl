Allan Huber "Bud" Selig (born 30 July 1934) is an American baseball executive who served as the Commissioner of Baseball from 1998 to 2015, having acted in the role since 1992. Before that he owned the Milwaukee Brewers franchise, which he founded by purchasing the bankrupt Seattle Pilots in 1970 and moving them to Milwaukee.

Commissionership

Selig's tenure brought major changes: interleague play (1997), the three-division format and Wild Card playoffs (1994), expanded instant replay, and the World Baseball Classic. It was also marked by controversy, including the cancellation of the 1994 World Series due to a players' strike and the sport's steroid era, which prompted the Mitchell Report (2007) and a strengthened drug-testing programme.

Legacy

Under Selig, Major League Baseball revenues grew enormously, from about $1.7 billion in 1992 to over $9 billion by the end of his tenure. He retired in January 2015, succeeded by Rob Manfred, and was elected to the National Baseball Hall of Fame by the Today's Game Era Committee in December 2016, being inducted in 2017.
