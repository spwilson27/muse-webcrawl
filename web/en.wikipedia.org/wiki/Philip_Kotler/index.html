Philip Kotler (born 27 May 1931) is an American marketing author, consultant and emeritus professor at the Kellogg School of Management of Northwestern University. Widely described as the "father of modern marketing", he gave the discipline its dominant textbook and much of its vocabulary, defining marketing as the process of identifying and meeting human and social needs profitably.

Career

Kotler, born in Chicago to Ukrainian immigrant parents, earned a master's degree in economics from the University of Chicago, where he studied with Milton Friedman, and a PhD in economics from MIT, where he was influenced by Paul Samuelson. He joined Northwestern in 1962 and became the S. C. Johnson & Son Distinguished Professor of International Marketing at Kellogg, a chair he held until his retirement.

Work

His textbook Marketing Management, first published in 1967 and continually revised (with co-author Kevin Lane Keller in later editions), became the most widely used marketing text in business schools worldwide. Kotler popularised the "4 Ps" classification (product, price, place, promotion) drawn from E. Jerome McCarthy, and developed the framework of segmentation, targeting and positioning (STP). He argued for broadening marketing beyond business: his 1969 paper with Sidney Levy on "broadening the concept of marketing", and later work on social marketing (with Gerald Zaltman), extended marketing to nonprofits, places, ideas and social causes. He has written dozens of books, including Marketing Places, Social Marketing and Marketing 4.0 (on the digital era), and consulted for companies and governments internationally.

Recognition

Kotler was the first recipient of the American Marketing Association's Distinguished Marketing Educator Award (1985) and has received numerous honorary doctorates; a museum of marketing bearing his name, the Philip Kotler Museum, opened in Jakarta. Rankings by the Financial Times and others have repeatedly placed him among the most influential business thinkers.
