OSCAR

OSCAR (Orbiting Satellite Carrying Amateur Radio) is the designation given to a long series of amateur radio satellites launched since 1961. The satellites are built and coordinated largely by AMSAT (the Radio Amateur Satellite Corporation) and other amateur groups worldwide, and they allow licensed radio amateurs to communicate through transponders in orbit. This article focuses on the OSCAR satellite programme; the acronym has also been used for other systems, including an ocean-surface current radar network and software projects.

History

OSCAR 1 was launched on 12 December 1961, barely four years after Sputnik 1, as a secondary payload on a United States Air Force launch from Vandenberg. Built by a group of California radio amateurs (Project OSCAR), the 10-pound satellite transmitted a simple "HI" beacon in Morse code on the 2-metre band for about three weeks. OSCAR 2 and 3 followed, and OSCAR 3 carried the first amateur radio transponder, enabling the first two-way amateur contacts via satellite in 1965.

Later satellites

More than a hundred OSCAR satellites have since flown, designated OSCAR plus a number, often with prefixes indicating the sponsoring organisation or country (such as AO for AMSAT-OSCAR, SO for Saudi-OSCAR, FO for Fuji-OSCAR and UO for UoSAT-OSCAR). Notable examples include OSCAR 7 (AO-7), launched in 1974 and still intermittently operational decades later, the Phase 3 series of high elliptical orbit satellites, and AO-91/AO-92 (Fox-1) CubeSats. AMSAT maintains a numbering register so that an amateur satellite receives an OSCAR designation once it is successfully operating in orbit.

  • OSCAR 1 (1961) — first amateur satellite; HI beacon
  • OSCAR 3 (1965) — first amateur transponder
  • OSCAR 7 (1974) — long-lived; revived after battery failure
  • OSCAR 40 / AO-40 (2000) — large Phase 3D satellite
