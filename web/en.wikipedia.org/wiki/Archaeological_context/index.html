Archaeological context

An archaeological context is an event in time which has been preserved in the archaeological record. In single-context recording, the basic unit of excavation and documentation, a context is a single distinct deposit, cut, fill or interface — a layer of soil, the fill of a pit, the cut of a ditch — that represents one action or event in the sequence of a site's formation.

Contexts and stratigraphy

Contexts are related to one another by stratigraphy: a context may lie above, below, cut through, or be cut by another. These relationships are recorded on site and assembled into a Harris matrix, a diagram of the relative sequence. By law of superposition, in undisturbed deposits, lower contexts are earlier than those above them.

Recording

Each context is given a unique number within its site and recorded on a context sheet describing its character, dimensions, composition and relationships. Artefacts and environmental samples recovered during excavation are attributed to the context from which they came; their "context" — where they were found and in association with what — is what allows interpretation. An object divorced from its context, for example through looting, loses much of its archaeological value.

Types

  • Positive contexts — deposits that add material, such as layers, fills and dumps.
  • Negative contexts — cuts and removals, such as pits, ditches and postholes.
  • Interfaces — surfaces, such as floors or the boundary between two deposits.
