Mount Kilimanjaro is a dormant volcano in Tanzania. It is the highest mountain in Africa and the highest single free-standing mountain above sea level in the world: 5,895 metres (19,341 ft) above sea level and about 4,900 metres (16,100 ft) above its plateau base. Kilimanjaro is the fourth most topographically prominent peak on Earth and forms part of Kilimanjaro National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Kilimanjaro's flat-topped summit, Kibo, seen across the plains of northern Tanzania.

Geology

Kilimanjaro is a stratovolcano composed of three distinct cones: Kibo, the highest; Mawenzi at 5,149 m; and Shira, the oldest and lowest, at 4,005 m. Kibo's crater is capped by glaciers, including the Furtwängler Glacier, which have shrunk dramatically since the early 20th century — over 80% of the ice cover has been lost, a decline attributed largely to climate change and deforestation rather than to local warming alone.

Climbing

The first recorded ascent to the summit was made on 6 October 1889 by German geographer Hans Meyer and Austrian mountaineer Ludwig Purtscheller. Today tens of thousands of people attempt the climb annually. Because no technical climbing is required on the main routes — including the popular Marangu and Machame routes — Kilimanjaro is often described as the most accessible of the Seven Summits, though altitude sickness is common and summit success rates on short itineraries may be below two-thirds.

Ecology and climate

The mountain's slopes pass through distinct climate zones: cultivated foothills, montane rainforest, moorland with giant groundsels and lobelias, alpine desert, and the arctic conditions of the summit. This compressed succession of ecosystems makes Kilimanjaro a notable site for ecological study.

In culture

Kilimanjaro is the setting of Ernest Hemingway's famous 1936 short story "The Snows of Kilimanjaro". The Chagga people have farmed its lower slopes for centuries, and the mountain is a symbol of Tanzania, appearing on its currency and in its national imagery.
