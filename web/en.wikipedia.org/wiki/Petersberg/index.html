Petersberg

Elevation: 331 m (1,086 ft)

Location: Königswinter, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany

Range: Siebengebirge

The Petersberg is a hill in the Siebengebirge mountain range near Königswinter, on the eastern bank of the Rhine near Bonn in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. Rising some 331 metres above sea level, it is best known for the grand hotel on its summit, which served as a seat of the Allied High Commission after World War II and later as an official guest house of the Federal Republic of Germany.

History

The summit bears remains of a prehistoric ring wall and was the site of a medieval chapel and a Cistercian-associated pilgrimage site. A hotel was first built on the Petersberg in the late nineteenth century. Following World War II, the Petersberg Agreement of 22 November 1949 — negotiated between Chancellor Konrad Adenauer and the Allied High Commissioners, who had their seat in the hotel — expanded the rights of the young West German government, among other things ending the dismantling of industrial plant and allowing the Federal Republic to join international organizations.

After the Allied High Commission departed in 1955, the hotel became the official guest house of the Federal Republic, hosting state visits by numerous heads of state and government. The German federal government acquired and renovated it; the luxury Steigenberger Grandhotel Petersberg reopened in 1990. Even after the capital moved to Berlin, the site hosted major meetings, including the 2001 Bonn climate talks follow-up sessions and the 2001 international conference on Afghanistan that produced the Bonn Agreement under its Petersberg auspices.

Petersberg tasks

The hill's name is also associated with the "Petersberg tasks" of the European Union, defined in the 1992 Petersberg Declaration of the Western European Union adopted at a meeting in the hotel — humanitarian, peacekeeping, and crisis-management operations later incorporated into the EU's treaties.
