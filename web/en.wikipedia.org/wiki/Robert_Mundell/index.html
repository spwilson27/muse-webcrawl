Robert Alexander Mundell (October 24, 1932 – April 4, 2021) was a Canadian economist and professor at Columbia University. He won the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1999 for his analysis of monetary and fiscal policy under different exchange rate regimes and his analysis of optimum currency areas. He has been called the "father of the euro".

Contributions

In the early 1960s, in papers written at the IMF and elsewhere, Mundell (with Marcus Fleming) developed the Mundell–Fleming model, extending the IS–LM framework to an open economy with capital mobility and deriving the "impossible trinity": a country cannot simultaneously maintain a fixed exchange rate, free capital movement and an independent monetary policy. His 1961 paper "A Theory of Optimum Currency Areas" laid the intellectual groundwork for monetary union in Europe, identifying the conditions — labour mobility, wage flexibility, fiscal transfers — under which regions benefit from sharing a currency.

Influence

Mundell's advocacy of supply-side policies and fixed exchange rates in the 1970s influenced American economic policy debates. He spent his later years partly in Italy, where he restored a palazzo near Siena and hosted conferences. The Mundell International University of Entrepreneurship in Beijing is named after him.
