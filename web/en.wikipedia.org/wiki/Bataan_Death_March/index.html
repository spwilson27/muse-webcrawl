The Bataan Death March was the forcible transfer by the Imperial Japanese Army of about 75,000 Filipino and American prisoners of war from the Bataan Peninsula to prison camps, beginning on 9 April 1942 after the three-month Battle of Bataan in the Second World War.

The march

The prisoners, already weakened by disease, malnutrition and combat, were marched roughly 105 km (65 miles) from Mariveles and Bagac to San Fernando under a tropical sun, with little food or water. Stragglers were beaten, bayoneted or shot; thousands died on the road before being crammed into rail cars for the final leg to Camp O'Donnell. Estimates of deaths on the march range from several thousand to around 18,000, the majority Filipino.

Aftermath

The march became a symbol of Japanese wartime brutality. After the war, Japanese commander General Masaharu Homma was tried by a US military commission, convicted of war crimes in connection with the march and executed in April 1946. The victims are commemorated in the Philippines and the United States, including by the annual Bataan Memorial Death March in New Mexico.
