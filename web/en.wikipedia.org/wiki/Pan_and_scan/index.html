Pan and scan

Pan and scan is a method of adjusting widescreen film images for display in the narrower aspect ratio of standard television, especially the former 4:3 format. The visible frame is cropped, and the view pans across the original image to follow the action or speaker.

Criticism

Because large portions of the original composition are lost, pan and scan was criticised by filmmakers and cinephiles, who preferred letterboxing, which preserves the whole frame with black bars. Some shots are recomposed or "squeezed" instead, and open matte transfers offer another alternative.

Decline

With the adoption of 16:9 widescreen televisions, pan and scan became much less common for modern films.
