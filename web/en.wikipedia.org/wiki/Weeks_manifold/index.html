Weeks manifold

In mathematics, the Weeks manifold, sometimes called the Fomenko–Matveev–Weeks manifold, is a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold obtained by (5,1) and (5,2) Dehn surgeries on the Whitehead link. It has volume approximately 0.9427 and is the smallest known closed orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold; it was shown by Gabai, Meyerhoff, and Milley in 2009 to have the smallest volume among all closed orientable hyperbolic 3-manifolds.

History

The manifold was independently discovered by Jeffrey Weeks (1985) and by Sergei Matveev and Anatoly Fomenko. Weeks also wrote the computer program SnapPea to study hyperbolic 3-manifolds, based on an earlier program of his for finding the manifold's hyperbolic structure from a triangulation.

Properties

The Weeks manifold is an arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold. Its invariant trace field has discriminant −503. It can be described as a quotient of hyperbolic 3-space H³ by a torsion-free subgroup of the arithmetic Kleinian group arising from the quaternion algebra over that field. Its homology is Z/5Z ⊕ Z/5Z, and it admits a triangulation with 9 tetrahedra in its minimal form discovered through census enumeration of small manifolds.
