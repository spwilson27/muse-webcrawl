Edward G. Robinson

Edward G. Robinson (born Emanuel Goldenberg; December 12, 1893 – January 26, 1973) was an American actor of stage and screen, popular during Hollywood's Golden Age. He is best remembered for his tough-guy roles as gangsters in films such as Little Caesar (1931) and Key Largo (1948).

Career

Born in Bucharest, Romania, Robinson emigrated to the United States as a child. After stage work he achieved stardom with Little Caesar. His notable films include Double Indemnity (1944), The Woman in the Window (1944), Scarlet Street (1945), The Stranger (1946), and The Ten Commandments (1956). His final film was Soylent Green (1973).

Honours

Robinson was never nominated for a competitive Academy Award but received an Honorary Oscar shortly after his death. He was also an avid art collector whose collection included works by major Impressionist and modern artists.
