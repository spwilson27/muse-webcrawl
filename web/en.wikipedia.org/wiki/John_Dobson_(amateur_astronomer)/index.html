John Dobson (amateur astronomer)

John Lowry Dobson (September 14, 1915 – January 15, 2014) was an American amateur astronomer best known for the Dobsonian telescope, a portable and inexpensive design for large-aperture Newtonian reflectors, and for popularising "sidewalk astronomy."

Life

Born in Beijing to missionary parents, Dobson grew up in San Francisco. He spent 23 years as a monk of the Ramakrishna Order before leaving in 1967 to devote himself to astronomy outreach. That year he co-founded the San Francisco Sidewalk Astronomers, who set up telescopes on city corners to show passers-by the Moon, planets, and stars free of charge.

The Dobsonian mount

Dobson's design mounted a simple mirror telescope on a cheap alt-azimuth mount built from plywood and Teflon bearings, using scrap materials such as porthole glass for mirrors. The design made large telescopes affordable to amateurs and transformed deep-sky observing from the 1970s onward, though Dobson's own aim was public outreach rather than equipment innovation.

Views and legacy

Dobson held unorthodox cosmological views, advocating a steady-state universe. He remained a tireless evangelist for public astronomy into his nineties. The asteroid 18024 Dobson is named for him.
