Complete graph

In the mathematical field of graph theory, a complete graph is a simple undirected graph in which every pair of distinct vertices is connected by a unique edge. The complete graph on n vertices is denoted Kn and has n(n − 1)/2 edges, the nth triangular-related binomial coefficient "n choose 2". A complete digraph is a directed graph in which every pair of distinct vertices is connected by a pair of unique edges pointing in both directions.

Properties

Kn is regular of degree n − 1, maximally edge-connected, and has diameter 1 for n ≥ 2. By Kuratowski's theorem, K5 is the smallest nonplanar complete graph; complete graphs K1 through K4 are planar. The number of matchings, spanning trees, and Hamiltonian cycles in complete graphs are all classical counting problems — Cayley's formula states that Kn has nn−2 spanning trees. The edges of Kn can be colored with n − 1 colors when n is even (and n when odd), a fact underlying round-robin tournament scheduling.

Related notions

In the study of social networks, a complete graph corresponds to a clique — the term used in graph theory for a set of pairwise-adjacent vertices, and in network analysis for a maximal tightly knit subgroup. Ramsey theory asks for the smallest complete graph whose edges, colored in two colors, guarantee a monochromatic complete subgraph of a given size; the Ramsey number R(5,5), for example, remains unknown. Complement graphs of complete graphs are empty graphs, and complete graphs serve as extremal examples throughout extremal graph theory (e.g., Turán's theorem).
