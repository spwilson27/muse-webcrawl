T-64

The T-64 is a Soviet tank manufactured in Kharkiv, Ukrainian SSR, and designed by Alexander Morozov's design bureau at the Malyshev Factory. First introduced in the early 1960s and entering service in 1966, the T-64 was the first tank to be fitted with an autoloader, composite armour, and a compact opposed-piston diesel engine, and it served as the technological basis for later Soviet tanks including the T-72, T-80 and T-84.

Design

The T-64 broke with previous Soviet tank design in nearly every respect. Its 115 mm (later 125 mm) smoothbore gun was loaded by an automatic loader, reducing the crew to three men — commander, gunner and driver. Its armour combined steel with ceramic and fibreglass layers (Combination K), offering protection well beyond its modest weight of about 38 tonnes. The 5TDF opposed-piston diesel engine was compact and powerful but mechanically complex and initially unreliable.

Because of its cost and complexity the T-64 was never exported, equipping only Soviet (and later Ukrainian, Russian and other post-Soviet) forces, while the simpler T-72 became the widely exported type.

Service history

Modernised variants such as the T-64BV served for decades. T-64s in Ukrainian service saw extensive combat during the war in Donbas from 2014 and in the full-scale Russo-Ukrainian War from 2022, where the heavily upgraded T-64BV model 2017 remained a mainstay of the Ukrainian armoured fleet.

Variants

Major variants include the original T-64 (115 mm gun), the T-64A (125 mm gun), the T-64B (capable of firing guided missiles through the gun barrel) and the T-64BV with explosive reactive armour.
