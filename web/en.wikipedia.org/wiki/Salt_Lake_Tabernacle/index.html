Salt Lake Tabernacle

LocationTemple Square, Salt Lake City, Utah, USA
Built1863–1867 (dedicated 1867; completed 1875)
Capacity~3,500 (after 2007 renovation; originally ~8,000)
Known forThe Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square; famous acoustics; large pipe organ

The Salt Lake Tabernacle, also known as the Mormon Tabernacle, is a large meeting hall on Temple Square in Salt Lake City, Utah, owned by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Built between 1863 and 1867 under the direction of church president Brigham Young, it was for over a century the venue of the Church's general conferences and is the historic home of the Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square.

Architecture and acoustics

The building is an elongated dome-like structure about 76 metres long and 46 metres wide. Its remarkable roof — a series of lattice trusses designed by bridge builder Henry Grow — was constructed of timber joined with wooden pegs and rawhide lashings, and spans the interior without supporting columns, an extraordinary feat for its time. The hall's acoustics are legendary: a pin dropped at one end can reportedly be heard at the other, a demonstration long given to visitors.

The organ

The Tabernacle organ, originally built by Joseph H. Ridges, is one of the largest pipe organs in the world, with about 11,600 pipes; its gold-leafed facade pipes are made from hand-hewn Utah pine. Daily recitals have been a Temple Square tradition since 1908.

History and renovation

The Tabernacle hosted general conferences from 1867 until 2000, when they moved to the larger Conference Center. It has hosted U.S. presidents, including Theodore Roosevelt and John F. Kennedy. Between 2005 and 2007 the building underwent an extensive seismic and interior renovation — the first thorough rebuild since its construction — and was rededicated in March 2007. It continues to host concerts and the Choir's weekly Music and the Spoken Word broadcasts.
