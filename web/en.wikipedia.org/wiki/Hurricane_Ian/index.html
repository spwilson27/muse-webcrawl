Hurricane Ian was a large and devastating Category 5 Atlantic hurricane that struck western Cuba and the southeastern United States in September 2022. It was the deadliest hurricane to strike Florida since the 1935 Labor Day hurricane and one of the costliest hurricanes in U.S. history, causing an estimated $112&nbsp;billion in damage. Ian made landfall near Fort Myers, Florida, at Category 4 strength on 28 September 2022, driving a catastrophic storm surge of up to 15 feet (4.6 m) into coastal communities.

Meteorological history

Ian developed from a tropical wave east of the Windward Islands on 23 September 2022. It intensified over the Caribbean Sea, struck western Cuba as a Category 3 hurricane on 27 September, knocking out power across the island, and emerged into the Gulf of Mexico. Over the warm waters of the southeastern Gulf it rapidly intensified to Category 5, then made landfall on Cayo Costa, Florida, on 28 September with maximum sustained winds of 150 mph (240 km/h). Ian crossed the Florida peninsula, emerged into the Atlantic, restrengthened, and made a final landfall in South Carolina on 30 September as a Category 1 hurricane.

Impact

The Fort Myers Beach, Sanibel Island, and Cape Coral areas were devastated by storm surge; the Sanibel Causeway was severed, and thousands of homes were destroyed. At least 161 deaths were attributed to the storm, the majority in Florida, many by drowning. More than two million customers lost power in Florida. Historic flooding occurred along the Peace and Myakka rivers and in the Orlando area as Ian dropped extreme rainfall across central Florida. Cuba suffered a nationwide blackout, and North and South Carolina experienced coastal flooding and damage.

Aftermath

The name Ian was retired by the World Meteorological Organization in 2023 and replaced with Idris. The disaster prompted debate over Florida's property insurance market, building codes in surge zones, evacuation timing in Lee County, and the influence of climate change on rapid intensification and rainfall. Federal disaster declarations covered Florida and the Carolinas, and recovery and rebuilding in southwest Florida continued for years.
