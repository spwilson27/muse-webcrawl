Video game development

Video game development is the process of designing and creating a video game. Development is undertaken by a game developer, which may range from a single person to a large team of hundreds. Traditional commercial PC and console games are normally developed in phases: concept, pre-production, production, testing, pre-launch, launch and post-launch support.

Process

A concept and game design document set out mechanics, story, art direction and technology. Pre-production builds prototypes and vertical slices; production creates code, levels, art, animation, audio and cinematics in milestones such as alpha (feature-complete) and beta (content-complete). Quality-assurance testers find bugs across target hardware, followed by certification by platform holders, gold master, release and patches or downloadable content.

Roles and teams

Teams combine producers, game designers, programmers (gameplay, engine, AI, network), artists (concept, 3D, technical), animators, sound designers, composers, writers and testers. Independent ("indie") development uses smaller teams and digital distribution; AAA development involves large budgets, sometimes exceeding major film productions.

Tools and technology

Most games are built on engines such as Unreal Engine and Unity, with middleware for physics and audio, languages such as C++ and C#, and pipelines for motion capture and procedural generation. Development kits are provided for consoles.

Industry issues

Crunch — extended overtime near deadlines — has drawn sustained criticism, alongside questions of job security, unionisation, and preservation of games after servers close.
