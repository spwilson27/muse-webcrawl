Esterification

Esterification is the general name for a chemical reaction in which two reactants (typically an alcohol and an acid) form an ester as the reaction product. Esters are common in organic chemistry and in biological materials, and often have a characteristic pleasant, fruity odour. This leads to their extensive use in the fragrance and flavour industry. Ester bonds are also found in many polymers.

Fischer esterification

The best-known esterification is the Fischer–Speier esterification, in which a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol in the presence of an acid catalyst, typically concentrated sulfuric acid, with the elimination of water:

RCO2H + R′OH ⇌ RCO2R′ + H2O

The reaction is an equilibrium process, so yields are improved by removing water as it forms or by using an excess of one reactant, in accordance with Le Chatelier's principle. The mechanism proceeds by protonation of the carbonyl oxygen, nucleophilic attack by the alcohol, proton transfers, and loss of water. Isotopic labelling experiments show that the oxygen lost as water comes from the carboxylic acid's hydroxyl group in the usual pathway.

Other methods

Esters can also be prepared by reacting alcohols with acid chlorides or acid anhydrides, by transesterification of one ester with another alcohol, and by the Steglich esterification, which uses a carbodiimide coupling reagent (often DCC with DMAP) under mild conditions suited to sensitive substrates. The reverse reaction — cleavage of an ester by water — is called hydrolysis; its base-promoted form is saponification.

Applications

Esterification underlies the manufacture of polyester polymers such as PET, of biodiesel by transesterification of triglycerides with methanol, and of numerous solvents, plasticisers, fragrances and pharmaceuticals.
