Per Erik Rutger Martin-Löf (born 1942) is a Swedish logician, philosopher and mathematician, professor emeritus at Stockholm University. He is best known as the creator of Martin-Löf type theory, a foundation for mathematics and computer science that underlies modern proof assistants, and for foundational work on the definition of randomness for individual sequences.

Work

In the 1960s Martin-Löf gave a rigorous definition of an algorithmically random sequence (Martin-Löf randomness), building on Kolmogorov complexity. From the 1970s he developed dependent type theory, presented in his influential 1984 book Constructive Mathematics and Computer Programming, in which propositions are types and proofs are programs — the Curry–Howard correspondence made into a full foundation. His philosophical lectures on the justification of logical laws and on meaning in natural language influenced analytic philosophy and linguistics alike.

Type theories descended from his work are implemented in systems such as Agda, Coq and Lean, and his ideas inform homotopy type theory. Outside academia, Martin-Löf is also a noted birdwatcher and member of the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences.
