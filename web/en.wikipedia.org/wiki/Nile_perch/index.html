The Nile perch (Lates niloticus) is a species of freshwater fish in the family Latidae, widespread throughout much of the Afrotropical realm. It can grow to nearly 2 m in length and weigh up to 200 kg, making it one of the largest freshwater fish. Despite its name, it is not a true perch. It is an important food fish in Egypt (where it appears in ancient tomb paintings) and throughout Africa.

Distribution and habitat

The Nile perch is native to the Nile, Congo, Niger and Senegal river basins and to lakes Chad, Turkana and others. It inhabits channels, lakes and irrigation canals, with juveniles found in shallow, sheltered water.

Introduction to Lake Victoria

The species is notorious for its introduction into Lake Victoria in the 1950s, intended to boost the fishery. The predator's population exploded in the 1980s, and it is blamed — along with eutrophication and overfishing — for the extinction or near-extinction of several hundred endemic cichlid species (haplochromines), one of the largest vertebrate extinction episodes attributable to a single introduced species. At the same time, Nile perch created a lucrative export fishery for fillets to Europe and Israel, dramatised in the documentary Darwin's Nightmare (2004). Its introduction is now a standard cautionary example in invasion biology, and it is listed among the world's 100 worst invasive alien species.

Commercial importance

Nile perch is sold extensively, sometimes mislabelled as other white fish. In Australia it is sold as "barramundi"-style fillets under names such as "ling" disputes aside; its close relative the barramundi (Lates calcarifer) is a separate Indo-Pacific species.
