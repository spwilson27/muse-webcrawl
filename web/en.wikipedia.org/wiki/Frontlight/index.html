A frontlight is a lighting system built into the front of an electronic display, used to illuminate reflective screens that do not emit their own light. Unlike a backlight, which shines light through a transmissive display towards the viewer, a frontlight directs light from LEDs along the edge of the screen across a transparent light-guide layer onto the surface of the display, from where it is reflected back to the eye.

Applications

Frontlights are most familiar in e-readers with electronic paper (e-ink) displays, such as the Amazon Kindle Paperwhite (introduced in 2012) and Kobo devices, where they allow reading in the dark while preserving the paper-like, low-glare appearance of the screen. Because the light is directed at the page rather than into the reader's eyes, frontlit e-readers are often considered more comfortable for extended reading than backlit tablets. Later implementations added adjustable colour temperature ("warm light") and automatic brightness adjustment.

Earlier frontlights appeared in devices such as reflective-LCD handheld games accessories, wristwatches and some industrial and medical displays. The technology requires careful design of the light guide to spread light evenly and avoid bright spots near the LEDs.
