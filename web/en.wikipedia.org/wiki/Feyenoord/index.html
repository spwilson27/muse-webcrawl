Feyenoord Rotterdam is a Dutch professional football club based in Rotterdam, South Holland. Founded in 1908, it is one of the country's "big three" clubs, alongside Amsterdam's Ajax and Eindhoven's PSV, and the only one of the three based in Rotterdam. Feyenoord play at the Stadion Feijenoord, universally known as De Kuip ("the Tub"), one of the most atmospheric stadiums in the Netherlands. The club's traditional colours are a red-and-white halved shirt, black shorts, and black socks.

History

The club was founded on 19 July 1908 in the Feijenoord district of Rotterdam as Wilhelmina, and after several name changes settled on SC Feijenoord in 1912, adopting the modern spelling "Feyenoord" in 1974 for international recognisability. Rooted in the working-class south bank of the Maas, Feyenoord became known as a people's club with a famously loyal and passionate support.

Feyenoord's golden era came under coach Ernst Happel: in 1970 the club became the first Dutch side to win the European Cup, defeating Celtic 2–1 after extra time in Milan, and added the Intercontinental Cup against Estudiantes the same year. A UEFA Cup followed in 1974 against Tottenham Hotspur. Later successes were sparser, but the club won the UEFA Cup again in 2002 — beating Borussia Dortmund 3–2 in the final at its own De Kuip — and ended an 18-year title drought by winning the Eredivisie in 2016–17 under Giovanni van Bronckhorst. Under coach Arne Slot, Feyenoord won the league again in 2022–23 with an attacking, high-pressing style, before Slot departed for Liverpool in 2024.

Rivalries and supporters

Feyenoord's fiercest rivalry is with Ajax; matches between them are known as De Klassieker ("The Classic") and rank among the most intense fixtures in Dutch football. The supporters, known collectively as Het Legioen ("The Legion"), are renowned for their loyalty through lean years and for the anthem "Hand in Hand", sung since the 1960s.

Notable players
