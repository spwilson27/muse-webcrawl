The Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup, commonly known as the U.S. Open Cup, is a knockout cup competition in men's soccer in the United States. It is the oldest ongoing national soccer competition in the country, having first been played in 1914 as the National Challenge Cup. The tournament is open to amateur and professional clubs affiliated with the United States Soccer Federation, making it one of the few competitions in which lower-division and amateur teams can face Major League Soccer clubs.

Format and history

The competition is a single-elimination tournament staged over the course of the season. The winners receive the Dewar Cup, donated in 1912 by Sir Thomas Dewar, and ordinarily qualify for the CONCACAF Champions Cup. Greek American AA, Bethlehem Steel, and Maccabee Los Angeles are among the most successful clubs historically; since MLS clubs began entering in 1996 they have dominated the later rounds, though occasional lower-league runs — such as the Rochester Rhinos' 1999 title, the last won by a non-MLS team — remain famous.

Name

In 1999 the federation renamed the tournament in honour of Lamar Hunt, a pioneering figure in American soccer and a founder of MLS, whose teams and investments were instrumental in the sport's growth in the United States.
