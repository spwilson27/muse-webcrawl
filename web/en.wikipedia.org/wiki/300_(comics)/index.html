300 is a five-issue American comic book limited series written and illustrated by Frank Miller and painted by Lynn Varley, first published by Dark Horse Comics in 1998. It is a fictionalised retelling of the Battle of Thermopylae (480 BC), in which King Leonidas of Sparta and 300 Spartan soldiers held a narrow coastal pass against the vast Persian army of Xerxes I.

Story

The narrative is framed as a tale told by the Spartan soldier Dilios to inspire Greek forces on the eve of the Battle of Plataea. Refused permission by the corrupted Ephors to take the full Spartan army to war, Leonidas marches north with 300 men styled as his personal bodyguard. At the Hot Gates the Spartans and their allies repel repeated Persian assaults, including the elite Immortals, until a deformed outcast, Ephialtes, betrayed by rejection, shows the Persians a mountain path around the pass. Dismissing his allies, Leonidas and his remaining men fight to the death. Dilios concludes the story as the Greeks prepare to meet the Persians at Plataea with an army of tens of thousands.

Style and historical basis

Each issue was printed in an unusually wide format, with double-page spreads emphasising massed phalanxes and Persian formations. Miller has said he was inspired by the 1962 film The 300 Spartans and by classical accounts, principally Herodotus. Critics praised the stark artwork and spare narration but also debated the comic's loose relationship to history — its exaggerated Persian decadence and fantastical designs, its depiction of the Spartan agoge, and the omission of the thousands of Thespians and other Greeks who also died at Thermopylae.

Adaptation

The comic was adapted into the 2006 film 300, directed by Zack Snyder and starring Gerard Butler, which closely reproduced Varley's colour palette and Miller's compositions. The film's success revived interest in the graphic novel, which returned to bestseller lists. In 2018 Miller returned to the setting with the prequel miniseries Xerxes: The Fall of the House of Darius and the Rise of Alexander.
