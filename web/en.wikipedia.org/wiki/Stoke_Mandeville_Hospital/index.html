Stoke Mandeville Hospital is an NHS hospital in Aylesbury, Buckinghamshire, England. It is world-renowned for its National Spinal Injuries Centre and as the birthplace of the Paralympic Games.

History

The hospital originated in the 1830s. In February 1944, the neurosurgeon Ludwig Guttmann opened the National Spinal Injuries Centre there to treat servicemen with spinal injuries. Guttmann revolutionised care, rejecting the expectation that paraplegic patients would die of complications and using sport as rehabilitation. On 29 July 1948—the day of the opening of the London Olympics—he organised an archery competition for 16 patients, the Stoke Mandeville Games, regarded as the forerunner of the Paralympics; the first official Paralympic Games followed in Rome in 1960.

The hospital was also the workplace of Jimmy Savile, whose extensive volunteering and fundraising there later became notorious when his sexual abuse on the premises was exposed after his death.

Services

Today the hospital provides acute general services for Buckinghamshire alongside national specialist spinal rehabilitation, burns and plastics, and ophthalmology services. Stoke Mandeville Stadium, adjacent to the hospital, is the National Centre for Disability Sport in the United Kingdom.
