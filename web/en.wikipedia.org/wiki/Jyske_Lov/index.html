Jyske Lov

Jyske Lov (the "Jutlandic Law" or "Law of Jutland") is a Danish provincial law code promulgated by King Valdemar II of Denmark in 1241. It applied to Jutland and the island of Funen, as well as the Duchy of Schleswig, and remained in force in parts of the region for more than four centuries; in Schleswig it was only replaced by the German Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch in 1900.

The preface

The code is famous for its preface, one of the most quoted passages in Danish legal history: "With law shall the land be built" (Mæth logh skal land byggæs). The preface declares that the law must be honourable, just, reasonable and in accordance with the customs of the people; that no law shall be made against God's law; and that the king may neither repeal nor change the law without the consent of the land, except where it is plainly against God. It states that the law is made for the sake of all — "the law shall not be made for the sake of any single man".

Contents

The code is written in Danish rather than Latin, reflecting the practical needs of lay courts (the landsting). It covers inheritance, marriage and property, theft and other crimes, ordeal and oath-taking, the duties of officials, and the treatment of outlaws. In many matters it is less severe than the Scanian Law (Skånske Lov) promulgated for Scania in the same period; capital punishment was less prominent, and compensation remained central.

Legacy

Jyske Lov is one of the three great Danish provincial laws of the 13th century, alongside the Scanian Law and the Zealand laws. It was printed several times in the early modern period, was translated into German and Latin, and its preface is still quoted in Danish debates on the rule of law. The opening words of the preface were inscribed in the Danish Supreme Court building in Copenhagen.
