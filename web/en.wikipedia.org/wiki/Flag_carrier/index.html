Flag carrier

A flag carrier is a transport company, such as an airline or shipping company, that, being locally registered in a given sovereign state, enjoys preferential rights or privileges accorded by the government for international operations.

Historically, the term was used to refer to airlines owned by the government of their home country and associated with the national identity of that country. Such an airline may also be known as a national airline or national carrier, although these terms have different legal meanings in some countries. Today, a flag carrier may be a legally private company, yet still be widely perceived as the national carrier because of its history, branding, and dominant presence in the home market.

Background

The term "flag carrier" derives from shipping, where vessels fly the flag of their country of registry. After the Second World War, the Chicago Convention of 1944 and bilateral air service agreements structured international aviation around designated national airlines, most of them state-owned. Carriers such as British Airways, Air France, Lufthansa and Alitalia were created or consolidated as instruments of national prestige and policy.

From the late 20th century, deregulation and privatisation changed the picture: many former flag carriers were sold partly or wholly to private investors, joined global airline alliances, and came to compete commercially, while governments retained only minority stakes or "golden shares". Nevertheless, in many states the flag carrier retains a symbolic role, carries the national flag on its livery, and may be called upon for state flights or emergency repatriation.

List of examples

Well-known flag carriers include Air Canada (Canada), Aer Lingus (Ireland), Air India (India), Qantas (Australia), Japan Airlines and All Nippon Airways (Japan), Korean Air (South Korea), Singapore Airlines (Singapore), Emirates and Etihad (United Arab Emirates), and Turkish Airlines (Türkiye). In the United States, no single official flag carrier exists, though Pan Am historically filled the role.
