Review article

A review article is an article in a scholarly journal that summarises and evaluates the current state of understanding on a particular topic, drawing on previously published primary research rather than reporting new experimental results. Review articles are a form of secondary literature, distinct from both original research articles and tertiary sources such as textbooks and encyclopedias.

Purpose and types

Reviews serve several functions: they organise a scattered literature, identify agreements and controversies, highlight gaps, and propose directions for future work. Narrative reviews survey a field qualitatively, often at the invitation of journal editors. Systematic reviews follow a predefined, reproducible methodology for searching, selecting and appraising studies, and may be combined with a meta-analysis, which statistically pools quantitative results. Other forms include scoping reviews, rapid reviews and "reviews of reviews" (umbrella reviews).

Importance

Because they condense a field into a single reference, review articles are typically among the most highly cited publications in science, and journals devoted largely to reviews — such as those in the Annual Reviews series, or Reviews of Modern Physics — often have very high impact factors. In medicine, systematic reviews, including those produced by Cochrane, sit near the top of the evidence hierarchy and directly inform clinical guidelines.

Criticism

Narrative reviews can be selective or biased toward the author's viewpoint, which motivated the development of reporting standards such as PRISMA for systematic reviews. The sheer growth of the literature has also led to concerns about redundant or low-quality reviews.
