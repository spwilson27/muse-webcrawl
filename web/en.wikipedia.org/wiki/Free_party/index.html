Free party

Free party
OriginsUnited Kingdom, late 1980s – early 1990s
FeaturesUnlicensed, free entry, sound systems, tekno music
Related movementsRave, teknival, squat party

A free party is a party "free" from the restrictions that apply to licensed clubs and events. Typically, the term is used to describe a party that is free to attend, outside the law and without charge, held outdoors or in a squatted or borrowed venue, and organised by a collective operating one or more sound systems. Free parties are strongly associated with the teknival movement and with hardcore techno ("tekno") music.

History

The free party scene grew out of the late-1980s British acid house and traveller cultures. Sound system collectives such as Spiral Tribe, Exodus and Bedlam organised unlicensed gatherings in warehouses, fields and squats, financed by donations rather than ticket sales and announced by word of mouth or last-minute phone lines. The huge Castlemorton Common Festival in May 1992, attended by tens of thousands, provoked a public backlash that led to the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994, which gave police powers against gatherings characterised by music "wholly or predominantly characterised by the emission of a succession of repetitive beats".

After the 1994 Act, many British sound systems moved to continental Europe, helping to spread the scene and giving rise to large multi-day teknivals, especially in France, the Czech Republic (CzechTek) and elsewhere, sometimes attracting tens of thousands of participants. In France, events above a certain size were eventually brought under a declaration regime by the 2001–2002 security legislation.

Culture

Free parties emphasise a do-it-yourself, non-commercial ethos: entry is free or by donation, DJs and live acts play without headline billing, and participants are expected to respect the site ("leave no trace"). Parties may last for days. Because events are unlicensed, organisers and attendees have frequently clashed with landowners and police, and the scene remains semi-underground in many countries.
