The International Weightlifting Federation (IWF) is the international governing body for the sport of Olympic weightlifting. Founded in 1905, it is recognised by the International Olympic Committee and organises the annual World Weightlifting Championships as well as the sport's competition at the Olympic Games. Its membership comprises more than 190 national federations.

History

Weightlifting was among the sports of the first modern Olympic Games in 1896, and an international federation was formed in 1905 to standardise rules and lifts. Over the 20th century the competition programme narrowed from a variety of one- and two-handed lifts to the two present-day lifts, the snatch and the clean and jerk, with the clean and press dropped after 1972. Women's world championships began in 1987, and women's weightlifting entered the Olympics at Sydney 2000.

Competitions and rules

The IWF sanctions the World Championships, Junior and Youth World Championships, the IWF World Cup and continental championships run by its five continental federations. Athletes compete in bodyweight categories, each performing up to three attempts in the snatch and three in the clean and jerk, with the combined total determining placings. The IWF maintains the sport's technical and competition rules, certifies referees and equipment, and ratifies world records.

Doping and governance controversies

Weightlifting has had persistent problems with doping. Numerous Olympic results have been annulled on re-analysis of stored samples, particularly from the 2008 and 2012 Games. A 2020 independent investigation by Richard McLaren documented corruption and doping cover-ups under long-serving president Tamás Aján, who resigned that year after nearly 44 years in IWF leadership roles. The scandal led the IOC to place weightlifting's Olympic status in jeopardy; the sport was initially excluded from the Los Angeles 2028 programme before being reinstated in 2023 after governance reforms and the transfer of anti-doping operations to the International Testing Agency.
