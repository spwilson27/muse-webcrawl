Pula Arena


The Pula Arena, one of the best-preserved Roman amphitheatres.

The Pula Arena (Croatian: Pulska Arena; Italian: Arena di Pola) is a Roman amphitheatre located in Pula, Croatia. It is the only remaining Roman amphitheatre to have four side towers entirely preserved. It was constructed between 27 BC and AD 68, and is among the world's six largest surviving Roman arenas. The arena is also the country's best-preserved ancient monument.

History

The amphitheatre was first built in timber during the reign of Augustus (2–14 AD), then replaced by a small stone structure. It was enlarged in the reign of Claudius and completed in its present form under Vespasian, around the same time as the Colosseum in Rome. Local legend connects its construction to Vespasian's mistress Antonia Cenide, who lived in the area. Gladiatorial contests were held in the arena until they were banned in the 5th century. In the Middle Ages the interior was used for grazing and occasional tournaments, and stone was quarried from it until that practice was forbidden in the 15th century under Venetian rule.

Description

The outer wall is built of limestone and stands 29.4 m (96 ft) high on the seaward side. The arena measures 132.45 m by 105.1 m externally, with an elliptical performance area of about 67.95 m by 41.65 m. The two main entrances and four towers survive; the towers once contained mechanisms for spraying perfumed water over the audience. The seating (cavea) could accommodate roughly 20,000–23,000 spectators.

Modern use

Today the arena hosts concerts, operas, and the Pula Film Festival, founded in 1954. It has also hosted ice hockey exhibitions and performances by major international artists, with modern capacities limited to about 5,000–12,500 depending on configuration. The underground passages once used by gladiators now house an exhibition of viticulture and olive growing in ancient Istria.
