Sunbeam Alpine

The Sunbeam Alpine is a two-seater open sports car produced by the Rootes Group from 1959 to 1968. The name had previously been used on the Sunbeam-Talbot Alpine of 1953–1955, a rally-proven tourer, before being revived for the new small sports car aimed largely at the North American market.

Design and development

The Alpine was developed from the Hillman Husky and Sunbeam Rapier underpinnings, using a unitary body styled by Raymond Loewy's team influences within Rootes. It was launched in July 1959 (Series I) with a 1.5-litre overhead-valve four-cylinder engine derived from the Rapier unit. Successive series (II through V) brought a larger 1.6-litre engine and finally a 1,725 cc unit, along with styling changes including the reduction of the tall tail fins of the earliest cars.

Competition and culture

An Alpine won the Index of Thermal Efficiency at the 1961 24 Hours of Le Mans. The car gained screen fame as the vehicle driven by Sean Connery's James Bond in Dr. No (1962), the first Bond film car, and a blue Alpine Series II was prominently featured. In the United States the car was also sold as the basis for Carroll Shelby's intervention: Shelby fitted a Ford small-block V8 into the Alpine shell to create the Sunbeam Tiger, produced from 1964 to 1967.

Production ended in 1968 after about 69,000 Alpines of all series had been built, as Chrysler — which had taken control of Rootes — rationalized the range. The name was later reused on a fastback derivative of the Hillman Avenger and on the Chrysler Alpine hatchback of the late 1970s.
