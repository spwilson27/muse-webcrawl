Techno-thriller

A techno-thriller or technothriller is a hybrid genre combining action, spy fiction, science fiction and war fiction, in which the plot places a strong emphasis on technology — particularly military technology — and on detailed technical explanations. Techno-thrillers tend to have a high degree of verisimilitude, drawing on real-world weapons systems, tactics and political situations.

Characteristics

In a techno-thriller, technology itself often functions almost as a character: submarines, fighter aircraft, satellites and computer systems are described in loving detail, and the outcome of the plot typically hinges on how that technology is used. Plots frequently involve military conflict, espionage, terrorism or the threat of war, and protagonists are often soldiers, intelligence officers, engineers or scientists.

History

The genre emerged in the late Cold War period. Michael Crichton is often credited as a founding figure with novels such as The Andromeda Strain (1969), which applied a scientific, procedural approach to a thriller plot. Tom Clancy popularized the modern form with The Hunt for Red October (1984), a bestseller noted for its meticulously researched portrayal of submarine warfare. Other prominent authors include Dale Brown, Larry Bond, Stephen Coonts, Frederick Forsyth and Dan Brown, whose novels blend technology with conspiracy elements.

In film and other media

Successful techno-thriller novels have frequently been adapted into films, including The Hunt for Red October (1990), Patriot Games (1992) and Jurassic Park (1993). The genre has also influenced video games and television series dealing with military and intelligence themes.
