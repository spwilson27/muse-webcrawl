Henryk Mikołaj Górecki (6 December 1933 – 12 November 2010) was a Polish composer of contemporary classical music. According to critic Alex Ross, no recent classical composer has had as much commercial success as Górecki. He became a leading figure of the Polish avant-garde during the post-Stalin cultural thaw and achieved international fame with his Symphony No. 3, the "Symphony of Sorrowful Songs".

Life

Górecki studied composition at the State Higher School of Music in Katowice and in Paris. His early works, such as the Symphony No. 1 (1959) and Scontri (1960), were serial and dissonant, placing him at the forefront of Polish modernism. From the mid-1960s his style turned toward a stark, modal simplicity often described as "holy minimalism".

Works

The Symphony No. 3 (1976), setting texts that include a medieval lament, a message scratched on a Gestapo prison wall, and a Silesian folk song, became a phenomenal commercial success after a 1992 recording by the London Sinfonietta with soprano Dawn Upshaw sold over a million copies. Other notable works include Beatus Vir (1979), the string quartets written for the Kronos Quartet, and the choral work Totus Tuus (1987).
