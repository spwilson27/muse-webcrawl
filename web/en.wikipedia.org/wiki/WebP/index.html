WebP is a raster graphics file format developed by Google intended as a replacement for JPEG, PNG, and GIF file formats. It supports both lossy and lossless compression, as well as animation and alpha transparency.

Google announced the WebP format in September 2010, and released the first stable version of its supporting library in April 2018. Support by different browsers and image editors has increased over the years; support by most browsers had reached near-universality by 2022. The format is based on the RIFF container format and uses the VP8 video codec's intra-frame coding for lossy compression, as well as a dedicated lossless coding format.

History

WebP was first announced in 2010, deriving from technology Google acquired with its purchase of On2 Technologies, the developer of the VP8 codec. Lossless compression and alpha transparency for lossy images were added in 2011, and animation support followed. Chrome was the first browser to support the format natively, with other major browsers adopting it over the following decade; Safari added support in 2020.

Technology

WebP's lossy compression uses predictive coding, borrowing ideas from VP8: blocks of pixels are predicted from neighboring blocks and only the difference is encoded. Lossless compression uses a dedicated format (VP8L) employing techniques such as LZ77, Huffman coding, and a color cache. Google has claimed that WebP lossy images are typically 25–34% smaller than comparable JPEG images, and lossless WebP images about 26% smaller than comparable PNGs.

Support

WebP is natively supported in Google Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Opera, and Safari, and by many image tools and libraries. Google also promoted a successor format, AVIF, based on the AV1 codec, which offers further compression gains.
