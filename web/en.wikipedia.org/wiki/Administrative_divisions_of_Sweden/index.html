Administrative divisions of Sweden

Image placeholder: Sweden's 21 counties

The administrative divisions of Sweden comprise 21 counties (Swedish: län) and 290 municipalities (kommuner). Counties are the principal areas of state administration and correspond, with some exceptions, to the areas of the 21 regions (regioner), which are self-governing bodies chiefly responsible for public healthcare. Municipalities are responsible for local services such as schools, social care, water supply and local planning.

Counties

Each county has a County Administrative Board (länsstyrelse), a government agency headed by a governor (landshövding) appointed by the national government. The board coordinates state activities in the county, including environmental protection, civil defence, and regional development. The county system dates largely from the reforms of Axel Oxenstierna in 1634.

Regions

The regions (formerly called county councils, landsting) are directly elected and levy income tax. Their main responsibility is the public health system, including hospitals and primary care, along with public transport in several counties and regional cultural and development tasks. Region Gotland is unique in combining regional and municipal functions, as the island has a single municipality.

Municipalities

Sweden's 290 municipalities vary enormously in size, from Stockholm with nearly a million inhabitants to small rural municipalities of a few thousand. They are governed by elected municipal councils and executives, and are financed chiefly by a proportional local income tax.

Historical divisions

Traditional provinces (landskap) such as Uppland, Scania (Skåne), Dalarna and Lapland no longer have administrative functions but remain important for cultural identity, dialects, heraldry and tourism.
