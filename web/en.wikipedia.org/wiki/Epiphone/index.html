Epiphone — American musical instrument brand, founded 1873

The Epiphone Company is an American musical instrument manufacturer founded in 1873 by Anastasios Stathopoulos in Smyrna, Ottoman Empire (now İzmir, Turkey), and now based in Nashville, Tennessee. Epiphone has been a subsidiary of Gibson since 1957, and is best known for its electric and acoustic guitars, which historically competed directly with Gibson's own models and today largely serve as Gibson's more affordable line.

History

Anastasios Stathopoulos began making fiddles and lutes in 1873; after emigrating to New York City, the family firm — run by his son Epaminondas ("Epi") Stathopoulos, from whose name plus the Greek phonē ("sound") the brand name was coined — became a major maker of mandolins and then of highly regarded archtop guitars in the 1930s, competing head-to-head with Gibson. The company also produced banjos, amplifiers, and some of the earliest electric guitars. Labour disputes and Epi's death in 1943 weakened the firm, and in 1957 it was bought by Gibson's parent company, CMI.

Instruments

Under Gibson, Epiphone introduced models of its own such as the Casino, Sheraton, and Riviera hollow-body electrics, and the Texan acoustic. The Casino became famous as the guitar used by all three guitar-playing members of the Beatles, and the brand was also played by artists from Django Reinhardt to Noel Gallagher and John Lee Hooker. From the 1970s onward, most Epiphones were manufactured in Asia (Japan, then Korea, then China and Indonesia). The brand's range today spans entry-level instruments through the "Inspired by Gibson" series to US-made and signature models.
