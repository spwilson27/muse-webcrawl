Wijk bij Duurstede
Country: Netherlands
Province: Utrecht
Population: approx. 23,000

Wijk bij Duurstede is a municipality and a city in the central Netherlands, in the province of Utrecht, located where the Rhine branches into the Lek and the Kromme Rijn. The municipality also includes the villages of Cothen and Langbroek.

History

The city stands near the site of Dorestad, one of the most important trading ports of north-western Europe in the early Middle Ages, which flourished in the 7th to 9th centuries before declining under Viking raids and silting. The present town received city rights in 1300 and is dominated by the ruins of Duurstede Castle, whose surviving keep dates from the 13th century and whose round tower was added by Bishop David of Burgundy in the 15th century.

Landmarks

Landmarks include Duurstede Castle, the Grote Kerk (Great Church) with its distinctive unfinished tower, and a windmill, the Rijn en Lek, which famously stands atop a city gate.
