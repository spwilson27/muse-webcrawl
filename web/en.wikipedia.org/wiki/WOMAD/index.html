WOMAD (World of Music, Arts and Dance) is an international arts festival. The central aim of WOMAD is to celebrate the world's many forms of music, arts and dance. The festival was founded in 1980 by English musician Peter Gabriel, together with Thomas Brooman, Bob Hooton, Mark Kidel, Stephen Pritchard, Martin Elbourne and Jonathan Arthur. The first WOMAD festival was held in Shepton Mallet, England, in July 1982.

History

Gabriel had a particular interest in world music and was keen to create a festival that would showcase musicians from around the world to audiences in the West. Despite critical praise, the first festival lost money and nearly ended Gabriel's solo career; he was rescued by a one-off reunion concert with his former band Genesis, organized at the suggestion of his manager, Tony Smith, which raised enough money to pay the festival's debts.

WOMAD festivals have since been staged in more than 27 countries, with events in the United Kingdom (at Charlton Park, Wiltshire), Australia (WOMADelaide in Adelaide), New Zealand (in New Plymouth, Taranaki), Spain, South Africa, the United Arab Emirates and elsewhere. Over the decades millions of people have attended WOMAD events, and hundreds of artists from scores of countries have performed.

Format

In addition to performances, WOMAD festivals typically include workshops led by artists, children's activities, food stalls representing many cuisines, and market stalls. The organization also runs educational projects through the WOMAD Foundation, bringing world music and arts into schools.
