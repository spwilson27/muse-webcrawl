Ius gentium

Ius gentium (Latin for "law of nations") was, in Roman law, the body of rules governing dealings between citizens and foreigners (peregrini), and between foreigners themselves, within the Roman state. It was distinguished from the ius civile, the civil law applicable only to Roman citizens.

Development

From 242 BC disputes involving foreigners were heard by a dedicated magistrate, the praetor peregrinus. In deciding cases, praetors drew on principles common to the legal systems of many peoples, producing a flexible, equity-based body of law that came to influence Roman private law generally. The jurist Gaius described the ius gentium as that which "natural reason has established among all human beings".

Legacy

Through medieval and early modern writers such as Francisco de Vitoria and Hugo Grotius, the term passed into modern usage as a synonym for international law, the law governing relations between states.
