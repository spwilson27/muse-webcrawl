Embryo

An embryo is the early developmental stage of a multicellular organism. In humans, the embryo is the developing organism from fertilisation until the end of the eighth week, after which it is called a fetus.

Development

The fertilised egg (zygote) divides into a blastocyst that implants in the uterus about a week after Fertilisation. Gastrulation lays down three germ layers — ectoderm (skin, nervous system), mesoderm (muscle, bone, blood) and endoderm (gut, lungs, liver) — from which all organs form (organogenesis). By eight weeks the human embryo is ~3 cm long with a beating heart (from ~week 3–4), limb buds and the beginnings of all major organs.

Research and ethics

Embryos are studied in developmental biology and produced in IVF; stem cells derived from embryos (embryonic stem cells) can form any tissue, powering Regenerative medicine — alongside long-running ethical debate about the moral status of embryos and the limits of embryo research.
