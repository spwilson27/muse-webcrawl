Sustainable Development Goal 5

Sustainable Development Goal 5 (SDG 5 or Global Goal 5) is about gender equality and is one of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals established by the United Nations in 2015. Its official wording is: "Achieve gender equality and empower all women and girls." Progress is measured against nine targets and fourteen indicators.

Targets

The targets include ending all forms of discrimination against women and girls; eliminating violence against women, trafficking, and harmful practices such as child marriage and female genital mutilation; recognising unpaid care and domestic work; ensuring women's full participation in political, economic, and public life; ensuring universal access to sexual and reproductive health and rights; and reforms to give women equal rights to economic resources, land, and property, alongside wider use of enabling technology and adoption of gender-equality legislation.

Background and progress

SDG 5 continues the work of the Millennium Development Goals, which had a narrower focus on education parity and maternal health. UN reports note that legal discrimination persists in many countries, women remain under-represented in parliaments and management, and at the current pace full gender equality remains far off — UN Women has estimated that gender parity in leadership and the closing of legal protection gaps could take generations. The COVID-19 pandemic disproportionately affected women's employment and increased unpaid care burdens, setting back progress.
