The Winter Palace (Russian: Зимний дворец, Zimniy dvorets) is a palace in Saint Petersburg that served as the official residence of the House of Romanov from 1732 to 1917. The present, fourth palace — a vast green-and-white Elizabethan Baroque building of some 1,500 rooms — was designed by Francesco Bartolomeo Rastrelli and built between 1754 and 1762 for Empress Elizabeth and completed under Catherine the Great.

Located on Palace Square beside the Neva River, the palace was the scene of the Bloody Sunday massacre of 1905 at its gates and of the storming by Bolshevik forces in October 1917, an event mythologised in Eisenstein's film October. Since the 1990s it has formed the main building of the State Hermitage Museum.

History

Peter the Great built the first Winter Palace in 1711. Successive palaces on the site grew grander under Anna and Elizabeth. Rastrelli's building, incorporating 1,786 doors, 1,945 windows and 117 staircases, was the fourth and last. After a devastating fire in 1837 the interiors were rebuilt within a year under Nicholas I.

Architecture

The palace is a monumental rectangle around a courtyard, its Neva facade nearly 200 metres long and rising in three storeys of columns, stucco and gilded ornament in Rastrelli's mature Baroque. State interiors include the Jordan Staircase, the Malachite Drawing Room, the White Hall and the Gallery of 1812.

Imperial residence

The Winter Palace was the principal winter residence of the tsars (the court summered at Tsarskoye Selo or Peterhof). Alexander II survived several assassination attempts there, including the 1880 bombing of the dining room by the revolutionary Khalturin. Nicholas II preferred Tsarskoye Selo but used the palace on state occasions; in 1905 troops fired on a peaceful march of workers approaching the palace, killing scores on Bloody Sunday.

Revolution and museum

In 1917 the palace housed the Provisional Government; its storming on the night of 25–26 October (O.S.) by Red Guards was later dramatised far beyond its modest scale. The palace and the Hermitage were declared a museum, and today the Winter Palace anchors one of the world's largest museum complexes, holding part of a collection of some three million items.
