Fuel oil

Fuel oil is a fraction obtained from the distillation of petroleum that is used as a fuel in burners, furnaces, boilers and engines. The term covers a range of products from light distillate heating oils to heavy residual oils used in marine propulsion and power generation.


Fuel oil storage

Classification

In the United States, fuel oils are traditionally graded from No. 1 (a light distillate similar to kerosene) through No. 6, the heavy residual oil also called Bunker C. No. 2 fuel oil is widely used for domestic and commercial heating in oil-fired furnaces. The heavier grades must often be preheated before they will flow and atomise properly in a burner.

Uses

Heavy fuel oil has long been the standard fuel for large marine diesel engines and for oil-fired power stations. Heating oil remains common in regions without natural gas distribution. Since 2020, the International Maritime Organization's IMO 2020 rules have limited the sulphur content of marine fuels to 0.5%, driving a shift to low-sulphur blends or the use of exhaust scrubbers.

Environmental issues

Burning residual fuel oil produces sulphur oxides, particulates and more carbon dioxide per unit of energy than lighter fuels, because the heavy fraction contains more carbon, sulphur and ash-forming constituents. Oil spills involving heavy fuel oil are particularly persistent and difficult to clean up.
