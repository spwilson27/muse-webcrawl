The Big Hole, open mine, or Kimberley Mine (Afrikaans: Groot Gat) is an open-pit and underground diamond mine in Kimberley, South Africa, claimed to be the deepest hole excavated by hand. It lies in the kimberlite volcanic pipe where diamonds were first discovered on Colesberg Kopje in 1871.

[Image: The vast open pit of the Big Hole at Kimberley filled with green water]

History

The discovery of the "Star of South Africa" diamond in 1869 triggered a rush to the Orange River banks and then to the dry diggings around Kimberley. Thousands of diggers worked small individual claims on the hill, which was progressively dug away into the pit; disputes, collapses, and inefficiency spurred consolidation, and by 1888 Cecil Rhodes's De Beers Consolidated Mines controlled the mine. Surface mining ended in 1914 when underground workings took over, continuing until 2005.

Description

From 1871 to 1914, some 22.5 million tonnes of earth were excavated, yielding about 14.5 million carats (roughly 2,722 kilograms) of diamonds. The pit is about 463 metres across and was dug to about 240 metres deep; it has since partly filled with water to a depth of about 215 metres below the rim. Today the Big Hole is a major tourist attraction with a museum and reconstructed old town adjacent to the rim.
