Simo Häyhä (17 December 1905 – 1 April 2002), nicknamed "The White Death" by the Red Army, was a Finnish military sniper during the Winter War of 1939–1940. He is credited with 505 confirmed kills of Soviet soldiers, the highest number recorded for a sniper in any major war, achieved in fewer than 100 days of combat in temperatures as low as −40 °C.

Häyhä after receiving his honorary rifle, 1940.

Winter War service

A farmer, hunter and champion marksman from Rautjärvi in Karelia, Häyhä served at the Battle of Kollaa, where the Finnish 12th Division held a defensive line against far larger Soviet forces. Dressed in all-white camouflage and using a Finnish M/28-30 rifle — a variant of the Mosin–Nagant — with iron sights rather than a telescopic scope (whose glass could fog and glint, and which required a higher head position), he packed snow in front of his muzzle to prevent the shot puffing it up, and held snow in his mouth so his breath vapour would not betray him. Soviet units reportedly directed artillery and counter-snipers against him personally.

On 6 March 1940, a week before the war ended, Häyhä was shot in the jaw by a Soviet soldier using an explosive bullet. He was found unconscious among corpses and regained consciousness on the day the peace was announced; the wound left his face permanently disfigured and required years of surgery. Promoted from corporal directly to second lieutenant — an exceptional jump — he returned to farming and moose hunting, and became a mentor to generations of Finnish hunters. Modest about his record ("I did what I was told, as well as I could"), he died in 2002 at the age of 96.
