Cabergoline

Cabergoline, sold under the brand name Dostinex among others, is a dopaminergic medication used in the treatment of high prolactin levels, prolactinomas, Parkinson's disease, and for other indications. It is taken by mouth. Cabergoline is an ergot derivative and a potent, long-acting dopamine D2 receptor agonist.

Medical uses

Cabergoline is a first-line treatment for prolactin-secreting pituitary adenomas (prolactinomas) and idiopathic hyperprolactinemia, normalising prolactin levels and often shrinking tumours, usually dosed once or twice weekly because of its long half-life. It has also been used at much higher doses as an adjunct in Parkinson's disease, though this use has declined, and off-label for suppression of lactation and for other endocrine conditions.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea, dizziness, headache and orthostatic hypotension. Of greater concern, high cumulative doses of ergot-derived dopamine agonists have been associated with fibrotic reactions, including cardiac valvulopathy — a risk that led to monitoring recommendations and reduced use in Parkinson's disease; the risk at the low doses used for prolactinoma is considered much smaller. Impulse control disorders (pathological gambling, hypersexuality) are a class effect of dopamine agonists.

Pharmacology

Cabergoline acts primarily as an agonist at dopamine D2 receptors on pituitary lactotroph cells, inhibiting prolactin secretion, with activity also at D3, serotonergic (notably 5-HT2B, implicated in valvulopathy) and adrenergic receptors.
