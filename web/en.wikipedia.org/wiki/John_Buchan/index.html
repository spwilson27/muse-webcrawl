John Buchan

John Buchan, 1st Baron Tweedsmuir (1875–1940) was a Scottish novelist, historian and politician who served as Governor General of Canada from 1935 until his death. He is best known for the thriller The Thirty-Nine Steps (1915) and its hero Richard Hannay.

Life and career

Born in Perth and educated at Glasgow and Oxford, Buchan worked in South Africa, publishing and law before the First World War, during which he wrote propaganda and served in intelligence. He was elected MP for the Combined Scottish Universities in 1927.

Writing

Besides the Hannay novels (Greenmantle, Mr Standfast, The Three Hostages, The Island of Sheep), he wrote biographies of Montrose, Sir Walter Scott and Augustus, and the memoir Memory Hold-the-Door. As Governor General he promoted Canadian arts and national unity; the John Buchan Society preserves his legacy.
