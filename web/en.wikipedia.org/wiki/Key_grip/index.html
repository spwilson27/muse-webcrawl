Key grip

Key grip
OccupationFilm crew
DepartmentGrip

In US and Canadian filmmaking, the key grip supervises all grip crews and grip equipment for a production. Grips are technicians who set up, move, and operate production equipment; the key grip is the head of the grip department, working closely with the director of photography (DP) to achieve the camera and lighting setups required for each shot.

The key grip's responsibilities include rigging camera supports such as dollies, cranes, and camera cars; building scaffolding and overhead rigs; shaping light with flags, cutters, silks, and diffusion frames; and ensuring the safety of all rigging on set. The key grip oversees the best boy grip, who manages the crew and equipment inventory, and the dolly grip, who operates the camera dolly.

Role by region

In the United States, grips handle camera support and lighting modification but do not handle electrical equipment, which belongs to the electric department under the gaffer. In much of Europe and Australia, grips may also perform some tasks assigned to electrics in the American system. The British system differs again, with the key grip primarily responsible for camera movement and support.

Safety

Because grips build structures that are often overhead or load-bearing, the key grip carries significant responsibility for on-set safety, checking rigging loads and securing equipment against wind and movement.
