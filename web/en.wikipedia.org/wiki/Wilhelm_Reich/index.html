Wilhelm Reich

Wilhelm Reich (1897–1957), Austrian psychoanalyst.

Wilhelm Reich (24 March 1897 – 3 November 1957) was an Austrian doctor of medicine and psychoanalyst, a member of the second generation of analysts after Sigmund Freud. His work on character analysis, sexuality, and the social roots of neurosis influenced the sexual revolution of the 1960s, while his later theory of "orgone energy" is regarded as pseudoscience.

Psychoanalytic career

Reich joined Freud's circle in Vienna in the 1920s, developing the technique of character analysis described in his book Charakteranalyse (1933). He argued that neuroses arise from repressive social and sexual conditions, founded free sex-counselling clinics for workers, and in The Mass Psychology of Fascism (1933) analysed the authoritarian family as the seed of fascist ideology. Expelled from both the Communist Party and the International Psychoanalytic Association, he fled the Nazis through Scandinavia and emigrated to the United States in 1939.

Orgone and final years

In America Reich claimed to have discovered "orgone", a universal life energy, and built "orgone accumulators" said to treat disease, including cancer. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration obtained an injunction against the devices in 1954; Reich was convicted of contempt for violating it and sentenced to two years in prison. He died in the federal penitentiary at Lewisburg, Pennsylvania, in 1957, and quantities of his books were burned under court order — one of the most notorious cases of literary censorship in American history.
