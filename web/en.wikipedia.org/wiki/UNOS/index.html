United Network for Organ Sharing

The United Network for Organ Sharing (UNOS) is a non-profit scientific and educational organisation that administers the United States' organ transplantation system. Based in Richmond, Virginia, UNOS has operated the Organ Procurement and Transplantation Network (OPTN) under contract with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services since the network was created by the National Organ Transplant Act of 1984.

Role

UNOS maintains the national waiting list of patients needing organ transplants and runs the computerised matching system that allocates deceased-donor organs — kidneys, livers, hearts, lungs, pancreases and intestines — according to policies developed with the transplant community. Its system operates around the clock, matching donor organs to recipients by medical criteria including blood type, tissue type, urgency, body size and waiting time. UNOS also collects and publishes transplant data, develops professional education, and promotes organ donation.

History and reform

UNOS was founded in 1977 as an outgrowth of a regional organ-sharing organisation and won the first OPTN contract in 1986, holding it continuously thereafter. In 2023 the federal government moved to modernise the OPTN, ending UNOS's decades-long monopoly by dividing the network's functions among multiple contractors, following criticism of the system's technology and performance; UNOS continues to perform core functions as the reforms are implemented.
