Political commentator

A political commentator is a person who offers analysis and opinion on politics for newspapers, magazines, television, radio or online media. Commentators — often called pundits — interpret elections, policy debates and political strategy for a general audience, and may also be academics, former politicians, campaign strategists or journalists.

Role

Unlike news reporters, who are expected to report facts neutrally, commentators are generally understood to argue from a viewpoint, which may be aligned with a political party or ideology. In the United States, the rise of 24-hour cable news channels created a large class of professional pundits appearing on panel programmes; in the United Kingdom, commentators are prominent in newspaper opinion pages and broadcast programmes such as Newsnight and Question Time. The internet and social media have further broadened the field to independent writers, podcasters and video creators.

Criticism

Political commentary is criticised for emphasising "horse-race" coverage of polls and strategy over policy substance, for rewarding confident prediction over accuracy, and for amplifying polarisation. Studies of pundit predictions have often found them little better than chance, a point popularised by Philip Tetlock's research on expert political judgment.
