By-law

A by-law (or bylaw) is a rule or law established by an organization or community to regulate itself, as allowed or provided for by some higher authority. The higher authority, generally a legislature or some other governmental body, establishes the degree of control that the by-laws may exercise.

Municipal by-laws

In Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and other Commonwealth countries, municipal governments enact by-laws on matters such as zoning, parking, noise, animals and building standards under powers delegated by provincial or state legislation. Such by-laws are typically enforced by bylaw enforcement officers and municipal courts.

Corporate and organizational bylaws

For corporations, non-profit organizations, condominiums and homeowners' associations, bylaws are internal governing rules subordinate to the articles of incorporation or charter. They typically cover membership, meetings, quorums, elections, officers' duties and amendment procedures. Robert's Rules of Order is a widely used parliamentary authority for conducting meetings held under bylaws.

Etymology

The word is thought to derive from Old Norse býjar, genitive of býr, a town or farm — hence a "town law" — entering English through the Danelaw.
