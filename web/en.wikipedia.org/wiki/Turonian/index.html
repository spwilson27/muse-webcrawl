Turonian

Turonian
Chronology: 93.9 – 89.8 Ma
Series: Late Cretaceous
Preceded by: Cenomanian · Followed by: Coniacian

The Turonian is, in the International Chronostratigraphic Chart, the second age in the Late Cretaceous epoch, spanning the time between approximately 93.9 and 89.8 million years ago. It was preceded by the Cenomanian and followed by the Coniacian. The stage was named after the French city of Tours (Latin: Turones).

Definition and events

The base of the Turonian is defined by the first appearance of the ammonite Watinoceras devonense. The Cenomanian–Turonian boundary is marked by Oceanic Anoxic Event 2, a global episode of marine anoxia and organic-carbon burial associated with a major extinction of marine invertebrates, high sea levels and some of the warmest conditions of the Phanerozoic; the Turonian saw the maximum extent of the Western Interior Seaway in North America and widespread chalk deposition in Europe.
