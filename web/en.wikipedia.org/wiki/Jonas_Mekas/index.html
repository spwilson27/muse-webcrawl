Jonas Mekas (December 24, 1922 – January 23, 2019) was a Lithuanian-American filmmaker, poet, and artist, often called the godfather of American avant-garde cinema. He was a central figure of the New American Cinema movement and a tireless promoter and archivist of experimental film.

Early life and emigration

Mekas was born in the village of Semeniškiai in northern Lithuania. During the Second World War he and his brother Adolfas were arrested by the Nazis and spent eight months in a forced labor camp in Germany, followed by years in displaced persons camps, where Jonas studied philosophy at the University of Mainz and began publishing poetry. In 1949 the brothers emigrated to the United States, settling in Williamsburg, Brooklyn. Two weeks after arriving, Jonas borrowed money to buy a 16mm Bolex camera and began filming his surroundings.

Champion of avant-garde cinema

In 1954 the Mekas brothers founded the journal Film Culture, and Jonas wrote the influential "Movie Journal" column in The Village Voice from 1958. He helped organize the Film-Makers' Cooperative (1962), a non-exclusive distribution network for independent filmmakers, and in 1970 co-founded Anthology Film Archives in New York, devoted to the preservation and exhibition of avant-garde cinema and its Essential Cinema Repertory.

Diary films

Mekas's own films pioneered the diary form, assembling footage of his daily life, friends, and journeys into lyrical, fragmentary works. Major films include Walden (Diaries, Notes & Sketches) (1969), Reminiscences of a Journey to Lithuania (1972), Lost, Lost, Lost (1976), and As I Was Moving Ahead Occasionally I Saw Brief Glimpses of Beauty (2000). His writings in Lithuanian poetry and his memoirs are also highly regarded in his native country, where the Jonas Mekas Visual Arts Center was established in Vilnius in 2007.
