Hypatian Codex

The Hypatian Codex (Ipatievsky Codex) is a compendium of three Rus' chronicles: the Primary Chronicle, the Kyiv Chronicle, and the Galician-Volhynian Chronicle. It is a fundamental source of the history of Kievan Rus', the medieval East Slavic state, and of the early history of Ukraine, Russia, and Belarus. The codex takes its name from the Ipatiev Monastery in Kostroma, where it was found in the late eighteenth century.

Manuscript

The codex is a paper manuscript of 307 folios, copied around 1425, most likely in Pskov. It was discovered in the library of the Ipatiev Monastery and brought to scholarly attention by the historian Nikolai Karamzin in the early nineteenth century. The manuscript is now kept in the Russian National Library in St. Petersburg. It is written in a Church Slavonic semi-uncial hand in two columns.

Contents

  • The Primary Chronicle (the "Tale of Bygone Years"), covering Slavic origins and the history of Rus' from the ninth century to about 1117.
  • The Kyiv Chronicle, a continuation covering the years 1118–1198, assembled from several princely chronicles.
  • The Galician-Volhynian Chronicle, covering 1201–1292, notable for its literary, panegyric style and its praise of Prince Daniel of Galicia.

Significance

Along with the Laurentian Codex, the Hypatian Codex preserves the fullest texts of the early Rus' chronicles. Its version of the Primary Chronicle differs in places from the Laurentian recension and is a key witness for reconstructing the original text. The Galician-Volhynian portion is unique to this codex and provides the principal narrative source for the history of southwestern Rus' in the thirteenth century, including the Mongol invasions and the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia.
