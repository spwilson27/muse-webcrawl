Kuril Islands

The Kuril Islands or Kurile Islands are a volcanic archipelago administered as part of Sakhalin Oblast in the Russian Far East. The islands stretch approximately 1,300 km (810 mi) northeast from Hokkaido in Japan to Kamchatka Peninsula in Russia, separating the Sea of Okhotsk from the north-western Pacific Ocean. There are 56 islands and many minor rocks, with a total land area of about 10,500 km² and around 20,000 inhabitants, mostly on the largest islands of Iturup, Paramushir, Kunashir and Shikotan.

Geography and history

The chain is volcanic in origin, with some 100 volcanoes, about 40 of them active, and frequent earthquakes and tsunamis; the highest point is the Alaid volcano on Atlasov Island (2,339 m). The indigenous Ainu people inhabited the islands before Russian and Japanese colonisation in the 18th and 19th centuries.

Under the Treaty of Shimoda (1855), the Russo-Japanese border ran between Iturup and Urup; the Treaty of Saint Petersburg (1875) gave Japan the entire chain in exchange for Russian control of Sakhalin. In August–September 1945, Soviet forces occupied the whole archipelago at the end of the Second World War, and the Japanese population was expelled. Japan continues to claim the four southernmost islands — Iturup (Etorofu), Kunashir, Shikotan and the Habomai group — as its "Northern Territories". This Kuril Islands dispute has prevented Russia and Japan from signing a formal peace treaty after the Second World War and remains a major obstacle in their relations.

The islands' economy is based mainly on fishing and fish processing; their surrounding waters are among the most productive fishing grounds in the North Pacific.
