The Pour le Mérite is an order of merit established in 1740 by King Frederick the Great of Prussia. The blue enamelled Maltese cross with golden eagles, worn on a black and silver neck ribbon, became Prussia's highest order of merit for both military gallantry and civil achievement in the arts and sciences.

Military class

During the First World War the military class, nicknamed the "Blue Max", was awarded to distinguished officers, most famously to fighter aces such as Manfred von Richthofen, Oswald Boelcke, and Max Immelmann, after whom the nickname derives. Hermann Göring was among its later recipients. Military awards ceased with the abdication of the Kaiser in 1918.

Civil class

Frederick William IV founded a separate civil class in 1842 for achievements in the arts and sciences; its members have included Alexander von Humboldt, Charles Darwin, and Käthe Kollwitz. The civil order was revived in 1952 in the Federal Republic of Germany and continues today under the patronage of the German Federal President.
