The Théâtre du Châtelet is a theatre and opera house in Paris, situated on the Place du Châtelet in the 1st arrondissement, facing the Théâtre de la Ville across the square. Designed by architect Gabriel Davioud under Baron Haussmann's renovation of Paris, it opened on 19 August 1862 in the presence of Empress Eugénie.

History

The theatre hosted a wide variety of performances in the 19th century, from plays and operettas — including the premiere of Tchaikovsky's Swan Lake excerpts and many Offenbach productions — to spectacular féeries. In the early 20th century it became famous for the Ballets Russes: Diaghilev's company premiered Stravinsky's Petrushka there in 1911, and Parade (music by Satie, designs by Picasso) in 1917.

Modern era

Since the late 20th century the theatre has been known for acclaimed productions of Broadway musicals in English, as well as concerts, dance, and opera. The building underwent an extensive renovation and reopened in 2019 with restored Second Empire decor and improved stage facilities.
