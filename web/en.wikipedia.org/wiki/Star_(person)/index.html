Star (person)

A star is a person who is widely recognised and celebrated for their work, most commonly in entertainment fields such as film, television, theatre, music and sport. The term derives from the idea of a person who "shines" above others in prominence, and is related to the broader concept of a celebrity. Individuals described as stars are typically considered to have exceptional drawing power — their name alone can attract audiences, sell tickets, or guarantee media attention.

Terminology

The vocabulary of stardom includes graduated or intensified forms such as superstar and megastar, as well as field-specific terms like movie star, rock star, pop star and sports star. The phenomenon by which a person attains this status is called stardom, and the system of production and promotion surrounding stars in the film industry was historically known as the star system, developed by Hollywood studios in the early 20th century.

History

Although celebrated performers existed earlier — for example, the actor David Garrick in the 18th century and the singer Jenny Lind in the 19th — the modern star system emerged with cinema. Studios initially kept actors anonymous, but audience demand for named performers, such as Florence Lawrence, "the Biograph Girl", led studios to publicise and cultivate their actors' images. During the Golden Age of Hollywood, stars were contracted to individual studios, which controlled their publicity, roles and public personas.

Characteristics

Sociological and film studies describe stardom as combining professional achievement, a distinctive public image, and a degree of mystique or charisma. Stars often serve as brands, endorsing products and influencing fashion and popular culture. In sports, stars similarly command the largest salaries, transfer fees and sponsorship deals.
