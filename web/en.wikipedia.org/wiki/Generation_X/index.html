Generation X (often shortened to Gen X) is the demographic cohort following the baby boomers and preceding the millennials. Researchers and commentators generally use birth years from the mid-1960s to around 1980; the U.S. Pew Research Center defines Gen X as those born from 1965 to 1980.

Characteristics

Gen Xers grew up during a period of shifting social values, rising divorce rates, and increased maternal participation in the workforce; many were "latchkey kids" who spent time unsupervised after school. The generation came of age with the advent of MTV, home computers, and video games, and its youth culture is associated with grunge and alternative rock, hip hop, punk, and independent film.

Commentators have often characterized the generation as independent, resourceful, and skeptical of institutions, though such generalizations are debated. The term "Generation X" was popularized by Douglas Coupland's 1991 novel Generation X: Tales for an Accelerated Culture, which depicted young adults working in low-prestige "McJobs."

Demographics and impact

Smaller than both the boomer and millennial cohorts, Gen X is sometimes called the "forgotten generation." Its members were the first generation to grow up with widespread access to personal computing, and many became entrepreneurs during the dot-com era. In politics and business, Gen Xers increasingly assumed leadership roles in the 2010s and 2020s.
