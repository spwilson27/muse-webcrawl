Pitot pressure

Pitot pressure (also called stagnation pressure or total pressure) is the pressure a moving fluid exerts when it is brought to rest isentropically at a stagnation point. It is the sum of the fluid's static pressure and its dynamic pressure, and it is what a Pitot tube measures. Pitot pressure is fundamental to airspeed measurement in aviation.

A Pitot tube faces into the flow and senses total pressure

Definition

For incompressible flow, Bernoulli's equation gives the total pressure p0 as

p0 = p + ½ρv2,

where p is the static pressure, ρ the fluid density and v the flow speed. The second term is the dynamic pressure. Measuring total and static pressure therefore yields the flow velocity: v = √(2(p0 − p)/ρ).

Compressible flow

At higher speeds the fluid compresses as it stagnates, and the relation between pitot pressure and Mach number is given by the isentropic flow relations for subsonic flow; for supersonic flow a normal shock stands ahead of the probe and the Rayleigh pitot formula applies. In a Pitot–static tube on an aircraft, pitot pressure feeds the airspeed indicator, while combinations of total pressure, static pressure and total temperature are used to compute Mach number and true airspeed.

Applications

Pitot pressure measurements are used in aircraft air data systems, wind tunnels, ventilation and industrial flow measurement, and in Formula One and other motorsport for aerodynamic testing. Blockage or icing of pitot probes has been a factor in several aviation accidents, which is why airliners carry multiple heated, independent probes.
