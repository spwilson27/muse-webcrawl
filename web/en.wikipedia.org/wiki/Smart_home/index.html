ControlSmartphone apps, voice assistants, automation rules

A smart home or home automation system monitors and controls home attributes such as lighting, climate, entertainment systems and appliances, using networked devices that can be operated locally or remotely, often through a smartphone app or voice assistant. Smart home technology may also include home security elements such as access control, cameras and alarm systems.

Applications

  • Lighting – smart bulbs and switches with dimming, colour control and schedules.
  • Climate – learning thermostats and connected heating, ventilation and air conditioning.
  • Security and access – smart locks, video doorbells, cameras and intrusion alarms.
  • Entertainment – multi-room audio, smart televisions and streaming devices.
  • Energy management – monitoring consumption and shifting loads to off-peak hours.
  • Assisted living – sensors supporting elderly or disabled residents living independently.

Technology

Smart home devices communicate over wireless protocols such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, Z-Wave and Thread, typically coordinated by a hub or by cloud services. Interoperability between vendors was long a problem; the Matter standard, launched in 2022 by the Connectivity Standards Alliance with backing from Apple, Google, Amazon and Samsung, aims to let devices from different ecosystems work together locally. Voice assistants such as Amazon Alexa, Google Assistant and Apple Siri provide a common control interface.

Concerns

Smart homes raise issues of privacy — microphones, cameras and usage data are collected in intimate spaces — and of security, since compromised devices have been recruited into botnets. Dependence on cloud services also means features can be lost when manufacturers shut down servers or discontinue products.
