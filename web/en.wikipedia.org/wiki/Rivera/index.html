Rivera is the capital city of Rivera Department in northern Uruguay. It lies on the border with Brazil, directly across an open, unmarked frontier from the Brazilian city of Santana do Livramento; the two cities together form a single urban area of well over 150,000 people, divided only by an avenue and a shared park, the Parque Internacional.

History

The settlement began in the 1860s as Ceballos, a customs and military post on the contested northern frontier, and was officially founded as Rivera in 1862, named in honour of General Fructuoso Rivera, the first president of Uruguay. Its position on the border made it a centre for trade — and historically for smuggling — between the two countries.

Economy and culture

Rivera's duty-free shops attract large numbers of Brazilian shoppers buying imported goods, and the city hosts a major casino and resort. Residents commonly speak a border mixture of Spanish and Portuguese known locally as Portuñol or "fronterizo". The city is served by Route 5 from Montevideo and by a railway line to the capital.
