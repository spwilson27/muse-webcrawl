Arabia Petraea

Province ofRoman Empire
EstablishedAD 106
CapitalPetra, later Bostra
Today part ofJordan, Syria, Israel, Egypt (Sinai)

Arabia Petraea, also called Provincia Arabia or simply Arabia, was a frontier province of the Roman Empire created in AD 106 when the emperor Trajan annexed the Nabataean kingdom. It stretched from Syria southward along the eastern side of the Jordan valley to the Red Sea, encompassing modern Jordan, southern Syria, the Negev, and the Sinai Peninsula.

History

The annexation, carried out by the governor of Syria, Aulus Cornelius Palma, appears to have been largely peaceful, and the Nabataean capital Petra initially remained the provincial centre before Bostra — home to the legion III Cyrenaica — became the seat of the governor. The Romans secured the caravan trade in frankincense and myrrh and built the Via Nova Traiana, a paved highway from Bostra to the Red Sea port of Aila (Aqaba), studded with forts that formed part of the eastern limes.

Administration and legacy

In Diocletian's reforms the province was divided, with the south becoming Palaestina Salutaris. Cities such as Petra, Gerasa (Jerash), and Philadelphia (Amman) flourished in late antiquity, and the province's well-preserved ruins — Petra's rock-cut tombs, Bostra's theatre, and desert fortifications — remain major archaeological sites.
