Living National Treasure (Japan)

Living National Treasure (人間国宝, Ningen Kokuhō) is a popular term for individuals designated by the Japanese government as Preservers of Important Intangible Cultural Properties. The designation was created under the 1950 Law for the Protection of Cultural Properties and has been awarded since 1955 to masters of traditional arts and crafts whose skills are considered of high historical or artistic value.

Designation

The Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology designates individuals (and, in some categories, groups) in fields such as:

  • Performing arts — noh, bunraku, kabuki, traditional dance, and traditional music such as gagaku and shakuhachi
  • Crafts — ceramics, textiles (including dyeing and weaving), lacquerware, metalworking, doll making, wood and bamboo crafts, and papermaking

Designees receive an annual grant (2 million yen for individuals) intended to support the transmission of their skills to successors. At any time there are roughly one hundred living holders in the individual categories; designation lapses on death.

Notable holders

Well-known recipients have included potter Shōji Hamada, kabuki actor Nakamura Utaemon VI, bunraku puppeteer Yoshida Bungorō, and swordsmiths recognised for preserving traditional blade-forging. The system influenced similar programmes elsewhere, including South Korea's holders of Important Intangible Cultural Properties, often translated by the same English term.
