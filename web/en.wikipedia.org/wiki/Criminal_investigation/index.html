Criminal investigation

A criminal investigation is the process by which law enforcement studies the facts of a suspected crime in order to identify the offender, establish what happened, and gather evidence suitable for prosecution. It involves interviewing witnesses and suspects, examining crime scenes, and applying forensic science.

Process

Investigations typically move from securing and documenting the scene, through collecting physical evidence and witness statements, to developing and testing hypotheses about the offence. Detectives coordinate specialist resources such as fingerprint, DNA, and digital evidence units.

Techniques

Methods include surveillance, forensic analysis, analysis of records and communications data, and undercover operations. Legal frameworks govern powers such as search and seizure, arrest, and interrogation, and vary considerably between jurisdictions.

Outcomes

A successful investigation leads to charges and prosecution; unsuccessful or mishandled investigations may result in cases being dropped or, in serious instances, miscarriages of justice, which later review bodies may examine.
