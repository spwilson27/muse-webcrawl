North Rhine-Westphalia (German: Nordrhein-Westfalen, commonly shortened to NRW) is a state (Land) in Germany. With more than 18 million inhabitants, it is the most populous of Germany's sixteen states and the fourth largest by area. Its capital is Düsseldorf and its largest city is Cologne. The state contains the Rhine-Ruhr metropolitan region, one of Europe's largest urban areas, including the cities of the Ruhr valley — Dortmund, Essen, Duisburg and Bochum — as well as Bonn, Münster, Bielefeld, Aachen and Wuppertal.

History

North Rhine-Westphalia was created by the British military government in 1946 (Operation Marriage), merging the northern Rhineland province of Prussia with the Prussian province of Westphalia; the small state of Lippe joined in 1947. The name reflects its two constituent regions: North Rhine (the lower Rhine lands) and Westphalia. Bonn, within the state, served as the provisional capital of West Germany from 1949 until reunification, and remained the seat of many federal institutions afterwards.

The Ruhr's coal and steel industries made the state the industrial heartland of post-war Germany's Wirtschaftswunder (economic miracle). Since the decline of heavy industry from the 1960s onward, the region has undergone structural change toward services, logistics, media, insurance and higher education, while lignite mining in the Rhenish coalfield — including the Garzweiler surface mine — has remained politically contentious.

Geography

The state lies in western Germany, bordering Belgium and the Netherlands to the west, and the German states of Lower Saxony, Hesse and Rhineland-Palatinate. The Rhine flows through its centre; other major rivers include the Ruhr, Lippe, Ems and Weser. Lowlands dominate the north (the Münsterland and the Westphalian Basin), while the south and east are hilly, with the Eifel, Sauerland (containing the Kahler Asten, 842 m, the state's highest point) and the Teutoburg Forest.

Politics

The state parliament is the Landtag of North Rhine-Westphalia, seated in Düsseldorf. Long a stronghold of the Social Democratic Party (SPD), which governed almost continuously from 1966 to 2005, the state has since alternated between SPD-led and CDU-led governments. Hendrik Wüst of the CDU became Minister-President in 2021, governing in coalition with the Greens after the 2022 state election.

Economy

NRW generates roughly a fifth of Germany's GDP — more than several EU member states. Major sectors include chemicals and pharmaceuticals (Bayer, Henkel), energy (E.ON, RWE), retail (Aldi, Rewe, Metro), media (Bertelsmann in Gütersloh, RTL in Cologne) and insurance. The state hosts the most universities of any German state and numerous trade fairs, including the Cologne and Düsseldorf exhibitions.

Culture

The state is known for Cologne's carnival, the Düsseldorf art scene, the Ruhrtriennale festival, and five UNESCO World Heritage Sites including Cologne Cathedral, Aachen Cathedral, the Zollverein Coal Mine in Essen and the castles of Augustusburg and Falkenlust in Brühl. Football is deeply rooted, with clubs such as Borussia Dortmund, FC Schalke 04, 1. FC Köln and Borussia Mönchengladbach.
