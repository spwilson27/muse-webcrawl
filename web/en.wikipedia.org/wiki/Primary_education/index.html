Primary education

Primary education is the first stage of formal schooling, typically for children aged about 5 to 11 or 12, following preschool and preceding secondary education. In the UNESCO ISCED classification it is Level 1. Its core purpose is to establish literacy, numeracy and basic knowledge of the world, together with social, motor and creative skills that form the foundation for all later learning.

Structure and curriculum

In most systems children are taught mainly by a single class teacher, moving later to subject specialists as they approach secondary school. Curricula centre on reading and writing in the home language, mathematics, elementary science, social studies, art, music and physical education, often with a second language introduced in later primary years. National systems differ in length: primary schooling lasts four years in Germany, five to six in the United States (elementary school, often followed by middle school), six in France and China, and seven in England (ages 4/5–11, divided into Key Stages 1 and 2).

Universal primary education

Free, compulsory primary education is recognised as a right under the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the Convention on the Rights of the Child. It was the focus of the second Millennium Development Goal (2000–2015) and remains central to Sustainable Development Goal 4. Global net enrolment rose from about 83 percent in 2000 to around 90 percent in the 2010s, but tens of millions of children — concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa and conflict zones — remain out of school, and the World Bank has warned of "learning poverty": many enrolled children finish primary school unable to read a simple text, a problem sharply worsened by COVID-19 school closures.

Debates

Recurring debates in primary education include the best method of teaching reading (phonics versus whole-language approaches), the age at which formal schooling should begin, the role of homework and standardised testing for young children, class sizes, ability grouping, and the place of play-based learning in the early years — approaches such as Montessori and Finland's late-start, low-testing system are frequent reference points.
