Abbott Laboratories is an American multinational medical devices and health care company headquartered in Abbott Park, Illinois. Founded in 1888 by physician Wallace Calvin Abbott, the company sells products in more than 160 countries and employs roughly 114,000 people.

History

Wallace Abbott founded the Abbott Alkaloidal Company in Chicago in 1888 to manufacture accurate-dosage alkaloidal medicines. The company adopted the name Abbott Laboratories in 1915 and expanded internationally during the twentieth century, developing products such as the anesthetics and the HIV diagnostic tests of the 1980s.

In 2004 Abbott spun off its hospital products business as Hospira. On 1 January 2013, Abbott split into two public companies: AbbVie, which took the research-based pharmaceuticals business including Humira, and a diversified products company retaining the Abbott name.

Business segments

Abbott's principal segments are diagnostics (including point-of-care and molecular testing), medical devices (including the FreeStyle Libre continuous glucose monitoring system and cardiovascular devices), nutritional products (including Similac infant formula, Ensure and Pedialyte), and established pharmaceuticals sold mainly in emerging markets.

COVID-19 and diagnostics

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Abbott developed widely used rapid antigen tests, including the BinaxNOW self-test, and molecular assays run on its m2000 and Alinity platforms.
