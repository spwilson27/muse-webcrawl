The foreign policy of India concerns the policy initiatives pursued by the Republic of India toward other states. Since independence in 1947, India's external relations have been shaped by its founding ideals of non-alignment and peaceful coexistence, its regional security concerns in South Asia, and, increasingly, its status as a major emerging economy and the world's most populous country.

Foundations

India's first prime minister, Jawaharlal Nehru, articulated the doctrine of non-alignment, refusing to join either Cold War bloc, and the Panchsheel (Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence) agreed with China in 1954. India was a founding force in the Non-Aligned Movement, inaugurated at the 1961 Belgrade conference. Although the Cold War's end and economic liberalisation in 1991 shifted Indian diplomacy toward pragmatic engagement with all major powers, "strategic autonomy" remains its guiding formulation.

Regional policy

Within South Asia, India's relations are dominated by its long-running disputes with Pakistan, including over Kashmir, and by a strategically significant, sometimes competitive relationship with China, marked by a disputed Himalayan border and the 1962 Sino-Indian War. Policies such as "Neighbourhood First" and "Act East" (formerly "Look East") aim to deepen ties with South and Southeast Asian partners.

Global engagement

India maintains close partnerships with the United States, Russia, the European Union, Japan and Australia, participates in the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (the Quad), and is a member of BRICS, the G20 and the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation. India campaigns for a permanent seat on the United Nations Security Council as part of the G4 group, and is a major contributor to UN peacekeeping. Its large diaspora, energy security needs and trade interests extend Indian diplomacy to Africa, the Middle East and Latin America.
