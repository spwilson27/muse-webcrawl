Alice Elizabeth Kober (23 December 1906 – 16 May 1950) was an American classicist whose rigorous statistical analysis of the undeciphered Bronze Age script Linear B laid the essential groundwork for its decipherment by Michael Ventris in 1952, two years after her early death.

Career

The daughter of Hungarian immigrants, Kober studied at Hunter College and took her PhD at Columbia in 1932, then taught classics at Brooklyn College for two decades, rising to assistant professor while carrying heavy teaching loads. A formidable linguist, she added languages steadily to her repertoire, learning among others Sanskrit, Hittite, Old Irish, Akkadian and Basque — partly so that no proposed identification of the Linear B language could be excluded for want of comparanda.

The Linear B work

From the 1940s Kober devoted herself to the Minoan script Linear B, then read only as pictures on clay tablets from Knossos. Working without computers, she cut some 180,000 index cards from cigarette cartons — wartime paper being scarce — and recorded each sign's occurrences, refusing to guess at sound values. In a famous series of papers (1945–1948) she demonstrated rigorously that the language behind Linear B was inflected: she identified sets of words ("Kober's triplets") sharing stems with different endings, which implied that signs encoded syllables and that certain signs shared consonants or vowels. She built partial syllabic "grids" organising the signs by these relationships — precisely the structure Ventris extended and populated to complete the decipherment, acknowledging her contribution in his announcement.

Death and recognition

Overworked and a heavy smoker, Kober fell ill and died in May 1950, aged 43, her analysis unpublished in full. Her papers are preserved at the University of Texas at Austin. Long overshadowed by Ventris, she has been restored to prominence by modern scholarship and biography — notably Margalit Fox's The Riddle of the Labyrinth (2013) — as the scholar whose methodical framework made the decipherment possible.
