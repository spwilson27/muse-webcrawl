Byōdō-in (平等院, "Temple of Equality") is a Buddhist temple in the city of Uji in Kyoto Prefecture, Japan, built in the late Heian period. It is jointly a temple of the Jōdo-shū (Pure Land) and Tendai-shū sects. Its Phoenix Hall, completed in 1053, is one of the most celebrated surviving buildings of Heian aristocratic culture and an icon of Japan — it appears on the reverse of the 10-yen coin.

History

The site was originally a villa of the courtier Minamoto no Tōru and later of the powerful Fujiwara no Michinaga. In 1052 Michinaga's son, the regent Fujiwara no Yorimichi, converted the villa into a temple, at a moment when belief in the coming of mappō, the latter age of the Buddhist law, and devotion to Amida Buddha's Pure Land were widespread. The following year the Amida Hall — now known as the Phoenix Hall (Hōō-dō) — was completed, housing a celebrated seated image of Amida carved by the master sculptor Jōchō, the defining work of Heian sculpture. Though many of the temple's other halls were burned during civil wars in the 14th century, the Phoenix Hall survived intact, an extraordinary rarity for Japanese wooden architecture of its age.

Architecture

The Phoenix Hall consists of a central hall flanked by two corridor wings ending in small turrets, with a tail corridor behind, giving the whole the appearance of a phoenix about to take flight; phoenix ornaments also crown the roof. Set on an island in a Pure Land garden pond, the hall was designed to evoke Amida's paradise on earth. Inside, the Amida statue sits amid painted walls depicting the descent of Amida to welcome the souls of the dying, and the upper walls carry 52 small carved wooden figures of bodhisattvas on clouds — the Unchū Kuyō Bosatsu — playing instruments and dancing, designated National Treasures alongside the building itself.

Heritage

Byōdō-in was inscribed in 1994 as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto." The temple's treasure house, the Hōmotsu-kan (Byōdō-in Museum), displays the original phoenix ornaments, the temple bell, and other surviving Heian artefacts. A full-size replica of the Phoenix Hall stands at the Valley of the Temples in Oahu, Hawaii, built in 1968 to mark the centenary of Japanese immigration to Hawaii.
