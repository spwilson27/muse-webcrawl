French wine is produced all across France, in quantities between 50 and 60 million hectolitres per year, or 7–8 billion bottles. France is one of the largest wine producers in the world, along with Italian, Spanish, and American wine-producing regions. French wine traces its history to the 6th century BC, with many of France's regions dating their wine-making history to Roman times. The wines produced range from expensive wines sold internationally to modest wines usually only seen within France, such as the Margnat wines of the post-war period.

From a very early period, French wine has been a byword for quality, elegance, sophistication, and luxury. The concept of terroir, and the Appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) system, have been the benchmark for winegrowing regions throughout the world. French wine styles are influenced by the climate regions in France, from the cool continental climates of the northeast, to the Mediterranean climate of the south.

History

French wine originated in the 6th century BC, with the founding of Massalia (modern-day Marseille) by Greek settlers. The Romans did much to spread viticulture across the country, planting vines in the regions that would become Bordeaux, Burgundy, Alsace, Champagne, Languedoc and the Loire and Rhône valleys. In the Middle Ages, monks maintained vineyards and improved winemaking knowledge, while the wines of Bordeaux were exported to England following the marriage of Eleanor of Aquitaine to Henry II.

The 19th century saw the devastating phylloxera epidemic, an aphid that destroyed vineyards across Europe; recovery came by grafting European vines onto resistant American rootstock. The 1855 Bordeaux Wine Official Classification, ordered by Napoleon III for the Exposition Universelle de Paris, remains famous to this day.

Wine regions

The major wine regions include Bordeaux, known for blends dominated by Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot; Burgundy, centred on Pinot noir and Chardonnay; Champagne, the only region permitted to produce the eponymous sparkling wine by the traditional method; the Loire Valley, spanning Muscadet, Sancerre and Vouvray; the Rhône Valley, divided between northern Syrah and southern blends such as Châteauneuf-du-Pape; Alsace, noted for aromatic whites such as Riesling and Gewürztraminer; and Languedoc-Roussillon, the largest region by area.

Classification

Since 2012, French wines fall into three EU-aligned categories: AOP (appellation d'origine protégée, corresponding to the former AOC), IGP (indication géographique protégée, formerly vin de pays), and Vin de France (formerly vin de table). Within regions, additional hierarchies apply, such as the Burgundy grand cru and premier cru designations.
