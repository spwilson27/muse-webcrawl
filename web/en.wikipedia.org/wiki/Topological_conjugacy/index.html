Topological conjugacy

FieldDynamical systems
RelatesPairs of maps under homeomorphism

In mathematics, particularly in the theory of dynamical systems, two functions are said to be topologically conjugate if there exists a homeomorphism that maps the orbits of one onto the orbits of the other while preserving the direction of time. Formally, continuous maps f : X → X and g : Y → Y are topologically conjugate if there is a homeomorphism h : X → Y such that h ∘ f = g ∘ h.

Significance

Conjugacy is an equivalence relation that classifies dynamical systems up to continuous change of coordinates: conjugate systems have the same qualitative dynamics, so periodic points, orbit structure and topological entropy correspond under the conjugating homeomorphism. A classic example is the logistic map at parameter 4, which is conjugate to the tent map and to the angle-doubling map on the circle. A weaker notion, topological semiconjugacy, requires only a continuous surjection satisfying the same commutation relation.
