Qiyas

Qiyas (Arabic: قياس, "analogy") is the process of deductive analogy in Islamic jurisprudence (fiqh), by which the ruling on a known case in the Quran or Sunnah is extended to a new case that shares the same underlying cause or rationale ('illa). It is traditionally regarded as the fourth source of Islamic law after the Quran, the Sunnah, and scholarly consensus (ijma).

Method

A valid analogy requires four elements: the original case (asl), the new case (far'), the ruling (hukm) of the original case, and the effective cause ('illa) shared by both. A classic example extends the prohibition of wine to other intoxicants, because the 'illa of the prohibition is intoxication. Qiyas allows jurists to derive rulings for matters not addressed directly in scripture.

Acceptance

The four major Sunni schools of law accept qiyas, though they differ on its conditions. The Zahiri school historically rejected it, and Twelver Shia jurisprudence substitutes reason ('aql) as the fourth source, using analogy only in restricted forms.
