Shinjuku (Japanese: 新宿区, Shinjuku-ku) is a special ward of Tokyo, Japan, and one of the city's principal commercial, administrative and entertainment centres. It is home to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and to Shinjuku Station, generally reckoned the world's busiest railway station, handling several million passengers a day.

History

The name Shinjuku means "new lodging": in 1698 a new post station (shuku) was established here on the Kōshū Kaidō road because the existing station was too far from the next stop. The district grew rapidly after the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake, when development shifted west from the devastated downtown, and again after the Second World War, when its black markets and entertainment quarters flourished. The skyscraper district of Nishi-Shinjuku rose from the 1970s on the site of the former Yodobashi water purification plant, beginning with the Keio Plaza Hotel in 1971.

Districts

West of the station, Nishi-Shinjuku contains Tokyo's largest concentration of skyscrapers, including the twin-towered Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building (1991), whose observation decks are free to the public. East of the station lie the shopping streets around Shinjuku-dōri, the department stores of Shinjuku San-chōme, and Kabukichō, Japan's best-known red-light and nightlife district, within which sits the tiny bar quarter of Golden Gai. The ward also includes Ōkubo, Tokyo's Koreatown; the student quarter around Waseda University; and Shinjuku Gyoen, a large landscape garden combining Japanese, English and French styles, famous for cherry blossom.

Transport

Shinjuku Station serves the JR Yamanote, Chūō and Saikyō lines, the private Odakyū, Keiō and Seibu Shinjuku lines, and three subway lines, making the ward a western gateway to the metropolis and to destinations such as Mount Takao, Hakone and the Fuji Five Lakes.
