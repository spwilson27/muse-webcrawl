Pope Pius VII

Pope Pius VII (born Barnaba Niccolò Maria Luigi Chiaramonti; 14 August 1742 – 20 August 1823) was head of the Catholic Church from 1800 to his death. A Benedictine monk and theologian, his pontificate was dominated by his long struggle with Napoleon Bonaparte.

Election and the Concordat

Chiaramonti was elected in Venice in March 1800, after his predecessor Pius VI had died a prisoner of revolutionary France. In 1801 he concluded the Concordat with Napoleon, restoring the Catholic Church in France. He attended Napoleon's coronation in Paris in 1804, blessing the new emperor but famously not crowning him.

Conflict and imprisonment

Relations collapsed as Napoleon annexed the Papal States; in 1809 Pius was arrested and held prisoner at Savona and then Fontainebleau for five years after excommunicating the invaders. His steadfastness under captivity made him a symbol of resistance. He was freed in 1814 and returned to Rome in triumph.

Restoration

After the Congress of Vienna restored the Papal States, Pius and his secretary of state, Cardinal Consalvi, pursued cautious reform. In 1814 he restored the Society of Jesus worldwide, and in 1817 he concluded a concordat with Bavaria, the first in a series of settlements with European states.
