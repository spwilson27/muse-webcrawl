Mattel

Mattel, Inc. is an American multinational toy manufacturing and entertainment company founded in 1945 and headquartered in El Segundo, California. It is the world's second-largest toy maker by revenue after Lego. Mattel's best-known brands include Barbie, Hot Wheels, Fisher-Price, American Girl, Thomas & Friends, Masters of the Universe and the board game Uno.

History

Mattel was founded in 1945 by Harold "Matt" Matson and Elliot Handler — the name combines "Matt" and "Elliot" — initially making picture frames and dollhouse furniture. Elliot's wife Ruth Handler drove the toy business. The company's Uke-A-Doodle toy ukulele (1947) was an early hit, and Mattel was a pioneer of television advertising, sponsoring the Mickey Mouse Club in 1955. In 1959 Ruth Handler introduced Barbie, inspired by the German Bild Lilli doll; Barbie became the best-selling fashion doll in history. Hot Wheels die-cast cars followed in 1968, competing successfully with Matchbox. Mattel acquired Fisher-Price in 1993 and Tyco Toys (including Matchbox) in 1997.

Products and media

Beyond toys, Mattel licenses its brands for films, television and consumer products. Mattel Films produced the Barbie movie (2023), directed by Greta Gerwig, which grossed over $1.4 billion worldwide and renewed corporate focus on brand-driven entertainment. Other divisions include Mattel Television (producers of Thomas & Friends and animated Barbie content) and the American Girl retail brand.
