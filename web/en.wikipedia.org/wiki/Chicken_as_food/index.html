Chicken as food

Chicken is the most common type of poultry in the world and the most widely consumed meat. Domesticated from the red junglefowl in Asia several thousand years ago, chickens are raised in enormous numbers for their meat — most commonly as fast-growing broilers — and feature in nearly every culinary tradition.

Production

Modern broiler chickens reach slaughter weight in five to seven weeks. The largest producers are the United States, Brazil, and China. Birds are sold whole or as cuts; the breast is the most valuable portion in Western markets, while thighs and other dark meat dominate exports.

Culinary uses

Chicken is prepared by roasting, frying, grilling, poaching, and stewing. Signature dishes include fried chicken, roast chicken, chicken soup, tikka masala, coq au vin, yakitori, and Hainanese chicken rice. Chicken eggs are covered separately as a foodstuff.

Nutrition and health

Skinless chicken breast is lean and high in protein. Food-safety authorities stress thorough cooking because raw poultry may carry Salmonella and Campylobacter. Intensive production has raised concerns about animal welfare, antibiotic use, and avian influenza.
