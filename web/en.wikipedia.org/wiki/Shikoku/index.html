Shikoku (Japanese: 四国, "four provinces") is the smallest of the four main islands of Japan. It is bordered by the Seto Inland Sea to the north and the Pacific Ocean to the south, and lies southwest of Honshu and northeast of Kyushu. The island comprises four prefectures — Ehime, Kagawa, Kōchi and Tokushima — corresponding to the four former provinces of Awa, Sanuki, Iyo and Tosa from which it takes its name.

Geography

A mountainous spine runs east–west along the island, dominated by Mount Ishizuchi in the west and Mount Tsurugi in the east, and dividing the milder northern coast along the Inland Sea from the wetter, storm-exposed Pacific coast of Kōchi. The Yoshino River, one of Japan's major rivers, flows eastward through Tokushima. Whirlpools formed by tidal currents in the Naruto Strait, between Shikoku and Awaji Island, are a celebrated natural sight.

Culture

Shikoku is best known for the Shikoku Pilgrimage, a circuit of 88 Buddhist temples associated with the monk Kūkai (Kōbō Daishi), the founder of Shingon Buddhism who was born on the island. Pilgrims known as o-henro-san, traditionally dressed in white, still walk or ride the roughly 1,200 km circuit. Other cultural hallmarks include the Awa Odori dance festival in Tokushima, the Yosakoi festival in Kōchi, udon noodles in Kagawa ("Udon Prefecture"), and the Dōgo Onsen hot spring in Matsuyama, one of Japan's oldest.

Transport

Until 1988 Shikoku was the only main island with no fixed link to Honshu. It is now connected by three bridge systems: the Seto-Ōhashi bridge from Okayama to Kagawa (carrying the island's only rail link), the Akashi Kaikyō–Naruto route from Kobe and Awaji Island to Tokushima, and the Shimanami Kaidō island-hopping route from Hiroshima to Ehime, popular with cyclists.
