Bernoulli family

The Bernoulli family of Basel was a dynasty of merchants, scholars, and above all mathematicians, who produced eight significant mathematicians across three generations in the 17th and 18th centuries. Descended from Protestants who fled Antwerp to escape Spanish persecution, the family settled in Basel, Switzerland. Their contributions span calculus, probability theory, fluid dynamics, celestial mechanics and mathematical physics; the family name is attached to numerous theorems, numbers, distributions and principles.

Principal members

Jacob Bernoulli (1655–1705) pioneered probability theory in his posthumous Ars Conjectandi, giving the law of large numbers and the Bernoulli numbers. His brother Johann Bernoulli (1667–1748) developed the calculus of variations and mentored Euler. Johann's sons included Daniel Bernoulli (1700–1782), whose Hydrodynamica (1738) contains Bernoulli's principle, and Nicolaus I and Johann II. Daniel's cousin Nicolaus II and the third generation — Johann III and Jacob II — continued the tradition in astronomy and mechanics.

Legacy

The Bernoullis quarrelled famously (Jacob and Johann over the brachistochrone problem; Daniel and Johann over priority in hydrodynamics), yet collectively they helped transform mathematics into its modern analytic form. Concepts named for them include the Bernoulli distribution, Bernoulli trial, Bernoulli's equation, the lemniscate of Bernoulli, and Bernoulli numbers.
