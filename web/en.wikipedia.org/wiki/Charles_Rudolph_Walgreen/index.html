Charles Rudolph Walgreen Sr. (October 9, 1873 – December 11, 1939) was an American businessman and the founder of Walgreens, one of the largest pharmacy chains in the United States.

Career

The son of Swedish immigrants, Walgreen worked as a pharmacist in Chicago before buying the drugstore where he was employed in 1901. He emphasized customer service, bright stores, and low prices, expanding the chain to hundreds of stores by the late 1920s, partly boosted by Prohibition-era sales of medicinal whiskey and the popularity of the malted milkshake, which his stores helped popularize.

Legacy

Walgreen's company grew into a national retail pharmacy giant. His son Charles R. Walgreen Jr. and grandson Charles R. Walgreen III continued to lead the business.
