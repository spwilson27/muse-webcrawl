Marikana mine strike

The Marikana massacre was the killing of 34 miners by the South African Police Service (SAPS) on 16 August 2012 during a wildcat strike at the Lonmin platinum mine at Marikana, near Rustenburg in North West province. It was the most lethal use of force by South African security forces against civilians since the end of apartheid.

Background and events

Rock drill operators at the mine went on strike on 10 August 2012 demanding a wage increase to 12,500 rand per month. The strike was marked by rivalry between the established National Union of Mineworkers (NUM) and the breakaway Association of Mineworkers and Construction Union (AMCU); ten people, including two police officers and two security guards, were killed in the days before 16 August. On that day police opened fire on a group of strikers gathered on a hill (a koppie), killing 34 and wounding 78.

Aftermath

The massacre provoked national and international outrage. The government-appointed Marikana Commission of Inquiry, chaired by retired judge Ian Farlam, reported in 2015, criticising the police operation and Lonmin. The event remains a potent symbol of inequality and state violence in post-apartheid South Africa.
