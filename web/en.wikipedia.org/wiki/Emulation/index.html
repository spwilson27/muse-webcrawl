Emulation

Emulation most commonly refers to the process by which one system imitates the behaviour of another. The term has specific technical meanings in computing, where an emulator enables a host system to run software or use peripherals designed for a different guest system, and broader uses in social learning, network theory, and other fields.

In computing

In computing, emulation is the capability of a computer program in an electronic device to imitate another program or device. Examples include video game console emulators that run game software on personal computers, terminal emulators, printer emulators, and hardware emulation used in semiconductor design verification. Emulation differs from simulation in that an emulator seeks to reproduce the external behaviour of the target system to a degree sufficient to run its unmodified software, whereas a simulator models internal behaviour for study. Compatibility layers and virtualization are related but distinct techniques.

In psychology and society

In social learning, emulation refers to learning about the effects of actions on the environment—copying the results of behaviour rather than its exact form, in contrast to mimicry or imitation. Comparative psychologists distinguish emulation learning in apes and young children, in which an observer reproduces the outcome of a model's action using their own methods. The term also describes the desire to equal or surpass others, historically used in education and moral philosophy.

Other uses

In network theory, emulation can mean testing network behaviour with live traffic. In competitive contexts, "emulation" may denote striving to match the achievements of a rival. This article is a broad overview; see the specific senses below.
