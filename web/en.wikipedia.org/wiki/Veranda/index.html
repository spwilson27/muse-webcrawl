Veranda

A veranda (or verandah) is a roofed, open-air gallery or porch attached to the exterior of a building, often extending across the front and sides of the structure. A veranda is frequently partly enclosed by a railing and typically at ground-floor level, serving as a shaded transitional space between indoors and outdoors. The word entered English from the languages of India (compare Hindi varaṇḍā and Bengali bārāndā), likely via Portuguese varanda.

Regional forms

In Australia and New Zealand the veranda is a defining element of domestic architecture from the colonial period onward: the classic Australian homestead is ringed by wide verandas that shade the walls and keep interiors cool, and cast-iron lacework ornamented verandas on Victorian terraces. In the southern United States, deep verandas (often called porches or galleries) perform the same climatic function in houses of the Creole, shotgun and Queen Anne styles. In India, verandas fronted bungalows for shade and social space. Related forms include the Brazilian varanda, the South African stoep, the Japanese engawa, and the wraparound porch of North American farmhouses.

Distinctions

A veranda differs from a balcony, which projects from an upper floor; from a loggia, which is recessed into the building volume; and from a portico, a roofed entrance structure. A Florida room or lanai is an enclosed variant. Because they cool buildings passively and provide outdoor living space, verandas have seen renewed interest in sustainable design in hot climates.
