Fine motor skill

Fine motor skills are the coordination of small muscles, in movements — usually involving the synchronisation of the hands and fingers — with the eyes. Fine motor skills contrast with gross motor skills, which involve large muscle groups in movements such as walking and jumping. The development of fine motor skills in children enables increasingly complex tasks: grasping objects, using tools, drawing, writing, buttoning clothes, and manipulating small items.

Development

Development proceeds in a predictable sequence through developmental milestones: infants progress from a reflexive grasp to a palmar grasp, then to the pincer grasp (thumb and forefinger) at roughly nine to twelve months, which allows picking up small objects. In preschool years, children learn to use scissors, hold crayons and pencils with a tripod grip, and build with blocks; handwriting fluency develops through the early school years. Delays in fine motor development can be an early indicator of developmental disorders, and occupational therapy is commonly used to support children who struggle with these skills.

Assessment

Fine motor function is assessed with instruments such as the Beery-Buktenica Developmental Test of Visual-Motor Integration, the Purdue Pegboard Test, and the Nine-Hole Peg Test, the latter also widely used in neurology and rehabilitation to measure finger dexterity, for example in multiple sclerosis and after stroke.
