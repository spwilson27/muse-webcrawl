Trần dynasty

The Trần dynasty (Vietnamese: Nhà Trần) ruled the kingdom of Đại Việt in present-day northern Vietnam from 1225 to 1400. It succeeded the Lý dynasty after the regent Trần Thủ Độ arranged the marriage of the last Lý empress, Lý Chiêu Hoàng, to his nephew Trần Cảnh and her abdication in his favour. The dynasty is best remembered for defeating three Mongol invasions and for a flowering of Vietnamese Buddhism, literature and military organisation.

Rise and consolidation

Under Trần Thái Tông and his successors, the court centralised administration, organised the country into provinces and prefectures, and developed the practice by which emperors abdicated to become "retired emperors" (Thái thượng hoàng) while continuing to guide their successors — an arrangement intended to ensure smooth succession.

Mongol invasions

The dynasty's defining achievement was its resistance to the Mongol Empire and its successor, the Yuan dynasty of China. Đại Việt repelled invasions in 1258, 1285 and 1287–88 under the command of Prince Trần Hưng Đạo, culminating in the destruction of the Yuan fleet at the Battle of Bạch Đằng in 1288. The victories preserved Vietnamese independence at a time when much of Asia had fallen under Mongol rule.

Decline and fall

In the 14th century the dynasty weakened through court factionalism, costly wars with Champa, and peasant unrest. In 1400 the powerful official Hồ Quý Ly deposed the last Trần emperor and founded the short-lived Hồ dynasty, prompting a Ming Chinese invasion in 1406.
