Sovereign citizen movement

OriginUnited States, 1970s
Associated withPosse Comitatus, tax protester movement, Moorish sovereigns, freemen on the land
StatusTheories rejected by courts as frivolous

The sovereign citizen movement is a loose grouping of litigants, activists, tax protesters, and others who believe that they are answerable only to their particular interpretations of common law and are not subject to any government statutes or proceedings. Adherents advance a variety of pseudolegal theories, which courts have consistently rejected as frivolous.

Beliefs

Common beliefs include the claim that the United States government is illegitimate — sometimes said to have been secretly replaced by a corporation — that birth certificates create a separate legal entity or "strawman", and that special wording, documents, or refusal to "contract" with authorities can exempt a person from taxes, licensing, and criminal jurisdiction. Related variants include the Moorish sovereign movement and, outside the United States, the freemen on the land in Commonwealth countries and the Reichsbürger movement in Germany.

History

The movement's roots lie in the white supremacist Christian Identity and Posse Comitatus movements of the 1970s and the farm crisis of the 1980s. It later spread to wider audiences, including through seminars and internet content, and diversified beyond its original racial ideology.

Legal response and violence

Judges routinely dismiss sovereign citizen filings, sometimes termed "paper terrorism" when used to harass officials with liens and lawsuits. Some adherents have committed violent acts, including the 2010 West Memphis shootings of two police officers. The FBI has identified some sovereign citizen extremists as a domestic terrorism concern.
