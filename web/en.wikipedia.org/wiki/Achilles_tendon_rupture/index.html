Achilles tendon rupture

Achilles tendon rupture is the partial or complete tearing of the Achilles tendon, the band of tissue connecting the calf muscles to the heel bone. It typically occurs during sudden forceful movements — pushing off, jumping or abrupt changes of direction — and is most common in recreationally active adults aged 30 to 50, often described as "weekend warriors".

DiagnosisClinical examination (Thompson test); ultrasound or MRI

Presentation and diagnosis

Sufferers often report feeling as if they were kicked in the calf, accompanied by a snap. A gap may be palpable in the tendon a few centimetres above the heel, and the Thompson (calf squeeze) test shows loss of plantar flexion of the foot. Risk factors include intermittent high-intensity sport, prior tendinopathy, and fluoroquinolone antibiotics or corticosteroid injections.

Treatment

Management is either surgical — open or minimally invasive repair rejoining the tendon ends — or non-operative, using a cast or walking boot with the foot pointed, followed by progressive weight-bearing and physiotherapy. Modern evidence suggests functional rehabilitation can achieve re-rupture rates close to surgery in many patients, while surgery modestly lowers re-rupture risk at the cost of wound complications. Full return to sport commonly takes six to twelve months.
