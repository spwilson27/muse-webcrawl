Fay Report

Full titleInvestigation of the Abu Ghraib Detention Facility and 205th Military Intelligence Brigade
AuthorsMaj. Gen. George Fay; Lt. Gen. Anthony Jones
ReleasedAugust 2004
SubjectAbu Ghraib prisoner abuse scandal

The Fay Report, formally the Investigation of the Abu Ghraib Detention Facility and 205th Military Intelligence Brigade, was a United States Army inquiry into the abuse of detainees at Abu Ghraib prison in Iraq. It was commissioned after photographs of prisoner abuse became public in the spring of 2004.

Investigation

Major General George Fay was appointed to investigate the role of military intelligence personnel; when questions arose about whether a major general could properly investigate officers of equal rank, Lieutenant General Anthony R. Jones was added, and the two reports were released together in August 2004. Unlike the earlier Taguba Report, which focused on military police, the Fay/Jones investigation examined the 205th Military Intelligence Brigade and civilian interrogation contractors.

Findings

The report documented dozens of instances of abuse and concluded that military intelligence personnel had solicited or encouraged harsh treatment, that interrogation policies had migrated confusingly from Guantánamo Bay and Afghanistan, and that leadership failures — including those of brigade commander Colonel Thomas Pappas and Lt. Col. Steven Jordan — created an environment in which abuse occurred. It also criticized contractor personnel from CACI and Titan. Human rights groups faulted the inquiry for not examining responsibility higher up the chain of command, including at the Pentagon.
