The Drac is a 130-kilometre (81 mi) long river in south-eastern France. A left tributary of the Isère, it rises in the southern French Alps in the Écrins National Park in the department of Hautes-Alpes, formed by the confluence of the Drac Noir and Drac Blanc near Champoléon in the Champsaur valley. Its name derives from draco, the dragon — local legend personified the torrential river, notorious for destructive floods, as a dragon.

Course

The Drac flows generally northward through the Champsaur, then forms part of the boundary between the Hautes-Alpes and Isère departments, passing the Lac du Sautet, the Lac de Monteynard-Avignonet — famous for its Himalayan suspension footbridges — and the deep Gorges du Drac. It joins the Isère just downstream (north-west) of Grenoble, near Sassenage. Its main tributaries include the Séveraisse, the Bonne, the Ébron and the Romanche near its mouth.

Dams and uses

The once-wild torrent has been heavily developed for hydroelectricity: major dams include the Barrage du Sautet (1935) and the Monteynard dam (1962), whose reservoir is popular for windsurfing and kitesurfing thanks to reliable thermal winds. The lower Drac's gravel beds also supply aggregates, and the river provides cooling and process water for industry in the Grenoble area.
