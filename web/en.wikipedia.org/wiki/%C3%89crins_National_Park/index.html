Écrins National Park

Écrins National Park (French: Parc national des Écrins) is a national park in the French Alps, straddling the departments of Hautes-Alpes and Isère in southeastern France. Established in 1973, its protected core zone covers about 918 square kilometres of high mountain terrain centred on the Écrins massif, whose highest summit, the Barre des Écrins, rises to 4,102 metres — the highest point in France outside the Mont Blanc massif.

Geography

The park encompasses more than a hundred peaks above 3,000 metres and around 10,000 hectares of glaciers, including the Glacier Blanc and Glacier Noir. Deep valleys such as the Vallouise, Valgaudemar, and Vénéon radiate from the central massif, and the park is drained by tributaries of the Durance, Romanche, and Drac rivers.

Wildlife and flora

The park shelters emblematic Alpine fauna including ibex, chamois, marmots, golden eagles, bearded vultures, and the reintroduced lynx, while its flora ranges from larch forests and alpine pastures to rare high-altitude cushion plants. Hundreds of kilometres of waymarked trails and a network of mountain refuges support hiking and mountaineering.

History

A precursor reserve, the Parc de la Bérarde, was created in 1913, making the area one of the earliest protected mountain areas in France. The modern national park was decreed on 27 March 1973.
