Saraswati is the Hindu goddess of knowledge, learning, music, speech and the arts, consort of Brahma and, with Lakshmi and Parvati, one of the Tridevi. She is depicted serene and in white — the colour of pure knowledge — seated on a lotus, playing the vīṇā, holding a book and rosary, accompanied by her swan, which tradition says can separate milk from water: the emblem of discernment.

Origins

Saraswati began as a river goddess: the Vedas hymn the mighty Sarasvatī river, on whose banks Vedic culture flourished, as mother, purifier and inspirer of speech (Vāc). When the river was lost — identified by many with the now-dry Ghaggar-Hakra system, a matter of continuing archaeological debate — the goddess detached from it and grew into the embodiment of learning itself. In the epics and Purāṇas she becomes Brahma's consort and the inventor of Sanskrit and its script in some accounts.

Worship

Her festival, Vasant Panchami in spring, sees students, musicians and writers — in Bengal especially — place books, pens and instruments before her image and refrain from reading that day so the goddess may bless the tools of learning. Children are often first taught to write in her rite (vidyārambham). She is also revered in Jainism and by some Buddhists (as consort or attendant of Manjushri), and as Benzaiten she is one of Japan's Seven Lucky Gods — among the most travelled of Hindu deities.
