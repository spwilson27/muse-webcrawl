N scale
Scale: 1:160 (1:148 in the UK, 1:150 in Japan)
Track gauge: 9 mm
Introduced: early 1960s

N scale is a popular model railway scale. Depending upon the manufacturer and country, the scale ranges from 1:148 to 1:160, but the track gauge is always 9 mm (hence the name "N", from nine millimetres, and in German Neun). Models are small enough to allow elaborate layouts in modest spaces, making N scale the second most popular scale worldwide after HO scale.

History

Although trains approximating N scale had been produced earlier (such as Lone Star's "Treble-O" in Britain), modern N scale began with the German firm Arnold's "Arnold Rapido" line in 1962. Japanese manufacturers adopted 1:150 scale for models of Shinkansen and domestic trains, which run on narrow-gauge prototypes; British N gauge uses 1:148.

Characteristics

Because of the 9 mm track, N scale models of standard-gauge trains use 1:160, while Japanese models of 1,067 mm gauge trains are made at 1:150 so that they run on the same track. Major manufacturers include Kato, Tomix, Bachmann, Atlas, and Fleischmann. The scale's popularity is especially high in Japan, where limited living space favours compact layouts, and strong in North America for modelling long freight trains.
