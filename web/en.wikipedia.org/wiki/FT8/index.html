FT8

FT8 (short for Franke–Taylor design, 8-FSK modulation) is a digital weak-signal communication mode used by amateur radio operators. Released in 2017 by Joe Taylor (K1JT) and Steve Franke (K9AN) as part of the WSJT-X software, FT8 quickly became the most popular digital mode on the HF bands.

Characteristics

FT8 transmissions use 8-tone frequency-shift keying at 6.25 baud within a bandwidth of about 50 Hz. Messages are sent in strictly timed 15-second slots synchronised to UTC, alternating transmit and receive periods. Each message carries about 77 information bits — enough for call signs, grid locators and signal reports — protected by LDPC error correction. FT8 can decode signals as weak as about −20 dB signal-to-noise ratio (in a 2500 Hz reference bandwidth), well below audibility, enabling contacts under poor propagation conditions.

Use and impact

Because exchanges are highly automated and brief, FT8 allowed a surge of DX contacts during the low sunspot years of the late 2010s. It attracted both praise for its weak-signal performance and criticism that it minimises operator interaction. Related modes include FT4, a faster variant designed for contests, and WSPR and JT65, also from the WSJT family.
