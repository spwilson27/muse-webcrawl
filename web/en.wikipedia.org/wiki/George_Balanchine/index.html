George Balanchine

George Balanchine
Born: Georgi Melitonovich Balanchivadze, January 22, 1904, Saint Petersburg, Russian Empire
Died: April 30, 1983, New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Choreographer, ballet dancer
Known for: Co-founding New York City Ballet

George Balanchine (born Georgi Melitonovich Balanchivadze; January 22, 1904 – April 30, 1983) was a Georgian-born American ballet choreographer who was one of the most influential figures in 20th-century dance. Trained at the Imperial Ballet School in Saint Petersburg and a choreographer for Diaghilev's Ballets Russes, he emigrated to the United States in 1933 and, with Lincoln Kirstein, founded the School of American Ballet and the New York City Ballet, which he directed until his death.

Career

Balanchine graduated from the Mariinsky theatre school into the corps de ballet, then left Russia on a 1924 tour and joined the Ballets Russes in Paris, becoming its principal choreographer at age 21. There he created Apollo (1928) and Prodigal Son (1929) to music by Igor Stravinsky, beginning a lifelong collaboration. Arriving in New York in 1933, he insisted on opening a school before a company; the School of American Ballet opened in 1934. His American companies culminated in the New York City Ballet (1948), resident at Lincoln Center from 1964.

Style and works

Balanchine is the central figure of neoclassical ballet: plotless works in practice clothes, such as Serenade (1934), Concerto Barocco (1941), The Four Temperaments (1946), Agon (1957) and Jewels (1967), subordinated spectacle to musical structure and speed, precision and off-balance extension. He also choreographed full-length productions including The Nutcracker (1954), a staple of American Christmas seasons, and worked on Broadway and in Hollywood. His total output exceeded 400 works.

Personal life and legacy

Balanchine was married to several of his ballerinas — Tamara Geva, Alexandra Danilova, Vera Zorina, Maria Tallchief and Tanaquil Le Clercq — and his final muse was Suzanne Farrell. He became a United States citizen in 1939. The Balanchine Trust preserves and licenses his ballets worldwide, and his technique is taught as the foundation of American ballet training.
