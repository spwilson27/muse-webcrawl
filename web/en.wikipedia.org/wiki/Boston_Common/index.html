Boston Common

Boston Common
Central public park, Boston, Massachusetts
Area: about 50 acres (20 ha)
Established: 1634 — oldest city park in the US

Boston Common is a central public park in downtown Boston, Massachusetts. Dating from 1634, it is the oldest city park in the United States. The Common consists of about 50 acres bounded by Tremont, Park, Beacon, Charles, and Boylston streets, and forms the southern end of the Emerald Necklace of parks designed by Frederick Law Olmsted; the start of the Freedom Trail is also located in the park.

History

The land was purchased from the first European settler, William Blackstone, and used as common pasture, for military training, and for public hangings. British troops camped on the Common at the start of the American Revolution and marched from it to Lexington and Concord. In 1830 grazing was banned and the Common became primarily a place of recreation, rallies, and public gatherings; Charles Dickens, Martin Luther King Jr., and Pope John Paul II are among those who have addressed crowds there.

Features

Landmarks include the Frog Pond, used for skating in winter; the Soldiers and Sailors Monument; the Parkman Bandstand; and the Central Burying Ground. The Boston Massacre and the Robert Gould Shaw memorial stand opposite the Massachusetts State House across Beacon Street, adjacent to the neighbouring Public Garden.
