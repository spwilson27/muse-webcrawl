Haversine formula

The haversine formula determines the great-circle distance between two points on a sphere given their longitudes and latitudes. It is a special case of the law of haversines in spherical trigonometry, relating the sides and angles of spherical triangles. The term "haversine" — half of the versed sine — was coined in 1835 by James Inman.

Formulation

For two points with latitudes φ₁, φ₂ and longitude difference Δλ, the central angle is computed as 2·arcsin(√(sin²(Δφ/2) + cos φ₁ · cos φ₂ · sin²(Δλ/2))), and the distance equals the radius of the sphere multiplied by this angle. The haversine form is numerically well conditioned even for very small distances, unlike the spherical law of cosines.

Applications

The formula was historically important for navigation, giving distances accurate to within about 0.5% when applied to the Earth treated as a sphere. It remains widely used in geographic information systems and programming libraries for quick distance estimates, with more precise ellipsoidal methods used when higher accuracy is required.
