Winterreise

ComposerFranz Schubert
TextWilhelm Müller
CatalogueD. 911, Op. 89
Composed1827
FormSong cycle for voice and piano, 24 songs

Winterreise ("Winter Journey") is a song cycle for voice and piano by Franz Schubert (D. 911, published as Op. 89 in 1828), setting 24 poems by Wilhelm Müller. Composed in 1827, the last full year of Schubert's life, it is regarded as the greatest of all song cycles and a pinnacle of the German Lied tradition.

Content and narrative

The cycle follows a solitary wanderer who, rejected in love, leaves the town by night and journeys through a frozen winter landscape. The poems move from grief and remembered happiness ("Der Lindenbaum") through desolation ("Einsamkeit") to the bleak final encounter with a hurdy-gurdy man ("Der Leiermann"), whose droning instrument accompanies the wanderer's unanswered question: "Will you play your hurdy-gurdy to my songs?" The journey is widely read as an allegory of the passage toward death.

Composition

Schubert found Müller's poems in two groups of twelve, composing the first part in early 1827 and the second in the autumn. Friends who heard the songs at a private performance were struck by their gloom; Schubert reportedly replied that these songs pleased him more than all the others and would please them too in time.

Performance and legacy

Written for tenor voice and piano, Winterreise has since been sung by every voice type and in numerous arrangements. Landmark recordings include those by Dietrich Fischer-Dieskau, Hans Hotter, Peter Schreier and Ian Bostridge. The cycle's psychological depth and the equal partnership of voice and piano have made it a touchstone for singers and pianists alike.
