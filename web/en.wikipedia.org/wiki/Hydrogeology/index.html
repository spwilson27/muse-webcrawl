Hydrogeology

Hydrogeology is the branch of geology concerned with the distribution and movement of groundwater in the soil and rocks of the Earth's crust, especially in aquifers. It combines geology, hydrology, chemistry and physics to study water beneath the surface.

Key concepts

Hydrogeologists study porosity and permeability, hydraulic head and conductivity, recharge and discharge, and flow governed by Darcy's law. Aquifers may be confined or unconfined; their sustainable yield, vulnerability to contamination, and interaction with surface water are central practical questions.

Applications

The field underpins water supply well design, remediation of contaminated sites, dewatering for mining and construction, geothermal energy, and management of groundwater resources under climate and population pressure. Methods include pumping tests, tracer studies, geophysics, and numerical groundwater flow models such as MODFLOW.
