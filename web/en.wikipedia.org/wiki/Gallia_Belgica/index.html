Gallia Belgica

Gallia Belgica ("Belgic Gaul") was a province of the Roman Empire located in the north-eastern part of Roman Gaul, in what is today primarily northern France, Belgium, Luxembourg and the southern Netherlands below the Rhine, together with the north bank of the Rhine in Germany. It was one of the three provinces of Gaul organised by Augustus, alongside Gallia Lugdunensis and Gallia Aquitania.

The province took its name from the Belgae, a confederation of tribes whom Julius Caesar described as the bravest of the Gauls when he conquered the region in the Gallic Wars (58–50 BC). Its capital was Durocortorum (modern Reims), and other important cities included Augusta Treverorum (Trier) — later an imperial residence — Samarobriva (Amiens) and Colonia Agrippina (Cologne, in the neighbouring province of Germania Inferior).

Later history

Under Diocletian, Belgica was divided into Belgica Prima (capital Trier) and Belgica Secunda (capital Reims). In the 5th century the province was overrun by Germanic peoples, notably the Franks, and became the heartland of the Merovingian kingdom. Belgium and "Belgica" as a Latin name for the Low Countries derive their names from the province and its tribal inhabitants.
