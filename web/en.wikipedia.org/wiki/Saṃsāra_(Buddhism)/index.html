Saṃsāra (Buddhism)

Saṃsāra in Buddhism is the beginningless cycle of repeated birth, mundane existence and dying again that all beings pass through, driven by ignorance (avidyā), craving and aversion, and perpetuated by karma. Liberation from samsara — nirvana — is the goal of Buddhist practice.

Characteristics

Buddhist cosmology describes samsara as divided into realms of rebirth, traditionally six: the realms of gods (devas), demigods (asuras), humans, animals, hungry ghosts (pretas) and hells (naraka). None of these states is permanent; even the long-lived gods eventually die and are reborn elsewhere. All conditioned existence within samsara is marked by the three marks of existence: impermanence (anicca), suffering or unsatisfactoriness (dukkha), and non-self (anattā).

Mechanism

The cycle is explained by dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), commonly presented as twelve links (nidānas) beginning with ignorance and ending in birth, ageing and death. Actions of body, speech and mind (karma) shape the realm and circumstances of future rebirths, but Buddhism denies an unchanging soul that transmigrates: rebirth is a causal continuum, likened to one candle lighting another.

Liberation

Following the Noble Eightfold Path — ethical conduct, meditation and wisdom — eradicates the defilements that bind beings to samsara. One who awakens (bodhi) becomes an arhat or a Buddha and, at death, enters parinirvana, beyond rebirth. In Mahayana Buddhism, bodhisattvas vow to remain engaged with samsara until all beings are liberated, and some schools teach that samsara and nirvana are ultimately not separate when reality is seen as it is.
