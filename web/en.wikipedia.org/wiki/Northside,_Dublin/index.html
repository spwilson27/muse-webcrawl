The Northside (Irish: An Taobh ó Thuaidh) is the portion of Dublin, Ireland that lies north of the River Liffey. It is not an official administrative unit but a widely used cultural and geographical designation, conventionally contrasted with the Southside across the river. Northside postal districts carry odd numbers (Dublin 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15 and 17).

Extent and character

The Northside includes the northern half of the city centre — O'Connell Street, the GPO, Henry Street and Parnell Square — together with inner suburbs such as Smithfield, Stoneybatter, Phibsborough, Drumcondra and Glasnevin, and extends to the coastal suburbs of Clontarf, Howth and Malahide and the inland towns of Swords, Blanchardstown and Finglas. Major institutions located here include Croke Park (headquarters of the GAA), the Mater and Rotunda hospitals, Dublin City University, the National Botanic Gardens, Glasnevin Cemetery and Dublin Airport (in Fingal, just north of the city).

Culture and stereotypes

A playful but persistent Dublin rivalry contrasts the traditionally more working-class Northside with the more affluent Southside — satirised in Ross O'Carroll-Kelly's books from the southside perspective. In practice both sides are socially mixed, and extensive regeneration (Smithfield, the Docklands, Grangegorman's TU Dublin campus) has reshaped the inner Northside since the 1990s. The Northside was historically the centre of the city's Georgian development around Mountjoy Square and Parnell Square before fashion shifted south in the 19th century.
