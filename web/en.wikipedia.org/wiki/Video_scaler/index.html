Video scaler

A video scaler is a system that converts video signals from one display resolution to another, typically scaling a lower-resolution signal up (upscaling or upconversion) or a higher-resolution signal down (downscaling or downconversion) to match the native resolution of a display. Video scalers are built into televisions, monitors, AV receivers and disc players, and are also sold as standalone processors.

Function

Because fixed-pixel displays such as LCD and OLED panels render images best at their native resolution, a scaler is required whenever the source resolution differs. A scaler resamples the image using interpolation algorithms — from simple nearest-neighbour or bilinear sampling to more advanced bicubic, Lanczos and edge-directed methods that better preserve sharpness. Many scalers also perform related processing such as deinterlacing, frame-rate conversion, aspect-ratio adjustment and noise reduction.

Applications and quality

Scaling quality varies considerably: inexpensive built-in scalers may soften detail or introduce artefacts, while dedicated external processors (historically including Faroudja and Anchor Bay designs) aimed at home-theatre enthusiasts offered superior deinterlacing and scaling. Scaling is also central to displaying standard-definition content on high-definition and 4K screens, and to retro gaming, where specialised low-latency scalers convert classic console output for modern displays with minimal input lag.
