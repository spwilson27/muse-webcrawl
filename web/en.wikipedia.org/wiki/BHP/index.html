BHP Group Limited, formerly BHP Billiton, is an Australian multinational mining and metals company headquartered in Melbourne. It is one of the world's largest mining companies by market capitalisation and revenue, producing iron ore, copper, potash and other commodities. The name derives from the Broken Hill Proprietary Company, founded in 1885 to mine silver, lead and zinc at Broken Hill in New South Wales.

History

The Broken Hill Proprietary Company was incorporated in 1885. It moved into steel production in 1915 with a steelworks at Newcastle, and later expanded into petroleum, shipping and minerals exploration. In 2001 BHP merged with the Anglo-Dutch company Billiton to form BHP Billiton, then the world's largest mining company, with a dual-listed company structure in Australia and the United Kingdom.

In 2015 BHP demerged a group of aluminium, manganese, nickel, silver and coal assets into a new company, South32. In 2018 it shortened its name to BHP, and in 2022 it unified its dual-listed structure under the Australian parent and sold its petroleum business to Woodside.

Operations

BHP's principal operations include iron ore mines in the Pilbara region of Western Australia, the Escondida copper mine in Chile (the world's largest), the Olympic Dam copper and uranium mine in South Australia, and the Jansen potash project in Canada. The company previously operated the Ok Tedi copper mine in Papua New Guinea and was involved in the Samarco dam collapse in Brazil in 2015, one of the country's worst environmental disasters.
