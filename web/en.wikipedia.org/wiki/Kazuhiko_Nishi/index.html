Kazuhiko Nishi

Kazuhiko Nishi (西 和彦)
Born: 10 February 1956, Kobe, Japan
Known for: co-founding ASCII Corporation; creating the MSX standard

Kazuhiko Nishi (born 10 February 1956) is a Japanese businessman and personal-computing pioneer. He co-founded ASCII Corporation in 1977 with Keiichiro Tsukamoto, and as a vice-president of Microsoft in the 1980s he was a key partner in the company's early Japanese business. Nishi is best known as the creator of the MSX home-computer standard.

Career

While a student at Waseda University, Nishi founded a magazine that grew into ASCII, one of Japan's first computer publishers and software houses. Through ASCII's relationship with Microsoft, he met Bill Gates and helped bring Microsoft products to Japan. In 1983 he launched the MSX standard, a unified hardware specification licensed to manufacturers such as Sony, Panasonic, Toshiba and Philips; MSX machines sold in the millions in Japan, Europe, Brazil and elsewhere.

Later work

After leaving Microsoft, Nishi remained head of ASCII until financial troubles forced changes in the late 1990s; ASCII was later absorbed into what became Kadokawa. Nishi founded the think tank and educational venture ASCII.21 and has taught at a digital media graduate school in Japan, while continuing to promote low-cost computing and retro-computing projects.
