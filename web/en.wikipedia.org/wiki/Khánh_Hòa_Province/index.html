Khánh Hòa Province

Khánh Hòa is a coastal province in the South Central Coast region of Vietnam. Its capital is Nha Trang, one of Vietnam's best-known beach resort cities. The province covers about 5,200 km² and has a population of roughly 1.25 million (2019). Offshore lie numerous islands, including parts of the disputed Spratly archipelago administered from the province's Trường Sa district, and the deep-water bay of Cam Ranh, one of the finest natural harbours in Southeast Asia.

History

The area formed part of the Hindu kingdom of Champa; the Po Nagar temple towers near Nha Trang, built between the 7th and 12th centuries, remain a major Cham monument. Vietnamese control was established in the 17th century under the Nguyễn lords. During the Vietnam War, Cam Ranh Bay was developed into a major American and then Soviet/Russian naval facility.

Economy

Tourism centred on Nha Trang and Cam Ranh, shipbuilding, fishing and aquaculture, salt production and the processing of agarwood and swiftlet nests (a regional delicacy) drive the provincial economy. Cam Ranh International Airport serves the resort coast.

Geography

Khánh Hòa has a long coastline on the South China Sea with bays at Nha Trang, Cam Ranh and Vân Phong, backed by mountains that give it one of the driest climates in Vietnam. Its highest peak is Mount Hòn Bà.
