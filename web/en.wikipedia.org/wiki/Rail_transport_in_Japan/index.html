Rail transport in Japan

Rail transport in Japan is a major means of passenger transport, especially for mass and high-speed travel between major cities and for commuter travel in urban areas. The rail network carries tens of billions of passengers per year, and the busiest stations in the world by passenger throughput are all in Japan.

History

Japan's first railway opened in 1872 between Tokyo and Yokohama. The network was largely built to the narrow gauge of 1,067 mm (3 ft 6 in). After the Second World War, Japanese National Railways developed the Shinkansen standard-gauge high-speed network, beginning with the Tōkaidō Shinkansen in 1964. JNR was privatised in 1987 and split into the six regional JR passenger companies and a freight company.

Operators and services

Alongside the JR Group, numerous private railways, subways and third-sector lines operate services. IC smart cards such as Suica and Pasmo are interoperable across most operators. Japanese trains are noted for punctuality; the Shinkansen network has carried over ten billion passengers without a fatal derailment or collision accident.
