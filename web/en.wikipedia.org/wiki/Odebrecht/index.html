Odebrecht (renamed Novonor in 2020) is a Brazilian conglomerate founded in 1944 by Norberto Odebrecht, a descendant of German immigrants. Through its construction arm (Construtora Norberto Odebrecht) and petrochemical company Braskem it became one of the largest engineering and construction groups in Latin America, building dams, highways, stadiums, ports and subways across Brazil, the Americas, Africa and Europe.

Corruption scandal

The company became the centre of Operation Car Wash (Lava Jato), the Brazilian corruption investigation launched in 2014. Investigators uncovered a dedicated internal "Division of Structured Operations" that administered a vast bribery system, paying hundreds of millions of dollars in bribes to politicians and officials in Brazil and at least eleven other countries in exchange for public contracts. In December 2016 Odebrecht and Braskem pleaded guilty in the United States and agreed to pay what was then the largest foreign-bribery penalty in history — a combined amount of at least US$3.5 billion to authorities in the US, Brazil and Switzerland. Marcelo Odebrecht, the group's chief executive, was arrested in 2015 and sentenced to 19 years in prison (later reduced under a plea bargain).

Aftermath

The scandal toppled or implicated presidents and ministers across Latin America, including in Peru, Colombia, Panama, Mexico and the Dominican Republic. Odebrecht filed for bankruptcy protection in Brazil in 2019, one of the largest such filings in the country's history, and in December 2020 rebranded the holding company as Novonor to distance itself from the scandal.
