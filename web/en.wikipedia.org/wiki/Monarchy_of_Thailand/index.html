The monarchy of Thailand is the constitutional monarchy of the Kingdom of Thailand. The King of Thailand (Thai: พระมหากษัตริย์ไทย) is the head of state and head of the ruling Royal House of Chakri. The reigning king is Vajiralongkorn (Rama X), who ascended the throne on 13 October 2016 upon the death of his father Bhumibol Adulyadej (Rama IX), the world's longest-reigning monarch at the time of his death.

History

The Thai monarchy traces its origins to the Sukhothai Kingdom of the 13th century. Through the Ayutthaya Kingdom and the Thonburi interregnum, the institution was reshaped by concepts of kingship drawn from Hindu and Buddhist traditions, in which the king was regarded as a devaraja (god-king) and a righteous Buddhist ruler (dhammaraja). The Chakri dynasty was founded by Rama I in 1782, with Bangkok as the capital.

King Mongkut (Rama IV) and King Chulalongkorn (Rama V) modernised the kingdom in the 19th century, keeping Siam independent while neighbouring states fell to European colonial powers. The absolute monarchy ended with the Siamese revolution of 1932, which established a constitutional monarchy. King Bhumibol, reigning from 1946 to 2016, became a central unifying figure through decades of political turbulence.

Constitutional role

Under Thailand's constitution, the King is head of state, head of the Royal Thai Armed Forces, upholder of religions, and is enthroned in a position of revered worship; he cannot be violated, and criticism is restricted by strict lèse-majesté laws under Article 112 of the Thai Criminal Code. The King exercises legislative power through the National Assembly, executive power through the Cabinet, and judicial power through the courts, formally acting on the advice of those bodies. He signs bills into law, appoints the prime minister and senior officials, and grants royal pardons.

Succession

Succession is governed by the 1924 Palace Law on Succession, which favours male primogeniture among the king's descendants, though a 1974 constitutional amendment allowed the Privy Council to nominate a princess in the absence of a male heir. The Crown Property Bureau historically managed the monarchy's assets; reforms under Rama X brought its holdings under the king's personal ownership.
