Trobairitz

The trobairitz were Occitan female troubadours of the 12th and 13th centuries, composing and performing lyric poetry, chiefly cansos and tensos, in the courts of southern France.

Corpus and figures

About twenty trobairitz are known by name, with some 32 surviving poems attributed. Notable figures include the Comtessa de Dia (Beatriz de Dia), author of A chantar m'er — the only trobairitz song surviving with music intact — Azalais de Porcairagues, Castelloza and Clara d'Anduza.

Themes

Unlike male troubadours singing of unattainable ladies, the trobairitz wrote from a woman's perspective within courtly love conventions. See Troubadour and Occitan literature.
