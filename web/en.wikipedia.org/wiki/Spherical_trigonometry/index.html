Spherical trigonometry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Spherical trigonometry is the branch of spherical geometry that deals with the metrical relationships between the sides and angles of spherical triangles — figures on the surface of a sphere bounded by great-circle arcs. Its fundamental formulas include the spherical law of cosines, cos a = cos b cos c + sin b sin c cos A, and the spherical law of sines, together with Napier's rules for right-angled spherical triangles.

History

Spherical trigonometry was developed by Greek, Indian and Islamic astronomers to solve problems in astronomy, and was systematised by medieval Muslim mathematicians such as Nasir al-Din al-Tusi, who presented it as a discipline independent of astronomy. For centuries it was essential to celestial navigation: solving the navigational triangle allowed mariners to determine their position from observations of the Sun and stars.

Applications

Although satellite navigation has reduced its everyday use at sea, spherical trigonometry remains fundamental to geodesy, astronomy, and computing distances and bearings between points on the Earth given by latitude and longitude, for example in flight planning and geographic information systems.
