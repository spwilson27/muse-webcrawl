Overview

The Red River — formally the Red River of the South, to distinguish it from the Red River of the North — is a major river of the southern United States. It rises in the Texas Panhandle, flows east forming most of the Texas–Oklahoma border and part of the Texas–Arkansas border, then turns southeast through Arkansas and Louisiana.

In Louisiana much of its flow is captured by the Atchafalaya River, a distributary of the Mississippi; the Old River Control Structure, completed in 1963, regulates the split to prevent the Mississippi from changing course down the Atchafalaya. The river takes its name from the red-coloured sediment and clay of its basin.

History

The Red River formed part of the boundary between the United States and Spanish (later Mexican) territory, and later the northern border of Texas. Shreveport, Louisiana, grew where Captain Henry Shreve cleared the 'Great Raft', a natural log jam more than 100 miles long, in the 1830s. Cities on the river include Wichita Falls, Texarkana, Shreveport and Alexandria.
