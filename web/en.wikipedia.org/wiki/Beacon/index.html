Beacon

A beacon is an intentionally conspicuous device designed to attract attention to a specific location. Beacons are used to guide travellers, mark hazards, and indicate positions in navigation, aviation, and emergency signalling. Historically, beacons were fires lit at high places — such as the signal fires used to warn of invasion — and the word derives from the Old English bēacen, meaning a sign or signal.

Types

Navigational beacons include lighthouses, lightships, buoys and daymarks used in marine navigation. Aeronautical beacons, such as rotating airport beacons and radio navigation aids like the non-directional beacon (NDB) and VOR, guide aircraft. Distress beacons, including Emergency Position-Indicating Radio Beacons (EPIRBs) and personal locator beacons, transmit signals to search-and-rescue satellite systems such as Cospas-Sarsat.

Modern uses

In computing, a web beacon (or web bug) is a small, often invisible object embedded in a web page or email used to track user activity. Bluetooth beacons are small radio transmitters using Bluetooth Low Energy to broadcast identifiers to nearby devices, enabling indoor positioning, proximity marketing, and asset tracking in museums, airports and retail environments.

History

Chains of hilltop beacon fires were used from antiquity: fires along the Great Wall of China signalled attacks, and a celebrated (though possibly legendary) chain of beacons carried news of the fall of Troy to Greece. In Britain, beacon systems warned of the Spanish Armada in 1588, and beacon lighting remains a ceremonial tradition for national celebrations.
