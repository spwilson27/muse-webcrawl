The 18th arrondissement of Paris (XVIIIe arrondissement), known as the arrondissement des Buttes-Montmartre, is one of the twenty arrondissements of the capital of France. Located on the Right Bank of the River Seine, it is best known for the hill of Montmartre, crowned by the white-domed Sacré-Cœur Basilica, and for the historic artists' quarter that flourished there around 1900.

Geography and quarters

The arrondissement is divided into four administrative quarters: Grandes-Carrières, Clignancourt, Goutte-d'Or and La Chapelle. Landmarks include the Moulin Rouge cabaret on the Boulevard de Clichy, the Montmartre Cemetery, the vineyard of the Clos Montmartre, and the bustling markets of Barbès and Château Rouge, which serve one of the most ethnically diverse populations in Paris.

History

The area was annexed to Paris in 1860, when the city expanded to the line of the Thiers fortifications. Montmartre had long been a village of windmills, gypsum quarries and vineyards outside the tax wall, which made its cafés and dance halls cheap and popular. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries it became the centre of bohemian Paris, home to artists including Picasso, Modigliani, Toulouse-Lautrec and Renoir, who painted the Bal du moulin de la Galette nearby.

Construction of the Sacré-Cœur began in 1875 as a national vow after the Franco-Prussian War and the Paris Commune; it was consecrated in 1919. The funicular of Montmartre carries visitors up the butte from the Place Saint-Pierre.

Transport

The arrondissement is served by Métro lines 2, 4, 5, 12 and 13, including the stations Anvers, Barbès–Rochechouart, Pigalle, Abbesses — famous for its Art Nouveau édicule by Hector Guimard — and Lamarck–Caulaincourt. The Gare du Nord, Paris's busiest railway station, lies just south of its border with the 10th arrondissement.
