Khaleda Zia (née Khanam; 15 August 1945/1946 – 30 December 2025) was a Bangladeshi politician who served as Prime Minister of Bangladesh from 1991 to 1996 and again from 2001 to 2006. She was the first woman prime minister of Bangladesh and the chairperson of the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP), which was founded by her husband, President Ziaur Rahman. Her decades-long rivalry with Awami League leader Sheikh Hasina dominated Bangladeshi politics and earned the two women the nickname "the Battling Begums."

Early life

Khaleda Zia was born in Jalpaiguri in British India to Iskandar Majumder, a tea businessman, and Taiyaba Majumder. The family later settled in Dinajpur. In 1960 she married Ziaur Rahman, then a captain in the Pakistan Army, who became a hero of the 1971 Liberation War, army chief, and eventually president of Bangladesh. After Ziaur Rahman's assassination in 1981, Khaleda, previously a housewife, entered politics and rose quickly within the BNP, becoming its vice-chairperson in 1983 and chairperson in 1984.

Political career

During the 1980s Zia led the movement against the military rule of Hussain Muhammad Ershad, boycotting elections under his regime and enduring repeated detention. After Ershad's fall, the BNP won the February 1991 general election and she became prime minister, presiding over the restoration of parliamentary government. Her government introduced free primary education and promoted girls' schooling. A disputed February 1996 election led to her brief second government resigning under opposition pressure; she returned to power in coalition with Jamaat-e-Islami in 2001.

Later years

After a military-backed caretaker government took power in 2007, Zia and Hasina were both imprisoned on corruption charges. In 2018 Zia was convicted in the Zia Orphanage Trust case and jailed, and was barred from leaving the country for medical treatment for several years while her health deteriorated. Following the fall of Sheikh Hasina's government in August 2024, her convictions were quashed on appeal and she was released; she travelled to London for treatment in 2025. She died in Dhaka on 30 December 2025.
