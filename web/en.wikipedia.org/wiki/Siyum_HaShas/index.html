Siyum HaShas

Siyum HaShas (Hebrew: סיום הש״ס, "completion of the Shas") is a celebration marking the completion of the entire Babylonian Talmud — 2,711 folios — through the Daf Yomi programme, in which participants study one folio page per day. The cycle takes approximately seven years and five months, and its completion is celebrated with mass gatherings around the world, immediately followed by the beginning of the next cycle.

History

The Daf Yomi ("daily page") programme was proposed by Rabbi Meir Shapiro of Lublin and launched at the First World Congress of Agudath Israel in Vienna in 1923; the first cycle began on Rosh Hashanah of that year. The first Siyum HaShas was held in 1931 at Shapiro's Yeshivas Chachmei Lublin in Poland.

Modern celebrations

Participation has grown enormously, and recent completions have drawn crowds filling major stadiums. The 12th Siyum in 2012 filled MetLife Stadium in New Jersey with over 90,000 attendees. The 13th Siyum, completing the cycle on 1 January 2020, again drew some 90,000 people to MetLife Stadium, with simultaneous celebrations in Jerusalem, London, and other cities, and was regarded as among the largest Jewish gatherings in modern times.

The ceremony

The event features the study of the final page of the Talmud (the closing lines of Tractate Niddah), recitation of the Hadran prayer and Kaddish, speeches by leading rabbis, music and dancing, and the ceremonial start of the first page of Tractate Berakhot, beginning the cycle anew.
