FanDuel Group is an American gambling and fantasy sports company based in New York City. Founded in 2009 by Nigel Eccles and partners in Edinburgh, Scotland, as a daily fantasy sports provider, FanDuel became one of the two dominant daily fantasy operators in the United States alongside DraftKings.

History

FanDuel grew out of Hubdub, a prediction market website. Its daily fantasy format compressed season-long fantasy sports into single-day contests for cash prizes. The company raised substantial venture funding during the fantasy boom of the early 2010s and spent heavily on advertising. Legal challenges followed, including a 2015 insider-information controversy and state rulings that daily fantasy constituted illegal gambling; New York's attorney general briefly shut the operators down before legislation legalized the contests in 2016.

Merger and sports betting

FanDuel agreed to merge with DraftKings in 2016, but the deal collapsed in 2017 after the Federal Trade Commission sued to block it. In 2018, Irish bookmaker Paddy Power Betfair (now Flutter Entertainment) acquired a controlling stake in FanDuel. Following the U.S. Supreme Court's 2018 decision striking down the federal ban on sports betting, FanDuel launched sportsbooks across many states and became the U.S. market leader in online sports betting, alongside its fantasy and casino products.
