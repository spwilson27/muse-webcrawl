VariantsLeft, right, and unconditional accelerationism

Accelerationism is a range of political and social theories that maintain that political and technological change — in particular the process of capital accumulation and technological development — should be dramatically accelerated rather than resisted. Accelerationists variously argue that intensifying capitalism's transformative forces can hasten a desirable post-capitalist order, or that the process should simply be pushed to its limits.

Origins

The term was popularised by Benjamin Noys in 2010, drawing on ideas in Deleuze and Guattari's Anti-Oedipus and Jean-François Lyotard's writings, and on the work of philosopher Nick Land and the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit at the University of Warwick in the 1990s.

Variants

Left accelerationism, set out by Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams in their 2013 manifesto, calls for repurposing capitalist technology and infrastructure toward a post-capitalist, post-work society. Right accelerationism, associated with Land's later writing, embraces capitalism's dissolution of traditional structures. In looser usage, "accelerationism" has also been applied to far-right extremist strategies of hastening societal collapse, a usage scholars distinguish from the philosophical theory.
