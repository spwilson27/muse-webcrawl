The Bugatti Type 35 is a Grand Prix racing car developed by Bugatti in 1924. It is the most successful racing car of all time, with more than 1,000 victories in period — by some counts over 2,000 — including five consecutive wins at the Targa Florio (1925–1929) and the inaugural Monaco Grand Prix in 1929. Instantly recognisable by its elegant boat-tail bodywork and cast aluminium wheels, the Type 35 combined light weight, precise handling, and a jewel-like straight-eight engine.

Design

Designed under Ettore Bugatti, the Type 35 used a 2.0-litre overhead-camshaft straight-eight engine with three valves per cylinder and a crankshaft running in five main bearings. Its hollow front axle saved weight, and its cast aluminium wheels with integral brake drums were a landmark of both engineering and aesthetics. The distinctive horseshoe radiator grille became a Bugatti hallmark retained on modern cars.

Variants

The original Type 35 was followed by the supercharged Type 35C, the larger-engined 2.3-litre Type 35T built for the Targa Florio, and the supercharged Type 35B of 1927, the definitive version with about 138 horsepower and a top speed near 210 km/h (130 mph). The cheaper Type 37 used a four-cylinder engine, and the Type 39 was a smaller-displacement variant. Around 340 Type 35s of all variants were built.

Racing record

Type 35s dominated the late-1920s Grand Prix scene, winning the French, Spanish, Italian, and European Grands Prix and the 1926 manufacturers' world championship for Bugatti. William Grover-Williams won the first Monaco Grand Prix in a Type 35B in 1929. Many examples survived racing into the postwar classic era, and original cars are today among the most valuable pre-war automobiles.
