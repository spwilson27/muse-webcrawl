Riohacha (Wayuu: Süchiimma) is a city in northern Colombia and the capital of the department of La Guajira. It sits on the Caribbean Sea at the mouth of the Ranchería River, on the edge of the arid Guajira Peninsula. Founded by Spanish settlers in 1545, it is one of the oldest Spanish-founded cities in Colombia and serves as the main gateway to the Indigenous Wayuu heartland and the Alta Guajira.

History

The area was inhabited by Wayuu and other Indigenous peoples long before Spanish colonisation. Riohacha grew wealthy in the 16th and 17th centuries from pearl diving and trade, and was repeatedly attacked by English and Dutch pirates — including raids associated with Francis Drake and John Hawkins — because of that pearl trade. Smuggling with the Dutch and English Caribbean islands remained a feature of its economy through the colonial era. In the independence period the city changed hands several times between patriots and royalists.

Geography and climate

Riohacha has a hot semi-arid climate moderated by northeast trade winds blowing off the Caribbean. Rainfall is low and concentrated in a short wet season around September to November. To the east the landscape becomes true desert in the upper Guajira; to the south lies the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta foothills and the bird-rich wetlands of the Los Flamencos Flora and Fauna Sanctuary, noted for American flamingos.

Culture and economy

The city is a centre of Wayuu culture; Wayuu artisans sell colourful woven mochila bags and hammocks (chinchorros) in its markets, and the Festival de la Cultura Wayuu is held in nearby Uribia. A large share of the population is Wayuu or Afro-Colombian. The economy depends on commerce, fishing, port activities, coal royalties from the Cerrejón mine in the department's interior, and growing tourism: Riohacha's malecón (seafront promenade) and pier are local landmarks, and travellers pass through en route to Cabo de la Vela and Punta Gallinas, the northernmost point of South America. Almirante Padilla Airport serves the city.
