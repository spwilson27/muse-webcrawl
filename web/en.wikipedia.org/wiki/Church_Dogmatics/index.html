The Church Dogmatics (German: Kirchliche Dogmatik) is the multi-volume systematic theology of the Swiss Reformed theologian Karl Barth (1886–1968), widely regarded as the most important theological work of the twentieth century. Published in thirteen part-volumes between 1932 and 1967 — amounting to more than 9,000 pages — it remained unfinished at Barth's death.

[Image: Volumes of the Church Dogmatics on a shelf]

Overview

Barth began the work as a replacement for his abandoned Christian Dogmatics (1927). It is organised around the doctrine of the Word of God and proceeds through the classical loci of dogmatics, radically re-centred on God's self-revelation in Jesus Christ:

VolumeDoctrineContents
I (1–2)The Word of GodProlegomena: revelation, Scripture, Trinity
II (1–2)The Doctrine of GodKnowledge and reality of God; election; the command of God
III (1–4)The Doctrine of CreationCreation, providence, anthropology, ethics of creation
IV (1–4)The Doctrine of ReconciliationChristology and soteriology: justification, sanctification, vocation; the community and the Holy Spirit
VThe Doctrine of RedemptionPlanned (eschatology) but never written

Themes

Key features include Barth's doctrine of election — Christ is both electing God and elected human, so predestination is a doctrine of grace rather than a double decree of damnation — his actualistic account of revelation, his insistence that theology is a science of the church's speech about God, and his rejection of natural theology and the analogia entis. Volume IV/3 contains the famous treatment of Jesus as "the true witness" and the parables of the kingdom; volume IV/4 breaks off after the fragment on baptism.

Reception

Pope Pius XII is reported to have called Barth the greatest theologian since Thomas Aquinas. The work shaped post-war theology across denominations and remains the central text of Barth studies, studied through secondary literature such as Hans Urs von Balthasar's The Theology of Karl Barth and Eberhard Jüngel's works.
