Perfect season

A perfect season is a sports season, including postseason play, in which a team remains undefeated and untied through the entire competition. Because draws are not permitted in some leagues, the term is most often applied to American football, where the best-known example is the 1972 Miami Dolphins, who finished 17–0 and won Super Bowl VII — still the only perfect season in NFL history. The 2007 New England Patriots went 18–0 before losing Super Bowl XLII to the New York Giants.

Other examples

In association football, teams finishing a league season unbeaten are sometimes called "invincibles"; Arsenal's 2003–04 Premier League campaign (26 wins, 12 draws) is a famous example, though the club did not win every cup competition. Perfect seasons with no draws at all have occurred in smaller leagues and in other sports such as rugby, basketball at collegiate level (e.g., several undefeated NCAA champions, first the 1956 San Francisco Dons), and in esports.

Related concepts

The opposite feat, losing every game, is sometimes called an "imperfect season" or "winless season", as with the 2008 Detroit Lions (0–16) and 2017 Cleveland Browns.
