Continuity (fiction)

Continuity is the consistency of the characteristics of people, plot, objects, and places seen by the reader or viewer over some period of time. It is of relevance to several media. The term is used especially in television, film, and comic books.

Description

In production, a script supervisor keeps records of each take to ensure props, costumes, and staging match between shots filmed out of order. In writing, continuity means that a character's age, backstory, and past actions remain coherent across episodes and instalments.

Continuity errors

Violations, often called goofs, include a disappearing prop, a character who is alive after having died, or technology that changes between scenes. Fans catalogued such errors long before the internet made compilation easy.

Continuity in shared universes

Long-running franchises such as Star Wars, Star Trek, and the Marvel and DC universes maintain staff or reference documents to reconcile decades of stories. When contradictions accumulate, publishers may issue a retcon (retroactive continuity) or a full reboot.

Canon

Which works "count" within continuity is called canon. Television spin-offs, novels, and games are often declared non-canonical, meaning their events need not be respected by later core works.
