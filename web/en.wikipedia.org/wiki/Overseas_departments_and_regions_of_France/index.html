Overseas departments and regions of France

The overseas departments and regions of France (French: départements et régions d'outre-mer, DROM) are five territorial collectivities that are fully integrated into the French Republic, with the same constitutional status as metropolitan departments and regions. Unlike the overseas collectivities, the DROM are part of the European Union as outermost regions, use the euro, and are represented in the French Parliament and the European Parliament.

The five departments

  • Guadeloupe (971) — Caribbean archipelago, department since 1946.
  • Martinique (972) — Caribbean island, department since 1946.
  • French Guiana (973) — on the north-east coast of South America, department since 1946.
  • Réunion (974) — island in the Indian Ocean east of Madagascar, department since 1946.
  • Mayotte (976) — island in the Mozambique Channel; became a department in 2011 following a 2009 referendum.

Governance

Since constitutional reforms, Guadeloupe and Réunion retain separate departmental and regional councils, while Martinique, French Guiana and Mayotte have moved to single territorial collectivities exercising both sets of powers. Together the five territories have about 2.2 million inhabitants.
