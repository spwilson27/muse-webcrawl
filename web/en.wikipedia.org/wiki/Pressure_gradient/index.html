Pressure gradient

In atmospheric science, the pressure gradient (typically of air but more generally of any compressible or incompressible fluid) is a physical quantity that describes in which direction and at what rate the pressure increases the most rapidly around a particular location. The pressure gradient is a vector quantity with dimensions of force per unit volume, or energy per unit volume per unit distance.

Mathematically, it is the gradient of pressure as a function of position. The negative gradient of pressure — pointing from high toward low pressure — is known as the pressure gradient force when divided by density, and it is one of the principal forces governing the motion of air and water in the atmosphere and oceans.

Definition

If p is pressure and the coordinates are x, y, z, the pressure gradient is

∇p = (∂p/∂x, ∂p/∂y, ∂p/∂z)

The acceleration of a parcel of fluid due to the pressure gradient alone is −(1/ρ)∇p, where ρ is the fluid density. Air accelerates from regions of higher pressure toward regions of lower pressure until balanced by other forces.

Meteorology

On weather maps, the horizontal pressure gradient is read from the spacing of isobars: closely packed isobars indicate a steep gradient and strong winds. Away from the surface, the pressure-gradient force is balanced by the Coriolis force, producing the geostrophic wind, which blows parallel to the isobars. The relationship between the pressure gradient and wind speed is summarized in Buys Ballot's law. Steep pressure gradients are characteristic of intense low-pressure systems such as tropical cyclones, in which the sharp radial gradient near the eyewall drives extreme winds.

Oceanography and other fields

In the oceans, horizontal pressure gradients arising from sea-surface height differences and density variations drive geostrophic currents. Subsurface pressure gradients in geology influence groundwater flow and petroleum migration, while in medicine pressure gradients across heart valves are measured (e.g. by echocardiography) to grade the severity of valve stenosis.
