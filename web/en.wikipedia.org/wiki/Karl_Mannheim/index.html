Karl Mannheim

Karl Mannheim
Born: 27 March 1893, Budapest
Died: 9 January 1947, London
Known for: Sociology of knowledge
Notable work: Ideology and Utopia (1929)

Karl Mannheim (27 March 1893 – 9 January 1947) was a Hungarian-born sociologist who founded the sociology of knowledge. His best-known work, Ideology and Utopia (1929), examined how social position shapes thought.

Life

Mannheim studied in Budapest, Berlin, Paris and Heidelberg, where he took his doctorate. After the fall of the Hungarian Soviet Republic he emigrated to Germany, teaching at Heidelberg and Frankfurt. Driven into exile by the Nazis in 1933, he settled in London, teaching at the London School of Economics.

Thought

Mannheim argued that ideas are bound to the social situations of their bearers — his concept of relationism — and distinguished ideology, which defends an existing order, from utopia, which seeks to transform it. He also wrote influentially on generations and on democratic planning in Man and Society in an Age of Reconstruction.
