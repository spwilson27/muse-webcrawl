Wiping

Wiping is the act of cleaning a surface by rubbing it with a cloth, sponge, paper, or the hand, typically to remove dirt, moisture, or other material. In various technical contexts the term has specialized meanings: in computing, data wiping is the deliberate, permanent erasure of data from a storage device; in film and television production, a wipe is a transition effect in which one image replaces another by seeming to push it off the screen; and in personal hygiene, wiping refers to cleaning the body after defecation.

Data wiping

Data wiping (secure erasure) overwrites stored information so that it cannot be recovered, going beyond simple file deletion, which only removes directory references. Standards such as NIST Special Publication 800-88 define "Clear," "Purge," and "Destroy" methods for media sanitization. Wiping is essential for organizations disposing of computers, complying with privacy law, and preventing data breaches from discarded drives.

The wipe transition

In cinema, the wipe was popular in the 1930s and was famously revived by George Lucas in Star Wars. One scene is "wiped" away by the next from any of several directions, sometimes following a natural element within the shot.

Hygiene

Anal cleansing practices vary by culture: toilet paper is standard in Western countries, while water washing with bidets, bidet showers, or vessels such as the lota is common in much of Asia, the Middle East, and elsewhere; historically, materials such as stones, leaves, and corn cobs were used.
