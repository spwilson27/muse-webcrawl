The Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) is an expendable medium-lift launch vehicle designed and operated by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). It was developed to allow India to launch its Indian Remote Sensing (IRS) satellites into Sun-synchronous orbits — a service that was, until the PSLV's advent, only commercially available from Russia. The PSLV can also launch small satellites into geostationary transfer orbit (GTO).

A PSLV launch from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota.

Design

The four-stage vehicle alternates solid and liquid propulsion: a large solid first stage (augmented by strap-on boosters in most variants), a liquid Vikas-engine second stage, a solid third stage, and a liquid fourth stage capable of multiple restarts for deploying payloads into different orbits. Variants include the standard PSLV-G, the Core Alone (PSLV-CA), and the more powerful PSLV-XL with extended strap-ons.

Record

Known as ISRO's "workhorse", the PSLV has flown more than 60 missions with very few failures. Landmark flights include Chandrayaan-1 (2008), the Mars Orbiter Mission (2013), and PSLV-C37 (2017), which set a then-world record by deploying 104 satellites on a single launch. It has launched hundreds of foreign satellites commercially through Antrix/NewSpace India Limited.
