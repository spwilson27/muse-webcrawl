Gilgit-Baltistan

Gilgit-Baltistan (Urdu: گلگت بلتستان), formerly known as the Northern Areas, is a region administered by Pakistan as an administrative territory. It constitutes the northern portion of the larger Kashmir region, which has been the subject of a dispute between India and Pakistan since 1947, and between India and China since the late 1950s.

Country: Pakistan
Capital: Gilgit
Largest city: Gilgit
Area: 72,971 km²
Population: approx. 1.5–2 million

The territory is home to some of the world's longest glaciers outside the polar regions and to five of the fourteen eight-thousander peaks, including K2, the second-highest mountain on Earth, and Nanga Parbat. The Karakoram Highway, connecting Pakistan to China through the Khunjerab Pass, is a major trade and tourism route and a flagship of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor.

Geography and people

Gilgit-Baltistan is divided into three divisions — Gilgit, Baltistan, and Diamer — and 14 districts. The population speaks a variety of languages including Shina, Balti, Burushaski, Wakhi, and Khowar, and includes Sunni, Shia, Ismaili, and Nurbakhshi Muslim communities. The administrative capital and largest city is Gilgit; Skardu is the principal city of Baltistan.

Status

The region is governed under the Gilgit-Baltistan Order of 2018, with an elected legislative assembly and a chief minister, though it is not formally listed as a province in Pakistan's constitution because of its connection to the Kashmir dispute.
