Stuxnet

Stuxnet is a malicious computer worm first uncovered in 2010 that targeted supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) systems. It is widely believed to have been responsible for causing substantial damage to Iran's nuclear program by sabotaging uranium-enrichment centrifuges at the Natanz facility.

Operation

Stuxnet spread via infected USB drives and Windows vulnerabilities — unusually, it used four zero-day exploits — but activated its payload only on systems running Siemens Step7 software connected to specific programmable logic controllers (PLCs). Once in place, it periodically altered the rotational speeds of centrifuge motors (raising them to 1,410 Hz and dropping them to 2 Hz against a normal operating frequency near 1,064 Hz), causing physical destruction, while replaying recorded normal sensor data so operators saw nothing wrong. Analysts estimate roughly 1,000 centrifuges — about a fifth of those at Natanz — were destroyed.

Attribution

Although no government has officially claimed responsibility, reporting by the New York Times and others attributes Stuxnet to a joint American–Israeli operation, code-named "Olympic Games", begun under President George W. Bush and accelerated under President Barack Obama.

Significance

Stuxnet is regarded as the first true cyberweapon: the first malware known to cause physical destruction of equipment. Its escape beyond Natanz and subsequent public analysis demonstrated both the potential and the blowback risk of state-built malware, and it spawned a family of related espionage tools including Duqu and Flame.
