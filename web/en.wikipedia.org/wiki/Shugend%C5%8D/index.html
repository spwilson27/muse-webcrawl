Shugendō

Shugendō is a Japanese syncretic religion that combines mountain asceticism with elements of Shinto, esoteric Buddhism (especially Tendai and Shingon), Daoism and shamanism. Its practitioners, called yamabushi, undertake rigorous austerities in sacred mountains to attain spiritual power and enlightenment.

History

Shugendō developed around mountain cults from the Heian period onward. It was suppressed during the Meiji period's forced separation of Shinto and Buddhism (1868) and officially banned in 1872, but revived after the Second World War. Sacred sites associated with Shugendō, including the Ōminesan and Kumano regions, are recognized as UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
