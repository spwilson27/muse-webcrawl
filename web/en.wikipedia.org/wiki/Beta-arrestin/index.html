Beta-arrestin

Beta-arrestin (β-arrestin) refers to two multifunctional adaptor proteins, arrestin beta 1 (ARRB1) and arrestin beta 2 (ARRB2), expressed in most tissues. They are best known for regulating the signalling of G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), the largest family of cell-surface receptors and drug targets.

Function

After a GPCR is activated by its ligand, G protein-coupled receptor kinases (GRKs) phosphorylate the receptor's cytoplasmic tail. Beta-arrestins bind the phosphorylated receptor, sterically blocking further G protein coupling — a process called desensitisation — and target the receptor for clathrin-mediated endocytosis, either recycling it to the membrane or sending it for degradation.

Beta-arrestins are not merely "off switches": they also act as scaffolds that assemble signalling complexes, initiating G protein-independent pathways such as the MAP kinase cascade. This dual role underlies the concept of biased agonism, in which a drug at a GPCR preferentially activates either G protein or beta-arrestin signalling, potentially separating therapeutic effects from side effects. Beta-arrestins also interact with many non-GPCR partners, including components of the Wnt and Hedgehog pathways.
