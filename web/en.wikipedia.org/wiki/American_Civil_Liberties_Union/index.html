The American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) is an American nonprofit civil rights organisation founded in 1920 "to defend and preserve the individual rights and liberties guaranteed to every person in this country by the Constitution and laws of the United States". It pursues its mission chiefly through litigation, legislation, and public education, with affiliates in every state and a membership in the millions.

History

The ACLU grew out of the Civil Liberties Bureau of the American Union Against Militarism, which defended conscientious objectors and opponents of conscription during the First World War. Under its first director, Roger Baldwin, the young ACLU took part in landmark cases such as the Scopes Monkey Trial (1925), the Sacco and Vanzetti case, and challenges to bans on works by James Joyce. In the 1940s it controversially declined to oppose the internment of Japanese Americans, though some of its West Coast affiliates did.

Landmark work

ACLU lawyers helped argue Brown v. Board of Education (1954) against school segregation and represented Clarence Gideon in Gideon v. Wainwright (1963), establishing the right to counsel. The organisation has litigated extensively on free speech — famously defending the right of American Nazis to march in Skokie, Illinois, in 1977–78 — as well as on reproductive rights, LGBTQ rights, voting rights, privacy and surveillance, prisoners' rights, and the separation of church and state. Its Women's Rights Project, co-founded by Ruth Bader Ginsburg in 1972, brought a series of sex-discrimination cases to the Supreme Court.

Organisation and criticism

The ACLU consists of two entities — a 501(c)(4) social-welfare organisation and the 501(c)(3) ACLU Foundation — and state affiliates that handle most local cases. It describes itself as nonpartisan but is frequently characterised as liberal; it has been criticised from the right for its positions on issues such as the Second Amendment and religious exemptions, and occasionally from the left, including debates over its traditional absolutist stance on free speech.
