Nightfall (novel)

Nightfall is a science fiction novel by Isaac Asimov and Robert Silverberg, published in 1990. It expands Asimov's short story "Nightfall", first published in Astounding Science Fiction in 1941 and repeatedly voted the finest science fiction short story ever written. The novel adds a long prelude and a sequel section to the original story, which forms its middle third.

Premise and plot

The planet Kalgash orbits within a system of six suns, so that at any point on its surface at least one sun is always in the sky, and darkness is unknown. Every 2,049 years, however, an eclipse by an undiscovered moon leaves one hemisphere in total darkness long enough for the stars to appear. Astronomers, archaeologists and a doomsday cult called the Apostles of Flame converge on the truth: previous civilisations have collapsed into madness and fire at each eclipse, because a people who have never seen darkness cannot endure it. The novel follows reporter Theremon 762, astronomer Beenay 25 and archaeologist Siferra 89 through the eclipse and into the chaotic aftermath, as survivors struggle to rebuild amid warring factions.

Background

Asimov wrote the original story at the age of 21 at the instigation of editor John W. Campbell, who took the premise from a quotation by Emerson. The novelisation, one of Asimov's last major works, was produced with Silverberg, who had previously expanded Asimov's stories "The Positronic Man" and "The Ugly Little Boy".
