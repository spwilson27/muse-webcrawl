Criminal damage in English law

Criminal damage in English law is governed principally by the Criminal Damage Act 1971, which replaced the earlier Malicious Damage Act 1861. The Act sets out offences of destroying or damaging property belonging to another.

Basic offence

Under section 1(1), a person who, without lawful excuse, destroys or damages any property belonging to another, intending to destroy or damage it or being reckless as to whether it is destroyed or damaged, is guilty of an offence. "Damage" is not defined in the Act and has been interpreted broadly by the courts to include temporary impairment of value or usefulness.

Aggravated offences

Section 1(2) creates an aggravated offence where the defendant intends or is reckless as to endangering the life of another by the damage. Section 1(3) covers damage by fire, charged as arson. Threats to destroy or damage property and possession of articles with intent to cause damage are offences under sections 2 and 3.

Lawful excuse

Section 5 provides a statutory "lawful excuse", including an honest belief that the owner consented, or that the damage was necessary to protect other property in immediate need of protection.
