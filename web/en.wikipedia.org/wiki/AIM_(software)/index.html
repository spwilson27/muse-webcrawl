AIM (AOL Instant Messenger) was an instant messaging and presence computer program created by AOL, launched in May 1997. Using the proprietary OSCAR protocol, AIM allowed registered users to exchange text messages in real time, maintain a "buddy list" of contacts, set away messages and profiles, and share files. At its peak in the early 2000s it was the dominant instant messaging service in North America and a defining communication medium for teenagers and young adults of the era.

History

AIM began as an internal tool built by AOL engineers and was initially released without senior management's full endorsement; it was offered as a free standalone download for anyone, not only AOL subscribers, helping it grow virally. Features accumulated over the years: buddy icons, chat rooms, stock tickers, voice and video chat, games and integration with AOL mail. The service's sign-on sound and "You've Got Mail"-era branding, along with the yellow Running Man mascot, became cultural landmarks.

Competition and decline

AIM fought the "instant messaging wars" with Microsoft's MSN Messenger and Yahoo Messenger, resisting interoperability despite pressure from rivals and regulators (interoperability conditions attached to the AOL–Time Warner merger in 2001). After 2005, growth of social networking (Facebook) and then smartphone messaging (SMS, iMessage, WhatsApp) eroded AIM's user base. AOL was sold to Verizon in 2015 and folded into Oath; in October 2017 Verizon announced AIM would shut down, and the service closed on 15 December 2017 after just over twenty years.

Cultural impact

AIM normalised screen names, chat abbreviations (such as "brb" and "lol" in mainstream usage), away messages as a form of self-expression, and the social etiquette of always-on text contact. Its warning and blocking features, buddy profiles and door-open/door-close sounds were widely imitated by later platforms. Journalists frequently cite AIM as a formative technology for millennials' online social lives.
