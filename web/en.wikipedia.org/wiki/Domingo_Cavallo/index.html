Domingo Cavallo

Born21 July 1946, San Francisco, Córdoba, Argentina
OfficeMinister of Economy of Argentina (1991–1996; 2001)

Domingo Felipe Cavallo (born 21 July 1946) is an Argentine economist and politician. He is best known as the architect of the Convertibility Plan of 1991, which pegged the Argentine peso one-to-one to the US dollar and ended the hyperinflation that had ravaged the country.

Convertibility

Appointed Minister of Economy by President Carlos Menem in 1991, Cavallo introduced a currency-board-like law requiring the central bank to back the monetary base with foreign reserves. Inflation fell from thousands of percent annually to single digits, and the economy grew strongly in the early 1990s. The rigidity of the peg later came under criticism for contributing to the stagnation and crisis of 1998–2002.

Second tenure

Recalled by President Fernando de la Rúa in 2001 during the deepening crisis, Cavallo imposed deposit restrictions — the corralito — in December 2001, triggering riots and the collapse of the government. He subsequently faced prosecutions over aspects of his policies, of which he was ultimately cleared in the principal cases.
