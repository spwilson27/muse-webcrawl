Dedham Pottery

Dedham Pottery was an American art pottery company associated with the Arts and Crafts movement. It is famous for its crackle-glazed stoneware decorated with cobalt blue borders of animals and flowers, most famously crouching rabbits.

History

The company's roots lie in the Chelsea Keramic Art Works founded in Chelsea, Massachusetts, in 1872 by members of the Robertson family. Hugh C. Robertson, who had long experimented with glazes including a sang-de-boeuf (oxblood) red and a fine crackle glaze inspired by Chinese wares, moved the pottery to Dedham, Massachusetts, in 1896, where it operated as Dedham Pottery.

Wares

Dedham's signature ware has a soft grey-white crackle glaze with a hand-painted blue border pattern. The rabbit, designed by Hugh Robertson and refined by painters including Maude Davenport, became the company's emblem; the rabbit pattern was registered in 1929. Other border motifs included ducks, turkeys, butterflies, grapes, lilies, magnolia, and other animals and plants, often with the animals' eyes dotted in orange or red. Pieces were marked with a printed rabbit mark and the name "Dedham Pottery", and some bore the painter's initials.

Closure and legacy

The pottery closed in 1943. Its wares are widely collected and are held by major museums, including the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston. Reproductions of the rabbit design have been made by later concerns.
