District (China)

The term district, in the context of China, is used to refer to several unrelated political divisions in both ancient and modern China. In modern China, a district (区, pinyin: qū) is a county-level administrative division of the People's Republic of China, forming part of prefecture-level cities and the direct-controlled municipalities.

Modern districts

Districts are subdivisions of larger cities and are predominantly urban or suburban in character. A prefecture-level city administers a set of districts covering its urban core alongside outlying counties and county-level cities. The four direct-controlled municipalities — Beijing, Shanghai, Tianjin, and Chongqing — are subdivided entirely into districts and (in Chongqing's case) counties. Well-known examples include Beijing's Chaoyang and Haidian districts and Shanghai's Pudong New Area.

District governments mirror the structure of the city government, with a people's government, people's congress, and courts, and they administer subdistricts (street offices) and towns within their boundaries.

Special districts

China also maintains special districts (特区) such as the Wolong Special Administrative Region in Sichuan, a nature-reserve administration with district-like status. New areas (新区), like Pudong before it became a district, are state-level development zones that may span several existing divisions.

Historical use

Imperial and Republican-era records used the term differently: under the Republic of China, districts were subdivisions of counties, a usage still reflected in the district offices (區公所) of Taiwan's special municipalities and cities.
