POSCO

POSCO (formerly Pohang Iron and Steel Company) is a South Korean steel-making company headquartered in Pohang, South Korea. Founded in 1968 under the leadership of Park Tae-joon with support from the South Korean government, it grew from a country with no modern steel industry into one of the world's largest steel producers, and is widely credited as a cornerstone of South Korea's rapid industrialization.

History

POSCO was established on 1 April 1968. Its first integrated steel mill at Pohang began production in 1973, built with funds that included Japanese reparations under the 1965 normalization treaty and loans arranged after earlier financing plans with Western banks fell through. A second, larger works at Gwangyang was completed in stages during the 1980s and early 1990s, making Gwangyang one of the largest single steelworks in the world.

The company was privatized in 2000. In 2022, POSCO reorganized under a holding-company structure, POSCO Holdings Inc., with the steel business operated by POSCO Co., Ltd. The group has expanded into lithium and battery materials, hydrogen, LNG, construction, and trading through affiliates such as POSCO International and POSCO Chemical (now POSCO Future M).

Operations

POSCO operates integrated steelworks at Pohang and Gwangyang with combined crude steel capacity of over 40 million tonnes per year, plus joint ventures and finishing plants overseas, including in Indonesia (Krakatau POSCO), Vietnam, India, and China. It is recognized for its FINEX ironmaking process, an alternative to the blast furnace that uses iron ore fines and non-coking coal directly.

Recognition

POSCO has repeatedly been ranked among the most competitive steelmakers in the world by industry analysts, and its growth is studied as a model of state-led industrial development.
