Neil Gershenfeld

Neil Adam Gershenfeld (born December 1, 1959) is an American professor at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where he directs the Center for Bits and Atoms. His research spans the boundaries between computer science and physical science, including quantum computing, nanotechnology, and digital fabrication.

Fab labs

Gershenfeld is widely credited with originating the concept of the fab lab — a small-scale workshop equipped with flexible computer-controlled tools (laser cutters, 3D printers, CNC mills, electronics benches) that aims to let users make "almost anything". Beginning as an outreach project of the Center for Bits and Atoms around 2001, fab labs grew into a global network of thousands of community fabrication spaces coordinated by the Fab Foundation.

Work and writing

His course "How To Make (Almost) Anything" became a template for hands-on digital fabrication education, and the distributed Fab Academy programme grew out of it. Gershenfeld is the author of When Things Start to Think (1999), Fab (2005), and, with his brothers Alan Gershenfeld and Joel Cutcher-Gershenfeld, Designing Reality (2017). Time magazine named him one of its "Time 100" in 2005, and Scientific American included him among its "Scientific American 50" leaders.
