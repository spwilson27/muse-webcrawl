Predictive analytics

Predictive analytics is a category of data analytics that uses statistical techniques, machine learning, and data mining to analyze current and historical data in order to make predictions about future events or unknown outcomes. It is widely used in business, healthcare, finance, marketing, and government.

Overview

Predictive analytics encompasses a variety of techniques from data mining, statistics, modeling, machine learning, and artificial intelligence. Whereas descriptive analytics summarizes what has happened and diagnostic analytics explains why it happened, predictive analytics focuses on what is likely to happen next. Typical applications include credit scoring, customer churn prediction, fraud detection, demand forecasting, and risk assessment.

Techniques

Common techniques include regression analysis (linear and logistic regression), decision trees and random forests, neural networks, support vector machines, time series forecasting, and ensemble methods. The modeling process generally involves defining the objective, collecting and preparing data, building and validating a model against held-out data, and deploying the model into operational use.

Applications

In retail and marketing, predictive analytics is used for customer segmentation, recommendation systems, and targeted advertising. In finance it supports credit risk modeling and algorithmic trading. In healthcare it is applied to predicting patient readmissions and disease outbreaks. Insurers use it for underwriting and claims triage, while manufacturers use it for predictive maintenance of equipment.

Criticism and limitations

Predictive models can inherit biases present in training data, may perform poorly when conditions change (model drift), and can raise privacy concerns when applied to personal data. Critics also note that correlation-based predictions do not establish causation, and that opaque "black box" models can be difficult to audit or explain.
