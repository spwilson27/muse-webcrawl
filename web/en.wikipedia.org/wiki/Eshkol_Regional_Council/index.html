The Eshkol Regional Council (Hebrew: מועצה אזורית אשכול, Mo'atza Ezorit Eshkol) is a regional council in the north-western Negev, in Israel's Southern District. It lies between Ashkelon in the north and Beersheba in the east, adjoining the Gaza Strip along its western border and Egypt to the south-west. The council provides municipal services to a network of kibbutzim, moshavim, and community settlements spread across one of the largest regional-council areas in Israel.

History

The council was established in 1951 under the name Ma'on Regional Council. In 1969 it was renamed for Levi Eshkol, Israel's third prime minister, who died that year. "Eshkol" also means a cluster (of grapes), a word associated with the Valley of Eshkol in the Bible. The settlements of the region were founded in waves — some kibbutzim in the 1940s (such as Be'eri, founded 1946), others as moshavim absorbing immigrants in the 1950s.

Geography and communities

The region is predominantly agricultural, with citrus groves, field crops, greenhouses, and dairy farming across the loess plains of the western Negev. Communities in the council include the kibbutzim Be'eri, Ein HaShlosha, Gvulot, Holit, Kerem Shalom, Kissufim, Magen, Nir Oz, Nir Yitzhak, Nirim, Re'im, Sufa, Tze'elim, and Urim, and moshavim such as Ein HaBesor, Mivtahim, Ohad, Pri Gan, Sde Nitzan, Talmei Eliyahu, Talmei Yosef, Yated, and Yesha, together with the community settlements Avshalom, Bnei Netzarim, Dekel, Naveh, Shlomit, and Tzohar.

Gaza envelope

Because many of its communities lie within a few kilometres of the Gaza Strip, the council forms part of the "Gaza envelope" (Otef Aza) and its residents have long lived with periodic rocket and mortar fire. Several of its kibbutzim and moshavim, including Be'eri, Nir Oz, Re'im, and Kissufim, were attacked during the October 7, 2023 Hamas-led attack on Israel, suffering heavy casualties and destruction, and most of the council's population was evacuated for an extended period.
