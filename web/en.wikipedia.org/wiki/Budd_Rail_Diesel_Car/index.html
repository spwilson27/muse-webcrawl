The Budd Rail Diesel Car (RDC), also known as the Buddliner, is a self-propelled diesel multiple unit railcar. Between 1949 and 1962, 398 RDC units were built by the Budd Company of Philadelphia, and an additional 65 were built under licence in Australia. The cars were primarily adopted for passenger service in rural areas with low traffic density, or in short-haul commuter service, and were less expensive to operate than a traditional diesel locomotive-drawn train.

Design

The RDC was a stainless steel self-propelled car powered by two underfloor Detroit Diesel Series 110 six-cylinder diesel engines, each driving an axle through a hydraulic torque converter. The design drew on Budd's experience with the Pioneer Zephyr streamliner and on wartime development of the General Motors diesel engines. Cars were equipped with controls at both ends so they could be run singly or in multiple-unit trains without turning.

Variants

Budd produced several standard variants: the RDC-1, an all-passenger coach seating 90; the RDC-2, a coach-baggage combine; the RDC-3, combining baggage, railway post office and a small coach section; the RDC-4, a short baggage/RPO-only car; and the RDC-9, a trailer coach with a single engine and no driving cab, built for the Boston and Maine Railroad.

Service

RDCs served on railroads throughout North America, with the largest fleets operated by the Boston and Maine, the New York, New Haven and Hartford, and Canadian National and Canadian Pacific in Canada. They were exported or licence-built for service in Australia, Brazil, Cuba and Saudi Arabia. Many remained in commuter and regional service into the 1980s and 1990s, and some continue in excursion, inspection and short-line service today.
