Northrop YF-17

The Northrop YF-17 (nicknamed "Cobra") was a prototype lightweight fighter aircraft designed for the United States Air Force's Lightweight Fighter (LWF) program.

Development

First flown on 9 June 1974, the twin-engine YF-17 competed against the General Dynamics YF-16. Though the YF-16 was selected by the Air Force in 1975, the YF-17 design was enlarged and adapted for the U.S. Navy, becoming the McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet, one of the most widely used carrier fighters.

Design

The aircraft featured twin canted vertical tails, leading-edge extensions for high angle-of-attack manoeuvrability, and two General Electric YJ101 engines. Two prototypes were built; both are now museum exhibits.
