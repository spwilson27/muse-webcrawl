Joseph Bernard Kruskal Jr. (29 January 1928 – 19 September 2010) was an American mathematician, statistician, computer scientist and psychometrician.

Work

Kruskal is best known in computer science for Kruskal's algorithm for finding the minimum spanning tree of a graph, published in 1956. In statistics he developed, with William Wallis, the Kruskal–Wallis one-way analysis of variance, a non-parametric rank test. At Bell Laboratories he made foundational contributions to multidimensional scaling and, with Roger Shepard, to the analysis of similarity data.

Life

Kruskal studied at the University of Chicago and Princeton, where he received his PhD in 1954. He spent most of his career at Bell Laboratories. His brothers Martin and William were also notable mathematicians and statisticians.
