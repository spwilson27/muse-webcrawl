Hostages Trial

Hostages Trial
Official name: United States v. Wilhelm List, et al.
Court: Nuremberg Military Tribunal, Case No. 7
Dates: 8 July 1947 – 19 February 1948
Defendants: 12 German generals

The Hostages Trial (or, officially, The United States of America v. Wilhelm List, et al.) was held from 8 July 1947 to 19 February 1948 and was the seventh of the twelve "Subsequent Nuremberg trials" for war crimes conducted by United States authorities at Nuremberg after the main trial before the International Military Tribunal.

Background and charges

The twelve defendants were senior German generals who had served in the Balkans campaign and the occupation of Greece, Yugoslavia, Albania, and Norway. The indictment charged them with war crimes and crimes against humanity, centering on the Wehrmacht's hostage and reprisal policies: the mass execution of civilian hostages in retaliation for partisan attacks (including ratios of 50 or 100 hostages shot for each German soldier killed, as in the Kraljevo and Kragujevac massacres), the murder of prisoners of war, and the wanton destruction of cities and villages such as the "scorched earth" withdrawal from Finnmark and Lapland.

Judgment

The tribunal's judgment, delivered in February 1948, is a significant source of the law on guerrilla warfare and reprisals. It held that partisans could be lawful combatants if they met conditions such as responsible command and carrying arms openly, and that while hostage-taking and reprisal executions were not entirely beyond the law of war as it then stood, the German practices — indiscriminate, excessive, and without judicial process — were criminal. Two defendants, Wilhelm List and Maximilian von Weichs, were severed from the trial for health reasons (Weichs was later released; List was convicted in the related judgment proceedings and sentenced to life imprisonment). Of the remaining ten, two were acquitted; the others received sentences from seven years to life imprisonment. Most were released during the 1950s following clemency reviews.
