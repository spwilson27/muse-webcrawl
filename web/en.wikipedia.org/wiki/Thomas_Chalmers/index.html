Thomas Chalmers (1780–1847) was a Scottish minister, theologian, and political economist, and the leading figure in the Disruption of 1843, in which more than a third of the ministers of the Church of Scotland left to form the Free Church of Scotland, with Chalmers as its first Moderator.

Career

Educated at the University of St Andrews, Chalmers became a celebrated preacher at the Tron Church in Glasgow, where his experiment in parish-based poor relief at St John's attracted national attention: he argued that voluntary, locally administered charity could replace compulsory poor rates. He later held the chair of moral philosophy at St Andrews and the professorship of divinity at the University of Edinburgh. His Bridgewater Treatise, On the Power, Wisdom and Goodness of God as manifested in the adaptation of External Nature to the Moral and Intellectual Constitution of Man (1833), was widely read.

The Disruption

Chalmers's evangelical campaign against lay patronage in ministerial appointments culminated when the civil courts overruled the church's veto. In May 1843 he led 474 ministers out of the General Assembly; within a few years the Free Church had built hundreds of churches, schools, and a theological college, financed by Chalmers's Sustentation Fund.
