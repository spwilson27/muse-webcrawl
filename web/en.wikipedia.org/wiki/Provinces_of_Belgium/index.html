Provinces of Belgium

The ten provinces and Brussels-Capital Region.

The Kingdom of Belgium is divided into three regions — Flanders, Wallonia and the Brussels-Capital Region — of which the first two are each subdivided into five provinces. Brussels is not part of any province. The provinces are the country's second-level administrative divisions, governed by an elected provincial council, a permanent deputation, and a governor appointed by the regional government.

List

The Flemish provinces are Antwerp, East Flanders, Flemish Brabant, Limburg and West Flanders. The Walloon provinces are Hainaut, Liège, Luxembourg, Namur and Walloon Brabant. Most take their names from the historical duchies and counties of the Low Countries. Walloon Brabant and Flemish Brabant were created in 1995 when the former province of Brabant was split along the language border; its remaining part became the Brussels-Capital Region.

Functions

Belgian provinces handle matters such as provincial schools, roads, watercourses, tourism, culture and environmental permits, though successive state reforms have shifted many powers to the regions and language communities, and the provinces' future role is periodically debated.
