Conformal map

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a conformal map is a function that locally preserves angles, but not necessarily lengths. More formally, a map is conformal at a point if it preserves oriented angles between curves through that point, as well as orientation. Conformal maps preserve both angles and the shapes of infinitesimally small figures, but not necessarily their sizes or curvature.

In the complex plane, a function of a complex variable is conformal at a point if it is holomorphic (complex differentiable) there with nonzero derivative. The Riemann mapping theorem, one of the great achievements of complex analysis, states that any simply connected open subset of the complex plane that is not the whole plane can be mapped conformally and bijectively onto the open unit disk.

Complex analysis

Examples of conformal maps include the exponential function, Möbius transformations (such as inversion and translation), the Joukowsky transform used in aerodynamics to model airflow around an airfoil, and the Schwarz–Christoffel mapping, which maps the upper half-plane onto polygons. The Cauchy–Riemann equations characterise holomorphic functions in terms of their real and imaginary parts; a holomorphic function with nonzero derivative rotates and scales infinitesimal figures by the argument and modulus of the derivative.

Composing conformal maps yields conformal maps, and the inverse of a bijective conformal map is conformal, so conformal maps between domains form a rich class of transformations central to complex analysis, potential theory, and geometry.

Applications

  • Cartography: conformal map projections, such as the Mercator projection and the stereographic projection, preserve local angles and shapes, which is invaluable for navigation charts.
  • Fluid dynamics and electrostatics: solutions of Laplace's equation in a complicated domain can be found by mapping to a simpler domain.
  • Engineering: stress analysis and heat conduction problems in two dimensions.
  • Computer graphics: texture mapping and surface parameterisation with minimal angle distortion.

Riemannian geometry

On a Riemannian manifold, a conformal map is a diffeomorphism whose pullback metric is a positive scalar multiple of the original metric. In dimensions three and above, Liouville's theorem greatly restricts such maps, in contrast to the abundance of conformal maps in two dimensions.
