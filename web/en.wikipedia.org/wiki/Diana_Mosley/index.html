Diana Mosley

Diana Mosley
Born: Diana Freeman-Mitford, (1910-06-17) June 17, 1910, London, England
Died: August 11, 2003 (aged 93), Paris, France
Spouse: Bryan Guinness (m. 1929; div. 1933); Sir Oswald Mosley (m. 1936; died 1980)

Diana Mosley, Lady Mosley (née Freeman-Mitford; 17 June 1910 – 11 August 2003), was one of the Mitford sisters. She married Sir Oswald Mosley, leader of the British Union of Fascists, and was imprisoned during the Second World War under Defence Regulation 18B because of her fascist sympathies and friendship with Adolf Hitler.

Early life and marriages

Diana Mitford was the fourth daughter of Lord Redesdale. In 1929 she married Bryan Guinness, heir to the Guinness brewing fortune, with whom she had two sons; the marriage ended in divorce in 1933 after she became involved with Oswald Mosley. She married Mosley in 1936 in a secret ceremony at the Berlin home of Joseph Goebbels, with Hitler as a guest. She had first met Hitler in 1935 through her sister Unity, and remained unrepentant about her admiration for him throughout her life.

Imprisonment and later life

In 1940, shortly after the birth of her son Max, she was arrested and interned in Holloway Prison, where she remained until November 1943, when she was released on health grounds and kept under house arrest. After the war she lived largely in France, editing the literary magazine The European and writing a memoir, A Life of Contrasts (1977). She died in Paris in 2003.
