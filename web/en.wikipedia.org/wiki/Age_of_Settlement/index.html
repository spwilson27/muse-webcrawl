Age of Settlement

The Age of Settlement (Icelandic: Landnámsöld) is the period in Icelandic history, conventionally dated from about 870 to 930 CE, during which Norse settlers colonised Iceland. It is described in the early twelfth-century Landnámabók (Book of Settlements) and Ari Þorgilsson's Íslendingabók.

Settlement

Tradition credits Ingólfr Arnarson, who threw his high-seat pillars overboard and settled where they washed ashore — at Reykjavík — as the first permanent settler, around 874. Landnámabók names some 400 settlers and thousands of dependants, who claimed land around the coasts and established farms. Genetic and archaeological evidence shows the settler population drew on both Norse and Gaelic (Irish and Scottish) ancestry, including women and slaves from the British Isles; Irish monks, the papar, may have preceded the Norse.

Significance

The end of the period is marked by the founding of the Althing in 930, beginning the Icelandic Commonwealth era. Tephrochronology uses a distinctive ash layer, the "settlement layer" of about 871, to date early sites.
