Breakfast at Tiffany's (film)

Breakfast at Tiffany's
Directed by: Blake Edwards
Screenplay: George Axelrod, based on Truman Capote's 1958 novella
Starring: Audrey Hepburn, George Peppard, Patricia Neal, Mickey Rooney
Music: Henry Mancini ("Moon River")
Released: 1961 (Paramount Pictures)
Awards: Academy Awards for Best Score and Best Original Song

Breakfast at Tiffany's is a 1961 American romantic comedy film directed by Blake Edwards and written by George Axelrod, loosely based on the 1958 novella by Truman Capote. It stars Audrey Hepburn as Holly Golightly, a naïve, eccentric New York socialite and café-society girl who falls for a young writer (George Peppard) who moves into her apartment building.

Plot

Holly Golightly survives on the generosity of wealthy men while dreaming of a secure future and taking comfort in breakfast pastries eaten outside Tiffany & Co. on Fifth Avenue, where she believes nothing bad can happen. Her friendship with struggling writer Paul Varjak, himself kept by a wealthy older woman, deepens into love, complicated by Holly's plans to marry for money and by the sudden reappearance of the husband she left behind in Texas. In a rain-soaked finale, Holly accepts Paul's love and rescues her nameless cat.

Reception and legacy

The film made Hepburn's little black dress by Givenchy, worn in the opening scene, one of the most iconic images of 20th-century cinema. Henry Mancini and Johnny Mercer's "Moon River" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song. The film has also been criticized for Mickey Rooney's caricatured portrayal of the Japanese landlord Mr. Yunioshi, now widely regarded as an offensive yellowface stereotype. In 2012 the film was selected for preservation in the U.S. National Film Registry.
