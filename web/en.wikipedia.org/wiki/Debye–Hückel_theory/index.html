Debye–Hückel theory

The Debye–Hückel theory, published in 1923 by Peter Debye and Erich Hückel, explains deviations of electrolyte solutions from ideal behaviour.

The theory proposes that each ion is surrounded by an "ionic atmosphere" of oppositely charged ions, which lowers its chemical potential. Its limiting law successfully predicts mean activity coefficients in dilute solutions, depending on the square root of ionic strength.

Extensions

Later refinements accounted for finite ion size, and the related Debye–Hückel–Onsager theory describes electrolyte conductivity.
