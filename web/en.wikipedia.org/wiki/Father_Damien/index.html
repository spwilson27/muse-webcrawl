Father Damien

Saint Damien of Molokai
Born3 January 1840, Tremelo, Belgium
Died15 April 1889, Kalaupapa, Molokaʻi, Hawaii
Canonised11 October 2009, by Pope Benedict XVI
Feast10 May

Father Damien, born Jozef De Veuster (3 January 1840 – 15 April 1889), was a Roman Catholic priest from Belgium and a member of the Congregation of the Sacred Hearts of Jesus and Mary, a missionary religious institute. He is best known for his ministry, which he led from 1873 until his death in 1889, in the Kingdom of Hawaii to people with leprosy (Hansen's disease) who lived in government-mandated medical quarantine in a settlement on the Kalaupapa Peninsula of Molokaʻi.

Mission to Molokai

Damien volunteered to serve the isolated settlement, where he dressed ulcers, built homes and coffins, dug graves, and shared the pipe and meals of the patients — eventually contracting the disease himself. He continued working after his diagnosis in 1885 and died of leprosy on 15 April 1889.

Legacy

Damien was beatified in 1995 and canonised on 11 October 2009 by Pope Benedict XVI. He is honoured as a spiritual patron for people with HIV/AIDS and outcasts, and his feast day is celebrated on 10 May in Hawaii and by the Congregation of the Sacred Hearts.
