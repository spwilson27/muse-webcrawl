The Argentine football league system is the system of interconnected football leagues in Argentina, organized by the Argentine Football Association (AFA). It is a pyramid of divisions with promotion and relegation, divided between clubs directly affiliated with the AFA — mostly from Buenos Aires and its metropolitan area — and clubs indirectly affiliated through regional leagues, which compete in parallel tournaments at the lower levels.

The Primera División is the top tier of Argentine football and one of the strongest leagues in South America; its most famous clubs include River Plate and Boca Juniors, whose rivalry, the Superclásico, is among the most intense in world football. The second tier is the Primera Nacional. Below that, the system splits: metropolitan clubs play in Primera B Metropolitana, Primera C, and Primera D, while clubs from the interior compete in the Torneo Federal A and Torneo Regional Federal Amateur, with local regional leagues beneath them.

History

The AFA, founded in 1893, is the oldest football association in South America. Professionalism was introduced in 1931. The league system has been reorganized repeatedly; the Nacional championship of 1967 first integrated clubs from the interior, and the current dual structure of metropolitan and federal tournaments developed from the 1980s onward. Champions of the Primera División qualify for the Copa Libertadores, and Argentine clubs have won the Intercontinental Cup and FIFA Club World Cup on multiple occasions.

Cup competitions

The principal domestic cup is the Copa Argentina, open to clubs across the divisions. Other competitions include the Copa de la Liga Profesional, the Supercopa Argentina, and the Trofeo de Campeones.
