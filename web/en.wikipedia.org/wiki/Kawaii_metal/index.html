Kawaii metal

Kawaii metal blends the aesthetics of Japanese idol pop with heavy metal instrumentation.

Kawaii metal (Japanese: カワイイメタル), also known as idol metal or cute metal, is a music genre that combines elements of heavy metal with Japanese idol pop music. The genre emerged in Japan in the early 2010s and is characterised by the contrast between harsh metal instrumentation — downtuned guitars, double-kick drumming and occasional growled vocals — and the melodic singing, synchronised choreography and "kawaii" (cute) presentation of idol performers.

History

The genre is generally credited to Babymetal, formed in 2010 as a subunit of the idol group Sakura Gakuin. Their singles "Doki Doki Morning" (2011) and "Iine!" and the viral success of "Gimme Chocolate!!" (2014) brought the style international attention. Other acts associated with the genre include Ladybaby, Deadlift Lolita, Doll$Boxx, Band-Maid (in part) and PassCode.

Characteristics

Kawaii metal songs typically juxtapose extreme metal subgenres such as death metal, metalcore and djent with J-pop melodies, electronic dance elements and idol-style group vocals. Live shows pair headbanging and mosh pits with choreographed dance routines. The visual style draws on idol fashion, including frilled dresses and school-uniform-inspired outfits.

Reception

The genre initially divided metal audiences, with some critics dismissing it as a novelty, but Babymetal's festival appearances, chart success abroad and collaborations with established metal musicians helped legitimise the style as a distinct fusion genre.
