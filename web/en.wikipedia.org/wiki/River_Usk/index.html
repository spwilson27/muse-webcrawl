The River Usk (Welsh: Afon Wysg) rises on the northern slopes of the Black Mountain (Fan Brycheiniog) in the Brecon Beacons National Park in Wales and flows for about 78 miles (125 km) south-eastwards through Brecon, Crickhowell, Abergavenny, and the town of Usk to the Roman port of Caerleon and the city of Newport, where it enters the Severn Estuary at Uskmouth. It is one of Britain's finest salmon and wild brown trout rivers, and almost its entire length is designated a Special Area of Conservation.

Course

From its source high on Fan Brycheiniog, the Usk is impounded in the Usk Reservoir before flowing down past Sennybridge and through Brecon, where the Honddu joins. It then runs east through a broad pastoral valley beneath the Brecon Beacons, passing Crickhowell and Abergavenny (where the Gavenny joins), turns south through the town of Usk and past the Roman legionary fortress of Caerleon, and becomes tidal at Newbridge-on-Usk. At Newport it is crossed by the Newport Transporter Bridge and several road and rail bridges, past the docks to Uskmouth, where the Pill and the Ebbw estuary join it on the edge of the Newport Wetlands reserve.

Wildlife and conservation

The Usk supports Atlantic salmon, sea trout (sewin), notable brown trout, and coarse fish, together with protected species including otter, twaite and allis shad, river, brook, and sea lampreys, Atlantic stream crayfish historically, and bullhead. Pressures include agricultural runoff and, increasingly, phosphate pollution, which has been a focus of regulatory attention in Wales. The river's shad runs and its salmon stock underpin its SAC designation.

History

The Romans built the legionary fortress of Isca (Caerleon) on the Usk, the lowest convenient crossing and a tidal port. The Normans built castles along the Marches valley at Abergavenny, Usk, and elsewhere. The river powered corn and fulling mills and, at Newport, became heavily industrialised in the 19th century with docks for coal and iron exports. Telford's contemporaries and later engineers bridged it at several points; the Transporter Bridge of 1906 remains one of only eight such structures in use worldwide.

Name

"Usk" derives from a Brittonic root meaning "abounding in fish" or simply "water"; the same root gives the rivers Esk and Exe and the word whisky (via uisge beatha, "water of life").
