A British citizen is a person holding the primary class of British nationality under the British Nationality Act 1981. British citizens have the right of abode in the United Kingdom — the unrestricted right to live, work, and enter the UK — and may hold a British passport, vote in all UK elections, and stand for Parliament. Most British citizens are also citizens of the Commonwealth, and historically were citizens of the European Union until Brexit.

History

Before 1983, the single status of Citizen of the United Kingdom and Colonies (CUKC), created by the British Nationality Act 1948, spanned the UK and its colonies. The concept of "patriality" under the Immigration Act 1971 limited the right of abode to CUKCs with a close UK connection. The British Nationality Act 1981, which took effect on 1 January 1983, replaced CUKC with three categories: British citizens (with right of abode in the UK), British Dependent Territories citizens (later British Overseas Territories citizens), and British Overseas citizens. Subsequent legislation — notably in 2002 — granted most British Overseas Territories citizens full British citizenship as well.

Acquisition

  • By birth: a child born in the UK is a British citizen if at least one parent is a British citizen or is "settled" in the UK (e.g. holds indefinite leave to remain). Mere birth on UK soil is no longer sufficient.
  • By descent: a child born abroad to a British citizen parent who is not themselves a citizen "by descent" generally acquires citizenship automatically; those born to citizens by descent usually do not, subject to registration provisions.
  • By registration: various entitlements and discretionary routes exist for children born in the UK who live there for the first ten years of life, stateless children, and certain holders of other British nationality classes.
  • By naturalisation: adults may apply after meeting residence requirements (typically five years, or three if married to a British citizen), good-character requirements, English-language and "Life in the UK" tests, and an intention to live in the UK.

Other classes of British nationality

Besides British citizens, British nationality law recognizes British Overseas Territories citizens, British Overseas citizens, British subjects, British Nationals (Overseas) — the status held by many Hong Kong residents — and British protected persons. Most of these classes do not by themselves carry a right to live in the UK, though since 2021 a bespoke immigration route has allowed BN(O) status holders from Hong Kong to settle and later naturalize.

Loss and deprivation

British citizenship may be renounced voluntarily. The Home Secretary may also deprive a person of citizenship where it is conducive to the public good — for example in cases involving terrorism or serious criminality — provided the person would not be made stateless (with a wider power available for naturalised citizens in some circumstances). Dual citizenship is permitted under UK law.
