Mount Scopus (Hebrew: הַר הַצּוֹפִים, Har HaTsofim, "Mount of the Watchers"; Arabic: جبل المشارف) is a mountain ridge in northeast Jerusalem, rising 826 metres above sea level and overlooking the Old City and the Judean Desert. Since ancient times it has been a vantage point for armies approaching Jerusalem — Roman legions camped here during the siege of 70 CE — and it was from Scopus that occupying and conquering forces traditionally surveyed the city.

Institutions

The mountain hosts the Mount Scopus campus of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, whose cornerstone was laid in 1918 and which opened in 1925; its campus holds the Jewish National and University Library and an amphitheatre overlooking the desert. The original Hadassah Hospital was built here in the 1930s. The British War Cemetery, the botanical garden, and the Mormon Center (Brigham Young University Jerusalem Center) also stand on or near the ridge.

1948–1967 enclave

During the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, an attack on a medical convoy to the hospital killed 78 people, and Mount Scopus became an Israeli-held enclave within Jordanian-controlled territory, demilitarised under UN supervision. Convoys under UN escort supplied the enclave fortnightly until the Six-Day War of 1967, when Israel captured East Jerusalem and reunified access to the mountain; the university and hospital were subsequently rebuilt and expanded on the site.
