Kirtan

Kirtan is call-and-response devotional chanting.

Kirtan (Sanskrit: kīrtana, "praising, narrating") is a form of devotional singing in Hindu and Sikh traditions, in which a lead singer chants the names or stories of a deity and the congregation responds, accompanied by instruments such as the harmonium, tabla or mridangam drum, and hand cymbals (kartals). It is a central practice of bhakti (devotional) religion.

Traditions

In Bengal, the saint Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (1486–1534) popularised public congregational chanting (nagara-kirtan) of the Hare Krishna mantra, a practice later spread worldwide by the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON). Maharashtra has a long tradition of narrative kirtan performed by a solo kirtankar interspersing song with commentary. In Sikhism, shabad kirtan — the singing of hymns from the Guru Granth Sahib, set to classical ragas — is the principal form of worship music in gurdwaras.

In the West

Since the late 20th century kirtan has gained popularity in Western yoga and New Age circles, with artists such as Krishna Das, Jai Uttal, and Snatam Kaur performing call-and-response chanting at concerts and festivals.
