Nagorno-Karabakh conflict

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Nagorno-Karabakh conflict was an ethnic and territorial conflict between Armenia and Azerbaijan over the region of Nagorno-Karabakh, inhabited mostly by ethnic Armenians until 2023 but internationally recognised as part of Azerbaijan. Azerbaijan has since re-established full control over the region and most Armenians have fled, ending the de facto self-governing Republic of Artsakh.

Background

Nagorno-Karabakh, known to Armenians as Artsakh, is a mountainous region in the South Caucasus. In 1923, the Soviet government established the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast (NKAO) within the Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic, despite the region's ethnic Armenian majority. As the Soviet Union began to dissolve in the late 1980s, the Karabakh movement demanding transfer of the oblast to the Armenian SSR grew, and intercommunal violence — including pogroms at Sumgait (1988) and Baku (1990) — escalated into open warfare.

First Nagorno-Karabakh War (1988–1994)

The First Nagorno-Karabakh War was fought from 1988 to a Russian-brokered ceasefire in May 1994 (the Bishkek Protocol). Armenian forces gained control of Nagorno-Karabakh and seven surrounding Azerbaijani districts, creating a buffer zone and a corridor at Lachin linking the region to Armenia. The war left an estimated 20,000–30,000 dead and displaced up to a million people, mostly Azerbaijanis from Armenia, Karabakh and the occupied districts, and Armenians from Azerbaijan. The Republic of Artsakh (Nagorno-Karabakh Republic) was proclaimed but never recognised internationally, including by Armenia itself.

Post-war stalemate

For over two decades the conflict remained frozen, with the OSCE Minsk Group (co-chaired by Russia, the United States and France) mediating without success. Periodic escalations included the April 2016 "Four-Day War", the deadliest flare-up since 1994, in which Azerbaijan retook small amounts of territory.

Second Nagorno-Karabakh War (2020)

On 27 September 2020, heavy fighting erupted and escalated into the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War. Backed by Turkey and using modern drones, including Turkish Bayraktar TB2s and Israeli loitering munitions, Azerbaijan recaptured the southern districts and, on 8 November, the strategic city of Shusha. A Russia-brokered ceasefire statement signed on 9–10 November 2020 ended the war: Azerbaijan retained its gains, Armenia returned the remaining occupied districts, and Russian peacekeepers were deployed to the Lachin corridor and the remaining Artsakh territory. Roughly 7,000 combatants were killed.

2022–2023: blockade and Azerbaijani offensive

From December 2022, the Lachin corridor — Artsakh's only road link to Armenia — was blocked, producing a humanitarian crisis. On 19 September 2023 Azerbaijan launched a final offensive; the Artsakh authorities capitulated within 24 hours and agreed to disarm. The Republic of Artsakh announced it would dissolve on 1 January 2024. In the following days more than 100,000 ethnic Armenians — essentially the entire population — fled to Armenia, an exodus Armenia and many observers characterised as ethnic cleansing. Russian peacekeepers withdrew in 2024.

Aftermath

Peace negotiations between Armenia and Azerbaijan continued over a treaty text, border delimitation, and transport links. The conflict's resolution by force reshaped the South Caucasus, diminishing Russian influence and raising Turkey's regional role.
