William Murdoch

Born: 21 August 1754, Lugar, Ayrshire, Scotland
Died: 15 November 1839, Birmingham, England
Occupation: Engineer and inventor
Known for: Coal-gas lighting; oscillating steam engine

William Murdoch (sometimes spelled Murdock; 21 August 1754 – 15 November 1839) was a Scottish engineer and inventor employed by Boulton and Watt, best known for pioneering the commercial use of coal gas for lighting.

Early career

Murdoch was born in Lugar, Ayrshire, the son of a millwright. Hired by Boulton and Watt in 1777, he was sent to Cornwall to erect and maintain steam engines for tin and copper mines, where his mechanical ingenuity — including improvements such as the sun-and-planet gear arrangement used by the firm — made him indispensable.

Gas lighting

Experimenting with the distillation of coal, Murdoch lit his house in Redruth, Cornwall, with gas in 1792. After moving to Birmingham he demonstrated gas lighting at the Soho works, illuminating the factory in 1802, and developed systems for factories and streets that launched the gas-lighting industry in Britain.

Other work

Murdoch built a working model steam carriage in 1784, contributed to marine steam engines, and devised the oscillating cylinder engine and methods for making isinglass and iron cement. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh and is commemorated by a statue in Birmingham alongside Boulton and Watt.
