24-hour news cycle

[Image: A rolling-news control room with clocks for world time zones]

The 24-hour news cycle is the continuous production of news around the clock, created by the launch of CNN on 1 June 1980 and extended by rival channels, news websites and social media. It transformed politics and crisis communication: officials must respond within the hour, and stories rise and exhaust themselves in days or hours rather than weeks.

Effects

Scholars and practitioners debate the cycle's shallowness, its appetite for live speculation, and its pressure on verification; its institutions — the live blog, the push alert, the rolling press conference — are now the default environment of public life.
