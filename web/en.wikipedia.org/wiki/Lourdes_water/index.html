Lourdes water is water flowing from a spring in the Grotto of Massabielle at the Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes in France. According to Catholic belief associated with the Lourdes apparitions, the spring was uncovered by Saint Bernadette Soubirous on 25 February 1858, when the Virgin Mary instructed her to drink from the spring and wash in it.

History

Following Bernadette's directions, workmen and pilgrims dug into the ground at the grotto and water began to flow; the spring still runs today at up to tens of thousands of litres per day in wet seasons. Pilgrims began reporting cures after drinking or bathing in the water, and the sanctuary built baths (piscines) and rows of taps where visitors fill bottles and containers to take home.

Composition and belief

Chemical analyses have found the water to be ordinary, potable spring water with no unusual properties. The Catholic Church does not teach that the water itself has inherent curative power, but regards cures as miracles obtained through the intercession of Our Lady of Lourdes. A Medical Bureau at the sanctuary investigates claimed cures; several dozen have been officially recognised as miraculous.

Distribution

Although the sanctuary does not sell the water, it is carried worldwide by pilgrims and organisations, and imitation "Lourdes water" from unauthorised sources is sold commercially, which the sanctuary disclaims.
