The Mass Transit Railway (MTR) is a major public transport network serving Hong Kong. Operated by the MTR Corporation (MTRCL), it consists of heavy rail, light rail, and feeder bus services within Hong Kong territory, the metro system having its first passenger operation in 1979. The network includes 99 stations and about 271 kilometres of heavy rail lines, and carries on the order of five million trips per weekday across all its services. It is widely considered one of the world's most efficient and profitable metro systems, famous for its "Rail + Property" financing model.

History

The MTR was built by the government-owned Mass Transit Railway Corporation, established in 1975, in response to rapid population growth and severe road congestion. The Modified Initial System between Shek Kip Mei and Kwun Tong opened on 1 October 1979, and the Tsuen Wan and Island lines followed during the 1980s. In 2000 the corporation was partially privatised and listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, with the Hong Kong government retaining a majority stake. In 2007 the MTR merged operations with the Kowloon–Canton Railway Corporation's network, adding the East Rail, West Rail and Ma On Shan lines and the Light Rail in the northwest New Territories, in return for a 50-year service concession.

Network

Heavy-rail lines include the Kwun Tong, Tsuen Wan, Island, Tung Chung, Tseung Kwan O, East Rail, Tuen Ma, South Island, Disneyland Resort and Airport Express lines. The system connects to mainland China through the Guangzhou–Shenzhen–Hong Kong Express Rail Link (opened 2018) at West Kowloon station and through East Rail border crossings at Lo Wu and Lok Ma Chau. Fares are paid overwhelmingly with the Octopus card, the smart card the MTR helped pioneer in 1997, which is now accepted across most retail in Hong Kong.

Rail + Property model

Because the government grants MTR development rights above and around stations, the corporation funds construction partly from property profits, allowing the railway to operate profitably without direct operating subsidies. This model has been studied and imitated by transit agencies worldwide.

Overseas operations

Through subsidiaries and joint ventures, MTR operates or consults on railways outside Hong Kong, including metro lines in Beijing, Shenzhen, Hangzhou and Sydney, the Stockholm metro and commuter services in Sweden, and (until 2025) London's Elizabeth line through its MTR Elizabeth line concession.
