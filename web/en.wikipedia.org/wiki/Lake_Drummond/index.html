Lake Drummond


Cypress trees on Lake Drummond

Lake Drummond is a freshwater lake at the centre of the Great Dismal Swamp, on the border of Virginia and North Carolina in the United States. Covering about 3,100 acres (13 km²) with an average depth of only about six feet (1.8 m), it is one of only two natural freshwater lakes in Virginia, the other being Mountain Lake. The lake is named for William Drummond, governor of the Albemarle colony in the 1660s, who is said to have discovered it while lost on a hunting expedition.

Geology and ecology

The origin of the lake is debated: proposals include a peat burn, a meteorite impact, and tectonic or wind-driven formation. Its tea-coloured water is stained by tannins leached from the surrounding peat and cypress, which also make it unusually acidic and preserve submerged timber. Bald cypress hung with Spanish moss ring the shore, and the lake supports bass, bowfin and other fish, as well as abundant birdlife; it lies within the Great Dismal Swamp National Wildlife Refuge, established in 1974.

History and folklore

George Washington surveyed the swamp and promoted the Dismal Swamp Canal, completed in the early nineteenth century, which passes the lake's eastern side via the Feeder Ditch. The lake features in local legend and literature, including Thomas Moore's ballad "The Lake of the Dismal Swamp" and Harriet Beecher Stowe's novel Dred: A Tale of the Great Dismal Swamp; the swamp also sheltered communities of escaped enslaved people known as maroons.
