Orlande de Lassus (various other names: Orlando di Lasso, Roland de Lassus, Roland Delattre; c. 1532, possibly 1530 – 14 June 1594) was a composer of the late Renaissance, chief representative of the mature polyphonic style of the Franco-Flemish school, and considered to be one of the three most famous and influential musicians in Europe at the end of the 16th century (the other two being Palestrina and Victoria).

Life

Lassus was born in Mons in the Habsburg Netherlands (modern Belgium). As a boy his beautiful singing voice led to him being kidnapped three times to sing in choirs elsewhere. At about the age of twelve he left the Low Countries with Ferrante Gonzaga and went to Sicily and later to Milan and Naples. He worked in Rome, where he became maestro di cappella of St John Lateran in 1553, and by 1556 was in Antwerp, where his first works were published. That year he joined the court chapel of Duke Albrecht V of Bavaria in Munich. He became maestro di cappella there in 1563 and remained in Munich for the rest of his life, marrying Regina Wäckinger in 1558 and attaining great fame, honours from emperors and popes, and a European reputation that brought composers such as Giovanni Gabrieli to study with him.

Music and influence

Lassus was one of the most prolific composers of all time, writing over 2,000 works in almost every genre then known: more than 500 motets, over 50 masses, more than 100 Magnificats, and hundreds of madrigals, chansons, villanellas and German lieder. His style combines the smooth polyphony of the Franco-Flemish tradition with Italian expressiveness and a remarkable sensitivity to text. Among his most celebrated works are the chromatic Prophetiae Sibyllarum, the Psalmi Davidis poenitentiales (Penitential Psalms), and the Lagrime di San Pietro, composed in the last year of his life. His famous chanson "Susanne un jour" and the villanella "Matona, mia cara" remain staples of the Renaissance repertory. In his last years he suffered from melancholy, which darkened the character of his final works.
