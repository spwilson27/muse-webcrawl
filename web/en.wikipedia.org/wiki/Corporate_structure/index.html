Corporate structure

Corporate structure refers to the way a corporation is organised: the arrangement of its departments, divisions, reporting lines and governance bodies that carry out the company's operations and management. It defines how tasks are allocated, supervised and coordinated in pursuit of the organisation's aims.

Governance

At the top of most corporations sits a board of directors, elected by shareholders, which appoints the chief executive officer and other executive officers. Beneath the executives, the firm is organised into functions (finance, marketing, operations, human resources) and/or operating divisions.

Forms of structure

  • Functional structure — grouping by speciality or function.
  • Divisional structure — semi-autonomous units organised by product, market or geography.
  • Matrix structure — employees report to both a functional and a product manager.
  • Flat structure — few levels of hierarchy, common in start-ups.
  • Holding structure — a parent company controlling subsidiaries, forming a corporate group.

The choice of structure affects communication, speed of decision-making and accountability, and firms commonly restructure as they grow or change strategy.
