Les Halles

Les Halles
Location1st arrondissement, Paris, France
Former useCentral wholesale food market
CurrentWestfield Forum des Halles; Châtelet–Les Halles station

Les Halles was Paris's central fresh-food market, located in the 1st arrondissement of Paris, and gave its name to the surrounding Halles district. For centuries it was the city's wholesale market—Émile Zola called it Le Ventre de Paris ("The Belly of Paris") in his 1873 novel of the same name. The market was demolished in the 1970s and its wholesale functions transferred to Rungis, outside the city; the site was redeveloped as the Forum des Halles shopping complex above Europe's largest urban railway station, Châtelet–Les Halles, and re-landscaped in the 2010s under a vast glass-and-steel canopy.

History

A market on the site dates to 1137, when Louis VI moved Paris's market to the plain of Champeaux. In the 1850s, under Napoléon III and Baron Haussmann, the architect Victor Baltard erected a celebrated ensemble of iron-and-glass pavilions—the pavillons Baltard—that housed the market for more than a century. By the 1960s, congestion made the central market untenable; in 1969 the wholesalers moved to Rungis, and between 1971 and 1973 the Baltard pavilions were demolished amid public controversy (one pavilion was saved and re-erected at Nogent-sur-Marne, another in Yokohama, Japan). The vast excavation that followed—mocked as the trou des Halles—gave way to the underground Forum des Halles (1979) and the Jardin des Halles.

Present day

A major renovation completed in stages between 2010 and 2018 replaced the Forum's entrances with the Canopée, a curving glass canopy designed by Patrick Berger and Jacques Anziutti, and re-ordered the Nelson Mandela gardens. The district remains a transport and retail hub: Châtelet–Les Halles, served by three RER lines and five Métro lines at the adjacent Châtelet, is among the busiest stations in Europe. Nearby landmarks include the church of Saint-Eustache, the Bourse de commerce, and the Centre Pompidou.
