Psychiatrist

A psychiatrist is a physician who specialises in psychiatry, the branch of medicine devoted to the diagnosis, treatment and prevention of mental, emotional and behavioural disorders. Unlike psychologists, psychiatrists complete medical school and residency training, and are licensed to prescribe medication and perform medical procedures such as electroconvulsive therapy.

Training

In the United States, becoming a psychiatrist requires a medical degree (MD or DO), followed by a four-year residency in psychiatry; further fellowship training is available in subspecialties including child and adolescent psychiatry, geriatric psychiatry, addiction psychiatry, forensic psychiatry, consultation-liaison psychiatry and sleep medicine. Board certification is granted by the American Board of Psychiatry and Neurology. Training routes in other countries are comparable, combining medical qualification with several years of supervised psychiatric practice.

Practice

Psychiatrists assess patients using clinical interviews, mental status examinations, psychological tests and laboratory investigations, and classify disorders using manuals such as the DSM-5-TR and ICD-11. Treatment typically combines psychotropic medications — antidepressants, antipsychotics, mood stabilisers, anxiolytics and stimulants — with psychotherapy, which many psychiatrists also provide, and in some cases brain-stimulation treatments. They work in hospitals, outpatient clinics, community mental health teams and private practice, often leading multidisciplinary teams.
