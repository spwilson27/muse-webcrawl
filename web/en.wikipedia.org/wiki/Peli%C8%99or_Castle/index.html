Pelișor Castle

LocationSinaia, Prahova County, Romania
Built1899–1902
ArchitectKarel Liman
StyleArt Nouveau

Pelișor Castle is a castle in Sinaia, Romania, part of the same complex as the larger Peleș Castle. It was built between 1899 and 1902 by King Carol I as a residence for his nephew and heir, the future King Ferdinand I, and Ferdinand's wife Marie.

Architecture

Designed by the Czech architect Karel Liman, Pelișor is smaller and more intimate than Peleș. Queen Marie, a talented artist, decorated many of its rooms herself in a personal Art Nouveau style blending Byzantine and Celtic elements. Notable interiors include the Golden Bedroom, the Golden Room and Marie's study, adorned with her favourite lilies and thistles reflecting her British and Romanian loyalties.

Museum

The castle is now a museum open to visitors, displaying furniture, decorative arts and works associated with Ferdinand and Marie, including paintings by the queen herself. Together with Peleș, it forms one of Romania's most visited historic attractions, set against the forested slopes of the Bucegi Mountains.
