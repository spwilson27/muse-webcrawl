County Louth (Irish: Contae Lú) is a coastal county in the province of Leinster, Ireland, on the border with Northern Ireland. It is the smallest county in Ireland by land area, hence its nickname "the Wee County". The county town is Dundalk, and the other principal town is Drogheda, near the mouth of the River Boyne.

Geography

Louth lies between Dublin and Belfast, bounded by the Irish Sea to the east and Counties Meath, Monaghan and Armagh inland. The Cooley Peninsula in the north-east, rising to Slieve Foy, overlooks Carlingford Lough and is steeped in the mythology of the Táin Bó Cúailnge (the Cattle Raid of Cooley), in which the hero Cú Chulainn defended Ulster single-handedly. Main rivers are the Boyne, Fane and Dee.

History

The county is exceptionally rich in antiquities: the early Christian monastic site of Monasterboice, with its high crosses and round tower; the Cistercian Mellifont Abbey, founded in 1142; and the medieval walled town of Carlingford. Drogheda was besieged by Oliver Cromwell in 1649 with great loss of life, and the Battle of the Boyne (1690) was fought just south of the county, at Oldbridge in County Meath. Because of its position on the Dublin–Belfast corridor, Louth developed strong trade and industry, and Drogheda and Dundalk now function partly within the greater Dublin commuter belt.

Sport and culture

Louth GAA's footballers won All-Ireland titles in 1910, 1912 and 1957; the county colours are red and white. Dundalk FC is one of Ireland's most successful football clubs, and in 2016 became the first Irish club to win matches in the UEFA Europa League group stage.
