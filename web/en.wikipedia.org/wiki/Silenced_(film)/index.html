Silenced (Korean: 도가니, Dogani) is a 2011 South Korean crime drama film directed by Hwang Dong-hyuk, based on Gong Ji-young's novel The Crucible and on real events at the Gwangju Inhwa School for deaf children. It stars Gong Yoo as a new teacher who discovers that students are being sexually abused by faculty, and fights, with a human-rights activist played by Jung Yu-mi, to expose the crimes against institutional resistance. The film's release provoked national outrage in South Korea and led directly to legal reform.

Background and plot

Between 2000 and 2005, teachers and administrators at the Inhwa School in Gwangju sexually abused at least nine deaf students; trials ended with light sentences, several offenders returning to their jobs. In the film, art teacher Kang In-ho arrives at the school, befriends three abused children, and, aided by activist Seo Yoo-jin, brings the case to court — only to face corrupt police, prosecutors and local elites, and a verdict widely seen as lenient.

Impact: the "Dogani Law"

Seen by more than 4.6 million people in South Korea, the film triggered a public outcry. Police reopened the investigation, the school was closed, and in October 2011 the National Assembly passed revisions to the sexual-crimes statutes — popularly named the "Dogani Law" — abolishing the statute of limitations for sexual crimes against children under 13 and people with disabilities, and increasing penalties, including life imprisonment. The case is frequently cited as an example of cinema changing legislation.

Reception

Silenced was praised for its restrained direction and the performances of its child actors, and it won awards including the Audience Award at the Udine Far East Film Festival. Its director, Hwang Dong-hyuk, later created the Netflix series Squid Game.
