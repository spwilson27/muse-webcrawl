Deux-Sèvres

Deux-Sèvres (French pronunciation: [dø sɛvʁ]) is a department in western France, in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. It was one of the original 83 departments created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790, formed from parts of the former provinces of Poitou and Saintonge. Its name, "Two Sèvres", refers to the two rivers that rise in the department: the Sèvre Nantaise and the Sèvre Niortaise.

The prefecture is Niort, an important centre for the insurance and banking industries in France, home to mutual insurers such as MAIF and MAAF. Other towns include Bressuire, Thouars, and Parthenay. The department is largely rural and agricultural, known in part for the Marais Poitevin, the "Green Venice" marshland partly within its borders.

Deux-Sèvres is department number 79 in the French numbering system.
