Hitler Youth

The Hitler Youth (German: Hitlerjugend, HJ) was the youth organisation of the Nazi Party in Germany. It existed from 1922 to 1945 and, together with its girls' counterpart, the League of German Girls (Bund Deutscher Mädel, BDM), was intended to indoctrinate young Germans in Nazi ideology and prepare boys for military service.

Origins

Founded in 1922 as the Jungsturm Adolf Hitler, the organisation was re-established in 1926 as the Hitler Youth. After the Nazis came to power in 1933, rival youth groups were dissolved or absorbed, and under Reich Youth Leader Baldur von Schirach the HJ expanded enormously. The Hitler Youth Law of 1936 made it the official state youth organisation, and a 1939 decree made membership effectively compulsory for boys and girls aged 10 to 18. By 1940 membership exceeded eight million.

Organisation and activities

Boys aged 10–14 belonged to the Deutsches Jungvolk ("German Youngsters") and those 14–18 to the Hitler Youth proper; girls joined the Jungmädelbund and then the BDM. Activities combined sport, camping, hiking, and marching with ideological instruction, paramilitary drill, and weapons training for older boys. The organisation fostered loyalty to Adolf Hitler personally through oaths, songs, and rituals.

Second World War

During the war, Hitler Youth members collected materials, served as air-raid auxiliaries (Flakhelfer), and aided the war effort. In 1943 the 12th SS Panzer Division "Hitlerjugend" was formed from HJ volunteers. In the final months of 1945, boys as young as twelve were conscripted into the Volkssturm and thrown into the defence of German cities, including Berlin, suffering heavy casualties.

Aftermath

The Hitler Youth was disbanded by the Allied Control Council in October 1945 and declared a criminal-adjacent organisation in the postwar denazification process, though ordinary members were generally amnestied. Schirach was sentenced to twenty years' imprisonment at Nuremberg for crimes against humanity.
