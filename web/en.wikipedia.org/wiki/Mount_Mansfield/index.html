Mount Mansfield

Elevation: 4,395 ft (1,340 m)
Location: Chittenden and Lamoille counties, Vermont, U.S.
Range: Green Mountains
Highest point: In the U.S. state of Vermont

Mount Mansfield is the highest mountain in the U.S. state of Vermont, with a summit elevation of 4,395 feet (1,340 m) above sea level. It lies in the Green Mountains, spanning the towns of Underhill in Chittenden County and Stowe in Lamoille County. The mountain's ridge, seen from the east or west, resembles the profile of a human face lying on its back, and its features are named accordingly: from north to south, the Forehead, Nose, Chin (the highest point), and Adam's Apple.

Geography and ecology

Mount Mansfield contains Vermont's largest expanse of arctic-alpine tundra — about 200 acres — a relict plant community left from the retreat of the glaciers, home to rare species such as diapensia and Bigelow's sedge. Much of the summit ridge is protected within Mount Mansfield State Forest and the Mount Mansfield Natural Area, designated a National Natural Landmark. The mountain drains westward to the Winooski River and Lake Champlain and eastward to the Lamoille River.

Recreation

The Long Trail, America's oldest long-distance hiking trail, crosses the summit ridge. Stowe Mountain Resort operates ski lifts on the eastern (Stowe) side, with terrain on both Mansfield and neighbouring Spruce Peak; Smugglers' Notch Resort lies to the north across Smugglers Notch, a steep pass between Mansfield and the Sterling range. The Mount Mansfield Toll Road and a gondola give non-hikers access to the summit ridge in summer and autumn. Television and radio transmitters for the Burlington–Plattsburgh market occupy the summit area.

Climate

The summit has a subarctic-alpine climate with long, snowy winters; average annual snowfall exceeds 300 inches, and weather instruments on the mountain have been maintained in cooperation with the National Weather Service since the 1950s.
