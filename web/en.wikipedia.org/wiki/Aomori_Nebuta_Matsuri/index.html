Aomori Nebuta Matsuri

The Aomori Nebuta Matsuri is a Japanese summer festival held annually in Aomori, Aomori Prefecture, in early August. It is famous for its parade of enormous illuminated paper floats, called nebuta, depicting warriors, historical figures, kabuki characters and scenes from mythology. The festival is one of the best-known of the Tōhoku region's summer festivals and attracts millions of visitors.

Floats and parade

Nebuta are constructed from painted washi paper over wire and wooden frames and lit from within. Large floats are carried through the streets accompanied by dancers called haneto, who wear distinctive costumes and chant “rassera, rassera” while moving in time with flutes, drums and cymbals. On the final evening, selected floats are placed on boats and shown over the harbour with fireworks.

Origins

The festival's origins are uncertain. Popular explanations connect it with Tanabata lantern customs, with summer work in the fields, or with traditions attributed to the ninth-century general Sakanoue no Tamuramaro. Whatever its source, the modern festival developed into a major civic celebration in the twentieth century.

Recognition

Nebuta craftsmanship is also preserved outside the festival season at the Nebuta Museum Wa Rasse in Aomori, where award-winning floats are displayed. Related nebuta festivals are held in other towns in the prefecture, including Goshogawara and Hirosaki.
