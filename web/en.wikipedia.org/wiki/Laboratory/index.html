Laboratory

A laboratory (UK: /ləˈbɒrətəri/; US: /ˈlæbrətɔːri/), informally a lab, is a facility that provides controlled conditions in which scientific or technological research, experiments, and measurement may be performed. Laboratories are found in schools, universities, industry, hospitals, government agencies, and dedicated research institutes, and range from single rooms to large multi-building complexes.

Types of laboratories

Scientific laboratories include chemistry, biology, and physics laboratories, each equipped for its discipline: wet laboratories handle chemicals and biological specimens with benches, fume hoods, and sinks, while dry laboratories focus on computation and instrumentation. Medical (clinical) laboratories analyse blood, tissue, and other samples to diagnose and monitor disease. Industrial laboratories support quality control, materials testing, and product development. Other specialised facilities include forensic laboratories, computer laboratories, cleanrooms for semiconductor fabrication, and high-containment laboratories for dangerous pathogens, graded by biosafety level (BSL-1 to BSL-4).

Techniques and equipment

Common laboratory equipment includes glassware such as beakers, flasks, and test tubes; instruments such as microscopes, spectrophotometers, centrifuges, balances, and pipettes; and safety equipment such as fume hoods, safety showers, eyewash stations, and personal protective equipment. Standardised methods and careful record keeping, traditionally in laboratory notebooks, underpin reproducibility.

Safety

Laboratory safety is governed by regulations and standards enforced by bodies such as OSHA in the United States. Hazards include chemical exposure, fire, biological agents, radiation, and compressed gases. Risk assessment, standard operating procedures, and safety training are central to laboratory management.

History

The modern laboratory emerged from the alchemical workshops of the medieval period and the private workrooms of early natural philosophers. Purpose-built teaching and research laboratories spread through universities in the nineteenth century, notably with Justus von Liebig's chemistry laboratory at Giessen, and grew into the large institutional laboratories of the twentieth century.
