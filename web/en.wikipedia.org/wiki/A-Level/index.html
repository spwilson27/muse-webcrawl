The A-Level (Advanced Level) is a subject-based school-leaving qualification conferred as part of the General Certificate of Education, as well as by examination boards internationally. Introduced in England and Wales in 1951, A-Levels are normally taken over two years by students aged 16–18 in the sixth form of a school or at a further-education college, following GCSEs. They are the standard entrance qualification for British universities, assessed through UCAS tariff points.

Structure

Students typically study three or four subjects in depth. From 2000 the Curriculum 2000 reforms split the course into AS (first year) and A2 units, but reforms in England from 2015 returned A-Levels to fully linear assessment, with AS decoupled as a standalone qualification; Wales and Northern Ireland retained somewhat different structures. Scotland uses Highers and Advanced Highers instead.

Assessment and results

Examinations are run by boards including AQA, Edexcel, OCR and WJEC/Eduqas, held mainly in May–June, with results published in mid-August — a day anticipated by university admissions departments and widely covered by the media. The A* grade at A-Level was introduced in 2010 to distinguish the highest achievers. Persistent debate surrounds grade inflation, subject difficulty disparities and the narrowness of early specialisation compared with broader baccalaureate systems.
