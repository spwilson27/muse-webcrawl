Cannoli

Cannoli (Italian: [kanˈnɔːli]; singular cannolo, meaning "little tube") are Sicilian pastries consisting of tube-shaped shells of fried pastry dough, filled with a sweet, creamy filling containing ricotta — a staple of Sicilian cuisine. They range in size from cannulicchi, no bigger than a finger, to the fist-sized proportions of Piana degli Albanesi, southwest of Palermo.

History

Cannoli are traditionally associated with the area of Palermo and with the Sicilian Carnevale season, when they may originally have been a fertility symbol. The pastry's roots are often traced to the period of Arab rule in Sicily (831–1091), when fried dough confections and ricotta-based sweets were developed; a popular legend places their invention in a convent near Caltanissetta. Marsala wine is traditionally included in the shell dough, which is rolled around metal tubes and deep-fried.

Preparation

The shells are made from flour, sugar, egg yolk, and wine, fried until crisp. The filling is traditionally sheep's-milk ricotta, sweetened and often studded with candied fruit or chocolate chips; in the United States, cow's-milk ricotta or mascarpone is common. The ends may be decorated with chopped pistachios from Bronte, candied orange peel, or a cherry, and the cannoli are dusted with confectioners' sugar. To keep the shell crisp, filling is added just before serving.

In culture

Cannoli are among the most internationally recognized Italian desserts, a fixture of Italian-American bakeries. The pastry is famously referenced in the 1972 film The Godfather in the line "Leave the gun. Take the cannoli." Regional variants include the Neapolitan cannoli alla napoletana and large festival cannoli in Sicily that can weigh several kilograms.
