Global Trade Item Number

The Global Trade Item Number (GTIN) is an identifier for trade items, developed by the international organization GS1. Such identifiers are used to look up product information in a database (often by entering the number found on a barcode into a search bar or point-of-sale system). The uniqueness and universality of the identifier are what enable a product to be sold internationally without needing to be relabeled or re-identified in each country.

GTINs may be 8, 12, 13, or 14 digits long, and can be encoded in barcodes such as EAN-8, UPC (GTIN-12), EAN-13, and ITF-14 (GTIN-14 for cases). The structure consists of a GS1 company prefix, an item reference, and a check digit calculated with a modulo-10 algorithm. GTIN is a data structure within GS1 standards and is used alongside identifiers such as the Global Location Number (GLN) and Serial Shipping Container Code (SSCC).

Usage

GTINs are used throughout retail and supply chains, and are required by major online marketplaces, including Amazon and Google Shopping, to identify products in catalogs. In publishing, the ISBN functions within the GTIN system as a "Bookland" EAN with the prefixes 978 and 979.
