South Shields is a coastal town in South Tyneside, Tyne and Wear, England, at the mouth of the River Tyne opposite North Shields. Historically in County Durham, it developed as a port, shipbuilding, and coal-shipping centre and is now also known as a seaside resort with sandy beaches at Sandhaven and the Marsden Grotto.

History

The Romans built the fort of Arbeia at the eastern end of Hadrian's Wall to guard the Tyne's mouth; its reconstructed gatehouse is a local museum. The town grew rapidly in the 19th century with coal, chemicals, glass, and shipbuilding, drawing migrants including a long-established Yemeni community, among Britain's oldest Muslim communities. The author Catherine Cookson was born in South Shields, and the town is the finishing point of the Great North Run half-marathon from Newcastle.

Culture

South Shields has a tradition of fish-and-chip shops, seaside amusements, and an annual summer festival. Its football club, South Shields F.C., won the FA Vase in 2017.
