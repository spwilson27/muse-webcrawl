VX is among the most toxic synthetic chemical agents known

VX is an extremely toxic synthetic chemical compound in the organophosphorus class, specifically a thiophosphonate, and a nerve agent. It is the best known of the V-series of nerve agents and is considered one of the most dangerous chemical weapons ever developed. The United Nations classifies it as a weapon of mass destruction, and its production and stockpiling are outlawed by the Chemical Weapons Convention of 1993, under which it is a Schedule 1 substance.

History

VX was discovered in the early 1950s by chemists Ranajit Ghosh and J. F. Newman at Imperial Chemical Industries in Britain, during research into organophosphate pesticides. Its extreme toxicity to humans was quickly recognised, and the work was passed to the British military research establishment at Porton Down. The United States subsequently adopted VX for its chemical arsenal, producing it at Newport, Indiana, while the Soviet Union developed a structurally similar counterpart known as Russian VX (VR). Declared stockpiles have been destroyed under international supervision, a process completed in the United States in 2023.

Properties and effects

VX is an amber, oily liquid with very low volatility, making it a persistent contact hazard that can remain on surfaces and terrain for days or longer. Like other nerve agents, it acts by irreversibly inhibiting the enzyme acetylcholinesterase, causing acetylcholine to accumulate at synapses and producing a cholinergic crisis: miosis, salivation, convulsions, paralysis and death by respiratory failure, potentially within minutes of a lethal exposure. Treatment involves atropine and oxime reactivators such as pralidoxime, together with anticonvulsants.

Incidents

VX achieved renewed notoriety in February 2017, when it was identified by Malaysian authorities as the agent used in the assassination of Kim Jong-nam, the half-brother of North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, at Kuala Lumpur International Airport. The incident underscored proliferation concerns surrounding the agent despite the global ban.
