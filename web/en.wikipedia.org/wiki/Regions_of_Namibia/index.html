Regions of Namibia

The regions of Namibia are the first-level administrative divisions of the country. Namibia is divided into 14 regions, which are in turn subdivided into 121 constituencies. The regions are governed by regional councils, and each region is represented in the National Council, the upper house of Namibia's bicameral Parliament.

When Namibia became independent in 1990, the country inherited the administrative divisions of the South African administration. A Delimitation Commission later restructured the country, and the number of regions was increased from 13 to 14 in 2013 when the Kavango Region was split into Kavango East and Kavango West.

The Khomas Region, containing the national capital Windhoek, is the most populous region, while the southern Karas Region is the largest by area yet among the least densely populated. The Zambezi Region (renamed from Caprivi in 2013) forms a narrow panhandle in the country's northeast.
