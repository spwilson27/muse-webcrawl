Weissenhof Estate

LocationStuttgart, Germany
Built1927
OrganizerDeutscher Werkbund
Artistic directorLudwig Mies van der Rohe

The Weissenhof Estate (German: Weißenhofsiedlung) is a housing estate built for the Deutscher Werkbund exhibition in Stuttgart in 1927. It was an international showcase of what later became known as the International Style of architecture.

The estate, which was designed by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe as artistic director, comprised twenty-one buildings comprising sixty dwellings, designed by seventeen European architects, among them Le Corbusier, Walter Gropius, J.J.P. Oud, Hans Scharoun, Mart Stam, Bruno Taut, and Peter Behrens. The flat-roofed, white, cubic buildings embodied modernist principles of functional, affordable housing.

History

The exhibition, titled Die Wohnung ("The Dwelling"), opened in July 1927 and drew some 500,000 visitors. Attacked under National Socialism as degenerate "Arab village" architecture, the estate survived; several buildings were destroyed in the Second World War. Two houses by Le Corbusier were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2016 as part of "The Architectural Work of Le Corbusier"; one houses the Weissenhof Museum.
