Central Bank of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea

The Central Bank of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea is the central bank of North Korea. Established on 6 December 1947, it issues the North Korean won and, in the country's centrally planned financial system, has traditionally combined central banking with commercial and retail banking functions.

History

The bank traces its origin to banking institutions set up under the Soviet Civil Administration after 1945. In February 1946 a central bank for the northern half of Korea was organized, and following the proclamation of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea in 1948 the institution became the state's sole bank under a monobank system, in which a single state bank handles currency issue, payments, credit allocation, and savings.

Functions

The bank issues banknotes and coins of the North Korean won, administers the payments system, holds household savings deposits through its branch network, and channels credit to state enterprises in accordance with the state plan. Foreign exchange operations have historically been handled in part through separate institutions, most notably the Foreign Trade Bank of the DPRK, founded in 1959. The president of the central bank is appointed by the state and the bank operates under the direction of the Cabinet and the Ministry of Finance.

Currency

The won was revalued in 1959 and again in 1979, when new notes were exchanged at par but with limits on amounts. A further redenomination was carried out at the end of November 2009, in which old notes were exchanged at a rate of 100 to 1, a measure that provoked significant disruption in private markets before exchange limits were partially relaxed.
