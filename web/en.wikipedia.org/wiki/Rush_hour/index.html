Rush hour

A rush hour (also called peak hour) is a part of the day when traffic congestion on roads and crowds on public transport are at their highest. The term normally refers to the morning period when people travel to work or school, and the evening period when they return, though in practice each peak may last two or three hours.

Causes and characteristics

Rush hours arise because most employers and schools start and finish at similar times, concentrating travel demand. Transport systems are usually designed around these peaks, so vehicles and trains are crowded and road speeds fall sharply. The morning peak is typically shorter and sharper than the more spread-out evening peak.

Responses

Cities manage rush hour with peak pricing and congestion charges, off-peak fares, extra transit services, high-occupancy vehicle lanes, and staggered working hours. Flexible working and remote work have flattened peaks in many cities, a change that became pronounced after 2020.
