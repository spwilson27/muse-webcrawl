Flatworm

KingdomAnimalia
PhylumPlatyhelminthes
Species~29,000 described
HabitatsMarine, freshwater, damp terrestrial; many parasitic

Flatworms, phylum Platyhelminthes, are a group of soft-bodied, bilaterally symmetrical invertebrates characterised by their dorsoventrally flattened bodies. They lack a body cavity (acoelomate), a circulatory system and respiratory organs; gas exchange and internal transport occur by diffusion, which is why the body must remain thin. Most species are less than a few centimetres long, though some tapeworms reach many metres.

Classification and lifestyle

The phylum includes free-living forms — the planarians and marine polyclads traditionally grouped as Turbellaria — and three wholly parasitic groups: the flukes (Trematoda), tapeworms (Cestoda), and monogeneans (Monogenea), ectoparasites of fish. Parasitic flatworms cause major human diseases, notably schistosomiasis (blood flukes, Schistosoma), affecting over 200 million people, and tapeworm infections from undercooked meat and fish.

Anatomy and biology

Flatworms have a simple ladder-like nervous system with a small brain (cerebral ganglia) and, in free-living species, eyespots sensitive to light. The gut, when present, has a single opening serving as both mouth and anus; tapeworms have lost the gut entirely and absorb nutrients through their skin-like tegument. Most are hermaphrodites with complex reproductive systems. Planarians are famous for regeneration: a fragment can regrow an entire worm, driven by pluripotent stem cells called neoblasts, making them model organisms in regenerative biology.
