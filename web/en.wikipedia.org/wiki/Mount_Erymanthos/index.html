Mount Erymanthos

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mount Erymanthos, on the border of Achaea, Elis, and Arcadia.

Mount Erymanthos (Greek: Ερύμανθος) is a mountain range in the southern part of Achaea and the northeastern part of Elis in the western Peloponnese, Greece, also extending into Arcadia. Its highest peak, Mount Olenos, rises to about 2,224 metres (7,297 ft). The mountain gives its name to a municipality of Achaea, Erymanthos.

Mythology

In Greek mythology the mountain was sacred to the goddess Artemis and was the home of the Erymanthian Boar, a huge and savage wild boar that ravaged the region around Psophis. Capturing the boar alive was the fourth of the Twelve Labours of Heracles, who chased the beast through deep snow, drove it into a snowdrift, and carried it back to King Eurystheus at Tiryns.

Geography

The Erymanthos river, a tributary of the Alfeios, rises on the mountain's southern slopes. The range is largely forested with Greek fir and pine below bare limestone summits, and is included in the Natura 2000 network for its habitats and birdlife. Settlements at its feet include Kalentzi and Tripotama.
