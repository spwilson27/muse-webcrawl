The Allgäu is a region in southern Germany, lying mainly in the Bavarian administrative region of Swabia and extending into south-eastern Baden-Württemberg. It stretches from the pre-Alpine lands around Kempten and Memmingen southward to the Allgäu Alps on the Austrian border. The name is traditionally derived from the Alemannic Alb (mountain pasture) and Gau (district).

Geography

The landscape grades from rolling moraine hills and meadows in the north to high Alpine valleys in the south, drained by the Iller, Wertach and Lech rivers. The Oberallgäu district, around Sonthofen and the resort of Oberstdorf, is the most mountainous part. The Westallgäu, including Lindenberg and Wangen, lies partly in Baden-Württemberg; the Ostallgäu, with Füssen and Marktoberdorf, borders the Ammergau Alps and the famous castles of Neuschwanstein and Hohenschwangau just outside the region's edge.

Economy and culture

The Allgäu is one of Germany's principal dairy regions, famous for Allgäuer Emmentaler and Bergkäse cheeses made from the milk of Brown Swiss cattle grazed on alpine pastures; the autumn Viehscheid, when cattle are driven down from the mountains, remains a major folk festival. Tourism — skiing in winter, hiking in summer — and machinery and packaging industries are the other pillars of the economy. The region speaks Swabian and Allgäu Alemannic dialects rather than Bavarian proper, a legacy of Alemannic settlement.
