Stratford-upon-Avon, commonly known as just Stratford, is a market town and civil parish in the Stratford-on-Avon district, in the county of Warwickshire, England, on the River Avon, 91 miles (147 km) north-west of London, 22 miles (35 km) south-east of Birmingham, and 8 miles (13 km) south-west of Warwick. The town is a popular tourist destination owing to its status as the birthplace and burial place of the playwright and poet William Shakespeare. It receives approximately 2.5 million visitors a year.

History

The name Stratford derives from the Old English strǣt (street, i.e. Roman road) and ford — the ford of the Roman road over the Avon. The town was founded as a borough by Bishop John de Coutances of Worcester in 1196, who laid out a grid of streets and granted a market charter. Stratford developed as a market town serving the surrounding agricultural area of the Feldon. Shakespeare was born in Henley Street in 1564 and died at New Place in 1616; he is buried in the chancel of Holy Trinity Church.

In the 18th and 19th centuries Stratford was an important coaching town and later a canal and railway centre. The Stratford-upon-Avon Canal opened in 1816, and the Stratford and Moreton Tramway followed. Tourism associated with Shakespeare grew steadily after David Garrick's Shakespeare Jubilee in 1769.

Culture

Stratford is home to the Royal Shakespeare Company, whose Royal Shakespeare Theatre and Swan Theatre stand on the banks of the Avon. The Shakespeare Birthplace Trust maintains five Shakespeare family houses in and around the town, including Shakespeare's Birthplace, Anne Hathaway's Cottage, and Hall's Croft. The town has several notable buildings, including Harvard House, the Guild Chapel, and the 15th-century Clopton Bridge.

Transport

Stratford-upon-Avon railway station has services to Birmingham and London via Leamington Spa. The town is served by the A46 and A3400 roads.
