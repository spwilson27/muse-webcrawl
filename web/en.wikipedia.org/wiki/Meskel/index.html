DateMeskerem 17 (16/17 September)
CelebratesFinding of the True Cross

Meskel (Ge'ez: መስቀል, Masqal) is a Christian holiday in the Ethiopian Orthodox and Eritrean Orthodox churches that commemorates the discovery of the True Cross by the Roman empress Helena in the fourth century. Meskel occurs on Meskerem 17 in the Ethiopian calendar, which corresponds to 16 or 17 September in the Gregorian calendar (27 September in older reckoning).

Celebration

The festival is marked by the lighting of a large bonfire, known as the demera, in the evening before the main day. In Addis Ababa, tens of thousands gather in Meskel Square to watch the patriarch light the demera, around which processions of priests, deacons and choirs in colourful vestments carry crosses and chant. Tradition holds that the direction in which the demera's main beam falls predicts the coming year's fortune. The meskel daisy, which blooms at the end of the rainy season, is a symbol of the holiday.

In 2013, Meskel was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
