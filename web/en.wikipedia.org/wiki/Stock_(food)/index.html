Stock (food)

Stock, sometimes called bone broth, is a savoury cooking liquid that forms the basis of many dishes—particularly soups, stews, sauces and rice dishes. It is made by simmering animal bones, meat, seafood or vegetables in water, often with aromatics such as onion, carrot, celery and herbs (a mirepoix and bouquet garni), and then straining out the solids.

Types

  • White stock — made from raw or blanched bones, pale in colour.
  • Brown stock — made from roasted bones and vegetables, deeper in flavour and colour.
  • Chicken, veal, beef, fish and vegetable stocks — named for their principal ingredient; fish stock (fumet) simmers only briefly.
  • Dashi — the Japanese stock based on kombu and katsuobushi.

Preparation

Long, gentle simmering extracts gelatin from connective tissue, giving stock body; a well-made stock sets to a jelly when chilled. Skimming removes impurities for clarity. Concentrated stocks are reduced to a glace. Commercially, stock is sold as cubes or powders based on salt, yeast extract and flavourings.

Stock versus broth

Usage varies, but broth is conventionally the seasoned liquid in which meat is poached and which may be served as-is, while stock is an unseasoned preparation intended as an ingredient.
