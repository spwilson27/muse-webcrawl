Idris of Libya

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Idris I

Reign: 24 December 1951 – 1 September 1969

Born: 12 March 1889, Jaghbub

Died: 25 May 1983, Cairo, Egypt

Idris (Arabic: إدريس, romanized: Idrīs; 12 March 1889 – 25 May 1983), also known as King Idris I, was the head of the Senussi order and the first and only king of Libya, reigning from independence in 1951 until his overthrow in the 1969 coup d'état led by Muammar Gaddafi.

Early life

Born at Jaghbub, the religious centre of the Senussi Sufi order, Idris was the grandson of the order's founder, Muhammad ibn Ali as-Senussi. He became leader of the Senussi in 1917 and negotiated with Italy and Britain during the colonial period, spending years of exile in Egypt during the Italian occupation and the Senussi resistance associated with Omar Mukhtar.

King of Libya

After the Second World War, Libya was placed under United Nations auspices; Idris became Emir of Cyrenaica and then, on 24 December 1951, King of the United Kingdom of Libya, a federal monarchy uniting Cyrenaica, Tripolitania and Fezzan. His reign saw the discovery of oil in 1959 transform the country's economy, closer ties with Britain and the United States (including the Wheelus Air Base), and growing domestic criticism over corruption, federal inefficiency and Libya's stance during the 1967 Arab–Israeli war.

Overthrow and death

On 1 September 1969, while Idris was in Turkey for medical treatment, a group of Free Officers under Muammar Gaddafi seized power and abolished the monarchy. Idris settled in Cairo, where he died in 1983; he was buried in Medina, Saudi Arabia. His reign was reassessed sympathetically by some Libyans after the 2011 civil war, and the Senussi-era flag he had adopted was restored as Libya's national flag.
