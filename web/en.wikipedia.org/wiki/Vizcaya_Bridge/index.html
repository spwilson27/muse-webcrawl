Vizcaya Bridge

Vizcaya Bridge
Location: Portugalete / Getxo, Spain
Crosses: Nervión River (Ibaizabal)
Opened: 1893
Type: Transporter bridge

The Vizcaya Bridge (Spanish: Puente de Vizcaya, Basque: Bizkaiko Zubia) is a transporter bridge that links the towns of Portugalete and Las Arenas (part of Getxo) in the province of Biscay, Spain, crossing the mouth of the Nervión River. Locals usually call it the Puente Colgante ("hanging bridge").

Design and construction

Designed by the Basque architect Alberto de Palacio, a collaborator of Gustave Eiffel, the bridge was completed in 1893. It is the world's oldest transporter bridge still in service. Two 61-metre towers are joined by a horizontal span 160 metres long, from which a gondola is suspended; the gondola carries pedestrians and vehicles across the river in about a minute and a half, without obstructing shipping to the port of Bilbao.

History

Service was briefly interrupted during the Spanish Civil War, when the top crossbeam was blown up in 1937; the bridge was rebuilt and reopened in 1941. It continues to operate around the clock, carrying millions of passengers per year.

UNESCO World Heritage

In 2006 the bridge was declared a World Heritage Site by UNESCO, recognized as an outstanding expression of the creative use of iron in the Industrial Revolution era and as one of the most famous surviving transporter bridges.
