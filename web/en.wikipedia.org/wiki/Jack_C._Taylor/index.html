Jack Crawford Taylor (April 14, 1922 – July 2, 2016) was an American businessman and billionaire who founded the Enterprise Rent-A-Car Company. Taylor was a decorated U.S. Navy fighter pilot during World War II and a prominent philanthropist in the St. Louis region.

Early life and military service

Taylor was born in St. Louis, Missouri. He dropped out of Washington University in St. Louis to join the U.S. Navy after the attack on Pearl Harbor, serving as an F6F Hellcat fighter pilot aboard the aircraft carriers USS Essex and USS Enterprise, from which he later took the name of his company. He flew combat missions in the Pacific and was awarded two Distinguished Flying Crosses and the Navy Air Medal.

Enterprise

After the war Taylor worked as a sales manager at a Cadillac dealership in St. Louis. In 1957, he took a pay cut and a stake in a leasing business, Executive Leasing Company, which began with seven cars. Noticing customers needed cars while theirs were in the shop, he expanded into rentals targeted at the local "home city" market rather than airports — the strategy that grew Enterprise into the largest car rental company in North America. He is credited with the customer-service philosophy "Take care of your customers and employees first, and profits will follow."

Wealth and philanthropy

The family company, renamed Enterprise Holdings, remained privately held, making Taylor one of the richest people in the United States, with Forbes estimating his fortune in the billions. His son Andrew C. Taylor succeeded him as head of the business. Taylor donated extensively in St. Louis, including major gifts to Washington University, the Saint Louis Symphony Orchestra, and the Missouri Botanical Garden, and supported the creation of the Taylor Community Science Resource Center for St. Louis public schools.
