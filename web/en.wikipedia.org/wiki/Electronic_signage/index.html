Electronic signage

Electronic signage (also called digital signage or electronic signs) refers to display technologies — such as LCD, LED, plasma, and projection screens — used to show information, advertising, menus, schedules, and other content, typically managed remotely through a content management system.

Applications

Electronic signage is used in retail stores and shopping centres for advertising and brand messaging, in restaurants as digital menu boards, in airports, railway stations, and bus terminals for departure and wayfinding information, in corporate offices and hotels, and outdoors as digital billboards along roadways. Interactive touchscreen kiosks extend the concept by letting viewers query information directly.

Technology

A typical system consists of displays connected to media players, which receive scheduled content over a network from a central server or cloud service. Modern installations may incorporate audience measurement, sensors, and integration with point-of-sale or social media systems so content can adapt in near real time. Compared with printed signs, electronic signage allows rapid updating of many locations at once, animation and video, and dayparting of messages, at the cost of higher capital and energy requirements.
