NetBSD

NetBSD is a free and open-source Unix-like operating system based on the Berkeley Software Distribution (BSD). It was the first open-source BSD descendant to be formally released, predating FreeBSD. NetBSD is known for its portability: its motto is "Of course it runs NetBSD", reflecting the very large number of hardware platforms it supports, from servers and desktops to embedded systems and game consoles.

History

NetBSD grew out of the 386BSD project and the unofficial patchkit maintained by Chris Demetriou, Adam Glass, Charles Hannum, and Theo de Raadt, who wanted a freely redistributable, multi-platform BSD. The first release, NetBSD 0.8, appeared in April 1993, derived from the 386BSD 0.1 and the Net/2 release. De Raadt later left to found OpenBSD. The NetBSD Foundation, a non-profit organisation, holds the project's copyrights and trademarks.

Portability

NetBSD is officially ported to dozens of machine architectures, both little- and big-endian, and its clean, layered design makes new ports comparatively easy. It runs on systems ranging from the DEC VAX and Sun workstations to the Sega Dreamcast, and has famously been demonstrated on a toaster. This portability arises from extensive use of machine-independent drivers and a hardware abstraction approach in the kernel.

Features

The system includes the pkgsrc package management framework, which originated with NetBSD and is portable to many other Unix-like systems. Features include the rump kernel (anykernel) architecture, allowing kernel drivers to run in userspace; the Lua scripting language in the kernel; support for the ZFS filesystem in later releases; and the Xen hypervisor, for which NetBSD was an early host.

Licensing and uses

NetBSD is released under permissive BSD licences, making it attractive for embedded and commercial products where copyleft terms are undesirable; its code has been used in network appliances, routers, and research systems.
