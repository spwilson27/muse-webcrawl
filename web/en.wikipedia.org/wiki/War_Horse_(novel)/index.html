War Horse (novel)

War Horse is a British children's novel by Michael Morpurgo, first published in London in 1982 by Kaye & Ward. Set before and during the First World War, the book recounts the experiences of Joey, a horse bought by the army and sent to France, told from the horse's point of view.

Plot

Joey is a half-thoroughbred foal bought at auction by Ted Narracott, a Devon farmer, and raised by his son Albert, who becomes devoted to the horse. When war breaks out in 1914, Ted sells Joey to the British Army without Albert's knowledge. Joey is shipped to France, where he serves as a cavalry mount, is captured by the Germans and used to pull ambulances and artillery, and experiences the horror of the Western Front. Albert, underage, lies about his age to enlist and searches for Joey amid the battles. The two are eventually reunited near the end of the war.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into a highly acclaimed stage play by Nick Stafford for the National Theatre, premiering in 2007, featuring life-size horse puppets created by the Handspring Puppet Company. A film adaptation directed by Steven Spielberg was released in 2011. The success of these adaptations made the book a bestseller decades after its first publication, where it had initially sold only modestly.

Themes

The novel explores the suffering of animals and ordinary people in war, the bond between humans and horses, and the futility of conflict, and has been used widely in schools to teach children about the First World War.
