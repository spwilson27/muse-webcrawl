Value of life

The value of life is an economic value used to quantify the benefit of avoiding a fatality. In cost–benefit analysis it is usually expressed as the value of a statistical life (VSL) — not the worth of any identified person's life, but the rate at which a population trades money for small reductions in mortality risk.

Estimation

VSL is most often estimated from wage-risk studies, which measure the extra pay workers demand for riskier jobs, and from stated-preference surveys. Estimates in the United States are on the order of ten million dollars per statistical life (US regulatory agencies have used values roughly in the $7–13 million range in recent years), varying by country, income and study method.

Applications

Governments use the VSL to evaluate regulations on road safety, air pollution, workplace hazards and public health, and related measures such as the quality-adjusted life year (QALY) inform healthcare funding decisions. The practice is ethically debated, but analysts stress it prices risk reduction, not human beings.
