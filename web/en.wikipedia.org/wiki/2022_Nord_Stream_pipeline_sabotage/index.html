2022 Nord Stream pipeline sabotage

On 26 September 2022, a series of underwater explosions and subsequent natural gas leaks occurred on three of the four Nord Stream pipelines in the Baltic Sea — Nord Stream 1 and the recently completed, never-operational Nord Stream 2 — which carry natural gas from Russia to Germany. The leaks, near the Danish island of Bornholm, were widely described by governments as deliberate sabotage. The incident was one of the largest single methane releases ever recorded.

Nord Stream sabotage

Date: 26 September 2022
Location: Baltic Sea, near Bornholm, Denmark
Target: Nord Stream 1 and 2 pipelines
Damage: Three of four strings ruptured
Perpetrator: Disputed / under investigation

Events

Seismologists in Denmark and Sweden recorded explosions of magnitudes around 2.1 and 2.3 on 26 September. Pressure in the pipelines dropped sharply, and vast plumes of methane bubbled to the surface, forming disturbed sea areas up to a kilometre wide. Neither pipeline was delivering gas at the time — Nord Stream 1 flows had been halted by Russia and Nord Stream 2 had never entered service after Germany suspended its certification following the invasion of Ukraine — but both were filled with pressurised gas.

Investigations

Denmark, Sweden and Germany opened criminal investigations; Sweden and Denmark later closed theirs, concluding they lacked jurisdiction to proceed further, while the German investigation continued. In 2024 German prosecutors issued an arrest warrant for a Ukrainian diving instructor in connection with a theory that a small team had planted explosives from a chartered yacht, the Andromeda. Responsibility remained disputed: Russia blamed the United States and its allies, claims which Washington denied.

Environmental impact

Estimates of leaked gas ranged from about 100,000 to 400,000 tonnes of methane, a potent greenhouse gas, making the event one of the largest known single methane emissions.
