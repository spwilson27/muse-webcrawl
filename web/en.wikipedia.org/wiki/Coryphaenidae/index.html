Coryphaenidae is a family of ray-finned fishes containing a single genus, Coryphaena, whose members are commonly known as dolphinfishes (not to be confused with the marine mammal dolphins, or with the unrelated fish family of "dolphins" in freshwater). The family contains two living species: the common dolphinfish or mahi-mahi (Coryphaena hippurus) and the pompano dolphinfish (Coryphaena equiselis).

Description and distribution

Dolphinfishes are fast-swimming, surface-dwelling predators of tropical and subtropical oceans worldwide. They have compressed, elongated bodies, a single long dorsal fin running from the head nearly to the tail, and deeply forked caudal fins. Adult males develop a prominent bony crest on the forehead. Living fish are brilliantly coloured in iridescent blues, greens and golds, which fade rapidly after death.

Importance to humans

The mahi-mahi is a highly prized food fish and one of the world's most popular game fish, known for its speed and acrobatic fights when hooked. It grows extremely fast and has a short lifespan of about four to five years, which helps make its fisheries relatively sustainable. It is sold fresh and frozen internationally, often marketed under the Hawaiian name "mahi-mahi" to distinguish it from dolphin meat.
