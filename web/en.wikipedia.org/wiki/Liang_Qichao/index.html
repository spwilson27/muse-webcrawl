Liang Qichao (Chinese: 梁啟超; 23 February 1873 – 19 January 1929), courtesy name Zhuoru and art name Rengong, was a Chinese politician, social and political activist, journalist, and intellectual who lived during the late Qing dynasty and the early Republic of China. He was one of the most influential reformers in modern Chinese history, and his writings — which introduced Western political and philosophical ideas to a wide Chinese readership — shaped the intellectual outlook of a generation.

Early life and reform movement

Liang was born in Xinhui, Guangdong. A child prodigy, he passed the provincial examinations at a young age and became a disciple of the reformist scholar Kang Youwei. Together, Kang and Liang led the movement that culminated in the Hundred Days' Reform of 1898, an attempt by the young Guangxu Emperor to modernise China's political, educational and military institutions. When the reform was crushed by the Empress Dowager Cixi's coup, Liang fled to Japan.

Exile and journalism

In exile in Japan, Liang founded influential periodicals, including Xinmin Congbao (New People Miscellany), through which he advocated constitutional reform, nationalism and the renewal of the Chinese people. His essays on history, fiction and national character were enormously influential; his 1902 essay on the relationship between fiction and the government of the people helped elevate the status of the novel in Chinese literature. During this period he also travelled in Canada, the United States and Australia.

Later career

After the 1911 Revolution Liang returned to China and served in several governments of the Republic, including as Minister of Justice and chief of the mint, while continuing to oppose Yuan Shikai's monarchist ambitions. In his later years he withdrew from active politics to write and teach, producing major works of intellectual history such as Intellectual Trends in the Ch'ing Period. He taught at Tsinghua University and died in Beijing in 1929. His son Liang Sicheng became a renowned architect and architectural historian.
