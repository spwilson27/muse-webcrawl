Impressionism in music

Impressionism in music was a movement among various composers in Western classical music (mainly during the late 19th and early 20th centuries) whose music focuses on mood and atmosphere, "conveying the moods and emotions aroused by the subject rather than expressing a detailed musical image". The term borrowed from painting—particularly the works of Monet and other French Impressionists—was applied to music by critics, sometimes pejoratively; its most famous exponent, Claude Debussy, disliked the label.

Characteristics

Musical impressionism is characterised by:

  • Use of non-traditional scales, especially the whole-tone scale, modes, and pentatonic scales, dissolving the pull of major–minor tonality
  • Extended and unresolved harmony—ninth, eleventh and thirteenth chords used for colour rather than function, and parallel chord movement ("planing")
  • Timbre and orchestration treated as primary expressive elements, with subtle, blended instrumental colour
  • Fluid, irregular rhythm and form, avoiding strong thematic argument in favour of evanescent textures
  • Titles evoking nature, water, light and atmosphere: Debussy's La mer, Prélude à l'après-midi d'un faune, and piano pieces such as "Clair de lune" and "Reflets dans l'eau"

Composers

Claude Debussy (1862–1918) is the central figure; his Prélude à l'après-midi d'un faune (1894) is often taken as a founding work of musical modernism. Maurice Ravel shared many techniques—brilliant orchestration in Daphnis et Chloé and works like Jeux d'eau—though he is often classed separately for his neoclassical clarity. Related figures include Paul Dukas, Charles Griffes in America, Ottorino Respighi in Italy, and Frederick Delius and Cyril Scott in England. Precursors include late Liszt and the Russian nationalists; influences ranged from the gamelan music heard at the 1889 Paris Exposition to medieval chant and Spanish dance.

Legacy

Impressionist harmony and orchestration profoundly influenced film music, jazz harmony, and later minimalism and ambient music. The emphasis on sound colour over structure helped open the way to the diverse styles of twentieth-century music.
