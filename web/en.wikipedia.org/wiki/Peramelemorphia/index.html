Peramelemorphia

Kingdom: Animalia

Class: Mammalia

Infraclass: Marsupialia

Order: Peramelemorphia

Families: Peramelidae, Thylacomyidae, Chaeropodidae

Peramelemorphia is the order of marsupials that includes the bandicoots and bilbies. Members of the order are found in Australia and New Guinea. They are mostly small to medium-sized, omnivorous, ground-dwelling marsupials with long pointed snouts and compact bodies.

Characteristics

Peramelemorphs are notable for their polyprotodont dentition, their syndactylous hind feet (two fused toes used for grooming), and an unusually short gestation — as little as 12 days in some bandicoots, among the shortest of any mammal. Their forelimbs are adapted for digging for invertebrates and fungi. The pouch opens to the rear.

Classification and conservation

The order contains about 20 living species in three families: Peramelidae (true bandicoots), Thylacomyidae (bilbies, including the greater bilby) and Chaeropodidae (the pig-footed bandicoot, now extinct). Several species have suffered severe declines from habitat change and introduced predators such as foxes and cats, and conservation breeding and fenced reserves are used to protect the most threatened.
