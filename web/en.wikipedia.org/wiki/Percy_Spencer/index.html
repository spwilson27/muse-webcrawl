Percy Spencer


Born: Percy LeBaron Spencer, 9 July 1894, Howland, Maine, US
Died: 8 September 1970 (aged 76)
Known for: Inventing the microwave oven

Percy LeBaron Spencer (9 July 1894 – 8 September 1970) was an American self-taught engineer and inventor best known for inventing the microwave oven while working for the Raytheon Company.

Early life

Orphaned as a child and raised in rural Maine, Spencer left school at twelve to work in a spool mill. Fascinated by electricity, he taught himself electrical engineering while working as an electrician and later joined the US Navy, where he learned radio technology. Hired by Raytheon in the 1920s, he became one of its leading experts in radar tube design; during the Second World War his improvements to magnetron production helped Raytheon manufacture thousands of radar tubes per day.

The microwave oven

In 1945, while working near an active magnetron, Spencer noticed that a peanut bar in his pocket had melted. He experimented—confirming that microwaves popped popcorn and heated an egg (which exploded)—and built a metal box into which microwave energy was fed, cooking food quickly. Raytheon filed a patent in October 1945 and in 1947 marketed the first commercial microwave oven, the Radarange: nearly six feet tall, water-cooled, and costing several thousand dollars. Spencer received only a token $2 bonus under his employment terms, though he was later honoured with many awards and held some 300 patents.

Legacy

Spencer rose to senior vice president at Raytheon despite never graduating from high school, and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 1999.
