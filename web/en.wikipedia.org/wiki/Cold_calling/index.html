Cold calling

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cold calling is a marketing and sales technique in which a salesperson contacts individuals or businesses who have not previously expressed interest in the offered products or services. It is typically done by telephone, but also includes door-to-door visits (sometimes called "cold canvassing") and, by extension, unsolicited emails or messages sent without prior contact ("cold emailing").

Practice

Cold calls are usually made from call lists purchased from data providers or built from directories and public sources. The caller typically works from a script, aiming to qualify the prospect, arrange a meeting, or make a sale directly. Because most calls result in rejection or no answer, success rates are low, and effective cold calling is often treated as a numbers game combined with research-based targeting.

Regulation

Many countries regulate unsolicited sales calls. In the United States, the National Do Not Call Registry allows consumers to opt out of telemarketing calls, and the Telephone Consumer Protection Act restricts autodialled and prerecorded calls. The United Kingdom regulates cold calling through the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations and the Telephone Preference Service, and some sectors, such as pension and mortgage cold calling, face outright bans. Similar do-not-call schemes exist in Canada, Australia and elsewhere.

Criticism and decline

Cold calling is frequently criticised as intrusive, and the rise of caller ID, spam filtering, and consumer preference for digital channels has reduced its effectiveness. Sales organisations increasingly combine it with "warmer" approaches such as referrals, inbound marketing and social selling, or use research-based "warm calls" to prospects with an identified need.
