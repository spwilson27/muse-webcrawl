Fitzroy Island (Queensland)

Fitzroy Island is a continental island located 29 kilometres south-east of Cairns, Queensland, Australia, in the Coral Sea. Covering about 339 hectares, the island is largely protected as the Fitzroy Island National Park and is surrounded by a fringing coral reef system that forms part of the Great Barrier Reef World Heritage Area.

Geography and nature

The island is covered in tropical rainforest and open woodland, with walking trails to a summit that offers views over the reef. Its beaches include Welcome Bay and Nudey Beach, which has been ranked among Australia's best beaches. The fringing reef supports hard and soft corals, giant clams, turtles, and reef fish, making snorkelling and diving popular activities. The island is home to a turtle rehabilitation centre that treats injured sea turtles found in the region.

History

The island's Aboriginal name is Gabarra, and it was traditionally used by the Gurubana Gunggandji people. Lieutenant James Cook named the island in 1770 after Augustus FitzRoy, 3rd Duke of Grafton. During the 19th and 20th centuries the island hosted a quarantine station, a mission, a giant clam farm, and a lighthouse on nearby Little Fitzroy Island.

Today Fitzroy Island is a resort destination reached by ferry from Cairns in about 45 minutes.
