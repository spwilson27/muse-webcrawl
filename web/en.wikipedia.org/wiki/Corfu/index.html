Corfu

Corfu (Greek: Κέρκυρα, Kérkyra) is a Greek island in the Ionian Sea, off the northwestern coast of Greece and near the Albanian coast. It is the second largest of the Ionian Islands, and its principal city is also named Corfu. The island's old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

In Greek mythology Corfu is Scheria, the island of the Phaeacians in Homer's Odyssey. Founded as a Corinthian colony (Corcyra) around 734 BC, it later came under Roman, Byzantine, and — crucially — Venetian rule from 1386 to 1797, which spared it Ottoman occupation and left a strong Italian imprint on its architecture and cuisine. After periods of French and British rule (as part of the United States of the Ionian Islands), Corfu was ceded to Greece in 1864.

Old Town

The Old Town of Corfu, inscribed by UNESCO in 2007, is defended by the Old and New Fortresses and filled with Venetian, French, and British-era buildings, the Liston arcade, and the palace of St Michael and St George.

Tourism

Corfu is one of Greece's most popular tourist destinations, known for its green landscape, beaches such as Paleokastritsa, the Achilleion palace built for Empress Elisabeth of Austria, and as the birthplace of Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh (1921).
