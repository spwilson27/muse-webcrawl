The House of Komnenos (Greek: Κομνηνός, pl. Κομνηνοί) was a Byzantine Greek noble family and imperial dynasty that ruled the Byzantine Empire from 1081 to 1185, and later the Empire of Trebizond from 1204 to 1461. Under the Komnenian emperors Alexios I, John II, and Manuel I, the empire experienced a sustained military, financial, and cultural recovery known as the Komnenian restoration.

Origins

The family first appears in the late 10th century with Manuel Erotikos Komnenos, a military commander who defended Nicaea against the rebel Bardas Skleros. The family's name is traditionally connected with the village of Komne in Thrace. His grandson Isaac I Komnenos briefly seized the throne in 1057 but abdicated two years later; Isaac's nephew Alexios I Komnenos founded the dynasty proper in 1081, following the empire's catastrophic defeat by the Seljuk Turks at Manzikert in 1071.

Komnenian restoration

Alexios I stabilized the empire, reformed its finances and military, and appealed to the West for mercenaries — an appeal that inadvertently precipitated the First Crusade. His son John II continued the reconquest of Anatolian territory and campaigned in Syria, and his grandson Manuel I pursued an ambitious foreign policy in Italy, the Balkans, and the Crusader states. The dynasty in Constantinople ended when Andronikos I Komnenos was overthrown and killed in 1185. After the Fourth Crusade sacked Constantinople in 1204, Alexios and David Komnenos, grandsons of Andronikos I, founded the Empire of Trebizond on the Black Sea coast, which survived until its conquest by the Ottoman sultan Mehmed II in 1461, outliving the restored Byzantine Empire itself.
