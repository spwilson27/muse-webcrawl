B'z

OriginJapan
GenresHard rock, pop rock
Years active1988–present
MembersTak Matsumoto, Koshi Inaba

B'z (Japanese: ビーズ, Bīzu) is a Japanese rock duo consisting of guitarist Tak Matsumoto and vocalist Koshi Inaba, formed in 1988. They are the best-selling music act in Japanese history, with over 100 million records sold worldwide, and hold numerous Japanese chart records, including dozens of consecutive number-one singles and albums.

Career

B'z debuted in 1988 and broke through with early-1990s albums such as Risky and In the Life, and singles like "Taiyō no Komachi Angel" and "Easy Come, Easy Go!". Their sound shifted from dance-influenced pop rock to hard rock, and their stadium-scale tours became a fixture of the Japanese music scene. In 2007 they became the first Asian artists to place their handprints in Hollywood's RockWalk.

Legacy

The duo holds the Oricon records for most number-one singles consecutively, most million-selling albums, and total sales. In 2025 they continued to top the charts decades into their career, a longevity unmatched in Japanese popular music.
