Fort Moore

Fort Moore
Location: near Columbus, Georgia, U.S.
Established: 1918 (as Camp Benning)
Known as: Fort Benning (1919–2023; restored 2025)
Role: Maneuver Center of Excellence

Fort Moore is a United States Army post near Columbus, Georgia, straddling the Georgia–Alabama state line along the Chattahoochee River. Established in 1918 as Camp Benning, it was known for most of its history as Fort Benning, after Confederate general Henry L. Benning. In May 2023, following the recommendations of the federal Naming Commission, it was redesignated Fort Moore in honour of Lieutenant General Hal Moore and his wife Julia Compton Moore; in 2025 the name Fort Benning was restored, officially re-dedicated to a different soldier of the same surname, Corporal Fred G. Benning. The post is the home of the US Army Maneuver Center of Excellence and the US Army Infantry School.

History and role

The installation covers about 182,000 acres, making it one of the largest US Army posts. Since the First World War it has been the centre of American infantry training; the Infantry School, the Armor School (moved there in the 2010 BRAC realignment), the US Army Ranger School, and airborne training — recognisable by its 250-foot jump towers — are all based at the post. Basic combat training and one-station unit training prepare tens of thousands of soldiers each year. The National Infantry Museum sits just outside the main gate.

The Moore name

Hal Moore commanded the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry at the Battle of Ia Drang in 1965, the first major battle of the Vietnam War, and co-authored the memoir We Were Soldiers Once... and Young. Julia Moore was recognised for her work reforming how the Army notifies families of casualties. The 2023 redesignation made the post unusual in honouring both a soldier and a spouse.
