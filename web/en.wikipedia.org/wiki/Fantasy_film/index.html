Fantasy film

Fantasy films are films that belong to the fantasy genre with fantastic themes, usually involving magic, supernatural events, mythology, folklore, or exotic fantasy worlds. The genre is considered a form of speculative fiction alongside science fiction films and horror films, although the genres do overlap.

Characteristics

Fantasy films often feature elements of magic, myth, wonder, escapism, and the extraordinary. Common subgenres include high fantasy, sword and sorcery, contemporary fantasy, and fairy tale films. Unlike science fiction, which typically grounds its wonders in some form of scientific or technological explanation, fantasy relies on supernatural forces.

History

The genre has roots in the earliest days of cinema: Georges Méliès, a pioneer of special effects, made numerous trick films with fantastical elements in the late 1890s and early 1900s. Major silent-era fantasy films included The Thief of Bagdad (1924). Sound-era landmarks include The Wizard of Oz and Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (both 1939).

The genre grew in the 1980s with films such as Conan the Barbarian (1982), The Princess Bride (1987), and Willow (1988), and reached unprecedented commercial heights in the 2000s with Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings trilogy and the Harry Potter series, among the highest-grossing film franchises of all time.

Subgenres

Subgenres include high fantasy set in fully imagined worlds, sword and sorcery centred on heroic warriors, dark fantasy blending fantasy with horror, contemporary and urban fantasy set in the modern world, and bang fantasy. Animated fantasy is a significant category dominated historically by Disney.
