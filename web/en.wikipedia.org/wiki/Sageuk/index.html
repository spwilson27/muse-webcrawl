Sageuk

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sageuk (Korean: 사극) in Korean denotes a genre of film and television drama that is set in historical periods of Korea. Literally "historical drama", sageuk typically draw on Korean history from the Three Kingdoms period through the Joseon dynasty and the early twentieth century, featuring period costume, court politics, warfare and formal, archaic speech.

Characteristics

Sageuk range from faithful dramatisations of documented events to "fusion sageuk", which mix historical settings with romance, martial arts and fictional protagonists. Long-running epics of 50 or more episodes traditionally aired on Korean broadcasters KBS, MBC and SBS, while shorter series have proliferated on cable and streaming services. Recurring subjects include kings such as Sejong the Great and Jeongjo, court intrigues, factional strife, and folk heroes.

Notable examples

Internationally acclaimed sageuk include the television series Dae Jang Geum (Jewel in the Palace, 2003), Jumong (2006), Queen Seondeok (2009), Dong Yi (2010) and Kingdom (2019), a Netflix fusion of Joseon politics and zombie horror. In cinema, films such as The King and the Clown (2005), Masquerade (2012) and The Admiral: Roaring Currents (2014) — the highest-grossing Korean film of its era — belong to the genre. Sageuk have been a major vehicle of the Korean Wave (Hallyu), exporting Korean history and culture worldwide.
