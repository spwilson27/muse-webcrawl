The Parapsychological Association (PA) is an international professional organisation of scientists and scholars engaged in the study of psi (or "paranormal") phenomena such as telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition, psychokinesis and survival of bodily death. It was founded in 1957, largely through the efforts of J. B. Rhine and his colleagues at the Duke University parapsychology laboratory, as a learned society for the field of parapsychology.

History and activities

In 1969 the Association was admitted as an affiliated organisation of the American Association for the Advancement of Science (AAAS), a decision that attracted controversy and prominent support from anthropologist Margaret Mead. The PA holds annual conventions, publishes the peer-reviewed Journal of Parapsychology and other proceedings, and issues a code of professional ethics and methodological guidelines for researchers. Membership is divided into full members, associates and affiliates, and the organisation counts members in roughly three dozen countries.

Criticism and standing

Parapsychology remains outside the scientific mainstream: critics argue that decades of research have not produced a repeatable demonstration of psi, and attribute positive results to methodological flaws and publication bias. The PA itself states that it takes no corporate position on the reality of psi phenomena, presenting itself as a forum for scientific inquiry.
