One-way mirror

A one-way mirror, also called a two-way mirror (or one-way glass, half-silvered mirror, and semi-transparent mirror), is a reciprocal mirror that appears reflective on one side and transparent on the other.

Principle

The glass is coated with a thin, nearly transparent layer of metal. The effect is not inherent in the glass but depends on lighting: the perception of one-way transmission is achieved when the mirror side is brightly lit and the observation side is kept dark. People on the bright side see their own reflection; people on the dark side see through, as through a tinted window. If the lighting is reversed, the effect reverses, since the mirror is optically reciprocal.

Uses

One-way mirrors are widely used in interrogation rooms and police line-up rooms, in psychological observation and market research facilities, in some retail and security installations, and in teleprompters and optical experiments. Historically they were also fitted to some vehicles and buildings for privacy, though the effect fails when light levels are reversed.

Construction

For durability the reflective coating may be applied to the inner surface of an acrylic or glass panel, or protected between layers. The coating must be thin enough to transmit a portion of incident light — a true opaque mirror would transmit none.
