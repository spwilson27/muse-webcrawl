River Taff

SourceBrecon Beacons, Powys
MouthCardiff Bay
Lengthabout 67 km (42 mi)
CountriesWales

The River Taff (Welsh: Afon Taf) is a river in South Wales. It rises as two rivers in the Brecon Beacons, the Taf Fechan (Little Taff) and the Taf Fawr (Great Taff), and joins at Cefn-coed-y-cymmer in Merthyr Tydfil. It flows south through Pontypridd and the Welsh capital, Cardiff, before discharging into Cardiff Bay.

Course

The Taf Fawr rises below Corn Du and Pen y Fan, the highest peaks in southern Britain, and flows through a series of reservoirs. The Taf Fechan rises south of Pen y Fan and joins its sister river at Cefn-coed-y-cymmer. From Merthyr the river runs through the South Wales Valleys past Aberfan, Treharris, Pontypridd and the Cardiff suburbs of Radyr and Llandaff, finishing at the Cardiff Bay barrage, built in the 1990s to create a permanent freshwater lake at the river's mouth.

History

The valley was central to the Industrial Revolution: ironworks at Merthyr Tydfil and coal from the Rhondda and Cynon valleys were carried down the Taff to the docks at Cardiff, once the world's busiest coal port. The Taff Vale Railway and the Glamorganshire Canal followed the river's course.

Wildlife

Once heavily polluted by industry, the Taff has recovered dramatically and now supports salmon, sea trout, otters and kingfishers. The Taff Trail, a long-distance walking and cycling route, follows the river from Brecon to Cardiff Bay.
