Fourth Chinese domination of Vietnam

The Fourth Chinese domination of Vietnam (1407–1427) was a period of twenty years during which Vietnam was ruled by the Ming dynasty of China. It followed the Ming invasion of 1406–1407, which defeated the Hồ dynasty, and ended with the victory of the Lam Sơn uprising led by Lê Lợi, who founded the Later Lê dynasty.

Vietnam under Ming occupation, administered as the province of Jiaozhi

Background

In 1400 Hồ Quý Ly seized the Vietnamese throne from the Trần dynasty. Claiming to restore the Trần, the Yongle Emperor of Ming China invaded Đại Việt (as Vietnam was then known) in 1406. The Hồ regime collapsed quickly, and in 1407 the Ming annexed the country outright, reviving the old provincial name Jiaozhi (Giao Chỉ).

Ming rule

Ming administration was harsh and assimilationist. Vietnamese books were confiscated and destroyed or sent to China, traditional customs were suppressed, and the population was subjected to heavy taxation, forced labour and the exploitation of resources such as gold, pearls and timber. Chinese officials and settlers were installed throughout the country, and Vietnamese were required to adopt Chinese dress and customs.

Resistance and liberation

Resistance was continuous. Early revolts under Trần loyalists were defeated, but in 1418 Lê Lợi raised the banner of revolt in Lam Sơn (in modern Thanh Hóa province). Aided by the strategist Nguyễn Trãi, his forces waged a ten-year guerrilla and conventional war, culminating in victories at Tốt Động–Chúc Động (1426) and Chi Lăng–Xương Giang (1427). The Ming garrison at Đông Quan (Hanoi) surrendered and was allowed to withdraw, and in 1428 Lê Lợi ascended the throne, restoring Vietnamese independence.

Legacy

The fourth domination is remembered in Vietnam as a period of cultural endangerment, during which many Vietnamese texts were lost, but also of heroic resistance. Nguyễn Trãi's Bình Ngô đại cáo ("Great Proclamation upon the Pacification of the Wu"), written in 1428, is regarded as a second declaration of Vietnamese independence.
