Dual graph

In graph theory, a dual graph of a plane graph is a graph that has a vertex for each face of the original graph, and an edge wherever two faces share an edge in the original. Dual graphs are central to planar graph theory: a graph is planar if and only if its dual can be defined consistently, and properties such as colourings, cuts and flows correspond between a planar graph and its dual.

Properties

The dual of a dual graph recovers the original graph when it is connected. Self-dual graphs and polyhedra, such as the tetrahedron and the square pyramid, are equal to their own duals. For a planar bridge, the dual edge is a loop; for a loop, the dual is a bridge. The concept extends to graphs embedded on other surfaces and to matroid duality.

Examples

The cube and octahedron are dual polyhedra, as are the dodecahedron and icosahedron.
