Lusheng

ClassificationFree-reed mouth organ
RegionSouthwestern China (Guizhou, Guangxi, Yunnan), Laos, Vietnam
PeoplesMiao (Hmong), Dong, Yi, Yao
RelatedSheng, khaen, bawu

The lusheng (Chinese: 芦笙; pinyin: lú shēng) is a mouth organ with multiple bamboo pipes fitted with free reeds, played by ethnic minority peoples of southwestern China — principally the Miao (Hmong), Dong, Yi and Yao — and by related groups in Laos and Vietnam. Each pipe has a reed cut into its base and passes through a wooden wind chest into which the player blows.

Construction

A lusheng typically has five or six pipes of graduated length bundled together, the longest of which can exceed the height of the player in the giant festival instruments of Guizhou. The pipes are bound with cord and lacquered. Because each pipe sounds continuously while air passes, the instrument produces droning chords and melodies suited to outdoor festival playing.

Cultural role

The lusheng is central to Miao festivals, courtship gatherings, funerals and New Year celebrations. Large lusheng festivals in Guizhou draw thousands of players who dance in formation while playing. In Miao tradition the instrument is said to allow communication with the spirit world, and its melodies encode phrases of the language. Related instruments include the Chinese sheng and the Lao khaen.
