Pozzuoli

Pozzuoli is a city and comune of the Metropolitan City of Naples, in the Italian region of Campania. Founded by Greek colonists as Dicaearchia around 530 BC and later the Roman port of Puteoli, it lies in the volcanic Phlegraean Fields and is famous for its Roman ruins and the slow ground movements known as bradyseism.

History

As Puteoli, the city was Rome's principal port for trade with the eastern Mediterranean until the harbour of Ostia was developed; grain ships from Alexandria landed here, and the Apostle Paul is said to have landed at Puteoli on his way to Rome. Surviving antiquities include the Flavian Amphitheatre, one of the largest in Italy, the macellum (market building) known as the Temple of Serapis, and the Rione Terra acropolis.

Bradyseism

Pozzuoli sits within the Campi Flegrei caldera, where the ground slowly rises and falls over years — a phenomenon called bradyseism. The columns of the macellum, bored by marine molluscs up to a height of several metres, famously recorded past subsidence and uplift and were used by Charles Lyell as evidence for gradual geological change. Uplift crises in 1969–1972 and 1982–1984 forced evacuations of the old town.

Volcanic features

Nearby are the Solfatara crater, with its fumaroles and mud pools, and Lake Avernus, a crater lake associated in Roman myth with the entrance to the underworld. The volcanic ash of the region, pozzolana, was the key ingredient of Roman concrete.
