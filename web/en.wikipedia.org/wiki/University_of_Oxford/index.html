The University of Oxford is a collegiate research university in Oxford, England. There is evidence of teaching as early as 1096, making it the oldest university in the English-speaking world and the world's second-oldest university in continuous operation, after Bologna.

History

The university grew rapidly from 1167, when Henry II banned English students from attending the University of Paris. The first colleges — University College (1249), Balliol (1263), and Merton (1264) — were founded in the 13th century. Oxford educated many notable alumni, including numerous British prime ministers, Nobel laureates, and heads of state and government worldwide.

Structure and teaching

Oxford is a federation of 39 self-governing colleges and a central university. Teaching for undergraduates is centred on the tutorial, in which students meet weekly with a tutor, often in pairs, to discuss an essay — a system shared with Cambridge, its ancient rival. The Bodleian Library, founded in 1602, is one of Europe's oldest libraries and a legal deposit library for the United Kingdom.

Admissions and rankings

Admission is highly competitive. Oxford consistently ranks at or near the top of global university rankings and is a member of the Russell Group and the League of European Research Universities.
