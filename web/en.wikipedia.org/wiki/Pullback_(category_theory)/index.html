Pullback (category theory)

In category theory, a pullback (also called a fiber product, fibre product, fibered product or Cartesian square) is the limit of a diagram consisting of two morphisms f : X → Z and g : Y → Z with a common codomain. Intuitively, the pullback glues X and Y together over the part where their images in Z coincide.

Universal property

The pullback is an object P with projections p₁ : P → X and p₂ : P → Y such that f ∘ p₁ = g ∘ p₂, and universal with this property: any other object with projections making the square commute factors uniquely through P. Pullbacks are unique up to unique isomorphism.

Examples

In the category of sets, the pullback of f and g is {(x, y) ∈ X × Y : f(x) = g(y)} with the coordinate projections. Special cases include inverse images (preimages) of a subset, intersections of subobjects, kernels and equalizers, and — in algebraic geometry — fibre products of schemes, central to base change. The dual notion is the pushout. A morphism is stable under pullback along a class of maps when those pullbacks exist and preserve its property; this underlies the theory of fibrations. Pullbacks need not exist in every category, but they do in all complete categories such as sets, groups, rings and topological spaces.
