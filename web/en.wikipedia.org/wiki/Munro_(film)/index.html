Munro (film)

Munro
Directed by: Gene Deitch
Story by: Jules Feiffer
Produced by: William L. Snyder
Release: 1960
Running time: 9 minutes

Munro is a 1960 American animated short film directed by Gene Deitch, written from a story by Jules Feiffer, and produced by William L. Snyder's Rembrandt Films in Prague. It won the Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film at the 33rd Academy Awards — generally cited as the first cartoon produced outside the United States to win the award.

Plot

Munro is a four-year-old boy who, through a bureaucratic blunder, is drafted into the United States Army despite constantly pointing out that he is only four. The adults around him ignore his protests until his case finally attracts public attention, at which point the Army, embarrassed, discharges him. The satire targets military red tape and adult refusal to listen to children.

Production and legacy

Feiffer adapted his own story, first published in Passionella and Other Stories (1959), and narrated the film. Made in communist Czechoslovakia by Deitch's expatriate unit — a fact that raised eyebrows during the Cold War — Munro is preserved in the Academy Film Archive and regarded as a landmark of literate, minimalist UPA-style animation.
