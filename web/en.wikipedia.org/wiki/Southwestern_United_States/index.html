The Southwestern United States, also known as the American Southwest, is a geographic and cultural region of the United States centred on Arizona and New Mexico and, by broader definitions, extending into parts of California, Colorado, Nevada, Oklahoma, Texas and Utah. It is characterised by arid and semi-arid landscapes, a strong overlay of Native American and Spanish colonial heritage, and distinctive cuisine, architecture and art.

Geography

The region spans the Sonoran, Mojave and Chihuahuan deserts, the Colorado Plateau with its canyons and mesas (including the Grand Canyon), and high mountain ranges such as the San Francisco Peaks and the Sangre de Cristo. Major rivers include the Colorado, Rio Grande and Gila. The climate is hot and dry in the lowlands, with a summer monsoon season; water supply — governed by the Colorado River Compact and long-running disputes — is a defining political issue.

History and culture

Ancestral Pueblo peoples built the great cliff dwellings and pueblos of Mesa Verde and Chaco Canyon; their descendants, the Pueblo peoples, together with the Navajo, Apache and other nations, remain central to the region. Spain colonised the area from 1598 (Santa Fe was founded in 1610); it passed to Mexico in 1821 and largely to the United States after the Mexican–American War (1848) and the Gadsden Purchase (1854). Southwestern culture blends Indigenous, Hispanic and Anglo traditions in adobe and Pueblo Revival architecture, New Mexican cuisine based on red and green chile, and a celebrated tradition of landscape art from the Taos Society of Artists to Georgia O'Keeffe.
