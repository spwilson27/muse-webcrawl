Jacksonville–Duval County merger

The Jacksonville–Duval County merger, effective 1 October 1968, consolidated the city government of Jacksonville, Florida, with that of Duval County, creating one of the largest city-county governments in the United States and making Jacksonville, by land area, the largest city in the contiguous United States at about 747 square miles.

Background

By the 1960s Jacksonville and Duval County suffered from corruption scandals, indicted officials, failing schools that lost accreditation, and suburban growth beyond the reach of city services. A state-appointed study commission drafted a consolidation charter, and voters approved the merger in a 1967 referendum by a large margin.

Structure and effects

The consolidated government has a strong mayor and a 19-member city council. The small municipalities of Jacksonville Beach, Neptune Beach, Atlantic Beach, and Baldwin chose not to merge and remain independent, though their residents vote in county-wide elections. Consolidation is generally credited with modernising services, funding infrastructure, and giving the city a stronger tax base and national profile.
