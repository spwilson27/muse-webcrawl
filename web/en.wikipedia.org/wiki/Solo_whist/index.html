Solo whist


Players: 4
Cards: 52-card pack
Type: Trick-taking, with bidding

Solo whist is a trick-taking card game for four players, a derivative of whist that originated in Flanders and became popular in Britain in the 19th and 20th centuries. Unlike whist, partnerships are not fixed: they are formed by bidding on each hand.

Play

Each player receives thirteen cards. Players bid in turn for contracts such as proposal and acceptance ("prop and cop"), in which a bidder invites a partner for a shared contract; solo, a lone bid to take five tricks; misère, to lose every trick; and abondance, to take nine tricks alone. Higher bids override lower ones, and the successful bidder (or pair) plays against the others.

Scoring

Contracts carry different values, traditionally settled with chips or points, with overtricks and honours sometimes counting in older rules. The game's shifting alliances give it a distinctive character among whist-family games.
