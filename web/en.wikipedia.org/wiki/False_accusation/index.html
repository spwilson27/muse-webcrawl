False accusation

A false accusation is a claim or allegation of wrongdoing that is untrue and/or otherwise unsupported by facts. False accusations can arise from deliberate deception, mistaken identity, misremembering, suggestion, or mental illness, and may lead to wrongful arrest or conviction.

False accusation
Related conceptsWrongful conviction, defamation, perjury
CausesMalice, error, coercion, false memory

Types

False accusations occur in contexts including crime reports, child custody disputes, workplace complaints and historical persecutions such as witch trials. They may be made knowingly — which can constitute crimes such as perjury or filing a false report and civil wrongs such as defamation and malicious prosecution — or in good faith error.

Consequences

The falsely accused may suffer imprisonment, reputational destruction, loss of employment and psychological harm. DNA evidence has exonerated many wrongfully convicted people, documented by organisations such as the Innocence Project, with eyewitness misidentification a leading contributing factor.

Prevalence and research

Estimates of false reporting vary widely by offence type and methodology and are contested; researchers stress both the harm of false claims and the far larger problem of under-reporting of genuine offences.
