Geology of Mars

The geology of Mars is the scientific study of the surface, crust, and interior of the planet Mars. It emphasises the composition, structure, history, and physical processes that have shaped the planet. The field is analogous to terrestrial geology. In planetary science, the term geology is used in its broadest sense to mean the study of the solid parts of planets and moons; the study of Mars specifically is sometimes called areology, from the Greek word for Mars, Ares.

Surface features

Mars displays a striking hemispheric dichotomy: the northern hemisphere consists of smooth lowland plains, while the southern hemisphere is heavily cratered highland terrain. The planet hosts Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the solar system, rising about 22 km above the surrounding plains, and Valles Marineris, a vast canyon system over 4,000 km long. Other major features include the Tharsis volcanic province, the Hellas impact basin, and polar ice caps of water and carbon dioxide ice.

Geological history

Martian history is divided into the Pre-Noachian, Noachian, Hesperian, and Amazonian periods. The Noachian saw intense bombardment, valley network formation, and evidence of liquid water on the surface. Activity declined through the Hesperian and Amazonian, with volcanism, glacial processes, and wind erosion shaping the modern landscape. Orbiters and rovers have identified minerals such as hematite, clays, and sulfates that point to past aqueous environments.

Interior and composition

Data from NASA's InSight lander indicate a differentiated interior with a liquid iron-alloy core roughly 1,830 km in radius, a rocky mantle, and a crust varying in thickness. The surface is dominated by basaltic rocks and iron-rich dust that gives the planet its red colour. Unlike Earth, Mars shows no evidence of active plate tectonics.
