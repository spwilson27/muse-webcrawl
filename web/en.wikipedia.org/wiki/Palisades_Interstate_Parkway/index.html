The Palisades Interstate Parkway (PIP) is a 37.02-mile (59.58 km) limited-access highway in the U.S. states of New Jersey and New York. It runs from the George Washington Bridge in Fort Lee, New Jersey, north to the Bear Mountain Bridge at the border of Orange and Rockland counties in New York, where it connects with the Bear Mountain State Parkway and US 9W/US 202.

Route description

The parkway travels along the top of the New Jersey Palisades, the cliffs overlooking the Hudson River, through a string of parklands administered by the Palisades Interstate Park Commission. It is known for its scenic overlooks, stone-arch overpasses, and a prohibition on commercial vehicles. The median is wide and wooded, and the rustic stone bridges were designed to blend with the park setting.

History

The idea for the parkway grew out of efforts by the Palisades Interstate Park Commission, led by George W. Perkins and supported by John D. Rockefeller Jr., to preserve the Palisades from quarrying. Rockefeller donated much of the clifftop land. Construction began in the 1940s after wartime delays, under the direction of engineer Gilmore Clarke and, later, Robert Moses's influence on the New York end. The New Jersey section opened in stages beginning in 1947, and the full route to Bear Mountain was completed by 1958.

Exit list

Major interchanges include the George Washington Bridge (I-95/US 1-9/US 46), NJ Route 4 vicinity access, Alpine and Englewood Cliffs exits, I-287/NY 59 near Nanuet, I-87 (New York State Thruway) near West Nyack, and NY 17/US 202 near Bear Mountain.
