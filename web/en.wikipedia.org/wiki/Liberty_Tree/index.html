Liberty Tree

The Liberty Tree was a famous elm tree that stood on Boston Common — near the corner of what is now Essex and Washington Streets in Boston, Massachusetts — in the years before the American Revolution. It became the rallying point for colonists protesting British taxation.

History

In 1765, the newly formed Sons of Liberty staged the first public protest against the Stamp Act beneath the elm, hanging effigies of stamp officials from its branches. The tree thereafter hosted meetings, celebrations, and displays of protest banners and lanterns. During the Siege of Boston, British loyalists cut the tree down in 1775, an act colonists mourned as a symbol of tyranny.

Legacy

"Liberty trees" and liberty poles were planted in towns throughout the colonies, and the symbol spread to revolutionary France. In 1964, the site in Boston was marked with a commemorative plaque and bas-relief, and the Liberty Tree remains an enduring emblem of American protest.
