ASHRAE is an American professional association for heating, ventilation, air conditioning and refrigeration (HVAC&R). Founded in 1894, it has more than 50,000 members worldwide and is best known for the technical standards and guidelines that underpin building codes internationally.

History

The society began as the American Society of Heating and Ventilating Engineers (ASHVE) in New York in 1894. In 1959 it merged with the American Society of Refrigerating Engineers (ASRE, founded 1904) to form ASHRAE. Its global headquarters, long in Atlanta, moved to a renovated net-zero-energy building in Peachtree Corners, Georgia, in 2020.

Standards

ASHRAE standards widely incorporated into law include Standard 90.1 (energy efficiency in buildings), Standard 62.1 (ventilation for acceptable indoor air quality), Standard 55 (thermal comfort), Standard 15 (refrigeration safety) and Standard 34 (refrigerant designations, source of the R-numbers such as R-134a). During the COVID-19 pandemic its epidemic task force guidance on ventilation and airborne infection control was widely cited; Standard 241, published in 2023, addressed control of infectious aerosols.

Publications and research

ASHRAE publishes the monthly ASHRAE Journal, the four-volume ASHRAE Handbook, and funds research through member contributions, historically several million dollars per year.
