Native Hawaiians

Native Hawaiians (Hawaiian: kānaka ʻōiwi, kānaka maoli) are the Indigenous Polynesian people of the Hawaiian Islands. Their ancestors voyaged from the central Pacific, settling the archipelago many centuries before European contact in 1778. The Native Hawaiian language, ʻōlelo Hawaiʻi, and cultural practices such as hula, surfing and traditional navigation remain central to their identity.

History

Following the unification of the islands under Kamehameha I and the establishment of the Kingdom of Hawaii, the Native Hawaiian population declined drastically from introduced diseases. The overthrow of the monarchy in 1893 and annexation by the United States in 1898 led to long-running movements for Native Hawaiian sovereignty and federal recognition. The Hawaiian Homes Commission Act of 1920 set aside lands for Native Hawaiian homesteads.

Demographics

Native Hawaiians live throughout Hawaii and the continental United States, with significant populations in California, Nevada and other western states. They are among the largest Pacific Islander groups in the United States.
