Midas

Midas (Greek: Μίδας) is the name of one of the kings of Phrygia in Greek mythology, famous for his ability to turn everything he touched into gold — the "Midas touch" or "golden touch". The historical King Midas of Phrygia ruled in the late 8th century BC and is a separate, if related, figure from the mythological character.

The golden touch

In the myth retold by Ovid in the Metamorphoses, Midas showed hospitality to the wandering Silenus, companion of the god Dionysus. In gratitude Dionysus offered Midas any wish; the king asked that everything he touched turn to gold. At first delighted, Midas found that his food and drink turned to gold as well, and in later versions even his daughter was transformed. Begging release, he was told to wash in the river Pactolus, which thereafter carried gold sands — the legendary source of Lydia's wealth.

The ass's ears

In a second famous tale, Midas judged a musical contest between Apollo and Pan (or Marsyas) in favour of Pan; Apollo punished his poor taste by giving him the ears of an ass. Midas hid them under a cap, but his barber, sworn to secrecy, whispered the secret into a hole in the ground — from which reeds grew that broadcast "Midas has ass's ears" to the world.

Historical Midas

King Midas of Phrygia (reigned c. 738–696 BC) ruled from Gordion, amassed great wealth, dedicated offerings at Delphi, and married a Greek princess. Assyrian records call him "Mita of the Mushki". Phrygia's power collapsed with the Cimmerian invasions, and ancient sources report Midas took his own life, traditionally by drinking bull's blood, as Gordion fell.
