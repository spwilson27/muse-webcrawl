Indian diaspora

The Indian diaspora comprises people of Indian origin living outside the Republic of India, including Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs). With more than 35 million people, it is often described as the world's largest diaspora. Major populations live in the United Arab Emirates, the United States, Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Myanmar, the United Kingdom, South Africa, Canada, Fiji, Mauritius, and the Caribbean.

History

Indians have traded and settled abroad for millennia, but the modern diaspora was largely shaped by colonial-era migration: from the 1830s, indentured labourers were shipped to sugar colonies in Mauritius, Fiji, Natal, and the Caribbean (where their descendants are called Indo-Caribbeans or "East Indians"), while traders and professionals settled in East Africa and Southeast Asia. A second great wave followed Indian independence in 1947 and especially the liberalisation of immigration laws in the United States, Canada, and Australia in the 1960s, plus labour migration to the Gulf states after the 1970s oil boom.

Economic and cultural influence

India receives the world's largest remittance inflows, exceeding US$100 billion annually in the early 2020s. The diaspora has been influential in technology, medicine, academia, and politics in host countries, and India courts it through institutions such as the Pravasi Bharatiya Divas convention and the Overseas Citizen of India card. Indian languages, religions — Hinduism, Sikhism, Islam, Jainism, and others — cuisine, and cinema have spread globally with the diaspora.
