International medical graduate


Doctors trained abroad form a large share of the physician workforce in several countries.

An international medical graduate (IMG), earlier referred to as a foreign medical graduate (FMG), is a physician who received their medical degree from a medical school outside of the country in which they practise or intend to practise medicine. In the United States the term also covers graduates of medical schools outside the United States and Canada, while in some other countries it is used more broadly for any doctor trained abroad.

Licensing requirements

IMGs typically must pass a series of examinations and credential verification before they may practise. In the United States, certification is administered by the Educational Commission for Foreign Medical Graduates (ECFMG), which verifies primary-source medical credentials and requires passage of the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE) Step 1 and Step 2 CK, an English-language proficiency test, and a clinical skills pathway. Certification is a prerequisite for entering accredited residency training and for full medical licensure.

Workforce role

International medical graduates make up roughly a quarter of the practising physician workforce in the United States and significant shares in countries such as the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia. They are disproportionately represented in primary care and in rural or otherwise underserved communities, and many enter through the J-1 exchange visitor visa or other immigration routes that include service obligations.

Challenges

IMGs often face hurdles including visa restrictions, competition for residency positions, additional documentation requirements, and questions about the comparability of training. Studies generally find that patient outcomes for IMG and domestically trained physicians are comparable.
