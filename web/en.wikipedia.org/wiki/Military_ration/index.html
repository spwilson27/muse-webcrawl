Military ration

A military ration (also called a field ration or combat ration) is a packaged meal provided to troops in the field, designed to be shelf-stable, nutritionally complete, and easy to carry and prepare without kitchen facilities.

History

Armies historically issued hardtack, salted meat, and grain; Napoleonic-era canning, originally developed to feed French armies, revolutionised preserved food. The two World Wars produced standardised rations such as the American K-ration and C-ration and the British compo ration. The modern standard is the individual retort-pouched meal — exemplified by the United States Meal, Ready-to-Eat (MRE), introduced in 1981, which includes a flameless ration heater.

Types

Rations range from individual 24-hour packs and group rations for small units, to specialised variants for cold-weather, jungle, or high-altitude operations, long-range patrols, and survival kits. National ration packs reflect local cuisines and are a perennial subject of soldier humour, trade, and review.
