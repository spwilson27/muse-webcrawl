Slugging percentage

In baseball statistics, slugging percentage (abbreviated SLG) is a measure of the batting productivity of a hitter. It is calculated as total bases divided by at-bats:

SLG = (1B + 2×2B + 3×3B + 4×HR) / AB

where AB is the number of at-bats for a given player, and 1B, 2B, 3B, and HR are the number of singles, doubles, triples, and home runs, respectively. Unlike batting average, slugging percentage gives more weight to extra-base hits such as doubles and home runs, as opposed to singles. Walks are specifically excluded from this calculation, as an appearance at the plate not counted as an at-bat does not affect a player's slugging percentage.

A maximum slugging percentage is 4.000, achieved if a player hits a home run in every at-bat. Babe Ruth holds the Major League Baseball career record with a .6897 slugging percentage, while Barry Bonds holds the single-season record at .863, set in 2001, when he hit 73 home runs.

Significance

Slugging percentage is one component of on-base plus slugging (OPS), a popular measure of a hitter's overall offensive value. Whereas batting average counts a home run and a single equally, slugging percentage reflects the power dimension of hitting.

Records

The all-time career leader is Babe Ruth (.6897), followed by Ted Williams (.6338), Lou Gehrig (.6324), and Barry Bonds (.6069). Josh Gibson is credited with a career mark near .974 in Negro league statistics recognized by MLB, though records vary.
