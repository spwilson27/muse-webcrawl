Embryogenesis

Embryogenesis is the process by which the embryo forms and develops. In organismal biology the process begins at fertilization and continues through the formation of the early structures of the organism. In mammals the term refers chiefly to the early stages of prenatal development, whereas the terms fetus and fetal development describe later stages.

Stages

After fertilization, the single-celled zygote undergoes rapid mitotic divisions called cleavage, forming a solid ball of cells (morula) and then a hollow blastula (blastocyst in mammals). Gastrulation then rearranges the cells into the three germ layers — ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm — from which all tissues and organs derive. Neurulation forms the neural tube, the precursor of the central nervous system, and organogenesis builds the organs themselves.

Control of development

Embryogenesis is directed by gene regulatory networks, morphogen gradients, and cell signaling pathways involving molecules such as Sonic hedgehog, Wnt, BMP, and retinoic acid. Homeobox (Hox) genes pattern the body axis in animals from flies to humans. Differential gene expression, rather than loss of genetic material, drives cell differentiation; nearly all cells retain the full genome.

Plant embryogenesis

In plants, embryogenesis occurs within the seed, establishing the apical–basal axis and the cotyledons, and ends with dormancy until germination.
