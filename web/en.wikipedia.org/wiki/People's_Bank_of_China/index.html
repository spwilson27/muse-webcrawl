People's Bank of China

The People's Bank of China (PBOC or PBC) is the central bank of the People's Republic of China, responsible for carrying out monetary policy, regulating financial institutions, and issuing the national currency, the renminbi (yuan). Headquartered in Beijing, it holds the largest stock of foreign exchange reserves of any central bank, and its financial assets are larger than those of any other single public institution.

The bank was founded on 1 December 1948, shortly before the establishment of the PRC. For its first decades it functioned both as central bank and as the country's dominant commercial bank (a "monobank" system); in 1984 its commercial lending functions were transferred to newly created state-owned banks, principally the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, leaving the PBOC as a true central bank.

The PBOC operates under the direction of the State Council rather than independently. Its governor is appointed by the National People's Congress. The bank has also been a pioneer in developing a central bank digital currency, the digital renminbi (e-CNY).
