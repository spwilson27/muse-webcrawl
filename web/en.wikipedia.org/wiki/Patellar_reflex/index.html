Patellar reflex

Patellar reflex
Also known as: Knee-jerk reflex
Stimulus: Tap on the patellar ligament
Response: Extension of the lower leg
Spinal level: L2–L4

The patellar reflex, commonly known as the knee-jerk reflex, is a stretch reflex that tests the L2, L3, and L4 segments of the spinal cord. It is one of the deep tendon reflexes routinely examined in a neurological examination.

Mechanism

When the patellar ligament is struck with a reflex hammer just below the kneecap, the quadriceps muscle is briefly stretched. Muscle spindles in the quadriceps detect the stretch and send signals via Ia sensory fibres to the spinal cord, where they synapse directly on motor neurons that cause the quadriceps to contract, extending the lower leg in a small kick. Because the pathway involves a single synapse, the patellar reflex is a monosynaptic reflex.

Clinical significance

An absent or diminished reflex (hyporeflexia) may indicate damage to the sensory or motor nerves, the spinal segments, or the muscle itself, as in peripheral neuropathy. An exaggerated reflex (hyperreflexia), sometimes with clonus, suggests an upper motor neuron lesion. The reflex can be reinforced by the Jendrassik manoeuvre, in which the patient interlocks and pulls apart their fingers to distract attention and reduce voluntary suppression.
