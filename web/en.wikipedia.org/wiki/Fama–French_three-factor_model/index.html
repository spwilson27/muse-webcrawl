The Fama–French three-factor model is a statistical model designed in 1992 by Nobel laureate Eugene Fama and Kenneth French to describe stock returns. It expands on the capital asset pricing model (CAPM) by adding size risk and value risk factors to the market risk factor. The model was developed after Fama and French found that two classes of stocks — small-cap stocks and stocks with a high book-to-market ratio ("value" stocks) — tended to outperform the market as a whole, and that CAPM's beta did a poor job of explaining the cross-section of average returns.

The model

Rit − RFt = αi + β1(RMt − RFt) + β2SMBt + β3HMLt + εit

  • RMt − RFt — the excess return of the market over the risk-free rate (as in CAPM).
  • SMB ("small minus big") — the return spread between portfolios of small-cap and large-cap stocks.
  • HML ("high minus low") — the return spread between portfolios of high and low book-to-market ratio stocks.

The factor returns are constructed from six size-and-value sorted portfolios and are published on Kenneth French's data library, making the model a standard tool for evaluating fund managers: a positive alpha (α) after controlling for the three factors is interpreted as performance not explained by exposure to market, size, and value risks.

Five-factor model

In 2015 Fama and French extended the model to five factors, adding profitability (RMW, "robust minus weak") and investment (CMA, "conservative minus aggressive") factors. The five-factor model largely absorbs the value factor HML for many samples, a point of ongoing debate. Related models include the Carhart four-factor model, which adds a momentum factor (MOM).

Criticism and evidence

Whether the size and value premiums reflect rational compensation for risk, behavioural mispricing, or data mining remains contested. The size premium weakened substantially after its discovery in the early 1980s, while the value premium has persisted internationally. The model remains one of the most widely used benchmarks in empirical asset pricing and performance evaluation.
