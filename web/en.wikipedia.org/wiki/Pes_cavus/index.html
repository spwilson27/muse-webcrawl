Pes cavus

Pes cavus
Also known asHigh arch, claw foot (in severe cases)
SpecialtyOrthopaedics, podiatry, neurology

Pes cavus, also known as high arch, is a human foot type in which the sole of the foot is distinctly hollow when bearing weight. That is, there is a fixed plantar flexion of the foot. A high arch is the opposite of a flat foot and is somewhat less common.

Causes

Pes cavus may be hereditary, idiopathic, or caused by an underlying neurological condition. A substantial proportion of cases are the presenting sign of Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease, a hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy; other causes include Friedreich's ataxia, spina bifida, poliomyelitis, spinal cord lesions and stroke. Muscle imbalance — relatively strong or weak peroneal and posterior tibial muscles, with intrinsic foot muscle weakness — drives progressive deformity, often with claw toes and hindfoot varus. New or one-sided cavus warrants neurological investigation.

Symptoms

Because weight is concentrated on the heel and the ball of the foot, symptoms include foot pain, callosities under the metatarsal heads, instability and frequent ankle sprains, stress fractures, and difficulty fitting shoes. The deformity may be flexible in early stages but becomes rigid over time.

Treatment

Conservative treatment includes custom orthotic insoles to redistribute pressure, physiotherapy and appropriate footwear. Surgery — soft-tissue releases, tendon transfers, osteotomies or, for severe rigid deformity, triple arthrodesis — is reserved for painful progressive cases, ideally addressing the underlying muscle imbalance.
