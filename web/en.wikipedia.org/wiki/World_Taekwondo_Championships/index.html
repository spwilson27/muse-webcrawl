The World Taekwondo Championships are the premier world championship event in the Korean martial art and Olympic sport of taekwondo, organised by World Taekwondo (WT). Held every two years since 1973, they bring together national teams from well over one hundred countries to contest sparring titles in eight men's and eight women's weight divisions.

History

The first championships were staged in Seoul, South Korea, in May 1973, days before the founding of the World Taekwondo Federation (now World Taekwondo), with ten countries participating and Korean athletes dominating the medals. Women's competition was added at the 1987 championships in Barcelona. The event has since rotated worldwide, with notable editions including Copenhagen 1983, New York 1993, Jeju 2001, Muju 2017 in the purpose-built Taekwondowon complex, Manchester 2019, Baku 2023 and Wuxi in 2025. Taekwondo became a full Olympic sport at Sydney 2000, but the biennial world championships remain the sport's largest event by participation and are central to Olympic ranking points.

Format

Competitors fight single-elimination bracket bouts over three rounds, wearing electronic protective scoring systems (PSS) that register kicks and punches to the trunk and headgear; head kicks carry the highest scores. Men's divisions range from 54 kg to over 87 kg and women's from 46 kg to over 73 kg. Two bronze medals are awarded in each class. South Korea has historically topped the all-time medal table by a wide margin, although China, Iran, Turkey, Thailand, Great Britain and others have produced multiple world champions in recent decades.

Notable champions

Multiple-time world champions include South Korea's Jung Kook-hyun, Turkey's Servet Tazegül, Iran's Hadi Saei (also a double Olympic champion), and British star Jade Jones, who won her world title in 2019 to complete a full set with her two Olympic golds. The championships also serve as a showcase for World Taekwondo's demonstration team and for poomsae (forms) events held at separate world championships.
