Dufourspitze

Elevation4,634 m (15,203 ft)
Prominence2,165 m

RangePennine Alps

The Dufourspitze is the highest summit of Monte Rosa, a mountain massif in the Pennine Alps on the border between Switzerland and Italy. At 4,634 metres (15,203 ft), it is the highest point in Switzerland and the second-highest mountain in Western Europe after Mont Blanc.

Name and geography

The peak is named after Guillaume Henri Dufour, a Swiss general and cartographer who led the survey of Switzerland that produced the Dufour map. The summit lies in the canton of Valais, above the resort town of Zermatt, and forms part of the Monte Rosa massif, which includes several other 4,000-metre peaks such as Zumsteinspitze and Signalkuppe.

Climbing

The first ascent was made on 1 August 1855 by a party led by Charles Hudson, including the guides Ulrich Lauener and Johann zum Taugwald. The normal route ascends from the Monte Rosa Hut via the Monte Rosa Glacier and is a long, glaciated climb graded PD+.
