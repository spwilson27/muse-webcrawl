The Austrian Empire (German: Kaisertum Österreich, modern spelling Kaiserthum Oesterreich) was a Central European multinational empire from 1804 to 1867, created by proclamation out of the realms of the Habsburg Monarchy. It was a major power and one of the largest states in Europe, comprising the crown lands of the House of Habsburg in central and southeastern Europe.

History

The empire was officially proclaimed on 11 August 1804 by Francis II, Holy Roman Emperor, who took the title Francis I, Emperor of Austria, in response to the coronation of Napoleon as Emperor of the French. After the Habsburg defeat at Austerlitz and the Treaty of Pressburg (1805), the Holy Roman Empire was dissolved in 1806, but Austria remained a leading European power and presided over the German Confederation after the Congress of Vienna in 1815.

Under Chancellor Klemens von Metternich, Austria championed conservative order in Europe for three decades. The Revolutions of 1848 shook the empire, prompting the abdication of Ferdinand I in favour of the young Franz Joseph I and a period of neo-absolutist centralisation. Defeats in the Second Italian War of Independence (1859) and the Austro-Prussian War (1866) cost Austria its Italian possessions and its influence in Germany.

The Ausgleich of 1867

Weakened and seeking stability, the empire negotiated the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 (the Ausgleich), which transformed the Austrian Empire into the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary, with the western and northern crown lands (Cisleithania) and the Kingdom of Hungary (Transleithania) sharing a monarch, foreign policy, and military affairs.

Composition

The empire included the Archduchy of Austria, the Kingdoms of Bohemia, Hungary, Croatia, Galicia and Lodomeria, Lombardy–Venetia (until 1859/1866), the Grand Duchy of Transylvania, and numerous other crown lands — a mosaic of peoples speaking more than a dozen languages, whose national movements would shape the empire's politics throughout the 19th century.
