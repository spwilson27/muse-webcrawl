Gordie Howe hat trick

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gordie Howe, for whom the feat is named.

A Gordie Howe hat trick is a variation of the hat trick in ice hockey in which a player scores a goal, records an assist, and gets into a fight, all in a single game. It is named after Gordie Howe, the Hall of Fame right winger renowned both for his scoring and his physical, combative style of play with the Detroit Red Wings.

Origin

The term was coined in the 1950s, yet ironically Howe himself accomplished the feat only twice in his long NHL career: on October 11, 1953, against the Toronto Maple Leafs, and on March 21, 1954, against the Montreal Canadiens. Howe was so prolific a scorer and so feared a fighter that opponents rarely challenged him, limiting his opportunities.

Records

The all-time leader in Gordie Howe hat tricks is Rick Tocchet, who recorded 18 during his career. Brendan Shanahan accumulated 17, and Brian Sutter 10. The feat has become rarer in the modern NHL as fighting has declined and the instigator rule has discouraged staged bouts.

Variations

A "natural Gordie Howe hat trick" is sometimes used for a goal, assist, and fight in a single period. Goal-scoring plays adapted the term to other contexts, but the original hockey meaning remains the standard.
