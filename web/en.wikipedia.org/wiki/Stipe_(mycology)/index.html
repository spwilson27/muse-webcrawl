Stipe (mycology)

In mycology, a stipe is the stem or stalk-like feature supporting the cap (pileus) of a mushroom. It consists of hyphal tissue and functions to raise the spore-bearing surface (the hymenium, borne on gills, pores, or teeth beneath the cap) above the substrate so that spores can be dispersed by air currents. Mushrooms possessing a stipe are called stipitate; those without are sessile.

Features used in identification

The form and surface of the stipe are important identification characters in agaric taxonomy:

  • Attachment — central, eccentric (off-centre), or lateral relative to the cap.
  • Shape — equal, tapering upward or downward, clavate (club-shaped), or bulbous at the base.
  • Surface texture — smooth, fibrillose, scaly (squamulose), scabrous, reticulate (netted, as in some boletes), or pruinose.
  • Consistency — solid, stuffed, or hollow; brittle (snapping cleanly, as in Russula) or fibrous.
  • Appendages — a ring (annulus) left by the partial veil, and a volva (cup) left by the universal veil at the base, both significant in genera such as Amanita.

Evolutionary note

Like most features of mushrooms, the stipe is thought to be an adaptation for spore dispersal: elevation allows spores falling from the cap's underside to escape the still layer of air near the ground and be carried away by the wind.
