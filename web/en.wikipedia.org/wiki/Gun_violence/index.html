Gun violence is violence committed with the use of a firearm. Gun-related violence may or may not be considered criminal. Criminal violence includes homicide (except when and where ruled justifiable), assault with a deadly weapon, and suicide, or attempted suicide, depending on jurisdiction. Non-criminal violence includes accidental or unintended injury and death (generally involving an unsecured firearm). Included in gun violence statistics are military or paramilitary activities.

According to GunPolicy.org, 75 percent of the world's 875 million guns are civilian controlled. Globally, millions are wounded or killed by the use of guns each year. A study by the Small Arms Survey estimated roughly 250,000 firearm deaths worldwide in 2019, of which the majority were homicides, followed by suicides and unintentional deaths.

By country

Levels of gun violence vary greatly among countries and are strongly associated with the availability of firearms and the stringency of gun control laws. The United States has by far the highest rate of firearm homicide among high-income developed countries and a high rate of civilian gun ownership. Several Latin American countries, including Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, and Venezuela, record the largest absolute numbers of gun homicides, often linked to organised crime and drug trafficking. Countries with restrictive licensing regimes, such as Japan and the United Kingdom, report very low rates of gun death.

Suicide

Firearms are the most lethal common method of suicide, with a case-fatality rate approaching 85–90 percent. In the United States, more than half of all suicides involve a firearm. Research on means restriction indicates that reducing access to firearms can lower suicide rates.

Prevention

Approaches to reducing gun violence include universal background checks, safe-storage requirements, licensing and registration systems, restrictions on particular weapon categories, community violence-interruption programmes, and focused-deterrence policing. The effectiveness and constitutionality of these measures remain subjects of active scientific and political debate in many countries.
