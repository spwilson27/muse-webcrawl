Articulation (phonetics)

In phonetics, articulation is the physical production of speech sounds by the coordinated movement of the speech organs — the lungs, vocal folds, tongue, lips, teeth, alveolar ridge, palate, velum and jaw. Articulatory phonetics describes how sounds are made, complementing acoustic phonetics (how they sound) and auditory phonetics (how they are perceived).

Articulators

Speech organs are divided into active articulators, which move (such as the lower lip and the various parts of the tongue), and passive articulators, which remain fixed (such as the upper teeth, alveolar ridge and hard palate). The constriction formed between them largely determines the resulting sound.

Consonants

Consonants are classified by place of articulation (bilabial, labiodental, dental, alveolar, postalveolar, retroflex, palatal, velar, uvular, pharyngeal, glottal), manner of articulation (plosive, nasal, trill, tap, fricative, approximant, lateral) and voicing (voiced versus voiceless). These parameters underpin the consonant chart of the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA).

Vowels

Vowels are described by tongue height (close, mid, open), tongue backness (front, central, back), lip rounding, and length/tenseness. See Vowel and Place of articulation.
