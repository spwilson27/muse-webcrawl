Taharqa (also spelled Taharka or Tirhakah) was a pharaoh of ancient Egypt and king of the Kingdom of Kush, ruling from about 690 to 664 BC as the third king of the Twenty-fifth Dynasty. A son of Piye, the founder of Kushite rule over Egypt, Taharqa inherited an empire that stretched from Nubia to the Mediterranean and is remembered both for an ambitious building programme and for his long struggle against the expanding Neo-Assyrian Empire.

Reign

Taharqa came to the throne after the death of his brother Shebitku. His early reign was prosperous: abundant Nile floods and good harvests are recorded in inscriptions, and the king undertook construction at Karnak, Memphis, and in Nubia, notably at Kawa and Jebel Barkal near his capital Napata. He also built a pyramid at Nuri, where he was eventually buried — the largest pyramid in the Nuri royal cemetery.

Conflict with Assyria

Taharqa supported the kings of the Levant in resisting Assyria, and he is generally identified with "Tirhakah king of Cush" in the Hebrew Bible, who is mentioned in connection with Sennacherib's campaign against Jerusalem in 701 BC (2 Kings 19:9). The Assyrian king Esarhaddon invaded Egypt in 671 BC, captured Memphis, and drove Taharqa south. Although Taharqa retook Memphis after Esarhaddon's death, Esarhaddon's successor Ashurbanipal campaigned in Egypt in about 667 BC and again later, pushing the Kushites out of Lower Egypt for good.

Death and succession

Taharqa died in 664 BC and was succeeded by his nephew Tanutamani, who briefly reoccupied Memphis before being defeated by the Assyrians, ending effective Kushite rule over Egypt. Taharqa's statuary — including famous granite statues from Kawa now in Khartoum and Copenhagen — is among the finest of the period.
