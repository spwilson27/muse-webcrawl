United Nations Peacekeeping Force in Cyprus

The United Nations Peacekeeping Force in Cyprus (UNFICYP) is a United Nations peacekeeping force established by Security Council Resolution 186 in 1964 to prevent further fighting between the Greek Cypriot and Turkish Cypriot communities on the island of Cyprus and to help maintain and restore law and order. After the 1974 Greek Cypriot coup d'état and the Turkish military intervention, the Security Council extended UNFICYP's mandate, and the force came to supervise ceasefire lines and a buffer zone dividing the island. UNFICYP is one of the longest-running UN peacekeeping missions.

Mandate

UNFICYP's mandate, renewed periodically by the Security Council, includes supervising the ceasefire lines, maintaining the buffer zone — which stretches about 180 kilometres across the island and covers roughly 3.3% of its territory — and undertaking humanitarian activities. The force also supports the work of the UN Secretary-General's good offices mission toward a negotiated settlement of the Cyprus problem.

History

The force was deployed in March 1964 following intercommunal violence that had erupted in December 1963 ("Bloody Christmas"). Following the events of 1974, the island was effectively partitioned between the Republic of Cyprus in the south and a Turkish Cypriot administration in the north (which declared itself the "Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus" in 1983, recognised only by Turkey). UNFICYP's strength, which peaked at over 6,000, has since been reduced to around 1,000.

Contributing countries

Troops have historically been contributed by countries including Argentina, Austria, Canada, Denmark, Finland, Ireland, Sweden, and the United Kingdom; the buffer zone is divided into sectors, with the largest contingent long provided by Argentina (including personnel from several Latin American states), while the United Kingdom, Slovakia, Hungary, and Serbia have also contributed in recent years. The force commander and the Special Representative of the Secretary-General head the military and civilian components respectively.
