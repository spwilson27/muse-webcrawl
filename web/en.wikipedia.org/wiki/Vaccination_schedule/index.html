Vaccination schedule

A vaccination schedule is a series of vaccinations, including the timing of all doses, which may be either recommended or compulsory, depending on the country of residence. Schedules are designed to protect individuals — especially infants and children — at the ages when they are most vulnerable to vaccine-preventable diseases, while building durable immunity.

Development

National schedules are set by health authorities, often advised by expert committees such as the United States Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) or the United Kingdom's Joint Committee on Vaccination and Immunisation (JCVI). The World Health Organization's Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI), launched in 1974, provides a model schedule used by many low- and middle-income countries.

Typical childhood schedule

Most schedules begin at birth or two months of age with vaccines against diseases such as diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, polio, Haemophilus influenzae type b, hepatitis B, pneumococcal disease and rotavirus, followed by boosters and later vaccines including measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) and HPV in adolescence. Combination vaccines reduce the number of injections required.
