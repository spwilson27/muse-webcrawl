The Linux Foundation

Type: 501(c)(6) non-profit organization

Founded: 2000 (as OSDL); merged 2007

Headquarters: San Francisco, California, U.S.

Focus: Promotion of Linux and open-source software

The Linux Foundation (LF) is a non-profit technology consortium founded in 2000 as the Open Source Development Labs (OSDL). In 2007, OSDL merged with the Free Standards Group to form the Linux Foundation. Its purpose is to sponsor the work of Linux creator Linus Torvalds and lead developers of the Linux kernel, and to promote, protect, and standardize Linux and other open-source software and technologies.

The Linux Foundation hosts a wide range of collaborative projects through its sub-foundations and initiatives, spanning cloud computing, networking, blockchain, automotive software, and embedded systems. Its membership includes many of the world's largest technology companies, and it funds fellowships, training, certification, and events such as the Open Source Summit.

History

The Open Source Development Labs was founded in 2000 by Global 2000 companies including IBM, Intel, HP, and NEC to accelerate the deployment of Linux for enterprise computing. The Free Standards Group, founded the same year, maintained standards such as the Linux Standard Base. Their merger in January 2007 created a single organization to promote the growth of Linux. Over the following years, the foundation expanded far beyond Linux itself to host hundreds of open-source projects.

Projects

Notable projects hosted or supported by the Linux Foundation and its associated foundations include the Linux kernel, Kubernetes (through the Cloud Native Computing Foundation), the Hyperledger blockchain initiative, the Automotive Grade Linux platform, the Yocto Project for embedded Linux, and the OpenJS Foundation's stewardship of JavaScript projects. The foundation also manages the Core Infrastructure Initiative, established in the wake of the Heartbleed vulnerability to fund critical open-source infrastructure.

Community and events

The Linux Foundation organizes conferences including Open Source Summit, KubeCon + CloudNativeCon (via CNCF), and the Linux Kernel Developers Summit. It also provides training and certification programs for system administrators and developers, and maintains resources such as the Linux.com website.
