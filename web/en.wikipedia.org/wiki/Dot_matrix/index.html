Dot matrix

A dot matrix is a two-dimensional patterned array, used to represent characters, symbols and images. Most types of modern technology use dot matrices for display of information, including mobile phones, televisions, and printers. The term is also used in weaving, knitting and other textile patterning.

Early precursor uses of dot patterns appeared in 1850s jacquard looms and in 1920s player piano rolls. The modern electronic dot matrix became common with computer displays and printers in the 1960s and 1970s.

In computing

In computing, a dot matrix may be used to represent a bitmap in which each dot corresponds to a pixel. Dot matrix displays arrange light-emitting elements — historically incandescent bulbs, later LEDs — in a grid; by lighting selected dots, letters, numerals and simple graphics can be shown. Such displays were widely used in railway station departure boards, road signs, calculators and pinball machines.

Dot matrix printing

A dot matrix printer is an impact printer that prints using a fixed number of pins or wires. The pins strike an ink-soaked ribbon against the paper, creating patterns of small dots that form characters and images. Dot matrix printers were popular in the 1970s and 1980s for home and office use; although largely displaced by inkjet and laser printers, they remain in use for multipart forms, receipts and industrial environments because impact printing can produce carbon copies.

Uses

  • LED and LCD displays for text and graphics
  • Dot matrix printers and receipt printers
  • Braille embossers and refreshable braille displays
  • Barcode-like matrix codes (though QR codes and Data Matrix codes are more precisely classed as 2D barcodes)
