Factor investing

Factor investing is an investment strategy that targets securities with specific quantifiable characteristics — called factors — that are associated with higher risk-adjusted returns. It sits between passive index investing and traditional active stock selection, and is often implemented through "smart beta" funds and ETFs.

Principal factors

The best-known factors are value (cheap versus expensive stocks, from Graham and Dodd and formalised in the Fama–French three-factor model of 1992–93), size (small-cap premium), momentum (recent winners continue to outperform, documented by Jegadeesh and Titman and added by Carhart), quality/profitability, low volatility, and carry and dividend yield. Fama and French's five-factor model (2015) added profitability and investment factors.

Explanations

Debate continues over whether factor premia are compensation for risk, products of investor behaviour and limits to arbitrage, or — as critics warn — partly artefacts of data mining, with hundreds of proposed factors dubbed a "factor zoo". Practitioners typically combine multiple factors for diversification, since individual factor returns are cyclical.

Implementation

Factor portfolios are built by ranking stocks on factor metrics and rebalancing periodically, long-only or long–short, and are offered by providers such as Dimensional Fund Advisors, AQR, and index firms including MSCI and FTSE Russell.
