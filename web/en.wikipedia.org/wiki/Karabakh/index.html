Karabakh

Karabakh (Azerbaijani: Qarabağ; Armenian: Ղարաբաղ, Gharabagh) is a geographic region in present-day southwestern Azerbaijan and eastern Armenia, extending from the highlands of the Lesser Caucasus down to the lowlands between the rivers Kura and Aras. Its name is generally held to derive from the Turkic and Persian words for "black garden".

Divisions

The region is traditionally divided into three zones: Highland Karabakh (Nagorno-Karabakh), Lowland Karabakh, and the Zangezur mountains. The highlands were historically inhabited mainly by ethnic Armenians, organised in the medieval period into Armenian melikdoms (principalities) that retained a degree of autonomy under Persian rule.

Modern history

Incorporated into the Russian Empire by the Treaty of Gulistan (1813), Karabakh became an arena of Armenian–Azerbaijani conflict after the First World War. Under Soviet rule, Highland Karabakh was made an autonomous oblast within the Azerbaijan SSR in 1923. Its status was the subject of the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict: a full-scale war in the early 1990s left the area under Armenian control as the unrecognised Republic of Artsakh, until Azerbaijan regained most of the surrounding districts and part of the region in the 2020 war, and the remainder following its offensive of September 2023, after which nearly the entire Armenian population fled to Armenia and the Artsakh republic formally dissolved.
