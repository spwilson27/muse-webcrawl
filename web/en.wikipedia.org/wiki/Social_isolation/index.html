Social isolation

Social isolation is a state in which an individual lacks a sense of belonging and has few social contacts or fulfilling relationships. It is distinguished from loneliness, which is the subjective feeling of being alone, and from chosen solitude: a person may be objectively isolated without feeling lonely, or lonely within a crowd. Social isolation can occur at any age but is a particular concern among older adults, people with disabilities or chronic illness, carers, and those who are bereaved or retired.

Health effects

Meta-analyses led by Julianne Holt-Lunstad have found that social isolation and loneliness are associated with substantially increased risks of premature mortality — comparable in magnitude to smoking and obesity — as well as cardiovascular disease, dementia, depression and weakened immune function. Proposed mechanisms include chronic stress, inflammation, health behaviours and lack of practical support in crises.

Causes and responses

Contributing factors include living alone, mobility limitations, hearing loss, poverty, rural residence, discrimination and life transitions. Responses include community programmes, befriending schemes, social prescribing by health services, accessible transport and digital inclusion efforts. Governments have increasingly treated the issue as a public-health priority; the United Kingdom appointed a Minister for Loneliness in 2018, and the US Surgeon General issued an advisory on loneliness and isolation in 2023.
