Alexander Afanasyev

Alexander Nikolayevich Afanasyev (Russian: Алекса́ндр Никола́евич Афана́сьев; 23 July 1826 – 5 October 1871) was a Russian Slavist and ethnographer who published nearly 600 Russian fairy tales and folk tales — one of the largest collections of folklore in the world. His work is often compared to that of the Brothers Grimm in Germany.

Life

Afanasyev was born in Boguchar, Voronezh Governorate, and studied law at Moscow University. Unable to pursue an academic career after his views drew official disapproval, he worked as a civil servant, including at the Main Archive of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, which gave him access to rich documentary material.

Works

Between 1855 and 1863 he published Russian Folk Tales (Narodnye russkiye skazki) in eight instalments, drawing on the archives of the Russian Geographical Society and on material collected by others, including Vladimir Dal. He also wrote the scholarly study The Poetic Outlooks of the Slavs on Nature (1865–1869), a major work of the mythological school of folklore studies. A further collection of earthy, satirical tales, Russian Secret Tales, had to be published anonymously in Geneva in 1872 because of censorship in Russia.
