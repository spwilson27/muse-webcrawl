Electronic centralised aircraft monitor

The Electronic Centralised Aircraft Monitor (ECAM) is the central display system on Airbus aircraft that monitors aircraft systems and provides information and warnings to the flight crew. It is Airbus's counterpart to Boeing's Engine Indicating and Crew Alerting System (EICAS). ECAM presents system data, checklists, and procedures on two cockpit displays: the upper Engine/Warning Display (E/WD) and the lower System Display (SD).

Design philosophy

ECAM is organized around Airbus's "dark cockpit" philosophy: when all systems operate normally, indicator lights are extinguished and the displays are uncluttered. When a failure occurs, ECAM detects it, displays the relevant system synoptic page automatically, and presents the crew with the title of the failure together with a checklist of actions, in priority order. Warnings (red, requiring immediate action) and cautions (amber) are accompanied by aural alerts—a continuous repetitive chime for warnings and a single chime for cautions.

Displays

The upper display normally shows engine parameters, fuel quantity, flap/slat position, and memo messages; in a failure it becomes the primary source of warnings and procedural checklists. The lower display shows synoptic diagrams of individual systems—hydraulics, pneumatics, electrical, flight controls, and others—either selected manually by the crew or called up automatically by ECAM in context. A status page summarizes the aircraft's state after procedures have been completed, including inoperative systems and approach limitations.

History

ECAM was introduced with the Airbus A310 and refined on the A320 family, becoming a defining feature of Airbus flight decks through the A330, A340, A380, and A350. Its procedure-driven approach contrasts with the Boeing philosophy, in which crews consult separate quick-reference checklists after being alerted by EICAS messages.
