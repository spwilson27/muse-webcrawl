Kwangmyong (intranet)

Kwangmyong (Chosongul: 광명; Hancha: 光明; lit. "bright light" or "bright star") is a North Korean national intranet service opened in the early 2000s. It provides access to a curated collection of websites, email, news groups, and a national search engine, but it is entirely disconnected from the global Internet, which is accessible only to a small number of authorised users in North Korea.

Services

The network hosts sites operated by state institutions, universities, libraries, and news agencies such as the Korean Central News Agency. Content includes digitised books and periodicals at the Grand People's Study House and other libraries, academic journals, distance-learning services, and bulletin boards. A national email service operates within the network. Estimates of the number of sites range from about 1,000 to 5,500.

Access

Kwangmyong is accessible from universities, government offices, libraries, schools, and some workplaces, and increasingly from home computers in Pyongyang. Users connect over North Korea's fibre-optic and dial-up infrastructure, and access is free of charge at public terminals. The system uses IP addresses in the private 10.x.x.x range, and domestically produced software such as the Red Star OS and the Naenara browser is commonly used to access it.

Relationship to the Internet

Unlike the World Wide Web, Kwangmyong content is produced or vetted domestically; material from the Internet is translated, filtered, and uploaded by state bodies such as the Korea Computer Center. Kwangmyong thus exemplifies a "walled-garden" model of a national information network.
