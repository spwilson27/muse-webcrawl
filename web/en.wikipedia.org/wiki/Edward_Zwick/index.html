Edward M. Zwick (born October 8, 1952) is an American film and television director, producer, and screenwriter. He is known for his epic historical films — including Glory (1989), Legends of the Fall (1994), The Last Samurai (2003), and Blood Diamond (2006) — and for co-creating the acclaimed television series thirtysomething with Marshall Herskovitz, his partner in the Bedford Falls Company.

Early life and television

Zwick was born in Chicago and graduated from Harvard University in 1974 before studying at the AFI Conservatory. He began his career on the television series Family, then made his feature debut with About Last Night... (1986). With Herskovitz he created thirtysomething (1987–1991), which won the Emmy Award for Outstanding Drama Series in its first season, and later series including My So-Called Life (1994–95) and Once and Again (1999–2002).

Films

Zwick's Civil War drama Glory (1989), about the African-American 54th Massachusetts Infantry Regiment, won three Academy Awards, including Best Supporting Actor for Denzel Washington. He went on to direct Leaving Normal (1992), Legends of the Fall (1994), Courage Under Fire (1996), The Siege (1998), The Last Samurai (2003) with Tom Cruise, Blood Diamond (2006), Defiance (2008), Love & Other Drugs (2010), Pawn Sacrifice (2014), Jack Reacher: Never Go Back (2016), and Trial by Fire (2018). As a producer he shared the Academy Award for Best Picture for Shakespeare in Love (1998) and was nominated for Traffic (2000). Zwick frequently collaborates with composer James Newton Howard.
