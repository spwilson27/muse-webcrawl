The Amundsen Sea is an arm of the Southern Ocean off the coast of Marie Byrd Land in western Antarctica, lying between Cape Flying Fish on Thurston Island to the east and Cape Dart on Siple Island to the west. It was named for the Norwegian explorer Roald Amundsen by the Norwegian expedition of 1928–1930 under Captain Nils Larsen.

Geography

The sea is largely covered by sea ice and receives the outflow of several major glaciers draining the West Antarctic Ice Sheet, including Pine Island Glacier and the enormous Thwaites Glacier. The embayment is relatively shallow where the continental shelf extends offshore, and warm circumpolar deep water can reach the undersides of the floating glacier tongues.

Climate change significance

The Amundsen Sea sector is one of the fastest-changing parts of Antarctica. Its glaciers rest on bedrock that lies below sea level and deepens inland, making them vulnerable to marine ice sheet instability and rapid retreat driven by warm ocean water melting their ice shelves from below. The sector's glaciers are among the largest contributors to global sea-level rise from Antarctica, and the Thwaites Glacier in particular — sometimes nicknamed the "Doomsday Glacier" — is the subject of intensive international research.

Wildlife

Polynyas and ice edges in the Amundsen Sea support phytoplankton blooms and populations of seals, penguins and whales, and the region has been studied for its relatively productive and biodiverse coastal ecosystems.
