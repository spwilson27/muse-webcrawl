Parks Canada

Banff, established in 1885, is Canada's oldest national park.

Parks Canada (French: Parcs Canada) is the agency of the Government of Canada responsible for the country's national parks, national historic sites and national marine conservation areas. Founded on 19 May 1911 as the Dominion Parks Branch, it was the world's first national park service.

Mandate

Under the Parks Canada Agency Act (1998), the agency protects and presents nationally significant examples of Canada's natural and cultural heritage and fosters public understanding, appreciation and enjoyment of them in ways that ensure their ecological and commemorative integrity for present and future generations.

System

Parks Canada administers over 200 sites, including 48 national parks and park reserves (beginning with Banff National Park in 1885), more than 170 national historic sites that it directly manages, several national marine conservation areas, and Canada's first national urban park, Rouge National Urban Park in Toronto. The agency employs about 4,000 people and reports to Parliament through the Minister of Environment and Climate Change.

Symbols

The agency's well-known emblem is a beaver, and its uniformed staff — recognisable by the campaign hat — include the park wardens responsible for law enforcement and conservation in the parks.
