The All-Ireland Senior Football Championship is the premier inter-county competition in Gaelic football, organised by the Gaelic Athletic Association since 1887. County teams compete through their provincial championships and an All-Ireland series for the Sam Maguire Cup, with the final traditionally played at Croke Park in Dublin.

History

The first championship, in 1887, was won by Limerick. For most of its history the competition was a straight knockout: provincial champions met in All-Ireland semi-finals. A qualifier system for beaten teams was introduced in 2001, and the format was restructured repeatedly in the 2010s and 2020s, including the short-lived "Super 8s" group stage (2018–2019) and, from 2023, a group stage of sixteen teams feeding preliminary quarter-finals. Counties that fail to reach the top tier compete for the Tailteann Cup, introduced in 2022.

Dominant counties

Kerry is the most successful county with thirty-eight titles, followed by Dublin, whose six consecutive championships from 2015 to 2020 (part of a run of eight in nine years under Jim Gavin) set a modern record. Galway, Cork, Down, Meath and Tyrone are among the other multiple winners.

The final

The All-Ireland final, held in July since the calendar was compressed in 2022 (previously September), draws crowds of more than 82,000 to Croke Park and is among the biggest days in Irish sport. The winning captain receives the Sam Maguire Cup, named for a Cork-born London GAA figure.
