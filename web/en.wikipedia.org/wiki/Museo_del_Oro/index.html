Museo del Oro

The Museo del Oro (Spanish for Gold Museum) is an archaeological museum located in Bogotá, Colombia. It is owned by the Banco de la República (Bank of the Republic), and displays a selection of the bank's collection of pre-Hispanic goldwork — the largest such collection in the world, comprising more than 55,000 pieces of gold and other materials from Colombia's pre-Hispanic cultures, of which some 6,000 pieces are on display. The museum opened in 1939 after the bank began acquiring gold artefacts to prevent them from being melted down or exported.

Collection

The collection includes goldwork from cultures including the Muisca, Quimbaya, Calima, Tolima, Sinú, Tierradentro, San Agustín, Nariño and Tayrona. The most famous piece is the Muisca raft (Balsa Muisca), a small golden sculpture depicting the El Dorado ceremony at Lake Guatavita. The top floor contains the "Offering Room", an immersive circular chamber in which thousands of gold objects are dramatically lit to recreate the atmosphere of an indigenous offering.

Building

The present museum building, opened in 1968 and extensively renovated and expanded between 2004 and 2008, stands on the eastern side of Santander Park in downtown Bogotá. Regional gold museums administered by the Banco de la República exist in other Colombian cities, including Cartagena, Cali, Santa Marta, Pasto, Armenia, Leticia and Manizales.
