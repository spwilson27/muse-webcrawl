Nikos Xilouris

Born7 July 1936, Anogeia, Crete, Greece
Died8 February 1980, Piraeus, Greece
InstrumentsVocals, Cretan lyra
Nickname"Psaronikos"; "the Archangel of Crete"

Nikos Xilouris (Greek: Νίκος Ξυλούρης; 7 July 1936 – 8 February 1980), nicknamed Psaronikos, was a Greek composer and singer from Crete, a master of the Cretan lyra whose music became a symbol of resistance during the Greek military junta (1967–1974).

Career

Born into the musical Xilouris family of Anogeia, he began playing the lyra as a child and recorded from the 1950s. His collaborations with composer Yannis Markopoulos — including the albums Chroniko and the Rizitika cycle — brought Cretan folk music to national prominence, and his songs were sung by students during the Athens Polytechnic uprising of 1973.

Legacy

Xilouris died of a brain tumour at 43. Revered in Greece as "the Archangel of Crete," he remains the emblematic voice of Cretan music; his brothers Antonis ("Psarantonis") and Yiannis also became notable lyra players.
