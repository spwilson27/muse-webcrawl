Security Analysis is a book written by Benjamin Graham and David Dodd of Columbia Business School, which laid the intellectual foundation for what would later be called value investing. The first edition was published in 1934, shortly after the Wall Street crash and the start of the Great Depression. Graham and Dodd's conservative, fundamental approach to investment — emphasising the analysis of financial statements, the concept of intrinsic value, and the margin of safety — stood in contrast to the speculation that had preceded the crash.

Content

The book sets out methods for analysing bonds and preferred and common stocks, distinguishing investment from speculation and insisting that an investment operation is one "which, upon thorough analysis, promises safety of principal and a satisfactory return." It develops techniques for appraising earnings power, balance-sheet values and senior securities, and argues that market prices frequently diverge from underlying business value, creating opportunities for the disciplined analyst.

Editions and influence

Successive editions appeared in 1940, 1951 and 1962; the sixth edition (2008), based on the 1940 second edition, carries commentaries by contemporary value investors. Graham later distilled its ideas for lay readers in The Intelligent Investor (1949). Warren Buffett, a student of Graham at Columbia, has called Security Analysis one of the most important books on investing ever written, and its approach underlies the modern discipline of fundamental analysis.
