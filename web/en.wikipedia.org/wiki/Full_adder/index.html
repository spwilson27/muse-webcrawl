Full adder

A full adder is a digital circuit that performs addition of three binary bits: two operands A and B and a carry-in bit Cin from a previous stage. It produces two outputs: a sum bit S and a carry-out bit Cout.

Logic

The truth table of the full adder gives S = A ⊕ B ⊕ Cin and Cout = (A · B) + (Cin · (A ⊕ B)). Unlike a half adder, which has no carry input, full adders can be cascaded: connecting the carry-out of one stage to the carry-in of the next forms a ripple-carry adder capable of adding multi-bit numbers, the basic building block of the arithmetic logic unit (ALU) in a processor.

Implementation

A full adder can be built from two half adders and an OR gate, or directly from XOR, AND, and OR gates; transistor-level implementations typically use around 28 transistors in static CMOS, with faster mirror-adder and transmission-gate variants used in high-performance designs.
