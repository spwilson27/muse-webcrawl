John William Pehle (2 May 1909 – 24 March 1999) was an American Treasury Department official who became the first executive director of the War Refugee Board, the United States government agency created in 1944 to rescue victims of Nazi persecution.

War Refugee Board

Pehle, a graduate of Yale and Yale Law School, headed the Treasury's Foreign Funds Control office. In late 1943 he and colleagues including Randolph Paul and Josiah DuBois Jr. documented how the State Department had obstructed rescue efforts and suppressed information about the Holocaust, producing the explosive "Report to the Secretary on the Acquiescence of This Government in the Murder of the Jews", which Secretary Henry Morgenthau Jr. presented to President Franklin D. Roosevelt in January 1944.

Roosevelt responded by creating the War Refugee Board, with Pehle as executive director. Working with limited funds and private organisations, the Board helped rescue tens of thousands of Jews and other refugees in the final phase of the war, supported Raoul Wallenberg's mission in Budapest, and established the Fort Ontario refugee shelter in Oswego, New York. After the war Pehle returned to private law practice in Washington, D.C.
