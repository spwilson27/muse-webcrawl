Hermione Jean Granger is a fictional character in J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter series. She first appears in the novel Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone (1997) as a new student on her way to Hogwarts. She is a Muggle-born Gryffindor who becomes best friends with Harry Potter and Ron Weasley, and is famous for her intelligence, work ethic, and mastery of magic.

Characterisation

Hermione is a brilliant, bookish student who typically tops her year in examinations. Rowling has described her as a caricature of herself as a young girl. Her logic and encyclopedic knowledge repeatedly rescue her friends — solving the potions riddle in Philosopher's Stone, identifying the basilisk in Chamber of Secrets, and using a Time-Turner in Prisoner of Azkaban. She also campaigns for social justice, founding S.P.E.W. (the Society for the Promotion of Elfish Welfare) to protest the enslavement of house-elves.

Role in the series

As part of the central trio, Hermione helps hunt and destroy Voldemort's Horcruxes in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows, demonstrating formidable magical skill and resourcefulness. After the war she works at the Ministry of Magic, rising in supplementary material to become Minister for Magic. She marries Ron Weasley, with whom she has two children, Rose and Hugo. In the stage play Harry Potter and the Cursed Child (2016), she appears as Minister for Magic.

Reception

Hermione is widely regarded as a feminist role model and one of the most popular characters in the series; Emma Watson's portrayal in the eight films made the character an icon for a generation of readers and viewers.
