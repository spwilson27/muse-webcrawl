The Lumières Awards (French: Lumières de la presse internationale) are film awards presented by the Académie des Lumières, an organisation of foreign press correspondents based in Paris, to honour the best in French and French-language cinema each year. Founded in 1995 by journalist Edward Behr and producer Daniel Toscan du Plantier, the awards were first presented in 1996 and are often described as France's equivalent of the Golden Globe Awards, since voters are international journalists rather than industry members.

Categories

The principal categories include Best Film, Best Director, Best Screenplay, Best Actor, Best Actress, Most Promising Actor and Actress, Best First Film, Best Cinematography, Best Music, Best French-Language Film from outside France, and awards for documentary and animated films. An honorary Lumière is occasionally presented for career achievement.

History

The ceremony is traditionally held in Paris in January or February, ahead of the César Awards. Voters comprise more than a hundred international correspondents from dozens of countries reporting from France. Over the years the Lumières have recognised films such as La Haine, Amélie, The Artist and Portrait of a Lady on Fire, often foreshadowing César and international success.
