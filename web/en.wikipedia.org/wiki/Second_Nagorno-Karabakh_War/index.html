The Second Nagorno-Karabakh War was an armed conflict fought in the autumn of 2020 between Azerbaijan, supported by Turkey, and the self-declared Republic of Artsakh together with Armenia, over the disputed region of Nagorno-Karabakh. The 44 days of fighting were the largest escalation of the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict since the First War ended in 1994, and ended with a decisive Azerbaijani victory and a Russian-brokered ceasefire statement signed on 9–10 November 2020.

Course of the war

Fighting began on 27 September 2020 with an Azerbaijani offensive along the line of contact. Azerbaijan's campaign made extensive, highly publicised use of armed drones — Turkish Bayraktar TB2s and Israeli Harop loitering munitions — which destroyed large numbers of Armenian tanks, artillery, and air-defence systems and attracted worldwide military attention as a demonstration of unmanned warfare. Azerbaijani forces advanced steadily from the south along the Iranian border, took the town of Jabrayil and the city of Füzuli, and in early November captured Shusha (Shushi), the strategically and symbolically crucial city overlooking Stepanakert, the Artsakh capital.

Ceasefire and settlement

Under the statement signed by the leaders of Azerbaijan, Armenia, and Russia, Armenia agreed to return the districts surrounding Nagorno-Karabakh that it had held since 1994 (Aghdam, Kalbajar, and Lachin), while some 2,000 Russian peacekeepers deployed to guard the Lachin corridor linking Armenia to the reduced Artsakh territory. Thousands of soldiers and dozens of civilians were killed on both sides, and most of the Azerbaijani population displaced in the 1990s gained the possibility of return, while the region's Armenian population remained under blockade pressure. In September 2023, after a nine-month blockade, Azerbaijan launched a further offensive; Artsakh's authorities capitulated within a day, the republic was dissolved, and virtually its entire Armenian population — over 100,000 people — fled to Armenia.

Legacy

The war reshaped the South Caucasus: it restored Azerbaijani control over most of the disputed territories, reduced Russian influence despite its peacekeeping role, entrenched Turkey as a regional power, and became a case study in how commercial and military drones, precision strike, and information warfare had changed conventional conflict.
