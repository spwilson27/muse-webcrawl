Stagnation point

In fluid dynamics, a stagnation point is a point in a flow field where the local velocity of the fluid is zero. The fluid arriving at such a point is brought to rest (stagnates) and its kinetic energy is converted into pressure energy. Stagnation points occur on the surface of a body immersed in a moving stream — for example, at the nose of an aircraft fuselage or on the leading edge of a wing — where the flow divides to pass around the body.

Properties

By Bernoulli's principle, the pressure at a stagnation point on a body in incompressible flow is the maximum pressure on that body, equal to the stagnation pressure (total pressure) of the oncoming flow:

p0 = p + ½ρv²

where p is the static pressure of the free stream, ρ its density and v its velocity. This relationship is exploited by the pitot tube, which measures stagnation pressure through an opening facing the flow and thereby allows the airspeed of an aircraft to be determined.

Stagnation point flow

The flow in the neighbourhood of a stagnation point — stagnation point flow — is a classical subject of fluid mechanics. For flow against a flat plate, the exact solution of the Navier–Stokes equations found by Karl Hiemenz (1911) describes how the boundary layer at a stagnation point has a constant thickness along the surface, in contrast to the growing boundary layer of a flat plate. Axisymmetric stagnation flow against a disk was treated by Homann (1936). These solutions are standard test cases in the theory of viscous flow.

Kutta condition

In the aerodynamics of airfoils, the position of the rear stagnation point is central to the Kutta condition: for a lifting airfoil the flow adjusts so that it leaves the sharp trailing edge smoothly, which fixes the circulation around the airfoil and hence the lift.
