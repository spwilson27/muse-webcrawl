Möbius inversion formula

Möbius inversion formula
FieldNumber theory, combinatorics
Introduced byAugust Ferdinand Möbius (1832)

In mathematics, the classic Möbius inversion formula is a relation between pairs of arithmetic functions, each defined from the other by sums over divisors. It was introduced into number theory by August Ferdinand Möbius in 1832.

Statement

For arithmetic functions f and g, g(n) equals the sum of f(d) over all positive divisors d of n if and only if f(n) equals the sum of μ(d) g(n/d) over the divisors d of n, where μ is the Möbius function.

The Möbius function

The Möbius function μ(n) is zero if n has a squared prime factor, +1 if n is a product of an even number of distinct primes, and −1 if it is a product of an odd number of distinct primes.

Generalizations

The formula is a special case of inversion in incidence algebras of locally finite partially ordered sets, a framework developed by Gian-Carlo Rota, which unifies many classical inversion principles in combinatorics, including inclusion–exclusion.

Applications

Möbius inversion is used to derive formulas for Euler's totient function summations, counting aperiodic necklaces and irreducible polynomials over finite fields, and in the theory of Dirichlet series.
