Supergalactic plane

Nearby galaxy clusters are concentrated along the supergalactic plane.

The supergalactic plane is a reference plane in the supergalactic coordinate system, a celestial coordinate system used to describe the distribution of nearby galaxies. The plane contains the majority of local galaxy clusters, including the Virgo Cluster, the Great Attractor, and the Perseus–Pisces Supercluster.

Definition

The supergalactic plane was first defined by Gérard de Vaucouleurs in 1953 from observations that bright galaxies in the nearby universe appeared concentrated along a flattened band across the sky. The plane is roughly perpendicular to the plane of the Milky Way. In supergalactic coordinates, positions are given by supergalactic longitude (SGL) and latitude (SGB), with the plane lying at SGB = 0°.

Contents

The supergalactic plane is the plane of the Laniakea Supercluster basin of attraction and includes major structures such as the Virgo Supercluster (containing the Local Group), the Hydra–Centaurus Supercluster, and the Perseus–Pisces Supercluster. The relative emptiness above and below the plane reflects the presence of large cosmic voids.

Significance

The flattened distribution of nearby galaxies along the supergalactic plane is a manifestation of the cosmic web: matter in the universe is arranged in sheets and filaments surrounding vast voids, a pattern produced by gravitational collapse from nearly uniform initial conditions.
