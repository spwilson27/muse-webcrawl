Leon Feldhendler

Leon Feldhendler (Lejb Feldhendler; 1 June 1910 – 6 April 1945) was a Polish Jewish resistance fighter and one of the two leaders of the prisoner uprising at the Sobibor extermination camp on 14 October 1943, one of the most successful revolts in a Nazi death camp.

Early life and deportation

Feldhendler was born in Turobin, Poland, the son of a rabbi, and before the war headed the Jewish council (Judenrat) in Żółkiewka. In late 1942 he was deported to Sobibor, where he was selected for forced labour rather than being sent immediately to the gas chambers, working sorting the possessions of murdered victims.

The uprising

Within the camp, Feldhendler built a clandestine prisoner organisation and sought a plan for mass escape. The arrival in September 1943 of Soviet Jewish prisoners of war, among them Alexander Pechersky, provided military expertise; Feldhendler and Pechersky jointly planned the revolt. On 14 October 1943, prisoners secretly killed a number of SS officers and guards, seized weapons, and stormed the fences. About 300 of the roughly 600 prisoners escaped into the surrounding forest, though many were killed by mines, pursuit, or later betrayals; around 50 survived the war. The revolt led the SS to close and demolish Sobibor.

Death and legacy

Feldhendler survived the escape and hid in Lublin, where he lived under an assumed identity after the city's liberation. On 6 April 1945 he was shot through the door of his apartment by unknown assailants — widely believed to have been Polish nationalist partisans in an antisemitic attack — and died days later. He is portrayed in films about the uprising, including Escape from Sobibor (1987) and Sobibor (2018), and is honoured at the Sobibor memorial.
