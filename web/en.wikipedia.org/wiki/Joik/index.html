Joik

A joik performance
OriginSámi people, Sápmi (northern Europe)
TypeTraditional vocal music

A joik (also spelled yoik) is a traditional form of song of the Sámi people, the Indigenous people of Sápmi in northern Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Russia. It is one of the oldest surviving song traditions in Europe.

Characteristics

A joik is not usually a song about its subject; instead, the performer is understood to joik the subject itself — a person, an animal such as a reindeer, or a place — evoking its essence through melody, rhythm, and sparse words or vocables. Joiks may be deeply personal and are traditionally passed between people as a form of connection and remembrance.

History and revival

Joiking was suppressed during periods of Christian missionary pressure and assimilation policies, but survived and has undergone a strong revival since the late 20th century. Artists such as Mari Boine have brought joik to international audiences, often blending it with jazz, rock, and electronic music.
