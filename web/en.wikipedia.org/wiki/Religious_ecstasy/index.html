Religious ecstasy

Religious ecstasy is a state of trance or altered consciousness characterised by a sense of union with the divine, timelessness, and intense joy or awe. The word derives from the Greek ekstasis, "standing outside oneself".

Across traditions

Ecstatic experience is reported in many religions: the mystical raptures of Christian saints such as Teresa of Ávila, famously sculpted by Bernini; the dhikr and whirling of Sufi practice; ecstatic devotion (bhakti) in Hinduism; shamanic trance in indigenous religions; and speaking in tongues in Pentecostal Christianity.

Interpretation

Practices that induce ecstasy include prayer, meditation, chanting, dance, fasting and sensory deprivation. Psychologists and neuroscientists have studied the neural correlates of ecstatic states, while philosophers such as William James treated them as a core subject of The Varieties of Religious Experience (1902).
