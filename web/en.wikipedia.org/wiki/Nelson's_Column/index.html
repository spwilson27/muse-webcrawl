Nelson's Column is a monument in Trafalgar Square in the City of Westminster, Central London, built to commemorate Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson, who died at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805. The monument was constructed between 1840 and 1843 to a design by architect William Railton at a cost of £47,000 (equivalent to roughly £5.3 million in modern terms). It is a Corinthian column built from Dartmoor granite.

The statue of Nelson, carved from Craigleith sandstone by sculptor Edward Hodges Baily, stands 5.5 m (18 ft 1 in) tall atop the 46 m (151 ft) column. The four bronze lions around its base, designed by Sir Edwin Landseer, were added in 1867. The top of the column is decorated with a Corinthian capital copied from the Temple of Mars Ultor in Rome. Bronze relief panels at the base of the pedestal depict Nelson's four great victories: the Battle of Cape St Vincent, the Battle of the Nile, the Bombardment of Copenhagen, and his death at Trafalgar.

History

In February 1838 a group of 121 MPs, peers and other gentlemen formed the Nelson Memorial Committee to raise funds for a monument, ideally sited in Trafalgar Square. A design competition was held, won by William Railton with a Corinthian column surmounted by a statue of Nelson. Construction began in 1840. The column was completed and the statue raised in November 1843. The bronze reliefs on the pedestal were completed between 1849 and 1854, cast in part from captured French cannon. The famous lions, modelled by Landseer, were not installed until 1867.

Later history

During the Second World War the column was a symbol of British resistance; Adolf Hitler reportedly planned to relocate it to Berlin after a successful invasion. In May 2003 the BASE jumper Gary Connery parachuted from the top in a publicity stunt. The column underwent a £420,000 restoration in 2006, during which scaffolding allowed a survey showing the statue had shifted slightly. The column's height is traditionally used as a reference: it is said that if Nelson's statue could see, it would look towards the Admiralty.
