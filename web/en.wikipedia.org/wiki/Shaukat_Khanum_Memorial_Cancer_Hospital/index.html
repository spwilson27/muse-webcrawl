Shaukat Khanum Memorial Cancer Hospital

Shaukat Khanum Memorial Cancer Hospital and Research Centre (SKMCH&RC) is a charitable cancer hospital and research centre in Lahore, Punjab, Pakistan. It was founded by cricketer-turned-politician Imran Khan in memory of his mother, Shaukat Khanum, who died of cancer in 1985. The hospital opened on December 29, 1994, and a second hospital opened in Peshawar in 2015, with a third built in Karachi.

History

Imran Khan's experience of his mother's illness — and the difficulty of obtaining cancer treatment in Pakistan — motivated a nationwide fundraising campaign that began around the time of Pakistan's 1992 Cricket World Cup victory, which Khan dedicated to the hospital appeal. Donations of land came from the government of Punjab, and funds were raised from the public in Pakistan and the diaspora; appeals famously included contributions from schoolchildren. The Lahore hospital was constructed in Johar Town and became the flagship project of the Shaukat Khanum Memorial Trust.

Services

The hospital provides surgical, medical, and radiation oncology, diagnostic imaging, pathology, palliative care, and a walk-in clinic system that screens patients for treatment. A large share of patients — commonly reported at around 75 percent — receive financially supported or free treatment, funded through donations and zakat. The institution also runs cancer awareness campaigns, a research programme, and nursing and medical training; its laboratories achieved international accreditation, and the hospital has been accredited by Joint Commission International.

Other campuses

Shaukat Khanum Memorial Cancer Hospital in Peshawar opened in December 2015, and a Karachi hospital, intended to be the largest of the three, was constructed and opened in the 2020s. Diagnostic centres and collection points operate in numerous Pakistani cities.
