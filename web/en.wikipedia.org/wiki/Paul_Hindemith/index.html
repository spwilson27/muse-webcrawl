Paul Hindemith

Paul Hindemith (16 November 1895 – 28 December 1963) was a German composer, violist, teacher and conductor, one of the most significant German composers of the 20th century. His music combined modernist energy with a renewed engagement with counterpoint, tonality and older forms.

Life

Born in Hanau, Hindemith studied at Frankfurt's Hoch Conservatory, became leader of the Frankfurt Opera orchestra and later of the Amar Quartet, and gained fame in the 1920s as a leading figure of the avant-garde and of Gebrauchsmusik — music for practical use. He taught composition in Berlin from 1927. Although initially tolerated by the Nazi regime, his music was denounced as degenerate; his opera Mathis der Maler and conflicts with the authorities led to his emigration, first to Switzerland and then, in 1940, to the United States, where he taught at Yale. He returned to Europe after the war, settling in Switzerland and teaching in Zurich.

Works

Major works include the operas Cardillac, Mathis der Maler and Die Harmonie der Welt; the symphony Mathis der Maler; Symphonic Metamorphosis of Themes by Carl Maria von Weber; the song cycle Das Marienleben; the requiem for Walt Whitman; and sonatas for nearly every orchestral instrument. His theoretical writings, including The Craft of Musical Composition, set out his system of tonal organisation.
