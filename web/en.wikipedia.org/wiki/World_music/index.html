World music

World music is a broad musical category encompassing many different styles of music from around the globe, including traditional and folk music, as well as fusions of local traditions with Western popular music. The term was popularised in the 1980s as a marketing category for non-Western traditional music.

Origins of the term

The phrase was adopted at a meeting of independent record labels in London in 1987, when companies specialising in international folk and traditional recordings agreed on "world music" as a shared retail category. It soon gained its own charts, magazine coverage, and a Grammy Award category (Best World Music Album, first awarded in 1992; later split into traditional and contemporary categories).

Forms and criticism

World music includes the traditional musics of Africa, Asia, Latin America, the Middle East and indigenous peoples worldwide, alongside hybrid genres such as Afrobeat, reggae, bossa nova, raï, and Celtic fusion. Critics have argued that the category lumps together unrelated traditions and reflects a Western, market-driven viewpoint; ethnomusicologists prefer more specific terms. Nevertheless, festivals such as WOMAD (founded by Peter Gabriel in 1982) and labels such as Real World Records and Putumayo helped bring artists like Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, Youssou N'Dour, Buena Vista Social Club and Anoushka Shankar to global audiences.
