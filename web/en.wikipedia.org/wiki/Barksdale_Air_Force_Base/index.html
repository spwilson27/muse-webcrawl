Barksdale Air Force Base

Barksdale Air Force Base (Barksdale AFB) is a United States Air Force base in northwestern Louisiana, adjacent to Bossier City and near Shreveport. It is headquarters of Air Force Global Strike Command and home to the 2nd Bomb Wing, one of only two U.S. Air Force units operating the B-52H Stratofortress strategic bomber.

History

The base was established in the early 1930s on land acquired after Shreveport and Bossier City competed to host an Army Air Corps field, and it opened in 1933 as Barksdale Field, named for Lieutenant Eugene Hoy Barksdale, a World War I aviator and test pilot killed in a 1926 crash. During the Second World War it served as a major training base. It became an important Strategic Air Command bomber base during the Cold War, operating aircraft including the B-47, B-52, and KC-135. In 2009–2010 it became the headquarters of the newly created Air Force Global Strike Command, responsible for all U.S. Air Force nuclear-capable bombers and intercontinental ballistic missiles.

Units and role

The 2nd Bomb Wing flies the B-52H in both conventional and nuclear roles. The Air Force Reserve Command's 307th Bomb Wing is also based at Barksdale and likewise flies the B-52H. The base covers about 22,000 acres, including a large weapons storage area and extensive recreational land, and its runway is among the longest in the Air Force.
