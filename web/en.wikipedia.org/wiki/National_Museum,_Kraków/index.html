National Museum, Kraków

The National Museum in Kraków (Polish: Muzeum Narodowe w Krakowie), founded in 1879, is the oldest of Poland's national museums and the country's largest museum by number of objects, holding some 780,000 works of art and artefacts across its Main Building and numerous divisions and branches throughout the city.

Collections and branches

The museum was born when the painter Henryk Siemiradzki donated his celebrated canvas Nero's Torches to the partitioned, stateless Polish nation in 1879; its first home was the Cloth Hall (Sukiennice) in the Main Market Square, whose gallery of nineteenth-century Polish painting remains one of its divisions today. The monumental Main Building on Aleja 3 Maja, begun in 1934 and finally completed in 1992, houses permanent galleries of twentieth- and twenty-first-century Polish art, decorative arts, and military artefacts. Other divisions include the Czartoryski Museum — home to Leonardo da Vinci's Lady with an Ermine and Rembrandt's Landscape with the Good Samaritan — the Bishop Erazm Ciołek Palace of medieval art, the Matejko House, the Mehoffer House and the Emeryk Hutten-Czapski Museum of numismatics.

History

During the Second World War the museum was looted by the German occupation authorities and many works were taken to Germany; post-war recovery efforts retrieved much, but losses are still being traced. The Czartoryski collection was purchased by the Polish state in 2016 and formally donated to the National Museum in 2019, reuniting one of Europe's great princely collections with the institution that had long displayed it on loan.
