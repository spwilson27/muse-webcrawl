Lamellipodium

The lamellipodium (plural lamellipodia) is a cytoskeletal protein actin projection on the leading edge of the cell. It contains a quasi-two-dimensional actin mesh, and the whole structure propels the cell across a substrate. Within the lamellipodia are filiform structures called microspikes, which protrude from the lamellipodial actin network and are thought to drive locomotory responses.

Structure

Lamellipodia are found primarily in mobile cells, such as keratinocytes and fibroblasts. The lamellipodium is composed almost exclusively of actin filaments arranged in a branched network, largely oriented at roughly 70-degree angles to one another by the Arp2/3 complex. ATP hydrolysis powers the assembly of the actin filaments at the leading edge, pushing the plasma membrane forward.

Role in migration

As a cell migrates, the lamellipodium extends forward, attaches to the substrate through adhesions, and the cell body is pulled forward by contraction of the actomyosin network behind it. The lamellipodium is also involved in chemotaxis, sensing chemical gradients and guiding the cell toward them.
