Multigraph (orthography)

A multigraph is a sequence of letters that functions as a single unit in the orthography of a language, representing a single sound (phoneme) or a conventional combination of sounds. Multigraphs of two letters are called digraphs; those of three are trigraphs; longer sequences are sometimes termed tetragraphs, and so on.

Examples

English digraphs include ch, sh, th, ph, ng, and the vowel pairs ea, ie, ou; its best-known trigraph is sch in a few words and the tch spelling after short vowels. German uses the trigraph sch for /ʃ/ and the tetragraph dsch in loanwords. Hungarian has digraphs such as sz, zs, cs, gy, ly, ny, ty and the trigraph dzs, several of which count as separate letters of the alphabet. Dutch ij is often treated as a single letter.

Status in alphabets

In some languages, digraphs are counted as independent letters: Welsh ch, dd, ff, ll, ng, ph, rh, and th are alphabet letters, as historically was Spanish ch and ll. In other languages, a multigraph is merely a spelling convention without alphabetical status.
