A sonic boom is the sound associated with the shock waves created whenever an object travels through the air faster than the speed of sound. Sonic booms generate enormous amounts of sound energy, sounding similar to an explosion or a thunderclap.

Physics

As an aircraft travels, it creates a series of pressure waves in front of and behind it, similar to the bow and stern waves of a boat. These waves travel at the speed of sound; as the object's speed increases, the waves bunch together into shock cones. An observer hears nothing until the shock cone passes, then perceives a sudden pressure change — often a double boom from the nose and tail shocks, forming an "N-wave". The boom is generated continuously along the flight path, sweeping a "boom carpet" across the ground.

Effects and regulation

Sonic booms can startle people and animals, rattle windows, and occasionally cause minor damage. Public reaction to supersonic testing in the 1960s, notably over Oklahoma City, led the United States and other countries to prohibit routine supersonic flight over land, restricting Concorde to transoceanic routes. Research aircraft such as NASA's X-59 aim to shape the boom into a quieter "thump" that might allow overland supersonic travel.

Similar phenomena

The crack of a bullwhip is a small-scale sonic boom from its tip, and the shock waves of meteors and re-entering spacecraft can also produce booms.
