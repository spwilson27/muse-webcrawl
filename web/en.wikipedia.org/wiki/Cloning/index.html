Cloning

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cloning is the process of producing genetically identical copies of a biological entity. The copies — clones — may be genes (molecular cloning), cells, or whole organisms. Cloning occurs naturally (identical twins, asexual reproduction, bacterial colonies) and is also a set of laboratory techniques central to Biotechnology.

Types

In molecular cloning, a DNA fragment is inserted into a vector such as a plasmid and copied inside bacteria. In reproductive cloning by somatic cell nuclear transfer (SCNT), the nucleus of an adult cell is placed into an egg whose own nucleus was removed; the resulting embryo, implanted in a surrogate, develops into an animal genetically matching the nucleus donor — the route that produced Dolly the sheep in 1996. Therapeutic cloning aims instead to derive patient-matched stem cells.

Ethics and applications

Cloning is used to multiply elite livestock and endangered species, produce research animals and copy prized plants (most crops are effectively clones). Human reproductive cloning is widely condemned and banned in many countries, on safety and ethical grounds; no verified human clone has been born. Debates continue over de-extinction projects that would clone or approximate extinct species.
