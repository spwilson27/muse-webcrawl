Nephi is a central figure in the Book of Mormon, a religious text published by Joseph Smith in 1830 and regarded as scripture by members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and other Latter Day Saint movement denominations. In the narrative, Nephi is the fourth son of the prophet Lehi, who led his family out of Jerusalem around 600 BC, across the Arabian desert and ocean, to a "promised land" in the Americas. Nephi is presented as the righteous younger brother opposed by his rebellious elder brothers Laman and Lemuel, and as the author of the first two books of the Book of Mormon, First Nephi and Second Nephi; his people, the Nephites, are named after him.

Narrative

According to his record, Nephi accompanied his brothers back to Jerusalem to obtain the brass plates — a record of scripture and genealogy — from the official Laban, whom Nephi, after finding him drunken and unconscious, slew with Laban's own sword at divine command. Nephi later recovered the Liahona, a compass-like instrument, led his brothers in building a ship under divine direction, and helped guide the colony across the ocean. After Lehi's death, conflict with Laman and Lemuel caused Nephi and his followers to separate and settle a land they called Nephi, where he was made king or teacher and began keeping the "small plates" record that became his books.

Second Nephi contains Nephi's sermons and extensive quotations from Isaiah, as well as a vision of the tree of life first recorded by his father. After Nephi, the name was borne by several later Book of Mormon figures, including prophets of the first century BC and a disciple of Jesus in the narrative of Christ's visit to the Americas (Third Nephi).

Significance

Nephi is one of the most prominent figures in Latter-day Saint scripture and teaching; his declaration "I will go and do the things which the Lord hath commanded" (1 Nephi 3:7) is among the most quoted verses in the Book of Mormon. The city of Nephi, Utah, founded in 1851, is named after him.
