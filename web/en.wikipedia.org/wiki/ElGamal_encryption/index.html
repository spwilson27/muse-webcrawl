In cryptography, the ElGamal encryption system is an asymmetric key encryption algorithm for public-key cryptography which is based on the Diffie–Hellman key exchange. It was described by Taher Elgamal in 1985. ElGamal encryption is used in the free GNU Privacy Guard software, in older versions of PGP, and in other cryptosystems. The Digital Signature Algorithm (DSA) is a variant of the ElGamal signature scheme, not to be confused with ElGamal encryption itself.

The algorithm

ElGamal consists of three components: key generation, encryption, and decryption.

Key generation. Alice generates an efficient description of a cyclic group G of order q with generator g. She chooses a random private key x and computes the public key h = gx. She publishes (G, q, g, h) and keeps x secret.

Encryption. To encrypt a message m for Alice, Bob chooses a random ephemeral value y, computes c1 = gy and the shared secret s = hy, and sends the ciphertext (c1, c2 = m · s).

Decryption. Alice computes s = c1x and recovers m = c2 · s−1, since c1x = gxy = hy.

Security

The security of ElGamal depends on the difficulty of the discrete logarithm problem and on the decisional Diffie–Hellman assumption: under that assumption the scheme is semantically secure against chosen-plaintext attack. However, the basic scheme is malleable and is not secure against chosen-ciphertext attack; practical systems use padded or hybrid variants. A critical implementation requirement is that the ephemeral value y must be fresh and random for every message: reusing it reveals information that can expose other messages.

Properties and variants

ElGamal is probabilistic — encrypting the same plaintext twice yields different ciphertexts — and it expands the message by a factor of two. The scheme generalises to any cyclic group, including elliptic curves. Hybrid encryption schemes such as DHIES/ECIES combine an ElGamal-like key exchange with symmetric encryption for efficiency.
