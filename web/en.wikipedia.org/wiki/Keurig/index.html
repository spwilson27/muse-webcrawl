Keurig is an American beverage brewing system for home and commercial use, known for its single-serve coffee makers and proprietary portion packs called K-Cup pods. The machines brew coffee, tea, hot chocolate, and other beverages by piercing a sealed pod and forcing hot water through it. Keurig is part of Keurig Dr Pepper, formed in 2018 by the merger of Keurig Green Mountain with the Dr Pepper Snapple Group.

History

Keurig was founded in Waitsfield, Vermont, in 1992 by John Sylvan and Peter Dragone; the name was taken from a Danish word for "excellence" that Sylvan found in a dictionary. Their aim was to solve the problem of office coffee pots that sat stewing for hours. The first brewer, the B2000, was aimed at offices and launched in 1998. Investor Green Mountain Coffee Roasters acquired a controlling stake in 1996 and the whole company in 2006, renaming itself Keurig Green Mountain in 2014. In 2016 the company was taken private by JAB Holding Company for about $13.9 billion; the 2018 merger with Dr Pepper Snapple created Keurig Dr Pepper, one of North America's largest beverage companies.

The K-Cup system

A K-Cup is a plastic cup sealed with foil, containing ground coffee and a paper filter; the brewer pierces the lid and base, and water passes through in about a minute. Licensed K-Cups are sold under many coffee brands, and the system's convenience drove very rapid adoption in the 2000s and 2010s, though the restrictive "razor and blades" model and the pods' plastic waste drew criticism and clone competitors. The Keurig 2.0 brewers of 2014 added digital rights management to reject unlicensed pods, a decision reversed after consumer backlash and falling sales. Reusable refillable filters are widely sold as an alternative.

Environmental criticism

Billions of pods are discarded annually and most are not readily recyclable in curbside programs; co-founder John Sylvan has publicly expressed regret about the waste. The company introduced recyclable polypropylene pods from 2016 and has committed to fully recyclable or compostable formats, while critics note that separation of pod components remains a barrier.
