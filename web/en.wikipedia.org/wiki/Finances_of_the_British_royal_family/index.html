The finances of the British royal family come from a number of sources. Public funding for the official duties of the monarch and members of the royal family is provided principally through the Sovereign Grant, while the monarch also receives private income from the Duchy of Lancaster and owns assets such as Sandringham House and Balmoral Castle in a private capacity. The Prince of Wales receives income from the Duchy of Cornwall.

Sovereign Grant

The Sovereign Grant replaced the Civil List and other grants-in-aid in 2012 under the Sovereign Grant Act 2011. It is calculated as a percentage of the surplus revenue of the Crown Estate, a large portfolio of land and property held in right of the Crown. The grant funds official travel, property maintenance of the occupied royal palaces, and the payroll of the Royal Household.

Duchies

The Duchy of Lancaster is a portfolio of land, property, and assets held in trust for the sovereign; its net income forms the Privy Purse, used for both official and private expenses. The Duchy of Cornwall, established in 1337, provides income to the Duke of Cornwall, the monarch's eldest son and heir, funding his public, charitable, and private activities.

Taxation

Since 1993 the monarch has voluntarily paid income tax and capital gains tax on private income, following arrangements set out in a memorandum of understanding with the government. The royal family is exempt from some taxes by statute, and inheritance arrangements for sovereign-to-sovereign transfers have been a subject of public debate.

Estimates of wealth

Estimates of the personal wealth of the monarch vary widely, in part because assets such as the Royal Collection and the occupied palaces are held in trust for the nation and cannot be sold. Rich lists typically value the monarch's private fortune in the hundreds of millions of pounds.
