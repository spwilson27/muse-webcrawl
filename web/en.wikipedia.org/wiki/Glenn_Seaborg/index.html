Glenn Seaborg

Glenn Theodore Seaborg (April 19, 1912 – February 25, 1999) was an American chemist whose involvement in the synthesis, discovery and investigation of ten transuranium elements earned him a share in the 1951 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

BornApril 19, 1912, Ishpeming, Michigan
DiedFebruary 25, 1999, Lafayette, California
AwardsNobel Prize in Chemistry (1951)
Known forTransuranium elements, actinide concept

Work

At UC Berkeley and during the Manhattan Project, Seaborg co-discovered plutonium, americium, curium, berkelium and californium, and contributed to others. His actinide concept rearranged the periodic table, placing actinides below lanthanides.

Legacy

Element 106, seaborgium, was named in his honour — unusually while he was still living. He chaired the Atomic Energy Commission (1961–1971) and advised ten US presidents. See Radioactive decay and Periodic table.
