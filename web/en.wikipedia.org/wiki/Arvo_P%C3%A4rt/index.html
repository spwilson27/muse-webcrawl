Arvo Pärt (born 11 September 1935) is an Estonian classical composer of contemporary and sacred music. Since the late 1970s he has worked in a distinctive minimalist style he calls tintinnabuli, and he is among the most performed living composers in the world.

Early career

Pärt studied in Tallinn and worked as a sound engineer for Estonian Radio. His early works ranged from neoclassicism to serialism and collage techniques; his Credo (1968) provoked official disapproval in the Soviet Union. After a period of near-silence spent studying early music, he emerged with the piano piece Für Alina (1976).

Tintinnabuli

The tintinnabuli technique pairs a melodic voice moving stepwise with a second voice sounding only the notes of a tonic triad, evoking the ringing of bells. Works in this style include Fratres, Spiegel im Spiegel, Tabula Rasa, the Berlin Mass, and the Passio. Pärt emigrated from the Soviet Union in 1980, living in Vienna and Berlin before returning to Estonia, where the Arvo Pärt Centre opened in Laulasmaa in 2018.
