Jean Dieudonné

Jean Alexandre Eugène Dieudonné (1 July 1906 – 29 November 1992) was a French mathematician, known for his work in abstract algebra and functional analysis, and for his close association with the Bourbaki group.

Career

Born in Lille, Dieudonné studied at the École Normale Supérieure. He was a founding member and the principal scribe of Nicolas Bourbaki, drafting much of the group's Éléments de mathématique. With Laurent Schwartz he founded the theory of distributions' rival framework through his work on topological vector spaces.

Writings

His solo works include the multi-volume Éléments d'analyse, a history of algebraic and differential topology, and writings on the history of mathematics. He taught in France, Brazil, and the United States. See also Nicolas Bourbaki.
