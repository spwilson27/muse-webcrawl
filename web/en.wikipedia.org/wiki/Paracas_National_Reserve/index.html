The Paracas National Reserve (Spanish: Reserva Nacional de Paracas) is a protected area in the Ica Region of Peru, encompassing desert, coastline and marine waters around the Paracas Peninsula, about 250 km south of Lima. Established in 1975, it protects one of the richest marine ecosystems on the Peruvian coast as well as the archaeological legacy of the ancient Paracas culture.

Geography and climate

The reserve covers some 335,000 hectares, of which roughly two-thirds is ocean. The land portion is coastal desert of the Atacama–Sechura system, among the driest places on Earth, shaped by strong southerly winds — the paracas winds Quechua-named and famous for sandstorms — and by the cold Humboldt Current offshore, which drives extraordinary marine productivity.

Wildlife

The reserve shelters South American sea lions, Humboldt penguins, Peruvian boobies, Inca terns, guanay cormorants and Andean condors that descend to scavenge on the shore, as well as marine otters and dolphins offshore. Its waters support anchoveta and other fisheries of national importance. Nearby — outside the reserve boundary but visited from the same port — the Ballestas Islands host one of Peru's great seabird colonies, historically mined for guano.

Cultural heritage

The peninsula was home to the Paracas culture (c. 800–100 BC), renowned for its embroidered funerary textiles preserved by the desert climate. On the northern flank of the peninsula lies the Paracas Candelabra, a giant hillside geoglyph some 180 m long whose date and purpose remain debated. The reserve also figured in the independence era: General José de San Martín's liberating expedition landed at Paracas in September 1820.

Tourism and threats

Boat tours to the Ballestas Islands and circuits of the peninsula's red beaches, cathedral-like rock formations and the Julio C. Tello site museum make Paracas one of Peru's most visited protected areas. Pressures include industrial fishing, port development, unregulated tourism and pollution; the reserve is administered by SERNANP, Peru's protected-areas service, and is listed as a Ramsar wetland of international importance.
