Website

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Collage-style image of several well-known website homepages as browser windows]

A website is a collection of web pages and related content that is identified by a common domain name and published on at least one web server, accessible via the World Wide Web. Examples of notable websites are Wikipedia, Google, Amazon and YouTube.

A website is typically dedicated to a particular topic or purpose, such as news, education, commerce, entertainment or social networking; hyperlinking between its pages guides the visitor's navigation, usually starting from a home page. Websites can be the work of an individual, a business or an organisation, and are hosted on servers ranging from a single home computer — the first sites of the early 1990s — to vast cloud platforms serving billions of requests.

The first website, built by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN, went live in 1991 to explain the web project itself. By the mid-1990s the web had commercialised explosively (the 'dot-com boom'), and websites became the default public face of organisations. Today there are on the order of two billion websites, though only a fraction are actively maintained; the rest are parked, abandoned or forgotten corners of the largest information system ever built.
