Dissertation

A dissertation or thesis is a document submitted in support of candidature for an academic degree or professional qualification presenting the author's research and findings. In some contexts, the word "thesis" or a cognate is used for part of a bachelor's or master's course, while "dissertation" is normally applied to a doctorate; usage varies between countries and institutions.

Structure

A typical dissertation contains a title page, abstract, table of contents, an introduction, a literature review, chapters presenting methodology and results, a discussion or conclusion, and a bibliography. Scientific dissertations commonly follow the pattern of introduction, methods, results, and discussion.

The defence

Many institutions require the candidate to defend the dissertation in an oral examination — the viva voce in the United Kingdom, the Disputation in Germany, or the defence in North America — before a committee of examiners. Outcomes range from outright pass to required corrections or, rarely, failure.

Terminology by country

In the United States, "dissertation" usually denotes the doctoral work and "thesis" the master's work; in the United Kingdom and Commonwealth the usage is largely reversed. In France, the thèse is the doctoral work. Historically the terms descend from the medieval university disputation and Latin dissertatio.

Submission and publication

Candidates usually deposit printed or digital copies with the university library. Many doctoral dissertations are indexed by services such as ProQuest Dissertations & Theses, and open-access repositories have made an increasing share freely available online.
