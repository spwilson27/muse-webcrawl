Maha Bodhi Society
FoundedMay 31, 1891, Colombo, Ceylon
FounderAnagarika Dharmapala
HeadquartersKolkata, India
FocusBuddhist revival, restoration of Buddhist holy sites in India

The Maha Bodhi Society is a South Asian Buddhist society founded by the Sri Lankan Buddhist leader Anagarika Dharmapala on May 31, 1891, in Colombo. Its original goal was the preservation and recovery of Buddhist control of the Mahabodhi Temple at Bodh Gaya in India, the site of the Buddha's enlightenment, which had passed into the hands of a Hindu mahant. The society became the institutional centre of the modern Buddhist revival movement in India and Ceylon (Sri Lanka).

History

Dharmapala visited Bodh Gaya in 1891 and was shocked by the temple's neglected condition and by the worship of the Buddha image as a Hindu deity. He founded the society that same year, initially as the Buddhagaya Maha Bodhi Society, and soon moved its headquarters to Calcutta to be nearer the seat of British Indian government and its legal campaigns. The society published journals, including The Maha-Bodhi (founded 1892), among the earliest English-language Buddhist periodicals.

Activities

The society built temples and rest houses for pilgrims at Buddhist sites including Sarnath — where it constructed the Mulagandha Kuti Vihara, opened in 1931 — as well as schools, hospitals, and libraries. It campaigned for decades for Buddhist administration of the Mahabodhi Temple, an aim partially achieved with the Bodh Gaya Temple Act of 1949, which created a joint Buddhist-Hindu management committee. The society also promoted the revival of Buddhism in India, a movement that later converged with the Dalit Buddhist movement inspired by B. R. Ambedkar, who was closely associated with the society in his final years.
