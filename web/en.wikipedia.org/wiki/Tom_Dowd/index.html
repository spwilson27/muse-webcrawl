Tom Dowd (October 20, 1925 – October 27, 2002) was an American recording engineer and producer for Atlantic Records. He was credited with innovating the multitrack recording process and the use of stereophonic sound, and shaped landmark records across jazz, R&B, soul, and rock.

Early career

A physics prodigy, Dowd worked on the Manhattan Project as a young man before turning to music. At Atlantic he engineered sessions for artists including Ray Charles, Aretha Franklin, Otis Redding, John Coltrane, and Ornette Coleman.

Innovations

Dowd pushed Atlantic to adopt the Ampex eight-track recorder and helped popularize faders over rotary knobs on mixing consoles. In Florida's Criteria Studios he recorded Eric Clapton's Derek and the Dominos album Layla and Other Assorted Love Songs and worked with the Allman Brothers Band and Lynyrd Skynyrd.

Recognition

He received a Grammy Trustees Award in 2002 and was the subject of the 2003 documentary Tom Dowd & the Language of Music.
