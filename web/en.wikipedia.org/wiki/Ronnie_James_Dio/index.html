Ronnie James Dio

Born: Ronald James Padavona, July 10, 1942, Portsmouth, New Hampshire, U.S.
Died: May 16, 2010 (aged 67), Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Genres: Heavy metal, hard rock
Occupations: Singer, songwriter
Years active: 1957–2010

Ronnie James Dio (born Ronald James Padavona; July 10, 1942 – May 16, 2010) was an American heavy metal singer and songwriter. Over the course of his career he fronted or founded numerous bands including Elf, Rainbow, Black Sabbath, Dio, and Heaven & Hell. He is credited with popularising the "devil horns" (mano cornuta) hand gesture in metal culture.

Early life

Dio was born in Portsmouth, New Hampshire, to an Italian-American family, and grew up in Cortland, New York. He began playing trumpet as a child and formed his first band, the Vegas Kings, in 1957. He briefly attended the University at Buffalo, majoring in pharmacy, before pursuing music full time.

Career

In the early 1970s Dio fronted the rock band Elf, which opened for Deep Purple. Guitarist Ritchie Blackmore recruited him to co-found Rainbow in 1975, producing albums such as Rising (1976). Dio joined Black Sabbath in 1979, replacing Ozzy Osbourne, and recorded the acclaimed albums Heaven and Hell (1980) and Mob Rules (1981). He then formed his own band, Dio, whose debut Holy Diver (1983) became a metal landmark, followed by The Last in Line (1984) and Sacred Heart (1985).

Death and legacy

Dio died of stomach cancer on May 16, 2010. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential heavy metal vocalists of all time, known for his powerful operatic voice and fantasy-themed lyrics.
