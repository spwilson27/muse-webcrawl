José Sánchez del Río

Saint José Sánchez del Río (28 March 1913 - 10 February 1928) was a Mexican Catholic martyr who was killed at the age of fourteen during the Cristero War, the rebellion against the anti-clerical laws of President Plutarco Elías Calles. He was canonized by Pope Francis in 2016.

Life

José was born in Sahuayo, Michoacán. When the Cristero War broke out in 1926, his older brothers joined the rebel forces fighting the federal government's enforcement of the Calles Law, which restricted Catholic worship. José, too young to fight, begged his mother and the Cristero general Prudencio Mendoza for permission to join; he was eventually accepted as a flag bearer and camp assistant.

Capture and martyrdom

In January 1928 José was captured by government troops during a battle in which he gave his horse to the fleeing general Guízar Morfín. Imprisoned in Sahuayo, he was pressured to renounce his faith and join the federal army; he refused. According to the accounts on which his cause rests, on 10 February 1928 he was tortured - the soles of his feet were cut - and forced to walk to the cemetery, where he was stabbed and shot, dying while crying out "Viva Cristo Rey" ("Long live Christ the King").

Canonization

José Sánchez del Río was beatified by Pope Benedict XVI in 2005, along with other Cristero martyrs, and canonized by Pope Francis on 16 October 2016. His story was dramatized in the 2012 film For Greater Glory. He is venerated as a patron of persecuted Christians and of adolescents.
