D'Alembert's formula

D'Alembert's formula, named after the French mathematician Jean le Rond d'Alembert, gives the general solution of the one-dimensional homogeneous wave equation, a second-order linear partial differential equation.

Statement

For the wave equation utt − c²uxx = 0 on the real line, with initial displacement f and initial velocity g, the solution is u(x,t) = ½[f(x−ct) + f(x+ct)] + (1/2c) ∫x−ctx+ct g(s) ds. The solution appears as the sum of two waves travelling left and right at speed c.

Method

The derivation uses characteristic coordinates ξ = x − ct and η = x + ct, which reduce the equation to ∂²u/∂ξ∂η = 0, integrable directly. The formula illustrates the finite speed of propagation and the domain of dependence central to hyperbolic equations.
