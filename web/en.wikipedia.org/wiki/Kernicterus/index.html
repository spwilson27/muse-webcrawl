Kernicterus

Kernicterus
SpecialtyPediatrics, neurology
CauseSeverely elevated unconjugated bilirubin in newborns

Kernicterus is a type of brain damage that occurs in a newborn with severe, untreated jaundice (hyperbilirubinemia). It arises when high levels of unconjugated bilirubin cross the blood–brain barrier and deposit in the basal ganglia and brainstem nuclei, staining them yellow (German Kern, "nucleus," hence the name) and causing permanent injury. The condition is largely preventable with modern monitoring and treatment, and is now rare in high-income countries.

Causes and risk factors

Newborn jaundice is common because infants produce bilirubin faster than their immature livers can conjugate it. Kernicterus risk rises sharply when bilirubin climbs to very high levels, especially with hemolysis from ABO or Rh blood group incompatibility, glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency, infection, bruising or cephalhematoma, and prematurity, in which the blood–brain barrier is more permeable and albumin binding lower. The classical setting was hemolytic disease of the newborn due to Rh incompatibility, greatly reduced by anti-D immunoglobulin prophylaxis.

Clinical features

The acute phase, bilirubin encephalopathy, presents with lethargy, poor feeding, hypotonia progressing to hypertonia, arching of the back (retrocollis and opisthotonus), fever, and a high-pitched cry. Chronic kernicterus leaves a characteristic pattern of injury: choreoathetoid cerebral palsy, sensorineural hearing loss, paralysis of upward gaze, and enamel dysplasia of the teeth, with intellect often relatively spared. This combination distinguishes it from many other causes of neonatal brain injury.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention rests on systematic assessment of jaundice, transcutaneous or serum bilirubin measurement plotted on hour-specific nomograms, and timely treatment. Phototherapy converts bilirubin into water-soluble isomers excreted without conjugation; severe or rapidly rising levels are treated with intravenous immunoglobulin or exchange transfusion. Once established, kernicteric brain damage is irreversible, making the condition a paradigm of a preventable cause of disability.
