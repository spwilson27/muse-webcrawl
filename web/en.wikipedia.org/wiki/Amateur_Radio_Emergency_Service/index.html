Amateur Radio Emergency Service

Amateur Radio Emergency Service (ARES)
Founded1935 (as the ARRL Emergency Corps)
SponsorAmerican Radio Relay League (ARRL)
PurposeVolunteer emergency communications
RegionUnited States and Canada

The Amateur Radio Emergency Service (ARES) is a corps of trained amateur radio operator volunteers organised by the American Radio Relay League (ARRL) to provide emergency communications support to public service agencies, disaster relief organisations and communities when normal communications fail or are overloaded. ARES members use their own equipment and frequencies allocated to the amateur radio service.


ARES volunteers staffing a radio position during a disaster exercise

Organisation

ARES operates under ARRL field organisation: each ARRL Section has a Section Emergency Coordinator, under whom District and local Emergency Coordinators recruit and train volunteers in counties and municipalities. Groups typically hold weekly radio nets, train in message handling and incident command (ICS/NIMS), and maintain memoranda of understanding with served agencies such as county emergency management offices, hospitals, the American Red Cross and the National Weather Service (through the SKYWARN programme).

Activities

ARES groups are activated for hurricanes, floods, wildfires, earthquakes, tornadoes and winter storms, passing health-and-welfare traffic, shelter reports and logistical messages when telephone and internet services are down. Notable activations include the aftermath of Hurricane Katrina (2005), Hurricane Maria in Puerto Rico (2017) — where ARRL deployed volunteer "Force of 50" operators — and numerous tornado outbreaks and floods. ARES also supports planned events such as marathons and parades. Annual training events include the ARRL Field Day emergency-preparedness exercise and the Simulated Emergency Test each autumn.

Relation to other programmes

Where amateur operators are formally enrolled as government disaster workers, they typically serve through RACES (Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service), regulated by FEMA and state/local emergency management, while ARES is the ARRL's broader community-service framework; many volunteers participate in both. In Canada, the Radio Amateurs of Canada sponsor a parallel ARES organisation.
