1958 Lituya Bay earthquake and megatsunami

Date9 July 1958
Magnitude7.8–8.3 Mw
LocationLituya Bay, Alaska, United States

Fatalities5

The 1958 Lituya Bay earthquake occurred on 9 July 1958 along the Fairweather Fault in the Alaska Panhandle. The earthquake triggered a massive rockslide of about 30 million cubic metres into Gilbert Inlet at the head of Lituya Bay, generating a megatsunami whose run-up reached 524 metres (1,720 feet) on the opposite shore — the highest wave run-up ever documented.

The wave

The displaced water surged up the mountainside across from the slide, stripping trees and soil down to bedrock up to the 524-metre trimline, then swept through the bay and out to sea. Three fishing boats were in the bay: one was sunk with the loss of two people, while two others famously rode out the wave — one anchored boat was carried over the submerged spit and its occupants watched trees passing beneath them. Five people died in total.

Scientific significance

Geologist Don Miller of the U.S. Geological Survey investigated the bay soon after the event and recognised that similar trimlines from earlier giant waves (in 1854, 1899, and 1936) showed Lituya Bay was prone to recurring megatsunamis caused by rockfalls. The event became the classic case study of landslide-generated impulse waves and is central to the modern understanding of megatsunami hazards in confined fjords and bays.
