Nuclear-weapon-free zone

A nuclear-weapon-free zone (NWFZ) is an area in which nuclear weapons are prohibited by treaty or other legal agreement, and whose status is recognised by the United Nations. Within such a zone, states parties commit not to develop, acquire, station or test nuclear weapons, and nuclear-weapon states typically give negative security assurances not to use or threaten to use nuclear weapons against them.

Existing zones

Five regional zones cover most of the Southern Hemisphere and Central Asia: Latin America and the Caribbean (Treaty of Tlatelolco, 1967), the South Pacific (Treaty of Rarotonga, 1985), Southeast Asia (Treaty of Bangkok, 1995), Africa (Treaty of Pelindaba, 1996), and Central Asia (Treaty of Semipalatinsk, 2006). Antarctica (1959), outer space (1967) and the seabed (1971) are also demilitarised of nuclear weapons, and Mongolia's single-state nuclear-weapon-free status has been internationally recognised. New Zealand additionally declared itself nuclear-free by domestic legislation in 1987.

Characteristics

The UN General Assembly's 1975 definition requires that a zone be established by agreement among the states of the region, prohibit all nuclear explosive devices, include a verification system (usually involving the IAEA), and be recognised by the nuclear-weapon states through protocols. Together, the zones cover more than 100 countries. Proposals for further zones — most prominently a Middle East zone free of weapons of mass destruction — have been discussed for decades but remain unrealised.
