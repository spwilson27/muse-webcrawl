Yang di-Pertuan Agong

StyleHis Majesty
ResidenceIstana Negara, Kuala Lumpur
TermFive years, non-renewable consecutively
Formation31 August 1957

The Yang di-Pertuan Agong (Jawi: يڠ دڤرتوان اݢوڠ), informally known as the King of Malaysia, is the constitutional monarch and head of state of Malaysia. The office is unique in that the Agong is elected for a five-year term by and from among the nine hereditary rulers of the Malay states, acting in the Conference of Rulers.

Role and election

The Agong acts largely on the advice of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, but holds discretionary powers including appointing a Prime Minister, withholding consent to dissolve Parliament, and granting pardons. The election follows a customary rotation among the rulers of Negeri Sembilan, Selangor, Perlis, Terengganu, Kedah, Kelantan, Pahang, Johor, and Perak. The Deputy Agong (Timbalan Yang di-Pertuan Agong) acts during absences or vacancies.

History

The institution was created at independence in 1957 for the Federation of Malaya and continued when Malaysia was formed in 1963. The first Agong was Tuanku Abdul Rahman of Negeri Sembilan.
