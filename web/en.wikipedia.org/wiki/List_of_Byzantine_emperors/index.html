List of Byzantine emperors

The Byzantine emperors ruled the Eastern Roman Empire from the foundation of Constantinople in 330 AD until its fall in 1453. The list conventionally begins with Constantine the Great, the first emperor to rule from Constantinople, and ends with Constantine XI Palaiologos, who died defending the city against the Ottoman Turks.

Theodosian and Leonid dynasties

Theodosius I (379–395) was the last emperor to rule a united empire. He was followed in the East by Arcadius (395–408), Theodosius II (408–450), Marcian (450–457), Leo I (457–474), Leo II (474), Zeno (474–491), Basiliscus (475–476, usurper) and Anastasius I (491–518).

Justinian dynasty

Justin I (518–527); Justinian I (527–565), under whom the empire reconquered North Africa, Italy and part of Spain and codified Roman law; Justin II (565–578); Tiberius II Constantine (578–582); Maurice (582–602).

Heraclian dynasty

Phocas (602–610); Heraclius (610–641), who defeated Persia but lost Syria and Egypt to the Arabs; Constantine III and Heraclonas (641); Constans II (641–668); Constantine IV (668–685); Justinian II (685–695, 705–711).

Later dynasties

Notable later emperors include Leo III the Isaurian (717–741), founder of the Isaurian dynasty; Constantine V (741–775); Irene of Athens (797–802); Nikephoros I (802–811); the Amorian Michael II (820–829) and Theophilos (829–842); the Macedonian dynasty's Basil I (867–886), Leo VI the Wise (886–912), Constantine VII (913–959) and Basil II (976–1025), under whom the empire reached its medieval height; the Komnenoi Isaac I (1057–1059), Alexios I (1081–1118), John II (1118–1143) and Manuel I (1143–1180); and the Palaiologoi, from Michael VIII (1261–1282), who recovered Constantinople from the Latin Empire, to the last emperor, Constantine XI (1449–1453).
