Irish Republican Army (1919–1922)

Active1917–1922
CountryIrish Republic
TypeGuerrilla army
Key commandersMichael Collins, Richard Mulcahy, Cathal Brugha

The Irish Republican Army (IRA) of 1919–1922, retrospectively known as the Old IRA, was a guerrilla army that fought the Irish War of Independence against British rule. It grew out of the Irish Volunteers, founded in 1913, and the remnants of the forces that had taken part in the Easter Rising of 1916.

War of Independence

In January 1919, deputies elected for Sinn Féin constituted themselves as Dáil Éireann and declared an Irish Republic. The IRA, under the political authority of the Dáil (to which it swore allegiance in August 1919), waged a campaign of ambushes, raids for arms, and assassinations of police and intelligence personnel. Its flying columns, and the intelligence network directed by Michael Collins, were central to the campaign, particularly in Dublin, Cork, Tipperary and Longford. British forces responded with the Black and Tans and Auxiliaries, whose reprisals included the burning of Cork and Bloody Sunday, 21 November 1920.

Truce and Treaty split

A truce in July 1921 led to the Anglo-Irish Treaty of December 1921, which established the Irish Free State as a dominion and partitioned Ireland. The IRA split over the Treaty: the pro-Treaty majority became the nucleus of the National Army, while anti-Treaty forces retained the IRA name and fought the Irish Civil War of 1922–1923. Later organisations using the name IRA claimed descent from the anti-Treaty side.
