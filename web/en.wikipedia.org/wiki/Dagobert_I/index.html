Dagobert I (c. 603/605 – 19 January 639) was King of Austrasia from 623 and King of all the Franks from 629 until his death. He was the last Merovingian king to exercise real personal authority over a united Frankish kingdom, and is remembered as one of the most powerful Merovingian rulers.

Reign

The son of Chlothar II, Dagobert was made king of Austrasia as a youth and succeeded his father in Neustria and Burgundy in 629, reuniting the Frankish realm. He moved his capital to Paris, campaigned against the Wends (defeated at Wogastisburg in 631/632 during Samo's revolt), intervened in Lombard and Visigothic affairs, and patronized the church and the abbey of Saint-Denis.

Administration and legacy

Dagobert relied on able ministers and bishops such as Eligius (Saint Éloi), the goldsmith who became his treasurer, and Audoin (Saint Ouen). Later tradition remembered him fondly in the French folk song "Le bon roi Dagobert". He was the first Frankish king buried in the royal necropolis of Saint-Denis. After his death, the kingdom was divided between his sons Sigebert III and Clovis II, and royal power gradually passed to the mayors of the palace.
