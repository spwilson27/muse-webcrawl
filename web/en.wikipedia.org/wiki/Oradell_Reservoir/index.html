The Oradell Reservoir is a reservoir formed by the Oradell Dam on the Hackensack River in Bergen County, New Jersey, in the northeastern United States. It is a major source of drinking water for northern New Jersey, supplying water to hundreds of thousands of residents in Bergen and Hudson counties. The reservoir is owned and operated by Veolia Water (formerly Suez North America / United Water).

Geography and history

The reservoir was created in the early 1920s when the Hackensack Water Company dammed the Hackensack River at Oradell, flooding low-lying land in the boroughs of Oradell, Haworth, Emerson, Closter, and Harrington Park. Construction of the dam was completed in 1923. The reservoir holds roughly 3 billion US gallons (about 11 billion litres) of water and has a surface area of approximately 660 acres (270 ha).

Water supply

Oradell Reservoir is the hub of a water supply system that also includes the Woodcliff Lake Reservoir and Lake Tappan, both also on Hackensack River tributaries. Water is treated at the Haworth Water Treatment Plant before distribution.

Wildlife and recreation

The reservoir is surrounded largely by protected watershed land and woodland, providing habitat for bald eagles, osprey, herons, and various waterfowl. Public access is restricted to protect water quality, though some limited permit fishing and hiking programs have been offered on watershed lands.
