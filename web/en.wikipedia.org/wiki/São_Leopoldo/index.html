São Leopoldo is a municipality in the southern Brazilian state of Rio Grande do Sul, in the metropolitan region of Porto Alegre, about 30 kilometres north of the state capital. It is historically famous as the birthplace of German immigration to Brazil: on 25 July 1824 the first group of German settlers arrived at the former royal linen factory (Real Feitoria do Linho Cânhamo) on the banks of the Rio dos Sinos, and that date is still celebrated as the start of German colonisation in the country.

History

The town was named in honour of Empress Maria Leopoldina, the Austrian-born wife of Emperor Pedro I, who encouraged European immigration. German colonists received small landholdings and spread through the Sinos valley, founding the communities that became today's municipalities of Novo Hamburgo, Ivoti, Dois Irmãos and others. São Leopoldo was raised to the status of a town in 1846 and to a city in 1864; in 1874 the Porto Alegre–São Leopoldo railway, the first in the state, was opened.

Economy and education

Historically part of the leather and footwear belt of the Sinos valley, the city's economy today includes metalworking, electronics, services and a technology park, Tecnosinos, associated with the Universidade do Vale do Rio dos Sinos (Unisinos), a major private Jesuit university headquartered in the city. São Leopoldo also hosts the Museu Histórico Visconde de São Leopoldo, which preserves documents and artefacts of German immigration.

Geography

The municipality lies on the plain of the Rio dos Sinos at low altitude, which has made it vulnerable to flooding; it was among the cities severely affected by the catastrophic Rio Grande do Sul floods of May 2024, when the Sinos overflowed and inundated large areas.
