Antonio Torres Jurado

Antonio de Torres Jurado (13 June 1817, La Cañada de San Urbano, Almería – 19 November 1892, Almería) was a Spanish guitarist and luthier, considered the most important Spanish guitar maker of the 19th century and the father of the modern classical guitar.

Life

Torres worked as a carpenter and cabinet-maker before turning to guitar making. He built his most innovative instruments in Seville in the 1850s–60s, then, after a period running a china shop in Almería, returned to guitar making in a second epoch from 1875 until his death.

Innovations

Torres standardised the essential design of the classical guitar: he enlarged the body, refined its proportions, adopted a 650 mm string scale, widened the neck and fingerboard, and — most importantly — developed fan bracing of the soundboard, distributing the struts symmetrically from the bridge. His guitars, built with thin, light soundboards (often of spruce) and sometimes with papier-mâché backs for experiments, achieved a new projection and tonal quality. Players such as Julián Arcas and Francisco Tárrega championed his instruments, and virtually all classical guitars since descend from his template.
