Maccabi Netanya F.C. (Hebrew: מועדון כדורגל מכבי נתניה) is an Israeli football club based in Netanya. Founded in 1934, the club is nicknamed "The Diamonds" after the diamond industry historically associated with the city. Its most successful period came in the 1970s and early 1980s, when it won five league championships.

History

The club was established in 1934 and played in the regional leagues of Mandatory Palestine before the creation of the Israeli league. Netanya rose to prominence in the 1970s under a generation of players that included Mordechai Spiegler, Israel's record goalscorer, winning league titles in 1970–71, 1973–74, 1977–78, 1979–80 and 1982–83, as well as the State Cup in 1978.

Stadium

For most of its history the club played at the Sar-Tov Stadium, known as "HaKufsa" (The Box). In 2012 it moved to the new Netanya Stadium, a modern arena with a capacity of about 13,600.

Honours

Israeli championships: 5. Israel State Cup: 1 (1977–78). The club has also competed in European competition, including the UEFA Intertoto Cup, which it entered several times during its peak years.
