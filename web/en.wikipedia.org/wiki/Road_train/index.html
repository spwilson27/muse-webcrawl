Road train


Type: Trucking vehicle
Region: Australia, Canada, United States
Trailers: 2–4+

A road train, also known as a land train or long combination vehicle, is a trucking vehicle used in remote areas of Australia and other countries to move freight efficiently. It consists of a powerful prime mover (tractor unit) towing two or more trailers or semi-trailers, far longer than a conventional articulated lorry.

History

Road trains developed in Australia in the early 20th century to supply remote cattle stations and mining communities across vast distances where railways did not reach. Early steam traction engines hauled strings of wagons; by the 1930s diesel trucks such as those operated by pioneer Kurt Gerhardt were hauling multiple trailers across the Northern Territory.

Configurations

Common configurations include the B-double (two semi-trailers), B-triple, and double and triple road trains combining converter dollies and trailers. In Australia, road trains may reach over 50 metres in length; the longest recorded combinations exceeded 1,000 metres in special record attempts. They are restricted to prescribed routes, mostly in Western Australia, the Northern Territory, South Australia and western Queensland.

Use elsewhere

Related long combination vehicles operate in Canada, the United States (as "doubles" and "triples" on certain highways), Mexico, Argentina, and parts of Europe under trials of high-capacity transport. In the Australian outback they remain essential for livestock, fuel, ore and general freight.
