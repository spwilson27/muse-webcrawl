Hubbell Trading Post National Historic Site is a historic site on Highway 191, near Ganado, Arizona. It is the oldest continuously operating trading post on the Navajo Nation.

History

John Lorenzo Hubbell (1853–1930) began trading in the Ganado area in the late 1870s, purchasing the post of William F. Bickford in 1878. Hubbell, the son of a Connecticut Yankee father and a Spanish mother, spoke Navajo, Spanish and English, and became an influential figure in relations between the Navajo people and the United States government in the years following the Navajo return from exile at Bosque Redondo (1868). He built the trading operation into a small empire of posts across northeastern Arizona and New Mexico.

Hubbell encouraged Navajo weavers and silversmiths to produce high-quality work for Eastern markets; the heavy, deep-red "Ganado red" rugs associated with the post became famous. His stone home and trading post, surrounded by barns, a bunkhouse, a bakery, corrals and farmland along Pueblo Colorado Wash, hosted artists, anthropologists, writers and tourists, making Ganado a cultural crossroads. The family continued to operate the post after his death in 1930.

National Historic Site

The site was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1960 and authorized as a National Historic Site in 1965; the National Park Service acquired the property in 1967. The Western National Parks Association operates the trading post, which still sells groceries and supplies to local residents and trades in Navajo rugs, jewelry and crafts, preserving the post's historic "bullpen" salesroom arrangement.

Facilities

The visitor center includes museum exhibits on the Hubbell family and Navajo culture, a bookstore, and demonstrations by Navajo weavers. The historic ranch grounds cover about 160 acres.
