Miles per gallon gasoline equivalent

Miles per gallon gasoline equivalent (MPGe or MPGge) is a measure of the average distance travelled per unit of energy consumed, used in the United States to compare the energy consumption of alternative fuel vehicles, plug-in hybrid electric vehicles and electric vehicles with the fuel economy of conventional internal combustion vehicles expressed in miles per US gallon.

Definition

One US gallon of gasoline contains approximately 33.7 kilowatt-hours (121 megajoules) of energy. The US Environmental Protection Agency converts an electric vehicle's consumption into MPGe by this equivalence: an EV that uses exactly 33.7 kWh of electricity to travel 100 miles would be rated at 100 MPGe. The figure appears on the Monroney (window) sticker of new vehicles sold in the United States.

Use and criticism

MPGe allows consumers to compare vehicles that use electricity, hydrogen or other fuels on a single scale, and it feeds into corporate average fuel economy (CAFE) compliance calculations. Critics note that the conversion ignores the energy used to generate and transmit electricity, that electricity and gasoline prices per unit of energy differ, and that comparing "miles per gallon" of electricity can confuse buyers; cost-per-mile and consumption measures such as kWh per 100 miles are offered as complements.

Related measures

Analogous units include the litre-per-100-kilometre gasoline equivalent used in some countries, and the kilogram-of-hydrogen equivalent for fuel cell vehicles. For plug-in hybrids, ratings are given separately for electric (MPGe) and gasoline (MPG) operation, together with an all-electric range figure.
