"Folsom Prison Blues" is a song by American singer-songwriter Johnny Cash. Written in 1953 and first recorded for Sun Records in 1955, it combines elements of folk, country, and blues, including imagery from train songs and prison songs. The song became famous again through Cash's live performance at California's Folsom State Prison on January 13, 1968, released on the album At Folsom Prison.

Composition

Cash wrote the song while serving in the United States Air Force in Germany, after seeing the 1951 film Inside the Walls of Folsom Prison. Its opening lines—"I hear the train a-comin', it's rolling 'round the bend"—draw on the traditional train song form, while the narrator's confession, "I shot a man in Reno, just to watch him die," became one of the most famous lines in country music. Part of the melody and lyrics were adapted from Gordon Jenkins's 1953 song "Crescent City Blues"; after Jenkins's work became widely known, Cash settled with his estate and shared credit arose in later releases, though the song remains credited to Cash.

Reception and legacy

The original recording reached number four on the Billboard country chart as the B-side and A-side pairing with "So Doggone Lonesome". The 1968 live version, released as a single, reached number one on the country chart and the top 40 of the pop chart, and won Cash a Grammy Award. The song is a staple of Cash's repertoire and of the outlaw country image; it has been covered by numerous artists and is listed among the greatest country songs of all time.
