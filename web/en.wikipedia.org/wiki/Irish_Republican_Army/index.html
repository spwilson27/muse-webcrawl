Irish Republican Army

The Irish Republican Army (IRA; Irish: Óglaigh na hÉireann) is a name used by a succession of Irish republican paramilitary organisations seeking the removal of British rule from Ireland and the creation of a united Irish republic. The name was first borne by the army of the Irish Republic proclaimed in 1916, which fought in the War of Independence (1919–1921).

History of the name

The original IRA split over the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921, producing pro- and anti-Treaty forces in the Irish Civil War. Later organisations claiming the name included the IRA of the 1930s–1960s, and after the 1969 split, the Official IRA and the Provisional IRA — the latter waging a 30-year campaign during the Troubles in Northern Ireland before decommissioning its weapons in 2005 following the Good Friday Agreement. Smaller dissident groups, such as the Continuity IRA, Real IRA, and New IRA, continue to use variants of the name.

Legacy

The term remains politically charged: in Ireland the state's defence forces are also called Óglaigh na hÉireann, claiming descent from the original IRA, while several groups bearing the name are proscribed as terrorist organisations in the United Kingdom and elsewhere.
