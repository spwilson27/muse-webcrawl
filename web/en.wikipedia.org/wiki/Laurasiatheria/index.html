Laurasiatheria

Laurasiatheria is a superorder of placental mammals proposed on molecular evidence, grouping together the orders Eulipotyphla (hedgehogs, moles, and shrews), Chiroptera (bats), Pholidota (pangolins), Carnivora, Perissodactyla (odd-toed ungulates), and Artiodactyla (even-toed ungulates, including cetaceans). The name reflects the hypothesis that these groups originated on the northern supercontinent of Laurasia.

Evolutionary relationships

Molecular phylogenetics in the late 1990s (notably Waddell and colleagues, 1999) revealed that placental mammals divide into major clades — Afrotheria, Xenarthra, Euarchontoglires, and Laurasiatheria — that correspond roughly to the breakup of Pangaea. Within Laurasiatheria, bats' placement surprised morphologists, since flight had suggested kinship with primates. Some analyses group Laurasiatheria with Euarchontoglires as Boreoeutheria.

Diversity

Laurasiatheria includes more than 2,000 living species, from the smallest shrews to whales. Its members dominate most terrestrial carnivore and herbivore niches of the Northern Hemisphere and have spread worldwide; bats are the second-largest order of mammals, and cetartiodactyls include the largest animals ever known.
