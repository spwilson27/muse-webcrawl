Postgraduate education

Postgraduate education, also called graduate education in North America, is study undertaken after completing a first (usually bachelor's) degree, leading to qualifications such as graduate certificates and diplomas, master's degrees, and doctoral degrees. Postgraduate study is generally more specialized and research-intensive than undergraduate education, and is undertaken either to deepen expertise, pursue an academic career, or meet professional licensing requirements.

Types of programmes

Taught programmes, such as most one-year master's degrees in the United Kingdom, consist mainly of coursework and a short dissertation. Research programmes, such as the MPhil and PhD, center on an extended original thesis defended in an oral examination (viva). Professional doctorates and degrees — the MD, JD, PharmD, and DBA — combine advanced coursework with clinical or practical training. In the United States, students typically enter PhD programmes directly from the bachelor's degree, often earning a master's en route, whereas in much of Europe the Bologna Process established a bachelor–master–doctorate sequence.

Funding and admissions

Admission usually requires a strong undergraduate record, references, and a statement of purpose; research degrees may require a proposal and supervisor agreement. Funding mechanisms include research assistantships, teaching assistantships, fellowships, and studentships, particularly for doctoral researchers, while taught master's students commonly self-fund.

Scale and criticism

Mass expansion of postgraduate education since the late twentieth century has accompanied concern about "credential inflation," adjunctification of academic labor, and the mental-health pressures of doctoral study, as well as debate about the return on investment for taught master's degrees.
