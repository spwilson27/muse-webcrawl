Radiometry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Radiometry is the science of measuring electromagnetic radiation in absolute physical terms — how much power is emitted, transferred or received, and how it is distributed in space and wavelength. It applies across the whole spectrum, from ultraviolet through visible light to infrared and beyond. Its counterpart photometry weights radiation by the sensitivity of the human eye.

Applications

Radiometric measurement underpins remote sensing and Earth observation, climate science (measuring Earth's radiation budget), astronomy, lighting engineering, and the calibration of solar cells and optical instruments. Planck's law and the Stefan–Boltzmann law provide the theoretical basis for thermal radiometry.
