A tuner is a subsystem or standalone electronic device that receives radio frequency (RF) transmissions, such as AM or FM radio broadcasts, and converts them into audio or video signals. In high-fidelity audio, a tuner is a separate component that receives radio broadcasts and outputs a line-level audio signal to an amplifier; combined with an amplifier in one chassis, it forms a receiver.

Design

Most tuners use the superheterodyne architecture: the incoming signal is mixed with a local oscillator to produce a fixed intermediate frequency, which is then amplified, filtered and demodulated. Analogue tuners tune with a variable capacitor or varactor diodes, traditionally indicated by a pointer moving across an illuminated frequency dial; digital tuners use phase-locked-loop frequency synthesis and can store preset stations. Stereo FM decoding, RDS data display, and automatic frequency control are standard features. Modern models may add DAB/DAB+ digital radio or internet streaming.

History

Separate tuners became common in the hi-fi market of the 1950s–1970s, when component audio systems flourished and FM broadcasting expanded. Notable high-performance tuners of the era are still prized by enthusiasts, though the component has largely been absorbed into AV receivers and network streamers.
