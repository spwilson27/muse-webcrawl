Norm Smith

Norman Walter "Norm" Smith (21 November 1915 – 29 July 1973) was an Australian rules football player and coach, widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in the history of the sport. As a player he was a key forward in Melbourne's premiership sides, and as coach he led Melbourne to six premierships in a golden era between 1955 and 1964.

Career

Smith played 227 games, mostly for Melbourne, winning four premierships as a player (1939, 1940, 1941, 1948) and the club's best and fairest in 1938. As Melbourne coach he built one of the VFL's greatest dynasties — six flags in ten years — with a disciplined, team-oriented style. His shock sacking by Melbourne in mid-1965, though reversed within a week amid public outcry, ended his relationship with the club; he later coached South Melbourne.

Legacy

The Norm Smith Medal, awarded since 1979 to the best player in the AFL Grand Final, is named in his honour. He was named coach of Melbourne's Team of the Century and is a Legend in the Australian Football Hall of Fame. His brother Len Smith was also an influential coach.
