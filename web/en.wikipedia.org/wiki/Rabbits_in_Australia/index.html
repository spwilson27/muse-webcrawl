Rabbits in Australia

European rabbits are one of Australia&rsquo;s best-known invasive species. Although rabbits arrived with early European settlement, the population explosion is usually traced to the release of wild rabbits for hunting in Victoria in 1859. With few effective predators and abundant grazing land, rabbits spread across much of the continent.

Impact

Large rabbit populations damage pasture and crops, compete with native animals and livestock, and accelerate erosion by removing vegetation and burrowing. Their grazing can prevent regeneration of native shrubs and trees, changing whole landscapes.

Control

Control measures have included fencing, warren destruction, poisoning, shooting and biological control. The release of myxomatosis in 1950 caused a dramatic initial decline, followed by increasing resistance. Rabbit haemorrhagic disease later became another major control tool. Rabbits remain a significant pest despite more than a century of management.

The rabbit-proof fence in Western Australia is a famous symbol of the scale of the problem.
