Polyethylene (polythene, PE) is the world's most produced plastic: the addition polymer of ethene, –(CH₂–CH₂)₃–. Its simple, saturated chains make it chemically inert, tough and cheap — the material of bags, bottles, pipes, films and cable insulation.

Two main forms dominate: low-density polyethylene (LDPE, branched chains, soft and flexible — films and squeeze bottles), first made by ICI in 1933, and high-density polyethylene (HDPE, linear chains, stiff and strong — milk bottles, pipes), enabled by Ziegler's catalysts in the 1950s. Ultra-high-molecular-weight grades make bulletproof fibres and artificial joints.

Environmental issues

Polyethylene does not biodegrade on human timescales; it is widely collected for recycling (resin codes 1–2 territory: HDPE is #2, LDPE #4) but film grades are often downcycled. Chemical recycling and bio-based ethene are active research areas.

Properties

Melting range around 110–135 °C by grade; excellent electrical insulator; attacked slowly by strong oxidising acids and UV (countered with carbon-black fillers in pipes).
