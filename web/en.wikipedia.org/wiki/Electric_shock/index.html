Electric shock

An electric shock is a physiological reaction caused by electric current passing through the body. The severity depends on the current magnitude, its path through the body, duration, and whether the current is AC or DC. Death caused by electric shock is called electrocution.

Effects by current

Current (AC, 60 Hz)Effect
1 mAFaint tingle; perception threshold
5 mASlight shock felt
10–20 mA"Let-go" threshold; muscle contractions may prevent release
50–100 mAVentricular fibrillation possible; potentially fatal
1 A and aboveSevere burns, cardiac arrest

Mechanism of injury

Current disrupts nerve and muscle function; alternating current at mains frequency readily triggers sustained muscle contraction and can induce ventricular fibrillation, the usual cause of death in low-voltage accidents. Burns occur at contact points and along the current path, and falls resulting from a shock cause further injuries.

Protection

Safety measures include insulation, earthing (grounding), double insulation, residual-current devices (ground-fault circuit interrupters), which trip on imbalances of a few milliamperes, and safe working practices such as isolation and lockout procedures.
