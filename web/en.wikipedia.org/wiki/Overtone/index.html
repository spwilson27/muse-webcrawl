Overtone

An overtone is any resonant frequency above the fundamental frequency of a sound. The fundamental is usually heard as the pitch, while the overtones together determine the timbre that distinguishes, for example, a violin from a flute playing the same note.

Harmonic and inharmonic overtones

When overtones are integer multiples of the fundamental, they are called harmonics: the first overtone is the second harmonic (2f), the second overtone the third harmonic (3f), and so on. Vibrating strings and air columns in most instruments produce nearly harmonic series, giving rise to the harmonic series of music theory. Bells, gongs and stiff strings produce inharmonic overtones (partials), whose frequencies deviate from integer ratios, contributing to their distinctive clangourous quality.

Perception and use

Listeners usually fuse overtones into a single perceived pitch rather than hearing them separately, but trained singers can amplify individual overtones, as in Tuvan throat singing (khöömei) and "overtone singing", and instrumentalists exploit them in techniques such as natural harmonics on strings and the altissimo register on winds. In speech, the resonances of the vocal tract (formants) shape vowel overtones.
