Ala-al-din abu Al-Hassan Ali ibn Abi-Hazm al-Qarshi al-Dimashqi, known as Ibn al-Nafis (1213–1288), was an Arab polymath whose areas of work included medicine, surgery, physiology, anatomy, biology, Islamic studies, jurisprudence, and philosophy. He is chiefly famous for being the first to describe the pulmonary circulation of blood, in 1242 — three centuries before Michael Servetus and William Harvey.

Pulmonary circulation

In his Commentary on Anatomy in Avicenna's Canon, Ibn al-Nafis rejected the Galenic doctrine that blood passes through invisible pores in the heart's septum. He argued instead that blood from the right ventricle must travel through the lungs — where it is refined and mixed with air — before reaching the left ventricle, and he correctly described the pulmonary artery, pulmonary vein, and the coronary circulation supplying the heart itself.

Other work

Ibn al-Nafis served as chief physician at the al-Naseri and al-Mansouri hospitals in Cairo. His Comprehensive Book on Medicine (Kitab al-Shamil) was one of the largest medical encyclopaedias of its era. He also wrote the theological novel Theologus Autodidactus, sometimes cited as an early work of science fiction, as well as works on logic, astronomy, and the biography of Muhammad.
