The Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway (京沪高速铁路, Jīnghù Gāosù Tiělù) is a 1,318-kilometre (819 mi) high-speed railway that connects two of China's major economic zones, the Bohai Economic Rim and the Yangtze River Delta. Construction began on 18 April 2008 and the line opened to the public on 30 June 2011. It is one of the busiest and most profitable high-speed lines in the world.

Route

From Beijing South station the line runs south through Tianjin, Jinan, Xuzhou, Bengbu and Nanjing — crossing the Yangtze on the Dashengguan Bridge — before reaching Shanghai Hongqiao. Roughly 86% of the line is on viaducts, including the Danyang–Kunshan Grand Bridge (164.8 km), the world's longest bridge. Intermediate cities include Langfang, Cangzhou, Dezhou, Tai'an, Qufu, Suzhou (Anhui), Huainan, Chuzhou, Zhenjiang, Changzhou, Wuxi and Suzhou (Jiangsu).

Service

Fastest trains complete the journey in about 4 hours 18 minutes, averaging over 300 km/h start-to-stop. Trains operate at up to 350 km/h, the design speed; services are worked by CRH380 and CR400 "Fuxing" trainsets. The line carries well over 100 million passengers a year and largely parallels the older, congested Beijing–Shanghai (Jinghu) conventional railway.

History and finance

The line cost about ¥220.9 billion (roughly US$34 billion). Operated by the Beijing–Shanghai High-Speed Railway Company, it became one of the first Chinese high-speed lines to report consistent operating profits, and the company listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange in January 2020. A second channel (the Beijing–Shanghai HSR Second Corridor) is under construction to relieve capacity.
