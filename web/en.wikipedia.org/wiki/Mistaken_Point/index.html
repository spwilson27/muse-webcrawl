Mistaken Point

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

LocationAvalon Peninsula, Newfoundland, Canada
AgeEdiacaran, c. 565 million years
StatusEcological reserve; UNESCO World Heritage Site (2016)

Mistaken Point is a headland on the Avalon Peninsula of Newfoundland, Canada, and the site of the Mistaken Point Ecological Reserve, one of the world's most important fossil localities for the Ediacaran biota — the earliest known large, complex multicellular organisms. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2016.

The fossils

The cliffs and bedding planes at Mistaken Point preserve thousands of soft-bodied organisms, dated by overlying volcanic ash layers to about 565 million years ago. Whole deep-sea communities were smothered in place by ash falls (a "Pompeii-style" preservation), leaving impressions of rangeomorphs such as Fractofusus and Charniodiscus, discs, and frond-like forms carpeting the ancient sea floor. Because the assemblages are preserved where the organisms lived, they allow palaeoecological study of the oldest animal ecosystems. Fossils here were first recognised in 1967 by S. B. Misra, then a graduate student, whose discovery announced the Avalon assemblage to science.

The name and reserve

The point earned its name from mariners who, in fog, mistook it for Cape Race and turned north into treacherous waters, causing shipwrecks. The fossil sites are protected: access to the fossil surfaces is by guided tour only, based at the Edge of Avalon Interpretation Centre in nearby Portugal Cove South.
