Trajan's Forum

Trajan's Forum (Latin: Forum Traiani; Italian: Foro di Traiano) was the last of the Imperial fora to be constructed in ancient Rome. The architect Apollodorus of Damascus oversaw its construction. It was built on the orders of the emperor Trajan with the spoils of war from the conquest of Dacia, which ended in 106 AD.

History

The forum was built on a site that required extensive excavation: workers leveled a low valley between the Quirinal and Capitoline Hills, cutting back the slopes of the Quirinal Hill. Trajan's Column, inaugurated in 113 AD, commemorated the emperor's Dacian Wars with a spiraling frieze depicting the campaigns. The forum complex included the Basilica Ulpia, one of the largest public buildings in Rome, flanked by two libraries (one Latin, one Greek), the Temple of Trajan, and an equestrian statue of the emperor at its center.

Layout

The open plaza measured roughly 300 meters long and 185 meters wide. The Basilica Ulpia stood at the northwestern end and served judicial and civic functions. Behind it, Trajan's Column rose in a small courtyard between the two libraries. After Trajan's death in 117, his successor Hadrian completed the complex with the Temple of the Deified Trajan.

Later history

The forum remained a center of public life for centuries. In the Middle Ages the area was built over with houses and churches, though Trajan's Column survived largely intact. Excavations in the 19th and 20th centuries, including work under Mussolini for the Via dei Fori Imperiali, uncovered much of the plaza. The nearby Trajan's Market, a multi-level complex of shops and offices on the Quirinal slope, is often associated with the forum.
