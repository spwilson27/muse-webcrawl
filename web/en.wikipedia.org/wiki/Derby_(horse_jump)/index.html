Derby (horse jump)

A derby is a type of natural fence obstacle used in show jumping and eventing courses, most famously in derby competitions such as the British Jumping Derby at Hickstead. The term also names the class itself: a show jumping competition held over a course of natural obstacles, including banks, ditches, hedges, walls, and water, designed to test the horse's boldness and the rider's skill over terrain resembling the hunting field.

The Hickstead Derby

The most famous derby course is that of the Al Shira'aa Derby at the All England Jumping Course at Hickstead, West Sussex, first held in 1961. The course is over 1,195 metres long with 21 jumping efforts, and includes the Derby Bank — a steep bank with a 10 ft 6 in (3.2 m) drop and a fence just two strides from its base — as well as the Devil's Dyke, a triple combination within a ditch, and rustic fences built from natural materials. Only a handful of clear rounds have ever been achieved over the course, making a clear round one of the most coveted results in the sport.

Course characteristics

Unlike a standard show jumping track, a derby course uses fixed, natural-looking obstacles and undulating ground. Fences may include:

  • Banks and drops
  • Ditches and water trays
  • Hedges and brush fences
  • Stone walls and gates
  • Open water jumps

Hamburg's German Jumping and Dressage Derby, held since 1920 at Klein Flottbek, is the oldest and longest derby course in the world and is noted for its own notorious bank.
