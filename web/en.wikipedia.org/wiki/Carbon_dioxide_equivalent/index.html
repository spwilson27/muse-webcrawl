Carbon dioxide equivalent

[Image placeholder: Industrial emissions being measured and reported in CO₂-equivalent units]

Carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e or CO2-eq) is a metric measure used to compare the emissions from various greenhouse gases on the basis of their global warming potential (GWP), by converting amounts of other gases to the equivalent amount of carbon dioxide with the same global warming potential.

Carbon dioxide equivalents are commonly expressed as millimetric tonnes (MtCO2e). The calculation converts the mass of a gas into an equivalent mass of CO2 by multiplying by its GWP over a chosen time horizon, most commonly 100 years (GWP100).

Example values

GasGWP (100-year, approx.)
Carbon dioxide (CO2)1
Methane (CH4)27–30
Nitrous oxide (N2O)273
Sulfur hexafluoride (SF6)~24,300

Thus one tonne of methane emitted is reported as roughly 28 tonnes of CO2e. The metric underpins greenhouse gas inventories under the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change, corporate reporting, and the accounting rules of emissions trading schemes.

Criticism

Because the climate effects of short-lived gases such as methane differ in character from those of CO2, some researchers argue that GWP-based equivalence misrepresents the warming caused by sustained versus one-off emissions, and alternative metrics such as GWP* have been proposed.
