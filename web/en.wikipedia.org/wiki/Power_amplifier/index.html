Power amplifier

A power amplifier is an electronic amplifier designed to provide a large output power, typically to drive a load such as a loudspeaker, an antenna, or a motor. Unlike voltage or pre-amplifiers, which mainly raise signal level, a power amplifier's primary concern is delivering significant power efficiently, with acceptable distortion.

Classes

Amplifier designs are grouped into classes by the conduction pattern of their output devices. Class A devices conduct continuously, giving low distortion but poor efficiency (at most about 25–50%). Class B and Class AB use paired devices conducting in alternation, improving efficiency at the cost of crossover distortion, and dominate audio practice. In Class C, used in radio transmitters, devices conduct for less than half the cycle. Switching designs — Class D and its relatives — switch the output devices fully on and off and filter the result, achieving efficiencies above 90% and dominating modern compact and high-power audio.

Applications

Audio power amplifiers range from the milliwatt stages in phones to multi-kilowatt units for concerts and public address. Radio-frequency power amplifiers drive transmitters from Wi-Fi routers to broadcast stations. Servo and industrial power amplifiers drive motors and actuators, and specialised designs serve sonar, medical imaging, and particle accelerators.
