Private pilot licence

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A private pilot licence (PPL), or, in the United States, a private pilot certificate, is a type of pilot licence that allows the holder to act as pilot in command of an aircraft privately (not for remuneration). Licence requirements are set by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), but implementation and practical details vary widely from country to country.

Requirements

Candidates must typically be at least 17 years old, hold an appropriate medical certificate, complete ground school and pass written examinations in subjects such as air law, meteorology, navigation, and principles of flight, and log a minimum number of flight hours. Under ICAO standards, a private pilot applicant for aeroplanes requires at least 40 hours of flight time, including dual instruction, solo flight, and cross-country navigation. The training concludes with a practical flight test (checkride) with an examiner.

Privileges and limitations

A private pilot may carry passengers and fly for personal and recreational purposes, and may share operating costs with passengers, but may not be compensated for flying except in limited circumstances. By default the licence permits visual flight rules (VFR) flying in good weather; additional ratings — such as an instrument rating, night rating, or multi-engine rating — extend the holder's privileges. The licence is a prerequisite step toward commercial pilot and airline transport pilot licences.
