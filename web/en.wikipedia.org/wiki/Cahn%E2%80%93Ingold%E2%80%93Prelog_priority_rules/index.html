Cahn–Ingold–Prelog priority rules

AbbreviationCIP rules
FieldStereochemistry
Named afterRobert Cahn, Christopher Ingold, Vladimir Prelog
PurposeAssigning R/S and E/Z descriptors

The Cahn–Ingold–Prelog (CIP) priority rules are a system of rules used in organic chemistry to name stereoisomers unambiguously. They assign priorities to the substituents around a stereocentre, allowing specification of absolute configuration as R (rectus) or S (sinister), and of double-bond geometry as E or Z.

The rules were developed by Robert Sidney Cahn, Christopher Kelk Ingold, and Vladimir Prelog, with the definitive exposition published in 1966.

Sequence rules

Substituents are ranked first by the atomic number of the atoms directly attached to the stereocentre: higher atomic number has higher priority. If a tie occurs, the sets of atoms attached to the tied atoms are compared level by level ('sphere by sphere') until a difference is found; multiple bonds are treated as duplicated atoms, and isotopes are ranked by mass number.

Assigning configuration

With priorities 1→2→3 traced and the lowest-priority group pointing away, a clockwise order is R and counter-clockwise is S. For alkenes, the higher-priority groups on the same side give Z (zusammen) and opposite sides give E (entgegen).

Status

The CIP system is maintained under IUPAC nomenclature and is the standard taught worldwide; periodic refinements address complex cases such as pseudoasymmetric centres (r/s) and axial chirality.
