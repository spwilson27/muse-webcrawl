The National Front (Persian: جبهه ملی ایران, romanized: Jebhe-ye Melli-ye Irân) is an Iranian political opposition movement and coalition founded by Mohammad Mosaddegh in 1949. It is the oldest continuously operating pro-democracy group in Iran. Originally a broad front of nationalist, liberal, social-democratic, and religious parties and organisations, the Front came to power in 1951 when Mosaddegh became Prime Minister and led the nationalisation of the Iranian oil industry.

History

The National Front was founded on 12 November 1949, when Mosaddegh and a group of supporters staged a sit-in at the palace of Mohammad Reza Shah protesting irregularities in elections to the Majlis. The Shah permitted free elections, and the Front won a bloc of seats in Tehran. In the Majlis, the Front championed the nationalisation of the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company, which was enacted in March 1951; weeks later Mosaddegh became Prime Minister.

The Mosaddegh government (1951–1953) pursued oil nationalisation, land and electoral reform, and curbs on the Shah's power. Britain responded with an embargo and legal action, and in August 1953 a coup organised by the CIA and MI6 (Operation Ajax) overthrew Mosaddegh. Many Front leaders were arrested and the movement was driven underground, though it resurfaced in the 1960s and again during the Iranian Revolution of 1979, when its leader Karim Sanjabi briefly served as foreign minister in the provisional government.

Ideology and programme

The Front has consistently advocated constitutional government, free elections, the rule of law, an independent judiciary, freedom of the press, and Iranian sovereignty over natural resources. Its member parties have included the Iran Party, the Party of the Iranian People, and the Nation Party of Iran. The Front has been banned or restricted for most of the period since 1953, yet it remains a symbolic reference point for Iranian nationalist and democratic opposition politics.
