Learning rate

In machine learning, the learning rate is a hyperparameter that controls how much a model's parameters are adjusted in response to the estimated error at each step of an optimization algorithm such as stochastic gradient descent. It determines the step size taken in the direction opposite to the gradient of the loss function.

Effect on training

If the learning rate is too small, training progresses very slowly and may stall in poor local minima; if it is too large, updates can overshoot minima, causing the loss to oscillate or even diverge. Choosing an appropriate learning rate is therefore one of the most important tasks in training a neural network, and it interacts strongly with batch size, model architecture and the choice of optimizer.

Schedules and adaptive methods

Common strategies include a fixed learning rate, step decay, exponential decay, cosine annealing, and warm-up periods in which the rate is gradually increased at the start of training, often combined with subsequent decay. Adaptive optimizers such as AdaGrad, RMSProp and Adam maintain separate, automatically adjusted effective learning rates for each parameter based on the history of gradients, reducing — but not eliminating — the need for manual tuning. Cyclical learning rates and learning-rate finders are also used to search for good values empirically.
