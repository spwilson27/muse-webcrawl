Birch beer is a beverage commonly found as a carbonated soft drink made from herbal extracts and birch bark. There are dozens of brands of birch beer available, and it is especially popular in the northeastern United States and Newfoundland and Labrador in Canada.

History and production

Birch beer derives from colonial-era drinks brewed from birch sap and bark, in the tradition of small beers and root-based tonics made by early European settlers in North America, who adapted Native American practices of tapping birch trees in spring. Traditionally, the drink was made by fermenting birch sap or a decoction of birch bark with sugar; modern commercial versions are typically non-alcoholic sodas flavoured with birch bark extract and sweeteners.

The most common variety is the deep red-coloured birch beer, especially associated with Pennsylvania Dutch country. Other varieties are coloured brown (closer to root beer) or are clear ("white birch beer"). The flavour derives chiefly from oil of birch (methyl salicylate, also found in wintergreen), which gives the drink a minty, wintergreen character similar to — but distinct from — root beer and sarsaparilla. Birch beer is commonly sold by regional soda bottlers and at Pennsylvania Dutch markets, county fairs and farmers' markets.
