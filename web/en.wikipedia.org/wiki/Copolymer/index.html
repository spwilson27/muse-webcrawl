Copolymer

A copolymer is a polymer derived from more than one species of monomer. The polymerization of monomers into copolymers is called copolymerization. Copolymers obtained from two different monomer species are often called bipolymers; those from three and four monomers are terpolymers and quaterpolymers. By contrast, polymers made from a single monomer are homopolymers.

Types

Copolymers are classified by the arrangement of their monomer units along the chain:

  • Alternating copolymers — regular alternation of the two monomers (ABABAB...)
  • Periodic copolymers — repeating multi-unit sequences
  • Statistical (random) copolymers — monomer sequence follows a statistical rule governed by reactivity ratios
  • Block copolymers — long sequences (blocks) of each monomer joined together; combining blocks of different solubility yields self-assembling materials
  • Graft copolymers — side chains of one polymer attached to a backbone of another

Examples and applications

Commercially important copolymers include acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS), styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), styrene-acrylonitrile (SAN), ethylene-vinyl acetate (EVA), and nitrile rubber. Copolymerization is a principal tool for tuning properties such as glass transition temperature, toughness, transparency, and chemical resistance; the composition of the product is described by the Mayo–Lewis equation in terms of monomer reactivity ratios.
