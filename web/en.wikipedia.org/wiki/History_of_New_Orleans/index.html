History of New Orleans

Founded1718
FounderJean-Baptiste Le Moyne de Bienville
Colonial powersFrance (1718–1763), Spain (1763–1801), France, United States (1803)

The history of New Orleans reflects its position on the Mississippi River near the Gulf of Mexico: founded by France, ruled by Spain, purchased by the United States, and shaped by French, African, Spanish and American cultures.

Colonial era

Jean-Baptiste Le Moyne de Bienville founded La Nouvelle-Orléans in 1718 for the French Mississippi Company. The city became the capital of French Louisiana. Under the Treaty of Fontainebleau (1762) it passed to Spain, which rebuilt it after fires in 1788 and 1794 — much of the "French Quarter" is actually Spanish colonial in style.

American era and the Civil War

The Louisiana Purchase (1803) made New Orleans American. Its port boomed on cotton and sugar, and it became one of the largest slave markets in the United States. In January 1815 Andrew Jackson defeated the British at the Battle of New Orleans. Union forces under David Farragut captured the city in April 1862.

20th century to Katrina

New Orleans gave birth to jazz around 1900. Levee and drainage works enabled expansion below sea level. In August 2005 Hurricane Katrina breached the levees, flooding 80% of the city and killing over a thousand people in Louisiana; the city's slow recovery reshaped its population and politics.
