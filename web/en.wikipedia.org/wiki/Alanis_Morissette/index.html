Alanis Morissette

Alanis Nadine Morissette (born 1 June 1974) is a Canadian-American singer, songwriter, and actress. Known for her emotive mezzo-soprano voice and confessional songwriting, she began her career in Canada in the early 1990s as a teen pop singer before achieving worldwide fame with the alternative rock album Jagged Little Pill (1995), one of the best-selling albums of all time, with more than 33 million copies sold. Often called the "Queen of Alt-Rock Angst", she has sold more than 75 million records worldwide.

Career

Morissette released two dance-pop albums in Canada, Alanis (1991) and Now Is the Time (1992), before moving to Los Angeles and collaborating with producer Glen Ballard. The resulting album, Jagged Little Pill, produced the hit singles "You Oughta Know", "Ironic", "Hand in My Pocket", "You Learn" and "Head over Feet", and won five Grammy Awards, including Album of the Year, making Morissette the youngest winner in that category at the time.

Subsequent albums include Supposed Former Infatuation Junkie (1998), which debuted at number one in the US with the hit "Thank U"; Under Rug Swept (2002); So-Called Chaos (2004); Flavors of Entanglement (2008); Havoc and Bright Lights (2012); Such Pretty Forks in the Road (2020); and the meditation album The Storm Before the Calm (2022). A jukebox musical, Jagged Little Pill, premiered on Broadway in 2019.

Legacy

Morissette was inducted into the Canadian Music Hall of Fame in 2015. Her confessional style is widely cited as an influence on later singer-songwriters including Avril Lavigne, Kelly Clarkson, Pink and Olivia Rodrigo.
