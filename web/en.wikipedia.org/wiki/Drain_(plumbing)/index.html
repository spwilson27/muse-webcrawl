Drain (plumbing)

A drain is the primary vessel or conduit for unwanted water or waste liquids to flow away, either to be re-used, stored in a cistern, or channelled into sewers for disposal. In plumbing, the term refers both to the fixture outlet in a sink, bath, shower or floor and to the drainage piping connected to it, forming part of the building's drain-waste-vent (DWV) system.

Design

Residential fixture drains typically incorporate a strainer or basket to catch solids, and below the fixture a trap — a U-, P- or S-shaped bend that retains a plug of water. The water seal in the trap prevents sewer gases and vermin from entering the building while allowing wastewater to pass. Drain pipes are installed with a slight downward slope (commonly about 1–2%) so that gravity carries water and suspended solids toward the soil stack and building sewer without allowing solids to settle and clog the pipe.

Types

  • Fixture drains for sinks, baths, showers and appliances
  • Floor drains in basements, garages, commercial kitchens and wet rooms
  • French drains and perimeter drains that collect groundwater around foundations
  • Storm drains that carry rainwater runoff from roofs and paved areas
  • Point drains and channel (trench) drains used in showers, driveways and pool decks

Maintenance and blockage

Drains commonly clog with hair, grease, soap residue and food waste. Blockages are cleared mechanically with plungers, drain augers ("snakes") or hydro-jetting, or chemically with alkaline or enzymatic drain cleaners, though chemical products can damage pipes and the environment. Building codes regulate pipe sizing, trap seals and venting to ensure drains flow freely and traps cannot be siphoned dry.
