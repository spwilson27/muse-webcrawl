The National Museum of African American History and Culture (NMAAHC) is a Smithsonian Institution museum located on the National Mall in Washington, D.C. Established by an Act of Congress in 2003, it opened to the public on September 24, 2016, in a ceremony led by President Barack Obama.

Building

The building, designed by architect David Adjaye, is clad in a bronze-coloured corona inspired by Yoruba sculpture and by the ironwork crafted by enslaved African Americans in the southern United States. It contains approximately 85,000 square feet of exhibition space.

Collection

The museum's collection includes more than 40,000 objects, among them Harriet Tubman's hymnal, a slave cabin from South Carolina, Chuck Berry's Cadillac, and Emmett Till's original casket. Exhibitions trace the history of slavery, Reconstruction, the civil rights movement, and African American culture and achievement.
