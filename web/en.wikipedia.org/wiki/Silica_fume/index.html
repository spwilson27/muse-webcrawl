Silica fume

Silica fume, also known as microsilica, is an amorphous (non-crystalline) polymorph of silicon dioxide collected as a byproduct of silicon and ferrosilicon alloy production in electric arc furnaces. It consists of extremely fine spherical particles with an average diameter of about 0.15 micrometres, roughly 100 times finer than average cement particles.

Use in concrete

Silica fume is a highly reactive pozzolan. Added to concrete, typically at 5–10% by weight of cement, it reacts with calcium hydroxide to form additional calcium silicate hydrate, greatly increasing strength and durability and reducing permeability. It is a key ingredient in high-strength and high-performance concretes used in bridges, marine structures and high-rise buildings.

Other uses

Microsilica is also used in refractory castables, oil-well grouting, polymer fillers and shotcrete. Its fine particle size requires superplasticisers to maintain workability in fresh concrete.
