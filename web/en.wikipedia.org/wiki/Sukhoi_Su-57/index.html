The Su-57 (NATO: Felon) is Russia's first stealth fighter.

The Sukhoi Su-57 (Russian: Сухой Су-57; NATO reporting name: Felon) is a twin-engine stealth multirole fighter aircraft developed by Sukhoi. It is the product of the PAK FA programme (Prospective Aviation Complex of Frontline Aviation), designed to succeed the MiG-29 and Su-27 in Russian service as the country's first operational stealth aircraft.

Design

The Su-57 combines stealth shaping, internal weapons bays, supercruise capability and advanced sensor fusion. It features the N036 Byelka radar system with multiple active electronically scanned arrays, an infrared search and track sensor, and Saturn AL-41F1 engines with thrust vectoring; the more powerful Izdeliye 30 engine has been in development as a follow-on. Armament includes a 30 mm cannon and air-to-air and air-to-surface missiles carried internally and on external hardpoints.

Development and service

The prototype T-50 first flew on 29 January 2010. The programme suffered delays, including an engine fire and the crash of the first production aircraft in December 2019. The first serial Su-57 entered service with the Russian Aerospace Forces in December 2020, with deliveries proceeding slowly. Su-57s were briefly deployed to Syria in 2018 for combat evaluation, and Russian sources and analysts report limited standoff strike use during the Russo-Ukrainian War.
