Simple function

FieldMeasure theory
TypeMeasurable function
ValuesFinitely many

A simple function is a measurable function that takes only finitely many values. In real analysis and measure theory, simple functions form the building blocks from which general measurable functions are constructed, much as step functions approximate continuous ones. Every nonnegative measurable function can be approximated from below by an increasing sequence of simple functions, a fact that underlies the definition of the Lebesgue integral.

Definition

A simple function is a finite linear combination of indicator functions of measurable sets: s(x) = sum of a_k times the indicator of E_k, where the a_k are constants (often nonnegative real numbers) and the E_k are measurable sets. When the sets are disjoint, this representation is called the canonical representation.

Integral

The Lebesgue integral of a simple function is defined as the sum of a_k times the measure of E_k. This definition extends to arbitrary nonnegative measurable functions by taking suprema over simple functions below them, and then to signed and complex functions by splitting into positive and negative, or real and imaginary, parts.

Properties

Simple functions are closed under addition, multiplication and scalar multiplication, forming an algebra of functions. The set of simple functions is dense in the space of measurable functions under pointwise convergence and in L^p spaces under the p-norm.
