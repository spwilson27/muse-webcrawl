Maslenica Bridge (Croatian: Maslenički most) is the name of two bridges spanning the Maslenica Strait near the village of Maslenica in Zadar County, Croatia, connecting Dalmatia with the rest of the country across the strait between the Velebit Channel and the Novigrad Sea.

The original bridge

The first Maslenica Bridge, a reinforced-concrete arch bridge 379.6 metres long with a 155-metre arch span, was completed in 1961 for the Adriatic coastal road. During the Croatian War of Independence the bridge was a strategically vital link; Croatian forces withdrew across it in 1991, and it was destroyed by explosives in November 1991. Its recapture in Operation Maslenica in January 1993 — which took its name from the area — was a major Croatian offensive, and a temporary pontoon bridge restored the connection.

Reconstruction and the motorway bridge

The original bridge was rebuilt to its former design and reopened in 2005. Meanwhile a second, new Maslenica Bridge, a steel arch bridge carrying the A1 motorway, had opened in 1997 a short distance to the north. The site is notorious for fierce bora winds, which periodically close the bridges and have caused accidents, prompting wind-protection measures and traffic restrictions.
