Paul Leonard Newman (26 January 1925 – 26 September 2008) was an American actor, film director, race car driver, entrepreneur and philanthropist. One of the most popular and enduring movie stars of the twentieth century, he received numerous accolades including an Academy Award, a BAFTA Award and three Golden Globe Awards, and was known for playing charming antiheroes in films such as The Hustler (1961), Hud (1963), Cool Hand Luke (1967), Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid (1969) and The Sting (1973).

Early life

Newman was born in Shaker Heights, Ohio, to a Jewish father of Hungarian and Polish descent and a Slovak Catholic mother. He served in the U.S. Navy in the Pacific during the Second World War, then studied drama at Kenyon College and at the Yale School of Drama, and with Lee Strasberg at the Actors Studio in New York, emerging as part of the postwar generation of Method-influenced actors alongside Marlon Brando and James Dean.

Film career

After a Broadway success in Picnic (1953), Newman rose to film stardom with Somebody Up There Likes Me (1956) and Cat on a Hot Tin Roof (1958), the latter earning the first of his ten Academy Award nominations. Through the 1960s and 1970s he headlined a string of classics: the pool hustler "Fast Eddie" Felson in The Hustler, the amoral rancher in Hud, the laconic prisoner in Cool Hand Luke, and two beloved pairings with Robert Redford, Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid and The Sting. He finally won the Best Actor Oscar for reprising Felson in Martin Scorsese's The Color of Money (1986), and continued acclaimed work into his seventies, including Nobody's Fool (1994) and Road to Perdition (2002), plus the voice of Doc Hudson in Pixar's Cars (2006). He also directed several films, including Rachel, Rachel (1968), starring his wife Joanne Woodward, to whom he was married for fifty years.

Racing

Newman became a serious racing driver after starring in Winning (1969). Competing under the name "P. L. Newman," he won four SCCA national championships and finished second overall at the 1979 24 Hours of Le Mans. As co-owner of Newman/Haas Racing, he fielded one of the most successful teams in American open-wheel racing. He was still winning races in his eighties.

Philanthropy

In 1982 Newman founded Newman's Own, a food company whose entire after-tax profits — eventually more than $600 million — are donated to charity. He co-founded the Hole in the Wall Gang Camp (1988) in Connecticut for seriously ill children, the model for the SeriousFun Children's Network of camps worldwide. A committed liberal, Newman was a prominent supporter of Eugene McCarthy in 1968 and appeared on Richard Nixon's "enemies list," an honour he professed to treasure. He died of lung cancer at his home in Westport, Connecticut, in 2008.
