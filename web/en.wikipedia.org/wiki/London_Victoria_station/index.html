London Victoria station, generally known as Victoria, is a central London railway terminus and connected London Underground station in the City of Westminster, managed by Network Rail. It is one of the busiest stations in Britain, serving around 70 million passengers a year in recent pre-pandemic figures, and is the London terminus of the Gatwick Express airport service.

History

The station was built in two halves by rival companies: the London, Brighton and South Coast Railway opened its side in 1860, and the London, Chatham and Dover Railway followed in 1862. The two stations stood side by side with a dividing wall until they were unified under the Southern Railway in the 1920s. The station was named after nearby Victoria Street rather than directly after Queen Victoria. The adjacent Underground station serves the District, Circle and Victoria lines; the Victoria line station opened in 1969.

Services

Main line services are operated by Southern to the south coast and Surrey, Southeastern to Kent via the Chatham main line, and the Gatwick Express non-stop service to Gatwick Airport. The station has 19 platforms: platforms 1–8 on the eastern (Chatham) side and 9–19 on the western (Brighton) side. The nearby Victoria Coach Station is London's principal coach terminal.
