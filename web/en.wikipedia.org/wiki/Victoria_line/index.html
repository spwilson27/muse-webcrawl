The Victoria line is a London Underground line running from Brixton in south London to Walthamstow Central in the north-east, coloured light blue on the Tube map. It is one of only two Underground lines to run entirely below ground (the other being the Waterloo & City line) and is notable as the world's first fully automatic underground railway when it opened, using Automatic Train Operation (ATO) in which the driver only opens and closes the doors and starts the train.

History

A north-east to south-west cross-London route was proposed as early as the 1940s ("Route C") to relieve congestion on existing lines and serve areas without rail service. The Victoria line was authorized in 1955 and construction began in 1962. The first section, from Walthamstow Central to Highbury & Islington, opened on 1 September 1968, followed by extensions to Warren Street and then Victoria in 1969. The final section to Brixton opened on 23 July 1971, with Pimlico station added in 1972 — the station being the only one on the line without an interchange.

Route and stations

The line serves 16 stations, all but Walthamstow Central, Seven Sisters, and Finsbury Park interchanging with other lines or National Rail. Key interchanges include King's Cross St Pancras, Oxford Circus, Green Park, Victoria, and Stockwell. Every station was originally decorated with tiled motifs identifying it — for example a maze at Warren Street and a portrait-derived motif at Brixton. Trains run as frequently as every 100 seconds at peak times, among the highest frequencies of any metro in Britain.

Rolling stock and automation

Original 1967 Stock trains ran under ATO from opening, with coded currents in the running rails dictating speed. They were replaced by the 2009 Stock, built by Bombardier, which entered service from 2009. The line was resignalled in the early 2010s, raising peak capacity. In 2016 the Victoria line joined the Night Tube network, running all night on Fridays and Saturdays.
