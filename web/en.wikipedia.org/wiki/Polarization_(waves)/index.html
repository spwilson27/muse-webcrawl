Polarization (waves)

Polarization (also polarisation) is a property of transverse waves which specifies the geometrical orientation of the oscillations. In a transverse wave, the direction of the oscillation is perpendicular to the direction of motion of the wave. A simple example of a polarised transverse wave is vibrations travelling along a taut string; examples in nature include electromagnetic waves such as light and radio waves, and gravitational waves.

Types of polarization

In linear polarization, the electric field of the wave oscillates in a single plane. In circular polarization, the electric field vector rotates about the direction of propagation with constant magnitude, tracing a helix; depending on the sense of rotation it is right-handed or left-handed. The general case is elliptical polarization, in which the tip of the field vector traces an ellipse. Unpolarised light, such as sunlight or light from an incandescent lamp, consists of a rapidly varying mixture of polarisation states.

Applications

Polarisation is exploited in polarising sunglasses and photographic filters, in liquid-crystal displays, in 3D cinema systems, in stress analysis of transparent materials (photoelasticity), and in radio and optical communications, where differently polarised channels can carry independent signals.
