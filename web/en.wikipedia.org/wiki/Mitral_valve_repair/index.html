Mitral valve repair

SpecialtyCardiothoracic surgery
IndicationsMitral regurgitation, mitral valve prolapse

Mitral valve repair is a cardiac surgery procedure performed to treat stenosis (narrowing) or regurgitation (leakage) of the mitral valve. It is an alternative to mitral valve replacement and, when feasible, is generally preferred for degenerative mitral regurgitation because it preserves the patient's own valve, avoids the need for lifelong anticoagulation in most cases, and is associated with better long-term survival.

Techniques

Repair techniques include annuloplasty, in which a prosthetic ring is sewn around the valve annulus to restore its shape and size; leaflet resection, in which excess or prolapsing leaflet tissue is removed; and the placement of artificial chordae tendineae made of expanded polytetrafluoroethylene to replace ruptured chords. Minimally invasive and robotic approaches, as well as transcatheter edge-to-edge repair devices, have expanded the population of patients who can be treated.

Outcomes

In experienced centres, repair of degenerative mitral valve disease carries low operative mortality and durable results, with freedom from reoperation exceeding 90% at ten years in many series. Repair is less often feasible in rheumatic or severely calcified valves.
