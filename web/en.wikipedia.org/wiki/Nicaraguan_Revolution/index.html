Nicaraguan Revolution

The Nicaraguan Revolution (Spanish: Revolución Nicaragüense) was the process by which the Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN) overthrew the US-backed Somoza dictatorship in 1979 and governed Nicaragua through the 1980s, fighting a counter-revolutionary war against the Contras until 1990.

Insurrection

The Somoza family had ruled Nicaragua since 1936. The FSLN, founded in 1961 and named for Augusto César Sandino, waged guerrilla war through the 1970s. Corruption after the 1972 Managua earthquake and repression by the National Guard broadened opposition; following the assassination of newspaper editor Pedro Joaquín Chamorro in 1978, mass insurrection spread. The Sandinistas' final offensive in 1979 forced Anastasio Somoza Debayle to flee on 17 July 1979; the FSLN entered Managua two days later.

Sandinista government

The revolutionary government, led after 1985 by elected president Daniel Ortega, launched literacy and health campaigns and land reform, while facing a Contra insurgency armed and financed by the United States, an economic embargo, and hyperinflation. The Iran–Contra affair revealed covert US funding of the Contras. The war ended with the 1990 elections, which the FSLN lost to Violeta Chamorro's UNO coalition, producing a peaceful transfer of power.
