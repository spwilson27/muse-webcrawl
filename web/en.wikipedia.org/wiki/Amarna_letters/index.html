Amarna letters

The Amarna letters are a collection of clay tablets from the royal archives at Amarna, the capital founded by Pharaoh Akhenaten in the 14th century BCE. Mostly written in Akkadian cuneiform, the diplomatic lingua franca of the age, they preserve correspondence between Egypt and rulers in Babylon, Assyria, Mitanni, the Hittite world, Cyprus and numerous Canaanite city-states.

Contents

Great kings addressed the pharaoh as “brother” and negotiated marriages, gifts, gold, trade and alliances. Vassal rulers in the Levant wrote in a more submissive style, requesting troops and reporting rivalries among city-states such as Byblos, Jerusalem and Shechem. Their mixture of Akkadian grammar with Canaanite features is an important source for early Northwest Semitic languages.

Significance

Discovered in the late nineteenth century, the tablets transformed understanding of Late Bronze Age diplomacy by showing a system of great powers connected by envoys, gift exchange and dynastic marriage. They also illuminate conditions in Canaan shortly before the upheavals that ended the Bronze Age palace system.
