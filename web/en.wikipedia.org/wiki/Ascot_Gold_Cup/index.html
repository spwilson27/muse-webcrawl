Gold Cup
Location: Ascot Racecourse, Berkshire
Race type: Flat / Thoroughbred
Distance: 2 miles 3 furlongs 210 yards
Inaugurated: 1807

The Gold Cup is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to horses aged four years or older. It is run at Ascot over a distance of 2 miles 3 furlongs and 210 yards (4,014 metres), and takes place each year in June at Royal Ascot. It is Britain's most prestigious event for "stayers" — horses which specialise in racing over long distances.

History

The event was established in 1807, and it was originally contested over three miles. The inaugural running was won by Master Jackey. The race is traditionally held on the third day of the Royal meeting, a day colloquially known as Ladies' Day.

Records

The most successful horse in the race's history is Yeats, who won four consecutive runnings from 2006 to 2009. Lester Piggott is the leading jockey with eleven victories, and Aidan O'Brien the leading trainer. Stradivarius won three consecutive Gold Cups from 2018 to 2020.

The trophy

The winning owner receives a gold trophy, which is traditionally presented by the monarch. The Gold Cup trophy is one of three at Royal Ascot made each year and retained by the winners.
