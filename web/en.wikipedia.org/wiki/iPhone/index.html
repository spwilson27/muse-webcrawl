The iPhone is a line of smartphones designed and marketed by Apple Inc. that run the iOS operating system. The first-generation iPhone was announced by then-Apple CEO Steve Jobs on January 9, 2007, and released on June 29, 2007. Since then, Apple has annually released new iPhone models and iOS updates.

The iPhone's user interface is built around a multi-touch screen. The device introduced widely influential design elements — a large capacitive touchscreen with a minimal physical button layout, an app-based software model distributed through the App Store (launched 2008), and tight integration of hardware and software. It is credited with popularizing the modern smartphone form factor and reshaping the mobile phone industry.

History

Development began in 2004 under the secretive "Project Purple". Jobs unveiled the device at Macworld 2007 describing it as "an iPod, a phone, and an Internet communicator" in one product. The iPhone 3G (2008) added 3G networking and the App Store; the iPhone 4 (2010) introduced the Retina display and a stainless-steel and glass design; later generations added Siri (4S), Touch ID (5s), larger displays (6 series), Face ID and OLED screens (X, 2017), 5G (12 series), and USB-C and titanium construction (15 Pro series, 2023).

Sales and impact

The iPhone is one of the best-selling consumer products in history, with well over two billion units sold, and it accounts for roughly half of Apple's revenue. Its success transformed Apple into one of the world's most valuable companies and helped establish the app economy.

Criticism

The line has attracted criticism over closed ecosystem policies, repair restrictions, battery throttling revelations in 2017 ("batterygate"), and environmental and labour concerns in its supply chain.
