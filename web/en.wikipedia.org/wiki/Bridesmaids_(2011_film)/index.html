Release date: May 13, 2011

Running time: 125 minutes

Budget: $32.5 million

Box office: $306 million

Bridesmaids is a 2011 American comedy film directed by Paul Feig, written by Kristen Wiig and Annie Mumolo, and produced by Judd Apatow. Wiig stars as Annie, a down-on-her-luck baker who is asked to be maid of honour at her best friend Lillian's (Maya Rudolph) wedding, and finds herself competing with Lillian's wealthy, glamorous new friend Helen (Rose Byrne).

Plot

Annie Walker, a single woman in her mid-thirties living in Milwaukee, has lost her bakery, her savings, and her boyfriend in the recession. When her best friend Lillian becomes engaged, Annie is chosen as maid of honour, but the role throws her into rivalry with Helen, the bride's polished and affluent friend. A series of escalating disasters — a disastrous engagement party competition, food poisoning at a bridal fitting, a chaotic flight to Las Vegas, and a meltdown at the bridal shower — strains Annie's friendship with Lillian. With help from a kind police officer, Nathan Rhodes, Annie rebuilds her confidence and makes amends in time for the wedding.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing over $306 million worldwide on a budget of about $32.5 million. It was nominated for two Academy Awards: Best Supporting Actress for Melissa McCarthy, whose scene-stealing performance made her a star, and Best Original Screenplay. Bridesmaids is widely credited with demonstrating the commercial viability of female-led R-rated comedies and influencing a wave of similar films in the 2010s.
