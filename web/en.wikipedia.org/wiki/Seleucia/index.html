Seleucia

Seleucia on the Tigris
Foundedc. 305 BC by Seleucus I Nicator
LocationNear modern Baghdad, Iraq
EmpiresSeleucid, Parthian, Roman (briefly), Sasanian

Seleucia, more fully Seleucia on the Tigris, was a major Hellenistic city founded by Seleucus I Nicator, one of Alexander the Great's successors, on the west bank of the Tigris opposite the later Ctesiphon. It became the eastern capital of the Seleucid Empire.

History

Built partly with materials and population from Babylon, Seleucia grew into one of the largest cities of the ancient world, with population estimates in the hundreds of thousands, laid out on a Hippodamian grid. It fell to the Parthians under Mithridates I around 141 BC but remained a great Greek-influenced trading city. Trajan captured it in AD 116; Avidius Cassius sacked it in 165. As the Tigris shifted course, the city declined and was abandoned in the Sasanian period.

Archaeology

Excavations by the University of Michigan (1927–37) and later Italian teams revealed temples, a theatre, archives with thousands of bullae, and rich evidence of Greek–Mesopotamian cultural fusion.

Other cities

Several other Seleucias were founded across the Seleucid realm, including Seleucia Pieria, the port of Antioch.
