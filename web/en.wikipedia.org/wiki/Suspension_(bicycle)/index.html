Bicycle suspension

Bicycle suspension is the system, or systems, used to suspend the rider and bicycle in order to insulate them from the roughness of the terrain. Bicycle suspension is used primarily on mountain bikes, but is also internal common on hybrid bicycles.

Bicycle suspension
TypesFront (fork), rear (frame linkage), full, seatpost, stem
Common usesMountain biking, trail riding, commuting

History

Early experiments with sprung bicycles date to the late 19th century, but practical suspension became widespread only in the 1990s with the growth of mountain biking. Telescopic front forks and later rear linkages allowed riders to tackle rougher trails at higher speeds with greater control and comfort.

Front suspension

A suspension fork replaces the rigid front fork with a telescopic design containing springs or air chambers and a damper. Travel typically ranges from 80 mm on cross-country bikes to 200 mm on downhill machines.

Rear suspension

Rear suspension uses a pivoting rear triangle connected to the main frame through a linkage that actuates a shock absorber. Designs include single-pivot, four-bar (Horst link), and virtual-pivot systems, each trading pedaling efficiency against bump absorption.
