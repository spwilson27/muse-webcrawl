Serie A, officially known as Serie A Enilive in Italy (and Serie A Made in Italy abroad) for sponsorship reasons, is a professional league competition for football clubs located at the top of the Italian football league system. The winners of the league are awarded the Scudetto shield and the Coppa Campioni d'Italia trophy.

History

Serie A has operated as a round-robin tournament since the 1929–30 season, replacing earlier regional championships dating to 1898. Juventus, with 36 titles, is the most successful club, followed by Inter Milan (20) and AC Milan (19). The league's golden age in the 1980s and 1990s — the era of Maradona's Napoli, the Dutch trio at Milan and the "Seven Sisters" — made it widely regarded as the world's strongest league, celebrated for tactical sophistication and defensive organisation (catenaccio). The 2006 Calciopoli scandal led to Juventus's relegation and revoked titles. In recent decades champions have included Juventus (nine straight titles 2012–2020), Inter, Milan and Napoli.

Format

Twenty clubs play each other twice (38 rounds) from August to May. Three points are awarded for a win; the top four qualify for the UEFA Champions League, and the bottom three are relegated to Serie B. Since 2022–23, if the championship or final relegation place is tied on points, a one-off play-off ("spareggio") decides it.
