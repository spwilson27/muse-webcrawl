Soil fertility

Soil fertility refers to the ability of soil to sustain agricultural plant growth – to provide plant habitat and result in sustained, consistent yields of high quality. A fertile soil supplies essential plant nutrients and water in adequate amounts and proportions, has suitable structure and pH, and is substantially free of toxic substances.

Factors

Primary macronutrients are nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P) and potassium (K); secondary nutrients include calcium, magnesium and sulphur, plus trace micronutrients (iron, zinc, boron etc.). Fertility also depends on soil organic matter, texture, structure, cation exchange capacity, drainage, and the soil microbiome, which mineralises nutrients and fixes nitrogen (e.g., legume–rhizobium symbiosis).

Management and depletion

Fertility is maintained by crop rotation, cover crops, manure, compost, liming acid soils, and mineral fertilisers guided by soil testing – underpinning Liebig's "law of the minimum". It is depleted by continuous cropping without replacement, erosion, overgrazing, salinisation and nutrient mining, leading to land degradation. Excess fertiliser causes nutrient runoff and eutrophication.
