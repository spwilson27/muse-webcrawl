Stephen A. Schwarzman Building

The Stephen A. Schwarzman Building, commonly known as the Main Branch of the New York Public Library, is a Beaux-Arts landmark at Fifth Avenue and 42nd Street in Midtown Manhattan, New York City. Opened on 23 May 1911, it is one of the most famous libraries in the world.

Architecture

Designed by the firm Carrère and Hastings, the marble building is celebrated for its grand entrance steps flanked by two marble lions — nicknamed "Patience" and "Fortitude" by Mayor Fiorello La Guardia during the Great Depression — and for the Rose Main Reading Room, a vast, recently restored space nearly two city blocks long. The building occupies the site of the former Croton Reservoir.

Collections and name

It houses the library's research collections in the humanities and social sciences, including the Berg Collection, the Map Division and extensive rare-book holdings. In 2008 the building was renamed for financier Stephen A. Schwarzman, who donated $100 million toward the library's expansion. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1965.
