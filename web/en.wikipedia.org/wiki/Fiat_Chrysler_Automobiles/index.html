Fiat Chrysler Automobiles N.V. (FCA) was an Italian-American multinational automobile manufacturer, formed in 2014 by the merger of Italy's Fiat S.p.A. and the American Chrysler Group. It was at the time the world's eighth-largest automaker. In January 2021 FCA merged with France's Groupe PSA to form Stellantis.

History

Fiat first took a stake in Chrysler in 2009 as part of the American company's government-brokered bankruptcy restructuring, gradually increasing its holding under CEO Sergio Marchionne until acquiring full ownership in January 2014. The combined group was incorporated in the Netherlands as Fiat Chrysler Automobiles N.V., listed in New York and Milan, with its portfolio spanning mass-market brands (Fiat, Chrysler, Dodge), the highly profitable Jeep and Ram truck brands, and premium marques Alfa Romeo and Maserati. FCA spun off Ferrari in 2015–16 and sold parts maker Magneti Marelli in 2019.

Marchionne, the architect of the merger, stepped down for health reasons in July 2018 and died days later; he was succeeded by Mike Manley. In December 2019 FCA and PSA announced a 50/50 "merger of equals"; the deal closed on 16 January 2021, creating Stellantis, then the world's fourth-largest automaker by volume.

Operations

FCA organised its business by region (NAFTA, LATAM, APAC, EMEA) plus the Maserati brand and components operations (including Comau robotics and Teksid). Its North American profits depended heavily on Jeep SUVs and Ram pickups, while the Fiat brand anchored its European and Latin American small-car business.
