Scalloway Castle is a tower house in Scalloway, on the Mainland of Shetland, Scotland. It was built in 1600 by Patrick Stewart, 2nd Earl of Orkney, during his oppressive rule over Orkney and Shetland.

History

Earl Patrick, notorious for forcing the islanders to build his residences with unpaid labour, constructed the castle on a promontory overlooking Scalloway harbour, then the capital of Shetland. The tower was intended as his Shetland seat, complementing the Earl's Palace in Kirkwall. After Patrick's execution for treason in 1615 the castle passed to the Crown; it was garrisoned by Cromwellian troops during the Commonwealth and gradually fell into ruin in the 18th century. It is now in the care of Historic Environment Scotland.

Architecture

The castle is an L-plan tower house of four storeys with corner turrets. An inscription panel above the original entrance is said to have warned that the house's foundations rested on law and justice; local tradition linked its fall to the Earl's tyranny.
