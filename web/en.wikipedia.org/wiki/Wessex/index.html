Wessex

Kingdom of Wessex
Existence:c. 519 – 927
Capital:Winchester
Language:West Saxon Old English
Notable kings:Cerdic (legendary founder), Egbert, Alfred the Great, Æthelstan
Fate:Became the core of the unified Kingdom of England

Wessex (Old English: Westseaxna rīce, "Kingdom of the West Saxons") was an Anglo-Saxon kingdom in southern England, from about 519 until England's unification in 927. Under Alfred the Great it was the last English kingdom to resist the Viking Great Heathen Army, and its dynasty founded the Kingdom of England.

Origins

According to the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, the West Saxons were founded by Cerdic and Cynric, who landed on the Hampshire coast in 495 — a tradition historians treat as at least partly legendary. The early kingdom centred on the upper Thames valley and gradually absorbed British and rival Saxon territories.

Expansion

Under kings such as Ceawlin and Ine (whose law code survives), Wessex expanded westward into Devon and Somerset. In the 820s Egbert of Wessex defeated Mercia and briefly became overlord (bretwalda) of all England south of the Humber; Wessex thereafter absorbed Kent, Sussex and Surrey.

Alfred and the Vikings

Alfred the Great (r. 871–899) saved Wessex from the Danes, winning at Edington in 878 after hiding in the Somerset marshes at Athelney. He rebuilt defence with fortified burhs, a standing navy and reforms of law and learning, translating Latin works into Old English.

Unification of England

Alfred's children Edward the Elder and Æthelflæd conquered the southern Danelaw; his grandson Æthelstan is regarded as the first king of England (927). The House of Wessex ruled until 1066, interrupted by Danish kings. Thomas Hardy later revived the name "Wessex" for the fictional setting of his novels.
