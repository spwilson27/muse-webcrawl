Capitoline Wolf

The Capitoline Wolf (Italian: Lupa Capitolina) is a famous bronze sculpture depicting a scene from the legend of the founding of Rome: a she-wolf suckling the twin founders, Romulus and Remus. The wolf figure measures about 75 cm (29.5 in) high and 114 cm (44.8 in) long, and is preserved in the Capitoline Museums in Rome. Long believed to be an Etruscan work of the 5th century BCE, the wolf's date has been debated since 2006, when technical studies suggested it might instead have been cast in the Middle Ages, in the 11th–12th centuries; the figures of the twins were added in the late 15th century, probably by the Florentine sculptor Antonio del Pollaiuolo.

Description

The she-wolf stands alert, her head turned toward the viewer, ears pricked and ribs visible beneath a stylised mane of tight spiralling curls; beneath her belly the infant twins reach up to suckle. The wolf's tense, watchful pose contrasts with the naturalism of the babies, which were modelled during the Renaissance.

History and dating debate

A statue of a wolf was recorded at the Lateran Palace in the Middle Ages, and the sculpture is documented on the Capitoline from 1471, when Pope Sixtus IV donated a collection of bronzes to the Roman people. Traditionally assigned to an Etruscan workshop of around 500–480 BCE, the wolf was redated by some scholars after restoration work (1997–2006) revealed it had been cast in a single piece using a lost-wax technique and mould material whose radiocarbon dating pointed to the medieval period. Other specialists defend the ancient date, and the question remains contested; the museum presents the work with its disputed dating.

Symbol and copies

The Capitoline Wolf is one of the most recognisable symbols of Rome, reproduced on countless souvenirs, emblems, and the insignia of AS Roma football club. Copies and variants stand in cities worldwide, including those given by the Italian state in the early 20th century to cities such as Cincinnati and Rome, Georgia, in the United States. The image also appeared on Italian euro coins (the 50 cent coin depicts the related equestrian statue of Marcus Aurelius, while the she-wolf appeared on pre-euro coinage and stamps).
