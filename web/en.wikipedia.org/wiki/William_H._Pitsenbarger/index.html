William H. Pitsenbarger

Born: 8 July 1944, Piqua, Ohio
Died: 11 April 1966, near Cam My, South Vietnam
Service: United States Air Force, Pararescue
Awards: Medal of Honor

William Hart Pitsenbarger (July 8, 1944 – April 11, 1966) was a United States Air Force pararescueman who was killed in action during the Vietnam War and posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor for his actions in the Battle of Xa Cam My — the first enlisted airman to receive the medal during the war.

Service

Born in Piqua, Ohio, Pitsenbarger enlisted in the Air Force in 1963 and volunteered for Pararescue. In Vietnam he flew with the 38th Aerospace Rescue and Recovery Squadron and was credited with saving more than 300 men over some 300 rescue missions.

Battle of Xa Cam My

On 11 April 1966, Pitsenbarger was lowered by helicopter into dense jungle to treat and evacuate wounded soldiers of the 1st Infantry Division who were pinned down by a much larger Viet Cong force. He refused evacuation himself, remained on the ground organising the defence and treating casualties, and was killed by enemy fire. His actions were credited with helping many of the wounded survive the night.

Recognition

Initially awarded the Air Force Cross, his decoration was upgraded to the Medal of Honor in 2000 after a long campaign by the soldiers he had aided. The 2019 film The Last Full Measure dramatises his story.
