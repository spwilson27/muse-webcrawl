American Silver Eagle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The American Silver Eagle, the official silver bullion coin of the United States.

The American Silver Eagle is the official silver bullion coin of the United States. It was first released by the United States Mint on November 24, 1986, under the authority of the Liberty Coin Act of 1985, which required the silver to come from the national defense stockpile (later, from newly mined domestic sources).

Design

The obverse reproduces Adolph A. Weinman's Walking Liberty design, originally used on the half dollar from 1916 to 1947, widely considered one of the most beautiful designs in American coinage. The reverse originally bore a heraldic eagle designed by John Mercanti; beginning in 2021 it was replaced by Emily Damstra's design of an eagle coming in for a landing, and the obverse was sharpened using Weinman's original plaster models.

Versions and collecting

The Mint issues bullion strikes for investors, proof versions for collectors (originally from San Francisco, later West Point), and uncirculated "burnished" versions. Annual sales have often run to tens of millions of coins, making the Silver Eagle one of the world's most popular silver bullion products. It is also a staple of precious-metals individual retirement accounts.
