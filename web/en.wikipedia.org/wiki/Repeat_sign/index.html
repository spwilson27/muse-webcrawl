Repeat sign

In music notation, a repeat sign is a sign that indicates a section should be repeated. If a piece has one repeat sign, it means the performer should play the section again; two signs enclose a passage to be repeated. Repeat signs save space and reduce the need to write out identical music twice.

Notation

The standard repeat sign consists of a double barline with two dots placed in the middle spaces of the staff, one above and one below the centre line. A sign facing right (𝄆) marks the beginning of a repeated section, and a sign facing left (𝄇) marks its end. If there is no left-facing sign at the start, the repeat returns to the beginning of the piece or of the movement.

First and second endings

When a repeated passage has a different ending each time, the endings are marked with brackets numbered 1 and 2 (or more), called prima volta and seconda volta. On the first pass the performer plays the first ending and repeats; on the second pass the first ending is skipped and the second ending is played instead.

Other repeat conventions

Related devices include the da capo (D.C.) and dal segno (D.S.) instructions, the coda sign, and the percent-like simile mark (𝄎) indicating repetition of the previous measure. In popular music lead sheets, repeat signs are combined with instructions such as "repeat and fade".
