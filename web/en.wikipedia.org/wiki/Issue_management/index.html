Issue management

Issue management is the process by which organisations identify, analyse, and respond to emerging social, political, regulatory, or public-opinion trends that could affect them. It is a discipline within public relations and corporate strategic planning, intended to narrow the gap between an organisation's actions and stakeholder expectations before an issue becomes a crisis.

Process

The standard model involves issue identification and scanning, analysis and prioritisation, strategy development, implementation, and evaluation. Analysts monitor media, legislation, and activist activity to detect "weak signals" of emerging concerns, then assess their likely impact and the time available to respond.

Origins

The concept was formalised in the late 1970s, notably by Howard Chase, who founded the Issue Management Council in 1982. It developed alongside crisis management and stakeholder theory, and is now embedded in many large corporations and government agencies.
