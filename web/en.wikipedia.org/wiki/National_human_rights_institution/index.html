National human rights institution

Abbreviation: NHRI
Governing standards: Paris Principles (1993)
Accreditation body: Global Alliance of NHRIs (GANHRI)
Forms: Commissions, ombudsman offices, institutes

A national human rights institution (NHRI) is an independent state body established by a constitution or statute to promote and protect human rights in a particular country. Typical forms include human rights commissions, ombudsman institutions and national human rights institutes.

The Paris Principles

The international standards for NHRIs are the Paris Principles, adopted by the UN General Assembly in 1993. They require a broad mandate, independence from government, pluralist membership, adequate funding, and powers to investigate, advise government, report publicly, hear complaints and monitor compliance with international human rights law.

Accreditation

NHRIs are peer-accredited by the Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions (GANHRI) through its Sub-Committee on Accreditation. A-status institutions, judged fully compliant with the Paris Principles, may speak and participate in the UN Human Rights Council and other UN bodies; B-status institutions are partially compliant observers. Well over one hundred countries have established NHRIs, from Denmark's and Germany's institutes to the commissions of South Africa, India, Canada and Australia.
