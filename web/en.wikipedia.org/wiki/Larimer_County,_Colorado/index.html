Larimer County, Colorado

Larimer County is a county located in the U.S. state of Colorado, along the state's northern border with Wyoming. As of the 2020 census its population was about 359,000, making it one of Colorado's most populous counties. The county seat and largest city is Fort Collins, home to Colorado State University; other communities include Loveland, Estes Park, Wellington, Timnath and Berthoud. The county was named for William Larimer Jr., a founder of Denver.

Geography

Larimer County spans about 2,600 square miles from the high plains in the east to the Continental Divide in the west, where Rocky Mountain National Park occupies much of its mountainous portion. The Cache la Poudre River — Colorado's only designated National Wild and Scenic River — flows through the county, as does the Big Thompson River. Much of the western county is Roosevelt National Forest land. The county is prone to both major flooding (notably the Big Thompson flood of 1976) and large wildfires, including the Cameron Peak Fire of 2020, the largest in Colorado history.

Economy and recreation

The economy mixes higher education, technology and brewing in Fort Collins (New Belgium and Odell brewing companies), agriculture on the plains, and tourism centred on Estes Park and the national park. Colorado State University, founded in 1870, is the county's largest employer.
