The Warsaw radio mast was a steel lattice mast at Konstantynów, Gąbin, Poland, used for longwave broadcasting by Polish Radio. Completed in 1974, it stood 646 metres (2,121 ft) tall, making it the tallest structure ever built at the time — a record it held until the Burj Khalifa surpassed it in 2008, years after its destruction.

Design and purpose

The guyed mast, designed by engineer Jan Polak, radiated a 2,000-kilowatt longwave signal on 227 kHz (later 225 kHz) that could be received across Europe and beyond. Its height — more than half a wavelength — provided exceptional ground-wave propagation.

Collapse

On 8 August 1991, during renovation work in which guy wires were being exchanged, a temporary guy failed in strong winds and the mast buckled and collapsed. Remarkably, no one was killed. Longwave broadcasts later resumed from a replacement facility at Solec Kujawski.
