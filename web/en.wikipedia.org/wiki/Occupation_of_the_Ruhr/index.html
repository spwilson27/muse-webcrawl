The occupation of the Ruhr was the occupation of Germany's industrial Ruhr valley by French and Belgian troops between January 1923 and August 1925, undertaken to enforce the payment of reparations under the Treaty of Versailles after Germany defaulted on deliveries of coal and timber.

Passive resistance and hyperinflation

The German government under Chancellor Wilhelm Cuno responded by ordering "passive resistance": workers struck and officials refused to cooperate, while Berlin printed money to support the strikers. This policy accelerated the German hyperinflation of 1923, which destroyed the value of the Papiermark and wiped out savings. Clashes between occupation forces and resisters, including the sabotage of railways and the Krupp works incident in Essen, left over a hundred Germans dead.

Resolution

Chancellor Gustav Stresemann called off passive resistance in September 1923. The crisis was resolved diplomatically through the Dawes Plan of 1924, which restructured reparations with American loans; French and Belgian forces withdrew by the summer of 1925.
