Paranormal

Paranormal events are purported phenomena described as being outside the scope of normal scientific understanding — beyond ("para") the normal. The term, in use since the early twentieth century, covers a wide range of claimed phenomena including extrasensory perception (telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition), psychokinesis, ghosts and hauntings, apparitions, near-death experiences, reincarnation, UFOs (in some usages), and cryptozoological creatures.

Study and investigation

Parapsychology, founded in the early twentieth century and associated with researchers such as J. B. Rhine, attempts to study paranormal claims using laboratory methods; it remains outside the scientific mainstream, as claimed effects have not proved consistently reproducible under controlled conditions. Ghost hunting and UFO investigation are largely amateur pursuits and popular entertainment, popularized by television programmes and "paranormal tourism" at reputedly haunted locations.

Scientific assessment

The scientific community generally regards paranormal phenomena as unsupported by reliable evidence, and explanations for reported experiences typically invoke misperception, sleep paralysis, hallucinations, cognitive biases such as apophenia and confirmation bias, fraud, and folklore. Organizations such as the Committee for Skeptical Inquiry investigate claims and offer natural explanations; the James Randi Educational Foundation's long-standing prize for demonstrating paranormal ability under controlled conditions was never claimed.

Prevalence of belief

Surveys in many countries find that belief in at least one paranormal phenomenon is common — Gallup polls of Americans have repeatedly found substantial minorities endorsing belief in ghosts, haunted houses, ESP, and astrology. The paranormal is a major theme in fiction, film, and television, from ghost stories to series such as The X-Files.
