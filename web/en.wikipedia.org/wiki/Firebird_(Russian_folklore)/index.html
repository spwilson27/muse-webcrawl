Firebird (Russian folklore)

The Firebird (Russian: Жар-птица, Zhar-ptitsa) is a magical bird in Russian folklore and fairy tales. It is a large bird with majestic plumage that glows brightly in red, orange, and yellow light, like a bonfire too bright for the eye; even a single feather continues to glow and can light a room.

In fairy tales

The Firebird is traditionally the object of a difficult quest. In the tale of Ivan Tsarevich and the Firebird, the bird steals golden apples from the Tsar's orchard, and the Tsar sends his sons to capture it. Ivan, aided by the Grey Wolf, journeys to distant kingdoms, captures the Firebird, and wins the hand of Princess Yelena the Beautiful. The Firebird also appears in the tale of Vasilisa the Beautiful and in other stories collected by Alexander Afanasyev.

The bird's capture typically involves a warning ignored: Ivan is told not to take the golden cage, and touching it sets off an alarm — a pattern of the "difficult task" motif in which the firebird is both a prize and a source of further adventures.

In art and music

The Firebird inspired Igor Stravinsky's ballet The Firebird (1910), written for Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes with choreography by Michel Fokine, which brought Stravinsky international fame. The legendary bird has also been a popular subject for Russian lacquer miniatures, especially from Palekh.
