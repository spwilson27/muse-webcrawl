Swing (musical theatre)

In musical theatre, a swing is a member of the ensemble who is responsible for learning multiple parts — called "tracks" — in the production's choreography and staging, and who steps into any of those tracks when a regular cast member is absent, ill, on holiday, or injured. Unlike an understudy, who covers a principal role, a swing covers several ensemble tracks and may play a different role every night.

The role

Swings are considered among the hardest-working performers in a company: a swing in a large dance musical may need to know a dozen or more tracks, each with its own choreography, vocal harmony, costume changes and props, recorded in detailed personal "swing bibles" of diagrams and notes. Some swings are designated "dance captains" who also maintain the show's choreography and teach new cast members. Because a swing may receive only a few hours' notice — occasionally learning mid-show that they must go on — the job demands exceptional memory, versatility and calm.

Types of cover

Productions distinguish swings (covering ensemble tracks), understudies (covering principal roles, often while playing an ensemble track themselves), standbys (covering a star role while performing no other track) and alternates. The system allows long-running shows to perform eight shows a week without cancellation despite the physical toll of the repertoire.
