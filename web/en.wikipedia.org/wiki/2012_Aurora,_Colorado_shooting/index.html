The 2012 Aurora, Colorado shooting was a mass shooting that occurred on 20 July 2012 during a midnight screening of the film The Dark Knight Rises at a Century 16 cinema in Aurora, Colorado. The gunman, James Holmes, set off tear gas grenades and shot into the audience, killing 12 people and injuring 70 others — at the time the largest number of casualties in a single US mass shooting.

Attack

Holmes bought a ticket, left through an emergency exit propped open, changed into tactical clothing and returned with a shotgun, a semi-automatic rifle and a handgun. He fired first into the screen area and then at the audience; his rifle's 100-round drum magazine jammed, likely preventing a higher toll. Police arrested him beside his car behind the theatre within minutes. His apartment was found elaborately booby-trapped with explosives, which bomb squads neutralised over the following day.

Perpetrator and trial

Holmes, a former neuroscience graduate student at the University of Colorado, pleaded not guilty by reason of insanity. In 2015 a jury convicted him on all counts including 24 counts of first-degree murder (two for each victim). Jurors could not unanimously agree on the death penalty, so he received twelve consecutive life sentences without parole plus 3,318 years.

Aftermath

The shooting reignited the American gun-control debate and led Colorado to enact expanded background checks and magazine limits in 2013. Warner Bros. made donations to victims, and the cinema reopened in 2013 as the Century Aurora and XD.
