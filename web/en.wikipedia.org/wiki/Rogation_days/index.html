Rogation days

Observed byWestern Christianity (Roman Catholic, Anglican, some Lutheran and Methodist traditions)
TypeDays of prayer, fasting, and processions
DatesMajor Rogation: 25 April; Minor Rogations: the three days before Ascension Thursday

Rogation days are days of prayer and fasting in the Western Christian liturgical calendar, traditionally accompanied by processions and litanies asking God's blessing on agriculture and protection against calamities. The name derives from the Latin rogare, "to ask." There are two sets: the Major Rogation on 25 April, and the Minor Rogations on the Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday before Ascension Day.

Origins

The Minor Rogations were instituted by St Mamertus, bishop of Vienne in Gaul, around the year 470, after earthquakes, fires, and other disasters afflicted his diocese; the observance spread through France and was adopted at Rome by Pope Leo III around 816. The Major Rogation of 25 April, of Roman origin, coincides with (and may have Christianized) the pagan Robigalia festival, in which processions prayed to the god Robigus to protect grain from mildew and rust.

Observance

Traditionally the faithful processed around parish boundaries singing the Litany of the Saints, blessing crops and fields and praying for good weather — a practice linked in England to "beating the bounds." Rogation processions featured a dragon banner in some places: the dragon led the procession for the first days (representing pestilence) and followed, tail lowered, on the last. After the Second Vatican Council, the Roman Catholic Church left Rogation observance to bishops' conferences, and many places transferred or dropped the processions; Anglican and Lutheran churches often retain Rogation Sunday (the Sunday before Ascension) and field blessings. The Eastern Orthodox equivalent is the procession with icons and blessing of fields, though the Western dates are not observed in the same form.
