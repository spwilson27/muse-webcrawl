Keihanshin

Osaka, the largest city of the Keihanshin region

Keihanshin (京阪神, "Kyoto–Osaka–Kobe") is a metropolitan region in the Kansai region of Japan, encompassing the metropolitan areas of the cities of Kyoto, Osaka and Kobe. The name combines one kanji character from each city: kei (京) from Kyoto, han (阪) from Osaka, and shin (神) from Kobe. It is the second most populous urban region in Japan after Greater Tokyo, with a population of roughly 19 million.

Geography and definition

The region lies around Osaka Bay at the eastern end of the Seto Inland Sea and extends inland to Lake Biwa. Depending on the definition used, the Keihanshin major metropolitan area covers much of Osaka, Hyōgo, Kyoto and Nara prefectures and parts of Shiga and Wakayama. Its principal cities are Osaka (a major commercial and industrial centre), Kobe (a great port city), and Kyoto (the former imperial capital and cultural centre), together with cities such as Sakai, Higashiosaka, Nishinomiya, Amagasaki and Toyonaka.

Economy and transport

Keihanshin is one of Japan's great economic regions, historically centred on Osaka's commerce and the Hanshin industrial belt between Osaka and Kobe. The region is served by Kansai International Airport and Osaka (Itami) Airport, the ports of Osaka and Kobe, the Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen lines, and an exceptionally dense network of JR and private railways operated by companies such as Hankyu, Hanshin, Keihan and Kintetsu.
