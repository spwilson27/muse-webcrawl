Cornelis Lely

Cornelis Lely (23 September 1854 – 22 January 1929) was a Dutch civil engineer and statesman. He designed the Zuiderzee Works, the vast system of dams, land reclamation and drainage by which the Dutch enclosed the Zuiderzee inlet, and served three times as Minister of Water Management.

Career

Lely studied engineering at the Polytechnic School in Delft. In 1891, as a young engineer working for the Zuiderzee Association, he produced the technically complete plan for closing off the Zuiderzee that would guide the works for decades: a great enclosure dam, the Afsluitdijk, and a series of polders, turning the salt-water inlet into the freshwater IJsselmeer. The catastrophic flood of 1916 helped secure passage of the Zuiderzee Act in 1918; work began in 1920 and the Afsluitdijk was completed in 1932, three years after Lely's death.

Legacy

The city of Lelystad, capital of the reclaimed province of Flevoland, is named after him, as are numerous streets and engineering works in the Netherlands. His statue stands on the Afsluitdijk, looking out over the land his plans created.
