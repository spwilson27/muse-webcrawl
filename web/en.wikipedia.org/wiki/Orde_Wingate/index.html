Orde Wingate

Orde Charles Wingate (26 February 1903 – 24 March 1944) was a senior British Army officer known for his creation of the Special Night Squads in Mandatory Palestine and of the Chindits, a long-range penetration force that operated behind Japanese lines in Burma during the Second World War. An unconventional and deeply religious officer, Wingate championed irregular warfare, small-unit deep-penetration tactics and close air support supplied from the air.

Early life and Palestine

Wingate was born in Nainital, India, into a family associated with the Plymouth Brethren. Commissioned into the Royal Artillery in 1923, he served in the Sudan Defence Force before being posted to Palestine in 1936 during the Arab revolt. There he organised the Special Night Squads, mixed British and Jewish units that carried out night ambushes against Arab insurgents, and became an ardent supporter of the Zionist cause.

Ethiopia and Burma

In 1941 Wingate led Gideon Force, a guerrilla formation that helped restore Emperor Haile Selassie during the East African campaign. In 1942 he was sent to Burma, where he formed the Chindits (77th Indian Infantry Brigade). In Operation Longcloth (1943), some 3,000 Chindits marched deep behind Japanese lines, supplied by air, damaging railways and gathering intelligence. A much larger second operation, Operation Thursday, began in March 1944 with tens of thousands of men flown in by glider. Wingate was killed on 24 March 1944 when his aircraft crashed in the Naga Hills; he was posthumously awarded the Distinguished Service Order and is remembered as one of Britain's most original, if controversial, commanders of the war.
