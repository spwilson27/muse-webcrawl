God the Son

God the Son is the second person of the Trinity in Christian theology. The doctrine of the Trinity states that God is one God in three divine persons: the Father, the Son (Jesus Christ), and the Holy Spirit. The Son is believed to be eternally begotten of the Father — "begotten, not made" — and consubstantial with him, as defined by the Nicene Creed.

"God the Son" is not a phrase found in the Bible itself; it emerged from later theological reflection on titles applied to Jesus such as "Son of God" and on the prologue of the Gospel of John, which identifies the Word (Logos) as God and as the one who "became flesh".

Theology

In orthodox Christology, God the Son eternally existed as the Logos before the Incarnation and became incarnate as Jesus of Nazareth, fully God and fully human in one person (the hypostatic union, defined at the Council of Chalcedon in 451). The Son is distinct from the Father yet of the same essence; the Son is begotten of the Father, while the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father (and, in Western theology, from the Son — the filioque).

Distinction from "Son of God"

"Son of God" is a biblical title applied to Jesus (and in the Old Testament to Israel, angels and kings), whereas "God the Son" is a doctrinal formulation emphasising the Son's full divinity — the reverse order stressing that the person is God, subsisting in the relation of sonship.

Views in other traditions

Nontrinitarian Christians reject the doctrine; in Islam, Jesus (Isa) is honoured as a prophet and Messiah but the notion of God having a son is firmly denied. In the Latter Day Saint movement, God the Father, Jesus Christ and the Holy Ghost are understood as three distinct beings united in purpose.
