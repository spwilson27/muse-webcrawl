Striding Edge

Elevationapprox. 850 m (2,790 ft)
LocationLake District, Cumbria, England
Parent peakHelvellyn
TypeArête

Striding Edge is a famous arête on the eastern side of Helvellyn in the Lake District of Cumbria, England. The knife-edged ridge, forming the head of the valley between Red Tarn and Nethermost Cove, is the most popular route to Helvellyn's summit and one of the best-known ridge walks in Britain, usually combined with its neighbouring arête, Swirral Edge, in a horseshoe circuit from Patterdale or Glenridding.

The ridge

The walk along the crest is an exposed grade 1 scramble with steep drops on both sides; the most awkward step is a short descent of a rock tower near the end of the ridge, before the final steep rise to the summit plateau. In winter conditions the route becomes a serious mountaineering undertaking. Alfred Wainwright, who called Striding Edge the finest mountain ridge in Lakeland, is commemorated by a memorial stone for Charles Gough, who died in a fall nearby in 1805 and whose dog's fidelity inspired poems by Wordsworth and Scott.
