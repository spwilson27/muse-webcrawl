AGM-65 Maverick

AGM-65 Maverick
Type: Air-to-surface missile
Manufacturer: Hughes / Raytheon
In service: 1972–present
Warhead: 57 or 136 kg shaped charge / blast-fragmentation

The AGM-65 Maverick is an American air-to-surface missile designed for close air support against armoured and hardened targets. It entered service in 1972 and has been used by the United States and more than 30 other countries.

Guidance and variants

Early A and B models used electro-optical (television) guidance; the D and F models use imaging infrared seekers for night operation, the E model uses laser guidance, and the G model carries the heavy blast-penetration warhead. The pilot locks the seeker onto the target before launch, making Maverick a fire-and-forget weapon in most variants.

Combat use

Mavericks were used extensively in the Vietnam War's final years, the Iran–Iraq War, the 1991 Gulf War — where they destroyed large numbers of Iraqi tanks — and subsequent conflicts. Launch aircraft include the F-16, A-10 Thunderbolt II and F/A-18. See also Air-to-surface missile.
