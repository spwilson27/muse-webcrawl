VORTEX projects

The VORTEX projects (Verification of the Origins of Rotation in Tornadoes Experiment) were a series of large field experiments in the United States designed to understand how tornadoes form, funded principally by the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) and the National Science Foundation (NSF). The original VORTEX ran in 1994–95, and VORTEX2 followed in 2009–10, becoming the largest tornado field study ever undertaken.

VORTEX (1994–1995)

Based at the National Severe Storms Laboratory in Norman, Oklahoma, the first VORTEX deployed mobile Doppler radars, instrumented vehicles, and teams of storm chasers across the Great Plains during the spring tornado seasons of 1994 and 1995. It documented the complete life cycles of several tornadoes, including those of the 3 June 1995 outbreak, and produced detailed observations of tornadogenesis in supercell thunderstorms.

VORTEX2 (2009–2010)

VORTEX2 fielded more than 100 scientists and 40 vehicles, including mobile radars such as the Doppler on Wheels, mobile mesonets, and unmanned aircraft, with the goal of surrounding a tornadic supercell with instruments. In June 2009 the armada fully sampled a tornado near LaGrange, Wyoming, the most comprehensively observed tornado in history at that time. The project studied why some supercells produce tornadoes and others do not, and the data have been used to improve tornado forecasting and warnings.

Related projects

Later efforts, including VORTEX-SE (focused on the southeastern United States, beginning in 2016) and the TORUS project, extended the approach to other regions and storm types.
