Free company

A free company (in Italian, compagnia di ventura) was an army of mercenaries that operated in Europe from the 12th to the 14th centuries, hiring itself out to states and lords in wartime and living by plunder or extortion in peacetime.

History

Free companies grew out of the bands of unemployed soldiers left behind by truces in the Hundred Years' War, such as the routiers and écorcheurs in France. In Italy, foreign-led companies such as the Great Company of Werner von Urslingen, the White Company of John Hawkwood, and the Catalan Company in Greece became powers in their own right, extorting protection money from cities and sometimes seizing territory. Their captains, the condottieri, increasingly formalised contracts (condotte) with employers.

The companies were feared for their brutality but were militarily formidable, and Italian states came to rely on them while working to control them. By the fifteenth century, permanent standing armies and more tightly supervised condottieri reduced the independence of the great free companies.
