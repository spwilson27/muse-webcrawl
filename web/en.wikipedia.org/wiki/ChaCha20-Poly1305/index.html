ChaCha20-Poly1305

ChaCha20-Poly1305 is an authenticated encryption with associated data (AEAD) algorithm that combines the ChaCha20 stream cipher with the Poly1305 message authentication code, both designed by Daniel J. Bernstein. It is widely used in Internet protocols, notably TLS 1.3, where it offers high performance in software, especially on devices without AES hardware acceleration.

ChaCha20 encrypts the plaintext while Poly1305 authenticates the ciphertext and associated data.

Construction

In the IETF variant specified in RFC 8439, encryption uses a 256-bit key, a 96-bit nonce and a 32-bit block counter. The first ChaCha20 block (counter 0) is generated and its first 32 bytes are used as a one-time Poly1305 key. The plaintext is then encrypted with ChaCha20 starting at counter 1. The Poly1305 tag is computed over the associated data, the ciphertext and their lengths, each padded to a multiple of 16 bytes. A related variant, XChaCha20-Poly1305, uses an extended 192-bit nonce.

Adoption

Google adopted ChaCha20-Poly1305 for HTTPS traffic from Android devices and Chrome in 2014, reporting substantially better performance than AES-GCM on processors lacking AES instructions. The construction was standardised for TLS in RFC 7905 and is one of only three cipher suites permitted in TLS 1.3. It is also used in the WireGuard VPN protocol, OpenSSH, and numerous libraries.

Security

The security of the construction rests on the ChaCha20 permutation and the Poly1305 one-time authenticator. As with other AEAD schemes, nonce reuse with the same key is catastrophic: it leaks the Poly1305 key and exposes the XOR of plaintexts. Implementations are typically constant-time and free of lookup tables, making the cipher attractive on embedded and mobile hardware.
