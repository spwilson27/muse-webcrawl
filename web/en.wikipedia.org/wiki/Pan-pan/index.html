Pan-pan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pan-pan is the international radiotelephony urgency signal, used in maritime and aviation communication to declare that a vessel or aircraft has an urgent problem that requires assistance but does not pose an immediate danger to life or to the craft itself. It is one level below the full distress signal mayday.

Etymology and use

The signal derives from the French word panne, meaning a breakdown or failure. Like mayday, it is spoken three times: "Pan-pan, pan-pan, pan-pan." Typical situations warranting a pan-pan call include engine failure on a vessel that is not in immediate peril, a fouled propeller, loss of steering, a medical problem that is serious but not life-threatening, or running out of fuel. The call takes priority over routine traffic but yields to any mayday distress call, and it alerts rescue authorities and nearby traffic to stand by.

Related signals

In Morse code and some jurisdictions the corresponding urgency signal is XXX. Aviation uses pan-pan in the same sense; a pilot may declare "pan-pan" for problems such as a rough-running engine or a passenger medical issue. A further step below pan-pan is the safety signal sécurité, used for navigational and meteorological warnings. Misuse of the urgency signal is discouraged, but mariners are taught to err on the side of calling early: a pan-pan can always be upgraded to a mayday if the situation worsens.
