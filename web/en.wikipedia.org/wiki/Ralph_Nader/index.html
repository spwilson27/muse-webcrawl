Ralph Nader (born February 27, 1934) is an American lawyer, political activist and author known for his longstanding advocacy of consumer protection, environmentalism and government reform. His critique of the automobile industry's safety record, set out in his 1965 book Unsafe at Any Speed, helped spur the passage of federal automobile safety legislation in the United States.

Early life and education

Nader was born in Winsted, Connecticut, the son of Lebanese immigrants Nathra and Rose Nader, who ran a restaurant and bakery where political discussion was a family staple. He graduated magna cum laude from Princeton University in 1955 and from Harvard Law School in 1958. After serving in the United States Army, he practised law in Hartford and lectured in history and government.

Consumer advocacy

Unsafe at Any Speed: The Designed-In Dangers of the American Automobile (1965) attacked the industry's resistance to safety features, devoting its first chapter to the Chevrolet Corvair's handling defects. General Motors responded by hiring private investigators to probe Nader's private life and discredit him; when this was exposed in Senate hearings, GM's president apologised publicly. Nader sued for invasion of privacy, won a settlement of $425,000, and used the money to fund his public-interest work. The book and the scandal helped pass the National Traffic and Motor Vehicle Safety Act of 1966.

Working with young volunteer researchers — dubbed "Nader's Raiders" — he published reports on the Federal Trade Commission, food safety, water pollution and other subjects, and in 1971 founded the consumer organisation Public Citizen. Nader and his network of groups are credited with helping to secure, among others, the Safe Drinking Water Act, the Freedom of Information Act amendments, the Consumer Product Safety Act, and the creation of the Environmental Protection Agency and the Occupational Safety and Health Administration's framework.

Presidential campaigns

Nader ran for President of the United States four times: as the Green Party nominee in 1996 and 2000, and as an independent in 2004 and 2008. In 2000 he won 2.74% of the national popular vote, and his candidacy in Florida — where he won 97,488 votes while the state was decided by 537 — led many Democrats to argue he had spoiled the election for Al Gore, a charge Nader and his supporters disputed. His campaigns emphasised campaign finance reform, universal health care, labour rights, and opposition to corporate influence in politics.

Legacy

Nader has been credited with launching the modern consumer movement and with inspiring a generation of public-interest lawyers. He has also been a perennial critic of the two-party system in the United States. In 2015 the American Museum of Tort Law, which he founded, opened in Winsted.
