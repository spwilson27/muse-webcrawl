Cordon bleu (dish)

Cordon bleu
Place of originSwitzerland (as veal cordon bleu)
Main ingredientsMeat (veal, chicken or pork), ham, cheese, breadcrumbs
VariationsChicken Kiev, schnitzel cordon bleu

A cordon bleu is a dish of meat wrapped around cheese (or with cheese filling), then breaded and pan-fried or deep-fried. Veal or pork cordon bleu is made of veal or pork pounded thin and wrapped around a slice of ham and a slice of cheese, breaded, and then pan fried or baked. Chicken cordon bleu, the most popular version in the United States, uses a nearly boneless chicken breast pounded thin instead of veal. The dish shares its name with the blue ribbon (cordon bleu) worn by members of the French Order of the Holy Spirit, by extension a mark of excellence — and with the Le Cordon Bleu culinary schools, to which it is not directly related.

Origins

The veal cordon bleu is generally credited to Swiss cooks; a recipe resembling it (a breaded veal escalope stuffed with cheese) is documented in Switzerland in the 1940s and first mentioned in a cookbook in 1949. Cheese-stuffed schnitzels were also a speciality of Moselle and other regions. Chicken cordon bleu appeared in the United States in the 1960s, reportedly popularised in New York and Cincinnati, and became a banquet and home-kitchen staple; the New York Times printed a chicken cordon bleu recipe in 1967.

Preparation

The meat is pounded thin, layered or rolled with ham and a melting cheese such as Gruyère, Emmental or mozzarella, coated in flour, egg wash and breadcrumbs, then fried in butter or oil (or baked) until golden, with the cheese melted inside. Variations use different meats and cheeses, add mushrooms, or are topped with a cream or supreme sauce. Related dishes include chicken Kiev (butter-filled), and the Italian saltimbocca and Valdostana.
