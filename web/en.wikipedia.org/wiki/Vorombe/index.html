Vorombe

Vorombe is a genus of elephant birds, an extinct family of large to enormous flightless birds endemic to Madagascar. Its name derives from the Malagasy vorona, "bird," and be, "large."

Taxonomy and size

The genus was erected in 2018 by James Hansford and Samuel Turvey after a comprehensive reassessment of elephant bird taxonomy, which reduced the confused historical naming to three genera: Aepyornis, Mullerornis, and Vorombe. Its single species, Vorombe titan, was estimated at an average mass of about 536 kg, with the largest females possibly exceeding 700 kg, making it arguably the heaviest bird known to have existed — surpassing Aepyornis maximus and rivalled only by the Australian Dromornis stirtoni.

Extinction

Like the other elephant birds, Vorombe disappeared during the late Holocene, probably within the last 1,500 years, through a combination of human hunting, egg collection, and habitat change following the settlement of Madagascar.
