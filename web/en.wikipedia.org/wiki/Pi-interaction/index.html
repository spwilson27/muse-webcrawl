Pi-interaction

In chemistry, π-interactions are a type of non-covalent interaction involving π systems of aromatic and unsaturated molecules. Although weaker than covalent and ionic bonds, they play important roles in molecular recognition, crystal packing, and the structure of biological macromolecules.

Types

The principal geometries include π–π stacking, in which aromatic rings lie parallel (face-to-face or offset) or perpendicular (edge-to-face, T-shaped), and cation–π interactions, between a cation and the face of an aromatic ring. Related effects include anion–π and lone pair–π interactions.

Significance

π-stacking between nucleobases stabilises the DNA double helix, while cation–π interactions are important in protein–ligand binding, ion channels, and neurotransmitter receptors. In materials science, π-interactions govern the packing and electronic properties of organic semiconductors.
