Generalization error

In machine learning, the generalization error (or out-of-sample error) of a model is a measure of how accurately the model predicts outcomes for previously unseen data, as opposed to the training data it was fitted on.

Definition

Formally, for a hypothesis h and loss function ℓ, the generalization error is the expected loss over the true data distribution, I[h] = E[ℓ(h(x), y)], while the empirical (training) error is the average loss on a finite sample. The difference between the two is the generalization gap. Controlling this gap — through cross-validation, regularization, and bounds from statistical learning theory such as VC dimension and Rademacher complexity — is a central problem of learning theory; excessive fitting to training data is called overfitting.
