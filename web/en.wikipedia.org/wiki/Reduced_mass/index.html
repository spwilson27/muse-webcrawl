Reduced mass

Reduced mass
Symbol: μ
Formula: μ = m1m2 / (m1 + m2)
Field: Classical mechanics, quantum mechanics

In physics, reduced mass is a concept that allows the two-body problem to be solved as if it were a one-body problem. Given two bodies, one with mass m1 and the other with mass m2, the equivalent one-body problem has an inertial mass

μ = 1 / (1/m1 + 1/m2) = m1m2 / (m1 + m2).

Derivation

In the centre-of-mass frame, the equations of motion for the two bodies depend only on their separation. The relative acceleration of the bodies is governed by the mutual force divided by the reduced mass, so the relative motion behaves like that of a single particle of mass μ moving in the central force field of a fixed centre.

Properties

The reduced mass is always smaller than either body mass; for equal masses it is exactly half of each. If one body is much heavier than the other (m1 ≫ m2), then μ ≈ m2, which is why the Earth's mass barely affects the motion of a small satellite treated as orbiting a fixed Earth.

Applications

Reduced mass appears throughout physics: in the Kepler problem and binary-star orbits, in the Bohr model corrected for nuclear motion (explaining the isotope shift between hydrogen and deuterium spectral lines), in the quantum harmonic oscillator applied to molecular vibrations, in scattering theory, and in the motion of the electron–positron pair in positronium.
