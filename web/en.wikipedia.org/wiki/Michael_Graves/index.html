Michael Graves (July 9, 1934 – March 12, 2015) was an American architect, designer, and educator, and a leading figure of postmodern architecture. He was a member of the New York Five — a group of modernist architects — before turning to a colourful, historically referential postmodernism. He taught at Princeton University for nearly 40 years and led the firm Michael Graves Architecture & Design.

The Portland Building (1982), Graves's landmark of postmodern architecture.

Architecture

Graves's breakthrough public commission was the Portland Building in Portland, Oregon (1982), a municipal office tower with a pastel façade, giant pilasters, and small square windows — hailed as the first major built work of postmodernism, though also long criticised for its dark interiors. Other notable buildings include the Humana Building in Louisville (1985), the Walt Disney World Dolphin and Swan hotels in Florida (topped with enormous statues), the Denver Public Library, and the Ministry of Health, Welfare and Sport in The Hague.

Product design

Graves became famous to the general public through product design: his 9093 kettle for Alessi (1985), with its bird-shaped whistle, is an icon of design, and from the late 1990s he designed hundreds of affordable household products for Target stores, helping pioneer "design for all". After an illness in 2003 left him paralysed from the waist down, he became a prominent advocate and designer of accessible healthcare and home products.
