Attica is a town in Wyoming County, New York, United States, with a small portion extending into Genesee County. Located roughly midway between Buffalo and Rochester, the town contains the village of Attica, its principal settlement. The town's name derives from Attica, the region of Greece surrounding Athens.

History

The town of Attica was formed in 1811 from part of the town of Sheldon. Agriculture dominated the local economy through the nineteenth century, and the village grew as a railroad and market center. In 1930 New York State opened Attica Correctional Facility on the outskirts of the village; the maximum-security prison became nationally known for the 1971 Attica Prison riot, in which inmates seized control of part of the facility for four days before a state police assault retook it, leaving 43 people dead in total. The prison remains one of the area's largest employers.

Geography

Attica lies in the rolling uplands of western New York, drained in part by Tonawanda Creek, which flows north through the town. New York State Route 98 and Route 238 serve the community, and the village of Attica is the town's center of population and commerce.

Demographics

The town's population was about 7,400 at the 2020 census, a figure that includes the inmate population of the correctional facility. The village of Attica, incorporated in the nineteenth century, has a population of roughly 2,500.
