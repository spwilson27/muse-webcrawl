The McDonnell Douglas MD-80 is a series of five-abreast single-aisle airliners developed by McDonnell Douglas. It was produced by the developer company until August 1997 and then by Boeing Commercial Airplanes. The MD-80 was the second generation of the DC-9 family, originally called the DC-9 Super 80 (short Super 80).

Stretched, enlarged wing and powered by more powerful Pratt & Whitney JT8D-200 series engines, the aircraft program was launched in October 1977. The MD-80 made its first flight on October 18, 1979, and was certified on August 25, 1980. The first airliner was delivered to launch customer Swissair on September 13, 1980, which introduced it into commercial service on October 10, 1980.

Design

The MD-80 retains the DC-9's rear-mounted engines and T-tail configuration. Keeping the same wing as the DC-9 but lengthened fuselage options, the main variants are the MD-81, MD-82, MD-83, MD-87, and MD-88, seating typically 130 to 172 passengers. The type was further developed into the MD-90 and the Boeing 717.

Operators

Major operators historically included American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, SAS, Alitalia, and Allegiant Air. American and Delta retired their large fleets in 2020. The type earned the affectionate nickname "Mad Dog." A total of 1,191 MD-80 series aircraft were delivered before production ended in 1999.
