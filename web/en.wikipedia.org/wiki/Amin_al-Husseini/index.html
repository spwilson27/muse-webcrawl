Amin al-Husseini

Born: 1897, Jerusalem, Ottoman Empire

Died: 4 July 1974 (aged 77), Beirut, Lebanon

Title: Grand Mufti of Jerusalem (1921–1937)

Political party: Arab Higher Committee

Mohammed Amin al-Husseini (Arabic: محمد أمين الحسيني; c. 1897 – 4 July 1974) was a Palestinian Arab nationalist and Muslim leader in Mandatory Palestine. Born into the al-Husayni family of Jerusalemite notables, he traceable roots in the city since the 12th century, al-Husseini rose to become one of the most influential figures in Palestinian politics during the British Mandate period.

Early life

Al-Husseini was born in Jerusalem around 1897, the son of Taher al-Husseini, who had served as Mufti of Jerusalem. He was educated in Jerusalem, at the Alliance Israélite school and at a Catholic school run by French missionaries, before briefly studying in Cairo and Istanbul. During the First World War he served as an artillery officer in the Ottoman army.

Grand Mufti of Jerusalem

In 1921, the British High Commissioner, Herbert Samuel, appointed al-Husseini as Grand Mufti of Jerusalem, a leading Islamic clerical position, and the following year he was elected President of the Supreme Muslim Council. From these posts he controlled extensive religious funds and appointments and became the de facto leader of the Palestinian Arab national movement.

The 1936–1939 Arab revolt

Al-Husseini chaired the Arab Higher Committee, formed in 1936, which led the general strike and revolt against British rule and Jewish immigration. In 1937 the British dissolved the committee and issued a warrant for his arrest; he fled to Lebanon and later to Iraq, where he supported the Rashid Ali al-Gaylani coup of 1941.

Second World War

After the failure of the Iraqi coup, al-Husseini fled to Fascist Italy and then Nazi Germany. In November 1941 he met Adolf Hitler in Berlin and spent much of the war in the Axis sphere, broadcasting propaganda to the Arab world and helping recruit Muslim volunteers, including Bosnian Muslims for the Waffen-SS Handschar division. His wartime activities remain a subject of intense historical and political controversy.

Later life

After the war al-Husseini was held in France but escaped in 1946 to Egypt. He remained involved in Palestinian politics, presiding over the All-Palestine Government in Gaza in 1948, but his influence waned as the Palestine Liberation Organization rose in the 1960s. He spent his final years in Lebanon and died in Beirut in 1974.
