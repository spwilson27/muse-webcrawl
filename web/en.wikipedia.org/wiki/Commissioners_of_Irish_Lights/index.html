The Commissioners of Irish Lights (Irish: Coimisinéirí Soilse na hÉireann), often shortened to Irish Lights, is the body that serves as the general lighthouse authority for the whole island of Ireland — both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland — plus its adjacent seas and islands. It maintains lighthouses, buoys, beacons and other aids to navigation around the Irish coast.

History

The body's origins date to 1786, when responsibility for Irish lighthouses was granted to the Dublin Port (Ballast) Board. In 1867 the Commissioners of Irish Lights were formally established as a separate body. Remarkably, it continued to operate on an all-island basis after the partition of Ireland in 1921, a cross-border arrangement later underpinned by agreements between the British and Irish governments and praised as a model of practical cooperation. Its costs are met from light dues pooled in the General Lighthouse Fund shared with Trinity House (England and Wales) and the Northern Lighthouse Board (Scotland and the Isle of Man).

Operations

Irish Lights maintains over 60 lighthouses and hundreds of buoys and beacons, operates the vessel ILV Granuaile, and provides differential GPS and AIS services. All Irish lighthouses were automated by the end of the 20th century, ending the era of resident keepers; many former stations have been converted to heritage tourism. Irish Lights also carries out hydrographic and wreck-marking duties and promotes the "Great Lighthouses of Ireland" tourism partnership.
