Northwich

Northwich is a town and civil parish in the unitary authority of Cheshire West and Chester, in the county of Cheshire, England. It lies at the confluence of the rivers Weaver and Dane, about 29 km (18 miles) east of Chester. The town's population was roughly 22,000 at the 2021 census.

History

Northwich's history is defined by salt. The Romans established a fort and settlement here—known as Condate—at the crossing of the Weaver, and extracted salt from the brine springs. The town's name, like those of nearby Middlewich and Nantwich, reflects this: "wich" towns are places where salt was produced. Salt extraction expanded enormously in the 19th century when rock salt mining began, and the chemical industry followed: Brunner Mond, later part of ICI, built the Winnington works, and soda ash production made the town an industrial centre. Solution mining and brine pumping caused severe subsidence, which damaged many buildings and led to the town's characteristic stock of movable timber-framed structures designed to be jacked level.

Landmarks and modern town

Historic features include the swing bridges over the Weaver Navigation, the Anderton Boat Lift nearby (a Victorian structure that raises boats between the river and the Trent and Mersey Canal), and the Lion Salt Works museum at Marston, one of the last open-pan salt works in Britain. St Helen's Church, Witton, is the town's Grade I listed parish church. See also Cheshire, River Weaver, and Anderton Boat Lift.
