Société des Artistes Indépendants

The Société des Artistes Indépendants (Society of Independent Artists), formed in Paris on 29 July 1884, is an association of artists promoting artworks through public exhibitions. Its founding motto was "sans jury ni récompense" – without jury nor reward – allowing any artist paying a fee to exhibit.

History

Young artists frustrated by the conservative official Salon created an open alternative. The first Salon des Indépendants was held in December 1884. It became the launchpad for Neo-Impressionism (Seurat's A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte was shown in 1886), and later showed Fauvism, Cubism and Duchamp. Its 1911 exhibition helped ignite public Cubism. The Salon continues annually in Paris.
