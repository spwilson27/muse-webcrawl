Animals in War Memorial

LocationPark Lane, London, United Kingdom
DesignerDavid Backhouse
Unveiled24 November 2004
Dedicated toAnimals that served and died alongside British and Allied forces

The Animals in War Memorial is a memorial in Park Lane, London, commemorating the animals that served, suffered and died alongside British and Allied forces in the wars and conflicts of the 20th century. It was unveiled on 24 November 2004 by Anne, Princess Royal.

Description

The memorial, sculpted by David Backhouse, consists of two Portland stone walls forming a crescent, depicting animals such as horses, mules, dogs, elephants, camels, pigeons and a glow worm. A separate bronze statue of a mule and a horse laden with equipment stands beyond the wall, symbolising the animals passing to their deaths. Inscriptions on the memorial include the words "They had no choice" and a tribute noting that the memorial is dedicated to all the animals that served and died alongside British and Allied forces.

Context

Millions of animals were used in warfare: horses and mules carried supplies and pulled artillery, carrier pigeons transported messages, and dogs performed roles including sentry duty and mine detection. The memorial campaign was supported by animal welfare organisations including the RSPCA and the PDSA, whose Dickin Medal has been awarded to animals displaying conspicuous gallantry in conflict.
