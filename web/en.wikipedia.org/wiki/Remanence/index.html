Remanence

Remanence or remanent magnetisation is the magnetisation left behind in a ferromagnetic material (such as iron) after an external magnetic field is removed. Colloquially, when a magnet is "magnetised", it has remanence. The remanence of magnetic materials provides the magnetic memory used in magnetic storage devices and is the source of the field of permanent magnets.

Types

Saturation remanence is the magnetisation remaining when a material has been driven to saturation and the field is then removed; it is the value measured in experiments on magnetic recording media and paleomagnetism. Other forms include isothermal remanent magnetisation (IRM), acquired in a steady field, and anhysteretic remanent magnetisation (ARM), acquired in the presence of an alternating field and a small steady bias.

Applications

In paleomagnetism, the natural remanent magnetisation of rocks records the direction and intensity of the Earth's magnetic field at the time the rocks cooled or were deposited, providing evidence for geomagnetic reversals and continental drift. In engineering, high remanence is desirable in permanent magnets, while low remanence is sought for transformer cores and recording heads.
