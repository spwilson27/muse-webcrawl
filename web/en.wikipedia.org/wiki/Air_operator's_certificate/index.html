Air operator's certificate

An air operator's certificate (AOC) is the approval granted by a civil aviation authority to an aircraft operator to allow it to use aircraft for commercial purposes. It certifies that the operator has the personnel, assets, and systems in place to ensure the safety of its employees and the general public. The AOC defines the types of operation authorised — for example passenger, cargo, scheduled or charter — the aircraft types that may be flown, and the areas of the world in which the operator may fly.

Every commercial airline operates under an AOC issued by its national aviation authority.

Requirements

To obtain an AOC an applicant must demonstrate an adequate organisation and management structure, with named accountable managers for flight operations, maintenance, training, and safety. It must produce an operations manual covering procedures for every phase of flight, maintain its aircraft under an approved continuing-airworthiness programme, and satisfy the authority as to its financial fitness. The certification process typically proceeds through pre-application, formal application, document evaluation, demonstration and inspection (including proving flights), and final certification, followed by continuing surveillance for as long as the certificate is held.

International framework

The requirement for an AOC stems from the Chicago Convention of 1944 and the standards of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), notably Annex 6 (Operation of Aircraft). Although certificates are national, ICAO standards and regional harmonisation — for example the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) rules within Europe — allow mutual recognition. In the United States the equivalent certification is conducted by the Federal Aviation Administration under 14 CFR Part 119, with operations specifications ("OpSpecs") attached to the certificate; airlines operate under Part 121 and charter and commuter operators generally under Part 135.

Suspension and revocation

An authority may suspend or revoke an AOC if the operator can no longer satisfy the conditions of certification, and the certificate lapses if the operator ceases flying for a prescribed period. Because an airline's right to carry fare-paying passengers depends entirely on its AOC, loss of the certificate immediately grounds the airline.
