Elbląg (German: Elbing) is a city in northern Poland, in the Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship, located on the Elbląg River near its mouth on the Vistula Lagoon, about 55 km (34 mi) southeast of Gdańsk. With a population of roughly 115,000, it is one of the oldest cities in Poland and a historic port of the Baltic region.

History

The Teutonic Knights founded Elbing in 1237 beside the older Old Prussian trading settlement of Truso, famous in Viking-age sources. The city received Lübeck law, joined the Hanseatic League, and grew rich on Baltic trade. In 1454 the city renounced the Teutonic Order and submitted to the Polish Crown; it remained part of Royal Prussia within the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth until the First Partition of Poland in 1772 transferred it to Prussia. As German Elbing it became an industrial centre, home to the Schichau shipyards and works. The city was heavily damaged in 1945, transferred to Poland after the war, and its German population expelled.

Old Town reconstruction

Unlike Gdańsk or Warsaw, Elbląg's Old Town was not immediately rebuilt; for decades the centre stood largely empty. From the 1990s it was reconstructed using a distinctive "retroversion" method: new buildings follow the historic street plan and plot divisions and evoke old forms without copying them, producing a postmodern reinterpretation of a Hanseatic old town. St Nicholas Cathedral, the Market Gate (Brama Targowa) and fragments of the city walls survived from the medieval city.

Transport and the Elbląg Canal

The city is noted for the nearby Elbląg Canal (completed 1860), an engineering landmark on which boats are carried overland on rail-mounted cradles across a series of inclined planes to overcome a height difference of about 100 metres. Elbląg also regained importance as a port after the 2022 opening of the Vistula Spit canal, which gives its harbour direct access to the Baltic without passing through Russian-controlled waters near Baltiysk.
