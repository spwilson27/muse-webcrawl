Oswald (c. 604 – 5 August 642) was King of Northumbria from 634 until his death, and is venerated as a saint and martyr. The son of Æthelfrith, he spent his youth in exile among the Scots of Dál Riata after Edwin seized the Northumbrian throne, and was baptised a Christian at Iona. After Edwin was killed by Cadwallon of Gwynedd and Penda of Mercia in 633, Oswald returned and destroyed Cadwallon's army at the Battle of Heavenfield in 634.

Reign

According to Bede, Oswald erected a wooden cross before the battle at Heavenfield and held overlordship over much of Britain; the later Anglo-Saxon Chronicle tradition counts him among the rulers who held imperium south of the Humber. His reign is chiefly remembered for the conversion of Northumbria: in 635 Oswald invited the monk Aidan from Iona to preach, granting him the island of Lindisfarne as his see, and the king himself acted as interpreter for the Irish-speaking missionary.

Death and cult

Oswald was killed fighting the pagan Penda at the Battle of Maserfield (traditionally identified with Oswestry) on 5 August 642. His body was dismembered and his head and arms displayed on stakes; the site became associated with miracles, and Oswald was venerated as a martyr throughout England and on the continent. His head was kept at Lindisfarne and later placed in St Cuthbert's coffin at Durham; his feast day is 5 August.
