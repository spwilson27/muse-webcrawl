Conon

Conon (Greek: Κόνων, Konōn; before 444 BC – after 394 BC) was an Athenian general at the end of the Peloponnesian War, who led the Athenian fleet during the decisive years of that conflict and later, in Persian service, won the victory at the Battle of Cnidus that ended Sparta's brief naval supremacy. He is remembered for rebuilding the Long Walls of Athens and restoring Athenian power after its defeat in 404 BC.

Conon restored Athens' Long Walls and harbour fortifications with Persian funding after 394 BC.

Early career

Conon first appears in command in 413 BC, when he was sent to Naupactus to watch the Corinthian Gulf. He was repeatedly elected strategos (general) in the following years and served in the Aegean, including at Samos, the main Athenian naval base in the later Peloponnesian War.

Aegospotami and exile

In 405 BC Conon commanded, with colleagues, the Athenian fleet destroyed by the Spartan Lysander at the Battle of Aegospotami in the Hellespont. When the Athenians ashore were surprised and their ships captured, Conon escaped with a handful of vessels, carrying the sacred state galley Paralus to safety, and took refuge with Evagoras, the philhellene king of Salamis in Cyprus. Well aware that he could expect harsh treatment at Athens for the disaster, he did not return home.

Service with Persia and Cnidus

Conon persuaded the Persian court to finance a fleet against Sparta and, together with the satrap Pharnabazus, commanded the Persian-funded navy in the Corinthian War. In 394 BC their fleet destroyed the Spartan navy at the Battle of Cnidus, off the south-west coast of Asia Minor, killing the Spartan commander Peisander and ending Sparta's maritime dominance. Conon and Pharnabazus then sailed through the Aegean, expelling Spartan harmosts and garrisons from the islands and coast.

Return to Athens

In 393 BC Conon returned to Athens in triumph, bringing Persian money with which he rebuilt the Long Walls connecting Athens to its port of Piraeus, and the Piraeus fortifications, which had been demolished after Athens' surrender in 404 BC. The work symbolised the recovery of Athenian independence. Late in his life Conon was sent as an ambassador to the Persian satrap Tiribazus at Sardis to counter Spartan diplomacy; he was arrested there during the negotiations that led toward the King's Peace of 387/386 BC, and the manner and date of his death are uncertain in the sources.
