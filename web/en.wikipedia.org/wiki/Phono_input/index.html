Phono input

Phono inputs on an amplifier connect to a turntable's low-level cartridge output.

A phono input is a set of input jacks, usually a pair of RCA connectors plus a ground terminal, on an audio amplifier or receiver, designed for connection to a phonograph turntable. Phono inputs differ from ordinary line-level inputs in two ways: they provide much greater amplification, because a phono cartridge produces only a few millivolts, and they apply RIAA equalization to reverse the frequency response shaping applied when a vinyl record is cut.

Why equalization is needed

When mastering a vinyl record, bass frequencies are reduced and treble frequencies boosted so that grooves take up less space and surface noise is rendered less audible. Playback equipment must apply the exact inverse curve, standardised by the Recording Industry Association of America in the 1950s, to restore a flat response. A dedicated phono stage (phono preamplifier) performs this equalization and boosts the signal to line level; turntables without a built-in phono stage must be connected either to a phono input or to an external phono preamplifier.

Cartridge types

Most phono inputs are designed for moving magnet (MM) cartridges; low-output moving coil (MC) cartridges require still more gain and different loading, provided by a switchable MC stage or a step-up transformer. Connecting a turntable to a line input without equalization produces thin, quiet sound, while connecting a line-level source to a phono input causes loud distortion.
