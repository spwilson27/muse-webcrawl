Inductive inference

Inductive inference, also called inductive learning, is the process of learning a general rule or function from specific observed instances. In computer science and mathematical learning theory, it asks which functions can be learned from examples, and how reliably.

Formal models

E. Mark Gold's 1967 model of language identification in the limit studies whether a learner, given an endless stream of examples, eventually converges to a correct description. Related frameworks include Solomonoff induction, which assigns probabilities to hypotheses according to their simplicity, and the PAC learning model of Leslie Valiant.

Relation to philosophy

Inductive inference connects to the philosophical problem of induction raised by David Hume: no finite set of observations logically guarantees a general conclusion. Formal learning theory sidesteps the philosophical problem by defining success criteria relative to classes of hypotheses and data presentations.
