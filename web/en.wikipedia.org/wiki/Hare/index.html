Hare

Hare
Kingdom: Animalia
Class: Mammalia
Order: Lagomorpha
Family: Leporidae
Genus: Lepus

Hares and jackrabbits are mammals belonging to the genus Lepus of the family Leporidae. They are herbivorous animals, generally larger than rabbits, with longer ears and hind legs.

Biology

Unlike rabbits, hares are precocial: their young (leverets) are born fully furred with eyes open, in shallow ground nests called forms rather than burrows. Hares are fast runners — the European hare can reach speeds around 70 km/h — relying on speed to escape predators.

Species and culture

There are more than 30 species, including the European hare, snowshoe hare and the jackrabbits of North America. Hares feature widely in folklore, from Aesop's "The Tortoise and the Hare" to the Moon rabbit of East Asian tradition.
