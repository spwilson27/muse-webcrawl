Tow (fibre)

In the textile industry, a tow is a large bundle of filament fibres. The more regularly spun fibres are called yarns, while tow is produced more cheaply and is usually crimped and cut into short staple lengths resembling wool or cotton.

Tow is also the name given to the shorter fibres removed in the processing of flax and hemp during hackling; these coarse, broken fibres were historically spun into rough fabrics and used for oakum in caulking ships' hulls.

Synthetic tow

For man-made fibres such as polyester, nylon, acrylic, and carbon fibre, tow is a continuous, untwisted bundle of filaments — sometimes numbering in the hundreds of thousands — produced directly from the spinneret. Carbon fibre tow is commonly designated by its filament count, such as 3K, 12K or 24K (thousands of filaments), and is woven into fabrics or wound into composite structures. Cigarette filter tow is made of cellulose acetate.
