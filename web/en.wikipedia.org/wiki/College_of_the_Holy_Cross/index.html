College of the Holy Cross

The College of the Holy Cross is a private Jesuit liberal arts college in Worcester, Massachusetts. Founded in 1843, it is the oldest Catholic college in New England and one of the oldest in the United States, enrolling roughly 3,000 undergraduate students.

History

Bishop Benedict Joseph Fenwick of Boston founded the college in 1843; it was chartered by the Commonwealth of Massachusetts in 1865. Holy Cross became coeducational in 1972. Its basketball team won the NCAA championship in 1947, led by future Hall of Famer Bob Cousy.

Academics

Holy Cross offers only undergraduate degrees in the liberal arts, is a member of the Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities, and is known for its pre-medical and classics programs. Alumni include Supreme Court Justice Clarence Thomas and immunologist Anthony Fauci.
