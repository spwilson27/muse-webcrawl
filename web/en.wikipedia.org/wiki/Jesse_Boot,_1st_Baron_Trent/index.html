Jesse Boot, 1st Baron Trent

Jesse Boot
Born: 2 June 1850, Nottingham, England
Died: 13 June 1931 (aged 81), Jersey
Occupation: Businessman
Known for: Transforming Boots the Chemist

Jesse Boot, 1st Baron Trent (2 June 1850 – 13 June 1931) was an English businessman who transformed the Boots pharmacy business founded by his father into a national chain of chemists' shops. Under his leadership, Boots the Chemist grew from a small Nottingham herbalist's shop into one of Britain's largest retail organisations.

Early life and business

Boot was born in Nottingham, the son of John Boot, a Wesleyan lay preacher who in 1849 had opened a small shop selling herbal remedies. Jesse joined the business as a boy and took control of it after his father's death in 1860, working alongside his mother Mary. He introduced a strategy of buying in bulk and selling patent medicines and toiletries at sharply reduced prices, advertising the savings and opening longer hours than competitors.

The business expanded rapidly: by the 1880s Boots had multiple branches, and Boot pioneered own-brand products, in-store lending libraries (the Boots Book-Lovers' Library), cafés, and purpose-built factories in Nottingham. The company was incorporated as Boots Pure Drug Company and expanded into manufacturing, research, and eventually overseas operations. In 1920 Boot sold a controlling interest to the American United Drug Company, though the company later returned to British ownership.

Philanthropy

Boot was a notable benefactor of Nottingham. He donated land and funds for the University College Nottingham (now the University of Nottingham), including the Trent Building and the lakeside Highfields park, and supported housing and community projects in the city. He was knighted in 1909, created a baronet in 1917, and raised to the peerage as Baron Trent in 1929.

Personal life and death

He married Florence Anne Rowe in 1886; she was an influential figure in the business and in the design of its stores and library service. Poor health led Boot to retire to Jersey, where he died in 1931. His son John Boot succeeded him in both the business and the barony.
