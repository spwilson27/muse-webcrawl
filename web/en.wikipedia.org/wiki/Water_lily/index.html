Water lily

Water lily is the common name for members of the family Nymphaeaceae, aquatic flowering plants that grow in still or slow-moving fresh water, with floating leaves and showy flowers. The genus Nymphaea alone contains roughly 60 species.

FamilyNymphaeaceae
GeneraNymphaea, Nuphar, Victoria, Euryale, Barclaya
HabitatPonds, lakes, slow streams worldwide

Description

Water lilies root in muddy bottoms and send long petioles to floating, often round and notched leaves that repel water. Flowers open above or on the surface and range from white and yellow to pink and blue. The giant water lilies of the genus Victoria, from the Amazon basin, bear leaves over two metres across with upturned rims strong enough to support a child's weight.

Cultural significance

The blue water lily was sacred in ancient Egypt, and Nymphaea species feature in art and religion across Asia; they are distinct from the true lotus (Nelumbo). Claude Monet's late series of Water Lilies paintings, made of his garden at Giverny, are among the most famous works of Impressionism.
