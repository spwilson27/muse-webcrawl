Taeniasis

CausesInfection with adult Taenia tapeworms

Taeniasis is a parasitic disease caused by infection of the human intestine with adult tapeworms of the genus Taenia. The principal species are Taenia saginata (the beef tapeworm), Taenia solium (the pork tapeworm), and Taenia asiatica.

Transmission and life cycle

Humans become infected by eating raw or undercooked beef or pork containing larval cysts (cysticerci). The larvae develop into adult tapeworms in the small intestine, where the worm, which may grow to several metres in length, attaches to the intestinal wall with its scolex and sheds egg-bearing segments (proglottids) in the faeces. Cattle or pigs ingest the eggs from contaminated pasture or feed, completing the cycle when larvae encyst in their muscles.

Taeniasis should be distinguished from cysticercosis, in which humans act as intermediate hosts and harbour larval cysts in tissues — including, in the case of T. solium, the brain (neurocysticercosis), which is much more serious.

Symptoms and treatment

Intestinal taeniasis is often asymptomatic or causes only mild abdominal discomfort, nausea, and weight loss; the passage of proglottids may be the first sign. Diagnosis is by stool examination. The infection is readily treated with a single dose of the anthelmintics praziquantel or niclosamide. Prevention relies on cooking meat thoroughly, meat inspection, and good sanitation.
