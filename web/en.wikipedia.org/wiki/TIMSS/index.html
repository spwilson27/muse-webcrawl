TIMSS (Trends in International Mathematics and Science Study) is an international assessment of the mathematics and science achievement of fourth- and eighth-grade students around the world, organised by the International Association for the Evaluation of Educational Achievement (IEA) and administered in partnership with Boston College's TIMSS & PIRLS International Study Center.

Design

First conducted in 1995 and repeated every four years (1999, 2003, 2007, 2011, 2015, 2019, 2023), TIMSS combines achievement tests with extensive questionnaires for students, teachers and school principals about curricula, teaching practices and learning contexts. More than 60 countries and numerous benchmarking regions have participated in recent cycles. TIMSS Advanced assesses students in their final year of secondary school taking advanced mathematics and physics. Since 2019 the study has transitioned to computer-based assessment (eTIMSS).

Findings

East Asian systems—Singapore, South Korea, Hong Kong, Taiwan and Japan—consistently top the TIMSS tables at both grades, and results are widely used by policymakers to benchmark national curricula and teaching. TIMSS complements the OECD's PISA study (which tests 15-year-olds' applied literacy in reading, maths and science) and the IEA's PIRLS reading study.
