Malê Revolt

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Malê Revolt (Portuguese: Revolta dos Malês), also known as the Great Revolt, was a Muslim slave rebellion that took place in Salvador, Bahia, Brazil, on the night of 24–25 January 1835, during the month of Ramadan. It was the largest slave revolt in Brazil and one of the largest in the Americas.

Background

The term malê derives from the Yoruba word imale, meaning a Yoruba Muslim. The rebels were largely enslaved and freed West African Muslims, many of them literate in Arabic, organised through Islamic teachers and networks in Bahia. Earlier Bahian revolts, including the 1807–1835 series of Hausa and Nagô uprisings, formed its background.

The uprising

Betrayed to the authorities shortly before the planned rising, the rebels took to the streets early. Around 600 slaves and freedmen fought soldiers and armed civilians through Salvador for several hours before being defeated. About 70 rebels and some 50 opponents were killed, depending on the source.

Aftermath

Repression was severe: leaders were executed, many participants were flogged, imprisoned or deported to Africa, and laws were tightened against Africans, including restrictions on movement and religious practice. The revolt nonetheless remains a symbol of resistance and of African Muslim culture in Bahia.
