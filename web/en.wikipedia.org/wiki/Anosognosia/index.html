Anosognosia

Anosognosia is a condition in which a person with a disability is cognitively unaware of having it. It results from physiological damage to brain structures, typically the parietal lobe or diffuse damage to the fronto-temporal-parietal area in the right hemisphere of the brain, and is most famously seen in patients who deny paralysis of a limb after stroke.

Signs and causes

The term was coined by neurologist Joseph Babinski in 1914 to describe patients with left hemiplegia who denied their paralysis. Anosognosia also occurs in psychiatric conditions: a large proportion of people with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder lack insight into their illness, which is a major cause of treatment non-adherence.

Related phenomena

Related conditions include anosodiaphoria (awareness but indifference to a deficit), Anton syndrome (denial of cortical blindness), and somatoparaphrenia (disowning a limb). Cold-water vestibular stimulation can temporarily restore awareness in some stroke patients.

Management

There is no specific cure; management focuses on rehabilitation, safety, and, in psychiatric cases, treatment of the underlying disorder.
