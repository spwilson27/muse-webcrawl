Yamatai

Yamatai (邪馬台国, Yamatai-koku) was an ancient country in Wa (Japan) during the late Yayoi period (c. 1st century – 3rd century CE). According to the Chinese history Wei Zhi (Records of Wei, compiled c. 297 CE), in the account known as the Gishi Wajinden, Yamatai was ruled by the shaman-queen Himiko (Pimiko), who united some thirty Wa communities after a period of civil war.

The Chinese account

The Wei Zhi describes Himiko as a woman who "occupied herself with magic and sorcery, bewitching the people." She lived secluded in a palace guarded by a thousand female attendants, communicated through a single male intermediary — her younger brother, who handled administration — and sent embassies to the Wei court in 238, 243, and 247 CE. Emperor Ming of Wei granted her the title "Queen of Wa, Friend of Wei" and a gold seal, along with bronze mirrors and other gifts. After her death, around 248 CE, a king was installed but rejected; a young relative, Iyo (Toyo), was made queen and order restored.

The location debate

Where Yamatai was located is one of the most famous unresolved questions in Japanese archaeology and historiography. The Wei Zhi gives distances and directions from the Korean commandery of Daifang that do not map cleanly onto real geography. The two main theories place Yamatai either in northern Kyushu, near the earlier Wa polities and the sea route from Korea, or in the Kinki (Yamato) region of Honshu, where supporters point to the great keyhole-shaped tomb (kofun) building tradition that began in the late third century, and to the Hashihaka kofun in Sakurai, Nara, sometimes identified as Himiko's tomb. The question bears on whether the Yamato state descended directly from Himiko's confederation.
