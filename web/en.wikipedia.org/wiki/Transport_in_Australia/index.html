Transport in Australia

Transport in Australia is shaped by the country's large area, low population density and concentration of settlement in coastal cities. Road transport dominates passenger travel and much freight movement, while extensive railways carry bulk commodities such as iron ore and coal, and coastal shipping and aviation link the widely separated state capitals.

Roads

Australia has one of the world's highest rates of car ownership, and roads carry the great majority of passenger-kilometres and non-bulk freight. The national highway network links the capital cities, and long-distance trucking includes the distinctive "road trains" — combinations of a prime mover with several trailers — used in remote outback areas. Driving is on the left.

Rail

The rail network totals roughly 33,000 km, a legacy of separate colonial systems built to three different gauges: standard (1,435 mm), broad (1,600 mm) and narrow (1,067 mm), a fragmentation that long hindered interstate traffic. Heavy-haul railways in the Pilbara region of Western Australia carry iron ore on some of the heaviest and longest trains in the world. Interstate standard-gauge lines now link all mainland capitals, and each major city has suburban passenger rail; Sydney also has Australia's only metro system.

Air and sea

Domestic aviation is important given the distances involved; the Sydney–Melbourne route is among the busiest air routes in the world. Qantas is the flag carrier. Australia's ports handle large volumes of bulk exports, and shipping remains central to the island continent's trade, though interstate coastal shipping has declined relative to road and rail.
