Zhou Yu (175–210), courtesy name Gongjin, was a Chinese military general and strategist of the late Eastern Han dynasty who served first under the warlord Sun Ce and then under his brother Sun Quan, founder of Eastern Wu. He is best remembered as the allied commander at the Battle of Red Cliffs in 208 CE.

Career

Zhou Yu formed a close friendship with Sun Ce in youth and supported his conquest of the Jiangdong region, the territorial base of Wu. After Sun Ce's assassination in 200, Zhou Yu helped stabilise the succession of the young Sun Quan and became the regime's foremost military commander, while also overseeing naval training.

Red Cliffs

In 208 the northern warlord Cao Cao marched south with a vast army to reunify China. Against the advice of officials favouring surrender, Zhou Yu argued for resistance and commanded the allied Sun–Liu fleet. Exploiting wind conditions and Cao Cao's chained, disease-weakened ships, the allies launched a fire attack that destroyed the northern fleet at Red Cliffs, preserving the south and setting the pattern for the Three Kingdoms division. Zhou Yu went on to defeat Cao Ren at Jiangling, but died in 210, aged 35, while preparing a campaign against Yi Province.

In culture

In the 14th-century novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, Zhou Yu is memorably — and unfairly, historians note — portrayed as a brilliant commander consumed by jealous rivalry with the strategist Zhuge Liang.
