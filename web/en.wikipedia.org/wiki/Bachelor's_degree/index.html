A bachelor's degree (from Medieval Latin baccalaureus) is an undergraduate academic degree awarded by colleges and universities upon completion of a course of study lasting three to six years, depending on the institution and academic discipline. In most countries it is the standard first degree and a prerequisite for postgraduate study such as master's and doctoral programmes.

History

The bachelor's degree descends from the medieval European universities — Paris, Bologna, Oxford and Cambridge — where the baccalaureate was the first degree, qualifying the holder to teach under supervision and to proceed to the master's and doctorates. In the Oxbridge tradition, the BA was (and at Oxford and Cambridge largely remains) the universal first degree, with the MA awarded later without further study. The degree spread through the British imperial and American university systems worldwide, and the Bologna Process (from 1999) harmonised the bachelor's as the standard first-cycle qualification across Europe, typically of three years.

Types and duration

The most common degrees are the Bachelor of Arts (BA) for humanities, social sciences and fine arts, and the Bachelor of Science (BSc or BS) for natural sciences, mathematics, engineering and technology. Professional bachelor's degrees include the Bachelor of Business Administration (BBA), Bachelor of Engineering (BEng), Bachelor of Laws (LLB) and the double bachelor MBBS in medicine. In the United States and Canada a bachelor's normally requires four years and about 120 semester credits, including general-education requirements; in England, Wales and Northern Ireland most degrees take three years, while Scottish honours degrees take four. Many systems distinguish ordinary (pass) degrees from honours degrees, classified in the UK as first-class, upper second (2:1), lower second (2:2) and third-class honours.
