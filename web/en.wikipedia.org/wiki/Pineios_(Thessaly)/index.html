Pineios (Thessaly)

The Pineios (Greek: Πηνειός, Pineiós; Latin: Peneus) is a river in Thessaly, Greece. The river is named after the god Peneus. It rises in the Pindus mountains, flows eastward across the Thessalian plain past Larissa, and empties into the Aegean Sea through the Vale of Tempe, between Mount Olympus and Mount Ossa. Its length is about 205 kilometres (127 miles), making it one of the longest rivers wholly within Greece.

Course and geography

Fed by tributaries including the Enipeas, Titarisios, and Portaikos, the Pineios drains most of the Thessalian plain, the country's principal agricultural region, known for cotton, wheat, and vegetables. Near its mouth it cuts through the narrow, wooded gorge of Tempe, celebrated in antiquity as a haunt of Apollo and the site of a temple and laurel groves from which branches were carried to Delphi for the Pythian games.

Mythology and history

In Greek mythology the river god Peneus was the father of Daphne, who, fleeing Apollo, was transformed into a laurel tree on its banks. In September 2023, severe flooding from Storm Daniel inundated the Thessalian plain along the Pineios, causing widespread damage to farmland and towns including Larissa and Karditsa.
