Morton Subotnick (born April 14, 1933) is an American composer of electronic music, best known for his 1967 composition Silver Apples of the Moon, the first electronic work commissioned by a record company (Nonesuch Records). He is a pioneering figure in electronic and multimedia performance and a co-founder of the San Francisco Tape Music Center.

Early life and career

Subotnick was born in Los Angeles, California, in 1933 and trained as a clarinetist. He studied composition with Darius Milhaud at Mills College. In the early 1960s, with Ramon Sender, he co-founded the San Francisco Tape Music Center, a hub for experimental music on the West Coast. There, together with engineer Don Buchla, he helped conceive the Buchla 100 modular synthesizer — one of the first voltage-controlled synthesizers — designed so composers could construct and sequence electronic sounds without relying on a keyboard.

Silver Apples of the Moon

In 1967 Nonesuch Records commissioned Subotnick to compose a work expressly for the LP record. The result, Silver Apples of the Moon — its title taken from a line in W. B. Yeats's poem "The Song of Wandering Aengus" — was composed on the Buchla synthesizer and became a landmark: an electronic album intended for home listening rather than the concert hall or tape studio. It was a commercial and critical success and has been selected for the United States National Recording Registry of the Library of Congress.

Later work

Subotnick moved to New York and then to the California Institute of the Arts, where he helped build the music program and taught for decades. His later works explore the combination of live instruments, electronics, and computer control, including the "ghost box" technique, in which recorded control signals shape a live performance; pieces such as Sidewinder (1971), Until Spring (1975), and A Sky of Cloudless Sulphur (1978) exemplify this approach. He has also created works for children and educational media, including the "Making Music" software series and the educational site Creating Music. His wife is the vocalist and composer Joan La Barbara.
