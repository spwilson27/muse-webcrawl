New River Gorge Bridge

Location: Fayetteville, West Virginia, USA
Carries: U.S. Route 19
Crosses: New River
Design: Steel arch bridge
Total length: 3,030 ft (924 m)
Longest span: 1,700 ft (518 m)
Height: 876 ft (267 m) above the river
Completed: 22 October 1977

The New River Gorge Bridge is a steel arch bridge 3,030 feet (924 m) long over the New River Gorge near Fayetteville, West Virginia, in the Appalachian Mountains. With an arch 1,700 feet (518 m) long, it was for many years the world's longest single-span arch bridge; it is now the fifth-longest, and at 876 feet (267 m) above the river it is one of the highest vehicular bridges in the world. The bridge carries U.S. Route 19 and is a symbol of West Virginia, appearing on the state's commemorative quarter.

Construction

The bridge was built by the Michael Baker Company (design) and the American Bridge Company between 1974 and 1977 at a cost of about $37 million. The steel structure, built of COR-TEN weathering steel, weighs about 88 million pounds including the deck. Construction used a cableway with trolleys to move arch sections across the gorge, and the two arch halves were tied back to the canyon walls until they met. Before the bridge opened, crossing the gorge required a 40-minute drive down winding roads to a small bridge at the bottom; the new bridge cut the crossing to about a minute.

Bridge Day

Since 1980, the bridge has hosted Bridge Day on the third Saturday of October, one of the largest extreme-sports festivals in the world. The bridge closes to traffic while hundreds of BASE jumpers parachute from the deck and thousands of spectators watch; rappelling and a high-line are also featured. A pedestrian walkway beneath the roadway, the "Bridge Walk" catwalk, is open to guided tours year-round.

Records and recognition

On completion in 1977 the bridge held the record for the longest steel arch span, which it kept until the Lupu Bridge opened in Shanghai in 2003. It remains the longest single-arch bridge in the Western Hemisphere and was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2013. The surrounding gorge became part of the New River Gorge National Park and Preserve in 2020.
