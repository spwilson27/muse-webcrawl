International Institute for Strategic Studies

The IISS is headquartered at Arundel House in London, with offices around the world.

The International Institute for Strategic Studies (IISS) is a British research institute and think tank in the area of international affairs and military conflict. Founded in 1958, it describes itself as a world-leading authority on global security, political risk, and military conflict. Its headquarters are at Arundel House in London, and it maintains offices in the United States, Singapore, Bahrain, and Berlin.

History

The institute was founded in 1958 as the Institute for Strategic Studies, with an initial focus on nuclear deterrence and arms control during the Cold War. Its early work, including the Adelphi Papers monograph series (begun in 1961), helped to establish strategic studies as an academic and policy discipline. The word "International" was added to its name in 1971 as its membership and agenda broadened.

Publications and events

The IISS is best known for The Military Balance, an annual assessment of the military capabilities and defence economics of some 170 countries, published since 1959 and widely cited by governments and media. Other products include the journal Survival, the Strategic Survey annual review, and the Armed Conflict Database. Since 2002 the institute has convened the Shangri-La Dialogue in Singapore, an annual intergovernmental security conference attended by defence ministers and officials from the Asia-Pacific and beyond, and since 2004 the Manama Dialogue in Bahrain on Middle East security.
