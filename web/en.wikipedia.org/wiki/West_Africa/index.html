West Africa, also called Western Africa, is the westernmost region of Africa, defined by the United Nations as the 16 countries Benin, Burkina Faso, Cape Verde, Côte d'Ivoire, The Gambia, Ghana, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Liberia, Mali, Mauritania, Niger, Nigeria, Senegal, Sierra Leone and Togo. Nigeria alone holds more than half the region's population of roughly 430 million (approximate).

The region encompasses Sahel savanna in the north (transition to the Sahara) and tropical forest on the Guinea coast. Its history includes the Ghana, Mali and Songhai empires and the scholarly city of Timbuktu, then coastal trade — including the transatlantic slave trade through forts such as Elmina and Gorée — colonisation by France, Britain, Portugal and Germany, and independence movements led by figures like Kwame Nkrumah (Ghana, 1957). ECOWAS (founded 1975) coordinates regional integration, though recent coups in Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger have strained it. Cultures are globally influential through languages (Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Wolof), religions (Islam and Christianity plus indigenous faiths), music (highlife, Afrobeats) and cuisine (jollof rice, groundnut stews).
