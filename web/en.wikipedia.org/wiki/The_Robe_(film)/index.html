The Robe (film)

The Robe is a 1953 American fictional Biblical epic film that tells the story of a Roman military tribune who commands the unit that is responsible for the Crucifixion of Jesus. It was directed by Henry Koster and produced by Frank Ross, starring Richard Burton, Jean Simmons, Victor Mature, and Michael Rennie.

The film is notable as the first movie ever released in the widescreen process CinemaScope. It was adapted from Lloyd C. Douglas's 1942 novel of the same name.

Plot

Marcellus Gallio, a Roman tribune, wins at dice the robe worn by Jesus at the Crucifixion. Haunted by guilt afterwards, he journeys to Palestine seeking the truth about the man he helped execute, is converted to Christianity, and is eventually condemned by the Emperor Caligula, going to his death alongside his beloved Diana.

Production and reception

Shot in Technicolor, the film was a major commercial success and won Academy Awards for Best Art Direction and Best Costume Design. Its sequel, Demetrius and the Gladiators (1954), continued the story of Marcellus's slave Demetrius, played by Victor Mature.
