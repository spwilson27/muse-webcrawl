IBM Z

Family of IBM mainframe computers

An IBM Z mainframe

IBM Z is a family name used by IBM for all of its mainframe computers that implement the z/Architecture, the 64-bit successor of the System/360 architecture. The name was introduced with the zSeries in 2000 and has been applied to successive generations including the zEnterprise, z13, z14, z15 and z16 systems.

Characteristics

IBM Z systems are designed for very high reliability, security and transaction throughput, and remain the backbone of banking, insurance, airlines and government data processing. They run the z/OS operating system as well as Linux (as Linux on IBM Z), and support extensive virtualisation through logical partitions (LPARs) and the z/VM hypervisor. Recent models emphasise pervasive encryption, on-chip AI acceleration (for example the Telum processor in the z16) and resistance to quantum-era cryptographic attacks.

History

The line descends from the System/360 of 1964 through System/370 and System/390. The "Z" branding has been read as standing for "zero downtime", reflecting the mainframe's focus on availability.
