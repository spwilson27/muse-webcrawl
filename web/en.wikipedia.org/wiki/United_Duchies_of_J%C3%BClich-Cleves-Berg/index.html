The United Duchies of Jülich-Cleves-Berg were a group of territories in the Lower Rhine region of the Holy Roman Empire held in personal union by a single duke from 1521 to 1609. They comprised the duchies of Jülich, Cleves, and Berg, together with the counties of Mark and Ravensberg and the lordship of Ravenstein.

Formation

The union came about through dynastic marriage: John III of Cleves inherited Jülich and Berg in 1521 through his wife Maria of Jülich-Berg. Their son, William "the Rich", ruled the united duchies and was a notable figure of the Reformation era, pursuing a moderate religious policy; he also contested the Guelders succession with Emperor Charles V.

Succession crisis

When the last duke, John William, died childless and insane in 1609, rival claimants — principally Brandenburg and the Palatinate-Neuburg — seized the territories, sparking the War of the Jülich Succession (1609–1614), one of the precursor conflicts of the Thirty Years' War. By the Treaty of Xanten (1614) and the final settlement of 1666, Jülich and Berg went to Palatinate-Neuburg, while Cleves, Mark, and Ravensberg went to Brandenburg.
