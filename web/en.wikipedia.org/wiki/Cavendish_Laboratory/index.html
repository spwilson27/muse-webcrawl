Cavendish Laboratory

The Cavendish Laboratory is the Department of Physics at the University of Cambridge. Founded in 1874 under James Clerk Maxwell, it is named for Henry Cavendish and funded initially by William Cavendish, 7th Duke of Devonshire.

Cavendish Laboratory
Established: 1874
Location: Cambridge, England
Affiliation: University of Cambridge
First head: James Clerk Maxwell

History and discoveries

Cavendish professors included Maxwell, Lord Rayleigh, J. J. Thomson, Ernest Rutherford and Lawrence Bragg. Landmark work at the laboratory includes Thomson's discovery of the electron (1897), Rutherford's nuclear model legacy, Chadwick's discovery of the neutron (1932), and Watson and Crick's determination of the structure of DNA (1953, with King's College data). Researchers associated with the Cavendish have won numerous Nobel Prizes.

Modern laboratory

In 1974 the laboratory moved to West Cambridge. Research spans astrophysics, condensed matter, biological physics and quantum technologies. A new building, the Ray Dolby Centre, was developed as its latest home.
