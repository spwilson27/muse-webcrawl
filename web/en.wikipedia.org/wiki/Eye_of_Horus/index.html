Eye of Horus

The Eye of Horus, also known as the wedjat eye, is an ancient Egyptian symbol of protection, royal power, and good health. The eye is personified in the goddess Wadjet and is associated with the sky god Horus. It was popular with the ancient Egyptians, who used it in amulets, jewellery, and funerary art.

Mythology

In the Osiris myth, Horus's left eye was injured or torn out in his struggle with Set and later restored by Hathor or Thoth. The healed eye became a symbol of wholeness and healing, while Horus's right eye was associated with the sun and the left with the moon.

The heqat fractions

The parts of the wedjat eye were used in a system of fractional notation for measuring grain (the heqat): the six parts correspond to the unit fractions 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, and 1/64, which sum to 63/64 — the missing 1/64 being supplied, in legend, magically by Thoth.

Usage

Wedjat amulets were placed within mummy wrappings and painted on coffins and on the bows of boats to ensure safe passage. The symbol is sometimes (incorrectly) said to be the origin of the modern Rx prescription symbol.
