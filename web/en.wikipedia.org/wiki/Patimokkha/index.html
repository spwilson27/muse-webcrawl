Patimokkha

The Pātimokkha (Pali; Sanskrit: Prātimokṣa) is the basic code of monastic discipline for Buddhist monks (bhikkhus) and nuns (bhikkhunis), consisting of a set of precepts or rules that govern communal life in the Sangha. It forms part of the Vinaya Pitaka, the division of the Buddhist canon concerned with monastic rules.

Contents

In the Theravada tradition, the Patimokkha contains 227 rules for monks and 311 rules for nuns. The rules are grouped by the severity of the offence involved: the four pārājika rules (entailing expulsion, "defeat"), rules requiring formal meetings of the Sangha, rules of forfeiture, rules requiring expiation, and rules of training (sekhiya), among other categories. Other early Buddhist schools preserved their own versions — the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya, still used in Tibetan Buddhism, lists 253 rules for monks.

Recitation

The Patimokkha is recited communally twice a month at the uposatha observance, on the new moon and full moon days. Before the recitation, monks confess any transgressions; the recitation proceeds on the assumption that the assembly is pure, and silence after each section signifies that no one present has broken those rules.

Origins

According to tradition, the Buddha laid down rules case by case as problems arose in the growing monastic community. Scholars regard the Patimokkha as one of the oldest strata of Buddhist literature, predating the schisms of the early schools, since the versions preserved in different traditions agree closely.
