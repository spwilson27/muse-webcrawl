Inca Civil War

The Inca Civil War, also known as the War of the Two Brothers, was fought between 1529 and 1532 between the half-brothers Huáscar and Atahualpa, sons of the Sapa Inca Huayna Capac, over succession to the Inca Empire.

Background

Huayna Capac died around 1527, probably of smallpox introduced by Europeans, along with his designated heir Ninan Cuyochi. The empire was split: Huáscar inherited the southern capital Cusco, while Atahualpa, favoured by his father's veteran generals, controlled the northern army based in Quito.

Course of the war

After initial skirmishes, Atahualpa's generals Quizquiz, Chalcuchimac and Rumiñahui won a series of battles, culminating at Quipaipán in April 1532, where Huáscar was captured. Cusco was occupied and Huáscar's family and supporters were massacred.

Aftermath

Atahualpa's victory left the empire reunified but weakened months before Francisco Pizarro arrived. Atahualpa was captured at the Battle of Cajamarca in November 1532, and Huáscar was executed on Atahualpa's orders in 1533, easing the Spanish conquest.
