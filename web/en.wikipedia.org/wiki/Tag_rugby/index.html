Tag rugby is a non-contact team sport derived from rugby, in which players wear a belt or shorts with two velcro-attached tags (or "tags"). Instead of tackling, a defender stops the ball carrier by pulling off one of the tags, shouting "tag!" The ball carrier must then stop within a few steps and pass the ball. Tag rugby preserves much of rugby's passing, running and evasion while removing scrums, lineouts, tackling and kicking in most versions, making it popular in schools, mixed social leagues and as an introduction to the full game.

Rules

While rule sets vary between governing organisations, common features include: teams of about seven; a limited number of tags (often six) after which possession turns over; scoring by grounding the ball over the try line; and restart plays instead of set pieces. In many codes—such as the Australian OzTag variant and League Tag—kicking in general play is permitted under restrictions, while the tag belt version promoted in the United Kingdom and Ireland (Try Tag Rugby, and the Irish Tag Rugby Association) is typically played without kicking.

History and spread

Tag-style games grew out of informal training games in rugby league and union. OzTag was formalised in Australia in the early 1990s by former player Perry Haddock, and grew into a large participation sport there and in New Zealand. In Ireland and Britain, tag rugby expanded rapidly in the 2000s as a summer, mixed-gender social sport for adults, with large leagues in Dublin and London. The game is widely used in primary schools because it teaches handling and evasion without contact.
