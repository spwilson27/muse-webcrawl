Operation Ezra and Nehemiah, named after the biblical leaders who led the return of the exiles from Babylon, was the airlift of nearly the entire Jewish community of Iraq to Israel in 1950–1952. It formed part of the broader exodus of Jews from Arab and Muslim countries in the years after Israel's founding.

Background

Iraq's Jewish community was one of the oldest in the world, dating to the Babylonian exile. After the Farhud pogrom of 1941 and rising persecution following the 1948 Arab–Israeli war, most Iraqi Jews sought to leave. In March 1950 Iraq passed a law permitting Jews to emigrate if they renounced their citizenship; a further law in 1951 froze the assets of those who left.

The airlift

Organised by the Israeli government with the Jewish Agency and American Jewish organisations, flights carried about 120,000–130,000 people, first via Cyprus and later directly to Lod Airport. The operation virtually ended a 2,600-year Jewish presence in Iraq; the immigrants initially lived largely in transit camps (ma'abarot) in Israel.
