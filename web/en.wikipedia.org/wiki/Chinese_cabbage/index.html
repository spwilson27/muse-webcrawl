Chinese cabbage

Species: Brassica rapa

Groups: Pekinensis (napa cabbage); Chinensis (bok choy)

Origin: China

Chinese cabbage refers to two groups of leafy vegetables of the species Brassica rapa widely used in Chinese and other East Asian cuisines: the Pekinensis group, known in English as napa cabbage, and the Chinensis group, known as bok choy.

Varieties

Napa cabbage forms a dense, elongated head of pale green, crinkled leaves with thick white ribs; it is the principal vegetable used in Korean kimchi and is common in hot pots, stir-fries and dumplings. Bok choy does not form a head; it has smooth, dark green leaves on thick white or green stalks, and is usually cooked briefly to retain its crispness. A smaller, tender form is sold as "baby bok choy"; Shanghai bok choy has green stalks.

History and cultivation

The plant was domesticated in China, where it has been cultivated for well over a millennium, and spread through East and Southeast Asia. As a cool-season crop, it is sown for spring and autumn harvests and bolts readily in heat. It became widely available in Western supermarkets during the late 20th century with the growth of East Asian diaspora communities.

Nutrition and use

Chinese cabbages are low in calories and provide vitamin C, vitamin K, folate and calcium. Napa cabbage is pickled as kimchi and suan cai; bok choy appears in soups, noodle dishes and stir-fries across Chinese regional cuisines.
