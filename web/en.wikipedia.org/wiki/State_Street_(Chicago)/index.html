State Street (Chicago)

State Street is a major north–south street in the city of Chicago, Illinois, USA, and one of its most famous. It runs from North Avenue (1600 N) to 127th Street on the far South Side, and through the downtown Loop, where it forms the zero point of Chicago's street numbering grid: all east–west addresses in the city are counted from State Street (and north–south addresses from Madison Street). Its downtown stretch — "State Street, that great street" of the song Chicago — was for a century one of the world's great shopping streets, lined with landmark department stores.

History

State Street began as a trail and early plank road along the state boundary survey line. After the Great Chicago Fire of 1871 it was rebuilt as the commercial heart of the city: Potter Palmer developed its north Loop blocks and persuaded Marshall Field's and other retailers to relocate there, making it the city's premier shopping district. By the early 20th century the street boasted great flagship stores — Marshall Field's (with its Tiffany dome and Walnut Room), Carson Pirie Scott (in Louis Sullivan's landmark building), Sears, Montgomery Ward, Wieboldt's and Goldblatt's — as well as theatres including the Chicago Theatre (1921), whose vertical "CHICAGO" sign became an icon.

Transit

State Street is served by the first of Chicago's subways, the State Street subway, opened in 1943 and now part of the CTA Red Line. From 1979 to 1996 the downtown street was converted into a transit mall for buses and pedestrians, a much-criticised experiment: the street was reopened to traffic in 1996 in a widely praised renovation that restored historic streetlights and the street's retail fortunes.

Today

Though department-store retail has shrunk — Marshall Field's became Macy's in 2006 — State Street remains a major shopping, theatre, university and transit corridor, home to DePaul and other university campuses, the Harold Washington Library at its south Loop end, and the Theatre District around Randolph Street.
