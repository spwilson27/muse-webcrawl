Au Bonheur des Dames

Au Bonheur des Dames (French: "The Ladies' Delight" or "The Ladies' Paradise") is the eleventh novel in the Rougon-Macquart series by Émile Zola, first published in 1883. It is a study of the rise of the modern department store in Second Empire Paris, and of the small shops and shopkeepers crushed by its growth.

Plot

Denise Baudu, a young woman from the provinces, comes to Paris with her two younger brothers after their parents' deaths and finds work as a shopgirl at Au Bonheur des Dames, a vast department store owned by the widower Octave Mouret. The store's innovative methods—fixed prices, free entry, mail order, seasonal sales, and returns—draw enormous crowds, ruining the small neighbouring drapers, including Denise's uncle. Denise's integrity and intelligence attract Mouret, who falls in love with her; she refuses him until he proposes marriage, and the novel ends with their union and her gentle influence humanizing the store's treatment of its staff.

Themes and background

Zola researched the great Paris stores, particularly Le Bon Marché and the Louvre, documenting retail innovation, advertising, consumer desire, and the feminization of shopping, as well as the harsh discipline and insecurity of shopworkers' lives. The character of Mouret was partly inspired by Aristide Boucicaut, founder of Le Bon Marché.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted several times, including a French silent film (1930), a 1943 film directed by André Cayatte, and the BBC/ITV television series The Paradise (2012–2013), which transposed the story to northern England.
