Priestly Blessing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
During the blessing, kohanim raise both hands with the fingers arranged in the traditional split-finger gesture.

The Priestly Blessing (Hebrew: ברכת כהנים, Birkat Kohanim), also known in rabbinic literature as dukhanen (from dukhan, "platform", because the blessing is given from a raised platform), is a Hebrew prayer recited by kohanim (descendants of the priestly line of Aaron). According to the Torah, Aaron blessed the people, and later the priests were commanded to bless the Israelites in the name of God (Numbers 6:22–27).

Text

The LORD bless you and keep you;
the LORD make his face shine on you and be gracious to you;
the LORD turn his face toward you and give you peace.

The text consists of three verses of increasing length, built on the name of God (YHWH), and is among the oldest passages of the Bible in continuous liturgical use. Two small silver scrolls found at Ketef Hinnom in Jerusalem, dated to the late 7th or early 6th century BC and inscribed with versions of the blessing, are the oldest surviving biblical texts known.

Performance of the ritual

In Orthodox practice, the blessing is performed during the repetition of the Amidah: the kohanim remove their shoes, wash their hands, cover their heads and hands with their prayer shawls (tallitot), and raise their hands with the fingers arranged so that the thumbs touch and the fingers are split into two pairs, forming the shape associated with the Hebrew letter shin. The congregation traditionally does not look directly at the kohanim's hands during the blessing. The custom is observed daily in Israel and on festivals in most diaspora communities.

Use in other traditions

The benediction is also used in Roman Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, and other Christian liturgies, usually at the close of worship. In the 20th and 21st centuries the kohanim hand gesture became widely recognized in popular culture through its adoption by Leonard Nimoy as the Vulcan salute in Star Trek.
