Modern pentathlon

The laser run combines running with laser pistol shooting

The modern pentathlon is an Olympic multisport event comprising five disciplines: fencing (épée), freestyle swimming, equestrian show jumping, and a final combined event of running and pistol shooting known as the laser run. It was created by Pierre de Coubertin, founder of the modern Olympic Games, to simulate the skills of an ideal 19th-century cavalry soldier — riding an unfamiliar horse, fighting with sword and pistol, swimming and running.

History and format

The event debuted at the 1912 Stockholm Olympics, and a women's competition was added at Sydney 2000. Originally spread over four or five days, it is now contested in a compact format: a fencing ranking round, then a semi-final and final in which swimming, fencing bonus rounds, riding and the laser run follow in quick succession. Scoring is by points in each discipline, with the laser run started on a handicap basis so that the first across the line wins.

Recent changes

After a scandal at the Tokyo 2020 Games involving the treatment of a horse in the riding phase, the governing body UIPM voted to replace equestrianism with obstacle course racing after the Paris 2024 Games, a change that secured the sport's place at Los Angeles 2028. The decision remains controversial among traditionalists and athletes.
