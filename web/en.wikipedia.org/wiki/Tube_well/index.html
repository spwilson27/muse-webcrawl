Tube well

A tube well is a type of water well in which a long tube or pipe is bored into an underground aquifer. The lower end is fitted with a strainer or slotted screen, and water is lifted to the surface by a pump, which may be a hand pump or a powered pump.

Construction

Tube wells are drilled by percussion, rotary drilling or jetting, depending on the geology. A casing pipe prevents collapse of the borehole, and the screened intake is placed within the water-bearing stratum. Shallow tube wells draw on near-surface aquifers, while deep tube wells reach confined aquifers tens or hundreds of metres below ground.

Use

Tube wells are extremely common across South Asia, where tens of millions provide drinking water and irrigation, particularly in India, Bangladesh, Pakistan and Nepal. Their spread from the 1970s onward transformed groundwater irrigation and rural water supply.

Health concerns

While tube wells reduced exposure to contaminated surface water, in parts of Bangladesh and West Bengal groundwater drawn from shallow aquifers has been found to contain naturally occurring arsenic, leading to widespread arsenicosis and major public-health programmes to test and mark wells.
