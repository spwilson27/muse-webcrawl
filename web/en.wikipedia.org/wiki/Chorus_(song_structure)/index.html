Chorus (song structure)

A chorus is a repeated section of a song, generally containing the song's primary musical and lyrical motifs. In popular music, the chorus typically repeats the title or central hook of the song, in contrast to the verses, whose lyrics change with each repetition while the music usually remains the same.

Structure

In the common verse–chorus form, choruses alternate with verses and are often preceded by a pre-chorus that builds tension. The chorus is usually the most memorable part of a song, designed so that an audience can sing along. In many songs the final chorus is repeated or modulated upward to supply a climax.

Refrain

The term refrain is sometimes used interchangeably with chorus, but strictly a refrain may be a single recurring line at the end of each verse, as in folk and hymn traditions, whereas a chorus is a self-contained repeated section.

Origins

The word derives from the chorus of Greek drama and from choral singing in church music, where a group joined in repeated responses. The modern pop usage consolidated in the Tin Pan Alley era, when songs were built around a 32-bar chorus following an introductory verse.
