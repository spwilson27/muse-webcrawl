Earth's rotation or Earth's spin is the rotation of planet Earth around its own axis, as well as changes in the orientation of the rotation axis in space. Earth rotates eastward, in prograde motion. As viewed from the northern polar star Polaris, Earth turns counterclockwise.

Period

Earth's rotation period relative to the fixed stars, called its stellar day, is about 86,164.0989 seconds of mean solar time (23 h 56 min 4.0989 s). The rotation period relative to the Sun — the mean solar day — is about 86,400 seconds (24 hours). Because tidal friction from the Moon is slowing Earth's rotation, the length of the day is gradually increasing, by roughly 1.7 milliseconds per century on average.

Effects

Rotation is responsible for the cycle of day and night. It also produces the Coriolis effect, which deflects moving air and water masses and shapes weather systems and ocean currents, and it causes the equatorial bulge: Earth's equatorial diameter is about 43 km greater than its polar diameter, making the planet an oblate spheroid.

Variations

The rotation is not perfectly constant. Changes in mass distribution — from earthquakes, glacial rebound, and atmospheric and oceanic motion — cause tiny variations in the length of day, and the axis itself wanders in the phenomena of polar motion and precession. International leap seconds are occasionally added to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) to keep it in step with the rotating Earth.
