Blithe Spirit is a 1945 British supernatural comedy film directed by David Lean. It was adapted by Lean, Ronald Neame and Anthony Havelock-Allan from the 1941 play of the same name by Noël Coward. The film stars Rex Harrison, Constance Cummings, Kay Hammond and Margaret Rutherford.

The story concerns novelist Charles Condomine, who invites the eccentric medium Madame Arcati to his home to conduct a séance as research for a new book. The séance unexpectedly summons the ghost of Charles's first wife, Elvira, who is visible only to him and who proceeds to disrupt his second marriage to Ruth.

Plot

Seeking material for a mystery novel about a homicidal medium, novelist Charles Condomine and his wife Ruth invite their neighbours, Dr and Mrs Bradman, to dinner and a séance conducted by the local medium, Madame Arcati. During the séance, conducted in darkness to the strains of Irving Berlin's "Always", Charles hears the voice of his first wife, Elvira, who died seven years earlier. After the guests leave, Elvira materialises — visible only to Charles — dressed in a diaphanous green gown.

Elvira, still in love with Charles, tries to sabotage his marriage and to engineer his death so that they can be reunited in the afterlife. Her attempt to tamper with the brakes of Charles's car instead kills Ruth, who returns as a second ghost. The two dead wives bicker, and Madame Arcati is recalled to banish them. The exorcism appears to succeed, but the spirits remain, and as Charles drives away his car crashes — killing him and reuniting all three.

Production

The film was produced by Coward's company Cineguild and photographed in Technicolor by Ronald Neame. Kay Hammond and Margaret Rutherford reprised the roles they had created in the original stage production. Coward disliked the film's altered ending, reportedly telling Lean, "You just fucked up the best thing I ever wrote."

Reception

The film was a modest commercial success and has since been regarded as one of the finest screen adaptations of Coward. Margaret Rutherford's performance as Madame Arcati is widely considered definitive.
