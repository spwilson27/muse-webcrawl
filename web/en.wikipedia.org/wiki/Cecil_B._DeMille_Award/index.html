Cecil B. DeMille Award

The Cecil B. DeMille Award is an honorary Golden Globe Award bestowed by the Hollywood Foreign Press Association (HFPA) for "outstanding contributions to the world of entertainment". The honour is presented annually at the Golden Globe Awards ceremony to a talented individual who has made an incredible impact on the film industry.

History

The award was named after its first recipient, director Cecil B. DeMille, who received it at the 10th Golden Globe Awards in February 1953. It has been given almost every year since, skipping only occasional ceremonies. Recipients are chosen by the HFPA board of directors and are traditionally announced in the months before the ceremony.

Recipients

Early recipients included Walt Disney (1954), Darryl F. Zanuck, Jean Hersholt (1956), Jack L. Warner, Maurice Chevalier and Bing Crosby. Judy Garland, honoured in 1962, was the award's first female recipient and remains among its youngest. Later honourees have included Alfred Hitchcock, Sidney Poitier, Elizabeth Taylor, Barbra Streisand, Robert De Niro, Morgan Freeman, Jodie Foster, Steven Spielberg, Meryl Streep, Oprah Winfrey (2018), Tom Hanks, Jane Fonda, Eddie Murphy and Carol Burnett. Woody Allen, honoured in 2014, famously did not attend; the award was accepted on his behalf by Diane Keaton.

Related honours

In 2019 the HFPA introduced a counterpart for television, the Carol Burnett Award, first presented to Burnett herself.
