Drawn-on-film animation

Drawn-on-film animation, also known as direct animation or animation without camera, is an animation technique where footage is produced by creating images directly on film stock, by scratching, painting, drawing or applying objects to the film.

Drawn-on-film animation
Type: Animation technique
Also called: Direct animation, cameraless animation
Pioneers: Len Lye, Norman McLaren

Technique and artists

Artists work on clear or black leader, using inks, dyes, stencils, scratching tools and adhesives; sound can likewise be drawn onto the optical soundtrack area. Because no camera is used, the method is cheap, intimate and highly experimental.

History

Early experiments came from futurist painters in the 1910s. Len Lye's A Colour Box (1935) and the prolific films of Norman McLaren at the National Film Board of Canada — including Begone Dull Care (1949) and Blinkity Blank (1955) — defined the form. Later practitioners include Stan Brakhage (who painted and collaged Mothlight, 1963, from moth wings and foliage) and contemporary makers who sustain the technique in the digital age.
