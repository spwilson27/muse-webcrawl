Earthquake engineering

Earthquake engineering is an interdisciplinary branch of engineering that designs and analyzes structures, such as buildings and bridges, with earthquakes in mind. Its overall goal is to make such structures more resistant to earthquakes. An earthquake (or seismic) engineer aims to construct structures that will not be damaged in minor shaking and will avoid serious damage or collapse in a major earthquake.

Methods

Key techniques include base isolation, in which the building is decoupled from ground motion by bearings or isolators; energy dissipation devices such as viscous and friction dampers; and ductile detailing of reinforced concrete and steel connections so structures can deform without failing. Modern practice increasingly uses performance-based design, which sets explicit performance objectives (operational, life safety, collapse prevention) for different levels of ground shaking.

Seismic assessment and retrofit of existing buildings is a major part of the field, as is the study of soil effects such as liquefaction, which can cause foundations to fail even when the superstructure is sound.
