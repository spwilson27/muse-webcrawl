Waterloo, London

Waterloo is a district in Central London, in the London Borough of Lambeth, on the south bank of the River Thames opposite the City of Westminster. It is best known for Waterloo station, one of Britain's busiest railway terminals, and for the cultural complex of the South Bank.

History

The area was marshy Lambeth land until the 19th century. Waterloo Bridge, opened in 1817, took its name from the Battle of Waterloo of 1815, and the station followed in 1848, later rebuilt on a grand scale (1900–1922). The neighbourhood became a byword for transport and entertainment, with the Old Vic theatre (1818) and, after the Festival of Britain in 1951, the Royal Festival Hall, National Theatre, Hayward Gallery and BFI Southbank.

Geography and transport

Waterloo station serves destinations across southern England and was London's Eurostar terminal until 2007. The Underground station connects the Northern, Bakerloo, Jubilee and Waterloo & City lines. Nearby landmarks include the London Eye, County Hall, St Thomas' Hospital and the Waterloo Millennium Green.
