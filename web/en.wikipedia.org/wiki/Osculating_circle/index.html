Osculating circle

An osculating circle (red) tangent to a curve at point P

In the geometry of curves, the osculating circle of a sufficiently smooth plane curve at a given point p on the curve has been defined by Ernest Cesàro as the circle whose center lies on the inner normal line and whose curvature equals (the reciprocal of the radius of curvature) that of the given curve at that point. The osculating circle is the circle that best approximates the curve near the point — it is tangent to the curve and shares its curvature there, so the curve and the circle have second-order contact.

The word osculate derives from the Latin osculari, "to kiss", because the circle "kisses" the curve. The center of the osculating circle is called the center of curvature, and its radius the radius of curvature. The locus of centers of curvature is the evolute of the curve.

Description

For a curve given parametrically, the radius of curvature at a point is ρ = 1/κ, where κ is the curvature. The osculating circle can also be constructed as the limiting circle obtained from three points on the curve as the outer two approach the middle one. Leibniz originally defined it in this way.

At points where the curvature is zero (inflection points), no finite osculating circle exists; the radius of curvature is infinite. At vertices of the curve — local maxima or minima of curvature — the osculating circle has third-order contact with the curve.

Properties

  • The osculating circle crosses the curve at the point of contact, except at vertices, where it lies locally on one side.
  • A curve of constant curvature (a circle or a line) is its own osculating circle everywhere.
  • The osculating circle at a generic point is the unique circle with second-order contact with the curve.
