Chudnovsky brothers

David (left) and Gregory Chudnovsky

David Volfovich Chudnovsky (born 1947) and Gregory Volfovich Chudnovsky (born 1952) are American mathematicians and engineers known for their world-record mathematical calculations and developing the Chudnovsky algorithm used to calculate the digits of π with extreme precision. The brothers, born in Kyiv, Ukrainian SSR, emigrated to the United States in the 1970s and have spent much of their careers at Columbia University and later at the Polytechnic Institute of New York University.

The Chudnovsky algorithm

In 1988 the brothers published a rapidly converging series for π based on a Ramanujan-type formula involving the Heegner number 163. The Chudnovsky algorithm, built on this series, became the standard method used in record computations of π, yielding roughly fourteen decimal digits per term. Using computers they built themselves — most famously a supercomputer assembled in their Manhattan apartment and nicknamed "m zero" — the brothers computed π to over two billion digits in 1991 and to over four billion digits in 1994, achievements widely reported, including in a noted New Yorker profile by Richard Preston titled "The Mountains of Pi".

Other work

The brothers have worked in number theory, mathematical physics, and computational mathematics. They helped apply image-processing and computational methods to the reconstruction of the Unicorn Tapestries at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, mathematically stitching together high-resolution photographs of the fragile textiles. They have also worked on the assembly of large computational projects in parallel computing, and founded the Institute for Mathematics and Advanced Supercomputing at Polytechnic University in Brooklyn.
