Nathan "Nearest" Green

Born: c. 1820
Died: Unknown (19th century)
Occupation: Master distiller
Known for: Teaching Jack Daniel whiskey making

Nathan "Nearest" Green (c. 1820 – unknown) was an American master distiller who was enslaved and later emancipated. He is credited with teaching the young Jack Daniel the craft of distilling Tennessee whiskey, including the charcoal-mellowing process that defines the style.

Life

Green worked on the Tennessee farm and distillery operation of Dan Call, a Lutheran minister and distiller, where the young Jack Daniel was sent to work in the 1860s. Green taught Daniel how to make whiskey, including filtering the spirit through sugar maple charcoal — a technique with roots in West African food and water practices. After emancipation, Green continued working for Daniel as his first master distiller when the Jack Daniel Distillery was established in Lynchburg, Tennessee.

Legacy

For generations Green's role was omitted from the company's official story. In 2016 the Brown-Forman company publicly acknowledged his contribution, and in 2017 the Uncle Nearest Premium Whiskey brand was launched in his honour. The Nearest Green Foundation supports descendants and preserves his story.
