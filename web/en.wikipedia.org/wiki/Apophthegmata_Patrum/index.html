Apophthegmata Patrum

The Apophthegmata Patrum (Greek for "Sayings of the Fathers"), also known in English as the Sayings of the Desert Fathers, is the name given to several collections of the wisdom sayings, anecdotes and teachings of the Desert Fathers and Desert Mothers, the early Christian monks and ascetics of the Egyptian desert in the 4th and 5th centuries AD.

Contents

The sayings address prayer, humility, silence, hospitality, non-judgement and manual labour, and preserve the voices of figures such as Anthony the Great, Macarius of Egypt, Poemen, and the desert mothers Syncletica and Theodora. The best-known recension, the Alphabetical Collection, arranges material under the names of about 130 elders; a Systematic Collection arranges sayings by theme, and an anonymous collection also survives.

Transmission

Compiled in the late 5th and 6th centuries, the sayings were translated early into Latin (as part of the Vitas Patrum tradition), Syriac, Coptic, Armenian, Georgian and Slavonic, spreading desert spirituality throughout Eastern and Western Christianity. They remain widely read in Orthodox and Catholic monastic life and in modern popular editions.
