PubMed Central (PMC) is a free digital repository that archives full-text scholarly articles in the biomedical and life sciences. It is maintained by the United States National Library of Medicine (NLM) at the National Institutes of Health, through the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), and was launched in February 2000. Whereas PubMed is a database of citations and abstracts, PubMed Central stores the complete text of articles, each identified by a unique PMCID.

Content and policy

Articles enter PMC by three main routes: journals that deposit their entire published content; author manuscripts submitted under public-access mandates; and digitised back files. The decisive growth driver was policy: the NIH Public Access Policy, voluntary from 2005 and mandatory from 2008, requires that peer-reviewed papers arising from NIH funding be deposited in PMC within twelve months of publication. Similar mandates from other funders (and sister archives such as Europe PMC) expanded coverage further. Copyright is retained by authors or publishers, so while reading is free, reuse rights vary by article, with a subset carrying open Creative Commons licences forming the PMC Open Access Subset used for text and data mining.

Relationship to PubMed and Europe PMC

Every PMC article is also indexed in PubMed, but only a fraction of PubMed's tens of millions of records have full text in PMC. Europe PMC, run by EMBL-EBI, mirrors and extends the corpus for European funders. In 2020 PMC played a prominent role in the COVID-19 pandemic response by hosting a rapidly growing open corpus of coronavirus research.
