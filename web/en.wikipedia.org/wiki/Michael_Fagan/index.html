Michael Fagan (born 8 August 1948) is a British citizen who, in 1982, intruded into Buckingham Palace in London and entered the bedroom of Queen Elizabeth II. The incident was one of the most serious breaches in royal security of the twentieth century and led to a review of palace security by Scotland Yard.

The intrusions

Fagan first entered the palace in early June 1982, reportedly drinking wine before leaving undetected. On 9 July 1982 he climbed a drainpipe, entered through an open window, and reached the Queen's bedroom, where he spoke with her for several minutes before a footman restrained him. Because trespass in a private home was then a civil rather than criminal matter, Fagan was not charged for entering the bedroom, though he was charged with theft of the wine from the earlier incident and later acquitted.

Aftermath

The break-in prompted the resignation of the officer commanding the palace police detail and changes to royal protection. The incident has been dramatized in television, including in the fourth season of The Crown.
