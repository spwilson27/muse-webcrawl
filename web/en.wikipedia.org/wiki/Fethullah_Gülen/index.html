Muhammed Fethullah Gülen (April 27, 1941 – October 20, 2024) was a Turkish cleric, preacher and author who founded the Gülen movement (known as Hizmet, "the Service"), a network of schools, businesses, media outlets and dialogue organisations active in Turkey and around the world. He lived in self-imposed exile in Pennsylvania, United States, from 1999 until his death.

Early life and preaching career

Gülen was born in the village of Korucuk, near Erzurum, the son of an imam. Trained in traditional Islamic scholarship and influenced by the teachings of Said Nursî, he became a licensed state preacher in 1959 and served in Edirne and Izmir, where his sermons on education, science and interfaith dialogue drew large followings.

The Hizmet movement

Inspired by Gülen's teaching, his followers established private schools, tutoring centres (dershane), universities, hospitals, the newspaper Zaman and the Samanyolu television network, first in Turkey and then in more than 100 countries. The movement emphasised education, interreligious dialogue and a synthesis of Islam with modern science and Turkish nationalism.

Conflict with the Turkish government

Gülen initially supported the rising Justice and Development Party (AKP) of Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, but the alliance collapsed after 2013 corruption investigations implicated government figures. The Turkish government accused Gülenists of forming a "parallel state" and blamed Gülen for orchestrating the failed coup attempt of July 15, 2016—an accusation Gülen denied. Turkey designated the movement a terrorist organisation ("FETÖ"), demanded Gülen's extradition from the United States, and purged tens of thousands of alleged followers from state institutions.

Death

Gülen died on October 20, 2024, at a hospital near his residence in the Pocono Mountains of Pennsylvania, aged 83.
