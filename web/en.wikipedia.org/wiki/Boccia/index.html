Boccia

Boccia is a precision ball sport, similar to bocce, and related to bowls and pétanque. The sport is contested at local, national and international levels by athletes with severe physical disabilities. It was originally designed to be played by people with cerebral palsy but now includes athletes with other severe disabilities affecting motor skills. Boccia has been a Paralympic sport since 1984.

About the game

Boccia is played on a flat, smooth surface in individual, pairs or team formats. Players propel coloured leather balls — red or blue — as close as possible to a white target ball called the jack. Balls may be thrown, kicked or rolled down an assistive ramp, and athletes with the most severe impairments may use an assistant and a head pointer. Matches consist of a set number of ends, after which points are awarded for each ball closer to the jack than the opponent's best ball.

Classification

Athletes compete in classes BC1 through BC4 based on the nature and impact of their disability. The sport is governed internationally by the Boccia International Sports Federation (BISFed).
