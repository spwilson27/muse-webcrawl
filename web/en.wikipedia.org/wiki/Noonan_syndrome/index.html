Noonan syndrome

SpecialtyMedical genetics, paediatrics
SymptomsCharacteristic facial features, short stature, congenital heart defects, webbed neck
CausesMutations in RAS/MAPK pathway genes (e.g. PTPN11)
InheritanceAutosomal dominant
FrequencyApprox. 1 in 1,000–2,500 live births

Noonan syndrome (NS) is a genetic disorder that may present with mildly unusual facial features, short stature, heart defects, bleeding problems, developmental delays, and malformations of the rib cage. It is one of the RASopathies, a group of syndromes caused by germline mutations in genes of the Ras/MAPK signalling pathway. The condition is named after Jacqueline Noonan, the paediatric cardiologist who described it in 1963.

Signs and symptoms

Features vary widely, even within a family. Characteristic findings include a webbed or short neck, low hairline, widely spaced eyes (hypertelorism) with down-slanting palpebral fissures, low-set ears, a broad forehead, and short stature. The most common heart problems are pulmonary valve stenosis and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Many children have feeding difficulties in infancy, mild learning difficulties, easy bruising or bleeding disorders, and lymphatic problems such as congenital lymphoedema.

Genetics

About half of cases are caused by missense mutations in PTPN11 (encoding the protein tyrosine phosphatase SHP-2) on chromosome 12; other cases involve SOS1, RAF1, KRAS, NRAS, BRAF, and related genes. Inheritance is autosomal dominant, though many cases arise from new (de novo) mutations; mildly affected parents may be diagnosed only after a more clearly affected child.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is clinical, supported by genetic testing, which identifies a causative mutation in a majority of patients. There is no cure; management is directed at individual problems — cardiac surgery or monitoring, growth hormone therapy for short stature, developmental support, and surveillance for complications. Most people with Noonan syndrome have a normal life expectancy, though this depends on the severity of heart disease.
