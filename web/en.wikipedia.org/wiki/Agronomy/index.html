Agronomy

Agronomy is the science and technology of producing and using plants by agriculture for food, fuel, fibre, chemicals, recreation, or land conservation. Agronomy has come to encompass work in plant genetics, plant physiology, meteorology, and soil science. It is the application of a combination of sciences like biology, chemistry, economics, ecology, earth science, and genetics.

Areas of study

Agronomists study crop rotation, irrigation and drainage, plant breeding, soil classification and fertility, weed and pest control, and the effects of climate on crops. Modern agronomy underpinned the Green Revolution of the mid-20th century, in which high-yielding cereal varieties, synthetic fertilisers and improved agronomic practices dramatically raised food production. Contemporary research addresses precision agriculture — using GPS, remote sensing and soil sensors to vary inputs within fields — as well as sustainable practices that reduce nutrient runoff, soil erosion and greenhouse-gas emissions while maintaining yields under a changing climate.
