Averroes (1126–1198), known in Arabic as Ibn Rushd, was an Andalusian Muslim polymath — philosopher, physician and judge — whose detailed commentaries on Aristotle earned him the Latin title 'The Commentator'. Writing in Almohad Córdoba, he defended philosophy against Al-Ghazali's Incoherence of the Philosophers in his own Incoherence of the Incoherence, arguing that revelation and philosophy are compatible paths to truth.

Influence

Averroes' commentaries, translated into Latin and Hebrew, profoundly shaped scholasticism: Thomas Aquinas cites him constantly, and his doctrine of the unity of the intellect sparked the Latin Averroist controversies in Paris. His medical encyclopedia, the Colliget, was used in European universities for centuries. Briefly exiled in a political-religious backlash, he was restored before his death in Marrakesh.
