P–n junction

A p–n junction is a boundary or interface between two types of semiconductor material, p-type and n-type, within a single crystal of semiconductor. It is the basic building block of most semiconductor electronic devices.

Formation and properties

The p-side contains an excess of holes (positive charge carriers) through doping with acceptor impurities, and the n-side an excess of electrons through donor impurities. When the two meet, electrons and holes diffuse across the junction and recombine, leaving a depletion region of immobile ionised dopants and a built-in electric field that opposes further diffusion.

Behaviour

The junction conducts readily under forward bias, when the applied voltage reduces the barrier, but blocks current under reverse bias (apart from a tiny leakage) until breakdown is reached. This rectifying behaviour underlies diodes, and junctions are combined to make bipolar transistors, solar cells, LEDs and the MOSFET structures of integrated circuits.
