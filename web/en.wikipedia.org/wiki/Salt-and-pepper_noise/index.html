Salt-and-pepper noise

Salt-and-pepper noise, also known as impulse noise, is a form of noise sometimes seen on digital images. It presents as sparsely scattered pixels of maximum brightness (white, "salt") and minimum brightness (black, "pepper") against an otherwise intact image, giving a speckled appearance.

Causes

This type of noise arises from sharp, sudden disturbances in the image signal — for example faulty sensor elements ("dead" or "hot" pixels), errors in analogue-to-digital conversion, bit errors during transmission, or memory faults. It is characterised by a low probability of occurrence at each pixel combined with extreme values at the affected pixels, in contrast to Gaussian noise, which perturbs every pixel slightly.

Removal

The standard remedy is the median filter, a non-linear filter that replaces each pixel with the median of its neighbourhood; because corrupted pixels are outliers, the median is usually a good estimate of the true value, and the filter preserves edges better than linear smoothing. Variants such as adaptive median filtering handle higher noise densities. Mean and Gaussian filters perform poorly on impulse noise, merely spreading the corrupted values into neighbouring pixels.
