The Academy Award for Best Director (officially the Academy Award of Merit for Directing) is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS). It is given in honour of a film director who has exhibited outstanding directing while working in the film industry. The award was first presented at the 1st Academy Awards in 1929, when two directing prizes (dramatic and comedy) were given to Frank Borzage and Lewis Milestone respectively.

History and records

John Ford holds the record with four wins, for The Informer, The Grapes of Wrath, How Green Was My Valley and The Quiet Man. Frank Capra and William Wyler won three times each; Wyler also holds the record for most nominations with twelve. The Best Director winner usually coincides with Best Picture, though the two have diverged more often in recent decades. Kathryn Bigelow was the first woman to win, for The Hurt Locker (2009); Chloé Zhao and Jane Campion followed. Damien Chazelle is the youngest winner, at 32, for La La Land.

Selection

Nominees are chosen by members of the Academy's directing branch, and the winner is selected by the full Academy membership. Directors are typically nominated for a single film, though some have been nominated for two films in one year in the early years of the awards.
