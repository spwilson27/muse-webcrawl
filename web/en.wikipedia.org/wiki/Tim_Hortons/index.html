Tim Hortons Inc., known colloquially as Tim's or Timmies, is a Canadian multinational coffeehouse and restaurant chain founded in Hamilton, Ontario, in 1964 by Canadian hockey player Tim Horton and entrepreneur Jim Charade. It is Canada's largest quick-service restaurant chain, serving coffee, doughnuts, and other fast-food items, and has become a widely recognized Canadian cultural symbol.

History

The first "Tim Horton" store opened in 1964 in Hamilton, initially selling hamburgers before focusing on coffee and doughnuts. Business partner Ron Joyce, a former Hamilton police constable who took over the chain after Horton's death in a 1974 car crash, drove its aggressive franchising expansion across Canada. Tim Hortons merged with the American chain Wendy's in 1995 under a deal led by Dave Thomas, was spun off as an independent company in 2006, and in 2014 was acquired for about US$11.4 billion by Brazilian investment firm 3G Capital, becoming part of Restaurant Brands International alongside Burger King and Popeyes.

Products and culture

The chain is famous for its coffee (the "double-double" — two creams, two sugars — entered Canadian slang and eventually dictionaries), its doughnuts and bite-sized "Timbits," and promotions such as the annual "Roll Up the Rim to Win" contest. Tim Hortons restaurants are fixtures of Canadian communities, including many small towns, hockey arenas, and Canadian Forces bases (a location operated at Kandahar Airfield in Afghanistan). The company has expanded into the United States (especially border and northeastern states) and, since the late 2010s, into markets such as Mexico, China, India, and the United Kingdom.

Criticism

The chain has faced recurring disputes with franchisees over costs and profitability, criticism of menu and coffee quality changes after the 2014 acquisition, and controversy in 2018 when some Ontario franchisees cut employee benefits in response to a minimum-wage increase.
