Emerald Buddha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Emerald Buddha in its rainy-season attire

The Emerald Buddha (Thai: พระแก้วมรกต Phra Kaeo Morakot, or พระพุทธมหามณีรัตนปฏิมากร Phra Phuttha Maha Mani Rattana Patimakon) is an image of the meditating Gautama Buddha seated in a yogic position, made of a semi-precious green stone (jasper rather than emerald or jade), clothed in gold. It is considered the sacred palladium of Thailand. It is housed in the Wat Phra Kaew (Temple of the Emerald Buddha) on the grounds of the Grand Palace in Bangkok.

Description

The Emerald Buddha is a Buddha image in the meditation posture in the style of the Lanna school of the north, dating from the 15th century. The statue is 66 cm (26 in) tall and rests on a high pedestal. By royal tradition, the King of Thailand performs the ceremony of changing the costume of the Emerald Buddha three times a year, corresponding to the summer, rainy, and cool seasons — a ritual believed to bring good fortune to the country.

History

According to legend, the Emerald Buddha was created in India in 43 BC by the sage Nagasena, and was brought to Sri Lanka and later to Cambodia before reaching northern Thailand. Historical sources indicate it was discovered in 1434 when lightning struck a stupa at Wat Pa Yia in Chiang Rai, revealing the image, which had been covered in plaster. It was later kept at Lampang and Chiang Mai. In 1552 it was taken to Luang Prabang and then Vientiane in Laos, where it remained for over 200 years. In 1778, the future King Rama I captured Vientiane and brought the image to Thonburi; it was installed at Wat Phra Kaew in Bangkok in 1784, where it remains.
