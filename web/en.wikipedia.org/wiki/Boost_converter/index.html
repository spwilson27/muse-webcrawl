Boost converter

Boost converter
Type: DC-to-DC converter; switched-mode power supply
Also called: Step-up converter
Key components: Inductor, switch (transistor), diode, capacitor
Gain: Vout/Vin = 1/(1 − D)

A boost converter (also called a step-up converter) is a DC-to-DC power converter that steps up voltage from its input to its output while stepping down current. It is a class of switched-mode power supply containing at least two semiconductor devices (a diode and a transistor switch) and at least one energy storage element, an inductor.

Principle of operation

When the switch is closed, current from the input supply ramps up through the inductor, storing energy in its magnetic field while the output capacitor alone supplies the load. When the switch opens, the inductor current continues to flow through the diode to the output; the inductor's voltage adds to the supply voltage, so the output voltage exceeds the input. In continuous conduction mode the ideal conversion ratio is Vout = Vin / (1 − D), where D is the duty cycle of the switch; output filtering is provided by a capacitor, often supplemented by further filtering stages.

Applications

Boost converters are used wherever a higher DC voltage must be derived from a lower-voltage source: in battery-powered equipment, power-factor-correction front ends, regenerative braking and hybrid vehicle systems, photovoltaic maximum-power-point tracking, and in generating high voltages for loads such as white LEDs and xenon flash circuits.
