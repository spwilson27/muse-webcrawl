Closing (morphology)

In mathematical morphology, the closing of a set (binary image) A by a structuring element B is the erosion of the dilation of that set: closing(A) = (A ⊕ B) ⊖ B, where ⊕ and ⊖ denote dilation and erosion respectively. Closing is the morphological dual of opening: closing an image is equivalent to opening its complement.

Properties

Closing smooths the contours of objects, fuses narrow breaks and thin gulfs, eliminates small holes, and fills gaps in outlines, while roughly preserving the size and shape of objects larger than the structuring element. Formally, closing is extensive (the result contains the original set), increasing (it preserves inclusion between sets), and idempotent (applying it twice gives the same result as applying it once). Because dilation first expands the foreground, small background features — holes, notches, and narrow channels — that the structuring element cannot fit into are removed, and the subsequent erosion returns surviving objects approximately to their original extent.

Applications

In image processing, closing is used to fill small holes inside segmented objects, join nearby components separated by thin dark lines, remove small dark noise specks, and prepare binary images for measurement and shape analysis. Applied to greyscale images, closing suppresses dark details smaller than the structuring element and is used in operations such as background estimation and top-hat transforms (the "black top-hat" is the difference between the closing and the original image).
