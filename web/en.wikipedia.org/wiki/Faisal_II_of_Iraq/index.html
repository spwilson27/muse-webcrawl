Faisal II of Iraq

Faisal II
Born: 2 May 1935, Baghdad
Died: 14 July 1958, Baghdad (aged 23)
Reign: 4 April 1939 – 14 July 1958
House: Hashemite
Predecessor: Ghazi I

Faisal II (2 May 1935 – 14 July 1958) was the last King of Iraq. He reigned from 4 April 1939 until July 1958, when he was killed during the 14 July Revolution that overthrew the Hashemite monarchy and established the Iraqi Republic.

Early life and regency

Faisal was the only son of King Ghazi I and Queen Aliya. He became king at the age of three when his father died in a car accident. During his minority Iraq was ruled by a regent, his uncle Prince 'Abd al-Ilah, who remained the dominant figure in Iraqi politics even after Faisal came of age in 1953. The young king was educated in Britain, at Harrow School alongside his second cousin Hussein of Jordan.

Reign

Faisal's Iraq was a close ally of Britain and the West: it joined the Baghdad Pact in 1955, and in February 1958 Iraq and Jordan formed the Arab Federation as a counterweight to the newly formed United Arab Republic of Egypt and Syria. Oil revenues funded development projects, but wealth was unevenly shared and the regime was widely seen as a British client. Tensions rose sharply after the Suez Crisis of 1956.

Death

On 14 July 1958, army units under Brigadier Abd al-Karim Qasim and Colonel Abdul Salam Arif seized Baghdad in a coup. Faisal, the former regent 'Abd al-Ilah, and other members of the royal family were shot in the courtyard of the Rihab Palace as they surrendered. His death ended the 37-year Hashemite monarchy in Iraq.
