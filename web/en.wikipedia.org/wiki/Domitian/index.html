Domitian

Domitian
Roman emperor: 81–96 AD
Born: 24 October 51 AD, Rome
Died: 18 September 96 AD (aged 44)
Dynasty: Flavian
Predecessor: Titus; Successor: Nerva

Domitian (Titus Flavius Caesar Domitianus Augustus; 51–96) was Roman emperor from 81 to 96, the last of the Flavian dynasty and younger brother of Titus, son of Vespasian.

Reign

Domitian strengthened the economy, expanded frontier defences on the Rhine and Danube, and undertook a large building programme in Rome, including completing the Flavian Palace. He was an able but authoritarian administrator who exalted imperial cult titles such as dominus et deus ('lord and god').

Conflict and assassination

Relations with the Senate deteriorated into treason trials and executions. He was assassinated by court officials in 96; the Senate condemned his memory (damnatio memoriae) and the elderly Nerva succeeded, beginning the Nerva–Antonine dynasty.

Historiography

Ancient senatorial sources such as Tacitus, Pliny, and Suetonius portray him as a tyrant, while modern historians give a more balanced assessment of his administration and military policy.
