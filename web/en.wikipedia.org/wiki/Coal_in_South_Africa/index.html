Coal in South Africa

Coal is the primary source of energy in South Africa, accounting for roughly 70–80% of the country's primary energy supply and the great majority of its electricity generation. South Africa is among the world's largest coal producers and exporters, with most production concentrated in the province of Mpumalanga.

Production and use

The state utility Eskom operates a fleet of coal-fired power stations, including some of the largest in the world, such as Kusile and Medupi. A significant share of production is consumed by Sasol's coal-to-liquids plant at Secunda, which produces synthetic fuels. Exports leave mainly through the Richards Bay Coal Terminal, one of the largest coal export terminals in the world.

Environmental and social issues

South Africa is one of the world's largest emitters of carbon dioxide per capita, and its coal regions suffer severe air and water pollution. The country's dependence on coal has made its energy transition — pursued under the Just Energy Transition Partnership announced in 2021 — a focus of international climate finance and domestic debate, complicated by Eskom's debt and prolonged electricity supply crises ("load shedding").
