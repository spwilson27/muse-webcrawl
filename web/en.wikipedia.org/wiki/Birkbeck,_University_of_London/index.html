Birkbeck, University of London

Birkbeck, University of London (formally Birkbeck College), is a public research university in Bloomsbury, London, England, and a constituent college of the University of London. Established in 1823 as the London Mechanics' Institute by its founder, Sir George Birkbeck, and several supporters including Jeremy Bentham, it is one of the few specialist institutions in the United Kingdom dedicated to evening higher education.

History

The institute was founded to provide education to working people, meeting initially at the Crown and Anchor Tavern on the Strand. It admitted women from 1830 — unusually early — and became part of the University of London in 1920. T. S. Eliot lectured in English literature at Birkbeck, and former students and staff include Nobel laureates and the historian Eric Hobsbawm, who taught there for much of his career.

Academics

Birkbeck offers undergraduate and postgraduate degrees taught principally in the evening across arts, business, law, science, and social sciences, serving a large population of mature and part-time students. Its main building is in Malet Street, Bloomsbury, with a campus in Stratford, East London.
