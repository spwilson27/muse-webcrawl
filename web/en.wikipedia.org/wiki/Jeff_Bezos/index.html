Jeff Bezos

Jeffrey Preston Bezos (born January 12, 1964) is an American entrepreneur, media proprietor and commercial astronaut. He is the founder, executive chairman and former president and CEO of Amazon, the world's largest e-commerce and cloud computing company. One of the world's wealthiest people, he also founded the space company Blue Origin and owns The Washington Post.

Early life and career

Born in Albuquerque and raised in Houston and Miami, Bezos graduated from Princeton in 1986 with degrees in electrical engineering and computer science. He worked at Fitel, Bankers Trust and the hedge fund D. E. Shaw, becoming its youngest vice president, before quitting in 1994 to sell books online as the internet grew.

Amazon

Bezos founded Cadabra, renamed Amazon, in a Bellevue garage in July 1994, launching Amazon.com in 1995. The company survived the dot-com crash, expanded from books into general retail, launched Amazon Web Services (2006), the Kindle (2007), Prime, Alexa and entertainment, becoming a trillion-dollar company. Bezos stepped down as CEO in July 2021, succeeded by Andy Jassy, remaining executive chair.

Other ventures

He founded Blue Origin in 2000; its New Shepard rocket carried Bezos to the edge of space on 20 July 2021. He bought The Washington Post in 2013 for $250 million. Through the Bezos Earth Fund (2020, $10 billion pledged) and the Day 1 Fund he conducts philanthropy. His high-profile divorce from MacKenzie Scott in 2019 and his management style, wealth and Amazon's labour and tax practices have drawn sustained public scrutiny.
