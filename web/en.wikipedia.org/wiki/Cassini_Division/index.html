Cassini Division

The Cassini Division is a 4,800 km (3,000 mi) wide gap in Saturn's ring system, separating the broad B Ring from the A Ring. It was discovered in 1675 by Giovanni Domenico Cassini, for whom it is named.

Structure

Seen from Earth, the division appears as a thin black line. It is not empty: its total material is roughly a fifth that of the B Ring, and it contains narrow ringlets, including the Huygens Ringlet at its inner edge. The division spans from about 117,500 km to 122,300 km from Saturn's centre.

Cause

The inner boundary of the division coincides with a strong 2:1 orbital resonance with the moon Mimas: particles orbiting there complete one orbit for every two of Mimas, and repeated gravitational tugs clear them from the region. The outer edge of the A Ring is similarly maintained by a 7:6 resonance with Janus and Epimetheus.

Observation

The division is visible in amateur telescopes under good seeing. Spacecraft imaging, especially from the Cassini orbiter (2004–2017), revealed fine structure and waves within it.
