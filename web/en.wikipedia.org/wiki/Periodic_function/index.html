Periodic function

In mathematics, a periodic function is a function that repeats its values at regular intervals or periods. The most important examples are the trigonometric functions, which repeat over intervals of 2π radians. Periodic functions are used throughout science to describe oscillations, waves, and other repetitive phenomena; any function that is not periodic is called aperiodic.

Definition

A function f is said to be periodic if there exists a nonzero constant P such that

f(x + P) = f(x)

for all values of x in the domain. A nonzero constant P for which this is the case is called a period of the function. If there exists a least positive constant with this property, it is called the fundamental period (also primitive period, basic period, or prime period). A function with period P will repeat on intervals of length P, and these intervals are referred to as periods.

Examples

  • The sine and cosine functions are periodic with fundamental period 2π: sin(x + 2π) = sin(x) for all x.
  • The tangent function is periodic with fundamental period π.
  • The function f(x) = {x}, the fractional part of x, is periodic with period 1; its graph is a sawtooth wave.
  • Constant functions are periodic with every positive period and therefore have no fundamental period.
  • The complex exponential e2πi x has fundamental period 1 along the real axis.

Properties

If f has period P, then f(kx) has period P/|k| for any nonzero constant k. Sums and products of periodic functions with the same period are periodic with that period; if two functions have periods whose ratio is rational, their sum is periodic with a common period, while a sum of functions with incommensurable periods is not periodic (though it may be quasiperiodic). Arithmetic combinations and the composition of periodic functions with arbitrary functions remain periodic.

Fourier series

Periodic functions can be decomposed into sums of sines and cosines by means of a Fourier series. This representation underlies Fourier analysis and its applications in signal processing, acoustics, heat conduction, and quantum mechanics, where periodic functions describe systems ranging from vibrating strings to alternating currents.

Generalizations

The notion extends to functions on groups and to doubly periodic functions — functions of a complex variable with two incommensurable periods, such as the elliptic functions. Quasiperiodic functions, almost periodic functions, and antiperiodic functions (for which f(x + P) = −f(x)) are related concepts.
