Marguerite Long
Born13 November 1874, Nîmes, France
Died13 February 1966, Paris, France
OccupationPianist, pedagogue

Marguerite Long (13 November 1874 – 13 February 1966) was a French pianist and teacher, one of the leading French pianists of the twentieth century and a celebrated interpreter of the music of Gabriel Fauré, Claude Debussy, and Maurice Ravel. With the violinist Jacques Thibaud she founded the Long-Thibaud Competition in 1943.

Life and career

Born in Nîmes, Long entered the Paris Conservatoire at the age of twelve and studied with Antoine François Marmontel, winning the premier prix in 1891. She worked closely with Fauré, who became a mentor, and became the outstanding interpreter of his piano music. She also enjoyed the friendship and collaboration of Debussy and Ravel. In 1932 she gave the premiere of Ravel's Piano Concerto in G major, which was dedicated to her, conducting performances of it throughout Europe.

Teaching

Long taught at the Paris Conservatoire from 1906 to 1940 and was one of the most influential piano pedagogues in France. Her students included Samson François, Lucette Descaves, and many others. In 1920 she founded her own school, the École Marguerite Long, and published the influential treatise Le Piano (1959) and a memoir of Fauré. She was known for her insistence on clarity, lightness of touch, and fidelity to French musical style.

Legacy

The Marguerite Long–Jacques Thibaud International Competition, established in 1943 for pianists and violinists, remains one of the major classical music competitions. Long was made a Grand Officier of the Légion d'honneur. She died in Paris in 1966, aged 91.
