Gibson SG

Manufacturer: Gibson

Period: 1961–present

Body type: Solid, double-cutaway

Woods: Mahogany

Pickup configuration: Two humbuckers

The Gibson SG is a solid-body electric guitar model introduced by Gibson in 1961, initially as a redesigned Les Paul. The "SG" stands for "Solid Guitar." It is famous for its distinctive sculpted double-cutaway mahogany body, slim fast neck, and biting tone, and it has been in continuous production longer than any other Gibson model — the best-selling Gibson guitar of all time.

History

By 1960, sales of the single-cutaway Les Paul were declining in the face of competition from Fender's lighter, double-cutaway Stratocaster. Gibson responded in 1961 with a completely redesigned guitar: a thin, flat-topped mahogany body with deeply beveled double cutaways and the neck joint moved to the 22nd fret. The new model was initially sold as the Les Paul, but Les Paul himself disliked the design and asked for his name to be removed; from 1963 the guitar was renamed the SG.

Design

The SG has a solid mahogany body about 35 mm thick, substantially thinner and lighter than the Les Paul, with pointed double cutaways that give unrestricted access to all 22 frets. Most models carry two humbucking pickups, a Tune-o-matic bridge and stopbar tailpiece, and some variants feature the sideways or Maestro Vibrola tremolo systems. The SG Standard is traditionally finished in heritage cherry red.

Variants

Notable variants include the SG Standard, SG Special (with P-90 pickups), SG Junior, SG Custom with three pickups, the twin-neck EDS-1275 made famous by Jimmy Page, and signature models for Tony Iommi and Angus Young.

Notable players

The SG is inextricably linked with Angus Young of AC/DC and Tony Iommi of Black Sabbath. Other prominent players include Eric Clapton (who played a psychedelically painted SG nicknamed "The Fool" in Cream), Pete Townshend, Frank Zappa, Derek Trucks, and Robby Krieger of The Doors.
