Irvin D. Yalom

Irvin David Yalom
Born: 13 June 1931, Washington, D.C., United States
Occupation: Psychiatrist, author, professor
Known for: Existential psychotherapy; group psychotherapy
Notable works: Existential Psychotherapy (1980); Love's Executioner (1989); When Nietzsche Wept (1992)

Irvin David Yalom (born 13 June 1931) is an American existential psychiatrist, author and emeritus professor of psychiatry at Stanford University. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential living psychotherapists, known both for his academic work on group psychotherapy and existential therapy and for his best-selling works of fiction and creative non-fiction about psychotherapy.

Career

Yalom received his medical degree from Boston University School of Medicine in 1956 and completed his psychiatric training at Johns Hopkins. He joined the Stanford faculty in 1963. His textbook The Theory and Practice of Group Psychotherapy (1970) became a standard work, and Existential Psychotherapy (1980) organised existential therapy around four "ultimate concerns": death, freedom, isolation and meaninglessness.

Writing

Yalom's later books brought psychotherapy to a wide readership. Love's Executioner and Other Tales of Psychotherapy (1989) presented case stories; the novel When Nietzsche Wept (1992) imagined a series of therapy sessions between Josef Breuer and Friedrich Nietzsche. Other works include Momma and the Meaning of Life (1999), The Schopenhauer Cure (2005), Staring at the Sun (2008), on overcoming the fear of death, and the memoir Becoming Myself (2017).
