James Rolph Jr. (August 23, 1869 – June 2, 1934) was an American politician. A Republican, he served five terms as Mayor of San Francisco — from 1912 to 1931, the longest-serving mayor in the city's history — and then as the 27th Governor of California from 1931 until his death in office.

Mayor of San Francisco

Rolph, a shipping magnate, presided over the rebuilding of San Francisco after the 1906 earthquake and fire, the construction of the Civic Center, the Panama–Pacific International Exposition of 1915, the municipal railway and the Hetch Hetchy water project. Affable and publicity-conscious, he was nicknamed "Sunny Jim".

Governor

Elected governor in 1930, Rolph governed during the onset of the Great Depression in California. He drew widespread condemnation in 1933 for publicly praising the lynching of two accused kidnappers in San Jose, remarks that cost him national support. He died of a heart attack in June 1934, shortly after winning renomination, and was succeeded by Frank Merriam.
