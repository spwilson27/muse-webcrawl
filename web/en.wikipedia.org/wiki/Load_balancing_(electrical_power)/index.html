Load balancing (electrical power)

Load balancing in electrical power systems is the continuous matching of electricity generation to demand across a power grid. Because electrical energy cannot be stored in large quantities on the grid itself, supply must equal demand at every instant; any imbalance shows up as a deviation in grid frequency, rising when generation exceeds demand and falling when demand exceeds generation.

Methods

Grid operators balance the system using several layers of response:

  • Primary (frequency) response — automatic governor action and inertia from spinning generators arrest frequency changes within seconds.
  • Secondary control — automatic generation control restores frequency and interchange flows over minutes.
  • Tertiary reserves and redispatch — operators schedule additional plant, storage discharge, pumped hydro or demand-side response over longer timescales.

Day-ahead and intraday markets schedule most generation in advance; balancing markets then buy the flexibility needed to correct forecast errors and plant outages. The growth of variable renewable generation from wind and solar has increased the importance of storage, interconnection and flexible demand in keeping the system balanced.
