Ceres (dwarf planet)

Ceres (minor-planet designation 1 Ceres) is a dwarf planet in the asteroid belt between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter. With a diameter of about 940 kilometres, it is the largest object in the belt and contains roughly a third of the belt's total mass. It was the first asteroid to be discovered, by Giuseppe Piazzi at Palermo on 1 January 1801, and was initially classified as a planet before being reclassified as an asteroid in the 1850s and as a dwarf planet in 2006.

Orbit and physical characteristics

Ceres orbits the Sun at an average distance of 2.77 astronomical units, completing one revolution in 4.6 Earth years, with a rotation period of about 9.07 hours. It is a differentiated body with a rocky core and an icy mantle; its surface is a mixture of water ice and hydrated minerals such as carbonates and clays. In 2014 the Herschel Space Observatory detected water vapour plumes, and NASA's Dawn spacecraft, which orbited Ceres from 2015 to 2018, found bright salt deposits (faculae) in craters such as Occator, interpreted as residues from briny cryovolcanic eruptions, most notably at the dome Ahuna Mons.

Exploration

Before Dawn, the best views came from the Hubble Space Telescope. Dawn visited the asteroid Vesta in 2011–2012 before travelling to Ceres, becoming the first spacecraft to orbit two extraterrestrial bodies. It mapped Ceres from successively lower orbits until its hydrazine fuel was exhausted in 2018, leaving it in a stable orbit around the dwarf planet. Dawn's findings — including evidence of a subsurface brine reservoir and organic materials near Ernutet crater — established Ceres as an "ocean world" remnant of the early solar system.

Name

Ceres is named after the Roman goddess of agriculture, grain crops, and fertility, the counterpart of the Greek Demeter. Its astronomical symbol is a sickle.
