Laboratory for Atmospheric and Space Physics

The Laboratory for Atmospheric and Space Physics (LASP) is a research institute of the University of Colorado Boulder, founded in 1948. It is one of the oldest space-physics laboratories in the United States and the only academic institution to have designed and operated instruments sent to every planet in the Solar System and Pluto. LASP combines instrument building, mission operations and scientific research in solar, planetary, space plasma and atmospheric science, employing several hundred scientists, engineers and students.

History

LASP began in 1948 as the Upper Air Laboratory, when university physicists began flying instruments on captured V-2 rockets and balloons from White Sands, New Mexico, to measure the Sun's ultraviolet radiation above the atmosphere. Renamed the Laboratory for Atmospheric and Space Physics in 1965, it grew with the NASA Mariner and planetary programmes, providing instruments for missions throughout the Solar System.

Activities

The laboratory builds flight instruments — including spectrographs, imagers and particle detectors — and operates complete missions from its Mission Operations and Data Systems Center in Boulder, where students work alongside professionals. Notable missions and instruments associated with LASP include the Solar Radiation and Climate Experiment (SORCE) and its successor TSIS, the Aeronomy of Ice in the Mesosphere (AIM) mission, instruments aboard MAVEN at Mars, the SUDA dust analyser on Europa Clipper, and the long-running student-operated Colorado Student Space Weather Experiment. LASP is also a partner in the Emirates Mars Mission (Hope probe) instrument programme.
