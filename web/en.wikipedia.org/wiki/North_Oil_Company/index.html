The North Oil Company (NOC) is a state-owned oil company under Iraq's Ministry of Oil, headquartered in Kirkuk. It is responsible for oil and gas exploration and production in northern Iraq, including the giant Kirkuk oil field, one of the largest and oldest producing fields in the world, and fields such as Bai Hassan, Jambur, and Khabbaz, as well as fields in the disputed territories administered at various times by the Kurdistan Regional Government.

History

The northern fields were developed under the British-controlled Iraq Petroleum Company, which struck oil at Baba Gurgur near Kirkuk in 1927. Iraq nationalized its oil industry in 1972, and the operations in the north passed to the state North Oil Company, along with refining and the pipeline system to the Turkish Mediterranean port of Ceyhan.

Operations

NOC operates production, the Kirkuk–Ceyhan pipeline's Iraqi section, and local processing. Production has repeatedly been disrupted: by the Iran–Iraq War and sanctions era, the 2003 invasion, attacks by insurgents and the Islamic State in 2014, and disputes between Baghdad and the Kurdistan Regional Government over control of Kirkuk's fields. Iraqi federal forces retook the fields from Kurdish Peshmerga control in October 2017. Exports through Ceyhan were again halted in 2023 following an arbitration ruling against independent Kurdish exports via Turkey.
