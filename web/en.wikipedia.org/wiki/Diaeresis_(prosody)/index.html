Diaeresis (prosody)

In prosody, diaeresis (also spelled dieresis; from Greek diaíresis, "division") is the coincidence of a word boundary with the boundary between two metrical feet in a line of verse. Where a caesura marks a word-end inside a foot, a diaeresis marks a word-end that falls exactly between feet, often giving a slight rhythmic lift or pause at that point.

Usage

The term is used mainly in the analysis of Greek and Latin metres. In dactylic hexameter, a diaeresis after the fourth foot — the so-called bucolic diaeresis, common in pastoral poetry following Theocritus and Virgil — often coincides with the main sense pause of the line. Classical Latin elegiac verse also shows preferred patterns of diaeresis in the pentameter, where a word boundary in the middle of the line became almost obligatory. The word should not be confused with the diaeresis in orthography, the two-dot mark (as in naïve) indicating separate vowel pronunciation, though both derive from the same Greek word for separation.
