Robert Melancton Metcalfe (born April 7, 1946) is an American engineer and entrepreneur who contributed to the development of the Internet in the 1970s. He co-invented Ethernet, co-founded 3Com, and formulated Metcalfe's law, which describes the effect of a telecommunications network: the value of a network is proportional to the square of the number of connected users. Metcalfe has also made several predictions that failed to materialize, including forecasting the demise of the Internet during the 1990s.

Early life and education

Metcalfe was born in Brooklyn, New York, and grew up in Bay Shore, Long Island. He graduated from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1969 with bachelor's degrees in electrical engineering and industrial management. He then earned a master's degree in applied mathematics and a PhD in computer science from Harvard University, the latter in 1973, with a dissertation on packet-switching networks.

Career

While working at Xerox PARC in 1973, Metcalfe and David Boggs invented Ethernet, a local area networking technology that used coaxial cable and a shared-medium access method (CSMA/CD). Metcalfe's famous memo of May 22, 1973 described the system named after the "luminiferous aether" once thought to carry electromagnetic waves. Ethernet became the dominant wired LAN technology.

In 1979 Metcalfe left Xerox and co-founded 3Com, a maker of computer networking equipment. He persuaded Digital Equipment Corporation, Intel, and Xerox (the "DIX" consortium) to standardize Ethernet, which became IEEE 802.3. In the 1990s he became a technology pundit, writing a column for InfoWorld, and in 1993 he founded IDG's InfoWorld Publishing. In 2001 he became a venture capitalist at Polaris Venture Partners. From 2011 to 2021 he was a professor at the University of Texas at Austin, focusing on innovation initiatives.

Metcalfe's law

Metcalfe's law states that the value of a telecommunications network is proportional to the square of the number of connected users of the system (n2). First formulated around 1980 in the context of Ethernet, the law reflects that a network's usefulness grows with pairwise connection possibilities, and it has been applied to later network technologies including the Web and social networks.

Awards and honors

Metcalfe received the IEEE Medal of Honor in 1996, the National Medal of Technology from President George W. Bush in 2003, and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2007. In March 2023 he received the 2022 ACM Turing Award "for the invention, standardization, and commercialization of Ethernet."
