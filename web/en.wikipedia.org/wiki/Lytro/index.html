Lytro

Lytro, Inc. was an American company founded in 2006 by Ren Ng that developed light-field cameras allowing photographs to be refocused after capture. The company was based in Mountain View, California.

Technology and products

Building on Ng's doctoral research at Stanford University, Lytro's cameras placed a microlens array in front of the image sensor to record not just the intensity of light but the direction of light rays — the 4D light field. Its first consumer camera, a distinctive rectangular tube-shaped device, shipped in 2012, followed by the more conventional Lytro Illum in 2014, aimed at enthusiasts and professionals. The resulting "living pictures" could be refocused and slightly re-perspectived in software.

Decline and acquisition

Despite raising substantial venture funding, the consumer cameras sold poorly, and Lytro pivoted toward cinema and virtual-reality light-field systems. The company ceased operations in 2018, with Google acquiring much of its technology and team; its consumer image-hosting service was shut down, and the patents and research output continued to influence computational photography development elsewhere.
