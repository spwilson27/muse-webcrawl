Calcium silicate hydrate

Calcium silicate hydrate (abbreviated C-S-H in cement chemist notation) is the main product of the hydration of Portland cement and is primarily responsible for the strength of cement-based materials. It is not a well-crystallized compound: C-S-H forms a poorly ordered gel with variable composition.

Formation

When water is mixed with Portland cement, the calcium silicates alite and belite react exothermically to produce C-S-H together with calcium hydroxide (portlandite). The gel grows outward from cement grains, interlocking to form the hardened cement paste that binds concrete together.

Structure

C-S-H is commonly modelled as a defective, layered structure related to the minerals tobermorite and jennite, with silicate chains of variable length and a Ca/Si ratio typically between about 1.2 and 2.1 in ordinary hydrated cement. Its nanoscale porosity governs creep, shrinkage and durability of concrete.

Importance

Because concrete is the most widely used construction material on Earth, C-S-H is sometimes described as the most important manufactured material in the world. Research into its atomic structure aims at more durable, lower-carbon cements.
