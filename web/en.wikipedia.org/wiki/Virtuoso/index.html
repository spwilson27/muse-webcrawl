Virtuoso

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A virtuoso is an individual who possesses outstanding technical ability in a particular art or field.

A virtuoso (from Italian virtuoso, meaning "virtuous" or "skilled", from Latin virtus, "virtue" or "excellence") is an individual who possesses outstanding technical ability in a particular art or field such as fine arts, music, singing, playing a musical instrument, or composition. This word also refers to a person who has cultivated a refined appreciation of artistic excellence, either as a connoisseur or as a collector. The plural form is either virtuosi or the anglicised virtuosos, and the feminine forms are virtuosa and virtuose.

According to music historian Robert Schumann, the term virtuoso originally referred to a musician who was a theorist as well as a performer. In the late 17th and 18th centuries, many soloists were also composers, and the term came to be applied to instrumentalists whose executive ability was exceptional. The current connotation also implies bravura, that is, dashing virtuosity and bravado in performance.

History

The meaning of virtuoso has its roots in the Italian usage of the 16th and 17th centuries, signifying an honorary term reserved for a person distinguished in any intellectual or artistic field. The term evolved during the course of history, and by the 18th century entered the vocabulary of music as a term for performers of exceptional ability.

In the 19th century, virtuoso performers such as Franz Liszt and Niccolò Paganini became celebrated touring artists, drawing large audiences and helping to establish the modern concert recital. The archetype of the virtuoso soloist performing feats of technical brilliance, often with an emphasis on showmanship, dates from this period.

Virtuosity and musical interpretation

The term virtuoso has been used pejoratively by critics who regard virtuosity as an end in itself, at the expense of musical expression or substance. Nevertheless, many virtuosos have been praised for combining flawless technique with deep interpretative insight, and technical mastery is widely regarded as a prerequisite for fully realising complex works.
