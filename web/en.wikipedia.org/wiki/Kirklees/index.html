Kirklees

Metropolitan Borough of Kirklees Country: England
County: West Yorkshire
Administered by: Kirklees Council
Largest town: Huddersfield
Other towns: Dewsbury, Batley, Holmfirth, Mirfield
Population: about 433,000

Kirklees is a metropolitan borough in West Yorkshire, England, with its administrative centre in the town of Huddersfield. Created in 1974 by the Local Government Act 1972, it takes its name from Kirklees Hall and Kirklees Priory near Brighouse, a site traditionally associated with the grave of Robin Hood.

Towns and geography

The borough covers the former county boroughs of Huddersfield and Dewsbury and a number of surrounding mill towns including Batley, Spenborough, Mirfield, Heckmondwike, Holmfirth and Meltham. The southern part of the borough rises into the Pennine moorlands of the Peak District, while the north is a densely settled urban area along the Calder and Colne valleys. Holmfirth is famous as the setting of the long-running television comedy Last of the Summer Wine.

Economy and history

The area grew wealthy in the Industrial Revolution from the worsted and woollen textile industries, powered by the fast-flowing Pennine streams and later by coal. Although textiles have declined, engineering, chemicals and food production remain significant, and the University of Huddersfield is a major employer. Huddersfield is also notable as the birthplace of rugby league, founded at the George Hotel in 1895.
