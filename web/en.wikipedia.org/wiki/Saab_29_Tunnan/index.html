The Saab 29, colloquially called Flygande Tunnan ("The Flying Barrel"), is a Swedish jet fighter aircraft designed and manufactured by Saab in the late 1940s. It was Sweden's second turbojet-powered combat aircraft, following the Saab 21R, and one of the first production fighters with a swept wing.

The barrel-shaped fuselage gave the aircraft its nickname, "The Flying Barrel".

Development

Design work began in 1945, drawing on German wartime research on swept wings that became available after the Second World War. The aircraft was built around the de Havilland Ghost turbojet, licence-produced in Sweden as the RM2. Its short, squat fuselage, needed to house the centrifugal-flow engine, produced the distinctive barrel-like appearance. The prototype first flew on 1 September 1948.

Design

The Tunnan had a wing swept at 25 degrees, automatic leading-edge slats, and a pressurised cockpit. Fighter versions were armed with four 20 mm Hispano cannons. Later variants included the S 29C reconnaissance aircraft with cameras in a modified nose, and the afterburner-equipped J 29F, which substantially improved performance.

Operational history

The type entered Swedish Air Force service in 1951 and was progressively improved; the J 29F remained in front-line service into the 1960s. Swedish Tunnans saw combat with the United Nations during the Congo Crisis (1961–1963), the only combat use of the type. Austria purchased 30 aircraft, which served until the early 1970s. A total of 661 were built.
