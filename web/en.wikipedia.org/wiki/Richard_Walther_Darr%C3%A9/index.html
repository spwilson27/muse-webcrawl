Richard Walther Darré

Richard Walther Darré (born Ricardo Walther Óscar Darré; 14 July 1895 – 5 September 1953) was one of the leading Nazi ideologists and the chief proponent of the Blut und Boden ("Blood and Soil") doctrine, which exalted the German peasantry as the racial foundation of the nation. He served as Reich Minister of Food and Agriculture from 1933 to 1942 and as head of the SS Race and Settlement Main Office (RuSHA).

Ideology

Darré was born in Argentina to German parents and raised in Germany, studying colonial agriculture. In books such as Das Bauerntum als Lebensquell der Nordischen Rasse (1928) and Neuadel aus Blut und Boden (1930), he argued that Germany's future lay in a racially pure peasant class settled on hereditary farms, and envisaged a new aristocracy bred from the SS.

In government

As agriculture minister he pushed through the Reich Hereditary Farm Law of 1933, which tied designated farms to single heirs, and organised the Reichsnährstand, the corporatist body controlling food production and prices. His influence waned as wartime economic management passed to others, and in 1942 he was replaced by his deputy Herbert Backe.

Trial and death

Darré was tried in the Ministries Trial at Nuremberg and sentenced in 1949 to seven years' imprisonment, being released in 1950. He died in Munich in 1953.
