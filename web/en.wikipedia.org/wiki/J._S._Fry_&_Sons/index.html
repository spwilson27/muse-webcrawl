J. S. Fry & Sons, Ltd. was a British chocolate company owned by Joseph Storrs Fry and his family. Based in Bristol, it was one of the great Quaker chocolate firms of Britain, alongside Cadbury and Rowntree's, and a pioneer of the modern chocolate industry.

History

Joseph Fry, a Quaker physician and apothecary, began selling chocolate in Bristol in 1761. His son Joseph Storrs Fry took over the business and patented a method of mixing cocoa powder with sugar and cocoa butter melted with water — the process that made eating chocolate, rather than drinking chocolate, practical on a large scale. In 1847 the firm produced what is generally credited as the first modern chocolate bar, and in 1866 it launched Fry's Chocolate Cream, one of the world's oldest chocolate brands.

Fry's also pioneered the chocolate Easter egg in Britain in 1873, and Fry's Turkish Delight followed in 1914. At its height the company employed thousands at its Union Street works in Bristol.

Merger with Cadbury

In 1919, under pressure from wartime and post-war trading conditions, J. S. Fry & Sons merged with Cadbury, though the Fry name was retained on products. Production moved to the new Somerdale factory at Keynsham, opened in 1935, famous for its model village and workers' facilities. The Somerdale site closed in 2011. Fry's Chocolate Cream and Fry's Turkish Delight continue to be sold under the Cadbury brand.
