Sheaf cohomology

In mathematics, sheaf cohomology is the application of homological algebra to analyze the global sections of a sheaf on a topological space. Broadly speaking, sheaf cohomology describes the obstructions to solving a geometric problem globally when it can be solved locally. The central work for the study of sheaf cohomology is Alexander Grothendieck's 1957 Tôhoku paper.

Sheaves, sheaf cohomology, and spectral sequences were introduced by Jean Leray while he was a prisoner of war in Austria from 1940 to 1945. Leray's definitions were simplified and clarified in the 1950s, and it became clear that sheaf cohomology was not only a new approach to cohomology in algebraic geometry, but also a powerful unifying language: many cohomology theories (singular, de Rham, étale) can be recovered as the sheaf cohomology of particular sheaves.

Cohomology measures the failure of local sections of a sheaf to glue into a global section.

Definition

Let X be a topological space and F a sheaf of abelian groups on X. The global section functor Γ(X, −), which assigns to a sheaf its group of sections over all of X, is left exact but not in general right exact. Sheaf cohomology groups H^i(X, F) are defined as the right derived functors of Γ(X, −), computed by taking an injective resolution of F and applying the global section functor. The zeroth group H^0(X, F) is simply the group of global sections.

Čech cohomology

For many spaces and sheaves, cohomology can be computed concretely using Čech cohomology with respect to an open cover: one forms the complex of sections over finite intersections of the covering sets with an alternating-sum differential. On paracompact spaces, Čech cohomology with respect to a sufficiently fine (Leray) cover agrees with derived-functor cohomology.

Applications

Sheaf cohomology is fundamental to modern algebraic geometry: the cohomology of the structure sheaf and of coherent sheaves underlies Serre duality, the Riemann–Roch theorem, and Kodaira vanishing. In complex analysis, the exponential sheaf sequence relates the cohomology of holomorphic functions to the Picard group of line bundles. Étale cohomology, the sheaf cohomology of the étale topology, provides the ℓ-adic cohomology theories used in the proof of the Weil conjectures.
