W. A. C. Bennett Dam

The W. A. C. Bennett Dam is a large hydroelectric dam on the Peace River in northern British Columbia, Canada. Completed in 1968, it was one of the world's largest earthfill dams and created Williston Lake, the largest lake in British Columbia. It is named after W. A. C. Bennett, the premier of British Columbia who championed the project.

LocationPeace River, British Columbia, Canada
Opened1968
ReservoirWilliston Lake
OperatorBC Hydro

Construction

The dam, 183 metres high and about 2 kilometres long, impounds the Peace River where it cuts through the Rocky Mountains near Hudson's Hope. Its underground powerhouse, the Gordon M. Shrum Generating Station, has an installed capacity of about 2,876 MW, making it one of Canada's largest hydroelectric stations. The project was the centrepiece of Bennett's "Two Rivers" development policy for provincial industrialisation.

Impacts

Flooding created a reservoir of about 1,773 km² and flooded the territories of First Nations including the Tsay Keh Dene and Kwadacha, whose displacement without adequate consultation or compensation has been the subject of lasting grievance and later settlements.
