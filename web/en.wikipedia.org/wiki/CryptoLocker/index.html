CryptoLocker

CryptoLocker
Type: Ransomware trojan
Active: 2013–2014
Platform: Microsoft Windows
Takedown: Operation Tovar (2014)

CryptoLocker was a ransomware trojan that appeared in September 2013. It propagated through malicious email attachments and the Gameover Zeus botnet, encrypting victims' files with strong RSA public-key cryptography and demanding payment, usually in Bitcoin, for the private key.

Operation

Once run, the malware contacted a command-and-control server, generated a 2048-bit RSA key pair, encrypted documents, images and other user files, and displayed a countdown (typically 72 hours) threatening to destroy the key. Payments of about $300 were commonly demanded.

Takedown

In May–June 2014, Operation Tovar, an international law-enforcement effort, disrupted Gameover Zeus and CryptoLocker's infrastructure. A security firm later recovered keys and offered free decryption. Estimates suggest millions of dollars were extorted and hundreds of thousands of computers infected.

Legacy

CryptoLocker spawned numerous imitators and established the modern ransomware business model, later escalated by WannaCry and double-extortion gangs.
