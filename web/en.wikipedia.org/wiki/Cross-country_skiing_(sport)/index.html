Cross-country skiing (sport)

Cross-country skiing is a form of skiing where skiers rely on their own locomotion to move across snow-covered terrain, rather than using ski lifts or other forms of assistance. Cross-country skiing is widely practiced as a sport and recreational activity; however, some still use it as a means of transportation.

Cross-country skiing
Highest governing body: FIS
First played: 19th century, Norway
Olympic: Since 1924 (men), 1952 (women)

History

Skiing originated in Scandinavia as a means of winter travel. The word "ski" comes from the Old Norse word "skíð" meaning "split piece of wood". Competitive cross-country skiing developed in Norway in the 19th century, with the first recorded race held in 1843. The sport was included in the first Winter Olympic Games at Chamonix in 1924.

Techniques

Cross-country skiing uses two main techniques: the classic style, where skiers use a forward stride along set tracks (grooves) in the snow, and the skate skiing (freestyle) technique, in which skiers push off with their skis in a motion similar to ice skating. Equipment differs between the techniques: classic skis have a cambered section with grip wax or a fish-scale pattern, while skate skis are shorter and entirely glide-waxed.

Competitions

The sport is governed internationally by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS). Major competitions include the FIS Cross-Country World Cup, the FIS Nordic World Ski Championships, the Tour de Ski, and Olympic events such as the sprint, skiathlon, individual distance races, relay, and team sprint. Famous long-distance races include Sweden's Vasaloppet and Norway's Birkebeinerrennet.
