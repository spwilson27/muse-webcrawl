Dodo

Dodo
Raphus cucullatus
Extinct (c. 1690)
Family: Columbidae (pigeons and doves)
Endemic to: Mauritius

The dodo (Raphus cucullatus) is an extinct flightless bird that was endemic to the island of Mauritius in the Indian Ocean. It was a member of the family that includes pigeons and doves, and its closest relative was the also-extinct Rodrigues solitaire.

Description

About 1 metre (3 ft 3 in) tall and weighing perhaps 10–18 kg, the dodo had grey plumage, a large hooked beak, small useless wings, and strong legs. Subfossil remains show it was more slender than many popular reconstructions suggest.

Behaviour and ecology

The dodo fed on fruit, nuts, seeds, and possibly shellfish. Having evolved without predators, it was unafraid of humans and nested on the ground.

Extinction

First recorded by Dutch sailors in 1598, the dodo was hunted by sailors and devastated by invasive species—rats, pigs, and monkeys—that ate its eggs. The last widely accepted sighting was in 1662; it is generally considered extinct by about 1690, a symbol of human-caused extinction, remembered in the phrase 'dead as a dodo'.
