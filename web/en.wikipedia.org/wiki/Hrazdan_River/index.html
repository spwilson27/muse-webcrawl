The Hrazdan (Armenian: Հրազդան) is a major river in Armenia, flowing from the northwestern extremity of Lake Sevan near the town of Sevan, southwest through Gegharkunik and Kotayk provinces, through the capital Yerevan, and south to join the Aras River on the border with Turkey. It is the only significant outflow of Lake Sevan and one of the principal left tributaries of the Aras.

Course

From Lake Sevan the river flows through the town of Hrazdan and the industrial city of Charentsavan before entering Yerevan, where it has carved a deep gorge—up to about 100 metres deep—splitting the city. Within the capital it passes by Yerevan Lake (an artificial reservoir completed in 1967), the Ararat cognac factory, and the Hrazdan stadium. South of Yerevan it crosses the Ararat plain and empties into the Aras.

Energy and irrigation

The river's drop of roughly 1,000 metres between Lake Sevan and Yerevan is exploited by the Sevan–Hrazdan Cascade, a system of seven hydroelectric power stations built between the 1930s and 1960s (Sevan, Atarbekyan, Gyumush, Arzni, Kanaker, Yerevan-1, and Yerevan-3). Diversions from the river, formalized in the 1920s–30s amid concerns over Lake Sevan's falling level, also supply irrigation water to the Ararat plain. Regulation of the lake outflow remains a sensitive environmental issue because of its effect on Sevan's water level.
