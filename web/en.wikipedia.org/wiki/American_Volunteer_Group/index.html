American Volunteer Group

The American Volunteer Group (AVG), better known as the Flying Tigers, was a group of American military pilots who volunteered to fight for the Republic of China against Japan in 1941–42, before and just after the United States formally entered the Second World War. Recruited under the authority of President Franklin D. Roosevelt and organised by Claire Lee Chennault, the AVG became famous for its shark-mouth-painted Curtiss P-40 fighters and its remarkable combat record in Burma and China.

Formation

Chennault, a retired US Army Air Corps officer serving as an adviser to Chiang Kai-shek, conceived the plan to recruit American pilots and ground crew who would resign their commissions and travel to Asia as civilian employees of the Central Aircraft Manufacturing Company (CAMCO). Roughly 300 men were recruited: about 100 pilots drawn from the Army, Navy and Marine Corps, plus support personnel. Pilots were offered salaries far above military pay, with a bonus promised for each Japanese aircraft destroyed.

The group was organised into three pursuit squadrons: the 1st Squadron "Adam & Eves", the 2nd Squadron "Panda Bears", and the 3rd Squadron "Hell's Angels". Its P-40s were diverted from a British order and shipped to Burma, where the AVG trained at Toungoo in the autumn of 1941.

Combat record

The AVG first saw combat over Kunming on 20 December 1941, shortly after Pearl Harbor, and then fought a celebrated defensive campaign over Rangoon against superior Japanese numbers. Over roughly seven months of combat, the Flying Tigers were credited with destroying around 296 Japanese aircraft while losing only about a dozen pilots in aerial combat — a record achieved despite chronically inferior numbers and severe supply difficulties. Chennault's tactics, emphasising dive-and-zoom attacks and teamwork rather than dogfighting, exploited the P-40's strengths.

Disbandment and legacy

The AVG was disbanded on 4 July 1942 and replaced by the US Army's China Air Task Force, later the Fourteenth Air Force, with the 23rd Fighter Group inheriting its traditions. Several AVG pilots rejoined US services; others flew transport aircraft over "the Hump". The Flying Tigers remain among the most famous American air units of the war, commemorated in museums in both the United States and China, and their shark-mouth motif has been copied by air units worldwide.
