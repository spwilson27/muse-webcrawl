The Bee Gees were a musical group formed in 1958 by brothers Barry, Robin, and Maurice Gibb. The trio were especially successful as a popular music act in the late 1960s and early 1970s, and later as prominent performers in the disco music era in the mid-to-late 1970s.

The group sang recognisable three-part tight harmonies: Robin's clear vibrato lead vocals were a hallmark of their earlier hits, while Barry's R&B falsetto became their signature sound during the mid-to-late 1970s and 1980s. The group wrote all of their own original material, as well as writing and producing several major hits for other artists, and are regarded as one of the most important and influential acts in pop-music history.

History

Formation and early success

The Gibb brothers were born on the Isle of Man to English parents and raised in Manchester before the family emigrated to Redcliffe, Queensland, Australia, in 1958. Performing as the Rattlesnakes and later the Bee Gees (short for "Brothers Gibb"), they signed with Festival Records and released singles in Australia. Returning to England in 1967, they achieved international success with New York Mining Disaster 1941, To Love Somebody, Massachusetts, Words and I've Gotta Get a Message to You, and the albums Bee Gees' 1st, Horizontal and Idea.

Disco era

After a period of decline, the group reinvented themselves in Miami with producer Arif Mardin. Jive Talkin' (1975) began a run of hits; their contributions to the Saturday Night Fever soundtrack (1977) — including Stayin' Alive, How Deep Is Your Love, Night Fever and More Than a Woman — made the album one of the best-selling soundtracks of all time. The album Spirits Having Flown (1979) produced three more number-one singles.

Later years

The brothers continued writing for other artists (Barbra Streisand, Diana Ross, Dionne Warwick, Kenny Rogers and Dolly Parton) and released successful albums such as Still Waters (1997). Maurice died suddenly in January 2003; Barry and Robin retired the name, reuniting only briefly from 2009 until Robin's death in May 2012.

Legacy

The Bee Gees have sold more than 220 million records worldwide, placing them among the best-selling music artists of all time. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997, and in 2015 received the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award. All three brothers were appointed CBEs.
