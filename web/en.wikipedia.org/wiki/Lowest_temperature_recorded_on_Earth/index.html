Lowest temperature recorded on Earth

Vostok Station, Antarctica
Record−89.2 °C (−128.6 °F)
LocationVostok Station, Antarctica
Date21 July 1983

The lowest natural temperature ever directly recorded at ground level on Earth is −89.2 °C (−128.6 °F; 184.0 K) at the Soviet Vostok Station in Antarctica on 21 July 1983, measured by a ground-based thermometer. It remains the lowest temperature recognized by the World Meteorological Organization.

Vostok record

Vostok Station sits at an elevation of about 3,488 metres (11,444 ft) on the East Antarctic Plateau, near the Southern Pole of Inaccessibility. During the polar night of 1983, a combination of clear skies, very dry air, calm conditions, and strong radiative cooling drove temperatures to the record low. The station's average winter temperature is around −66 °C, and its warmest recorded temperature is only −14 °C.

Satellite measurements

Satellite remote sensing has measured surface temperatures as low as −93.2 °C (−135.8 °F) near the ridge between Dome Argus and Dome Fuji in East Antarctica, recorded on 10 August 2010. A later reanalysis identified temperatures as low as −98 °C in small depressions along the ice sheet. Because these are satellite skin-temperature readings rather than direct air-temperature measurements at standard height, they are not recognized as official records by the WMO.

Other contenders

The northern hemisphere record is −67.8 °C, recorded at Verkhoyansk and Oymyakon in Siberia. The lowest temperature in North America is −63 °C at Snag, Yukon, in 1947.
