The Argentina–Brazil football rivalry, known in Spanish and Portuguese as the Superclásico de las Américas, is a sporting rivalry between the national football teams of Argentina and Brazil and their respective fans. It is regarded as one of the fiercest rivalries in international football, pitting the two most successful South American nations — Brazil with five World Cups and Argentina with three — against each other.

History

The first official match was played in Buenos Aires on 20 September 1914, Argentina winning 3–0. The rivalry deepened through the Copa América, which the teams have contested since 1916, and through episodes such as the 1946 match in which Brazilian player Ademir's challenge broke the leg of Argentine captain José Salomón, sparking a mass brawl. In World Cups the sides have met four times (1974, 1978, 1982, 1990); the 1978 match, a 0–0 draw, was followed by Argentina's controversial 6–0 win over Peru that eliminated Brazil on goal difference.

Notable matches

Memorable meetings include Brazil's 3–1 win in the 1982 World Cup, Argentina's 1–0 victory in the 1990 World Cup round of 16 (the "holy water" match, after Brazilian Branco claimed he was given spiked water), Brazil's wins in the 2004 and 2007 Copa América finals, and Argentina's 1–0 win in the 2021 Copa América final at the Maracanã, Lionel Messi's first major international trophy. The individual rivalry between Pelé and Maradona, and later comparisons between Messi and Neymar, have long fuelled the debate over superiority.

Honours comparison

Brazil leads in World Cups (5 to 3) and Confederations Cups; Argentina leads in Copa América titles (16 to 9, after its 2024 triumph). Head-to-head tallies are close and disputed in detail, with over 100 meetings and near-even wins, depending on which early fixtures are counted.
