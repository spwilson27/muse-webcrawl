Diogenes Laertius

Flourished3rd century AD
Notable workLives and Opinions of Eminent Philosophers
SubjectAncient Greek philosophy

Diogenes Laertius (Greek: Διογένης Λαέρτιος) was a biographer of the Greek philosophers who flourished in the 3rd century AD. Almost nothing is known of his own life; his surname suggests a connection with Laerte in Cilicia or Caria. His Lives and Opinions of Eminent Philosophers is one of the most important surviving sources for ancient Greek philosophy.

The Lives

The work, written in Greek, consists of ten books covering philosophers from Thales to Epicurus, organised by school into "Ionian" and "Italian" successions. It mixes biography, anecdote, summaries of doctrine, lists of writings, and the philosophers' wills and deathbed sayings.

Importance

Although Diogenes is often uncritical and fond of gossip, his compilation preserves much that would otherwise be lost — including, in Book 10, three letters and the Principal Doctrines of Epicurus, and extensive material on the Stoics, the Cynics, and the Pyrrhonist sceptics. His accounts of Plato, Aristotle and Diogenes the Cynic remain standard starting points for study.
