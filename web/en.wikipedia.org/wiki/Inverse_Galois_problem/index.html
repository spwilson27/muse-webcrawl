Inverse Galois problem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Galois theory, the inverse Galois problem asks whether every finite group appears as the Galois group of some Galois extension of the rational numbers ℚ. The problem, which dates to the nineteenth century and was studied by David Hilbert, remains open in general, although a great many groups have been shown to occur.

Partial results

Every finite solvable group occurs as a Galois group over ℚ: this follows from work of Igor Shafarevich (announced 1954, with later corrections to the proof), together with the fact that every abelian group is realized by cyclotomic fields. Symmetric groups Sn and alternating groups An were realized by Hilbert using his irreducibility theorem. Many finite simple groups have been realized individually, including most sporadic groups; the largest sporadic group, the Monster, was shown to occur as a Galois group over ℚ by work building on Thompson's rigidity method.

Approaches

A central technique is the rigidity method, which constructs Galois extensions with a prescribed group from rigid tuples of conjugacy classes in the group. Another route is through Hilbert's irreducibility theorem: if a group G is the Galois group of a regular extension of the function field ℚ(t), then it also occurs over ℚ. The related regular inverse Galois problem and the study of rational points on Hurwitz spaces and modular curves provide additional leverage; Serre's work connecting Galois representations and modular forms fed into the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem.

Status

The problem remains unresolved in full generality: it is not known whether every finite group occurs over ℚ. The analogous problem over other fields can be easier — over ℂ(t) every finite group occurs — or have a negative answer, as over the p-adic fields. Variants ask for realizations with prescribed ramification or as Galois groups of extensions with restricted local behavior.
