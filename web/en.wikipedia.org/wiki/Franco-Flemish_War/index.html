Franco-Flemish War

LocationCounty of Flanders
BelligerentsKingdom of France vs. County of Flanders
ResultTreaty of Athis-sur-Orge (1305)

The Franco-Flemish War (1297–1305) was a conflict between the Kingdom of France under Philip IV and the County of Flanders under Count Guy of Dampierre. Philip sought to annex wealthy, strategically vital Flanders — whose cloth cities depended on English wool — while Guy resisted French encroachment and allied with Edward I of England.

Course

French forces invaded in 1297 and by 1300 occupied most of the county; Guy surrendered and was imprisoned, and Flanders was placed under the French governor Jacques de Châtillon. Heavy taxation and arrogance provoked revolt: in the Bruges Matins of May 1302, French garrisons were massacred, and on 11 July 1302 a Flemish army of urban militia and infantry destroyed a French chivalric army at the Battle of the Golden Spurs near Kortrijk — a celebrated triumph of commoners over knights, commemorated in Belgium to this day.

France recovered: at Mons-en-Pévèle (1304) Philip's army fought the Flemish to a standstill, and at sea the French-Genoese fleet destroyed Flemish ships at Zierikzee. The Treaty of Athis-sur-Orge (1305) ended the war: Flanders remained nominally independent under Guy's son Robert III, but paid a heavy indemnity and ceded the castellanies of Lille, Douai and Béthune (Walloon Flanders) to France.
