Trisong Detsen

Trisong Detsen (Tibetan: ཁྲི་སྲོང་ལྡེ་བཙན, Wylie: khri srong lde btsan; c. 742 – c. 797) was the 38th emperor of the Tibetan Empire, reigning from about 755 to 797. He is remembered as one of the three "Dharma Kings" of Tibet for his decisive role in establishing Buddhism in the country.

Reign

Trisong Detsen ascended the throne as a minor and consolidated power against anti-Buddhist factions at court. Under him the Tibetan Empire reached its greatest extent, and Tibetan forces briefly occupied the Tang Chinese capital Chang'an in 763.

Establishment of Buddhism

The emperor invited the Indian scholar Śāntarakṣita to Tibet and, when obstacles arose, summoned the tantric master Padmasambhava. Together they founded Samye, Tibet's first Buddhist monastery (c. 779), and ordained the first Tibetan monks. Trisong Detsen sponsored large-scale translation of scriptures from Sanskrit and convened the Samye (Lhasa) council (c. 792–794), which adjudicated in favour of the Indian gradualist school over the Chinese Chan teachings of Heshang Moheyan, shaping the future course of Tibetan Buddhism.
