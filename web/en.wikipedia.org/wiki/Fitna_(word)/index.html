Fitna (word)

Fitna
Arabicفتنة (fitnah)
Literal meaningTrial, test, temptation
Extended sensesSedition, civil strife, discord

Fitna (or fitnah) is an Arabic word that is often translated as "trial", "affliction", "temptation" or "tribulation". It has a wide range of meanings in the Qur'an and in Islamic history, encompassing both a test of faith sent by God and, in historical writing, civil war and communal strife within the Muslim community.

Etymology and Qur'anic usage

The word derives from an Arabic root associated with the testing of metal by fire — smelting gold or silver to separate the pure from the impure. In the Qur'an the term and its derivatives occur many times, frequently describing trials by which believers are tested, and in some verses describing persecution of believers by unbelievers. A well-known phrase states that "fitna is worse than killing", a verse historically debated by commentators.

Historical usage

In Islamic historiography, the great civil wars of the early caliphate are conventionally called fitnas: the First Fitna (656–661), which followed the assassination of the caliph Uthman and included the Battle of the Camel and the Battle of Siffin; and the Second Fitna (680–692), associated with the revolt of Ibn al-Zubayr and the events surrounding Husayn ibn Ali at Karbala. Later conflicts, including the Third and Fourth Fitnas of the Abbasid period, continue the pattern of the name.

Modern usage

In modern Arabic the word is commonly used for sedition, sectarian strife or social discord. It also became widely known in Western media as the title of a controversial 2008 short film by Dutch politician Geert Wilders.
