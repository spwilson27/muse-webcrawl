Charkha

A charkha used for spinning cotton

The charkha is a hand-operated spinning wheel used in India to spin cotton and other short-staple fibres into yarn. One of the oldest known forms of the spinning wheel, it became a powerful political symbol during the Indian independence movement.

Gandhi and the freedom movement

Mahatma Gandhi promoted the charkha and hand-spun khadi cloth as symbols of self-reliance and resistance to imported British textiles. He urged Indians to spin daily, and the charkha appeared on early versions of the Indian national flag; the modern flag bears the Ashoka Chakra, a related wheel motif.

Operation

The spinner turns a large drive wheel with one hand while drawing out and twisting the fibres with the other, winding the finished yarn onto a spindle. Compact "book charkhas" fold away for travel and were popularized during the independence campaign.
