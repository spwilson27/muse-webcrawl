D'Hondt method

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The D'Hondt method is a highest-averages rule for allocating seats in party-list proportional representation, named after the Belgian jurist Victor D'Hondt (1878) and mathematically equivalent to the Jefferson method. Votes for each party are divided successively by 1, 2, 3, … and seats are awarded to the highest resulting quotients until all seats are filled. The method slightly favours larger parties compared with the Sainte-Laguë method.

D'Hondt is used for the European Parliament in most member states, and in Spain, Poland, Finland, the Netherlands (in effect), Japan (for its proportional tier) and many other systems, as well as for committee allocations within parliaments. Its bias toward larger parties is defended as promoting stable government and criticised as distorting representation; small adjustments (thresholds, district magnitude) are usually combined with it.
