Drood is a musical with book, music and lyrics by Rupert Holmes, based on Charles Dickens's unfinished final novel The Mystery of Edwin Drood. Because Dickens died before revealing the solution to the story's mystery, the musical solves it by audience vote: at each performance the audience chooses the murderer, the identity of the detective in disguise, and a pair of lovers, so the cast must be prepared to perform several different endings.

Productions

The musical premiered at the New York Shakespeare Festival's Delacorte Theater in Central Park in August 1985 and transferred to Broadway's Imperial Theatre, where it opened in December 1985 under the title The Mystery of Edwin Drood and ran for 608 performances. The original company included George Rose as the music-hall Chairman, Cleo Laine, Betty Buckley in the trouser role of Edwin Drood, and Howard McGillin as the villainous John Jasper. It won five Tony Awards, including Best Musical, Best Book and Best Score — making Holmes the first person to win Tonys for book, music and lyrics of the same show. A Roundabout Theatre Company revival opened on Broadway in 2012 with Stephanie J. Block, Will Chase, Chita Rivera and Andy Karl.

Structure and songs

The show is framed as a performance by a Victorian music-hall company, the Theatre Royale, whose chairman leads the audience through the tale and calls the votes. Songs include "There You Are", "Two Kinsmen", "Moonfall", "The Wages of Sin", "Perfect Strangers", "Both Sides of the Coin", "Don't Quit While You're Ahead" and "The Writing on the Wall".
