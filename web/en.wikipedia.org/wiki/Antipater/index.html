Antipater (Greek: Ἀντίπατρος; c. 400 BC – 319 BC) was a Macedonian general and statesman under Philip II and Alexander the Great, and one of the Diadochi. While Alexander campaigned in the east, Antipater governed Macedon and Greece as regent, and after Alexander's death he became the dominant figure among the Successors until his death.

Service under Philip and Alexander

Antipater was a trusted associate of Philip II, who employed him as a diplomat — notably negotiating peace with Athens after Chaeronea (338 BC). When Alexander crossed into Asia in 334 BC, he left Antipater as regent (strategos of Europe) with an army to hold Macedon and Greece. Antipater defeated a revolt led by Agis III of Sparta at the Battle of Megalopolis (331 BC) and suppressed other unrest, while maintaining a sometimes tense correspondence with Alexander and with Olympias.

After Alexander's death

Following Alexander's death in Babylon (323 BC), the settlement confirmed Antipater in Europe. When Athens and its allies rose in the Lamian War, Antipater was initially besieged in Lamia but, reinforced by Craterus, won the Battle of Crannon (322 BC) and imposed a settlement on Athens, ending its democracy and the age of Demosthenes. In 321 BC he became regent of the whole empire and guardian of the kings, and at the settlement of Triparadisus he redistributed the satrapies.

Death and legacy

Antipater died of natural causes in 319 BC, passing over his son Cassander to appoint the veteran Polyperchon as his successor as regent — a decision that triggered a new round of wars among the Diadochi. Antipater is notable as the one major Successor who never claimed kingship himself and died in bed.
