Ternary plot

A ternary plot, also called a ternary graph, triangle plot, simplex plot, or Gibbs triangle, is a barycentric plot on three variables which sum to a constant. It graphically depicts the ratios of the three variables as positions in an equilateral triangle. It is used in physical chemistry, petrology, mineralogy, metallurgy, and other physical sciences to show the compositions of systems composed of three species. In population genetics, a triangle plot of genotype frequencies is called a de Finetti diagram. In game theory, ternary plots are often used to depict the evolution of replicator dynamics.

Reading a ternary plot

Because the three values must sum to a constant (usually 100%), any point in the triangle represents a unique composition. The value for each component increases toward the vertex at which that component is 100% and decreases toward the opposite edge, where it is 0%. Lines parallel to an edge correspond to constant values of the component opposite that edge. To read a point, one draws lines from the point parallel to two of the sides (or perpendicular to the axes, depending on the convention) and reads off the corresponding percentages from the axes.

Common uses

Ternary plots are widely used in soil science to classify soils by the proportions of sand, silt, and clay; in petrology to display the chemical composition of rocks, such as in the IUGS classification of igneous rocks; and in metallurgy to represent phase diagrams of three-component alloy systems at constant temperature and pressure.
