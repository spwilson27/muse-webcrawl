Lithophile

In geochemistry, a lithophile element (from the Greek lithos, "rock", and phile, "loving") is a chemical element that concentrates in the Earth's crust and mantle silicates, readily combining with oxygen to form oxides and silicate minerals. The term belongs to the Goldschmidt classification, devised by the geochemist Victor Goldschmidt in the 1920s, which divides elements according to their affinity for iron (siderophile), sulfur (chalcophile), rock/oxygen (lithophile) or the atmosphere (atmophile).

Characteristics

Lithophile elements have electron configurations that make them bond ionically, typically with oxygen. During the differentiation of the early Earth, they floated upward with the silicate slag rather than sinking with metallic iron to the core, and so they are enriched in the crust relative to the bulk Earth. They are generally hard to reduce to the metal by smelting and are extracted from ores by other means (for example, aluminium by electrolysis, and lithium from brines and pegmatites).

Examples

Typical lithophile elements include:

  • Silicon (Si) and aluminium (Al) — the main constituents of the crust
  • Magnesium (Mg), calcium (Ca), sodium (Na) and potassium (K)
  • Titanium (Ti), zirconium (Zr), hafnium (Hf)
  • The rare-earth elements (lanthanides)
  • Thorium (Th) and uranium (U)
  • Lithium (Li), beryllium (Be), boron (B)
  • Rubidium (Rb), strontium (Sr), barium (Ba)
  • Niobium (Nb), tantalum (Ta), scandium (Sc), yttrium (Y)
  • Oxygen-affined metalloids and halogens in many contexts

Some elements show mixed behaviour: iron, for example, is siderophile under reducing conditions but lithophile near the surface where it oxidises; sulfur-affined elements may also display lithophile tendencies.

Significance

The classification explains the distribution of elements between core, mantle and crust, the composition of meteorites, and the geochemical behaviour of elements in magmas. Because lithophile elements are concentrated in accessible crustal rocks, they include many elements of economic importance obtained from silicate and oxide ores, pegmatites and evaporite deposits.
