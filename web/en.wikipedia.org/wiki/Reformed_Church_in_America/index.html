The Reformed Church in America (RCA) is a mainline Protestant denomination in the United States and Canada, with historical roots in the Dutch Reformed Church of the Netherlands. It is among the oldest Protestant denominations in North America: the first congregation was organized in New Amsterdam (New York) in 1628.

History

Dutch settlers brought the Reformed faith to New Netherland in the 1620s. After the English conquest, the churches remained under the supervision of the Classis of Amsterdam until American independence; a fully independent American synod was organized in 1792. Through the 18th and 19th centuries the church spread westward with Dutch immigration, notably to Michigan and Iowa, founding institutions such as Rutgers University (1766), New Brunswick Theological Seminary (1784), Hope College (1866) and Central College (1853).

Beliefs and polity

The RCA confesses the historic Reformed standards — the Heidelberg Catechism, the Belgic Confession, the Canons of Dort, and the Belhar Confession. Its polity is presbyterian in structure, with consistories of elders and deacons at the local level, classes (regional bodies), regional synods, and a General Synod. The denomination ordains women to all offices (fully since 1979) and is a member of the World Communion of Reformed Churches and the National and World Councils of Churches.

Recent history

Debates over human sexuality led a number of conservative congregations to depart in the early 2020s, many joining the new Alliance of Reformed Churches, while the RCA restructured its governance. The denomination reports roughly 1,000 churches and some 180,000 members in the United States and Canada, with strength in Michigan, New York, New Jersey and the Midwest, and extensive global mission partnerships.
