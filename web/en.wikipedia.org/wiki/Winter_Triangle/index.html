Winter Triangle

The Winter Triangle is an asterism in the northern hemisphere's winter sky formed by three of the brightest stars: Sirius in Canis Major, Procyon in Canis Minor, and Betelgeuse in Orion. The three stars form a nearly equilateral triangle prominent on clear winter evenings in the Northern Hemisphere (and summer evenings in the Southern Hemisphere).

Membership

Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky at magnitude −1.46, anchors the triangle's southern vertex. Procyon, eighth-brightest, lies to the northeast, while the red supergiant Betelgeuse, marking Orion's shoulder, completes the figure to the northwest. The triangle encloses much of the faint constellation Monoceros, and the winter Milky Way passes through it.

Related asterisms

The Winter Triangle is often considered alongside the larger Winter Hexagon (or Winter Circle), which adds Rigel, Aldebaran, Capella, and Pollux to Sirius and Procyon. Its seasonal counterpart in the summer sky is the Summer Triangle of Vega, Altair, and Deneb.
