Reactive armour

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Reactive armour is a type of vehicle armour used on tanks and other armoured fighting vehicles that reacts to the impact of a weapon in a way that reduces the damage done to the vehicle. The most common type is explosive reactive armour (ERA), consisting of an explosive layer sandwiched between metal plates; when struck by a shaped-charge jet, the explosive detonates and drives the plates into the jet, disrupting and dispersing it before it can penetrate the main armour.

History

The concept was explored in the Soviet Union in the 1940s and 1950s and independently by the German researcher Manfred Held in the 1970s. Israel first fielded ERA — the "Blazer" system — on tanks used in the 1982 Lebanon War, where it proved highly effective against RPG-7s and anti-tank missiles. The Soviet Union responded with Kontakt-1 tiles fitted to T-64, T-72 and T-80 tanks from 1985, followed by the heavier Kontakt-5, designed also to degrade kinetic-energy long-rod penetrators, and the later Relikt system.

Explosive reactive armour

An ERA tile contains a thin layer of explosive between two plates. The impact of a warhead detonates the explosive, accelerating the plates asymmetrically across the path of the shaped-charge jet, which must then traverse constantly shifting material — an effect that can reduce penetration by more than half. Against tandem-charge warheads, developed specifically to defeat ERA by firing a precursor charge to trigger the tile, modern tiles use insensitive explosives and layered arrangements.

Non-explosive and electric variants

Non-explosive reactive armour (NxRA) uses bulging elastomer or composite layers that swell under impact to disrupt a jet without an explosion, making it safer for nearby infantry and usable on lighter vehicles. Experimental electric armour passes a powerful electrical discharge through plates to vaporise or destabilise an incoming jet.

Drawbacks

ERA tiles are single-use and leave gaps once expended; their detonation endangers accompanying infantry and lightly armoured vehicles, and they add weight and maintenance burden. Nevertheless, reactive armour remains a standard upgrade on tanks worldwide, including Western appliqué packages for Bradley, Leopard 2 and Abrams vehicles.
