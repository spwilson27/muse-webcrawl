Vulnerable species

A vulnerable species is a species categorised by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) as likely to become endangered unless the circumstances threatening its survival and reproduction improve. It is one of the three threatened categories on the IUCN Red List, below Critically Endangered and Endangered.

SystemIUCN Red List
CategoryVU (Vulnerable)
Risk levelHigh risk of extinction in the wild

Criteria

A species may be listed as vulnerable when its population has declined substantially (for example, by more than 50% over ten years or three generations), when its geographic range is restricted and declining, when its population is small and declining, or when quantitative analysis shows a significant probability of extinction in the wild.

Examples

Well-known vulnerable species include the giant panda (downlisted from Endangered in 2016 following population recovery), the African bush elephant (in part of its range), the polar bear, and many species of sharks and rays threatened by overfishing.
