The Virgin of Montserrat (Catalan: Mare de Déu de Montserrat), affectionately known as La Moreneta ("the little dark one") because of the dark colour of her face and hands, is a statue of the Virgin and Child venerated at the Benedictine abbey of Santa Maria de Montserrat in Catalonia, Spain. She is one of the famous Black Madonnas of Europe and the patroness of Catalonia.

The statue

The present image is a Romanesque polychrome wooden carving, generally dated to the late 12th century. The Virgin sits enthroned holding the Christ Child, and in her right hand a sphere representing the universe; the darkening of the varnish over centuries accounts for the colour that gives La Moreneta her popular name. Legend holds that the image was carved by St Luke, brought to Barcelona by St Peter, and hidden in the Holy Cave (Santa Cova) during the Muslim invasion, to be rediscovered by shepherds guided by a mysterious light around the year 880.

Veneration

The shrine has been a pilgrimage destination since the Middle Ages; Ignatius of Loyola famously laid down his sword before the statue in 1522. Pope Leo XIII proclaimed the Virgin of Montserrat patroness of Catalonia in 1881. Pilgrims traditionally climb to touch the sphere in her hand, and the abbey's boys' choir, the Escolania, sings the Virolai hymn in her honour. A replica brought to the Caribbean gave the island of Montserrat its name, and the Virgin is also venerated in parts of Latin America.
