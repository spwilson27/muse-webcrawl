Sicyon

Sicyon (Greek: Σικυών, Sikyon) was an ancient Greek city state on the northern Peloponnese, situated between Corinth and Achaea on the Gulf of Corinth. In classical antiquity it was famous as a centre of art — particularly bronze sculpture and painting — and as the homeland of the sculptor Lysippos. Its acropolis and lower city lay near the modern village of Vasiliko (formerly also called Vasilika), in Corinthia, Greece.

History

Greek tradition made Sicyon one of the oldest kingdoms in Greece; Pausanias lists a legendary succession of kings reaching back to Aegialeus. In the 7th century BC the city was ruled by the Orthagorid tyrants, the most famous of whom, Cleisthenes of Sicyon (c. 600–570 BC), grandfather of the Athenian reformer of the same name, pursued anti-Dorian policies and won fame for marrying his daughter Agariste to Megacles of Athens — a contest of suitors recounted by Herodotus.

In the classical period Sicyon was normally an ally and dependency of Sparta, contributing ships and troops in the Peloponnesian War. Its territory, the Sicyonia, was famed for olives, artichokes, and skilled craftsmen. The city's school of painting, led by Eupompos and his pupil Pamphilos, and the bronze-casting school crowned by Lysippos — court sculptor to Alexander the Great — gave Sicyon an artistic reputation rivalled only by Athens.

Hellenistic and Roman periods

Demetrios Poliorketes refounded the city on its defensible plateau site around 303 BC, renaming it Demetrias for a time. In the 3rd century BC Sicyon was liberated from its tyrant by Aratos of Sicyon (251 BC), who brought the city into the Achaean League, becoming the League's dominant statesman. Sicyon prospered under the League until the Roman destruction of Corinth in 146 BC, after which it declined, although Pausanias still described its temples, theatre, and stadium in the 2nd century AD.

Archaeology

Excavations by the American School of Classical Studies in the 1920s and by Greek archaeologists since have uncovered a large theatre (one of the biggest in Greece), a bouleuterion, a stadium, gymnasium, stoas, and temples. The site's museum is housed in part of a Roman bath complex.
