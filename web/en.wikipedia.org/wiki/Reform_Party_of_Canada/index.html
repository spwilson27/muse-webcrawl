Reform Party of Canada

Reform Party of Canada
Founded: 1987, Winnipeg
Leader: Preston Manning (1987–2000)
Headquarters: Calgary, Alberta
Dissolved: 2000 (succeeded by the Canadian Alliance)

The Reform Party of Canada (French: Parti réformiste du Canada) was a federal political party in Canada that existed from 1987 to 2000. It was founded in Western Canada by Preston Manning as a populist, fiscally conservative, and socially conservative protest movement under the slogan "The West Wants In".

History

Reform grew out of western alienation with the Progressive Conservative government of Brian Mulroney. After winning a 1989 by-election and the Alberta Senate nominee election, the party broke through nationally in the 1993 federal election, winning 52 seats — almost all in the West — as the Progressive Conservatives collapsed. In 1997 Reform won 60 seats and Manning became Leader of the Official Opposition.

Policies

The party advocated a Triple-E Senate (equal, elected, effective), balanced budgets and lower taxes, direct democracy instruments such as referendums and recall, free votes in Parliament, reform of the young offenders and criminal justice systems, and opposition to official bilingualism policies, distinct-society status for Quebec, and taxpayer funding of multicultural programmes.

Succession

Seeking to unite the right, Reform was dissolved in 2000 into the Canadian Alliance under Stockwell Day; the Alliance merged with the Progressive Conservatives in 2003 to form the modern Conservative Party of Canada, whose first leader, Stephen Harper, was a former Reform MP and later prime minister (2006–2015).
