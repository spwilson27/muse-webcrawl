Ethanol (also called ethyl alcohol or simply alcohol) is an organic compound with the chemical formula C2H6O. It is the principal alcohol found in alcoholic beverages, produced by fermentation of sugars by yeasts, and one of the oldest recreational drugs used by humans. Ethanol is a versatile solvent, a disinfectant, and an increasingly important renewable fuel.

Production and uses

Most ethanol is made by fermenting plant sugars and starches (corn in the United States, sugarcane in Brazil, grains and sugar beet in Europe); a smaller share is synthesised industrially from ethylene. As a fuel, ethanol is blended into petrol (commonly 10%, "E10"; up to 85% in "E85" flex-fuel), raising octane and cutting carbon monoxide emissions; Brazil's Proálcool programme made sugarcane ethanol a pillar of its fuel supply. In medicine and hygiene, 60–80% ethanol solutions are standard hand sanitisers and antiseptics, killing microbes — including the viruses and bacteria discussed under waterborne disease control — by denaturing proteins. It is also the solvent in perfumes, tinctures and vanilla extract, and a platform chemical for industry.

Effects and harms

Ethanol is a central-nervous-system depressant: in beverages it produces relaxation and euphoria at low doses, and impaired judgement, coordination, memory and consciousness as doses rise; very high doses cause coma and death. Chronic heavy use causes dependence, liver cirrhosis, several cancers, heart disease and fetal alcohol spectrum disorders; the World Health Organization attributes roughly 2.6 million deaths a year to alcohol. The body metabolises ethanol first to toxic acetaldehyde, then to acetate; differences in these enzymes partly explain varying tolerance across populations.
