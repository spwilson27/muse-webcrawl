Conseil d'État (France)

The Conseil d'État (French for "Council of State") is a French public body created by Napoleon Bonaparte in 1799. It performs two distinct functions: it is the supreme administrative court of France, with final jurisdiction over disputes between citizens and the administration, and it is a legal adviser to the executive branch, which must consult it on government bills and certain decrees. Its seat is the Palais Royal in Paris, opposite the Louvre.

History

The Conseil d'État succeeded the King's Council (Conseil du roi) of the Ancien Régime, which combined advisory and judicial duties. Established under the Constitution of Year VIII in December 1799, it drafted the Napoleonic Code. Restricted by the Restoration and the July Monarchy, it regained its central role under the Second Empire and the Third Republic. The famous Blanco decision of 1873, meanwhile, laid the foundations of modern French administrative law by affirming that the state's liability is governed by special rules distinct from the Civil Code. Reforms in 1945 and 1953 created administrative tribunals below it, making the Conseil the appellate and cassation court for administrative justice.

Functions

Advisory role

Under Articles 38 and 39 of the Constitution of 1958, the government must seek the Conseil's opinion on bills before they are submitted to Parliament, and on ordinances and decrees in Council of State. The opinion is advisory; the government may depart from it, and the opinions remained secret until a 2015 practice of publication for certain bills.

Judicial role

As the supreme administrative jurisdiction, the Conseil d'État judges, at first and last instance, challenges to acts of national importance — including decrees of the President and Prime Minister, and ministerial orders of nationwide application — and hears cassation appeals from lower administrative courts and appeals from the administrative courts of appeal. It sits in sections and, for the most important cases, in the Assemblée du contentieux.

Membership

Members (conseillers d'État, maîtres des requêtes and auditeurs) are recruited predominantly from the top ranks of the national school of administration, with an external "tour extérieur" quota. The vice-president of the Conseil d'État — effectively its head, since the Prime Minister is its nominal president — is by protocol the highest-ranking civil servant of France.
