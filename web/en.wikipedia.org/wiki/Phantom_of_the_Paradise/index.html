Phantom of the Paradise is a 1974 American rock musical comedy horror film written and directed by Brian De Palma, with songs by Paul Williams. A satirical blend of The Phantom of the Opera, Faust, The Picture of Dorian Gray and glam rock, it stars William Finley as Winslow Leach, a composer whose music is stolen by the sinister record mogul Swan (Paul Williams), and Jessica Harper as the singer Phoenix.

Plot

Swan frames and imprisons Winslow, whose face is disfigured in a record-press accident. Escaping, Winslow haunts Swan's new rock palace, the Paradise, and signs a contract in blood to write a cantata for Phoenix — not realising Swan has made his own pact with the Devil and intends to betray them both. The film climaxes in assassination plots, a televised wedding and revenge at the Paradise.

Reception and legacy

The film performed poorly on release in most markets — famously excepting Winnipeg, Canada, where it became a local phenomenon — but it has since become a major cult film, praised for its music, stylised direction and satire of the music industry. It influenced later rock musicals, and figures such as Daft Punk have cited it as an inspiration.
