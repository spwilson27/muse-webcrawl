Replenishment oiler

A replenishment oiler is a naval auxiliary ship with fuel tanks and dry cargo holds that can supply both fuel and dry stores to other ships while underway. In the United States Navy hull classification system, such ships are designated AO (fleet oiler), AOR (replenishment oiler, a now-retired designation) or AKE/AOE for combined fast combat support ships that also carry ammunition and stores.

Role

Replenishment oilers are a key element of underway replenishment (UNREP), the technique that allows a fleet to remain at sea for weeks or months. Using connected replenishment (CONREP) rigs, hoses are passed between ships sailing side by side so fuel can be pumped while both are moving; dry cargo is transferred by rig or by helicopter in vertical replenishment (VERTREP).

Classes

Important classes include the US Navy's Henry J. Kaiser class and the newer John Lewis class fleet oilers, the Royal Navy's Tide class and Wave class, and similar vessels in many other navies. Earlier "replenishment oilers" such as the US Wichita class and the British Fort Victoria class combined fuel with substantial dry stores and ammunition, blurring the line with fast combat support ships.

History

The concept developed from coaling ships and the first oilers of the early 20th century. Its decisive demonstration came in the Second World War, when US Navy fleet oilers and service squadrons enabled carrier task forces to operate continuously across the Pacific.
