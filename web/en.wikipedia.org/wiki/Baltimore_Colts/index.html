Baltimore Colts

The Colts at Memorial Stadium, Baltimore

The Baltimore Colts were a professional American football team that played in Baltimore, Maryland, in the National Football League (NFL) from 1953 to 1983. The franchise — whose name honoured Maryland's horse-racing heritage, notably the Preakness Stakes — became one of the NFL's marquee teams of the late 1950s and 1960s.

History

Led by quarterback Johnny Unitas, coach Weeb Ewbank, and stars such as Raymond Berry, Lenny Moore, Art Donovan and Gino Marchetti, the Colts won the NFL championship in 1958 and 1959. The 1958 title game against the New York Giants — the first NFL playoff to go to sudden-death overtime — is often called "The Greatest Game Ever Played" and is credited with sparking the NFL's television-era popularity. Baltimore won another NFL championship in 1968 but lost Super Bowl III to Joe Namath's New York Jets in one of the sport's greatest upsets; the team then won Super Bowl V over the Dallas Cowboys in 1971 under coach Don McCafferty. After declining years under owner Robert Irsay, the franchise notoriously moved to Indianapolis in March 1984, with Mayflower moving vans carrying its equipment out of Maryland overnight; it continues today as the Indianapolis Colts. The Colts' Baltimore records and history are treated as part of the Indianapolis franchise's history. Baltimore later gained a new NFL team, the Ravens, in 1996.
