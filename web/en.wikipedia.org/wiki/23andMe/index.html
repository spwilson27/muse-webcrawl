23andMe, Inc. is an American personal genomics and biotechnology company based in Sunnyvale, California. Founded in 2006, it is known for pioneering direct-to-consumer genetic testing: customers mail in a saliva sample and receive reports on ancestry composition, inherited traits and health-risk markers.

History

The company launched its first Personal Genome Service in 2007, named one of Time magazine's "Inventions of the Year" in 2008. In 2013 the US Food and Drug Administration ordered it to stop marketing health-related reports until they were validated; 23andMe returned to offering health reports with FDA authorisation beginning in 2015, including carrier screening and, later, genetic health risk reports. The company went public in 2021 through a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company, at a valuation of about $3.5 billion.

Products and research

23andMe's reports cover ancestry breakdowns, maternal and paternal haplogroups, Neanderthal ancestry, and relative-matching through its DNA Relatives feature. With customers' consent, anonymised data has been used in published research and in drug-discovery partnerships, including a collaboration with GlaxoSmithKline announced in 2018.

Controversies

In 2023 a credential-stuffing attack exposed profile data of millions of users, prompting lawsuits and increased scrutiny of genetic-data privacy. Falling demand and share prices led to layoffs, the departure of the board's independent directors in 2024, and in March 2025 the company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection and announced the sale of its assets, prompting public debate over the fate of its genetic database.
