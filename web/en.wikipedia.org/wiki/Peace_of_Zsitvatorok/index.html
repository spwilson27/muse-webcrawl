Peace of Zsitvatorok

Signed11 November 1606
LocationZsitvatorok (mouth of the Žitava, near modern Radvaň nad Dunajom, Slovakia)

The Peace of Zsitvatorok (Hungarian: Zsitvatoroki béke; Turkish: Žitva-Torok Antlaşması) was a peace treaty concluded on 11 November 1606 between the Habsburg Monarchy and the Ottoman Empire, ending the Long Turkish War of 1593–1606. Negotiated at the mouth of the river Žitava (Hungarian: Zsitva) where it joins the Danube in present-day Slovakia, it is remembered chiefly for a diplomatic revolution: for the first time the Ottoman Sultan formally treated the Habsburg ruler as an emperor of equal rank rather than a subordinate "king of Vienna".

Background

The Long War had begun in 1593 with fighting along the Croatian and Hungarian frontier and escalated into a grinding struggle for the fortresses of Ottoman Hungary — Esztergom, Győr, Kanizsa and Eger changed hands amid sieges, plague and empty treasuries. Neither side could achieve a decision: the Habsburgs were distracted by internal confessional conflict (and, from 1604, by the Bocskai uprising of Hungarian Protestants), while the Ottomans faced war with Safavid Persia and the Celali revolts in Anatolia. The related Habsburg settlement with Stephen Bocskai at Vienna (1606) opened the way to the Ottoman negotiations.

Terms

The peace, agreed for twenty years and repeatedly renewed, left territorial possession essentially as it stood: the Ottomans kept Eger, Nagykanizsa and Esztergom, while the Habsburgs retained their reconquests elsewhere in Upper Hungary. The annual Habsburg payment of 30,000 florins to the Porte — humiliatingly regarded in Vienna as tribute and in Constantinople as taxes from a vassal — was abolished and replaced by a single ceremonial "gift" of 200,000 florins. Most strikingly, Sultan Ahmed I addressed Emperor Rudolf II (negotiating through his brother and successor-in-waiting, Archduke Matthias) as "emperor" and his equal, abandoning the Ottoman practice of styling the Habsburg ruler merely "king" (kıral); reciprocal protocol was established for future diplomacy. Border raiding was banned, with commissions to settle disputes — though low-level frontier violence never fully ceased.

Significance

Zsitvatorok marked the effective end of Ottoman expansion into Central Europe by conquest of the Hungarian plain and the stabilisation of the Habsburg–Ottoman frontier for more than half a century, until the war of 1663–64. Its protocol clauses are treated by historians of diplomacy as an early step toward a European system of formally equal sovereigns, extracted from a Porte that had long claimed universal superiority — a concession made possible by Ottoman difficulties on two fronts and by the Habsburgs' demonstrated staying power.
