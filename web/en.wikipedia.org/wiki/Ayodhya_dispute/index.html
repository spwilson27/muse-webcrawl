Ayodhya dispute

The Ayodhya dispute was a political, historical, and socio-religious debate in India over a plot of land in the city of Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh. The dispute centred on a site traditionally regarded by Hindus as the birthplace of the deity Rama, where the Babri Masjid mosque (built 1528–29 under the Mughal emperor Babur) stood until its demolition in 1992. The dispute was resolved by the Supreme Court of India in 2019.

Background

Hindu tradition holds the site to be Ram Janmabhoomi, Rama's birthplace. Conflicts over access to the site are recorded from the 19th century; in 1949 idols of Rama were placed inside the mosque, after which the site was locked and became the subject of civil suits. In the 1980s a movement led by the Vishva Hindu Parishad and supported by the Bharatiya Janata Party campaigned for a Rama temple on the site. On 6 December 1992 a large gathering of activists demolished the mosque, triggering nationwide communal riots in which around 2,000 people died.

Resolution

In 2010 the Allahabad High Court divided the site three ways among Hindu and Muslim parties and a Hindu trust, a ruling all sides appealed. On 9 November 2019 a five-judge bench of the Supreme Court unanimously awarded the entire 2.77-acre disputed site for the construction of a Rama temple, and directed that five acres elsewhere in Ayodhya be allotted for a mosque. Construction of the Ram Mandir followed; the temple was consecrated on 22 January 2024.
