Stokes parameters

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Stokes parameters are a set of values that describe the polarization state of electromagnetic radiation. They were defined by George Gabriel Stokes in 1852, as a mathematically convenient alternative to the more common description of incoherent or partially polarized radiation in terms of its total intensity (I), (fractional) degree of polarization (p) and the shape parameters of the polarization ellipse. The parameters are commonly denoted S0, S1, S2, and S3, and together form the Stokes vector.

Definitions

In terms of the amplitudes of the electric field components Ex and Ey and their relative phase, the Stokes parameters can be written as:

S0 = I = |Ex|2 + |Ey|2

S1 = Q = |Ex|2 − |Ey|2

S2 = U = 2 Re(Ex Ey*)

S3 = V = 2 Im(Ex Ey*)

Here S1 describes the preponderance of horizontal over vertical linear polarization, S2 the preponderance of +45° over −45° linear polarization, and S3 the preponderance of right- over left-circular polarization. For fully polarized light, S02 = S12 + S22 + S32; for partially polarized light the sum of the squares of the last three is smaller.

Poincaré sphere and Mueller calculus

The three polarization parameters can be plotted as Cartesian coordinates on the Poincaré sphere, with totally polarized states lying on its surface. Optical elements acting on polarized light are represented by 4×4 Mueller matrices that transform one Stokes vector into another, a formalism known as Mueller calculus, widely used in polarimetry and remote sensing.
