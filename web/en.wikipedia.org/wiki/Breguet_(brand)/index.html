Breguet (brand)

Breguet is a luxury watch, clock and jewelry manufacturer founded by Abraham-Louis Breguet in Paris, France, in 1775. It is one of the oldest surviving watchmaking brands and is today part of the Swatch Group, headquartered at L'Abbaye in the Vallée de Joux, Switzerland.

History

Abraham-Louis Breguet founded the company on Quai de l'Horloge in Paris in 1775. His inventions included the tourbillon (patented 1801), the first self-winding watch (the perpétuelle, 1780), gongs for repeating watches, and the Breguet balance spring with its distinctive overcoil. Breguet supplied watches to Marie Antoinette, Napoleon Bonaparte, and much of European royalty; the famous No. 160 "Marie-Antoinette" pocket watch, commissioned in 1783 and completed decades later, incorporated every known complication of its era.

Through the 19th and 20th centuries the firm passed through the Breguet family and later owners, remaining synonymous with high horology. In 1970 the brand was acquired by the Chaumet jewellers, in 1987 by Investcorp, and in 1999 by the Swatch Group, under whose ownership production was consolidated in the Vallée de Joux.

Characteristics and collections

Breguet watches are recognizable by their guilloché dials engine-turned by hand, blued steel "Breguet" hands with eccentric moon tips, coin-edge case bands, and individually numbered pieces. Major collections include the Classique, Marine, Tradition, Reine de Naples (inspired by a wristwatch made for Caroline Murat in 1810, often cited as the first wristwatch), Type XX chronographs (developed from 1950s French naval aviation specifications), and the Haute Joaillerie lines.
