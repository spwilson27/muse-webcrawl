Siyum

A siyum (Hebrew: סיום, "completion") is the completion of any established unit of Torah study in Judaism, most commonly the completion of a tractate of the Talmud or a book of the Mishnah or Bible. The completion is marked by a ceremony and a celebratory meal, the seudat mitzvah, because finishing a substantial body of learning is treated as a religious accomplishment and a joyous occasion.

The ceremony

At a siyum, the person or group completing the study recites the final passage of the work aloud, followed by a special prayer text known as the Hadran ("we shall return"), which expresses the intention to revisit the completed material — "we shall return to you, tractate X, and you shall return to us" — and includes a passage thanking God for the privilege of study. Kaddish is then recited (a special form, Kaddish de-Rabbanan or, at the completion of the entire Talmud, the "long Kaddish"), and the gathering proceeds to the festive meal. It is customary for a student beginning the work again immediately, symbolising that Torah study is never truly finished.

Siyum HaShas

The most prominent siyum is the Siyum HaShas, celebrating the completion of the entire Babylonian Talmud under the Daf Yomi programme, in which participants study one folio page a day and finish all 2,711 pages in about seven and a half years. These events draw enormous crowds: the 12th Siyum HaShas in 2012 filled MetLife Stadium in New Jersey with more than 90,000 attendees, and the 13th, on 1 January 2020, again drew tens of thousands at venues around the world.

Other customs

A siyum has practical halakhic significance as well: a festive siyum meal is one of the occasions that can exempt firstborn sons from the Fast of the Firstborn on the day before Passover, and studying to complete a tractate during a period of mourning or on a minor fast day may likewise permit a celebratory meal. Schools and youth groups commonly hold a siyum when a class finishes a book of Tanakh or a tractate.
