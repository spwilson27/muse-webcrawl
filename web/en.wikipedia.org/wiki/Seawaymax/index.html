Seawaymax

A Seawaymax vessel transiting a Seaway lock.

Seawaymax is a ship-size category describing the largest vessels able to transit the locks of the Saint Lawrence Seaway, which connects the Atlantic Ocean with the Great Lakes of North America. Seawaymax dimensions are set by the smallest locks in the system, on the Welland Canal and the upper Saint Lawrence River.

Maximum length: 225.6 m (740 ft)

Maximum beam: 23.8 m (78 ft)

Maximum draught: 8.1 m (26 ft 6 in)

Maximum air draught: 35.5 m (116.5 ft)

Typical deadweight: c. 28,000–30,000 tonnes

Constraints

The governing restrictions come from the Seaway's lock chambers, which are 233.5 m long and 24.4 m wide, and from bridge clearances and channel depths. Because the Seaway draught limit is much shallower than the deeper Great Lakes channels, "lakers" built purely for the upper lakes can be far larger — up to the Poe Lock limit at Sault Ste. Marie — but cannot leave the lakes, while Seawaymax "salties" trade between the lakes and overseas ports.

Usage

Seawaymax bulk carriers move grain, iron ore, coal and project cargo. Design of Seawaymax ships balances the strict beam and draught limits against cargo capacity, often with distinctive straight-stem bows and self-unloading booms in the case of lake-fitted vessels.
