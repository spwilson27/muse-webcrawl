Battle of Crannon

The Battle of Crannon (322 BC) was fought between the Macedonian forces of the regent Antipater and his general Craterus, and a coalition of Greek city-states led by Athens, during the Lamian War. Fought on the plain of Crannon in Thessaly, the battle ended in a Macedonian victory that effectively concluded the Greek revolt against Macedonian domination following the death of Alexander the Great.

Background

News of Alexander's death in Babylon in 323 BC sparked a widespread Greek revolt, the Lamian War, named for the town of Lamia where the Greeks besieged Antipater. The arrival of Craterus with veteran troops from Asia allowed Antipater to break out and take the offensive in the summer of 322 BC. The Greek army, now commanded by Antiphilus after the death of Leosthenes, advanced into Thessaly to meet him, though its numbers had dwindled as contingents from several states drifted home.

The battle

According to Diodorus Siculus, the battle opened with the Thessalian cavalry under Menon, the finest horsemen in Greece, driving back their Macedonian opponents. However, the Macedonian phalanx, stiffened by Craterus's veterans, steadily pushed the Greek hoplites off the field and onto rough high ground. Unable to exploit their cavalry success against the formed phalanx, and with their infantry in retreat, the Greeks withdrew, having lost relatively few men but the strategic initiative.

Aftermath

Although the battle was no disaster in casualties, it broke the coalition. Antipater refused to negotiate with the Greeks collectively, dealing with each city separately, and the allies sued for peace one by one. Athens capitulated: Antipater imposed a Macedonian garrison on Munychia, executed or exiled the anti-Macedonian leaders including Demosthenes (who took poison to avoid capture) and Hypereides, and replaced the democracy with an oligarchy based on a property franchise. Crannon thus marks the effective end of classical Greek independence.
