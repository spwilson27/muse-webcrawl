The ventral tegmental area (VTA), also known as the ventral tegmental area of Tsai or simply the ventral tegmentum, is a group of neurons located close to the midline on the floor of the midbrain. The VTA is the origin of the dopaminergic cell bodies of the mesocorticolimbic dopamine system and other dopamine pathways; it is widely implicated in the drug and natural reward circuitry of the brain.

Structure

The VTA lies in the ventral midbrain, medial to the substantia nigra. Its neurons are heterogeneous: roughly 60–65 percent are dopaminergic, with substantial populations of GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons. The region includes nuclei such as the parabrachial pigmented nucleus and the paranigral nucleus. Its dopaminergic projections divide into the mesolimbic pathway, to the nucleus accumbens and other limbic structures, associated with reward and motivation; the mesocortical pathway, to the prefrontal cortex, involved in executive function; and projections to the amygdala and hippocampus.

Function

VTA dopamine neurons fire in response to unexpected rewards and to cues predicting reward, encoding a reward prediction error signal used in reinforcement learning. The VTA plays a central role in motivation, reward-seeking behaviour, orgasm and romantic love, and in the reinforcing effects of addictive drugs: cocaine, amphetamine, opioids, nicotine and alcohol all increase dopamine signalling in the VTA's projection areas, particularly the nucleus accumbens.

Clinical significance

Dysfunction of VTA dopamine signalling is implicated in addiction, schizophrenia (particularly through the mesolimbic system), ADHD, depression and Parkinson's disease, though the latter principally involves degeneration of the neighbouring substantia nigra.
