DjVu

DjVu (pronounced "déjà vu") is a computer file format designed primarily to store scanned documents, especially those containing a combination of text, line drawings, indexed color images, and photographs. It was developed by Yann LeCun, Léon Bottou, Patrick Haffner, and Paul G. Howard at AT&T Labs in 1996, and was later commercialized by LizardTech.

Technology

DjVu achieves very high compression ratios for scanned pages — typically 5 to 10 times smaller than JPEG for photographs and far smaller than PDF for scanned text — by separating an image into layers: a foreground layer containing text and line art, compressed with the JB2 algorithm (similar to JBIG2), and a background layer containing color and texture, compressed with a wavelet-based codec called IW44.

Uses

DjVu has been used by the Internet Archive and various digital libraries to distribute scanned books. It is an open format: the specification and a reference library (DjVuLibre) are released under the GNU General Public License. A layered text (OCR) layer can be embedded so documents remain searchable and copyable.
