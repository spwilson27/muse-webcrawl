Butterfly door

Butterfly doors are a type of car door sometimes seen on high-performance automobiles. They are similar to scissor doors, but while scissor doors move straight up via hinges at the front of the door, butterfly doors move up and outward via hinges along the A-pillar (windscreen pillar), giving them a motion resembling a butterfly opening its wings. This provides somewhat easier access to the cabin than scissor doors while still saving lateral space compared with conventional doors.

History and use

The design is generally traced to the Alfa Romeo 33 Stradale of 1967, one of the first production cars with such doors. McLaren has made the style a signature, calling its version "dihedral doors" on cars including the F1, P1, and 720S; the McLaren F1's doors additionally incorporated part of the roof. Other notable examples include the Enzo Ferrari and LaFerrari, the Mercedes-Benz SLR McLaren, the Toyota Sera (a modest production coupé with butterfly doors), and the BMW i8 plug-in hybrid sports car.

Advantages and disadvantages

Butterfly doors open upward, so they need little side clearance and ease entry in tight parking spaces and into cars with wide carbon-fibre tubs and high sills. Against this, they are mechanically complex, expensive, and can obstruct exit if the car rolls onto its roof; some designs incorporate hinges that let the door be pushed outward or detached in such an event.
