Private police

Private police are law enforcement bodies that are owned or controlled by non-governmental entities, or officers hired by private organisations to protect property and persons. Their legal authority varies widely: in some jurisdictions specially commissioned private officers — such as railroad police or university police — hold full police powers on their employer's property, while ordinary security guards have only the powers of any private citizen.

History

Private policing predates modern public forces; in the 19th century, agencies such as Pinkerton in the United States provided detective, guard, and strike-breaking services, and railroad companies formed their own police departments. In Britain, some docks, railways, and parks still maintain small constabularies.

Legal status and debate

Critics raise questions about accountability and training, since private officers may not be subject to the same constitutional constraints and public-records laws as public police. Supporters argue private police supplement overstretched public forces. In many countries the number of private security personnel exceeds the number of public police officers.
