Rail transport in Wales links the country's major cities and towns and provides connections to England. Services are operated principally by Transport for Wales Rail under the Welsh Government, with cross-border services by Great Western Railway, Avanti West Coast and CrossCountry.

Network

The principal routes are the South Wales Main Line through Newport, Cardiff and Swansea; the North Wales Coast Line to Holyhead (with ferry connections to Ireland); the Marches line between Newport and Shrewsbury; and scenic rural lines such as the Cambrian and Heart of Wales lines. Commuter services around Cardiff form the Valley Lines, being developed into the South Wales Metro with electrification and new trains.

History

Railways grew rapidly in industrial South Wales to move coal and iron, with the Taff Vale Railway (1840) among the earliest. Beeching-era closures cut many branches, and devolution has since brought increasing Welsh control over the network, including the Core Valley Lines transfer in 2020.
