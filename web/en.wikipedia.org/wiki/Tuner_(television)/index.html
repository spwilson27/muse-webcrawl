Tuner (television)

A television tuner is the front-end circuit of a television set, set-top box, video recorder or computer TV card that selects a particular television channel from the many signals received by an antenna or cable feed, and converts it to a form the rest of the receiver can display. Modern tuners are typically built as a compact shielded module (or a silicon tuner chip) containing a radio-frequency amplifier, a local oscillator and mixer, and intermediate-frequency filtering, implementing the superheterodyne principle.

Analog tuners

Classic analog tuners covered the VHF band (channels 2–13) and UHF band (channels 14 and up). Early sets used turret or rotary switches that physically switched inductors and capacitors; later designs used varactor diodes whose capacitance is varied by a control voltage, enabling electronic tuning and remote control. The tuner outputs an intermediate frequency (for example 45.75 MHz picture carrier in the North American NTSC system) that carries the composite video and audio signals to the demodulator stages.

Digital tuners

With the transition to digital television broadcasting, tuners must also demodulate digital modulation schemes and output a transport stream. Different regions use different standards: ATSC in North America, DVB-T/T2 in Europe and much of the world, ISDB-T in Japan and parts of South America, and DTMB in China. Cable and satellite receivers use related tuner front-ends for QAM (DVB-C) and satellite (DVB-S/S2) signals. Many televisions combine terrestrial, cable and sometimes satellite tuners in a single unit, and USB "TV sticks" provide the same functions for computers.
