Piezo2

Piezo2 is a protein that in humans is encoded by the PIEZO2 gene. It is a mechanosensitive ion channel — a large transmembrane protein that opens in response to mechanical force, allowing cations to flow into the cell. Piezo2 is a central molecular transducer of touch and proprioception.

Function

Piezo2 is expressed in sensory neurons of the dorsal root ganglia, in Merkel cells of the skin, and in other mechanosensory cells. In response to membrane stretch it generates rapidly adapting currents that underlie light-touch sensation, vibration detection, and the sense of body position and movement (proprioception). It also contributes to mechanical pain sensitization and to stretch sensing in internal organs such as the lungs and bladder.

Clinical significance

Gain-of-function mutations in PIEZO2 cause distal arthrogryposis syndromes, including Gordon syndrome and Marden–Walker syndrome, marked by congenital joint contractures. Loss-of-function mutations cause a syndrome of profound proprioceptive and touch deficits with scoliosis and joint abnormalities. The related channel Piezo1, discovered alongside Piezo2 in the laboratory of Ardem Patapoutian, mediates mechanosensation in vascular and other tissues; the discovery of the Piezo channels was recognized by the 2021 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
