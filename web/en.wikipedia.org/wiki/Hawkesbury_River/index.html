The Hawkesbury River, or Deerubben in the Dharug language, is one of the major rivers of the coastal region of New South Wales, Australia. Together with its main tributary, the Nepean River, it forms a great arc around the western and northern edges of the Sydney metropolitan area before emptying into Broken Bay and the Tasman Sea.

The river is generally taken to begin at the confluence of the Nepean and Grose rivers near Richmond and flows generally north and then east for about 120 kilometres (75 mi). Its catchment, around 21,624 square kilometres, is one of the largest on the New South Wales coast.

History

The Hawkesbury valley was inhabited for millennia by the Dharug and Darkinjung peoples, who called the river Deerubben. Governor Arthur Phillip explored the lower river in 1789, and in June that year a party led by Phillip reached the river near present-day Richmond and Windsor; Phillip named it after Charles Jenkinson, 1st Earl of Liverpool, then Baron Hawkesbury. The fertile floodplain made the district Sydney's early breadbasket, and river towns such as Windsor and Richmond were founded from 1794.

Relations between settlers and Aboriginal people deteriorated into the Hawkesbury and Nepean Wars of the 1790s–1810s. The river was also notorious for destructive floods, culminating in major inundations of Windsor and Richmond; the March 2021 and 2022 floods again caused extensive damage. The catastrophic 1867 flood, the highest recorded at Windsor, reached about 19.7 metres.

Geography and ecology

From Richmond the river flows past Sackville and Wisemans Ferry, where it is still tide-influenced, and winds through steep sandstone gorges flanked by Ku-ring-gai Chase National Park and other reserves. Tributaries include the Macdonald River, Colo River, Berowra Creek and Brisbane Water. The broad, drowned-valley estuary below Spencer is a major recreational waterway, with settlements such as Brooklyn and Dangar Island accessible largely by boat or train.

The Hawkesbury and its estuary support commercial and recreational fisheries, historically including the Sydney rock oyster industry centred on the lower estuary, as well as prawns, bream and mulloway.

Bridges and transport

Notable crossings include the bridges at Windsor and Richmond, the Peats Ferry Bridge and the Hawkesbury River railway bridge at Brooklyn, which carries the Main North line across the wide estuary. Ferries still operate at Wisemans Ferry, Sackville, Lower Portland and Webbs Creek, among the last public road ferries in metropolitan Sydney.
