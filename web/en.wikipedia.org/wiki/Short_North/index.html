Short North

The Short North is a neighborhood in Columbus, Ohio, United States, centered on the main strip of High Street immediately north of downtown and extending to the south end of the Ohio State University campus area. It is known as the city's arts district, distinguished by the street arches over High Street, art galleries, restaurants, bars, and boutiques.

History

The name originated in the early 20th century from police slang for the area just north of downtown, beyond the "short" distance of the main rail yards; another account attributes it to its location between downtown and the "Long North" of Clintonville. The neighborhood declined in the mid-20th century and was revitalized beginning in the 1980s as artists and galleries, including the Roy G. Biv Gallery, moved in. Seventeen lighted steel arches, installed in 1988 and modeled on wooden arches of 1888, span High Street and are a signature landmark.

Culture

The monthly Gallery Hop, held on the first Saturday of each month, draws crowds to gallery openings. Major events include the Stonewall Columbus Pride parade (moved to the neighborhood in 1982), Doo Dah Parade, and HighBall Halloween. The neighborhood includes the Short North Arts District, Harrison West, and Victorian Village at its edges.
