Official nameCar Allowance Rebate System (CARS)

Cash for Clunkers, officially the Car Allowance Rebate System (CARS), was a United States federal scrappage programme in the summer of 2009 that provided cash incentives for consumers to trade in older, fuel-inefficient vehicles and purchase new, more fuel-efficient ones.

Operation

Consumers received vouchers worth ,500 or ,500 toward a new vehicle, depending on the improvement in fuel economy; the traded-in vehicle had to be drivable, less than 25 years old, and rated at 18 miles per gallon or less, and its engine was destroyed to prevent resale. The programme proved far more popular than expected: its initial billion was exhausted within days, Congress added billion, and by its end on 24 August 2009 nearly 680,000 vehicles had been traded in. Economists have debated whether it generated new demand or merely pulled forward purchases that would have happened anyway.
