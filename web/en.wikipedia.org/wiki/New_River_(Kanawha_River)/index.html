The New River is a river in the southeastern United States, approximately 320 miles (510 km) long. It flows from the Blue Ridge Mountains of North Carolina northward through Virginia into West Virginia, where it joins the Gauley River to form the Kanawha River at Gauley Bridge. Despite its name, the New River is among the oldest rivers in North America, geologically estimated to be hundreds of millions of years old.

Course and gorge

In West Virginia the river cuts the deep New River Gorge, spanned by the New River Gorge Bridge near Fayetteville—one of the longest steel arch bridges in the world. In 2020 the gorge was designated New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, the United States' newest national park at the time. The river is renowned for whitewater rafting, rock climbing, and smallmouth bass fishing.

The river's unusual northward course across the Appalachians, cutting through ridges rather than following them, is evidence that it predates the uplift of the modern terrain it crosses. Major tributaries include the Bluestone and Greenbrier rivers.
