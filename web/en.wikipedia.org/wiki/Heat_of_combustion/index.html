Heat of combustion

The heat of combustion is the total energy released as heat when a substance undergoes complete combustion with oxygen under standard conditions. It is usually expressed per unit mass (MJ/kg) or per mole (kJ/mol) and is numerically equal to the negative of the enthalpy change of combustion, ΔHc.

Higher and lower heating values

The higher heating value (HHV, or gross calorific value) assumes the water produced is condensed to liquid, recovering its latent heat of vaporisation. The lower heating value (LHV, or net calorific value) assumes water leaves as vapour. Natural gas, for instance, has an HHV of roughly 55 MJ/kg, while petrol is about 47 MJ/kg.

Measurement

Heats of combustion are measured experimentally with a bomb calorimeter, in which a sample is burned in pressurised oxygen and the temperature rise of the surrounding water is recorded.
