Zafar (Arabic: ظفار, Ẓafār) was the capital city of the ancient Himyarite kingdom in the highlands of present-day Yemen. Situated south of the modern town of Yarim in Ibb Governorate at roughly 2,800 metres above sea level, it was one of the largest cities of ancient Arabia and a major centre of the late antique world.

The highland site of Zafar, capital of the Himyarite kingdom.

History

Zafar rose to prominence when the Himyarites consolidated control of South Arabia, and from around the late 3rd century CE it served as their royal capital, succeeding earlier centres. At its height in the 4th to 6th centuries the city may have housed tens of thousands of inhabitants and controlled trade routes carrying frankincense and other goods. Himyar's kings variously patronised pagan cults, Judaism, and later Christianity; Zafar contained churches as well as temples. The city declined after the Aksumite invasion of 525 CE and the subsequent Persian occupation, and was largely abandoned in the Islamic period.

Archaeology

Systematic excavations by a Heidelberg University team under Paul Yule (1998–2010) documented palaces, temples, fortifications, and an extensive corpus of sculpture and inscriptions, as well as hundreds of thousands of animal bones indicating a sophisticated urban economy. Finds are held by the Zafar site museum and regional collections.
