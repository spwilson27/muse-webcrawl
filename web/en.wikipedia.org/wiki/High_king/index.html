High king

A high king is a king who holds a position of seniority or overlordship over a group of lesser kings, without necessarily exercising direct rule over their territories. The institution is especially associated with early medieval Ireland and Scotland, but analogous titles and roles appear in many cultures, from the "king of kings" of ancient Persia to the Bretwalda of Anglo-Saxon England.

Ireland

In Ireland, the High King (Ard Rí) was said to rule from Tara over the provincial kings of Ulster, Leinster, Munster, Connacht and Meath. Historians debate how much real power early high kings wielded; the title was largely honorific and contested for centuries, but from the 9th and 10th centuries kings such as Máel Sechnaill and Brian Boru built something closer to a genuine national overlordship. Brian's victory at Clontarf in 1014 made him the most famous holder of the office. The high kingship effectively ended with the Anglo-Norman intervention and the last high king, Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair, in the 12th century.

Other traditions

Early Scottish kings of Alba were styled high kings (ard rí Alban) in Irish annals. In fiction and legend, the concept survives in figures such as the High King of Britain in Welsh and Arthurian tradition.
