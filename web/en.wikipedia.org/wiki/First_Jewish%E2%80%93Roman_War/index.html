First Jewish–Roman War

Date: 66–73/74 CE
Location: Judaea
Result: Roman victory; Jerusalem and Second Temple destroyed
Principal source: Josephus

The First Jewish–Roman War (66–73/74 CE), also called the Great Jewish Revolt, was the first of three major rebellions by the Jews of Judaea against the Roman Empire, culminating in the destruction of Jerusalem and the Second Temple in 70 CE.

Course

Revolt erupted in 66 amid ethnic violence and misrule by the procurator Gessius Florus; a Roman punitive force under Cestius Gallus was ambushed at Beth Horon. Nero sent Vespasian, who subdued Galilee in 67 — capturing the commander Josephus, our main source — and, after becoming emperor in 69, left his son Titus to besiege Jerusalem in 70. The city fell after famine and bitter factional infighting; the Temple was burned and its treasures paraded in Rome, depicted on the Arch of Titus.

Aftermath

Remaining strongholds fell in turn, ending with the mass suicide at Masada (73/74). Judaea was reorganised, the Temple tax redirected to Rome (Fiscus Judaicus), and Jewish life re-centred around rabbinic Judaism at Yavneh. See Siege of Jerusalem and Masada.
