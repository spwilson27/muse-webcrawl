The national parks of France are a system of eleven protected areas managed under the French national parks law of 1960 (reformed in 2006). Each park consists of a strictly protected core zone (cœur), where regulations are stringent, and an adhesion zone of surrounding communes that voluntarily adopt the park's charter of sustainable development. Together the parks protect Alpine, Pyrenean, Mediterranean, Atlantic forest, and tropical overseas ecosystems.

The parks

The metropolitan parks are Vanoise (1963, the first, adjoining Italy's Gran Paradiso), Port-Cros (1963, marine), Pyrénées (1967), Cévennes (1970, also a biosphere reserve and dark-sky reserve), Écrins (1973), Mercantour (1979), Calanques (2012, near Marseille), and Forêts (2019, lowland forest between Champagne and Burgundy). Overseas parks are Guadeloupe (1989), French Guiana (2007, the largest, covering Amazonian rainforest), and Réunion (2007).

Governance

Each park is run by a public establishment with a board including state and local representatives, and affiliated nationally with the French Office for Biodiversity. Parks protect emblematic species such as the Alpine ibex, Pyrenean chamois, bearded vulture, and wolf, and regulate tourism, pastoralism, and forestry within their cores.
