The Screen Actors Guild Awards (also known as SAG Awards) are accolades given by the Screen Actors Guild–American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (SAG-AFTRA). The awards were founded in 1995 to recognize outstanding performances in film and prime-time television. Voted on solely by actors' peers — the union's membership of over 100,000 performers — they are considered among the most prestigious acting awards and a key precursor to the Academy Awards.

The ceremony

The statuette, a nude male figure holding both a mask of comedy and a mask of tragedy, is called "The Actor" and was designed by Edward Saidi. The highlight of the telecast is traditionally the opening segment "I Am an Actor", in which performers in the audience briefly recount how they became actors. The ceremony has been held most years at the Shrine Auditorium in Los Angeles and has aired on TNT/TBS and, from 2024, streamed on Netflix.

Categories

  • Film: Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture; Male Actor in a Leading Role; Female Actor in a Leading Role; Male and Female Actor in a Supporting Role; Stunt Ensemble in a Motion Picture.
  • Television: Ensemble in a Drama Series; Ensemble in a Comedy Series; Male and Female Actor in a Drama Series and in a Comedy Series; Male and Female Actor in a Miniseries or Television Movie; Stunt Ensemble in a Television Series.
  • Life Achievement Award: presented annually since 1962 (predating the ceremony) for career achievement and humanitarian accomplishment; recipients have included Eddie Cantor (its first), Sidney Poitier, Julie Andrews, Robert De Niro and Barbra Streisand.

Significance

Because the Academy's largest voting branch is actors, SAG Award winners frequently repeat at the Oscars, especially in the four individual acting categories; the cast award is widely treated as an analogue of the Academy Award for Best Picture. The awards show is also distinctive for having no host for most of its history.
