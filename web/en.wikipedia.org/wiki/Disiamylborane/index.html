Disiamylborane

Disiamylborane
Names: Bis(1,2-dimethylpropyl)borane
Formula: C10H23B
Appearance: colourless liquid

Disiamylborane (bis(1,2-dimethylpropyl)borane, abbreviated Sia2BH) is an organoborane reagent used in organic synthesis. Its name derives from "sec-isoamyl" groups attached to boron.

Preparation

Disiamylborane is prepared by the hydroboration of 2-methyl-2-butene with diborane or borane complexes, adding two bulky alkyl groups to the boron atom while leaving one B–H bond available for reaction.

Reactivity

Because of its bulky substituents, disiamylborane reacts selectively with less sterically hindered alkenes and adds to alkynes only once, giving vinylboranes that can be oxidised to aldehydes. It is a classic reagent for the hydroboration–oxidation of terminal alkynes to aldehydes, complementing less hindered reagents such as borane itself. The selective hydroboration chemistry of such dialkylboranes was developed extensively by Herbert C. Brown.
