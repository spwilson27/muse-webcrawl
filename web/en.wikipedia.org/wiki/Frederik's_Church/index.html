Frederik's Church (Danish: Frederiks Kirke), popularly known as The Marble Church (Marmorkirken), is an Evangelical Lutheran church in Copenhagen, Denmark. Standing opposite Amalienborg Palace in the district of Frederiksstaden, it has the largest church dome in Scandinavia, with a span of 31 metres, and is one of Copenhagen's best-known landmarks.

History

The church was commissioned by King Frederick V as the centrepiece of Frederiksstaden, founded to mark the 300th anniversary of the House of Oldenburg. The foundation stone was laid in 1749 to designs by Nicolai Eigtved in the Rococo style. Construction stalled for over a century because of cost overruns, and the partly built church stood as a picturesque ruin until the industrialist C. F. Tietgen financed its completion. Under architect Ferdinand Meldahl it was finished — largely in Danish limestone rather than the originally planned Norwegian marble — and opened in 1894.

Architecture

The copper-clad dome, inspired by St. Peter's Basilica in Rome, rests on twelve pillars. Around the exterior stand statues of Danish church figures and theologians, including Grundtvig and Kierkegaard. The interior is richly decorated with frescoes depicting the apostles beneath the dome.
