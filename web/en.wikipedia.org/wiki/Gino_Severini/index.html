Gino Severini

Born: April 7, 1883, Cortona, Italy

Died: February 26, 1966 (aged 82), Paris, France

Movement: Futurism, Cubism, Neoclassicism

Known for: Painting, mosaic

Gino Severini (April 7, 1883 – February 26, 1966) was an Italian painter and a leading member of the Futurist movement. A signatory of the 1910 Manifesto of Futurist Painters, he acted as the principal link between the Italian Futurists and the Parisian avant-garde, and later moved through Cubism toward a classical, ordered style in painting and mosaic.

Early life

Severini was born in Cortona, Tuscany, the son of a junior court official. Expelled from school after a theft incident, he moved to Rome in 1899 and studied art while working as an accountant's assistant. He met Giacomo Balla, who introduced him to Divisionist painting technique, and Umberto Boccioni. In 1906 he settled in Paris, where he came into contact with the Neo-Impressionists, Modigliani, and later Picasso and Braque.

Futurism

Severini signed the Futurist painting manifestos of 1910 and promoted the movement's landmark 1912 exhibition in Paris. His Futurist paintings depict the energy of modern urban life — Parisian nightclubs and dancers, boulevards, and war — using Divisionist brushwork and Cubist fragmentation: works such as Dynamic Hieroglyphic of the Bal Tabarin (1912) and Armored Train in Action (1915) are among the movement's most celebrated images.

Later work

During the First World War Severini's style shifted toward Cubism, and in 1921 he published From Cubism to Classicism, an influential treatise advocating mathematical harmony and classical composition. He painted still lifes, portraits, and landscapes in a clear neo-classical manner, and from the 1920s received major commissions for frescoes and mosaics in churches and public buildings in Switzerland and Italy. He returned to a decorative, semi-abstract style in his final decades in Paris.
