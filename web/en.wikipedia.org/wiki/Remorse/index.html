Remorse

Remorse is the distressing emotion experienced by an individual who regrets actions which they have done in the past that they deem to be shameful, hurtful, or wrong. Remorse is closely allied to guilt and self-directed resentment. When a person regrets an earlier action or failure to act, it may be because of remorse or in response to various other consequences, including being punished for the act or omission.

Psychology

People who feel remorse have typically harmed another person or acted against their own moral standards. Studies suggest that remorse, unlike shame, is focused on the specific act rather than the self, and is associated with empathy, apology, confession and attempts at reparation. The absence of remorse is one of the diagnostic criteria associated with antisocial personality disorder and psychopathy.

Buyer's remorse is a specific form: the sense of regret after making a purchase, often a large one, when the buyer doubts the wisdom of the decision.

Legal aspects

In criminal law, remorse is often taken into account in sentencing: genuine remorse may mitigate a sentence, while its absence may aggravate it. Expressions of remorse are also relevant to parole decisions and restorative justice, where offenders may apologise to victims and make amends.

Religious views

In Christianity, remorse for sin (contrition) is a necessary element of repentance and penance. In Buddhism, remorse (kukkucca) unaccompanied by corrective action is considered an unwholesome hindrance, whereas moral shame and dread of wrongdoing (hiri and ottappa) are wholesome. Concepts of repentance — such as teshuvah in Judaism and tawba in Islam — involve regret, confession, restitution and resolve not to repeat the offence.
