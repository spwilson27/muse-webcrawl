Confederate States Army

The Confederate States Army, also called the Confederate Army or the Southern Army, was the military land force of the Confederate States of America during the American Civil War (1861–1865), fighting against the United States forces. On February 28, 1861, the Provisional Confederate Congress established a provisional volunteer army; the permanent regular army was authorized the following week, though it never grew large, and the great bulk of Confederate soldiers served in the Provisional Army of the Confederate States.

Organization

The army was organized into field armies, the most famous being the Army of Northern Virginia, commanded from 1862 by General Robert E. Lee, and the Army of Tennessee in the western theater. Conscription was introduced in April 1862 — the first general draft in American history — with exemptions and substitution provisions that proved controversial. Estimates of the total number who served range from about 750,000 to over one million men.

Course of the war

Confederate forces won notable victories at First Bull Run, Fredericksburg, and Chancellorsville, but suffered decisive defeats at Gettysburg and Vicksburg in July 1863. Increasingly outnumbered and undersupplied, Lee surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia at Appomattox Court House on April 9, 1865, and the remaining Confederate armies surrendered over the following weeks. The last Confederate general to surrender was Stand Watie, in June 1865.

Dissolution

The Confederate States Army ceased to exist with the collapse of the Confederacy in 1865. Its veterans later formed organizations such as the United Confederate Veterans, and its symbols and memory have remained subjects of public debate.
