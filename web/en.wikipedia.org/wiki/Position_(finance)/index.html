Position (finance)

Position

Field: Finance / trading
Types: Long, short; open, closed
Measured by: Net exposure

In finance, a position is the amount of a particular security, commodity or currency held by a person, company or institution, together with the commitments arising from contracts such as futures and options. A position represents an economic exposure to changes in the price of the underlying asset.

Long and short positions

An investor who owns an asset, and profits when its price rises, is said to be long the asset. An investor who sells an asset they do not own — borrowing it first, as in short selling — or who holds a contract that gains when the price falls, is short. Positions can also be created synthetically with derivatives: buying a call option is a long position in the underlying, while writing options creates more complex exposures.

Opening and closing

A position is open while the exposure remains in force and becomes closed when an offsetting trade is made — selling what was bought, or buying back what was sold short. For a portfolio, the net position in an asset is the sum of all long exposures minus all short exposures; traders also speak of gross position (the sum of absolute exposures) and of position limits set by exchanges and regulators to control risk.
