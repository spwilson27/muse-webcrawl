Puri


The Jagannath Temple, Puri, one of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites.

Puri is a coastal city and a municipality in the state of Odisha in eastern India. It is the district headquarters of Puri district and is situated on the Bay of Bengal, about 60 kilometres (37 mi) south of the state capital Bhubaneswar. It is one of the original Char Dham pilgrimage sites for Hindus and is famous for its Jagannath Temple and the annual Ratha Yatra (chariot festival).

History

Puri, known in ancient texts as Purushottama Kshetra and by other names including Shrikshetra and Nilachala, has been a sacred centre for over a thousand years. The present Jagannath Temple was begun in the 12th century under the Eastern Ganga king Anantavarman Chodaganga. The city was repeatedly invaded and the temple plundered in the medieval period, but worship continued, and Puri remained one of Hinduism's holiest cities, associated also with the saint Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, who lived there in the 16th century.

Ratha Yatra

The Ratha Yatra, held in June–July, draws over a million pilgrims. The deities Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra are carried in procession on enormous wooden chariots from the Jagannath Temple to the Gundicha Temple. The English word "juggernaut" derives from this festival. The temple's Mahaprasad food offering is cooked in earthen pots in what is often called one of the world's largest kitchens.

Geography and economy

Puri's economy is dominated by religious tourism; the great majority of the city's income is connected to the temple and its festivals. Its long sandy beach on the Bay of Bengal—including Golden Beach, awarded Blue Flag certification—is a popular resort, and the city is a centre for Odisha's crafts and the Pattachitra painting tradition.
