Guided bus

A guided bus is a bus capable of being steered by external means, usually small horizontal guide wheels running against kerbs on a dedicated track known as a guided busway. The technology allows buses to combine the high capacity and speed of a segregated alignment with the flexibility of ordinary road running at either end of the route.

Systems

The concept was developed in Germany (the Kerb Guided Bus system) and first applied at scale in Essen. Major installations include the O-Bahn Busway in Adelaide, Australia (opened 1986), the Cambridgeshire Guided Busway in England (opened 2011), and guided sections of the bus network in Nagoya, Japan, where the vehicle legally becomes a train on the guided section.

Advantages and criticism

Guided busways are narrower than ordinary bus lanes and cheaper to build than light rail, but critics note that capacity and ride quality may not match rail systems, and that the infrastructure serves only specially equipped buses.
