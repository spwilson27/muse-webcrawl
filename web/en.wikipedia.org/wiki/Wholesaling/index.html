Wholesaling

Wholesaling or distributing is the sale of goods or merchandise to retailers; to industrial, commercial, institutional or other professional business users; or to other wholesalers and related subordinated services. In general, it is the sale of goods in bulk to anyone other than a standard consumer.

Role in the supply chain

Wholesalers sit between manufacturers and retailers. They typically buy large quantities of goods, warehouse them, break bulk into smaller lots, and resell to retailers or business customers, providing services such as credit, assortment building, transport, and market information. By doing so they reduce the number of transactions needed between producers and sellers and absorb some of the risks of holding stock.

Types of wholesalers

Merchant wholesalers take title to the goods they handle and include full-service and limited-service wholesalers (such as cash-and-carry operators, truck jobbers, and drop shippers). Agents and brokers do not take title but negotiate purchases and sales for a commission. Manufacturers' sales branches and offices are wholesaling operations owned by producers. Cash-and-carry wholesalers, such as those serving small food retailers and tradespeople, require customers to pay in cash and transport goods themselves.

Taxation and regulation

In many jurisdictions wholesale sales are exempt from sales tax because the tax is collected at the final retail sale. Wholesale trade statistics are used as economic indicators; in the United States the Census Bureau publishes monthly wholesale trade data, and the sector employs millions of workers in distribution and logistics.
