Back focal length

In optics, back focal length (BFL) is the distance from the last (rear) optical surface of a lens or optical system to the focal plane where a sharp image of distant objects is formed. It differs from the effective focal length, which is measured from the system's rear principal plane.

Importance

Back focal length is critical in camera design: in a single-lens reflex camera, the flange focal distance must leave room for the reflex mirror to swing between the lens mount and the film or sensor. Retrofocus (inverted telephoto) designs create a back focal length longer than the effective focal length for wide-angle lenses, while telephoto designs shorten it. Mirrorless cameras, lacking a mirror, permit much shorter flange distances, enabling smaller lenses and adapters for SLR lenses.
