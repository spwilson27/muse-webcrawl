William Kentridge

William Kentridge (born 28 April 1955) is a South African artist best known for his prints, drawings, and animated films, especially the series of charcoal-drawn stop-motion films known as the Drawings for Projection (1989–2011). His work addresses the history of South Africa, apartheid, memory and time.

Early life and training

Kentridge was born in Johannesburg to Lithuanian-Jewish parents, both anti-apartheid lawyers. He studied at the University of the Witwatersrand and the Johannesburg Art Foundation, then trained in mime and theatre in Paris.

Work

Kentridge animates by drawing, erasing and redrawing charcoal scenes, leaving ghost traces of earlier states. His recurring alter egos—the pinstriped businessman Soho Eckstein and the artist Felix Teitlebaum—feature in the Drawings for Projection. He has directed major opera productions, including The Magic Flute, The Nose and Alban Berg's Wozzeck and Lulu, and created the 2016 Roman mural Triumphs and Laments along the Tiber.
