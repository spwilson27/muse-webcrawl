Limehouse Basin is a body of water in Limehouse, in the London Borough of Tower Hamlets, connecting the Regent's Canal to the River Thames. Opened in 1820 as the Regent's Canal Dock, it allowed seagoing vessels to transfer cargo to canal barges for transport into the English canal network.

History

The dock was built by the Regent's Canal Company between the Thames at Limehouse Reach and the canal's eastern end. Coal from the north-east, timber and grain were transshipped here; a hydraulic accumulator tower (1869) powered the dock's machinery. The basin passed to the British Waterways Board after nationalisation in 1948, and commercial traffic declined as freight left the canals.

Redevelopment

From the 1980s the surrounding Limehouse area was redeveloped with housing; the basin itself became a marina with residential and leisure moorings, managed today by the Canal & River Trust. Limehouse Basin Marina is a popular staging point for boats transiting between the Thames and the canal system. The Limehouse Cut links nearby to the River Lea.
