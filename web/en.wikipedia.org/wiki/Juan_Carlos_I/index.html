Juan Carlos I (born 5 January 1938) is a member of the Spanish royal family who reigned as King of Spain from 22 November 1975 until his abdication on 19 June 2014. Designated as successor by the dictator Francisco Franco, he instead presided over Spain's transition to democracy, and famously helped defeat the attempted military coup of 23 February 1981.

Early life

Juan Carlos was born in Rome, where the Spanish royal family lived in exile, the son of Infante Juan, Count of Barcelona, and Princess María de las Mercedes of Bourbon-Two Sicilies. He first came to Spain in 1948, after Franco and his father agreed he would be educated there. He underwent military training at the academies of all three services and studied law and politics at the University of Madrid. In 1962 he married Princess Sophia of Greece in Athens; they have three children.

Accession and the transition to democracy

In 1969 Franco designated Juan Carlos as his successor and future king, bypassing his father. He was sworn in as king two days after Franco's death on 20 November 1975. Contrary to the expectations of Francoists, the king appointed the reformist Adolfo Suárez as prime minister in 1976, oversaw the Political Reform Act that dismantled the Francoist Cortes by democratic means, and supported the legalisation of political parties including the Communists. Democratic elections were held in June 1977, and the new Spanish Constitution was approved by referendum in 1978, establishing a parliamentary constitutional monarchy.

The 23-F coup

On 23 February 1981, Civil Guard officer Antonio Tejero seized Congress during an attempted coup. Appearing on television in the early hours of 24 February in uniform as captain general of the armed forces, Juan Carlos ordered the military to stand down and defend the constitutional order. The coup collapsed, and the king's intervention cemented his standing as guarantor of Spanish democracy.

Later reign and abdication

For most of his reign Juan Carlos was one of Europe's most popular monarchs, credited with modernising Spain and representing it abroad. His standing suffered late in his reign from the Nóos corruption case involving his son-in-law, a controversial elephant-hunting trip to Botswana in 2012 during the financial crisis, and declining health. On 2 June 2014 he announced his abdication in favour of his son, who succeeded as Felipe VI. After further financial investigations into his affairs became public, Juan Carlos left Spain in August 2020 and has since lived in Abu Dhabi, in the United Arab Emirates, while retaining the title of king emeritus.
