Courmayeur

Courmayeur (Arpitan: Cortemayœr) is a town and comune in northern Italy, in the autonomous region of Aosta Valley. It lies at an elevation of about 1,224 metres (4,016 ft) at the foot of the southern side of Mont Blanc, the highest point in the Alps and western Europe, and has a population of roughly 2,600.

Geography and tourism

Courmayeur shares administration of Mont Blanc with its neighbouring municipality of Saint-Gervais-les-Bains in France — a long-running boundary question, since the summit itself is claimed by both countries. The town is the Italian terminus of the Mont Blanc Tunnel, which connects it to Chamonix in France, and it lies on the route of the Tour du Mont Blanc long-distance walking trail.

A renowned ski resort and mountaineering centre since the nineteenth century, Courmayeur is home to one of the oldest alpine guide societies, the Società delle Guide Alpine, founded in 1850. The Skyway Monte Bianco cable car, opened in 2015, ascends from the village of La Palud to Punta Helbronner at 3,466 metres, with rotating panoramic cabins.

History

The area was settled in Roman times on the road over the Little St Bernard Pass. Courmayeur became a fashionable spa town in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries thanks to its mineral springs, before winter sports and alpinism came to dominate its economy.
