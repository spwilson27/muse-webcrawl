Soil quality

Soil quality is the capacity of a soil to function, within ecosystem and land-use boundaries, to sustain plant and animal productivity, maintain or enhance water and air quality, and support human health and habitation. The concept overlaps with "soil health", which emphasises the soil as a living system.

Indicators

Soil quality is assessed through physical, chemical and biological indicators:

  • Physical: texture, structure, aggregate stability, bulk density, infiltration and water-holding capacity.
  • Chemical: pH, nutrient status (N, P, K), cation-exchange capacity, salinity and soil organic carbon.
  • Biological: microbial biomass, earthworm counts, respiration, enzyme activity and diversity of soil fauna.

Assessment

Frameworks such as the Cornell Comprehensive Assessment of Soil Health and the USDA Soil Quality Test Kit combine indicator scores into an overall index, weighted by management goals. Because a soil's inherent properties (set by its parent material and horizons) differ from its dynamic, management-sensitive properties, quality is judged relative to a soil's potential.

Degradation and improvement

Erosion, compaction, salinisation, contamination and organic-matter loss degrade quality worldwide. Improvement strategies include adding organic amendments, cover crops, diverse rotations, reduced tillage and controlled grazing.
