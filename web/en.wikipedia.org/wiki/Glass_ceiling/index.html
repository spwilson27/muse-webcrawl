Glass ceiling

A glass ceiling is a metaphor for an invisible barrier that prevents women and minorities from advancing to senior positions in organizations, despite qualifications and achievements.

Glass ceiling
Concept: Workplace inequality
Popularised: 1980s, Wall Street Journal 1986
Related: Glass escalator, bamboo ceiling, sticky floor

Origin

The term was used by Marilyn Loden in 1978 and popularised by a 1986 Wall Street Journal article. In 1991 the U.S. Department of Labor defined it as artificial barriers based on attitudinal or organizational bias, and Congress created the Glass Ceiling Commission.

Evidence and discussion

Studies cite under-representation of women among Fortune 500 CEOs and board members, pay gaps, and promotion patterns. Related concepts include the "glass cliff" (women promoted in crises), "sticky floor", and intersectional barriers often discussed alongside feminist movements.
