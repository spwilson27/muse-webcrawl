Genotype–phenotype distinction

The genotype–phenotype distinction is fundamental in genetics. The genotype is the complete set of genetic material inherited by an organism, while the phenotype is the set of observable characteristics resulting from the interaction of the genotype with the environment. The terms were introduced by the Danish botanist Wilhelm Johannsen in 1909.

Significance

The distinction clarifies that inheritance transmits genotypes, not phenotypes: acquired characteristics, such as a suntan or a learned skill, are not passed to offspring because they do not alter the genotype. Two organisms with the same genotype may differ phenotypically because of environmental influences, developmental noise, or epigenetic factors.

Genotype–phenotype map

The relationship between genotype and phenotype is often complex: a single gene may affect many traits (pleiotropy), and a single trait may be influenced by many genes (polygeny). Mapping this relationship is a central problem in evolutionary biology and medicine.
