Inyo National Forest

Inyo National Forest is a United States National Forest covering parts of the eastern Sierra Nevada of California and extending into Nevada. Established on May 25, 1907, the forest covers about 1.9 million acres (770,000 hectares) and includes nine designated wilderness areas comprising over 800,000 acres. Its name comes from a Paiute word meaning "dwelling place of the great spirit".

Geography

The forest stretches from the crest of the Sierra Nevada — including Mount Whitney, the highest point in the contiguous United States at 14,505 feet (4,421 m) — eastward across the Owens Valley to the White and Inyo Mountains. The Ancient Bristlecone Pine Forest within it protects some of the oldest living trees on Earth, including the bristlecone pine known as Methuselah, more than 4,800 years old. The forest also includes Mono Lake's southern shore area, the Mammoth Lakes basin, and parts of the John Muir and Ansel Adams Wildernesses.

Recreation

Inyo National Forest is one of the most visited national forests in the United States, drawing several million visitors a year for hiking, fishing, skiing at Mammoth Mountain, and climbing in the Whitney Portal area. Wilderness permits are required for overnight use in many areas, and a quota system operates during the summer season.

Administration

The forest is administered by the U.S. Forest Service, with headquarters in Bishop, California, and ranger districts in communities including Lee Vining, Mammoth Lakes, Lone Pine, and Independence.
