Water of Leith

The Water of Leith is the principal river flowing through Edinburgh, Scotland. It rises in the Pentland Hills at Harperrig Reservoir and flows for about 35 kilometres (22 miles) through the city, passing Balerno, Currie, the Dean Village and Stockbridge, before entering the Firth of Forth at the port of Leith.

History

The river once powered more than 70 mills producing flour, paper, textiles and snuff, and its valley was a focus of industry as Edinburgh grew. The steep gorge at the Dean Village is spanned by Thomas Telford's Dean Bridge (1831). As industry declined, the river was cleaned up, and its banks became valued greenspace.

The Walkway

The Water of Leith Walkway follows the river for some 13 miles from Balerno to Leith and is popular with walkers and cyclists; a visitor centre stands at Slateford. The river supports brown trout, grayling and birdlife including herons, dippers and kingfishers, despite running through the heart of the capital.
