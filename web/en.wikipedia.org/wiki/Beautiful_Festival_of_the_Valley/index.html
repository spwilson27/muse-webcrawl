Beautiful Festival of the Valley

The Beautiful Festival of the Valley (Egyptian: ḥb nfr n jnt) was one of the most important annual festivals of ancient Thebes, celebrated from the Middle Kingdom into the Roman period. During the festival the sacred bark of the god Amun of Karnak crossed the Nile to the temples of the west bank, where families feasted in the tomb chapels of their ancestors — making it simultaneously a state procession of the gods and a popular feast of the dead.

The celebration

The statues of the Theban Triad — Amun, his consort Mut and their son Khonsu — were carried in portable barks from Karnak Temple, placed aboard the great river barque Userhet, and ferried across the Nile amid music, flower garlands and crowds. On the west bank the procession visited the royal mortuary temples, resting at Deir el-Bahari, where Amun was united in ritual with the goddess Hathor. The night spent on the west bank — the climax of the festival — was when the god was believed to mediate between the living and the dead.

Role of the people

Ordinary Thebans joined the procession and then celebrated in the courtyards and chapels of their family tombs in the necropolis, eating, drinking, singing and offering bouquets to their ancestors. Tomb paintings of the New Kingdom — for example in the tombs of Nakht and Rekhmire — preserve lively scenes of these banquets, musicians and offerings. The festival declined with the fading of pharaonic religion in late antiquity.
