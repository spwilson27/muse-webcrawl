Nuclear power in Armenia

Nuclear power in Armenia is provided by a single plant, the Metsamor Nuclear Power Plant (Armenian Nuclear Power Plant), located near the town of Metsamor about 36 kilometres west of the capital Yerevan. The plant supplies roughly 30–40% of Armenia's electricity.

The Metsamor plant

The plant comprises two Soviet-designed VVER-440 pressurized water reactors. Unit 1 entered service in 1976 and Unit 2 in 1980. Both units were shut down following the 1988 Spitak earthquake, which caused no serious damage to the plant but prompted precautionary closure. Facing a severe energy crisis after independence, Armenia restarted Unit 2 in 1995; Unit 1 remained shut down.

Safety debate

Metsamor's VVER-440/270 design is an early model lacking a full containment structure, and the plant lies in a seismically active region. The European Union and neighbouring Turkey have repeatedly pressed Armenia to close it, while Armenia — blockaded on two borders and dependent on imported gas — regards the plant as essential to energy security. With Russian assistance, Unit 2's operating licence has been extended through modernization programmes, with operation envisaged into the 2030s.

Future plans

Armenia has long discussed replacing Metsamor with a new plant, including consideration of small modular reactors, and has cooperated with Russia, the United States, and the IAEA on feasibility and safety work.
