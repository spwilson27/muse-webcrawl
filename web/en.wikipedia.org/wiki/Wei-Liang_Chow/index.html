Wei-Liang Chow

Born: 1 October 1911, Shanghai, China
Died: 10 August 1995, Baltimore, Maryland, U.S.
Fields: Algebraic geometry
Institutions: National Tsing Hua University, Johns Hopkins University
Known for: Chow ring, Chow's theorem, Chow variety

Wei-Liang Chow (Chinese: 周煒良; 1 October 1911 – 10 August 1995) was a Chinese mathematician born in Shanghai, known for his work in algebraic geometry. He studied in the United States and in Germany, where he worked with Bartel Leendert van der Waerden and published with him a fundamental paper on intersection theory in 1937.

Career

Chow studied at the University of Chicago before going to Leipzig, receiving his doctorate in 1936. He returned to China to teach at National Tsing Hua University and later at National Central University, spending the war years in China. In 1947 he moved to the United States, working at the Institute for Advanced Study and then joining Johns Hopkins University in 1948, where he remained until his retirement in 1977, serving as department chair for several years.

Contributions

Chow introduced several concepts that now bear his name. The Chow ring of an algebraic variety generalises the ring of cycle classes used in intersection theory; Chow's lemma and Chow's theorem — that a closed analytic subspace of complex projective space is algebraic — are basic tools in algebraic geometry. The Chow variety (or Chow form) parametrises algebraic cycles, and his work with van der Waerden on associated forms founded much of the modern theory. He also worked on Abelian varieties and on the arithmetic of algebraic varieties in the tradition of André Weil.
