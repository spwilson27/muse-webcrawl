The Toluid Civil War was a war of succession fought between 1260 and 1264 among the descendants of Tolui, the youngest son of Genghis Khan, following the death of the Great Khan Möngke in 1259. The rival claimants were Möngke's brothers: Kublai Khan, based in China, and Ariq Böke, based at the traditional capital of Karakorum in Mongolia.

Background

Möngke Khan died in August 1259 while campaigning in Sichuan, leaving no designated successor. In the spring of 1260, two rival kurultais (assemblies) elected two different khans: Kublai was proclaimed at his base of Shangdu (Xanadu) in northern China, while Ariq Böke was elected at Karakorum with the support of much of the imperial family, the Mongol heartland, and the house of Ögedei.

Course of the war

Kublai controlled the rich resources of North China and cut off food supplies to Karakorum, which depended on imports. After early campaigning in 1260–1261, including a victory near Lake Shimultai, Kublai's position strengthened. Ariq Böke's alliance fractured when his supporter Alghu, khan of the Chagatai Khanate, defected; weakened by famine and defections, Ariq Böke surrendered to Kublai at Shangdu in August 1264 and died in captivity two years later.

Consequences

Although Kublai was victorious and went on to found the Yuan dynasty in China, the war — fought alongside the concurrent Berke–Hulagu war between the Golden Horde and the Ilkhanate — completed the effective fragmentation of the unified Mongol Empire into four largely independent khanates: the Yuan dynasty, the Ilkhanate, the Chagatai Khanate, and the Golden Horde.
