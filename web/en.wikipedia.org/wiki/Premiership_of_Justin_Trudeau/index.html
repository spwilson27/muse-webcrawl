The premiership of Justin Trudeau began on 4 November 2015, when Justin Trudeau and the first members of his cabinet were sworn in by Governor General David Johnston, and ended on 14 March 2025, when he was succeeded by Mark Carney. Trudeau, leader of the Liberal Party of Canada since 2013, won a majority government in the 2015 federal election and minority governments in 2019 and 2021. He is the son of former prime minister Pierre Elliott Trudeau and the second-longest-serving Liberal prime minister of the 21st century.

First term (2015–2019)

Trudeau's first cabinet was noted for being gender-balanced. Early initiatives included the Canada Child Benefit, a middle-class tax cut offset by a new top rate, withdrawal of Canadian combat jets from the Iraq/Syria air campaign, an inquiry into missing and murdered Indigenous women, and the legalization of assisted dying. Canada welcomed some 25,000 Syrian refugees in his first months in office. Despite a campaign promise that 2015 would be the last election under first-past-the-post, the government abandoned electoral reform in 2017. In 2018 Canada legalized recreational cannabis nationwide — the first G7 country to do so. Trade policy was dominated by the renegotiation of NAFTA into the USMCA, and the government bought the Trans Mountain pipeline to ensure its expansion. The SNC-Lavalin affair in 2019, involving alleged pressure on Attorney General Jody Wilson-Raybould, significantly damaged the government.

Second term (2019–2021)

Reduced to a minority in 2019, the government's tenure was dominated from March 2020 by the COVID-19 pandemic, during which it delivered large-scale income supports such as the Canada Emergency Response Benefit (CERB), procured vaccines, and ran large deficits. The WE Charity scandal and the prorogation of Parliament in 2020 drew opposition criticism. A federal carbon-pricing framework survived constitutional challenges and was upheld by the Supreme Court in 2021.

Third term (2021–2025)

After the snap election of September 2021 returned another Liberal minority, Trudeau's government concluded a confidence-and-supply agreement with the New Democratic Party in March 2022, enabling policies including a national dental care program and steps toward pharmacare. In February 2022 the government invoked the Emergencies Act for the first time to end the "Freedom Convoy" protests occupying Ottawa. Later challenges included high inflation and housing costs, sharply declining approval ratings, and pressure following the loss of a Toronto-area by-election in 2024. Amid a caucus revolt and the resignation of Finance Minister Chrystia Freeland in December 2024, Trudeau announced on 6 January 2025 that he would resign as prime minister and Liberal leader once a successor was chosen.

Foreign policy and legacy

Internationally, Trudeau promoted multilateralism, gender equality, and climate action, signing the Paris Agreement and attending successive COP summits; relations with China deteriorated sharply over the detention of Meng Wanzhou and the "two Michaels". Assessments of his legacy commonly cite cannabis legalization, the child benefit's reduction of child poverty, COVID-era supports, and progress on some Indigenous files, balanced against unmet commitments on electoral reform, housing affordability, and emissions targets.
