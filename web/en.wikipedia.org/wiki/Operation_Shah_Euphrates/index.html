Operation Shah Euphrates

Operation Shah Euphrates (Turkish: Şah Fırat Operasyonu) was an operation by the Turkish military to relocate the Tomb of Suleyman Shah in Syria, conducted on the night of 21–22 February 2015. The tomb, located near the village of Karakozak on the Euphrates River in Aleppo Governorate, was an exclave of Turkish territory under the 1921 Treaty of Ankara and was guarded by a small contingent of Turkish soldiers.

Background

By early 2015, the Syrian Civil War and the advance of the Islamic State (ISIL) in northern Syria had left the tomb site and its garrison increasingly exposed. The Turkish government decided to evacuate the guards and relocate the remains of Suleyman Shah — the grandfather of Osman I, founder of the Ottoman Empire — to a safer location.

The operation

Some 572 Turkish troops, with tanks and armoured vehicles, crossed into Syria in two columns. One force secured a new site near the village of Esmeler (Eşme), close to the Turkish border, while the other reached the tomb, evacuated its 38 guards, and removed the remains. The old tomb structure was demolished to prevent its misuse. The operation was completed without combat casualties, though one soldier died in an accident.

Reactions

The Syrian government condemned the incursion as a violation of its sovereignty, while the Turkish government maintained the relocation was temporary and consistent with its treaty rights. The new tomb site at Eşme lies within sight of the Turkish border.
