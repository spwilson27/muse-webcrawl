Chicago is a 2002 American musical black comedy crime film based on the 1976 stage musical of the same name, which itself was based on a 1926 play by Maurine Dallas Watkins. Directed by Rob Marshall in his feature directorial debut, the film explores the themes of celebrity, scandal and corruption in Jazz Age Chicago, telling the story of two rival murderesses on death row who become media sensations as their lawyer manipulates public opinion.

Plot

Aspiring vaudevillian Roxie Hart (Renée Zellweger) murders her lover and is imprisoned alongside vaudeville star Velma Kelly (Catherine Zeta-Jones), a double-murderer. Both hire the slick lawyer Billy Flynn (Richard Gere), who turns their trials into tabloid circuses, staging Roxie's trial as a vaudeville performance. Musical numbers unfold as Roxie's fantasies of stardom, culminating in her acquittal and a duet with Velma.

Reception

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing over $306 million worldwide. It received thirteen Academy Award nominations and won six, including Best Picture — the first musical to win the award since Oliver! in 1968. Catherine Zeta-Jones won Best Supporting Actress for her role as Velma Kelly. The film is credited with helping to revive the film musical genre in Hollywood.
