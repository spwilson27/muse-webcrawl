Beetles are insects that form the order Coleoptera (/koʊliːˈɒptərə/), in the superorder Holometabola. Their front pair of wings are hardened into wing-cases, elytra, distinguishing them from most other insects. The Coleoptera, with about 400,000 described species, is the largest of all orders, constituting almost 40% of described insects and 25% of all known animal species; new species are discovered frequently, with estimates suggesting the total number of extant species ranges into the millions. The biologist J. B. S. Haldane is said to have remarked that the Creator, if he exists, must have "an inordinate fondness for beetles."

Anatomy

Like all insects, beetles have three body segments (head, thorax, abdomen), six legs, and antennae. The hardened elytra meet in a straight line down the back and shelter the folded membranous hindwings used for flight. Beetles have chewing mouthparts. The largest are the Goliath beetles of Africa, among the heaviest insects at up to 100 g, and the titan beetle of South America, which can exceed 16 cm in length; the smallest, featherwing beetles, are under 1 mm.

Life cycle

Beetles undergo complete metamorphosis: egg, grub-like larva, pupa, and adult. Larvae usually have hardened heads and chewing mouthparts, and many feed in concealed habitats — soil, wood, carrion, dung, or plant tissue — while adults may have quite different diets.

Ecology and human interaction

Beetles occupy nearly every habitat except the oceans and polar regions. Ladybirds are valued predators of aphids; dung beetles recycle nutrients; longhorn and bark beetles are major forest pests; and stored-product beetles damage grain. Famous groups include scarabs (sacred in ancient Egypt), stag beetles, fireflies — beetles despite the name, producing light by bioluminescence — and click beetles. Some species, such as the Colorado potato beetle and boll weevil, have shaped agricultural history.

Classification

The four living suborders are Polyphaga (the great majority, including scarabs, ladybirds, longhorns and weevils), Adephaga (ground and water beetles), Archostemata, and the tiny Myxophaga. Weevils (Curculionidae) form the largest family of animals, with tens of thousands of species.
