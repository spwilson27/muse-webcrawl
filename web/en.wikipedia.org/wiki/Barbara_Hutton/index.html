Barbara Hutton

Barbara Woolworth Hutton (14 November 1912 – 11 May 1979) was an American debutante, socialite and heiress. Granddaughter of Frank Winfield Woolworth, founder of the F. W. Woolworth Company five-and-dime empire, she inherited one of the largest fortunes in the United States as a young woman. Dubbed the "Poor Little Rich Girl" by the press — a phrase from a popular song — she became famous for her lavish spending, her art and jewellery collections, and her seven marriages, including to actor Cary Grant.

Early life

Hutton was born in New York, the daughter of Edna Woolworth and stockbroker Franklyn Laws Hutton. Her mother died by suicide when Barbara was four; her father later co-founded the investment firm E. F. Hutton. On her 21st birthday she came into a fortune estimated in the tens of millions of dollars, one of the largest inheritances of the era, and gave a lavish debutante ball during the Great Depression that drew public criticism.

Marriages and life

Her seven husbands included Georgian prince Alexis Mdivani; Count Kurt von Haugwitz-Hardenberg Reventlow, father of her only child, racing driver Lance Reventlow; Cary Grant (the press dubbed them "Cash and Cary"); Russian prince Igor Troubetzkoy; Dominican diplomat and playboy Porfirio Rubirosa; German tennis champion Gottfried von Cramm; and Prince Pierre Doan, a marriage made partly for his title. She collected jade and Fabergé, built palaces in Mexico and Tangier, and renounced U.S. citizenship in the 1930s before later resuming it.

Later years and death

After Lance Reventlow's death in a plane crash in 1972, Hutton's health and fortune declined sharply; she died in Beverly Hills in 1979, reportedly with only a few thousand dollars left of her inheritance. Her life has been the subject of books and the 1987 television film Poor Little Rich Girl: The Barbara Hutton Story.
