The Gion Matsuri (祇園祭) is one of the most famous festivals in Japan, held annually during the month of July in Kyoto. It is associated with Yasaka Shrine (formerly called Gion Shrine) in the Gion district. The festival originated in 869 as a purification ritual (goryō-e) intended to appease the gods during an epidemic.

History

In 869, plague spread through Kyoto, and the emperor ordered a ritual at Yasaka Shrine: sixty-six halberds, one for each province of Japan, were erected at Shinsen-en garden to drive away pestilence. The rite was repeated whenever epidemics struck, and by the tenth century it had become an annual event. The towering decorated floats known as yamaboko evolved during the Muromachi period, financed by Kyoto's wealthy merchant class.

Yamaboko Junko

The festival's climax is the Yamaboko Junko procession of floats, held on 17 July (Saki Matsuri) and, since 2014, a second procession on 24 July (Ato Matsuri). The floats are of two types: the massive hoko, some weighing up to twelve tonnes and carrying musicians, and the smaller yama, many bearing sacred pine trees and effigies. The floats are decorated with elaborate tapestries, some imported from Europe and Asia centuries ago. In 2009 the Yamaboko procession was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Yoiyama

On the three evenings before each procession, the streets around Shijō and Karasuma are closed to traffic for yoiyama, when the floats are displayed lit by lanterns and crowds in yukata fill the streets.
