Nine-Power Treaty

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Delegates at the Washington Conference, where the treaty was signed

The Nine-Power Treaty was a treaty signed on 6 February 1922 by all of the attendees to the Washington Naval Conference: Belgium, China, France, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands, Portugal, the United Kingdom, and the United States. It affirmed the sovereignty and territorial integrity of China as per the Open Door Policy.

Background

The treaty grew out of the Washington Conference of 1921–22, convened to limit naval armaments and to reduce tensions in the Pacific. The Open Door Policy, first articulated by U.S. Secretary of State John Hay in 1899, held that all nations should enjoy equal trading rights in China and that no power should seek exclusive spheres of influence at China's expense. The Nine-Power Treaty converted that informal policy into a formal multilateral commitment.

Provisions

The signatories agreed to respect the sovereignty, independence, and territorial and administrative integrity of China; to give China the fullest opportunity to develop and maintain an effective and stable government; to use their influence to establish equal opportunity for trade for all nations throughout China; and to refrain from taking advantage of conditions in China to seek special rights or privileges that would abridge the rights of other states.

Legacy

The treaty had no enforcement mechanism and no sanctions for violation. It was effectively repudiated when Japan invaded Manchuria in 1931, an act the United States answered with the Stimson Doctrine of non-recognition. The Nine-Power Treaty remained nominally in force until it was superseded by the settlement following the Second World War.
