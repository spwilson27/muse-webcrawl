Republic Pictures

Republic Pictures Corporation was an American motion picture production and distribution company in operation from 1935 to 1967. Founded by Herbert J. Yates through the consolidation of several Poverty Row studios — including Monogram Pictures, Mascot Pictures and Liberty Pictures — Republic became best known for westerns and cliffhanger serials, while occasionally producing prestigious films such as John Ford's The Quiet Man (1952).

History

Yates, whose Consolidated Film Industries processed film for the major studios, merged the small studios that owed him money into Republic in 1935. The company built its business on B-movies: Gene Autry and later Roy Rogers made it the leading producer of singing-cowboy westerns, and its serials — including Adventures of Captain Marvel (1941), widely considered among the finest chapter plays — set standards for action choreography and miniature effects through its Lydecker brothers team.

Notable productions

Republic occasionally ventured upmarket, distributing Orson Welles's Macbeth (1948) and producing Ford's Rio Grande (1950) and The Quiet Man, which won Ford his fourth Academy Award for Best Director. It was also the home studio of John Wayne for much of his early career, including Sands of Iwo Jima (1949). In the 1950s the studio embraced Trucolor and widescreen "Naturama" productions, but television eroded the market for its programme pictures.

Decline and legacy

Yates ended film production in 1959 and sold the studio lot (now part of CBS Studio Center in Studio City); the corporation continued as a diversified company until 1967. Republic's library later passed through several owners and is now largely controlled by Paramount Pictures (via Melange Pictures/ViacomCBS), while the Republic Pictures name has been revived as a label. The studio's serials and westerns remain influential, notably on George Lucas, whose Star Wars and Indiana Jones films homage its chapter-play structure.
