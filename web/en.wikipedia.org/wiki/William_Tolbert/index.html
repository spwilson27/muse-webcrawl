William Tolbert

William R. Tolbert Jr.
Born: 13 May 1913, Bensonville, Liberia
Died: 12 April 1980 (aged 66), Monrovia, Liberia
Office: 20th President of Liberia (1971–1980)

William Richard Tolbert Jr. (13 May 1913 – 12 April 1980) was a Liberian politician who served as the 20th president of Liberia from 1971 until his assassination in 1980. A member of the Americo-Liberian elite and the True Whig Party, he had served as vice president under William Tubman for 19 years before succeeding him on Tubman's death.

Presidency

Tolbert's administration initially pursued liberalization and modest reforms under slogans such as "Total Involvement for Higher Heights" and the "Mat to Mattress" development programme. He sought to open political space, reduce corruption, and improve conditions for Liberia's indigenous majority, but he faced mounting economic difficulties, including the effects of the global oil crises and falling export prices. In April 1979, proposed increases in the price of rice—Liberia's staple food—triggered the Monrovia rice riots, in which dozens of protesters were killed by security forces, badly damaging the regime's legitimacy.

Assassination

On 12 April 1980, Tolbert was murdered in the Executive Mansion during a coup d'état led by Master Sergeant Samuel Doe and a group of enlisted men of indigenous origin. The coup ended more than a century of Americo-Liberian political dominance and ushered in a period of military rule and instability.
