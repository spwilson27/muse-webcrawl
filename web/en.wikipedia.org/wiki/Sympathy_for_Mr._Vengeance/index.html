Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance

Directed by: Park Chan-wook

Starring: Shin Ha-kyun, Song Kang-ho, Bae Doona

Release date: 29 March 2002

Country: South Korea

Language: Korean

Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance (Korean: 복수는 나의 것) is a 2002 South Korean neo-noir crime thriller film directed by Park Chan-wook. It is the first installment in Park's thematically linked Vengeance Trilogy, followed by Oldboy (2003) and Lady Vengeance (2005).

Plot

Ryu, a deaf-mute steel worker, needs money for a kidney transplant for his dying sister. Unable to donate his own kidney because of a blood type mismatch, he turns to black-market organ dealers, who rob him. When a legitimate donor is finally found, Ryu has lost both his job and his money. His anarchist girlfriend Yeong-mi persuades him to kidnap the young daughter of his former boss, Park Dong-jin, to raise the ransom. The kidnapping unravels when Ryu's sister, learning of the crime, kills herself, and the child accidentally drowns. Both Ryu and Park set out on intertwined paths of revenge that end in mutual destruction.

Reception

The film was a box-office disappointment in South Korea on release but has since gained critical acclaim and a cult following, particularly after the international success of Oldboy. Critics praised its austere visual style, dark humor and unflinching depiction of the futility of revenge.
