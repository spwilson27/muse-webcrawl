Pope Callixtus II (c. 1065 – 13 December 1124), born Guy of Burgundy, was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 2 February 1119 until his death in 1124. Born into the powerful House of Ivrea as a son of Count William I of Burgundy, he served for three decades as Archbishop of Vienne before his election at Cluny following the death of Gelasius II.

Pontificate

Callixtus II is best known for concluding the Investiture Controversy with the Holy Roman Emperor Henry V through the Concordat of Worms (1122), under which the emperor renounced investiture with ring and staff while the Church conceded elections in the emperor's presence in Germany and investiture with temporal regalia. In 1123 he presided over the First Lateran Council, which ratified the concordat and issued canons on clerical discipline. He also issued the bull Sicut Judaeis (1120), which set out the Church's official position protecting Jews from forced conversion, violence and desecration of their cemeteries, a bull reissued by many later popes. He promoted the pilgrimage to Santiago de Compostela; the Codex Calixtinus is named in his honour, and he granted Compostela archiepiscopal status.
