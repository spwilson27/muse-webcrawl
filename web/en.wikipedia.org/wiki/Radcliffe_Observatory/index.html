LocationWoodstock Road, Oxford, England

ArchitectHenry Keene; James Wyatt
Operated1773–1934 (Oxford); Pretoria to 1978

The Radcliffe Observatory was the astronomical observatory of the University of Oxford from 1773 until 1934, when its work transferred to South Africa. Its building in north Oxford, centred on the octagonal Tower of the Winds, is now part of Green Templeton College.

History

Founded with funds from the estate of physician John Radcliffe, the observatory was built between 1772 and 1794 to designs by Henry Keene and James Wyatt. The tower is modelled on the ancient Tower of the Winds in Athens and is topped by a figure of Atlas carrying a celestial globe.

Move to South Africa

By the 20th century, Oxford's weather and light made observations difficult, and the Radcliffe trustees funded a new observatory at Pretoria, South Africa, equipped with a 74-inch reflector — for a time the largest telescope in the southern hemisphere. The Oxford building was sold and used for medical purposes before becoming part of Green Templeton College.

Legacy

Radcliffe observers produced important star catalogues, and the observatory's name continues at the South African Astronomical Observatory's Radcliffe telescope tradition.
