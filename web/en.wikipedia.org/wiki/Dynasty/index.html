Dynasty

A dynasty is a sequence of rulers from the same family, usually in the context of a monarchical system, but sometimes also appearing in republics. Dynasties rule for generations, with power passed hereditarily, often from parent to child.

Etymology

The word derives from the Greek dynasteia (δυναστεία), meaning dominion or lordship, from dynastes, a ruler. The term is commonly applied to the ruling houses of China, Egypt, Persia, and Europe.

Examples

Famous dynasties include the Han, Tang, Ming and Qing dynasties of China; the Ptolemaic dynasty of Egypt; the Habsburgs and Bourbons of Europe; and the Tokugawa shogunate of Japan. Political families in republics, such as the Kennedys in the United States or the Nehru–Gandhi family in India, are also sometimes described as dynasties.

Dynastic cycle

Chinese historiography describes a dynastic cycle in which dynasties rise through virtue and fall through corruption, losing the Mandate of Heaven.
