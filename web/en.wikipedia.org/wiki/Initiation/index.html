Initiation

Initiation is a rite of passage marking entrance into, or acceptance into, a group, society, or new status. Initiations occur in religious communities (such as mystery religions and churches), secret societies and fraternities, schools, gangs, military units, and traditional societies marking the transition from childhood to adulthood. The word comes from the Latin initium, "beginning."

Coming-of-age initiations are found in cultures worldwide.

Structure

Drawing on the work of Arnold van Gennep, scholars commonly analyse initiation as a three-stage rite of passage: separation from the previous state, a liminal (threshold) period of testing, instruction, or seclusion, and incorporation into the new status. Mircea Eliade emphasised the pattern of symbolic death and rebirth in traditional initiations. Trials may include ordeals, the transmission of secret knowledge, new names or clothing, scarification or other marks, and oaths of loyalty or secrecy.

Examples

Religious initiations include baptism and confirmation in Christianity, the Eleusinian Mysteries of ancient Greece, upanayana in Hinduism, and bar and bat mitzvah in Judaism. Societal examples range from Aboriginal Australian walkabouts and Maasai age-set ceremonies to university fraternity pledging — where initiation practices, when they involve dangerous hazing, have been widely condemned and regulated. Freemasonry and similar orders admit members through graded initiation rituals.
