Ogata Kenzan
Born1663, Kyoto, Japan
Died1743, Edo (Tokyo)
Known forPottery, ceramics (Rinpa)

Ogata Kenzan (1663–1743), born Ogata Shinsei, was a Japanese potter and painter of the Edo period, and, with his elder brother Ogata Kōrin, a leading figure of the Rinpa school. He is celebrated for stoneware that integrates ceramic form, painting and calligraphy.

Life and work

The Ogata brothers were born into a wealthy Kyoto textile merchant family serving the imperial court. Kenzan turned to pottery relatively late, establishing a kiln at Narutaki in northwest Kyoto in 1699, where he took the name Kenzan ("northwest kiln"). He later worked in central Kyoto and, in his final years, in Edo. Kōrin frequently painted designs for Kenzan's ceramics, while Kenzan decorated works in Kōrin's style after his brother's death.

Style

Kenzan's wares favour warm, freely brushed designs — autumn grasses, flowers, landscapes and poems — painted in iron-brown, underglaze blue and coloured enamels on creamy or dark grounds. He made inventive forms including square and shaped dishes, poem-slip (tanzaku) plates, and sets of dishes whose decorations continue across the set. His inscribed signature became so admired that many later imitations and forgeries bear his mark.

Legacy

Kenzan ware influenced later Kyoto ceramics and was much admired by 20th-century studio potters, including Bernard Leach, who wrote about Kenzan's work after visiting Japan. Major pieces are held by museums in Japan and the West.
