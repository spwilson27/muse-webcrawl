The Cantigas de Santa Maria ("Songs to Saint Mary") are 420 poems with musical notation, written in Galician-Portuguese and produced during the reign of Alfonso X of Castile ("the Wise", r. 1252–1284). They constitute one of the largest and most important collections of solo songs (monody) from the Middle Ages, and are celebrated both for their music and for the richly illuminated manuscripts that preserve them.

Content

Every tenth cantiga is a hymn of praise (cantiga de loor) to the Virgin Mary; the remainder are miragres, narrating miracles attributed to her intercession — stories of rescued pilgrims, healed sick people, punished blasphemers and protected sinners drawn from across medieval Europe and from Alfonso's own kingdom. The poems typically use refrain forms such as the virelai/zajal structure, in which a refrain frames stanzas sung by a soloist.

Manuscripts

Four manuscripts survive. The two most famous, the Códice Rico (T) and the Florence codex (F), contain hundreds of miniatures depicting miracles, medieval instruments and scenes of Christian, Muslim and Jewish life in 13th-century Iberia; the Códice de los músicos (E) at El Escorial preserves the most complete musical notation. These illuminations are a major source for knowledge of medieval musical instruments and daily life.

Authorship

Alfonso X is presented in the manuscripts as the collection's author and "troubadour of Our Lady"; scholars believe he personally composed some cantigas (including autobiographical miracle poems) and supervised a workshop of court poets and musicians for the rest.
