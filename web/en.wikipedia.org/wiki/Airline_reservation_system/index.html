An airline reservation system, or computer reservation system (CRS), is the software platform an airline uses to store and sell its inventory: schedules, seat availability, fares, and passenger bookings recorded in passenger name records (PNRs). When reservation systems are marketed to travel agents across many airlines, they are known as global distribution systems (GDS). Modern platforms bundle reservations with revenue management, e-ticketing, loyalty, and departure-control functions, and are increasingly called passenger service systems (PSS).

History

Before computers, airlines tracked seats with manual card systems and centralized "request and reply" offices. American Airlines' work with IBM produced the Magnetronic Reservisor in the 1950s and then Sabre, launched in 1960 and fully operational by 1964 — one of the pioneering large real-time transaction-processing systems. Rival airlines built their own systems (United's Apollo, TWA's PARS, and Europe's Amadeus and Galileo consortia, both founded in 1987). In 1976 United began placing terminals in travel agencies; deregulation after 1978 made CRS access commercially decisive, and regulators in the U.S. and Europe imposed rules against display bias favoring host airlines.

How it works

An airline loads schedules and fare classes; revenue-management algorithms open and close booking classes to maximize revenue per flight. A booking creates a PNR containing the passenger's name, itinerary segments, contact details, ticketing time limit, and special service requests, referenced by a short record locator. E-tickets (standardized industry-wide by IATA in 2008) are stored as electronic coupons linked to the PNR. At departure, control of the flight passes to the departure control system for check-in, seat assignment, baggage, and load planning. Distribution now also flows through airline websites, metasearch engines, and IATA's New Distribution Capability (NDC) XML standard, which lets airlines retail richer offers than legacy GDS messages allow.

Major providers

The three dominant GDS/PSS companies are Amadeus IT Group (Altéa suite), Sabre Corporation, and Travelport (Galileo and Worldspan). Other significant passenger service systems include Navitaire (widely used by low-cost carriers), SITA, Radixx, and Hitit's Crane platform.
