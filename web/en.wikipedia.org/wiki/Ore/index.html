Ore

Ore is natural rock or sediment that contains one or more valuable minerals or metals that can be mined, treated, and sold at a profit. Whether a deposit counts as ore is an economic judgement as much as a geological one: it depends on the concentration of the valuable material (the grade), its accessibility, and the market price of the commodity.

Ore minerals and gangue

The valuable components are called ore minerals — for example hematite and magnetite for iron, chalcopyrite and bornite for copper, galena for lead, and sphalerite for zinc. The surrounding worthless material is the gangue. Ores are classified by how they formed: magmatic deposits (such as layered chromite and nickel sulfide bodies), hydrothermal deposits (veins and porphyry copper systems), sedimentary deposits (banded iron formations, placer gold), and residual deposits (bauxite and laterite nickel) formed by weathering.

Extraction and processing

Ore is extracted by surface or underground mining, then crushed and processed — by flotation, gravity separation, leaching, or magnetic separation — to produce a concentrate, which is smelted or refined into metal. Falling grades worldwide mean ever more rock must be processed per tonne of metal, with major implications for energy use, waste (tailings), and mine economics.
