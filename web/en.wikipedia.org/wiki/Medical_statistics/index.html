Medical statistics

Medical statistics (sometimes referred to as health statistics) deals with applications of statistics to medicine and the health sciences, including epidemiology, public health, forensic medicine, and clinical research. Medical statistics has been a recognised branch of statistics in the United Kingdom for more than 40 years, but the term has not come into general use in North America, where the wider term biostatistics is commonly used.

Pharmaceutical statistics

Pharmaceutical statistics is the application of statistics to matters concerning the pharmaceutical industry. This can be from issues of design of experiments, to analysis of drug trials, to issues of commercialisation of a medicine. Groups and organisations in this area include the International Society for Clinical Biostatistics and regulators such as the US Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency, which employ statisticians to assess trial evidence.

Clinical biostatistics

Clinical biostatistics is concerned with the design and analysis of clinical trials and observational studies. Key concepts include:

  • Randomisation and blinding
  • Statistical hypothesis testing and p-values
  • Confidence intervals
  • Survival analysis, including the Kaplan–Meier estimator and Cox proportional hazards model
  • Sensitivity and specificity of diagnostic tests
  • Intention-to-treat analysis
  • Sample size and statistical power calculations
  • Meta-analysis

Basic concepts

Important measures in medical statistics include incidence and prevalence, relative risk, odds ratios, hazard ratios, and numbers needed to treat. Understanding measures of central tendency, dispersion, and the distinction between association and causation is fundamental to interpreting the medical literature and practising evidence-based medicine.

Applications

Medical statistics underpins epidemiology (the study of the distribution and determinants of disease), clinical trials of new treatments, screening programme evaluation, health services research, and the routine compilation of vital statistics such as birth and death rates.
