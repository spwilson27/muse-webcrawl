Quartet

A string quartet in performance.

A quartet is a method of instrumentation (or a medium), used to perform music, with four parts. The word is also used for a musical composition written for such a combination, and for the ensemble of four performers itself. Quartets are a staple of chamber music; the most famous configuration is the string quartet of two violins, viola, and cello, a form perfected by Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven and regarded as the most demanding test of compositional craft.

Classical forms

  • String quartet — two violins, viola, cello.
  • Piano quartet — piano with violin, viola, and cello (or, occasionally, two violins and cello).
  • Wind quartets — e.g. flute or oboe with three strings, or four like winds, as in brass and saxophone quartets.
  • Vocal quartet — four voices, typically soprano, alto, tenor, and bass, in opera ensembles, part-songs, and oratorio.

In popular music and jazz

In jazz, a quartet usually combines a rhythm section (piano, bass, drums) with one horn, or two horns with a reduced rhythm section. Barbershop quartets — four male voices in close harmony — are an established American tradition with competitive societies. Rock and pop acts with four members are commonly described as quartets, though the term there simply counts personnel.
