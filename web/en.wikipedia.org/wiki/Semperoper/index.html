Semperoper

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Semperoper, Dresden, Germany. Opened 1841; rebuilt 1878 and 1985. Home of the Saxon State Opera and the Staatskapelle Dresden.

The Semperoper is the opera house of the Saxon State Opera (Sächsische Staatsoper) and the concert hall of the Staatskapelle Dresden in Dresden, Germany. It stands on the Theaterplatz in the historic centre of the city, near the Elbe river. The building is named after its architect, Gottfried Semper, and is regarded as one of the most beautiful opera houses in the world.

History

The first opera house on the site opened in 1841, with Carl Maria von Weber's works associated with the house and Richard Wagner serving as court Kapellmeister; Wagner's Rienzi, The Flying Dutchman, and Tannhäuser all premiered in Dresden. After that building burned down in 1869, Semper, then in exile for his part in the May Uprising, designed a second house, built under the direction of his son Manfred and completed in 1878. The Semperoper premiered most of Richard Strauss's stage works, including Salome (1905), Elektra (1909), and Der Rosenkavalier (1911).

The house was almost completely destroyed in the bombing of Dresden on 13 February 1945. Reconstruction, restoring Semper's design while enlarging the stage and modernizing the technical facilities, took decades under East Germany; the Semperoper reopened on 13 February 1985, exactly forty years after its destruction, with Weber's Der Freischütz — the last opera performed before its closure in 1944. The building was damaged again by the Elbe flood of 2002 and restored the following year.

Architecture

The building combines early Renaissance and Baroque elements characteristic of Semper's style, with a richly sculpted facade crowned by a quadriga bearing Dionysus and Ariadne. Inside, the horseshoe auditorium seats around 1,300. A famous detail is the five-minute clock above the stage, made by Johann Christian Friedrich Gutkaes, which displays the time with rotating numerals in five-minute steps.
