G. I. Taylor

Sir Geoffrey Ingram Taylor (7 March 1886 – 27 June 1975) was a British physicist and mathematician, a central figure in fluid dynamics and wave theory. The grandson of George Boole, Taylor spent most of his career at Trinity College, Cambridge, and applied classical physics with exceptional physical intuition to problems from oceanography to supersonic flight.

Work

Taylor's contributions include the theory of dislocations in crystals (1934), which explained how metals deform plastically; the study of turbulence and diffusion, including the Taylor dispersion of solutes in a flowing fluid; and the Rayleigh–Taylor instability, which occurs when a dense fluid is supported by a lighter one. During the Second World War he worked on blast waves and underwater explosions, and he famously estimated the yield of the first atomic bomb from declassified photographs of the Trinity test fireball by dimensional analysis.

Honours

Taylor was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1919, received its Copley Medal in 1944, was knighted in 1944, and was appointed to the Order of Merit in 1969. Concepts bearing his name include Taylor columns, Taylor vortices, and the Taylor number.
