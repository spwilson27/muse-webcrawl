European Poker Tour

The European Poker Tour (EPT) is a series of televised poker tournaments held in casinos across Europe and sponsored by PokerStars. It was created in 2004 by John Duthie, a professional poker player and television producer, who modelled it on the World Poker Tour in the United States but with lower buy-ins intended to appeal to the European market, where the Texas hold 'em boom was just beginning.

History

The first season, in 2004–05, consisted of seven stops including Barcelona, London, Dublin, Copenhagen, Deauville and Vienna, culminating in the Grand Final in Monte Carlo. The first main event was won by Alexander Stevic in Barcelona. The tour grew rapidly through the late 2000s; the PokerStars Caribbean Adventure in the Bahamas was long counted as an EPT stop despite its location. Landmark winners include Victoria Coren, who in 2006 became the first woman to win an EPT main event, in London, and in 2014 became the first player to win two EPT main event titles, in Sanremo.

In 2017 PokerStars briefly rebranded its live tours under the PokerStars Championship and PokerStars Festival banners, but the EPT name was restored in 2018 after player feedback. In 2022 the tour returned to full schedules after the disruption of the COVID-19 pandemic, with regular stops in Monte Carlo, Barcelona, Paris, Prague and Cyprus.

Format

Each festival is built around a no-limit hold 'em Main Event, typically with a buy-in of around €5,300, flanked by side events including high roller tournaments with buy-ins of €25,000 and above, and €100,000 super high rollers at some stops. From its first seasons the tour was noted for its television coverage: hole cards were filmed using under-table cameras, and commentary on early seasons was provided by creator John Duthie alongside Colin Murray, with later broadcasts featuring James Hartigan and Joe Stapleton.
