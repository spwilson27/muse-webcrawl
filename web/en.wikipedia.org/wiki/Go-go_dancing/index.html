Go-go dancers are entertainers whose work is dancing to recorded music in nightclubs and discothèques, typically on raised platforms, in cages, or behind bars, to enliven the atmosphere rather than as the sole focus of attention. The phenomenon emerged in the early 1960s in the United States.

History

The go-go craze is usually traced to the Whisky a Go Go club in Los Angeles (opened 1964), where a suspended cage with a dancing DJ became a sensation; the club took its name from the French phrase à gogo ("in abundance"). Television shows such as Hullabaloo and Shindig! spread the look nationwide — short fringed dresses, white go-go boots (immortalised by Nancy Sinatra's "These Boots Are Made for Walkin'"), and energetic freestyle steps like the Frug, the Twist and the Watusi.

Later developments

In the late 1970s and 1980s go-go dancing in some New York clubs overlapped with performance art and punk culture. In contemporary club and rave culture, particularly in electronic dance music events and in Thailand's nightlife, go-go dancers remain a standard feature, though styles and expectations vary widely by venue.
