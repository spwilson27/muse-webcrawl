Siege of Jasna Góra

The siege of Jasna Góra took place in the winter of 1655 during the Second Northern War, as part of the Swedish invasion of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth known as the Deluge. The Swedish forces, commanded by Burchard Müller von der Lühnen, attempted to capture the fortified Pauline monastery of Jasna Góra in Częstochowa, which held the revered icon of the Black Madonna of Częstochowa.

Background

After the rapid Swedish advance through Poland in 1655, the monastery was one of the few strongholds still holding out. Prior Augustyn Kordecki organised the defence with a small garrison of monks, soldiers and local nobility against a Swedish force of roughly 3,000 men with heavy artillery.

The siege

The siege lasted from 18 November to 27 December 1655. Despite repeated bombardments and assaults, the defenders repelled the Swedish attacks. The Swedes eventually withdrew, and the successful defence became a powerful symbol of Polish resistance, credited in popular tradition to the protection of the Virgin Mary.

Legacy

The event was later immortalised in Henryk Sienkiewicz's novel The Deluge and is commemorated annually at the shrine.
