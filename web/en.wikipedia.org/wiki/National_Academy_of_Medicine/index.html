National Academy of Medicine

The National Academy of Medicine (NAM), formerly called the Institute of Medicine (IoM), is an American nonprofit, non-governmental organisation. It is part of the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, along with the National Academy of Sciences, the National Academy of Engineering, and the National Research Council. The academy provides independent, evidence-based advice on health, medicine, and related policy to decision-makers and the public.

History

The Institute of Medicine was chartered in 1970 under the National Academy of Sciences. Its consensus reports — including To Err Is Human (1999), which estimated that medical errors cause tens of thousands of deaths annually in the United States, and Crossing the Quality Chasm (2001) — have been highly influential in health policy. In 2015 the organisation was renamed the National Academy of Medicine, aligning it with its sister academies and becoming the third academy in its own right rather than an institute of the National Academies.

Activities

Membership is an honour conferred by election by current members for distinguished contributions to medicine and health. The NAM publishes the journal NAM Perspectives, issues consensus studies through the Health and Medicine Division of the National Academies (formerly the IOM's study arm), convenes the National Academy of Medicine's annual meeting, and awards the Gustav O. Lienhard Award and other honours.
