Manager (association football)

In association football, the manager is the person who has overall responsibility for a team and its players: selecting the team and tactics, directing training, making substitutions and tactical decisions during matches, and — depending on the club's structure — buying and selling players. In many countries the role is called head coach, particularly where a sporting director or director of football handles transfers, whereas the British-style manager traditionally has broader authority over football operations.

Responsibilities

  • Selecting the starting line-up and match-day squad.
  • Setting tactics, formations, and set-piece routines.
  • Coaching and developing players, with the support of assistant coaches and analysts.
  • Motivating the team and managing the dressing room.
  • Media duties, including press conferences.
  • At many clubs, identifying transfer targets and negotiating contracts.

Appointment and tenure

Managers are usually appointed by the club's board or owner and are among the most visible figures in the sport. Job security is famously precarious: managers in top leagues are frequently dismissed ("sacked") after poor runs of results, and average tenures are often measured in months. A caretaker manager (or interim manager) fills the role temporarily between permanent appointments.

Notable long-serving managers include Alex Ferguson at Manchester United (1986–2013) and Arsène Wenger at Arsenal (1996–2018). Other highly decorated managers include Pep Guardiola, José Mourinho, Carlo Ancelotti, Jürgen Klopp, and Johan Cruyff, the latter also profoundly influential as a thinker on tactics.

Licensing

Top-flight managers are generally required to hold coaching licences — in Europe, the UEFA Pro Licence — obtained through national associations' coaching pathways.
