Yilgarn Craton

Location: Western Australia
Area: ~657,000 km²
Age: Archaean, up to ~4.4 billion years (detrital zircons)
Resources: Gold, nickel, iron ore, bauxite

The Yilgarn Craton is a vast expanse of ancient continental crust in the southern part of Western Australia, forming one of the oldest preserved pieces of the Earth's crust. Together with the Pilbara Craton to the north, it makes up much of the Western Australian Shield. The craton is composed mainly of Archaean granite–greenstone terranes assembled between about 3.7 and 2.6 billion years ago.

Geology

The Yilgarn is divided into several terranes, including the Narryer, Youanmi, and Eastern Goldfields terranes, separated by major shear zones. Its greenstone belts — sequences of metamorphosed volcanic and sedimentary rocks — host world-class mineral deposits. Detrital zircon crystals from Jack Hills in the Narryer Terrane have been dated to about 4.404 billion years, making them the oldest known terrestrial materials and evidence that a hydrosphere existed on Earth very early in its history.

Mineral wealth

The craton contains more than 30 percent of the world's known gold reserves and has been mined since the gold rushes of the 1890s at Coolgardie and Kalgoorlie, whose "Golden Mile" remains one of the richest goldfields ever found. Major nickel deposits occur at Kambalda and elsewhere in komatiite-hosted systems, and the craton also yields iron ore, bauxite, tantalum, and lithium. The Super Pit at Kalgoorlie is one of Australia's largest open-cut mines.

Land and ecology

Much of the Yilgarn lies within the Western Australian wheatbelt and goldfields regions. Its ancient, deeply weathered landscape is noted for extreme soil infertility combined with remarkable plant biodiversity, particularly in the southwest, one of the world's recognised biodiversity hotspots. The name derives from an Aboriginal word, yilgarn, associated with the white quartz found across the region.
