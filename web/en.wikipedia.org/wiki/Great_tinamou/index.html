Great tinamou

Great tinamou
Scientific nameTinamus major
FamilyTinamidae (tinamous)
RangeCentral and northern South America
Conservation statusNear Threatened (IUCN)

The great tinamou (Tinamus major) is a species of tinamou ground bird native to Central and South America, ranging from southern Mexico to northern Bolivia and Brazil. It is one of the largest tinamous, measuring 38–46 cm in length and weighing up to about 1.2 kg. There are several recognised subspecies.

Behaviour

The great tinamou lives on the floor of lowland rainforest, feeding on fallen fruit, seeds, roots and small invertebrates. It is secretive and rarely seen, but its mournful whistling call — often heard at dawn and dusk — is one of the characteristic sounds of the Neotropical forest. Unlike most birds, the male alone incubates the eggs and raises the chicks; the eggs are glossy and strikingly bright green.

Description

Plumage is olive-brown above, barred with black, with a paler greyish throat and a black cap. The species is a weak flier and escapes danger mainly by running or freezing in the undergrowth.

Conservation

Hunting and deforestation have reduced populations, and the species is assessed as Near Threatened by the IUCN.
