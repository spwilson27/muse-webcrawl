Blackout (electricity)

A blackout is the total loss of electric power in an area, the most severe form of power outage. Blackouts can last from minutes to weeks depending on the nature of the failure and the configuration of the electrical network.

Causes

Blackouts may result from damage to transmission lines (for example by storms or lightning), short circuits, overloaded transformers, equipment failure, or deliberate load shedding when demand exceeds generation capacity. Cascading failures, in which one failure overloads neighbouring components, caused major events such as the Northeast blackout of 2003 in North America and the 2003 Italy blackout.

Prevention and recovery

Grid operators use protective relays, redundancy and islanding to limit the spread of failures. Restoring power after a widespread blackout may require a "black start", in which generators that can start without external power energise the network step by step.
