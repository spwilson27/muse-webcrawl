Peyote

Scientific name: Lophophora williamsii
Family: Cactaceae
Active compound: Mescaline
Native range: Mexico and southwestern Texas

Peyote (Lophophora williamsii) is a small, spineless cactus native to Mexico and southwestern Texas. It is known for its psychoactive alkaloids, particularly mescaline, and has been used for centuries in Indigenous spiritual and healing practices.

Description

The plant is a low, blue-green, button-shaped cactus, usually less than about 10 cm across, with tufts of soft hairs (areoles) instead of spines and a thick taproot. It grows very slowly and produces small pink flowers.

Religious use

Peyote is the sacrament of the Native American Church, whose all-night peyote meetings combine prayer, singing and ritual. Religious use by enrolled members is protected under United States law, while general possession of mescaline is controlled in most jurisdictions.

Conservation

Wild populations have declined through habitat loss and overharvesting, and the species is listed as vulnerable; cultivation and conservation efforts are ongoing.
