Ryūjin (Japanese: 龍神, "Dragon God"), also known as Ryōjin or Watatsumi, is the tutelary deity of the sea in Japanese mythology. A great dragon embodying the power of the ocean, he lives in Ryūgū-jō, a palace beneath the waves built of red and white coral, from where he controls the tides with his magical tide jewels.

Mythology

Ryūjin's daughters include Toyotama-hime, who married the hunter Hoori and became the grandmother of Emperor Jimmu, Japan's legendary first emperor, and her sister Tamayori-hime, who raised the child. Through this descent the dragon god is reckoned an ancestor of the Japanese imperial line.

The god is also central to the tale of Urashima Tarō, the fisherman who rescues a turtle — in some versions a servant or daughter of Ryūjin — and is rewarded with a visit of apparent days to the Dragon Palace that turns out to have lasted hundreds of years.

According to legend, when Empress Jingū planned her invasion of Korea, Ryūjin gave her the tide jewels so she could ebb and flood the sea against enemy fleets. The dragon god was also credited with sending the jellyfish, in a popular folktale, to retrieve the monkey's liver as a cure for his illness — which is why, the story explains, jellyfish have no bones.

Worship

Ryūjin is venerated at shrines throughout Japan, especially in coastal communities and in the Ryukyu Islands, where dragon kings (Ryūō) feature in local religion. He is associated with water, rain, fishing and safe voyages, and is linked with the Buddhist dragon kings (nāgarāja) transmitted from India and China.
