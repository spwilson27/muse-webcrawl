Jacobi rotation

In numerical linear algebra, a Jacobi rotation is a rotation of a two-dimensional linear subspace of an n-dimensional inner product space, chosen to zero out a symmetric pair of off-diagonal entries of an n × n real matrix. It is named after the German mathematician Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi.

Definition

A Jacobi rotation matrix J(p, q, θ) is an identity matrix except in rows and columns p and q, where a 2×2 rotation appears. Applying the similarity transformation A′ = JTAJ with the rotation angle chosen appropriately makes the (p, q) and (q, p) entries of A′ vanish. The angle is computed from the entries of the matrix; a numerically stable formulation uses the quantity τ = (aqq − app) / (2apq) and t = sign(τ) / (|τ| + √(1 + τ²)).

Applications

Jacobi rotations are the basis of the Jacobi eigenvalue algorithm, which iteratively applies rotations to drive all off-diagonal entries of a symmetric matrix to zero, converging to a diagonal matrix whose entries are the eigenvalues, while the accumulated rotations give the eigenvectors. They are also used in computing the singular value decomposition and in the classical Jacobi method for solving linear systems. Although superseded for large dense problems by the QR algorithm, the Jacobi method is highly accurate for certain matrix classes and is well suited to parallel computation.
