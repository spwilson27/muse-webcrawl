The Baltimore Orioles (often referred to as the O's) are an American professional baseball team based in Baltimore, Maryland. They compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member of the American League (AL) East division. The franchise originated in Milwaukee as the Brewers in 1894, became the St. Louis Browns in 1902, and moved to Baltimore in 1954, taking the Orioles name from the state bird of Maryland and from earlier Baltimore clubs.

Oriole Park at Camden Yards, the Orioles' home since 1992.

History

Managed by Earl Weaver, the Orioles became one of baseball's dominant teams from the mid-1960s to the early 1980s, built on pitching, defence and the "three-run homer". They won the World Series in 1966 (sweeping the Los Angeles Dodgers behind Frank Robinson and a young Jim Palmer), 1970 (over the Cincinnati Reds, with Brooks Robinson's celebrated defence), and 1983 (over the Philadelphia Phillies). Cal Ripken Jr., the "Iron Man", set the record for consecutive games played with 2,632, breaking Lou Gehrig's mark in 1995.

Camden Yards

Oriole Park at Camden Yards, opened in 1992, pioneered the retro-classic ballpark movement: a brick-and-steel stadium with the B&O Warehouse beyond right field, which influenced nearly every MLB park built after it.

Notable players

Hall of Famers associated with the club include Frank Robinson, Brooks Robinson, Jim Palmer, Eddie Murray, Cal Ripken Jr. and manager Earl Weaver. More recently the club rebuilt around young stars such as Adley Rutschman and Gunnar Henderson, winning 101 games and the AL East in 2023.
