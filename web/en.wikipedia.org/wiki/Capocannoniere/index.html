The Capocannoniere is Serie A's top-scorer title

The Capocannoniere (Italian for "head gunner") is the title awarded to the highest goalscorer in Italian football's top flight, Serie A, each season. It is informally known as the titolo di capocannoniere; since 2021 the official award presented by Lega Serie A is the Premio Paolo Rossi, named after the 1982 World Cup top scorer.

History

Swedish striker Gunnar Nordahl of Milan holds the record with five Capocannoniere titles between 1950 and 1955. The record for goals in a single Serie A season is 36, set by Gonzalo Higuaín for Napoli in 2015–16 and equalled by Ciro Immobile for Lazio in 2019–20, when Immobile also won the European Golden Shoe. Silvio Piola, the all-time leading scorer in Serie A history, won the title twice.

Notable winners

Multiple winners include Michel Platini (three consecutive titles with Juventus), Marco van Basten, Gabriel Batistuta, Francesco Totti, Zlatan Ibrahimović, and Ciro Immobile (four titles). The youngest top scorers and the award's role in Italian football culture have made the Capocannoniere one of the most closely followed individual honours in the league.
