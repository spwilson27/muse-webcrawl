Avebury is a Neolithic henge monument in Wiltshire, England, containing three stone circles, including the largest megalithic stone circle in Britain. Built over several hundred years in the third millennium BCE, during the Neolithic and early Bronze Age, the monument comprises a huge circular bank and ditch (a henge) enclosing an area of about 11.5 hectares, within which stand an outer stone circle and two separate inner circles. The modern village of Avebury partly lies inside the monument.

Description

The outer circle originally had about 100 sarsen stones, of which 30 survive; the northern and southern inner circles each had around 30 stones. Two avenues of paired stones — the West Kennet Avenue and the Beckhampton Avenue — led from the entrances across the landscape, linking Avebury to other monuments including the Sanctuary, Windmill Hill, and Silbury Hill, the largest prehistoric artificial mound in Europe. The purpose of the complex remains debated; it is generally interpreted as a ceremonial and ritual landscape.

History and preservation

In the medieval and early modern periods many stones were toppled and buried or broken up for building material, sometimes as deliberate Christian suppression of a pagan site. Antiquarians John Aubrey and William Stukeley recorded the monument in the 17th and 18th centuries. In the 1930s, Alexander Keiller bought the site, excavated it, and re-erected many fallen stones; the village and monument are now cared for by the National Trust and English Heritage, and form part of the Stonehenge, Avebury and Associated Sites World Heritage Site (inscribed 1986).
