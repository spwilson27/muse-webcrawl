Dan Kelly (bushranger)

Daniel Kelly (1 June 1861 – 28 June 1880) was an Australian bushranger and outlaw. The younger brother of Ned Kelly, he was a core member of the Kelly gang, along with Joe Byrne and Steve Hart. He died at the siege of Glenrowan in 1880.

Early life

Dan Kelly was born at Beveridge, Victoria, the son of John "Red" Kelly and Ellen Quinn. The Kelly family was poor and frequently in conflict with colonial authorities. As a teenager, Dan was arrested several times for stock theft and other offences, and developed a reputation as a skilled horseman.

The Kelly gang

After the Fitzpatrick incident of April 1878, in which Constable Alexander Fitzpatrick claimed to have been wounded at the Kelly home, Dan and Ned fled into the bush. Following the killing of three policemen at Stringybark Creek in October 1878, the gang was declared outlawed. Dan participated in the gang's bank robberies at Euroa (1878) and Jerilderie (1879). At Jerilderie he helped guard hostages while Ned dictated his famous letter.

Death at Glenrowan

On 28 June 1880, the gang made its last stand at Glenrowan. Dan, wearing makeshift plate armour like his brother, fought police from the Glenrowan Inn. He died during the siege, along with Joe Byrne and Steve Hart, as the inn caught fire. He was 19 years old.
