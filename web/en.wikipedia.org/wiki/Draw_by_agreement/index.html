Draw by agreement

A draw by agreement is a type of draw in chess and some other board games that occurs when both players agree that the game should end in a draw. Under the FIDE Laws of Chess, a player may offer a draw to the opponent, who may accept or decline; if accepted, the game is scored as a draw, each player receiving half a point.

Rules and restrictions

To discourage short, pre-arranged draws, many tournaments impose restrictions, such as the Sofia rules, under which players may not agree a draw before a specified move number or without the arbiter's permission. FIDE rules also provide that a draw offer cannot be conditional and, once declined, cannot be immediately renewed.

Other draws

Other ways a chess game can be drawn include stalemate, threefold repetition, the fifty-move rule, dead position and mutual inability to checkmate.
