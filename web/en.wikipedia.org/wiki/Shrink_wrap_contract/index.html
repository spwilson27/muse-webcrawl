Shrink wrap contract

A shrink wrap contract or shrink wrap licence is a licence agreement whose terms are enclosed inside a product's packaging — historically the plastic shrink wrap around a box of computer software — so that the buyer cannot read the full terms until after purchase. The concept is that opening the package, or keeping and using the product after an opportunity to review the terms and return the product, constitutes acceptance of the contract.

Legal status

Shrink wrap contracts debated the classic contract-law questions of offer, acceptance, and terms added after payment. Courts in the United States have reached mixed results. In Step-Saver Data Systems, Inc. v. Wyse Technology (3d Cir. 1991), the court refused to enforce a licence printed on a software box under the Uniform Commercial Code's battle-of-the-forms rules. In ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg (7th Cir. 1996), Judge Frank Easterbrook upheld a shrink wrap licence, reasoning that a contract need not be formed all at once and that a buyer who can return the product after reading the terms accepts them by retaining it. Hill v. Gateway 2000 (7th Cir. 1997) extended similar reasoning to terms shipped inside a computer box.

Related forms

The concept is the ancestor of the clickwrap agreement, in which a user clicks "I agree" to terms presented on screen, and the browsewrap agreement, in which terms are merely posted on a website. Enforceability typically turns on whether the user had reasonable notice of the terms and an opportunity to reject them, and on general doctrines such as unconscionability. In the European Union, consumer-protection law and the rules on unfair contract terms can limit the effect of terms not made available before purchase.

Criticism

Critics argue that one-sided terms revealed only after payment — restricting copying, resale, reverse engineering, or criticism — undermine the notice function of contract law and consumer expectations, including doctrines such as first sale. Industry groups counter that mass-market licensing requires standard terms and that return rights adequately protect buyers.
