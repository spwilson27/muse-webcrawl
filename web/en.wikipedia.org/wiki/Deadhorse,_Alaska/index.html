Deadhorse is an unincorporated community in North Slope Borough, Alaska, United States, near the Arctic Ocean. Built to support the Prudhoe Bay Oil Field operations, it consists mainly of facilities for oilfield workers and support services. Although the permanent resident population is tiny, several thousand transient workers are housed there at any given time.

Geography and climate

Deadhorse lies on Alaska's North Slope coastal plain, a few miles inland from Prudhoe Bay. It has a tundra climate with long, severe winters, brief cool summers, and continuous daylight in midsummer balanced by polar night in midwinter. The Dalton Highway (Alaska Route 11) ends at Deadhorse after running roughly 414 miles (666 km) north from the Fairbanks area, making it one of the northernmost points reachable by road in North America.

Economy and facilities

The community's facilities—work camps, hotels for rotational workers, an airport (Deadhorse Airport), a general store, and equipment and logistics yards—exist to serve oil production at Prudhoe Bay and neighboring fields. Alcohol sales are prohibited. Most buildings are prefabricated modular structures and utilidors carry utilities above the permafrost.

Tourism

A small number of tourists visit Deadhorse as the end point of the Dalton Highway, some continuing on guided tours to the Arctic Ocean shore, which is otherwise restricted because of oilfield security. The community is a starting or finishing point for cyclists and motorcyclists traveling the length of the Americas.
