Corporate Transparency Act

The Corporate Transparency Act (CTA) is a United States federal law enacted on January 1, 2021, as part of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021, after Congress overrode President Donald Trump's veto of the NDAA. The CTA was designed to combat money laundering, terrorist financing, and the use of anonymous shell companies by requiring many U.S. companies to report information about their beneficial owners to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), a bureau of the U.S. Department of the Treasury.

Beneficial ownership reporting

Under the Act, "reporting companies" — generally corporations, limited liability companies, and similar entities created or registered to do business in the United States — must disclose their beneficial owners: individuals who own or control at least 25 percent of the company or exercise substantial control over it. Reports include each beneficial owner's full legal name, date of birth, residential address, and a unique identifying number from a document such as a passport or driver's license, together with an image of that document. The beneficial ownership information (BOI) reporting rule took effect on January 1, 2024, with companies existing before that date given until January 1, 2025, to file their initial reports. The database maintained by FinCEN is not public; it is accessible to law enforcement, financial institutions conducting due diligence, and certain other authorized users.

Exemptions

The Act exempts 23 categories of entities, including publicly traded companies, banks, credit unions, tax-exempt organizations, and "large operating companies" with more than 20 full-time U.S. employees, more than $5 million in U.S.-source gross receipts or sales, and a physical operating presence in the United States. Willful failure to report carries civil penalties and criminal penalties of up to two years' imprisonment.

Legal challenges and 2025 changes

The CTA faced constitutional challenges in federal court; in March 2024 a federal district court in Alabama held it unconstitutional as applied to the plaintiffs in National Small Business United v. Yellen, and other litigation produced conflicting injunctions that were repeatedly stayed and reinstated through late 2024 and early 2025. In March 2025, the Treasury Department announced it would not enforce the Act against U.S. citizens and domestic companies, and FinCEN issued an interim final rule narrowing the reporting requirement to foreign companies registered to do business in the United States.
