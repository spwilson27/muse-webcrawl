Thrift store

A thrift store (North American), charity shop (British), or op shop (Australian and New Zealand) is a retail establishment run by a charitable organisation to raise money, staffed largely by volunteers and selling donated second-hand goods — clothing, books, furniture and household items — at low prices. Well-known operators include Goodwill Industries, the Salvation Army, St. Vincent de Paul, Oxfam and Cancer Research UK.

History

Charity retailing grew from 19th-century charitable bazaars and the Salvation Army's salvage operations. The Salvation Army opened some of the earliest thrift stores in the 1890s, and Goodwill, founded in Boston in 1902 by Edgar Helms, built its model of employing people to repair and resell donated goods. Oxfam opened its first permanent charity shop in Oxford in 1948.

Operations and culture

Donations are sorted, priced and displayed; unsold goods may be recycled as textile fibre or sold in bulk. In recent decades thrift shopping has acquired cultural cachet — celebrated in the 2012 song "Thrift Shop" — and is promoted for its environmental benefits in diverting goods from landfill, though critics note rising prices and competition from online resale platforms.
