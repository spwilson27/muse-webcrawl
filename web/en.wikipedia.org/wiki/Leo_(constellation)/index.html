Leo (constellation)

Leo
Genitive: Leonis
Abbreviation: Leo
Pronunciation: LEE-oh
Symbolism: the Lion
Right ascension: ~10.7h
Declination: ~+14°
Area: 947 sq. deg. (12th)
Brightest star: Regulus (α Leo)
Bordering: Ursa Major, Leo Minor, Lynx, Cancer, Hydra, Sextans, Crater, Virgo, Coma Berenices

Leo is one of the constellations of the zodiac, lying between Cancer to the west and Virgo to the east. Its name is Latin for lion, and its symbol is ♌. One of the 48 constellations described by Ptolemy in the 2nd century, it remains one of the 88 modern constellations. It is recognisable by the "Sickle" asterism, a backward question-mark pattern marking the lion's head.

Stars

Leo contains many bright stars. Regulus (α Leonis), a blue-white multiple system, marks the lion's heart and lies almost exactly on the ecliptic. Algieba (γ) is a binary of orange giants; Denebola (β) marks the tail; Zosma (δ) and Chertan (θ) form the hindquarters; Ras Elased and Algenubi form the head. The red dwarf Wolf 359, one of the nearest stars to the Sun at 7.8 light-years, lies in Leo.

Deep-sky objects

Leo hosts the Leo Triplet of galaxies (M65, M66 and NGC 3628) and the M96 Group, including M95, M96 and M105. The giant elliptical Leo Ring of hydrogen gas surrounds two elliptical galaxies in the group.

Mythology and astrology

In Greek myth Leo represents the Nemean lion killed by Heracles as the first of his twelve labours; its impenetrable hide was thereafter worn by the hero. In astrology, Leo is a fire sign ruled by the Sun, covering birthdays roughly from 23 July to 22 August in the tropical zodiac. The Leonid meteor shower peaks each November, radiating from the Sickle.
