Organ of Zuckerkandl

The organ of Zuckerkandl is a collection of chromaffin tissue derived from the neural crest located along the aorta, most prominently at the level of the bifurcation of the aorta and the origin of the inferior mesenteric artery. It was described by the Austrian anatomist Emil Zuckerkandl in 1901. In the fetus and infant the organ is relatively large and functions as a source of catecholamines, helping to regulate blood pressure before the adrenal medulla fully matures; it normally regresses during childhood.

Clinical significance

The organ of Zuckerkandl is the most common site of extra-adrenal pheochromocytoma (paraganglioma) arising outside the adrenal glands. Tumours here secrete norepinephrine and can cause episodic hypertension, headaches, palpitations and sweating. Because the tissue is chromaffin-positive, it takes up tracers such as MIBG and is visible on functional imaging used to locate such tumours. Surgical excision is the usual treatment.
