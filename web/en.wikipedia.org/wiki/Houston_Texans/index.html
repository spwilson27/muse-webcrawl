The Houston Texans are a professional American football team based in Houston. The Texans compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the American Football Conference (AFC) South division. The team plays its home games at NRG Stadium.

History

The Texans were founded in 1999 by businessman Bob McNair, who was awarded the NFL's 32nd franchise after Houston's previous team, the Oilers, relocated to Tennessee in 1997. The Texans began play as an expansion team in 2002, winning their first regular-season game against the Dallas Cowboys. The team's first decade was largely unsuccessful, but under head coach Gary Kubiak the Texans won their first AFC South division title and first playoff game in the 2011 season, led by stars such as wide receiver Andre Johnson, running back Arian Foster, and defensive end J. J. Watt, the latter a three-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year.

Later years brought further division titles under coach Bill O'Brien, but also repeated early playoff exits. Following a period of turmoil and rebuilding, the Texans hired former player DeMeco Ryans as head coach in 2023 and drafted quarterback C. J. Stroud, who won Offensive Rookie of the Year honours as Houston returned to the playoffs.

Rivalries and identity

The Texans' name honours the heritage of Texas and was chosen through public focus groups. Their principal rivals are the other AFC South teams — the Indianapolis Colts, Jacksonville Jaguars, and Tennessee Titans, the franchise's Houston predecessor. The in-state clash with the Dallas Cowboys is played for the Governor's Cup in years the teams meet.
