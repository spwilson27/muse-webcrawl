Cohousing

Cohousing
OriginDenmark, 1960s–1970s (as bofællesskab)
Key figuresKathryn McCamant, Charles Durrett
Typical size20–40 households
FeaturesPrivate homes, common house, shared meals, resident management

Cohousing is an intentional community of private homes clustered around shared space. Each attached or single-family home has traditional amenities, including a private kitchen. Shared spaces typically feature a common house, which may include a large kitchen and dining area, laundry, and recreational spaces. Shared outdoor space may include parking, walkways, open space, and gardens. Neighbors also share resources like tools and lawnmowers.

Origins

The modern theory of cohousing originated in Denmark in the 1960s among groups of families who were dissatisfied with existing housing and communities that they felt did not meet their needs. The Danish term bofællesskab means "living community." Early communities included Sættedammen (1972) and Skråplanet. The concept was brought to the United States by architects Kathryn McCamant and Charles Durrett, who visited Danish communities and wrote the foundational book Cohousing: A Contemporary Approach to Housing Ourselves (1988).

Characteristics

  • Participatory process — residents participate in the design and development of their community.
  • Neighbourhood design — physical layout encourages a strong sense of community, with pedestrian-oriented clusters.
  • Shared common facilities — a common house supplements private living spaces.
  • Resident management — residents manage the community themselves, usually by consensus.
  • Non-hierarchical structure — decision-making roles are shared rather than concentrated.
  • Separate income sources — the community is not a source of income for its members.

Community life

Households have independent incomes and private lives, but neighbours collaboratively plan and manage community activities and shared spaces. Common dinners, typically several times a week, are a hallmark of cohousing social life. Communities are usually designed to be multigenerational, and newer forms include senior cohousing and ecovillages.
