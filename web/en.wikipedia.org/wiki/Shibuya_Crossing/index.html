Shibuya Crossing

Shibuya Crossing (渋谷スクランブル交差点, Shibuya sukuranburu kōsaten), commonly called the Shibuya Scramble Crossing, is a popular pedestrian scramble crossing in Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan. It is located in front of the Hachikō exit of Shibuya Station and stops vehicles in all directions to allow pedestrians to inundate the entire intersection. At peak times, as many as 3,000 people cross at each green light, and estimates of daily crossings exceed one million, making it often cited as the busiest pedestrian crossing in the world. The statue of Hachikō, the loyal dog, in the square on the northwest corner is a famously popular rendezvous point.

Shibuya Crossing seen from above, filled with pedestrians on a green light.

Overview

The scramble crossing was introduced in 1973 alongside Shibuya's development as a youth-culture and retail district. Three large video screens mounted on nearby buildings and extensive advertising make the crossing an iconic symbol of Tokyo, frequently compared to Times Square in New York. It appears in numerous films, including Lost in Translation and The Fast and the Furious: Tokyo Drift, and was a centrepiece of broadcasts during the 2020 Summer Olympics held in Tokyo.

Events and use

The crossing area is a traditional site of spontaneous public celebration, particularly for Halloween gatherings and New Year's Eve countdowns and after Japanese national football team victories; turnout has been so large that since the late 2010s Shibuya Ward has restricted street drinking and, in some years, effectively cancelled official Halloween and New Year events around the station. A Starbucks on the second floor of the QFRONT building overlooking the crossing is one of the most-photographed vantage points.

Redevelopment

The area is undergoing continuous large-scale redevelopment tied to Shibuya Station, including the Shibuya Scramble Square tower (opened 2019, 47 storeys) whose rooftop observation deck, Shibuya Sky, overlooks the crossing.
