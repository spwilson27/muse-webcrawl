War college

A war college is a senior military academy or staff college that provides graduate-level instruction in military strategy, national security, and command to senior officers, typically of the rank of lieutenant colonel and above, and sometimes to senior civilian officials.

History and role

The model derives from the Prussian Kriegsakademie, founded in Berlin in 1810, which professionalised staff training for the Prussian (later German) general staff. Other nations created comparable institutions in the 19th and 20th centuries as warfare grew more complex and joint, requiring officers educated in logistics, strategy, and statecraft rather than tactics alone.

Examples

Well-known war colleges include the U.S. Army War College at Carlisle, Pennsylvania (founded 1901), the Naval War College at Newport, Rhode Island (1884), and the Air War College at Maxwell Air Force Base, together with the National War College in Washington, D.C., which emphasises joint and interagency education. The United Kingdom has the Joint Services Command and Staff College, and many other countries maintain equivalents.

Graduates typically receive a master's degree in strategic studies or a related field, and attendance is often a prerequisite for promotion to general or flag rank.
