POLIN Museum of the History of Polish Jews

The POLIN Museum of the History of Polish Jews (Polish: Muzeum Historii Żydów Polskich POLIN) is a museum on the site of the former Warsaw Ghetto in Warsaw, Poland. The name "Polin," meaning "Poland" in Hebrew, also carries the legend po lin—"rest here"—said to have been heard by Jews fleeing persecution who arrived in Poland in the Middle Ages. The museum's core exhibition presents 1,000 years of Jewish life in Poland.

Building

The museum building was designed by Finnish architect Rainer Mahlamäki and opened to the public in April 2013, on the 70th anniversary of the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising. It stands opposite the Monument to the Ghetto Heroes, unveiled in 1948. The building's undulating main hall evokes both the parting of the Red Sea and the gorges of the Vistula riverbanks; its glass facade is printed with the word "Polin" in Latin and Hebrew letters.

Core exhibition

The core exhibition, opened in October 2014, was developed by an international team of scholars and designers led by Barbara Kirshenblatt-Gimblett. Its eight galleries—Forest; First Contacts; Paradisus Iudaeorum; Into the Country; Encounters with Modernity; On the Jewish Street; Holocaust; and Postwar Years—trace Polish Jewish history from medieval settlement through the flourishing of Polish Jewry, the Holocaust, and postwar revival. Highlights include a full-scale reconstruction of the painted ceiling and bimah of the wooden synagogue of Gwoździec, destroyed during the Second World War.

Institution and recognition

The museum was created as a public-private partnership between the Polish Ministry of Culture, the City of Warsaw, and the Association of the Jewish Historical Institute of Poland. In 2016 it won the European Museum of the Year Award. It hosts exhibitions, concerts, educational programs, and the resource center, and attracts hundreds of thousands of visitors annually.
