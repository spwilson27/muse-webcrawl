Exmoor is a national park in Somerset and Devon in South West England, taking its name from the River Exe. It consists of an upland plateau of open moorland and wooded valleys (combes) rising to Dunkery Beacon (519 m), the highest point in Somerset, and meets the Bristol Channel in some of England's highest sea cliffs, including the 318 m Great Hangman. The park covers about 692 km², roughly two-thirds in Somerset and one-third in Devon.

Landscape and wildlife

Exmoor is famous for its semi-wild Exmoor ponies, one of Britain's oldest native pony breeds, and for its herds of red deer. Habitats range from blanket bog and heather moor to the ancient oakwoods of the East Lyn and Barle valleys. The coastline includes the twin towns of Lynton and Lynmouth, where catastrophic flooding in August 1952 killed 34 people.

History and culture

The moor preserves prehistoric standing stones, barrows, and hillforts; in the Middle Ages it was a royal hunting forest. R. D. Blackmore set his novel Lorna Doone (1869) in Exmoor's Doone Valley, an association the region's tourism still trades on. Designated a national park in 1954, Exmoor became Europe's first International Dark Sky Reserve in 2011.
