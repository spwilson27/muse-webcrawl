Her Majesty's Ship

Her Majesty's Ship (or His Majesty's Ship when the monarch is male), abbreviated HMS, is the ship prefix used for ships of the navy of a monarchy, most famously the Royal Navy of the United Kingdom. The prefix signifies that the vessel is commissioned in the sovereign's service.

Usage in the Royal Navy

The abbreviation HMS came into regular use in the Royal Navy in the late 18th century; before that, expressions such as "His Majesty's Ship" appeared in full in documents. The prefix applies to commissioned warships, including submarines ("Her Majesty's Submarine" is also abbreviated HMS). Shore establishments, known as "stone frigates", may also be commissioned as HMS.

Variants in other navies

Commonwealth realms and other monarchies use localised forms, such as HMCS (His Majesty's Canadian Ship), HMAS (Australian), HMNZS (New Zealand), and HSwMS (Swedish). The Royal Navy also uses subsidiary prefixes, such as RFA for Royal Fleet Auxiliary vessels and RMS for Royal Mail Ships.

Related prefixes

Other states use prefixes denoting ownership or function rather than the sovereign: USS (United States Ship), and civilian prefixes such as MV (motor vessel) and SS (steamship).
