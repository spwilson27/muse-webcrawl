Tooth of Time

The Tooth of Time is a distinctive igneous rock formation and landmark on the Philmont Scout Ranch near Cimarron, New Mexico, in the United States. Rising roughly 2,500 feet (760 m) above the surrounding plains to an elevation of 9,003 feet (2,744 m), its jagged granite ridge resembles a row of teeth.

Geography and history

The Tooth is a dacite porphyry stock formed about 25–40 million years ago and exposed by erosion. It served as a navigational marker for travellers on the Santa Fe Trail, who could see it days before reaching it, and for earlier Indigenous peoples including the Jicarilla Apache and Ute. The formation lies within the 140,177-acre Philmont Scout Ranch, donated by oilman Waite Phillips in 1938 and 1941.

Climbing

Thousands of Scouts and Scouters hike to the summit each summer, typically as the culminating climb of a Philmont trek; the sunrise ascent is a celebrated tradition. A standard route climbs from the Tooth Ridge campsite. The nearby Tooth of Time Ridge and the ranch's emblem both feature the formation.
