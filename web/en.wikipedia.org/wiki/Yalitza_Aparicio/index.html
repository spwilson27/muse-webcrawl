Yalitza Aparicio Martínez (born 11 December 1993) is a Mexican actress of Mixtec and Triqui heritage. She made her film debut as the domestic worker Cleo in Alfonso Cuarón's 2018 drama Roma, a performance that earned her an Academy Award nomination for Best Actress — the first Indigenous Mexican actress and only the second Mexican actress ever nominated in the category.

Early life

Aparicio was born and raised in Tlaxiaco, Oaxaca. She trained and worked as a preschool teacher and had no acting experience when she accompanied her sister to a casting call for Cuarón's film; her sister, then pregnant, asked her to audition in her place. She was selected from hundreds of candidates and learned the Mixtec language for the role.

Career

Roma premiered at the Venice Film Festival in 2018, winning the Golden Lion, and Aparicio's understated performance drew universal acclaim. She appeared on the cover of Vogue México and was named one of Time magazine's 100 most influential people of 2019. In 2019 she was appointed a UNESCO Goodwill Ambassador for Indigenous peoples, and she has since taken further screen roles, including in the anthology series and films in Mexico and the United States, while advocating for domestic workers' rights and Indigenous representation.
