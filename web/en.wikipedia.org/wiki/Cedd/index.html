Cedd (c. 620 – 26 October 664) was an Anglo-Saxon monk and bishop from the kingdom of Northumbria. A monk of Lindisfarne under St Aidan, he was sent as a missionary to the Middle Angles and then to the East Saxons, becoming Bishop of the East Saxons in about 654. He founded monasteries at Tilbury and at Bradwell-on-Sea in Essex, where the Chapel of St Peter-on-the-Wall still stands, and at Lastingham in Yorkshire, where he died of plague in 664. He is venerated as a saint.

Life

Cedd was the eldest of four brothers, all of whom became priests: his brothers were Chad (Saint Chad of Mercia), Cynibil and Caelin. Trained in the Irish tradition at Lindisfarne, Cedd was chosen around 653 to evangelise the Middle Angles, and the following year he was recalled and sent to convert the East Saxons at the request of King Sigeberht the Good, being consecrated bishop by Finan of Lindisfarne. Our principal source for his life is Bede's Ecclesiastical History of the English People.

Synod of Whitby and death

Cedd acted as interpreter at the Synod of Whitby in 664, where the Roman and Irish traditions debated the dating of Easter; although formed in the Irish tradition, he accepted the synod's decision in favour of the Roman reckoning. Later the same year he died of plague at his monastery of Lastingham, having consecrated its site, according to Bede, after fasting and prayer. His brother Chad succeeded him at Lastingham.

Legacy

Cedd's chapel at Bradwell remains in occasional use and is a site of pilgrimage; the annual Bradwell Pilgrimage commemorates him. He is the patron saint of Essex and is commemorated in place names and churches across the region.
