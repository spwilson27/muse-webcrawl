Mastani

Mastani (1699–1740) was the second wife of the Maratha Peshwa Bajirao I. She was the daughter of the Hindu king Chhatrasal of Bundelkhand and his Persian Muslim wife Ruhaani Bai, and is remembered for her beauty, her military skills, and the controversy her marriage caused in the orthodox Brahmin court of Pune.

Life

According to tradition, Chhatrasal, hard-pressed by Mughal forces, appealed to Bajirao for aid; in return for his assistance, he gave Bajirao his daughter Mastani in marriage in 1729, along with territory and wealth. Mastani was trained in horse riding, sword fighting, and warfare, and is said to have accompanied Bajirao on some of his military campaigns.

The marriage was not accepted by Bajirao's family, who objected to his taking a wife of mixed Hindu-Muslim parentage. After Bajirao's death in 1740, Mastani died shortly afterwards — accounts differ as to whether she died of grief, poisoned herself, or was killed. Their son, Krishna Rao (later Shamsher Bahadur), was raised by Bajirao's first wife, Kashibai, and later served in the Maratha army, dying of wounds after the Third Battle of Panipat in 1761.

Legacy

Mastani's story has become one of the most romanticised in Indian popular culture. Her palace in Pune, the Mastani Mahal, survived until the 20th century. The romance of Bajirao and Mastani was the subject of the 2015 Hindi film Bajirao Mastani, directed by Sanjay Leela Bhansali.
