Virtuous circle and vicious circle

A virtuous circle and a vicious circle are opposite kinds of self-reinforcing feedback loops. In a virtuous circle, a favourable outcome produces further favourable outcomes: each step in the chain improves the conditions for the next. In a vicious circle, an unfavourable outcome makes matters worse, which in turn worsens the original problem, so the system spirals into an increasingly bad state. Both are examples of positive feedback — reinforcement of an existing trend — distinguished by whether the trend is desirable or harmful.

Examples

Economics

In economics, a virtuous circle might run: investment raises productivity, which raises incomes, which raises demand, which justifies further investment. A vicious circle appears in poverty traps and deflationary spirals: unemployment cuts spending, which cuts business revenue, which causes layoffs, which further cut spending. Gunnar Myrdal developed the related idea of "circular cumulative causation" to explain regional inequality.

Other fields

In medicine, vicious circles include cycles in which a disease symptom worsens its own cause, such as anxiety causing insomnia causing greater anxiety. In climate science, feedbacks like ice–albedo loss are vicious in effect. In personal behaviour, confidence built on small successes is the classic virtuous circle. Breaking a vicious circle usually requires intervention at its weakest link; sustaining a virtuous circle requires that the feedback not be interrupted.
