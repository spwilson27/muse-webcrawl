Denominazione di origine controllata (DOC; literally "controlled designation of origin") is a quality assurance label for Italian wine. Modelled on the French appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC), the system was established by law in 1963 and the first wines were recognised in 1966. A DOC wine must be produced within a specified region using defined methods — prescribed grape varieties, maximum yields, minimum alcohol and ageing requirements — and must meet defined quality standards verified by tasting and analysis before it can bear the designation.

The Italian classification system

Italian wine law, reformed by the Goria Law of 1992 and aligned with European Union rules in 2009–2011, traditionally recognised four tiers. At the base are generic wines (formerly vino da tavola); above them IGT (Indicazione geografica tipica), a flexible regional category introduced in 1992 that permitted wines such as the Super Tuscans; then DOC; and at the top DOCG (Denominazione di origine controllata e garantita), created in 1980 with stricter controls. Under the EU framework, DOC and DOCG both fall within the Protected Designation of Origin (DOP/PDO) category, while IGT corresponds to Protected Geographical Indication (IGP/PGI). There are more than 330 DOCs in Italy.

Rules and control

Each DOC has a production code (disciplinare) specifying the delimited zone, permitted grape varieties and blends, viticultural practices, maximum yield per hectare, vinification and ageing rules, and the wine's required analytical and organoleptic characteristics. Compliance is monitored by consortia and authorised inspection bodies; DOC wines carry a government-issued neck strip in a characteristic colour (traditionally lilac/pink for DOC, with DOCG strips in a different scheme), allowing traceability of each bottle.

Examples

Well-known DOCs include Chianti (elevated to DOCG for its core zone in 1984), Prosecco DOC (created in 2009, spanning Veneto and Friuli), Soave, Valpolicella, Barbera d'Asti (now DOCG), Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, Frascati, Orvieto and Etna (Sicily's first DOC, from 1968). The creation of the Prosecco DOC in 2009 — simultaneously with the elevation of Conegliano Valdobbiadene to DOCG and the renaming of the grape variety from Prosecco to Glera — illustrates how the system is used to protect names under international trade law.
