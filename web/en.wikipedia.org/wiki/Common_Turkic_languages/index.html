The Common Turkic languages are the larger of the two branches of the Turkic language family, comprising every Turkic language except those of the Oghuric branch, whose only living member is Chuvash. The distinction, first drawn from features of early loanwords, is based on systematic sound correspondences: Common Turkic shows z and š where Oghuric shows r and l (hence the labels "z-Turkic" and "r-Turkic").

Classification

Common Turkic is conventionally divided geographically and linguistically into:

  • Oghuz (southwestern) — Turkish, Azerbaijani, Turkmen, Gagauz, and Qashqai.
  • Kipchak (northwestern) — Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Tatar, Bashkir, Karakalpak, Kumyk, and Nogai.
  • Karluk (southeastern) — Uzbek and Uyghur, descended in part from the medieval Chagatai literary language.
  • Siberian (northeastern) — Sakha (Yakut), Tuvan, Khakas, Altai, and Shor, sometimes treated as a separate branch of Turkic altogether.

Characteristics

Common Turkic languages share the family's characteristic agglutinative morphology, vowel harmony, subject–object–verb word order, and absence of grammatical gender. Their deep mutual similarities reflect descent from Proto-Turkic spoken in the eastern steppe, followed by successive waves of expansion with the Göktürk, Uyghur, and later khanates and empires.
