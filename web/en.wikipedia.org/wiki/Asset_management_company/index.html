Asset management company

Large asset managers are headquartered in global financial centres

An asset management company (AMC) is a firm that invests pooled funds from clients into securities and other assets, putting capital at risk with the aim of generating returns. Clients range from individual retail investors to pension funds, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, and other institutions.

Business model

AMCs earn fees, typically calculated as a percentage of assets under management (AUM), for managing mutual funds, exchange-traded funds, hedge funds, and segregated mandates. The largest firms — such as BlackRock, Vanguard, and Fidelity — manage trillions of dollars and benefit from scale in index (passive) investing.

Related senses

In India, "asset management company" specifically denotes the SEBI-regulated entity that manages a mutual fund's schemes. In banking crises, the term has also been used for state-owned "bad bank" vehicles — for example Sweden's Securum or China's asset management companies — created to take over and resolve non-performing loans.
