1948 Arab–Israeli War
Date: 15 May 1948 – 10 March 1949
Location: Former British Mandate of Palestine, Sinai, southern Lebanon
Result: Israeli victory; 1949 Armistice Agreements

The 1948 Arab–Israeli War, known in Israel as the War of Independence and in Arabic as the Nakba ("catastrophe"), was fought between the newly declared State of Israel and a military coalition of Arab states. It was the second stage of the 1947–1949 Palestine war, following the civil war that began after the UN Partition Plan of November 1947.

Course of the war

On 14 May 1948 David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the establishment of Israel; the next day armies of Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon and Iraq invaded. Fighting proceeded in phases separated by UN-brokered truces. Israeli offensives in late 1948 secured the Negev and Galilee, while Transjordan's Arab Legion held East Jerusalem and the West Bank, and Egypt held the Gaza Strip.

Aftermath

The 1949 Armistice Agreements left Israel with about 78% of the former Mandate territory. More than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled from their homes, and were not permitted to return; the refugee question and the war's legacy remain central to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.
