The Bozeman Trail was an overland route in the Western United States, connecting the goldfields of Montana Territory with the Oregon Trail in eastern Wyoming. Blazed in 1863–64 by John Bozeman and John Jacobs, the trail ran north from Fort Laramie along the eastern front of the Bighorn Mountains, through the Powder River country, and into present-day Montana, where it joined routes to Virginia City and the Yellowstone valley.

Conflict

The trail crossed the last great hunting grounds of the Lakota Sioux, Northern Cheyenne, and Northern Arapaho, territory guaranteed to them under the Treaty of Fort Laramie of 1851. Native resistance led by the Oglala Lakota chief Red Cloud escalated after the U.S. Army built Forts Reno, Philip Kearny, and C. F. Smith along the trail in 1866 without tribal consent. The resulting conflict, Red Cloud's War (1866–1868), included the Fetterman Fight of December 1866 — the Army's worst defeat on the northern plains until Little Bighorn — and the Wagon Box Fight and Hayfield Fight of 1867.

Abandonment

Under the Treaty of Fort Laramie of 1868 the United States agreed to abandon the Bozeman Trail forts, which were burned by the Lakota after the troops withdrew. Traffic shifted to routes farther west and south, and the coming of the transcontinental railroad ended the trail's brief importance, though portions later served as cattle and stage routes.
