Flyball

Flyball is a dog sport in which teams of dogs race against each other over a line of hurdles, to a box that releases a tennis ball to be caught when the dog presses the spring-loaded pad, then back to their handlers while carrying the ball. It is a relay race with four dogs on a team.

History

Flyball began in California in the late 1960s and early 1970s, when Herbert Wagner developed the first tennis-ball launcher for dogs; the sport was demonstrated on television and spread through obedience clubs. The North American Flyball Association (NAFA) was founded in 1985 and held its first tournament the same year. The sport subsequently spread to Europe, Australia, and South Africa, with national bodies such as the British Flyball Association organizing competitions.

The race

Two teams of four dogs compete head-to-head on parallel courses, each typically 51 feet long with four hurdles. The dog races over the hurdles, triggers the flyball box to launch a ball, catches it, and returns over the hurdles to the start line; the next dog may not cross the start line until the previous dog has returned with its nose across the line. Faults include early starts, dropped balls, and missed jumps. The fastest clean team wins the heat.

Jump heights and divisions

Hurdle height is set from the height of the smallest dog on the team, keeping the sport accessible to dogs of many sizes and breeds. Teams are seeded into divisions by their best times so that closely matched teams race each other. Exceptionally fast teams complete a full four-dog relay in under 15 seconds.
