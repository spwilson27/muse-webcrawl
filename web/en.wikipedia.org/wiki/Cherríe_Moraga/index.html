Cherríe Moraga

Cherríe Moraga (born September 25, 1952) is an American essayist, playwright, feminist activist, author, and an artist-in-residence at Stanford University. She is a prominent figure within Chicano literature and feminist theory, known for works exploring the intersections of race, gender, sexuality, and class.

Early life and education

Moraga was born in Los Angeles County, California, to a Chicana mother and an Anglo-American father. She attended Immaculate Heart College in Los Angeles, graduating in 1974, and later earned a master's degree in Feminist Writings from San Francisco State University in 1980.

Career

Moraga co-edited, with Gloria E. Anzaldúa, the landmark anthology This Bridge Called My Back: Writings by Radical Women of Color (1981), a foundational text of women-of-color feminism. Her first book of essays and poems, Loving in the War Years: lo que nunca pasó por sus labios (1983), was among the first published by an out Chicana lesbian. Her memoir Native Country of the Heart appeared in 2019.

As a playwright, Moraga co-founded the Latina theatre troupe Teatro Luna and has written plays including Heroes and Saints (1992), about farmworker struggles and pesticide exposure in California's Central Valley.
