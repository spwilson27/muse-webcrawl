Jose de San Martín

José Francisco de San Martín y Matorras (25 February 1778 – 17 August 1850) was an Argentine general and statesman who led the successful struggle for independence from the Spanish Empire in the southern part of South America. He is regarded, along with Simón Bolívar, as one of the Liberators of Spanish South America, and he is a national hero of Argentina, Chile and Peru.

Early life and military career in Spain

San Martín was born in Yapeyú, in present-day Argentina, the son of a Spanish colonial official. He was educated in Spain and joined the Spanish army as a cadet at the age of eleven. He fought against the Moors at Oran, against the British, and in the Peninsular War against Napoleon, rising to the rank of lieutenant colonel.

Return to South America

In 1812 San Martín resigned his commission and sailed to Buenos Aires, where he offered his services to the revolutionary government of the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata. He organized the Regiment of Mounted Grenadiers and won his first victory at the Battle of San Lorenzo in 1813.

Crossing of the Andes

Appointed governor of Cuyo, San Martín prepared an audacious plan: to cross the Andes with an army and liberate Chile, then move by sea to attack Peru, the stronghold of Spanish power. In January 1817 the Army of the Andes crossed the mountains in one of the greatest feats of military logistics of the age. After the victories of Chacabuco (1817) and Maipú (1818), Chile's independence was secured.

Liberation of Peru

In 1820 San Martín sailed with a liberating expedition to Peru. He entered Lima and proclaimed Peruvian independence on 28 July 1821, becoming Protector of Peru. After a famous meeting with Bolívar at Guayaquil in 1822, San Martín resigned his powers and withdrew from public life, allowing Bolívar to complete the liberation of Peru.

Later life and legacy

San Martín spent his final decades in Europe, mostly in France, where he died at Boulogne-sur-Mer in 1850. His remains were repatriated to Argentina in 1880 and rest in Buenos Aires Cathedral. He is commemorated across South America as a model of selfless patriotism, and the Order of the Liberator General San Martín is Argentina's highest decoration.
