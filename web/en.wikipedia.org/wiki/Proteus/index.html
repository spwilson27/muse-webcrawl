Proteus

In Greek mythology, Proteus (Ancient Greek: Πρωτεύς) is an early prophetic sea god, one of several deities called "Old Man of the Sea". He is a servant or son of Poseidon, associated particularly with the island of Pharos off the Egyptian coast in Homer's Odyssey. Proteus can foretell the future, but he changes his shape to avoid having to — he answers only those who can capture and hold him despite his transformations.

Mythology

In Book 4 of the Odyssey, Menelaus, stranded in Egypt, is advised by Proteus's daughter Eidothea to ambush the god as he emerges from the sea to nap among his seals. Despite Proteus turning into a lion, a serpent, a leopard, a boar, water, and a tree, Menelaus holds on until the god relents and reveals how Menelaus may return home, and the fates of the other Greek heroes.

Virgil's Georgics retells the motif with Aristaeus capturing Proteus to learn why his bees died. Proteus is also named in some traditions as a king of Egypt and father of several children.

Legacy

From his shape-shifting, the adjective "protean" came to mean versatile, mutable, or capable of assuming many forms. The name has been given to a moon of Neptune, to the bacterial genus Proteus (whose colonies swarm across plates), to the olm (Proteus anguinus), a cave salamander, and to the medical condition Proteus syndrome.
