Christopher Francis Patten, Baron Patten of Barnes (born 12 May 1944) is a British politician who was the 28th and last Governor of Hong Kong from 1992 to 1997, overseeing the transfer of sovereignty to China. He subsequently served as a European Commissioner and as Chancellor of the University of Oxford.

Early political career

Patten was educated at St Benedict's School, Ealing, and Balliol College, Oxford. He worked for the Conservative Research Department and became MP for Bath in 1979. He held ministerial posts including Minister for Overseas Development, and as Secretary of State for the Environment introduced the Community Charge ("poll tax"). As Chairman of the Conservative Party he masterminded the party's 1992 general election victory but lost his own seat.

Governor of Hong Kong

Appointed Governor in 1992, Patten introduced democratic reforms expanding the franchise for the Legislative Council, which brought him into conflict with Beijing, whose officials denounced him; Chinese official Lu Ping famously called him a "sinner of a thousand years". He presided over the handover ceremony on 30 June 1997 and left aboard the royal yacht Britannia.

Later career

Patten chaired the Independent Commission on Policing for Northern Ireland, whose 1999 report led to the creation of the Police Service of Northern Ireland. He served as European Commissioner for External Relations (1999–2004), Chancellor of Newcastle University, and from 2003 to 2024 Chancellor of the University of Oxford. He chaired the BBC Trust from 2011 to 2014. His books include East and West (1998) and The Hong Kong Diaries (2022).
