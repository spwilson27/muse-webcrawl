Kandahar (Pashto: کندهار; Dari: قندهار) is a city in southern Afghanistan and the capital of Kandahar Province, situated on the Arghandab River at an elevation of about 1,010 metres. It is the country's second-largest city and the historic heartland of the Pashtun people. Ahmad Shah Durrani made Kandahar the first capital of the Durrani Empire in 1747, and the city has remained a political and commercial centre of the Afghan south; it was the birthplace and spiritual centre of the Taliban movement in the 1990s. History

The region around Kandahar, ancient Arachosia, was settled for millennia on trade routes between Persia, India, and Central Asia. Tradition attributes the city's founding to Alexander the Great, who founded Alexandria in Arachosia nearby. The area was ruled successively by Achaemenids, Greeks, Mauryans, Kushans, Sasanians, and Arab caliphates; Ashoka's bilingual Greek-Aramaic edicts were discovered near the city. Mongols under Genghis Khan and Timur devastated the region, and for centuries Mughals and Safavids fought over Kandahar as the gateway between their empires.

In 1709 Mirwais Hotak led a Pashtun revolt that freed Kandahar from Safavid rule; his Hotak dynasty briefly conquered Persia itself. After Nader Shah's campaigns, Ahmad Shah Durrani — crowned at Kandahar in 1747 — founded the Durrani Empire and chose the city as his capital, building its great mausoleum and the shrine housing the Cloak of Muhammad. The capital later moved to Kabul, but Kandahar remained Afghanistan's second city. It was besieged during both Anglo-Afghan wars, was the Taliban's de facto capital from 1994 to 2001, and saw heavy fighting between Taliban and NATO-backed forces after 2001 before the Taliban retook it in August 2021.

Geography and climate

Kandahar lies in a semi-arid plain irrigated by the Arghandab and other rivers. Summers are very hot, with temperatures above 40 °C, and winters mild; rainfall is low and concentrated in winter. The surrounding province is a major producer of pomegranates, grapes, and melons.

Economy

The city is a regional trading hub for agricultural produce and dried fruit and has an international airport — Kandahar International Airport — that served as a major coalition military base during the 2001–2021 war. Pomegranates from Kandahar are prized across the region and exported to Pakistan, India, and the Gulf.

Landmarks

Principal sites include the Shrine of the Cloak, which holds a cloak attributed to the Prophet Muhammad and is among the holiest sites in Afghanistan; the domed mausoleum of Ahmad Shah Durrani; the Chilzina, a rock-cut chamber with a staircase of forty steps overlooking the plain; and the ruins of Old Kandahar (Mundigak-Shahr) nearby.
