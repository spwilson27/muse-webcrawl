Martinez (cocktail)

Martinez
IBA official cocktail
Type: Cocktail
Base spirit: Gin
Served: Straight up, chilled
Standard garnish: Lemon twist
Ingredients: Gin, sweet vermouth, maraschino liqueur, bitters

The Martinez is a classic cocktail made with gin, sweet vermouth, maraschino liqueur, and bitters. It is widely regarded as the direct precursor of the Martini, and in its earliest printed forms it closely resembles the Manhattan cocktail made with gin in place of whiskey.

History

Recipes appeared in print in the 1880s: O. H. Byron's The Modern Bartenders' Guide (1884) listed a Martinez as essentially a Manhattan, while Jerry Thomas's 1887 Bartender's Guide gave a version with Old Tom gin, sweet vermouth, maraschino, and Boker's bitters. Legends variously credit its invention to bartenders in Martinez, California, or at the Occidental Hotel in San Francisco. As Old Tom gave way to drier London gin, the drink evolved toward the dry Martini.

Preparation

The International Bartenders Association recipe calls for 45 ml gin, 45 ml sweet red vermouth, one bar spoon of maraschino liqueur, and two dashes of orange bitters, stirred with ice, strained into a chilled cocktail glass, and garnished with a lemon twist.
