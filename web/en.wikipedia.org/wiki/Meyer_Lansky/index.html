Meyer Lansky

Meyer Lansky (born Meyer Suchowljansky; July 4, 1902 – January 15, 1983), known as the "Mob's Accountant", was an American organized crime figure of Polish-Jewish origin who, along with his associate Charles "Lucky" Luciano, was instrumental in the development of the National Crime Syndicate in the United States. Lansky built a gambling empire that stretched from Saratoga Springs and New Orleans to Las Vegas, Cuba and the Bahamas, and is credited with pioneering large-scale money laundering through Swiss bank accounts and offshore companies.

Early life

Lansky was born in Grodno (then in the Russian Empire) to a Polish-Jewish family and emigrated to the United States in 1911, settling on the Lower East Side of Manhattan. As a teenager he formed the Bugs and Meyer Mob with his friend Benjamin "Bugsy" Siegel, providing protection and bootlegging during Prohibition, and became a close associate of Luciano and Frank Costello.

The Syndicate and Las Vegas

Lansky was a key figure in the 1931 reorganisation of American organised crime after the Castellammarese War, and in Murder, Inc., the syndicate's enforcement arm. After the Second World War — during which the US government controversially used his underworld contacts in Operation Underworld to secure the New York docks and gather intelligence for the invasion of Sicily — Lansky backed Siegel's Flamingo hotel in Las Vegas and, after Siegel's murder in 1947, channelled syndicate money into the growing casino industry.

Cuba and decline

Under a close arrangement with Cuban dictator Fulgencio Batista, Lansky made Havana a gambling and tourism hub, opening the Hotel Riviera in 1957 and hosting the notorious Havana Conference of mob leaders in 1946. The Cuban Revolution of 1959 destroyed his island empire, costing him millions. In 1970 he fled to Israel to escape tax evasion charges; Israel refused him citizenship under the Law of Return and expelled him in 1972. Back in the United States he was acquitted or saw charges dropped in a series of trials, and he lived quietly in Miami Beach until his death from lung cancer in 1983. His estate, despite FBI claims of a hidden $300 million fortune, proved modest.

In popular culture

Lansky was the model for Hyman Roth in The Godfather Part II (1974), played by Lee Strasberg, and has been portrayed by Richard Dreyfuss (Lansky, 1999), Harvey Keitel (Lansky, 2021), and in the series Boardwalk Empire and The Making of the Mob.
