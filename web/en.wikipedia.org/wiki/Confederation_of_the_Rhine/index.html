Confederation of the Rhine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Confederation of the Rhine in 1812

The Confederation of the Rhine (German: Rheinbund) was a confederation of German client states of the First French Empire under Napoleon Bonaparte. It was formed on 12 July 1806, initially by sixteen German states after they had been mediatised and separated from the Holy Roman Empire, whose emperor, Francis II, abdicated on 6 August 1806, effectively ending the thousand-year-old Empire.

Formation and structure

The founding members included Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden, Hesse-Darmstadt, Nassau, and the newly created Grand Duchy of Berg, among others; Napoleon was its "Protector". The Confederation eventually grew to include 36 states and over 15 million inhabitants. Member states were bound to supply troops to France and undertook extensive reforms modeled on the French system — abolishing serfdom and feudal privilege, introducing the Napoleonic Code in several states, and reorganizing administration, finance, and the judiciary.

Collapse

The Confederation's armies fought alongside France in the campaigns of 1806–1813. After Napoleon's defeat at the Battle of Leipzig in October 1813, the Confederation disintegrated as member states defected to the Allies by the Treaty of Ried and similar agreements. The Congress of Vienna (1814–15) replaced it with the German Confederation of 39 states under Austrian presidency.

Legacy

Although a French satellite, the Confederation permanently reshaped Germany: the mediatisation of hundreds of small territories into a few dozen medium states and the legal reforms of the era laid groundwork that the later German Confederation and Empire inherited.
