The Sudetenland (German: Sudetenland; Czech: Sudety) is the historical name for the northern, southern and western border regions of Bohemia, Moravia and Czech Silesia in what is now the Czech Republic. The name derives from the Sudetes mountain range. Before 1945 the region was inhabited predominantly by ethnic Germans, the Sudeten Germans, whose ancestors had settled there from the 13th century onward at the invitation of Bohemian kings.

History

Origins and the interwar period

German-speaking settlement in the Bohemian borderlands grew during the medieval Ostsiedlung. After World War I, the region was incorporated into the new state of Czechoslovakia despite the wishes of many local Germans, who proclaimed provinces such as German Bohemia and Sudetenland in 1918 and sought union with German Austria. Czechoslovak troops suppressed these declarations. In the interwar republic, Sudeten Germans numbered over three million — more than the Slovak population — and grievances over language policy, land reform and the effects of the Great Depression fuelled radicalisation.

The Sudeten crisis and Munich

In the 1930s Konrad Henlein's Sudeten German Party, subsidised by Nazi Germany, demanded autonomy and then annexation to the Reich. The crisis culminated in the Munich Agreement of 30 September 1938, by which Britain, France, Italy and Germany — without Czechoslovak participation — ceded the Sudetenland to Germany. Adolf Hitler's troops occupied the region in October 1938; most of it became the Reichsgau Sudetenland, administered from Reichenberg (Liberec). Czechs, Jews and anti-Nazi Germans fled or were expelled, and after Kristallnacht the region's Jewish population was largely deported and murdered during the war.

Expulsion after 1945

Following Germany's defeat, the Beneš decrees stripped ethnic Germans of Czechoslovak citizenship and property, and some three million Sudeten Germans were expelled to Germany and Austria in 1945–46, in transfers sanctioned at the Potsdam Conference. Tens of thousands died during the "wild" expulsions and organised transfers. The border regions were resettled by Czechs, Slovaks and others, but many areas remained underpopulated for decades.

Legacy

The Sudeten question long strained Czech–German relations. The 1997 Czech–German Declaration addressed the historical grievances, and Sudeten German expellee organisations remain active in Bavaria. The term "Sudetenland" itself is avoided in official Czech usage, where pohraničí ("borderlands") is preferred.
