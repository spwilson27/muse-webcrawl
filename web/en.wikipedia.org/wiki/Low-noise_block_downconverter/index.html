Low-noise block downconverter

Low-noise block downconverter

Abbreviation: LNB

Location: Focal point of a satellite dish

Function: Amplify and downconvert satellite signals to L-band

A low-noise block downconverter (LNB) is the receiving device mounted on satellite dishes used for satellite TV reception, which collects the radio waves from the dish and converts them to a signal which is sent through a cable to the receiver inside the building. Also called a low-noise block, low-noise converter (LNC), or even low-noise downconverter (LND), the device is sometimes inaccurately called a low-noise amplifier (LNA).

Operation

The satellite signal is very weak by the time it reaches the dish, so the LNB amplifies it with a low-noise amplifier before mixing it with a local oscillator signal to downconvert the high microwave frequencies (e.g. the Ku band, 10.7–12.75 GHz) to a lower intermediate frequency in the L band (950–2150 MHz), which can travel through ordinary coaxial cable with acceptable loss. The LNB is powered through the same coaxial cable from the receiver.

Types

Universal LNBs, standard in Europe, switch between polarisations using the supply voltage (13 V vertical / 18 V horizontal) and between high and low bands using a 22 kHz tone. Multi-output LNBs — twin, quad and octo — feed several receivers; Unicable (SCR) LNBs allow many receivers to share one cable. Monoblock LNBs contain two feedhorns aimed at two nearby satellite positions.
