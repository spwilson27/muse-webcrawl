Nagaland

Capital: Kohima

Largest city: Dimapur

Area: 16,579 km²

Population (2011): ~1.98 million

Statehood: 1 December 1963

Nagaland (/ˈnɑːɡəlænd/) is a state in the north-eastern region of India, bordered by Arunachal Pradesh to the north, Assam to the west, Manipur to the south, and the Sagaing Region of Myanmar to the east. Its capital is Kohima and its largest city is Dimapur. Predominantly mountainous, it is home to the Naga peoples, comprising 16 major tribes and many sub-tribes, each with distinct languages and customs.

History

The Naga Hills came under British administration in the 19th century after a series of expeditions and annexations. During the Second World War, the Battle of Kohima (1944) — fought around the deputy commissioner's tennis court — halted the Japanese advance into India and is remembered as a turning point of the Burma campaign. After Indian independence, Naga leaders pressed for sovereignty; following years of insurgency, the State of Nagaland was created on 1 December 1963 under the Sixteen-Point Agreement, with special protections for Naga customary law under Article 371A of the Indian Constitution. Peace talks with Naga insurgent groups, notably the NSCN, have continued for decades under a ceasefire dating to 1997.

Culture

Nagaland is predominantly Christian (mostly Baptist), a legacy of 19th- and 20th-century American missionaries, making it one of India's few Christian-majority states. English is the official language, while Nagamese creole serves as a lingua franca. The ten-day Hornbill Festival, held each December at the Naga Heritage Village near Kohima, showcases the music, dance, dress and cuisine of the tribes and is one of India's best-known cultural festivals.

Geography and economy

The state is mostly hilly, with Mount Saramati (3,826 m) its highest peak. Agriculture — rice, maize, millets, pulses and shifting (jhum) cultivation — employs most of the population; the state is also known for its biodiversity, including the Blyth's tragopan, its state bird.
