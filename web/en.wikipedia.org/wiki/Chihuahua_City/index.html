Chihuahua (Spanish pronunciation: [tʃiˈwawa]) is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Chihuahua. Founded in 1709 by Antonio Deza y Ulloa as San Francisco de Cuéllar, it grew as a mining and commercial centre on the route between Mexico City and Santa Fe. The city's historic centre features the baroque Chihuahua Cathedral, begun in 1725.

History

During the War of Independence, the insurgent priest Miguel Hidalgo was captured and executed in the city in 1811; his shrine stands in the Plaza Hidalgo. The city was twice the seat of Benito Juárez's government during the French intervention, and in the Mexican Revolution it was a stronghold of Pancho Villa, whose home, Quinta Luz, is now a museum. The state dog, the Chihuahua breed, is named after the state.

Economy and geography

Situated at an elevation of about 1,433 metres where the Chuviscar and Sacramento rivers meet, Chihuahua has an arid to semi-arid climate. In recent decades its economy has shifted toward manufacturing, with numerous maquiladora plants in aerospace, automotive and electronics, alongside cattle ranching in the surrounding region.
