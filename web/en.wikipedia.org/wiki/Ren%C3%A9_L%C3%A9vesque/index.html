René Lévesque (24 August 1922 – 1 November 1987) was a Canadian journalist and politician who served as the 23rd premier of Quebec from 1976 to 1985. He was the founder of the Parti Québécois and the leading figure of the Quebec sovereignty movement, presiding over the 1980 Quebec referendum on sovereignty-association.

Early career

Raised in New Carlisle in the Gaspé region, Lévesque worked as a broadcaster for Radio-Canada and reported from Korea during the Korean War. Entering politics with the Quebec Liberal Party in 1960, he became a key minister in Jean Lesage's Quiet Revolution government and drove the nationalization of electricity under Hydro-Québec.

Premier of Quebec

After founding the Parti Québécois in 1968, Lévesque led it to victory in 1976. His government enacted the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101), reformed political party financing, and held the 1980 referendum, in which 59.6 percent of voters rejected sovereignty-association. He remained premier until 1985. His phrase on referendum night, "À la prochaine fois" ("Until next time"), became famous.
