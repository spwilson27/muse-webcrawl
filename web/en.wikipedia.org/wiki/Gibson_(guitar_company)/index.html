Gibson (guitar company)

Gibson, Inc. (formerly Gibson Guitar Corporation) is an American manufacturer of guitars and other musical instruments, best known for producing the Les Paul guitar. The company was formerly known as Gibson Guitar Corporation and renamed Gibson Brands, Inc. on June 11, 2013. The company's headquarters are located in Nashville, Tennessee.

Industry: Musical instruments
Founded: October 11, 1902
Founder: Orville Gibson
Headquarters: Nashville, Tennessee, U.S.
Products: Electric and acoustic guitars, basses

History

Orville Gibson founded the company in 1902 as the Gibson Mandolin-Guitar Mfg. Co. Ltd. in Kalamazoo, Michigan, to make mandolin-family instruments. Gibson invented archtop guitars by constructing the same type of carved, arched tops used on violins. By the 1930s, the company was also making flattop acoustic guitars and became one of the first companies to produce commercially available hollow-body electric guitars, used and popularized by Charlie Christian.

In 1952, Gibson introduced its first solid-body electric guitar, the Les Paul, designed in collaboration with guitarist Les Paul. The company later introduced the ES-335 semi-hollow guitar in 1958 and iconic shapes such as the Flying V, Explorer, and Firebird. Gibson was owned by Norlin Corporation from 1969 to 1986, a period often criticized for declining quality, before a revival under new ownership.

Bankruptcy and revival

After years of expansion into consumer electronics, Gibson filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in May 2018. The company emerged from bankruptcy in November 2018 under new leadership, refocusing on its core guitar business and heritage brands including Epiphone, Kramer, and Mesa/Boogie.
