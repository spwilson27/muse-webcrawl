Ellen DeGeneres

Ellen Lee DeGeneres (born January 26, 1958) is an American comedian, television host, actress and writer. She starred in the sitcom Ellen (1994–1998) and hosted the syndicated talk show The Ellen DeGeneres Show (2003–2022), winning numerous Daytime Emmy Awards and becoming one of the most recognisable figures in American entertainment.

Early life and stand-up

DeGeneres was born in Metairie, Louisiana, and worked jobs including waitressing and house painting before performing stand-up comedy in the early 1980s. A successful appearance on The Tonight Show in 1986 — Johnny Carson invited her to sit down after her set, a distinction rarely given to a first-time comic — launched her television career.

Ellen and coming out

Her sitcom Ellen made television history in 1997 with "The Puppy Episode", in which her character came out as a lesbian, shortly after DeGeneres herself came out on the cover of Time magazine ("Yep, I'm Gay"). The episode won a Peabody Award and was watched by some 42 million viewers, though the show was cancelled the following year amid controversy and falling ratings.

Talk show and other work

The Ellen DeGeneres Show premiered in 2003 and ran for 19 seasons, known for celebrity interviews, games, giveaway segments and DeGeneres's dancing. She voiced Dory in Pixar's Finding Nemo (2003) and Finding Dory (2016), hosted the Academy Awards twice (2007 and 2014 — the latter famous for a record-breaking selfie), and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016. In 2020 the show faced allegations of a toxic workplace, after which DeGeneres apologised on air; the programme ended in 2022. She is married to actress Portia de Rossi.
