Boston Marathon bombing

The Boston Marathon bombing was a domestic terrorist attack that took place during the annual Boston Marathon on April 15, 2013. Two homemade pressure-cooker bombs detonated near the finish line, killing three people and injuring hundreds of others, including 17 who lost limbs.

Boston Marathon bombing
Date: April 15, 2013
Location: Boylston Street, Boston
Deaths: 3 (at bombing); more in manhunt
Perpetrators: Dzhokhar and Tamerlan Tsarnaev

Attack

The two bombs exploded about 12 seconds and 210 yards apart at 2:49 p.m. EDT, near the finish line on Boylston Street in Boston's Back Bay. The victims killed were Krystle Campbell, 29; Lingzi Lu, 23, a Boston University graduate student; and Martin Richard, 8.

Manhunt

Brothers Dzhokhar and Tamerlan Tsarnaev were identified from surveillance footage. On April 18–19 they killed MIT police officer Sean Collier, carjacked a driver, and fought a gun battle with police in Watertown, during which Tamerlan was killed. After a day-long shutdown of the Boston area, Dzhokhar was found hiding in a boat in a Watertown backyard and captured.

Aftermath

Dzhokhar Tsarnaev was convicted on all 30 federal counts in 2015 and sentenced to death; his sentence was vacated on appeal in 2020 and reinstated by the U.S. Supreme Court in 2022. The slogan "Boston Strong" became a symbol of the city's response, and April 15 is now observed as One Boston Day.
