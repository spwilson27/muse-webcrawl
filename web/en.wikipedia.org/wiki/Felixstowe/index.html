Felixstowe is a port town and civil parish in Suffolk, England. It lies at the mouth of the River Orwell, opposite Harwich, and grew from a small village into a Victorian seaside resort after the arrival of the railway in 1877. The Port of Felixstowe, on the town's southern edge, is the United Kingdom's busiest container port, handling a large share of the country's containerised trade.

History

The area shows evidence of Roman occupation, and Landguard Fort guarded the harbour entrance from the 16th century onward. In the early 20th century Felixstowe was a fashionable resort and, during the First World War, hosted a Royal Naval Air Service seaplane station. Wallis Simpson stayed in the town in 1936 while awaiting her divorce during the abdication crisis.

The port and town today

The container port, developed from the 1960s and now operated by Hutchison Ports, includes some of the world's largest ship-to-shore cranes. The town retains Edwardian seafront gardens, a pier, and the Landguard Peninsula nature reserve.
