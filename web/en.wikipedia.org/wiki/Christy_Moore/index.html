Christopher Andrew "Christy" Moore (born 7 May 1945) is an Irish folk singer, songwriter and guitarist, one of the founding figures of the modern Irish folk revival. He was a founding member of both Planxty (1972) and Moving Hearts (1981), and his solo repertoire — including "Ride On", "The Voyage" and "Lisdoonvarna" — has made him one of Ireland's best-loved performers. In 2007 he was named Ireland's greatest living musician in RTÉ's People of the Year Awards.

Early life

Moore was born in Newbridge, County Kildare, the son of a Fine Gael election agent. He worked as a bank clerk before moving to England in 1966, where he played the folk clubs of the north and recorded his first album, Paddy on the Road (1969).

Planxty and Moving Hearts

Returning to Ireland, Moore formed Planxty with Andy Irvine, Dónal Lunny and Liam O'Flynn; the band's fusion of song and instrumental dance music transformed Irish traditional music. After Planxty's first incarnation ended, Moore recorded the landmark solo album Prosperous (1972) and Whatever Tickles Your Fancy (1975), then co-founded Moving Hearts, which brought rock instrumentation and explicitly political songs to the tradition.

Solo career

Moore's solo albums of the 1980s — Ride On (1984), Ordinary Man (1985), Voyage (1989) — contain many of his signature songs, frequently championing the marginalised: Travellers, emigrants, striking workers and prisoners. His version of "Fairytale of New York" and his live album At the Point: Live (1994) were major commercial successes. Moore has struggled publicly with alcoholism and depression, themes treated candidly in his memoir One Voice: My Life in Song (2003).
