Tower restaurant

A tower restaurant is a restaurant located at or near the top of a tall tower or skyscraper, offering diners panoramic views of the surrounding city or landscape. Many tower restaurants are revolving restaurants, whose circular dining floor slowly rotates — typically completing one revolution every 30 to 90 minutes — so that all guests can enjoy the full panorama without leaving their seats.

The concept flourished in the mid-20th century, when telecommunications towers and observation towers were built in cities around the world. Restaurants were often placed just below the antenna section, combined with observation decks as a source of revenue for the tower.

Notable examples

Well-known tower restaurants include the restaurant in the Fernsehturm in Berlin, the Skylon Tower restaurant overlooking Niagara Falls, the Stratosphere tower in Las Vegas, the CN Tower's 360 Restaurant in Toronto, and the restaurant atop the Eiffel Tower in Paris. The Top of the World restaurant in New York's World Trade Center was destroyed in the September 11 attacks.

Design

Revolving restaurants use a turntable floor ring that rotates around a fixed central core containing stairs, lifts and kitchens. The rotation is driven by a small electric motor and proceeds at a barely perceptible speed. Non-revolving tower restaurants rely on floor-to-ceiling windows arranged around the perimeter.

History

One of the earliest purpose-built revolving restaurants was the Florian Tower (Florianturm) restaurant in Dortmund, Germany, opened in 1959, followed by the Cairo Tower and the Space Needle's restaurant in Seattle, built for the 1962 World's Fair. The fashion peaked in the 1960s and 1970s; many examples have since closed or been converted to observation use, though the type remains popular in Asia.
