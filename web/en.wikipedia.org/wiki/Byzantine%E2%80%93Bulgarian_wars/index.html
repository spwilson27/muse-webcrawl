Byzantine–Bulgarian wars

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Byzantine–Bulgarian wars were a series of conflicts fought between the Byzantine Empire and the Bulgarian Empire, stretching intermittently from the late 7th century, when the Bulgars first settled in the Balkans, until the 14th century.

Early wars

After Khan Asparuh founded the First Bulgarian Empire in 681, Bulgarian rulers expanded southward at Byzantine expense. Under Krum (803–814) the Bulgarians annihilated an imperial army and killed Emperor Nikephoros I at the Battle of Pliska in 811. Under Simeon I (893–927) Bulgaria reached its greatest extent and Simeon campaigned repeatedly against Constantinople, styling himself Emperor of the Bulgarians and the Romans.

Conquest and restoration

Emperor Basil II, remembered as the "Bulgar-Slayer", conquered Bulgaria by 1018 after his victory at Kleidion (1014), ending the First Bulgarian Empire. Bulgaria was restored in 1185 with the uprising of Asen and Peter, and under Ivan Asen II the Second Bulgarian Empire again became the leading Balkan power, defeating the Empire of Thessalonica at Klokotnitsa in 1230. Later centuries saw intermittent warfare until both empires fell to the Ottomans.
