United Nations Secretariat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The United Nations Secretariat is one of the six principal organs of the United Nations, the others being the General Assembly, the Security Council, the Economic and Social Council, the Trusteeship Council and the International Court of Justice. Headed by the Secretary-General and staffed by an international civil service (tens of thousands across headquarters in New York and offices in Geneva, Vienna, Nairobi and the field), it carries out the day-to-day work of the organization as mandated by the member states.

Work and character

The Secretariat administers peacekeeping and political missions, services conferences and treaty deposits, runs departments for political and peacebuilding affairs, humanitarian coordination (OCHA), economic and social affairs, management and safety — and performs the quiet functions on which the system runs (translation in six official languages, statistics, legal counsel). Charter Article 100 obliges staff independence — neither seeking nor receiving instructions from any government — and Article 101 balances efficiency with equitable geographical distribution, the twin commitments (merit versus representation, loyalty versus independence) that drive its perennial personnel politics. Secretariat officials draft the reports and options from which intergovernmental decisions are made, an agenda-setting power that makes the bureaucracy itself a diplomatic actor — praised as the world's conscience's staff, attacked as an unaccountable mandarinate, and audited by both.
