Signage

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Examples of wayfinding and commercial signage.

Signage is the design or use of signs and symbols to communicate a message. Signage also means signs collectively or being considered as a group. The term has been popular in recent years and is used in contexts including wayfinding, branding, traffic control, and safety.

Purpose

Signage serves purposes such as:

  • Information — indicating services and facilities, such as maps, directories, and opening hours.
  • Direction / wayfinding — guiding people to locations, as in street signs and exit signs.
  • Identification — naming places and buildings, such as building name signs and badging.
  • Safety and regulation — warnings and instructions, such as traffic signs, fire exit signs, and no-smoking notices.
  • Advertising and branding — promoting businesses and products.

Types

Signage may be static or digital (including LED and LCD displays), illuminated or non-illuminated, and produced in materials from metal and acrylic to vinyl banners. Conventions such as pictograms, standardized colors (e.g., red for prohibition, green for emergency exits), and legibility standards govern many categories, particularly traffic and safety signs governed by instruments such as the Vienna Convention on Road Signs and Signals and the U.S. Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices.

History

Signs have been used since ancient times — Roman milestones, medieval trade signs, and inn signs hung perpendicular to streets identified businesses in eras of low literacy. Modern signage developed alongside advertising, electrification (neon signs), and standardized road networks.
