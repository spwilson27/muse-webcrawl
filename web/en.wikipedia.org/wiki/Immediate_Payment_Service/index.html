Immediate Payment Service

Immediate Payment Service (IMPS) is an instant payment inter-bank electronic funds transfer system in India. IMPS offers an inter-bank electronic fund transfer service through mobile phones and internet banking. The service is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including on bank holidays — unlike the older NEFT and RTGS systems which originally operated only during banking hours.

History

IMPS was developed and is operated by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI). It was publicly launched on 22 November 2010, after a pilot with a small group of banks including the State Bank of India, ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank and others. It later provided the technological foundation on which the Unified Payments Interface (UPI), launched in 2016, was built; the two systems share much of their underlying infrastructure.

How it works

Customers register their mobile number with their bank and receive a Mobile Money Identifier (MMID), a seven-digit code. Transfers can be made using the beneficiary's mobile number and MMID, or using an account number with the bank's IFS code. Transactions are settled in near real time, subject to per-transaction and daily limits set by banks and by the Reserve Bank of India; IMPS supports person-to-person, person-to-account, merchant and request-based transactions, accessible through mobile banking apps, internet banking, ATMs and bank branches.

Growth

IMPS volumes grew steadily into hundreds of millions of transactions per month, and the system has been cited internationally as an early example of a large-scale, low-cost instant payment infrastructure.
