Gaffer (filmmaking)

A gaffer in the motion picture and television industries is the head electrician, responsible for the execution (and sometimes design) of the lighting plan for a production. The term originates from British slang for a supervisor or foreman, and in film it came to be applied to the chief lighting technician who manages the electrical crew and lighting equipment.

The gaffer works closely with the director of photography (cinematographer) to realise the intended visual style of each scene, selecting lighting instruments, positioning them, and shaping the light with flags, gels, diffusion and other modifiers. The gaffer's chief assistant is the best boy electric, who manages the crew, equipment and logistics.

Role and responsibilities

The gaffer's duties include planning the electrical distribution for the set, rigging lights, ensuring electrical safety, and executing lighting changes between setups. On smaller productions the gaffer may also design the lighting scheme; on larger ones the design is led by the cinematographer and realised by the gaffer. The lighting department under the gaffer works alongside the grip department, headed by the key grip, which handles rigging and light-shaping equipment that is not itself electrically powered.

Etymology

"Gaffer" is a contraction of "godfather" and was a general term of respect for an older man or a boss before its specific film-industry usage. Another folk explanation links it to the gaff hook once used to adjust overhead lights in early theatres and studios.
