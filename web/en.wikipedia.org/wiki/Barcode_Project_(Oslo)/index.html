Barcode Project (Oslo)

The Barcode Project is a section of the Bjørvika waterfront redevelopment in Oslo, Norway, consisting of a row of distinct high-rise buildings built between Oslo Central Station's tracks and the Oslofjord. The master plan, won in competition in 2003 by the Dutch firm MVRDV with Norwegian partners, arranges a series of long, thin towers separated by narrow gaps so that, seen from a distance, the alternating buildings and slots resemble the bars of a barcode.

Buildings

Developed largely by Oslo S Utvikling and completed through the 2010s, the project comprises about a dozen buildings housing offices, housing and cultural functions. Major tenants have included the consultancy firms Deloitte, PwC and KPMG, the bank DNB, and media and technology companies; individual buildings were designed by practices including MVRDV, A-lab, Snøhetta and Lund Hagem. The Barcode forms the commercial core of the Fjord City (Fjordbyen) programme, which also produced the nearby Oslo Opera House and the MUNCH museum.

Criticism

The project was controversial from the outset. Critics argued that the tall slab buildings form a wall cutting the city off from the fjord and overshadowing the medieval park and the Ekeberg hillside, while supporters praised the density near the central station and the preservation of view corridors through the gaps between towers.
