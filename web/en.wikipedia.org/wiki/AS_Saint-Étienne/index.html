AS Saint-Étienne

Association Sportive de Saint-Étienne Loire, commonly known as AS Saint-Étienne or simply Saint-Étienne, is a French professional football club based in Saint-Étienne, in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region. Founded in 1933, the club is one of the most successful in French football history, having won ten Ligue 1 titles, most famously during a golden era in the 1960s and 1970s. Nicknamed Les Verts (The Greens) for their green shirts, they play at the Stade Geoffroy-Guichard and contest the Derby du Rhône with Olympique Lyonnais.

History

The club grew out of a sports association formed by employees of the Casino grocery chain in 1919 and turned professional in 1933, adopting green — the Casino chain's colour — as its shirt. Saint-Étienne's greatest period came between 1957 and 1981, winning ten league championships and six Coupes de France, and reaching the 1976 European Cup final, where a side featuring Dominique Rocheteau and a young Michel Platini era squad lost narrowly to Bayern Munich at Hampden Park. Financial scandal in the early 1980s (the caisse noire affair) led to decline and relegations, and the club has alternated between divisions since, winning the Coupe de la Ligue in 2013.

Supporters and stadium

The Stade Geoffroy-Guichard, nicknamed le Chaudron (the Cauldron), is renowned for its atmosphere, and the club retains one of France's largest and most loyal fanbases despite decades outside the top honours.
