Macaronic language

Macaronic language is any expression using a mixture of two or more languages, especially bilingual puns or situations in which the speaker intentionally switches languages mid-sentence. The term derives from the Italian maccherone (a kind of dumpling or pasta), used pejoratively for a coarse mixture.

History

Macaronic verse flourished in Renaissance Italy, where poets such as Teofilo Folengo mixed Latin with Italian vernacular, giving Latin endings to Italian words for comic effect. The form was later used by writers including Geoffrey Chaucer and James Joyce, and survives in modern mixed-language texts such as Spanglish and Hinglish lyrics and comedy.
