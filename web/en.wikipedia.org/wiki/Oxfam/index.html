Oxfam is a British-founded confederation of independent charitable organisations working in more than 90 countries to fight poverty, inequality and injustice. Its name derives from the Oxford Committee for Famine Relief, founded in Oxford, England, in 1942; "OXFAM" was the committee's telegraphic address, adopted as its name in 1965.

History

The Oxford Committee for Famine Relief was founded on 5 October 1942 by a group of Quakers, academics and social activists — among them Canon Theodore Richard Milford and the former League of Nations official Gilbert Murray — to press the British government to allow food relief through the Allied blockade to the starving civilian population of Axis-occupied Greece. After the war the committee continued, sending aid to refugees and famine victims worldwide, and inspired sister committees in Canada, the United States and elsewhere.

In 1995 a group of Oxfam organisations formed Oxfam International as a formal confederation and secretariat; it now comprises some 21 member affiliates. The confederation combines emergency relief (water, sanitation, shelter and food in disasters and conflicts), long-term development programmes, and campaigning on trade rules, climate change, land rights and economic inequality — its annual inequality reports, timed to the Davos World Economic Forum, are widely covered.

Shops and fundraising

Oxfam GB pioneered the modern charity shop: its first permanent shop opened in Broad Street, Oxford, in 1948, and the Oxfam shop network — staffed largely by volunteers and selling donated second-hand goods — became a fixture of British high streets and an important income stream. Affiliates also run fair-trade retail and large public fundraising events.

Criticism and the 2018 scandal

In 2018 it emerged that senior Oxfam staff in Haiti after the 2010 earthquake had engaged in sexual exploitation and that the organisation had not fully disclosed this to regulators; the scandal led to government funding suspensions, an inquiry by the Charity Commission, a major loss of supporters, and reforms in safeguarding across the aid sector. Oxfam apologised and restructured; its Oxfam GB arm was temporarily barred from new UK government funding until 2021.
