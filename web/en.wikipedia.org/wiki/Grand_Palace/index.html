Grand Palace

The Grand Palace in Bangkok has been the official residence of the Kings of Siam since 1782.

The Grand Palace (Thai: พระบรมมหาราชวัง) is a complex of buildings at the heart of Bangkok, Thailand. The palace has been the official residence of the Kings of Siam (and later Thailand) since 1782. The king, his court and his royal government were based on the grounds of the palace until 1925. Although the present monarch no longer resides there, the Grand Palace is still used for official events and ceremonies.

History

Construction began on 6 May 1782 by order of King Phutthayotfa Chulalok (Rama I), who had moved the capital from Thonburi across the Chao Phraya River to Bangkok. Successive kings added new buildings and pavilions, giving the palace its eclectic mixture of traditional Thai and European-influenced architecture. The complex is roughly rectangular, covers about 218,000 square metres, and is surrounded by four walls 1,900 metres in length.

Layout

The palace is divided into quarters: the Outer Court, once housing government departments; the Middle Court, with the great state halls including the Chakri Maha Prasat and Dusit Maha Prasat throne halls; and the Inner Court, historically reserved for the king's female household. Within the palace grounds stands the Temple of the Emerald Buddha, Wat Phra Kaew, Thailand's most sacred Buddhist temple, housing the Emerald Buddha statue carved from a single block of jade.

Today

The Grand Palace is one of Bangkok's most famous landmarks and a major tourist attraction, with parts open as a museum. Visitors must observe a strict dress code. Royal ceremonies, including coronations and state funerals, continue to be held within the complex.
