Chameleons or chamaeleons (family Chamaeleonidae) are a distinctive and highly specialized clade of Old World lizards with about 200 species described as of 2015. These species come in a range of colors, and many species have the ability to change color. Chameleons are distinguished by their zygodactylous feet, their prehensile tails, their laterally compressed bodies, their head casques, and their long, rapidly extensible tongues. Almost half of all chameleon species live on the island of Madagascar.

Colour change

Some chameleon species are able to change their skin coloration. Different chameleon species are able to vary their coloration and pattern through combinations of pink, blue, red, orange, green, black, brown, light blue, yellow, turquoise, and purple. Colour change is effected by special cells called chromatophores, which contain pigment and can expand or contract under hormonal and nervous control. Colour change serves functions in camouflage, but is also used for social signalling and in reactions to temperature and light.

Vision

Chameleons have the most distinctive eyes of any reptile. The upper and lower eyelids are joined, with only a pinhole large enough for the pupil to see through. Each eye can pivot and focus independently, allowing the chameleon to observe two different objects simultaneously with 360-degree vision. Their eyesight is acute, allowing them to spot small insects from 5 to 10 metres away.

Tongue

The chameleon's tongue can be up to twice the length of its body and is launched ballistically using a specialized accelerator muscle, projecting in as little as 0.07 seconds to snare prey with a sticky tip. Small chameleons project their tongues with accelerations exceeding 2,600 m/s².

Distribution and habitat

Chameleons are adapted for climbing and visual hunting in warm habitats, from rainforest to savanna, montane forest and scrub, mainly in Madagascar and mainland Africa, with a few species in southern Europe and Asia.
