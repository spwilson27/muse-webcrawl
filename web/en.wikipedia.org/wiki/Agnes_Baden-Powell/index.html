Agnes Baden-Powell

Agnes Smyth Baden-Powell (16 December 1858 – 2 June 1945) was the youngest sister of Robert Baden-Powell, founder of the Scout Movement. At her brother's request she took charge of the organisation of the Girl Guides in 1910, serving as its first president and shaping the new movement for girls in its formative years.

Early life

Agnes was the youngest daughter and tenth child of the Reverend Professor Baden Powell, Savilian Professor of Geometry at Oxford, and his wife Henrietta Grace Smyth. Her father died when she was two, and the family added "Baden" to its surname in his memory. Like her brothers she grew up in a household that prized outdoor pursuits, natural history, and music; Agnes was an accomplished musician, playing organ, piano, and violin, and became a knowledgeable amateur naturalist and beekeeper.

Founding the Girl Guides

When Robert Baden-Powell launched the Boy Scouts in 1908, large numbers of girls began turning up at scout events, calling themselves "Girl Scouts". Deciding that girls needed an organisation of their own, conducted in a way suited to the social expectations of Edwardian Britain, Baden-Powell asked his sister to lead it. In 1910 Agnes became the first president of the Girl Guide Association and adapted the scouting scheme for girls, writing the first Guide handbook, The Handbook for the Girl Guides, or How Girls Can Help to Build Up the Empire (1912), adapted from Scouting for Boys with the help of her brother.

Her task was delicate: many contemporaries thought camping, hiking, and outdoor skills unwomanly, and she deliberately presented guiding as training in homemaking, nursing, citizenship, and character as much as outdoor adventure, which helped secure public acceptance for the young movement.

Later years

In 1917, as guiding internationalised and views shifted, Agnes stepped aside in favour of Princess Mary as president, while Olave Baden-Powell, her brother's wife, became Chief Guide and the movement's driving force. Agnes remained involved as a vice-president and continued to support guiding and charitable work. She never married. She died in London on 2 June 1945, aged 86.

Legacy

Agnes Baden-Powell's organising work in 1910–1917 laid the institutional foundation on which the World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts (WAGGGS) was later built. Her handbook remained in use in revised forms for decades, and she is commemorated within the guiding movement as its first president.
