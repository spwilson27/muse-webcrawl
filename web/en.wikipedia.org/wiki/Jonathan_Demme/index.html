Jonathan Demme

Robert Jonathan Demme (February 22, 1944 – April 26, 2017) was an American film director, producer, and screenwriter. He won the Academy Award for Best Director for The Silence of the Lambs (1991), one of only three films to win the "Big Five" Academy Awards. His diverse body of work ranged from exploitation films for Roger Corman to acclaimed dramas, concert films, and documentaries.

Early life

Demme was born in Baldwin, New York, and grew up in Rockville Centre and Miami, Florida. He attended Southwestern Miami High School and the University of Florida. He began his film career working for producer Roger Corman, co-writing and producing exploitation films before making his directorial debut with the women-in-prison film Caged Heat (1974).

Career

Demme gained critical recognition with Melvin and Howard (1980), which won two Academy Awards, and the Talking Heads concert film Stop Making Sense (1984), often cited as one of the greatest concert films ever made. He followed with the comedies Something Wild (1986) and Married to the Mob (1988).

The Silence of the Lambs (1991), adapted from Thomas Harris's novel, won Academy Awards for Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor (Anthony Hopkins), Best Actress (Jodie Foster), and Best Adapted Screenplay. Demme's next film, Philadelphia (1993), was one of the first major Hollywood films to address the AIDS crisis and won Tom Hanks his first Oscar. Later works included Beloved (1998), The Manchurian Candidate (2004), Rachel Getting Married (2008), and a series of Neil Young concert documentaries.

Death

Demme died in New York City on April 26, 2017, at age 73, from complications of esophageal cancer and heart disease.
