Heinrich Emil Albert Knoevenagel (18 June 1865 – 11 August 1921) was a German chemist best known for discovering the Knoevenagel condensation reaction, a fundamental carbon–carbon bond-forming reaction in organic synthesis.

Life and career

Knoevenagel studied chemistry at Göttingen, where he worked under Viktor Meyer and obtained his doctorate in 1889. He moved with Meyer to Heidelberg, where he habilitated in 1892 and became a professor in 1900, later succeeding to a chair of chemistry there. He served as a captain in the German army during the First World War.

The Knoevenagel condensation

In work published in the 1890s, Knoevenagel showed that aldehydes and ketones condense with compounds bearing an activated methylene group (such as malonic esters) in the presence of a weakly basic amine catalyst, with loss of water, giving α,β-unsaturated products. A celebrated variant, developed by Walter Doebner, uses malonic acid in pyridine and is accompanied by decarboxylation. The reaction remains a standard tool for building carbon skeletons in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals, fragrances and fine chemicals.
