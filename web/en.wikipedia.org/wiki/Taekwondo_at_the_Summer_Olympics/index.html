Taekwondo has been a core sport of the Summer Olympic Games since the 2000 Games in Sydney, Australia. The Korean martial art first appeared as a demonstration sport at the 1988 Seoul Olympics and again at Barcelona 1992. The tournament is governed by World Taekwondo (formerly the World Taekwondo Federation). Competitors fight one-on-one bouts in weight divisions, scoring points with kicks and punches to the trunk and head while wearing electronic protective scoring equipment.

Each event is a single-elimination bracket of three two-minute rounds; athletes beaten by a finalist enter a repechage for two bronze medals. Scoring is registered by electronic sensors in the trunk protector and headgear, supplemented by judges and video replay. Head kicks and turning (spinning) techniques earn more points, encouraging the spectacular aerial techniques for which the sport is known. A field of about 128 athletes competes at each Games, with qualification through world rankings, continental tournaments, and host and universality places.

History and notable results

South Korea, the sport's homeland, dominated the early Olympic tournaments, but the medal table has broadened considerably: China, Chinese Taipei, the United States, Mexico, Thailand, Iran, Great Britain, and many others have produced champions. Notable figures include Steven López of the United States, the first Olympic champion at 68 kg in 2000 and again in 2004; Hadi Saei of Iran and Hwang Kyung-seon of South Korea, multiple gold medalists; Jade Jones of Great Britain, champion in 2012 and 2016; and Panipak Wongpattanakit of Thailand, gold medalist in Tokyo and Paris. At the Paris 2024 Games, medals were shared among an especially wide range of nations, reflecting the sport's global depth.

Medal table summary

South Korea leads the all-time Olympic taekwondo medal table, followed by China and other major powers including Great Britain, Chinese Taipei, Iran, the United States, and Thailand. Cuba's Ulises Rosales and others helped make the sport one of the most internationally distributed medal programs in the Games.
