Forward (rugby)

In rugby football, the forwards are the players who form the pack in the scrum and contest possession in set pieces and close contact. They are generally larger and stronger than the backs, whose main roles involve running and passing in open play.

Rugby union

A rugby union team fields eight forwards, numbered 1–8: the front row (loosehead prop 1, hooker 2, tighthead prop 3), the second row or locks (4 and 5), and the back row (blindside flanker 6, openside flanker 7, and number eight). The forwards take part in scrums and lineouts, drive mauls, and compete at rucks. Collectively the forward pack is judged on set-piece dominance, which provides the platform for the backs.

Rugby league

In rugby league, a team has six forwards, numbered 8–13: two props, a hooker, two second-row forwards, and a loose forward (lock). Scrums in league are less contested, so league forwards emphasize ball-carrying and defence, though they perform the same numbering-backwards convention (forwards wear the higher numbers in league, reflecting the removal of flankers from the scrum positions).
