Crosshead

A crosshead is a mechanical element used in large reciprocating engines and compressors to join the piston rod to the connecting rod. It slides within guides that absorb the sideways thrust generated by the angular motion of the connecting rod, so that the piston rod and piston move in a purely linear path.

Function

In a trunk-piston engine the piston skirt itself takes the side thrust, but in large engines — particularly slow-speed marine diesel engines and steam engines — a separate crosshead bearing is used. The piston rod is rigidly attached to the crosshead block, which carries slippers sliding between guide surfaces; the connecting rod's small end pivots on the crosshead pin. This arrangement allows the cylinder to be sealed with a diaphragm and keeps crankcase oil separate from the combustion space.

Applications

Crossheads were universal in reciprocating steam engines, including steam locomotives and marine engines, and remain standard in large two-stroke marine diesel engines that propel most ocean-going ships. They are also used in large reciprocating compressors and pumps.
