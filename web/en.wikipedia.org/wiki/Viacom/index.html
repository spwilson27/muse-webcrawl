Viacom Inc. (an abbreviation of Video & Audio Communications) was an American multinational mass media and entertainment conglomerate. Founded in 1952 as the television syndication division of CBS, it was spun off in 1971 and renamed Viacom. Under chairman Sumner Redstone, who took control in 1987 through National Amusements, Viacom grew through acquisitions including MTV Networks (1985), Paramount Pictures (1994), Blockbuster Video (1994), and CBS itself (2000).

History

Viacom began as CBS's syndication arm, distributing reruns of network programmes. After the FCC's financial interest and syndication rules forced CBS to divest it, Viacom became independent in 1971 and expanded into cable television, launching or acquiring channels including MTV, Nickelodeon, Showtime and The Movie Channel. The 1994 merger with Paramount Communications brought the Paramount film studio, theme parks and the Simon & Schuster publishing house. In 2000 Viacom acquired CBS in what was then the largest media merger in history.

Split

On December 31, 2005, the original Viacom split into two publicly traded companies: a new CBS Corporation, which retained the broadcast network, television production and Infinity Broadcasting, and a new Viacom, which took the cable networks and Paramount Pictures. The two companies recombined in 2019 as ViacomCBS, later renamed Paramount Global.
