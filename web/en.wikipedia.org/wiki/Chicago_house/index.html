Chicago house

Chicago house, also known simply as house music, is a genre of electronic dance music created by DJs and music producers in Chicago in the early 1980s. It emerged from the city's underground club culture, particularly the Warehouse club, where DJ Frankie Knuckles mixed disco, soul, funk and electronic records for a predominantly Black and gay audience — the genre takes its name from the Warehouse. Chicago house is characterised by a four-on-the-floor beat at roughly 115–130 beats per minute, repetitive rhythms produced by drum machines such as the Roland TR-808 and TR-909, and synthesiser basslines, notably from the Roland TB-303, which gave rise to the subgenre acid house.

History

House developed as disco's popularity waned, with DJs extending records using reel-to-reel edits and drum machines. Frankie Knuckles' sets at the Warehouse (1977–1982) and later the Power Plant defined the sound, while Ron Hardy at the Music Box took a rawer, more intense approach. Producers began releasing original tracks on local labels such as Trax Records and DJ International: Jesse Saunders' "On and On" (1984) is often cited as the first house record, and Mr. Fingers (Larry Heard) brought a jazz-inflected depth with "Can You Feel It" (1986). Marshall Jefferson's "Move Your Body" (1986) carried the genre's name in its subtitle, "The House Music Anthem".

Spread and influence

By 1986–87 Chicago house records were major hits in the United Kingdom, where they inspired the acid house explosion and the Second Summer of Love in 1988. House became the foundation of modern dance music, spawning deep house, garage house and countless other styles, and exerted a formative influence on Detroit techno. The city of Chicago honoured Knuckles by naming a street Frankie Knuckles Way in 2004 and later declared August 25 "Frankie Knuckles Day".
