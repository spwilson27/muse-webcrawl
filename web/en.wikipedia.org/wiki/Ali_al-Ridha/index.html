Ali al-Ridha (also rendered Ali al-Rida or Ali ibn Musa al-Ridha, c. 766–818 CE) was a descendant of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and the eighth Imam in Twelver Shia Islam. He is renowned for his scholarship and for his controversial appointment as heir apparent to the Abbasid caliph al-Ma'mun.

Life

Born in Medina, the son of the seventh Imam, Musa al-Kazim, Ali succeeded his father in 799 after the latter's death in an Abbasid prison in Baghdad. For two decades he taught in Medina, attracting students from across the Muslim world. In 816 the caliph al-Ma'mun, seeking to reconcile the Abbasid caliphate with the Alid loyalists after a civil war, summoned Ali to Merv in Khorasan and designated him heir apparent, marrying him to the caliph's daughter and striking coins in his name. Many Shia sources hold that Ali accepted only under compulsion.

Death and shrine

Ali died suddenly in 818 at Tus, while travelling with the caliph toward Baghdad. Shia tradition holds that he was poisoned at al-Ma'mun's instigation. He was buried beside the caliph Harun al-Rashid; the site became the city of Mashhad ("place of martyrdom") in present-day Iran, and his shrine there is one of the holiest sites in Shia Islam, drawing tens of millions of pilgrims annually.

Works

Traditions attribute to him extensive theological debates at al-Ma'mun's court with Muslim, Christian, Jewish and Zoroastrian scholars, as well as the medical treatise Al-Risala al-Dhahabiyya ("The Golden Treatise"), though the attribution of the latter is uncertain.
