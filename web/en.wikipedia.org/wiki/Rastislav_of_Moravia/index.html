Rastislav (Latin: Rastiz; also Rostislav) was the second ruler of Great Moravia, reigning from 846 to 870. Installed by the East Frankish king Louis the German, he sought independence from Frankish overlordship, strengthened Moravia's fortifications, and invited the Byzantine mission of Saints Cyril and Methodius, which introduced the Glagolitic script and the Slavonic liturgy.

Accession and independence

After deposing Mojmir I in 846, Louis the German set his nephew Rastislav over Moravia expecting a loyal vassal. Rastislav instead allied with the Bohemians and Louis's rebellious son Carloman, defeating Frankish expeditions in 855 and consolidating an effectively independent realm.

The Byzantine mission

To counter Frankish clergy and gain Byzantine support, Rastislav asked Emperor Michael III in 862 for teachers who could preach in Slavonic. The brothers Cyril and Methodius arrived in 863, translated scripture into what became Old Church Slavonic, devised the Glagolitic alphabet, and obtained papal approval for the Slavonic liturgy in 868. Rastislav was later canonised by the Czech and Slovak Orthodox Church.

Downfall

In 870 Rastislav was betrayed by his nephew Svatopluk, handed over to Carloman, tried in Regensburg, blinded, and imprisoned in a Bavarian monastery, where he died.
