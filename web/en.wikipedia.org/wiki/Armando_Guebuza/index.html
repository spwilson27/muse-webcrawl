Armando Emílio Guebuza (born 20 January 1943) is a Mozambican politician who was the third President of Mozambique from 2005 to 2015. He led the ruling FRELIMO party and oversaw a period of rapid economic growth driven by foreign investment in coal, gas and infrastructure, alongside controversy over hidden state-guaranteed loans revealed after his presidency.

Early life and liberation struggle

Guebuza was born in Murrupula, in Nampula Province, in what was then Portuguese Mozambique. He joined FRELIMO at the age of 20, shortly after its founding, and left for training in Tanzania. During the Mozambican War of Independence he rose through the movement's ranks, becoming a member of its Central Committee and a close aide to its first leader, Eduardo Mondlane, and to his successor Samora Machel.

Government career

After independence in 1975, Guebuza held a series of senior posts, including Minister of the Interior in the government of Samora Machel — during which he issued the infamous "24/20" order giving Portuguese settlers 24 hours to leave the country with 20 kilograms of luggage — and later chief negotiator in the talks that ended the Mozambican Civil War with RENAMO, culminating in the 1992 Rome General Peace Accords. He also built substantial business interests during Mozambique's transition to a market economy.

Presidency

Guebuza won the 2004 presidential election and was inaugurated on 2 February 2005, succeeding Joaquim Chissano; he was re-elected in 2009 with about 75 percent of the vote. His administration promoted the slogan of "fighting poverty", expanded infrastructure, and attracted large-scale investment in the Tete coalfields and offshore natural gas in the Rovuma Basin. Critics accused him of concentrating power within FRELIMO and of intolerance of dissent.

After leaving office in 2015, Guebuza was succeeded by Filipe Nyusi. The subsequent "hidden debts" scandal — some $2 billion in state-guaranteed loans contracted in 2013–2014 and not disclosed to parliament or the IMF — led to trials in which Guebuza testified, and his son Ndambi Guebuza was convicted in 2022.
