Turntable

A turntable is a rotating circular platform. The word is used for several different devices, most prominently the platter of a phonograph (record player), on which a vinyl record rests and rotates beneath the stylus; by extension, "turntable" often denotes the entire record player, and in DJ culture a pair of turntables is the classic instrument of mixing, scratching, and turntablism.

Record players and DJing

On a phonograph, the turntable platter is driven by an electric motor, either through a belt, an idler wheel, or directly (direct drive), at standard speeds of 33⅓, 45, or historically 78 revolutions per minute. The record sits on the platter, sometimes on a slipmat — essential for DJs, who manipulate the record by hand while it plays. Classic DJ turntables such as the Technics SL-1200 series feature high-torque direct-drive motors and pitch adjustment, enabling beatmatching and scratching techniques central to hip hop, in which the turntable is used as a musical instrument.

Other turntables

  • Railway turntable — a rotating bridge at a roundhouse or depot used to turn locomotives around or direct them into stalls.
  • Vehicle turntable — a rotating driveway platform that turns a car so it can exit a confined space forwards.
  • Lazy Susan — a rotating tray for serving food, historically also called a turntable.
  • Rotating stage — a revolving platform in theatres (German: Drehbühne) for rapid scene changes.
  • Microwave turntable — the rotating glass plate that turns food for even heating.
  • Potter's wheel and machining — turntables appear in ceramics, display stands, and as parts of machine tools such as rotary tables.
