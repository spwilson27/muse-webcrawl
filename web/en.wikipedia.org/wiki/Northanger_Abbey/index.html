Northanger Abbey

Northanger Abbey is a coming-of-age novel and satire of the Gothic novel by Jane Austen. It was completed in 1803 — the first of Austen's novels to be finished in full — though it was not published until December 1817, after her death, when it appeared together with Persuasion. The work parodies the Gothic fiction popular in the 1790s, particularly Ann Radcliffe's The Mysteries of Udolpho.

Plot summary

Seventeen-year-old Catherine Morland, an avid reader of Gothic novels, travels from her family's home in Fullerton, Wiltshire, to the fashionable spa town of Bath with family friends, Mr. and Mrs. Allen. In Bath she befriends the scheming Isabella Thorpe and is courted by Isabella's boorish brother John, while falling in love with the witty clergyman Henry Tilney. Catherine is invited by Henry's father, General Tilney, to the family home, Northanger Abbey. Influenced by her novel reading, she imagines Gothic mysteries in the abbey — suspecting the General of having murdered his wife — and is mortified when Henry discovers and gently rebukes her fancies. Turned out of the abbey abruptly when the General learns (falsely, through John Thorpe's gossip) that Catherine is poor, she returns home; Henry follows, explains, and proposes. The two marry after the General's mistaken beliefs are corrected.

Themes

The novel is a satire of Gothic fiction and of the reading habits it encouraged, contrasting Catherine's overheated imagination with the real, quieter selfishness and mercenary motives around her. It also contains one of literature's famous defences of the novel as a form, in which the narrator protests against the contempt shown for novels — especially by reviewers and by novelists themselves — and lists works by Fanny Burney and Maria Edgeworth as worthy of respect. Other themes include the education of a young woman, the dangers of confusing fiction with life, and the economics of the marriage market.

Publication history

Austen sold the manuscript, then titled Susan, to the London publisher Crosby & Co. in 1803 for £10, but it was never published; she bought it back in 1816, revised it, and renamed the heroine Catherine. After her death in 1817 her brother Henry arranged publication under the title Northanger Abbey. The novel has since been adapted several times for stage, film, and television, including a 2007 ITV film starring Felicity Jones and JJ Feild.
