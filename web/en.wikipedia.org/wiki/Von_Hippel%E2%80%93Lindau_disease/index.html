Von Hippel–Lindau disease

Von Hippel–Lindau disease (VHL) is a genetic disorder characterised by the formation of tumours and cysts in many different parts of the body. It is caused by mutations in the VHL tumour suppressor gene on chromosome 3 and is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern.

GeneVHL (chromosome 3p25.3)
InheritanceAutosomal dominant
Named afterEugen von Hippel, Arvid Lindau

Signs and symptoms

Tumours associated with VHL include hemangioblastomas of the brain, spinal cord and retina; clear-cell renal cell carcinoma; pheochromocytomas; and pancreatic cysts and neuroendocrine tumours. Retinal hemangioblastomas (von Hippel tumours) were described by German ophthalmologist Eugen von Hippel, and Swedish pathologist Arvid Lindau established the systemic nature of the disease.

Genetics

The VHL protein normally targets hypoxia-inducible factors (HIF) for degradation. When VHL is lost, HIF accumulates and drives expression of angiogenic factors such as VEGF, promoting the growth of highly vascular tumours. The drug belzutifan, a HIF-2α inhibitor, was approved in 2021 for VHL-associated tumours.
