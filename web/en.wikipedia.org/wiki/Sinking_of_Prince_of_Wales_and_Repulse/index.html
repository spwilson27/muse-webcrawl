The sinking of Prince of Wales and Repulse was a naval engagement in the Second World War that took place on 10 December 1941 in the South China Sea off the east coast of Malaya, near Kuantan, Pahang. The British battleship HMS Prince of Wales and battlecruiser HMS Repulse were sunk by land-based bombers and torpedo bombers of the Imperial Japanese Navy Air Service.

Background

The two capital ships formed the core of Force Z, sent to Singapore to deter Japanese aggression. After the Japanese landings in northern Malaya and southern Thailand on 8 December 1941, Force Z's commander, Admiral Sir Tom Phillips, sortied to attack the invasion convoys, sailing without air cover after the intended aircraft carrier, HMS Indomitable, had run aground in the Caribbean and RAF fighters were committed elsewhere.

The attack

Spotted by Japanese reconnaissance aircraft and the submarine I-65, Force Z was attacked in successive waves by Mitsubishi G3M "Nell" and G4M "Betty" bombers flying from bases near Saigon. Repulse, skilfully handled by Captain William Tennant, combed the tracks of many torpedoes before being overwhelmed by a coordinated anvil attack; she capsized and sank at about 12:33. Prince of Wales was crippled early by a torpedo hit on her port propeller shaft that flooded the ship, degraded her electrical power and anti-aircraft capability, and she sank at about 13:18. Admiral Phillips and Captain John Leach went down with her. In total 840 men were lost; the escorting destroyers rescued 2,081 survivors.

Significance

Coming three days after Pearl Harbor, the loss demonstrated conclusively that capital ships operating without air cover were fatally vulnerable to air attack — the first time in history that battleships under way and free to manoeuvre were sunk solely by aircraft. Winston Churchill recalled that he had never received a greater shock during the war. The sinking removed the only Allied capital ships in the region, opened the way for the conquest of Malaya and the fall of Singapore, and confirmed the aircraft carrier's ascendancy over the battleship.

The wrecks

The wrecks lie upside down in about 68 metres (223 ft) of water and are designated war graves. They have suffered damage from illegal salvage operations in the 2010s and 2020s that removed large quantities of steel and other material.
