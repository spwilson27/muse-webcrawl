Proper noun

A proper noun is a noun that identifies a single unique entity, such as a person, place, or organization, distinguishing it from a common noun that denotes a class of entities. In English and many other languages, proper nouns are capitalized: examples include London, Mary, Jupiter, and Microsoft.

Proper adjectives

Words derived from proper nouns, such as Shakespearean or French, function as proper adjectives and are also capitalized. Over time, some words derived from proper names have lost their capitalization as they became generic, for example sandwich or quixotic.

Philosophy of language

Proper names have been a central topic in the philosophy of language: John Stuart Mill held that they have denotation but no connotation, while later philosophers debated description theories (Frege, Russell) and causal theories of reference (Kripke), which treat names as rigid designators.
