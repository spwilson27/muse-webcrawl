Square thread form

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The square thread form is a screw thread profile with a square cross-section, used principally for power transmission in machine screws, jacks, presses and vices where high efficiency and strength are required.

Properties

Because the thread faces are perpendicular to the axis, there is no radial 'bursting' force on the nut and friction is lower than in V-threads, giving square threads the highest efficiency of the common power-screw profiles. They are, however, difficult and expensive to machine, cannot easily be cut with standard dies, cannot be adjusted for wear with a split nut, and are weaker at the root than Acme threads. For these reasons they are often superseded by Acme and buttress thread forms.

Standards

Square threads are standardised in metric series with pitch equal to the thread's width and space; the profile has thread thickness and depth each equal to half the pitch.
