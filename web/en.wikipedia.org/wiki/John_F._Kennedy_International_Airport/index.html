John F. Kennedy International Airport (IATA: JFK, ICAO: KJFK) is a major international airport in Queens, New York City, United States, 16 miles (26 km) southeast of Midtown Manhattan. Operated by the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey, it is the busiest of the seven airports in the New York metropolitan area and the busiest international air passenger gateway into North America.

History

The airport opened in 1948 as New York International Airport, commonly called Idlewild after the golf course it replaced. It was renamed in December 1963 in memory of President John F. Kennedy, assassinated the previous month. Its mid-century terminals became icons of the jet age, notably Eero Saarinen's bird-like TWA Flight Center (1962), now the TWA Hotel, and the Worldport. Concorde services operated to JFK from 1977 to 2003.

Operations

JFK has five active passenger terminals and four runways on 5,200 acres. It is a hub for American Airlines and Delta Air Lines and the primary operating base for JetBlue. More than 90 airlines serve destinations on six continents, and the airport handles upwards of 60 million passengers annually.

Redevelopment

A sweeping redevelopment programme launched in the 2020s is replacing older terminals with new Terminal 1 and Terminal 6 facilities and expanding Terminal 4 and Terminal 8, alongside the AirTrain JFK people-mover system connecting the airport to the subway and Long Island Rail Road.
