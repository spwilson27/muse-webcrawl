Adam Ludwig Lewenhaupt

Adam Ludwig Lewenhaupt
Born: 15 April 1659, Copenhagen, Denmark
Died: 12 February 1719 (aged 59), Moscow, Russia
Allegiance: Swedish Empire
Rank: General of the infantry
Battles: Great Northern War — Battle of Gemauerthof, Battle of Lesnaya

Adam Ludwig Lewenhaupt (15 April 1659 – 12 February 1719) was a Swedish general of the infantry, best known for his role in the Great Northern War and for the capitulation of his army at Perevolochna in 1709, after the Battle of Poltava.

Early life and career

Lewenhaupt was born in Copenhagen, where his father, Count Ludwig Wierich Lewenhaupt, was serving in Swedish diplomatic service; both his parents died while he was young, and he was educated at the universities of Lund and Uppsala and abroad at Rostock. He entered military service in the 1680s, first in Bavarian service against the Ottoman Empire in Hungary, then in Dutch service, before returning to Sweden. He rose steadily in rank and built a reputation as a capable and disciplined officer.

Great Northern War

At the outbreak of the Great Northern War in 1700, Lewenhaupt was appointed colonel of a newly raised regiment. He campaigned successfully in Courland and Polish Livonia, defeating Russian forces at Gemauerthof in 1705, one of the most notable Swedish victories of the war's middle phase. In 1708, commanding the army corps advancing from Livonia to join Charles XII's main army in Ukraine, Lewenhaupt was intercepted by Peter the Great at the Battle of Lesnaya. His supply train was largely lost, and he reached the king with only part of his force, a blow that contributed materially to the Swedish defeat at Poltava the following year.

Capitulation and death

After Poltava, Lewenhaupt commanded the remnants of the Swedish army that retreated to the Dnieper crossing at Perevolochna, where, with the king having escaped across the river, he surrendered some 14,000 men to Prince Menshikov on 11 July 1709. The capitulation was controversial in Sweden, and Lewenhaupt spent the rest of his life as a prisoner of war in Russia, dying in Moscow in 1719 without returning home.
