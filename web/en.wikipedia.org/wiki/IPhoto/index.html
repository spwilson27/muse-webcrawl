iPhoto

iPhoto was a digital photograph manipulation software application developed by Apple Inc. It was included with every Macintosh personal computer from 2002 until 2015, when it was replaced by Photos. Originally sold as part of the iLife suite, iPhoto let users import, organize, edit, print, and share photos from digital cameras, scanners, and image files.

History

iPhoto was announced by Steve Jobs at Macworld in January 2002 as part of Apple's "digital hub" strategy, in which the Mac would serve as the center of a user's digital devices. Successive versions added features such as Faces (face recognition), Places (geotagging on maps), slideshows, and ordering of printed books, calendars, and cards directly from the application.

Discontinuation

In June 2014 Apple announced that iPhoto and Aperture would be succeeded by a new Photos app, which launched with OS X Yosemite updates in spring 2015 and unified the desktop library with iCloud Photo Library. iPhoto libraries could be migrated to Photos; the app stopped receiving updates after that transition.
