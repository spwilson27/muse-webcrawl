Adrienne Rich (1929–2012)

Adrienne Cecile Rich (16 May 1929 – 27 March 2012) was an American poet, essayist and feminist. Widely regarded as one of the most influential poets of the second half of the twentieth century, she brought the realities of women's lives, the civil rights movement, and opposition to war into the centre of American poetry. Her collection Diving into the Wreck (1973) shared the National Book Award for Poetry in 1974; she accepted the award jointly with Audre Lorde and Alice Walker on behalf of all women.

Life

Rich was born in Baltimore, the elder daughter of a pathologist father and a pianist mother, and was educated at home until the fourth grade. She graduated from Radcliffe College in 1951, the year her first collection, A Change of World, was selected by W. H. Auden for the Yale Series of Younger Poets. She married Alfred Conrad in 1953 and had three sons; the marriage ended, and Conrad died by suicide in 1970. From 1976 until her death her partner was the writer Michelle Cliff. Rich lived for many years in California and died in Santa Cruz in 2012.

Poetry

Rich's early work was formally accomplished and restrained; from the 1960s her poetry grew progressively more open, angry, and politically engaged. Snapshots of a Daughter-in-Law (1963), The Will to Change (1971), and Diving into the Wreck (1973) chart this transformation. Later collections include An Atlas of the Difficult World (1991) and Midnight Salvage (1999). She won the Ruth Lilly Poetry Prize and a MacArthur Fellowship, among many honours.

Prose and activism

Her prose includes Of Woman Born: Motherhood as Experience and Institution (1976), a landmark of feminist thought, and the essay "Compulsory Heterosexuality and Lesbian Existence" (1980). In 1997 she declined the National Medal of Arts in protest at the Clinton administration's art policy and social inequality. Rich was a lifelong activist for civil rights, feminism, and peace.
