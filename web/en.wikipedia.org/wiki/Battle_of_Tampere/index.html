Battle of Tampere

The Battle of Tampere was fought in March–April 1918 during the Finnish Civil War, between the Finnish Whites under C. G. E. Mannerheim and the Finnish Reds defending the industrial city of Tampere. It was the largest and decisive battle of the war — the biggest battle fought in the Nordic countries up to that time — and ended on 6 April 1918 with the fall of the city to the Whites.

The battle

Mannerheim chose Tampere, Red Finland's principal industrial and military stronghold in the north, as his main objective. After encircling the city and cutting its rail links, White forces — including the Swedish Brigade — stormed Tampere on 3–6 April in bitter street fighting among the wooden working-class districts, much of which burned. The Red commander Hugo Salmela was killed when his own headquarters' munitions exploded.

Aftermath

The fall of Tampere broke Red resistance in the north and was the turning point of the war. Some 11,000 Reds were taken prisoner; many were held in the Kalevankangas prison camp, where large numbers died of disease and malnutrition in the following months. The Whites went on to take Viipuri and, with German help, Helsinki.
