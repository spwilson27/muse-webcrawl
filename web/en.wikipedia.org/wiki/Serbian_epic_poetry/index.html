Serbian epic poetry

A guslar performing with the single-stringed gusle.

Serbian epic poetry is a form of epic poetry created by Serbs originating in today's Serbia and neighbouring regions. The poems were composed and transmitted orally by wandering bards called guslari, who sang them to the accompaniment of the gusle, a single-stringed instrument. The verse form is the decasyllabic line (deseterac).

Cycles

The corpus is usually divided into cycles: the non-historical cycle of gods and mythical beings; the pre-Kosovo cycle of the Nemanjić dynasty and feudal lords; the Kosovo cycle, centred on the Battle of Kosovo in 1389, including the hero Prince Lazar and the traitor-or-hero Vuk Branković; the cycle of Marko Kraljević, the most popular hero; the cycle of the hajduks and uskoks, outlaws resisting Ottoman rule; and poems of the Serbian uprisings of the 19th century.

Collection and study

The reformer of the Serbian language Vuk Karadžić collected and published the poems in the early 19th century, bringing them to European attention; they were admired and translated by figures such as Jacob Grimm and Goethe. In the 1930s Milman Parry and Albert Lord recorded South Slavic epic singers, including Avdo Međedović, whose performances underpinned the oral-formulaic theory of composition.
