Photochromism

Photochromism is the reversible change of the colour of a chemical species induced in one or both directions by absorption of electromagnetic radiation. In other words, a photochromic compound changes colour when exposed to light and returns to its original state when the light is removed or under different illumination.

The best-known application is photochromic lenses for eyeglasses, which darken in bright sunlight and clear indoors. Photochromic materials — including spiropyrans, diarylethenes and silver halide systems — are also studied for optical data storage, switches, inks and smart windows.
