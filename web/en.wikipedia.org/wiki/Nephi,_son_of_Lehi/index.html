Nephi, son of Lehi

Nephi (NEE-fy) is a central figure in the Book of Mormon, the scripture of the Latter Day Saint movement. He is presented as the fourth son of the prophet Lehi, who led his family from Jerusalem around 600 BC across the ocean to the Americas. Nephi is the author of the first two books of the Book of Mormon, First Nephi and Second Nephi, and the founder of the people known as the Nephites. Members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints regard him as a historical prophet; mainstream historians and archaeologists do not accept the Book of Mormon narrative as a record of ancient American history.

Narrative

According to the account, Nephi was born in Jerusalem to Lehi and Sariah. When Lehi prophesied the city's destruction and fled into the wilderness, Nephi accompanied him, returning to Jerusalem with his brothers Laman, Lemuel, and Sam to obtain the brass plates — a scriptural record — from Laban, whom Nephi slew after finding him drunk in the street. In the wilderness Nephi broke a steel bow, received the Liahona (a compass-like director), and later, under God's command, built a ship that carried the family across the ocean to the promised land.

In the promised land

After Lehi's death, conflict with Laman and Lemuel led Nephi to separate with his followers, founding a people called the Nephites and becoming their first king and teacher. He engraved his record on metal plates, built a temple, and prophesied of Christ. His writings in Second Nephi include extensive commentary on Isaiah and an influential doctrinal discourse.

Legacy

Nephi's writings open the Book of Mormon, and his declaration "I will go and do the things which the Lord hath commanded" (1 Nephi 3:7) is one of the most quoted verses in Latter-day Saint teaching. Several later Book of Mormon figures also bear the name Nephi.
