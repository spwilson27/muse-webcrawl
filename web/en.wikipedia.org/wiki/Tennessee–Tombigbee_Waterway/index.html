Tennessee–Tombigbee Waterway

The Tennessee–Tombigbee Waterway (popularly the Tenn-Tom) is a 234-mile (377 km) artificial waterway in the United States connecting the Tennessee River with the Tombigbee River and, via Demopolis, Alabama, with the Gulf of Mexico at Mobile. It runs through northeastern Mississippi and western Alabama.

Construction

First proposed in the 18th century and authorised in 1946, construction by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers began in 1972 and the waterway opened in 1985. It was one of the largest earth-moving projects in history, requiring excavation of about 310 million cubic yards of earth — more than for the Panama Canal. The waterway includes ten locks and dams, the Tennessee River Divide Cut, and Bay Springs Lake.

Use

The Tenn-Tom shortens shipping routes between the Tennessee Valley and the Gulf, carrying commodities such as coal, timber, grain, and aggregates by barge, and provides recreation lakes and wildlife areas along its banks.
