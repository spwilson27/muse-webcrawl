Partita No. 2 (Bach)

ComposerJohann Sebastian Bach
CatalogueBWV 1004
KeyD minor
InstrumentSolo violin
Composedc. 1720

The Partita No. 2 in D minor, BWV 1004, by Johann Sebastian Bach is the second of the set of six Sonatas and Partitas for solo violin. It was written during Bach's years at Cöthen and is dated around 1720, the year of the surviving autograph fair copy. The partita is renowned above all for its concluding movement, the Ciaccona (Chaconne), one of the most celebrated works in the violin repertoire.

Movements

  1. Allemanda — a flowing binary-form dance in 4/4.
  2. Corrente — a lively Italian-style courante in 3/4, with a Double (ornamented variation) following each of the first movements in this partita.
  3. Sarabanda — a grave, expressive sarabande in 3/4.
  4. Giga — a fleet gigue in 12/8 with continuous semiquaver figuration.
  5. Ciaccona — a monumental chaconne: a set of some 64 variations over a four-bar bass pattern, lasting as long as the other four movements combined.

The Ciaccona

The Chaconne is widely regarded as a pinnacle of the solo violin literature. Its variations explore nearly every technique of Baroque violin playing, including elaborate arpeggiation, bariolage and chordal writing, and it is structured as a vast arch, with a contrasting middle section in D major. Johannes Brahms called it "one of the most wonderful, incomprehensible pieces of music", and arranged it for piano left hand; Ferruccio Busoni and many others made transcriptions for piano, guitar and orchestra. It has been speculated that Bach composed the movement as a tombeau for his first wife, Maria Barbara, who died in 1720, though this remains conjecture.
