Fareed Zakaria

Fareed Rafiq Zakaria (born 20 January 1964) is an Indian-American journalist, political commentator and author. He is the host of CNN's Fareed Zakaria GPS, a weekly programme on international affairs, and a columnist for The Washington Post. He previously served as editor of Newsweek International and as editor-at-large of Time magazine.

Early life and education

Zakaria was born in Mumbai, India, to a Muslim family; his father was a politician associated with the Indian National Congress and his mother a magazine editor. He studied at Yale University, where he was president of the Yale Political Union, and earned a PhD in government from Harvard University in 1993, studying under Samuel P. Huntington and Robert Keohane. His dissertation addressed American foreign policy.

Career

After teaching at Harvard, Zakaria became managing editor of Foreign Affairs at 28. In 2000 he joined Newsweek as editor of its international editions. His 2001 Newsweek cover essay "Why They Hate Us" was widely discussed after the September 11 attacks. His books include The Future of Freedom (2003), arguing that liberal democracy requires constitutional liberalism and the rule of law, not elections alone; The Post-American World (2008), describing the "rise of the rest"; and Ten Lessons for a Post-Pandemic World (2020).

Plagiarism controversy

In 2012 Zakaria was briefly suspended by CNN and Time after a column on gun control was found to closely paraphrase a New Yorker article without full attribution. He apologised, describing it as a serious lapse, and was reinstated after a review.
