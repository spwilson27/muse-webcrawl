Options Clearing Corporation

The Options Clearing Corporation (OCC), doing business as OCC (formerly styled The Options Clearing Corporation), is a United States central clearing counterparty (CCP) based in Chicago. Founded in 1973, it is the world's largest equity derivatives clearing organisation. As the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer, OCC guarantees the performance of contracts traded on U.S. options exchanges, eliminating counterparty credit risk between individual traders.

Operations

OCC clears transactions for all U.S. listed options exchanges — including Cboe, Nasdaq's options markets, NYSE American and Arca Options — covering equity options, index options, ETF options and, through its CFTC-regulated capacity, some futures (initially via OneChicago). Clearing members post margin calculated with OCC's STANS risk model; a guarantee fund mutualises losses if a member defaults.

Volume has grown enormously: OCC cleared a record of more than 10 billion contracts in 2023, driven by the boom in short-dated (0DTE) index options. In 2012 the Financial Stability Oversight Council designated OCC a systemically important financial market utility under the Dodd–Frank Act, subjecting it to enhanced Federal Reserve supervision.

History

OCC was founded in 1973 as the clearing arm of the Chicago Board Options Exchange, which pioneered standardised listed options that year; it soon became the common clearer for all U.S. options exchanges. The organisation rebranded simply as "OCC" in 2019.
