Muzak

Muzak is an American brand of background music played in retail stores and other public establishments. The name, a portmanteau of "music" and "Kodak", has been in use since 1934 and was first used by the company founded by George Owen Squier, who developed systems for transmitting music over electrical lines. The word Muzak has become a generic term for the kind of bland, unobtrusive background music — often called "elevator music" — that the company popularised.

History

Squier's Wired Radio company, rebranded Muzak in the 1930s, distributed recorded music to businesses by leased line and later by FM subcarrier, satellite and internet streaming. Muzak developed "Stimulus Progression" programming, in which blocks of music arranged in ascending order of tempo and brightness were intended to increase worker productivity and shopper dwell time while remaining below the threshold of conscious attention. The company supplied background music to offices, factories, hotels, restaurants and lifts, and its recordings were specially orchestrated instrumental versions of popular songs, made by its own orchestras.

Later years and legacy

Muzak passed through several owners and a 2009 bankruptcy, and in 2011 was acquired by Mood Media, which retired the Muzak brand name in 2013. The term survives as a frequently pejorative label for formulaic background music, though scholars of music and consumer psychology have studied its genuine effects on behaviour, and reissues of classic Muzak recordings have attracted interest as documents of mid-century commercial culture.
