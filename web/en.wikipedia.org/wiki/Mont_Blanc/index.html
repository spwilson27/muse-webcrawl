Mont Blanc ('White Mountain') is the highest mountain in the Alps and in Western Europe, at 4,808 metres (about 15,774 ft; height varies slightly with snow cover). It lies in the Mont Blanc massif on the border between France (Haute-Savoie) and Italy (Aosta Valley), above the town of Chamonix.

Mont Blanc's first ascent, by Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard on 8 August 1786, is treated as the birth of modern mountaineering. The Mont Blanc Tunnel (11.6 km, opened 1965) links France and Italy beneath the massif; a disastrous fire in 1999 killed 39 people. The Tour du Mont Blanc hiking circuit and the Ultra-Trail du Mont-Blanc race circle the massif; its glaciers, like the Alps' generally, are shrinking measurably.
