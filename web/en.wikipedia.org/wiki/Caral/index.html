Caral

Caral, also known as Caral-Supe, is a large settlement in the Supe Valley, near Supe in the Barranca province of Peru, about 200 kilometres north of Lima. It is the most extensively researched site of the Caral-Supe civilization (also called the Norte Chico civilization), the oldest known civilization in the Americas, flourishing roughly between 3500 and 1800 BCE, contemporary with the pyramids of Egypt.

Archaeology

The site covers about 66 hectares and contains six large pyramidal mounds, the largest about 18 metres high, arranged around plazas, along with sunken circular courts, residential areas, and an amphitheatre. Excavations led by Ruth Shady from the 1990s revealed a complex, planned city with no evidence of warfare or fortifications. Finds include a quipu, suggesting a recording system, and flutes made from condor and pelican bones.

Economy and society

The inhabitants combined irrigation agriculture — cotton, squash, beans and guava — with fishing on the nearby coast, trading cotton and dried fish. Cotton for fishing nets may have underpinned the economy. Caral was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2009 as the Sacred City of Caral-Supe.
