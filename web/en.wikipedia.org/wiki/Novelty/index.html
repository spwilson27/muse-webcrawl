Novelty

Novelty is the quality of being new, original or unusual. Derived from the Latin novus ("new"), the term applies to objects, ideas, experiences and fashions whose appeal lies in their newness or strangeness rather than in lasting utility or value.

Psychology

Novelty is a powerful stimulus for attention and learning. Novel stimuli activate the brain's reward and memory systems, including dopaminergic pathways linked to the hippocampus, which is thought to promote exploration and the encoding of new information. The "novelty effect" describes the temporary increase in interest or performance that follows the introduction of something new — for instance new technology in a classroom — which tends to fade as the novelty wears off. Repeated exposure leads to habituation, while total predictability can produce boredom; the preference for novelty, or neophilia, varies between individuals and contrasts with neophobia, the fear or avoidance of the new.

Consumer culture

In commerce, novelty is a major driver of fashion cycles and product design. "Novelties" or "novelty items" are small, inexpensive decorative or humorous objects sold as gifts and souvenirs — a category including novelty songs, novelty architecture (buildings shaped like the products they sell) and novelty formats in retailing. Critics from Thorstein Veblen onward have analysed the pursuit of novelty as a feature of consumer society, in which newness itself becomes a commodity and goods are replaced before they wear out.

Law and science

In patent law, novelty is a formal requirement for patentability: an invention must not form part of the prior art. In science, novelty is likewise a core value, as journals and funding systems reward original contributions, though this can discourage replication studies.
