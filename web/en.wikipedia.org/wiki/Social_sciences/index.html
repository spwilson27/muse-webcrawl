Social sciences

The social sciences are the branches of science that study human society and social relationships. Major social science disciplines include anthropology, archaeology, economics, geography, history, linguistics, political science, psychology, and sociology. The term is also sometimes used more broadly to include the humanities, law, and related professional fields such as education, social work, and public policy.

History

The roots of social science lie in the Age of Enlightenment, when thinkers such as Adam Smith, Montesquieu, and later Auguste Comte sought to apply systematic, evidence-based methods to the study of society. Comte coined the term "sociology" and advocated positivism. In the 19th century, Karl Marx, Émile Durkheim, and Max Weber laid the foundations of modern sociology, while economics developed from moral philosophy into a formal discipline. The 20th century saw the professionalisation and institutionalisation of the social sciences in universities, including the founding of dedicated institutions such as the London School of Economics and the New School for Social Research.

Branches

  • Anthropology — the study of human societies and cultures, past and present.
  • Archaeology — the study of past human activity through material remains.
  • Economics — the study of the production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services.
  • Geography — the study of places and the relationships between people and their environments.
  • History — the study of past events, particularly in human affairs.
  • Linguistics — the scientific study of language and its structure.
  • Political science — the study of government, politics, and political behaviour.
  • Psychology — the study of mind and behaviour, including social psychology.
  • Sociology — the study of society, social institutions, and social relationships.

Methodology

Social scientists use both quantitative methods — surveys, statistical analysis, econometrics, and experiments — and qualitative methods such as ethnography, interviews, and archival research. Long-running debates concern the extent to which social phenomena can be studied with the same methods as the natural sciences, the role of values and interpretation, and the limits of prediction in complex social systems.
