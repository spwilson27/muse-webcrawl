Hereditary spastic paraplegia

Hereditary spastic paraplegia
SpecialtyNeurology, medical genetics
Also known asFamilial spastic paraparesis; Strümpell–Lorrain disease
CauseMutations in many genes (SPG loci)

The hereditary spastic paraplegias (HSP) are a large group of inherited neurological disorders whose main feature is progressive spasticity and weakness of the lower limbs, caused by degeneration of the long axons of the corticospinal tracts.

Classification

"Pure" HSP involves only lower-limb spasticity, brisk reflexes and bladder disturbance, while "complicated" forms add features such as ataxia, intellectual disability, epilepsy, peripheral neuropathy or optic atrophy. More than 80 genetic loci (designated SPG1, SPG2, ...) have been identified, with autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive and X-linked inheritance. The commonest dominant form, SPG4, is caused by mutations in the spastin gene SPAST.

Symptoms and management

Onset ranges from childhood to late adulthood. Gait becomes stiff and scissoring as spasticity progresses. There is no cure; treatment is symptomatic, using antispasticity medication, physiotherapy and mobility aids. The condition was described by Adolf Strümpell and Maurice Lorrain in the late 19th century.
