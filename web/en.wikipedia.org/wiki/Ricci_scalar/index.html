Ricci scalar

In Riemannian geometry, the Ricci scalar (or scalar curvature) is the simplest invariant measure of the curvature of a Riemannian manifold. It is obtained by taking the trace of the Ricci curvature tensor with respect to the metric, and assigns a single number to each point of the manifold.

Definition

Given the Ricci tensor Rij and the inverse metric gij, the Ricci scalar is R = gij Rij. Intuitively, it measures the amount by which the volume of a small geodesic ball in the manifold differs from the volume of a ball in flat Euclidean space of the same radius.

In physics

The Ricci scalar is central to general relativity: the Einstein–Hilbert action is proportional to the integral of R over spacetime, and the Einstein field equations relate R, together with the Ricci tensor, to the distribution of matter and energy. In two dimensions the scalar curvature is twice the Gaussian curvature; for a sphere of radius r it is constant and equal to 2/r².
