Pungmulnori

Pungmulnori (Korean: 풍물놀이), also known as nongak (농악, "farmers' dance" or "farmers' music"), is a Korean folk music and performance tradition that includes drumming, dancing, theatre, and acrobatics. Performed outdoors by troupes in colourful costumes, it has roots in the communal labour and ritual celebrations of farming villages.

Performance

The ensemble is built around percussion instruments: the kkwaenggwari (small hand gong, whose player leads the troupe), jing (large gong), janggu (hourglass drum), and buk (barrel drum), sometimes joined by wind instruments such as the taepyeongso. Dancers perform spinning dances wearing sangmo hats fitted with long ribbons or feathers, while comic stock characters — including a monk, a nobleman, and gossipy women — act out satirical skits. The performance traditionally seeks to drive away evil spirits, pray for abundance, and celebrate the harvest and communal solidarity.

Recognition

Nongak was inscribed in 2014 on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Modern groups such as SamulNori, founded in 1978, adapted the tradition for the concert stage as samulnori, a quartet format featuring the four main percussion instruments.
