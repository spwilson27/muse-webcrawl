Siberian Craton

The Siberian Craton (also called the Angara Craton or Siberian Platform) is an ancient continental block forming the core of north-eastern Eurasia and the heart of Siberia. It corresponds to the independent craton that underlay the ancient continent of Siberia (Angaraland) in the Palaeozoic. It is bounded by the Yenisey River to the west, the Lena River delta to the east, the Arctic Ocean to the north, and Lake Baikal to the south.

Geology

The craton consists of Archaean and Proterozoic crystalline basement, exposed principally in the Anabar Shield in the north and the Aldan Shield in the south-east, and elsewhere covered by great thicknesses of younger sedimentary rock. It was assembled from separate terranes during the Proterozoic and has remained tectonically stable for over a billion years.

Around 250 million years ago, at the end of the Permian period, the craton was the site of the Siberian Traps, one of the largest flood-basalt eruptions known, which covered much of the platform with lava and is associated with the Permian–Triassic mass extinction, the most severe in Earth's history.

Resources

The craton hosts major mineral wealth. Kimberlite pipes intruding the platform in Yakutia (such as Mir and Udachnaya) made Russia one of the world's principal diamond producers. The region also contains large deposits of nickel, copper and platinum-group metals around Norilsk, associated with the Siberian Traps, as well as coal, gold, iron ore, oil and natural gas.
