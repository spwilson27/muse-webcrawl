Mount Holyoke College is a private liberal arts women's college in South Hadley, Massachusetts. Founded in 1837 by Mary Lyon as the Mount Holyoke Female Seminary, it is the oldest of the Seven Sisters colleges and the oldest institution of higher education for women in the United States still operating as a women's college.

History

Mary Lyon raised funds door-to-door to open a school offering a rigorous curriculum in the sciences and humanities modelled on men's colleges, uncommon for women's education at the time; chemistry was taught with laboratory work, and the college's early science tradition later included the pioneering physical chemist Emma Perry Carr. The seminary became Mount Holyoke Seminary and College in 1888 and simply Mount Holyoke College in 1893. It was a model for women's colleges founded abroad and for Vassar and Wellesley.

Academics and campus

Mount Holyoke enrols about 2,200 students from more than 80 countries and is known for strong international enrolment. It is a member of the Five Colleges consortium with Amherst, Hampshire, Smith and UMass Amherst, allowing cross-registration. The campus, partly designed by the Olmsted firm, covers some 800 acres with lakes, waterfalls and the Mount Holyoke Range nearby. Notable alumnae include the poet Emily Dickinson (who attended the seminary), Frances Perkins, the first woman in a US cabinet, and playwrights Wendy Wasserstein and Suzan-Lori Parks.
