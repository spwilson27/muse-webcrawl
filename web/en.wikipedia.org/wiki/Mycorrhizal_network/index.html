Mycorrhizal network

A mycorrhizal network is an underground network of fungal hyphae that connects the roots of multiple plants, allowing the transfer of water, carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, and signalling compounds between them. Mycorrhizal fungi colonise plant roots in a mutualistic symbiosis: the plant supplies the fungus with sugars from photosynthesis, and the fungus greatly extends the plant's access to soil water and mineral nutrients. When the same fungal mycelium links two or more plants — sometimes of different species — it forms a common mycorrhizal network.

Types

Arbuscular mycorrhizal networks, formed by fungi of the phylum Glomeromycota inside root cells, associate with most land plant species. Ectomycorrhizal networks, in which fungi sheath the outside of roots, dominate in many temperate and boreal forests and link trees such as pines, oaks, and birches through fungi including Suillus, Rhizopogon, and Laccaria species.

Resource transfer and signalling

Experiments using isotope tracers have shown that carbon can move from one tree to another through shared fungal networks, for example from sunlit "source" trees to shaded "sink" seedlings. Studies have also reported the transfer of defence signals: plants connected to neighbours attacked by aphids or pathogens increased their own defensive chemistry. Popular accounts have dubbed the system the "wood wide web", and ecologist Suzanne Simard's research popularised the idea of large, old "mother trees" supporting seedlings through networks.

Scientific debate

The existence of physical networks and of small-scale resource transfer is well supported, but the magnitude and ecological significance of tree-to-tree transfer in real forests remain contested. A 2023 review argued that positive citation bias had inflated claims about common mycorrhizal networks, finding field evidence for large, adaptive transfers weak. Researchers continue to debate how often fungal networks benefit plants, as opposed to serving the fungus's own interests in distributing its nutrient sources.
