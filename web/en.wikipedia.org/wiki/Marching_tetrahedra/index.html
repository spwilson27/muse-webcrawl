Marching tetrahedra

Marching tetrahedra is an algorithm in computer graphics for extracting a polygonal mesh of an isosurface from a three-dimensional scalar field (voxel grid). It is a variant of the marching cubes algorithm, published in 1991 by Doi and Koide, which avoids the ambiguities of the cube-based method by decomposing each cube into tetrahedra.

Algorithm

Each cube of the grid is split into five or six tetrahedra. Within a tetrahedron, the surface's intersection is simple: the signs of the scalar values at the four vertices give only a few distinct cases, producing either no triangle, one triangle, or a quadrilateral formed of two triangles. Vertex positions along edges are found by linear interpolation, as in marching cubes.

Comparison with marching cubes

Because the tetrahedron cases are unambiguous, marching tetrahedra cannot produce the holes that marching cubes generates when its ambiguous face configurations are resolved inconsistently. The cost is a larger number of triangles for the same grid resolution, since each cube is subdivided. The algorithm is widely used in scientific visualisation of medical and volumetric data.
