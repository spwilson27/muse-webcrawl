The National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) is a United States national laboratory dedicated to research, development, commercialization and deployment of renewable energy and energy efficiency technologies. Located in Golden, Colorado, it is the only national laboratory solely focused on renewable energy.

History

NREL began in 1977 as the Solar Energy Research Institute, established in response to the 1970s energy crisis. In 1991 President George H. W. Bush elevated it to national laboratory status and renamed it the National Renewable Energy Laboratory.

Research areas

The laboratory's work spans solar photovoltaics, wind energy, bioenergy, hydrogen and fuel cells, geothermal energy, energy storage, grid modernization and energy-efficient buildings and transportation. Its perovskite solar cell research and efficiency charts are widely cited benchmarks in the photovoltaic field.

Campus

The South Table Mountain campus in Golden is noted for its own sustainable design, including the net-zero Research Support Facility, and for high-performance computing resources such as the Eagle and Kestrel supercomputers used for energy systems modelling.
