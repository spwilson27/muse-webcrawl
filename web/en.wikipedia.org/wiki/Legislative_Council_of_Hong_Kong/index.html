The Legislative Council of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (LegCo) is the unicameral legislature of Hong Kong. It was established under the Basic Law of Hong Kong and first sat in its modern elected form in 1998, succeeding the colonial Legislative Council founded in 1843. Its functions include enacting, amending and repealing laws, approving public expenditure, and monitoring the work of the government.

Composition

Following the electoral overhaul imposed in 2021, the council has 90 members: 40 returned by the Election Committee, 30 by functional constituencies representing professional and trade sectors, and 20 by direct election in geographical constituencies. Members serve four-year terms. The changes greatly reduced the directly elected element and followed Beijing's decision that only "patriots" should govern Hong Kong.

History

The colonial council began as an appointed advisory body. Indirect elections were introduced in 1985 and the first direct elections in 1991. After the 1997 handover, China replaced the council with a provisional legislature for a year before elections resumed in 1998. Pro-democracy legislators were long a significant bloc until the post-2019 changes, resignations and disqualifications left the chamber dominated by pro-Beijing members.
