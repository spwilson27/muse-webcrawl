Viète's formulas

FieldAlgebra
Named afterFrançois Viète (1540–1603)
RelatesPolynomial coefficients to roots

In mathematics, Viète's formulas relate the coefficients of a polynomial to sums and products of its roots. They are named after the French mathematician François Viète, who published them in the late 16th century.

Statement

For a quadratic x² + bx + c with roots r and s, the formulas give r + s = −b and rs = c. In general, for a polynomial of degree n written as xⁿ + a₁xⁿ⁻¹ + ... + aₙ with roots r₁, ..., rₙ, the sum of the roots taken k at a time (the k-th elementary symmetric polynomial in the roots) equals (−1)ᵏ aₖ. In particular, the sum of all the roots is −a₁ and the product of all the roots is (−1)ⁿ aₙ.

Applications

Viète's formulas follow immediately from expanding the factored form of the polynomial. They are used to check candidate solutions of polynomial equations, to construct equations with given roots, and in competition problems where the sum or product of roots can be found without solving the equation explicitly. They are also the simplest case of Newton's identities relating power sums of roots to coefficients.
