Welsh (Cymraeg) is a Celtic language of the Brittonic subgroup, native to Wales and spoken also by a Welsh diaspora, notably the Y Wladfa settlement in Patagonia, Argentina. It is the most widely spoken Celtic language.

History

Welsh evolved from Common Brittonic; Old Welsh (c. 800–1100), Middle Welsh (the language of the Mabinogion) and Modern Welsh (from the 14th century, marked by the poetry of Dafydd ap Gwilym) are its stages. The poets Taliesin and Aneirin composed in its early form. English legal and educational dominance, including the 1847 "Treachery of the Blue Books", drove decline from over 80% of Wales in the 19th century to a low of 18.9% in 1991.

Revival

The Welsh Bible (1588) had preserved a literary standard. Twentieth-century campaigning (including Saunders Lewis's 1962 lecture Tynged yr Iaith), Welsh-medium education, the S4C television channel (1982), the Welsh Language Acts of 1967 and 1993 and the Welsh Language (Wales) Measure 2011, giving Welsh official status, supported recovery. The Welsh Government's Cymraeg 2050 strategy targets one million speakers. Welsh is known for initial consonant mutations and words like Llanfairpwllgwyngyll…, one of Europe's longest place names. Its history exemplifies sound change in action.
