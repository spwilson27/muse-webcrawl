The Cosby Show

The Cosby Show is an American television sitcom co-created by and starring Bill Cosby. It aired on NBC from September 20, 1984, to April 30, 1992, spanning eight seasons and 201 episodes. The show focuses on the Huxtables, an upper-middle-class African-American family living in Brooklyn, New York.

Overview

Cliff Huxtable (Bill Cosby) is an obstetrician whose basement office allows him to be close to his family, while his wife Clair (Phylicia Rashad) is a successful attorney. Their children are Sondra, Denise, Theo, Vanessa, and Rudy. The plots typically revolve around family life, education, and parenting, with Cliff's gentle humour mediating everyday crises.

Reception

The Cosby Show was a phenomenon: it was the number-one rated show in the United States for five consecutive seasons (1985–1990), a feat matched only by All in the Family and American Idol. It is widely credited with reviving the sitcom genre and NBC's fortunes in the 1980s, and with anchoring the network's "Must See TV" Thursday night block. The series won six Emmy Awards including Outstanding Comedy Series in 1985.

Legacy

The show spawned the spin-off A Different World. Its depiction of an affluent, loving Black family was widely discussed for its cultural and political significance. In later years, syndication of the series was curtailed following the sexual assault allegations against and conviction of its star, which significantly altered its standing in popular culture.
