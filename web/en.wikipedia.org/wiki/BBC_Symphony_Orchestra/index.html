BBC Symphony Orchestra

The BBC Symphony Orchestra (BBC SO) is a British orchestra based in London. Founded in 1930, it is the principal broadcast orchestra of the British Broadcasting Corporation (BBC) and one of the BBC's five radio orchestras. Its home base is the Maida Vale Studios in London, and it performs regularly at the Barbican Centre, where it is an associate orchestra.

History

The orchestra was founded in 1930 under the direction of Adrian Boult, its first chief conductor, who established its high standards and wide-ranging repertoire. The BBC SO has long been known for its commitment to contemporary music, premiering works by many major composers, and it plays a central role in the BBC Proms, traditionally performing at both the First Night and the Last Night of the Proms at the Royal Albert Hall.

Chief conductors

Chief conductors have included Adrian Boult (1930–1950), Malcolm Sargent, Antal Doráti, Colin Davis, Pierre Boulez, Gennady Rozhdestvensky, Leonard Slatkin, Jiří Bělohlávek, and Sakari Oramo, who became chief conductor in 2013. Dalia Stasevska has served as principal guest conductor.

Role and repertoire

The orchestra performs a regular season of concerts at the Barbican, studio concerts at Maida Vale (many free to audiences), and broadcasts extensively on BBC Radio 3. It is particularly noted for championing new music and for its Total Immersion days devoted to a single composer.
