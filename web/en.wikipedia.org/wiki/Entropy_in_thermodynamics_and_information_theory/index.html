Entropy in thermodynamics and information theory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The mathematical expressions for thermodynamic entropy in statistical thermodynamics and for information entropy in information theory are strikingly similar. In both fields, entropy is a logarithmic measure of the number of possible states or configurations of a system, and the close formal relationship between the two concepts has been a subject of study since the mid-20th century.

The two formulas

Ludwig Boltzmann's formula for thermodynamic entropy is S = kB ln W, where W is the number of microstates consistent with a given macrostate and kB is Boltzmann's constant. Claude Shannon's 1948 formula for information entropy is H = −Σ pi log2 pi, the expected information content of a message. The Gibbs entropy formula, S = −kB Σ pi ln pi, has exactly the same form as Shannon's, differing only in the constant and logarithm base.

Historical connection

According to a well-known account, when Shannon asked John von Neumann what to call his measure, von Neumann suggested "entropy", noting the formula's identity with the thermodynamic one. E. T. Jaynes later argued, in his maximum entropy principle, that statistical mechanics can be understood as a form of statistical inference. Landauer's principle establishes a physical link: erasing one bit of information must dissipate at least kB T ln 2 of heat, a result used to resolve the Maxwell's demon paradox.
