Battle of Nuʿ

Date1289
LocationNear the Nuʿ river plain, northern Vietnam
ResultĐại Việt victory
BelligerentsYuan dynasty (Mongol Empire) vs. Đại Việt (Trần dynasty)

The Battle of Nuʿ was an engagement fought in 1289 during the third Mongol invasion of Đại Việt, in which the forces of the Vietnamese Trần dynasty ambushed and defeated a Yuan detachment near the Nuʿ river crossing in the northern part of the country. The battle formed part of the war that culminated in the decisive Vietnamese victory at the Battle of Bạch Đằng.

Invasion routes of the Yuan armies into Đại Việt, 1287–1288.

Background

After repelling invasions in 1258 and 1285, Đại Việt faced a third and largest Yuan expedition in 1287–1288 under Prince Toghon, son of Kublai Khan. The Vietnamese command, directed by Prince Trần Hưng Đạo, avoided pitched battle, evacuated the capital Thăng Long, and harassed the invaders' supply lines. A Yuan supply fleet was destroyed at Vân Đồn, leaving the occupying army short of food.

The battle

As the Yuan columns began their withdrawal through the northern river valleys, Trần forces laid ambushes along the routes of retreat. Near the Nuʿ crossing, Vietnamese troops attacked a Yuan detachment guarding the line of march, inflicting heavy losses and capturing baggage and prisoners. The action, though smaller than the great river battle at Bạch Đằng, contributed to the disintegration of the Yuan retreat.

Aftermath

The Yuan court abandoned further attempts at conquest, and Đại Việt resumed a tributary relationship that preserved its independence. The campaigns of 1285–1288 remain a central episode in Vietnamese national memory, and their commanders are venerated as national heroes.
