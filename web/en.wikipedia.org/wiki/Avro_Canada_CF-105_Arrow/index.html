Avro Canada CF-105 Arrow

The Avro Canada CF-105 Arrow was a delta-winged interceptor aircraft designed and built by Avro Canada. Intended to serve as the Royal Canadian Air Force's primary interceptor for the 1960s and beyond, it was designed to reach speeds of Mach 2 at altitudes above 50,000 feet (15,000 m) to counter Soviet bomber aircraft. The programme was abruptly cancelled on 20 February 1959, a date that became known in Canada as "Black Friday".

Design and development

Development began in the early 1950s in response to a Royal Canadian Air Force requirement for a supersonic all-weather interceptor. The Arrow featured a large delta wing, advanced fly-by-wire controls, and was to be powered by the Canadian-designed Orenda Iroquois turbojet engine. Five pre-production Arrows, powered by interim Pratt & Whitney J75 engines, were completed and flown.

The first Arrow, RL-201, made its maiden flight on 25 March 1958, piloted by Janusz Żurakowski. The test aircraft demonstrated impressive performance, reaching nearly Mach 2 in level flight even with the less powerful interim engines.

Cancellation

On 20 February 1959, Prime Minister John Diefenbaker announced the cancellation of the Arrow and Iroquois engine programmes, citing escalating costs and the changing threat environment in the age of intercontinental ballistic missiles. The completed aircraft, tooling, and technical documentation were ordered destroyed, ostensibly for security reasons. Some 14,500 Avro employees lost their jobs, and many Canadian engineers emigrated to the United States, where a number joined NASA's space programme.

The cancellation remains one of the most controversial episodes in Canadian aviation history and continues to be debated as a symbol of lost technological independence.
