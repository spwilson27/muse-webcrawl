Prime Minister of Iraq

The Prime Minister is Iraq's head of government.

The Prime Minister of Iraq is the head of government of Iraq and the commander-in-chief of the Iraqi Armed Forces in practice. Under the 2005 constitution, Iraq is a parliamentary republic: executive authority rests chiefly with the prime minister and the Council of Ministers, while the President of Iraq is largely a ceremonial head of state.

StyleHis Excellency
AppointerPresident of Iraq, with the confidence of the Council of Representatives
Term lengthFour years, renewable

History

The office was created under the British Mandate in 1920. Prime ministers served under the Hashemite monarchy until the 14 July Revolution of 1958 established a republic. During the Ba'athist era (1968–2003), real power lay with the president, and Saddam Hussein himself held the premiership from 1979/1994 periods. After the 2003 invasion of Iraq, an interim government under Iyad Allawi (2004–2005) was followed by the first elected government of Ibrahim al-Jaafari, then Nouri al-Maliki (2006–2014).

Appointment

Following elections, the President charges the nominee of the largest bloc in the Council of Representatives with forming a government within 30 days. The cabinet must then win a vote of confidence. By the informal muhasasa convention of power-sharing, the prime minister is a Shia Arab, the president a Kurd, and the parliamentary speaker a Sunni Arab.

Recent officeholders

  • Haider al-Abadi (2014–2018)
  • Adil Abdul-Mahdi (2018–2020)
  • Mustafa Al-Kadhimi (2020–2022)
  • Mohammed Shia' al-Sudani (2022– )

Powers

The prime minister directs the general policy of the state, commands the armed forces, chairs the Council of Ministers, and may dismiss ministers with parliamentary approval.
