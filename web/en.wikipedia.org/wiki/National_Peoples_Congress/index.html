National People's Congress

Established1954
SeatsAbout 2,900–3,000 deputies
Meeting placeGreat Hall of the People, Beijing

The National People's Congress (NPC) is the national legislature of the People's Republic of China and, under the Chinese constitution, the highest organ of state power. With nearly 3,000 deputies it is the largest parliamentary body in the world. It convenes in full session for roughly two weeks each March in Beijing, alongside the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference — the "Two Sessions" that dominate China's political calendar.

Composition and powers

Deputies are elected for five-year terms by provincial-level people's congresses and by the People's Liberation Army; voters elect only local congresses directly, so the NPC is indirectly chosen through a pyramid of congresses. Formally the NPC amends the constitution, enacts basic laws, elects the President and Vice President, chooses the Premier on the President's nomination, approves the budget and national plans, and can declare war. Because it meets only briefly each year, most of its authority is exercised by its Standing Committee of about 175 members, chaired by one of China's top leaders, which legislates and interprets the constitution year-round.

Role in practice

In practice the NPC operates under the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party: senior appointments and major legislation are decided by the Party beforehand, and plenary votes pass with overwhelming or unanimous margins, leading foreign observers to describe it as a "rubber stamp". Scholars note, however, that Standing Committee lawmaking has become more professional and consultative over time, with draft laws published for public comment and occasional significant dissenting or abstention votes at the margins of policy.
