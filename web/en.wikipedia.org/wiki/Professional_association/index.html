Professional association

A professional association (also called a professional body, professional organisation, or professional society) is a group that usually seeks to further a particular profession, the interests of individuals and organisations engaged in that profession, and the public interest. In the United States, such an association is typically a nonprofit business league, while in the United Kingdom professional bodies may also act as learned societies or as regulators with statutory powers.

Professional association
TypeNonprofit organisation (typically)
PurposeAdvance a profession; set standards; represent members
ExamplesAMA, IEEE, Royal Society of Chemistry, Law Society

Roles

Professional associations typically perform some combination of the following:

  • Standard setting — establishing codes of ethics and standards of practice for the profession;
  • Certification and accreditation — examining and credentialing practitioners, and accrediting educational programmes;
  • Regulation — in some countries, maintaining an official register and disciplining members (sometimes under statutory authority, as with medical and legal regulators);
  • Continuing professional development — providing courses, conferences and journals;
  • Representation — lobbying government and speaking for the profession in public debate;
  • Knowledge exchange — publishing journals, organising conferences and networking activities.

Professional body vs trade union

Unlike a trade union, whose primary role is collective bargaining over pay and conditions for employees, a professional association is primarily concerned with the standing, standards and knowledge of the profession. Some bodies, such as the British Medical Association, function as both.

History

Modern professional associations grew out of medieval guilds and the learned societies of the scientific revolution. The 19th century saw the formal organisation of medicine, law, engineering and accountancy; many bodies received royal charters in the United Kingdom and Commonwealth countries, conferring the right to award chartered status (e.g. Chartered Engineer, Chartered Accountant).
