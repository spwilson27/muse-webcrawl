Soldier Field

Soldier Field is an American football stadium located in the Near South Side of Chicago, Illinois, near Lake Michigan. Opened in 1924 as the Municipal Grant Park Stadium, it was renamed in 1925 as a memorial to American soldiers who died in combat. It has been the home of the Chicago Bears of the National Football League since 1971.

History

Designed in the Neoclassical style with its famous Doric colonnades, the stadium hosted events far beyond football: the 1926 Army–Navy game, the 1927 Long–Tunney heavyweight rematch ("the long count fight"), the 1928 Eucharistic Congress, races, rallies, and concerts. It also hosted matches of the 1994 FIFA World Cup.

Renovation

A controversial renovation in 2002–2003 inserted a modern steel-and-glass bowl inside the preserved historic colonnades, reducing capacity to about 61,500 — the smallest in the NFL — and causing the stadium to lose its National Historic Landmark status in 2006. The futuristic interior contrasting with the classical shell has been likened to a spaceship landing on the old building.

Tenants and future

Besides the Bears, Soldier Field hosts the Chicago Fire FC of MLS (since 2020) and major concerts and events. The Bears have explored a new stadium, including purchasing the former Arlington Park racetrack site in Arlington Heights in 2023, though the team has also studied sites in Chicago.
