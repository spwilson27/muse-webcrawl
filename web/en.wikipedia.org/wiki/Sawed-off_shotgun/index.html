Sawed-off shotgun

A sawed-off shotgun (also called a sawn-off shotgun in British English and a short-barreled shotgun in American terminology) is a type of shotgun with a shorter gun barrel — typically under 18 inches (460 mm) — and often a shortened or absent stock. The term is usually applied to illegal weapons that were created by cutting down a standard shotgun's barrel, stock, or both, rendering the weapon more compact and concealable.

Effects of shortening

Compared to a standard shotgun, the sawed-off shotgun has a shorter effective range, due to a lower muzzle velocity and wider spread of shot. Its reduced size makes it easier to conceal. Such a powerful and compact weapon is especially suitable for use in small, confined spaces, such as by military units in close-quarters combat and by criminals in vehicle interiors. Removing the stock makes the weapon harder to aim accurately at distance, but at very close range the spread of shot compensates for rough aiming. Shortening the barrel removes any choke, dramatically widening the pattern.

Legal status

Short-barreled shotguns are heavily regulated in many jurisdictions. In the United States, shotguns with barrels shorter than 18 inches or an overall length under 26 inches are regulated under the National Firearms Act of 1934, requiring registration and a tax stamp; unregistered manufacture or possession is a federal felony. Factory-made short shotguns and weapons such as the Serbu Super-Shorty exist legally under this regime. In the United Kingdom, shotguns with barrels under 24 inches fall into the prohibited "section 5" category. Similar minimum-barrel laws exist in Canada, Australia, and most European countries.

History and use

Short shotguns have a long pedigree: coach guns and "whipit" guns were carried by stagecoach guards and express messengers in the 19th century, and blunderbuss-like short arms predate them. Sawed-off shotguns acquired a strong criminal association during Prohibition-era America — Clyde Barrow's cut-down Browning Auto-5 "whipit gun" is a famous example — and in British armed robbery culture, where the "sawn-off" became a stereotypical weapon of bank robbers. The term lupara refers in Sicily to a sawn-off shotgun traditionally associated with the Mafia.
