Bata is a port city in the Litoral Province of Equatorial Guinea and the largest city on the mainland portion of the country, Río Muni. Situated on the Atlantic coast, it served as the capital of Spanish Guinea during the colonial period and remains a major economic centre, rivalling the capital, Malabo, on Bioko Island.

Geography and economy

Bata lies on a coastal plain backed by forested hills. Its deep-water port handles timber, cocoa and petroleum-related traffic, and the city is connected by road to the rest of Río Muni and by ferry and air to Malabo. The oil boom of the 1990s and 2000s brought rapid growth, new government buildings, a promenade along the seafront (the Paseo Marítimo) and the Estadio de Bata, built for the 2012 Africa Cup of Nations.

History

The area was long inhabited by Ndowe peoples. Spain developed Bata as the administrative centre of its mainland colony. After independence in 1968 the city suffered under the Macías Nguema regime but revived following the discovery of offshore oil.

2021 explosions

On 7 March 2021 a series of explosions at a military base in the Nkoa Ntoma district, caused by improperly stored munitions, killed more than one hundred people and injured hundreds, devastating surrounding neighbourhoods and prompting an international relief effort.
