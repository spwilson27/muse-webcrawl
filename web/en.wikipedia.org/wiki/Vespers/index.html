Vespers

Vespers is the evening prayer service in the Liturgy of the Hours of the Catholic Church and in the traditions of Eastern Orthodoxy, Lutheranism and Anglicanism (where it corresponds closely to Evening Prayer/Evensong). It takes place as dusk falls, around 6 p.m., and its name derives from the Latin vesper, "evening".

Structure

In the Roman Rite, Vespers (Evening Prayer) consists of an introduction, a hymn, psalmody — typically two psalms and a New Testament canticle — a short reading, a responsory, the Magnificat (the canticle of Mary), intercessions, the Lord's Prayer and a concluding prayer. The lighting of lamps and incense recall the ancient lucernarium.

Music

Vespers has inspired major compositions, most famously Monteverdi's Vespro della Beata Vergine (1610), as well as settings by Mozart and Rachmaninoff's All-Night Vigil, whose Russian title is often rendered "Vespers".

History

Evening prayer has roots in Jewish prayer at day's end and was part of the daily monastic office described by St Benedict. The Sicilian Vespers of 1282 — a revolt that began at evening prayer in Palermo — takes its name from the service.
