USS United States (1797)

USS United States was a wooden-hulled, three-masted heavy frigate of the United States Navy and the first of the six original frigates authorized by the Naval Act of 1794. Launched on 10 May 1797 in Philadelphia and designed by Joshua Humphreys, she was the first American warship launched under the new federal navy, and President George Washington named her for the nation itself.

Early service

Commissioned under Captain John Barry, United States patrolled the Caribbean during the Quasi-War with France, capturing several French vessels including the privateers La Croyable and L'Amour de la Patrie. She later carried Oliver Hazard Perry and served in the Mediterranean Squadron against the Barbary states.

War of 1812

Under Captain Stephen Decatur, on 25 October 1812 United States encountered HMS Macedonian in the mid-Atlantic and, in a celebrated action, battered the British frigate into surrender using her heavier long guns at range. The victory — the Macedonian was brought into Newport as a prize — was an enormous morale boost for the United States. In June 1813 Decatur was chased into New London, Connecticut, where the frigate remained blockaded for the rest of the war.

Later years and fate

After further Mediterranean service and a goodwill cruise to the Pacific, the aging frigate was laid up at Norfolk in 1849. Seized by Virginia at the start of the Civil War and commissioned as CSS United States, she was scuttled as an obstruction in the Elizabeth River in 1862; raised by Union forces, she was broken up in 1865–66.
