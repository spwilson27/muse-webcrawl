Snapdragon (system on chip)

Snapdragon is a family of systems on a chip (SoC) semiconductor products designed and marketed by Qualcomm Technologies for mobile devices, laptops, and other embedded platforms. A Snapdragon chip integrates a central processing unit (CPU), a graphics processing unit (GPU, branded Adreno), a digital signal processor (Hexagon), an image signal processor (Spectra), a cellular modem, and other accelerators on a single die.

History

Qualcomm announced the first Snapdragon chip, the QSD8250 with its custom Scorpion CPU core, in the mid-2000s; it shipped in devices such as the HTC HD2 and the Google Nexus One (2010). The S1 through S4 generations gave way in 2013 to a tiered numbering scheme — the 200, 400, 600, 700, and 800 series — and in 2021 Qualcomm rebranded the flagship line as the Snapdragon 8 series (beginning with Snapdragon 8 Gen 1), with custom Oryon CPU cores arriving in later generations. Snapdragon processors powered a large share of Android smartphones from Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus, and many other manufacturers, and the platform expanded into Windows laptops (Snapdragon X series), wearables, automotive cockpits, and extended-reality headsets.

Components

Snapdragon CPUs historically licensed ARM architectures, with Qualcomm designing custom cores such as Scorpion, Krait, Kryo, and Oryon. The Adreno GPU handles graphics and compute; the Hexagon DSP and later dedicated neural processing units accelerate AI workloads; and integrated modems (such as the X-series) made Snapdragon a leader in 4G LTE and 5G connectivity. The chips are fabricated by foundries including TSMC and Samsung on leading process nodes.

Market position

Snapdragon is one of the dominant platforms in the Android ecosystem and a principal competitor to Apple's A-series silicon, MediaTek's Dimensity line, and Samsung's Exynos. Qualcomm's combination of application processing and best-in-class modems made Snapdragon the default choice for premium Android phones in many markets, particularly North America.
