Fourth-wave feminism

Fourth-wave feminism is a feminist movement that began around 2012 and is characterised by a focus on the empowerment of women, the use of internet tools and social media, and intersectionality. Building on earlier waves — suffrage (first wave), the social and legal campaigns of the 1960s–80s (second wave) and the individualist, diversity-conscious 1990s (third wave) — the fourth wave centres on sexual harassment, body shaming, rape culture and violence against women.

Characteristics

Scholars and commentators associate the fourth wave with online campaigns and hashtag activism, including #YesAllWomen (2014), the #MeToo movement (which went viral in 2017 and exposed widespread sexual abuse, notably in the Harvey Weinstein cases), #TimesUp, and campaigns against everyday sexism such as the Everyday Sexism Project founded by Laura Bates in 2012. The wave emphasises that gender inequality intersects with race, class, disability and sexuality.

Criticism and debate

Critics question the "wave" framework itself, noting it can flatten overlapping histories; others argue that online activism risks substituting visibility for structural change, or that fourth-wave campaigns can be exclusionary — debates that have played out in controversies over trans inclusion and sex work. The term remains in wide use in media and scholarship to distinguish post-2012 feminism from its predecessors.
