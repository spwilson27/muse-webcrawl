Interface (matter)

The curved meniscus of water in a tube is a visible interface shaped by surface tension.

In the physical sciences, an interface is the boundary between two spatial regions occupied by different matter, or by matter in different physical states. The interface between a body and its surroundings — or between two substances — is a region a few atoms thick where properties change from those of one phase to those of the other. Interfaces are central to colloid and surface science.

Types

Common interfaces include liquid–gas (a surface), liquid–liquid, solid–liquid, solid–gas, and solid–solid boundaries. Grain boundaries in polycrystalline solids and phase boundaries in alloys are solid-state interfaces.

Properties

Interfaces carry excess free energy per unit area, manifested as surface tension (interfacial tension) for fluid interfaces. Molecules at an interface experience asymmetric forces, driving phenomena such as capillary action, wetting, adsorption, and the formation of emulsions and foams. Surfactants adsorb at interfaces and lower interfacial tension, stabilising mixtures of immiscible fluids.

Measurement and study

Interfacial tension is measured by tensiometers using pendant-drop, Wilhelmy plate, or spinning-drop methods. Interface structure is probed by techniques such as neutron and X-ray reflectivity, ellipsometry, and surface-specific spectroscopies.

Importance

Interfacial phenomena govern catalysis, corrosion, lubrication, biological membranes, detergent action, and many industrial processes including oil recovery and food processing.
