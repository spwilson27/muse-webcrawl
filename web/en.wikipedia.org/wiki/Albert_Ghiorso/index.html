Albert Ghiorso

Albert Ghiorso (July 15, 1915 – December 26, 2010) was an American nuclear scientist who co-discovered a record twelve chemical elements — more than any other person — as a researcher at the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. Working with Glenn T. Seaborg and many collaborators, Ghiorso was a credited co-discoverer of americium, curium, berkelium, californium, einsteinium, fermium, mendelevium, nobelium, lawrencium, rutherfordium, dubnium and seaborgium (elements 95–106).

Ghiorso at Berkeley, where he led the discovery of twelve elements.

Career

Born in Vallejo, California, Ghiorso built a telegraph-receiver business as a teenager and graduated in electrical engineering from UC Berkeley in 1937. He joined Seaborg's plutonium group during the Second World War, where his sensitive radiation-detection instruments — he invented the 48-channel pulse-height analyser — enabled the identification of new elements from minute samples, including the discovery of einsteinium and fermium in the debris of the first hydrogen bomb test. He designed the Berkeley Heavy Ion Linear Accelerator (HILAC) and later the Bevalac, and remained scientifically active at Berkeley into his nineties, pursuing element 118 and beyond. Element discoverers' priority disputes with Soviet teams at Dubna occupied much of his later career. See also Glenn T. Seaborg and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.
