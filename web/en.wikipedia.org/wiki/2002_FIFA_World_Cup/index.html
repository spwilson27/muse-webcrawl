The 2002 FIFA World Cup was the 17th FIFA World Cup, the quadrennial international football championship for men's national teams. It was held from 31 May to 30 June 2002 in South Korea and Japan, the first World Cup to be held in Asia and the first to be hosted by more than one country. Thirty-two teams competed in 64 matches across twenty stadiums.

Brazil won the tournament for a record-extending fifth time, defeating Germany 2–0 in the final at the International Stadium in Yokohama, Japan. Ronaldo, who had missed much of the previous two seasons through injury, scored both goals in the final and finished as the tournament's top scorer with eight goals, winning the Golden Boot. Turkey finished third and co-hosts South Korea finished fourth, both achieving their best-ever results; South Korea's run to the semi-finals was the best performance by an Asian team in World Cup history.

Background

South Korea and Japan were chosen as joint hosts by FIFA in 1996, an arrangement intended in part to ease tensions between the two countries. China, Senegal, Slovenia, and Ecuador made their World Cup debuts. Defending champions France were eliminated in the group stage without scoring a goal, one of the biggest shocks in tournament history, after losing their opening match 1–0 to debutants Senegal.

Knockout stage

The knockout rounds produced several memorable results. South Korea defeated Italy in the round of 16 with a golden goal by Ahn Jung-hwan and then beat Spain on penalties in the quarter-finals. The United States reached the quarter-finals, losing narrowly to Germany. Brazil, led by the attacking trio of Ronaldo, Rivaldo, and Ronaldinho, beat England 2–1 in the quarter-finals — Ronaldinho scoring the winning goal with a long-range free kick — and Turkey 1–0 in the semi-finals.

Final

The final, played on 30 June 2002 in Yokohama, was the first World Cup meeting between Brazil and Germany, the two most successful nations in the competition's history. After a goalless first half in which German goalkeeper Oliver Kahn — voted the tournament's best player — kept Brazil at bay, Ronaldo scored twice in the second half, in the 67th and 79th minutes, the first after Kahn spilled a shot from Rivaldo. Brazil's captain Cafu lifted the trophy, becoming the only player to appear in three consecutive World Cup finals.

Legacy

The tournament is remembered for the success of the co-hosts, the redemption of Ronaldo after his troubled 1998 final, and a series of controversial refereeing decisions in South Korea's knockout matches. It remains the last World Cup won by a South American team as of the early 2020s.
