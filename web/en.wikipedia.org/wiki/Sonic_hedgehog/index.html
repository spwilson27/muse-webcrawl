Sonic hedgehog

Sonic hedgehog protein
Gene: SHH (chromosome 7q36.3 in humans)
Family: Hedgehog signalling proteins
Function: Morphogen in embryonic development
Named after: Sonic the Hedgehog

Sonic hedgehog (SHH) is a protein encoded by the SHH gene in humans and a key signalling molecule in the hedgehog signalling pathway. It acts as a morphogen during embryonic development, patterning the central nervous system, limbs, and many other structures.

Discovery and naming

The hedgehog gene was first identified in the fruit fly Drosophila melanogaster, where mutations produced larvae covered in pointy denticles resembling a hedgehog's spines. When three mammalian homologues were found in the early 1990s, two were named after real hedgehog species (desert hedgehog and Indian hedgehog), while the third was named after the video game character Sonic the Hedgehog — a choice that has occasionally drawn comment from clinicians discussing serious genetic disorders with patients.

Function

SHH is essential for the development of the ventral neural tube, the anterior–posterior patterning of the limbs via the zone of polarizing activity, and the formation of the eyes and forebrain (holoprosencephaly can result from SHH mutations). The protein is modified by cholesterol and palmitate, and signals through the Patched and Smoothened receptors to Gli transcription factors. In adults, aberrant hedgehog pathway activation is implicated in cancers such as basal cell carcinoma and medulloblastoma, and pathway inhibitors (e.g. vismodegib) are used therapeutically.
