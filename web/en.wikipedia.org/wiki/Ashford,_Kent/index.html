Ashford, Kent

Population: ~83,000 (2021, built-up area)
County: Kent
District: Borough of Ashford
Region: South East England

Ashford town centre

Ashford is a town in the county of Kent, England. It lies on the River Great Stour at the southern edge of the North Downs, about 61 miles (98 km) southeast of London. Historically an agricultural market town, Ashford grew rapidly with the coming of the railway in the 1840s and is now one of Kent's largest towns and a designated growth area.

History

The settlement's name derives from the Old English æscet-ford, a ford near a clump of ash trees. Ashford received a market charter in 1243 and became an important centre for the surrounding farming country. The South Eastern Railway opened its locomotive works here in 1847, making the town a significant railway centre; the works closed in 1981.

Transport

Ashford International station is a stop on High Speed 1, with domestic high-speed services to London St Pancras in under 40 minutes; international Eurostar services were suspended in 2020. The M20 motorway passes the town, linking London with the Channel Tunnel and Dover.
