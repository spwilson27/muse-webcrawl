Charleston Harbor is an estuary and inlet of the Atlantic Ocean in Charleston, South Carolina, formed by the confluence of the Ashley, Cooper, and Wando rivers. One of the finest natural harbors on the American Atlantic coast, it has been central to Charleston's history since the city's founding in 1670 and was the site of the opening battle of the American Civil War.

Charleston Harbor and Fort Sumter

Geography

The harbor lies where the Ashley River (to the west) and Cooper River (to the east) meet — Charlestonians joke that the two rivers join "to form the Atlantic Ocean" — with the Wando River entering from the northeast. The main shipping channel passes between Sullivan's Island and Morris Island to the open sea. The city of Charleston occupies the peninsula between the Ashley and Cooper. A harbor-deepening project completed in the 2020s made Charleston's channel 52 feet deep, among the deepest on the U.S. East Coast, serving the Port of Charleston's container terminals.

History

English colonists established Charles Town in 1670, first at Albemarle Point and from 1680 at the present peninsula site. The harbor's commerce in rice, indigo, and enslaved Africans made Charleston one of the wealthiest cities in British America. During the Revolutionary War, repulsing the British fleet in 1776 at the Battle of Sullivan's Island protected the harbor, though the British captured the city in 1780 after a siege.

Civil War

On 12 April 1861, Confederate batteries around the harbor opened fire on the Union garrison of Fort Sumter, the island fort at the harbor entrance, beginning the American Civil War. For much of the war the harbor was blockaded and contested; the Confederate submarine H. L. Hunley sank the USS Housatonic off the harbor entrance in 1864, the first successful submarine attack on a warship. Union forces evacuated the harbor forts' opponents only in February 1865.

Today

Charleston Harbor remains a working port and a major tourist destination. Fort Sumter and Fort Moultrie are preserved as Fort Sumter and Fort Monroe National Historical Park units; harbor tours, the Arthur Ravenel Jr. Bridge (2005) over the Cooper River, and the annual Southeastern Wildlife Exposition and Spoleto Festival draw on the harbor setting.
