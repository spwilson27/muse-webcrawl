Free reed aerophone

A free reed aerophone is a musical instrument in which sound is produced by a reed that vibrates freely through a closely fitting frame, driven by a stream of air. In the Hornbostel–Sachs classification, free reed aerophones are category 412.13. Unlike beating reeds (as in the clarinet), the free reed swings through its frame without striking it, and the pitch is determined mainly by the reed's own dimensions and stiffness.

Principle

Each reed is a thin tongue of metal (or, historically, bamboo or other materials) fixed at one end over a slot slightly larger than the reed itself. Air pressure or suction causes the reed to vibrate through the slot, periodically interrupting the airflow and producing a tone. In many instruments separate reeds are used for the blow and draw directions, or a single reed may serve both.

History

Free reed instruments have a long history in East and Southeast Asia: the Chinese sheng dates back some three thousand years, and related instruments include the Japanese shō and the Lao–Thai khaen. The free reed reached Europe around the late 18th century and inspired a wave of 19th-century inventions: the harmonica, the concertina, the accordion, the melodeon, and the reed organ (harmonium).

Instruments

  • Harmonica (mouth organ)
  • Accordion and concertina
  • Sheng, shō, and khaen
  • Reed organ / harmonium and the American pump organ
  • Melodica, pitch pipes, and the regal (historically)
