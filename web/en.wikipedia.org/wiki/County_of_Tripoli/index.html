County of Tripoli

County of Tripoli
Crusader state, 1102–1289
Capital: Tripoli (Lebanon)
First count: Raymond IV of Toulouse

The County of Tripoli was the last of the four major Crusader states to be founded, established in the Levant around the city of Tripoli in modern Lebanon after the First Crusade. Founded by Raymond IV of Toulouse, it existed from 1102 until the fall of Tripoli to the Mamluks in 1289.

History

Raymond began the siege of Tripoli in 1102, building the fortress of Mons Peregrinus; the city itself fell in 1109, shortly after his death. The counts ruled a coastal strip protected by great castles including Krak des Chevaliers (held by the Hospitallers). After the fall of Antioch in 1268 the county stood alone, and its capital fell to Sultan Qalawun in 1289.
