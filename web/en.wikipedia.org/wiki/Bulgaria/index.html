Bulgaria

Bulgaria, officially the Republic of Bulgaria, is a country in Southeast Europe on the Balkan Peninsula, bordered by Romania, Serbia, North Macedonia, Greece and Turkey, with a Black Sea coast. Its population is about 6.4 million, declining rapidly, and its capital is Sofia.

The Bulgarian Empire was one of medieval Europe's great powers and the homeland of the Cyrillic script, developed in the 9th century. Bulgaria fell under Ottoman rule for nearly five centuries (1396-1878), was a Soviet ally in the 20th century, and joined the EU in 2007. It is known for yogurt and Lactobacillus bulgaricus, rose oil from the Kazanlak valley, Thracian gold treasures, Orthodox monasteries such as Rila, and Black Sea resorts.
