British Academy Television Awards

The BAFTA Television Awards recognise outstanding achievement in British television.

The British Academy Television Awards, often referred to simply as the BAFTA TV Awards, are presented in an annual award show hosted by the British Academy of Film and Television Arts (BAFTA). They have been awarded annually since 1955 and are the television equivalent of the British Academy Film Awards. The awards recognise excellence in British television programming across drama, comedy, entertainment, factual and news categories.

History

The first television awards were presented in 1955, when there were just six categories. For many years the film and television awards were presented together in a single ceremony, but to give television its own prominence they were split in 1998. The ceremony is traditionally held in April or May and broadcast on British television, usually on BBC One.

A separate ceremony, the British Academy Television Craft Awards, honours behind-the-scenes achievement in areas such as directing, writing, editing, production design and original music.

Categories

Major categories include Best Drama Series, Best Mini-Series, Best Single Drama, Best Comedy Series, Best Situation Comedy, Best Entertainment Performance, Best Actor, Best Actress, Best Supporting Actor and Actress, Best Factual Series, Best News Coverage and Best International Programme. The Radio Times Audience Award, chosen by public vote, was among the best-known audience-voted honours.

Trophy

Winners receive a bronze mask trophy, the BAFTA mask, designed by American sculptor Mitzi Cunliffe and based on the theatrical masks of tragedy and comedy.
