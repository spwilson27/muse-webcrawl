The Toyota Sprinter (Japanese: トヨタ・スプリンター) is a compact car manufactured by Toyota as a counterpart of the Toyota Corolla. It was produced from 1968 to 2001 over eight generations. The Sprinter was sold through the Toyota Auto Store (later Toyota Vista Store) dealer network in Japan, while the Corolla was sold through the Corolla Store network; the two cars shared platforms and engines but differed in styling details and equipment.

Generations

The first-generation Sprinter (E10) debuted in 1968 as a fastback coupé version of the Corolla. Subsequent generations followed the Corolla range closely, offering sedans, coupés, hatchbacks, and wagons (including the Sprinter Carib). The fifth generation (E80, 1983–1987) is especially notable because it included the Sprinter Trueno, the rear-wheel-drive AE86 coupé that became famous in motorsport and popular culture. Later generations adopted front-wheel drive like the Corolla. The nameplate was discontinued in 2001, when the Sprinter sedan was replaced by the Toyota Allex hatchback.

Motorsport

The Sprinter Trueno and its Corolla Levin counterpart enjoyed considerable success in touring car racing and rallying, and the AE86 became an icon of drifting culture.
