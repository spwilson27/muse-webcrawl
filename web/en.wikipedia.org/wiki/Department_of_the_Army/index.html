The United States Department of the Army (DA) is one of the three military departments within the Department of Defense of the United States. It is the federal government agency within which the United States Army (U.S. Army) is organized, and it is led by the Secretary of the Army, who has statutory authority to conduct all the business of the department.

History

The Department of the Army was formed in 1947 under the National Security Act, which reorganised the former Department of War — in existence since 1789 — within the new National Military Establishment, renamed the Department of Defense in 1949. The Secretary of the Army, once a cabinet officer as Secretary of War, now reports to the Secretary of Defense.

Organization

The department's headquarters comprises the Office of the Secretary of the Army and the Army Staff, headed by the Chief of Staff of the Army. It administers the Regular Army, the Army Reserve on active duty, and — when federalised — the Army National Guard, together with installations, acquisition, and civilian employees worldwide.
