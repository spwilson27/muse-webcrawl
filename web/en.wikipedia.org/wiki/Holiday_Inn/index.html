Holiday Inn

Industry: Hospitality
Founded: 1 August 1952; Memphis, Tennessee, U.S.
Founder: Kemmons Wilson
Parent: IHG Hotels & Resorts

Holiday Inn is an American chain of hotels based in Atlanta, Georgia, and a brand of IHG Hotels & Resorts. Founded in 1952 by Memphis home-builder Kemmons Wilson, it grew to become one of the world's largest hotel chains, pioneering the standardised roadside motel-hotel for automobile travellers and business guests.

History

Wilson was inspired by a frustrating family road trip to Washington, D.C., during which motels charged extra for children and were of inconsistent quality. He opened the first Holiday Inn on Summer Avenue in Memphis in 1952; the name was a joking reference by his architect to the 1942 Bing Crosby film Holiday Inn. Each property offered air conditioning, a swimming pool, a restaurant, free ice, and children staying free. Franchising from 1954 fuelled rapid growth, and the chain's tall roadside "Great Sign" became an American icon. By 1972, Holiday Inn operated more than 1,400 hotels, and Time magazine called Wilson's creation a revolution in travel.

Ownership and brand family

The company expanded into other businesses in the 1970s and 1980s but later refocused. British brewer Bass plc acquired the chain in stages (1988–1990); Bass's hotel division became InterContinental Hotels Group (IHG) in 2003. Brand extensions include Holiday Inn Express (launched 1991 as a limited-service brand, now one of the largest hotel brands in the world), Holiday Inn Select, Holiday Inn Club Vacations, and the upscale Crowne Plaza spinoff. As of recent years, there are well over 1,100 Holiday Inn hotels open worldwide, with the Holiday Inn Express portfolio adding several thousand more properties.
