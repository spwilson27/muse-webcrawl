Albert III of Austria (9 September 1349 – 29 August 1395), known as Albert with the Braid (Albert mit dem Zopf), was a Duke of Austria from 1365 until his death. He was the founder of the Albertinian line of the House of Habsburg, from which all later Habsburg rulers descended.

Life

Albert was the third son of Duke Albert II of Austria and Joanna of Pfirt. After his father's death and the brief reign of his eldest brother Rudolf IV, Albert jointly inherited the Habsburg lands with his younger brother Leopold III in 1365. The brothers ruled together, though with recurring friction over revenues and authority.

Division of the Habsburg lands

By the Treaty of Neuberg in 1379, Albert and Leopold formally divided their inheritance: Albert retained Austria proper (the Danube lands), while Leopold received Styria, Carinthia, Carniola, Tyrol and the dynasty's western possessions. This partition created the Albertinian and Leopoldinian lines and weakened the house for a century, until the lines were reunited under Albert's descendants.

After Leopold III was killed at the Battle of Sempach in 1386, Albert also acted as regent and guardian for his brother's young sons, temporarily restoring a measure of unity to the Habsburg possessions.

Patronage and later life

Albert was an energetic ruler and patron: he expanded the University of Vienna, originally founded by Rudolf IV, securing a theological faculty from the Pope in 1384, and promoted building works at St. Stephen's Cathedral. His nickname "with the Braid" derives from his long plaited hair. He founded the hunting lodge at Laxenburg, where he died in 1395, and was succeeded by his son Albert IV.
