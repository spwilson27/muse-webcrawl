Art song

An art song is a vocal music composition, usually written for one singer and piano accompaniment, setting a poem as a self-contained concert piece. The term is used to distinguish serious, through-composed settings in the classical tradition — the German Lied, French mélodie and their counterparts — from folk song and popular song.


A song recital

Characteristics

In the art song, voice and piano are equal partners: the accompaniment illustrates and comments on the poem rather than merely supporting the melody. Composers set texts by major poets, and songs are often grouped into cycles (Liederkreis) that trace a narrative or emotional arc, such as Schubert's Die schöne Müllerin and Winterreise.

National traditions

The genre flowered in the 19th century. Franz Schubert essentially created the modern Lied, followed by Schumann, Brahms, Wolf and Mahler. In France, Fauré, Debussy and Poulenc developed the mélodie; Russia produced the romances of Tchaikovsky and Rachmaninoff; England and America contributed through Vaughan Williams, Britten, Barber and Ives, among many others.

Performance

Art songs are performed in recital, typically by a singer and pianist as a duo, and form a central part of conservatoire vocal training. The intimacy of the genre demands precise diction, tonal control and close musical collaboration.
