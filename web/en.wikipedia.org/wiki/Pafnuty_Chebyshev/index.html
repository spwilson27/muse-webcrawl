Pafnuty Lvovich Chebyshev (Russian: Пафну́тий Льво́вич Чебышёв; 16 May 1821 – 8 December 1894) was a Russian mathematician. Regarded as a founding figure of Russian mathematics, he made fundamental contributions to number theory, probability theory, analysis, and applied mechanics. His surname has been transliterated in many ways, including Tchebychev, Tschebyscheff, and Chebychev.

Biography

Chebyshev was born into a family of landed gentry in the village of Okatovo near Borovsk. One of nine children, he was tutored at home — a childhood ailment left him with a limp that turned him toward intellectual pursuits. He entered Moscow University in 1837, won a silver medal for an essay on the calculation of roots of equations, and graduated in 1841. After earning his master's degree with a dissertation on probability theory, he moved to St Petersburg in 1847, where he taught at the university for the rest of his career, becoming one of its most influential professors. He was elected to the St Petersburg Academy of Sciences and to several foreign academies, including the Royal Society.

Mathematical work

In number theory, Chebyshev proved Bertrand's postulate — that for every n > 1 there is always at least one prime between n and 2n — and obtained important bounds on the distribution of prime numbers, bringing the prime number theorem within reach of the methods later completed by Hadamard and de la Vallée Poussin. In probability, he formulated the law of large numbers for sequences of independent random variables and the inequality named after him, P(|X − μ| ≥ kσ) ≤ 1/k², laying rigorous foundations on which his students Markov and Lyapunov built. In analysis he created the theory of the Chebyshev polynomials, central to approximation theory, and developed orthogonal polynomials and the theory of continued fractions.

Chebyshev also had a strong interest in practical mechanics. He designed more than forty mechanisms, including a famous "plantigrade machine" — a walking linkage exhibited at world's fairs — and the parallel-motion linkage that bears his name. His approach of tying pure mathematics closely to engineering problems became characteristic of the St Petersburg mathematical school he founded.

Legacy

Chebyshev's students and intellectual descendants shaped mathematics across Russia and beyond. The lunar crater Chebyshev and the asteroid 2010 Chebyshev are named in his honour.
