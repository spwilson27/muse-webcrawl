States of Austria

Austria is a federal republic consisting of nine states (German: Länder, singular Land; also called Bundesländer). Each state has its own constitution, legislature (Landtag), and government headed by a governor (Landeshauptmann or Landeshauptfrau). The federal constitution of 1920 distributes competences between the federation and the states, though the states have comparatively limited legislative powers.

History

Most of the states derive from the historical crown lands of the Habsburg monarchy. When the Republic of German-Austria was proclaimed in 1918 and then the First Austrian Republic established in 1919, the provinces organized themselves as constituent states. Burgenland, formerly part of Hungary, joined in 1921. Vienna was separated from Lower Austria in 1922 and became a state in its own right. The state structure was restored after the Second World War, when Austria was divided into occupation zones that partly followed state boundaries.

Government

Each state elects a Landtag (state parliament), which in turn elects the state government. State governments are usually formed proportionally or by coalition. The governor represents the state externally and administers much of federal law indirectly through the state administration (mittelbare Bundesverwaltung). States are represented at the federal level in the Federal Council (Bundesrat), whose members are delegated by the Landtage in proportion to population.

Competences

The federal constitution enumerates federal competences extensively; residual powers lie with the states. Typical state responsibilities include building regulations, local government supervision, hunting and fishing, nature conservation, kindergartens, and some aspects of health and social welfare. Critics note that Austria's federalism is comparatively weak in fiscal terms, since most tax revenue is raised federally and redistributed under the fiscal equalization system (Finanzausgleich).
