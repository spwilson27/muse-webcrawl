Riemann–Stieltjes integral

In mathematics, the Riemann–Stieltjes integral is a generalisation of the Riemann integral, named after Bernhard Riemann and Thomas Joannes Stieltjes. It integrates a function f with respect to another function g, written ∫ f(x) dg(x), reducing to the ordinary Riemann integral when g(x) = x. Stieltjes introduced the concept in 1894 in his research on continued fractions.

Definition

Given a partition of an interval [a, b], one forms sums of the form Σ f(ci) [g(xi) − g(xi−1)], where ci is a sample point in each subinterval. If these sums approach a limit as the partition is refined, that limit is the Riemann–Stieltjes integral of f with respect to g. The function f is called the integrand and g the integrator.

Properties

If g is differentiable, then ∫ f dg = ∫ f(x) g′(x) dx. If g has a jump discontinuity, the integral can pick out the values of f at those points, so the integral unifies summation and integration: integrating against a step function yields weighted sums. Integration by parts holds in the symmetric form ∫ f dg + ∫ g df = f(b)g(b) − f(a)g(a) whenever both integrals exist.

Applications

The Riemann–Stieltjes integral is used in probability theory, where expectations can be written as integrals with respect to a cumulative distribution function (which may have jumps and continuous parts), and in physics and spectral theory. It is subsumed by the more general Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral.
