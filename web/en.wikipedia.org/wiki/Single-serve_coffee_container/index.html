A single-serve coffee container is a method for preparing coffee in a single serving, in which a pre-measured quantity of ground coffee is sealed in an individual pod, capsule or cup that is placed in a compatible brewing machine. Hot water is forced through the container, producing one cup of coffee quickly and with minimal cleanup. Well-known formats include Keurig's K-Cup, Nestlé's Nespresso and Dolce Gusto capsules, and the paper E.S.E. (Easy Serving Espresso) pod.

A selection of single-serve coffee containers: K-Cups, capsules and paper pods.

History

Paper coffee pods predate plastic capsules: Eric Favre developed the capsule concept at Nestlé in 1975, leading eventually to the Nespresso system (launched 1986), while illy standardised the paper E.S.E. pod in the 1990s. Keurig, founded in 1992, popularised the K-Cup in North American offices and homes from the late 1990s onward, and Philips and Sara Lee's Senseo (2001) brought paper pods to European households. After Keurig's key K-Cup patent expired in 2012, third-party and refillable pods proliferated.

Environmental concerns

Single-serve systems have been widely criticised for generating large volumes of composite plastic-and-aluminium waste that is difficult to recycle; billions of capsules are discarded annually. Manufacturers have responded with recycling take-back programmes, aluminium capsules, certified compostable pods and reusable refillable containers, while some cities have banned non-recyclable pods in municipal facilities. Even Keurig co-founder John Sylvan has publicly expressed regret about the format's waste footprint.
