Pretoria is the administrative capital of South Africa — one of the country's three capital cities, with Cape Town (legislative) and Bloemfontein (judicial). It lies in the province of Gauteng about 55 km north-east of Johannesburg, in the transitional belt between the Highveld and the bushveld. Founded in 1855 by the Voortrekker leader Marthinus Pretorius and named after his father Andries Pretorius, victor of Blood River, it became capital of the Boer South African Republic (Transvaal), then seat of the Union government in 1910 and of the apartheid state after 1948.

Landmarks and the name question

The city's defining building is Herbert Baker's Union Buildings (1913), sandstone terraces on Meintjieskop hill, from whose lawns Nelson Mandela was inaugurated in 1994 before jubilant crowds — the symbolic end of the regime those buildings had housed. Below lie the jacaranda avenues of the old suburbs, the Voortrekker Monument (1949) on its southern hill, Freedom Park, the University of Pretoria, and the diplomatic corps second in number only to Washington's. Since 2000 the city has formed the core of the City of Tshwane metropolitan municipality, and proposals to rename Pretoria itself "Tshwane" have been debated for two decades without final resolution (the metro name is official; the city name persists). Its twin obsessions remain government and gardens: a bureaucratic capital with a student's energy and a springtime canopy of purple.
