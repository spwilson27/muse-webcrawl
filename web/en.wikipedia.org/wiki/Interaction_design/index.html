Interaction design
FocusDesigning interactive products, services, and systems
Related fieldsHuman–computer interaction, user experience design, industrial design
Key figuresBill Moggridge, Don Norman, Gillian Crampton Smith

Interaction design, often abbreviated IxD, is the practice of designing interactive digital products, environments, systems, and services. It focuses on shaping the behaviour of products and the dialogue between people and the things they use — defining how controls respond, how information is presented, and how a user's actions produce meaningful outcomes. The term was coined in the mid-1980s by industrial designers Bill Moggridge and Bill Verplank.

Interaction designers prototype behaviour, not just appearance.

Dimensions and principles

Gillian Crampton Smith proposed that interaction design involves words, visual representations, physical objects or space, time, and behaviour. Practitioners rely on principles such as affordances and constraints (popularised by Don Norman), feedback, consistency, and mapping between controls and effects. The process typically involves user research, ideation, prototyping, and usability testing in iterative cycles.

Relationship to other fields

Interaction design overlaps with human–computer interaction (HCI), which supplies research methods and theory, and with user experience (UX) design, which encompasses the broader experience including visual design, content, and brand. As computing has spread into physical products and services, the field has expanded to include voice interfaces, wearables, ambient systems, and service design.
