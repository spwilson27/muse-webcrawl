Ixil language

Ixil (pronounced [iʃil]) is a Mayan language of the Mamean branch, spoken by the Ixil people in the Cuchumatanes highlands of the Quiché Department of Guatemala. Estimates of the number of speakers range from about 70,000 to over 100,000.

Geographic distribution

Ixil is centred on the three municipalities known as the "Ixil Triangle": Santa María Nebaj, San Gaspar Chajul, and San Juan Cotzal. The variety of Nebaj is generally used as the standard for writing and broadcasting. Ixil-speaking communities suffered severely during the Guatemalan Civil War (1960–1996), when the Ixil region was the scene of massacres that a UN-sponsored truth commission characterised as acts of genocide.

Linguistic features

Like other Mayan languages, Ixil is ergative–absolutive and verb-initial, with a rich system of aspectual marking and person markers split between ergative and absolutive sets. It contrasts plain and glottalised (ejective) consonants, and distinguishes retroflex affricates; its vowel system includes a length contrast. A standard alphabet was officialised in 1987 under the auspices of the Academy of Mayan Languages of Guatemala (ALMG), which promotes Ixil literacy and education.
