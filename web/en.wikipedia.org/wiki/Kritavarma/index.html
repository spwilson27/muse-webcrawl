Kritavarma

Kritavarma, commander of Krishna's Narayani Sena (image placeholder)

Kritavarma (Sanskrit: कृतवर्म, Kṛtavarma) is a warrior of the Yadava clan in the Hindu epic Mahabharata. A close associate of Krishna and a commander of the Vrishni Yadavas, he led Krishna's mighty Narayani Sena (Narayana Gopala army) on the Kaurava side in the Kurukshetra War, after Duryodhana chose Krishna's army over Krishna himself. Kritavarma was one of only three survivors of the Kaurava army — together with Kripa and Ashvatthama — and he perished decades later in the drunken fratricidal brawl (the Mausala Parva) that annihilated the Yadavas.

Background and the Narayani Sena

Kritavarma belonged to the Vrishni branch of the Yadavas, son of Hridika, and was counted among the great chariot-warriors (maharathas) of Dvaraka. Before the war both Duryodhana and Arjuna came to Dvaraka to seek Krishna's support. Krishna offered a choice: himself, unarmed, on one side, and his entire Narayani army on the other. Arjuna chose Krishna; Duryodhana gladly took the army — and with it Kritavarma as its commander, so that the Yadavas were split, with Satyaki fighting for the Pandavas and Kritavarma for the Kauravas.

The Kurukshetra War

Kritavarma fought throughout the eighteen days, engaging Pandava heroes including Bhima, Arjuna, Satyaki and the sons of Draupadi. He was stationed to defend Jayadratha on the fourteenth day, survived the fall of the great Kaurava commanders, and defended the rear of the army in the closing battles. On the final day he fought in the chaotic combat around the lake of Duryodhana's fall.

The night raid and survival

On the night after the eighteenth day Kritavarma and Kripa accompanied Ashvatthama to the Pandava camp. At Ashvatthama's request they set fire to the camp's exits and cut down those fleeing the flames while Ashvatthama slaughtered the sleeping Panchala army and the five Upapandavas within. Afterwards the three reported to the dying Duryodhana, who rejoiced at the news before expiring. Kritavarma then withdrew to Dvaraka, escaping the curses that Gandhari pronounced on Krishna's clan.

Death in the Yadava civil war

Thirty-six years later, at Prabhasa, the Yadavas fell to quarrelling in their cups (the Mausala Parva). Satyaki taunted Kritavarma for his role in the night massacre of sleeping men; Kritavarma retorted by recalling Satyaki's killing of the unarmed Bhurishravas. Satyaki beheaded Kritavarma on the spot, and was himself overwhelmed and killed; the brawl with iron clubs (the transformed erakā reeds) then destroyed nearly the whole clan, including Krishna's sons, before Krishna himself departed the world.
