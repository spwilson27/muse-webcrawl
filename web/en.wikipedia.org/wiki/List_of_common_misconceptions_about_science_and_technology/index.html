List of common misconceptions about science and technology

This is a list of common misconceptions about science and technology: widely held beliefs that are contradicted by scientific evidence. Each entry summarises the misconception and the established facts.

Physics and astronomy

Seasons are not caused by Earth's varying distance from the Sun but by axial tilt; Earth is in fact near perihelion in early January. The Great Wall of China is not visible from the Moon with the naked eye, a claim astronauts have refuted. Lightning can strike the same place twice — tall structures such as the Empire State Building are struck many times a year.

Biology and evolution

Humans did not evolve from modern chimpanzees; both share a common ancestor. Evolution is not a ladder of progress toward humans. The "five-second rule" for dropped food has no scientific basis: bacterial transfer is effectively immediate. Bulls are not enraged by the colour red — cattle are red-green colour-blind and react to the cape's motion. Bats are not blind; most have good eyesight in addition to echolocation.

The human body and senses

People do not use "only ten percent" of their brains; imaging shows virtually all regions active over a day. Hair and fingernails do not continue growing after death — the skin retracts, creating the appearance of growth. Different parts of the tongue are not uniquely specialised for single tastes; all taste qualities are sensed across the tongue.

Technology

Thomas Edison did not invent the light bulb from scratch; he developed a practical, long-lasting bulb and the system to power it, building on decades of earlier work. The first computer programmer is generally considered to be Ada Lovelace, who wrote an algorithm for Babbage's Analytical Engine in the 1840s.
