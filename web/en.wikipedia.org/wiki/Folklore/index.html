Folklore

Folklore is the expressive culture shared by a group — its myths, legends, folktales, jokes, proverbs, songs, dances, costumes, crafts and customs — transmitted informally, often orally, and varying in the telling. The word was coined in 1846 by William Thoms (replacing 'popular antiquities'); the discipline that studies it, folkloristics, emerged with collectors like the Brothers Grimm, whose Children's and Household Tales (1812) both preserved and reshaped German tradition.

Study

Scholars classify tales by motif (the Aarne–Thompson–Uther index), analyse their structure (Propp's morphology of the folktale), and debate function: entertainment, education, social control, the venting of forbidden wishes. Digital culture has revived the field — internet memes and urban legends are folklore in real time.
