Presentation of Jesus at the Temple

The Presentation of Jesus at the Temple is an episode in the infancy narrative of Jesus described in the Gospel of Luke (Luke 2:22–40). According to the account, Joseph and Mary brought the infant Jesus to the Temple in Jerusalem forty days after his birth to complete Mary's ritual purification after childbirth and to redeem the firstborn son, in accordance with the Law of Moses.

The episode

At the Temple the family encountered Simeon, a righteous man who had been promised he would not die before seeing the Messiah; holding the child, he uttered the canticle known as the Nunc dimittis ("Now you dismiss your servant in peace") and prophesied to Mary that the child would be a sign spoken against and that a sword would pierce her own soul. The prophetess Anna, an elderly widow who worshipped constantly in the Temple, also gave thanks and spoke of the child to those awaiting the redemption of Jerusalem.

Feast day

The event is celebrated as the Feast of the Presentation of the Lord on 2 February, forty days after Christmas. In the Roman Catholic and other Western traditions the feast is also known as Candlemas, from the blessing of candles; in Eastern Christianity it is called Hypapante, "the Meeting".
