Siege of Mobile

Date: 27 March – 12 April 1865
Location: Mobile, Alabama
Result: Union victory

The siege of Mobile was a Union operation in the final weeks of the American Civil War, in which forces under Major General Edward Canby besieged and captured the Confederate defences of Mobile, Alabama, one of the last major Confederate ports.

Background

Mobile Bay had been closed to blockade runners by Admiral Farragut's victory in August 1864, but the city itself remained in Confederate hands, protected by fortifications east of the bay — principally Spanish Fort and Fort Blakeley — manned by troops under Major General Dabney H. Maury.

The campaign

Canby advanced with some 45,000 men. Spanish Fort was invested on 27 March 1865 and fell on 8 April after a formal siege. Fort Blakeley was stormed on 9 April — the same day Lee surrendered at Appomattox — in one of the last major infantry assaults of the war. Confederate forces evacuated Mobile, which Union troops occupied on 12 April 1865.

Aftermath

The fall of Mobile removed one of the Confederacy's last ports and supply points, though fighting in the Trans-Mississippi continued for some weeks. An accidental explosion of a captured ammunition magazine on 25 May 1865 killed several hundred people in the city.
