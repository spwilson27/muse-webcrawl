Booking (law enforcement)

Booking is the process by which law enforcement records an arrest into official records. After taking a suspect into custody, officers create an arrest record that includes the person's identifying information, a photograph (mug shot), fingerprints, the alleged offense, and the results of a records check for outstanding warrants. The term comes from the historic practice of entering the arrest into a police "book."

Procedure

A typical booking in the United States involves recording the arrestee's name, address, and charges; photographing and fingerprinting them (now usually by digital livescan, submitted to state and FBI databases); conducting a pat-down or search and inventorying personal property; a brief medical and mental-health screening; and placement in a holding cell. DNA samples may be collected for felony arrests in many jurisdictions, a practice upheld by the U.S. Supreme Court in Maryland v. King (2013). Booking may take from under an hour to many hours depending on the facility and backlog.

Legal context

Booking is an administrative process, distinct from questioning; routine booking questions (name, address, date of birth) fall outside the Miranda warning requirement under the "booking exception" recognized in Pennsylvania v. Muniz (1990). Following booking, the arrestee is brought before a magistrate for an initial appearance and bail determination, constitutionally required within about 48 hours (County of Riverside v. McLaughlin, 1991). Mug shots and booking records are public records in many jurisdictions, which has led to controversy over commercial "mug shot websites" and to laws restricting their publication or requiring removal.
