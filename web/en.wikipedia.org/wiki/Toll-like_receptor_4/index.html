Toll-like receptor 4

The TLR4–MD-2 complex recognises lipopolysaccharide (LPS) from Gram-negative bacteria.

LigandLipopolysaccharide (LPS/endotoxin)

Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the TLR4 gene. It is a pattern recognition receptor of the innate immune system that detects lipopolysaccharide (LPS), a component of the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria, and activates inflammatory signalling. TLR4 was the first mammalian Toll-like receptor shown to recognise a specific pathogen-associated molecular pattern.

Function

LPS is delivered to TLR4 by LPS-binding protein and CD14, and binds within a complex of TLR4 with the accessory protein MD-2. Ligand binding dimerises the receptor and triggers two signalling pathways — the MyD88-dependent pathway at the plasma membrane and the TRIF-dependent pathway from endosomes — leading to production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and type I interferons through transcription factors such as NF-κB and IRF3.

Clinical significance

TLR4 activation by large amounts of LPS is a central driver of septic shock. Genetic polymorphisms in TLR4 have been associated with altered susceptibility to Gram-negative infection and inflammatory conditions, and the receptor is an important drug target: the synthetic lipid A analogue eritoran was developed as a TLR4 antagonist, while monophosphoryl lipid A is used as a vaccine adjuvant.
