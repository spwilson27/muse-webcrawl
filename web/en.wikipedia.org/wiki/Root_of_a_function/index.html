Root of a function

In mathematics, a root (or zero) of a function f is an element x in the domain of f such that f(x) = 0. Geometrically, the real roots correspond to the x-intercepts of the graph of the function. Finding roots — solving equations of the form f(x) = 0 — is one of the oldest problems in mathematics.

Polynomial roots

Linear and quadratic equations have closed-form solutions (the quadratic formula). Cubic and quartic formulas exist but are cumbersome; the Abel–Ruffini theorem proves no general algebraic solution exists for degree five and above. The fundamental theorem of algebra states that every non-constant polynomial of degree n with complex coefficients has exactly n complex roots counted with multiplicity. A root's multiplicity is the number of times it repeats as a factor; a multiple root is also a root of the derivative.

Finding roots

For general functions, roots are found numerically by root-finding algorithms such as bisection, the secant method and Newton's method. The intermediate value theorem guarantees a root in any interval where a continuous function changes sign.
