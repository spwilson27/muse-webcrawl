Giallo

The yellow covers of classic Italian crime paperbacks

Giallo (from the Italian for "yellow") is a genre of Italian literature and cinema encompassing crime and mystery fiction with strong thriller, horror and erotic elements. The name comes from the yellow covers of the cheap crime novels published by Mondadori from 1929 onward, and in Italy it remains the general term for mystery fiction of any origin.

Film genre

In English, "giallo" refers specifically to a distinctive Italian film style of the 1960s and 1970s: stylish murder mysteries featuring black-gloved killers, elaborate set-piece murders, vivid colour cinematography and experimental scores. Mario Bava pioneered the form with The Girl Who Knew Too Much (1963) and Blood and Black Lace (1964), and Dario Argento brought it to international prominence with The Bird with the Crystal Plumage (1970), Deep Red (1975) and Suspiria (1977). Other key directors include Lucio Fulci, Sergio Martino and Aldo Lado.

Influence

Giallo's fusion of mystery plotting, graphic violence and virtuoso camerawork strongly influenced the American slasher film and later horror and thriller directors worldwide.
