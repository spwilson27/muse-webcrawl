Conversion of units

Conversion of units
Examples1 inch = 2.54 cm exactly; 1 mile = 1.609344 km; °F = °C × 9/5 + 32

Conversion of units is the conversion of the unit of measurement in which a quantity is expressed, typically through a multiplicative conversion factor that changes the unit without changing the quantity. A conversion factor is an expression for the relationship between units that is used to change the units of a measured quantity without changing the value. The factor-label method, also known as dimensional analysis or the unit-factor method, chains conversion factors so that unwanted units cancel.

Techniques

To convert, one multiplies the original measurement by a ratio (conversion factor) equal to 1, formed from the equivalence between the two units — for example, multiplying by (2.54 cm / 1 in) converts inches to centimetres while cancelling "in". Multi-step conversions chain such factors. Offset conversions, such as temperature between Celsius and Fahrenheit, are affine rather than purely multiplicative and require adding or subtracting a constant. Dimensional analysis also serves as a check that an equation is dimensionally homogeneous.

Common conversions

Length: 1 foot = 0.3048 m exactly; 1 yard = 0.9144 m; 1 mile = 1.609344 km. Mass: 1 pound = 0.45359237 kg exactly; 1 ounce ≈ 28.3495 g; 1 stone = 6.35029 kg. Volume: 1 US gallon ≈ 3.78541 litres; 1 imperial gallon ≈ 4.54609 litres. Pressure: 1 atmosphere = 101.325 kPa = 760 torr. Energy: 1 calorie ≈ 4.184 J; 1 kilowatt-hour = 3.6 MJ.

History and pitfalls

The coexistence of metric (SI) and imperial/US customary units has caused famous failures, most notably the loss of NASA's Mars Climate Orbiter in 1999, when thrust data in pound-force seconds were used where newton seconds were expected. Most of the world uses SI units, while the United States retains customary units in everyday use; conversion tables and software libraries are standard tools in engineering to avoid such errors.
