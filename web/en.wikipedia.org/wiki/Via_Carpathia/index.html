Via Carpathia

The planned Via Carpathia corridor through Central and Eastern Europe.

Via Carpathia is a planned north–south trans-European transport route connecting the Baltic Sea port of Klaipėda in Lithuania with Thessaloniki in Greece. The corridor runs through eastern Poland along the Carpathians, then through Slovakia, Hungary, Romania, and Bulgaria — largely following existing roads that are being upgraded to expressway and motorway standard, including Poland's S19 expressway.

Development

The initiative was launched by transport ministers meeting at Łańcut, Poland, in 2006, and was reinforced by later declarations under the Three Seas Initiative of Central and Eastern European states. Via Carpathia was incorporated into the EU's Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) Baltic Sea–Black Sea–Aegean Sea corridor. Individual sections have opened progressively: much of the Polish S19 between Lublin and Rzeszów was completed in the early 2020s, while Slovakia's R4 and Romanian sections, including the A7 motorway in Moldavia, remain under staged construction.

Purpose

Supporters present the route as a way to improve connectivity and economic development along the EU's eastern flank, provide an alternative to routes through Russia, and strengthen north–south links lacking since the era of east–west Cold War infrastructure. The corridor also acquired strategic significance for military mobility following Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine.
