The Triple Alliance was a defensive military alliance between Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy, formed on 20 May 1882 and renewed periodically until it effectively collapsed in the First World War. It grew out of the Dual Alliance of 1879 between Germany and Austria-Hungary, which Chancellor Otto von Bismarck had arranged to protect against Russia and France. Italy, aggrieved by France's seizure of Tunisia in 1881, sought support against France and joined the arrangement.


Europe's alliances on the eve of the First World War

Terms and renewal

Under the treaty, the parties promised mutual assistance if Italy or Germany were attacked by France, or if Austria-Hungary were attacked by Russia; they also pledged benevolent neutrality in other contingencies. The alliance was renewed in 1887 (with supplementary agreements promising Italy compensation in the Balkans and support in North Africa), 1891, 1902, 1907 and 1912. Italy simultaneously hedged its position, reaching accommodations with France (1902) and Russia.

First World War

In August 1914 Italy declared neutrality, arguing that Austria-Hungary's attack on Serbia made the alliance's defensive casus foederis inapplicable. In April 1915, by the secret Treaty of London, Italy agreed to join the opposing Triple Entente in return for territorial promises, and declared war on Austria-Hungary in May 1915. Germany and Austria-Hungary fought on as the core of the Central Powers, joined by the Ottoman Empire and Bulgaria.
