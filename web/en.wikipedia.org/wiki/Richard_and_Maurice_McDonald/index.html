Richard James McDonald (February 16, 1909 – July 14, 1998) and Maurice James McDonald (November 26, 1902 – December 11, 1971), collectively known as the McDonald brothers, were American entrepreneurs who founded the McDonald's fast-food restaurant chain. Their streamlined "Speedee Service System" of 1948, developed at their restaurant in San Bernardino, California, pioneered the assembly-line principles that defined modern fast food.

Early life

The brothers were born in Manchester, New Hampshire, to Irish immigrant parents. In the 1920s the family moved to California, where their father ran a food stand. In 1937 the brothers opened a small drive-in restaurant called the Airdrome near Monrovia, and in 1940 they moved their operation to San Bernardino, opening the original McDonald's barbecue restaurant at 1398 North E Street. The drive-in, with carhop service and a menu of some 25 items, proved highly profitable.

Speedee Service System

In 1948 the brothers took the bold step of closing the successful restaurant for three months to redesign it around production speed. They eliminated carhops and indoor seating, cut the menu to nine items (centred on a 15-cent hamburger), prepared food in advance on an assembly line, and served customers at a walk-up counter with no plates or cutlery. Each worker was assigned a specialised task in what they called the Speedee Service System. Sales initially dipped, then far exceeded the old operation, attracting families and spawning imitators across the country.

Franchising and Ray Kroc

The brothers began franchising their system in 1953, opening locations including the Phoenix, Arizona restaurant with its now-famous Golden Arches architecture, designed by Richard with architect Stanley Clark Meston. In 1954 they met Ray Kroc, a Multimixer milkshake-machine salesman impressed by the volume of machines their restaurants needed. Kroc became their franchising agent, founded McDonald's Corporation in 1955, and opened his first restaurant in Des Plaines, Illinois. In 1961 Kroc bought out the brothers for $2.7 million; the brothers retained their original San Bernardino restaurant, renamed The Big M, which eventually closed after competing with a nearby McDonald's. A handshake understanding of ongoing royalties was, by the family's account, never honoured.

Later life and legacy

Maurice McDonald died of heart failure in Riverside, California, in 1971. Richard lived in retirement in New Hampshire, dying in 1998; he is reported to have been the first customer served at Kroc's Des Plaines restaurant on its opening day in 1955. The brothers' story is dramatised in the 2016 film The Founder, in which they are played by Nick Offerman (Richard) and John Carroll Lynch (Maurice). The oldest surviving McDonald's, opened by the brothers in Downey, California in 1953, still operates with its original design.
