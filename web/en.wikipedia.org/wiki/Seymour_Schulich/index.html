Seymour Schulich

Seymour Schulich (born January 6, 1940) is a Canadian businessman, investor, author and philanthropist. He built his fortune principally in the mining industry, notably through Franco-Nevada Mining Corporation, a pioneering royalty and streaming company in the gold sector. He is among Canada's most prominent benefactors of higher education; the Schulich School of Business at York University in Toronto is named after him.

Early life and education

Schulich was born in Montreal, Quebec, and grew up in the city. He studied at McGill University, where he earned a Bachelor of Science degree and a Master of Business Administration. He became one of the first Canadians to receive the Chartered Financial Analyst designation.

Career

Schulich began his career in investment management in Montreal, including work at the pension fund of Shell Canada. In the early 1980s he and partner Pierre Lassonde founded Franco-Nevada Mining Corporation, which acquired royalty interests in gold mines rather than operating mines directly — a model that reduced risk while retaining exposure to gold prices. Franco-Nevada grew into one of the world's most successful gold royalty companies and later merged with Newmont Mining in 2002; the royalty business was subsequently re-established as a public company in 2007.

Schulich is the author of a memoir and business guide, Get Smarter: Life and Business Lessons (2007), aimed at young entrepreneurs.

Philanthropy

Schulich has donated hundreds of millions of dollars to universities across Canada and abroad. Institutions bearing his name include the Schulich School of Business at York University, the Schulich School of Medicine & Dentistry at Western University, the Schulich School of Engineering at the University of Calgary, the Schulich Faculty of Chemistry at the Technion in Israel, and the Schulich Faculty of Music at McGill University. In 2011 he founded the Schulich Leader Scholarships, a major undergraduate STEM scholarship program at Canadian universities, later extended to Israel.

He was appointed to the Order of Canada in recognition of his contributions to business and philanthropy.
