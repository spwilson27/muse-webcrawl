Dexamethasone suppression test

The dexamethasone suppression test (DST) is used in the diagnosis of Cushing's syndrome and to assess the function of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis. Dexamethasone is a potent synthetic glucocorticoid that, in healthy people, suppresses pituitary ACTH secretion and thereby lowers cortisol production. Failure of cortisol to suppress suggests autonomous cortisol secretion.

Dexamethasone suppression test
PurposeDiagnose Cushing's syndrome
DrugDexamethasone
MeasuredSerum cortisol (and ACTH)

Forms

  • Overnight 1 mg test — 1 mg dexamethasone at 23:00, serum cortisol at 08:00; normal suppression is typically below 50 nmol/L (1.8 µg/dL). Used as a screening test.
  • Low-dose test — 0.5 mg every 6 hours for 48 hours; failure to suppress confirms hypercortisolism.
  • High-dose test — 2 mg every 6 hours for 48 hours (or 8 mg overnight); suppression suggests a pituitary source (Cushing's disease), while most ectopic ACTH tumours do not suppress. Its diagnostic role has diminished with modern imaging and inferior petrosal sinus sampling.

Interpretation and pitfalls

False positives occur with obesity, depression, alcohol misuse, pregnancy, oestrogen therapy and drugs that accelerate dexamethasone metabolism (e.g. carbamazepine, rifampicin). The DST was also once promoted as a biological test for melancholic depression, an application now largely abandoned.
