Khojaly (Azerbaijani: Xocalı; Armenian: Իվանյան, Ivanyan) is a town in the Karabakh region of Azerbaijan, the administrative centre of Khojaly District. Before the First Nagorno-Karabakh War it had a predominantly Azerbaijani population of several thousand and hosted the only airport in the Nagorno-Karabakh region.

History

During the Soviet period Khojaly lay within the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast of the Azerbaijan SSR. As the Karabakh conflict escalated, the town, situated on the road between Stepanakert and Aghdam, acquired strategic importance and its population swelled with Azerbaijani refugees expelled from Armenia and from other parts of Karabakh.

Khojaly massacre

On the night of 25–26 February 1992, Armenian and CIS forces captured Khojaly. As the town's inhabitants fled through a corridor towards Aghdam, large numbers were killed; Azerbaijan maintains that 613 civilians, including women and children, were massacred, and the event is commemorated in Azerbaijan and recognised as a massacre or genocide by several states and international observers, including Human Rights Watch, which called it the largest massacre of the conflict. The killings remain a central and contested episode in the history of the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict.

Later history

After 1992 the town was administered by the unrecognised Republic of Artsakh under the name Ivanyan. Following Azerbaijan's offensive of September 2023 and the dissolution of Artsakh's institutions, Khojaly returned to Azerbaijani control and its Armenian population fled, as did almost the entire Armenian population of Nagorno-Karabakh.
