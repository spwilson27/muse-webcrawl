User guide

A user guide, also commonly called a user manual, is a technical communication document intended to give assistance to people using a particular product or system. User guides are most commonly associated with electronic goods, computer hardware and software, but are also produced for vehicles, machinery, household appliances and many other products.

Contents

A typical user guide contains both a tutorial for beginning users and reference material for more experienced users. Common sections include:

  • An introduction and overview of the product's features.
  • Installation and setup instructions.
  • Step-by-step procedures for common tasks.
  • Troubleshooting guidance and frequently asked questions.
  • Safety warnings, maintenance information and specifications.
  • A glossary and index.

Formats

Historically, user guides were printed booklets shipped with a product. Increasingly they are distributed electronically as PDF files, web-based help systems, or context-sensitive help embedded in the product itself. Structured documentation standards such as DITA allow content to be authored once and published in several formats.

Writing

User guides are written by technical writers, sometimes in collaboration with product engineers and testers. Good practice emphasises task-oriented writing ("how to do X") rather than merely describing interface elements, plain language, and usability testing of the documentation itself. In the European Union and other jurisdictions, certain products are legally required to be supplied with instructions, and translation into local languages may be mandated.
