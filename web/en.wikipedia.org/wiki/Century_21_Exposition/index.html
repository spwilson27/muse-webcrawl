The Century 21 Exposition (also known as the Seattle World's Fair) was a world's fair held April 21 to October 21, 1962, in Seattle, Washington, United States. Nearly 10 million people attended the fair. Unlike many other world's fairs of its era, Century 21 made a profit.

The fair was planned in the late 1950s, and its theme of science and the future was strongly influenced by the Space Race following the launch of Sputnik in 1957. The United States Science Pavilion, designed by Minoru Yamasaki, and the futuristic Space Needle, designed by John Graham Jr. and Victor Steinbrueck, were the fair's signature structures. The monorail, built by the German company ALWEG, carried visitors between downtown Seattle and the fairgrounds.

Theme and exhibits

The fair's theme, "Man in the Space Age," was organized around science, commerce, and art. Major exhibits included the World of Science, the World of Tomorrow, the World of Commerce and Industry, the World of Art, and the World of Entertainment. NASA and other federal agencies mounted large displays on space exploration, and foreign nations contributed pavilions and exhibits.

Legacy

After the fair closed, the grounds became the Seattle Center, a civic and cultural campus. The Space Needle, the Pacific Science Center (housed in the former U.S. Science Pavilion), the monorail, and KeyArena (originally the Washington State Coliseum) all survive as legacies of the exposition. Elvis Presley filmed It Happened at the World's Fair on the grounds in 1962.
