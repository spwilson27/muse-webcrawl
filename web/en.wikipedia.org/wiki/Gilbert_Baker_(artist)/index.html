Gilbert Baker (artist)

Gilbert Baker (June 2, 1951 – March 31, 2017) was an American artist, designer and gay rights activist best known as the creator of the rainbow flag (1978), which became the worldwide symbol of LGBTQ pride and social movements. A close associate of Harvey Milk, Baker hand-dyed and hand-stitched the first flags with a circle of friends for San Francisco's 1978 Gay Freedom Day parade, and spent the rest of his life promoting the flag — which he deliberately never trademarked — as a universal emblem of diversity and inclusion.

Early life

Baker was born in Chanute, Kansas, and raised in Parsons, Kansas, where his grandmother owned a women's clothing store — an early influence on his sewing skills. He served in the US Army from 1970 to 1972 as a medic, stationed in San Francisco, and settled there after an honourable discharge, teaching himself to sew and making banners for gay rights and anti-war marches. He became part of Harvey Milk's circle and designed flags and banners for Milk's campaigns.

The rainbow flag

In 1978, at Milk's encouragement that the movement needed a positive symbol of its own (rather than the reclaimed pink triangle of Nazi persecution), Baker and a team of about thirty volunteers dyed and stitched two enormous flags in a San Francisco attic and raised them in United Nations Plaza on 25 June 1978. The original design had eight stripes, each with a meaning: hot pink (sex), red (life), orange (healing), yellow (sunlight), green (nature), turquoise (magic/art), indigo (serenity) and violet (spirit). Hot pink was dropped when the flag went into mass production because the fabric was unavailable, and turquoise was removed in 1979 to make an even six stripes for display on lampposts — producing the now-standard six-colour flag: red, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet.

Later work and death

Baker worked as a designer for the Paramount Flag Company and created flags, banners and art for marches, the Democratic National Convention, the Oscars and heads of state. For the flag's 25th anniversary in 2003 he sewed a mile-long rainbow flag in Key West. In 2017, shortly before his death, he created a nine-stripe version adding a lavender stripe to represent diversity. Baker died in his sleep in New York City on 31 March 2017, aged 65. An original 1978 eight-stripe flag segment, long thought lost, was rediscovered and donated to the GLBT Historical Society in San Francisco in 2021.
