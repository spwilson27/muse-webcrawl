Zengzi (曾子)

Born: 505 BCE, State of Lu

Died: 435 BCE

Personal name: Zeng Shen (曾參)

Courtesy name: Ziyu (子輿)

School: Confucianism

Teacher: Confucius

Zengzi (505–435 BCE), born Zeng Shen and styled Ziyu, was a Chinese philosopher and one of the most prominent disciples of Confucius. He is traditionally credited with composing the Great Learning and is especially associated with the Confucian virtue of filial piety (xiao).

Life and teaching

A native of the state of Lu, Zengzi was 46 years younger than Confucius and studied under him directly; his father, Zeng Dian, had also been a disciple. In the Analects, Zengzi is quoted reflecting daily on three questions — whether he has been loyal in acting for others, trustworthy with friends, and diligent in practising what he was taught — a passage that made him a model of moral self-examination. He is one of the few disciples addressed as "Master" (zi) in the Analects itself, indicating the early reverence of the school for him.

Legacy

Zengzi taught Confucius's grandson Zisi, through whom the teaching passed to Mencius, making Zengzi a key link in the orthodox Confucian lineage recognized by later Neo-Confucians. The Classic of Filial Piety is traditionally framed as a dialogue between Confucius and Zengzi. In the Confucian temple cult he is honored as one of the Four Sages (Si Pei), alongside Yan Hui, Zisi, and Mencius.
