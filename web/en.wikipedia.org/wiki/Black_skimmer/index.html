Black skimmer

KingdomAnimalia
OrderCharadriiformes
FamilyLaridae
GenusRynchops
SpeciesR. niger
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The black skimmer (Rynchops niger) is a tern-like seabird of the Americas, one of only three species in the skimmer genus Rynchops. It is instantly recognisable by its unique bill: the lower mandible is substantially longer than the upper, an adaptation to its remarkable method of feeding in flight.

Description

The black skimmer is 40–50 cm long with a wingspan of about 107–127 cm. Adults have black upperparts and crown, white underparts and forehead, and a red-orange bill with a black tip; the legs are red. Males are larger than females and have longer bills. Unusually among birds, skimmers have vertical, slit-like pupils, which help reduce glare over water and sand.

Feeding

Skimmers feed mainly at dusk, dawn, and night. Flying low over calm water with the elongated lower mandible slicing the surface, the bird snaps its bill shut and flips its head back when the mandible strikes a small fish, shrimp, or other prey near the surface. Touch rather than sight guides the strike. Colonies often feed in loose flocks over estuaries, lagoons, and sheltered bays.

Distribution and breeding

The species breeds along the Atlantic and Gulf coasts of North America (the northern subspecies R. n. niger), and in South America along coasts and large rivers (including the freshwater R. n. intercedens of the Amazon and other river systems). Northern populations migrate south in winter. Black skimmers nest colonially on sandy beaches and barrier islands, often with terns, laying three to five speckled eggs in a shallow scrape. Because they nest on open beaches, colonies are highly vulnerable to human disturbance, flooding, and predation, and the species is a conservation priority in parts of its North American range.
