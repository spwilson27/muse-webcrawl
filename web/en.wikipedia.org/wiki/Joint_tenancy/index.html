Joint tenancy

A joint tenancy is a form of concurrent ownership of real property in which two or more persons hold equal, undivided interests in the whole property. Joint tenancy is distinguished by the right of survivorship: when one joint tenant dies, their interest automatically passes to the surviving joint tenants rather than forming part of their estate.

The four unities

At common law, a joint tenancy traditionally requires the four unities: unity of time (interests vest at the same time), unity of title (acquired by the same instrument), unity of interest (equal shares), and unity of possession (each tenant entitled to possess the whole).

Severance

A joint tenant may sever the joint tenancy, typically by conveying their interest, converting the arrangement into a tenancy in common, under which each co-owner holds a distinct share that can be passed by will.
