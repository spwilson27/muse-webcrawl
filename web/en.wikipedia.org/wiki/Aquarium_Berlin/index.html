Aquarium Berlin

Opened1913
LocationBudapester Straße, Berlin, Germany
OperatorZoologischer Garten Berlin AG

The Aquarium Berlin is an aquarium in Berlin, Germany, adjoining the Berlin Zoological Garden, with which it shares management. Opened in 1913, it is one of Germany's largest and most species-rich aquaria, housing fish, reptiles, amphibians, and invertebrates.

History

The aquarium was built in 1911–1913 as part of the zoo complex, to designs reflecting the ambitious natural-history ambitions of the pre-war city, and quickly gained a world reputation. Largely destroyed by Allied bombing in November 1943 — most of its animals perished, including nearly all its large reptiles — it was rebuilt after the war and reopened progressively from 1952, expanded in the 1980s with modernised systems and the crocodile hall and tropical houses.

Collection

Three floors present freshwater and marine habitats, a terrarium section with snakes, lizards, and crocodilians, and an insectarium. Notable holdings have included jellyfish breeding displays, sharks and rays, Australian lungfish, and extensive amphibian collections supporting captive-breeding programmes for endangered frogs. The building's richly decorated façade, reconstructed after wartime damage, incorporates restored dinosaur sculptures echoing the original decoration. See also Berlin Zoological Garden.
