Xuanwu (god)

Xuanwu (玄武)
Also known asZhenwu, Dark Warrior, Mysterious Warrior
DirectionNorth
SymbolBlack Tortoise entwined with a snake
Sacred mountainMount Wudang

Xuanwu (Chinese: 玄武, "Dark Warrior" or "Mysterious Warrior"), later worshipped under the elevated name Zhenwu (真武, "True Warrior"), is a powerful deity in Chinese religion and Taoism. He is the god of the north, associated with water, winter, martial prowess and longevity, and is traditionally depicted as a warrior in dark robes with long flowing hair, barefoot, standing upon a tortoise and a snake.

Origins

Xuanwu began as one of the Four Symbols of Chinese astronomy: the Black Tortoise of the North, a constellation group whose stars governed the northern sky. By the Song dynasty the astral deity had developed a full human mythology. Because the character xuan formed part of the personal name of Song imperial ancestors, the god was renamed Zhenwu, and his cult was lavishly promoted by the emperors Zhenzong and Huizong.

Legend

According to legend, Zhenwu was originally a prince who withdrew to Mount Wudang to cultivate the Dao, practising asceticism for forty-two years before ascending to heaven. In another popular tale, he was once a butcher who, repenting of his slaughter, cut open his own stomach and intestines to wash away his sins in a river — the stomach and intestines becoming his tortoise and snake attendants.

Worship

The Ming Yongle Emperor, who credited Zhenwu with aiding his seizure of the throne, built an extensive complex of temples on Mount Wudang, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. As a martial, exorcistic god, Xuanwu/Zhenwu is widely venerated in China, Taiwan, Vietnam and Chinese diaspora communities.
