Ansoff matrix

The Ansoff matrix is a strategic planning tool that provides a framework to help executives, senior managers, and marketers devise strategies for future business growth. It is named after Russian-American applied mathematician and business manager Igor Ansoff, who created the concept in a 1957 paper in the Harvard Business Review.

The four strategies

The matrix crosses products (existing vs new) with markets (existing vs new), yielding four growth strategies:

  • Market penetration (existing products, existing markets): sell more of the same to the same customers — lowest risk.
  • Market development (existing products, new markets): enter new geographies or segments.
  • Product development (new products, existing markets): create new offerings for current customers.
  • Diversification (new products, new markets): the highest-risk strategy, unrelated or related.

Use and criticism

The matrix is a staple of marketing and MBA teaching for classifying growth options by risk. Critics note it simplifies strategy, ignores competitors and synergy, and says little about implementation.
