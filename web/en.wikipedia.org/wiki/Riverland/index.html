Riverland

The Riverland is a region of South Australia, Australia, centred on the Murray River, stretching from the Victorian border downstream to about Blanchetown. Its principal towns include Renmark, Berri, Barmera, Loxton, Waikerie, and Morgan.

Economy

The Riverland is one of Australia's most important irrigated agricultural regions, producing a large share of the nation's citrus fruit, stone fruit, almonds, and wine grapes. Irrigation began in the late nineteenth century with the Chaffey brothers' settlement at Renmark (1887), and expanded with soldier-settler schemes after both world wars. The region is a major source of bulk and bottled wine.

Geography and environment

The region is characterised by the Murray's floodplains, wetlands, and red sand mallee country. Sites of ecological significance include the Riverland Ramsar site, Chowilla floodplain, and Lake Bonney at Barmera. Environmental water management, salinity, and the health of the river system are major regional concerns.
