Passover Haggadah

The Haggadah (Hebrew: הַגָּדָה, "telling") is the Jewish text that sets forth the order of the Passover Seder. Reading it is a mitzvah — fulfilling the commandment to tell one's children the story of the Exodus of the Israelites from slavery in ancient Egypt, as described in the Book of Exodus.

Contents

The Haggadah includes the Four Questions (Ma Nishtana) asked by the youngest child, the account of the Four Sons, the Ten Plagues, the song Dayenu ("it would have been enough"), blessings over the ritual foods — matzah, maror and the four cups of wine — and closing songs such as "Next year in Jerusalem", Echad Mi Yodea and Chad Gadya. Its core text took shape in the Mishnaic and Talmudic periods, with additions through the Middle Ages.

Editions

Thousands of editions exist, from lavish illuminated manuscripts — the Sarajevo Haggadah (c. 1350) and the Golden Haggadah — to modern, denominational, feminist, secular and themed versions, and the widely distributed Maxwell House Haggadah (from 1932). More than 7,000 editions have been published, making it one of the most printed Jewish texts.
