The 1961 World Figure Skating Championships were scheduled to be held in Prague, Czechoslovakia, in February 1961, but were cancelled following the crash of Sabena Flight 548, which killed the entire United States figure skating team en route to the event. It remains the only World Figure Skating Championships of the modern era to be cancelled.

Sabena Flight 548

On February 15, 1961, Sabena Flight 548, a Boeing 707 flying from New York to Brussels, crashed on approach to Brussels Airport, killing all 72 people aboard and one person on the ground. Among the dead were all 34 members of the U.S. delegation: the 18-member skating team, coaches, judges, officials, and family members. The team included national champions Laurence Owen (ladies), Bradley Lord (men), and pairs champions Maribel Owen and Dudley Richards.

Cancellation and aftermath

The International Skating Union's executive committee, meeting in the aftermath, voted to cancel the championships as a mark of respect. The loss devastated American skating, wiping out a generation of skaters and coaches, including nine-time U.S. champion and coach Maribel Vinson-Owen. In response, the United States Figure Skating Association established the USFSA Memorial Fund, which supports the training of young American skaters; the fund has raised tens of millions of dollars. The 1961 team was inducted into the U.S. Figure Skating Hall of Fame in 2011, fifty years after the crash.

Subsequent championships

The World Championships resumed in 1962, again in Prague, where Donald Jackson of Canada became the first skater to land a triple Lutz in international competition.
