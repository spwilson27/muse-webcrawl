Post-traumatic growth

Post-traumatic growth (PTG)

Field: Positive psychology
Developed by: Richard Tedeschi, Lawrence Calhoun
Measured by: Posttraumatic Growth Inventory (PTGI)

Post-traumatic growth (PTG) is the positive psychological change experienced as a result of struggling with highly challenging, stressful or traumatic life circumstances. The concept was developed in the mid-1990s by psychologists Richard Tedeschi and Lawrence Calhoun at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte.

Domains of growth

Tedeschi and Calhoun identify five domains: greater appreciation of life and changed priorities; warmer, more intimate relationships; increased personal strength ("if I survived that, I can face anything"); recognition of new possibilities in life; and spiritual or existential development. Growth is measured with the Posttraumatic Growth Inventory (PTGI), a 21-item self-report scale.

Process and debate

PTG is thought to arise not from the trauma itself but from the struggle with it, especially through rumination that gradually becomes deliberate reflection, rebuilding of shattered assumptions about the world, and disclosure to supportive others. Growth and distress commonly coexist. Critics question whether reported growth reflects genuine change or a coping illusion, and self-report measures have been challenged by prospective studies; researchers distinguish "constructive" from "illusory" growth. PTG has been reported after bereavement, serious illness, combat, natural disasters and assault.
