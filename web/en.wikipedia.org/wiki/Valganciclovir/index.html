Valganciclovir, sold under the brand name Valcyte among others, is an antiviral medication used to treat cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection, in particular CMV retinitis in people with HIV/AIDS and CMV disease following solid-organ transplantation. It is taken by mouth and is a prodrug of ganciclovir.

Pharmacology

Valganciclovir is the L-valyl ester of ganciclovir. After oral dosing it is hydrolysed by intestinal and hepatic esterases to ganciclovir, with a bioavailability of about 60 percent — far higher than oral ganciclovir itself — so that standard oral doses achieve systemic exposure comparable to intravenous ganciclovir. Inside infected cells, ganciclovir is phosphorylated first by the viral UL97 protein kinase and then by cellular enzymes to ganciclovir triphosphate, which competitively inhibits viral DNA polymerase and terminates viral DNA chain elongation.

Uses and safety

Principal uses are induction and maintenance treatment of CMV retinitis in immunocompromised patients and prophylaxis of CMV disease in kidney, heart and kidney–pancreas transplant recipients at high risk. Because ganciclovir is toxic to bone marrow, valganciclovir carries warnings for severe leukopenia, neutropenia, anaemia and thrombocytopenia; it is also potentially teratogenic and carcinogenic based on animal studies, so tablets should not be broken or handled by pregnant women. The drug was approved for medical use in the United States in 2001 and is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
