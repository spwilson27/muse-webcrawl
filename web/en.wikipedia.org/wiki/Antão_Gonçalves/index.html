Antão Gonçalves

Antão Gonçalves (fl. 15th century) was a Portuguese sea captain in the service of Prince Henry the Navigator. He is known for leading expeditions down the west coast of Africa in 1441 and 1443, during which he reached the region of Cape Blanc and the Rio de Oro, and for bringing back the first African captives taken by the Portuguese — an episode chronicled by Gomes Eanes de Zurara that marks a notorious early milestone in the European slave trade with Africa.

Voyage of 1441

In 1441 Gonçalves was sent by Prince Henry in a small vessel to the coast beyond Cape Bojador to hunt monk seals and load a cargo of seal skins and oil. Having completed his mission near the Rio de Oro, and wishing to distinguish himself, he and his men went ashore and seized a lone African man and a Black woman gathering shellfish nearby. Meanwhile Nuno Tristão arrived in a second caravel, and the two crews together raided further inland, capturing about ten more people after a skirmish in which several Africans were killed. Among the captives was a local notable, Adahu, whose later ransom negotiations led to Portuguese contacts and the naming of the "Rio de Ouro" from the gold dust offered in exchange.

Voyage of 1443

Gonçalves returned to the region in 1443, meeting Adahu again and learning more of the coast and peoples to the south. His voyages, together with those of Nuno Tristão (who reached the Arguin area and in 1443–44 pushed further south), extended Portuguese knowledge of the Saharan coast and established the pattern of trade — and slave raiding — centred on the factory later built on Arguin island.

Historical assessment

The chronicler Zurara celebrated Gonçalves as a hero of Henry's enterprise, and his capture of the first prisoners is depicted in Portuguese tradition as a foundational moment of the Age of Discovery. Modern historians instead treat the 1441 voyage as the opening episode of the Atlantic slave trade, in which the Portuguese carried growing numbers of enslaved West Africans to Europe and, later, to Atlantic colonies.
