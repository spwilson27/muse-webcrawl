JBIG

JBIG is an early lossless image compression standard from the Joint Bi-level Image Experts Group, standardized as ITU-T Recommendation T.82 and ISO/IEC 11544 and first published in 1993. It is a lossless method for compressing binary images — primarily documents and faxes — that typically achieves substantially better compression than the earlier CCITT Group 3 and Group 4 fax standards.

Compression

JBIG uses context-based arithmetic coding (the QM-coder) with adaptive templates, and supports progressive transmission through resolution-reduction layers. It can also compress greyscale and colour images by bit-plane coding.

JBIG2

Its successor, JBIG2 (standardized in 2000), adds lossy and lossless compression of text and halftone regions and is used as an encoding format within PDF files.

Patents

JBIG implementations were historically subject to patent licences, which limited its adoption; the relevant patents have since expired.
