Improvisational comedy

Improvisational comedy, often called improv, is a form of live theatre in which scenes, characters and dialogue are created spontaneously, usually from audience suggestions. Performers work without a script, building scenes through agreement and collaboration, summarised in the principle of "yes, and…".

Forms

Short-form improv consists of brief games with explicit rules, popularised on television by Whose Line Is It Anyway?. Long-form improv develops extended scenes and narratives from a single suggestion; the Harold, developed by Del Close in Chicago, is its best-known structure.

History

Modern improv grew out of Viola Spolin's theatre games in Chicago and the work of The Compass Players and The Second City (founded 1959). It has since spread worldwide as both entertainment and a training method for actors and comedians.

References: Spolin, Viola. Improvisation for the Theater (1963); histories of The Second City.
