Simple Lie algebra

In mathematics, a simple Lie algebra is a Lie algebra that is non-abelian and contains no non-trivial proper ideals. Simple Lie algebras play a central role in the classification of Lie algebras and Lie groups.

Classification

Over the complex numbers, finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras were classified by Wilhelm Killing and Élie Cartan. They fall into four infinite families — An, Bn, Cn and Dn, corresponding to the classical Lie algebras sl(n+1), so(2n+1), sp(2n) and so(2n) — and five exceptional types: G2, F4, E6, E7 and E8.

The classification is encoded in Dynkin diagrams and root systems. Every finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra is a direct sum of simple Lie algebras.

Properties

A simple Lie algebra has a non-degenerate Killing form. Its representations are studied via highest-weight theory, and over the reals each complex simple algebra has several real forms.
