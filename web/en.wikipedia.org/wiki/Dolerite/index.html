Dolerite

Dolerite (known in North America as diabase) is a mafic, holocrystalline, subvolcanic rock equivalent to volcanic basalt or plutonic gabbro. It has a composition similar to basalt but a medium grain size, reflecting slower cooling in shallow intrusions such as dikes and sills.

Occurrence

Dolerite occurs worldwide in dike swarms and sills associated with basaltic magmatism — famously in the Karoo of South Africa, Tasmania (where it forms the columns of mountains such as Mount Wellington), the British Tertiary igneous province, and the Palisades Sill of New York and New Jersey. The bluestones of Stonehenge are spotted dolerite from the Preseli Hills of Wales.

Uses

Hard and durable, dolerite is quarried as crushed stone for road aggregate and concrete, and polished for dimension stone and monuments.
