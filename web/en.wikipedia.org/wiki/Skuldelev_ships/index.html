Skuldelev ships

The Skuldelev ships are five Viking ships excavated in 1962 from the Peberrenden channel at Skuldelev in Roskilde Fjord, Denmark. Around the year 1070 they had been deliberately sunk, filled with stones, to form an underwater barrier defending the maritime approach to Roskilde, then a major royal and ecclesiastical centre. The recovered timbers were conserved and are displayed at the Viking Ship Museum in Roskilde.

The ships

  • Skuldelev 1 — a sturdy ocean-going cargo ship (knarr), about 16 m, built of pine in western Norway.
  • Skuldelev 2 — a great longship (skeid), about 30 m, built of oak near Dublin, Ireland, around 1042; it would have carried some 65–70 warriors.
  • Skuldelev 3 — a small coastal trader (byrding), about 14 m, of Danish oak.
  • Skuldelev 5 — a small warship (snekkja), about 17.5 m, of mixed Danish timber.
  • Skuldelev 6 — a small fishing or ferry boat of Norwegian pine.

(No ship numbered 4 was found; a hull once thought separate proved to be part of Skuldelev 2.)

Replicas

The Viking Ship Museum has built full-scale reconstructions of all five ships using traditional methods, most famously the Sea Stallion from Glendalough, a replica of Skuldelev 2 that sailed from Roskilde to Dublin in 2007, retracing the original ship's origins.
