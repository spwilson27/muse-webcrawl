Barnardo

Barnardo may refer most commonly to Thomas John Barnardo (1845–1905), an Irish philanthropist and the founder of the children's charity Barnardo's, which became one of the largest children's charities in the United Kingdom.

Thomas John Barnardo

Born in Dublin in 1845, Barnardo moved to London in 1866 intending to train as a medical missionary. Appalled by the number of homeless and destitute children in the East End of London, he opened his first home for boys in Stepney Causeway in 1870. Under the principle "No destitute child ever refused admission," his organisation grew to run more than ninety homes caring for thousands of children.

Barnardo pioneered foster care as an alternative to institutional homes, and between 1882 and 1939 his organisation sent more than 30,000 children — known as "Home Children" — to Canada and Australia, a practice later the subject of official apologies for the hardships many endured.

The charity today

Barnardo's no longer operates residential orphanages, having closed its last traditional home in 1989. It now supports vulnerable children and families through community services, fostering and adoption, and campaigns on issues such as child poverty and exploitation.
