The Minions are fictional characters from Illumination's Despicable Me franchise. They are small, yellow, cylindrical creatures with one or two eyes, dressed in blue overalls, black gloves and metal goggles, who serve as the loyal but hapless henchmen of the supervillain Gru. Originally supporting characters in Despicable Me (2010), their popularity made them the franchise's mascots and the stars of the spin-off prequel films Minions (2015) and Minions: The Rise of Gru (2022).

According to the films' backstory, Minions have existed since the dawn of life on Earth, evolving from single-celled organisms with a single purpose: to serve the most despicable master available. Their schemes habitually end in disaster for their masters — from Tyrannosaurus rex to Napoleon — until they find Gru. Prominent named Minions include Kevin, Stuart and Bob, the trio at the centre of the 2015 film.

Creation and design

The Minions were created by directors Pierre Coffin and Chris Renaud with character designer Eric Guillon. They were not in the original concept for Despicable Me — early designs had Gru's henchmen as large, hulking figures — but were reconceived as small, cute creatures who made Gru more sympathetic and provided comic relief. Coffin voices nearly all of the Minions himself.

Language

The Minions speak a constructed gibberish, often called "Minionese" or "Banana Language", mixing fragments of Spanish, French, Italian, English, and other languages with nonsense sounds; simple words such as "banana", "bello" (hello) and "poopaye" (goodbye) are recognisable. The speech is written in the scripts but is largely improvised in tone by Coffin.

Appearances

The Minions appear in all Despicable Me films (2010, 2013, 2017, 2024) and headline Minions (2015), set in 1968, and Minions: The Rise of Gru (2022), set in the 1970s. Minions grossed over $1.1 billion worldwide, making it one of the highest-grossing animated films of all time. They also feature in short films (such as Banana, 2010), the ride Despicable Me Minion Mayhem at Universal theme parks, and extensive merchandise.

Reception and cultural impact

The Minions became a global merchandising and social-media phenomenon, particularly with the 2015 film's marketing campaign. They have been adopted — sometimes ironically — as internet memes. Critics have been divided: praise for their slapstick and design coexists with complaints about their ubiquity in advertising. In 2021, a trend known as "Gentleminions", in which young fans attended screenings of The Rise of Gru dressed in suits, went viral.
