Margaret Ward

Margaret Ward (c. 1550 – 30 August 1588) was an English Catholic martyr, canonized by the Roman Catholic Church as one of the Forty Martyrs of England and Wales. She is sometimes called the "Pearl of Tyburn."

Born at Congleton, Cheshire, Ward was living in London in the service of a Catholic family when she learned that the priest William Watson was imprisoned and being tortured in the Bridewell prison. She visited him repeatedly, bringing food, and smuggled in a rope by which he escaped—though he injured himself in the descent and a boatman who helped him, John Roche, was also implicated. The rope was traced to Ward; she was arrested, whipped, suspended by her wrists, and kept in irons, but refused under interrogation to reveal Watson's whereabouts or to ask pardon and conform.

Execution

Ward was tried at the Old Bailey and condemned for high treason for aiding a priest's escape. She was executed at Tyburn on 30 August 1588 alongside Edward Shelley, Richard Martin, Richard Leigh, Richard Lloyd, and John Roche, during the wave of persecution following the Spanish Armada. She was beatified in 1929 and canonized by Pope Paul VI in 1970; her feast day is kept with the Forty Martyrs.
