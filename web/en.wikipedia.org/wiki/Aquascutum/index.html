Aquascutum

An Aquascutum trench coat, the style the brand made famous.

Aquascutum is a British luxury fashion house and retailer founded in 1851 by tailor John Emary. Headquartered in London, the brand is best known for inventing waterproof wool and for its raincoats and trench coats. The name is derived from the Latin words aqua (water) and scutum (shield).

History

Emary opened his first shop in Regent Street, London, in 1851, and in 1853 patented a waterproof wool fabric. Aquascutum supplied waterproof coats to officers in the Crimean War and, most famously, trench coats to soldiers in both World Wars. The brand dressed royalty and celebrities alike, receiving royal warrants from the Prince of Wales (later Edward VII), George V, and Elizabeth II. In the 1970s it introduced its signature "Club Check" — a tan, black, and red plaid — as a lining and trim.

Ownership

After periods under Renown and Jaeger, the company entered administration in 2012; its brand and intellectual property were acquired by YGM Trading, a Hong Kong–listed company, and the British business was later relaunched with new investment. Its flagship store returned to Great Marlborough Street in London.
