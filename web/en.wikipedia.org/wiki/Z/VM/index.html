z/VM

z/VM is the current family of IBM VM operating systems, a hypervisor for IBM Z mainframe computers. z/VM runs on IBM's z/Architecture hardware and provides the ability to run many virtual machines, each capable of running its own guest operating system — including Linux on IBM Z, z/OS, z/VSE, z/TPF and CMS — on a single physical machine.

History

z/VM descends from CP-67 and VM/370 of the late 1960s and 1970s, the pioneering commercial virtualization systems developed from IBM's Cambridge Scientific Center work. The "VM" name stands for "Virtual Machine". The z/VM branding arrived with the 64-bit z/Architecture around 2000, succeeding VM/ESA.

Features

The z/VM Control Program (CP) manages virtual machines with strong isolation and resource scheduling. Its traditional single-user guest, the Conversational Monitor System (CMS), remains in use for administration and legacy applications. z/VM is widely used to consolidate large numbers of Linux servers — sometimes hundreds or thousands of virtual machines on one mainframe — and as a hosting environment for other mainframe operating systems in development and test.
