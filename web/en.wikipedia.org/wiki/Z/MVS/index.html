MVS (Multiple Virtual Storage) is a mainframe operating system developed by IBM, first released in 1974 for the System/370. It became the flagship OS of IBM's large systems and is the direct ancestor of today's z/OS.

Design

MVS gave each job its own virtual address space supported by demand paging, allowing very large batch and transaction workloads to run concurrently with strong isolation. It centred on batch processing with JCL (Job Control Language), the JES2/JES3 spooling subsystems, TSO for interactive users, and CICS and IMS for transaction processing and databases.

Evolution

MVS/XA extended addressing to 31 bits in the 1980s; MVS/ESA, OS/390, and finally 64-bit z/OS continued the line while preserving application compatibility — a defining trait of the platform under the Z family.
