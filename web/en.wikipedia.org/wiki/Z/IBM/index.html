IBM Z is the family name for IBM's mainframe computer line descended from the System/360 (1964) through the System/370 and System/390 and the zSeries. IBM Z systems are known for very high reliability, availability, security certification, and backward compatibility stretching back decades, and they process a large share of the world's banking and airline transactions.

Characteristics

Machines emphasise redundant components, hot-swappable parts, massive I/O capacity, hardware cryptography, and logical partitioning (LPARs) alongside the z/VM hypervisor. Generations such as the z16 added on-chip AI inference acceleration and features aimed at fraud detection, plus quantum-safe cryptography readiness.

Software

The flagship operating system is z/OS, with z/VM for virtualisation, z/TPF for very high-volume transaction processing, and Linux distributions running natively on the platform (Linux on IBM Z). The URL variants that lead to this topic reflect IBM's long naming history for the mainframe line.
