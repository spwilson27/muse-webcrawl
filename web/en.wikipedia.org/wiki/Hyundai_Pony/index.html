Hyundai Pony

Compact car produced by Hyundai, 1975–1990

First-generation Hyundai Pony

The Hyundai Pony (Korean: 현대 포니) is a small family car manufactured by the South Korean manufacturer Hyundai from 1975 until 1990. The Pony was South Korea's first mass-produced and exported automobile, and it played a central role in establishing Hyundai Motor Company as a global carmaker. It was a rear-wheel-drive car, notable at a time when most rivals in its class were moving to front-wheel drive.

Development

Hyundai hired the former British Leyland executive George Turnbull to lead the project, and the car's styling was by ItalDesign (Giorgetto Giugiaro). Mechanical components were licensed from Mitsubishi Motors, including engines and transmissions. The Pony made its debut at the Turin Motor Show in 1974 and went on sale the following year.

Generations

The first generation (1975–1982) was offered as a sedan, hatchback, wagon, coupe utility and van. The second generation (1982–1990), sometimes called the Pony II, received revised styling and remained in production in South Korea until 1990. The Pony was exported widely, including to Canada, where it became one of the country's best-selling cars in the mid-1980s, though it was never sold in the United States because it could not meet emissions standards there.

Legacy

The name was later revived in some markets, and the Pony inspired Hyundai's retro-styled Pony heritage concept shown alongside the Ioniq 5 electric car, which took its design cues from the original hatchback.
