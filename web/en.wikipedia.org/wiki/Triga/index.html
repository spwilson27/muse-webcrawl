TRIGA

TRIGA (Training, Research, Isotopes, General Atomics) is a class of small nuclear research reactors designed and supplied by General Atomics. The TRIGA reactor is a pool-type reactor that can be installed without a containment building and is used worldwide for research, training, isotope production, and neutron radiography.

Design

TRIGA's distinctive feature is its uranium zirconium hydride (UZrH) fuel, which has a strong prompt negative temperature coefficient: if the fuel heats up, reactivity falls immediately, making the reactor inherently safe and able to be "pulsed" safely to very high peak power (thousands of megawatts) for a few milliseconds. The first TRIGA went critical in 1958; the design team was led by physicist Freeman Dyson.

Use

More than 70 TRIGA reactors have been built in over 20 countries, at universities, hospitals, and national laboratories, with steady-state power levels typically between 20 kW and 16 MW. See research reactor.
