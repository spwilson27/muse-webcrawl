Grodno Region
Country: Belarus
Capital: Grodno (Hrodna)
Area: 25,127 km²
Population: about 990,000 (2024)

Grodno Region (Belarusian: Гродзенская вобласць, Hrodzienskaja voblasć; Russian: Гродненская область) is one of the six regions (oblasts) of Belarus, located in the west of the country on the borders with Poland and Lithuania. Its administrative centre is the city of Grodno (Hrodna).

Geography

The region covers about 25,100 km² of lowland and gentle upland, drained chiefly by the Neman (Nioman) River and its tributaries. Forests cover roughly a third of the territory; the Belarusian part of the ancient Białowieża Forest (Belavezhskaya Pushcha) lies partly within it. Major towns besides Grodno include Lida, Slonim, Volkovysk, Smorgon, and Novogrudok.

History

The territory belonged successively to the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Russian Empire, and interwar Poland before its incorporation into the Byelorussian SSR in 1939–1944. The oblast was formally established on 20 September 1944. The region has notable Polish and Catholic minorities alongside its Belarusian majority.

Economy and sights

Agriculture, food processing, chemicals (the Grodno Azot fertilizer works), and cross-border trade anchor the economy. Landmarks include Mir Castle (a UNESCO World Heritage Site, near the regional border), the Old and New Castles of Grodno, the Augustów Canal, and numerous Catholic and Orthodox churches reflecting the region's layered history.
