Century of humiliation

The century of humiliation (Chinese: 百年国耻) is a term used in China to describe the period of intervention and subjugation of the Qing dynasty and the Republic of China by Western powers and Japan from roughly 1839 to 1949.

Events

The period is usually dated from the First Opium War (1839–1842) and the Treaty of Nanking, through further unequal treaties, the Second Opium War, the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95), the Boxer Rebellion and Eight-Nation Alliance intervention (1900–01), Japan's Twenty-One Demands (1915), and the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–45).

Legacy

The narrative is central to modern Chinese nationalism and patriotic education, framing the founding of the People's Republic in 1949 as ending the humiliation. Historians debate its periodisation and political uses.
