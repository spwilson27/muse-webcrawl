Ketoacidosis

Specialty: Endocrinology, emergency medicine
Causes: Uncontrolled diabetes, alcoholism, starvation
Treatment: Intravenous fluids, insulin, electrolytes

Ketoacidosis is a metabolic state caused by uncontrolled production of ketone bodies, which are acidic byproducts of fat metabolism. When ketones accumulate faster than the body can buffer or excrete them, the blood becomes dangerously acidic, with a pH below 7.3 and low serum bicarbonate.

Types

The three main forms are diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), occurring chiefly in type 1 diabetes when insulin deficiency allows unchecked lipolysis; alcoholic ketoacidosis, arising in chronic alcohol use combined with poor nutrition and vomiting; and starvation ketoacidosis, a milder state after prolonged fasting.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms include nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, dehydration, rapid deep breathing (Kussmaul respiration), a fruity acetone odour on the breath, confusion and, in severe cases, coma. Laboratory findings show hyperglycaemia (in DKA), an elevated anion gap, and ketones in blood and urine.

Treatment

Management centres on intravenous fluid resuscitation, insulin infusion to halt ketogenesis, correction of potassium and other electrolyte abnormalities, and treatment of the precipitating cause, such as infection or missed insulin doses.
