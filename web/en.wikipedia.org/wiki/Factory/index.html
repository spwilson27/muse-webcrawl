Factory

A factory, manufacturing plant, or production plant is an industrial facility, often a complex of several buildings filled with machinery, where workers manufacture items or operate machines that process one item into another. Most modern factories have large warehouses or warehouse-like facilities that contain heavy equipment used for assembly line production.

An assembly line inside a factory.

History

Factories arose with the introduction of machinery during the Industrial Revolution, when the high capital and space requirements of powered equipment became too great for cottage industry and small workshops. Early factories concentrated workers and machinery in one place, powered first by water and then by steam. The factory system spread through the 19th century, transforming manufacturing, labour, and urban life.

Modern factories

Contemporary factories use assembly lines, automation, robotics, and computer-integrated manufacturing to produce goods at scale. Production methods include mass production, just-in-time manufacturing, and lean manufacturing. Factories are central to industrial economies but raise concerns about pollution, occupational safety, and labour conditions, which have been addressed through regulation since the 19th century.
