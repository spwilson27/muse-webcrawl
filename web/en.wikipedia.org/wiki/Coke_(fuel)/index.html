Coke (fuel)

Coke is a grey, hard, and porous coal-based fuel with a high carbon content. It is made by heating coal or petroleum in the absence of air: a destructive distillation process. It is an important industrial product, used mainly in iron ore smelting, but also as a fuel in stoves and forges. The unqualified term "coke" usually refers to the product derived from low-ash and low-sulphur bituminous coal by a process called coking.

Production

Coke is produced in a coke oven battery, where coal is baked in an airless kiln at temperatures as high as 1,100 °C (2,000 °F) for up to 18 hours. The by-products of this process include coal tar, ammonia, light oils, and coal gas. Coke may also be formed naturally as the result of igneous intrusion or a coal seam fire.

Uses

Coke is used as a fuel and as a reducing agent in smelting iron ore in a blast furnace. The carbon monoxide produced by its combustion reduces iron oxide (hematite) to the base metal iron. Coke is also widely used as the major fuel in open-hearth steelmaking. Smaller quantities are used for domestic heating and in foundries, the so-called foundry coke.

History

The Chinese are historically credited with being the first to use coke as a fuel, reportedly as early as the fourth century AD. In Britain, coke was first used in blast furnaces by Abraham Darby I at Coalbrookdale in 1709, a step that made large-scale iron production economically viable and helped launch the Industrial Revolution.
