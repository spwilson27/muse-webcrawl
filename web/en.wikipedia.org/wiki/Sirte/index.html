Sirte (Arabic: سرت, Sirt), also spelled Surt, is a Mediterranean port city in Libya, located on the southern shore of the Gulf of Sidra (Gulf of Sirte), roughly halfway between Tripoli and Benghazi. It is the birthplace of Muammar Gaddafi, who ruled Libya from 1969 to 2011, and the city where he was captured and killed at the end of the First Libyan Civil War.

History

The site lies near the ancient Phoenician and Roman settlement of Charax/Issina and the early Islamic fortress town of Sultan. Modern Sirte grew as an administrative and fishing town under Italian colonial rule. Gaddafi, born in a tent near the village of Qasr Abu Hadi south of the city in 1942, lavished development on his hometown: Sirte hosted the signing of the Sirte Declaration of 9 September 1999, by which the Organisation of African Unity resolved to establish the African Union, and housed the Libyan parliament (General People's Congress) and lavish conference facilities.

Civil wars

During the 2011 civil war, Sirte was the regime's last stronghold; after weeks of fighting and NATO airstrikes under Operation Unified Protector, National Transitional Council forces took the city on 20 October 2011, and Gaddafi was killed while fleeing his compound. In 2015 the Islamic State seized Sirte—its most important base outside Iraq and Syria—and held it until Libyan forces backed by U.S. airstrikes retook the city in December 2016 after the six-month Battle of Sirte. Front lines of the second civil war later stabilized near the city, which changed hands again in 2020.

Geography and economy

Sirte lies at the edge of the coastal plain where the Great Man-Made River project brings fossil groundwater north to irrigate farmland. Its economy rests on fishing, agriculture, administration, and a university, though successive wars have severely damaged the city and displaced much of its population.
