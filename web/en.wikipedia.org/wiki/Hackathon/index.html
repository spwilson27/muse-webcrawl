A hackathon (also known as a hack day, hackfest, datathon or codefest; a portmanteau of "hacking" and "marathon") is an event in which computer programmers and others involved in software development — including graphic designers, interface designers, product managers and domain experts — collaborate intensively over a short period, usually 24 to 48 hours, to create functioning software or hardware by the end of the event. Hackathons typically begin with presentations and idea pitches, team formation, and end with demonstrations and judging.

History

The word appears in 1999 in connection with an OpenBSD cryptographic development event in Calgary and, independently, a Sun Microsystems challenge at the JavaOne conference. The format spread in the mid-2000s through events such as Yahoo!'s Hack Days and, from 2009–2010, organised student and civic competitions; Major League Hacking (founded 2013) built a collegiate circuit. Companies use internal hackathons for rapid prototyping and recruitment — several well-known products, including Facebook's "Like" button and early forms of group messaging apps, emerged from such events.

Types

Hackathons may be organised around a programming language, platform or API; a cause (civic, open-data and "hack for good" events); corporate innovation; student competitions; or single-application development. Some are competitive with prizes, others are purely collaborative, and several long-running open-source projects hold regular hackathons for bug fixing and features.

Criticism

The format has been criticised for rewarding demo polish over sustainable code, for sleep-deprived "crunch", and for questionable intellectual-property terms at corporate events.
