Eid al-Ghadir

Eid al-Ghadir is one of the most significant feasts in Shia Islam

Eid al-Ghadir (Arabic: عيد الغدير) is a Shia Muslim holiday commemorating the event of Ghadir Khumm, at which, according to Shia belief, the Prophet Muhammad appointed Ali ibn Abi Talib as his successor. It is observed on 18 Dhu al-Hijjah of the Islamic calendar and is a public holiday in Iran.

Background

In 632 CE, returning from the Farewell Pilgrimage, Muhammad stopped at the pond (ghadir) of Khumm between Mecca and Medina and addressed the assembled pilgrims, in a sermon reported in both Sunni and Shia sources. Holding Ali's hand, he declared words reported as "For whomever I am his mawla, Ali is his mawla." Shia Muslims interpret mawla as "master/leader" and regard the event as the explicit designation (nass) of Ali as successor; Sunni Muslims generally interpret it as an affirmation of Ali's high standing and friendship.

Observance

The feast is marked by sermons recounting the event, devotional poetry, charitable giving, feasting, wearing new clothes, and acts of brotherhood. In Iran it is an official public holiday with mass celebrations; large communal events, including multi-kilometre festival gatherings in Tehran, have been promoted since the 2020s. Shia communities in Iraq, Lebanon, Yemen, Azerbaijan, Bahrain and the diaspora also observe the day, some treating it, on the basis of hadith, as the greatest of the Islamic eids.
