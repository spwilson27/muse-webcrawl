Deformation (mechanics)

In physics and continuum mechanics, deformation is the change in the shape or size of an object, or in the relative positions of its parts, caused by an applied force, a temperature change, or another external influence. It is quantified by the strain, the ratio of the displacement of particles in the body to a reference length. Deformation is described mathematically using the deformation gradient and strain tensors of continuum mechanics.

Types of deformation

Elastic deformation is reversible: the object returns to its original shape when the load is removed. For most materials the relation between stress and strain in this regime is approximately linear, following Hooke's law, with the constant of proportionality given by an elastic modulus such as Young's modulus. Plastic deformation is permanent and occurs when stress exceeds the material's yield strength; in metals it is mediated by the motion of dislocations. Other modes include viscous deformation in fluids and viscoelastic behaviour in polymers.

Deformation may be uniform (homogeneous), as in the simple stretching of a bar, or non-uniform, as in bending, twisting (torsion) or localised necking before fracture. Compressive deformation shortens a body, tensile deformation lengthens it, and shear deformation changes its angles without necessarily changing its volume.

Measurement and analysis

Strain is measured in practice with strain gauges, extensometers and optical techniques such as digital image correlation. Engineers analyse deformation using stress–strain curves obtained from tensile tests, and computational methods such as the finite element method predict deformation in complex structures. Excessive deformation can constitute failure even without fracture, for example where deflections impair the function of a structure or machine part.
