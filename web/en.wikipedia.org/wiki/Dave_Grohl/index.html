Dave Grohl

David Eric Grohl (born January 14, 1969) is an American musician, best known as founder, lead singer, guitarist and principal songwriter of Foo Fighters, and as drummer for Nirvana from 1990 to 1994. He is regarded as one of the most influential rock musicians of his generation.

Early life and Nirvana

Raised in Springfield, Virginia, Grohl joined Washington D.C. hardcore band Scream at 17. In 1990 he became Nirvana's drummer, playing on Nevermind (1991) and In Utero (1993), until Kurt Cobain's death in 1994 dissolved the band.

Foo Fighters

Grohl recorded a solo demo that became Foo Fighters' self-titled debut (1995), then built a full band. Albums including The Colour and the Shape (1997), Wasting Light (2011) and Medicine at Midnight (2021) made them stadium headliners and multiple Grammy winners. After drummer Taylor Hawkins died in 2022, the band continued with Josh Freese. Grohl has also drummed for Queens of the Stone Age and Them Crooked Vultures, directed the documentary Sound City (2013), and published memoir The Storyteller (2021). He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice, with Nirvana (2014) and Foo Fighters (2021).
