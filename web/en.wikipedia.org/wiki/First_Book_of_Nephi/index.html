The First Book of Nephi is the first book of the Book of Mormon, a text published by Joseph Smith in 1830 and regarded as scripture by members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and other denominations in the Latter Day Saint movement. According to the text, it was written by Nephi, a prophet who lived in Jerusalem and the Americas around 600 BC.

Narrative

The book recounts how the prophet Lehi, warned of Jerusalem's coming destruction, leads his family into the wilderness. His sons Laman and Lemuel rebel against their younger brother Nephi, who receives visions and obtains the brass plates—records containing scripture and genealogy—from Laban. After years of journeying, including the construction of a ship under Nephi's direction, the family crosses the ocean to a promised land in the Americas.

Themes and content

The book includes Nephi's vision of the tree of life, an account also given by Lehi, and extensive quotations and commentary on the writings of Isaiah. It establishes themes of obedience, covenant, and the scattering and gathering of Israel that recur throughout the Book of Mormon.

Origins

According to Joseph Smith, the book was part of an ancient record written on golden plates, which he translated by divine power. Mainstream scholars generally view the Book of Mormon as a nineteenth-century work; the First Book of Nephi replaced an earlier lost portion of the manuscript known as the Book of Lehi.
