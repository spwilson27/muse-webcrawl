Watkins Glen is a village in Schuyler County, New York, United States, located at the southern end of Seneca Lake, the deepest of the Finger Lakes. It serves as the county seat and had a population of roughly 1,800 at recent censuses. The village is world-renowned both for its dramatic gorge in Watkins Glen State Park and for its motor racing heritage.

Watkins Glen State Park

The state park's gorge trail winds for about two miles past 19 waterfalls, stone bridges and sculpted rock formations carved by Glen Creek. The park opened to the public in 1906 after passing through private resort ownership in the nineteenth century.

Motor racing

Road racing began in Watkins Glen in 1948 on a course through the village streets, organised by Cameron Argetsinger. The permanent Watkins Glen International circuit, opened in 1956, hosted the United States Grand Prix from 1961 to 1980 and today stages NASCAR Cup Series, IMSA and vintage racing events.

Other notes

The area is also known for salt mining — a major salt works operates beneath Seneca Lake — and for the 1973 Summer Jam rock festival at the racetrack, which drew an estimated 600,000 people.
