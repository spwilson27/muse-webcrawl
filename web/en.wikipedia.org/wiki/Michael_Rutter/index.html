Sir Michael Llewellyn Rutter CBE FRS FMedSci (15 August 1933 – 23 October 2021) was a British child psychiatrist described as the "father of child psychology". He was a professor of developmental psychopathology at the Institute of Psychiatry, King's College London, and founder of the Medical Research Council's Social, Genetic and Developmental Psychiatry Centre.

Work

Rutter's Isle of Wight studies established the epidemiology of child psychiatric disorder. He led the English and Romanian Adoptees study, which tracked children adopted from Ceaușescu-era orphanages and demonstrated both the effects of early deprivation and remarkable developmental recovery. His concept of resilience — that some children thrive despite adversity — transformed developmental psychology, and he played a central role in classifying autism as a neurodevelopmental disorder distinct from childhood schizophrenia.

Honours

He was knighted in 1992, elected a Fellow of the Royal Society, and received many international prizes for his contributions to child and adolescent mental health.
