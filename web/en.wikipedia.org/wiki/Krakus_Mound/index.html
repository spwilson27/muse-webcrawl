Krakus Mound (Polish: Kopiec Krakusa) is a tumulus on Lasota Hill in the Podgórze district of Kraków, Poland. According to legend it is the burial mound of King Krakus, the mythical founder of Kraków. It is one of the city's four great mounds and the oldest of them.

Description and investigations

The mound rises about 16 metres above its base, which has a diameter of roughly 57 metres. Excavations in 1934–1937, led by Józef Żurowski and Roman Jakimowicz, found no burial chamber or human remains—only a carefully layered internal structure with a central pole, suggesting the mound was built deliberately over a long period, in stages. Small finds, including an Avar bronze belt fitting dated to around the 8th century, indicate the mound was raised in the early medieval period, perhaps between the 8th and 10th centuries, though its precise date and purpose remain debated; theories include a princely tomb, a cult site, and an astronomical marker—the mound's axis aligns with sunrise on certain Celtic festivals, as do the axes of the nearby Wanda Mound.

Traditions

Until the 19th century the folk festival of Rękawka was held at the mound on the Tuesday after Easter, when food and coins were thrown down its slopes to the poor gathered below; the festival still takes place at the nearby Church of St. Benedict. The mound offers a panoramic view over Kraków and the Vistula.
