Super Bowl LI was an American football game played at NRG Stadium in Houston, Texas, on February 5, 2017, to determine the champion of the National Football League (NFL) for the 2016 season. The American Football Conference champion New England Patriots defeated the National Football Conference champion Atlanta Falcons, 34–28 in overtime, completing the largest comeback in Super Bowl history.

The game

Atlanta dominated the first two and a half quarters, building a 28–3 lead behind MVP quarterback Matt Ryan, a pick-six of Tom Brady by Robert Alford, and scores from Devonta Freeman, Austin Hooper and Tevin Coleman. The Patriots then scored 31 unanswered points: James White caught a touchdown and a two-point conversion and ran for another score, Danny Amendola added a touchdown and conversion, and Stephen Gostkowski kicked two field goals, tying the game at 28–28 with 57 seconds left. In the first overtime period in Super Bowl history, White's 2-yard touchdown run on the opening drive won the game. Brady threw for a Super Bowl-record 466 yards and was named MVP for a record fourth time; the Falcons' collapse is remembered by the shorthand "28–3".

Legacy

The game is widely ranked among the greatest Super Bowls ever played. It gave Brady and head coach Bill Belichick their fifth championship together, surpassing all other quarterback–coach combinations, and it remains the only Super Bowl decided in overtime until Super Bowl LVIII.
