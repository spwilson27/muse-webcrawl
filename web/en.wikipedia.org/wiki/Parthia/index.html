Parthia

Parthia (Old Persian: Parθava; Parthian: Parθaw) was a historical region in northeastern Iran, roughly corresponding to the modern provinces of Khorasan. It gave its name to the Parthian Empire (247 BC – AD 224), ruled by the Arsacid dynasty.

Geography

Parthia was bounded by the Kopet Dag mountains to the north, the Dasht-e Kavir desert to the south, Media to the west and Aria and Margiana to the east. Its cities included Hecatompylos, an early Arsacid capital, and Nisa, the dynastic necropolis near modern Ashgabat.

History

Parthia appears in Achaemenid inscriptions as a satrapy; it rebelled under Darius I and was reconquered. After Alexander the Great it passed to the Seleucids. In the 3rd century BC the satrap Andragoras asserted independence, but was overthrown by Arsaces I, leader of the Parni nomads, who founded the Arsacid kingdom. From this Parthian base the Arsacids built an empire stretching from the Euphrates to the Indus, Rome's great eastern rival, famous for its mounted archers and the "Parthian shot", until the last Arsacid fell to Ardashir I, founder of the Sasanian Empire.
