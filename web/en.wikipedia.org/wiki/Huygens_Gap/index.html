Huygens Gap

The Huygens Gap is a prominent gap in the rings of Saturn, located at the inner edge of the Cassini Division. It is named after Dutch astronomer Christiaan Huygens, who discovered Saturn's largest moon Titan and explained the nature of the rings in the 1650s.

Description

The gap lies between the outer edge of the B ring and the Cassini Division proper. The narrow, eccentric Huygens Ringlet runs near the middle of the gap; it is maintained by gravitational interactions and is slightly elliptical, with its width varying around its circumference. A second, fainter ringlet lies near the gap's inner portion.

Formation

Like other gaps in Saturn's rings, the Huygens Gap is swept relatively clear of particles by orbital resonances — here, notably a 2:1 resonance with the moon Mimas influences the nearby B-ring edge — combined with the dynamics of dense ring particles. Images from the Cassini spacecraft (2004–2017) revealed fine structure, density waves and propeller-like disturbances in and around the gap.
