Diffuse sky radiation

Diffuse sky radiation is solar radiation reaching the Earth's surface after having been scattered from the direct solar beam by molecules or particulates in the atmosphere. It is also called sky radiation, diffuse skylight, or simply skylight.

Why the sky is blue

Under cloudless skies about a quarter of incoming solar radiation is scattered. Short (blue) wavelengths are scattered much more strongly than long ones by Rayleigh scattering from air molecules, which is why the sky appears blue and why diffuse skylight is bluish. At sunrise and sunset the remaining direct light is reddened.

Effects

Diffuse radiation lights shaded areas and the undersides of leaves, contributing significantly to photosynthesis and to the output of solar panels, especially on cloudy days when it can be nearly all of the available sunlight. Its polarisation pattern is used by some animals for navigation.
