Baden-Württemberg is a German state (Land) in the southwest of the country, east of the Rhine. Its capital and largest city is Stuttgart. With an area of about 35,752 square kilometres and some 11.2 million inhabitants, it is Germany's third-largest state by both measures.

Geography

The state encompasses the Black Forest (Schwarzwald), the Swabian Alb, the upper Rhine valley and part of Lake Constance (Bodensee). Major cities include Mannheim, Karlsruhe, Freiburg, Heidelberg and Ulm. The rivers Rhine, Neckar and Danube all flow through or originate in the state.

History

Baden-Württemberg was created in 1952 by the merger of the states of Württemberg-Baden, South Baden and Württemberg-Hohenzollern, themselves formed from the historical territories of Baden, Württemberg and the Hohenzollern lands after the Second World War.

Economy

The state is one of Europe's strongest economic regions, noted for its automotive and engineering industries — Daimler (Mercedes-Benz), Porsche and Bosch are headquartered here — and for its many export-oriented mid-sized firms, the celebrated Mittelstand.
