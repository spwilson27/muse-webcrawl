Jehoiachin (Hebrew: יְהוֹיָכִין, romanized: Yəhōyāḵīn; also rendered Jeconiah and Coniah) was the nineteenth and penultimate king of Judah, reigning for three months and ten days. He was the son and successor of King Jehoiakim and the grandson of King Josiah. His reign ended when the Neo-Babylonian emperor Nebuchadnezzar II besieged Jerusalem and Jehoiachin surrendered, after which he was taken captive to Babylon along with much of the Judean court and population.

Reign

Jehoiachin succeeded his father Jehoiakim at the age of eighteen (according to 2 Kings 24) or eight (according to the Masoretic text of 2 Chronicles 36, a figure most scholars regard as a scribal error). Within months, Nebuchadnezzar's forces besieged Jerusalem. Jehoiachin surrendered the city on 16 March 597 BC — a date confirmed by the Babylonian Chronicles. The Babylonians plundered the Temple treasury and carried the king, his mother Nehushta, his wives, officials and thousands of craftsmen and soldiers into exile.

Exile in Babylon

Babylonian ration tablets excavated at Babylon record deliveries of oil to "Ya'u-kīnu, king of the land of Judah" and his five sons, confirming his presence and status at the Babylonian court. Jehoiachin remained a prisoner for 37 years. In 562 BC the new Babylonian king Amel-Marduk (Evil-Merodach in the Bible) released him, "spoke kindly to him and gave him a seat above the seats of the kings who were with him in Babylon" (2 Kings 25:27–30), granting him a regular allowance for the rest of his life.

Legacy

Jehoiachin stands in the genealogy of Jesus in the Gospel of Matthew, where he is called Jeconiah. The prophet Jeremiah pronounced a curse that no descendant of Coniah would prosper on the throne of David, a passage that has generated much theological discussion. His uncle Zedekiah succeeded him as Babylon's puppet king, reigning until the final destruction of Jerusalem in 586 BC.
