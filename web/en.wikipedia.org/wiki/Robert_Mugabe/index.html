Overview

Robert Gabriel Mugabe (1924–2019) was a Zimbabwean revolutionary and politician who governed Zimbabwe from independence in 1980 until he was forced from power by a military intervention in 2017 — first as prime minister, then as executive president. A teacher turned nationalist, he led ZANU and its guerrilla army ZANLA from Mozambique during the liberation war against Rhodesia's white-minority government.

Rule and fall

Mugabe's early government expanded education and health and promised reconciliation, but his rule darkened: the Gukurahundi massacres of Ndebele supporters of rival ZAPU in Matabeleland (1983–87, deaths estimated in the tens of thousands by some accounts, lower by others), the chaotic fast-track land seizures after 2000, hyperinflation peaking in 2008, and disputed elections made him an international pariah to many — while others in Africa continued to honour the liberation hero. In November 2017 the army confined him; he resigned facing impeachment, succeeded by Emmerson Mnangagwa, and died in Singapore in 2019.
