Aspect ratio (image)

The aspect ratio of an image is the ratio of its width to its height, conventionally expressed as two numbers separated by a colon, such as 16:9. It describes the proportional relationship between the sides of a rectangular image regardless of its absolute size, and is fundamental to photography, cinematography, television and display design.

Common ratios

Traditional television and early computer monitors used 4:3 (1.33:1). 35 mm still photography popularised 3:2 (1.5:1), while 1:1 square format is associated with medium-format cameras and social media. Modern high-definition television and most video use 16:9 (1.78:1); cinema widescreen formats include 1.85:1 ("flat") and 2.39:1 (anamorphic "scope"). Computer displays have also used 16:10 and, increasingly, taller ratios such as 3:2 on laptops and 19.5:9 or similar on smartphones.

Terminology

In film, the image aspect ratio is distinct from the display aspect ratio of the device showing it; mismatches are handled by letterboxing, pillarboxing, cropping ("pan and scan") or stretching. Pixel aspect ratio describes the shape of individual pixels in digital imaging.
