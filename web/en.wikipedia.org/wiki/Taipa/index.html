Taipa (Chinese: 氹仔; Portuguese: Ilha da Taipa) is an area in Macau, China. Located on the northern half of the island bearing the same name south of the Macau Peninsula, Taipa was historically a separate island but is now physically joined to Coloane island through the reclaimed land strip of Cotai. Taipa is connected to the Macau Peninsula by three bridges: the Governador Nobre de Carvalho Bridge (the "Old Bridge"), the Friendship Bridge (Ponte da Amizade), and the Sai Van Bridge.

Geography

The island was originally formed from two smaller islets, Greater Taipa and Lesser Taipa, which were joined by silting and reclamation. Its two hills, Taipa Grande and Taipa Pequena, remain landmarks. The University of Macau's former campus, Macau International Airport, the Taipa Ferry Terminal at Pac On, and the Macau Jockey Club racecourse are located on the island.

History

Taipa was settled by Chinese fishing communities and came under Portuguese administration in the 19th century; the Qing authorities maintained a customs presence nearby. In 1851 the Portuguese occupied the island, and it was formally part of the Portuguese colony thereafter. For much of the 20th century Taipa was a quiet area of villages, duck farms, firework factories and shipyards, known for the manufacture of firecrackers and incense. Rapid development followed the opening of the first bridge to the peninsula in 1974 and especially after the handover of Macau to China in 1999.

Modern Taipa

The old village of Taipa (Vila da Taipa) survives around Rua do Cunha, a narrow pedestrian street famous for its restaurants and shops selling almond cookies, pork chop buns and other Macanese snacks. The Taipa Houses–Museum, a group of restored colonial residences by the former shoreline, preserves the atmosphere of the Portuguese-era community. Modern Taipa is largely residential, with large housing estates, while the adjacent Cotai Strip hosts many of Macau's largest casino resorts.
