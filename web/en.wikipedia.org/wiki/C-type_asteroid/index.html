C-type asteroid

C-type asteroid
Composition: Carbonaceous
Albedo: Very low (~0.03–0.10)
Share of known asteroids: ~75%
Examples: 1 Ceres, 253 Mathilde, Ryugu, Bennu

C-type (carbonaceous) asteroids are the most common variety, forming around 75% of known asteroids. They are distinguished by a very low albedo, reflecting their composition of carbonaceous material, and are thought to contain clays and silicate rocks together with water ice in their interiors.

Characteristics

C-types are among the most primitive objects in the Solar System, largely unchanged since its formation. Their spectra resemble those of carbonaceous chondrite meteorites. Most reside in the outer asteroid belt, where they dominate.

Exploration

JAXA's Hayabusa2 sampled the C-type asteroid Ryugu and returned material to Earth in 2020; NASA's OSIRIS-REx did the same at Bennu in 2023. These samples are prized for studying the early Solar System and the origins of water and organic compounds on Earth.
