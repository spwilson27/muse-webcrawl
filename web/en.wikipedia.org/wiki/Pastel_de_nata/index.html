Pastel de nata

A pastel de nata (plural pastéis de nata) is a Portuguese custard tart made of crisp, flaky puff pastry filled with egg custard, baked at high temperature until the top is blistered and caramelised, and typically dusted with cinnamon and powdered sugar.

History

The tarts were created by monks at the Jerónimos Monastery in Belém, Lisbon, before the 18th century, using leftover egg yolks from starching habits and making wine. After the monastery's closure in the 1830s, the recipe was sold to the nearby Fábrica de Pastéis de Belém, founded in 1837, which still sells them as Pastéis de Belém under a secret recipe.

Spread

Pastéis de nata are eaten throughout Portugal and the Lusophone world and have become popular worldwide, influencing the egg tarts of Macau and Hong Kong. They are best eaten warm, shortly after baking.
