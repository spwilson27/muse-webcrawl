Pączki

Pączki (Polish: [ˈpɔ̃t͡ʂki], singular pączek) are filled doughnuts found in Polish cuisine. They are made from especially rich dough containing eggs, fats, sugar, yeast, and sometimes milk, and are typically filled with fruit jam — traditionally rosehip, prune, or raspberry — and topped with powdered sugar, icing, or glaze.

Tradition

Pączki have been known in Poland since at least the Middle Ages. Their golden age came under King Augustus III in the 18th century, when the dough was lightened with the help of French bakers. They are traditionally eaten on Fat Thursday (Tłusty Czwartek), the last Thursday before Lent, when using up rich ingredients before the Lenten fast was customary.

In the diaspora

In Polish-American communities, particularly in the Midwest — Detroit, Hamtramck, Chicago, Buffalo, and Cleveland — pączki are associated with Fat Tuesday (Mardi Gras), and bakeries draw long lines on "Pączki Day".
