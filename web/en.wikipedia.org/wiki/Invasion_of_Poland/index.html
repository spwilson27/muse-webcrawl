Invasion of Poland
Date1 September – 6 October 1939

AttackersNazi Germany, Soviet Union, Slovakia
ResultGerman–Soviet victory; Poland partitioned; start of World War II in Europe

The invasion of Poland began on 1 September 1939, when Nazi Germany attacked Poland, and was joined from the east by the Soviet Union on 17 September. It marked the start of the Second World War in Europe; Britain and France declared war on Germany on 3 September.

Background

The attack followed the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of 23 August 1939, whose secret protocol divided Eastern Europe between Germany and the USSR. Hitler demanded the Free City of Danzig and an extraterritorial corridor across the Polish Corridor; Poland, guaranteed by Britain and France, refused. On 31 August, SS operatives staged the Gleiwitz incident — a fake Polish attack on a German radio station — as a pretext.

The campaign

Germany committed some 1.5 million men, with armoured spearheads and Luftwaffe attacks that destroyed much of the Polish air force and struck cities including Wieluń and Warsaw. The Polish army fought major actions at Mokra, the Bzura (the largest Polish counterattack), and in the defence of Warsaw, which capitulated on 28 September. The Soviet invasion from the east on 17 September destroyed any prospect of a Romanian bridgehead defence. The last Polish units surrendered at Kock on 6 October.

Consequences

Poland was partitioned between Germany and the Soviet Union along the Bug River. Both occupiers conducted mass repression: Germany began the Intelligenzaktion and ghettoisation of Jews, while the USSR deported hundreds of thousands eastward and murdered Polish officers at Katyń in 1940. The Polish government escaped into exile and Polish forces continued fighting with the Allies, but Poland remained occupied until 1945.
