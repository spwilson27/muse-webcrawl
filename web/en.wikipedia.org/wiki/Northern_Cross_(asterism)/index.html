Northern Cross (asterism)

The Northern Cross is a prominent asterism in the northern celestial hemisphere, formed by the brightest stars of the constellation Cygnus, the Swan. It consists of a long upright bar and a shorter transverse bar, resembling a cross lying along the band of the Milky Way, and serves as the backbone of the swan figure.

The Northern Cross, formed by the principal stars of Cygnus

Stars

The asterism's six main stars are:

  • Deneb (Alpha Cygni), at the top of the cross and the tail of the swan, one of the most luminous supergiants known;
  • Sadr (Gamma Cygni), at the centre of the cross;
  • Albireo (Beta Cygni), at the foot of the cross and the swan's beak, a famous gold-and-blue double star;
  • Delta Cygni and Epsilon Cygni (Gienah), forming the crossbar—the swan's wings—with Eta Cygni on the long axis.

Observation

The Northern Cross is a familiar sight in summer and autumn skies in the Northern Hemisphere. Deneb forms one corner of the Summer Triangle with Vega and Altair. Because the cross stands upright on the northwestern horizon around Christmas time, it has sometimes been associated in popular lore with the season, earning it the occasional comparison with the Southern Cross of the southern hemisphere.
