Janet Damita Jo Jackson (born 16 May 1966) is an American singer, songwriter, actress and dancer. The youngest child of the Jackson family, she began performing on stage with her family at age seven and appeared on the television series Good Times and Fame. With her third album, Control (1986), produced with Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis, she established one of the most influential sounds in contemporary R&B, blending pop, funk and industrial elements.

Career

Control yielded five top-five singles, including "What Have You Done for Me Lately" and "Nasty". Rhythm Nation 1814 (1989) addressed social injustice and produced seven top-five singles, a chart record. In 1991 she signed with Virgin Records in one of the largest recording contracts of the time. Janet (1993) and The Velvet Rope (1997) explored sensuality, depression and social themes, with singles such as "That's the Way Love Goes" and "Together Again". Later albums include All for You (2001), Damita Jo (2004) and Unbreakable (2015), her seventh US number-one album.

Her career was affected by controversy in 2004 after a "wardrobe malfunction" during the Super Bowl XXXVIII halftime show; Jackson later maintained she was unfairly treated compared with her co-performer. As an actress she starred in films including Poetic Justice (1993), Nutty Professor II: The Klumps (2000) and several Tyler Perry productions.

Artistry and legacy

Jackson is celebrated for elaborate choreography, socially conscious songwriting and meticulous studio production. She has sold over 100 million records worldwide, won five Grammy Awards, and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019. Her influence is frequently cited by artists such as Beyoncé, Britney Spears, Rihanna and Bruno Mars.

Personal life

Jackson was married to James DeBarge (1984–85), René Elizondo Jr. (1991–2003) and Qatari businessman Wissam Al Mana (2012–2017), with whom she has a son, Eissa, born in 2017.
