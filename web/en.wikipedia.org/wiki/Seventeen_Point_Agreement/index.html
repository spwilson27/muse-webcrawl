Seventeen Point Agreement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Seventeen Point Agreement, formally the Agreement of the Central People's Government and the Local Government of Tibet on Measures for the Peaceful Liberation of Tibet, was signed in Beijing on 23 May 1951 by delegations of the People's Republic of China and the government of the 14th Dalai Lama. It affirmed Chinese sovereignty over Tibet while promising to preserve Tibet's existing political system, the status of the Dalai Lama, and religious freedom.

Background

Following the founding of the People's Republic of China in 1949, Beijing sought to incorporate Tibet. In October 1950 the People's Liberation Army defeated the Tibetan army at Chamdo, opening the way to negotiations. A five-man Tibetan delegation led by Ngabo Ngawang Jigme travelled to Beijing in April 1951.

Terms

Among its seventeen points, the agreement provided that the Tibetan people would "drive out imperialist aggressive forces" and return to the "big family of the Motherland"; that the central government would not alter Tibet's existing political system or the established status, functions and powers of the Dalai Lama; that religious beliefs, customs and habits would be respected and monasteries' incomes protected; that Tibetan troops would be reorganised into the PLA; and that reforms in Tibet would be carried out only in consultation with Tibetan leaders, with no compulsion by the central authorities.

Aftermath and dispute

The Dalai Lama initially accepted the agreement, sending a telegram to Mao Zedong in October 1951 endorsing it. Following the Lhasa uprising of March 1959 and his flight into exile in India, the Dalai Lama repudiated the agreement, stating that it had been signed under duress and that China had violated its terms. The Tibetan government-in-exile maintains that the agreement is invalid because the negotiators exceeded their authority and because it was imposed under threat of military force. The Chinese government maintains that the agreement was freely negotiated and represents Tibet's peaceful liberation.
