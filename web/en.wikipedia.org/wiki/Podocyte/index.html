Podocyte

Podocytes are cells in Bowman's capsule in the kidneys that wrap around the capillaries of the glomerulus. They are highly specialized epithelial cells and form a crucial component of the glomerular filtration barrier, alongside the fenestrated endothelium of the capillaries and the glomerular basement membrane. The name derives from the Greek pous (foot) and kytos (cell), referring to the foot-like processes, or pedicels, that extend from the cell body.

Structure

Each podocyte has a large cell body containing a prominent nucleus, a well-developed Golgi apparatus, and abundant rough endoplasmic reticulum, reflecting its high rate of protein synthesis. Primary processes extend from the cell body and give rise to smaller secondary processes called pedicels (foot processes), which interdigitate with pedicels from neighbouring podocytes around the capillary surface. Between adjacent pedicels lie filtration slits, bridged by a thin diaphragm known as the slit diaphragm, whose key structural protein is nephrin.

Function

Podocytes help prevent plasma proteins such as albumin from entering the urinary filtrate. The slit diaphragm acts as a size- and charge-selective barrier. Podocytes also synthesize and maintain components of the glomerular basement membrane, regulate glomerular filtration rate through their contractile apparatus, and secrete growth factors that support the glomerular endothelium.

Clinical significance

Podocyte injury or loss is central to many proteinuric kidney diseases. Effacement (flattening and retraction) of foot processes is a hallmark of minimal change disease and focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS). Mutations in genes encoding slit-diaphragm and cytoskeletal proteins, such as nephrin (NPHS1) and podocin (NPHS2), cause congenital nephrotic syndromes. Because podocytes have limited capacity to proliferate in adults, their progressive loss contributes to glomerulosclerosis and chronic kidney disease.
