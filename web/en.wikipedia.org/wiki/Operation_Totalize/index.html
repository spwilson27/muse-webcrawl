Operation Totalize
Part ofOperation Overlord, Battle of Normandy, World War II
Date8–9 August 1944 (first phase; operation continued to 13 August)
LocationSouth of Caen toward Falaise, Normandy, France
CommandersGuy Simonds (II Canadian Corps)
ResultAllied advance toward Falaise; set up Operation Tractable

Operation Totalize (also spelt Totalise in British sources) was an offensive by the First Canadian Army under Lieutenant-General Guy Simonds, conducted from 8 to 13 August 1944 during the closing stages of the Battle of Normandy. Its objective was to break through German defences south of Caen, along the road to Falaise, thereby cutting off German forces still west of the city and contributing to their encirclement.

Planning and innovation

Totalize was notable for several innovations. The attack opened at night behind a heavy bomber raid, with infantry carried forward in improvised armoured personnel carriers — "Kangaroos", converted from M7 Priest self-propelled guns with their armament removed — one of the first mass uses of APCs in battle. Columns were guided by searchlights reflected on clouds ("artificial moonlight"), Bofors tracer fire marking the axis of advance, and radio direction-finding. The attacking force comprised Canadian and British divisions of II Canadian Corps alongside the 1st Polish Armoured Division.

Course of the operation

The night attack of 8 August achieved a substantial penetration, advancing several kilometres and capturing villages along the Caen–Falaise road. A second phase on 9 August, involving armoured thrusts toward the high ground beyond, met stiff resistance from the 12th SS Panzer Division; one Canadian armoured column, "Worthington Force", became isolated and was largely destroyed on 9 August. German defences stiffened thereafter, and although the Allies continued to gain ground, Falaise itself was not reached.

Aftermath

Totalize was succeeded by Operation Tractable (14–21 August), which finally took Falaise and closed the Falaise pocket, completing the destruction of the German Seventh Army and Fifth Panzer Army in Normandy.
