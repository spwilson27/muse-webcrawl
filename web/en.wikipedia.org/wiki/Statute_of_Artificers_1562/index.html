Statute of Artificers 1562

The Statute of Artificers 1562 (5 Eliz. 1. c. 4), commonly called the Statute of Apprentices, was an Act of the Parliament of England enacted under Elizabeth I. It was a national labour code that regulated wages, hours and training, and remained a cornerstone of English labour law for more than two centuries.

Provisions

The statute consolidated and extended earlier labour legislation. It required a seven-year apprenticeship before anyone could practise most trades, authorised justices of the peace to assess maximum wages in each county, fixed working hours, restricted workers from leaving their parish without a testimonial, and made able-bodied people without a trade liable to be compelled into agricultural service.

Legacy

The apprenticeship clauses were repealed in 1814 and the wage-fixing provisions in 1813, but the statute shaped occupational training and labour regulation in England for generations and influenced later factory and trade-union law.
