Bishōjo game

Bishōjo games (Japanese: 美少女ゲーム, bishōjo gēmu), literally "pretty girl games", are a genre of Japanese video games centred on interactions with attractive anime-style girls, who follow certain archetypal character idols by appearances and characteristics. The majority of bishōjo games involve romance, dating, and sometimes sexual content. Bishōjo games began to appear in Japan in the 1980s and were mainly produced for personal computers such as the NEC PC-8801 and PC-9801; the genre is closely related to visual novels, dating sims, and eroge.

Terminology

The equivalent genre focusing on attractive young men aimed at a female audience is called otome games or, more broadly, games featuring bishōnen ("beautiful boys"). A bishōjo game in which female characters are the central focus but presented without romance elements may also be marketed as a "gal game" (gyarugē).

History

Early examples on Japanese home computers in the 1980s included adventure and simulation titles with romantic elements. The genre was transformed in the 1990s: Tokimeki Memorial (1994) popularised the non-adult dating sim on consoles, while Leaf's visual novels and titles like Kanon (1999) by Key demonstrated that story-driven "crying games" (nakigē) could achieve mainstream critical and commercial success. In the 2000s and 2010s, digital distribution through Steam and mobile platforms brought many titles to international audiences, often in all-ages versions.

Characteristics

Typical features include a male protagonist, a cast of heroines each with her own story route, branching narratives determined by player choices, and extensive voice acting and illustration. Gameplay ranges from pure text-based visual novels with minimal interaction to complex raising simulations with statistics and schedules.

Industry and culture

The genre forms a substantial segment of the Japanese PC game market and has influenced anime, manga, and light novel adaptations of successful titles. Fan-made dōjin games, such as Tsukihime (2000) by Type-Moon, have grown into major franchises.
