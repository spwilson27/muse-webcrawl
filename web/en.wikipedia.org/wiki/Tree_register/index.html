Tree register

Volunteers recording a veteran tree for a national register.

A tree register is a database of notable trees, typically recording champion trees (the largest known specimens of a species), ancient and veteran trees, and trees of historical or cultural significance. Registers are maintained by forestry bodies, conservation charities and enthusiast societies in many countries.

Examples

In Britain and Ireland, the Tree Register, founded in 1988, records the tallest and largest trees; its measurements underpin the recognition of "champion trees." The Ancient Tree Inventory, run by the Woodland Trust with partner organisations, maps ancient, veteran and notable trees across the United Kingdom with the help of volunteer recorders. In the United States, American Forests has maintained a National Register of Champion Trees since 1940, scoring trees by a formula combining trunk circumference, height and crown spread. Australia's National Register of Big Trees and registers in New Zealand, South Africa and several European countries serve similar purposes.

Purpose

Registers help identify trees deserving legal or practical protection, track the growth and health of remarkable specimens over time, and provide data on the maximum sizes species can attain. Because ancient trees support rich communities of fungi, invertebrates and other wildlife, registering them is also a tool for biodiversity conservation.
