Herzog & de Meuron Basel Ltd. is a Swiss architecture firm with its head office in Basel, founded in 1978 by Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron. The practice is renowned for its inventive use of materials and for landmark cultural and sporting buildings worldwide.

Major works

The firm rose to international prominence with the conversion of Bankside Power Station into Tate Modern in London (2000). Other major projects include the Beijing National Stadium (the "Bird's Nest") for the 2008 Olympics, the Allianz Arena in Munich, the de Young Museum in San Francisco, the Elbphilharmonie in Hamburg, and the M+ museum in Hong Kong.

Recognition

Herzog and de Meuron received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2001, the Praemium Imperiale in 2007, and the Mies Crown Hall Americas Prize. Both founders studied at the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology (ETH) in Zurich, where they later taught.
