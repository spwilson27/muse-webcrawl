A Christmas Carol. In Prose. Being a Ghost Story of Christmas, commonly known as A Christmas Carol, is a novella by Charles Dickens, first published in London by Chapman & Hall in 1843. It tells the story of Ebenezer Scrooge, a miserly old money-lender who is visited on Christmas Eve by the ghost of his former partner Jacob Marley and the spirits of Christmas Past, Present and Yet to Come, and who awakes on Christmas morning transformed into a generous man.

Plot

Scrooge, who dismisses Christmas as "humbug" and rebuffs his clerk Bob Cratchit and nephew Fred, is shown scenes from his lonely childhood and lost love, the warmth of the Cratchit family's Christmas despite the illness of Tiny Tim, and a bleak future in which his own death is unmourned and Tim has died. Shaken, Scrooge vows to change, raises Cratchit's salary, and becomes "as good a friend, as good a master, and as good a man" as the city knew.

Impact

Written in six weeks amid concern over child poverty in industrial Britain, the book sold out its first edition of 6,000 copies by Christmas Eve 1843. It has never been out of print, fixed many modern associations of Christmas — the phrases "Merry Christmas" and "Bah! Humbug!", and the use of "Scrooge" as a synonym for miser — and has been adapted countless times for stage, film, television and opera, including versions featuring the Muppets and Mickey Mouse.
