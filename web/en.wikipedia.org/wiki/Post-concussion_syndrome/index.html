Post-concussion syndrome

Post-concussion syndrome (PCS) is a set of symptoms that may continue for weeks, months, or occasionally years after a concussion. Symptoms may include headaches, dizziness, fatigue, irritability, anxiety, insomnia, loss of concentration and memory, and sensitivity to noise and light. Estimates of how many concussion patients develop PCS vary widely, from around 10% to considerably higher in some studies.

Causes and diagnosis

The cause of PCS is debated: it may involve microscopic structural damage to the brain, disturbances in neurotransmitter systems, psychological factors, or a combination of these. There is no single definitive test; diagnosis rests on the history of head injury and the symptom pattern, and the condition appears in both the DSM and ICD classifications with somewhat different criteria.

Treatment

Treatment is mainly supportive, addressing individual symptoms such as headache and sleep disturbance, together with education and reassurance. Most patients recover within weeks to a few months. Early light activity and gradual return to normal routines are generally encouraged rather than prolonged strict rest. Risk factors for a prolonged course include pre-existing anxiety or depression, prior concussions, and the severity of early symptoms rather than the severity of the initial injury itself.
