Quay

A quay is a structure on the shore of a harbour or on the bank of a river or canal where ships may dock to load and unload cargo or passengers. It is usually a reinforced bank or platform of concrete, stone, or metal, built parallel to the shoreline, distinguishing it from a pier or jetty, which extends out into the water.

Characteristics

Quays are typically equipped with bollards for mooring, cranes and other cargo-handling equipment, and adjacent warehousing or transit sheds. The length of berthing frontage along a quay is sometimes measured in "quay length". In modern container terminals, quays carry ship-to-shore gantry cranes on rails.

Etymology

The word derives from the Old North French cai, of Celtic origin, originally referring to a sandbank. The older spelling "key" survives in some place names, and the word has become common in harbour place names such as Circular Quay in Sydney and Clarke Quay in Singapore.
