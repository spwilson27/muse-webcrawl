Michael Walzer

Born: 3 March 1935, New York City
Field: Political theory
Institutions: Harvard; Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton
Notable works: Just and Unjust Wars (1977); Spheres of Justice (1983)

Michael Laban Walzer (born 3 March 1935) is an American political theorist and public intellectual, professor emeritus at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. A longtime co-editor of the democratic socialist journal Dissent, he is known for reviving just war theory and for a pluralist account of distributive justice.

Just and Unjust Wars

Walzer's Just and Unjust Wars (1977) restated the classical just war tradition for the nuclear age, distinguishing justice in going to war (jus ad bellum) from justice in its conduct (jus in bello). Responding to the Vietnam War, it defended non-combatant immunity, criticised area bombing, and discussed "supreme emergency" and the problem of "dirty hands" — the idea that political leaders may have to do wrong to do right. The book remains the standard modern treatment and is taught in military academies.

Spheres of Justice and communitarianism

In Spheres of Justice (1983) Walzer argued that different social goods — money, office, education, health care, political power — should be distributed according to different criteria internal to their own "spheres", and that injustice arises when one good, above all wealth, dominates others. This "complex equality" formed part of the communitarian response to John Rawls's liberalism. Other works include Interpretation and Social Criticism, On Toleration, Exodus and Revolution, and The Jewish Political Tradition, which he co-edited.
