Panhandle

A panhandle (also, in some countries, a salient) is an elongated, narrow projection of a territory — such as a U.S. state or a country — that is situated so that residents of the panhandle are often more culturally and geographically connected to neighboring regions than to the main body of their own jurisdiction. The term derives from the handle of a pan, with the main territory as the pan's body.

United States

The United States has numerous state panhandles, most of them products of the straight-line borders drawn in the 18th and 19th centuries:

  • Alaska Panhandle — the long southeastern strip of Alaska along the Pacific coast.
  • Florida Panhandle — the northwestern part of Florida, bordering Alabama and Georgia, culturally closer to the Deep South than to peninsular Florida.
  • Oklahoma Panhandle — the westernmost strip of Oklahoma, once "No Man's Land," acquired its shape from the Missouri Compromise and the annexation of Texas.
  • Texas Panhandle — the northernmost 26 counties of Texas, anchored by Amarillo.
  • Idaho Panhandle — the northern ten counties of Idaho.
  • West Virginia has two panhandles: the Northern Panhandle along the Ohio River and the Eastern Panhandle.
  • Maryland, Nebraska, and Connecticut (the "Connecticut Panhandle" in Fairfield County) also have panhandle regions.

Elsewhere

At the international level, elongated territorial projections are usually called salients. Well-known examples include the Wakhan Corridor of Afghanistan, the Caprivi Strip of Namibia, and the "Chicken's Neck" (Siliguri Corridor) of India. Within other countries, comparable features include the Misiones Province of Argentina and the "panhandles" of several Canadian provinces and Australian states in colloquial usage.

Causes

Panhandles typically result from historical boundary compromises, the drawing of borders along lines of latitude and longitude, river boundaries, or access to a resource or port — for example, a strip of land giving an otherwise landlocked area reach to a coastline or navigable river.
