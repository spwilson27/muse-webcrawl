Hexamethylenediamine

FormulaC6H16N2
Molar mass116.21 g/mol
AppearanceColourless solid
Melting point39–42 °C
Boiling point205 °C

Hexamethylenediamine (HMDA, systematically hexane-1,6-diamine) is an organic compound with the formula H2N(CH2)6NH2. It is a colourless, hygroscopic solid with a strong amine odour. The molecule consists of a hexamethylene hydrocarbon chain terminated with two amine groups.

Production

Hexamethylenediamine is produced industrially by the hydrogenation of adiponitrile over metal catalysts, typically cobalt or iron, in the presence of ammonia. It was first reported by Theodor Curtius in the early 20th century. Annual production is on the order of a million tonnes.

Uses

The overwhelmingly dominant use of HMDA is as a monomer, together with adipic acid, in the production of nylon 66, one of the most important engineering polymers. It is also used as a cross-linking agent in epoxy resins, in polyurethanes and polyamides, and as a building block for the diisocyanate HDI (hexamethylene diisocyanate) used in coatings.

Safety

Hexamethylenediamine is corrosive and moderately toxic; exposure can cause burns to skin and eyes and irritation of the respiratory tract.
