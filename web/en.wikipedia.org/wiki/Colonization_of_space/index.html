Colonization of space

Space colonization (also called space settlement) is the hypothetical permanent settlement and exploitation of natural resources at celestial objects other than Earth. As such it is a form of human presence in space, beyond the scope of human spaceflight and the operation of space outposts. Hypothetical locations include the Moon, Mars, asteroids, and free-floating space habitats.

The primary arguments for space colonization include the long-term survival of human civilization and terrestrial life in the event of a planetary-scale disaster, the availability of additional resources in space that could enable the expansion of human society, and the pursuit of scientific knowledge.

History of the idea

Early advocates included rocket pioneer Konstantin Tsiolkovsky, who wrote that humanity would eventually colonize the Milky Way. In the 1970s, Gerard K. O'Neill popularized large rotating orbital habitats in The High Frontier. Advocacy organizations such as the National Space Society and the Mars Society have promoted settlement goals.

Locations

The Moon

The Moon is the closest celestial body and has been proposed for a permanent base, with polar water ice deposits usable for propellant and life support.

Mars

Colonization of Mars is a major focus of long-term planning, owing to its day length similar to Earth's, an atmosphere of carbon dioxide, and accessible water ice.

Free space

Rotating habitats such as the O'Neill cylinder could provide artificial gravity and be positioned at Lagrange points or in solar orbit.

Other locations

Proposals have also considered Mercury, Venus (including floating habitats in its upper atmosphere), the moons of Jupiter and Saturn such as Europa and Titan, and near-Earth asteroids.

Challenges

  • Radiation exposure outside Earth's magnetosphere
  • Health effects of low gravity
  • Life-support closure and food production
  • Psychological effects of isolation
  • Enormous transportation costs
  • Legal frameworks, including the Outer Space Treaty of 1967

Objections and ethics

Critics question the expense, the danger, and the priority relative to problems on Earth. The discovery of possible extraterrestrial life raises planetary protection concerns about contaminating other worlds. Some ethicists argue colonization replicates patterns of terrestrial colonialism.
