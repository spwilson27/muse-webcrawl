Delray Beach, Florida

Delray Beach is a city in Palm Beach County, Florida, United States, on the Atlantic coast between Boca Raton and Boynton Beach. Its population was about 66,846 at the 2020 census. The city calls itself the "Village by the Sea" and is known for its beaches, its revitalised downtown along Atlantic Avenue, and its arts district.

History

The area was settled in the 1880s and 1890s, when William S. Linton, a Michigan congressman and land promoter, bought land and laid out a town originally named Linton. After his venture faltered, the settlement was renamed Delray — reportedly after the Detroit neighbourhood of Delray — and incorporated in 1911; it merged with Delray Beach (the beachside community) in 1927. Japanese settlers founded the nearby Yamato Colony farming settlement in 1905; its history is preserved at the Morikami Museum and Japanese Gardens.

Geography and attractions

Delray Beach lies in southeast Florida, crossed by the Intracoastal Waterway. Attractions include the downtown Pineapple Grove Arts District, the Old School Square cultural centre, two miles of public beach, and the Wakodahatchee Wetlands, a popular birdwatching site built on reclaimed wastewater land. The city also hosts the Delray Beach Open, an ATP Tour tennis tournament, and the Delray Beach Tennis Center.
