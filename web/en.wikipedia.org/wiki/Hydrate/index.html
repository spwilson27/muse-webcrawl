Hydrate

In chemistry, a hydrate is a substance that contains water or its constituent elements incorporated into its structure. In inorganic chemistry, hydrates are salts containing a definite number of water molecules bound within their crystal lattice — water of crystallisation — indicated in formulae such as CuSO4·5H2O (copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate).

Types

Organic hydrates form when water adds across a double bond, for example the hydrate of an aldehyde or ketone in aqueous solution. Gas hydrates (clathrate hydrates) are crystalline solids in which gas molecules such as methane are trapped in cages of water ice, abundant in deep-sea sediments and permafrost.

Stability

Many hydrates lose water on heating or in dry air (efflorescence) and some anhydrous salts absorb water from air (deliquescence). The number of waters per formula unit is named with prefixes mono-, di-, tri-, penta-, and so on.
