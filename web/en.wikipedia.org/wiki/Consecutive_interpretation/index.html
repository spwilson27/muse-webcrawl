Field: Translation and interpreting

Consecutive interpretation

Consecutive interpretation is a mode of interpreting in which the interpreter listens to a segment of speech, often taking structured notes, and then renders it into the target language when the speaker pauses. The speaker and interpreter alternate, so the delivery takes roughly twice as long as in simultaneous interpretation.

Technique

Working interpreters develop a personal system of note-taking based on symbols, abbreviations, and the logical structure of the speech rather than word-for-word transcription. Trained interpreters can render segments of ten minutes or more. Memory, analysis, and note-taking are the three pillars of training in the consecutive mode.

Uses

Consecutive interpretation is common in court proceedings, medical consultations, business meetings, press conferences, and high-level diplomacy, where no booth equipment is available or where precision and visibility of the interpreter matter. It was the dominant mode at the League of Nations and at the Nuremberg trials before simultaneous interpretation became standard at large conferences.
