ABL1 is a proto-oncogene located on chromosome 9 in humans that encodes a non-receptor tyrosine kinase. It is the human homolog of the v-abl oncogene carried by the Abelson murine leukemia virus, from which it takes its name. The Abl1 protein shuttles between the cytoplasm and nucleus and participates in cell division, adhesion, differentiation, and responses to DNA damage and stress.

BCR-ABL and leukemia

ABL1 is famous for its role in chronic myeloid leukemia (CML). A reciprocal translocation between chromosomes 9 and 22—the Philadelphia chromosome—fuses ABL1 to the BCR gene, producing the BCR-ABL fusion protein, a constitutively active tyrosine kinase that drives uncontrolled growth of white blood cells. The same fusion occurs in some cases of acute lymphoblastic leukemia and acute myeloid leukemia.

Therapy

The BCR-ABL fusion became the target of imatinib (Gleevec), the first successful targeted cancer therapy, approved in 2001; later generations of inhibitors such as dasatinib, nilotinib, and ponatinib were developed to treat resistant mutations, most notably the T315I "gatekeeper" mutation.
