The 2018 lower Puna eruption was a volcanic eruption on the island of Hawaiʻi, on Kīlauea volcano's East Rift Zone, that began on 3 May 2018. It followed the collapse of the Puʻu ʻŌʻō crater floor on 30 April and a magnitude 6.9 earthquake — the largest in Hawaii since 1975 — on 4 May. Over the following months, 24 fissures opened in and near the Leilani Estates and Lanipuna Gardens subdivisions, destroying more than 700 homes and displacing thousands of residents in the costliest eruption in Kīlauea's recorded history.

Course of the eruption

The first fissures opened in Leilani Estates on 3 May, emitting lava spatter, steam, and high levels of sulfur dioxide. Activity concentrated from late May at Fissure 8, which built a cone more than 50 metres high and fed a channelised ʻaʻā flow that reached the ocean at Kapoho on 3 June, filling Kapoho Bay and destroying hundreds of homes in Kapoho Beach Lots and Vacationland Hawaii. Simultaneously, the summit of Kīlauea subsided: the lava lake in Halemaʻumaʻu drained and the caldera floor collapsed in dozens of near-daily events, deepening the crater by more than 500 metres, as magma drained to feed the lower-rift eruption.

Impact

Lava covered about 13.7 square miles (35.5 km²) and added roughly 875 acres of new land along the coast. The flows destroyed the Puna Geothermal Venture plant's access and wells, cut Highway 130's lower section, and buried the popular Ahalanui hot pond and Kapoho tidepools. Remarkably, no deaths were directly caused; one man was seriously injured by a lava bomb. Federal disaster declarations were issued, and evacuees filled shelters for months.

End of the eruption

Effusion from Fissure 8 waned rapidly in early August 2018 and had essentially stopped by mid-August; the USGS lowered alert levels in September. The eruption ended the 35-year-long Puʻu ʻŌʻō eruption and reshaped Kīlauea's summit, which refilled with a new lava lake in December 2020.
