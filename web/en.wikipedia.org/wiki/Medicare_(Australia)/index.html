Medicare (Australia)

PredecessorMedibank
Administered byServices Australia
FundingMedicare levy (2%), general taxation

Medicare is the publicly funded universal health care insurance scheme in Australia, operated by the national government. Founded with passage of the Health Legislation Amendment Act 1983 and coming into effect on 1 February 1984, Medicare is the primary way Australian citizens and permanent residents access most health care, subsidizing treatment by medical practitioners, public hospital care, and listed services in the Medicare Benefits Schedule (MBS).

History

Medicare succeeded the Whitlam government's Medibank scheme (1975), which the Fraser government had wound back. The Hawke government re-established universal coverage as Medicare in 1984, funded in part by a Medicare levy on taxable income, currently 2 percent, supplemented by general revenue. A related scheme, the Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme, subsidizes prescription medicines.

Operation

Enrolled residents hold a Medicare card. When a doctor "bulk bills", Medicare pays the benefit directly and the patient pays nothing; otherwise patients claim a rebate covering all or part of the schedule fee. Treatment in public hospitals is free for Medicare patients. Private health insurance operates alongside Medicare for private hospital and extras cover. Debates continue over bulk-billing rates, out-of-pocket costs, and the scheme's long-term sustainability.
