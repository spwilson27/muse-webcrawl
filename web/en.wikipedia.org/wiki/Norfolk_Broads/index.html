Norfolk Broads

The Norfolk Broads, usually referred to simply as the Broads, are a network of mostly navigable rivers and lakes in the English counties of Norfolk and Suffolk. The region, protected as The Broads National Park, covers about 303 square kilometres and features over 200 kilometres of navigable waterways. The "broads" themselves are shallow lakes formed by the flooding of medieval peat excavations.

Geography and history

The Broads lie along the rivers Bure, Yare and Waveney and their tributaries, including the Ant, Thurne and Chet. For centuries the lakes were thought to be natural features, but research in the 1950s by Joyce Lambert and others showed they are artificial: vast pits dug for peat between the 9th and 14th centuries, when sea levels rose and flooded the workings. The region became a popular boating holiday destination in the Victorian era, promoted by holiday companies such as Hoseasons from the 20th century.

Governance

Since 1989 the area has been managed by the Broads Authority, a special statutory body established by the Norfolk and Suffolk Broads Act 1988, with duties to conserve the natural beauty of the Broads, promote public enjoyment and protect the interests of navigation. The Broads has status equivalent to a national park, though it is not technically one under the same legislation.

Wildlife

The Broads are Britain's largest protected wetland and a haven for wildlife, including the rare swallowtail butterfly, bitterns, marsh harriers, otters and Norfolk hawker dragonflies. Many areas are designated Sites of Special Scientific Interest and form part of the Broadland Ramsar site and Special Protection Area.
