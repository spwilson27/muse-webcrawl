Omega Force (Japanese: ω-Force) is a Japanese video game development studio and a division of Koei Tecmo Games. Founded in 1996, it is best known as the creator of the Warriors (Japanese: Musou) series of hack-and-slash action games, whose defining formula places a single powerful hero against thousands of enemy soldiers on sprawling battlefields.

History

Omega Force's breakthrough came with Dynasty Warriors 2 (2000) for the PlayStation 2, which transformed the original one-on-one fighting game into a large-scale battlefield action title set in China's Three Kingdoms period. The formula proved enormously successful in Japan and abroad, generating numerous sequels and spin-offs.

Franchises

  • Dynasty Warriors — Three Kingdoms China; the flagship series (1997–present).
  • Samurai Warriors — Sengoku-period Japan (2004–present).
  • Warriors Orochi — crossover of the two series.
  • Licensed "Musou" games — collaborations applying the formula to other properties, including Hyrule Warriors (The Legend of Zelda) with Nintendo, Fire Emblem Warriors, One Piece: Pirate Warriors, Dragon Quest Heroes, Persona 5 Strikers, and Berserk and the Band of the Hawk.
  • Other titles include the hunting-action series Toukiden, Attack on Titan games, and Wild Hearts (2023), a monster-hunting game developed with Electronic Arts.

Style and influence

The studio's games are characterised by huge enemy counts, crowd-clearing "Musou" attacks, character rosters that can exceed eighty playable warriors, and a blend of arcade action with light strategy in the management of a battlefield. The "one vs. many" template Omega Force created became a recognised genre in its own right, widely imitated by other developers.
