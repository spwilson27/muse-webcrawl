The Luna programme (Russian: Луна, "Moon") was a series of robotic spacecraft missions sent to the Moon by the Soviet Union between 1958 and 1976. Fifteen missions were successful, each designed as either an orbiter or a lander, and they accomplished many firsts in space exploration. The programme was a central Soviet effort in the Space Race with the United States.

Firsts

Luna 1 (1959) became the first spacecraft to escape Earth's gravity and pass near the Moon, entering orbit around the Sun. Luna 2 struck the lunar surface in September 1959, the first human-made object to reach another celestial body. Luna 3 photographed the far side of the Moon for the first time in October 1959. Luna 9 achieved the first soft landing in February 1966 and transmitted the first photographs from the surface, while Luna 10 became the Moon's first artificial satellite two months later.

Sample return and rovers

Three missions — Luna 16 (1970), Luna 20 (1972) and Luna 24 (1976) — drilled into the lunar regolith and returned a total of about 300 grams of Moon rock to Earth robotically. Two missions delivered Lunokhod rovers: Lunokhod 1, carried by Luna 17 in 1970, was the first rover to operate on another world, travelling 10.5 km over ten months; Lunokhod 2 (Luna 21, 1973) covered roughly 42 km.

Later years

Although a crewed Soviet lunar programme was cancelled, the robotic Luna missions continued until 1976. After a gap of nearly half a century, Russia revived the name with Luna 25, launched in August 2023; the lander crashed into the Moon during a pre-landing manoeuvre, ending the mission. The programme's orbiters, landers and sample returns remain landmark achievements of uncrewed exploration.
