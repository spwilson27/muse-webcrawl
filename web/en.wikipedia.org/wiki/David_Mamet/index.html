David Alan Mamet (born 30 November 1947) is an American playwright, screenwriter, film director and author. He won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama in 1984 for Glengarry Glen Ross, a play about desperate real-estate salesmen that he later adapted into the acclaimed 1992 film.

Style

Mamet is famous for his distinctive dialogue — terse, profane, overlapping exchanges with interrupted sentences, sometimes called "Mamet speak" — and for plays such as American Buffalo (1975), Sexual Perversity in Chicago (1974) and Speed-the-Plow (1988). As a screenwriter he received Academy Award nominations for The Verdict (1982) and Wag the Dog (1997), and he directed films including House of Games (1987), Things Change (1988), The Spanish Prisoner (1997) and State and Main (2000). He created the television series The Unit and co-founded the Atlantic Theater Company in New York.

Views

Mamet has also written essays and books on theatre, politics and culture, including On Directing Film and True and False. In 2008 he publicly announced a shift from liberal to conservative political views, which has informed much of his later non-fiction.
