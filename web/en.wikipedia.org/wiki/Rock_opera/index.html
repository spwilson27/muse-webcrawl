Rock opera

Rock opera
Stylistic originsRock music, opera, concept album, musical theatre
Cultural originsLate 1960s, United Kingdom and United States
Key worksTommy (1969), Jesus Christ Superstar (1970), The Wall (1979)

A rock opera is a collection of rock songs that tell a coherent story, with the songs' lyrics relating to a common plot and characters. Unlike a typical rock album, a rock opera is composed as a dramatic narrative—often with different singers (or vocal characters) taking roles—and may be performed on stage with actors, sets and costumes, or released solely as an audio recording. Rock operas are a developed form of the concept album.

Rock operas such as The Who's Tommy moved from album to film and full stage productions.

History

Early candidates for the first rock opera include The Pretty Things' S.F. Sorrow (1968), a song cycle tracing a protagonist's life. The form's breakthrough came with The Who's Tommy (1969), written mainly by Pete Townshend, about a "deaf, dumb and blind" boy who becomes a pinball champion and messianic figure; it sold millions and was adapted into a 1975 film and a Broadway musical. Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice's Jesus Christ Superstar (1970) began as a concept album before becoming a long-running stage musical retelling the last weeks of Jesus's life through rock music. Other celebrated examples include Pink Floyd's The Wall (1979), Meat Loaf's Bat Out of Hell (1977), The Kinks' Arthur (1969), Queensrÿche's Operation: Mindcrime (1988), Green Day's American Idiot (2004) and My Chemical Romance's The Black Parade (2006).

Characteristics

Rock operas typically feature recurring musical themes (leitmotifs), narration or linking passages, characters sung by specific voices, and large-scale structures closer to the musical than to a loose thematic album. The term is sometimes applied loosely to any strongly narrative concept album; purists reserve it for works conceived with a dramatic plot. The genre overlaps with the "rock musical"—stage works written directly for theatre, such as Hair (1967), which predates most recorded rock operas.
