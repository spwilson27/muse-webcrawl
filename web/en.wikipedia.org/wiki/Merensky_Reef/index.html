Merensky Reef

The Merensky Reef is a layer of igneous rock in the Bushveld Igneous Complex of South Africa, rich in platinum group metals (PGMs). It is one of the world's principal sources of platinum, palladium, rhodium, ruthenium and iridium, as well as a significant source of nickel, copper, cobalt and gold.

Geology

The reef is a thin, persistent layer — typically between about 30 cm and a few metres thick — within the Upper Critical Zone of the Rustenburg Layered Suite. It consists largely of pyroxenite and anorthosite with chromite stringers, and the platinum group minerals occur associated with base-metal sulphides such as pentlandite, chalcopyrite and pyrrhotite.

Discovery and mining

The reef is named after Hans Merensky, the geologist and prospector who in 1924 traced platinum-bearing float near Lydenberg (Mashishing) back to its source, opening the eastern limb of the Bushveld to mining. The reef is mined extensively around Rustenburg and on both the western and eastern limbs of the complex, alongside the similar UG2 chromitite layer. Together they underpin South Africa's dominant position in world platinum production.
