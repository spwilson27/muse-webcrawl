District of Columbia home rule

The District of Columbia home rule is the District's ability to govern local affairs, granted by the United States Congress under the District of Columbia Home Rule Act of 1973. The act established an elected mayor and a 13-member Council of the District of Columbia, though Congress retains ultimate authority over the District under the Constitution's District Clause.

D.C. home rule
Legal basis: Home Rule Act, 1973
Government: Mayor and Council of D.C.

History

Washington, D.C. had elected governments in the 19th century, but after 1874 Congress governed through appointed commissioners for nearly a century. Residents gained a non-voting House delegate in 1971 and presidential electors via the 23rd Amendment (1961).

The Home Rule Act

Signed by President Richard Nixon on 24 December 1973, the act allowed elections in 1974; Walter Washington became the first elected mayor under home rule. Congress may review and annul D.C. legislation and controls the District's budget process and local taxing of federal property, leading to recurring conflicts — for example over abortion funding, marijuana legalisation and gun laws.

Statehood debate

Home rule falls short of full self-government: D.C. residents lack voting representation in Congress, fuelling the D.C. statehood movement, which proposes admitting the residential areas as the 51st state while retaining a small federal district.
