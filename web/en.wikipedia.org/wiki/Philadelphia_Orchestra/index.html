Philadelphia Orchestra

The Philadelphia Orchestra is an American symphony orchestra, based in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. One of the "Big Five" American orchestras, the orchestra is founded in 1900, and its principal concert hall is Verizon Hall at the Kimmel Center for the Performing Arts, where the orchestra performs for its subscription season. Summer home since 1976 is the Mann Center for the Performing Arts. It is also the resident orchestra of the Saratoga Performing Arts Center in Saratoga Springs, New York, during the summer.

From its founding until 2001, the Philadelphia Orchestra gave concerts at the Academy of Music. The orchestra continues to own the Academy, which it leases for opera and ballet performances. The orchestra is famous for the lush "Philadelphia Sound," particularly cultivated under Leopold Stokowski and Eugene Ormandy.

History

The orchestra was founded in 1900 by Fritz Scheel, who served as its first conductor until his death in 1907. He was succeeded by Carl Pohlig and then, in 1912, by Leopold Stokowski, whose flamboyant leadership made the orchestra internationally famous through recordings, broadcasts, and tours. Stokowski led the orchestra in the film Fantasia (1940) and introduced many American premieres.

Eugene Ormandy became music director in 1938 and held the post for 42 years, the longest tenure of any major American orchestra. Under Ormandy the orchestra made hundreds of recordings and in 1973 became the first American orchestra to tour the People's Republic of China. Riccardo Muti (1980–1992) and Wolfgang Sawallisch (1993–2003) followed; Christoph Eschenbach and Charles Dutoit led in the 2000s. Yannick Nézet-Séguin became music director in 2012; his contract has been extended into the late 2020s, and in 2023 his title expanded to music and artistic director.

Recording and firsts

The orchestra was a pioneer in recording and broadcasting: it made the first commercial electrical recordings (1925), the first orchestral radio broadcasts, and early long-playing records with Columbia. It gave the American premieres of works including Mahler's Eighth Symphony and Rachmaninoff's Symphonic Dances, which it premiered in 1941. In 2011 the orchestra filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection, emerging in 2012 after restructuring.
