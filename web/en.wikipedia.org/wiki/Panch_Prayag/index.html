Panch Prayag

Panch Prayag (Sanskrit: पञ्चप्रयाग, "five confluences") is the expression used for the five sacred river confluences in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand, India, where tributaries merge to form the Ganges. In Hindu tradition a prayag is a place of confluence regarded as especially holy, and pilgrims traditionally visit the five prayags in sequence from downstream to upstream or the reverse along the pilgrimage route.

The five confluences

  • Devprayag — the Bhagirathi meets the Alaknanda; below this point the combined river takes the name Ganga (Ganges).
  • Rudraprayag — the Mandakini meets the Alaknanda.
  • Karnaprayag — the Pindar meets the Alaknanda.
  • Nandaprayag — the Nandakini meets the Alaknanda.
  • Vishnuprayag — the Dhauliganga meets the Alaknanda, near Joshimath.

Each confluence has temples and bathing ghats, and the towns that grew around them serve as staging points on the Char Dham pilgrimage routes to Badrinath and Kedarnath. The Alaknanda, considered the principal source stream of the Ganges in hydrological length, collects its tributaries one by one at these prayags.
