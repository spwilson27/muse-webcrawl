Ceoltóirí Chualann (pronounced [ˈcoːlˠt̪ˠoːɾʲiː ˈxuəlˠən̪ˠ], "the musicians of Cualann") was an Irish traditional music ensemble founded by composer Seán Ó Riada in 1960. The group is widely credited with transforming the performance of Irish traditional music by arranging dance tunes and airs for a mixed ensemble of folk instruments, played with formal discipline and attention to ensemble texture, rather than as solo or unison session playing.

History

Ó Riada formed the group for the music of the play The Song of the Anvil at the Abbey Theatre in Dublin, drawing together musicians he had met through Raidió Éireann, where he was music director. The ensemble — typically including fiddles, flutes, uilleann pipes, tin whistles, accordion, bodhrán, bones and Ó Riada himself on harpsichord and bodhrán — performed widely, made influential recordings, and provided the music for the landmark documentary film Mise Éire (1959 score by Ó Riada) and its successor Saoirse?.

Legacy

When Ceoltóirí Chualann disbanded at the end of the 1960s, several of its members — Paddy Moloney, Seán Keane, Martin Fay, Michael Tubridy and Peadar Mercier among them — went on to form The Chieftains, who carried Ó Riada's ensemble approach to international fame. The group's recordings, including The Playboy of the Western World (1961) and Ceol na nUasal, remain landmarks of the Irish folk revival.
