Castelo Branco

Castelo Branco (Portuguese pronunciation: [kɐʃˈtɛlu ˈβɾɐ̃ku]) is a city and municipality in central Portugal, capital of the district of the same name in the Centro region, near the border with Spain. The municipality has a population of around 56,000, of whom about 35,000 live in the city itself.

History

The city takes its name — "white castle" — from a castle attributed to the Knights Templar, who received the lands in the early 13th century. Castelo Branco received its first charter (foral) in 1213–1215 under Pedro Alvito, Master of the Order of the Temple. The town grew as a frontier settlement guarding the routes between the Tagus valley and the Spanish border. In the 16th and 17th centuries it was a seat of bishops of Guarda, whose palace and gardens — the Jardim do Paço Episcopal, with its famous baroque staircases and statues of the kings of Portugal — remain its best-known monument. The city was sacked during the Peninsular War. It became a district capital in 1835.

Economy and culture

Castelo Branco is a service and education centre for the Beira Baixa sub-region, home to a polytechnic institute, and known for its embroidered bedspreads (colchas de Castelo Branco), worked in silk on linen with oriental-inspired motifs, and for regional cheeses bearing the name of the city. Landmarks include the Templar castle ruins, the Episcopal Palace (now the Francisco Tavares Proença Júnior Museum), the Misericórdia church, and the Cargalegas viewpoint over the plain.

Climate

The city has a hot-summer Mediterranean climate, with some of the hottest summer temperatures in Portugal, frequently exceeding 40 °C, and cool, wetter winters.
