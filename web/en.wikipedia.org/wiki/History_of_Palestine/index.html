The history of Palestine is the study of the past in the region of Palestine, roughly corresponding to the territory between the Mediterranean Sea and the Jordan River. It is a region of immense religious and historical significance, sacred to Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.

Prehistory and the ancient period

Human settlement in the region dates to the Paleolithic era. By the Bronze Age, the land was inhabited by the Canaanites, whose city-states traded with Egypt and Mesopotamia. Around the 12th century BCE the Philistines settled the coastal plain, while the Israelites established kingdoms in the highlands. The region came successively under Egyptian, Assyrian, Babylonian, and Persian rule.

Hellenistic and Roman periods

Alexander the Great conquered the region in 332 BCE, after which it was contested by the Ptolemaic and Seleucid empires. The Hasmonean dynasty briefly restored Jewish independence before Rome intervened in 63 BCE. Under Herod the Great the region prospered, but revolts against Roman rule led to the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE and the Bar Kokhba revolt of 132–136 CE, after which the province was renamed Syria Palaestina.

Byzantine and early Islamic periods

Under Byzantine rule Palestine became a center of Christian pilgrimage. Muslim forces conquered the region in the 630s CE, and Jerusalem came under Islamic rule, marked by the construction of the Dome of the Rock in 691 CE. The area was governed by the Umayyad, Abbasid, and Fatimid caliphates.

Crusades and later Islamic rule

The Crusaders established the Kingdom of Jerusalem in 1099, which lasted, with interruptions, until 1291. Saladin recaptured Jerusalem in 1187. The Mamluks ruled from the 13th century until the Ottoman conquest of 1516–17.

Ottoman and British periods

Ottoman rule lasted four centuries. In the late 19th century, Zionist immigration began, seeking a Jewish homeland. Britain captured Palestine during the First World War and issued the Balfour Declaration in 1917. The League of Nations granted Britain the Mandate for Palestine in 1922.

1948 and after

The 1947 UN Partition Plan proposed two states; war followed the declaration of the State of Israel in 1948, and more than 700,000 Palestinians fled or were expelled in what Palestinians call the Nakba. Jordan annexed the West Bank and Egypt administered Gaza. In the 1967 Six-Day War, Israel occupied the West Bank, Gaza Strip, and East Jerusalem. The Oslo Accords of the 1990s created the Palestinian Authority, but a final settlement remains unresolved.
