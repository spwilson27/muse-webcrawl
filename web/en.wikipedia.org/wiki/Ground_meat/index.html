Ground meat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Ground beef, the most common ground meat in many countries

Ground meat, called mince or minced meat outside North America, is meat finely chopped by a meat grinder or a chopping knife. A common type of ground meat is ground beef, but many other types of meat are prepared in this way, including pork, veal, lamb, goat meat, and poultry.

Production

Ground meat is produced by passing cuts and trimmings of meat through a grinder fitted with a perforated plate; the size of the holes determines the coarseness of the grind. In many countries, the fat content of ground meat is regulated by law, and ground products may be required to accurately state the proportions of lean meat and fat. Meat ground at a butcher's counter from a single cut is generally distinguishable from industrially produced ground meat blended from many animals.

Dishes

Ground meat is the basis of many dishes worldwide, including hamburgers, meatballs, meatloaf, Bolognese sauce, kofta, kebabs, sausages, and fillings for dumplings, empanadas, and stuffed vegetables.

Food safety

Grinding distributes any surface bacteria throughout the product, so ground meat carries a higher risk of foodborne illness than intact cuts if undercooked. Health authorities recommend cooking ground meats to specified internal temperatures — commonly 71 °C (160 °F) for ground beef — and refrigerating promptly. Contamination incidents involving E. coli O157:H7 and Salmonella have driven stricter testing and traceability requirements in many jurisdictions.
