Whitman, Massachusetts

Whitman is a town in Plymouth County, Massachusetts, United States. It is notable as the place where the chocolate chip cookie was invented by Ruth Graves Wakefield in 1938. The population was 15,121 at the 2020 census. Whitman is a largely residential suburb about 20 miles (32 km) south of Boston, served by the MBTA Commuter Rail's Kingston Line.

History

The area was first settled by Europeans in 1670 as part of Bridgewater, later becoming part of Abington. In 1875 the southern parish split off as the town of South Abington, renamed Whitman in 1886 — the new name is commonly said to honour Augustus Whitman, a prominent resident. Shoe manufacturing drove its 19th-century growth, and rail connections tied it to Boston and Brockton.

Toll House cookies

In 1930 Ruth and Kenneth Wakefield bought a tourist lodge on Bedford Street which they named the Toll House Inn. Around 1938 Ruth Wakefield added chopped semi-sweet Nestlé chocolate to a butter cookie recipe, expecting it to melt — instead it held its shape, creating the chocolate chip cookie. The recipe, printed on Nestlé packaging as the Toll House cookie, became an American icon; the inn burned in 1984, and a sign marks the site. The town is also part of the Whitman-Hanson Regional School District, shared with neighbouring Hanson.
