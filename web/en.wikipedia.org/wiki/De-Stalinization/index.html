De-Stalinization

De-Stalinization was the process of political reform in the Soviet Union and the Eastern Bloc after the death of Joseph Stalin in March 1953, consisting of reversing his cult of personality, rehabilitating victims of his purges, curbing the secret police, and releasing millions from the Gulag.

De-Stalinization
Period: 1953–1964 (Khrushchev era)
Key event: Secret Speech, 25 Feb 1956
Leader: Nikita Khrushchev
Outcome: Khrushchev Thaw, unrest in Poland and Hungary 1956

The Secret Speech

At a closed session of the 20th Congress of the Communist Party on 25 February 1956, Nikita Khrushchev delivered "On the Cult of Personality and Its Consequences", denouncing Stalin's crimes, the Great Purge, deportations and the cult around him. The speech, though secret, was read in party organisations and leaked abroad, stunning communists worldwide.

Measures

An amnesty and review commissions freed Gulag prisoners; Lavrenty Beria was arrested and executed in 1953; places and monuments named for Stalin were renamed (Stalingrad became Volgograd in 1961, and Stalin's body was removed from Lenin's Mausoleum that year); censorship eased in the "Thaw".

Consequences

De-Stalinization helped trigger the Polish October and Hungarian Revolution of 1956, deepened the Sino-Soviet split (Mao viewed it as revisionism), and was partly rolled back under Brezhnev. A renewed campaign accompanied Gorbachev's glasnost in the late 1980s.
