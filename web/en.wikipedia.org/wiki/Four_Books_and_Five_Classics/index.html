Four Books and Five Classics

The Four Books and Five Classics (四書五經) are the authoritative texts of Confucianism in China, central to the education of scholar-officials and, for centuries, to the imperial examination system.

The Four Books

Selected and commented on by the Song-dynasty philosopher Zhu Xi, the Four Books are: the Great Learning, the Doctrine of the Mean, the Analects of Confucius, and the Mencius. They served as the core curriculum of Neo-Confucian learning and were memorised by examination candidates from the Yuan dynasty until the system's abolition in 1905.

The Five Classics

The Five Classics are older texts traditionally edited or compiled by Confucius: the Classic of Poetry (Shijing), the Book of Documents (Shujing), the Book of Rites (Liji), the I Ching (Yijing, Book of Changes), and the Spring and Autumn Annals (Chunqiu). A sixth classic, on music, was lost in antiquity.

Influence

Together the nine texts shaped Chinese philosophy, ethics, historiography and statecraft for two millennia, and were studied throughout East Asia, including Korea, Japan and Vietnam. Standard translations into English include those of James Legge.
