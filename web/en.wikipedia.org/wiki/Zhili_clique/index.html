Zhili clique (直隸系)

Active: c. 1916–1928

Named for: Zhili Province (present-day Hebei)

Leaders: Feng Guozhang, Cao Kun, Wu Peifu

Rivals: Anhui clique, Fengtian clique

Foreign backing: Britain and the United States (generally)

The Zhili clique (Chinese: 直隸系) was one of the major factions of warlords that emerged from the Beiyang Army in the Republic of China during the Warlord Era. It took its name from Zhili Province (roughly modern Hebei), its territorial base around Beijing and Tianjin.

History

The clique formed under Feng Guozhang as a counterweight to Duan Qirui's Anhui clique. After Feng's death, leadership passed to Cao Kun and the brilliant field commander Wu Peifu. The Zhili clique defeated the Anhui clique in the Zhili–Anhui War of 1920, and won the First Zhili–Fengtian War in 1922 against Zhang Zuolin. Cao Kun became President of the Republic in 1923 by openly bribing the National Assembly, damaging the clique's legitimacy.

Decline

In the Second Zhili–Fengtian War (1924), the defection of Feng Yuxiang — the "Christian General" — in the Beijing coup destroyed the Zhili position; Wu Peifu retreated south to the Yangtze. Remnants under Wu and later Sun Chuanfang resisted the Nationalists' Northern Expedition, but by 1928 the clique had ceased to exist as an organized force.
