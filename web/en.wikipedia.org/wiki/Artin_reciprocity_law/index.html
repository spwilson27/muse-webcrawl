The Artin reciprocity law, established by Emil Artin in a series of papers culminating in 1927, is the fundamental theorem of class field theory. It gives a precise correspondence between finite abelian extensions of a number field K and certain subgroups (norm groups) of generalised ideal class groups of K, realised by the Artin map.

Statement

Let L/K be a finite abelian extension of number fields with Galois group G. Let 𝔪 be a modulus of K divisible by all primes ramifying in L (for instance, the conductor of the extension). For each fractional ideal 𝔞 of K coprime to 𝔪, define the Artin symbol (𝔞, L/K) as the product of the Frobenius elements at the prime factors of 𝔞. The law asserts:

  • The Artin symbol depends only on the class of 𝔞 in the ray class group modulo 𝔪.
  • The resulting homomorphism from the ray class group onto G is surjective.
  • Its kernel is the image in the ray class group of the norm group NL/K(CL)—in ideal terms, the classes containing ideals that are norms from L.

Consequently the ray class group modulo the conductor, quotiented by the norm subgroup, is canonically isomorphic to G.

History

The law completed a programme running from Gauss's quadratic reciprocity through the work of Eisenstein, Kummer, Weber, and Hilbert. Takagi's existence theorem (1920) had shown that every generalised ideal class group defines an abelian extension, but the splitting of primes in that extension remained undescribed. Artin, guided by his study of L-functions and the analytic class number formula, found the map in 1923 and proved its well-definedness and surjectivity over the following years; Helmut Hasse, Philipp Furtwängler, and others contributed simplifications and extensions, including the treatment of infinite primes and the principal ideal theorem (proved by Furtwängler in 1930 using Artin's formalism).

Examples

For L = ℚ(i) over ℚ, the law recovers the characterisation of primes that are sums of two squares: an odd prime splits exactly when p ≡ 1 (mod 4). For cyclotomic fields ℚ(ζn), the Artin map sends an ideal (a) to the automorphism ζ ↦ ζa, directly generalising power reciprocity. Over a general base field, the law governs the splitting of primes in Hilbert class fields and ray class fields.

Reformulations

Claude Chevalley's idelic formulation recast the law as an isomorphism between the Galois group and a quotient of the idele class group, a language in which the theorem extends elegantly to function fields and local–global settings. This formulation underlies local class field theory and the reciprocity maps of the Langlands program.
