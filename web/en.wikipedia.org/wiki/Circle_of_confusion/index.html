Circle of confusion

In optics, a circle of confusion (CoC) is an optical spot caused by a cone of light rays from a lens not coming to a perfect focus when imaging a point source. It is the basis for calculating depth of field and depth of focus.

Circle of confusion
Field: Optics, photography
Related: Depth of field, bokeh, hyperfocal distance

Definition

The largest blur circle judged indistinguishable from a point — the acceptable circle of confusion — depends on format size, viewing distance and print size. For full-frame 35 mm photography a value around 0.029 mm is commonly used; smaller sensors use proportionally smaller values.

Applications

CoC limits are used in depth-of-field calculators and lens engravings. Out-of-focus circles produce the aesthetic quality known as bokeh.

History

The concept dates to 19th-century optics; standards were formalised by manufacturers such as Zeiss in the 20th century.
