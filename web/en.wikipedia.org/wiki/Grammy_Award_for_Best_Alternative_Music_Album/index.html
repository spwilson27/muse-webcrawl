Grammy Award for Best Alternative Music Album

The Grammy Award for Best Alternative Music Album is an award presented to recording artists for quality albums in the alternative genre at the Grammy Awards, a ceremony established in 1958 and originally called the Gramophone Awards. The category was introduced in 1991 as Best Alternative Music Performance, for recordings by alternative rock artists.

History

The first recipient was Sinéad O'Connor for I Do Not Want What I Haven't Got (1991); O'Connor famously refused the award. The category definition has been adjusted several times, and in 2023 a companion category, Best Alternative Music Performance, was introduced for individual tracks. Eligibility is generally based on albums containing a majority of newly recorded alternative material.

Recipients

Radiohead has won the award three times, for OK Computer, Kid A, and In Rainbows. Beck has won twice, as have The White Stripes. Other winners include R.E.M. (Out of Time), Nirvana (MTV Unplugged in New York), Björk, Coldplay, Arcade Fire (The Suburbs), Bon Iver, Gotye, St. Vincent, David Bowie (Blackstar), The National, Vampire Weekend, Tame Impala (Currents), Fiona Apple, and Wet Leg.
