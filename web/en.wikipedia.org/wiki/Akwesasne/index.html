Akwesasne (Mohawk: Ahkwesáhsne) is a Mohawk Nation territory that straddles the intersection of the international border between the United States and Canada and the provincial/state borders of Ontario, Quebec and New York. Radiating along the St. Lawrence River, it is one community divided by borders it predates.

History

The community was founded in 1755 by Mohawk families from Kahnawake who settled near the mission of St. Regis, and it long formed part of the Seven Nations of Canada. The international boundary drawn after the American Revolution and the War of 1812 split the territory between British North America (later Canada) and the United States. Akwesasne members retain cross-border rights recognised by the Jay Treaty of 1794.

Governance

Akwesasne is governed by three bodies: the Mohawk Council of Akwesasne (for the Canadian portion), the Saint Regis Mohawk Tribe (the federally recognised tribal government on the U.S. side), and the traditional Mohawk Nation Council of Chiefs. The community operates schools, health services, police and media, including the newspaper Indian Time.

Issues

The territory's position has made cross-border trade, smuggling enforcement and border restrictions recurring concerns, notably during the 2009 dispute over arming Canadian border officers stationed on Cornwall Island, and during the COVID-19 pandemic.
