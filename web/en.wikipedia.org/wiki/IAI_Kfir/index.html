The IAI Kfir (Hebrew: כְּפִיר, "Lion Cub") is an Israeli all-weather multirole combat aircraft developed by Israel Aircraft Industries (IAI) from the IAI Nesher, itself derived from the French Dassault Mirage 5. The Kfir replaced the Nesher's Atar engine with the more powerful General Electric J79 turbojet — the engine of the F-4 Phantom II — requiring a shortened, wider rear fuselage, additional cooling intakes, and structural changes. Small canard foreplanes added on later variants improved manoeuvrability and short-field performance.

Development and service

The prototype first flew in June 1973, and the Kfir entered Israeli Air Force service in 1976. Variants included the original Kfir C.1, the improved C.2 with canards and dogtooth wing leading edges, the two-seat TC.2 trainer, and the C.7 with upgraded avionics and a more powerful engine variant. Israel also leased 25 Kfirs to the United States Navy and Marine Corps in the 1980s, where they served as adversary aircraft under the designation F-21A Lion.

Export sales were limited by U.S. approval requirements for the J79 engine, but Kfirs were sold to Colombia, Ecuador, and Sri Lanka, where they saw combat in counter-insurgency operations. Upgraded versions with modern radar and avionics have kept the type in service with the Colombian Air Force into the 2020s. More than 220 Kfirs were built.
