Marine biology

Marine biology is the scientific study of the biology of marine life, organisms in the sea. Given that in biology many phyla, families and genera have some species that live in the sea and others that live on land, marine biology classifies species based on the environment rather than on taxonomy.

A large proportion of all life on Earth exists in the ocean. The exact size of this large proportion is unknown, since many ocean species remain to be discovered. The ocean is a complex three-dimensional world covering approximately 71% of the Earth's surface. The habitats studied in marine biology include everything from the tiny layers of surface water in which organisms and abiotic items may be trapped in surface tension between the ocean and atmosphere, to the depths of the oceanic trenches, sometimes 10,000 metres or more beneath the surface of the ocean.

History

The study of marine biology dates back to Aristotle, who described a variety of marine species in the fourth century BC. Modern marine biology began with the voyages of exploration of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, notably the Challenger expedition of 1872–1876, which systematically sampled deep-sea life and described thousands of new species. The founding of marine stations such as those at Naples and Woods Hole gave scientists permanent laboratories on the coast.

Marine life

Marine life ranges from microscopic phytoplankton, which produce much of the world's oxygen through photosynthesis, through zooplankton, crustaceans, molluscs, fish, marine reptiles, seabirds and marine mammals such as whales and dolphins. Primary production in the sunlit surface waters forms the base of most ocean food webs, while deep-sea communities may depend on marine snow or on chemosynthesis at hydrothermal vents.

Habitats

Important marine habitats include estuaries, coral reefs, kelp forests, seagrass meadows, tidal pools, the open pelagic zone and the deep seabed. Many organisms move between habitats during their life cycles, and estuaries serve as nurseries for commercially important fish.

Related fields

Marine biology is related to oceanography, which studies the physical, chemical and geological processes of the ocean, and to fisheries science, ichthyology and marine conservation. Organisations such as the Marine Stewardship Council apply marine science to the management of seafood resources.
