Lenin's Mausoleum

Lenin's Mausoleum is a mausoleum on Red Square in Moscow, Russia, that serves as the resting place of Soviet leader Vladimir Lenin. His embalmed body has been on public display there since shortly after his death in 1924, with rare exceptions during wartime.

LocationRed Square, Moscow, Russia
ArchitectAlexey Shchusev
Completed1930 (stone structure)
OccupantVladimir Lenin

History

After Lenin's death on 21 January 1924, a temporary wooden mausoleum was erected within days. It was replaced by a larger wooden structure and then, in 1930, by the present stepped pyramid of red granite, porphyry and black labradorite designed by Alexey Shchusev.

The embalmed body

Lenin's body was embalmed using experimental techniques developed by scientists including Vladimir Vorobiev and Boris Zbarsky. A dedicated laboratory maintains the remains, periodically re-embalming them. From 1953 to 1961 the body of Joseph Stalin lay beside Lenin's before being removed and buried near the Kremlin wall.

Public access

The mausoleum is open to visitors on specified days, with long queues forming along Red Square. Photography inside is forbidden. The future of the tomb has been debated repeatedly in post-Soviet Russia, but the body remains in place.
