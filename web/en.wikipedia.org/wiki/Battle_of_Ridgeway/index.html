Battle of Ridgeway

Date2 June 1866
LocationRidgeway, Canada West (now Ontario)
ResultFenian tactical victory; raiders withdraw to the United States

The Battle of Ridgeway was fought on 2 June 1866 near the village of Ridgeway, Canada West, during the Fenian raids. A column of Canadian militia encountered a force of Irish-American Fenian veterans of the American Civil War who had crossed the Niagara River intending to seize Canadian territory as leverage for Irish independence from Britain.

Background

On the night of 31 May – 1 June 1866, around 800–1,000 Fenians under Colonel John O'Neill crossed from Buffalo and occupied Fort Erie. A hastily assembled Canadian force — the Queen's Own Rifles and the 13th Battalion under Lieutenant-Colonel Alfred Booker, with further troops under Colonel George Peacocke converging from the west — moved to intercept them.

The battle

Booker's roughly 850 militiamen met the Fenians on Limestone Ridge near Ridgeway on the morning of 2 June. The Canadians initially drove in the Fenian skirmish line, but confusion — including a false report of Fenian cavalry that led the Queen's Own to form square, followed by contradictory orders — threw the militia into disorder. The Fenians, many of them experienced Civil War soldiers, counterattacked, and the Canadian column broke and retreated toward Port Colborne, losing about nine killed on the field and more dying of wounds.

Aftermath

Despite their victory, O'Neill's men were cut off from reinforcement by American authorities, short of supplies, and faced by converging regulars; they recrossed the Niagara on the night of 2–3 June and surrendered to US naval forces. The embarrassment of Ridgeway spurred Canadian militia reform and is often cited as a catalyst for Canadian Confederation in 1867. The battle was the last fought in Ontario against a foreign invader.
