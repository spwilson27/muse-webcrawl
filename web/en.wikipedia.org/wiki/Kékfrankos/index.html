Kékfrankos

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Kékfrankos (Blaufränkisch) grapes]

Kékfrankos is the Hungarian name for the dark-skinned wine grape variety known internationally as Blaufränkisch (German) and as Lemberger in some regions. It is the most widely planted red wine grape in Hungary and a signature variety of Central European winemaking, also important in Austria, Slovakia (Frankovka), Croatia (Frankovka/Borgonja), and Germany (Lemberger).

Characteristics

The name means "blue Frankish." The grape ripens late and produces wines noted for dark cherry and dark berry fruit, peppery spice, firm tannins, and refreshing acidity. In Hungary it is a key component of Egri Bikavér ("Bull's Blood of Eger") and of blends from Szekszárd, and increasingly bottled as a varietal wine.

Origins

The variety is believed to be of Austrian or broader Central European origin, descending from Gouais blanc (Heunisch) parentage. DNA profiling has also shown it to be a parent of Zweigelt, Austria's most planted red grape.
