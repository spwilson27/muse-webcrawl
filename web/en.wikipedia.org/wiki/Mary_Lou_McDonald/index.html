Mary Lou McDonald

Mary Louise McDonald (born 1 May 1969) is an Irish politician who has served as Leader of the Opposition in Ireland since June 2020 and President of Sinn Féin since February 2018. She has been a Teachta Dála (TD) for the Dublin Central constituency since 2011, and previously served as a Member of the European Parliament for the Dublin constituency from 2004 to 2009.

Early life and education

McDonald grew up in the south Dublin suburb of Rathgar and was educated at the private Notre Dame Des Missions school in Churchtown. She read English literature at Trinity College Dublin, earned an MA in European Integration Studies from the University of Limerick, and a qualification in human resource management from Dublin City University. She worked for the Institute of European Affairs, the Irish Trade Union Trust and the Economic and Social Research Institute.

Political career

McDonald began her political career in Fianna Fáil before joining Sinn Féin in 1998. She stood unsuccessfully for the Dáil in Dublin West in 2002, then won a seat in the European Parliament in 2004, becoming Sinn Féin's first MEP in the Republic of Ireland. She lost the seat in 2009, the same year she became the party's vice-president under Gerry Adams, and was elected to the Dáil for Dublin Central at the 2011 general election.

As public accounts committee vice-chair and a prominent media performer, McDonald was Adams's expected successor; she was elected unopposed as party president and took office on 10 February 2018. In the 2020 general election, Sinn Féin won the largest share of first-preference votes and 37 seats, its best result in the modern era; when Fine Gael, Fianna Fáil and the Greens formed a coalition government, McDonald became Leader of the Opposition, the first woman to hold the role.

Political positions

McDonald advocates Irish reunification through a referendum, expanded public housing and health services, and cost-of-living measures. She has aimed to reposition Sinn Féin as a mainstream left-of-centre party of government in the Republic while remaining the largest nationalist party in Northern Ireland.
