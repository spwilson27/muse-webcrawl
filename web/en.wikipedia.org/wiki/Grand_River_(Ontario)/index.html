The Grand River is a river in southwestern Ontario, Canada. Rising in the highlands near Dundalk in Grey County, it flows south for about 280 kilometres through the cities of Kitchener, Waterloo, Cambridge, Paris, and Brantford before emptying into the north shore of Lake Erie at Port Maitland. Its watershed, at roughly 6,800 square kilometres, is the largest in southern Ontario.

History

French explorers called it the Grande Rivière; it was known to Indigenous peoples long before. In 1784, under the Haldimand Proclamation, a large tract of land six miles deep on each side of the river was granted to the Six Nations of the Grand River in recognition of their alliance with the British during the American Revolutionary War — land whose extent remains the subject of ongoing claims. During the nineteenth century the river powered mills and was improved for navigation with locks and the Grand River Navigation Company works.

Geography and ecology

Tributaries include the Speed, Eramosa, Conestogo, and Nith rivers. The Elora Gorge, cut through limestone near the town of Elora, is one of its most scenic stretches. The watershed supports a notable cold-water trout fishery and Carolinian forest remnants. In 1994 the Grand was designated a Canadian Heritage River in recognition of its human heritage and recreational values.

Management

The Grand River Conservation Authority, founded in 1932 as one of Ontario's first conservation authorities, manages flood control, reservoirs such as Belwood Lake and Conestogo Lake, and conservation areas throughout the basin.
