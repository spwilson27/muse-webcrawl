Sanskrit literature

Sanskrit literature is the body of literature written in the Sanskrit language of ancient and medieval India, spanning more than three millennia. It encompasses sacred scriptures, epic poetry, drama, lyric and narrative verse, and extensive works on philosophy, science, law and grammar.

Vedic literature

The earliest layer is the Veda: the four collections (Rigveda, Samaveda, Yajurveda, Atharvaveda) of hymns and ritual texts, followed by the Brahmanas, Aranyakas and Upanishads, composed broadly between c. 1500 and 500 BCE. This corpus forms the foundation of Hindu religious thought.

Epics and Puranas

The great Sanskrit epics, the Mahābhārata (which contains the Bhagavad Gita) and the Rāmāyaṇa attributed to Valmiki, combine narrative with moral and philosophical teaching. The Purāṇas retell myths, genealogies and cosmology for a broad audience.

Classical literature

In the classical period, poets and dramatists such as Kālidāsa (author of the play Abhijñānaśākuntalam and the poem Meghadūta), Bhavabhūti, and Bāṇa produced sophisticated court literature under the rules of Sanskrit poetics (alaṅkāraśāstra). Story collections such as the Panchatantra and Kathāsaritsāgara influenced narrative traditions worldwide. Sanskrit also served as the scholarly language for works on mathematics, astronomy, medicine (Ayurveda), grammar — notably Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī — and philosophy.
