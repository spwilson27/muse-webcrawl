The Polish 1st Armoured Division (Polish: 1 Dywizja Pancerna) was an armoured division of the Polish Armed Forces in the West during the Second World War. Created in Scotland in February 1942 and commanded throughout its existence by General Stanisław Maczek, it fought with distinction in north-west Europe from Normandy to Wilhelmshaven.

Formation

The division was formed at Duns, Scotland, from Polish troops who had escaped to Britain after the fall of France and of Poland. Its men adopted the winged hussar as their emblem, recalling the famous Polish cavalry of the seventeenth century.

Normandy and the Falaise Pocket

The division landed in Normandy in late July and early August 1944 and went into action on 8 August during Operation Totalize. Its most famous action came in mid-August, when it seized Mont Ormel (Hill 262, "the Mace") and held the escape route of the German Seventh Army shut at the Battle of the Falaise Pocket, fighting off desperate counter-attacks by SS formations until relieved by Canadian troops.

Advance through Belgium and the Netherlands

The division pursued the retreating Germans across Belgium, liberating Ypres, Ghent and other towns, and crossed into the Netherlands, liberating Breda on 29 October 1944 without civilian casualties — an achievement the city still commemorates. In April 1945 it crossed into Germany, captured the naval base of Wilhelmshaven on 6 May and liberated the Stalag VI-C camp at Oberlangen, freeing some 1,700 Polish women soldiers of the Warsaw Uprising.

Disbandment

After occupation duties in Germany, the division was disbanded in 1947. Many of its soldiers, unable to return to communist-ruled Poland, settled in Britain and Canada.
