A legislative assembly is the name given in a number of countries to a legislature, or to one house of a legislature. The term is most common in Commonwealth and federal systems, where it often denotes the directly elected lower house of a state or provincial parliament, or a unicameral legislature for a territory. Members are commonly styled MLA (Member of the Legislative Assembly).

Usage by country

In Canada, each province has a legislative assembly whose members, together with the Crown's representative, form the provincial legislature; Ontario, Quebec, and other provinces use titles such as MPP or MNA for their members in some cases. In India, the lower house of a state legislature is the Vidhan Sabha, officially translated as the Legislative Assembly, elected for five-year terms. In Australia, the lower houses of New South Wales, Victoria, Western Australia, and the unicameral-style assemblies of the territories bear the name. In the United States, several states call their lower house—or their whole legislature, as in Nevada and formerly California's lower house—the Assembly or Legislative Assembly; the California State Assembly and the New York State Assembly are examples. Similar bodies exist in Pakistan, South Africa, Brazil (state legislative assemblies), and various British Overseas Territories and Crown dependencies.

Functions

Like other legislatures, a legislative assembly debates and enacts laws, approves budgets and taxation, and scrutinizes the executive through question periods, committees, and votes of confidence. Its procedures—including devices like the quorum call and motions for cloture—are governed by its standing orders. An ambassador or official the assembly's government finds unacceptable may be declared persona non grata in the parallel diplomatic sphere.
