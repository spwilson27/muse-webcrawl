Southern Tang

The Southern Tang (937–976) was one of the Ten Kingdoms of China during the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period. Founded by Li Bian, who restored the Tang dynastic name, it was for a time the most powerful and cultured of the southern kingdoms, ruling much of the lower Yangtze region from its capital at Jinling (modern Nanjing).

History

Li Bian took the throne from the state of Wu in 937 and ruled as Emperor Liezu, promoting agriculture, silk and tea production and stable administration. His son Li Jing expanded the kingdom at the expense of Min and Chu but suffered costly defeats by the Later Zhou in the 950s, losing the territory north of the Yangtze and accepting tributary status.

Li Yu and the fall

The last ruler, Li Yu (r. 961–975), is remembered less as a statesman than as one of China's greatest poets, whose ci lyrics of loss and longing became canonical. Militarily outmatched, he surrendered to the Song dynasty in 975 and was taken to Kaifeng, where he died in 978; the Southern Tang was formally absorbed the following years. Despite its fall, the kingdom's patronage of painting, printing and poetry gave it an outsized cultural legacy.
