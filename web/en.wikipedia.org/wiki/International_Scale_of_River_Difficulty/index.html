International Scale of River Difficulty

Whitewater is graded from Class I (easy) to Class VI (extreme and exploratory).

The International Scale of River Difficulty is an American system used to rate the difficulty of navigating a stretch of river, or a single rapid, for whitewater boating. It was created by the American Whitewater Association (now American Whitewater) and is used worldwide, with some regional variations.

The classes

ClassDescription
IEasy — fast-moving water with riffles and small waves; few obstructions, all obvious and easily avoided.
IINovice — straightforward rapids with wide, clear channels; occasional manoeuvring may be required.
IIIIntermediate — moderate, irregular waves and obstacles; complex manoeuvres in fast current often required.
IVAdvanced — intense, powerful but predictable rapids requiring precise boat handling in turbulent water.
VExpert — extremely long, obstructed, or violent rapids; drops may contain large, unavoidable waves and holes; rescue is often difficult.
VIExtreme and exploratory — rapids at the limits of navigability, unpredictable and dangerous; Class VI runs, once attempted, are usually re-graded once descended successfully.

Application

Ratings are given at a reference water level and may rise sharply with higher flows; plus and minus signs (e.g., III+) are sometimes used for finer gradations. Because the scale is subjective and conditions vary, ratings serve as a rough guide rather than a precise measure, and the same river may be listed with different ratings in different guidebooks.
