Danske Bank money laundering scandal

The Danske Bank money laundering scandal is one of the largest money laundering scandals in European history. It concerns some €200 billion of suspicious transactions that flowed through the Estonian branch of Danske Bank, Denmark's largest bank, between 2007 and 2015.

Background

Danske Bank acquired the Finnish Sampo Bank in 2007, gaining its Estonian branch. The branch's "non-resident portfolio" — accounts held by customers from Russia and other former Soviet states — generated large profits but attracted repeated warnings from Estonian and Danish regulators and from a whistleblower, later identified as Howard Wilkinson, a former employee of the branch, who reported suspicious activity internally in 2013.

Revelations and consequences

Journalistic investigations, notably by the Danish newspaper Berlingske and the Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project, brought the scale of the flows to public attention in 2017–2018. In September 2018 Danske Bank published an internal investigation concluding that a large part of the €200 billion that passed through the branch was suspicious. Chief executive Thomas Borgen resigned, and the bank's share price fell sharply.

The bank closed its Estonian operations at the order of the Estonian regulator, and exited its other Baltic and Russian businesses. In December 2022 Danske Bank pleaded guilty in the United States and agreed to forfeit about $2 billion to resolve American and Danish investigations; it also accepted significant fines and penalties in Denmark.
