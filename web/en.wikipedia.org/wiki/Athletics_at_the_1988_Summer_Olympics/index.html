Athletics at the 1988 Summer Olympics

Athletics at the 1988 Summer Olympics in Seoul, South Korea, was held from 23 September to 2 October 1988 at the Seoul Olympic Stadium. A total of 42 events were contested — 27 by men and 15 by women — with athletes from 149 nations taking part. The programme is remembered above all for the men's 100 metres final and the drugs scandal that followed it.

Men's 100 metres and the Johnson scandal

The men's 100 metres final, run on 24 September, was billed as a showdown between Canada's Ben Johnson and the defending champion Carl Lewis. Johnson won in a world record 9.79 seconds, but three days later he was disqualified after his urine sample tested positive for the anabolic steroid stanozolol. Lewis was elevated to gold with 9.92, itself a world record once Johnson's mark was annulled; Linford Christie took silver and Calvin Smith bronze. The race later became known as "the dirtiest race in history" after revelations that most of the finalists were involved in doping at some point in their careers.

Women's sprinting: Florence Griffith Joyner

American Florence Griffith Joyner dominated the women's sprints, winning the 100 metres in an Olympic record 10.62 seconds and the 200 metres in a world record 21.34 seconds, together with gold in the 4 × 100 m relay and silver in the 4 × 400 m relay. Her 100 m world record of 10.49, set at the US trials that summer, and her 200 m record from Seoul both still stand.

Other highlights

Jackie Joyner-Kersee won the heptathlon with a world record 7,291 points and also took gold in the long jump. In the men's long jump, Lewis retained his title ahead of Mike Powell. The Soviet Union topped the athletics medal table, with notable distance-running victories by Kenyan athletes including John Ngugi (5000 m) and Peter Rono (1500 m). Morocco's Brahim Boutayeb won the 10,000 m, and Said Aouita took bronze in the 800 m. East Germany's Sigrun Wodars and Petra Felke won the women's 800 m and javelin respectively.

Medal summary

The USSR won 10 gold medals in athletics, the United States 13, and East Germany 6, in one of the last Games at which the three great Cold War sporting powers all competed.
