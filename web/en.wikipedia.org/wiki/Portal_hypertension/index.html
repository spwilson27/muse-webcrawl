Portal hypertension

Portal hypertension is abnormally increased portal venous pressure — pressure in the portal vein and its branches, which carry blood from the gastrointestinal tract, spleen, and pancreas to the liver. It is most commonly caused by cirrhosis.

Causes

Causes are classified as pre-hepatic (e.g. portal vein thrombosis), intrahepatic (cirrhosis, schistosomiasis, and other liver diseases), and post-hepatic (e.g. Budd–Chiari syndrome, constrictive pericarditis). Clinically significant portal hypertension corresponds to a hepatic venous pressure gradient of 10 mmHg or more.

Signs and complications

Raised portal pressure drives the development of portosystemic collateral vessels, producing oesophageal and gastric varices (which may rupture and cause life-threatening bleeding), caput medusae, splenomegaly with hypersplenism, and ascites. Portal hypertensive gastropathy and hepatic encephalopathy are also associated complications.

Treatment

Management includes non-selective beta blockers (such as propranolol or carvedilol) to reduce portal pressure, endoscopic band ligation of varices, treatment of the underlying liver disease, and, in refractory cases, transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) or liver transplantation.
