Guan Moye (born 17 February 1955), better known by the pen name Mo Yan (Chinese: 莫言; literally "don't speak"), is a Chinese novelist and short-story writer. He won the 2012 Nobel Prize in Literature, the first Chinese citizen (a resident of mainland China) to do so; the Swedish Academy praised him as a writer "who with hallucinatory realism merges folk tales, history and the contemporary." He is best known in the West for the 1987 novel Red Sorghum, whose first two parts were adapted into the film Red Sorghum (1988) directed by Zhang Yimou.

Life and work

Born to a peasant family in Gaomi, Shandong, Mo Yan left school during the Cultural Revolution to work in a factory, and joined the People's Liberation Army in 1976, beginning to write while a soldier. Most of his fiction is set in his fictionalised home country of "Northeast Gaomi Township." His major works include The Garlic Ballads (1988), The Republic of Wine (1992), Big Breasts and Wide Hips (1996), Sandalwood Death (2001), Life and Death Are Wearing Me Out (2006) and Frog (2009), which addresses China's one-child policy and won the Mao Dun Literature Prize in 2011. His style blends magical realism, satire, folk storytelling and graphic depictions of violence and hunger, drawing comparisons with Gabriel García Márquez and William Faulkner.

Reception

The Nobel award was celebrated officially in China but also criticised by some dissidents and fellow writers, who objected to Mo Yan's position within the state literary establishment — he has served as vice-chair of the China Writers Association — and to remarks perceived as accommodating censorship; his pen name itself, he has explained, was a reminder from his parents not to speak his mind aloud in the political climate of his youth.
