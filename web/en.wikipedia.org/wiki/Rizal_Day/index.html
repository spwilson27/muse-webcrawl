Rizal Day

Rizal Day is a Philippine national holiday commemorating the life and works of José Rizal, the national hero of the Philippines. It is celebrated every December 30, the anniversary of Rizal's execution in 1896 at Bagumbayan, now Rizal Park, in Manila.

History

José Rizal, a writer, physician, and reform advocate, was executed by firing squad by the Spanish colonial government on December 30, 1896, on charges of rebellion arising from his novels and involvement in the reform movement. His death became a rallying point of the Philippine Revolution. On December 20, 1898, President Emilio Aguinaldo issued a decree declaring December 30 a day of national mourning in honor of Rizal and other victims of Spanish rule. In 1902, under the American Insular Government, the Philippine Commission enacted Act No. 345, which made December 30 an official public holiday.

Observance

The centerpiece of the commemoration is a flag-raising and wreath-laying ceremony at the Rizal Monument in Rizal Park (Luneta), led by the President of the Philippines. Simultaneous ceremonies are held at Rizal monuments and shrines throughout the country, including the Rizal Shrine in Calamba, Laguna, his birthplace, and the shrine in Dapitan, where he was exiled. The Philippine flag is flown at half-mast at the monument on this day.

The centenary of Rizal's death in 1996 was marked by major national commemorations, and the 150th anniversary of his birth in 2011 was likewise observed with a year-long program. Historians occasionally contrast Rizal Day with Bonifacio Day (November 30), noting the difference between commemoration on the date of death rather than birth.
