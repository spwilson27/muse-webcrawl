Tight oil

Tight oil (also known as shale oil in common usage, or light tight oil, abbreviated LTO) is light crude oil contained in petroleum-bearing formations of low permeability, often shale or tight sandstone. Production requires horizontal drilling combined with multi-stage hydraulic fracturing ("fracking") to create flow paths through the rock. Tight oil should not be confused with oil shale, from which shale oil is extracted by retorting kerogen.

Development

The combination of horizontal wells and fracturing, pioneered in the Barnett Shale gas play in Texas, was applied to oil in the Bakken Formation of North Dakota and Montana from the mid-2000s, and then to the Eagle Ford (Texas) and the Permian Basin (Texas and New Mexico). The resulting "shale revolution" made the United States the world's largest crude producer by 2018.

Characteristics

Tight-oil wells characteristically show very high initial production followed by steep decline — often 60–70% in the first year — so sustained output requires continuous drilling. Tight oil is typically light and sweet, commanding good prices, but the plays are capital-intensive and sensitive to oil prices, as the 2014–2016 price collapse demonstrated.
