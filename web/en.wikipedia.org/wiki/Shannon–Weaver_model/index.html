Shannon–Weaver model

The Shannon–Weaver model is a model of communication developed in 1948 by Claude Shannon, with an introduction by Warren Weaver, in the paper "A Mathematical Theory of Communication". It describes communication as a linear process in which a message travels from a sender to a receiver through a channel, subject to interference called noise.

Components

The model identifies five main elements: an information source, a transmitter (or encoder), a channel, a receiver (or decoder), and a destination. Noise in the channel can distort the signal, and redundancy in the message helps the receiver reconstruct it. Weaver's later elaboration added feedback in some versions and distinguished technical, semantic and effectiveness problems in communication.

Influence and criticism

The model became foundational in communication studies and information theory. Critics note that its linear, one-directional structure understates the interactive, contextual and social dimensions of human communication, leading to later transactional models.
