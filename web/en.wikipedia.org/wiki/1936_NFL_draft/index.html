1936 NFL draft

DateFebruary 8, 1936
LocationRitz-Carlton Hotel, Philadelphia
First selectionJay Berwanger
Selecting teamPhiladelphia Eagles
SignificanceFirst NFL draft

The 1936 NFL draft was the first annual player selection meeting of the National Football League. It was held on February 8, 1936, at the Ritz-Carlton Hotel in Philadelphia. The draft was introduced by commissioner Bert Bell as a way to strengthen weaker clubs, distribute college talent more evenly and restrain bidding wars for new players.

First selection

The Philadelphia Eagles used the first overall pick on University of Chicago halfback Jay Berwanger, winner of the inaugural Downtown Athletic Club Trophy, later known as the Heisman Trophy. Berwanger's rights were traded to the Chicago Bears, but he never signed an NFL contract; salary expectations and the modest pay of early professional football led him toward a business career instead.

Format and selections

Nine NFL teams selected from a pool of college players over nine rounds, choosing 81 players in all. The order generally reflected the previous season's standings, with weaker teams selecting earlier. Because scouting was limited and many draftees chose other work, only a minority of the selected players appeared in the league. The class is remembered more for its institutional importance than for star power, and its selections illustrate the rough economics of the 1930s game.

Legacy

Although rudimentary by later standards, the 1936 draft established the central mechanism by which the NFL allocated amateur talent. It evolved into a multi-day public spectacle, but its original purpose remained competitive balance and cost control. See also NFL Draft, Jay Berwanger, Bert Bell and 1936 NFL season.
