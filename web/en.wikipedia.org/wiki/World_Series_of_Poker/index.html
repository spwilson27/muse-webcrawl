The World Series of Poker (WSOP) is a series of poker tournaments held annually in Paradise, Nevada, and since 2004 owned and operated by Caesars Entertainment. It traces its origins to 1970, when Benny Binion invited seven of the best-known poker players to the Horseshoe Casino for a single event, with the winner determined by a secret ballot of the players. Johnny Moss was voted the first champion.

Main Event

The series' prestige event is the US$10,000 no-limit Texas hold'em championship, known as the Main Event, whose winner receives a multi-million-dollar prize, a gold bracelet and is considered the world poker champion for the year. The field grew enormously during the poker boom of the 2000s, following amateur Chris Moneymaker's 2003 victory after qualifying in an online satellite; the 2006 Main Event drew 8,773 entrants. The 2023 Main Event set a new record with 10,043 entrants. Winners of every bracelet event receive a WSOP gold bracelet in addition to prize money; Phil Hellmuth holds the record for most bracelets.

Expansions

The brand has expanded to the World Series of Poker Europe (from 2007), the WSOP Asia Pacific, the WSOP Circuit series of regional tournaments, and online bracelet events. The series moved from Binion's Horseshoe to the Rio All-Suite Hotel and Casino in 2005, and in 2022 to the Paris Las Vegas and Horseshoe Las Vegas on the Strip.
