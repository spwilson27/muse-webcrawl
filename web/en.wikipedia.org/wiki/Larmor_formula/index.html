Larmor formula

In electrodynamics, the Larmor formula is used to calculate the total power radiated by a non-relativistic point charge as it accelerates. It was first derived by J. J. Larmor in 1897, in the context of the wave theory of light. The formula states that the radiated power P is proportional to the square of the charge and the square of its acceleration:

P = q²a² / (6πε₀c³)

where q is the charge, a the acceleration, ε₀ the permittivity of free space and c the speed of light (the formula is also written with a prefactor of 2/3 in Gaussian units).

Significance

The result implies that any accelerating charge loses energy by radiation, so a classical electron in orbit around a nucleus would spiral inward in about 10⁻¹¹ seconds — one of the stark failures of classical physics that motivated the development of quantum mechanics and Bohr's stationary-state postulate. The relativistic generalisation is the Liénard formula. The Larmor formula is also the basis for understanding radiation reaction (the Abraham–Lorentz force), synchrotron radiation losses in accelerators, and thermal bremsstrahlung emission from plasmas.
