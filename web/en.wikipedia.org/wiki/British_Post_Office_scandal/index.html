The British Post Office scandal, also known as the Horizon IT scandal, is a miscarriage of justice in the United Kingdom. Between 1999 and 2015, the Post Office prosecuted hundreds of sub-postmasters for theft, fraud and false accounting after shortfalls appeared in their branch accounts. The shortfalls were in fact caused by bugs and errors in Horizon, the computerised accounting system supplied by Fujitsu. It has been described as the most widespread miscarriage of justice in British legal history.

Background

Sub-postmasters are self-employed agents who run local post office branches and are personally liable for any losses in their accounts. When Horizon was introduced from 1999, some sub-postmasters began reporting unexplained discrepancies; required to make good the apparent losses, many were made bankrupt or lost their homes. More than 900 sub-postmasters were convicted across the United Kingdom, and some were imprisoned. The Post Office maintained that Horizon was robust and that no remote access to branch accounts was possible — a claim later shown to be false.

Campaign and litigation

The former sub-postmaster Alan Bates founded the Justice for Subpostmasters Alliance in 2009 and connected victims through a website after a Computer Weekly investigation drew attention to the cases. In 2017, 557 claimants brought a group action; the Post Office settled in 2019 for £58 million, and in the associated judgments the judge found Horizon to have contained bugs capable of causing the shortfalls. Criminal convictions began to be quashed in 2020–2021, when 39 convictions were overturned in a single Court of Appeal hearing. A statutory public inquiry, chaired by Sir Wyn Williams, began in 2021.

Aftermath

Public anger intensified in January 2024 with the broadcast of the ITV drama Mr Bates vs The Post Office. The Prime Minister described the scandal as "one of the greatest miscarriages of justice in our nation's history", and the government legislated in 2024 to quash the convictions of affected sub-postmasters in England and Wales en masse — an unprecedented step — alongside compensation schemes. Post Office chief executive Paula Vennells returned her CBE in February 2024 after a petition that gathered over a million signatures.
