Twilight is the illumination of the lower atmosphere when the Sun is below the horizon and not directly visible. Sunlight scattered in the upper atmosphere illuminates the surface, so that it is neither fully lit nor completely dark. Twilight occurs before sunrise (morning twilight, ending at dawn) and after sunset (evening twilight, ending at dusk).

Definitions

Three stages of twilight are defined by how far the centre of the Sun's disc lies below the horizon. During civil twilight (0–6°), enough light remains for ordinary outdoor activities and the brightest stars and planets become visible; civil dawn and dusk mark the legal limits used in many jurisdictions. During nautical twilight (6–12°), sailors can still distinguish the horizon at sea for celestial navigation. During astronomical twilight (12–18°), the sky is dark enough for most astronomical observations; beyond 18° is full night.

Duration

The duration of twilight depends on latitude and season: it is shortest at the equator, where the Sun crosses the horizon steeply, and longest near the poles, where in summer twilight can last all night (the "white nights" of high latitudes) and where polar twilight precedes and follows the polar night.
