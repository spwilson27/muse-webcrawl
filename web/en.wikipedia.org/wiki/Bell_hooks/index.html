bell hooks

bell hooks (born Gloria Jean Watkins; September 25, 1952 – December 15, 2021) was an American author, theorist, educator, and social critic. She adopted the pen name of her great-grandmother, Bell Blair Hooks, writing it in lowercase letters to emphasise the substance of her work over her identity. Her writing examined the intersection of race, gender, class, and capitalism in what she called the "imperialist white-supremacist capitalist patriarchy".

Life and career

Born in Hopkinsville, Kentucky, hooks studied English at Stanford University and earned a PhD from the University of California, Santa Cruz. She taught at several universities, including Yale and Oberlin, and from 2004 was a professor at Berea College in Kentucky, where the bell hooks center was later named for her.

Major works

Her first major book, Ain't I a Woman: Black Women and Feminism (1981), argued that mainstream feminism marginalised Black women. Feminist Theory: From Margin to Center (1984) developed her critique, while the trilogy beginning with All About Love: New Visions (2000) explored love in American culture. Her work spans more than thirty books on feminism, cultural criticism, education, and memoir.

Legacy

hooks is regarded as a foundational figure in intersectional feminism and critical pedagogy, influencing generations of scholars and activists.
