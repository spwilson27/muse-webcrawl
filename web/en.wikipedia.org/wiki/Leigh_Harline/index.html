Leigh Adrian Harline (March 26, 1907 – December 10, 1969) was an American film composer and songwriter. He is best remembered for composing, with lyricist Ned Washington, the song "When You Wish Upon a Star" for Walt Disney's Pinocchio (1940), which won the Academy Award for Best Original Song and later became the signature anthem of the Walt Disney Company.

Career

Born in Salt Lake City, Harline studied piano and organ and worked in radio in California as an arranger, conductor, and vocalist. He joined Walt Disney Studios in 1932, first as an arranger and orchestrator. He contributed music to more than fifty Silly Symphonies shorts, including The Old Mill, and co-composed the score of Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937) with Frank Churchill and Paul J. Smith, helping to write songs such as "I'm Wishing" and "One Song".

For Pinocchio, Harline shared the Academy Award for Best Original Score with Paul J. Smith and Ned Washington in addition to the song award. After leaving Disney in 1941 he worked at RKO and other studios, scoring dozens of features across genres — film noir, westerns, and family pictures — including Mr. Blandings Builds His Dream House (1948), Pickup on South Street (1953), Broken Lance (1954), Warlock (1959), and The Wonderful World of the Brothers Grimm (1962). He received eight Academy Award nominations in total across his career.

Legacy

"When You Wish Upon a Star" has been recorded by innumerable artists, was named the greatest Disney song in several polls, and is preserved in the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry as part of the Pinocchio soundtrack. Harline was posthumously named a Disney Legend in 2001.
