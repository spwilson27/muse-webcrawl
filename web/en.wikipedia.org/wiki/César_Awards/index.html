César Awards

The César Awards (French: Cérémonie des César) are the national film awards of France, presented annually by the Académie des Arts et Techniques du Cinéma since 1976. They are the French equivalent of the American Academy Awards, and the ceremony is one of the most prominent events in French cinema.

History

The awards were founded by Georges Cravenne and named after the sculptor César Baldaccini (1921–1998), who designed the trophy, a compressed piece of metal art. The first ceremony took place on 3 April 1976 at the Palais des congrès in Paris. The Academy has around 4,700 voting members drawn from all branches of the film industry.

Categories

Awards are given in more than twenty categories, including Best Film, Best Director, Best Actor, Best Actress, Best Supporting Actor and Actress, Best Original Screenplay, Best Foreign Film and the César d'honneur for lifetime achievement. The record for most wins by a single film is shared by several titles with ten awards each, including The Last Metro, Cyrano de Bergerac and Amour.

Ceremony

The ceremony is held in Paris, in recent years at the Olympia or the Salle Pleyel, and is broadcast live on television. It traditionally takes place in late February, shortly before the Oscars.
