Western Chalukya Empire
Existedc. 973 – 1189 CE
CapitalManyakheta; later Kalyani
RegionWestern Deccan, India
Notable rulersTailapa II, Vikramaditya VI

The Western Chalukya Empire ruled large parts of the western Deccan, in present-day India, between the 10th and 12th centuries. Founded by Tailapa II after the overthrow of the Rashtrakutas around 973, the dynasty was a successor to the earlier Chalukyas of Badami and a rival of the Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi and the Chola Empire.

History

Under Vikramaditya VI (r. 1076–1126), the empire reached its greatest extent and enjoyed a golden age. The Western Chalukyas fought repeatedly with the Cholas over Vengi and the fertile Raichur Doab. In the later 12th century the empire fragmented as feudatories — the Hoysalas, Kakatiyas and Seuna Yadavas — asserted independence.

Culture

The period saw major developments in temple architecture (the Gadag style), Kannada and Sanskrit literature, and the Virashaiva movement associated with Basava, whose vachana poetry reshaped Karnataka's religious life.
