The MLS Cup is the annual championship match of Major League Soccer (MLS), contested by the winners of the Eastern and Western Conference finals at the end of the MLS Cup Playoffs. The winner is crowned league champion and receives the Philip F. Anschutz Trophy, named for one of the league's founders. The competition has been held since the league's inaugural season in 1996.

History and format

Early MLS Cups were played at predetermined neutral venues; the first, in 1996, saw D.C. United defeat the LA Galaxy 3–2 in extra time in heavy rain at Foxboro Stadium. Since 2012 the final has been hosted by the participating team with the better regular-season record. The playoffs have been restructured several times, most recently with an expanded field and a best-of-three first round introduced in 2023 alongside the league's Apple TV broadcast era.

Records

The LA Galaxy are the most successful club in MLS Cup history with six championships (2002, 2005, 2011, 2012, 2014, 2024). D.C. United won three of the first four finals. Notable finals include Toronto FC's 2017 victory completing the first domestic treble in MLS history, and the Columbus Crew's 2023 win over Los Angeles FC. The MLS Cup winner traditionally qualifies for the CONCACAF Champions Cup.
