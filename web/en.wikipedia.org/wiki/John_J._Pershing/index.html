John Joseph Pershing (September 13, 1860 – July 15, 1948) was a senior United States Army officer. He served most famously as the commander of the American Expeditionary Forces (AEF) on the Western Front during World War I, from 1917 to 1918. He was the only American promoted in his own lifetime to the highest rank in the United States Army, General of the Armies.

Early career

Pershing was born near Laclede, Missouri, and graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1886. He served in the closing campaigns of the Indian Wars against the Sioux and Apache, taught military science at the University of Nebraska — where he also earned a law degree — and commanded the 10th Cavalry Regiment, one of the African-American "Buffalo Soldier" regiments, an association that earned him the nickname "Black Jack".

Wars in Cuba, the Philippines and Mexico

During the Spanish–American War Pershing fought at San Juan Hill. He then served in the Philippines, campaigning against the Moro resistance on Mindanao and governing Moro Province. In 1905–06 he was a military attaché and observer in the Russo-Japanese War. In 1916 President Woodrow Wilson placed him in command of the Punitive Expedition into Mexico in pursuit of Pancho Villa; the expedition failed to capture Villa but gave Pershing — and many future Second World War commanders — valuable experience of mobile, motorised operations. His wife Frances and three of their four children died in a fire at the Presidio in 1915; only his son Warren survived.

World War I

Appointed to command the AEF in May 1917, Pershing insisted that American troops fight as an independent army rather than being amalgamated into British and French units, and built a force that grew to some two million men in France. American divisions played a decisive role in the battles of Cantigny, Belleau Wood, Château-Thierry, Saint-Mihiel and the Meuse–Argonne Offensive of 1918. Pershing's insistence on open warfare and marksmanship training was controversial, and AEF casualties were heavy, but the arrival of American forces in strength helped compel Germany to seek the armistice of 11 November 1918.

Later life

Pershing served as Army Chief of Staff from 1921 to 1924, then chaired the American Battle Monuments Commission. His memoir My Experiences in the World War (1931) won the Pulitzer Prize for History. He died in Washington, D.C., in 1948 and was buried in Arlington National Cemetery. The Pershing tank (M26) and the Pershing II missile were named in his honour.
