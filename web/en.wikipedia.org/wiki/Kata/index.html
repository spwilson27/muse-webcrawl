Kata

Kata (Japanese: 型 or 形, "form") is a detailed choreographed pattern of movements practised in Japanese martial arts, either solo or in pairs. Kata are the traditional means by which techniques, principles and applications of arts such as karate, judo, aikido, iaido and kendo were transmitted from teacher to student before the era of written manuals. The word is also used in other Japanese arts — tea ceremony, theatre and even business and programming, where "code kata" denotes a small practice exercise.

Karate kata

In karate, kata combine blocks, strikes, kicks and stances in set sequences against imaginary opponents, often beginning and ending at the same spot (embusen). Major styles preserve overlapping sets inherited from Okinawan masters: Shuri-te kata such as Naihanchi and Kūsankū, and Naha-te kata such as Sanchin and Seisan. The hidden applications of the movements are studied as bunkai. Kata performance is also a competition discipline, judged on precision, power and expression, and was included in the Tokyo 2020 Olympic karate programme.

Judo kata

Judo's kata, formally demonstrated by two partners, were codified largely at the Kodokan. The Nage-no-kata (forms of throwing) and Katame-no-kata (forms of grappling) are central to dan grade examinations; others preserve older jujutsu self-defence methods, such as the Kime-no-kata.
