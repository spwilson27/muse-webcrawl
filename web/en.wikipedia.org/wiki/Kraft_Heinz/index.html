The Kraft Heinz Company is an American multinational food company formed in 2015 by the merger of Kraft Foods Group and the H. J. Heinz Company. It is one of the largest food and beverage companies in the world, owning many of the most familiar packaged-food brands in North America, including Heinz ketchup and Kraft Macaroni & Cheese — whose packaged sauces sit in a very different culinary tradition from scratch emulsions such as hollandaise.

History

Heinz was founded in Pittsburgh in 1869 by Henry J. Heinz and built its name on ketchup and its "57 Varieties" slogan. Kraft grew from James L. Kraft's Chicago cheese business, founded in 1903, into a vast grocery conglomerate. In 2013 the Brazilian private-equity firm 3G Capital, together with Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway, bought Heinz for about $23 billion. Two years later they merged Heinz with Kraft Foods Group in a deal valued at roughly $46 billion, creating Kraft Heinz, with 3G's cost-cutting "zero-based budgeting" management model installed across the combined company.

Business and brands

The company's portfolio spans condiments and sauces (Heinz, HP, Lea & Perrins), cheese and dairy (Kraft, Philadelphia, Velveeta), meats (Oscar Mayer), meals (Kraft Dinner/macaroni and cheese), nuts (Planters), coffee and frozen products, sold in more than 190 countries. Its strategy has centred on a small number of large "iconic" brands and on platform categories such as "Taste Elevation" (sauces and condiments).

Criticism and setbacks

In February 2019 Kraft Heinz announced a $15.4 billion write-down of its Kraft and Oscar Mayer brands, cut its dividend, disclosed an SEC investigation into its accounting, and saw its share price fall by more than a quarter in one day — a landmark moment in criticism of aggressive cost-cutting in the packaged-food industry. The company has since restructured, sold its nuts business (Planters, to Hormel, 2021) and its natural-cheese business, and refocused on growth categories.
