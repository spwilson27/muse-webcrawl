Penalty shoot-out

A penalty shoot-out (officially "kicks from the penalty mark") is a tie-breaking method in association football used when a knockout match remains drawn after extra time. Each team takes five penalty kicks alternately; if still level, the shoot-out continues to sudden death.

Procedure

A coin toss decides which goal is used and which team kicks first. Only players on the field at the end of extra time may take kicks, and no player may kick a second time until all eligible teammates have kicked. Kicks are scored simply as made or missed; goals in the shoot-out do not count toward the match score or individual scoring records.

History

The shoot-out was adopted by FIFA in 1970, replacing lots and replays. The first World Cup final decided by a shoot-out was in 1994, when Brazil beat Italy; the 2006 and 2022 finals also ended in shoot-outs. Psychologically notorious, shoot-outs have produced famous moments from Antonín Panenka's chipped penalty in 1976 to England's long history of eliminations — and its Euro 2020 final defeat to Italy.
