Harry Wu

Harry Wu (February 8, 1937 – April 26, 2016) was a Chinese-American human rights activist. Wu spent 19 years in Chinese labour camps (laogai) after being labelled a rightist, and after emigrating to the United States he devoted his life to documenting the laogai system.

Imprisonment

Born in Shanghai to a prosperous family, Wu studied geology and was sent to labour camps in 1960 following criticism of the Soviet invasion of Hungary and the Communist Party. He survived famine and brutal conditions in camps across northern China before his release in 1979.

Activism

Wu emigrated to the United States in 1985. He founded the Laogai Research Foundation in 1992, made undercover return trips to China to film camps, and wrote memoirs including Bitter Winds. In 1995 he was arrested on a return trip and sentenced to 15 years in prison, but was expelled following international pressure. He later established the Laogai Museum in Washington, D.C.
