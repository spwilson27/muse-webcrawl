Alcázar of Seville

The Alcázar of Seville (Spanish: Reales Alcázares de Sevilla) is a royal palace in Seville, Spain, built for the Christian king Peter of Castile on the site of an earlier Muslim fortress. It is widely regarded as one of the most outstanding examples of Mudéjar architecture in Spain and is the oldest royal palace still in use in Europe; the Spanish royal family uses its upper apartments when in Seville.

History

The site was occupied from the 10th century by a fortified residence of the Muslim rulers of Seville, expanded under the Abbadid and Almohad dynasties. After Ferdinand III captured Seville in 1248, the complex became a residence of the Castilian monarchs. Pedro I (Peter the Cruel) rebuilt much of the palace in the 1360s, employing Muslim craftsmen from Granada and Toledo, producing the celebrated Mudéjar palace with its intricate tilework, stucco, and carved wooden ceilings.

Highlights

Notable spaces include the Patio de las Doncellas (Courtyard of the Maidens), with its sunken garden and lobed arches; the Salón de Embajadores (Ambassadors' Hall), crowned by a gilded cedar dome; the Patio de las Muñecas (Courtyard of the Dolls); and extensive gardens blending Islamic and Renaissance designs, with pavilions, fountains, and the Gallery of the Grotesque. Charles V added Renaissance elements, including tapestries and the rooms associated with the Admiral of the Indies, linked to the Casa de Contratación and the voyages to the Americas.

Recognition

In 1987 the Alcázar, together with Seville Cathedral and the General Archive of the Indies, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is one of Spain's most visited monuments and has served as a filming location for productions including Lawrence of Arabia and Game of Thrones (as the Water Gardens of Dorne).
