PageMaker was a pioneering desktop publishing application, introduced in 1985 by Aldus Corporation for the Apple Macintosh. Running with Apple's LaserWriter printer and Adobe PostScript page-description technology, it is widely credited with launching the desktop publishing (DTP) revolution, allowing users to compose pages of text and graphics on screen (WYSIWYG) and print camera-ready copy.

History

Aldus founder Paul Brainerd coined the term "desktop publishing" and marketed PageMaker alongside the Macintosh and LaserWriter as a low-cost alternative to professional typesetting. Early versions were criticised by typesetters but rapidly improved; the program's pasteboard metaphor — a virtual sheet on which text and image blocks could be placed and moved — became the standard interface model for page-layout software.

A Windows version followed in 1987. PageMaker's chief rival was QuarkXPress, which came to dominate professional publishing in the 1990s. Adobe acquired Aldus in 1994. Adobe positioned the more powerful InDesign, released in 1999, as PageMaker's successor for professional work, while PageMaker continued briefly for business users; development ended after version 7.0 (2001), with a final update in 2004.

Features

PageMaker popularised style sheets, master pages, text wrap around graphics, and plug-in extensibility in consumer page layout. Its native file format was superseded by InDesign's, and Adobe shipped converters for legacy documents.
