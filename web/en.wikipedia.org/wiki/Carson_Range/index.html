The Carson Range is a spur of the Sierra Nevada in eastern California and western Nevada in the United States. It lies primarily in Nevada, and separates the Lake Tahoe basin to the west from the valleys of Reno, Washoe Lake, and Carson City to the east.

Unlike the main crest of the Sierra Nevada, which forms a continuous divide, the Carson Range is the easternmost ridge of the northern Sierra in this area; the Truckee River crosses the Sierra west of Reno through a gap between the main crest and the Carson Range.

Geography

The range runs roughly north–south for about 50 miles (80 km). Its highest summit is Mount Rose at 10,785 feet (3,287 m), northwest of Reno. Other notable peaks include Slide Mountain, Snow Valley Peak, and Freel Peak on the southern end near Lake Tahoe — at 10,881 feet Freel Peak is the highest point in the Tahoe basin, though it is often considered part of the Carson Range's southern extremity or of the main Sierra crest.

The Mount Rose Highway (Nevada State Route 431) crosses the range between Reno and Incline Village, reaching a summit of 8,911 feet, the highest year-round pass in the Sierra Nevada. The western slopes of the range form the eastern shore of Lake Tahoe and lie partly within Lake Tahoe Basin Management Unit and Humboldt–Toiyabe National Forest lands.

Recreation

The Carson Range is popular for hiking, mountain biking, and skiing. The Tahoe Rim Trail traverses the range, and ski areas including Mount Rose Ski Tahoe and Diamond Peak operate on its slopes. The range takes its name from the frontiersman Kit Carson, who guided John C. Frémont's expedition across the Sierra in the winter of 1844 near Carson Pass to the south.
