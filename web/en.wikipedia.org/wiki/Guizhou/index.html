Guizhou
Capital: Guiyang
Area: ~176,200 km²
Population: ~38.5 million (2020)
A landlocked province in southwestern China.

Guizhou is a landlocked province in southwestern China, bordered by Sichuan and Chongqing to the north, Guangxi to the south, Yunnan to the west and Hunan to the east. Its capital and largest city is Guiyang. The province is known for its mountainous karst terrain, its humid subtropical climate, and its large population of ethnic minorities.

Geography

Guizhou is a mountainous province on the eastern end of the Yungui Plateau, with an average elevation around 1,100 metres. Its dramatic limestone (karst) landscapes include the Huangguoshu Waterfall, one of the largest waterfalls in Asia. The province has China's largest natural bridge and cave systems.

Demographics

About 37% of the population belongs to ethnic minorities, including the Miao, Buyi, Dong, Tujia, Yi and Gelao peoples — the Dong are renowned for their polyphonic "grand song" and drum towers, and the Miao for silverwork and embroidery festivals.

Economy

Historically one of China's poorer provinces, Guizhou has grown rapidly on the back of infrastructure building, hydropower, coal, tobacco (and baijiu — the famous Moutai distillery is at Maotai in Guizhou), and a major push into big-data centres, taking advantage of its cool climate and cheap electricity. Its many record-breaking high bridges, such as the Duge Bridge, have become symbols of its development.
