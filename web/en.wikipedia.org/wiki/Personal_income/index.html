Personal income

In economics, personal income is the total earnings of an individual from labour, investment, and other ventures, or, in national accounting, the aggregate income received by all individuals or households in a country. It is a key indicator used by statistical agencies such as the US Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) and the Census Bureau.

Definitions and measurement

The BEA defines personal income as income received from all sources, including compensation of employees, proprietors' and rental income, personal income receipts on assets (interest and dividends), and transfer receipts such as Social Security, minus contributions for government social insurance. Subtracting personal current taxes yields disposable personal income, the amount available for spending and saving. The Census Bureau measures money income differently, producing statistics such as median household income and per capita personal income, which are widely used to compare living standards between states and metropolitan areas.

Uses

Monthly personal income and outlays reports move financial markets because they track consumer earnings and spending power. Distribution statistics — income shares by quintile, and measures of inequality such as the Gini coefficient — inform debates on taxation, minimum wages, and welfare policy.
