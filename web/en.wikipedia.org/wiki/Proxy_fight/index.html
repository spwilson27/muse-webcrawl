Proxy fight

A proxy fight, proxy contest or proxy battle is a contest in which one or more shareholders of a public company seek to persuade other shareholders to vote their shares — by proxy — in favour of a change in corporate control or management, typically the election of a dissident slate of directors at the annual general meeting.

Mechanism

Because most shareholders do not attend meetings in person, votes are cast by proxy using proxy cards. Management solicits proxies with its own (in the United States, "white") card, while a dissident group files its own proxy statement with the Securities and Exchange Commission and mails a differently coloured card to shareholders. The universal proxy rules adopted by the SEC in 2022 require both sides' nominees to appear on a single card in contested US elections, letting shareholders mix and match candidates.

Strategy and use

Proxy fights are a classic tool of activist investors and hostile acquirers, who may seek board seats to force a sale of the company, a change of strategy, a spin-off, or the removal of executives. They are expensive — involving lawyers, proxy solicitors and public-relations campaigns — so they are often threatened and then settled, with the company agreeing to add dissident directors without a vote. Well-known contests include the 2017 fight at Procter & Gamble involving Nelson Peltz, then the largest proxy fight in history, and the 2022–2023 campaign at Disney.
