Belém Palace

LocationBelém, Lisbon, Portugal
Built1559 onwards; main palace 18th century
Architectural styleMannerist, Baroque, Neoclassical
Current useOfficial residence of the President of Portugal

The Belém Palace (Portuguese: Palácio de Belém) is a palace in the Belém district of Lisbon, Portugal, and the official residence of the President of the Portuguese Republic. The palace, recognisable by its pale pink main building, stands on the north bank of the Tagus River near the Jerónimos Monastery and the Belém Tower. Its name, like that of the district, derives from Bethlehem.

History

The earliest buildings on the site date from 1559, when Manuel de Almada built a small palace with gardens and fountains on land bought from the Jerónimos monks. In the early 18th century King John V acquired the estate and greatly enlarged it, adding the main corps de logis and riding school under architects including João Frederico Ludovice. The palace survived the 1755 Lisbon earthquake largely intact and was used by successive monarchs; Queen Maria I kept a small zoo in its grounds.

After the republican revolution of 1910, Belém became the official residence of the heads of state of Portugal, and it has served as the presidential palace and workplace ever since. The former royal riding school now houses the National Coach Museum (Museu Nacional dos Coches), one of the finest collections of historic carriages in the world. The palace gardens, with their box parterres and statues, overlook the Praça Afonso de Albuquerque; the changing of the guard takes place on the third Sunday of each month.
