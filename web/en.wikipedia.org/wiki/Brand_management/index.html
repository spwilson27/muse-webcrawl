Brand management

Brand management is a function of marketing that uses techniques to increase the perceived value of a product line or brand over time. Effective brand management enables the price of products to grow and builds loyal customers through positive brand associations and images or a strong awareness of the brand. Developing a strategic plan to maintain brand equity or gain brand value requires a comprehensive understanding of the brand, its target market, and the overall vision of the company.

History

Modern brand management is generally credited to a 1931 internal memorandum by Neil McElroy at Procter & Gamble, which proposed dedicated "brand men" responsible for a single product line, acting as internal advocates and coordinators of marketing activity. The system spread through the consumer packaged-goods industry after the Second World War and was codified in textbooks and by firms such as Unilever, Nestlé, and General Foods. In the late 20th century the emphasis shifted from individual products toward the brand as a strategic corporate asset, with concepts such as brand equity and brand architecture.

Key concepts

  • Brand equity — the commercial value derived from consumer perception of the brand name, rather than from the product itself.
  • Brand identity — the visible elements of a brand, such as name, logo, design, and tone of voice, that distinguish it in consumers' minds.
  • Brand positioning — the distinctive place a brand occupies in the minds of the target market relative to competitors.
  • Brand architecture — the structure of brands within an organization, ranging from a branded house to a house of brands.
  • Brand extension — the use of an established brand name on new products or categories.

Role of the brand manager

A brand manager is responsible for a brand's strategy, marketing mix, budgets, market research, and financial performance. The role involves monitoring market trends and competitors, planning advertising and promotions, managing packaging and pricing decisions, and coordinating with sales, supply chain, and product development teams. Digital media and social platforms have added responsibilities in online reputation management and direct consumer engagement.
