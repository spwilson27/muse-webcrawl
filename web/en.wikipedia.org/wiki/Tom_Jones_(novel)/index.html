The History of Tom Jones, a Foundling, often known simply as Tom Jones, is a comic novel by the English playwright and novelist Henry Fielding. It is a Bildungsroman and a picaresque novel, first published on 28 February 1749 in London, and is among the earliest English works to be classified as a novel.

Plot

Tom Jones, a foundling of unknown parentage raised by the kindly Squire Allworthy on his Somerset estate, grows into a good-hearted but impulsive young man whose escapades continually thwart his love for the virtuous Sophia Western. Egged on by the hypocritical Blifil, Allworthy's presumed heir, Tom is banished and travels the road to London, encountering rogues, soldiers, highwaymen and Man of the Hill along the way, while Sophia flees an unwanted marriage to pursue him. After a series of comic reversals, Tom's true parentage is revealed, clearing the way for his marriage to Sophia.

Style and significance

The novel runs to eighteen books, each prefaced by an essayistic chapter in which Fielding, through an intrusive omniscient narrator, discourses directly to the reader on his art. Praised for its meticulously engineered plot and panoramic portrait of Georgian society, Tom Jones influenced the development of the English novel; Samuel Taylor Coleridge judged its plot one of the three most perfect ever planned. It has been adapted many times, most famously as the 1963 film Tom Jones, which won the Academy Award for Best Picture.
