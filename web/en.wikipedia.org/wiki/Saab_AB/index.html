Saab AB (originally Svenska Aeroplan Aktiebolaget, "Swedish Aeroplane Corporation") is a Swedish aerospace and defence company founded in 1937. Headquartered in Stockholm, Saab produces the Gripen fighter aircraft, airborne early-warning systems, submarines, radar and sensor systems, missiles and military training equipment for customers worldwide.

History

Saab was formed to provide aircraft for the Swedish Air Force as Europe rearmed. Its first design, the Saab 17 reconnaissance bomber, flew in 1940. Post-war jet fighters such as the Tunnan, Lansen, Draken and Viggen embodied Sweden's policy of armed neutrality, pairing advanced aerodynamics with the ability to operate from dispersed road bases.

In 1947 Saab diversified into automobiles with the Saab 92. The car division was merged into Saab-Scania in 1969 and later sold to General Motors; Saab Automobile went bankrupt in 2011 and is legally separate from Saab AB, which retained rights to the Saab name and griffin trademark.

Products

Aeronautics

The JAS 39 Gripen multirole fighter, in its C/D and newer E/F versions, has been exported to countries including Brazil, the Czech Republic, Hungary, South Africa and Thailand. Saab is also a partner in the T-7 Red Hawk trainer developed with Boeing for the US Air Force, and produces the GlobalEye airborne early warning aircraft based on the Bombardier Global 6000.

Naval and underwater

Through Saab Kockums, the company builds submarines — including the Gotland class, noted for its air-independent propulsion, and the Blekinge class — as well as surface vessels and the Visby-class corvette.

Sensors and weapons

Saab's Giraffe radar family, Arthur weapon-locating radar, the Carl-Gustaf recoilless rifle, the NLAW anti-tank weapon and the RBS 15 anti-ship missile are widely exported.
