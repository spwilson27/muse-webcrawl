Bachelor of Science

A Bachelor of Science (abbreviated BSc, BS, or S.B.) is an undergraduate academic degree awarded for programmes in the sciences, engineering, technology, mathematics and related fields. It contrasts with the Bachelor of Arts (BA), which is more often associated with the humanities and social sciences, although the boundary varies by university and country.

Duration and structure

In most countries a Bachelor of Science takes three to four years of full-time study; four years is standard in the United States and Scotland, and three years in most of England, Wales, Australia and India. Some institutions distinguish between ordinary/pass degrees and honours degrees, the latter requiring additional coursework or a research dissertation.

History

The University of London was a pioneer in awarding the BSc as a distinct degree in the 1860s, separating scientific study from the traditional liberal arts curriculum. The degree spread widely through the British Commonwealth and the United States, where the BS became the standard first degree in scientific and technical subjects.

Variations

Some universities, such as Oxford and Cambridge, historically awarded the BA for all first degrees, including in sciences, although both now also award science-designated titles in some form. Latin formulae such as Baccalaureus Scientiae are used in some institutions. In the United States, some schools award the SB (Scientiae Baccalaureus), as at Harvard and MIT.
