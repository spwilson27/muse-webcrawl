Business oligarch

ConceptWealthy business figure with political influence
Associated withRussian oligarchs, Ukrainian oligarchs, post-Soviet transitions
Related conceptsOligarchy, plutocracy, crony capitalism

Business oligarchs combine large private fortunes with informal political power.

A business oligarch is a businessperson whose great wealth derives from, and is sustained by, close connections to political power, allowing them to exert disproportionate influence over government policy and public life. The term derives from "oligarchy", rule by the few, and rose to prominence in the 1990s to describe the magnates who amassed fortunes during the privatisation of state assets in post-Soviet Russia and Ukraine.

Post-Soviet context

During Russia's transition to a market economy, programmes such as voucher privatisation and the 1995 "loans-for-shares" scheme transferred controlling stakes in oil, metals, and media companies to a small group of businessmen at low prices. Figures such as Boris Berezovsky, Mikhail Khodorkovsky, and Roman Abramovich became emblematic of the class. Under Vladimir Putin, the Russian state reasserted control, prosecuting or exiling some oligarchs while others retained their fortunes in exchange for staying out of politics. In Ukraine, oligarchs such as Rinat Akhmetov and Ihor Kolomoyskyi built industrial and media empires that long dominated politics.

Wider usage

The term is also applied, more loosely and often polemically, to very wealthy business figures in other countries — including technology and media magnates — whose ownership of platforms or donations are said to confer political influence. Scholars distinguish the post-Soviet oligarch, whose property rights depend on state favour, from the merely rich entrepreneur in a rule-of-law system.
