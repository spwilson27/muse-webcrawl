Trade deadline

A trade deadline is a rule regulating the trading of professional players in sports leagues. In North American professional sports, it is a date near the end of a season after which teams are no longer permitted to trade players. The deadline is typically set in the second half of the season, so that contending teams can acquire players for a playoff push while struggling teams trade away veterans for future assets such as draft picks or prospects.

By league

In Major League Baseball, the trade deadline has traditionally fallen on July 31 (moved to nearby dates in some seasons); since 2019 it has been a single hard deadline, replacing the former August waiver-trade period. In the National Basketball Association, the deadline usually falls in early-to-mid February, roughly ten days before the All-Star break ended under older schedules. The National Hockey League deadline is typically in late February or early March, about 40 days before the end of the regular season. The National Football League deadline is usually in late October or early November, around the midpoint of the season, though trades in the NFL are historically less frequent.

Purpose and effects

Deadlines are intended to prevent wealthy or contending teams from stockpiling talent at the very end of a season and to preserve competitive integrity during playoffs. In practice, the approach of the deadline produces intense speculation and media coverage, and frequently a flurry of last-minute transactions as "buyers" and "sellers" assess their positions.
