The War of the Second Coalition (1798–1802) was the second war fought by Revolutionary France against a coalition of European monarchies, led by Britain, Austria and Russia, during the French Revolutionary Wars.

Course

The war opened with Napoleon Bonaparte's expedition to Egypt in 1798, where his fleet was destroyed by Nelson at the Battle of the Nile. In 1799 Austro-Russian armies under Alexander Suvorov drove the French from most of Italy, but coalition disunity and Russia's withdrawal weakened the alliance. Napoleon returned to France, seized power in the coup of 18 Brumaire, and in June 1800 crossed the Alps to defeat the Austrians at Marengo. General Moreau's victory at Hohenlinden followed in December.

Peace

Austria signed the Treaty of Lunéville (1801), confirming French gains on the Rhine and in Italy. Britain, left alone, concluded the Treaty of Amiens (1802), producing a short-lived general peace in Europe before war resumed in 1803 as the Napoleonic Wars.
