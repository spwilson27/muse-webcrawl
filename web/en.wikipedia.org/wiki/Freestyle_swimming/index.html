Freestyle swimming

The front crawl is used almost universally in freestyle races.

Freestyle is a category of swimming competition, defined by the rules of World Aquatics (formerly FINA), in which competitors may swim any stroke they choose, with a few exceptions. In practice, nearly all freestyle swimmers use the front crawl, the fastest stroke, so "freestyle" is often used as a synonym for the front crawl.

Rules

In freestyle events some part of the swimmer must break the surface of the water throughout the race, except that the swimmer may be completely submerged during the turn and for a distance of not more than 15 metres after the start and each turn. Medley swimming events include a freestyle leg, in which any stroke other than backstroke, breaststroke, or butterfly must be used.

Competition

Freestyle races are contested over distances from 50 metres to 1500 metres in pools, and in open water over longer distances. It is the most popular category of competitive swimming, featuring in the Olympic Games since 1896. Notable freestyle sprinters and distance swimmers include Ian Thorpe, Michael Phelps, Katie Ledecky, and Caeleb Dressel.

Technique

The front crawl involves alternating arm strokes, a flutter kick, and breathing to the side. Efficiency depends on body position, a high-elbow catch, and coordinated rotation.
