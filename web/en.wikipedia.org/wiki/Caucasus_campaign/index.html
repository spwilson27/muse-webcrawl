Caucasus campaign

The Caucasus campaign (1914–1918) was a theatre of the First World War fought between the Russian Empire and the Ottoman Empire (later involving Armenia, Azerbaijan, and Britain) across the Caucasus and eastern Anatolia.

Major operations

The Ottoman Third Army's offensive under Enver Pasha ended in disaster at the Battle of Sarikamish (December 1914–January 1915), where winter conditions and Russian counterattacks destroyed much of the army. Russian forces under Nikolai Yudenich then captured Erzurum, Trabzon, and Erzincan in 1916. The period saw the Armenian genocide carried out by the Ottoman authorities against the Armenian population.

Collapse and aftermath

The Russian Revolutions of 1917 dissolved the Russian Caucasus Army; the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk and later the Armistice of Mudros reshaped the region, leading to brief Armenian, Georgian, and Azerbaijani independence and the Turkish–Armenian War of 1920.
