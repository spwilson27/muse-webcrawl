Lobster roll

A lobster roll is a sandwich native to New England in the United States, made of lobster meat served in a grilled hot-dog-style bun. The filling usually consists of chunks of cooked lobster — often knuckle, claw, and tail meat — lightly dressed and sometimes mixed with diced celery or scallions, and the bun is typically a top-split roll buttered and toasted.

Styles

Two classic preparations dominate. The Maine style is served cold, with the lobster meat dressed in mayonnaise. The Connecticut style is served warm, with the meat tossed in melted drawn butter instead of mayonnaise and sometimes accompanied by lemon. Debates over which is authentic are a staple of regional food writing.

History

Lobster sandwiches appeared in New England as lobsters became a fashionable food in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The warm buttered version is often credited to Perry's restaurant in Milford, Connecticut, in the 1920s, while the cold mayonnaise version became the signature of Maine seafood shacks. The roll spread nationally in the twenty-first century as lobster shacks and fast-casual chains popularised it far beyond the coast.

Serving

Lobster rolls are commonly served with potato chips, French fries, coleslaw, or a pickle spear. Because lobster prices fluctuate with the catch, the sandwich is frequently sold at "market price".
