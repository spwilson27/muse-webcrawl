Third Swedish Crusade

The Third Swedish Crusade to Finland was a Swedish military expedition traditionally dated to 1293, directed against the pagan Karelians. The expedition was led by the regent Torkel Knutsson and resulted in the founding of the fortress of Viborg (Viipuri) at the head of the Gulf of Finland, consolidating Swedish control over western Karelia.

Background

The crusade followed earlier Swedish expeditions to Finland, conventionally numbered the First (possibly legendary, mid-12th century) and Second (around 1238–1249). By the late 13th century Swedish influence in Finland Proper and Häme was established, but the frontier with Novgorod in Karelia remained contested. Swedish ambitions were both religious — extending Latin Christianity — and strategic, seeking control of the trade routes around Lake Ladoga and the Neva.

The expedition

According to the Chronicle of Eric (Erikskrönikan), Torkel Knutsson, marshal of the realm and de facto regent during the minority of King Birger Magnusson, led a fleet eastward in 1293. The Swedes built the castle of Viborg on a small island in the bay, establishing a permanent garrison. The expedition is also associated with the founding of Kexholm (Käkisalmi) region campaigns and further fighting in 1294–1295 against Novgorod, during which the Swedes briefly advanced toward Lake Ladoga before being driven back.

Consequences

Viborg became the easternmost outpost of the Swedish realm and the seat of a major fief. The frontier between Sweden and Novgorod was later formalised by the Treaty of Nöteborg (1323), the first known peace treaty between the two powers, which split Karelia between them. The Third Crusade is thus conventionally treated as the final stage in the incorporation of Finland into the Swedish kingdom, a union that lasted until 1809.

Sources

The principal narrative source is the Chronicle of Eric, a rhymed chronicle composed in the 1320s. Modern historians debate the crusading character of the expedition and the reliability of the conventional tripartite division of the Swedish crusades to Finland.
