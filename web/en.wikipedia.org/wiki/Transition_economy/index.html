Transition economy

A transition economy or transitional economy is an economy which is changing from a centrally planned economy to a market economy. Transition economies undergo a set of structural transformations intended to develop market-based institutions, including price liberalisation, privatisation of state-owned enterprises, the creation of legal and financial systems, and opening to international trade and investment.

History

The term came into wide use after the fall of communism in Eastern Europe and the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1989–1991, when countries across Central and Eastern Europe and Central Asia began economic transformation. Reforms differed in speed and strategy: Poland adopted a rapid "shock therapy" programme (the Balcerowicz Plan) in 1990, while other countries pursued gradualism; Russia's mass privatisation in the 1990s was accompanied by a severe output decline. China and Vietnam followed distinctive gradual paths, liberalising markets while retaining one-party rule and significant state ownership.

Indicators and institutions

The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD), founded in 1991 to assist the transition, publishes transition indicators measuring progress in areas such as large- and small-scale privatisation, price liberalisation, trade and foreign exchange reform, competition policy, and banking and financial-sector reform. Common challenges in transition have included inflation, unemployment, corruption, and the weakness of legal institutions.
