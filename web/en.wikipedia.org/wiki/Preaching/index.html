Preaching

Preaching is the public proclamation of religious teaching, typically by a member of the clergy or an authorised teacher, intended to instruct, persuade, exhort, or console an audience. In Christianity the formal discourse delivered in worship is the sermon; comparable institutions include the Islamic khutbah delivered at Friday prayers, the Jewish derasha, and exhortations in many other traditions.

In Christianity

Christian preaching takes the ministry of Jesus and the apostolic kerygma as its model, and is grounded in scriptural commands such as the Great Commission. Paul of Tarsus, John Chrysostom ("the golden-mouthed"), and Augustine of Hippo were formative preachers of the early church. The medieval period saw the rise of the mendicant friars, who preached in marketplaces to lay audiences, and preaching manuals (artes praedicandi) systematised sermon structure. The Protestant Reformation elevated preaching to the centre of worship — pulpits replaced altars as the architectural focus — while the Catholic Counter-Reformation produced orators such as Jacques-Bénigne Bossuet. Later movements include Puritan "plain style", Methodist field preaching by John Wesley and George Whitefield during the Great Awakenings, and the global growth of Pentecostal and televangelist preaching in the twentieth century.

Forms and study

The academic study of preaching is homiletics. Sermons are commonly classified as expository (working through a biblical text), topical, or textual, and as deductive or inductive in structure. Contemporary preaching research also addresses narrative preaching, dialogical preaching, and the use of digital media and podcasts.

In other religions

In Islam, the khatib delivers the khutbah before the Friday congregational prayer and on Eid festivals; preaching and religious exhortation (da'wah) are central activities. In Judaism, sermons are delivered by rabbis, especially on Shabbat and festivals. Buddhist monks have preached sermons expounding the dharma since the time of the Buddha, and preaching tours by monks remain a feature of Theravada and Mahayana practice.
