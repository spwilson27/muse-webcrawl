Overview

The Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque is the main mosque in the Sultanate of Oman, in Muscat's Bawshar district. Commissioned by Sultan Qaboos bin Said to mark thirty years of his reign and inaugurated in 2001, it is built of Indian sandstone in a contemporary Islamic style, with a central minaret of about 90 metres and four flanking minarets.

Interior

The main prayer hall held records at its opening: its Swarovski chandelier (about 14 metres tall) was the world's largest, and its hand-woven Persian carpet — made by 600 weavers over four years in a single piece of some 4,300 m² and 21 tonnes — was the world's largest single-piece carpet until Abu Dhabi's Sheikh Zayed Mosque surpassed it in 2007. The mosque is one of the few in Oman open to non-Muslim visitors at set hours.
