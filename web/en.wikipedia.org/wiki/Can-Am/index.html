Can-Am

The Canadian-American Challenge Cup, abbreviated Can-Am, was a sports car racing series jointly organized by the Sports Car Club of America (SCCA) and the Canadian Automobile Sport Clubs (CASC). It ran from 1966 to 1987 and is remembered for its minimal rules, enormous engines, and some of the most powerful racing cars ever built. The original series folded in 1974 amid the oil crisis and rising costs; a modified version using Formula 5000-based cars ran from 1977 to 1987.

The original series (1966–1974)

Can-Am was created in 1966 as a series for two-seat, open-top sports prototypes grouped under FIA Group 7 regulations, which imposed almost no limits on engine size, turbocharging, or aerodynamics. The philosophy was often summed up as "unlimited" racing. Bruce McLaren's team dominated the early years, winning five consecutive championships from 1967 to 1971, leading rivals to nickname the series "the Bruce and Denny show" after McLaren and his teammate Denny Hulme.

The series reached its technological peak with the Porsche 917/30 of 1973, a turbocharged flat-twelve producing well over 1,100 horsepower in race trim and more in qualifying. Driven by Mark Donohue for Penske Racing, it won every race it finished that year, prompting rule changes for fuel consumption that effectively ended the formula. Other notable builders included Lola, Chaparral — whose innovative 2E and 2J cars pioneered wings and ground effects — and Shadow.

Revival (1977–1987)

In 1977 the SCCA revived the Can-Am name for a series based on the defunct Formula 5000 championship, with rebodied single-seaters wearing sports car bodywork. Drivers such as Al Unser, Jacky Ickx, and Patrick Tambay won titles, but the series never regained its former glory and ended after 1986, replaced in 1987 by a short-lived final season.

Legacy

The original Can-Am is celebrated as a golden age of essentially unrestricted racing innovation, and its cars remain stars of historic racing festivals such as the Goodwood Festival of Speed and Monterey Motorsports Reunion.
