Regions of Italy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The regions of Italy (Italian: regioni d'Italia) are the first-level constituent entities of the Italian Republic, constituting its second highest level of political subdivision after the national government. There are twenty regions, five of which have greater autonomy than the others, granted by special statute. Under the Italian Constitution, each region is an autonomous entity with defined powers. With the exception of the Aosta Valley and Friuli-Venezia Giulia, each region is divided into a number of provinces.

History

During the Kingdom of Italy, regions had no official status, although statistical compartments (compartimenti) modelled on the French departments were used for census purposes. The regions acquired formal status with the promulgation of the Constitution of the Italian Republic in 1948, which recognised, promoted and protected regional autonomy. The five special-statute regions (Sicily, Sardinia, Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol, Aosta Valley and Friuli-Venezia Giulia) were established first, between 1946 and 1963; the fifteen ordinary-statute regions were implemented in 1970.

Government

Each region has a directly elected president (presidente della giunta regionale) and a legislative council (consiglio regionale). A 1999 constitutional reform introduced the direct election of regional presidents. Regions have legislative power in all matters not expressly reserved to the state, including health care, which is largely administered at regional level.
