Amidah

The Amidah (Hebrew: תפילת העמידה, "the Standing Prayer"), also called the Shemoneh Esreh (שמונה עשרה, "the Eighteen", referring to its original eighteen blessings), is the central prayer of Jewish liturgy. Observant Jews recite it three times daily — at Shacharit (morning), Mincha (afternoon), and Ma'ariv (evening) services — standing, facing Jerusalem, and usually in a whisper; it is repeated aloud by the prayer leader in communal services.

Structure

The weekday Amidah consists of nineteen blessings (the original eighteen plus Birkat haMinim, added at Yavneh). The first three blessings are praise, including Avot (the patriarchs) and Gevurot (God's might); the middle thirteen are petitions — for knowledge, repentance, forgiveness, redemption, healing, agricultural prosperity, the ingathering of exiles, justice, the rebuilding of Jerusalem, and the Davidic kingdom; the final three are thanksgiving, including Avodah (temple service), Hoda'ah (gratitude), and Birkat Shalom (peace).

Variations

On Shabbat, festivals, and the High Holy Days, the middle petitions are replaced by a single blessing sanctifying the day, leaving seven blessings; additional services (Musaf, and Ne'ila on Yom Kippur) include their own Amidah forms. Wording differs slightly between Ashkenazi, Sephardi, and other rites, and Conservative and Reform prayer books introduce variations such as naming the matriarchs alongside the patriarchs.

Tradition attributes the prayer's fixed form to the Men of the Great Assembly and its later codification to Rabban Gamliel II at Yavneh in the 1st century CE, after the destruction of the Second Temple, when prayer took the place of the sacrificial service.
