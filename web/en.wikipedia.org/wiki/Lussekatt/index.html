Lussekatt

Alternative namesSaffron bun, lussebulle
TypeSweet bun
Place of originSweden
Main ingredientsFlour, saffron, sugar, raisins or currants
Associated feastSaint Lucy's Day (13 December)

A lussekatt (plural lussekatter, literally "Lucy cat") is a traditional Swedish saffron bun eaten during Advent, especially on Saint Lucy's Day, 13 December. The bun is flavoured with saffron, which gives it a vivid yellow colour, and is usually shaped into a reversed S with the ends curled, each curl centred with a raisin or currant.

Shapes and names

Although the S-shape — sometimes called julgalten ("Yule boar") — is the most common, bakers form many traditional shapes, including crosses, crowns, hearts and figures of girls and cats. The "cat" name may refer to the curled shape resembling a sleeping cat, or to older associations of Saint Lucy with light returning in the darkness of winter.

Tradition

Lussekatter are served with coffee or glögg at Lucia processions, in which a girl wearing a crown of candles leads a train of attendants. Saffron buns became widespread in Sweden in the late 19th century as saffron grew cheaper. Similar saffron buns are baked in Norway, and a related cornish saffron bun exists in Cornwall, England.
