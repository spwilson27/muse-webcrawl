St Andrews

St Andrews (Latin: Sanctus Andreas; Scots: Saunt Aundraes; Scottish Gaelic: Cill Rìmhinn) is a town on the east coast of Fife in Scotland, 10 miles (16 km) southeast of Dundee and 30 miles (50 km) northeast of Edinburgh. It had a recorded population of around 17,000 as of 2021. The town is named after Saint Andrew, the apostle, and is home to the University of St Andrews, the third-oldest university in the English-speaking world and the oldest in Scotland.

History

The settlement grew around the religious site associated with the relics of Saint Andrew, said to have been brought to the site by Saint Rule (Regulus). St Andrews Cathedral, consecrated in 1318, was the largest church in Scotland and the centre of the medieval Scottish church until it fell into disuse during the Scottish Reformation. The ruins of St Andrews Castle, once the residence of the bishops, overlook the sea.

Golf

St Andrews is known worldwide as the "home of golf". The Old Course at St Andrews, administered by the St Andrews Links Trust, is one of the oldest golf courses in the world, with the game played on the links since the 15th century. The Royal and Ancient Golf Club of St Andrews, founded in 1754, historically served as a governing body for the sport worldwide and now shares that role through The R&A. The Open Championship is regularly held on the Old Course.
