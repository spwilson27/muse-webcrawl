Roh Moo-hyun (Korean: 노무현; 1 September 1946 – 23 May 2009) was a South Korean politician and lawyer who served as the ninth president of South Korea from 2003 to 2008. A former human rights lawyer, he championed political reform, balanced regional development, and engagement with North Korea.

Life and presidency

Born into a poor farming family, Roh passed the bar exam in 1975 without a university degree and became a judge before opening a law practice in Busan, where he defended pro-democracy activists. He entered politics in 1988. As president, he continued the Sunshine Policy toward North Korea, held a summit with Kim Jong Il in Pyongyang in 2007, and pursued anti-corruption reforms. In 2004 the National Assembly impeached him, but the Constitutional Court reinstated him. His presidency saw strong economic growth alongside polarising debates over capital relocation and media reform.

Death and legacy

After leaving office, Roh retired to his home village of Bongha. In 2009, amid a bribery investigation involving his family, he died by suicide. His death prompted widespread mourning, and he remains an influential symbol for South Korea's progressive movement; his aide Moon Jae-in later became president.
