BGM-71 TOW

The BGM-71 TOW ("Tube-launched, Optically tracked, Wire-guided", pronounced /toʊ/) is an American anti-tank guided missile developed by Hughes Aircraft Company in the 1960s and first produced in 1970. It is one of the most widely used anti-tank guided missiles in the world, employed by more than 45 countries and mounted on ground vehicles, helicopters and tripod launchers. The missile is command-guided: the operator keeps the sight on the target while guidance commands travel to the missile along two thin wires that unspool from the missile in flight.

Development

Development began in 1961–62 under a US Army requirement for a heavy anti-tank weapon for infantry and helicopters. Hughes won the development contract, and the XBGM-71A entered production in 1970, replacing the 106 mm recoilless rifle and the French-designed ENTAC missile in US service.

Design

The missile is stored and fired from a smoothbore launch tube. After launch, wings and control fins deploy, and an infrared beacon in the missile's tail lets the sight tracker measure its deviation from the line of sight; steering commands are sent through the wire link. The standard warhead is a high-explosive anti-tank (HEAT) shaped charge. Improved TOW (ITOW), TOW 2, TOW 2A (with an extended probe for explosive reactive armour) and TOW 2B (a top-attack variant with two downward-firing warheads) progressively increased range, penetration and countermeasure resistance. A wireless version, TOW 2B Aero RF, replaces the wires with a radio link.

Service history

TOW first saw combat in 1972 in Vietnam, fired from helicopters and ground mounts against North Vietnamese armour. It has since been used in the 1973 Yom Kippur War, the Iran–Iraq War, the Gulf War of 1991, and numerous later conflicts, including extensive use by anti-government forces in the Syrian civil war and in the Russo-Ukrainian War. Platforms include the M2 Bradley, the Stryker, the Humvee, the AH-1 Cobra and dedicated M901 Improved TOW Vehicle.
