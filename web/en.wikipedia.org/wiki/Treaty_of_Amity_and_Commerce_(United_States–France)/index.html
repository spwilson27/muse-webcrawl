Treaty of Amity and Commerce (United States–France)

The Treaty of Amity and Commerce between the United States and France was signed on 6 February 1778 in Paris, together with the Treaty of Alliance. It was the first treaty concluded by the newly independent United States with a foreign power, and through it France formally recognized American independence from Great Britain.

Background

American envoys Benjamin Franklin, Silas Deane, and Arthur Lee negotiated in Paris while the American Revolutionary War was under way. News of the American victory at Saratoga convinced the French government of Louis XVI to commit openly to the American cause.

Terms

The treaty established commercial relations on a most-favoured-nation basis, protected shipping and merchants of both nations, and set rules for wartime conduct including the treatment of prizes and privateers. The accompanying Treaty of Alliance bound France to support American independence militarily, bringing France — and later Spain and the Netherlands — into war with Britain.

Aftermath

The Franco-American treaties reshaped the Revolutionary War into a global conflict. Relations later soured during the French Revolution, and the treaties were effectively ended by the Convention of 1800.
