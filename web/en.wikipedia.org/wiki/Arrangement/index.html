Arrangement (music)
Related terms: Orchestration, transcription, adaptation
Role: Arranger

In music, an arrangement is a musical adaptation of an existing composition. Differences from the original composition may include reharmonisation, paraphrasing or developing a theme, and changing the instrumentation or forces for which the piece is written. A person who writes arrangements is an arranger.

Classical music

Arrangements have long been a staple of classical music. Composers have reworked their own and others' music for centuries: piano reductions of orchestral scores made works accessible to domestic performers, while orchestral versions of piano pieces — such as Ravel's orchestration of Mussorgsky's Pictures at an Exhibition — created some of the repertoire's most popular concert works. An arrangement for a different instrument of the same family is often called a transcription, although the terms overlap in practice.

Popular music and jazz

In popular music, the arrangement is the total musical setting of a song: the choice of instruments, grooves, backing parts and harmonic treatment. In the big band era, arrangers such as Duke Ellington and Fletcher Henderson defined the sound of their orchestras, and in jazz a head arrangement is one worked out collaboratively and memorised rather than written down. Film and studio arrangers shape the final recorded sound far beyond the songwriter's melody and chords.
