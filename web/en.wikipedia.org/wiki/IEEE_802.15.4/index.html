IEEE 802.15.4

IEEE 802.15.4 is a technical standard which defines the operation of a low-rate wireless personal area network (LR-WPAN). It specifies the physical layer and media access control for LR-WPANs, and is maintained by the IEEE 802.15 working group, which defined it in 2003. It is the basis for the Zigbee, ISA100.11a, WirelessHART, MiWi, 6LoWPAN, Thread, and SNAP specifications, each of which further extends the standard by developing the upper layers not defined in IEEE 802.15.4.

Overview

The standard is intended to serve as the foundation for low-cost, low-speed ubiquitous communication between devices. It emphasizes low cost and low power consumption, trading off higher transfer rates: raw over-the-air data rates are 250 kbit/s in the 2.4 GHz band, 40 kbit/s in the 915 MHz band, and 20 kbit/s in the 868 MHz band. Later revisions added further physical layers and higher rates.

Network topologies

IEEE 802.15.4 networks can operate in star or peer-to-peer topologies. Devices are either full-function devices, which can serve as network coordinators and route traffic, or reduced-function devices intended for simple applications such as sensors. Addressing uses 64-bit IEEE addresses or short 16-bit addresses assigned by the coordinator.

Applications

Because of its low power requirements, the standard is widely used in home automation, industrial monitoring, smart lighting, wireless sensor networks, and Internet of Things devices.
