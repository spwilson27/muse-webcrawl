Astronomical clock
Displays: Time, calendar, zodiac, Sun and Moon positions
Famous examples: Prague Orloj (1410), Strasbourg Cathedral clock

An astronomical clock is a clock with special mechanisms and dials to display astronomical information, such as the relative positions of the Sun, Moon, zodiacal constellations, and sometimes major planets, together with the time and calendar date. Famous medieval examples were built for cathedrals and town halls across Europe.

Notable clocks

The Prague astronomical clock (Orloj), installed in 1410, is the oldest still operating; it shows Babylonian, Old Bohemian and modern time, sunrise and sunset, and features animated figures including the Apostles and Death. The Strasbourg Cathedral clock, in its third incarnation from 1843, computes Easter and displays planetary orbits.

Mechanisms

Astronomical dials typically centre on an astrolabe-like plate over which hands and rings rotate at different rates to model the sky. In China, Su Song's 11th-century water-powered tower clock anticipated such displays.
