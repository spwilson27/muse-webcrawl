Eleanor Rosch

Eleanor Rosch (born 9 July 1938) is an American psychologist, professor of psychology at the University of California, Berkeley, specializing in cognitive psychology. She is best known for her work on categorization, in particular her development of prototype theory, which she advanced in the 1970s.

Work

Rosch's research challenged the classical view that categories are defined by a set of necessary and sufficient features. In experiments conducted with the Dani people of Papua and with American undergraduates, she showed that categories have internal structure: some members are "better examples" (prototypes) than others, and categories are often organized around a privileged "basic level" (e.g., chair rather than furniture or kitchen chair).

Her work on colour categories, building on the research of Brent Berlin and Paul Kay, demonstrated that colour naming reflects perceptual salience centres (focal colours) rather than arbitrary linguistic boundaries. Prototype theory subsequently influenced cognitive science, linguistics — notably George Lakoff's work — and artificial intelligence.

Later in her career, Rosch turned to the psychology of religion and the cognitive science of concepts in contemplative traditions, particularly Buddhism.
