Matrimonial regime

A matrimonial regime, or marital property system, is the set of legal rules governing the ownership and management of property between spouses during a marriage and its division when the marriage ends through divorce or death. Legal systems typically offer a default regime that applies automatically, while allowing couples to choose an alternative, often by means of a marriage contract or prenuptial agreement.

Principal types

Under a community of property regime, assets acquired during the marriage are owned jointly by both spouses, usually in equal shares; this is the default in France, Spain, much of Latin America and the community-property states of the United States. Under separation of property, each spouse retains sole ownership of his or her assets, a system common as the default in many common-law jurisdictions. Hybrid regimes, such as the German Zugewinngemeinschaft (community of accrued gains), keep property separate during the marriage but equalise gains when it ends.

Choice of regime

Couples may opt out of the default regime by executing a marriage contract before a notary, particularly in civil-law countries. Choices affect liability for debts, inheritance rights and the treatment of business assets.

International aspects

Cross-border marriages raise conflict-of-laws questions about which country's regime applies. The European Union's Matrimonial Property Regulation (2016/1103) harmonises jurisdiction and applicable-law rules for participating member states.
