Burgos

Burgos is a city in northern Spain, the capital of the province of Burgos in the autonomous community of Castile and León. It lies on the Arlanzón river at the edge of the northern Meseta, on the Camino de Santiago, and had a population of about 175,000 in recent censuses. It was the historic capital of the Kingdom of Castile.

History

Burgos was founded in 884 by Count Diego Rodríguez Porcelos, acting for Alfonso III of Asturias, as a fortified frontier outpost against the Emirate of Córdoba. It became the capital of the County, then the Kingdom of Castile, and prospered in the later Middle Ages on the wool trade with Flanders and England, its merchants organised in the Consulado del Mar (1494). After the court moved permanently to Madrid in the 16th century the city declined, reviving as an industrial centre in the 20th century. During the Spanish Civil War, Burgos was the seat of Franco's Nationalist government from 1936 to 1939.

Sights

The Burgos Cathedral, begun in 1221, is one of the masterpieces of Spanish Gothic architecture and a UNESCO World Heritage Site; it contains the tomb of El Cid and his wife Jimena beneath the crossing. Other landmarks include the Arco de Santa María, the ruined castle on the hill above the city, the Monasterio de las Huelgas, the Cartuja de Miraflores, and the Museum of Human Evolution, which displays fossils from the nearby Atapuerca sites, among the most important in palaeoanthropology.

Burgos is renowned in gastronomy for morcilla de Burgos (blood sausage with rice), roast lamb (lechazo) and Ribera del Duero wines from the province.
