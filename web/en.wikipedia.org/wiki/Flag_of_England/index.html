Flag of England

Flag of England
Name: St George's Cross
Proportion: 3:5
Design: A red cross on a white field; the cross is centred and extends to the edges, its width being one fifth of the flag's height

The flag of England is the national flag of England, a constituent country of the United Kingdom. It consists of the red cross of Saint George — patron saint of England since the 14th century — on a white field: the cross is centred, extends to all four edges, and has a width one-fifth of the height of the flag. The design dates to the Middle Ages; the red cross on white was used by the city of Genoa and was adopted by England and the City of London in 1190 for their ships entering the Mediterranean, both for protection by the Genoese fleet and in honour of Saint George.

History

Saint George, a soldier-saint martyred in the early 4th century and reputed dragon-slayer, rose to prominence in England during the Crusades; Richard I and his troops adopted his cross in the late 12th century. Edward III made George patron saint of England and of the Order of the Garter (founded 1348), displacing Edward the Confessor. By the reign of Henry VIII the cross banner was the standard of English armies. Under the Stuarts it flew over English (and, in combination with the Scottish saltire after 1606, British) ships. When the United Kingdom's Union Jack was formed in 1606 and given its present form in 1801, St George's Cross formed its base, superimposed with the crosses of Saint Andrew (Scotland) and Saint Patrick (Ireland).

Usage

Within England the flag flies from government buildings in its own right and, more prominently, is the emblem of English sporting teams — above all the national football team — and their supporters, a visibility that grew markedly from the 1990s after devolution made English (rather than British) identity more salient; England teams in most sports use it instead of the Union Jack. The flag is also flown by the Church of England (often with the arms of the diocese in the canton) and forms the basis of the flags of several English cities, including the City of London and York. Because the England flag is not protected by statute, any citizen may fly it without permission.
