Infant baptism

Infant baptism (or paedobaptism) is the Christian practice of baptizing infants or young children. In theological discussions, the practice is sometimes contrasted with what is called "believers' baptism" (or credobaptism), in which only those able to make a profession of faith are baptized.

Practice

Infant baptism is practised by the Roman Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodoxy, Oriental Orthodoxy, and most mainline Protestant traditions including Anglicans, Lutherans, Presbyterians, Methodists and Congregationalists. Baptists, most Pentecostals and several other traditions baptize only professing believers.

Theological arguments

Supporters appeal to the doctrine of original sin, the covenant theology of circumcision as its Old Testament counterpart, and New Testament accounts of whole households being baptized. Opponents argue that baptism requires a personal confession of faith and repentance that infants cannot make, and that no explicit command or example of infant baptism appears in scripture.
