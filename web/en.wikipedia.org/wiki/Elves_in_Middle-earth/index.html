Elves in Middle-earth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Elves are a race in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, the Firstborn of the Children of Ilúvatar, who awoke before Men under the starlight at Cuiviénen. In Tolkien's works — The Hobbit, The Lord of the Rings, and The Silmarillion — Elves are immortal, tall, fair, keen-sensed, and skilled in craft and song, but their long history across the Ages is one of gradual decline and departure from Middle-earth.

Origins and sundering

The Elves awoke beside the lake Cuiviénen in the far east of Middle-earth. Summoned by the Valar, many undertook the Great Journey westward to Valinor, giving rise to the great division of the Elves: the Vanyar, Noldor, and Teleri who reached the Blessed Realm became the High Elves (Calaquendi), while those who refused or strayed — including the Sindar of Beleriand and the Silvan Elves — remained in Middle-earth. Their languages, Quenya and Sindarin, reflect this sundering.

History

The First Age is dominated by the Noldor's return in exile and their doomed war against Morgoth over the stolen Silmarils, as told in The Silmarillion: the realms of Doriath, Gondolin, and Nargothrond rose and fell, and figures such as Fëanor, Fingolfin, Lúthien, and Galadriel shaped the age. In the Second Age, Elven smiths of Eregion forged the Rings of Power, deceived by Sauron. In the Third Age the remaining Elves dwelt in fading refuges — Rivendell, Lothlórien, and the Grey Havens — and, after Sauron's defeat, most sailed west over the Sea, ending the Dominion of Elves and beginning the Age of Men.

Characteristics

Elves are immortal in the sense that they do not die of age or disease, though they can be slain or die of grief; their spirits then go to the Halls of Mandos and may be re-embodied. They are resistant to ageing's decay but bound to the life of the world, and their long memory makes them both wise and sorrowful. Tolkien insisted his Elves were not the diminutive fairies of folklore but a noble, human-like people representing humanity "enhanced" in beauty, craft, and closeness to nature.

Influence

Tolkien's Elves reshaped modern fantasy: tall, graceful, long-lived, and magically gifted Elves became a standard race in role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons and in the works of countless later authors, though Tolkien himself drew on Old English, Norse, Finnish, and Celtic traditions of the álfar and the Welsh hills' fair folk.
