Feeding America
Founded: 1979
Headquarters: Chicago, Illinois, U.S.
Focus: Hunger relief, food banking
Network: 200+ food banks

Feeding America is a United States–based nonprofit organisation that operates the nation's largest network of food banks. Through more than 200 member food banks and some 60,000 food pantries and meal programmes, it distributes food to tens of millions of people facing hunger across the United States. It is regularly ranked among the largest charities in the country by revenue.

History

The food banking concept began with John van Hengel, who opened St. Mary's Food Bank in Phoenix, Arizona, in 1967. In 1979 van Hengel helped found a national network, Second Harvest, to connect local food banks with national food donors. The organisation later became America's Second Harvest and, in 2008, relaunched under the name Feeding America.

Operations

Feeding America sources donated food from manufacturers, retailers, farmers and government programmes, as well as food drives and financial donations used to purchase food. Member food banks distribute it to local pantries, soup kitchens and shelters. The network reports distributing more than five billion meals' worth of food per year in recent years. The organisation also publishes research on food insecurity, notably the annual Map the Meal Gap study estimating hunger at county level.

Programmes

Initiatives include the BackPack Program providing weekend meals to children, school pantries, mobile pantries serving rural and underserved areas, senior grocery programmes, and disaster relief distribution following hurricanes and other emergencies.
