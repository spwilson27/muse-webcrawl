The New Jersey Meadowlands are a large ecosystem of wetlands in northeastern New Jersey, a few miles west of New York City, along the Hackensack and Passaic Rivers. The region, much of it within the Meadowlands District, hosts sports venues, warehouses and protected marshes.

History

Before development, the Meadowlands were extensive cedar swamps and salt marshes. From the 19th century onward, large areas were drained, filled and used for industry, agriculture (including pig farming) and landfills.

In 1969 the state created the Hackensack Meadowlands Development Commission (later the New Jersey Meadowlands Commission, merged into the NJSEA in 2015) to oversee development and environmental protection in the district.

Sports and development

The Meadowlands Sports Complex in East Rutherford includes MetLife Stadium, home of the NFL's New York Giants and New York Jets and a host venue of the 2026 FIFA World Cup; the Meadowlands Racetrack; and the American Dream retail and entertainment complex, opened in 2019.

Environment

Remaining wetlands provide habitat for migratory birds on the Atlantic Flyway and are the focus of restoration projects, including efforts to clean up the polluted lower Hackensack River. Parks such as Richard W. DeKorte Park in Lyndhurst offer trails and birdwatching.
