Perciformes, also called the Acanthopteri, is an order (or superorder) of ray-finned fish. In traditional classification it was by far the largest order of vertebrates, containing about 41% of all bony fish species — more than 10,000 species in about 1,600 genera and over 160 families, including perches, basses, cichlids, gobies, mackerels, tunas and many reef fishes.

Characteristics

Perciforms are typically characterised by spiny rays at the front of the dorsal and anal fins, pelvic fins placed far forward on the body (thoracic or jugular position) with one spine and up to five soft rays, and ctenoid (rough-edged) scales. Most are marine shorefishes, though many groups, such as cichlids, have radiated extensively in fresh water.

Classification

Modern phylogenetic studies using DNA have shown that the traditional Perciformes is not a natural (monophyletic) group. The fifth edition of Fishes of the World (2016) and subsequent classifications therefore split the old order into a series of smaller orders within the clade Percomorpha — such as Perciformes in the strict sense (perches and their closest relatives), Scorpaeniformes, Gobiiformes, Scombriformes and others — while retaining the familiar name for a reduced core group. The "perciform" body plan remains a useful descriptive grade even as the formal taxonomy changes.

Importance

The group includes many commercially important food fishes: tunas, mackerels, sea basses, snappers, groupers and perches, as well as popular aquarium fishes such as cichlids, angelfish and gobies.
