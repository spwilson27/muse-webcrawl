Sally Ride

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ride aboard Challenger, 1983.

Sally Kristen Ride (26 May 1951 – 23 July 2012) was an American physicist and astronaut. On 18 June 1983, at age 32, she became the first American woman in space and the youngest American to fly, as a mission specialist aboard the Space Shuttle Challenger on mission STS-7, where she operated the shuttle's robotic arm to deploy and retrieve a satellite.

Ride joined NASA as part of Astronaut Group 8 in 1978, the first class to include women. She flew a second mission, STS-41-G, in 1984, and was training for a third when the Challenger disaster grounded the fleet; she served on the Rogers Commission that investigated the accident, and later on the board that investigated the Columbia loss. After leaving NASA she became a physics professor at the University of California, San Diego, and co-founded Sally Ride Science to encourage girls in STEM education.
