Texas House of Representatives

Type: Lower house of the Texas Legislature

Seats: 150

Presiding officer: Speaker of the House

Term length: 2 years

Meeting place: Texas State Capitol, Austin

The Texas House of Representatives is the lower house of the bicameral Texas Legislature. It consists of 150 members elected from single-member districts for two-year terms. As of the 2020 census, each representative serves approximately 194,000 people. There are no term limits.

Composition and elections

Members must be at least 21 years old, residents of Texas for two years, and residents of their district for one year. Elections are held in November of even-numbered years. The House is presided over by the Speaker, who is elected by the membership; the Speaker wields substantial procedural power, including appointing committee chairs and members in consultation with the membership.

Function

Along with the Texas Senate, the House meets in regular session for 140 days in odd-numbered years. The House shares legislative power with the Senate: bills may originate in either chamber, but revenue bills must originate in the House. The House alone has the power to bring articles of impeachment, which are then tried by the Senate.

History

The House traces its origins to the Congress of the Republic of Texas. Notable episodes in its modern history include the "Killer Bees" quorum break of 1979 and the 2003 walkout by Democratic members to Oklahoma during mid-decade redistricting, as well as quorum-breaking departures in later redistricting fights.
