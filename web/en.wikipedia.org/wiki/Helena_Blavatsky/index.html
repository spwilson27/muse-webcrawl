Helena Blavatsky, co-founder of the Theosophical Society.

Helena Petrovna Blavatsky (1831–1891), often known as Madame Blavatsky, was a Russian and American occultist who co-founded the Theosophical Society in New York in 1875 with Henry Steel Olcott and William Quan Judge. Claiming to channel teachings from hidden Himalayan adepts, the "Masters of the Ancient Wisdom," she developed Theosophy, an esoteric synthesis drawing on Hinduism, Buddhism, Platonism, and Western occultism that played a major role in introducing Eastern religious ideas to the West.

Life

Born into an aristocratic family in Ukraine, Blavatsky travelled widely from her teens — she claimed journeys to Tibet, India, and the Americas that remain partly unverifiable — and worked variously as a circus rider, pianist, and businesswoman. In 1873 she emigrated to New York, where her alleged psychic phenomena drew attention; in 1879 she and Olcott moved to India, relocating the Society's headquarters to Adyar, Madras.

Works and controversy

Her two great works are Isis Unveiled (1877), an attack on materialist science and dogmatic religion, and The Secret Doctrine (1888), which presents a vast evolutionary cosmology of root races and universal cycles governed by karma and reincarnation. The Society for Psychical Research's Hodgson Report (1885) denounced her phenomena as fraudulent; the SPR later published a reassessment critical of that conclusion. Whatever the verdict on her claims, her influence on modern esotericism, alternative spirituality, and even Indian nationalism was enormous.
