Musical ear syndrome

Musical ear syndrome (MES) is a condition in which people with hearing loss experience musical hallucinations — hearing music, songs or singing that has no external source. It is considered analogous to Charles Bonnet syndrome, in which people with visual impairment experience visual hallucinations.

Characteristics

The hallucinated music is often a familiar tune, hymn or song from the person's past, and is typically perceived as coming from a radio or another room. Unlike musical hallucinations associated with psychiatric illness, people with musical ear syndrome generally retain insight that the sounds are not real. The condition is most common in older adults with significant hearing loss and is thought to arise from the auditory system's response to sensory deprivation, a phenomenon sometimes called "release" hallucinations.

Management

There is no standard treatment. Management may include improving hearing with hearing aids or cochlear implants, reducing background sensory deprivation, reassurance, and, in some cases, medication. Many sufferers find that the hallucinations lessen when ambient sound is increased.
