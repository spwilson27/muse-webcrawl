Royal Mile

Royal Mile
Location: Old Town, Edinburgh, Scotland
Length: One Scots mile (approx. 1.8 km)
Connects: Edinburgh Castle to the Palace of Holyroodhouse

The Royal Mile (Scottish Gaelic: Am Mìle Rìoghail) is a succession of streets forming the main thoroughfare of the Old Town of Edinburgh, Scotland. It runs downhill from Edinburgh Castle at the top to the Palace of Holyroodhouse and the ruined Holyrood Abbey at the bottom, a distance of roughly one Scots mile, from which it takes its name.

Sections

From west to east, the Royal Mile consists of Castle Esplanade, Castlehill, the Lawnmarket, the High Street, the Canongate and Abbey Strand. The Lawnmarket was historically the site of a market for luxury goods, while the High Street contains St Giles' Cathedral, the City Chambers and the Mercat Cross. The Canongate was formerly a separate burgh, home to the Canongate Tolbooth and, since 1999, the Scottish Parliament Building at its foot.

History and character

The street follows the tail of the crag-and-tail glacial ridge on which Edinburgh Castle sits. It is lined with tall tenement "lands", closes and wynds (narrow alleys) running steeply off either side. Notable landmarks include the Heart of Midlothian mosaic, John Knox House, the World's End pub, and Deacon Brodie's Tavern.

During the Edinburgh Festival Fringe each August, the Royal Mile becomes a hub of street performance and flyering. It is also the route of processions associated with the Opening of the General Assembly of the Church of Scotland and the Riding of the Marches.
