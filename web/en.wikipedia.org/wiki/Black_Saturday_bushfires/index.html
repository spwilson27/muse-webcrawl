The Black Saturday bushfires were a series of bushfires that ignited across the Australian state of Victoria on Saturday, 7 February 2009, amid a record heatwave and extreme fire weather. They remain Australia's deadliest bushfire disaster, killing 173 people and injuring more than 400.

Conditions and course

Melbourne recorded 46.4 °C, its hottest day on record, with humidity near zero and strong northerly winds ahead of a violent wind change. Fires started from sources including fallen power lines, lightning and arson; the Kilmore East fire, caused by a failed power line, raced south-east and devastated Kinglake, Strathewen and surrounding communities, while the Murrindindi fire destroyed the town of Marysville. Firestorms generated their own weather, including pyrocumulus clouds and lightning.

Aftermath

More than 2,000 homes were destroyed and about 450,000 hectares burnt. The Victorian Bushfires Royal Commission investigated the disaster and made 67 recommendations, leading to reforms in warnings, building codes in bushfire-prone areas and the advice to leave early on extreme days. Class actions against electricity companies produced major settlements, and one arsonist was convicted for the Churchill fire.
