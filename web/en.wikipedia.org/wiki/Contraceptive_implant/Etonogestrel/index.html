Etonogestrel

Etonogestrel is a progestin medication used in hormonal contraception, most notably as the active ingredient in subdermal contraceptive implants (Implanon and Nexplanon) and in the combined contraceptive vaginal ring (NuvaRing, with ethinylestradiol). It is the biologically active metabolite of desogestrel.

Medical uses

The etonogestrel implant is a single rod, about 4 cm long, inserted under the skin of the upper arm by a trained clinician. It releases a low daily dose of etonogestrel, primarily preventing pregnancy by inhibiting ovulation and thickening cervical mucus. It is approved for three years of use and is among the most effective reversible contraceptives, with a failure rate around 0.05% per year. Nexplanon, the current version, is radiopaque so it can be located by X-ray.

Side effects

The most common side effect is irregular menstrual bleeding, including amenorrhoea, which is also the most common reason for discontinuation. Other reported effects include headache, weight gain, acne and mood changes. Removal is performed through a small incision; fertility returns rapidly afterwards.

Pharmacology

Etonogestrel is a progestogen, an agonist of the progesterone receptor, with weak androgenic activity and no significant oestrogenic activity. It is also known as 3-ketodesogestrel.
