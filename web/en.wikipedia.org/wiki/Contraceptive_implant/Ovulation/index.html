Ovulation

Ovulation is the release of an egg (ovum) from an ovary. In humans, this event occurs when the ovarian follicles rupture and release the secondary oocyte ovarian cells. Ovulation typically occurs around the middle of the menstrual cycle—about day 14 of a 28-day cycle—after which the egg is available to be fertilised by sperm for roughly 12 to 24 hours.

Process

During the follicular phase, rising oestrogen from the developing follicle triggers a surge of luteinising hormone (LH) from the anterior pituitary. The LH surge, occurring about 24–36 hours before ovulation, causes the dominant follicle to rupture and release the oocyte, which is swept into the fallopian tube by the fimbriae. After ovulation, the ruptured follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone during the luteal phase.

Signs and detection

Signs of approaching ovulation can include changes in cervical mucus, a slight rise in basal body temperature after ovulation, and sometimes mid-cycle pain (mittelschmerz). Ovulation predictor kits detect the urinary LH surge. Fertility awareness methods use these signs to identify the fertile window.

Disorders and suppression

Failure to ovulate is called anovulation and is a common cause of infertility; it is associated with conditions such as polycystic ovary syndrome. Hormonal contraceptives work primarily by suppressing ovulation, while fertility drugs such as clomifene can induce it.
