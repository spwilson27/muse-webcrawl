Progestogen

Progestogens, also sometimes spelled progestagens or gestagens, are a class of steroid hormones that bind to and activate the progesterone receptor. The most important progestogen in the body is progesterone. Synthetic progestogens used in medicine are called progestins. Together with oestrogens, progestogens are the major sex hormones of females.

Biological functions

Progestogens prepare and maintain the endometrium for pregnancy, suppress ovulation during pregnancy, and support breast development. Progesterone is produced mainly by the corpus luteum after ovulation and by the placenta during pregnancy.

Medical uses

Progestins are used in hormonal contraception—including pills, injectables, the etonogestrel implant and hormonal IUDs—and in menopausal hormone therapy, where they protect the endometrium from unopposed oestrogen. They are also used to treat menstrual disorders, endometriosis and certain cancers, and to support early pregnancy in some settings.

Types

Progestins are classified by generation and chemical structure, including pregnanes (such as medroxyprogesterone acetate), norpregnanes, and estranes and gonanes derived from 19-nortestosterone (such as norethisterone, levonorgestrel and desogestrel). Different progestins vary in androgenic, antimineralocorticoid and glucocorticoid activity.
