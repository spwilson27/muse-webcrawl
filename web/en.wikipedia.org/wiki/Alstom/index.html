Alstom SA is a French multinational rolling stock manufacturer operating worldwide in rail transport markets. It is the builder of France's TGV high-speed trains, the first-generation Pendolino tilting trains, and a leading supplier of metros, trams, regional trains, and railway signalling. Following its acquisition of Bombardier Transportation in January 2021, Alstom became the world's second-largest rail equipment manufacturer, behind China's CRRC.

History

The company originated in 1928 as Alsthom, a merger of the French Thomson-Houston company and the Société Alsacienne de Constructions Mécaniques of Belfort, a historic locomotive builder. Through the 20th century it diversified into power generation, shipbuilding (Chantiers de l'Atlantique, builder of the Queen Mary 2), and transport. In 1989 its Anglo-French joint venture GEC Alsthom was formed; the group was listed in 1998 as Alstom. A financial crisis in 2003 required a French state-backed rescue. In 2014–15 Alstom sold its power and grid businesses to General Electric, refocusing entirely on transport.

Products and markets

Alstom's flagship products include the TGV family — including the AGV and the Avelia high-speed platform, with Amtrak's NextGen Acela in the United States built on Avelia Liberty — the Coradia range of regional trains, X'Trapolis suburban trains, Citadis trams (a fixture of cities from Dublin to Algiers), and Metropolis metro cars. It is a major supplier of signalling, including ETCS and its Urbalis CBTC metro system, and of services and maintenance contracts. Operators such as France's SNCF run Alstom stock across services including the low-cost Ouigo trains.

Bombardier acquisition and recent years

Alstom completed the €5.5 billion purchase of Bombardier Transportation on 29 January 2021, after EU approval conditioned on divestments, expanding strongly in Germany and North America. An earlier proposed merger with Siemens Mobility (announced 2017) had been blocked by the European Commission in 2019 on competition grounds. In 2023–24 the company worked through cash-flow and integration problems inherited from Bombardier contracts, raising capital and selling assets to reduce debt, while promoting hydrogen trains — its Coradia iLint entered the first regular passenger hydrogen service in Germany in 2022 — and automated metros.
