Nautilus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The nautilus (from Latin nautilus, from Ancient Greek ναυτίλος nautílos, "little sailor") is a marine cephalopod mollusc of the family Nautilidae. Nautiluses are the only living members of the subclass Nautiloidea, whose fossil record stretches back more than 500 million years; they are therefore often described as "living fossils". The family includes the living genera Nautilus and Allonautilus, with about six recognised living species.

Anatomy

The nautilus is best known for its large coiled external shell, divided internally into camerae (chambers) by septa. The animal lives in the outermost, largest chamber; the older chambers are filled with gas and liquid and are connected by a cord of tissue called the siphuncle, which the animal uses to regulate buoyancy by adjusting the gas and fluid content of the chambers — a principle echoed in submarine ballast tanks.

Unlike octopuses and squid, nautiluses have no suckers; instead they possess up to 90 short tentacles (cirri) arranged in two rings around the mouth, which secrete adhesive substances. They move by jet propulsion, drawing water into and expelling it from the mantle cavity through a funnel called the hyponome. Their eyes are simple pinhole eyes without lenses. The shell, with its brown zebra-striping above and white underside, is a classic example of countershading, and its spiral approximates a logarithmic spiral.

Habitat and behaviour

Nautiluses inhabit deep fore-reef slopes in the tropical Indo-Pacific, from the Andaman Sea to Fiji and from Japan to Australia's Great Barrier Reef, typically at depths of 100–300 metres by day and migrating to shallower water at night to feed. They are scavengers and opportunistic predators of shrimp, small fish and crustacean moults, locating food mainly by smell. They are long-lived for cephalopods, maturing at 10–15 years and living more than 20 years, but reproduce slowly, laying few large eggs.

Conservation

Because their ornate shells are traded as curios and in the shell trade, nautilus populations have declined in parts of their range. In 2016 the family Nautilidae was listed in Appendix II of CITES, regulating international trade, and several species have been assessed as threatened or vulnerable.

In culture

The nautilus inspired Oliver Wendell Holmes Sr.'s poem "The Chambered Nautilus" (1858), named Jules Verne's submarine Nautilus in Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas, and gave its name to the first nuclear-powered submarine, USS Nautilus (SSN-571).
