Mirza Ghulam Ahmad

Mirza Ghulam Ahmad (13 February 1835 – 26 May 1908) was an Indian religious leader from Qadian, Punjab, and the founder of the Ahmadiyya movement in Islam. He claimed to be the promised Mahdi and Messiah awaited by Muslims, a claim rejected by mainstream Islamic scholars but central to Ahmadi belief.

Life and claims

Ghulam Ahmad came from a landowning family of Mughal descent. From the 1880s he published works defending Islam in debate with Christian missionaries and Hindu revivalists, beginning with the multi-volume Barahin-e-Ahmadiyya. In 1889 he took the first initiation (bay'ah) from followers at Ludhiana, founding the Ahmadiyya community; in 1891 he announced his claim to be the Promised Messiah and Mahdi.

Teachings and legacy

He taught that Jesus had survived the crucifixion and died a natural death in Kashmir, rejected violent jihad in the conditions of his time, and wrote more than eighty books in Urdu, Arabic and Persian. After his death in Lahore in 1908, the community elected a caliph (Khalifatul Masih) and later split over the finality of his prophetic status into the Ahmadiyya Muslim Community and the Lahore Ahmadiyya movement.
