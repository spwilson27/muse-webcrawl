International Literacy Day

Date8 September
Observed byUNESCO member states
First observed1967
Related toLiteracy · UNESCO

International Literacy Day is an international observance celebrated each year on 8 September. It was proclaimed by UNESCO on 26 October 1966 at the 14th session of UNESCO's General Conference and was first celebrated in 1967. The day aims to highlight the importance of literacy to individuals, communities and societies, and to draw attention to the hundreds of millions of adults and children worldwide who cannot read or write.

Background

The idea for the day arose from the World Congress of Ministers of Education on the Eradication of Illiteracy, held in Tehran in September 1965, which recommended a worldwide literacy day. UNESCO estimates suggest that global adult literacy has risen steadily — from roughly 76 per cent in 1990 to about 86–87 per cent in recent years — yet more than 750 million adults remain illiterate, about two-thirds of them women, and many millions of children leave school without basic reading skills.

Celebration

Each year UNESCO marks the day with a conference and ceremony, usually at its Paris headquarters or in a host country, centred on an annual theme. Recent themes have included literacy in a digital world, literacy for a human-centred recovery, and promoting multilingual education. On the day, UNESCO awards its International Literacy Prizes — the UNESCO King Sejong Literacy Prize and the UNESCO Confucius Prize for Literacy — to outstanding literacy programmes.

Related initiatives

International Literacy Day forms part of broader United Nations efforts, including the United Nations Literacy Decade (2003–2012) and Sustainable Development Goal 4, which calls for ensuring inclusive and equitable quality education and promoting lifelong learning, with a specific target that all youth and a substantial proportion of adults achieve literacy and numeracy by 2030.
