Calvinism (Reformed theology) is the Protestant tradition flowing from John Calvin's Geneva, systematised in his Institutes of the Christian Religion (final edition 1559). It stresses God's absolute sovereignty, scripture's authority, and — in its later five-point summary (TULIP) — total depravity, unconditional election, limited atonement, irresistible grace and perseverance of the saints: salvation wholly by grace, predestined, yet lived in disciplined worldly vocation.

Influence

Reformed churches spread to France (Huguenots), the Netherlands, Scotland (Presbyterianism under Knox), and Puritan New England; Weber famously linked its inner-worldly asceticism to capitalism's spirit — a thesis debated ever since. Modern neo-Calvinism (Kuyper) and the global Reformed resurgence keep it a major theological current.
