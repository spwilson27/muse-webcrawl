Seven Dials

Seven Dials
Location: Covent Garden, London
Borough: Camden / City of Westminster
Laid out: 1690s

Seven Dials is a famous road junction in Covent Garden, London, where seven streets converge on a circular junction marked by a tall pillar bearing sundials. The surrounding neighbourhood, also called Seven Dials, lies between Covent Garden and Soho and is known today for its theatres, independent shops, and restaurants.

History

The layout was designed in the 1690s by Thomas Neale, who commissioned a Doric pillar topped with six sundials — the seventh "dial" being the pillar's own shadow falling on the junction. Neale intended an affluent development, but the area instead became known in the 18th and 19th centuries for poverty, gin shops, and lodging houses, a district Charles Dickens described in Sketches by Boz.

The pillar

The original pillar was removed in 1773 and later re-erected at Weybridge in Surrey. A replica, unveiled in 1989, was the first new public monument in London commissioned by a reigning monarch's initiative in many years, built with support from the Seven Dials Trust.

The neighbourhood today

Seven streets radiate from the junction: Earlham Street, Mercer Street, Monmouth Street, Neal Street, Neal's Yard (nearby), Short's Gardens, and Tower Street. The area is a conservation area and a centre of London's theatre district, home to the Donmar Warehouse and close to the Cambridge Theatre, which stands on the junction itself.
