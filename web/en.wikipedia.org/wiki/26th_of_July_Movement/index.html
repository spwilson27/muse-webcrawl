The 26th of July Movement (Spanish: Movimiento 26 de Julio; abbreviated M-26-7) was a Cuban revolutionary organisation founded by Fidel Castro that overthrew the government of Fulgencio Batista in 1959. Its name commemorates the failed attack on the Moncada Barracks on 26 July 1953.

Origins

After the Moncada attack, Castro and his surviving followers were imprisoned on the Isle of Pines, then released under a general amnesty in 1955. In exile in Mexico, Castro reorganised the movement, met the Argentine doctor Ernesto "Che" Guevara, and trained a guerrilla expedition. In December 1956, 82 expeditionaries landed in Cuba aboard the yacht Granma; the survivors retreated to the Sierra Maestra mountains.

Revolutionary war

From the Sierra Maestra, the movement waged guerrilla warfare while an urban underground (the llano) organised sabotage, propaganda and fundraising in the cities. Radio Rebelde broadcasts built national support. In late 1958 the guerrillas descended from the mountains; after the victory at Santa Clara, Batista fled on 1 January 1959 and Castro's forces entered Havana.

Aftermath

The movement formed the core of the revolutionary government. In 1961 it merged with the Popular Socialist Party and the Directorio Revolucionario into the Integrated Revolutionary Organizations, the predecessor of the modern Communist Party of Cuba. The date 26 July remains Cuba's principal national holiday.
