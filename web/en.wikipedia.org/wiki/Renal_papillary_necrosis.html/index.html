Renal papillary necrosis

Renal papillary necrosis is a disorder of the kidneys involving necrosis (death) of the renal papillae, the tips of the renal pyramids where urine collects before entering the calyces. It is associated with conditions that reduce blood flow to the medulla, including diabetes mellitus, analgesic overuse (analgesic nephropathy), sickle cell disease, urinary tract obstruction and pyelonephritis.

Sloughed papillae can obstruct the urinary tract, causing flank pain, haematuria and colic. Diagnosis is by imaging and treatment is directed at the underlying cause, with relief of any obstruction.
