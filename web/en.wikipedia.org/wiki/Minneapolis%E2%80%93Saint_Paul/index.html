Minneapolis–Saint Paul is a metropolitan area in the U.S. state of Minnesota centred on the twin cities of Minneapolis and Saint Paul, which face each other across the Mississippi River. Known together as the Twin Cities, the two cities differ in character: Minneapolis is the larger and more modern commercial centre, while Saint Paul is the state capital and retains a more historic, European feel.

Geography and economy

The metropolitan statistical area extends across numerous counties in Minnesota and into western Wisconsin and is home to roughly 3.7 million people, making it one of the largest metropolitan areas in the Midwest. The region is a major centre for banking, manufacturing, food processing, retail, healthcare technology, and higher education, hosting the headquarters of companies such as Target, U.S. Bancorp, 3M (in nearby Maplewood), General Mills, Best Buy, and the Mayo Clinic in nearby Rochester.

Culture

The Twin Cities are noted for their theatre scene — including the Guthrie Theater — orchestras, museums, and extensive park systems. The area is served by Minneapolis–Saint Paul International Airport and by all four major North American professional sports leagues, with teams split between the two cities. The rivalry and interdependence of the two cities, which grew up separately — Saint Paul as a river port and Minneapolis around the milling power of St. Anthony Falls — is a defining feature of the region.
