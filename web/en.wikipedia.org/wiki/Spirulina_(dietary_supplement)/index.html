Spirulina is a dietary supplement and food ingredient made from the dried biomass of cyanobacteria of the genus Arthrospira, principally Arthrospira platensis and Arthrospira maxima. (True Spirulina species are not used commercially; the name survives as a trade term.) Spirulina is cultivated in open alkaline ponds and raceways in warm climates and sold as a blue-green powder, flake or tablet, commonly added to smoothies and health foods.

History

Algal cakes made from lake-dwelling cyanobacteria were eaten by the Aztecs around Lake Texcoco — the product called tecuitlatl — and spirulina-like biomass has long been harvested as dihé around Lake Chad in Africa. Commercial production began in the 1970s, with large farms established in Mexico, Thailand, China, India, Hawaii and California. NASA and the European Space Agency have studied spirulina as a compact, protein-rich food for long-duration space missions, and the United Nations' World Food Conference of 1974 described it as an excellent food resource.

Nutrition

Dried spirulina is roughly 55–70% protein by weight containing all essential amino acids, though with lower levels of methionine, cysteine and lysine than animal proteins. It provides B vitamins (but its vitamin B12 analogues are largely inactive in humans), iron, manganese, provitamin A carotenoids, gamma-linolenic acid, and the blue pigment-protein phycocyanin, from which a natural blue food colouring is also derived.

Health claims and safety

Spirulina is marketed with claims relating to cholesterol, blood sugar, immunity and fatigue. Evidence from small clinical trials suggests modest effects on blood lipids, but major medical bodies consider the evidence insufficient for most therapeutic claims. Safety concerns centre on quality control: open-pond cultivation can allow contamination with toxin-producing microcystis species (microcystins), heavy metals, or bacteria, so reputable producers monitor batches. Spirulina is generally not recommended for people with phenylketonuria or certain autoimmune conditions without medical advice.
