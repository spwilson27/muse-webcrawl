The UCI World Tour is the premier men's elite road cycling tour, sitting above the UCI ProSeries and the continental circuits in the global racing hierarchy. It comprises the most prestigious races in the sport, including the three Grand Tours — the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia and Vuelta a España — the five Monument classics, and other major stage races and one-day events in Europe, North America, Asia and Oceania.

History

The World Tour developed out of the UCI ProTour, launched in 2005 amid a prolonged dispute between the UCI and the organisers of the Grand Tours. A unified system emerged in 2009 with the UCI World Ranking, and the UCI World Tour name was adopted in 2011. Top-division teams, known as UCI WorldTeams, are licensed for multi-year periods based on sporting, ethical, financial and administrative criteria, and are guaranteed — and obliged — to enter every World Tour event.

Events

The calendar traditionally opens with the Tour Down Under in Australia in January and closes in the autumn with races such as Il Lombardia and the Tour of Guangxi. Points awarded at each event feed the UCI World Ranking, which determines rider and team standings used for licences and invitations.

Women's equivalent

The UCI Women's World Tour, launched in 2016, fulfils the same role for women's professional road racing.
