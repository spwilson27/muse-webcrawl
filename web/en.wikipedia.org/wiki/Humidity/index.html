Humidity

Humidity
Field: Meteorology, atmospheric science
Types: Absolute, relative, specific
Instrument: Hygrometer, psychrometer
Related: Dew point, water vapour

Humidity is the concentration of water vapour present in the air. Water vapour, the gaseous state of water, is generally invisible to the human eye. Humidity indicates the likelihood of precipitation, dew, or fog.

Types

Absolute humidity is the mass of water vapour per unit volume of air. Relative humidity (RH) is the ratio, expressed as a percentage, of the current amount of water vapour to the maximum possible at that temperature; warm air can hold more moisture than cold air. Specific humidity is the ratio of water vapour mass to total air mass. The dew point is the temperature at which air becomes saturated and condensation begins.

Effects

High humidity reduces evaporation of sweat and makes heat feel more oppressive — captured by the heat index — while very low humidity dries skin and mucous membranes and increases static electricity. Humidity is controlled in museums, industry, and buildings to protect health and materials, and it is a key variable in weather forecasting and climate.

Measurement

Humidity is measured with hygrometers and psychrometers; satellite and radiosonde observations profile atmospheric moisture globally.
