Pacific Plate

The Pacific Plate is a oceanic tectonic plate that lies beneath the Pacific Ocean. With an area of about 103 million square kilometres, it is the largest tectonic plate on Earth. It underlies much of the Pacific basin and a sliver of coastal California and Baja California.

Boundaries

The plate is mostly surrounded by subduction zones, which correspond to the volcanic "Ring of Fire". Its north-eastern side is bounded by divergent and transform boundaries with the North American, Rivera, Juan de Fuca, and Cocos plates, including the San Andreas Fault along California. To the east it meets the Nazca Plate along the East Pacific Rise. To the south it borders the Antarctic Plate, and to the west it subducts beneath the Philippine Sea, Australian, and Okhotsk plates, forming deep trenches such as the Japan Trench and the Mariana Trench.

Motion and history

The Pacific Plate moves generally northwestward at roughly 7–11 centimetres per year relative to the interior of the plate. Its motion over stationary mantle hotspots created island chains such as the Hawaiian–Emperor seamount chain, whose change in direction about 47 million years ago records a change in the plate's direction of motion. The plate formed during the Jurassic period, about 190 million years ago, at a triple junction between the ancient Farallon, Phoenix, and Izanagi plates, and has since consumed much of those neighbouring oceanic plates by subduction.
