Ki-67, also known as antigen KI-67 or MKI67 (marker of proliferation Ki-67), is a nuclear protein that is associated with cellular proliferation. It is present during all active phases of the cell cycle (G1, S, G2 and mitosis) but absent from quiescent cells (G0), which makes it one of the most widely used markers of cell proliferation in pathology and cancer research.

History

The protein was identified in the early 1980s by Johannes Gerdes and colleagues at the University of Kiel, Germany, using a mouse monoclonal antibody raised against a Hodgkin lymphoma cell line. The name derives from its city of origin (Kiel) and the number of the original clone in the 96-well plate (67). The antibody clone MIB-1, raised later against a recombinant fragment, made Ki-67 staining possible on routinely processed, paraffin-embedded tissue.

Function

Ki-67 is a very large protein (about 320–359 kDa depending on isoform) rich in a repeated motif. It localises to the nucleolus during interphase and coats the surface of chromosomes during mitosis, where it acts as a biological surfactant — its positively charged, brush-like molecules keep condensed chromosomes from sticking to one another. It also participates in ribosome biogenesis and the organisation of heterochromatin. Surprisingly, cells can divide without Ki-67, though chromosome behaviour is less orderly; the protein is therefore required for efficient, faithful proliferation rather than for division itself.

Use as a biomarker

In diagnostic histopathology, the Ki-67 labelling index — the percentage of tumour cells staining positive — measures a cancer's growth fraction. High indices indicate aggressive growth and are used in grading neuroendocrine tumours (where Ki-67 thresholds define G1, G2 and G3 categories), in assessing breast cancer alongside hormone-receptor and HER2 status, and in lymphomas and other malignancies for prognosis and treatment decisions. Standardising counting methods (hot-spot versus average counts, manual versus digital image analysis) remains an active concern, coordinated in breast cancer by the International Ki67 Working Group.
