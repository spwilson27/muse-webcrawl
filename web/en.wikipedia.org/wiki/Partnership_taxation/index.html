Partnership taxation

Partnership taxation is the system of taxing the income of a partnership. In the United States, partnerships are governed for federal income tax purposes by Subchapter K of the Internal Revenue Code. A partnership is generally not itself taxed: instead it is a flow-through (pass-through) entity, and each partner reports their share of the partnership's income, gains, losses and deductions on their own return.

Key concepts

Partnerships file an informational return (Form 1065) and issue each partner a Schedule K-1 stating their distributive share. Partners track their basis in their partnership interest, which limits loss deductions and determines gain or loss on distributions and sales. Contributions of property are generally tax-free, and allocations of income and loss must have substantial economic effect to be respected.

Other jurisdictions

Many other countries likewise treat partnerships as transparent, taxing partners rather than the firm, though details vary — for example, the United Kingdom taxes individual partners on their share of profits while limited liability partnerships are treated similarly for most tax purposes.
