Korean Wave

The Korean Wave, or Hallyu (Korean: 한류, 韓流), is the dramatic increase in the worldwide popularity of South Korean culture since the 1990s — first in East and Southeast Asia, and from the 2010s globally. The term, literally "Korean flow", was coined by Chinese journalists around 1999 to describe the surge of Korean television dramas and pop music among Chinese audiences.

Development

After the 1997 Asian financial crisis and the lifting of cultural restrictions, South Korea deliberately cultivated cultural exports as an economic strategy. Television dramas such as Winter Sonata (2002), a sensation in Japan, and Jewel in the Palace (2003) established the first wave across Asia. A second, digital wave followed with K-pop: idol groups spread through YouTube and social media, PSY's "Gangnam Style" (2012) became the first video to reach a billion views, and BTS went on to top charts in the United States — the first Korean act at number one on the Billboard Hot 100.

In the 2020s the phenomenon extended to film and streaming television: Parasite (2019) won the Academy Award for Best Picture, and Squid Game (2021) became Netflix's most-watched series launch. Interest spills over into Korean food, cosmetics ("K-beauty"), fashion, tourism, and Korean-language study.

Impact

Hallyu is a significant source of export revenue and national soft power; the South Korean government supports it through cultural diplomacy, and studies credit it with measurable effects on tourism and on sales of Korean consumer goods. Scholars note contributing factors including high production values, hybrid East–West aesthetics, Confucian family themes with broad Asian appeal, and adept use of online fandom. Backlashes have also occurred, notably China's informal restrictions on Korean entertainment during the 2016–17 THAAD dispute.
