Queenborough

Population: approx. 3,700 (2021 Census)
District: Swale
County: Kent
Region: South East England
Country: England

Queenborough is a small town on the Isle of Sheppey in the Swale borough of Kent, England, situated near the mouth of the Swale where it meets the River Medway and the Thames Estuary. Despite its small size, it was once an important royal borough and naval port with a medieval castle.

History

Queenborough was founded by King Edward III, who built Queenborough Castle between 1361 and 1377 to guard the Swale channel; he named the new borough in honour of his queen, Philippa of Hainault. It was the only new royal castle built in England in the later Middle Ages, constructed to a concentric plan with a circular inner bailey—a design without close parallel in Britain. The borough received its charter in 1368 and sent two members to Parliament from 1572 until the Reform Act 1832, when it was disenfranchised as a rotten borough.

The castle was demolished under the Commonwealth in the 1650s, having been judged useless, and only earthworks and the well survive. In June 1667, during the Dutch Raid on the Medway in the Second Anglo-Dutch War, Dutch forces landed and briefly occupied the town. Samuel Pepys visited and recorded his impressions. From the eighteenth century the town developed oyster fisheries, and later industries included a glue and fertiliser works, glass manufacture, and potteries. Queenborough was also a port for cross-channel traffic; a pier served sailings to Flushing (Vlissingen) in the Netherlands in the late nineteenth century.

Geography and transport

The town lies at the western end of the Isle of Sheppey, near Sheerness. Queenborough railway station is on the Sheerness line, with services to Sittingbourne. The town has a harbour and creek used by fishing boats and leisure craft, and its guildhall, built in 1793, now houses a small museum.
