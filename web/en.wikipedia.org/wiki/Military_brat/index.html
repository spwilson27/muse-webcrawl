Military brat

Military brat (also known as a base brat) is a term for a child whose parent or parents serve full-time in the armed forces. The term is used in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and other countries with professional militaries, and is generally not considered pejorative — many brats embrace it as an identity. The dandelion, which scatters its seeds on the wind and roots wherever it lands, is a widely used symbol of military children.

Characteristics of brat life

Military families typically move every two to four years, so brats may attend six or more schools and grow up on bases in several countries. Common features of the upbringing include living in a close-knit, hierarchical community; familiarity with military ritual and rank; long parental absences during deployments, especially since 2001; and a sense of "home" defined by the service community rather than a place. Research describes brats as often adaptable, resilient and comfortable with diversity, while also noting challenges around rootlessness and the transition to civilian life.

Culture and identity

Scholars class military brats as a classic example of "third culture kids" — children raised in a culture distinct from both their parents' and their host countries'. In the United States, April is designated the Month of the Military Child. Organisations, reunions and a substantial memoir literature sustain a distinct brat subculture, and brats are statistically overrepresented among astronauts, actors and public figures relative to their share of the population.
