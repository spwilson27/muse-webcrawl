Sustainable Safety

Sustainable Safety (Dutch: Duurzaam Veilig)
Origin: Netherlands, 1992
Developed by: SWOV

Sustainable Safety (Dutch: Duurzaam Veilig) is a Dutch road-safety vision launched in the early 1990s aiming to prevent crashes by designing the road system around human limitations, rather than relying mainly on behaviour change.

Principles

The original five principles are: functionality of roads; homogeneity of mass, speed and direction; predictability of road course and user behaviour; forgivingness of the environment and road users; and state awareness by the road user. A 2018 update (Sustainable Safety 3rd edition) reorganised them around safe road design, safe speeds and human-centred principles.

Road categorisation

Roads are classed as through roads, distributor roads, or access roads, each with a distinct function, separated traffic where speeds or masses differ, and extensive use of roundabouts, 30 km/h zones and separated cycling infrastructure — contributing to the Netherlands' low road fatality rate.
