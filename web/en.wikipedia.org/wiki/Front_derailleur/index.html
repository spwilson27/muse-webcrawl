Front derailleur

A front derailleur is a mechanism for shifting gears on a bicycle, forming the front part of a derailleur drivetrain. It moves the chain sideways between the chainrings of the crankset when the rider operates a shifter, allowing the gear ratio to be changed while pedalling.


A front derailleur

Design

The front derailleur consists of a cage formed by two parallel metal plates through which the chain passes. A linkage, typically a parallelogram mechanism, moves the cage laterally. In cable-actuated systems a steel cable pulled by the shifter moves the linkage against the tension of a return spring. Electronic systems, such as Shimano Di2 and SRAM eTap, use a small electric motor instead.

Front derailleurs are mounted either by clamping around the seat tube or by brazed-on or direct-mount tabs on the frame. The position of the cage relative to the largest chainring must be carefully adjusted: typically the outer plate sits 1–3 mm above the teeth of the big ring.

Operation

Because the chain must be moving for a shift to occur, the rider must keep pedalling while changing gears. Front shifts move the chain across a large difference in ring size, so they produce a much bigger change in gear ratio than a single rear shift. Limit screws restrict the cage's travel so the chain is not pushed off the rings, and an indexing adjustment aligns the cage positions with the rings.
