Marine diesel engine

A marine diesel engine is a diesel engine designed for use aboard ships and boats, providing propulsion and auxiliary electrical power. Marine diesels range from small units in pleasure craft to the largest engines ever built, powering container ships and tankers.

Types

Slow-speed two-stroke crosshead engines, running at 60–120 rpm, drive the propeller directly and burn heavy fuel oil; examples include the MAN B&W and WinGD designs. Medium- and high-speed four-stroke trunk-piston engines are used in smaller vessels, ferries and as generator sets (diesel-electric drive).

Characteristics

Large two-stroke engines can exceed 80 MW of output with thermal efficiencies above 50 percent, among the most efficient prime movers in existence. The Wärtsilä-Sulzer RTA96-C, once the largest reciprocating engine in the world, stands about 13.5 metres tall and weighs over 2,300 tonnes.

Fuels and emissions

Traditionally fuelled by heavy fuel oil, marine engines increasingly use low-sulphur fuels, liquefied natural gas, methanol and other alternatives to meet IMO sulphur and greenhouse-gas regulations.
