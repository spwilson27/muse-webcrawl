Edgar Reitz (born November 1, 1932) is a German filmmaker best known for the Heimat trilogy, a monumental series of films chronicling German life in the twentieth century through the people of a fictional village in the Hunsrück region. He is a leading figure of the New German Cinema.

Heimat

Heimat: A Chronicle of Germany (1984) runs to nearly sixteen hours and follows the Simon family of the village of Schabbach from 1919 to 1982. Made for television, it was acclaimed internationally as a landmark of long-form filmmaking and as a counterweight to the American miniseries Holocaust, which Reitz felt had simplified German history.

Die Zweite Heimat (The Second Heimat, 1992) follows the young Hermann Simon to Munich in the 1960s and runs about twenty-five hours. Heimat 3 (2004) covers the years after German reunification. In 2013 Reitz released the feature prequel Home from Home: Chronicle of a Vision, set in the 1840s.

Other work

Reitz was a signatory of the 1962 Oberhausen Manifesto that launched the New German Cinema, and co-founded the influential film school at Ulm. His earlier features include Mahlzeiten (1967) and The Tailor from Ulm (1978).
