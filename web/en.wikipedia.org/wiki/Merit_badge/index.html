Merit badges are awards earned by youth members of Scouting America (formerly the Boy Scouts of America) based on activities within an area of study by completing a list of periodically updated requirements. The purpose of the merit badge program is to allow Scouts to examine a subject to determine whether they would like to further pursue it as a career or vocation. Originally, the program also introduced Scouts to the life skills of contacting an adult they had not met before, arranging a meeting, and then demonstrating skills — expectations used in a job interview or similar process.

Description

Each merit badge is a circular embroidered cloth patch about 1.5 inches (38 mm) in diameter, depicting a symbol of its subject — a canoe for Canoeing, a bugle for Bugling, a lifebuoy ring for Lifesaving. Badges are worn on a sash draped over the shoulder at formal occasions, or sewn to the sleeve in earlier decades. Scouts earn a badge by working with a registered merit badge counselor, completing the requirements published in the official pamphlet for the subject, and being signed off by the counselor and Scoutmaster.

Role in advancement

Merit badges are central to the ranks of Star, Life and Eagle Scout. For Eagle, a Scout must earn at least 21 merit badges, including a set of required ("Eagle-required") badges such as First Aid, Citizenship in the Community, Camping, Personal Fitness and Environmental Science or Sustainability. There are more than 130 merit badges covering sports, crafts, sciences, trades, business and outdoor skills; the earliest list, in 1911, offered 57, including subjects such as Taxidermy and Pathfinding that were later discontinued.
