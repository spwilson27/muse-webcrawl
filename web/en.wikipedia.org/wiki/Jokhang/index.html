Jokhang

LocationBarkhor Square, Lhasa, Tibet Autonomous Region, China
Foundedc. 647 CE
TraditionTibetan Buddhism (Gelug)

The Jokhang (Tibetan: ཇོ་ཁང་, "House of the Buddha") is a Buddhist temple in Lhasa, Tibet, and is considered the most sacred and important temple in Tibetan Buddhism. Located on Barkhor Square in the old city, it is listed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site centred on the Potala Palace.

History

The temple was founded in the 7th century, traditionally dated to about 647 CE, during the reign of Emperor Songtsen Gampo of the Tibetan Empire. It was built to house precious Buddha statues brought to Tibet by the emperor's two Buddhist wives: the Chinese princess Wencheng, who brought the statue of Jowo Mikyo Dorje, and the Nepalese princess Bhrikuti, who brought the statue of Akshobhya. The temple's name derives from the Jowo statue, the most revered object in Tibetan Buddhism.

The Jokhang was built on the site of a lake; according to legend, the lake was filled in and the temple constructed over it. Over the centuries the temple was enlarged and repeatedly renovated, notably under the 5th Dalai Lama in the 17th century.

Architecture

The Jokhang blends Tibetan, Chinese Tang-dynasty and Nepalese architectural styles. Its golden roof is visible across the Barkhor. The temple covers about 25,000 square metres and centres on a courtyard surrounded by chapels housing images of buddhas, bodhisattvas and protective deities. The most sacred chapel shelters the Jowo statue, a gilded bronze image of the Buddha Shakyamuni at age twelve.

Religious significance

Pilgrims from across Tibet prostrate themselves on the Barkhor circuit around the temple, and the Jokhang is the focal point of the Great Prayer Festival (Monlam), founded by Tsongkhapa in 1409. A popular Tibetan saying holds that "the Jokhang was built first, and Lhasa grew around it."
