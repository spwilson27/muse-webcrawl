Politics of India

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The politics of India are conducted within the world's largest democracy: a federal parliamentary republic under the Constitution of India (1950). The bicameral Parliament (Lok Sabha, directly elected by first-past-the-post in 543 constituencies; Rajya Sabha, largely elected by state legislatures) legislates alongside state assemblies in a quasi-federal system in which the Union government retains strong centralising powers. The president is head of state; the prime minister and council of ministers, responsible to the Lok Sabha, govern.

Party politics moved from Congress Party dominance (the “Congress system”, 1947–1989 with interruptions) through a coalition era of national and regional parties (1989–2014) to single-party BJP majorities from 2014 under Narendra Modi, on a Hindu-nationalist programme combined with welfare delivery and economic reform. Elections are administered by the Election Commission for an electorate approaching a billion voters; turnout and peaceful alternation remain democracy's strengths, while scholars' indices (V-Dem, Freedom House) have in recent years registered democratic backsliding concerns — media pressure, use of investigative agencies against opponents, and communal polarisation — which the government rejects as biased. Caste, religion, language and region structure political mobilisation as much as class, and coalition arithmetic at the centre changed again at the 2024 general election, which returned the BJP as largest party without a single-party majority.
