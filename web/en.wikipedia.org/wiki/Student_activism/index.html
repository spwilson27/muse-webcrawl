Student activism or campus activism is work by students to cause political, environmental, economic, or social change. Students have frequently been at the forefront of social movements, in part because universities concentrate large numbers of young people with the time, networks and relative freedom to organise.

History

Student movements played roles in nineteenth-century nationalism and in the revolutions of 1848 in Europe. In the twentieth century, students were central to the Chinese May Fourth Movement (1919) and, in the United States, to the civil rights movement—through organisations such as the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC)—and to opposition to the Vietnam War, which produced mass campus protests, the founding of Students for a Democratic Society (SDS), and the 1970 Kent State shootings of protesting students by the National Guard.

In 1968, student protests erupted worldwide, most famously in France in May 1968, where student demonstrations grew into a general strike. In 1989, Chinese students led the Tiananmen Square protests. Students were also prominent in anti-apartheid divestment campaigns of the 1980s, the 2010 Chilean and Quebec student protests over fees, and the global Fridays for Future climate strikes beginning in 2018.

Issues and methods

Campus activism addresses both university-specific concerns—tuition fees, divestment from fossil fuels or arms, campus speech policies, sexual-assault handling—and wider social questions such as war, racism and climate change. Typical methods include demonstrations, sit-ins and building occupations, teach-ins, student strikes and walkouts, petitions, and, increasingly, coordinated social-media campaigns. Student unions and dedicated activist organisations provide continuity between cohorts.

Debates

Student activism attracts debate over free speech on campus, the proper role of universities in politics, and the effectiveness of protest tactics; participants have historically faced sanctions ranging from expulsion to, in some countries, imprisonment and violence.
