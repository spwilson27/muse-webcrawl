Tea in the United Kingdom

Tea in the UK
Type: Black tea, usually with milk
Introduced: 17th century
Rituals: Afternoon tea, builder's tea, tea breaks

Since the 18th century, the United Kingdom has been one of the world's greatest tea consumers, and tea is a defining element of British culture. The British typically drink black tea with milk (and sometimes sugar), several cups a day.

History

Tea arrived from China in the 1650s and was popularised at court by Catherine of Braganza, wife of Charles II. Initially a luxury taxed heavily — encouraging smuggling — it became a mass drink in the 19th century, supplied by the East India Company and later by plantations in India and Ceylon. Anna Russell, Duchess of Bedford, is credited with establishing afternoon tea in the 1840s.

Customs

Customs include the tea break at work, builder's tea (strong, in a mug), tea with biscuits for dunking, and formal afternoon tea with sandwiches, scones with clotted cream and jam, and cakes. Debates persist over milk in first or last and the pronunciation and ordering of cream and jam on scones (the Cornish and Devon methods).

Consumption

Per-capita consumption historically ranked among the highest in the world and remains high, though coffee has gained ground. Tea is offered as a comfort in crises — a cultural trope summed up by the idea that a cup of tea solves everything.
