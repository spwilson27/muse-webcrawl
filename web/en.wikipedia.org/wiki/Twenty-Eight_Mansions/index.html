Twenty-Eight Mansions

The Twenty-Eight Mansions (Chinese: 二十八宿, èrshíbā xiù) are the divisions of the ecliptic in traditional Chinese astronomy. They form a system of lunar mansions analogous to, and possibly predating, the twelve-sign zodiac of Western astronomy, and were used to track the motion of the Moon and planets against the fixed stars.

Structure

The mansions are grouped into four palaces of seven mansions each, corresponding to the Four Symbols: the Azure Dragon of the East, the Vermilion Bird of the South, the White Tiger of the West and the Black Tortoise of the North. Each mansion is defined by a determinative star and has an unequal width measured in du (degrees).

History and use

The full system is attested by the 5th century BCE, notably on a lacquer chest from the tomb of Marquis Yi of Zeng (c. 433 BCE) inscribed with the mansion names. The mansions served in calendar-making, astrology and feng shui, and the system spread to Korea, Japan and Vietnam. Similar lunar mansion systems exist in Indian astronomy (nakshatra) and Arabic astronomy (manazil), and scholars have long debated the directions of influence among them.

Names

The eastern mansions are Horn, Neck, Root, Room, Heart, Tail and Winnowing Basket. The northern palace includes Legs, Bond, Stomach, Hairy Head, Net, Turtle Beak and Three Stars. Despite their name, the mansions are asterisms rather than single constellations, and several survive in the names of stars used in modern astronomy.
