Amharic (አማርኛ, Amarəñña) is an Ethiopian Semitic language, a member of the Semitic branch of the Afroasiatic family. It is the native language of the Amhara people and serves as the working language of the Ethiopian federal government and as a lingua franca across much of Ethiopia. Amharic is the second-most widely spoken Semitic language in the world, after Arabic.

History

Amharic developed in the southern Ethiopian highlands and became the language of the imperial court of the Solomonic dynasty, supplanting Ge'ez as the spoken language of the court by the 13th–14th centuries, though Ge'ez remained the liturgical and literary language. Written Amharic literature grew from royal chronicles and songs; it became the language of administration, education and media under the modern Ethiopian state.

Writing system

Amharic is written with the Ge'ez script (fidäl), an abugida in which each character represents a consonant–vowel syllable. The script has 33 basic characters, each in seven "orders" reflecting the seven vowels, plus additional labialised forms. Amharic uses its own punctuation, including the word separator ፡ and the full stop ።, and traditionally uses Ethiopic numerals alongside Arabic numerals.

Grammar and phonology

Amharic is a subject–object–verb language with complex verb morphology marking person, number, gender, tense, aspect and mood, and with ejective consonants typical of Ethiopian languages. It has absorbed vocabulary from Cushitic languages such as Oromo, as well as from Ge'ez, Arabic, Portuguese, Italian and English.
