Frank Vincent Zappa (December 21, 1940 – December 4, 1993) was an American composer, guitarist, singer and bandleader. His work is characterized by nonconformity, free-form improvisation, sound experimentation, musical virtuosity and satire of American culture. In a career spanning more than 30 years, Zappa composed rock, pop, jazz, jazz fusion, orchestral and musique concrète works, and produced almost all of the more than 60 albums that he released with his band the Mothers of Invention and as a solo artist.

Early life

Zappa was born in Baltimore, Maryland, to a Sicilian immigrant father and a mother of Italian and French descent. His father worked as a chemist and mathematician at the Aberdeen Proving Ground, and the family moved frequently, eventually settling in California. As a teenager Zappa became fascinated both by rhythm-and-blues and by modernist composers such as Edgard Varèse and Igor Stravinsky — a dual allegiance that shaped all his later work.

Career

In 1966 Zappa's band, renamed the Mothers of Invention, released its debut album Freak Out!, one of the first rock double albums and concept albums. Subsequent records such as Absolutely Free, We're Only in It for the Money and Hot Rats mixed doo-wop, jazz, orchestral writing and biting satire. In the 1970s he scored unlikely commercial success with songs such as "Don't Eat the Yellow Snow" and the album Sheik Yerbouti, and later with "Valley Girl" (1982), featuring his daughter Moon.

Zappa was also a prolific composer of orchestral music, performed by ensembles including the London Symphony Orchestra, and a pioneer of the Synclavier digital workstation. A fierce defender of free expression, he testified before the U.S. Senate in 1985 against the Parents Music Resource Center's campaign for warning labels on records.

Death and legacy

Zappa died of prostate cancer on December 4, 1993, aged 52. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1995 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 1997. His catalogue has been administered and reissued by the Zappa Family Trust, and his influence extends across progressive rock, jazz fusion and experimental music.
