The Monarchy of Spain (Spanish: Monarquía de España), constitutionally referred to as the Crown (La Corona), is a constitutional institution and the highest office of Spain. The monarchy comprises the reigning monarch, his or her family, and the royal household organization which supports and facilitates the monarch in the exercise of his duties and prerogatives. The Spanish monarchy is currently represented by King Felipe VI, Queen Letizia, and their daughters Leonor, Princess of Asturias, and Infanta Sofía.

The Spanish Constitution of 1978 re-established a constitutional monarchy as the form of government for Spain after the end of the Francoist regime and the restoration of democracy. The 1978 constitution affirmed the role of the King of Spain as the living personification and embodiment of the Spanish State and a symbol of Spain's enduring unity and permanence, while also acting as a moderator and arbiter in the proper functioning of its institutions.

History

The monarchy in Spain has its roots in the Visigothic Kingdom. The dynastic union of the Crown of Castile and the Crown of Aragon in the late 15th century, through the marriage of Isabella I of Castile and Ferdinand II of Aragon in 1469, laid the foundation for a unified Spanish monarchy. Their grandson, Charles I (better known as Emperor Charles V), inherited both crowns in 1516 and is generally regarded as the first King of Spain.

The Habsburg dynasty ruled Spain through the 16th and 17th centuries, presiding over a global empire. After the War of the Spanish Succession (1701–1714), the Bourbon dynasty came to the throne with Philip V. The monarchy was interrupted several times: by the First Spanish Republic (1873–1874), the Second Spanish Republic (1931–1939), and the dictatorship of Francisco Franco (1939–1975), who nevertheless declared Spain a kingdom in 1947 with himself as regent for life.

Franco designated Juan Carlos, grandson of Alfonso XIII, as his successor. Juan Carlos I became king on 22 November 1975, two days after Franco's death, and played a central role in Spain's transition to democracy, including opposing the attempted coup of 23 February 1981. He abdicated on 19 June 2014 in favour of his son, Felipe VI.

Constitutional role

Title II of the Spanish Constitution, articles 56 to 65, deals with the Crown. The King is the head of state, the symbol of the country's unity and permanence, and arbitrates and moderates the regular functioning of the institutions. The King's person is inviolable and he is not subject to responsibility; his acts must be countersigned by the appropriate government officials, who assume responsibility for them.

Functions of the King include sanctioning and promulgating laws, calling and dissolving the Cortes Generales, calling elections and referendums, proposing a candidate for President of the Government, appointing and dismissing members of the Government, issuing decrees, and being informed of the affairs of State. The King is also commander-in-chief of the Spanish Armed Forces and exercises the supreme command of them, although effective command rests with the government.

Succession

Succession to the throne follows male-preference cognatic primogeniture: sons take precedence over daughters, but women may inherit if there are no sons. The heir apparent holds the title Prince or Princess of Asturias. The current heir is Leonor, Princess of Asturias, elder daughter of Felipe VI.

Royal household

The Household of His Majesty the King (Casa de S.M. el Rey) supports the monarch. Royal residences include the Royal Palace of Madrid (the official residence, used for state ceremonies), the Palace of Zarzuela (the working and private residence), the Royal Palace of El Pardo, the Royal Site of Aranjuez, and others administered by Patrimonio Nacional.
