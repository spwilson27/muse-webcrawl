A chemical reaction is a process in which one or more substances (reactants) are converted into different substances (products) by breaking and forming chemical bonds. Reactions are written as chemical equations, balanced so that atoms of each element are conserved.

Everyday reactions include burning, rusting, cooking and respiration. Reactions may release energy (exothermic, such as combustion) or absorb it (endothermic, such as photosynthesis), and their speed depends on temperature, concentration, surface area and catalysts.

[Image: A colourful chemical reaction in a flask, with bubbles and a colour change]

Main types

Common classifications include combination (synthesis), decomposition, single displacement, double displacement, combustion, redox (electron transfer) and acid–base (proton transfer) reactions. Many real reactions fit several categories at once.

Energy and equilibrium

Reactions proceed toward lower energy and greater entropy, but many are reversible and reach a dynamic equilibrium, described by Le Chatelier's principle: a system disturbed by heat, pressure or concentration shifts to partly counteract the change.
