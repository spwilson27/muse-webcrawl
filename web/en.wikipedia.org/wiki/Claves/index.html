Claves (pronounced "KLAH-ves") are a percussion instrument consisting of a pair of short, thick wooden sticks, traditionally of rosewood or other dense hardwood. One stick is cradled in the cupped hand and struck with the other, producing a bright, penetrating clicking sound.

Role in music

Claves are the rhythmic heart of Afro-Cuban music: the player articulates the clave pattern — a five-stroke timeline (in son clave, either 3–2 or 2–3) around which son, salsa, and rumba are organized. Because the sound cuts through large ensembles, claves were adopted into bossa nova, Latin jazz, and orchestral percussion (e.g., in Steve Reich's Music for Pieces of Wood).

Technique

The resting hand is cupped to form a resonating chamber; the struck stick is held loosely and hit at its center for maximum resonance. Modern claves are also made of fiberglass for durability and volume.
