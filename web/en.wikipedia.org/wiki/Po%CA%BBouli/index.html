Poʻouli

Poʻouli
Scientific name: Melamprosops phaeosoma
Family: Fringillidae (Hawaiian honeycreepers)
Status: Extinct (2023 declared)
Native to: Maui, Hawaii

The poʻouli (Melamprosops phaeosoma) is an extinct species of Hawaiian honeycreeper that was endemic to the island of Maui. Unusual among honeycreepers, it fed largely on snails, insects, and spiders gleaned from the understory of wet rainforest on the windward slopes of Haleakalā. Its striking plumage featured a black mask, white cheeks, and brown upperparts; its Hawaiian name refers to its dark head.

Discovery and decline

The species was unknown to science until 1973, when students from the University of Hawaiʻi discovered it during the Hana Rainforest Project; it was described in 1974 as the sole member of a new genus. Fossil remains showed it had once occurred at lower elevations and on Molokaʻi. Its population collapsed owing to habitat loss, mosquito-borne avian malaria, and predation and competition from introduced species, including predation of its snail prey by the rosy wolfsnail.

Extinction

By 2002 only three individuals were known. Attempts to bring the birds together for breeding failed; the last captive bird died in November 2004, and the last confirmed sighting in the wild was that same year. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service formally declared the poʻouli extinct in 2023.
