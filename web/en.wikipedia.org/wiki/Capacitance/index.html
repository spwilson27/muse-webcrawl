Capacitance
SymbolC
SI unitFarad (F)
RelationC = Q / V

Capacitance is the capacity of a material object or device to store electric charge. It is measured by the charge in response to a difference in electric potential, expressed as the ratio of those quantities. Capacitance is defined as C = Q/V, where Q is the stored charge and V is the voltage.

Units and magnitude

The SI unit of capacitance is the farad (F), named after Michael Faraday. One farad is a very large capacitance for most purposes: practical capacitors range from picofarads (10−12 F) in electronics to farads in supercapacitors. The capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere of the radius of the Earth would be only about 710 microfarads.

Parallel-plate capacitor

For two parallel conducting plates of area A separated by a distance d filled with a dielectric of permittivity ε, the capacitance is approximately C = εA/d. Capacitance therefore increases with plate area and with the permittivity of the insulator, and decreases with separation. Inserting a dielectric also raises the maximum voltage before breakdown.

Energy storage

A charged capacitor stores energy U = ½CV² in its electric field. Capacitors in circuits block direct current while passing alternating current, and are used for filtering, timing, coupling, energy buffering, and sensing. Devices designed to exploit capacitance are called capacitors; unintended capacitance between conductors is termed stray or parasitic capacitance.
