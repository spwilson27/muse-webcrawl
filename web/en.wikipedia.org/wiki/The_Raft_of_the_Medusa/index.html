The Raft of the Medusa

The Raft of the Medusa (French: Le Radeau de la Méduse) is an oil painting of 1818–19 by the French Romantic artist Théodore Géricault (1791–1824). Enormous in scale (491 × 716 cm), it depicts the aftermath of the wreck of the French naval frigate Méduse off the coast of Mauritania in July 1816.

Subject

Of some 147 people set adrift on a hastily built raft, only 15 survived thirteen days of starvation, mutiny, and cannibalism before rescue by the brig Argus. Géricault shows the moment the survivors sight the distant rescue ship, arranging their bodies in a pyramidal composition that contrasts the dead and despairing in the foreground with the waving figures at the apex.

Significance

Exhibited at the Salon of 1819, the painting was a political indictment of the incompetent, aristocrat-appointed captain whose blunders caused the disaster, and it remains an icon of French Romanticism. It is held by the Louvre in Paris.
