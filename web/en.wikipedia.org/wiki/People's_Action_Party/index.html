The People's Action Party (PAP) is a political party in Singapore that has governed the country continuously since 1959, one of the longest-ruling elected parties in the world. Founded by Lee Kuan Yew and his colleagues, it led Singapore through independence from Malaysia in 1965 and its transformation into a global financial and trading hub, and is frequently analysed as a dominant-party system alongside Japan's LDP.

History

The PAP was founded in 1954 by English-educated professionals together with trade unionists, winning the 1959 general election that brought self-government. After merger with Malaysia (1963) and separation (1965), the party consolidated power, winning every seat in parliament from 1966 to 1981. Successive leaders — Lee Kuan Yew, Goh Chok Tong (1990), Lee Hsien Loong (2004) and Lawrence Wong (2024) — combined market-oriented economics with strong state direction in housing, education, savings (through the Central Provident Fund) and industrial policy.

Governance model

The PAP's model rests on meritocratic cadre recruitment, incorruptibility, multiracialism and long-term planning. Critics point to constraints on press freedom, defamation suits against opponents and electoral rules favouring incumbents, including Group Representation Constituencies; opposition parties have nonetheless increased their presence since 2011, and the Workers' Party won ten seats in 2020.
