East Grinstead is a town and civil parish in the Mid Sussex district of West Sussex, England, near the borders with Surrey, Kent and East Sussex. It lies about 43 km south of London and 34 km north-east of Brighton. The Greenwich Meridian passes through the town.

East Grinstead's High Street has one of England's longest runs of 14th-century timber-framed buildings.

History

East Grinstead developed as a market town on the London-to-Lewes road. Its High Street contains a celebrated row of timber-framed buildings, including Sackville College, a Jacobean almshouse founded in 1609. The parish church of St Swithun was rebuilt in the 18th century after its tower was struck by lightning. During the Second World War, on 9 July 1943, a German air raid bombed the town, killing 108 people, one of the worst civilian losses of life in a single raid on Sussex.

Points of interest

The town is known for the Queen Victoria Hospital, where during the Second World War the plastic surgeon Archibald McIndoe pioneered reconstructive surgery for burned airmen, whose patients formed the famous Guinea Pig Club. Nearby are Standen House, an Arts and Crafts house designed by Philip Webb, and the Bluebell Railway, a heritage steam line. The town has an unusually varied religious life and is a centre for several faiths and movements, including the UK headquarters of the Church of Scientology at Saint Hill Manor, once the home of L. Ron Hubbard.

Transport

East Grinstead station is the terminus of a branch of the Oxted line, with services to London Victoria. The town is a hub for the surrounding villages of the High Weald.
