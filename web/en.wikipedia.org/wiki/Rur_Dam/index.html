Rur Dam

Location: North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany
River: Rur
Construction: 1934–1939; raised 1955–1959
Reservoir: Rursee

The Rur Dam (German: Rurtalsperre Schwammenauel) is a dam in the Eifel mountains of western Germany, in the state of North Rhine-Westphalia, which impounds the Rur river to form the Rursee reservoir. Together with the nearby Urft Dam it regulates the Rur's flow through the region.

History

The dam was built between 1934 and 1939 and heightened between 1955 and 1959, increasing the reservoir's capacity to about 200 million cubic metres, making the Rursee one of Germany's largest reservoirs by volume. In February 1945, retreating German forces opened the discharge valves and damaged the dam's machinery to flood the Rur valley and delay the Allied advance during Operation Grenade.

Uses

The reservoir serves flood control, water supply, hydroelectric generation, and recreation; the Rursee is a popular area for sailing and tourism within the Eifel National Park region.
