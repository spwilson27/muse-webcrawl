Rhythm game

Rhythm games (or rhythm action) are a genre of music-themed action video games that challenge a player's sense of rhythm. Games in the genre typically focus on dance or the simulated performance of musical instruments, and require players to press buttons (or move) in a sequence dictated by the music and shown on screen.

Gameplay

The core mechanic is timing input to a beat: notes scroll toward targets, and the player scores by hitting the correct pad, button, key or movement at the correct moment. Performance is graded for accuracy, combos build multipliers, and failing too many notes can end the song.

History

The genre emerged in Japanese arcades and consoles in the mid-1990s. PaRappa the Rapper (1996) is often credited as the first true rhythm game, followed by Konami's Bemani line — Beatmania (1997), Dance Dance Revolution (1998) and GuitarFreaks — which popularised dedicated controllers such as dance mats and turntables.

Instrument games boom

In the 2000s, Guitar Hero (2005) and Rock Band (2007) turned rhythm games into a mass-market phenomenon with plastic guitar, drum and microphone controllers, sold with licensed popular music. The market peaked around 2008 and then declined sharply under the weight of oversaturation, though titles such as Beat Saber in virtual reality, Crypt of the NecroDancer, and mobile and indie rhythm games later revived interest.
