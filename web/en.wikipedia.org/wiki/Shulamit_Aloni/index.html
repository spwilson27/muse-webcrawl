Shulamit Aloni (Hebrew: שולמית אלוני; 29 November 1928 – 24 January 2014) was an Israeli politician and left-wing activist, a leading advocate of civil rights, separation of religion and state, and Israeli–Palestinian peace. She founded the Ratz (Citizens' Rights Movement) party and led the Meretz alliance, and served as Minister of Education under Yitzhak Rabin.

Early life

Aloni was born in Tel Aviv to parents who had emigrated from Poland. As a teenager she was a member of the Hashomer Hatzair youth movement and served in the Palmach during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, and was captured by Jordanian forces. She studied law, worked as a teacher and journalist, and hosted a popular radio programme on consumer rights, which led her into politics; her book The Citizen and His Country became a standard civics text.

Political career

Aloni was first elected to the Knesset in 1965 with the Alignment. Breaking with the Labour establishment over religious coercion and civil liberties, she founded Ratz in 1973, winning three seats on a platform of human rights, electoral reform, and a written constitution. She served briefly as a minister without portfolio in 1974. Ratz later joined Shinui and Mapam to form Meretz in 1992, winning 12 seats. As Minister of Education (1992–93) she clashed with religious coalition partners — notably over teaching evolution and over remarks about ultra-Orthodox pilgrimages — and was moved to other portfolios; she retired from the Knesset in 1996.

Activism and legacy

After politics Aloni remained a prominent voice for a two-state solution, women's rights, and secularism, co-founding the International Commission for Peace in the Middle East. In 2000 she received the Israel Prize for her special contribution to society and the state. She died in 2014 and was widely eulogised across the Israeli political spectrum as a fearless pioneer of civil rights.
