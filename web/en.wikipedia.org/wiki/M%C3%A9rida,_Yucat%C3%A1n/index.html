Mérida, Yucatán

Mérida (Spanish pronunciation: [ˈmeɾiða]) is the capital of the Mexican state of Yucatán, and the largest city in southeastern Mexico. The city is also the seat of the eponymous municipality. It is located roughly 35 kilometres (22 miles) inland from the coast of the Gulf of Mexico. In 2020 it had a population of over 920,000 in the city proper and around 1.2 million in its metropolitan area, making it the largest city on the Yucatán Peninsula.

The city was founded in 1542 by the Spanish conquistador Francisco de Montejo "el Mozo" and built on the site of the Maya city of T'Hó, which was also called Ichkanzihóo ("city of five hills") in reference to its pyramids. Many of the carved Maya stones of the old city were used in the construction of the Spanish colonial buildings.

History

Mérida was named after Mérida in Extremadura, Spain. During the colonial period it was a walled city; several of the old Spanish city gates survive. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the surrounding area prospered from the production of henequen, and the city became very wealthy, as reflected in its grand mansions along the Paseo de Montejo.

Culture

Mérida is a cultural center of the region, with numerous museums, art galleries, restaurants, and music venues. The city is noted for its distinctive cuisine, blending Maya, Spanish, Caribbean, and Middle Eastern influences, and for traditions such as the weekly vaquería regional dance performances in front of the municipal palace. It has frequently been ranked among the safest cities in Mexico and the Americas.
