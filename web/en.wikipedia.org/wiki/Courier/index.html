Courier

[Image: a bicycle courier delivering parcels in a city centre]
OccupationMessenger / delivery service
DistinctionPremium, tracked, time-sensitive delivery versus ordinary mail

A courier is a person or company that delivers messages, packages and mail, distinguished from an ordinary postal service by features such as speed, security, tracking, specialisation and individualised service. Couriers may be local — including bicycle and motorcycle messengers in cities — or global networks moving freight between continents, and they are typically used for items that are urgent, valuable or sensitive.

History

Organised couriers are ancient: the Persian Empire's mounted relay couriers impressed Herodotus, the Roman Empire ran the cursus publicus, and medieval rulers, merchants and universities all maintained messenger services. The Inca Empire used relay runners called chasqui along its road system. Modern courier companies emerged in the 19th and 20th centuries — Wells Fargo in the American West, and later air-express pioneers such as DHL (1969), FedEx (1971) and UPS — transforming logistics with hub-and-spoke air networks and computerised tracking.

Types of service

Services range from same-day local delivery and overnight national express to international door-to-door shipping, medical and legal couriers carrying transplant organs, specimens or documents, and onboard couriers who hand-carry critical items by air. "White glove" couriers handle art and antiques. Diplomatic couriers carry the diplomatic bag under the protection of the Vienna Convention. In recent decades, app-based gig-economy couriers have come to dominate food and parcel delivery in many cities, raising debates about employment status.
