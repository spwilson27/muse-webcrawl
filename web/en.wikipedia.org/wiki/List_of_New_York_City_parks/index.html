List of New York City parks

New York City has more than 1,700 parks, playgrounds, and recreation facilities across its five boroughs, encompassing about 30,000 acres (12,000 ha) — roughly 14% of the city's land. The parks are administered by the New York City Department of Parks and Recreation (NYC Parks), while some large parks are managed by conservancies or federal and state agencies.

Central Park, the city's most famous park, covers 843 acres.

Selected parks by borough

Manhattan

Central Park, Riverside Park, Fort Tryon Park (containing The Cloisters), Inwood Hill Park, Battery Park, Bryant Park, Madison Square Park, Washington Square Park, Tompkins Square Park, Union Square, and the High Line.

Brooklyn

Prospect Park, Brooklyn Bridge Park, Marine Park, Dyker Beach Park, Sunset Park, McCarren Park, and Fort Greene Park.

Queens

Flushing Meadows–Corona Park (site of the 1939 and 1964 World's Fairs), Forest Park, Astoria Park, Cunningham Park, and Alley Pond Park.

The Bronx

Pelham Bay Park — the city's largest — Van Cortlandt Park, Bronx Park (home to the Bronx Zoo and the New York Botanical Garden), Crotona Park, and Ferry Point Park.

Staten Island

The Greenbelt, Freshkills Park (being developed on a former landfill), Clove Lakes Park, and Silver Lake Park.

History

The city's park system grew from 19th-century movements for public green space; Central Park, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux, opened in stages from 1858. Under Robert Moses in the 1930s–1950s, hundreds of playgrounds, pools, and parkways were added.
