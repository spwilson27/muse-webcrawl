Rakı

TypeAnise-flavoured spirit
Country of originTurkey
ABV40–50%
Main ingredientsGrape pomace (suma), aniseed

Rakı is an unsweetened, anise-flavoured alcoholic drink that is a national drink of Turkey and popular throughout the Balkans and the Eastern Mediterranean. It is traditionally made from twice-distilled grape pomace, known as suma, flavoured with aniseed. Similar drinks include Greek ouzo, Bulgarian and Macedonian mastika, and Levantine arak.

Serving

Rakı is usually diluted with chilled water, which turns it a milky white — an effect known as the "louche" — earning it the nickname aslan sütü, "lion's milk". It is traditionally served with meze in taverns called meyhanes, often accompanied by melon and white cheese, or with fish.

History

Anise-flavoured spirits distilled from wine and pomace spread across the Ottoman Empire from the eastern Mediterranean. In the 19th century, rakı production was liberalised in the empire, and it became firmly established as Turkey's national spirit in the republican era, when state and private distilleries standardised production.
