Rock band

A rock band or rock group is a small musical ensemble that performs rock music. The classic line-up, established in the 1960s, consists of a lead vocalist, an electric guitarist, a bass guitarist and a drummer — a four-piece often called a quartet — though three-piece "power trios" and larger groups with keyboards, rhythm guitar or horn sections are also common. Vocals are often shared, with backing harmonies from other members.

History

The rock band format grew out of rock and roll groups of the 1950s and was codified in the early 1960s by the success of bands such as the Beatles and the Rolling Stones, whose self-contained model of writing and performing their own material displaced the solo-singer-plus-backing-band arrangement. From the 1970s onward, rock bands diversified into progressive rock, heavy metal, punk and alternative rock, while remaining the dominant commercial format of popular guitar music.
