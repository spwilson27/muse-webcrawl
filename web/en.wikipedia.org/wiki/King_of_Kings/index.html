King of Kings

King of Kings is a royal title used by several historical monarchies, originating in the ancient Near East. In Old Persian it is xšāyaθiya xšāyaθiyānām, and the title was used by the Achaemenid emperors, notably Cyrus the Great and Darius I, who ruled over numerous subordinate kings. The corresponding Middle Persian form, šāhān šāh (Shahanshah), was revived by the Sasanian Empire and used by Persian monarchs into the 20th century.

Historical usage

The title appears earliest in Assyria (šar šarrāni) and was employed by rulers of Babylon and Media. It was used in Armenia, Georgia (mepe-mepeta), and Ethiopia, where the emperor was styled Nəguśä Nägäśt, "King of Kings". The Macedonian and later Hellenistic kings adopted the Greek form basileus basileōn. In India the analogous title was Maharajadhiraja, "great king of kings".

Religious usage

In Judaism the phrase "King of Kings" (Melech Malchei HaMelachim) is a reverential circumlocution for God. In the New Testament it is applied to Jesus Christ, described in 1 Timothy and in Revelation (19:16) as "King of kings, and Lord of lords". The title also figures in Baháʼí writings, where Bahá'u'lláh addresses the rulers of the world collectively.
