The Asian Football Confederation (AFC) is the governing body of association football in Asia and Australia. Founded on 8 May 1954 in Manila, Philippines, during the Asian Games, it is one of the six continental confederations of FIFA and comprises 47 member associations organised into five regional federations: West (WAFF), Central (CAFA), South (SAFF), East (EAFF) and ASEAN (AFF).

History

The AFC was formed by twelve founding members including Afghanistan, Burma (Myanmar), Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Japan, Korea Republic, Pakistan, the Philippines, Singapore, Taiwan and Vietnam. Its headquarters moved to Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, and its flagship national-team competition, the AFC Asian Cup, was first held in Hong Kong in 1956 and won by South Korea. Australia joined the AFC from the Oceania Football Confederation in 2006.

Competitions

The AFC organises the Asian Cup and Women's Asian Cup, World Cup and Olympic qualifying tournaments, the AFC Champions League Elite and other club competitions, the AFC Futsal Asian Cup, and youth championships at under-23, under-20 and under-17 levels. Its club competitions were restructured into a three-tier system from the 2024–25 season.
