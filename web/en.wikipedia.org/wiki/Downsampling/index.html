Downsampling

In digital signal processing, downsampling, also called decimation, is the process of reducing the sampling rate of a signal, usually to reduce the amount of data to be stored or transmitted or to match the requirements of later processing stages. The opposite operation, increasing the sampling rate, is upsampling; together they enable multirate systems and sample-rate conversion.

Process

Downsampling by an integer factor M consists of two steps: first, applying a low-pass anti-aliasing filter to limit the signal's bandwidth to below the new Nyquist frequency; second, keeping only every M-th sample and discarding the rest. If the filtering step is skipped, high-frequency components fold back into the retained band as aliasing, an irreversible distortion.

Applications

Downsampling is used in digital audio (for example converting 96 kHz recordings to 44.1 kHz CD rate), image processing (creating thumbnails and image pyramids, where the equivalent operation is spatial decimation), communications receivers, and statistics, where it may also refer to selecting a subset of samples from a dataset — including the rebalancing of class distributions in machine-learning datasets.

Decimation in the frequency domain

In the frequency domain, downsampling stretches the spectrum and superimposes M copies of it; the anti-aliasing prefilter prevents the copies from overlapping and corrupting the baseband signal.
