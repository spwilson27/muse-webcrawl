The California Aqueduct, formally the Governor Edmund G. Brown California Aqueduct, is a system of canals, tunnels and pipelines that forms the backbone of the California State Water Project. It conveys water collected from the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta southward through the San Joaquin Valley, over the Tehachapi Mountains, and onward to Southern California, supplying irrigation water to farmland and drinking water to some 27 million Californians. It is one of the largest water-conveyance systems in the world.

Route and operation

Water enters the aqueduct at the Clifton Court Forebay in the Delta and is lifted by the Harvey O. Banks Pumping Plant into the main canal, a concrete-lined open channel that runs roughly parallel to Interstate 5 down the western side of the San Joaquin Valley. Near the southern end of the valley, the Edmonston Pumping Plant lifts the water 1,926 feet (587 m) over the Tehachapi Mountains — the highest single water lift in the world — in a series of pipes visible beside the Grapevine section of Interstate 5. South of the Tehachapis the aqueduct divides into West and East Branches, delivering water to reservoirs including Pyramid Lake, Castaic Lake and Lake Perris, and to treatment plants of agencies such as the Metropolitan Water District of Southern California.

The aqueduct is operated by the California Department of Water Resources. Its operation is closely tied to the ecology of the Delta, and pumping has at times been restricted to protect endangered fish species such as the delta smelt.
