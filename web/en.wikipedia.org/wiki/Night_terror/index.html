Night terror

SpecialtySleep medicine, psychiatry
SymptomsScreaming, sweating, confusion, rapid heart rate during sleep
Usual onsetChildhood (ages 3–12)
TypeNREM parasomnia

A night terror, also called a sleep terror, is a parasomnia in which a person — most often a child — suddenly sits up in bed screaming, sweating, thrashing and inconsolable, usually during the first third of the night in deep (slow-wave, NREM) sleep. The episode typically lasts a few minutes and is usually not remembered the next morning.

Causes and course

Night terrors arise from partial arousal from deep sleep and are associated with sleep deprivation, fever, stress and some medications; they tend to run in families. Most children outgrow them by adolescence. Adults with sleep terrors should be evaluated for other sleep or medical disorders.

Management

Treatment is usually reassurance and safety measures rather than medication: ensuring adequate sleep, keeping a regular schedule, and gently guiding the person back to bed without fully waking them. Scheduled awakenings and, rarely, medication may be used in severe cases.
