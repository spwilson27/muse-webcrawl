Franklin's lost expedition

Franklin's lost expedition was a failed British voyage of Arctic exploration led by Captain Sir John Franklin that departed from England in 1845 aboard the two ships HMS Erebus and HMS Terror. Tasked with traversing the last unnavigated section of the Northwest Passage in the Canadian Arctic, the expedition's ships became icebound in Victoria Strait near King William Island and all 129 officers and men were lost.

Date: departed 19 May 1845
Ships: HMS Erebus, HMS Terror
Complement: 129 officers and men
Outcome: all hands lost; wrecks found 2014 and 2016

Background

By 1845 the search for a sea route across the top of North America had narrowed to a single unexplored channel. The Royal Navy mounted its best-equipped attempt: the two bomb vessels were reinforced for ice, fitted with auxiliary steam engines and screw propellers, stocked with three years of provisions including thousands of tins of canned food, and carried a 1,200-volume library. Franklin, a veteran Arctic traveller, commanded Erebus; Francis Crozier captained Terror.

Disappearance

The expedition was last seen by Europeans in Baffin Bay in July 1845. The ships wintered at Beechey Island (1845–46), where three crewmen died and were buried. The only written record ever found, the Victory Point note, records that the ships were beset in ice from September 1846, that Franklin died on 11 June 1847, and that in April 1848 the 105 survivors abandoned the ships and set out on foot for the Back River and the mainland. None survived.

Searches and discoveries

Dozens of search expeditions were mounted from 1848 onward, spurred by Lady Jane Franklin. John Rae learned from Inuit testimony in 1854 that the men had starved, with evidence of cannibalism; Leopold McClintock found the Victory Point record and skeletons in 1859. Inuit oral history long held that one ship had sunk in deep water and the other farther south. Modern analysis has suggested lead poisoning from tinned food and botulism as contributing causes.

In September 2014 the wreck of Erebus was located in Queen Maud Gulf by a Parks Canada–led search guided by Inuit knowledge; in September 2016 Terror was found, remarkably intact, in Terror Bay off King William Island. The wrecks are jointly administered by Canada and the Inuit Heritage Trust.

Legacy

The disaster became a defining story of the Victorian heroic age of exploration, commemorated in song ("Lady Franklin's Lament", Stan Rogers's "Northwest Passage"), literature and the 2018 television series The Terror.
