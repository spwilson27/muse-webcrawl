Vishnu is one of the principal deities of Hinduism, worshipped as the preserver and protector of the universe within the Trimūrti, alongside Brahma the creator and Shiva the destroyer. For Vaishnavas, the largest Hindu tradition, Vishnu is not merely preserver but the Supreme Being himself. He is depicted as blue-skinned and four-armed, bearing the conch, discus, mace and lotus, reclining on the cosmic serpent Shesha upon the ocean of milk, with his consort Lakshmi, goddess of fortune.

Avatars

Vishnu descends into the world as avatars whenever dharma declines — a promise made in the Bhagavad Gita. The standard list of ten (Daśāvatāra) runs: Matsya the fish, Kūrma the tortoise, Varāha the boar, Narasiṃha the man-lion, Vāmana the dwarf, Paraśurāma, Rama, Krishna, the Buddha (in most lists), and Kalki, yet to come. The epics centre on Rama (Rāmāyaṇa) and Krishna (Mahābhārata), and stories such as the churning of the ocean of milk and Vishnu's three strides as Vāmana are among the best-known in Hindu mythology.

Worship

Vaishnavism's theologians — Rāmānuja, Madhva and later Caitanya — developed sophisticated devotional philosophies around Vishnu and his avatars. Major temples include Tirupati (Venkateswara), among the world's most visited pilgrimage sites, and the Jagannath temple at Puri, famous for its chariot festival. Vishnu's devotees mark the forehead with the U-shaped tilak.
