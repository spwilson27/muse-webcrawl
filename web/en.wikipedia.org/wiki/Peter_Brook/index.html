Peter Brook

Born: Peter Stephen Paul Brook, 21 March 1925, London, England

Died: 2 July 2022 (aged 97), Paris, France

Occupation: Theatre and film director

Known for: The Empty Space; Marat/Sade; The Mahabharata

Peter Stephen Paul Brook (21 March 1925 – 2 July 2022) was an English theatre and film director who worked first in England and later in France, where he was based from the early 1970s. Regarded as one of the most influential theatre directors of the 20th century, he sought to strip theatre back to its essentials — the actor, the audience and an empty space.

Career in England

Educated at Westminster School and Magdalen College, Oxford, Brook directed his first productions while still in his teens. He worked with the Royal Shakespeare Company through the 1950s and 1960s, directing landmark Shakespeare productions and, in 1964, Peter Weiss's Marat/Sade, which won the Tony Award for Best Direction on Broadway. His white-box production of A Midsummer Night's Dream (1970) — played on a bare set with trapezes and juggling — became one of the most celebrated Shakespeare stagings of the century.

His 1968 book The Empty Space classified theatre as Deadly, Holy, Rough and Immediate, and remains a foundational text of modern theatre practice. His films include Lord of the Flies (1963) and King Lear (1971).

Paris and the Bouffes du Nord

In 1970 Brook founded the International Centre for Theatre Research in Paris, and in 1974 took over the derelict Théâtre des Bouffes du Nord, whose scarred, unrestored interior became his signature performance space. With an international company he toured productions to quarries, villages and deserts. His nine-hour stage adaptation of the Indian epic The Mahabharata (1985), later filmed, was his most ambitious work. Later productions included The Suit, The Valley of Astonishment and a chamber Magic Flute (Une flûte enchantée).

Legacy

Brook won multiple Tony and Emmy Awards, the Praemium Imperiale and, in 2021, India's Padma Shri. He died in Paris in 2022, aged 97.
