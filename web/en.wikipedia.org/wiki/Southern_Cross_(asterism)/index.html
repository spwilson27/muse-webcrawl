Southern Cross (asterism)

The Southern Cross is a prominent asterism formed by the brightest stars of the constellation Crux, the smallest of the 88 modern constellations. Visible from the Southern Hemisphere throughout the year, its distinctive cross shape has served for centuries as a navigational aid for finding the south celestial pole.

Stars

Four bright stars form the cross: Acrux (Alpha Crucis) at the base, Mimosa (Beta Crucis), Gacrux (Gamma Crucis) at the top, and Imai (Delta Crucis). A fifth, fainter star, Ginan (Epsilon Crucis), sits between Acrux and Imai. The line from Gacrux through Acrux, extended about four and a half times, points close to the south celestial pole.

Cultural significance

The Southern Cross appears on the national flags of Australia, New Zealand, Brazil, Papua New Guinea and Samoa, and on numerous regional flags and emblems. Aboriginal Australian peoples incorporated the cross and the dark Coalsack Nebula beside it into Dreaming stories, while European sailors of the age of exploration treated their first sight of it as a landmark of southern seas.

False Cross

The Southern Cross is sometimes confused with the nearby, larger False Cross, formed by stars of Vela and Carina, which lacks the pointer stars and fifth star.
