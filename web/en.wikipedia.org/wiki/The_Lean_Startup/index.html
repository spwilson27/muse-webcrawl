The Lean Startup

Author: Eric Ries
Published: 2011
Publisher: Crown Business
Subject: Entrepreneurship, business methodology

The Lean Startup: How Today's Entrepreneurs Use Continuous Innovation to Create Radically Successful Businesses is a 2011 book by American entrepreneur Eric Ries. It presents a methodology for developing businesses and products under conditions of extreme uncertainty, drawing on Ries's experience at the startup IMVU and on principles adapted from lean manufacturing.

Core concepts

The method revolves around the Build–Measure–Learn feedback loop: build the smallest possible experiment, measure how customers respond, and learn whether to persevere or pivot — a structured change of strategy. Key tools include the minimum viable product (MVP), validated learning, innovation accounting, and continuous deployment. Ries argues that startups should treat their plans as hypotheses to be tested rather than predictions to be executed.

Influence

The book became a bestseller and its vocabulary — MVP, pivot, lean — entered mainstream business and technology culture. The lean startup movement influenced product management far beyond Silicon Valley, including established corporations and public-sector innovation programs, while also attracting criticism that its methods suit software better than other industries.
