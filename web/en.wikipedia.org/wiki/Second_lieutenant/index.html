Second lieutenant is a junior commissioned officer military rank in many armed forces. It is normally the lowest rank held by a commissioned officer, awarded on completion of officer training or graduation from a military academy, and precedes promotion to lieutenant (or first lieutenant).

Role

A second lieutenant typically commands a platoon or troop of some twenty to forty soldiers, assisted by a senior non-commissioned officer such as a platoon sergeant. In staff and specialist branches, newly commissioned officers may instead fill junior staff appointments while they gain experience. The probationary character of the rank is reflected in colloquial nicknames in several armies.

United Kingdom

In the British Army and Royal Marines, second lieutenant is the lowest commissioned rank; officers usually spend one to two years in the rank before promotion to lieutenant. The rank is denoted by a single pip on the shoulder strap. The Royal Air Force used the rank of pilot officer as its equivalent at commissioning.

United States

In the United States Army, Marine Corps, Air Force and Space Force, second lieutenant (grade O-1) is the entry-level commissioned rank. Its insignia is a single gold bar, giving rise to the slang term "butter bar". Officers are normally promoted to first lieutenant after roughly eighteen to twenty-four months of service. The United States Navy and Coast Guard use the equivalent rank of ensign.

Other countries

Equivalent ranks exist in most Commonwealth and many other armed forces, sometimes under translated titles such as sous-lieutenant in France and Canada or Sekondløjtnant in Denmark. In some countries the rank has been abolished, with officers commissioned directly as lieutenants.
