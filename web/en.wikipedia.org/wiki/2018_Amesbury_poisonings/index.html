The 2018 Amesbury poisonings occurred on 30 June 2018 in Amesbury, Wiltshire, England. Two British nationals, Charlie Rowley and Dawn Sturgess, were admitted to hospital in critical condition after being exposed to the Novichok nerve agent. Sturgess died on 8 July 2018, while Rowley recovered and was discharged later that month.

Background

The incident followed the poisoning of Sergei and Yulia Skripal in nearby Salisbury four months earlier, in March 2018, in which the same class of nerve agent was used. Police concluded that the Amesbury victims had been exposed after handling a contaminated item, believed to be a perfume bottle containing the agent, which Rowley had found and given to Sturgess.

Incident

On 30 June 2018, emergency services were called to a flat in Muggleton Road, Amesbury, after Sturgess collapsed; Rowley fell ill later the same day. Both were treated at Salisbury District Hospital. On 4 July, the Metropolitan Police announced that the pair had been poisoned by a Novichok agent, and a major incident was declared. Counter-terrorism officers took over the investigation.

Victims

Dawn Sturgess, a 44-year-old mother of three, died on 8 July 2018. Charlie Rowley survived after regaining consciousness, although he suffered serious health effects. An inquest and later a public inquiry examined the circumstances of Sturgess's death.

Investigation and response

Police searches recovered a small bottle from Rowley's home which was confirmed to contain Novichok. Hundreds of officers and military personnel took part in decontamination and search operations across Amesbury and Salisbury. The UK government stated that the agent was the same as that used against the Skripals, an attack it attributed to Russian military intelligence; Russia denied involvement.
