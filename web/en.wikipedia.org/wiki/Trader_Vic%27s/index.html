Trader Vic's

Trader Vic's is a restaurant and tiki bar chain founded in Oakland, California, by Victor Jules Bergeron Jr. (1902–1984), known as "Trader Vic". One of the two great names of the mid-20th-century tiki culture boom (alongside Don the Beachcomber), Trader Vic's is credited with creating the Mai Tai cocktail in 1944 and helped popularise Polynesian-themed dining and exotic rum drinks in the United States and abroad.

History

Bergeron opened a small bar called Hinky Dink's in Oakland in 1934. After a trip to the Caribbean and a visit to Don the Beachcomber in Hollywood, he remodelled it in a tropical style, renamed it Trader Vic's in 1937, and built a menu of Cantonese-influenced dishes cooked in Chinese wood-fired ovens alongside elaborate rum cocktails. A San Francisco location followed in 1951, and the chain expanded into hotels — notably Hilton properties — across the US and internationally.

The Mai Tai

Bergeron maintained that he created the Mai Tai in 1944 for friends visiting from Tahiti, one of whom exclaimed "Mai Tai — Roa Ae!" ("Out of this world — the best!"). His claim was long disputed by rival Donn Beach, but cocktail historians generally credit Bergeron with the recipe as it is known today.

Decline and survival

The tiki fad faded in the 1970s and 1980s and most locations closed, but a revival of interest in tiki culture in the 2000s sustained the remaining restaurants, including the long-running London location (opened 1963) and outlets in the Middle East and Asia.
