Iodised salt

Iodised salt (also spelled iodized salt) is table salt mixed with a minute amount of various salts of the element iodine. Its consumption prevents iodine deficiency, which affects about two billion people worldwide and is the leading preventable cause of intellectual and developmental disabilities.

History

Salt iodisation was pioneered in Switzerland and the United States in the 1920s, following trials showing that iodine supplements prevented goitre in schoolchildren. The practice spread globally, and in many countries iodisation of salt for human consumption is required by law.

Health effects

Inadequate iodine intake causes goitre and hypothyroidism, and in pregnancy can cause cretinism and reduced intelligence in children. Universal salt iodisation is regarded by UNICEF and the World Health Organization as one of the most cost-effective public health interventions; it costs only a few cents per person per year.

Chemistry

Iodine is added as potassium iodide, potassium iodate, or calcium iodate, typically at 15–40 mg of iodine per kilogram of salt. Iodate is preferred in hot, humid climates because it is more stable.
