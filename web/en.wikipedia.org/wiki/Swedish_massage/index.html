Swedish massage

Swedish massage is a form of massage therapy that involves manipulating soft tissues of the body with the hands, using techniques such as long gliding strokes, kneading, friction, tapping and vibration. It is the most common form of massage in Western countries and is the basis of many other types of therapeutic massage.

Techniques

The classic Swedish massage techniques were codified in the 19th century, drawing on the work of the Swedish fencing instructor and physical educator Per Henrik Ling, who developed a system of medical gymnastics, and the Dutch practitioner Johann Georg Mezger, who gave the techniques their French names:

  • Effleurage — long, gliding strokes, usually at the beginning and end of a session.
  • Petrissage — kneading, squeezing and rolling of the muscles.
  • Friction — deep, circular movements that cause layers of tissue to rub against each other.
  • Tapotement — rhythmic tapping or percussion with cupped hands or fingertips.
  • Vibration — fine shaking movements of the tissues.

Benefits

Swedish massage is used to promote relaxation, relieve muscle tension, improve circulation and reduce stress. Sessions typically last 30 to 90 minutes and use oil or lotion to reduce friction on the skin.
