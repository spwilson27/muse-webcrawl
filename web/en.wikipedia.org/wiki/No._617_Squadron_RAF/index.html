No. 617 Squadron RAF

Number 617 Squadron is a Royal Air Force aircraft squadron, originally based at RAF Scampton in Lincolnshire. It is most famous as the "Dambusters", the squadron formed in March 1943 to carry out Operation Chastise, the attack on German dams using Barnes Wallis's bouncing bombs. The squadron re-formed in 2018 as the RAF's first operational unit with the F-35B Lightning, based at RAF Marham.

Active1943–1955, 1958–1981, 1983–2014, 2018–present

Operation Chastise

On the night of 16–17 May 1943, 19 Avro Lancasters under Wing Commander Guy Gibson attacked the Möhne, Eder and Sorpe dams in the Ruhr valley. The Möhne and Eder dams were breached, causing catastrophic flooding. Of the 133 aircrew who took part, 53 were killed. Gibson was awarded the Victoria Cross.

Later wartime service

The squadron went on to drop the largest conventional bombs of the war, Wallis's Tallboy and Grand Slam, against targets including the battleship Tirpitz, which it helped sink in November 1944, U-boat pens and V-weapon sites.

Post-war

Post-war, 617 Squadron flew Canberras, Vulcans (including in the nuclear deterrent role) and Tornado GR1/GR4 aircraft, serving in the Gulf War and Afghanistan before disbanding in 2014. It stood up again in April 2018 as an F-35B unit, operating from RAF Marham and deploying aboard HMS Queen Elizabeth.
