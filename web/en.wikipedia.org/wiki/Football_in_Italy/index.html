Football in Italy

Football (calcio) is Italy's most popular sport.

Football (Italian: calcio) is the most popular sport in Italy. The Italy national football team is considered one of the best national teams in the world. They have won the FIFA World Cup four times (1934, 1938, 1982 and 2006), trailing only Brazil (five) and equalling Germany, and have won the UEFA European Championship twice (1968 and 2020). Italian clubs have won 29 major European trophies, making them the second most successful nation in European football.

History

The modern game was introduced to Italy in the late 19th century by British expatriates; Genoa Cricket and Football Club, founded in 1893, is Italy's oldest surviving club. The Italian Football Federation (FIGC) was founded in 1898, and the first championship was held the same year. The game also has a claimed indigenous ancestry in calcio fiorentino, a violent ball game played in Florence since the 16th century. Serie A, the top-flight league, was established in its single round-robin format in 1929.

Italy's club scene is dominated by the northern giants Juventus (Turin), AC Milan and Inter Milan, with strong followings for Roma, Lazio, Napoli and Fiorentina. Italian football has been renowned for tactical sophistication, famously the defensive catenaccio system, and has produced players such as Giuseppe Meazza, Gianni Rivera, Paolo Maldini, Roberto Baggio, Francesco Totti and Gianluigi Buffon.

Structure and culture

The professional league system runs from Serie A through Serie B and Serie C, with promotion and relegation. The Coppa Italia is the main domestic cup. Football is deeply woven into Italian civic identity: derbies such as the Derby della Madonnina (Milan–Inter), Derby della Capitale (Roma–Lazio) and Derby d'Italia (Juventus–Inter) are major cultural events. Organised ultras groups are a prominent, sometimes controversial, feature of Italian fan culture. The sport has also faced major scandals, notably the 2006 Calciopoli match-influence affair, which saw Juventus relegated.
