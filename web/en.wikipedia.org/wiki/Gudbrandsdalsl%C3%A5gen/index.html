Gudbrandsdalslågen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gudbrandsdalslågen (often simply called Lågen) is a river in Innlandet county, Norway. At about 204 kilometres (127 mi) in length, it is one of the longest rivers in Norway. The river flows through the Gudbrandsdalen valley, from which it takes its name, beginning at the lake Lesjaskogsvatnet in Lesja municipality and running southeast through the municipalities of Dovre, Sel, Nord-Fron, Sør-Fron, Ringebu, Øyer and Lillehammer before emptying into Lake Mjøsa, Norway's largest lake.

Course

The river drains a large catchment in the mountains of southern Norway. Along its course it receives numerous tributaries, including the Otta, the Sjoa, the Vinstra, the Gausa and the Frya. The valley it carved is one of the historic main routes between Oslo and Trondheim, followed today by the E6 highway and the Dovre Railway.

History and culture

Gudbrandsdalen has been settled since prehistoric times, and the river features in Norse sagas. The valley was the route of King Óláfr Haraldsson's journey through Norway in 1021, an event commemorated by the later Peer Gynt tradition associated with the valley. In August 1940 and during later periods the river has flooded; significant floods also occurred in 1995 and 2013, causing damage in Lillehammer and the lower valley.

Nature

The river supports populations of brown trout and other fish, and its floodplains and delta at the northern end of Mjøsa, near Lillehammer, form an important wetland nature reserve (Lågendeltaet) for migratory birds.
