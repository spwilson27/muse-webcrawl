Elephant birds are extinct flightless birds in the family Aepyornithidae that once lived on the island of Madagascar. They were among the largest birds ever to exist: the biggest species, Vorombe titan, stood around 3 metres tall and may have weighed about 800 kilograms, and their eggs — with a volume equivalent to some 150 chicken eggs — are the largest eggs known from any animal.

Taxonomy

The family comprises the genera Aepyornis, Mullerornis and Vorombe. Despite their size, DNA studies show their closest living relatives are the small kiwi of New Zealand, implying that the ratites dispersed by flight across oceans rather than solely by the breakup of Gondwana.

Extinction

Elephant birds survived into the second millennium AD but disappeared, probably by the seventeenth or eighteenth century, most likely through a combination of hunting, egg collection and habitat change following human settlement of Madagascar. Travellers' tales of the giant "roc" of Arabian legend are sometimes linked to the elephant bird.
