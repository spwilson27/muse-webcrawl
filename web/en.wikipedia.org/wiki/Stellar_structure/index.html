Stellar structure

Stellar structure is the study of the internal composition and physics of stars. Models of stellar structure describe how temperature, pressure, density and energy production vary with radius inside a star in hydrostatic equilibrium.

Equations of stellar structure

The classical model rests on four differential equations: conservation of mass, hydrostatic equilibrium (the balance between gravity and the pressure gradient), conservation of energy (luminosity generated by nuclear fusion in the core), and energy transport, which occurs by radiation in radiative zones and by convection in convective zones. These are supplemented by an equation of state, opacity data and nuclear reaction rates.

Interior regions

In a Sun-like star, fusion in the central core generates energy that flows outward through a radiative zone and then a convective envelope before radiating from the photosphere. Massive stars instead have convective cores and radiative envelopes. The structure determines a star's luminosity, lifetime and evolution off the main sequence.
