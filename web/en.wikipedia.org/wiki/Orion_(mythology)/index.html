Orion (Ancient Greek: Ὠρίων, Ōríōn) was a giant huntsman in Greek mythology whom Zeus (or perhaps Artemis) placed among the stars as the constellation of Orion. Ancient sources tell several different, inconsistent stories about Orion: there are two major versions of his birth and several versions of his death. The most important recorded episodes are his birth in Boeotia, his visit to Chios where he met Merope and was blinded by her father Oenopion, his recovery of sight at Lemnos, his hunting with Artemis on Crete, his death by the blow of a scorpion or by Artemis's arrows, and his elevation to the heavens.

Birth

One tradition makes Orion the son of the sea god Poseidon and Euryale, daughter of Minos; from Poseidon he inherited the ability to walk upon the water. Another famous story recounts that the gods Zeus, Poseidon and Hermes, visiting the poor peasant Hyrieus in Boeotia, urinated upon a bull's hide and buried it in the earth, from which Orion was born ten months later — a folk etymology connecting his name with ouron, "urine".

Merope and the blinding

Orion travelled to the island of Chios, where he fell in love with Merope, daughter of King Oenopion. In one account he cleared the island of wild beasts and brought the spoils to win her, but Oenopion kept postponing the marriage. Orion, in a drunken rage, assaulted Merope (in some versions), and Oenopion blinded him and cast him out. Guided by the boy Cedalion, whom he carried on his shoulders, Orion travelled east to Lemnos, the forge of Hephaestus, and then to the rising of the Sun (Helios), where his sight was restored.

Artemis and death

Orion became a hunting companion of the goddess Artemis. The accounts of his death vary: in one, the earth (Gaia) sent an enormous scorpion to kill him because he boasted that he would slay every animal on earth; in another, Artemis killed him, either accidentally tricked by her brother Apollo, who feared for her chastity, or deliberately after Orion challenged her or assaulted her. After his death Orion was placed in the sky as a constellation, eternally pursued by Scorpius: as Orion sets, Scorpius rises, so the two never share the sky.

The constellation

The constellation Orion is among the most conspicuous in the night sky, recognizable by the three stars of Orion's Belt. Greek astronomers identified the figure with the hunter holding a club and lion's skin, chasing the Pleiades and followed by his dogs, Canis Major and Canis Minor, while fleeing the scorpion. Orion is mentioned in the works of Homer, Hesiod and Virgil, and the myth figured widely in later literature and astronomy.
