Ensenada

Ensenada is a city in Ensenada Municipality, Baja California, Mexico. Located on the Pacific coast of the Baja California Peninsula, about 125 kilometres (78 mi) south of the United States border, it is the third-largest city in the state, after Tijuana and Mexicali, with a population of roughly 330,000 in the city (2020 census) and over 440,000 in the municipality. Ensenada is an important commercial and fishing port, a centre of Mexican wine production, and a popular tourist and cruise-ship destination.

History

The bay, Bahía de Todos Santos, was entered by the expedition of Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo in 1542 and later charted by Sebastián Vizcaíno in 1602. For centuries the area was home to the Kumeyaay (Diegueño) people and sparsely settled ranchos under the mission system. The modern town dates from the 1880s, when gold discoveries inland and land-colonisation schemes spurred development; it served as capital of the northern district of Baja California territory until 1915. During the Mexican Revolution, and again during Prohibition in the United States, Ensenada's casinos and resorts drew American visitors.

Economy and attractions

The Port of Ensenada handles cargo, commercial tuna and sport-fishing fleets, and cruise ships; the city is known as the birthplace of the fish taco. Just inland lies the Valle de Guadalupe, Mexico's premier wine region, which anchors food and wine tourism, while the Riviera del Pacífico cultural centre, the bufadora (marine geyser) at Punta Banda, and offshore Isla Todos Santos — famous for big-wave surfing — are major attractions. Ensenada is also a hub of Mexican science, hosting CICESE, one of Mexico's leading research centres, and the port is the start and finish of the Baja 1000 off-road race and the Newport-to-Ensenada yacht race.
