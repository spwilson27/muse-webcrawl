The Guide Star Catalog (GSC), also known as the Hubble Space Telescope, Guide Catalog, is a star catalogue compiled to support the Hubble Space Telescope in acquiring off-axis guide stars for its Fine Guidance Sensors. Hubble's pointing requires knowing, anywhere on the sky, the accurate positions of pairs of moderately bright stars falling within the sensors' fields of view; the GSC provides those stars.

Versions

GSC-I, published in 1989 by the Space Telescope Science Institute, contained about 19 million objects to magnitude ~15, derived from scans of photographic survey plates (the Palomar and UK Schmidt surveys) made for the Digitized Sky Survey. Entries carry identifiers of the form of a region number and a running number, e.g. GSC 1234-05678.

GSC-II (2008) vastly expanded the catalogue to about 945 million objects — over 455 million of them classified as stars — extending to magnitude ~19.5–20 with improved positions, magnitudes in multiple passbands, and a star/galaxy classification. It was compiled from multi-epoch Schmidt plate surveys in collaboration with observatories in Italy and elsewhere, and is used to support HST, other space missions, and ground-based telescopes.
