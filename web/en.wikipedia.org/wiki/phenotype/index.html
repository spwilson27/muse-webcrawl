Phenotype

FieldGenetics
Coined byWilhelm Johannsen (1911)

An organism's phenotype is the set of its observable characteristics or traits, resulting from the interaction of its genotype with the environment. Phenotypes include morphology, development, biochemical properties, physiology, and behavior.

Definition and origin

Danish botanist Wilhelm Johannsen coined "phenotype" and "genotype" in 1911 to distinguish an organism's hereditary material from what that material produces. The distinction is central to genetics: identical genotypes can yield different phenotypes in different environments (phenotypic plasticity), and different genotypes can yield similar phenotypes.

Variation

Phenotypic variation arises from genetic variation, environmental factors, and their interaction. Some traits are discrete (such as blood type), while most are continuous and polygenic (such as height). Natural selection acts on phenotypes, driving evolution of the underlying genotypes.

Extended phenotype

Richard Dawkins proposed the "extended phenotype," in which the effects of a gene extend beyond the organism's body, as in a beaver's dam or a parasite manipulating its host.
