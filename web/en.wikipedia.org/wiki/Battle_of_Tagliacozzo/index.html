Battle of Tagliacozzo
Date23 August 1268
LocationPalentine plain near Tagliacozzo, Abruzzo, Italy
ResultAngevin (Guelph) victory; end of Hohenstaufen rule in Sicily

The Battle of Tagliacozzo was fought on 23 August 1268 between the Guelph army of Charles I of Anjou and the Ghibelline forces of Conradin, the sixteen-year-old Hohenstaufen claimant to the Kingdom of Sicily. Charles's victory, achieved by a concealed reserve that fell on Conradin's army after it had begun celebrating a premature success, destroyed the last Hohenstaufen bid for Sicily. Conradin was captured and executed at Naples two months later.

Background

Charles of Anjou, backed by the papacy, had conquered the Kingdom of Sicily from Manfred at Benevento in 1266. In 1267 Conradin, grandson of Frederick II, crossed the Alps with German and Spanish supporters and Ghibelline allies, raised Italy in revolt, and marched south to challenge Charles.

The battle

On the Palentine plain near the Salto river the two armies met. Conradin's forces, including Henry of Castile's Spanish knights, drove Charles's first two divisions from the field and began looting the Angevin camp, believing the battle won. Charles, however, had kept a third division hidden behind a hill on the advice of the crusader veteran Alard de Saint-Valery; its sudden counter-attack shattered Conradin's disordered army.

Aftermath

Conradin escaped the field but was captured near Astura and beheaded at Naples on 29 October 1268 — the extinction of the Hohenstaufen line. Dante mentions the battle in the Inferno. Angevin rule in Sicily lasted until the Sicilian Vespers revolt of 1282.
