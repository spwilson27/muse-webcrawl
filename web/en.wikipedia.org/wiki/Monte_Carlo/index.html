Monte Carlo


The Monte Carlo Casino and the harbour of Monaco.

Monte Carlo (French: Monte-Carlo; Monégasque: Montecarlo) is an administrative area of the Principality of Monaco, internationally known for its casino, the Monte Carlo Casino, and as a home to the Monaco Grand Prix and the Monte Carlo Rally.

Monte Carlo is one of Monaco's four traditional quarters (quartiers), together with Monaco-Ville, La Condamine and Fontvieille. It is sometimes mistakenly regarded as the capital of Monaco, although Monaco is a city-state whose functions are distributed among its districts.

History

The area was named in honour of Prince Charles III of Monaco (Italian: Carlo). The casino was established in the 1860s under François Blanc and the Société des Bains de Mer, and the fortunes of the casino transformed the previously impoverished principality. The famous song "The Man Who Broke the Bank at Monte Carlo" commemorated the gambler Charles Wells.

Sport and culture

The Circuit de Monaco hosts the Monaco Grand Prix of Formula One, run through the streets of Monte Carlo and La Condamine since 1929. The Monte Carlo Masters tennis tournament and stages of the Monte Carlo Rally are also held here. In simulation and computation, the name has been borrowed for the Monte Carlo method, a class of randomised algorithms named for the casino's association with games of chance.
