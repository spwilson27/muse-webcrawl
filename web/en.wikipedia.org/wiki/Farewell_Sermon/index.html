The Farewell Sermon (Arabic: خطبة الوداع, Khuṭbat al-Wadāʿ), also known as Muhammad's Final Sermon or the Last Sermon, is a religious sermon delivered by the Islamic prophet Muhammad on Friday the 9th of Dhu al-Hijjah, 10 AH (6 March 632 CE) in the Uranah valley of Mount Arafat, during the Islamic pilgrimage of Hajj. It is referenced in almost all books of hadith and sira, and was the final major statement of his teachings before his death a few months later.

Content

Though versions differ in detail across sources, the sermon's recurrent themes include:

  • The sanctity of life and property: "Verily your blood, your property are as sacred as this day of yours, in this month of yours, in this city of yours."
  • The abolition of usury (riba), beginning with the interest owed to his own clan, and the end of blood vengeance from the days of ignorance (Jahiliyyah).
  • Faithfulness in trusts, fulfilment of contracts, and just treatment of wives: "Fear God concerning women; verily you have taken them on the security of God."
  • The brotherhood of Muslims and the rejection of racial hierarchy — in a famous passage, "An Arab has no superiority over a non-Arab, nor a white over a black, except by piety and good action."
  • Adherence to the Qur'an (and in some versions, his Sunnah or his family, the Ahl al-Bayt) after his death: "I leave behind me two things, the Qur'an and my example, the Sunnah, and if you follow these you will never go astray."

Sources and significance

The sermon is narrated by Jabir ibn Abd Allah and others in Sahih Muslim and in the sira of Ibn Hisham, with variants in other hadith collections. Because it summarises core ethical teachings — equality before God, the inviolability of life and property, and the rights of women — it has been described by many commentators as a charter of human rights and is frequently quoted in Islamic teaching. Shia tradition associates the return journey from this Hajj with the event of Ghadir Khumm.
