The Yorktown campaign, also known as the Virginia campaign, was a series of military manoeuvres and battles during the summer and autumn of 1781 that culminated in the siege of Yorktown and the surrender of a British army under Lieutenant General Lord Cornwallis. It was the last major land campaign of the American Revolutionary War in North America, and its outcome effectively decided American independence.

Background

After years of inconclusive campaigning in the north, British strategy shifted to the southern colonies. Cornwallis won costly victories at Camden and Guilford Courthouse but, controversially, marched into Virginia in spring 1781. There his army raided and skirmished with forces led by the Marquis de Lafayette, eventually entrenching at Yorktown on the York River in August, where he expected resupply and evacuation by the Royal Navy.

The allied move south

General George Washington and the Comte de Rochambeau, initially preparing to attack New York, learned that the French fleet of Admiral de Grasse was sailing from the West Indies to the Chesapeake. In a celebrated deception, the allies feinted toward New York, then marched south in August–September 1781. On 5 September, de Grasse's fleet fought the British under Admiral Graves at the Battle of the Chesapeake — a tactical draw but a strategic French victory that sealed Cornwallis's fate by denying him relief by sea.

Siege of Yorktown

Washington and Rochambeau reached Williamsburg in mid-September and invested Yorktown on 28 September with some 17,000–19,000 American and French troops against Cornwallis's roughly 9,000. Formal siege parallels were dug, allied artillery battered the British works, and on 14 October French and American storming parties captured Redoubts 9 and 10 — the American assault led by Lieutenant Colonel Alexander Hamilton. A failed British breakout attempt and Cornwallis's inability to evacuate across the York River forced him to seek terms; his army surrendered on 19 October 1781.

Aftermath

News of the surrender shocked London; Lord North reportedly exclaimed "Oh God, it is all over." The result brought down his ministry in March 1782 and led to peace negotiations, concluded by the Treaty of Paris (1783), in which Britain recognised the independence of the United States.
