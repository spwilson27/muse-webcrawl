Béziers (French: [bezje]; Occitan: Besièrs) is a city in the Hérault department of the Occitania region in southern France, situated on a bluff above the river Orb about 10 km from the Mediterranean Sea. One of the oldest towns in France, with settlement traces going back to the Neolithic, Béziers is best known for the massacre of its population during the Albigensian Crusade in 1209, for its position on the Canal du Midi, and for its feria and rugby union club, AS Béziers Hérault.

History

The site was occupied by the Celtic Volcae and became the Roman town of Baeterrae, a prosperous colony for veterans on the Via Domitia between Italy and Spain. In July 1209, in one of the most notorious episodes of the Albigensian Crusade against the Cathars, crusader forces stormed Béziers and massacred much of its population — Catholics and Cathars alike. The papal legate Arnaud Amalric is apocryphally reported to have said, "Kill them all; God will know His own." The city's cathedral of Saint-Nazaire, rebuilt in Gothic style after the crusade, still dominates the skyline.

In the 17th century Béziers was linked to the Canal du Midi, engineered by the city's most famous son, Pierre-Paul Riquet; the canal's nine locks at Fonseranes, just outside the town, are a celebrated feat of engineering and a UNESCO World Heritage site. In the 19th and early 20th centuries Béziers grew rich on wine — it called itself the world capital of wine — and suffered accordingly in the Languedoc winegrowers' revolt of 1907, a major social uprising suppressed by troops.

Economy and culture

Today the economy rests on wine production, tourism, services and light industry. The five-day August Feria, centred on bullfighting at the Arènes de Béziers, draws around a million visitors. The city's rugby club, AS Béziers, founded in 1911, was the dominant force in French rugby in the 1970s and early 1980s, winning eleven national championships. Béziers is also the birthplace of resistance hero Jean Moulin, prefect and leader of the French Resistance, commemorated by a museum and memorials in the city.

Sights

Principal sights include the cathedral of Saint-Nazaire, the medieval Pont Vieux over the Orb, the Fonseranes locks and canal bridge of the Canal du Midi, the Plateau des Poètes gardens designed by the Bühler brothers, and the musée du Biterrois. The Oppidum d'Ensérune, an Iron Age hill town, lies nearby.
