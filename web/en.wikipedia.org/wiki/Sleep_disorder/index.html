Sleep disorder

A sleep disorder, or somnipathy, is a medical disorder of an individual's sleep patterns. Some sleep disorders are serious enough to interfere with normal physical, mental, social, and emotional functioning. The International Classification of Sleep Disorders groups them into insomnias, sleep-related breathing disorders, central disorders of hypersomnolence, circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders, parasomnias, and sleep-related movement disorders.

Common disorders

  • Insomnia — difficulty falling or staying asleep; the most common sleep complaint. Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is first-line treatment.
  • Obstructive sleep apnea — repeated collapse of the upper airway during sleep, causing pauses in breathing, snoring, and daytime sleepiness; treated mainly with CPAP.
  • Narcolepsy — excessive daytime sleepiness with cataplexy, sleep paralysis, and hypnagogic hallucinations, caused by loss of orexin neurons.
  • Restless legs syndrome — an urge to move the legs, worse at rest and in the evening.
  • Circadian rhythm disorders — including delayed sleep phase disorder and shift-work disorder.
  • Parasomnias — abnormal behaviours during sleep such as sleepwalking, night terrors, and REM sleep behaviour disorder.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis uses sleep history, questionnaires such as the Epworth Sleepiness Scale, actigraphy, and polysomnography (an overnight sleep study measuring EEG, eye movements, muscle tone, airflow, and oxygen saturation). Sleep medicine is a recognized subspecialty within neurology, psychiatry, and internal medicine.
