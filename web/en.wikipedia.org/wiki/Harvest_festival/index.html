A harvest festival is an annual celebration that occurs around the time of the main harvest of a given region. Given the differences in climate and crops around the world, harvest festivals can be found at various times at different places. Harvest festivals typically feature feasting, both family and public, with foods that are drawn from crops that come to maturity around the time of the festival. Ample food and freedom from the necessity to work in the fields are two central features of harvest festivals: eating, merriment, contests, music and romance are common features of harvest festivals around the world.

History

Harvest thanksgiving observances are among the oldest human festivals, tied to the agricultural cycle of sowing and reaping. In Britain, celebrations of the harvest date to pagan times, when the last sheaf of corn — often woven into a "corn dolly" — was ceremonially gathered at Lammas and Michaelmas. The modern church-based Harvest Festival in the Church of England is usually credited to the Reverend Robert Stephen Hawker, who held a special harvest thanksgiving service at Morwenstow, Cornwall, in 1843; the custom spread with the singing of hymns such as "We Plough the Fields and Scatter" and the decoration of churches with produce, now commonly donated to food banks.

Around the world

Examples include Thanksgiving in North America, the Jewish Sukkot, China's Mid-Autumn Festival, India's many harvest festivals such as Pongal, Onam, and Baisakhi, the German Erntedankfest, and Ghana's Homowo. In the southern hemisphere equivalent festivals fall in March–May.
