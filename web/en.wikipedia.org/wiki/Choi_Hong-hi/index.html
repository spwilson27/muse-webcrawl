Choi Hong-hi (Korean: 최홍희; 9 November 1918 – 15 June 2002) was a South Korean Army general and martial artist who was a major figure in the founding of taekwondo, often regarded as its founder. He founded the International Taekwon-do Federation (ITF) in 1966.

Early life

Born in what is now North Korea during the Japanese occupation, Choi studied calligraphy and was sent to Japan for education, where he earned a black belt in Shotokan karate. Returning to Korea, he served in the newly formed Republic of Korea Army, rising to the rank of major general.

Founding of taekwondo

Choi led the effort to unify the Korean martial arts schools (kwans) under a new name; "taekwondo" was adopted in 1955. He developed the Chang Hon set of patterns (tul), wrote foundational taekwondo texts, and led demonstration teams abroad, spreading the art worldwide. In 1966 he founded the ITF in Seoul as the international governing body.

Exile and the ITF/WT split

Conflict with the South Korean government of Park Chung-hee led Choi to move the ITF headquarters to Toronto, Canada, in 1972. South Korea then created the Kukkiwon and the World Taekwondo Federation (now World Taekwondo), producing the enduring division between ITF and WT styles. Choi's later visits to North Korea, where he introduced taekwondo, were controversial in the South. He died in Pyongyang in 2002 and is buried there in the Patriotic Martyrs' Cemetery.
