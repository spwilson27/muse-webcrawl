The Siyasatnama (Persian: سیاست‌نامه, "Book of Government"), also known as the Siyar al-muluk, is the most famous work of Nizam al-Mulk (1018–1092), the Persian vizier of the Seljuk sultans Alp Arslan and Malik-Shah I. Written near the end of his life at the sultan's request, it is a classic of the "mirror for princes" genre, offering advice on kingship, administration, justice and intelligence gathering.

Contents

The book comprises about fifty chapters (the exact number varies by recension) covering the duties of rulers, the organisation of the court and army, taxation, the management of officials, and the suppression of heresy — with notable warnings against the Ismailis (Assassins), by whom Nizam al-Mulk was murdered in 1092. It draws on Persian administrative tradition and anecdotes about earlier kings, both Muslim and pre-Islamic Iranian.

Influence

The Siyasatnama shaped Seljuk government and remained a standard model of Persian statecraft for centuries, influencing later Islamic political literature. It was first translated into European languages in the 19th century; Hubert Darke's English translation, The Book of Government or Rules for Kings, appeared in 1960.
