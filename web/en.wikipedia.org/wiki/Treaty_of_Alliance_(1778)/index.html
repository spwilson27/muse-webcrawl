Treaty of Alliance (1778)

The Treaty of Alliance, also known as the Franco-American Treaty, was a defensive alliance between the Kingdom of France and the United States, formed amid the American Revolutionary War. It was signed on 6 February 1778 in Paris, alongside the Treaty of Amity and Commerce, after the American victory at Saratoga convinced France that the rebellion could succeed.

Terms

France recognized the independence of the United States, and each party pledged to make no separate peace with Great Britain; the two would fight until American independence was secured. If war broke out between France and Britain, the alliance obliged the United States to guarantee French possessions in the Americas, and established rules for conquests, prizes, and mutual support. Negotiators included Benjamin Franklin, Silas Deane, and Arthur Lee for the Americans, and Conrad Alexandre Gérard for France.

Consequences and end

The alliance brought French troops, fleets, and finance that proved decisive, notably at the Siege of Yorktown in 1781. Relations frayed in the 1790s over the French Revolutionary Wars, the Citizen Genêt affair, and American neutrality; the United States treated the treaty as void during the Quasi-War, and it was formally terminated by the Convention of 1800 (Mortefontaine). Historians often call it America's "first alliance" — a status that shaped the young republic's later reluctance toward permanent alliances.
