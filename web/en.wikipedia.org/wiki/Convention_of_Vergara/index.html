Convention of Vergara

The Convention of Vergara (Spanish: Convenio de Vergara) was a treaty signed on 31 August 1839 between the Liberal general Baldomero Espartero and the Carlist general Rafael Maroto, which effectively ended the First Carlist War in northern Spain. The signing is famous for the embrace between the two generals — the Abrazo de Vergara — before their assembled troops at Vergara (Bergara), in the province of Gipuzkoa.

Background

The First Carlist War (1833–1840) erupted after the death of Ferdinand VII, pitting supporters of his daughter Isabella II, backed by the Liberal government, against supporters of his brother Don Carlos, who defended traditionalist and absolutist claims under the banner of "God, Fatherland, King." By 1839 the Carlist forces in the Basque Country and Navarre were exhausted and divided, and Maroto, who had taken command of the Carlist Army of the North, opened negotiations with Espartero.

Terms

The convention's key article committed Espartero to press the government to uphold the fueros — the traditional charters and privileges of the Basque provinces and Navarre — or modify them as required, and allowed Carlist officers to be incorporated into the Liberal army retaining their ranks and pay. Don Carlos rejected the agreement and fled to France, while Maroto was denounced as a traitor by hardline Carlists.

Aftermath

Fighting continued in the Maestrazgo under Ramón Cabrera until May 1840, but the convention ended organized Carlist resistance in the north. The question of the fueros remained a long-running grievance in Basque and Navarrese politics, shaping Spanish regional conflicts well into the following century.
