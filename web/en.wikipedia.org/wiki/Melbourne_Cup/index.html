The Melbourne Cup is an annual Group 1 Thoroughbred horse race conducted by the Victoria Racing Club as part of the Melbourne Spring Racing Carnival. It is a handicap race for horses three years old and over, run over a distance of 3,200 metres (1.988 mi) at Flemington Racecourse in Melbourne, Victoria, on the first Tuesday in November. It is famously described as "the race that stops a nation" — Cup Day is a public holiday in metropolitan Melbourne.

The first Melbourne Cup was run in 1861 and won by Archer, who won again the following year. The race grew into Australia's richest and most prestigious horse race, attracting international entries, particularly staying horses from Europe and Japan.

Notable winners and records

  • Phar Lap won the 1930 Cup and remains an Australian cultural icon.
  • Makybe Diva won three consecutive Cups (2003, 2004, 2005), the only horse to win three since the race began; Think Big, Rain Lover, and Archer each won two.
  • Kingston Rule set the race record time of 3:16.3 in 1990.
  • The youngest-winning jockey and trainer records, and five wins by trainer Bart Cummings ("the Cups King," 12 wins as trainer), are part of Cup lore.

Culture

Cup Day features elaborate fashion ("Fashions on the Field"), office sweepstakes across Australia and New Zealand, and record attendances that have exceeded 120,000 at Flemington (2003). The race is broadcast to hundreds of millions of viewers worldwide.
