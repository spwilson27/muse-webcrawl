Wildcat cartridge

Wildcat cartridges are created by modifying existing cases.

A wildcat cartridge, often shortened to wildcat, is a custom-made cartridge for which ammunition and firearms are not mass-produced. Wildcats are developed by hobbyists and experimenters, usually by modifying an existing commercial case — necking it up or down to a different calibre, changing the shoulder angle, or increasing case capacity — to improve velocity, accuracy, or performance for a specific purpose such as varmint hunting or benchrest shooting.

Development

Making a wildcat typically requires a custom chamber reamer, custom reloading dies, and hand-formed brass produced by fire-forming or neck-sizing parent cases. Because wildcats are not standardised, their dimensions vary between makers; some are later adopted commercially and standardised by bodies such as SAAMI or CIP.

Examples

  • .22-250 Remington — began as a wildcat necking down the .250-3000 Savage before commercial adoption in 1965.
  • .35 Whelen — a .30-06 Springfield necked up to .35 calibre, long a wildcat before Remington standardised it.
  • 7mm Shooting Times Easterner and the cartridges of P. O. Ackley, whose "Improved" designs blew out case shoulders for extra capacity.
  • .300 Whisper — a wildcat that evolved into the commercial .300 AAC Blackout.
