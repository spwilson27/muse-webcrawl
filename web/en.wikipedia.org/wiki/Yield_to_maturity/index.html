Yield to maturity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Yield to maturity (YTM)
YTM equates the bond's price with the present value of its future cash flows
Also calledBook yield; redemption yield
Applies toBonds and other fixed-interest securities
DefinitionThe internal rate of return earned if the security is held to maturity

The yield to maturity (YTM), book yield or redemption yield of a bond or other fixed-interest security is an estimate of the total rate of return anticipated to be earned by an investor who buys the security at its market price and holds it until maturity, assuming all coupon and principal payments are made on schedule and reinvested at the same rate.

Formula

For a bond with face value F, annual coupon C, price P and n years to maturity, the yield to maturity y solves:

P = Σt=1..n C / (1+y)t + F / (1+y)n

Because y appears in every denominator, the equation generally has no closed-form solution and is solved by trial and error or numerical iteration. For bonds priced at par, YTM equals the coupon rate; for bonds at a discount it is higher, and for bonds at a premium, lower.

Assumptions and limitations

  • The investor holds the bond to maturity.
  • All coupon payments are reinvested at the YTM itself — often unrealistic, which is why realised return can differ from the promised YTM.
  • The issuer makes every payment in full and on time (no default).

Variants

  • Yield to call (YTC): computed assuming the bond is redeemed at its first call date.
  • Yield to worst (YTW): the lowest of the yields to maturity and to each possible call date.
  • Yield to sink: computed assuming a sinking-fund schedule.
  • Current yield: simply the annual coupon divided by the current price; ignores capital gain or loss at maturity.
