Haroldson Lafayette Hunt Jr. (February 17, 1889 – November 29, 1974) was an American oil magnate and political activist. By rolling the dice in a poker game for an oil lease in 1930 and acquiring rights in the East Texas Oil Field, he became one of the wealthiest men in the world. He was a staunch conservative and funded right-wing causes through much of his later life. Hunt was the father of fifteen children, including sports magnate Lamar Hunt and the silver speculators Nelson Bunker Hunt and William Herbert Hunt.

Early life

Hunt was born near Ramsey, Illinois, one of eight children of a prosperous farmer and grain dealer. Largely self-educated, he worked a variety of jobs in his youth, including as a prospector, lumberjack and mule-team driver, and developed a reputation as a skilled gambler. He drilled his first oil wells in Arkansas in the early 1920s and made and lost several modest fortunes in oil speculation.

East Texas and Hunt Oil

In November 1930, wildcatter Columbus Marion "Dad" Joiner struck oil near Kilgore, Texas, opening the East Texas Oil Field, the largest oil field yet found in the continental United States. Hunt acquired Joiner's lease for a package of cash and production payments — a transaction sometimes said to have been sealed with poker winnings. He founded the Hunt Oil Company in 1934 and built pipelines, refineries and a far-flung producing empire. By the late 1940s he was commonly described in the press as the richest man in America.

Politics and media

Hunt used his fortune to promote conservative politics. He sponsored the "Life Line" radio programme and the Facts Forum organisation, which produced radio and television broadcasts attacking communism and federal power, and he supported politicians including Senator Joseph McCarthy and General Douglas MacArthur, whom he hoped to see drafted for the presidency. His political activities attracted scrutiny from the IRS and Congress.

Personal life and family

Hunt maintained three families. With his first wife, Lyda Bunker, he had seven children, including Margaret Hunt Hill, H. L. Hunt III, Lamar Hunt, Nelson Bunker Hunt and William Herbert Hunt. He also had four children with Frania Tye and four with Ruth Ray Wright, whom he eventually married after Lyda's death. His sons Nelson Bunker and William Herbert were later famous for attempting to corner the world silver market in 1979–80. His son Lamar Hunt founded the American Football League and the Kansas City Chiefs and coined the term "Super Bowl".

Hunt died in Dallas on November 29, 1974. He is a central figure in accounts of the Texas oil boom and is often cited as an inspiration for the character of Jett Rink in Edna Ferber's novel Giant — though Ferber's character was more closely modelled on another wildcatter, Glenn McCarthy.
