Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney (January 9, 1875 – April 18, 1942) was an American sculptor, art patron and collector, and founder in 1931 of the Whitney Museum of American Art in New York City. She was a prominent social figure and hostess, born into the wealthy Vanderbilt family.

Early life

Gertrude Vanderbilt was born in New York City, the eldest surviving daughter of Cornelius Vanderbilt II (1843–1899) and Alice Claypoole Gwynne (1845–1934). She was the great-granddaughter of the "Commodore" Cornelius Vanderbilt. She was educated privately and later studied sculpture with Hendrik Christian Andersen in Rome and with Auguste Rodin in Paris.

Career

Whitney established a studio in Greenwich Village in 1907 and began promoting the work of living American artists. In 1914 she founded the Whitney Studio, which staged exhibitions for artists whose work was excluded from conventional academically oriented shows. When the Metropolitan Museum of Art declined her offer of her collection of some 500 works in 1929, she founded her own museum, the Whitney Museum of American Art, which opened in 1931 in West Eighth Street, New York.

As a sculptor, Whitney received numerous public commissions, including the Peter Stuyvesant Monument in Stuyvesant Square, New York, and the Titanic Memorial in Washington, D.C., unveiled in 1931.

Personal life and death

In 1896 she married Harry Payne Whitney (1872–1930), a sportsman and heir to the Whitney oil and tobacco fortune. They had three children: Flora, Cornelius Vanderbilt Whitney, and Barbara. She died on April 18, 1942, at the age of 67.
