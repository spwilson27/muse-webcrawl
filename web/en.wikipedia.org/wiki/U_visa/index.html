U visa

The U visa is a United States nonimmigrant visa for victims of certain crimes who have suffered substantial mental or physical abuse and are willing to assist law enforcement in the investigation or prosecution of the criminal activity. It was created by the Victims of Trafficking and Violence Protection Act of 2000 to strengthen the ability of law enforcement agencies to investigate and prosecute cases of domestic violence, sexual assault, human trafficking, and other crimes while protecting victims.

Created2000 (Victims of Trafficking and Violence Protection Act)
EligibilityVictims of qualifying crimes who cooperate with law enforcement
Annual cap10,000 principal visas
DurationUp to 4 years, extendable in some cases

Eligibility

An applicant must show that they were the victim of a qualifying crime (such as domestic violence, sexual assault, felonious assault, trafficking, kidnapping, or witness tampering), suffered substantial abuse, possess information about the crime, and have been, are being, or are likely to be helpful to law enforcement — certified by a law-enforcement agency on a formal certification form. The crime must have occurred in the United States or violated U.S. law.

Benefits and limits

U status generally lasts up to four years and carries work authorization. After three years in U status, holders may apply for lawful permanent residence if they meet additional requirements. Congress capped the number of principal U visas at 10,000 per year; heavy demand has produced a long waiting list, with eligible applicants placed on a waiting list and granted deferred action and work permits while they wait.
