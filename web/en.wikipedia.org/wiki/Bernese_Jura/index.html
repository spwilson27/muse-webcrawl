The Bernese Jura (French: Jura bernois) is the French-speaking northern part of the canton of Bern in Switzerland, lying in the Jura Mountains along the border with France. Since 2010 it has formed the Jura bernois administrative region, whose seat is Courtelary; its principal towns include Moutier, Saint-Imier, Tavannes, and La Neuveville on Lake Biel.

History

The region historically belonged largely to the Prince-Bishopric of Basel. It was assigned to the canton of Bern by the Congress of Vienna in 1815, in exchange for Bern's loss of the Vaud and Aargau. Being French-speaking and, in part, Catholic, while the rest of Bern was German-speaking and Protestant, the region developed a distinct identity.

The Jura question

From the mid-20th century, separatist movements sought the creation of a French-speaking canton of Jura. After a series of plebiscites in the 1970s, the three northern districts voted to form the new canton of Jura (created in 1979), while the three southern districts — Courtelary, Moutier, and La Neuveville — voted to remain with Bern, forming today's Bernese Jura. The status of Moutier remained contested: voters there approved transferring to the canton of Jura in a 2021 referendum, with the transfer scheduled to take effect on 1 January 2026.

Economy

The Bernese Jura is a traditional centre of Swiss precision industry, in particular watchmaking around Saint-Imier and machine-tool manufacturing in the Moutier valley, and is known for the Tête de Moine cheese, produced in the region since the 12th century.
