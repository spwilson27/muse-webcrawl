Fort Tryon Park

Fort Tryon Park
Location: Inwood / Washington Heights, Manhattan, New York City
Area: 67 acres (27 ha)
Opened: 1935
Known for: The Met Cloisters

Fort Tryon Park is a public park in the Inwood and Washington Heights neighbourhoods of northern Manhattan, New York City. Covering about 67 acres on a high, rocky ridge above the Hudson River, it offers some of the finest river views in the city, looking across to the Palisades of New Jersey. The park contains The Met Cloisters, the branch of the Metropolitan Museum of Art devoted to medieval European art and architecture.

History

The site takes its name from Fort Tryon, an outwork of Fort Washington in the American Revolutionary War, named by the British for Sir William Tryon, the last colonial governor of New York. The Battle of Fort Washington was fought in the vicinity in November 1776. In the early 20th century, philanthropist John D. Rockefeller Jr. assembled the land from estates including that of Cornelius Vanderbilt II and the horticulturalist C. K. G. Billings, whose mansion drive and stone archways survive. The park was designed by the Olmsted Brothers firm and opened in 1935; Rockefeller also purchased the New Jersey Palisades opposite to preserve the view.

Features

The Met Cloisters, opened in 1938, incorporates elements of five medieval French cloisters into a purpose-built museum. The park's Heather Garden — one of the largest public gardens in New York — contains hundreds of varieties of heather plus perennials and shrubs arranged for year-round interest. Other features include Linden Terrace, the Billings Arcade and Lawn, the Alpine Garden, and extensive exposed Manhattan schist outcroppings. The park hosts an annual medieval festival.
