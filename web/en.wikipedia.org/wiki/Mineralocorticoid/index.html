Mineralocorticoid

Mineralocorticoids are a class of corticosteroid hormones that influence salt and water balance in the body — hence their name, from their effect on mineral electrolytes. The principal mineralocorticoid in humans is aldosterone. They are produced in the zona glomerulosa, the outermost layer of the adrenal cortex.

Physiology

Mineralocorticoids act mainly on the distal tubules and collecting ducts of the kidney, where they increase reabsorption of sodium ions and water while promoting excretion of potassium and hydrogen ions. Their secretion is regulated primarily by the renin–angiotensin system and by plasma potassium concentration, rather than directly by ACTH. Aldosterone binds the intracellular mineralocorticoid receptor, which alters gene expression for sodium channels (ENaC) and the Na+/K+-ATPase pump.

Clinical aspects

Excess mineralocorticoid activity (hyperaldosteronism, as in Conn's syndrome) causes hypertension, hypokalemia, and metabolic alkalosis. Deficiency, as in Addison's disease, leads to salt wasting, dehydration, and hyperkalemia, and is treated with the synthetic mineralocorticoid fludrocortisone. Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists such as spironolactone and eplerenone are used as diuretics and in heart failure. Mineralocorticoids contrast with the other major corticosteroid class, the glucocorticoids, which primarily regulate metabolism and immune function.
