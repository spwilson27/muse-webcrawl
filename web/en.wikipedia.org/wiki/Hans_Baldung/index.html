Hans Baldung

Hans Baldung (called Grien)
Born: c. 1484, Schwäbisch Gmünd, Germany
Died: September 1545, Strasbourg
Known for: Painting, woodcuts, engravings
Movement: German Renaissance

Hans Baldung (c. 1484 – September 1545), called Hans Baldung Grien, was a German painter, engraver, and printmaker in woodcut. A pupil of Albrecht Dürer, he is considered the most gifted student of the Nuremberg master and one of the leading artists of the German Renaissance.

Life and work

Born in Schwäbisch Gmünd into a family of academics, Baldung trained in Strasbourg and then in Dürer's Nuremberg workshop (1503–1507). He spent 1512–1517 in Freiburg im Breisgau painting the high altar of Freiburg Minster, his largest surviving altarpiece, before settling permanently in Strasbourg, where he became a leading citizen and embraced the Reformation.

Themes

Baldung is especially known for his vivid and unsettling treatments of witches, death, and the erotic — works such as The Three Ages of Woman and Death, the Death and the Maiden images, and numerous witchcraft woodcuts. He also produced portraits, religious altarpieces, stained-glass designs, and book illustrations.
