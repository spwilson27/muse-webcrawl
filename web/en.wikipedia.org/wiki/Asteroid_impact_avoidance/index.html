Asteroid impact avoidance
Field: Planetary defence
Key mission: DART (2022)
Methods: Kinetic impactor, gravity tractor, nuclear deflection

Asteroid impact avoidance comprises the methods by which near-Earth objects (NEOs) on a potential collision course with Earth could be diverted. A large NEO impact could cause catastrophic damage, so planetary defence programmes survey the sky to catalogue hazardous asteroids and comets and study deflection techniques.

Deflection methods

Proposed methods include the kinetic impactor — colliding a spacecraft with the object to change its velocity — the gravity tractor, which tugs an asteroid gravitationally over years, and, as a last resort, nuclear explosions near or on the surface. In 2022 NASA's DART spacecraft deliberately struck the asteroid moonlet Dimorphos and measurably shortened its orbital period, the first full-scale test of kinetic deflection.

Detection

Surveys such as Pan-STARRS, the Catalina Sky Survey and ATLAS search for NEOs; the planned NEO Surveyor space telescope is designed to find smaller, darker objects. With sufficient warning time, civil defence and evacuation are considered options for small impactors.
