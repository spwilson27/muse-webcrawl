Shoegaze

Stylistic originsAlternative rock, indie rock, dream pop, noise pop, psychedelia, ethereal wave

Shoegaze (originally called shoegazing and sometimes conflated with "the scene that celebrates itself") is a subgenre of indie and alternative rock characterised by its ethereal mixture of obscured vocals, guitar distortion and effects, feedback, and overwhelming volume. It emerged in Ireland and the United Kingdom in the late 1980s among groups influenced by dream pop, noise rock, and psychedelia.

Characteristics

The shoegaze sound typically layers heavily processed guitars — using reverb, delay, tremolo, and distortion — into a dense "wall of sound" in which individual instruments are difficult to distinguish. Vocals are usually soft, breathy, and mixed low, functioning as another texture rather than the focal point. Key techniques include the "glide guitar" of Kevin Shields, created by strumming while manipulating the tremolo arm. Live, performers stood still and looked down at their effects pedals, which inspired the genre's name.

History

Pioneering acts included My Bloody Valentine, whose 1991 album Loveless is the genre's defining record, along with Slowdive, Ride, Lush, Chapterhouse, and Swervedriver. The British music press, initially enthusiastic, turned on the scene in the early 1990s as Britpop and grunge rose to dominance, and most shoegaze bands broke up or changed direction. Loveless reportedly nearly bankrupted Creation Records due to its enormous recording costs.

Revival

From the 2000s onward, shoegaze underwent a sustained revival: the original bands reformed to acclaim (My Bloody Valentine released m b v in 2013; Slowdive and Ride returned with new albums in 2017), while new generations of artists — from "nu gaze" acts to the metal fusion of blackgaze (e.g., Deafheaven) — carried the sound forward. In the 2020s shoegaze found a large new audience through social media, becoming one of the most-streamed guitar genres among younger listeners.
