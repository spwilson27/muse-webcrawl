Son (music)

Son, commonly called son cubano, is a genre of Cuban music and dance that originated in the eastern region of Cuba (Oriente) in the late 19th century. It fuses Spanish guitar-and-lyric traditions with African rhythms and percussion, and it is the principal root of salsa music, as well as a major influence on Latin jazz and other Afro-Cuban styles.

Characteristics

Son is built on the clave rhythm — typically the son clave in 2–3 or 3–2 form — over a repetitive harmonic pattern played by the tres (a Cuban three-course guitar) and guitar, with an anticipated bass line (tumbao) that lands before the downbeat. Traditional instrumentation also includes bongos, maracas, claves, and later trumpets in the septeto format. Songs commonly alternate sung verses (canto) with a call-and-response refrain section (montuno) over which soloists improvise.

History

Son emerged in Oriente province, with early forms such as changüí in Guantánamo and son groups in Santiago de Cuba. By the 1910s–1920s it had reached Havana, where groups like the Sexteto Habanero and Trío Matamoros popularised it despite initial resistance from elites who considered it crude. Arsenio Rodríguez expanded the ensemble in the 1940s (adding conga drums, piano, and more trumpets), laying foundations for mambo and salsa. In the 1990s the Buena Vista Social Club recordings brought classic son to a worldwide audience.

Related styles

Regionally named variants include son montuno, sucu sucu, and the Mexican sones — son jarocho, son huasteco, and son jalisciense (mariachi) — which developed their own traditions in Mexico. The term son in Spanish simply means "sound", and the various sones share roots in colonial-era Spanish music blended with African and Indigenous elements.
