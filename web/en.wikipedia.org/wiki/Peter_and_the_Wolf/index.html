Peter and the Wolf

Composer: Sergei Prokofiev

Opus: Op. 67 (1936)

Form: Symphonic fairy tale for narrator and orchestra

Peter and the Wolf (Russian: Петя и волк, Op. 67) is a "symphonic fairy tale for children" by the Russian composer Sergei Prokofiev, written in 1936 for Moscow's Central Children's Theatre. A narrator tells the story while the orchestra illustrates it, each character represented by a particular instrument and musical theme — a celebrated introduction to the instruments of the orchestra.

Story and themes

Young Peter, disobeying his grandfather, ventures into the meadow. His animal friends are characterised by leitmotifs:

  • Peter — the string quartet (violin, viola, cello, double bass)
  • The bird — flute
  • The duck — oboe
  • The cat — clarinet
  • The grandfather — bassoon
  • The wolf — three French horns
  • The hunters — woodwind theme with timpani and bass drum "rifle shots"

When the wolf swallows the duck and menaces the bird and cat, Peter, with the bird's help, lassos the wolf by its tail from a tree branch. The hunters arrive, but Peter persuades them to take the wolf alive in a triumphant procession to the zoo — with the duck, still alive, quacking faintly inside the wolf's belly.

Reception

The work premiered on 2 May 1936 in Moscow. It has been recorded by innumerable narrators, from Basil Rathbone and Peter Ustinov to David Bowie and Sean Connery, and Walt Disney produced an animated version in 1946 as part of Make Mine Music. Prokofiev's cheerful score remains one of the most frequently performed works in the orchestral repertoire.
