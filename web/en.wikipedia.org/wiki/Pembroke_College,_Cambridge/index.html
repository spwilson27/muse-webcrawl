Pembroke College is a constituent college of the University of Cambridge, England. Founded in 1347 by Marie de St Pol, Countess of Pembroke, it is the third-oldest of Cambridge's colleges. Pembroke has a long tradition of scholarship and a distinguished roll of alumni, and its courts include Christopher Wren's first architectural commission.

History

On Christmas Eve 1347, Edward III granted Marie de St Pol licence to found a house of scholars in Cambridge. The college was first known as Pembroke Hall. Its chapel, consecrated in 1665, was designed by the young Christopher Wren—himself a nephew of the Master, Matthew Wren—and is regarded as the first completed building of his career. The college admitted women undergraduates from 1983.

People

Notable Pembroke alumni and fellows include the poets Edmund Spenser and Thomas Gray, the statesman William Pitt the Younger, abolitionist Thomas Clarkson, and in recent times the actor Tom Hiddleston and the writer and comedian David Mitchell. The college is also known for a strong literary tradition and for the gardens behind its main courts.
