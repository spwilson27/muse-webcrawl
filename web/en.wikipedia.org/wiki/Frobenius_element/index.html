Frobenius element

In algebraic number theory, a Frobenius element is a special element of a Galois group of a Galois extension of number fields, associated with a prime ideal that remains unramified in the extension. Named after Ferdinand Georg Frobenius, these elements generalise the Frobenius automorphism of finite fields — the map that raises every element to the p-th power — and they play a central role in class field theory and in the Chebotarev density theorem.

Definition

Let L/K be a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group G, and let p be a prime ideal of K unramified in L, with P a prime of L above it. The decomposition group at P maps onto the Galois group of the residue field extension. The Frobenius element FrobP is the unique element of the decomposition group whose action on the residue field is the Frobenius automorphism x ↦ xq, where q is the norm of p. Changing the prime P above p conjugates the element, so the Frobenius conjugacy class Frobp in G is well defined.

Properties and applications

The order of the Frobenius element equals the residue degree of the prime, and the prime splits completely in L if and only if its Frobenius element is the identity. The Chebotarev density theorem states that the Frobenius conjugacy classes are equidistributed among the conjugacy classes of G according to their sizes; this generalises Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions and Frobenius's own density theorem. In the theory of L-functions, local factors at unramified primes are defined using Frobenius elements, and the formalism extends to Galois representations and the Langlands programme, where Frobenius elements label the local parameters.
