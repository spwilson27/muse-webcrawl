Beef Wellington

Beef Wellington, sliced to show the layers of pastry, duxelles and fillet.

Beef Wellington is a steak dish of English origin, made of fillet steak coated with pâté (often pâté de foie gras) and duxelles, wrapped in puff pastry and baked. Some recipes include a wrapping of prosciutto or a crêpe around the fillet to hold in moisture and prevent the pastry from becoming soggy.

Origin of the name

The name is popularly said to commemorate Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington, and his victory at the Battle of Waterloo in 1815, though no contemporary evidence connects the dish to the duke; the name appears in print only in the twentieth century, and similar dishes of meat baked in pastry, such as the French filet de bœuf en croûte, predate it. The association may rest on the finished dish's resemblance to a Wellington boot.

Preparation

A centre-cut beef fillet is seared, brushed with mustard, coated with a finely chopped mushroom duxelles (sometimes with pâté), wrapped in ham and puff pastry, egg-washed and baked until the pastry is golden and the meat medium-rare. The dish was strongly popularised in the late twentieth century and is closely associated with the chef Gordon Ramsay. Variations include individual portions and vegetarian versions using beetroot or celeriac.
