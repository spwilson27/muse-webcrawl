De novo peptide sequencing

A tandem mass spectrum annotated with the fragment-ion series used to read a peptide sequence directly.

In proteomics, de novo peptide sequencing is the determination of a peptide's amino acid sequence directly from its tandem mass spectrum (MS/MS), without reference to a protein sequence database. The method is essential when the genome of the organism under study is unknown, when working with antibodies, venoms or other novel peptides, and when the peptide carries unexpected modifications.

Principle

A peptide selected for fragmentation in a mass spectrometer typically breaks along its backbone, producing series of fragment ions — conventionally called b-ions (retaining the N-terminus) and y-ions (retaining the C-terminus). The mass difference between consecutive ions in a series equals the mass of a single amino acid residue, so the sequence can in principle be read by "walking" along the ladder of peaks in the spectrum. In practice, spectra are noisy, fragment ions may be missing, and some residues are difficult to distinguish: leucine and isoleucine are isobaric (identical in mass), and lysine and glutamine differ by only a fraction of a dalton, requiring very accurate mass measurements.

Algorithms

Computational approaches to de novo sequencing include spectrum-graph methods, in which peaks are treated as nodes and mass differences matching amino acid residues as edges, and dynamic-programming and machine-learning methods that score candidate sequences against the observed fragments. Widely used tools include PEAKS, Novor, pNovo and DeepNovo. Modern deep-learning approaches have substantially improved accuracy, particularly on high-resolution spectra from Orbitrap and time-of-flight instruments. De novo results are often combined with database searching in hybrid workflows: short, confidently identified sequence tags are used to anchor database matches, while the full de novo sequence serves to detect novel peptides and unanticipated post-translational modifications.

Applications

  • Sequencing of monoclonal antibodies for therapeutic development
  • Characterisation of peptide toxins and venoms
  • Metaproteomics of organisms without sequenced genomes
  • HLA immunopeptidomics, where presented peptides do not follow genomic templates directly
