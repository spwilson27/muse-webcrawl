Climbing plant

For other uses, see Vine.

A climbing plant is a plant that climbs up trees and other tall objects. Many climbers are vines whose stems wrap around trees and branches, but there are other methods of climbing as well. Botanists divide climbing plants into two broad groups: bines, which twine their stems around a support, and vines, which climb using tendrils, suckers, thorns, or other methods.

Growth habits

Climbers can be distinguished by their attachment mechanisms:

  • Twining stems — the stem itself wraps around a support, as in morning glory, honeysuckle, and hops.
  • Tendrils — modified stems, leaves, or petioles that coil around objects, as in peas and grapes.
  • Adhesive pads or aerial rootlets — as in English ivy and Virginia creeper.
  • Scramblers — plants that lean on other vegetation, sometimes aided by thorns or hooks, as in roses and bougainvillea.

Ecology and horticulture

Climbing plants are especially abundant in tropical forests, where lianas form a significant part of the flora. They are widely used in horticulture and landscaping to cover walls, fences, pergolas, and trellises, and some, such as the kiwi and passion fruit, are grown for their fruit.
