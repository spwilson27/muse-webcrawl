Dicta Boelcke

The Dicta Boelcke is a list of fundamental tactics of aerial warfare formulated in 1916 by Oswald Boelcke, the German World War I flying ace and pioneering air tactician. It is considered one of the first codified sets of rules for air combat maneuvering, and many of its principles remain relevant to fighter pilots today.

The rules

The eight rules, in paraphrase, advise pilots to: (1) secure the advantages of position — height, sun, and surprise — before attacking; (2) once an attack begins, carry it through to the end; (3) fire only at close range when the opponent is properly in the sights; (4) never lose sight of the opponent and beware of ruses; (5) attack from behind wherever possible; (6) if dived upon from above, turn to meet the attack rather than flee; (7) when over enemy lines, always remember one's own line of retreat; and (8) attack in groups of four or six, avoiding several pilots going after the same opponent.

Legacy

Boelcke, mentor of Manfred von Richthofen (the "Red Baron"), distilled the dicta from his experience organizing Jagdstaffel 2. The principles influenced fighter doctrine in the Second World War and beyond, and Boelcke is regarded as the father of the German fighter air force.
