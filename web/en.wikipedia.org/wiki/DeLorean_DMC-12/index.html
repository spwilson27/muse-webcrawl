The DeLorean DMC-12 is a rear-engine two-passenger sports car manufactured and marketed by John DeLorean's DeLorean Motor Company (DMC) for the American market from 1981 until 1983. It was the only automobile produced by the company, which was founded by DeLorean, a former General Motors executive.

The car is best known for its distinctive design features, including gull-wing doors and a fiberglass body structure with a brushed stainless steel outer skin, as well as its lackluster power relative to its looks. It achieved lasting fame after its appearance as the time machine in the Back to the Future film trilogy.

History

John DeLorean left General Motors in 1973 and founded the DeLorean Motor Company in 1975. The first prototype appeared in 1976, and production officially began in January 1981 at the DMC factory in Dunmurry, Northern Ireland. Approximately 9,000 DMC-12s were made before production halted in early 1983, following DMC's bankruptcy that year.

The British government had provided substantial financial incentives to locate the factory in Northern Ireland, and some 2,500 jobs were created. However, the car suffered from quality problems, a high price of $25,000, and an underpowered engine that delivered only about 130 horsepower.

Design

The DMC-12 was designed by Giorgetto Giugiaro of ItalDesign and is noted for its wedge shape, gull-wing doors, and unpainted stainless steel body panels. The chassis, engineered with assistance from Lotus Cars, used a steel backbone frame derived from the Lotus Esprit. The car's name, DMC-12, derived from its intended original price of US$12,000, though by launch the price had more than doubled.

Engine and performance

The car is powered by a 2,849 cc Peugeot-Renault-Volvo (PRV) ZMJ-159 V6 engine mounted behind the rear axle. In United States specification it produced 130 hp (97 kW), giving a 0–60 mph time of about 8.8 to 10.5 seconds depending on transmission—slower than many contemporary sports cars. European-specification cars produced about 156 hp.

In popular culture

The DMC-12 gained worldwide fame as the basis for the time machine in Back to the Future (1985) and its sequels. About 100 cars were modified for the films. The association transformed the DeLorean into a cultural icon and greatly increased the value and preservation of surviving examples.

Successors and revival

In 1995, mechanic Stephen Wynne founded a new DeLorean Motor Company in Humble, Texas, acquiring the remaining parts inventory and the DMC trademark. The company supplies parts and services to owners and has periodically discussed limited new production. In 2022, a separate enterprise, DeLorean Motors Reimagined, unveiled the Alpha5 electric sports car concept.
