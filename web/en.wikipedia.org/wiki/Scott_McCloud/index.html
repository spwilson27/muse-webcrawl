Scott McCloud, comics theorist and cartoonist.

Scott McCloud (born June 10, 1960) is an American cartoonist and comics theorist, best known for his non-fiction books about comics — themselves written in comic form — beginning with Understanding Comics (1993), a widely acclaimed analysis of the medium's language and history.

Career

McCloud first gained attention with the science-fiction/superhero series Zot! (1984–1991), published by Eclipse Comics, which mixed manga influences with an optimistic tone. He was a leading figure in the 1980s creator's-rights movement and drafted the Creator's Bill of Rights in 1988. He also initiated the "24-hour comic" challenge, in which a cartoonist attempts to complete a 24-page comic in 24 hours.

Understanding Comics and sequels

Understanding Comics: The Invisible Art (1993) defines comics as "juxtaposed pictorial and other images in deliberate sequence," and explores concepts including closure (the reader's construction of events between panels), the picture plane, iconography and the "masking effect" of simplified characters. Narrated by a cartoon avatar of McCloud, it became a standard text in comics studies and visual communication. Its sequels are Reinventing Comics (2000), which examines digital production and online distribution — including the "infinite canvas" idea central to webcomics — and Making Comics (2006), a practical guide to storytelling craft.

Later work

McCloud created the Google Chrome comic explaining the browser in 2008 and published the graphic novel The Sculptor in 2015, a Faustian story about a sculptor granted the power to shape matter with his hands. He lectures internationally on comics and visual communication.
