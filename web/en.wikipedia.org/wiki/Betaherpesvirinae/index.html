Betaherpesvirinae

[Image placeholder: Betaherpesvirus particle]

Betaherpesvirinae is a subfamily of viruses in the order Herpesvirales and family Herpesviridae. Mammals serve as natural hosts. There are currently around 30 species in this subfamily, divided among five genera. Diseases associated with this subfamily include infection of mucous membranes, mononucleosis-like illness, and congenital defects.

The betaherpesviruses are characterized by a slow reproductive cycle in vitro and latency in secretory glands, lymphoreticular cells, kidneys and other tissues. The best-known members are the cytomegaloviruses (such as human cytomegalovirus, HHV-5) and the roseoloviruses HHV-6 and HHV-7, which cause roseola infantum.

Genera

The subfamily includes the genera Cytomegalovirus, Muromegalovirus, Proboscivirus, Quwivirus and Roseolovirus. Human cytomegalovirus is a leading infectious cause of congenital hearing loss and a serious pathogen in immunocompromised transplant recipients.
