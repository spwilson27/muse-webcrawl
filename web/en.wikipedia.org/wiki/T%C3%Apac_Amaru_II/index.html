Túpac Amaru II (1738–1781), born José Gabriel Condorcanqui Noguera, was a Peruvian Indigenous leader who led a massive rebellion against Spanish colonial rule in 1780–1781. He claimed descent from Túpac Amaru, the last Inca ruler of Vilcabamba, and took his name.

Rebellion

Condorcanqui was a wealthy kuraka (hereditary Indigenous chief) and muleteer who had petitioned colonial authorities against abuses of the mita labour draft, the repartimiento system, and rising taxes. On 4 November 1780 he seized and executed the corregidor Antonio de Arriaga, launching a revolt that spread across the Andes and drew tens of thousands of Indigenous, mestizo, and some creole supporters. His forces won early victories but failed to take Cusco, and he was captured in April 1781.

Death and legacy

Túpac Amaru II was brutally executed in Cusco's main plaza on 18 May 1781, after being forced to witness the execution of his family. The rebellion continued under relatives such as Túpac Katari in Upper Peru. He remains a national hero in Peru and an enduring symbol of Indigenous resistance; his name was later adopted by movements including Uruguay's Tupamaros and Peru's MRTA, and by the rapper Tupac Shakur.
