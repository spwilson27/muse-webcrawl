Virtual assistant

A virtual assistant (also called an AI assistant or digital assistant) is a software agent that can perform a range of tasks or services for a user based on voice commands, text input, or other inputs. Well-known examples include Apple's Siri, Amazon's Alexa, Google Assistant and Microsoft's Cortana. Such systems typically interpret human speech using natural language processing, answer questions, make recommendations and perform actions by delegating requests to web services.

Virtual assistants run on phones, smart speakers and other devices

History

The first tool that enabled digital speech recognition was the IBM Shoebox, presented in 1961. Speech-recognition experiments continued for decades; the introduction of Siri on the iPhone 4S in 2011 brought voice assistants to mass-market consumer devices. Amazon Echo (2014) popularised the always-listening smart speaker, and assistants spread to cars, wearables and home appliances. Since the early 2020s, assistants have increasingly been rebuilt around large language models, enabling more open-ended conversational and task-completion abilities, including generative chatbots such as ChatGPT-based systems.

Capabilities

Virtual assistants can set timers and reminders, manage calendars, place calls, send messages, search the web, report weather and news, control smart-home devices, play media, and perform transactions. They access device data such as contacts, location and messages — with the user's permission — to personalise responses.

Privacy concerns

Because assistants process voice recordings and personal data, they have raised privacy concerns about always-on microphones, accidental recordings, human review of audio snippets, and the retention of interaction history. Manufacturers have responded with on-device processing options, mute switches and deletion controls; regulators in some jurisdictions have investigated consent and data-protection compliance.
