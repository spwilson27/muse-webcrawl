U.S. Agency for Global Media

The U.S. Agency for Global Media (USAGM), known until August 2018 as the Broadcasting Board of Governors (BBG), is an independent agency of the United States federal government that oversees the country's international civilian broadcasting services. Its networks broadcast news and information in more than 60 languages to audiences in over 100 countries, with a stated mission to inform, engage and connect people around the world in support of freedom and democracy.

History

American international broadcasting began with the Voice of America (VOA), founded in 1942. During the Cold War, surrogate broadcasters Radio Free Europe (1950) and Radio Liberty (1953) beamed news into communist states. The Broadcasting Board of Governors was created by the International Broadcasting Act of 1994, consolidating oversight of VOA, Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty, Radio Free Asia (1996), the Middle East Broadcasting Networks (Alhurra and Radio Sawa), and Radio and TV Martí for Cuba. In 2018 the agency was renamed the U.S. Agency for Global Media.

Structure

USAGM supervises two federal networks — the Voice of America and the Office of Cuba Broadcasting — and funds independent grantee organizations: Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty, Radio Free Asia, the Middle East Broadcasting Networks, and the Open Technology Fund, which supports internet-freedom technologies. A statutory "firewall" is intended to protect the networks' journalistic independence from political interference, a principle that has been the subject of recurring political debate. The agency is headed by a chief executive officer; governance reforms enacted in 2016 replaced the part-time bipartisan board with a CEO appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.

Reach

The agency reports a weekly audience of several hundred million people across television, radio, digital and social media platforms, concentrated in countries where local media are restricted or state-controlled.
