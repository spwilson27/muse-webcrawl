Water fluoridation in the United States

Roughly three quarters of Americans on public water systems receive fluoridated water.

Since the mid-20th century, water fluoridation has been decided largely at the state and local level in the United States, where it is widespread: around 73% of the population served by community water systems — over 200 million people — receives fluoridated water. Decision-making is decentralized, resting with city councils, water districts or voters in referendums, while federal agencies set recommendations and safety standards.

History

The United States pioneered fluoridation: Grand Rapids, Michigan became the first city in the world to deliberately fluoridate its supply in January 1945, in a controlled study paired with nearby Muskegon. A parallel Newburgh–Kingston trial in New York compared a fluoridated city with an unfluoridated one. Early results showed dramatic reductions in children's cavities, and hundreds of communities adopted the practice during the 1950s and 1960s, despite organized opposition portraying it as a communist plot or as mass medication. Federal recommendations evolved from a temperature-dependent range of 0.7–1.2 mg/L to a single recommended level of 0.7 mg/L in 2015, reflecting additional fluoride exposure from toothpaste and other sources.

Regulation and debate

The Environmental Protection Agency sets a maximum contaminant level of 4.0 mg/L for naturally occurring fluoride and a secondary standard of 2.0 mg/L, while the Department of Health and Human Services recommends the optimal level. Debate in the United States has persisted: some cities, including Portland, Oregon, long declined to fluoridate, and voters have started and stopped fluoridation in various communities. In 2024, a federal court ruled that fluoridation at current levels posed an unreasonable risk warranting EPA regulatory review under the Toxic Substances Control Act, following a National Toxicology Program review of fluoride and neurodevelopment — a decision contested by dental and public-health organizations, which continue to endorse the practice as safe and effective.
