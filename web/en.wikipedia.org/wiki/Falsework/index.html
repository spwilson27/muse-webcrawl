Falsework

Falsework
PurposeTemporary support of permanent works during construction
Typical materialsTimber, steel tubes and fittings, proprietary frames
RelatedFormwork, shoring, scaffolding, centering

Falsework consists of temporary structures used in construction to support a permanent structure until its construction is sufficiently advanced to support itself. In bridge construction, falsework supports the spans (or arches) during assembly and until the deck can carry its own weight; in building work it supports formwork for cast in-situ concrete slabs until the concrete has gained adequate strength.

Types

Falsework ranges from simple vertical props and timber posts to engineered systems of steel scaffold tubes, adjustable jacks, and heavy-duty shoring towers. "Centering" is the specialised falsework used to support arches during construction. Trestle falsework, built as a temporary bridge of bents and stringers, is used to support long spans over water or deep valleys.

Design and safety

Because falsework carries the full weight of wet concrete, reinforcement and construction loads, it must be designed as carefully as the permanent structure. Standards such as BS 5975 in the UK and various national codes set out procedures for design approval, erection, inspection and dismantling. Falsework failures — often caused by inadequate foundations, bracing or material quality — have caused several catastrophic collapses during construction, making falsework coordination and independent checking a standard part of temporary-works management.

Removal

Falsework is struck (removed) in a controlled sequence once the permanent structure can support itself, allowing loads to transfer gradually. Timber wedges, screw jacks or sand boxes are used to lower the falsework evenly before dismantling.
