Rogallo wing

The Rogallo wing is a type of flexible wing with a shape consisting of two partial conelike surfaces meeting at the front, invented by aeronautical engineer Francis Rogallo and his wife Gertrude Rogallo in the late 1940s. Originally developed as a simple, inexpensive flexible "parawing", it was investigated by NASA in the 1960s as a steerable recovery system for returning spacecraft such as Gemini capsules to a gliding land landing.

Design and use

The wing's two lobes billow under aerodynamic pressure into a lifting shape held by a keel and two leading-edge spars. Although NASA's paraglider programme was abandoned in favour of parachutes and ocean splashdown, the design found lasting application in sport aviation: it underlies the modern hang glider, and derivatives are used in ultralight trikes, kite systems and some unmanned aerial vehicles.
