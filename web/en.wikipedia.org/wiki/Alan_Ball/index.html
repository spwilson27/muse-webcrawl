Alan James Ball (12 May 1945 – 25 April 2007) was an English professional footballer and manager. He was the youngest member of the England team that won the 1966 FIFA World Cup, and was widely credited as the man of the match in the final against West Germany, in which his tireless running and crossing set up goals for Geoff Hurst.

Club career

Ball began his career with Blackpool, making his league debut as a teenager in 1962. After the World Cup triumph he joined Everton for a British record fee of £112,000, and formed the famous "Holy Trinity" midfield with Howard Kendall and Colin Harvey, winning the First Division title in 1969–70. He moved to Arsenal in 1971 for another record fee of £220,000, and later played for Southampton, with spells in North America and Hong Kong in between.

International career

Ball won 72 caps for England between 1965 and 1975, scoring 8 goals. He also played at the 1970 World Cup in Mexico. His high-pitched voice and small stature made him one of the most recognisable English players of his era.

Managerial career and death

After retiring, Ball managed several clubs, including Blackpool, Portsmouth, Stoke City, Exeter City, Southampton and Manchester City. He died of a heart attack at his home in Warsash, Hampshire, in 2007 while attempting to tackle a garden fire.
