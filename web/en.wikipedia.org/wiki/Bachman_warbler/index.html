Bachman's warbler (Vermivora bachmanii) is an extinct species of New World warbler that once bred in the swampy canebrakes of the south-eastern United States and wintered in Cuba. It was among the rarest songbirds in North America and was last reliably seen in the early 1960s.

Description and history

The species was discovered near Charleston, South Carolina, in 1832 by the naturalist John Bachman, a friend of John James Audubon, who painted it without ever seeing a live bird. Males were yellow below with an olive back and black throat patch and crown. The warbler depended on dense stands of giant cane in seasonally flooded bottomland forests — habitat largely destroyed by logging and drainage — and on Cuban wintering grounds that were also cleared.

Extinction

After decades without confirmed records, the US Fish and Wildlife Service proposed delisting the species due to extinction in 2021 and formally declared it extinct in 2023.
