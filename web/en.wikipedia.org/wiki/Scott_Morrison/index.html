Scott John Morrison (born 13 May 1968) is an Australian former politician who served as the 30th prime minister of Australia from 2018 to 2022. He held office as leader of the Liberal Party of Australia and was the member of parliament (MP) for the New South Wales division of Cook from 2007 until his resignation in 2024.

Morrison was born in Sydney and studied economic geography at the University of New South Wales. Before entering politics, he worked in tourism, including as managing director of Tourism Australia. He was first elected to the Australian House of Representatives at the 2007 election. Following the Coalition's victory at the 2013 election, Morrison was appointed Minister for Immigration and Border Protection in the Abbott government, where he was responsible for Operation Sovereign Borders. In 2015 he became Treasurer in the Turnbull government.

In August 2018, after two leadership spills in the Liberal Party, Morrison was elected leader and became prime minister. He led the Coalition to a surprise victory at the 2019 federal election. His government responded to the COVID-19 pandemic and established the AUKUS security partnership with the United Kingdom and the United States in 2021. Morrison's government was defeated at the 2022 federal election, and he resigned as Liberal leader. It was later revealed that during the pandemic he had secretly appointed himself to administer five additional ministries, a revelation that attracted significant criticism. He retired from politics in February 2024.

Early life and career

Morrison was born in Waverley, Sydney, the younger of two sons of Marion and John Douglas Morrison, a policeman who served on the Waverley Council. He was educated at Sydney Boys High School and studied economic geography at the University of New South Wales, graduating with honours. He worked in the New Zealand tourism sector and later as managing director of Tourism Australia from 2004 to 2006.

Early political career

Morrison was the state director of the Liberal Party in New South Wales from 2000 to 2004. After a disputed preselection process, he won the seat of Cook at the 2007 election. As immigration minister from 2013, he oversaw the reintroduction of offshore processing and the turnback of asylum seeker boats. As social services minister and then treasurer, he developed a profile as a leading figure of the party's conservative wing.

Prime Minister (2018–2022)

Morrison became prime minister on 24 August 2018 after defeating Peter Dutton in a leadership ballot prompted by Malcolm Turnbull's resignation. His government's first term was dominated by drought, the 2019–20 Black Summer bushfires — during which his decision to holiday in Hawaii attracted wide criticism — and the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. Australia's pandemic response, combining border closures and suppression measures, kept early case numbers low; the vaccine rollout was initially slow but reached high coverage by late 2021.

In foreign policy, Morrison's government announced the AUKUS pact in September 2021, strained relations with France over the cancelled submarine contract, and saw relations with China deteriorate. His government lost the May 2022 election to Anthony Albanese's Labor Party. In November 2022, Morrison became the first former prime minister to be censured by the House of Representatives, over his secret self-appointments to multiple ministries.

Post-politics

Morrison remained a backbencher after the 2022 election and announced his resignation from parliament in January 2024, triggering a by-election in Cook. He subsequently took up roles in the private sector, including advisory work in the defence industry.

Personal life

Morrison is married to Jenny Warren, whom he met through church in his teens; they have two daughters. He is a Pentecostal Christian and attends Horizon Church in Sutherland. He is a supporter of the Cronulla-Sutherland Sharks rugby league team.
