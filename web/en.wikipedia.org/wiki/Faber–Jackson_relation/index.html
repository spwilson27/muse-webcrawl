Faber–Jackson relation

The Faber–Jackson relation is an empirical power law relating the luminosity L of an elliptical galaxy to the central velocity dispersion σ of its stars. It was first described by Sandra Faber and Robert Jackson in 1976 and is approximately

L ∝ σγ, with γ ≈ 4.

Explanation

The relation follows from the virial theorem combined with the observed constancy of surface brightness among elliptical galaxies: for a self-gravitating system in equilibrium, velocity dispersion, size and mass are linked, and if the mass-to-light ratio and surface brightness are roughly constant, luminosity scales as the fourth power of velocity dispersion.

Uses

Because velocity dispersion can be measured spectroscopically independent of distance, the relation allows astronomers to estimate a galaxy's intrinsic luminosity, and hence its distance, by comparing with its apparent brightness. It is one projection of the more general fundamental plane of elliptical galaxies. Its spiral-galaxy counterpart is the Tully–Fisher relation.
