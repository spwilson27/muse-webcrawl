The National September 11 Memorial & Museum (also known as the 9/11 Memorial & Museum) is a memorial and museum in New York City commemorating the September 11, 2001 attacks, which killed 2,977 people, and the 1993 World Trade Center bombing, which killed six. It is located at the World Trade Center site, the former location of the Twin Towers, in Lower Manhattan.

The memorial

The memorial, titled Reflecting Absence, was designed by architect Michael Arad with landscape architect Peter Walker, chosen from more than 5,000 design entries. Its centrepiece is a pair of nearly one-acre reflecting pools set in the footprints of the Twin Towers, fed by the largest man-made waterfalls in North America. The names of every victim of the 2001 and 1993 attacks are inscribed on bronze parapets around the pools, arranged in "meaningful adjacencies". The plaza is planted with swamp white oak trees, including the "Survivor Tree", a callery pear recovered from the wreckage. The memorial opened on the tenth anniversary of the attacks, 11 September 2011.

The museum

The underground museum, opened in May 2014, houses artefacts of the attacks — including twisted steel, damaged emergency vehicles, the "Last Column", and personal effects — together with exhibitions on the events of the day, their antecedents, and their aftermath. It sits largely within the archaeological footprint of the original complex, alongside remnants of the slurry wall and the towers' column bases.
