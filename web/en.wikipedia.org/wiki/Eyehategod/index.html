Eyehategod

Eyehategod (also abbreviated and referred to as EHG) is an American sludge metal band from New Orleans, Louisiana, formed in 1988. The band is known for their dark, distorted and misanthropic sound, drawing on a mixture of Black Sabbath-influenced doom metal, hardcore punk and Southern rock. They have become one of the most important bands to emerge from the NOLA (New Orleans, Louisiana) metal scene, and are frequently credited with helping to pioneer sludge metal alongside Crowbar, Buzzov•en and Acid Bath.

History

Eyehategod was formed by guitarist Jimmy Bower and drummer Joey LaCaze, and the lineup solidified with vocalist Mike Williams (also known as Mike IX Williams), guitarist Brian Patton and bassist Mark Schultz, later replaced by Gary Mader. Their debut album In the Name of Suffering (1990) was followed by Take as Needed for Pain (1993), widely regarded as a sludge classic, and Dopesick (1996), recorded amid personal and addiction struggles. Confederacy of Ruined Lives followed in 2000.

The band's career was disrupted by Hurricane Katrina in 2005 and by members' personal difficulties. Drummer Joey LaCaze died in August 2013 of respiratory failure shortly after a European tour; Aaron Hill replaced him. Mike Williams underwent a liver transplant in 2016, funded partly by a fan campaign, and returned to performing in 2017. The album A History of Nomadic Behavior was released in March 2021 on Century Media Records.

Musical style

Eyehategod's music combines down-tuned, feedback-drenched guitar riffs with bluesy grooves, abrupt tempo shifts into hardcore tempos, and Williams's anguished screamed vocals. Lyrics deal with addiction, despair, poverty and misanthropy. Their sound influenced generations of sludge and doom bands.
