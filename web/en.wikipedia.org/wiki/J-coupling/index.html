J-coupling

Multiplet splitting in an NMR spectrum arising from scalar (J) coupling.

In nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, J-coupling, also called scalar coupling, is the indirect interaction between two nuclear spins mediated by the electrons of the chemical bonds connecting them. Unlike the direct dipole–dipole interaction, scalar coupling is transmitted through bonding electrons, principally by the Fermi contact interaction.

Manifestation in spectra

J-coupling causes the resonance signal of a nucleus to split into a multiplet — a doublet, triplet, quartet, and so on — according to the number and spin states of its coupled neighbours. The separation between the components, the coupling constant J, is measured in hertz and is independent of the strength of the applied magnetic field. Couplings are labelled by the number of bonds between the nuclei: geminal (²J, two bonds), vicinal (³J, three bonds), and long-range (⁴J and beyond), with magnitudes generally shrinking as the bond count increases.

Structural information

Because vicinal coupling constants depend on the dihedral angle between bonds — a relationship described by the Karplus equation — ³J values are a powerful source of conformational information in organic chemistry and structural biology. Two-bond and three-bond couplings also reveal connectivity, forming the basis of correlation experiments such as COSY in two-dimensional NMR.
