Wujek Coal Mine

The Wujek Coal Mine (Polish: Kopalnia Węgla Kamiennego "Wujek") is a coal mine in Katowice, in the Silesian Voivodeship of southern Poland. It is best known internationally as the site of the Pacification of Wujek, a violent suppression of striking workers by communist security forces in December 1981, shortly after the declaration of martial law in Poland, in which nine miners were shot dead.

History

Coal extraction began on the site in the early 20th century, when the region was part of the German Empire. The mine, known in German as Oheim, was organised as two shafts; after the Silesian plebiscite and the partition of Upper Silesia in 1922, the area became part of Poland and the mine took the Polish name Wujek.

Pacification of 1981

Following the imposition of martial law on 13 December 1981 by General Wojciech Jaruzelski, workers at Wujek went on strike to protest the internment of trade union activists, including Jan Ludwiczak, chairman of the Solidarity organisation at the mine. On 16 December 1981, units of the motorised riot police (ZOMO), supported by the army and by special platoons, stormed the mine. During the operation nine miners were killed and many others wounded. The victims became symbols of resistance to the communist regime.

After 1989

After the fall of communism, commemorations were held at the mine, and a memorial and museum were established near the site of the shooting. In 2008 a court convicted former ZOMO commander Czesław Kiszczak in connection with the events. The mine continued operating as part of Polska Grupa Górnicza; in 2009 a methane explosion at the associated Ruch Śląsk part of the mine killed 20 miners. A merger of the Wujek and Murcki-Staszic mines was later carried out as part of restructuring of the Polish coal industry.
