Osteopathic medicine

Osteopathic medicine is a branch of medicine, most fully developed in the United States, in which physicians — Doctors of Osteopathic Medicine (DOs) — are trained and licensed to practise the full scope of medicine and surgery, with an additional emphasis on preventive care, the musculoskeletal system and osteopathic manipulative treatment (OMT).

The discipline was founded by Andrew Taylor Still, an American physician who opened the first school of osteopathy in Kirksville, Missouri, in 1892. Its traditional principles hold that the body has an inherent capacity for self-healing, that structure and function are interrelated, and that treatment should consider the patient as a whole.

In the United States, DOs complete four years of osteopathic medical school followed by residency training, and are fully licensed physicians; they make up a substantial and growing share of the US physician workforce. In many other countries, "osteopaths" practise a more limited form of manual therapy without full medical licensure, which can cause confusion between the two professions.
