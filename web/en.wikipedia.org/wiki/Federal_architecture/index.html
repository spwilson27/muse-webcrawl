Federal architecture

Federal-style architecture is the name for the classicizing architecture built in the newly founded United States between c. 1780 and 1830, and particularly from 1785 to 1815, which was influenced by the Adam style of Great Britain (hence also called Adam style in America).

Characteristics

Federal buildings typically feature a symmetrical facade, three-part (Palladian) windows, decorative crown moulding, elliptical fanlights over the front door, and slender, refined ornament. Interiors often have three-dimensional plaster decoration and oval rooms.

Designers

Prominent designers include Charles Bulfinch in Boston, Samuel McIntire in Salem, Massachusetts, and Asher Benjamin, whose pattern books spread the style. The style gave way to Greek Revival in the 1820s–1830s.
