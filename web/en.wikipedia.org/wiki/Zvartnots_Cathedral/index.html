Zvartnots Cathedral (Armenian: Զվարթնոց, "celestial angels") is a 7th-century centrally planned aisled tetraconch type Armenian cathedral built by order of Catholicos Nerses III the Builder from 643 to 661, near Vagharshapat (Etchmiadzin) in modern Armenia. Now in ruins, it was one of the most ambitious churches of early medieval Armenia and remains a symbol of Armenian architecture; it appears on the former 100-dram banknote and gave its name to Zvartnots International Airport in Yerevan.

History

According to tradition, the church was built where Saint Gregory the Illuminator was believed to have met King Tiridates III, and it was dedicated to Saint Gregory. At its dedication, the Byzantine emperor Constans II was reportedly present and so admired the building that he wished to erect a copy in Constantinople. The cathedral stood for some three centuries before collapsing, probably in an earthquake in the 10th century; the reasons for its tall, daring structure's failure are still debated. Buried under debris, the site was re-excavated in the early 20th century (excavations 1901–1907 directed by Archimandrite Khachik Dadian, and architectural reconstruction proposed by Toros Toramanian).

Architecture

Zvartnots was a three-storey circular structure, about 45 metres high, built around a tetraconch (four-apsed) core surrounded by a polygonal ambulatory. Its interior was ringed by carved columns with distinctive capitals, including eagle capitals, and rich relief decoration with pomegranates and vines. The design influenced later Armenian churches, most notably the church of Saint Gregory of Gagikashen at Ani, commissioned as a copy of Zvartnots. A museum on the site displays excavated capitals and a model of Toramanian's reconstruction.

World Heritage status

In 2000 the ruins were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List together with the churches of Etchmiadzin, as an outstanding example of early Armenian church architecture.
