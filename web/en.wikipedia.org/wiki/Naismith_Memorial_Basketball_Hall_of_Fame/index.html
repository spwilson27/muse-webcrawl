The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame is an American history museum and hall of fame located in Springfield, Massachusetts, the city where basketball was invented in 1891 by James Naismith, for whom the hall is named. It honours players, coaches, referees, teams and other major contributors to the game from around the world, at professional, collegiate and amateur levels.

History

The Hall of Fame was founded in 1959 and inducted its first class that year, although its first building did not open until 1968, on the campus of Springfield College. The current facility, a large basketball-shaped building on the Connecticut River, opened in 2002. Unlike the halls of fame of the major North American leagues, the Basketball Hall of Fame covers the entire sport internationally, and enshrines men and women from the NBA, WNBA, NCAA, international competition and the early barnstorming era.

Selection

Candidates are screened by committees covering North American, women's, international, veteran and contributor categories. A player becomes eligible several years after retirement. The induction ceremony, held annually in Springfield, is the centrepiece of the hall's calendar and is attended by many of the sport's most celebrated figures. More than 400 individuals and several teams have been inducted.
