Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–1948

The Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–1948, also known as the First Kashmir War, was the first of four wars fought between the newly independent dominions of India and Pakistan. It began in October 1947 when Pashtun tribal militias, supported by Pakistan, invaded the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir, whose ruler, Maharaja Hari Singh, had been undecided about accession. The Maharaja acceded to India, and Indian troops were airlifted to Srinagar, beginning a conflict that lasted until a United Nations-brokered ceasefire took effect on 1 January 1949.

Course of the war

Tribal lashkars, later reinforced by regular Pakistani forces (officially acknowledged by Pakistan in 1948 under officers such as Major General Akbar Khan), advanced on Srinagar but were delayed at Baramulla, allowing the Indian airlift of 27 October 1947 to secure the airfield. Fighting spread across Jammu, the Kashmir Valley, Poonch, Gilgit and Ladakh, where Indian forces under difficult conditions held Leh and retook Kargil and Dras in November 1948 (Operation Bison, including the celebrated assault on Zoji La with tanks at extreme altitude). Meanwhile the Gilgit Scouts revolted and Gilgit-Baltistan came under Pakistani control.

Outcome

India referred the dispute to the UN Security Council in January 1948. UN resolutions called for a plebiscite after demilitarisation, which was never held. The Karachi Agreement of July 1949 demarcated a Ceasefire Line, leaving roughly 55–65% of the former princely state (the Kashmir Valley, Jammu and Ladakh) under Indian administration and the remainder (Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan) under Pakistani control. The Line of Control that divides Kashmir today follows this line closely, and the war established the Kashmir conflict at the centre of India–Pakistan relations.
