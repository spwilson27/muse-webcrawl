Dreimal sieben Gedichte aus Albert Girauds "Pierrot lunaire" ("Three times seven poems from Albert Giraud's Pierrot lunaire"), commonly known simply as Pierrot Lunaire, Op. 21, is a melodrama by Arnold Schoenberg. It is a setting of 21 selected poems from Otto Erich Hartleben's German translation of Albert Giraud's cycle of French poems of the same name. The première took place in Berlin on 16 October 1912 with Albertine Zehme as the vocalist.

Description

The narrator (voice) performs in Sprechstimme, a cross between speaking and singing in which notated pitches are approximated but not sustained. The work is atonal and is regarded as a landmark of musical modernism. The poems depict Pierrot, the sad clown of commedia dell'arte, in a series of moonlit, grotesque and nightmarish vignettes.

Structure

The work is divided into three parts of seven poems each:

  1. Part One: "Mondestrunken" (Moon-Drunk), "Colombine", "Der Dandy" (The Dandy), "Eine blasse Wäscherin" (A Pallid Washerwoman), "Valse de Chopin", "Madonna", "Der kranke Mond" (The Sick Moon).
  2. Part Two: "Die Nacht" (Night), "Gebet an Pierrot" (Prayer to Pierrot), "Raub" (Theft), "Rote Messe" (Red Mass), "Galgenlied" (Gallows Song), "Enthauptung" (Beheading), "Die Kreuze" (The Crosses).
  3. Part Three: "Heimweh" (Homesickness), "Gemeinheit" (Vulgarity), "Parodie" (Parody), "Der Mondfleck" (The Moon Spot), "Serenade", "Heimfahrt" (Journey Home), "O alter Duft" (O Ancient Scent).

Instrumentation

The ensemble — flute doubling piccolo, clarinet doubling bass clarinet, violin doubling viola, cello, and piano — became the model for the standard "Pierrot ensemble" used by many later composers. Different subsets of the instruments are used in each movement; the full ensemble plays together only a few times.

Reception and influence

Reactions to the première were mixed, but the work soon became one of Schoenberg's most frequently performed compositions and an iconic work of expressionism in music. It influenced composers including Igor Stravinsky and, later, Peter Maxwell Davies, whose Eight Songs for a Mad King is written for a Pierrot ensemble plus additional percussion.
