Bertrand Goldberg

Bertrand Goldberg (4 July 1913 – 8 October 1997) was an American architect best known for the Marina City complex in Chicago (completed 1964), whose corncob-shaped twin residential towers became icons of the Chicago skyline. His work explored curvilinear, sculptural forms in poured concrete at a time when the glass-and-steel box dominated modernism.

Life and career

Goldberg studied at Harvard and then at the Bauhaus in Berlin (1932), working briefly in the office of Ludwig Mies van der Rohe before returning to Chicago ahead of the Nazi era. He opened his own practice in 1937, designing prefabricated housing, mobile vaccine units and furniture before winning larger commissions after the Second World War.

Major works

Marina City (1959–1964) combined two 65-storey residential towers with an office building, theatre, shops and a marina on the Chicago River — a "city within a city" intended to lure residents back downtown. Its cylindrical towers, with semicircular balconies radiating from a central core, remain among the most recognisable buildings in America. Other projects included River City in Chicago, with its serpentine plan; hospitals in Tacoma and Boston; and the cloverleaf-shaped Prentice Women's Hospital in Chicago (1975), whose controversial demolition in 2013–14 — over preservationists' protests — revived interest in Goldberg's work.
