The Đinh dynasty was the first imperial dynasty of independent Vietnam, ruling from 968 to 980. Founded by Đinh Bộ Lĩnh after he defeated the rival warlords of the Anarchy of the 12 Warlords, the dynasty established the state of Đại Cồ Việt with its capital at Hoa Lư, in present-day Ninh Bình Province.

Background

After the Ngô dynasty collapsed in 965, Vietnam fragmented among twelve competing warlords. Đinh Bộ Lĩnh, a general from Hoa Lư, systematically defeated or won over his rivals and reunified the country by 968, ending nearly three decades of disorder following the expulsion of Chinese rule.

Reign of Đinh Tiên Hoàng

Proclaiming himself emperor as Đinh Tiên Hoàng, he named the country Đại Cồ Việt, organised a court and army on imperial lines, and established diplomatic relations with Song-dynasty China, which recognised him in 972. He reformed the military into units of ten, built palaces and temples at Hoa Lư, and promoted Buddhism and Taoism alongside court administration.

Fall

In 979 Đinh Tiên Hoàng and his eldest son were assassinated by the court official Đỗ Thích. His surviving heir, the child emperor Đinh Toàn, was unable to hold power against the threat of a Song invasion, and in 980 the throne passed to the general Lê Hoàn, founder of the Early Lê dynasty. Though short-lived, the Đinh dynasty is remembered as the foundation of Vietnam's enduring imperial independence.
