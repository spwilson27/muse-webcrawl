Transport in Wales

Transport in Wales is shaped by the country's mountainous interior, which funnels most routes along the north and south coasts and through the South Wales Valleys. Since devolution, transport policy has largely been the responsibility of the Welsh Government and its agency Transport for Wales (TfW), though rail infrastructure remains partly a UK Government responsibility.

Roads

The principal roads are the M4 motorway across South Wales from the Severn crossings to Carmarthenshire, the A55 North Wales Expressway along the north coast, and north–south links such as the A470 from Cardiff to Llandudno. In 2023 Wales became one of the first countries to make 20 mph the default speed limit on restricted roads.

Rail

The Wales & Borders franchise is operated by Transport for Wales Rail, a publicly owned operator that took over in 2021. Key lines include the South Wales Main Line to Swansea, the North Wales Coast Line to Holyhead, the Cambrian Line, and the Valley Lines network radiating from Cardiff, which is being upgraded as part of the South Wales Metro with electrification and new trains.

Ports, air, and buses

Major ports include Holyhead (ferries to Ireland), Milford Haven (a major energy port), Cardiff, Newport, and Swansea. Cardiff Airport is the country's principal airport. Most bus services are commercial, with TrawsCymru providing long-distance routes; the Welsh Government has pursued bus franchising reforms. Wales also has a network of heritage railways, including the Ffestiniog and Welsh Highland lines.
