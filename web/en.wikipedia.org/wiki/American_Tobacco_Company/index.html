American Tobacco Company

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The American Tobacco Company's factories in Durham, North Carolina.

The American Tobacco Company was a tobacco company founded in 1890 in Durham, North Carolina, by James Buchanan Duke through the merger of five leading cigarette manufacturers, including W. Duke, Sons & Co. and Allen & Ginter. It became one of the great American industrial trusts of the era.

Rise of the tobacco trust

Duke's enterprise exploited the Bonsack cigarette-rolling machine, aggressive advertising, and vertical integration to dominate the cigarette trade, and it expanded into plug, snuff, and cigars through subsidiaries, gaining control of some 200 firms. The company acquired brands such as Lucky Strike and promoted them with pioneering national advertising campaigns.

Breakup

The United States Supreme Court ruled in United States v. American Tobacco Co. (1911) that the combination was an unreasonable restraint of trade under the Sherman Antitrust Act. Under the resulting decree the trust was dissolved into several companies, principally a reconstituted American Tobacco Company, R.J. Reynolds, Liggett & Myers, and Lorillard.

Later history

The successor American Tobacco Company remained a major manufacturer through the twentieth century, marketing brands including Lucky Strike, Pall Mall, and Tareyton, and diversifying under the American Brands holding company from 1969. In 1994 its cigarette business was sold to Brown & Williamson, ending its history as a tobacco manufacturer.
