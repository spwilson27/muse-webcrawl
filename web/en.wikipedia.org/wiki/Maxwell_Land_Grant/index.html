Maxwell Land Grant

Maxwell Land Grant
Location: Northeastern New Mexico and southern Colorado
Size: about 1.7 million acres (as confirmed)
Original grant: Beaubien-Miranda grant, 1841 (Mexican era)

The Maxwell Land Grant, also known as the Beaubien-Miranda land grant, was one of the largest private landholdings in United States history, covering roughly 1.7 million acres in what is now northeastern New Mexico and southern Colorado. It originated as a 1841 Mexican grant to Charles Beaubien and Guadalupe Miranda.

History

Beaubien's son-in-law Lucien B. Maxwell, a former mountain man and associate of Kit Carson, acquired the interests of the other heirs and became sole owner. After the U.S. conquest of New Mexico, the grant was confirmed by Congress, though its vast claimed extent was disputed. Maxwell sold the grant in 1870 to a syndicate that formed the Maxwell Land Grant and Railway Company, with Dutch and English investors.

Colfax County War

Efforts by the company to evict settlers and miners it regarded as squatters triggered the violent Colfax County War in the 1870s. Litigation over the grant's boundaries culminated in the U.S. Supreme Court upholding the company's title in 1887. Much of the land was later sold off; a large tract became the Philmont Scout Ranch.
