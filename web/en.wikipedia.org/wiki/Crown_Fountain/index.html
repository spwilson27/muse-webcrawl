The Crown Fountain is an interactive work of public art and video sculpture in Chicago's Millennium Park, designed by Catalan artist Jaume Plensa and executed by Krueck + Sexton Architects. Opened in July 2004, it was funded by a donation from the Crown family, after whom it is named.

Description

The fountain consists of two 50-foot (15.2 m) glass-brick towers standing at either end of a shallow black-granite reflecting pool 232 feet (71 m) long. LED screens behind the glass bricks display digital videos of the faces of about 1,000 Chicago residents, filmed for the project. Periodically, the face puckers its lips and a stream of water pours from a spout at the mouth — a modern, playful reference to the gargoyles of traditional fountains. Water also cascades down the tower sides, and the pool is a popular place for children to splash and wade.

Operation and reception

The fountain operates between mid-spring and mid-autumn, with images shown year-round. It cost $17 million of the park's construction budget and was initially controversial for its price and for surveillance concerns during filming, but it has become one of Chicago's most beloved pieces of public art, praised for celebrating the city's diversity and for inviting public interaction rather than passive viewing.
