Oliver Wendell Holmes Sr.

Oliver Wendell Holmes Sr. (August 29, 1809 – October 7, 1894) was an American physician, poet, and polymath based in Boston. A member of the Fireside Poets, he was acclaimed as one of the best writers of the nineteenth century in America. His son, Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr., became a justice of the US Supreme Court.

Medicine

Holmes studied medicine in Paris and at Harvard Medical School, where he later served as professor of anatomy and dean. In 1843 he published "The Contagiousness of Puerperal Fever", arguing — before the germ theory of disease — that childbed fever was carried between patients by physicians themselves, a landmark essay in the history of antisepsis. He also coined the word "anaesthesia".

Literary career

Holmes achieved early fame with the poem "Old Ironsides" (1830), which helped save the frigate USS Constitution from being scrapped. As a co-founder of The Atlantic Monthly (1857), he published his popular "Breakfast-Table" series, beginning with The Autocrat of the Breakfast-Table (1858). He was renowned as a conversationalist and wit in Boston's literary circles, alongside friends such as Ralph Waldo Emerson, Henry Wadsworth Longfellow and James Russell Lowell.
