Vostok programme

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Vostok programme was the Soviet human spaceflight project that put the first humans into orbit. Between April 1961 and June 1963, six Vostok spacecraft — essentially a spherical reentry capsule bolted to an instrument module, derived from the Zenit spy satellite — carried cosmonauts on progressively longer flights that opened the Space Race.

The series began with Vostok 1 and Yuri Gagarin's single orbit on 12 April 1961. It continued with Gherman Titov's day-long Vostok 2, the first dual flight of Vostok 3 and 4 (1962), and closed with the five-day Vostok 5 and Valentina Tereshkova's Vostok 6 — the first woman in space — flying simultaneously in June 1963.

Design and quirks

Vostok's spherical capsule could not be steered during reentry, and the cosmonaut ejected at about 7 km altitude to land separately by parachute — a fact concealed at the time because aviation rules required a pilot to land with his craft for the flight to count as a record. The capsule's rugged simplicity, though, made it dependable: the same basic design family flew as uncrewed satellites for decades.
