The Argentine Primera División is the top division of the Argentine football league system, organized by the Argentine Football Association (AFA). Founded in 1891, its championship is among the oldest in the world outside the British Isles, and Argentina was the first country outside the United Kingdom to establish a football league.

History

The first championship was played in 1891, and professionalism was adopted in 1931. For decades the championship was dominated by the so-called "big five" clubs: River Plate, Boca Juniors, Independiente, Racing, and San Lorenzo. Their rivalry, especially the Superclásico between River and Boca, is among the most famous derbies in world football.

Format

The format has changed repeatedly: single annual tournaments, the Apertura and Clausura split seasons (1990–2012), and various league-cup structures. In recent years the season has featured a league competition (Liga Profesional) with roughly 28–30 teams, alongside the Copa de la Liga Profesional. Relegation has traditionally been determined by points averages (promedios) over multiple seasons.

Champions

River Plate is the most successful club in the competition's history, followed by Boca Juniors. Argentine clubs have also been highly successful in the Copa Libertadores, with Independiente holding a record seven titles.
