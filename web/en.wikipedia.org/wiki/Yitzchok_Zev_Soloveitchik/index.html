Yitzchok Zev Soloveitchik

Born: 1886, Volozhin, Russian Empire
Died: 1959, Jerusalem
Known for: Brisker method of Talmud study

Yitzchok Zev Halevi Soloveitchik (Hebrew: יצחק זאב הלוי סולובייצ'יק; 1886–1959), known as the Brisker Rav, was an Orthodox rabbi and rosh yeshiva of the Brisk yeshiva in Jerusalem. A son of Rabbi Chaim Brisker (Chaim Soloveitchik), the founder of the Brisker method of Talmudic analysis, he became the leader of the Brisk dynasty and of much of the Haredi world in Israel after the Second World War.

Life

Soloveitchik was born in Volozhin, in the Russian Empire, into one of the most prominent rabbinic families of Lithuania. He grew up in Brisk (Brest-Litovsk), where his father served as rabbi, and succeeded him in that position. Fleeing the Nazi advance in 1941, he escaped to Mandatory Palestine and settled in Jerusalem, where he re-established his yeshiva and became a central authority for the Lithuanian (non-Hasidic) Haredi community.

Scholarship and influence

The Brisker Rav was renowned for his extraordinary stringency in halakhic observance and for refining his father's analytic method, which distinguishes between two aspects or definitions (tzvei dinim) within a single halakhic concept. His novellae on Maimonides' Mishneh Torah were published posthumously from his writings and students' notes and are studied throughout the yeshiva world. He was noted for refusing formal communal office while exerting wide influence through his rulings and counsel.
