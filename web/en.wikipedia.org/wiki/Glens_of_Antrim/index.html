The Glens of Antrim (Irish: Gleannta Aontroma), known locally as simply The Glens, are a region of County Antrim, Northern Ireland, comprising nine deep, steep-sided glens cut into the Antrim Plateau and running down to the north-east coast. The area is designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty and is a popular scenic tourist destination along the Causeway Coast.

The nine glens

From north to south the glens are traditionally listed as: Glentaisie (near Ballycastle), Glenshesk, Glendun, Glenaan, Glenballyeamon, Glenariff — the largest, known as the "Queen of the Glens", with its famous waterfalls — Glencloy, Glenarm and Glenmore. Principal villages include Cushendun, Cushendall, Waterfoot (Glenariff), Carnlough and Glenarm.

History and culture

The Glens were historically relatively isolated, preserving strong Gaelic traditions and a distinct local dialect and culture. The area was once part of the Gaelic lordship of the MacDonnells of Antrim. It is celebrated in song and folklore, including associations with the legendary warrior-poet Ossian, whose grave is said to lie near Cushendall, and the Children of Lir legend at Ballycastle. The Ould Lammas Fair at Ballycastle and the Heart of the Glens Festival at Cushendall are major annual events.
