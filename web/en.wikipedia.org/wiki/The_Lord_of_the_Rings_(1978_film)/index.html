The Lord of the Rings is a 1978 American animated fantasy film directed by Ralph Bakshi. It is an adaptation of J. R. R. Tolkien's The Fellowship of the Ring (1954) and part of The Two Towers (1954), covering the story up to the Battle of Helm's Deep.

Production

Bakshi filmed much of the action in live action in Spain and rotoscoped the footage, tracing over it to produce the animation, a technique that gives the battle scenes a distinctive, fluid look. The film was financed by producer Saul Zaentz and distributed by United Artists. A planned sequel covering the remainder of the story was never made, though the film was a financial success and influenced later adaptations, including Peter Jackson's live-action trilogy, which borrowed some shot compositions.

Plot

The hobbit Frodo Baggins inherits the One Ring from his uncle Bilbo and, guided by the wizard Gandalf, journeys with the Fellowship of the Ring toward Mordor to destroy it. The film ends after the Fellowship has broken apart and won the defence of Helm's Deep against Saruman's army.

Reception

Reviews were mixed: critics praised the ambition and visuals but found the compression of the story confusing. The film has since gained a cult following and is noted as a landmark of adult-oriented animation.
