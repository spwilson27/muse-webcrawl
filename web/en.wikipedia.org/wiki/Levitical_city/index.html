Levitical city

A Levitical city (Hebrew: ערי הלויים) is one of the towns allocated in the Hebrew Bible to the tribe of Levi. Because the Levites received no tribal territory of their own in the land of Canaan — their "inheritance" being service at the sanctuary and the tithes of Israel — the other tribes were commanded to yield towns from within their allotments, together with the common land around each town, for Levitical habitation and livestock.

Legal framework

Numbers 35 defines the arrangement: the pastureland (migrash) was measured from the town wall outward, and later tradition treated the belt as inalienable, neither to be built upon nor converted to other use. The Levites held their houses in these cities under special rules of redemption described in Leviticus 25: a Levite could redeem a sold house at any time, and unsold houses reverted automatically in the Jubilee year — an exception to the usual rules for urban property, reflecting the Levites' lack of ancestral farmland.

Function

Each Levitical city served as a local centre of Torah instruction and judgment, since the Levites were charged with teaching the law (Deuteronomy 33:10). Six of the cities additionally functioned as cities of refuge, where those guilty of unintentional manslaughter could live safely until the death of the high priest. The institution thus combined housing policy, religious education, and criminal justice in a single network of towns.

This article treats the institution in the singular; for the full list of 48 towns and scholarly debate, see Levitical cities.
