Fade (audio engineering)

In audio engineering, a fade is a gradual increase or decrease in the level of an audio signal. The term can also be used for film and theatre lighting. A fade-in begins with silence and increases to full volume, while a fade-out gradually lowers the volume to silence. Fades are generally performed by a recording engineer or editor using a fader on a mixing console or automation in a digital audio workstation.

Types

A fade-in opens many songs and broadcasts, easing the listener in; the Beatles' "Eight Days a Week" is a notable early pop example. A fade-out — gradually reducing volume toward the end of a recording — became a standard way to end pop songs in the 1950s and 1960s, implying the performance continues beyond the record. A crossfade fades one source out while fading another in, used by DJs to blend tracks seamlessly and in editing to join takes.

Curves

Fades follow different curves: linear fades change level at a constant rate; logarithmic (audio-taper) fades better match human perception of loudness; S-curves change slowly at the ends and faster in the middle. In crossfades, equal-power curves are used so the combined loudness stays roughly constant in the middle of the transition, avoiding a dip.
