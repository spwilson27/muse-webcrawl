Eirik Håkonsson

Eirik Håkonsson, also known as Eric of Lade or Eric Hakonsson (960s – 1020s), was Earl of Lade, Governor of Norway, Earl of Northumbria, and the de facto ruler of Norway from 1000 to 1012 under the Danish king Sweyn Forkbeard. He was the son of Earl Hákon Sigurðarson.

Life

Eirik's father Hákon was killed in 995 after Olaf Tryggvason seized Norway. Eirik fled to Sweden and later Denmark, raiding in the Baltic and Russia. In 1000 he fought alongside Sweyn Forkbeard and Olof Skötkonung at the Battle of Svolder, where Olaf Tryggvason was killed, and where, according to saga tradition, Eirik captured Olaf's ship, the Long Serpent. Norway was then divided between Denmark and Sweden, and Eirik governed the western part as a Danish vassal together with his brother Sveinn Hákonarson.

After accompanying Cnut the Great's invasion of England in 1015–1016, Eirik was made Earl of Northumbria. He disappears from English records in the early 1020s and is generally believed to have died around that time, possibly on a pilgrimage to Rome or in England.

Legacy

Eirik is remembered in Icelandic sagas, including Heimskringla and Fagrskinna, as a capable and comparatively tolerant ruler who, though nominally Christian, permitted the practice of the old Norse religion in Norway.
