Piegan Blackfeet

The Piegan Blackfeet (Blackfoot: Piikáni) are a federally recognized tribe of Native American people in the United States, based on the Blackfeet Indian Reservation in northwestern Montana. They are the southernmost of the four peoples of the Blackfoot Confederacy (Niitsitapi), the others being the Kainai (Blood), the Northern Peigan (Piikani Nation in Alberta), and the Siksika, the latter three now largely in Canada.

History

The Piegan traditionally roamed a vast territory of the northern Great Plains, from the Rocky Mountains east across the plains of present-day Montana and Alberta, hunting bison. In 1855 they signed the Lame Bull Treaty with the United States, establishing a reservation. Successive agreements and executive orders reduced the reservation's size, ceding lands including what became Glacier National Park. The winter of 1883–84, known as the Starvation Winter, killed hundreds after bison herds collapsed.

Government and culture

The tribe is governed under a 1935 constitution adopted under the Indian Reorganization Act, with an elected business council. Enrolled membership is in the tens of thousands. The Blackfeet language, an Algonquian language, is taught in immersion programs such as the Piegan Institute. Annual events include North American Indian Days, held in Browning, the tribal capital.
