The MGB is a two-door sports car manufactured and marketed from 1962 until 1980 by the British Motor Corporation (BMC), later the Austin-Morris division of British Leyland. Initially announced and its details first published on 19 September 1962, as a BMC B-series engine-powered replacement for the earlier MGA, the MGB continued in production for 18 years — a longevity almost unmatched by any other British sports car — with more than half a million examples built, the great majority exported to the United States.

Design

Unlike its body-on-frame predecessors, the MGB used a unitary (monocoque) structure, which reduced weight and manufacturing cost while improving rigidity. The standard engine was the 1,798 cc BMC B-series four-cylinder producing about 95 bhp in early form, driving the rear wheels through a four-speed manual gearbox with optional overdrive. Front suspension was by coil springs and wishbones, with a live rear axle on leaf springs. The car was styled at MG's Abingdon works under chief designer Don Hayter.

Variants

In 1965 the range was joined by the MGB GT, a fixed-roof coupé with a large opening rear hatch — an early hatchback — whose roofline was styled with input from Pininfarina. The MGC (1967–1969) fitted a 2,912 cc six-cylinder engine, and the MGB GT V8 (1973–1976) used the lightweight Rover 3.5-litre aluminium V8, an idea pioneered by cost-conscious tuner Ken Costello. For 1975, to meet US safety and emissions rules, cars received large black polyurethane "rubber bumpers" and a raised ride height, changes that added weight and dulled the handling and are less prized by collectors.

Competition and legacy

MGBs competed widely in rallying and racing, with class successes at events including the Monte Carlo Rally, the Spa 1000 km, and Sebring. Production ended in October 1980 when British Leyland closed the Abingdon factory. The model's popularity endures through a large owners' club network and an unusually complete supply of spare and heritage body parts; the badge was revived for the MG RV8 of 1992–1995 and later for unrelated models.
