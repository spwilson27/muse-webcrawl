Landscape painting
SubjectNatural scenery: mountains, valleys, water, sky
Major traditionsWestern landscape; Chinese shan shui
Key figuresClaude Lorrain, Turner, Constable, Caspar David Friedrich, Monet

Landscape painting, also known as landscape art, is the depiction of natural scenery such as mountains, valleys, trees, rivers, and forests, especially where the main subject is a wide view — with the sky almost always included. Landscape developed as an independent genre in both the Chinese shan shui ("mountain-water") tradition, established by the Tang and Song dynasties, and in early modern Europe.

Western development

In Renaissance art landscape served as background to religious and mythological scenes, though artists such as Albrecht Altdorfer and the Danube school painted pure landscapes by the 1520s. The Dutch Golden Age made landscape a major commercial genre (Jacob van Ruisdael, Meindert Hobbema), while Claude Lorrain and Nicolas Poussin defined the classical, idealised landscape in Rome. Academic theory, however, ranked landscape below history painting in the hierarchy of genres.

19th century

Romanticism transformed the genre: J. M. W. Turner and John Constable in England, Caspar David Friedrich in Germany, and the Hudson River School in America made landscape the vehicle for emotion, national identity, and the sublime. The Barbizon school and then the Impressionists took painting outdoors (plein air), and landscape remained central through Post-Impressionism (Cézanne, Van Gogh) into modern art.
