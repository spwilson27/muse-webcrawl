St James's Palace

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

St James's Palace is the most senior royal palace in London, United Kingdom. Built by Henry VIII in the 1530s on the site of a leper hospital dedicated to Saint James the Less, it stands in the City of Westminster, just off Pall Mall.

History

The palace was a principal residence of the monarch from the reign of William III until Queen Victoria moved the court to Buckingham Palace in 1837. It remains the formal official residence of the sovereign: foreign ambassadors to the United Kingdom are still accredited to the 'Court of St James's', and the Accession Council meets there to proclaim a new monarch, as it did for Charles III in 2022.

Today

The palace complex, including Clarence House and Marlborough House nearby, contains the London offices and residences of members of the Royal Family and the Chapel Royal. It is not generally open to the public.
