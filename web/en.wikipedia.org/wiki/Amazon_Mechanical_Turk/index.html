Amazon Mechanical Turk (MTurk) is a crowdsourcing marketplace operated by Amazon that enables individuals and businesses ("Requesters") to outsource small tasks — called Human Intelligence Tasks (HITs) — to a distributed workforce ("Turkers" or Workers) who complete them virtually. Launched in November 2005, it is one of the oldest and best-known microtask platforms.

Name

The name refers to the Mechanical Turk, an 18th-century chess-playing "automaton" that was secretly operated by a hidden human. Amazon has described the service as "artificial artificial intelligence": tasks that are easy for humans but hard for computers, such as identifying objects in images, transcribing audio, moderating content, categorising data and completing surveys.

How it works

Requesters post HITs with a reward, often a few cents per task, and workers browse and complete them; submitted work can be accepted or rejected, affecting payment and the worker's approval rating. Turkers' earnings vary widely, and academic studies have found median hourly wages well below U.S. minimum wage, prompting debate about labour protections for crowdworkers.

Uses and research

MTurk became a major tool in social-science research — behavioural experiments and surveys — because it provided quick access to a large subject pool, though concerns about data quality, bots and participant non-naïveté have grown. It has also been used extensively to label training data for machine learning, including image annotation and text classification.

Criticism

The platform has been criticised for low pay, wage theft through rejected work, lack of worker protections since Turkers are independent contractors, and Amazon's limited responsiveness to worker grievances. Worker communities such as Turkopticon and forums like r/mturk developed to share information about fair Requesters.
