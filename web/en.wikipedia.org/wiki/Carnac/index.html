Carnac (Breton: Karnag) is a commune in the Morbihan department of Brittany in north-western France. It is world-famous for the Carnac stones, one of the most extensive Neolithic megalithic complexes in existence.

The Carnac stones

More than 3,000 prehistoric standing stones were hewn from local granite and erected by Neolithic peoples between about 4500 and 2000 BC. The stones form alignments (long parallel rows), dolmens, tumuli, and stone circles across several sites, including the Ménec, Kermario, and Kerlescan alignments. Their purpose remains debated — proposed functions include astronomical observation, ritual, and territorial marking.

The commune

Modern Carnac combines the inland village (Carnac-Ville), with its 17th-century church of Saint-Cornély, and the seaside resort of Carnac-Plage, developed in the early 20th century with fine sandy beaches on the Bay of Quiberon. Tourism, oyster farming, and thalassotherapy drive the local economy.

Legends

Local folklore holds that the stones are a Roman legion turned to stone by Saint Cornelius, patron of horned animals, who is still honoured in the annual pardon seeking blessings for cattle.
