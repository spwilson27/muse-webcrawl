Scholium

A scholium (plural scholia, from Greek σχόλιον, "comment" or "interpretation") is a marginal note or commentary on a text, originally on a classical Greek or Latin author, written by an ancient commentator known as a scholiast. Scholia differ from glosses in being fuller, discursive explanations rather than brief definitions of single words.

History

The tradition of scholia grew out of the scholarly activity of Alexandria and the ancient schools, where commentators explained passages of Homer, the tragedians and other standard authors. Byzantine scribes copied these comments into the margins of manuscripts, often abridging and combining material from several sources, so that surviving scholia are frequently compilations of much older learning.

Importance

Scholia are a major source for ancient scholarship: they preserve fragments of lost works, variant readings, mythological information and the interpretations of Hellenistic critics such as Aristarchus. Modern editions of classical authors print the scholia alongside the text, and papyri from Egypt have added substantially to the corpus. The term is also used in mathematics and philosophy for a remark appended to a proposition, notably in Newton's Principia, which contains a famous "General Scholium".
