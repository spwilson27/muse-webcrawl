Bouillon (Walloon: Bouyon) is a city and municipality of Wallonia located in the province of Luxembourg in the Ardennes, Belgium. On 1 January 2018 the municipality, which covers 149.09 km², had 5,454 registered inhabitants, giving a population density of 37 inhabitants per km².

Geography

The town sits in a sharp bend of the River Semois, surrounded by wooded hills of the Belgian Ardennes, close to the French border. Its quarters include Ucimont, Sensenruth, Noirefontaine, Dohan, Poupehan, Rochehaut, Corbion, Bellevaux and Mogimont.

History

Bouillon is famous for its medieval castle, dramatically situated on a rocky spur above the town. The lordship of Bouillon was held by Godfrey of Bouillon, a leader of the First Crusade, who mortgaged the castle to the prince-bishop of Liège in 1095 to finance his expedition. The castle was later remodelled under French occupation in the era of Vauban. The Duchy of Bouillon passed through several hands before the territory was assigned to the Netherlands (and then Belgium) by the Congress of Vienna settlement, having briefly been claimed by France.
