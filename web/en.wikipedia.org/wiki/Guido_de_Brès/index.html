Guido de Brès

Born1522, Mons, Habsburg Netherlands
Died31 May 1567, Valenciennes
OccupationReformed pastor and theologian

Guido de Brès (also Guy de Bray; 1522 – 31 May 1567) was a Walloon Reformed pastor and theologian, best known as the principal author of the Belgic Confession of Faith (1561), one of the foundational doctrinal standards of the Dutch Reformed churches.

Life

Born in Mons, de Brès converted to Protestantism as a young man and spent periods of exile in London and in Frankfurt and Geneva, where he came under the influence of John Calvin and Theodore Beza. Returning to the southern Netherlands, he preached under persecution in Lille, Tournai and Valenciennes. In 1561 he famously threw a copy of the Confession over the castle wall at Tournai so that it would reach the authorities with a letter pleading for toleration.

Death and legacy

Captured after the siege of Valenciennes, de Brès was hanged on 31 May 1567. The Belgic Confession he drafted remains an official confessional standard of Reformed churches worldwide, including the Christian Reformed Church and the Reformed Church in America.
