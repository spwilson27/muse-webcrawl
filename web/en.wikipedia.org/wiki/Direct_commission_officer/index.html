Direct commission officer

A direct commission officer (DCO) is a uniformed officer who has received an appointment as a commissioned officer in United States armed forces (active, reserve, or National Guard) without having completed a traditional commissioning program such as a service academy, Reserve Officers' Training Corps (ROTC), or Officer Candidate School (OCS). Direct appointments are granted to individuals who possess specialized professional skills or education that the services need, and are most common in professional staff corps.

Purpose

The direct commission allows the military to recruit physicians, dentists, nurses, chaplains, attorneys (judge advocates), engineers, scientists, and other professionals whose civilian credentials already qualify them to practice their specialty. Because these officers serve primarily as practitioners rather than as combat arms leaders, they undergo abbreviated military orientation training instead of the full officer development pipeline, and their entry grade is often determined by their education and professional experience rather than by time in service.

Branches and corps

Direct commissions are regularly used by:

  • The United States Army, particularly in the Army Medical Department, the Judge Advocate General's Corps, and the Chaplain Corps; the Army has also used direct commissions for certain cyber and technical specialties.
  • The United States Navy, through its restricted line and staff corps communities, with training at the Officer Development School in Newport, Rhode Island.
  • The United States Air Force and United States Space Force, for medical, legal, and chaplain officers.
  • The United States Coast Guard, which runs a Direct Commission Officer program for several specialties.
  • The commissioned corps of the NOAA and the U.S. Public Health Service.

Training

Before or shortly after reporting to their first assignment, direct commission officers complete a short orientation course that teaches military customs and courtesies, uniform wear, military law, leadership fundamentals, and the structure of their service. Courses vary in length from roughly two to six weeks depending on the branch.
