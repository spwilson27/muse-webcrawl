Prahlada

Prahlada (Sanskrit: प्रह्लाद, Prahlāda) is a saintly figure in Hindu tradition, celebrated in the Puranas as an ardent devotee of the god Vishnu. Despite being born into the family of the asura (demon) king Hiranyakashipu, Prahlada steadfastly worshipped Vishnu, defying his father's persecution, and was rescued by Vishnu in his avatar as Narasimha. His story, told most fully in the Bhagavata Purana and the Vishnu Purana, is a central narrative of Vaishnavism and is associated with the festival of Holi in many traditions.

Legend

While still in his mother's womb, Prahlada is said to have heard the sage Narada's teachings on devotion to Vishnu, and he was born already a devotee. His father Hiranyakashipu, who had obtained a boon making him nearly invulnerable, demanded that the universe worship him alone. When Prahlada refused and insisted that Vishnu was omnipresent, Hiranyakashipu subjected him to a series of torments — poison, trampling by elephants, being thrown from a cliff, and burning — from which the boy was repeatedly and miraculously saved.

The pillar and Narasimha

Finally, the king mocked his son: if Vishnu is everywhere, is he in this pillar? Striking the pillar, he was confronted by Vishnu bursting forth in the man-lion form Narasimha, who killed Hiranyakashipu at twilight, on a threshold, with his claws — circumventing every condition of the boon. Prahlada's prayers then calmed the avatar's rage.

Later life and legacy

Prahlada succeeded his father as a righteous king and is remembered as one of the twelve Mahajanas (great devotees) of Vaishnava tradition. His grandson was Bali Maharaja, and his great-grandson Bana. The story underlies the Holika bonfire on the eve of Holi, commemorating the escape of Prahlada from the fire intended to kill him.
