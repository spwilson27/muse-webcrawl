Compound parabolic concentrator

A compound parabolic concentrator (CPC) is a non-imaging optical device that concentrates light (or other radiation) from a wide range of incoming angles onto a smaller area. It consists of two parabolic reflector segments arranged so that radiation entering the aperture within the acceptance angle is reflected down to the absorber at the exit aperture.

Principle

The CPC was developed in the 1960s–70s by Roland Winston and others in the field of non-imaging optics. Its design follows the edge-ray principle: rays entering at the extreme acceptance angle are reflected to the edge of the absorber. A CPC achieves close to the thermodynamic limit of concentration for a given acceptance angle, C = 1/sin²θ for a three-dimensional concentrator.

Applications

Because they accept diffuse light and need no tracking, CPCs are used in solar thermal collectors and photovoltaic systems, in radiation detectors such as Cherenkov counters, and as light collectors in scintillation detectors and photomultiplier assemblies. Winston cone light collectors on telescopes are a related design.
