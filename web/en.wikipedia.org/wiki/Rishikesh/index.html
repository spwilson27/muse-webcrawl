Rishikesh, also spelt as Hrishikesh, is a city near Dehradun in Dehradun district of the Indian state Uttarakhand. It is situated on the right bank of the Ganges River and is a pilgrimage town for Hindus, with ancient temples and ashrams. The city is known as the "Gateway to the Garhwal Himalayas" and as the "Yoga Capital of the World".

History and significance

Rishikesh has been a part of the legendary "Kedarkhand" region. Legends state that Lord Rama did penance here for killing Ravana, and his younger brother Lakshmana crossed the Ganges at the point where the present Lakshman Jhula bridge stands. The city is one of the starting points of the Char Dham pilgrimage and is close to the holy city of Haridwar.

Rishikesh gained worldwide fame in 1968 when the Beatles visited the ashram of Maharishi Mahesh Yogi to study Transcendental Meditation, writing many songs that later appeared on the White Album and Abbey Road.

Tourism and activities

The city is renowned for its yoga and meditation centres, attracting students and seekers from around the world; the International Yoga Festival is held here annually in March. Rishikesh is also a hub for adventure sports, particularly white-water rafting on the Ganges, camping, cliff jumping, and trekking in the surrounding Himalayan foothills. Alcohol and non-vegetarian food are prohibited within the municipal area, reflecting its religious character.

Prominent landmarks include the suspension bridges Lakshman Jhula and Ram Jhula, the Triveni Ghat where the evening Ganga Aarti ceremony is held, the thirteen-storey Trayambakeshwar Temple, and the Beatles Ashram (Chaurasi Kutia).

Geography

Rishikesh lies at an elevation of about 372 metres (1,220 ft) where the Ganges emerges from the foothills of the Himalayas into the plains of northern India. The Chandrabhaga River joins the Ganges in the city.
