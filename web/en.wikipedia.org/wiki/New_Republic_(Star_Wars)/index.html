New Republic (Star Wars)

Universe: Star Wars
Type: Federal republic
Founded: 4 ABY (after the Battle of Endor)
Capital: Chandrila; rotating capitals (canon); Hosnian Prime (destroyed 34 ABY)
Head of state: Chancellor
Key figures: Mon Mothma, Leia Organa

The New Republic is a fictional government in the Star Wars franchise. It was founded by the Rebel Alliance after the death of Emperor Palpatine at the Battle of Endor in Return of the Jedi (1983) to replace the Galactic Empire as the galaxy's dominant government. The New Republic restores the Galactic Senate and the ideals of the Old Republic, and it is the ruling government during the era of the sequel trilogy and the Disney+ series The Mandalorian and Ahsoka.

In-universe history

Following the victory at Endor, Mon Mothma proclaimed the New Republic and became its first chancellor. The new government's capital was initially her homeworld of Chandrila; in the current canon, the capital and Senate later rotated among member worlds, eventually residing on Hosnian Prime. After the Empire's defeat at the Battle of Jakku and the signing of the Galactic Concordance, the New Republic demilitarised much of its fleet, a policy championed by Mon Mothma to prevent a return to tyranny — but one that left it complacent toward the rising First Order.

In The Force Awakens (2015), the First Order destroys Hosnian Prime and the Senate with Starkiller Base, decapitating the New Republic; the surviving story centres on the Resistance led by Leia Organa. Television series set earlier in the era depict New Republic rangers, amnesty programmes for former Imperials, and a bureaucracy struggling to govern — and to recognise the Imperial remnant threat.

Legends continuity

In the former Expanded Universe ("Legends"), the New Republic was founded in 4 ABY and endured for decades of novels, facing the campaigns of Grand Admiral Thrawn (Timothy Zahn's Heir to the Empire trilogy), the reborn Emperor, and the Yuuzhan Vong invasion, before reorganising as the Galactic Federation of Free Alliances. Its capital was Coruscant.

Reception and themes

Commentators have read the New Republic's fragility — disarmament, factionalism between Centrists and Populists in novels such as Claudia Gray's Bloodline, and underestimation of authoritarian remnants — as a political allegory about the difficulty of defending democracy after victory.
