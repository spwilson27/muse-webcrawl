Crashworthiness

Crashworthiness is the ability of a structure to protect its occupants during an impact. It is a key measure of vehicle safety, evaluated through crash tests and accident data by organisations such as Euro NCAP and the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety.

Design principles

Crashworthy design combines crumple zones that absorb kinetic energy by controlled deformation, a rigid safety cell that preserves survival space, and restraint systems — seat belts and airbags — that manage occupant deceleration. Load paths are engineered to divert forces around the passenger compartment.

History

The concept was pioneered in automotive engineering by Béla Barényi at Mercedes-Benz in the 1950s, whose patents on the crumple zone and safety cell transformed car design. The term is also applied to aircraft, trains and spacecraft.
