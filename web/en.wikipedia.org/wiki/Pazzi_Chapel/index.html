The Pazzi Chapel (Italian: Cappella dei Pazzi) is a chapel located in the first cloister of the Basilica of Santa Croce in Florence, Italy. Commissioned by the Pazzi family and designed by Filippo Brunelleschi, it is considered one of the masterpieces of Renaissance architecture and a defining example of Brunelleschi's rational, harmonious style.

History

Andrea de' Pazzi commissioned the building in 1429 to serve as a chapter house for the Franciscan friars of Santa Croce and as a family burial chapel. Construction began around 1442 and continued for decades; the dome was completed in the 1460s, while the portico was finished later and may not follow Brunelleschi's original design.

Architecture

The plan is a rectangle divided into three bays, with a central space covered by a hemispherical dome on pendentives, flanked by barrel-vaulted wings. The interior is articulated in grey stone (pietra serena) against white plaster, with Corinthian pilasters setting out a calm, mathematical rhythm. Terracotta roundels of the Apostles by Luca della Robbia, and tondi of the Evangelists in the pendentives, provide restrained colour. The harmony of its proportions makes the chapel a canonical study in Renaissance central-plan design, complementing Brunelleschi's Old Sacristy at San Lorenzo.
