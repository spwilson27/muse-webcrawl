Thuringia

Free State of Thuringia (Freistaat Thüringen)
Country: Germany
Capital: Erfurt
Largest city: Erfurt
Area: 16,172 km²
Population: approx. 2.1 million
Government: Landtag of Thuringia

Thuringia (German: Thüringen), officially the Free State of Thuringia, is a state of central Germany covering 16,172 square kilometres, with its capital at Erfurt. It is known as "the green heart of Germany" (das grüne Herz Deutschlands) for its extensive forests, above all the Thuringian Forest mountain range in the south. Other major cities include Jena, Gera, Weimar, Gotha, and Eisenach.

The Thuringian Forest, the mountain range that gives the state its "green heart" nickname.

History

The Thuringii were a Germanic people who established a kingdom in the early medieval period, destroyed by the Franks in 531. In the Middle Ages the landgraviate of Thuringia was held by the Ludowingians and later the Wettins, and the Wartburg castle above Eisenach became famous for the Sängerkrieg contest, St. Elizabeth of Hungary, and Martin Luther's exile there, during which he translated the New Testament into German.

From the early modern period Thuringia was divided among a patchwork of small Ernestine duchies — Saxe-Weimar-Eisenach, Saxe-Gotha, Saxe-Meiningen, and others — that made the region a byword for German particularism but also fostered rich court cultures, including Weimar Classicism under Goethe and Schiller. The modern state was first formed in 1920, dissolved into East German districts in 1952, and re-established at German reunification in 1990.

Economy and culture

Jena is a centre of optics and photonics, home to the firms Zeiss and Schott and to Friedrich Schiller University. Weimar, a UNESCO World Heritage site, was home to Goethe, Schiller, Herder, and the Bauhaus school founded there in 1919. Thuringian cuisine is noted for Thuringian bratwurst and potato dumplings (Klöße), and Erfurt's Krämerbrücke — a bridge lined with inhabited houses — is a landmark of the region.
