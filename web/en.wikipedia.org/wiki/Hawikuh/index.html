Hawikuh

Hawikuh was a large pueblo of the Zuni people in what is now New Mexico, United States. Founded around the 13th century, it was one of the largest of the Zuni settlements, with several hundred rooms, and is remembered chiefly as the first pueblo encountered—and attacked—by the Spanish expedition of Francisco Vásquez de Coronado.

History

In 1539 the Franciscan friar Marcos de Niza reported seeing the settlement and, exaggerating, described the "Seven Cities of Cíbola." In July 1540 Coronado's force stormed Hawikuh after the Zuni resisted; the battle is sometimes described as the first between Europeans and Native Americans in the present-day western United States. The Zuni abandoned Hawikuh during the Pueblo Revolt era and it was not reoccupied after the late 17th century.

Archaeology

Excavations by the Museum of the American Indian (1917–1923), led by Frederick Webb Hodge, uncovered thousands of artifacts and burials, raising enduring questions about the treatment of Native remains. Hawikuh was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1960. Zuni cultural authorities have since sought the repatriation of materials taken from the site.
