Munn v. Illinois, 94 U.S. 113 (1877), was a landmark United States Supreme Court decision that upheld the power of state governments to regulate private industries "affected with a public interest." The case arose from an Illinois law setting maximum rates that grain storage elevator companies could charge.

Background

Chicago grain elevator firms, including Munn and Scott, were convicted of violating Illinois's Granger Laws, which capped storage charges. The firms argued the regulation deprived them of property without due process under the Fourteenth Amendment.

Decision

Chief Justice Morrison Waite, writing for the majority, held that when private property is devoted to a public use, it is subject to public regulation. The decision became a cornerstone of the regulatory state and was later relied upon in cases upholding railroad and utility rate regulation.

Legacy

The "affected with a public interest" doctrine governed American regulatory law until it was narrowed in the 1930s, though the broader principle of state police power in economic regulation endured.
