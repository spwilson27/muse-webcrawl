Lord-lieutenant

Lord-lieutenant
AppointerThe British monarch, on the advice of the prime minister
JurisdictionA ceremonial county (lieutenancy area) in England, Scotland or Wales; counties in Northern Ireland
Origins1540s, reign of Henry VIII

A lord-lieutenant (plural: lords-lieutenant) is the British monarch's personal representative in each lieutenancy area of the United Kingdom. Historically, each of the counties had a lord-lieutenant, who was responsible for organising the county's militia. In modern times, the office is largely ceremonial: lords-lieutenant arrange royal visits, present honours and awards on behalf of the sovereign, and act as a link between the Crown and local communities.

History

The office emerged in the 1540s during the reign of Henry VIII, when lords-lieutenant were appointed to take over the military functions of the sheriff and to command the county militia in times of unrest or invasion — notably during the Armada crisis of 1588. During the English Civil War the struggle between king and Parliament to control the lords-lieutenant and the militia was a central issue.

The Militia Act 1662 vested the lords-lieutenant with formal authority over the militia. When the militia was replaced under the Cardwell Reforms of the 1870s, much of the military role passed away, though lords-lieutenant remain associated with the reserve forces and cadets. From 1888, the lord-lieutenant was also ex officio chairman of the county's standing joint committee, but administrative functions transferred to the new county councils.

Modern role

Today there are 98 lords-lieutenant in the United Kingdom. They are appointed by the sovereign on the advice of the prime minister, normally retire at 75, and are assisted by vice lords-lieutenant and deputy lieutenants. Their duties include escorting members of the royal family on official visits to the county, presenting medals and the King's Awards for Enterprise and Voluntary Service, advising on honours nominations, and serving as the county's connection with the armed forces.

The lord-lieutenant is the first gentleman or lady of the county in precedence. In London, the equivalent office is the Lord Lieutenant of Greater London; in the City of London the role is performed by the Lord Mayor and the Commission of Lieutenancy.

Lieutenancy in Ireland

Before Irish independence, the Lord Lieutenant of Ireland was the monarch's representative and head of the Irish executive — a quite different office of viceregal rank. Lords-lieutenant of Irish counties continued in the Irish Free State until abolished in 1922; in Northern Ireland county lords-lieutenant still exist.
