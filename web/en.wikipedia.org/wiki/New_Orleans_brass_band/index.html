New Orleans brass band

A New Orleans brass band is a musical ensemble of the type that developed in New Orleans, Louisiana, in the late 19th century, combining European brass band instrumentation and marching traditions with African-American musical practices, ragtime and early jazz. Brass bands are central to New Orleans musical culture, playing for parades, funerals, weddings and second line processions.

Instrumentation and style

A typical band includes trumpets, trombones, clarinets or saxophones, a sousaphone or tuba providing the bass line, and snare and bass drums. Traditional bands such as the Eureka Brass Band and the Tuxedo Brass Band played marches, hymns and dirges; a jazz funeral traditionally features slow dirges on the way to the cemetery and upbeat numbers on the return.

Modern era

From the 1970s, bands such as the Dirty Dozen Brass Band and later Rebirth Brass Band fused the tradition with funk, bebop and hip hop, using sousaphone-driven bass lines and contemporary repertoire. Groups including the Hot 8, Soul Rebels and Stooges Brass Band carried the style forward and to international audiences, while traditional bands such as the Preservation Hall Brass Band maintain the older repertoire.
