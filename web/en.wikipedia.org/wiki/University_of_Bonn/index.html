University of Bonn

University
Founded: 1818
Location: Bonn, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany
Students: about 33,000
Full name: Rheinische Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität Bonn

The University of Bonn (German: Rheinische Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität Bonn) is a public research university in Bonn, Germany. Founded in 1818 by King Frederick William III of Prussia, it is one of Germany's leading universities and a member of the German Universities Excellence Initiative.

History

The university was established in the former Electoral Palace on the Rhine. It quickly became a center of German scholarship; Karl Marx studied there briefly in 1835–36. In the 20th century Bonn served as capital of West Germany (1949–1990), and the university hosted institutions linked to the federal government. It has produced numerous Nobel laureates, including Wolfgang Paul and Reinhard Selten, and Fields Medalists such as Peter Scholze, who leads its mathematics community centered on the Hausdorff Center for Mathematics.

Faculties

The university has seven faculties: Catholic Theology, Protestant Theology, Law and Economics, Medicine, Arts, Mathematics and Natural Sciences, and Agriculture. Its economics department and mathematics are internationally renowned.

Campus and collections

The main building occupies the former palace of the Electors of Cologne, with the Hofgarten park in front. University museums include the Egyptian Museum and the Arithmeum, devoted to calculating machines.
