Dholavira

LocationKhadir island, Kutch district, Gujarat, India
CivilisationIndus Valley (Harappan)
Occupiedc. 3000–1500 BCE
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site (2021)

Dholavira is an archaeological site on Khadir island in the Great Rann of Kutch, Gujarat, India, containing the ruins of a major city of the Indus Valley Civilisation. Occupied for roughly 1,500 years, it is one of the five largest Harappan sites and the most prominent one in India. Locally the site is called Kotada Timba.

Layout

The city, discovered in 1967–68 and excavated from 1990 by the Archaeological Survey of India under R. S. Bisht, is unusual among Harappan cities for being built largely of stone and for its tripartite division into a citadel, middle town and lower town, each fortified. Its most celebrated features are sophisticated water-management works: enormous rock-cut reservoirs, dams and channels that harvested seasonal streams in an arid landscape. A large wooden signboard bearing ten Indus script characters — among the longest known inscriptions — was found at the north gate.

Decline

Dholavira passed through seven stages of rise and decline; occupation contracted after about 2000 BCE, possibly as regional rivers dried or shifted and trade waned, and the site was briefly reoccupied later before final abandonment. In 2021 it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
