Soleus muscle

The soleus is a powerful muscle in the back part of the lower leg (the calf). It runs from just below the knee to the heel, lying beneath the gastrocnemius, and together with it forms the triceps surae. Its name derives from the Latin solea (sandal), from its flat shape resembling a sole fish.

Structure

The soleus arises from the soleal line on the posterior tibia and the upper third of the fibula, bridged by a tendinous arch under which the popliteal vessels pass. Its fibres are predominantly slow-twitch (type I), suited to sustained postural work. It joins the gastrocnemius aponeurosis to form the Achilles (calcaneal) tendon.

Function

The soleus plantarflexes the foot and is a key postural muscle: it continuously counteracts the body's tendency to topple forward at the ankle while standing. Its rhythmic contractions also act as a "peripheral heart", pumping venous blood from the leg back toward the heart — the calf muscle pump — which is why prolonged immobility increases the risk of deep vein thrombosis.

Clinical significance

Soleus strains ("tennis leg" more often involves the gastrocnemius) occur in runners and ageing athletes. Soleus tear and chronic exertional compartment syndrome are recognised causes of calf pain.
