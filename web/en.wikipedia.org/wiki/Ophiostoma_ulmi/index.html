Ophiostoma ulmi

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Fungi
Division: Ascomycota
Class: Sordariomycetes
Order: Ophiostomatales
Family: Ophiostomataceae
Genus: Ophiostoma
Species: O. ulmi

Ophiostoma ulmi is a species of fungus in the family Ophiostomataceae. It is one of the causative agents of Dutch elm disease, a devastating wilt disease of elm trees. The fungus is spread from tree to tree by elm bark beetles, which carry its spores as they feed and breed under the bark.

Disease cycle

Once introduced into the tree's water-conducting xylem vessels, the fungus grows and produces spores that are carried throughout the tree. The tree responds by blocking its own vessels, which leads to wilting, yellowing foliage and eventually death. Root grafts between neighbouring elms can also transmit the pathogen.

History

Ophiostoma ulmi was responsible for the first pandemic of Dutch elm disease in the early twentieth century, which killed large numbers of elms in Europe and North America. A second, more destructive pandemic was later caused by the related and more aggressive species Ophiostoma novo-ulmi, which largely displaced O. ulmi.
