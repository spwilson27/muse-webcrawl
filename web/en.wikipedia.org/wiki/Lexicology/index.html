Lexicology

Lexicology is the branch of linguistics that studies the lexicon, or vocabulary, of a language — its stock of words and fixed expressions. The word derives from Greek lexiko- (of words) and -logy (study). Lexicology examines the nature of the word, word formation, the historical development of vocabulary, and relations between words and meanings.

Scope

Major topics include the structure of the lexeme, morphology and word-formation processes (derivation, compounding, conversion, borrowing), phraseology and idioms, etymology, and the semantic organisation of the vocabulary. Lexicology is closely related to lexicography, the practical craft of compiling dictionaries; lexicology provides the theoretical foundation for dictionary-making. It also overlaps with lexical semantics, which focuses specifically on meaning.

History

Systematic study of vocabulary grew in the 19th century with comparative philology and historical linguistics, and was formalised as an academic discipline in the 20th century, particularly in European and Russian linguistics. Modern lexicology uses large text corpora to study frequency, collocation, and usage patterns.
