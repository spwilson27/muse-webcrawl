St. Louis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

St. Louis
State: Missouri, USA
Founded: 1764, as a French fur-trading post
Landmark: Gateway Arch (1965), 192 m
Population: about 280,000 (city)

St. Louis is an independent city in Missouri, on the western bank of the Mississippi River just below its confluence with the Missouri. Founded in 1764 by French fur traders, it became the great gateway of American westward expansion — the departure point of Lewis and Clark (1804) — and, by 1900, the fourth-largest city in the United States.

Landmarks and history

The Gateway Arch (completed 1965), a 192-metre stainless-steel catenary designed by Eero Saarinen, commemorates that westward role and is the tallest monument in the US. St. Louis hosted the 1904 World's Fair (the Louisiana Purchase Exposition) and the 1904 Olympics. Its musical heritage runs from ragtime (Scott Joplin) through blues and Chuck Berry's rock and roll to hip-hop; its culinary signatures include toasted ravioli and a peculiar thin-crust pizza.

The twentieth century was harder: suburbanisation, industrial decline and racial segregation — St. Louis was a landmark of redlining, and the 2014 Ferguson protests began in its county — cut the city's population by more than two-thirds from its 1950 peak. It remains a centre of medicine, agriculture science and the US intelligence mapping agency, and the symbolic hinge between East and West in the American story, downriver from New Orleans's delta world.
