Security deposit

A security deposit is a sum of money held in trust either as an initial part-payment in a purchasing process — often used in real estate dealings — or as a guarantee that a rented property will be returned in good condition. It is most commonly associated with residential and commercial leases, in which the tenant pays the landlord an amount, frequently equal to one or two months' rent, before taking possession.

Purpose

The deposit protects the landlord against costs arising from unpaid rent, damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, cleaning, and the removal of abandoned property. If the tenant fulfils the lease terms and leaves the premises in the required condition, the deposit is refunded in full.

Legal regulation

Many jurisdictions regulate security deposits by statute. Common provisions cap the amount that may be charged, require the deposit to be held in a separate escrow or trust account, require the landlord to pay interest on the deposit, and set deadlines — often 14 to 60 days — for returning the deposit or providing an itemised statement of deductions after the tenancy ends. Some jurisdictions impose penalties, including multiple damages, for deposits wrongfully withheld.

In England and Wales, deposits for assured shorthold tenancies must be protected in a government-backed tenancy deposit scheme. In Germany, the equivalent Kaution is capped at three months' cold rent and may be paid in three instalments.

Disputes

Disputes commonly concern the distinction between damage and normal wear and tear, and the adequacy of documentation. Move-in and move-out condition reports and photographs are widely recommended. Small claims courts and deposit-protection scheme adjudication services resolve many such disputes.
