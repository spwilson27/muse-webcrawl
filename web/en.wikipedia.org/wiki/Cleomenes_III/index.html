Cleomenes III

Cleomenes III (Greek: Κλεομένης; c. 260 – 219 BC) was the Agiad king of Sparta from 235 to 222 BC and the leader of a radical programme of social and military reform intended to restore Spartan power. He was the son of Leonidas II and was influenced by the example of the earlier reforming king Agis IV, whose widow Agiatis he married.

Reforms

After eliminating his opponents — including having the ephors murdered at their dinner table in 227 BC and abolishing the ephorate — Cleomenes cancelled debts, redistributed the land of Laconia into 4,000 equal lots for citizens, enfranchised perioikoi and selected foreigners to fill the citizen body, and restored the austere communal upbringing (agoge) and common messes of the classical Spartan system. He re-equipped the army in the Macedonian style, with the long sarissa pike and phalanx tactics.

War and defeat

In the Cleomenean War against the Achaean League, Cleomenes won a string of victories and briefly made Sparta master of much of the Peloponnese, but the League's appeal to Antigonus III Doson brought a Macedonian army south. Defeated at the Battle of Sellasia in 222 BC, Cleomenes fled to Alexandria in Ptolemaic Egypt.

Death

Kept under comfortable detention by Ptolemy IV, Cleomenes escaped in 219 BC and attempted to raise a revolt in Alexandria. When the rising failed, he and his companions killed themselves. His life was written by Plutarch, paired with that of Agis IV, and the two reforming kings became emblematic figures for later revolutionaries and social reformers.
