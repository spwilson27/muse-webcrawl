Charlie Todd (born November 18, 1978) is an American comedian and performance artist based in New York City. He is the founder of Improv Everywhere, a comedy collective that stages elaborate public scenes and pranks — which Todd calls "missions" — in everyday spaces. Improv Everywhere's best-known recurring event is the annual No Pants Subway Ride, in which participants ride public transit in winter wearing no trousers while otherwise dressed for the weather.

Improv Everywhere

Todd founded Improv Everywhere in 2001 after an early prank in which he impersonated musician Ben Folds at a New York bar. The group's early missions included the first No Pants Subway Ride in 2002, performed by seven participants, and a stunt in which dozens of "agents" simultaneously froze in place in Grand Central Terminal in 2008 ("Frozen Grand Central"), a video that became one of YouTube's early viral hits with tens of millions of views. Other celebrated missions include the "Mp3 Experiment" series, in which thousands of participants follow synchronized instructions downloaded to their music players, and a fake bookstore reading featuring a look-alike author.

Todd co-authored the group's book Causing a Scene: Extraordinary Pranks in Ordinary Places with Improv Everywhere (2009). His TED talk on the absurdity and community of shared public experiences has been widely viewed. In addition to Improv Everywhere, Todd is a longtime performer at the Upright Citizens Brigade Theatre in New York.

Approach

Todd describes his work as creating moments of surprise and joy for bystanders rather than humiliating targets, distinguishing the group's approach from mean-spirited prank culture. The chapter-based model of Improv Everywhere encouraged similar groups to form in cities around the world, and the No Pants Subway Ride grew into an international event held in dozens of cities.
