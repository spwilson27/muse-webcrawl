Tdap

Tdap
Type: Combination vaccine
Targets: Tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis
Used in: Adolescents and adults

Tdap is a combination vaccine that protects against three bacterial diseases: tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis (whooping cough). The name is derived from the components: Tetanus, diphtheria and acellular pertussis. The lower-case "d" and "a" indicate the reduced doses of diphtheria toxoid and pertussis antigens used in formulations for older children, adolescents and adults, compared with the childhood DTaP vaccine.

Use

In the United States, health authorities recommend that adolescents receive a single dose of Tdap, typically at 11 to 12 years of age, in place of the tetanus-diphtheria (Td) booster. Adults who have never received Tdap are advised to get one dose, and Tdap is recommended during each pregnancy — generally in the early part of the third trimester — so that antibodies pass to the newborn, who is too young to be vaccinated against pertussis. Afterwards, Td (or Tdap) boosters are given every ten years.

History

Tdap vaccines were first licensed in the United States in 2005, with two products approved that year for adolescents and adults. Their introduction responded to the resurgence of pertussis in older age groups, in whom immunity from childhood vaccination wanes.

Safety

Common adverse effects are mild and include pain, redness or swelling at the injection site, low-grade fever and tiredness. Severe allergic reactions are rare.
