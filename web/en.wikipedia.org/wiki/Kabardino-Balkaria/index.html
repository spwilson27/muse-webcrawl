The Kabardino-Balkarian Republic, or Kabardino-Balkaria, is a republic of Russia situated in the North Caucasus. Its capital is Nalchik. The republic is named for its two principal ethnic groups: the Kabardians, a Circassian people speaking a Northwest Caucasian language, and the Balkars, a Turkic people.

Geography

Kabardino-Balkaria borders Stavropol Krai, North Ossetia–Alania, Chechnya, Ingushetia, and Karachay-Cherkessia, and Georgia to the south. It contains Mount Elbrus, the highest peak in Europe at 5,642 m, on its western border, along with much of the Greater Caucasus range and the Prielbrusye National Park.

History

The region came under Russian control in the 18th and 19th centuries during the Caucasian War. An autonomous oblast was created in the Soviet period, becoming the Kabardino-Balkarian ASSR in 1936. The Balkars were deported to Central Asia in 1944 on accusations of collaboration and were permitted to return only in 1957, when the republic was restored. After the dissolution of the USSR it remained within the Russian Federation.

Economy and tourism

The economy is based on agriculture, food processing, and light industry, while the Elbrus region is a major centre for mountaineering, skiing, and tourism.
