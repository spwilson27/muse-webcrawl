Bailiff of Jersey

The Bailiff of Jersey is the chief justice of the Bailiwick of Jersey and the president of the States Assembly, the island's parliament. The Bailiff is appointed by the Crown and holds office until retirement, typically at age 65 or 70. The office combines judicial and civic functions unique among the Crown Dependencies.

Functions

As chief justice, the Bailiff presides over the Royal Court of Jersey. As president of the States Assembly, the Bailiff chairs sessions of the island's legislature, though the role of presiding officer has been a subject of reform debate, including in the Clive Report of 2000 and subsequent reviews. The Bailiff is also the civic head of the island, representing Jersey on ceremonial occasions, and is guardian of the island's constitution and the conduit for communications between the Crown and the island government.

History

The office dates to the medieval period, when the bailiff was the representative of the Norman dukes and later the English Crown in the island. Over centuries the office absorbed judicial functions previously exercised by the jurats and the royal court.

Deputy Bailiff

The Bailiff is assisted by a Deputy Bailiff, also a Crown appointment, who acts in the Bailiff's absence and presides over sittings of the Royal Court.
