Melanocyte-stimulating hormone

Melanocyte-stimulating hormones, collectively MSH or melanotropins, are a family of peptide hormones produced by the pituitary gland and other tissues from the precursor proopiomelanocortin (POMC). They include α-MSH, β-MSH and γ-MSH.

Function

MSH acts on melanocortin receptors. In many animals it stimulates melanocytes to produce and disperse melanin, darkening skin or changing colour. In humans α-MSH contributes to pigmentation modestly, and melanocortin signalling also influences appetite and energy balance (via MC4R), inflammation and sexual function.

Clinical relevance

Excess ACTH from pituitary or ectopic sources — which shares the POMC precursor — can cause hyperpigmentation, a clue in Addison's disease. Synthetic analogues such as afamelanotide are used to increase photoprotection in erythropoietic protoporphyria.
