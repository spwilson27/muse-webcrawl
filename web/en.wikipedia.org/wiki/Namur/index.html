Namur (French: [namyʁ]; Dutch: Namen; Walloon: Nameur) is a city in Belgium, serving as the capital of the province of Namur and of the Walloon Region. It stands at the confluence of the Sambre and Meuse rivers, in the central part of the country.

History

The town grew up around a strategic rocky spur between the two rivers, where a fortress — the Citadel of Namur — was built. The counts of Namur ruled the medieval county, and the city's position made it a repeated target of sieges: it was famously besieged in 1692 by the forces of Louis XIV of France, directed by Vauban, and again in 1695 by William III. The citadel was rebuilt and enlarged several times and remained a military stronghold into the 20th century.

In both World Wars Namur's forts formed part of Belgium's defensive lines. In August 1914 the fortified position of Namur fell to German forces after a short siege using heavy siege artillery, mirroring the earlier fall of Liège.

Sights

The Citadel, accessible by cable car, offers views over the old town. Landmarks include Namur Cathedral (Saint Aubin), the belfry (a UNESCO World Heritage site as part of the Belfries of Belgium and France), the baroque church of Saint-Loup, and the Hospice Saint-Gilles, seat of the Parliament of Wallonia.

Events

Namur hosts the annual Namur International French-Language Film Festival (FIFF) and the traditional Combat de l'Échasse d'Or, a jousting-on-stilts contest between teams from the old town districts, a custom inscribed on UNESCO's list of intangible cultural heritage.
