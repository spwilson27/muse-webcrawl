William Averell Harriman (November 15, 1891 – July 26, 1986) was an American Democratic politician, businessman and diplomat. The son of railroad baron E. H. Harriman, he founded the investment bank Brown Brothers Harriman & Co. He went on to serve as Secretary of Commerce under President Harry S. Truman and later as the 48th governor of New York. He was also a prominent foreign policymaker within the Democratic Party.

Harriman was a key figure in the administration of the Marshall Plan and served as President Franklin D. Roosevelt's special envoy to Europe and as United States ambassador to the Soviet Union during the Second World War. During the Cold War he advised Presidents Truman, Kennedy and Johnson, and was a chief negotiator of the Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty of 1963 and of the Paris Peace Accords relating to Vietnam.

Early life and business career

Harriman was born in New York City, the son of Edward Henry Harriman, president of the Union Pacific Railroad, and Mary Williamson Averell. He attended Groton School and Yale University, where he was a member of Skull and Bones. After graduating in 1913 he entered the railroad business, and in 1915, at age 23, he became a vice president of the Union Pacific. He later moved into banking, founding W. A. Harriman Co. & Co., which merged in 1931 with Brown Bros. & Co. to form Brown Brothers Harriman.

Public service

Harriman entered government service in the 1930s and became an important adviser to President Roosevelt. As ambassador to Moscow (1943–1946) he dealt directly with Joseph Stalin during the final years of the war. He served as ambassador to the United Kingdom in 1946 and then as Secretary of Commerce. As coordinator of the Marshall Plan in Europe from 1948, he played a central role in postwar recovery.

Governor of New York and later years

Elected governor of New York in 1954, Harriman defeated Senator Irving Ives and served one term before losing the 1958 election to Nelson Rockefeller. He twice sought the Democratic presidential nomination in the 1950s. Under President John F. Kennedy he served as assistant secretary of state and undersecretary of state for political affairs. He died in 1986 at his home in Yorktown Heights, New York, aged 94.
