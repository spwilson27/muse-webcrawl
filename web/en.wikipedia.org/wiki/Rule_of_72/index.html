Rule of 72

Rule of 72
FieldFinance, mathematics
FormulaYears to double ≈ 72 ÷ (interest rate in %)

The rule of 72 is a method for estimating an investment's doubling time. The number 72 is divided by the annual interest rate (expressed as a percentage) to obtain the approximate number of years required for the initial investment to double in value. The rule also works in reverse: dividing 72 by the number of years gives the approximate annual rate of return needed to double an investment in that period.

Examples

At a 6% annual return, money doubles in about 72 / 6 = 12 years (the exact answer is about 11.9 years). At 8%, the estimate is 9 years; at 9%, about 8 years. The rule is most accurate for rates between about 5% and 10%, the typical range of investment returns.

Derivation

If an amount grows at rate r per year, the doubling time t satisfies (1 + r)t = 2, so t = ln 2 / ln(1 + r). For small r, ln(1 + r) ≈ r, giving t ≈ 0.693 / r, or 69.3 when the rate is written as a percentage. The number 72 is preferred in practice because it approximates the correction needed for typical interest rates and is divisible by many small integers (2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12), making mental arithmetic easy. For continuously compounded growth, the exact constant is 69.3 (the "rule of 69.3"); a "rule of 70" is also used, especially for population growth.

History

The rule is an old one: an early reference appears in Luca Pacioli's Summa de arithmetica (1494), where the rule is presented without derivation, implying it was already known in his time.

Related rules

Analogous rules estimate tripling (rule of 114) and quadrupling (rule of 144) times, and the rule can also be applied to inflation: at 3% inflation, prices double — and the purchasing power of cash halves — in about 24 years.
