Mini DisplayPort

The Mini DisplayPort (MiniDP or mDP) is a miniaturised version of the DisplayPort digital display interface. It was announced by Apple in October 2008 and first appeared on the unibody MacBook Pro, MacBook and MacBook Air, as well as the 24-inch LED Cinema Display. The connector was subsequently included in the DisplayPort 1.2 specification by VESA, and other manufacturers adopted it for laptops and monitors.

Characteristics

The Mini DisplayPort connector is 4.5 mm high and 18.2 mm wide and carries the full DisplayPort signal on 20 pins. Using passive adapters it can drive HDMI, DVI or VGA displays; with DisplayPort 1.2 it supports resolutions up to 4K at 60 Hz and daisy-chaining of multiple monitors.

Thunderbolt

Apple's and Intel's Thunderbolt interface, launched in 2011, adopted the Mini DisplayPort connector shape, so Thunderbolt 1 and 2 ports accept Mini DisplayPort cables for display output. Later Thunderbolt versions moved to the USB-C connector.
