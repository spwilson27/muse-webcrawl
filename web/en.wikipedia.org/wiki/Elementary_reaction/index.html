Elementary reaction

An elementary reaction is a chemical reaction in which one or more chemical species react directly to form products in a single reaction step, passing through a single transition state. Elementary reactions are the building blocks of reaction mechanisms: any overall chemical reaction can be described as a sequence of elementary steps.

Molecularity

Elementary reactions are classified by their molecularity, the number of molecules that come together in the step. Unimolecular reactions involve a single reactant molecule (for example, the isomerisation or decomposition of one species); bimolecular reactions involve two; termolecular reactions, involving three, are rare because of the low probability of a three-body collision.

Rate laws

For an elementary reaction, the rate law follows directly from the stoichiometry of the step: the rate is proportional to the product of the concentrations of the reactants, each raised to the power of its stoichiometric coefficient. This is not generally true of overall reactions, whose rate laws must be determined experimentally. At equilibrium, the rates of the forward and reverse elementary steps are equal, a condition known as detailed balancing.
