Elliot Goldenthal

Elliot Goldenthal (born May 2, 1954) is an American composer of contemporary classical music and film and theatre scores. A student of Aaron Copland and John Corigliano, he is best known for his distinctive, often avant-garde orchestral film scores and for his long collaboration with director Julie Taymor, his partner. He won the Academy Award for Best Original Score for Frida (2002).

Goldenthal's scores blend modernist orchestral techniques with world-music elements.

Early life and training

Goldenthal was born in Brooklyn, New York, the son of a house painter and a seamstress. He studied trumpet and composition, attended the Manhattan School of Music, and studied with Aaron Copland and John Corigliano. His early concert works and the ballet Othello (choreographed by Lar Lubovitch, 1997) established his voice in the concert hall before and alongside film work.

Film scores

Goldenthal's breakthrough in film came with Drugstore Cowboy (1989) and David Fincher's Alien 3 (1992), whose dissonant, large-scale score is widely admired. He scored Neil Jordan's Interview with the Vampire (1994, Academy Award nomination) and Michael Collins (1996), Joel Schumacher's Batman Forever (1995) and Batman & Robin (1997), Heat (1995, with Michael Mann), Titus (1999), Frida (2002), for which he won both the Academy Award and Golden Globe for Best Original Score, plus a nomination for the song "Burn It Blue," and Across the Universe (2007). His style mixes modernist orchestral writing, brass-heavy textures, electronics, period pastiche, and non-Western instruments.

Stage and concert works

With Julie Taymor he created the opera Grendel (2006), based on the Beowulf legend and John Gardner's novel, premiered at Los Angeles Opera. His oratorio Fire Water Paper: A Vietnam Oratorio (1995) marked the 20th anniversary of the end of the Vietnam War. In 2019, a serious accident (a head injury from a fall) and recovery became a subject of interviews; he has continued composing for film and stage, including Taymor's The Glorias (2020).
