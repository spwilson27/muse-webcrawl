Czech music

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Czech music encompasses the musical traditions of Bohemia, Moravia and Czech Silesia, the historical lands forming the modern Czech Republic. The Czech lands have a rich musical heritage: a saying holds that "every Czech is a musician" (Co Čech, to muzikant). Czech composers such as Antonín Dvořák, Bedřich Smetana, Leoš Janáček and Bohuslav Martinů rank among the most performed in the classical repertoire, and Czech folk traditions — polka, furiant and Moravian song — influenced music far beyond the country's borders.

Early history

Music in the Czech lands is documented from the early Middle Ages, including the hymn "Hospodine, pomiluj ny" and Hussite chorales of the 15th century. During the Baroque era, composers such as Jan Dismas Zelenka and Adam Michna worked at Czech and imperial courts, while many Czech musicians — the "Bohemian diaspora" — shaped musical life across Europe; Johann Stamitz, born in Bohemia, founded the famous Mannheim school.

The national revival and the 19th century

The Czech National Revival of the 19th century placed music at the centre of cultural identity. Bedřich Smetana (1824–1884), with his cycle of symphonic poems Má vlast ("My Country") and the opera The Bartered Bride, is regarded as the founder of Czech national music. Antonín Dvořák (1841–1904) achieved worldwide fame with his symphonies, Slavonic Dances, and chamber works, blending Czech folk idioms with classical forms; he also directed the National Conservatory in New York. Josef Suk and Vítězslav Novák continued the tradition into the 20th century.

20th century

Leoš Janáček (1854–1928), from Moravia, developed a highly original style based on the rhythms of spoken Czech and Moravian folk song, producing operas such as Jenůfa and The Cunning Little Vixen. Bohuslav Martinů (1890–1959) wrote prolifically in exile in France and the United States. Under the communist regime after 1948, official culture promoted socialist realism, while jazz, rock and underground music became forms of dissent; the band The Plastic People of the Universe inspired the Charter 77 movement.

Folk and popular music

Bohemian folk music is associated with the polka, which originated in Bohemia in the 1830s and spread worldwide. Moravian traditions feature the cimbalom (hammered dulcimer), slower lyrical songs, and distinctive regional dances. Popular genres include dechovka (brass band music), tramping songs, and a strong tradition of jazz and swing. Prague hosts major festivals including Prague Spring (founded 1946), one of Europe's leading classical music festivals.
