The Encyclopædia Britannica Eleventh Edition (1910–1911) is one of the most famous editions of the encyclopaedia, widely regarded as a pinnacle of early-20th-century scholarship and prose. Edited by Hugh Chisholm and published in association with the University of Cambridge, it comprised 29 volumes and more than 40,000 articles written by some 1,500 contributors, including many of the era's leading scholars, scientists and writers.

Character

The eleventh edition shifted the Britannica toward shorter, more readable articles aimed at a broad educated public, while retaining distinguished long essays. Contributors included Ernest Rutherford, Marie Curie (on radium), Algernon Swinburne, Bertrand Russell and G. K. Chesterton. Its entries reflect the knowledge, assumptions and prejudices of the Edwardian era, and historians value it as a snapshot of that world.

Legacy

Now in the public domain, the eleventh edition has been digitised by projects such as Project Gutenberg and Wikisource, and early versions of Wikipedia drew on it as a source for thousands of articles. Sets remain common in libraries and second-hand bookshops.
