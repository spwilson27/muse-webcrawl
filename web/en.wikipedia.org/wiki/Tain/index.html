Tain

Tain (Scottish Gaelic: Baile Dhubhthaich) is a royal burgh and parish in the county of Ross, in the Highlands of Scotland. It lies on the south shore of the Dornoch Firth and is Scotland's oldest royal burgh, having received its charter in 1066 from Malcolm III.

History

The town grew around the shrine of St Duthac, a native saint whose sanctuary drew pilgrims, including James IV, who visited repeatedly. The collegiate church of St Duthus, dating from the 14th century, is a notable landmark. Tain was also known for the Glenmorangie whisky distillery, founded nearby in 1843.

Geography and transport

Tain stands on the A9 trunk road between Inverness and Thurso, and is served by a station on the Far North Line. The Dornoch Firth Bridge, opened in 1991, shortened the road journey north by carrying the A9 across the firth. RAF Tain, a coastal air weapons range, lies to the east of the town.
