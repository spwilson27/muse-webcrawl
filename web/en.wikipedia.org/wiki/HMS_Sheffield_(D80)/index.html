HMS Sheffield was a Type 42 guided missile destroyer of the Royal Navy and the second ship to be named after the city of Sheffield in Yorkshire. She was the first of her class in service. Commissioned on 16 February 1975, Sheffield was part of the Task Force 317 sent to the Falkland Islands during the Falklands War. She was struck by an Exocet air-launched anti-ship missile from an Argentine Super Étendard aircraft on 4 May 1982 and foundered while under tow on 10 May 1982.

The attack

Sheffield was acting as a radar picket south-east of the Falklands when two Argentine Super Étendards armed with AM39 Exocet missiles approached at low level. One missile struck the ship starboard side amidships, penetrating the hull near the operations room and starting fires fed by cable insulation and furnishings. Whether the warhead detonated remains disputed; the fire and loss of water pressure in the fire main made firefighting impossible. Twenty of her crew were killed and 26 injured.

The crew fought the fires for several hours before Captain Sam Salt ordered the ship abandoned. Sheffield was taken in tow by HMS Yarmouth, but on 10 May, in heavy seas, she took on water and sank at 53°04′S 56°56′W — the first Royal Navy warship sunk in action since the Second World War.

Legacy

The loss of Sheffield caused shock in Britain and prompted changes to Royal Navy damage control, fire-resistant materials, and clothing. The official Board of Inquiry criticised aspects of the ship's readiness but the findings remained classified for many years; a redacted report was released in 2006 and a fuller version in 2017. The wreck is a designated war grave.
