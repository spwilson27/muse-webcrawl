Kaluza–Klein theory

Kaluza–Klein theory is a classical unified field theory of gravitation and electromagnetism built around the idea of a fifth dimension beyond the usual four of space and time. Proposed by Theodor Kaluza in 1919 (published 1921) and extended by Oskar Klein in 1926, it showed that general relativity in five dimensions, with the extra dimension curled up into a tiny circle, naturally yields both four-dimensional gravity and Maxwell's equations.

The idea

Kaluza wrote the five-dimensional metric in terms of the four-dimensional metric, the electromagnetic vector potential, and a scalar field. Klein proposed that the fifth dimension is compactified at roughly the Planck length, explaining why it is not observed, and that momentum in the compact direction appears as electric charge — an early explanation of charge quantization.

Legacy

Although the original theory did not incorporate the strong and weak forces, its mechanism of extra compact dimensions and Kaluza–Klein towers of massive states became a cornerstone of modern string theory and supergravity.
