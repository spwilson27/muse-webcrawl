Yorkshire and the Humber is one of nine official regions of England at the first level of statistical subdivision used by the Office for National Statistics. It comprises most of the historic county of Yorkshire, together with the part of northern Lincolnshire that was formerly part of Humberside. The region covers an area of about 15,420 square kilometres and has a population of roughly five and a half million people.

Geography

The region stretches from the North Sea coast in the east to the Pennine hills in the west. It includes the North York Moors and Yorkshire Dales national parks, the uplands of the South Pennines, and the low-lying Humberhead Levels and Holderness plain. The River Humber, formed by the Ouse and the Trent, is the region's great estuary and gives the region the second half of its name. Major cities include Leeds, Sheffield, Bradford, Hull (Kingston upon Hull) and York, with large towns such as Doncaster, Halifax, Harrogate, Huddersfield, Rotherham, Scunthorpe, Wakefield and Grimsby.

History and administration

The region largely matches the territory of the historic county of Yorkshire, traditionally divided into the East, North and West Ridings. Local government is provided by a mix of unitary authorities and two-tier county and district councils; the former county of Humberside (1974–1996) spanned the estuary before being split between the East Riding of Yorkshire and Lincolnshire unitary authorities. Combined authorities, including the West Yorkshire and South Yorkshire mayoral combined authorities, have taken on devolved powers in recent years.

Economy

Historically the region's economy rested on wool textiles, steel, coal mining, engineering and the fishing and port industries of the Humber. Modern strengths include financial and legal services in Leeds, advanced manufacturing and materials research in Sheffield, food processing, chemicals and renewable energy around the Humber — the estuary has become a major centre for offshore wind — and tourism drawn by the historic city of York, the national parks and the coast.
