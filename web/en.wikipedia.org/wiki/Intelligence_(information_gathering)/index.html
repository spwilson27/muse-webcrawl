Intelligence (information gathering)

Intelligence is information gathered by governments, militaries, and organizations about adversaries, competitors, and environments, and the process of analyzing that information to support decision-making. Intelligence work is typically organized around the "intelligence cycle": direction, collection, processing, analysis, and dissemination.

Collection disciplines

Collection is commonly divided into disciplines including human intelligence (HUMINT) from agents and informants, signals intelligence (SIGINT) from intercepted communications, imagery intelligence (IMINT) from satellite and aerial photography, measurement and signature intelligence (MASINT), and open-source intelligence (OSINT) from publicly available material.

Organizations

Major intelligence organizations include the United States' CIA and NSA, the United Kingdom's MI6, GCHQ, and MI5, and comparable services in most states. Counterintelligence seeks to defeat the intelligence activities of adversaries.

Analysis and oversight

Analysts evaluate sources, weigh competing hypotheses, and produce estimates for policymakers. In democracies, intelligence agencies operate under legal frameworks and legislative oversight, and their work raises recurring debates about secrecy, civil liberties, and politicization.
