San Fernando, officially the City of San Fernando, is a component city and the capital of the province of Pampanga, Philippines, and the regional centre of Central Luzon. It lies about 67 kilometres north of Manila along the North Luzon Expressway corridor.

Giant Lantern Festival

The city is nationally known as the "Christmas Capital of the Philippines" because of its parol (Christmas lantern) industry and the Giant Lantern Festival (Ligligan Parul), held each December. Competing barangays display lanterns up to six metres across, built with thousands of bulbs and intricate rotor-driven light displays, a tradition that grew from smaller lantern processions in the early 20th century.

History

Founded as a town in 1754 and named after King Ferdinand VI of Spain, San Fernando grew as a trading centre on the road between Manila and the provinces. It briefly served revolutionary purposes during the Philippine Revolution and the Philippine–American War, and suffered heavy damage in the Second World War. It became a city in 2001 under Republic Act 8990.

Economy and transport

Agriculture — rice and sugarcane — and lantern-making anchor the traditional economy, increasingly supplemented by commerce, malls and industrial estates. The city is a transport hub where the MacArthur Highway and Jose Abad Santos Avenue meet, serving buses across Central and Northern Luzon.
