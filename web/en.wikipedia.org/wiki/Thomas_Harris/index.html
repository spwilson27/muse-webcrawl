Thomas Harris

Thomas Harris (born 22 September 1940) is an American writer best known for a series of suspense novels centred on his most famous creation, the cannibalistic serial killer and former psychiatrist Dr. Hannibal Lecter. All of his Lecter novels have been adapted into films, most famously The Silence of the Lambs (1991). Harris is famously reclusive, rarely giving interviews and avoiding publicity.

Life and career

Harris grew up in Mississippi and studied English literature at Baylor University in Waco, Texas, where he covered the police beat for the local newspaper. He later worked as a reporter and editor for the Associated Press in New York. His first novel, Black Sunday (1975), about a terrorist plot against the Super Bowl, was a bestseller and was filmed in 1977.

The Lecter novels

Red Dragon (1981) introduced Hannibal Lecter in a supporting role; The Silence of the Lambs (1988) brought him to the centre alongside FBI trainee Clarice Starling and became a major critical and commercial success. Sequels Hannibal (1999) and the prequel Hannibal Rising (2006) followed. In 2019 Harris published Cari Mora, his first novel in 13 years and only his second outside the Lecter series. His meticulous research — including time spent at the FBI's Behavioral Science Unit at Quantico — is credited with helping to define the modern serial-killer thriller.
