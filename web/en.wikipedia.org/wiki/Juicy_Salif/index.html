The Juicy Salif is a citrus reamer designed by the French designer Philippe Starck in 1990 for the Italian housewares company Alessi. Cast in polished aluminium, it stands 29 cm (11.4 in) tall on three long legs, resembling a rocket ship or a squid. It has become one of the most famous — and most debated — products of postmodern industrial design: an icon displayed in museums including the Museum of Modern Art in New York, and a byword for form triumphing over function.

Design and origin

Starck famously sketched the design on a paper placemat (or napkin) in a restaurant while squeezing lemon over a plate of squid — the dish that suggested the creature-like form: a bulbous ribbed body tapering to a point, held high on three slender, talon-like legs, with the fruit pressed onto the pointed cone and the juice running down into a glass placed beneath. Alberto Alessi put the object into production as part of his company's strategy of commissioning playful, sculptural "design objects" from leading architects and designers.

Function and criticism

As a juicer the Juicy Salif is widely considered impractical: it tips, lacks a strainer, sends juice running down its legs, and aluminium is attacked by citric acid, so it must not be left in contact with juice. Starck embraced the criticism, remarking that his juicer "is not meant to squeeze lemons, it is meant to start conversations" and that it was designed as a social object — even a wedding gift signifying a new household. Design critics cite it as a pre-eminent example of design as sculpture and of the 1980s–90s "designer object" phenomenon.

Cultural status

The Juicy Salif is held in the permanent collections of major design museums and has sold in large numbers despite — or because of — its impracticality. A limited gold-plated edition was produced in 2000; Alessi warned that the gold version must not be used with citrus at all, further underlining its status as pure ornament. It remains a staple of design-school syllabuses and debates about the proper balance between utility and expression in product design.
