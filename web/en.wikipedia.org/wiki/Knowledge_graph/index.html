Knowledge graph

A knowledge graph, also known as a semantic network, is a knowledge base that uses a graph data model or topology to represent and operate on data. Knowledge graphs are often used to store interlinked descriptions of entities – objects, events, situations or abstract concepts – while also encoding the semantics underlying the used terminology. Since the development of the Semantic Web, knowledge graphs have often been associated with linked open data projects, focusing on connections between concepts and entities.


Entities as nodes, relations as edges

History

The concept descends from semantic networks developed in the 1960s and 1970s and from frame-based and description-logic systems in artificial intelligence. The term gained wide currency after Google introduced its Knowledge Graph in 2012, a massive graph of entities and facts used to enhance search results with direct answers in "knowledge panels". Other large knowledge graphs include Wikidata, DBpedia, Microsoft's Bing Satori, Amazon's product graph, and enterprise graphs used for data integration.

Structure and representation

Knowledge graphs typically represent facts as triples of subject–predicate–object (for example, "Paris — capital of — France"). Standard models include the Resource Description Framework (RDF) and labelled property graphs; ontologies written in RDFS or OWL provide the schema of types and relations. More recent research embeds graphs into vector spaces (knowledge graph embeddings) so that links can be predicted and graphs completed by machine learning, an approach also used to ground large language models and reduce hallucination in retrieval-augmented generation.

Applications

Applications include semantic search, question answering, recommender systems, biomedical research and drug discovery, fraud detection, and enterprise data integration. Construction techniques range from manual curation (Wikidata) to automatic extraction from text and other sources.
