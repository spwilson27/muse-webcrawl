The News of the World was a British national red-top newspaper published from 1843 to 2011 by News Group Newspapers, part of Rupert Murdoch's News International. Once the world's best-selling English-language newspaper, famous for celebrity scoops and investigations, it was closed in July 2011 amid the phone-hacking scandal.

History

Founded in 1843 by John Browne Bell, the paper built a mass Sunday readership on crime, sensation and sport. Murdoch bought it in 1969, his first British title, and it became the flagship of his tabloid empire.

Style and influence

Its investigations and kiss-and-tell exclusives made and broke reputations; the 'Fake Sheikh' Mazher Mahmood became its best-known reporter. At its height it sold several million copies each Sunday.

Phone hacking and closure

Revelations that its journalists had hacked voicemails — culminating in the Milly Dowler case in July 2011 — triggered public outrage, advertiser flight and the paper's abrupt closure after 168 years; the final edition appeared on 10 July 2011. The scandal led to the Leveson Inquiry and the replacement Sunday title, The Sun on Sunday (2012).
