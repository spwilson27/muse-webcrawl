List of fountains in Rome

Rome has more than 2,000 fountains, more than any other city in the world. They range from the great Baroque showpieces built by the popes to hundreds of small public drinking fountains called nasoni. In Roman times, the city's eleven aqueducts supplied hundreds of public fountains, baths and basins.

The Trevi Fountain, the terminal display of the Acqua Vergine aqueduct, is the largest Baroque fountain in the city and one of the most famous fountains in the world; tradition holds that throwing a coin into it ensures a return to Rome. During the Renaissance and Baroque eras, popes restored the ancient aqueducts and commissioned terminal fountains (mostre) to celebrate them.

Nasoni

Rome's nasoni ("large noses") are cast-iron drinking fountains, first installed in the 1870s, which run continuously with fresh water; about 2,500–3,000 remain in service across the city and surrounding areas.
