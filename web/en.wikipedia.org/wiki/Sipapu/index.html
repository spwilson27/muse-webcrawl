Sipapu

A sipapu is a small hole or indentation in the floor of a kiva, the ceremonial and communal room of the Pueblo peoples of the American Southwest. The word is Hopi, sìpapu, and in Hopi and other Pueblo belief the sipapu symbolises the portal through which the first human beings emerged into the world from the underworlds below — the place of emergence in the Pueblo emergence narrative.

Meaning and use

The sipapu is typically located near the centre or north side of the kiva floor, traditionally aligned with the ladder entrance and the fire pit. It represents both the point of emergence from the previous world and a continuing connection to the spiritual realm below; in ceremony it serves as a symbolic conduit between worlds. In Hopi cosmology, humanity passed successively through lower worlds before emerging into the present Fourth World, and the sipapu in each kiva commemorates that journey. The Sipapu natural bridge area of the Grand Canyon — a travertine formation over the Little Colorado River — is regarded by the Hopi as the actual place of emergence and remains sacred.

Archaeology

Small floor holes interpreted as sipapus appear in ancestral Pueblo (Anasazi) kivas from roughly the Pueblo II period onward, and their presence is one of the features archaeologists use to identify a room as a kiva. Modern Pueblo communities such as the Hopi, Zuni, and Rio Grande Pueblos continue to incorporate sipapus into their ceremonial architecture.
