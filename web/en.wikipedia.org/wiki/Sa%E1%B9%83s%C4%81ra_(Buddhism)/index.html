Saṃsāra (Buddhism)

In Buddhism, saṃsāra is the beginningless cycle of repeated birth, mundane existence and dying again that all beings pass through. It is characterised by dukkha (suffering, unsatisfactoriness) and is driven by ignorance (avidyā) and karma.

The six realms

Beings are reborn according to their karma into one of six realms: the gods (deva), demigods (asura), humans, animals, hungry ghosts (preta), and hell beings (naraka). The human realm is considered the most favourable for attaining enlightenment, since beings there experience both pleasure and suffering.

Liberation

Escape from saṃsāra is achieved by attaining nirvana, the extinguishing of the fires of greed, hatred and delusion. The Buddha's Noble Eightfold Path is taught as the way leading to the end of rebirth. One who is fully liberated is an arhat or a Buddha and is no longer reborn.

The wheel of life

The cycle is often depicted by the bhavacakra or "wheel of life", a symbolic painting held by Yama, the lord of death, showing the six realms and the twelve links of dependent origination.
