Torticollis

Torticollis, also known as wry neck, is a condition in which the head is tilted to one side and the chin rotated toward the opposite shoulder, caused by shortening or spasm of the neck muscles, principally the sternocleidomastoid. The name derives from the Latin tortus ("twisted") and collum ("neck").

Types and causes

Congenital muscular torticollis is present at or shortly after birth, often associated with a fibrous mass in the sternocleidomastoid and with breech delivery; it may be accompanied by developmental dysplasia of the hip and, if untreated, plagiocephaly and facial asymmetry. Acquired torticollis can result from muscle spasm (for example after sleeping awkwardly or with upper respiratory infection — Grisel syndrome in children), trauma, cervical dystonia (spasmodic torticollis), infection, tumours, or drug reactions.

Treatment

Most congenital cases resolve with a programme of stretching and physiotherapy in the first year of life; persistent cases may need surgical release of the muscle. Cervical dystonia is commonly treated with botulinum toxin injections, and acute spasm with heat, analgesics, and muscle relaxants while serious causes are excluded.
