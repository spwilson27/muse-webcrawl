Woomera, South Australia

StateSouth Australia
Established1947
Location~487 km north of Adelaide

Woomera is a town in the Far North region of South Australia, established in 1947 to support the Woomera Test Range (now the RAAF Woomera Range Complex), one of the largest land-based weapons testing ranges in the world. Its name derives from an Aboriginal word for a spear-throwing device.

History

The Anglo-Australian Long Range Weapons Establishment began rocket and missile testing from Woomera in the late 1940s, and the town grew to house thousands of defence personnel and contractors during the Cold War, supporting projects such as the Blue Streak missile and the European Launcher Development Organisation. In 1967 the WRESAT satellite, launched from Woomera, made Australia the third country to launch a satellite from its own territory. The town also hosted the Nurrungar joint intelligence facility nearby until 1999.

The village today

Woomera remains a closed-support defence town administered by the Royal Australian Air Force, with a small permanent population, a heritage centre, missile and aircraft displays in the town centre, and facilities for range personnel. The surrounding Woomera Prohibited Area covers about 122,000 km² of desert.
