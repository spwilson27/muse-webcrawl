Noah Strausser Speer Wyle (born June 4, 1971) is an American actor, producer, director and writer. He is best known for his role as Dr. John Carter in the NBC medical drama ER (1994–2005, with later guest returns including the 2009 finale), for which he received five consecutive Primetime Emmy Award nominations and three Golden Globe nominations. He has since starred as Flynn Carsen in The Librarian franchise, Tom Mason in the TNT science-fiction series Falling Skies (2011–2015), and Daniel Calder in The Red Line, and leads the Max medical drama The Pitt (2025– ) as Dr. Michael "Robby" Robinavitch, also serving as a writer and executive producer.

Early life

Wyle was born in Hollywood, California, the son of an orthopedic surgeon and a registered nurse and health-care executive, and was raised in the Reform Jewish tradition. He was educated at the Thacher School in Ojai, California, and Northwestern University, where he participated in a theatre-arts programme before leaving to pursue acting, studying with Larry Moss.

Career

Early film roles included A Few Good Men (1992) and Swing Kids (1993). Cast in ER in 1994, Wyle's John Carter evolved from medical student to the show's central figure; he was the only original cast member to remain until 2005 and appeared in 254 episodes. He won acclaim playing Steve Jobs in the television film Pirates of Silicon Valley (1999). In the 2000s he headlined The Librarian television films (2004–2008) and later the spin-off series as a recurring guest, produced and starred in Falling Skies, and appeared in Donnie Darko (2001) and W. (2008). His role in The Pitt, a real-time hospital drama praised for its medical realism, brought him a new wave of critical recognition and major award nominations.

Personal life and activism

Wyle has been involved in advocacy on health-care and humanitarian issues, including work with Doctors of the World and the Human Rights Watch, and has supported arts coverage in public education. He married Sara Wells in 2014; he has three children.
