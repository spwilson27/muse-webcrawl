City Lights Bookstore

City Lights Bookstore
Founded: 1953, San Francisco
Founders: Lawrence Ferlinghetti, Peter D. Martin
Known for: Beat Generation publishing; Howl obscenity trial (1957)
Status: San Francisco Designated Landmark

City Lights is an independent bookstore and publisher in the North Beach neighbourhood of San Francisco, founded in 1953 by poet Lawrence Ferlinghetti and Peter D. Martin. It is America's first all-paperback bookstore and a landmark of the Beat Generation.

Publishing and Howl

City Lights Publishers issued the Pocket Poets Series, including Allen Ginsberg's Howl and Other Poems (1956). Ferlinghetti and store manager Shig Murao were tried for obscenity in 1957; their acquittal was a landmark free-speech decision. The store published or championed writers such as Jack Kerouac, William S. Burroughs and Diane di Prima.

Legacy

The bookstore at 261 Columbus Avenue remains a literary meeting place and tourist destination. Ferlinghetti remained owner until 2007 and a San Francisco cultural figure until his death in 2021 at 101.
