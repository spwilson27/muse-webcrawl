Ecstasy (emotion)

Ecstasy (from Ancient Greek ἔκστασις, ékstasis, meaning "outside of oneself") is a subjective experience of total involvement of the subject with an object of their awareness. It is an intensely pleasurable emotional state characterised by feelings of euphoria, awe, and transcendence, in which ordinary self-consciousness and awareness of time may seem suspended.

Types

Religious ecstasy is a central feature of many spiritual traditions: it is reported in Christian mysticism (for example, the experiences of Saint Teresa of Ávila), in Sufi practice, in shamanic trance, and in ecstatic dance and music rituals worldwide. Natural or aesthetic ecstasy can arise from music, nature, love, or creative work. Pharmacological agents, particularly MDMA — itself nicknamed "ecstasy" — can induce related states of euphoria and empathy.

Psychological accounts

Psychologists relate ecstasy to altered states of consciousness and, in some respects, to the concept of "flow" described by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, though ecstasy typically involves a stronger sense of self-transcendence. William James analysed ecstatic religious experience in The Varieties of Religious Experience (1902), stressing its noetic quality — the feeling of encountering profound truth. Ecstatic states also appear in some psychiatric contexts, such as mania, and in certain forms of epilepsy.
