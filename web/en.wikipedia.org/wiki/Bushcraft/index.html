Bushcraft

Bushcraft is the usage and practice of skills needed to survive and thrive in the natural environment, especially in forests and bush country. The term originated in Australia and South Africa and was popularized in the Northern Hemisphere by the Canadian outdoorsman Mors Kochanski and the British instructor Ray Mears. Bushcraft emphasizes knowledge of the environment — plants, animals, weather, and materials — rather than reliance on modern equipment.

Skills

Core bushcraft skills typically include:

  • Firecraft — gathering tinder and fuel, and lighting fires by matches, ferrocerium rods, bow drill, and other friction methods.
  • Shelter building — debris huts, lean-tos, and tarp shelters.
  • Tracking — identifying animal tracks and sign, and trailing animals.
  • Hunting, trapping, and fishing — snares, deadfalls, and improvised tackle.
  • Foraging — identifying edible and medicinal plants and fungi.
  • Water procurement — finding, filtering, and purifying water.
  • Tool use — safe handling of knives, axes, and saws; carving utensils and implements.
  • Cordage and knots — making rope from natural fibres and tying essential knots.
  • Navigation — map and compass work and natural navigation.

In popular culture

Bushcraft reached a wide audience through television series such as Ray Mears's Bushcraft and World of Survival, and through books like Kochanski's Bushcraft (1987) and Lars Monsen's outdoor writings. It overlaps with, but differs from, survivalism: bushcraft is generally framed as comfortable living with nature rather than emergency escape from it.
