Chicken tikka masala is a dish consisting of roasted marinated chicken chunks (chicken tikka) in a spiced sauce. The sauce is usually creamy and orange-coloured. The dish was created by cooks of the Indian subcontinent in Great Britain and is offered at restaurants around the world. It is considered a staple of British cuisine and has been described as a true British national dish.

Composition

Chicken tikka masala is composed of chicken tikka — boneless chunks of chicken marinated in spices and yogurt, then roasted in a tandoor oven — served in a masala sauce. A tomato and coriander sauce is common, but no recipe for chicken tikka masala is standard; a survey found that of 48 different recipes, the only common ingredient was chicken. The sauce usually includes tomatoes, cream, coconut cream and a masala spice mix. The sauce and chicken pieces may be coloured orange using food colourings, turmeric or paprika.

Origins

The origin of the dish is disputed. One popular account attributes it to Glasgow in the 1970s, where chef Ali Ahmed Aslam of the Shish Mahal restaurant is said to have improvised a sauce from tomato soup, spices and cream after a customer complained his chicken tikka was too dry. Others argue the dish evolved from butter chicken (murgh makhani), a dish developed in Delhi in the 1950s. In 2009, Glasgow's campaign to have the dish recognised with Protected Designation of Origin status drew international attention.

Popularity

Chicken tikka masala is served in restaurants around the world and is among the most popular dishes in British Indian restaurants. In 2001, British Foreign Secretary Robin Cook declared it "a true British national dish" in a speech on multiculturalism. It has been estimated that in Britain tens of millions of portions are served each year, and it regularly tops polls of the nation's favourite dishes, competing with fish and chips and roast dinner. Similar dishes include butter chicken and other curries.
