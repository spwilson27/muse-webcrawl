Greek royal family

The Greek royal family, of the House of Glücksburg

The Greek royal family is the former ruling family of the Kingdom of Greece, a cadet branch of the Danish royal house of Glücksburg. The dynasty was founded in 1863 when the young Prince William of Denmark was elected King of the Hellenes as George I. Members of the family reigned, with interruptions, until the monarchy was abolished following a referendum in 1974, after the fall of the military junta.

History

George I reigned for fifty years until his assassination in 1913. His descendants included Constantine I, Alexander, George II, Paul and Constantine II, whose reigns were punctuated by wars, national schism, exile and restoration. The family is closely connected to other European royal houses: Queen Sofía of Spain and Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, were both born members of the Greek royal family. Constantine II, the last king, went into exile in 1967 and lived for many years in London before returning to Greece in 2013; he died in Athens in 2023.

Status after the abolition of the monarchy

Following the 1974 referendum, the former royal family's property in Greece was expropriated under a 1994 law, which also stripped its members of Greek citizenship unless they adopted a surname, Greek law not recognising titles of nobility. The European Court of Human Rights later ordered Greece to compensate the family for the confiscated property. Members of the family hold Danish passports and titles as princes and princesses of Greece and Denmark, though these titles are not officially recognised by the Greek republic. In 2024, legislation enabled members of the family to reacquire Greek citizenship under the surname "De Grèce", and several did so.

Members

The head of the family since 2023 is Crown Prince Pavlos, the eldest son of Constantine II and Queen Anne-Marie, herself a Danish princess. The wider family includes the descendants of King Paul and Queen Frederica, among them the Spanish queen emerita Sofía, and branches settled in Britain, Spain, Serbia and elsewhere in Europe.
