Waxing

Waxing is a form of semi-permanent hair removal that removes hair from the root. Warm or cold wax is applied to the skin and then pulled off, taking the hairs with it. New hair typically does not grow back for two to eight weeks, though this varies by individual and body area.

Methods

Soft wax is spread thin and removed with a cloth or paper strip; hard wax hardens on the skin and is peeled off directly, without strips. Sugaring, which uses a paste of sugar, lemon, and water, is a related technique. Waxing is performed on eyebrows, legs, arms, underarms, the bikini area, and the face.

Effects and risks

Compared with shaving, waxing gives longer-lasting smoothness but is more painful and can cause redness, ingrown hairs, and, rarely, burns from overheated wax or skin irritation. Exfoliation and moisturising between sessions help reduce ingrown hairs.

History

Hair removal with wax-like substances dates back to ancient Egypt and the Middle East; modern salon waxing became widespread in the twentieth century.
