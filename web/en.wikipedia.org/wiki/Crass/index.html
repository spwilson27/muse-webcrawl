Crass were an English art collective and punk rock band formed in Epping, Essex, in 1977, who promoted anarchism as a political ideology, a way of life, and a resistance movement. They are widely regarded as the founders of the anarcho-punk subgenre, combining confrontational music with activism, communal living at Dial House, and a rigorous do-it-yourself ethic.

The Crass symbol, designed by Dave King.

History

Crass emerged from the Dial House commune, founded by drummer Penny Rimbaud and artist Gee Vaucher. Their debut album The Feeding of the 5000 (1978) — pressed independently after a record plant refused some tracks as blasphemous — set out their anti-war, anti-capitalist, feminist, and vegetarian positions. Through their own Crass Records they released material by other bands, sold records at cost-plus prices printed on the sleeve, and organised benefit gigs and squats.

Activism

The band's campaigns included support for CND and opposition to the Falklands War: their single "How Does It Feel (to Be the Mother of 1000 Dead?)" attacked Margaret Thatcher, and in 1983 the "Thatchergate" hoax tape — a spliced fake phone conversation between Thatcher and Ronald Reagan — was traced to the band and debated in Parliament. True to an early pledge, Crass disbanded in 1984 (the year of Orwell's dystopia), having announced the date from the start.

Legacy

Crass influenced generations of punk and DIY musicians through their music, collage artwork, stencilled symbol, and example of self-organisation; members later worked in performance art, publishing, and projects such as The Ex and the Last Amendment.
