Vilnius Castle Complex

The Vilnius Castle Complex (Lithuanian: Vilniaus pilių kompleksas) is a group of cultural and historic structures on the left bank of the Neris River, near its confluence with the Vilnia River, in Vilnius, Lithuania. The complex, built between the 10th and 18th centuries, consisted of three castles: the Upper, the Lower, and the Crooked Castle (Lithuanian: Kreivoji pilis).

History

Fortifications on Gediminas Hill date back to the early history of Lithuania. Grand Duke Gediminas is said to have founded Vilnius around 1323 after dreaming of an iron wolf on the hill. The Upper Castle on the hill was repeatedly rebuilt in brick and withstood sieges by the Teutonic Order. The Lower Castle, at the foot of the hill, contained the grand-ducal palace, cathedral, and arsenal.

The Crooked Castle was burned by the Teutonic Knights in 1390 and never rebuilt. The Palace of the Grand Dukes in the Lower Castle was demolished by the Russian imperial authorities around 1800 after the partitions of Poland-Lithuania; a reconstruction of the palace, built on the surviving foundations, opened in 2013 as a museum.

Today

The most visible surviving element is Gediminas Tower, the western tower of the Upper Castle, a symbol of Vilnius and of Lithuania, flying the national flag. The complex is part of the Vilnius Old Town UNESCO World Heritage Site and is administered with the National Museum of Lithuania.
