Siege of Petropavlovsk

The siege of Petropavlovsk (Russian: Оборона Петропавловска) was a military operation of the Crimean War fought in August–September 1854 at Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky, Russia's remote Pacific port on the Kamchatka Peninsula. An Anglo-French naval squadron bombarded the town and landed troops, but the small Russian garrison under Major-General Vasily Zavoyko repelled the landings with heavy losses to the attackers — the only significant Allied defeat in the Pacific theatre of the war.

Background

With the Crimean War underway, Britain and France sent a squadron into the North Pacific to attack Russian possessions and commerce. Petropavlovsk, founded by Vitus Bering in 1740, was the principal Russian harbour in the region, garrisoned by fewer than a thousand men with some forty guns in shore batteries, reinforced by the crews of the frigate Aurora and the transport Dvina, which had slipped into the harbour ahead of the enemy.

The action

The Allied squadron of six warships under Rear-Admiral David Price arrived in Avacha Bay on 29 August 1854. Price died on the eve of the attack — by his own hand, according to most accounts — and command devolved on the French admiral Febvrier-Despointes. Bombardments on 31 August knocked out some batteries but failed to silence the defences. Landing parties of sailors and marines put ashore on 1 and 4 September were met by determined counterattacks across difficult ground and driven back to their boats with severe casualties — the Allies lost several hundred men killed, wounded, and captured, against light Russian losses. The squadron withdrew on 7 September.

Aftermath

Zavoyko was fêted in Russia, and the defence became a celebrated example of a small garrison beating off a superior naval force. Judging Petropavlovsk untenable against a stronger return, the Russians quietly evacuated it by sea in the spring of 1855, and a second Allied expedition found the town empty. Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky today bears the honorary title "City of Military Glory" in part for this defence.
