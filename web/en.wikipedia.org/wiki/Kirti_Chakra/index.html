Kirti Chakra

Kirti Chakra Country: India
Type: Military decoration (peacetime gallantry)
Established: 1952 (as Ashoka Chakra Class II; renamed 1967)
Precedence: Second-highest peacetime gallantry award, after the Ashoka Chakra
Eligibility: Military personnel and civilians

The Kirti Chakra is an Indian military decoration awarded for valour, courageous action or self-sacrifice away from the field of battle. It is the peacetime equivalent of the Maha Vir Chakra and ranks second in the order of precedence of Indian peacetime gallantry awards, after the Ashoka Chakra and before the Shaurya Chakra.

History

The award was instituted on 4 January 1952 as the "Ashoka Chakra, Class II" and formed the second tier of a three-class decoration. On 27 January 1967 the classes were separated into independent awards, and Class II was renamed the Kirti Chakra. The decoration may be awarded posthumously and is open to officers, men and women of all ranks of the Army, Navy and Air Force, members of the paramilitary and police forces, and civilians.

Design

The medal is a circular silver disc bearing the Ashoka Chakra in the centre, surrounded by a lotus wreath; the reverse carries the words "Kirti Chakra" in Hindi and English separated by lotus motifs. It is worn suspended from a dark green ribbon divided by two orange vertical stripes.

Notable recipients

Recipients include soldiers and police officers honoured for gallantry in counter-insurgency and counter-terrorism operations and in internal security duties across Jammu and Kashmir and India's north-east. A bar is awarded for a second act of gallantry qualifying for the decoration.
