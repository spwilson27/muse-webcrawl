An Caighdeán Oifigiúil ("The Official Standard"), often referred to simply as An Caighdeán, is the official grammar and spelling standard for the Irish language. It was published by the Houses of the Oireachtas, the Irish parliament, to provide a standard written form of Irish for use in official documents, legislation and education.

Development

Before the standard existed, Irish spelling reflected the conventions of the various spoken dialects — principally those of Munster, Connacht and Ulster — making official publication difficult. Work on a simplified spelling system began in the 1940s, and the grammar standard Gramadach na Gaeilge agus Litriú na Gaeilge: An Caighdeán Oifigiúil was issued in 1958.

Content

The standard sets out rules for spelling, the inflection of nouns, adjectives and verbs, the copula, prepositional pronouns and the use of initial mutations. It does not prescribe a spoken standard; speakers continue to use the pronunciation and idiom of their own dialect areas.

Revision

A revised edition was published in 2017 following a public consultation, modernising some forms and clarifying areas where the 1958 text had been ambiguous. The standard remains the reference for Irish-language teaching in schools and for the translation of legislation.
