Crevalle jack

Scientific name: Caranx hippos
Family: Carangidae
Conservation status: Least Concern

The crevalle jack (Caranx hippos), also known as the common jack, black-tailed trevally, couvalli jack, black cavalli, jack crevale, or yellow cavalli, is a common species of large marine fish classified within the jack family, Carangidae. It is distributed across the tropical and temperate waters of the Atlantic Ocean, ranging from Nova Scotia, Canada, to Uruguay in the western Atlantic and from Portugal to Angola in the eastern Atlantic, including the Mediterranean Sea.

Description

The crevalle jack is a large fish, growing to a known maximum length of 124 cm and a weight of 32 kg. It has a deep, compressed body, a black spot on the pectoral fin and operculum, and scutes along the rear of the lateral line. Juveniles bear dark vertical bars and inhabit estuaries and shallow coastal waters, while adults move offshore and may form schools.

Ecology

The species is a fast-swimming predator feeding on smaller fish, shrimps and other invertebrates. It inhabits both inshore and offshore waters to depths of around 350 m, frequently entering harbours, bays and river mouths.

Relation to humans

The crevalle jack is of minor commercial importance but is highly regarded by recreational anglers as a powerful game fish taken on lures and flies. Its flesh is considered fair to poor eating, and it is often used as bait or released.
