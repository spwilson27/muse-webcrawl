McTeague

McTeague: A Story of San Francisco
Author: Frank Norris
Published: 1899
Genre: Naturalist novel
Setting: San Francisco, California

McTeague: A Story of San Francisco is a novel by Frank Norris, first published in 1899. A landmark of American literary naturalism, it tells the story of a brutish San Francisco dentist whose life disintegrates under the pressures of greed, jealousy and heredity.

Plot

McTeague, a slow-witted but powerful former miner practising dentistry without a licence, marries Trina Sieppe, the cousin of his best friend Marcus Schouler, who had courted her himself. Trina wins $5,000 in a lottery, and the windfall poisons all three lives: Marcus, consumed by envy, denounces McTeague to the authorities, ending his practice; Trina becomes a pathological miser, hoarding the money while the couple sinks into squalor; McTeague's alcoholism and violence escalate until he murders Trina and flees to the gold country, pursued by Marcus. The novel closes in Death Valley, where McTeague kills Marcus in a struggle but remains handcuffed to his corpse, facing death in the desert.

Themes and reception

Written under the influence of Émile Zola, McTeague applies naturalist determinism to American material: its characters are driven by atavistic instinct, alcohol, the lure of gold and sheer economic force. Contemporary reviewers were shocked by its sordidness, but it is now considered Norris's masterpiece and a founding text of the American naturalist tradition that runs to Dreiser and Steinbeck.

Adaptations

The novel was the source for Erich von Stroheim's legendary 1924 film Greed, originally some nine hours long and cut by the studio to under two and a half, still ranked among the greatest films ever made. It has also been adapted as an opera by William Bolcom (1992).
