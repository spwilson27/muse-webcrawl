The Kunstakademie Düsseldorf is the Academy of Fine Arts of the German city of Düsseldorf, one of Europe's most influential art schools. Founded in 1773 under the Elector Palatine Charles Theodore as an academy of painting, sculpture and architecture, it was reorganised as a Prussian royal academy in 1819.

History

In the nineteenth century the academy gave its name to the Düsseldorf school of painting, a landscape and genre movement that attracted students from Scandinavia and the United States. After the Second World War the academy again became a centre of avant-garde art: Joseph Beuys held a professorship from 1961 until his dismissal in 1972, and his students and contemporaries — including Gerhard Richter, Sigmar Polke, Günther Uecker and the photographers Bernd and Hilla Becher — made it a hub of German postwar art. The Bechers' teaching founded the influential Düsseldorf school of photography, continued by Andreas Gursky, Thomas Ruff and Candida Höfer.

Today

The academy occupies its historic building on Eiskellerstraße, completed in 1879, organises its teaching in free-art classes under individual professors, and is famous for the public Rundgang exhibition held each winter.
