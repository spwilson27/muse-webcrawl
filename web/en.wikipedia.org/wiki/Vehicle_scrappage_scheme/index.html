Vehicle scrappage scheme

A vehicle scrappage scheme (or scrappage program) is a government incentive program designed to promote the replacement of old, polluting vehicles with newer, more fuel-efficient models, usually by offering a cash rebate or discount when the old vehicle is scrapped.

Prominent examples include the United States' 2009 "Cash for Clunkers" program (Car Allowance Rebate System), Germany's Abwrackprämie of 2009, and the United Kingdom's 2009–2010 scrappage scheme, as well as more recent schemes targeting air quality in cities, such as London's ULEZ scrappage schemes.

Evaluation

Supporters credit such schemes with stimulating the auto industry during recessions and reducing emissions. Critics argue benefits are often overstated, since many purchases would have happened anyway, newer-car production carries its own emissions, and destroying serviceable vehicles can raise used-car prices for low-income buyers.
