Excalibur

Excalibur is the legendary sword of King Arthur, sometimes also attributed with magical powers or associated with the rightful sovereignty of Britain. In some versions it is the "sword in the stone" that only the rightful king can draw; in others Arthur receives it from the Lady of the Lake.

Excalibur
Owner: King Arthur
Type: Legendary sword
First appearance: Geoffrey of Monmouth, Historia Regum Britanniae

Etymology and origins

The name derives through Old French from Geoffrey of Monmouth's Latin Caliburnus, likely from Welsh Caledfwlch, appearing in Culhwch and Olwen. Geoffrey described it as forged in Avalon.

Legend

In Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur there are two swords: the sword in the stone proving Arthur's kingship, which breaks, and Excalibur proper, given by the Lady of the Lake, whose scabbard prevents blood loss. On Arthur's death, Sir Bedivere returns it to the lake, where an arm rises to catch it.

In popular culture

Excalibur appears in countless adaptations including John Boorman's film Excalibur (1981), T.H. White's The Once and Future King, and modern fantasy.
