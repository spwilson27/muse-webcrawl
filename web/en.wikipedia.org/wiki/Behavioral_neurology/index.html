Behavioral neurology
FieldNeurology subspecialty
FocusEffects of brain damage and disease on behaviour, memory, and cognition

Behavioral neurology is a subspecialty of neurology that studies the impact of neurological damage and disease upon behavior, memory, and cognition, and the treatment thereof. The field relates closely to neuropsychiatry and neuropsychology, and practitioners use neuropsychological testing, brain imaging, and clinical observation to localise and characterise disorders.

Scope

Conditions commonly addressed include the dementias (such as Alzheimer's disease and frontotemporal dementia), the cognitive and behavioural effects of stroke, traumatic brain injury, epilepsy, movement disorders, and focal lesions causing aphasia, agnosia, apraxia, or amnesia. The case of Phineas Gage and the lesion studies of Paul Broca in the 19th century are landmark early contributions to the discipline.

Practice

Behavioural neurologists typically complete fellowship training after neurology residency; in the United States the subspecialty is certified under Behavioral Neurology and Neuropsychiatry through the United Council for Neurologic Subspecialties. Norman Geschwind is often regarded as a founder of modern behavioural neurology in America.
