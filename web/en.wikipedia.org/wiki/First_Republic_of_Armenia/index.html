The First Republic of Armenia (Armenian: Հայաստանի առաջին հանրապետություն), officially the Republic of Armenia, was the first independent Armenian state since the fall of the medieval Kingdom of Cilicia in 1375. It was proclaimed on 28 May 1918 after the collapse of the Russian Empire, the dissolution of the Transcaucasian Democratic Federative Republic, and the Armenian victories over Ottoman armies at the battles of Sardarapat, Bash Abaran, and Karakilisa, which halted the Turkish advance on Yerevan.

History

The new republic, governed mainly by the Armenian Revolutionary Federation (Dashnaktsutyun) under Prime Ministers including Hovhannes Kajaznuni and Alexander Khatisian, was landlocked, impoverished, and overwhelmed by hundreds of thousands of refugees from the Armenian genocide. It fought border wars with Georgia (1918) and Azerbaijan (1918–1920) over disputed districts, suffered famine and typhus that killed a large share of its population, and received limited Allied and American relief aid.

Diplomatically, Armenia signed the Treaty of Batum with the Ottoman Empire in June 1918, was recognised de facto by the Allies in January 1920, and in August 1920 received, under the Treaty of Sèvres, a much larger territory in eastern Anatolia in an arbitral award by U.S. President Woodrow Wilson ("Wilsonian Armenia"). The award was never implemented: Turkish nationalist forces under Kâzım Karabekir invaded in September 1920 (the Turkish–Armenian War), captured Kars and Alexandropol, and imposed the Treaty of Alexandropol. Simultaneously the Red Army entered from Azerbaijan; on 2 December 1920 the government handed power to a Bolshevik revolutionary committee, and Armenia became a Soviet republic, later part of the USSR. Independence Day, 28 May, is again a national holiday in the modern Republic of Armenia, which declared its sovereignty from the USSR in 1990–1991 and consciously styles itself the successor of the First Republic.
