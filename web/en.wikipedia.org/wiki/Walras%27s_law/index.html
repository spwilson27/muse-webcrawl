Walras's law

Walras's law is a principle in economics, named after the French economist Léon Walras, which states that the total value of excess demand across all markets in an economy must equal zero, taking into account both goods and money. A corollary is that if all but one market are in equilibrium, the remaining market must also be in equilibrium.

Statement

If each agent's budget constraint holds with equality, then summing across agents, the value of aggregate excess demand is identically zero at all price vectors. Hence one market-clearing equation in a general equilibrium system is redundant: if there are n markets, only n − 1 independent equilibrium conditions exist.

Implications

Walras's law implies that an excess supply in one market must be matched by an excess demand elsewhere. It is frequently invoked in debates about Say's law and about whether a "general glut" of goods is possible within general equilibrium theory.
