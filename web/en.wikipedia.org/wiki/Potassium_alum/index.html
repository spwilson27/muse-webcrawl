Potassium alum

Potassium alum, potash alum, or potassium aluminium sulfate is a chemical compound: the double sulfate of potassium and aluminium, with chemical formula KAl(SO4)2. It is commonly encountered as the dodecahydrate, KAl(SO4)2·12H2O. It crystallizes in an octahedral structure and is the most important member of the class of compounds called alums.

Production and occurrence

Potassium alum occurs naturally as the mineral alum-(K) and was historically obtained from alunite and from alum shales. Today it is produced by adding potassium sulfate to a concentrated solution of aluminium sulfate, itself derived from bauxite or clay treated with sulfuric acid.

Uses

Alum has been used since antiquity as a mordant in dyeing, in leather tanning, and as a flocculant in water purification. It is used in styptics to stop minor bleeding, as an aftershave and natural deodorant crystal, in baking powder as an acidulant, in pickling to keep vegetables crisp, and in fire retardants. In medicine it served historically as an astringent and emetic.

Properties

The dodecahydrate forms colourless octahedral crystals with a sweetish, astringent taste. It is soluble in water and insoluble in ethanol. On heating it liquefies in its water of crystallization and, at higher temperatures, gives the anhydrous salt known as "burnt alum".
