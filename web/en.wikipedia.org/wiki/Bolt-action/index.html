Bolt action

Bolt action is a type of manual firearm action that is operated by directly manipulating the bolt via a bolt handle placed on the right-hand side of the weapon (most often) or the left-hand side. The bolt handle is lifted up and pulled to the rear to open the chamber, ejecting the spent cartridge case, and is then pushed forward and down to chamber a fresh round, cock the firing mechanism, and lock the bolt in place.

Most bolt-action firearms are rifles, and the design dominated military small arms from the late 19th century until the mid-20th century, when it was largely superseded by semi-automatic and automatic designs. Famous military bolt-action rifles include the German Mauser Gewehr 98, the British Lee-Enfield, the Soviet Mosin–Nagant, and the American Springfield M1903.

History

The first bolt-action rifle to enter military service was the Prussian Dreyse needle gun of 1841, which used a rotating bolt to seal the breech. Subsequent designs by Chassepot, Mauser and others refined the mechanism, and the introduction of smokeless powder and magazine feed in the 1880s and 1890s produced the classic military bolt-action rifles used in both World Wars.

Modern use

Today bolt actions remain common in precision shooting and hunting because the rigid, locked breech and lack of moving parts during firing favour accuracy. Sniper rifles such as the Accuracy International Arctic Warfare and the Remington 700 series use bolt actions, as do most big-game hunting rifles.
