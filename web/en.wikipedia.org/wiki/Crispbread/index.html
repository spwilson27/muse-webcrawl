Crispbread

Crispbread (Swedish: knäckebröd) is a flat, dry type of cracker, containing mostly rye flour. It originated in Scandinavia, where it has been baked for centuries as a storable staple food, and is light, keeps for long periods and is typically high in dietary fibre.

History

Crispbread has been baked in central Sweden since the late 6th century. Traditional crispbreads were baked twice a year and stored on drying poles; a central hole allowed the rounds to be threaded onto a pole above the oven. Finland and Sweden have among the highest per-capita consumption in the world.

Production and varieties

Modern crispbread is made from whole-grain rye, wheat or multigrain doughs, sometimes leavened with yeast or sourdough and topped with seeds. Major producers include Wasa, founded in Sweden in 1919. Crispbread is commonly eaten with butter, cheese, caviar paste or other spreads.
