Order dimension

Field: Order theory, combinatorics

Introduced by: Dushnik and Miller (1941)

Also called: Poset dimension

In mathematics, the dimension of a partially ordered set (poset) is the smallest number of total orders whose intersection is the given partial order. This concept of order dimension was introduced by Ben Dushnik and E. W. Miller in 1941 and measures how far a partial order is from being a ranking: a total order has dimension 1, and any partial order that is not total has dimension at least 2.

Definition

A realizer of a poset P is a family of linear extensions (total orders consistent with P) such that two elements are comparable in P exactly when they appear in the same relative order in every linear extension in the family. The dimension of P is the minimum size of a realizer.

Examples

The standard example Sn, consisting of n minimal elements and n maximal elements with each minimal element below all but one maximal element, has dimension exactly n, showing that dimension is unbounded even for posets of height two. The Boolean lattice of subsets of an n-element set has dimension n.

Properties and complexity

Every poset with n elements has dimension at most n/2 (Hiraguchi's theorem). Deciding whether a poset has dimension at most k is polynomial for k = 2 but NP-complete for every fixed k ≥ 3 (Yannakakis, 1982). Dimension is related to, but distinct from, the interval dimension and boxicity of graphs.

Applications

Dimension arises in scheduling, preference aggregation and the analysis of concept lattices, wherever several independent rankings must be reconciled into a single partial order.
