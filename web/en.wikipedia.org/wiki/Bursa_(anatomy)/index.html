Bursa (anatomy)

A bursa (plural bursae or bursas) is a small fluid-filled sac lined by synovial membrane with an inner capillary layer of viscous synovial fluid, similar in composition to egg white. It provides a cushion between bones and tendons and/or muscles around a joint. This helps to reduce friction between the bones and allows free movement. Bursae are filled with synovial fluid and are found around most major joints of the body.

Structure

There are four types of bursa: adventitious, subcutaneous, synovial, and sub-muscular. Among these, only adventitious bursae are non-native. When any surface of the body is subjected to repeated stress, an adventitious bursa develops under it. Examples are Students' elbow and bunion. Inflammation of a bursa is known as bursitis.

Synovial bursae are thin-walled sacs interposed between tissues such as tendons, muscles, and bones, facilitating movement by reducing friction. They are lined by synovial membrane and contain a thin film of synovial fluid.

Clinical significance

Bursitis

Bursitis is the inflammation of one or more bursae of synovial fluid in the body. They are lined with a synovial membrane that secretes a lubricating synovial fluid. Symptoms of bursitis include pain, swelling, and tenderness around the affected joint. The most common locations are the shoulder, elbow, hip, and knee.

Chronic bursitis can occur from repetitive motion or sustained pressure. Acute bursitis may follow trauma, infection, or crystal deposition diseases such as gout. Treatment typically involves rest, ice, and anti-inflammatory medication; infected bursae may require antibiotics or surgical drainage.

History

The term bursa derives from the Medieval Latin for "purse", reflecting the sac-like shape of the structure.
