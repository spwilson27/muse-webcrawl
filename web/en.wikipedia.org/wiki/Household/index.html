Household

A household consists of one or more persons who live in the same dwelling and share meals or living accommodation. It may consist of a single family, one individual living alone, or any other group of people. The household is a basic unit of analysis in many social, microeconomic and governmental statistical models, and is closely related to, though distinct from, the concept of family.

Definitions

Statistical agencies define the household in terms of shared residence and housekeeping arrangements. The United States Census Bureau defines a household as all persons who occupy a housing unit, whether related or not. In the European Union, a household is defined by the sharing of household expenses and residence. A single person living alone forms a one-person household; groups sharing a dwelling without sharing other arrangements may form multi-person non-family households.

Types and trends

Households are commonly classified as one-person, single-parent, couple-without-children, couple-with-children and multi-family households. In many developed countries average household size has fallen over the past century, to around two or fewer persons in parts of northern Europe, while the share of one-person households has risen sharply — exceeding a quarter of all households in the United States. In much of the developing world, extended-family households with several generations under one roof remain common.

Household economics

In economics, households are treated as units of consumption and of labour supply, pooling income and making joint decisions about spending, saving, housing and child-rearing. Household production, including cooking, cleaning and care work, is a major component of economic activity not captured in GDP. Census data on households underpin measures of poverty, housing demand and energy use.
