COBIT

Full name: Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies
Publisher: ISACA
First published: 1996
Latest version: COBIT 2019

COBIT (Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies) is a framework created by ISACA for information technology (IT) management and IT governance. The framework defines a set of generic processes for the management and governance of IT, providing an implementable set of controls over information technology, organised into governance and management objectives.

History

COBIT was first released in 1996, initially as an audit framework for the financial sector. Successive editions broadened its scope: COBIT 3 (2000) added management guidelines; COBIT 4/4.1 (2005/2007) aligned it more closely with governance; COBIT 5 (2012) integrated ISACA's other frameworks, including Val IT and Risk IT, and introduced enabling-process models based on ISO/IEC 15504. COBIT 2019 introduced design factors and a performance management system, and structured the framework around 40 governance and management objectives.

Structure

The framework distinguishes governance (directed by the board, covering evaluate, direct and monitor — EDM — objectives) from management (organised into the domains Align, Plan and Organize (APO); Build, Acquire and Implement (BAI); Deliver, Service and Support (DSS); and Monitor, Evaluate and Assess (MEA)). Each objective includes purpose statements, practice descriptions, capability levels and suggested metrics. COBIT is widely used to help organisations comply with requirements such as those of the Sarbanes–Oxley Act, and it is mapped to complementary standards and frameworks including ITIL, ISO/IEC 27001 and TOGAF.
