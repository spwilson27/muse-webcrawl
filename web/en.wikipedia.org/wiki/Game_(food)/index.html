Game (food)

Game or quarry is any animal hunted for its meat or for sport. The term encompasses wild mammals and birds that are pursued under hunting regulations, as distinct from domesticated livestock. Common game species include deer, rabbit, hare, pheasant, partridge, grouse, wild boar, duck and goose. Game meat is often leaner and more strongly flavored than farmed meat.

Types

In British usage, game is traditionally divided into game birds (such as pheasant, grouse and partridge), ground game (rabbits and hares), and deer. Many jurisdictions legally define "game" species and set open seasons, bag limits and licensing requirements for hunting them. Large species such as moose, elk and bear are called big game, while smaller animals are small game.

Preparation

Game is traditionally hung for a period after killing to tenderize the meat and develop flavor — pheasants and grouse were classically hung until quite high. Because game meat is lean, it is often barded with fat or marinated before roasting, braising or stewing, and features in dishes such as jugged hare and game pie.

Regulation

Hunting of game is regulated in most countries to conserve wildlife populations. In the United Kingdom, game seasons date in part from the Game Act 1831; in the United States, state agencies manage seasons and licenses, with migratory birds additionally regulated federally.
