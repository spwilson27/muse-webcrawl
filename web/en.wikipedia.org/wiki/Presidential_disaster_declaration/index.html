Presidential disaster declaration

A presidential disaster declaration is a formal statement by the President of the United States that a major disaster or emergency exists, issued under the Robert T. Stafford Disaster Relief and Emergency Assistance Act. Such a declaration makes federal assistance available to state, tribal, and local governments and to certain private nonprofit organizations, and in the case of a major disaster declaration, to individuals and households in the affected area.

Process

Before a declaration is issued, the governor of the affected state (or the chief executive of a tribal government) must request it, certifying that the disaster is of such severity and magnitude that an effective response is beyond the capabilities of the state and that federal assistance is necessary. The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) conducts a preliminary damage assessment jointly with state officials and makes a recommendation to the President, who has sole discretion to approve or deny the request.

Types of declarations

The Stafford Act provides for two main kinds of declarations. An emergency declaration supplements state and local efforts in an emergency involving federal primary responsibility or where assistance is needed to save lives and protect property. A major disaster declaration follows a natural catastrophe — such as a hurricane, tornado, flood, earthquake, or wildfire — or, under certain conditions, an explosion or fire, and unlocks a broader range of federal assistance programs, including the Individual Assistance and Public Assistance programs and hazard mitigation grants.

Assistance programs

Public Assistance reimburses eligible governments and nonprofits for debris removal, emergency protective measures, and repair of public infrastructure, generally at a federal cost share of at least 75 percent. Individual Assistance can include housing assistance, crisis counseling, unemployment assistance, and low-interest disaster loans administered by the Small Business Administration.
