Isosafrole is an organic compound of the phenylpropene class, closely related to safrole, from which it is produced by isomerisation of the allyl side chain to a propenyl group. It occurs as a mixture of cis and trans isomers and has a sweet, anise-like odour.

Production and uses

Isosafrole is manufactured by heating safrole with an alcoholic potassium hydroxide solution, which migrates the double bond into conjugation with the aromatic ring. Its principal legitimate use has been in the fragrance industry: oxidation of isosafrole yields piperonal (heliotropin), an important perfume ingredient, and hydrogenation gives dihydrosafrole. It was also used directly in soaps and fragrances.

Regulation

Because isosafrole can be converted through intermediates into the controlled drug MDMA, it is listed as a Table I precursor under the 1988 UN Convention Against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and as a List I chemical by the US Drug Enforcement Administration. Transactions in the compound are therefore subject to record-keeping and reporting requirements in many jurisdictions. Its parent compound safrole is additionally restricted as a suspected carcinogen following studies in rodents.
