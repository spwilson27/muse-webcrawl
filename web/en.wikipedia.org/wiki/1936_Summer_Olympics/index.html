The 1936 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XI Olympiad, were held from 1 to 16 August 1936 in Berlin, Germany. Awarded to Berlin before the Nazi Party came to power, the Games were exploited by Adolf Hitler's regime as a showcase for the supposed superiority of the "Aryan race" and the strength of the new Germany, despite international calls for a boycott over the regime's racial policies.

Jesse Owens

The outstanding athlete of the Games was the African-American sprinter and long jumper Jesse Owens, who won four gold medals — in the 100 metres, 200 metres, long jump, and 4 × 100 metres relay — directly contradicting Nazi racial ideology before enormous German crowds. German long jumper Luz Long, who befriended Owens during the competition, took silver in the long jump.

Propaganda and production

The Berlin Games pioneered the Olympic torch relay, introduced for these Games, and were the first to be televised, being broadcast to local viewing halls. Filmmaker Leni Riefenstahl's documentary Olympia (1938), commissioned by the German Olympic Committee, remains one of the most famous and controversial sports films ever made. The purpose-built Olympiastadion, designed by Werner March, seated about 100,000 spectators.

Medal table

Germany topped the medal table for the first time, with 33 gold medals, ahead of the United States with 24. The Games were the last held before the Second World War caused the cancellation of the 1940 and 1944 editions.
