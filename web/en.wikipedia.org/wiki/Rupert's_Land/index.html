Rupert's Land

Rupert's Land was a vast territory in British North America comprising the drainage basin of Hudson Bay. It was granted in 1670 by King Charles II of England to the Hudson's Bay Company, whose first governor was Prince Rupert of the Rhine, the king's cousin, after whom the land was named.

Extent

The charter granted the Company a monopoly on trade and effective governmental authority over all lands whose rivers drained into Hudson Bay — some 3.9 million square kilometres, covering much of present-day central and northern Canada, from Labrador to the Rockies and south into the northern United States. In practice, Company power was exercised through fur-trading posts such as York Factory.

Transfer to Canada

In 1868 the British Parliament passed the Rupert's Land Act, enabling the surrender of the Company's rights. The Deed of Surrender followed in 1869, and on 15 July 1870 the territory was transferred to the Dominion of Canada, which paid the Company £300,000 and granted it land around its posts. The transfer, made without consulting Indigenous peoples or the Métis settlers of the Red River Colony, helped provoke the Red River Resistance led by Louis Riel, and led to the creation of the province of Manitoba.
