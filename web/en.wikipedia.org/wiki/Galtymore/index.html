Galtymore

Galtymore (Irish: Cnoc Mór na nGaibhlte, "big hill of the Galtees") is a mountain in Ireland, rising to 917.9 metres (3,011 ft). It is the highest point of the Galtee Mountains, the tallest inland mountain range in Ireland, and stands on the border between County Tipperary and County Limerick in the province of Munster.

Geography

The Galtees form a high east–west ridge of Old Red Sandstone south of the Glen of Aherlow. Galtymore's summit is a broad plateau marked by a cairn and a white-painted cross, with views in clear weather extending over the plains of Tipperary to the Shannon. The mountain is classed as a Marilyn and is the county top of both Tipperary and Limerick; it counts among the thirteen Irish peaks over 3,000 feet sometimes called the Irish "Furths" grouping with the high peaks of Scotland and Wales.

Climbing

The most popular ascent starts from the car park near Skeheenarinky on the R639 and follows the Black Road track before striking up open hillside past the corrie lake Lough Curra below the summit cliffs. A longer route approaches from King's Yard in the Glen of Aherlow. The mountain forms the centrepiece of the annual Galtee Challenge walk.

Name

The Galtee name derives from the Irish Sléibhte na gCoillte, "mountains of the woods", a reference to the forests that once clothed their slopes.
