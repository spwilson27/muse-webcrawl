Polynomial ring

In mathematics, especially in the field of algebra, a polynomial ring or polynomial algebra is a ring (which is also a commutative algebra) formed from the set of polynomials in one or more indeterminates (traditionally also called variables) with coefficients in another ring, often a field.

Definition

The polynomial ring K[X] over a field (or more generally a commutative ring) K consists of all expressions anXn + ... + a1X + a0 with ai in K, together with term-wise addition and multiplication defined by the distributive law and the rule Xm·Xn = Xm+n.

Properties

Polynomial rings over a field are Euclidean domains, hence principal ideal domains and unique factorisation domains. Hilbert's basis theorem states that polynomial rings in finitely many variables over a Noetherian ring are Noetherian. Polynomial rings are central to algebraic geometry, where they serve as coordinate rings of algebraic varieties, and to invariant theory and commutative algebra more broadly.

Multivariate case

Polynomials in several indeterminates, K[X1, ..., Xn], can be built by iterating the single-variable construction. Gröbner bases provide an algorithmic theory for ideals in such rings.
