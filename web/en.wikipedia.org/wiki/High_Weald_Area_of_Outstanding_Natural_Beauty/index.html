High Weald Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty

The High Weald Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty (now also styled a National Landscape) covers 1,461 km² across Kent, East and West Sussex and Surrey in south-east England. Designated in 1983, it preserves a medieval landscape of rolling sandstone ridges, small irregular fields, ancient woodland, sunken lanes and dispersed farmsteads.

Landscape and history

The High Weald's pattern of assart fields cleared from the great Wealden forest, its hammer ponds from the Tudor iron industry, and villages such as Burwash and Robertsbridge give it a character little changed since the 14th century. Ashdown Forest lies at its heart.
