Silas Deane

Born1738, Groton, Connecticut
Died1789, at sea
OccupationMerchant, politician, diplomat

Silas Deane (1738–1789) was an American merchant, politician and diplomat, and a delegate to the Continental Congress. In 1776 he was sent secretly to France to procure arms and supplies for the American cause in the Revolutionary War.

Mission to France

Working with the playwright Pierre Beaumarchais and his front company, Roderigue Hortalez et Cie, Deane arranged shipments of French arms, ammunition and equipment that reached the Continental Army. He later served, alongside Benjamin Franklin and Arthur Lee, as a commissioner to negotiate the Franco-American alliance of 1778.

Recall and controversy

Feuds with Arthur Lee led to Deane's recall in 1778 amid accusations about his accounts, which were never fully settled. He returned to Europe, and after intercepted letters critical of Congress became public he was branded a traitor by some; he died in 1789 while preparing to return to America. Congress posthumously compensated his estate in 1842 after a review vindicated his accounts.
