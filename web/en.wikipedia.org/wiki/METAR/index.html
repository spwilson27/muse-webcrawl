METAR

METAR
Full nameMeteorological Aerodrome Report
PurposeSurface weather observation for aviation
IssuedHourly or half-hourly; SPECI when conditions change
ElementsWind, visibility, weather, cloud, temperature, dew point, pressure

A METAR is a format for reporting weather information used primarily by pilots in fulfilment of a part of a pre-flight weather briefing, and by meteorologists, who use aggregated METAR information to assist in weather forecasting. The name comes from the French message d'observation météorologique régulière pour l'aviation.

Format

A METAR report is a compact coded string. It begins with the station's ICAO airport code, followed by the date and time of the observation in UTC, then groups reporting wind direction and speed, visibility, runway visual range where relevant, present weather (such as rain, snow, fog, or thunderstorms), cloud amount and height, air temperature and dew point in degrees Celsius, and the altimeter setting or QNH pressure.

An example is METAR EGLL 291120Z 25012KT 9999 FEW045 15/07 Q1013, indicating London Heathrow reporting a westerly wind of 12 knots, visibility of 10 km or more, few clouds at 4,500 feet, a temperature of 15 °C, a dew point of 7 °C, and a QNH of 1013 hPa.

Special reports

Routine METARs are supplemented by SPECI reports whenever specified criteria for significant change are met between scheduled observations, such as abrupt shifts in wind, visibility, or the onset of thunderstorms.
