Perry Nuclear Generating Station

Perry Nuclear Generating Station

Location: Perry, Ohio, United States

Operator: Vistra (formerly FirstEnergy/Energy Harbor)

Reactor type: Boiling water reactor (BWR-6)

Output: about 1,256 MW

Commissioned: 1987

The Perry Nuclear Generating Station is a nuclear power plant on the shore of Lake Erie near Perry, Ohio, about 40 miles northeast of Cleveland. Its single General Electric BWR-6 boiling water reactor, housed in a Mark III containment, generates roughly 1,256 megawatts of electricity.

History

Construction began in 1974 under the Cleveland Electric Illuminating Company consortium (CAPCO). A second unit was started but cancelled in 1985 as costs soared; the completed Unit 1 entered commercial service in November 1987, having cost about $6 billion, among the most expensive plants of its era. Its operating licence was renewed by the Nuclear Regulatory Commission to 2046. Ownership has passed from FirstEnergy to its subsidiary Energy Harbor and, in 2024, to Vistra.

Safety record

The plant has experienced operating events typical of the US fleet; in 2016 the NRC gave it heightened oversight after a series of findings, from which it later recovered. Like other Great Lakes plants it uses lake water for cooling and has been discussed in debates over Ohio's nuclear subsidies, including the controversial House Bill 6 of 2019.
