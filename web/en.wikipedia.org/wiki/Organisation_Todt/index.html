Organisation Todt

Abbreviation: OT

Formed: 1938

Dissolved: 1945

Founders/Leaders: Fritz Todt; Albert Speer (from 1942)

Headquarters: Berlin, Nazi Germany

Organisation Todt (OT) was a civil and military engineering organisation in Nazi Germany, named after its founder Fritz Todt, responsible for a vast range of construction projects in Germany and in the territories occupied during the Second World War. It built motorways, fortifications, submarine pens and armaments infrastructure, relying heavily on conscripted, forced and slave labour, including concentration camp prisoners; by late 1944 it controlled roughly 1.4 million workers.

History

Todt, as Inspector General for German Roadways, had built the Reichsautobahnen during the 1930s. In 1938 he founded the OT to construct the Westwall (Siegfried Line) defences opposite France's Maginot Line, mobilising hundreds of thousands of workers in a quasi-military structure of uniformed staff. After the conquest of Western Europe the OT followed the Wehrmacht across the continent and into the Soviet Union, building the Atlantic Wall coastal fortifications, U-boat pens at Lorient and other bases, airfields, and rocket sites such as those associated with the V-weapons. When Todt died in a plane crash in February 1942, Albert Speer succeeded him as Minister of Armaments and head of the OT.

Labour and crimes

The OT's workforce evolved from German conscripts to foreign civilian conscripts, prisoners of war, and concentration camp inmates supplied by the SS. Conditions for forced labourers, particularly Eastern Europeans, Jews, and camp prisoners, were frequently lethal; the organisation's projects consumed workers through exhaustion, starvation and brutality, and OT officials participated in the machinery of Nazi exploitation. After the war, its role was examined at Nuremberg in connection with the slave labour programme administered by Fritz Sauckel and Speer's ministry, though the OT itself was not declared a criminal organisation.
