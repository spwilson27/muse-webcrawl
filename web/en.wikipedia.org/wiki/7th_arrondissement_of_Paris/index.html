The 7th arrondissement of Paris (VIIe arrondissement) is one of the twenty arrondissements of the French capital, located on the Left Bank (Rive Gauche) of the Seine. Known as the arrondissement du Palais-Bourbon, it is among the city's most prestigious and affluent districts, containing many of France's national institutions and a large concentration of museums and ministries.

Landmarks

The arrondissement contains the Eiffel Tower and the Champ de Mars, Les Invalides (with the tomb of Napoleon), the Musée d'Orsay, the Rodin Museum, the National Assembly at the Palais Bourbon, and the Hôtel Matignon, the prime minister's residence. The Faubourg Saint-Germain quarter, with its aristocratic hôtels particuliers, many now embassies and ministries, gives the district its stately character.

Character

Largely built up in the 17th–19th centuries as the aristocracy moved west from the Marais, the 7th combines monumental public architecture with quiet, expensive residential streets. Its population is roughly 50,000, among the smallest of the central arrondissements, with some of the highest property prices in Paris.
