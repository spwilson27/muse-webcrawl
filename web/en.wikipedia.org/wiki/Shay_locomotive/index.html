Shay locomotive

The Shay locomotive is a geared steam locomotive invented by Ephraim Shay and built principally by the Lima Locomotive Works of Lima, Ohio, from the 1880s to 1945. About 2,770 were constructed. Shays were the most widely used of the geared locomotive types, which also include the Climax and Heisler designs.

Design

A Shay has a conventional boiler offset to the left of the frame and a vertical steam engine of two or three cylinders mounted on the right side. The engine drives a longitudinal line shaft through universal joints, and bevel gears transmit power to every wheel — including the wheels of the tender on many examples — via swiveling trucks. Because all wheels are driven and geared down, a Shay delivers very high tractive effort at low speed.

Use

Geared locomotives were built for logging, mining, and industrial railroads, where steep grades, sharp curves, light rail, and poorly built track made conventional rod locomotives impractical. Shays were slow — rarely exceeding about 20 km/h — but could climb grades of 10 percent or more and haul heavy loads of logs from mountain forests. Many survive in preservation, operating on tourist lines such as the Cass Scenic Railroad in West Virginia and the Roaring Camp Railroads in California.
