69 Stations of the Nakasendō

The 69 Stations of the Nakasendō (中山道六十九次, Nakasendō Rokujūkyū-tsugi) are the rest areas along the Nakasendō, one of the Edo Five Routes — the major highways of Edo-period Japan. The Nakasendō ran through the interior mountains between Edo (modern Tokyo) and Kyoto, in contrast to the coastal Tōkaidō, which had 53 stations.

Post stations

Each post station (shukuba) provided lodging, food, porters, and horses for travelers, including the daimyō processions traveling to and from Edo under the sankin-kōtai system. Counting the starting point at Nihonbashi in Edo and the terminus at Kusatsu, where the Nakasendō joined the Tōkaidō, the route had 69 stations spread across the modern prefectures of Tokyo, Saitama, Gunma, Nagano, Gifu, and Shiga.

In art

The stations are famous as the subject of a series of woodblock prints begun by Keisai Eisen and completed by Utagawa Hiroshige in the 1830s. Well-preserved stations such as Magome, Tsumago, and Narai in the Kiso Valley remain popular destinations for walking the historic road.
