China Construction Bank

China Construction Bank Corporation (CCB) is one of the "big four" state-owned commercial banks in China and one of the largest banks in the world by total assets and market capitalisation. Founded in 1954 as the People's Construction Bank of China, it is headquartered in Beijing and listed on the Hong Kong and Shanghai stock exchanges.

History

CCB was established in 1954 under the Ministry of Finance to administer funds for infrastructure and construction projects. For decades it functioned as a fiscal agent of the state rather than a true commercial bank. In 1994, as part of China's banking reforms, its policy-lending functions were transferred to the newly created China Development Bank, and in 1996 it adopted the name China Construction Bank. It restructured as a joint-stock company in 2004 and completed a major initial public offering in Hong Kong in 2005 — the largest IPO in the world that year — followed by a Shanghai listing in 2007.

Operations

The bank provides personal, corporate and treasury services, with particular strength in infrastructure lending, mortgage lending and construction-related finance. It operates roughly 15,000 branches in mainland China and maintains overseas branches and subsidiaries in major financial centres including Hong Kong, New York, London, Frankfurt, Sydney and Johannesburg. Its customer base numbers in the hundreds of millions.

Significance

Together with ICBC, Agricultural Bank of China and Bank of China, CCB forms China's "big four" banking group. It has consistently ranked among the top tier of the Financial Stability Board's list of global systemically important banks.
