Robert Hugh Benson

Robert Hugh Benson
Born: 18 November 1871, Wellington College, Berkshire, England
Died: 19 October 1914, Salford, England
Occupation: Anglican then Roman Catholic priest, novelist
Notable work: Lord of the World (1907)
Father: Edward White Benson, Archbishop of Canterbury

Robert Hugh Benson (18 November 1871 – 19 October 1914) was an English Catholic priest and novelist. The youngest son of Edward White Benson, Archbishop of Canterbury, he was first ordained an Anglican priest before converting to Roman Catholicism in 1903 — a sensation at the time — and being ordained a Catholic priest in 1904. He became a well-known preacher, lecturer, and prolific author of religious and historical fiction.

Writings

Benson is best remembered for Lord of the World (1907), an apocalyptic dystopian novel imagining a future world of secular humanism under the Antichrist; it has been cited with praise by Popes Benedict XVI and Francis and is often compared with later dystopias by Aldous Huxley and George Orwell. His other works include the companion novel The Dawn of All (1911), historical novels about the English Reformation such as By What Authority?, Come Rack! Come Rope!, and The King's Achievement, the ghost-story collection The Light Invisible, and numerous devotional works. He also wrote contemporary "novels of modern life" addressing social questions.

Life and ministry

Educated at Eton and Trinity College, Cambridge, Benson served as a curate and then in the Anglican Community of the Resurrection at Mirfield before his conversion. As a Catholic he worked in Cambridge, where he was chaplain to Catholic undergraduates, and from 1911 lived at Hare Street House in Hertfordshire. Pope Pius X made him a chamberlain in 1911 and a monsignor in 1912. He died of pneumonia in 1914, aged 42, while on a preaching mission in Salford.
