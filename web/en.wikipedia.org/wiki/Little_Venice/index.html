Little Venice

Narrowboats at the canal junction in Little Venice, Maida Vale.

Little Venice is a district of London, England, in the City of Westminster, centred on the junction of the Regent's Canal and the Grand Union Canal (Paddington Arm) near Maida Vale. The area is known for its canalside streets of white stucco Regency houses, its moored houseboats, and a wide triangular pool where the two canals meet.

History and description

The canals were built in the early nineteenth century, and the surrounding streets were developed as part of Paddington and Maida Vale. The name "Little Venice" is said to have been coined in jest by the poet Robert Browning, who lived nearby, though the attribution is disputed — some credit Lord Byron. The name became established in the twentieth century and is now used by estate agents and the local council for the surrounding neighbourhood.

Attractions

The pool at the junction, Browning's Pool, is lined with narrowboats, including a floating café and theatre boats. The Puppet Theatre Barge and the Canal Café Theatre are long-standing local institutions. Waterbus services run along the Regent's Canal from Little Venice to London Zoo and Camden Lock. The annual Canalway Cavalcade festival, held over the May Day bank holiday weekend since 1983, draws hundreds of boats to the area.
