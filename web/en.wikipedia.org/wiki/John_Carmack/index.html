John Carmack

Born: John D. Carmack II, 21 August 1970, Kansas City, Missouri, U.S.

Occupation: Computer programmer, video game developer, engineer

Known for: Co-founding id Software; Wolfenstein 3D, Doom, Quake

John D. Carmack II (born August 21, 1970) is an American computer programmer and video game developer. He co-founded the company id Software and was the lead programmer of its landmark first-person shooters Wolfenstein 3D (1992), Doom (1993) and Quake (1996).

Work

Carmack pioneered or popularised numerous techniques in 3D computer graphics, including adaptive tile refresh for side-scrolling in Commander Keen, binary space partitioning in Doom, and surface caching in Quake. The fast inverse square root algorithm in the Quake III source code became famous in its own right. His engines were widely licensed and helped establish the first-person shooter genre and PC gaming.

Later ventures

Carmack also founded Armadillo Aerospace, which competed in rocketry challenges. In 2013 he left id Software to become chief technology officer of Oculus VR, playing a central role in the development of modern consumer virtual reality, before stepping down in 2019. In 2022 he founded Keen Technologies to pursue artificial general intelligence research. He received the BAFTA Fellowship in 2016.
