Acidosis is the condition of increased acidity in the blood and other body tissues, conventionally an arterial blood pH below 7.35. It arises either from an increased production or decreased excretion of acid (metabolic acidosis) or from a build-up of carbon dioxide due to inadequate ventilation (respiratory acidosis). The opposite condition is alkalosis. Severe acidosis can depress the central nervous system, impair cardiac contractility, and be life-threatening.

Metabolic acidosis

Metabolic acidosis results from the accumulation of acid or the loss of bicarbonate. The anion gap helps classify causes: a high anion gap suggests lactic acidosis, ketoacidosis (as in uncontrolled diabetes), renal failure, or toxins such as methanol, ethylene glycol, and salicylates; a normal anion gap occurs with diarrhoea or renal tubular acidosis. Compensation occurs through hyperventilation (Kussmaul breathing) to lower carbon dioxide.

Respiratory acidosis

Respiratory acidosis occurs when alveolar ventilation fails and carbon dioxide accumulates, as in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, severe asthma, drug-induced respiratory depression, or neuromuscular weakness. Chronic cases are partly compensated by renal retention of bicarbonate.

Signs and diagnosis

Symptoms can include deep rapid breathing, fatigue, headache, confusion, and, in severe cases, shock. Diagnosis rests on arterial blood gas measurement together with serum electrolytes, bicarbonate, and the calculated anion gap; the underlying cause must be identified and treated, and sodium bicarbonate is given only in selected severe cases.
