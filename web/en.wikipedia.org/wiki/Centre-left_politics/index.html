Centre-left politics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Centre-left politics is a political position within the left–right spectrum between the centre and the left. Centre-left parties and movements advocate a mixed economy with a substantial welfare state, regulated markets, progressive taxation and social reform within a capitalist and democratic framework. They are distinguished from the far left by accepting private property and markets in principle, and from the centre by prioritising equality and public provision.

The principal centre-left families are social-democratic and labour parties (the British Labour Party, the German SPD, Scandinavian social democrats), together with some progressive liberal and green parties. Their historic achievements include welfare states, public health and education systems, labour rights and the Nordic model in Scandinavia. Since the 1990s many centre-left parties adopted Third Way programmes moderating their economic positions, a shift debated ever since between modernisers and traditionalists, and challenged by populist movements of left and right.
