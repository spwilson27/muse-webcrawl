Engelbrekt rebellion

Date1434–1436
LocationSweden
LeaderEngelbrekt Engelbrektsson

The Engelbrekt rebellion was a popular uprising in Sweden in 1434–1436 against Eric of Pomerania, king of the Kalmar Union. Led by the mine-owner and nobleman Engelbrekt Engelbrektsson, the revolt began in the mining district of Dalarna and Bergslagen, driven by grievances over heavy taxation, Danish bailiffs, and disruption of the iron trade.

Course

Rebel forces captured royal castles across much of Sweden, and Engelbrekt was recognised as commander of the realm by a council of the aristocracy in 1435. A meeting at Arboga in 1435, to which peasants were summoned, is sometimes described as Sweden's first Riksdag. The rebellion split when the high nobility, led by Karl Knutsson, moved to take control; Engelbrekt was murdered in 1436 by a member of the nobility.

Legacy

Although Eric was temporarily restored before his final deposition in 1439, the rebellion weakened the Kalmar Union and made Engelbrekt a Swedish national hero, celebrated especially in the 19th century.
