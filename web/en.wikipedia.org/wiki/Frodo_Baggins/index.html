Frodo Baggins

Frodo Baggins is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's writings, and one of the protagonists in The Lord of the Rings. Frodo is a hobbit of the Shire who inherits the One Ring from his cousin (commonly called uncle) Bilbo Baggins and undertakes the quest to destroy it in the fires of Mount Doom in Mordor.

Frodo is introduced in The Fellowship of the Ring as the adopted heir of Bilbo Baggins. After learning from the wizard Gandalf that Bilbo's magic ring is the One Ring forged by the Dark Lord Sauron, Frodo flees the Shire with his gardener Samwise Gamgee and his friends Merry Brandybuck and Pippin Took.

Appearances

At the Council of Elrond in Rivendell, Frodo volunteers to carry the Ring to Mordor. He travels with the Fellowship of the Ring; after its breaking at Amon Hen, he continues with Sam alone, guided — and eventually betrayed — by Gollum. Captured briefly in Cirith Ungol after being stung by the giant spider Shelob, Frodo is rescued by Sam. At the Cracks of Doom he finally succumbs to the Ring's power and claims it for himself; the Ring is destroyed only when Gollum bites it from his hand and falls into the fire.

Aftermath

Wounded in body and spirit — by the Witch-king's Morgul blade, Shelob's sting, and the burden of the Ring — Frodo finds no peace in the Shire. In T.A. 3021 he sails West over the Sea with Bilbo, Gandalf and the Elves to the Undying Lands, an honour granted to him as a Ring-bearer. He is also the principal author of the later parts of the Red Book of Westmarch.

Character and analysis

Tolkien described Frodo as a quiet, gentle hobbit whose heroism lies in endurance and pity rather than martial prowess. Scholars have noted his role as a Christ-like figure in suffering, his failed renunciation of the Ring, and the depiction of his lasting trauma as reflecting Tolkien's experience of war.
