Louis II de la Trémoille (20 September 1460 – 24 February 1525), also known as La Trimouille, was a French general and nobleman, viscount of Thouars and prince of Talmond. He served three French kings — Charles VIII, Louis XII and Francis I — and was one of the most distinguished commanders of the Italian Wars. Contemporaries called him the chevalier sans reproche ("knight without reproach").

Early career

Born at Thouars into the powerful La Trémoille family, he entered royal service at a young age. In 1488 he commanded the royal army that defeated the Breton and rebel forces at the Battle of Saint-Aubin-du-Cormier, capturing the Duke of Orléans (the future Louis XII), a victory that paved the way for the union of Brittany with the French crown.

Italian Wars

La Trémoille accompanied Charles VIII on the invasion of Italy in 1494 and fought at the Battle of Fornovo in 1495. Under Louis XII he campaigned in the Duchy of Milan, governed Milan for a time, and fought at the Battle of Agnadello (1509). In 1513 he was defeated by Swiss forces at the Battle of Novara, but later recovered Dijon from Swiss besiegers through negotiation.

Death

Still in command in his sixties, La Trémoille accompanied Francis I on the 1525 Italian campaign and was killed at the Battle of Pavia, the disastrous French defeat in which the king himself was captured.

Family

He married Gabrielle de Bourbon in 1484. His grandson, François de La Trémoille, continued the family's prominence at court.
