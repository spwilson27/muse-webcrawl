Solicitor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A solicitor is a legal practitioner who traditionally deals with most legal matters in some jurisdictions, preparing cases, advising clients directly, drafting documents (contracts, wills, conveyances), and conducting litigation up to trial, instructing barristers for specialist advocacy where the profession is split. In England and Wales, solicitors are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, qualify through examinations and training contracts (now the SQE route), and number well over 150,000 practising certificate holders — the larger branch of the profession.

Work

Solicitors practise in private firms (from sole practitioners to global "Magic Circle" and international firms), in-house for companies, and in government. Their work spans conveyancing, probate, family and criminal defence (including police-station and magistrates' court representation as duty solicitors), employment, corporate and litigation practice. Since the 1990s, solicitors have gained full rights of audience as solicitor-advocates, blurring the historic divide with the Bar — while conveyancing and litigation remain their core.
