Ten Crucial Days

Dates25 December 1776 – 3 January 1777
WarAmerican Revolutionary War
CommanderGeorge Washington

The Ten Crucial Days were the period of the American Revolutionary War from 25 December 1776 to 3 January 1777, during which General George Washington's Continental Army won three battles in New Jersey — at Trenton, Assunpink Creek and Princeton. Coming after a string of defeats and a retreat across New Jersey that had brought the American cause near collapse, the victories revived Patriot morale, induced re-enlistments, and are widely considered the turning point that saved the Revolution.

Background

After losing New York City and Fort Washington, Washington's dwindling army retreated across New Jersey into Pennsylvania in late 1776. With enlistments expiring at year's end and Congress fled to Baltimore, Thomas Paine's pamphlet The American Crisis ("These are the times that try men's souls") sought to rally support. Washington resolved on a counterstroke against the chain of Hessian and British winter outposts in New Jersey.

The battles

On Christmas night, Washington's force crossed the ice-choked Delaware River in a storm and at dawn on 26 December surprised and captured most of the Hessian garrison at Trenton. Crossing back into New Jersey, Washington held off Cornwallis's counterattack at the Battle of Assunpink Creek on 2 January 1777, then slipped away overnight, leaving campfires burning, and marched around the British flank to defeat a British brigade at Princeton on 3 January. The army then withdrew to winter quarters at Morristown.

Significance

The victories forced the British to abandon most of New Jersey, ending their attempt to pacify the middle colonies that winter, and transformed enlistment and foreign perceptions of the American cause. Historians and the interpretive programs of the National Park Service — which manages Washington Crossing and related sites — commemorate the period as the moment the Revolution was saved.
