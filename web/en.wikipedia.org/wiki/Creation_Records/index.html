Creation Records Ltd. was a British independent record label founded in 1983 by Alan McGee, Dick Green and Joe Foster, named after the 1960s band The Creation. It became one of the most influential UK indie labels, central to the shoegaze, indie and Britpop movements.

History

Early releases by The Jesus and Mary Chain — whose chaotic gigs and 1984 single "Upside Down" made the label's name — were followed by records from The Pastels, Primal Scream and Felt. Around 1990–91 Creation released landmark albums including Primal Scream's Screamadelica and My Bloody Valentine's Loveless, the latter's protracted recording nearly bankrupting the label. McGee signed Oasis in 1993 after seeing them in Glasgow; their debut Definitely Maybe (1994) and (What's the Story) Morning Glory? (1995) made Creation, by then half-owned by Sony, a commercial giant of Britpop.

Closure and legacy

McGee wound the label down in 2000, exhausted and disillusioned, its final releases including work by Super Furry Animals and Primal Scream. Creation's story is told in the 2021 biographical film Creation Stories. See also the orchestra article for a very different corner of the music world: the Philadelphia Orchestra.
