Raising the bar (fallacy)

Raising the bar (also known as moving the goalposts) is an informal fallacy in which evidence presented in response to a specific claim is dismissed on the grounds that some other, often greater, evidence is demanded. Instead of acknowledging that the original standard of proof was met, the goalposts are moved after the fact.

Description

The idiom derives from sports with goalposts, where moving them during play would obviously be unfair. A classic pattern is: a demand is made (for example, that a psychic demonstrate an ability under test conditions); when the demand is satisfied, the sceptic responds that the ability must now be demonstrated under new, harder conditions. The fallacy is related to the No true Scotsman argument, in which a definition is repeatedly revised to exclude counterexamples.
