Ibrahim Ferrer (20 February 1927 – 6 August 2005) was a Cuban singer of son, bolero and guaracha who achieved worldwide fame late in life as a member of the Buena Vista Social Club.

Life and career

Born in San Luis, near Santiago de Cuba, Ferrer was orphaned young and sang on the streets for money. He joined his first group at 14 and sang with leading Cuban orchestras including Chepín-Chovén and Benny Moré's Banda Gigante, and for many years with Pacho Alonso's group Los Bocucos. Despite his respected voice, he never achieved star billing in Cuba and had largely retired — shining shoes for a living — when producer Juan de Marcos González sought him out in 1996.

Buena Vista Social Club

Ferrer became the lead bolero singer of the Buena Vista Social Club album (1997), produced by Ry Cooder, contributing beloved versions of "Dos gardenias" and "Silencio" (a duet with Omara Portuondo). The album and Wim Wenders' 1999 documentary made him an international star in his seventies. His solo album Buena Vista Social Club Presents: Ibrahim Ferrer (1999) won a Latin Grammy, and he toured the world until shortly before his death in Havana in 2005; his final album, Mi sueño, a collection of boleros, was released posthumously.
