First-person shooter

The first-person perspective typical of the genre.

A first-person shooter (FPS) is a subgenre of shooter video games centered on gun and other weapon-based combat from a first-person perspective, with the player experiencing the action through the eyes of the protagonist and controlling them in a three-dimensional space.

History

The genre's earliest examples include Maze War (1973) and Spasim (1974). id Software's Wolfenstein 3D (1992) is credited with popularizing the genre, and Doom (1993) made it a cultural phenomenon, popularizing networked multiplayer deathmatches and user-created modifications. Half-Life (1998) integrated continuous narrative into the format, while Halo: Combat Evolved (2001) established the FPS on home consoles. Later landmarks include Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare (2007), which shaped modern military shooters, and team-based and "hero shooter" titles such as Overwatch (2016).

Characteristics

FPS games typically show the player's weapon at the bottom of the screen with a heads-up display conveying health, ammunition and a crosshair. Common modes include single-player campaigns, competitive multiplayer (deathmatch, capture the flag, team modes) and cooperative play. Design conventions include regenerating health or health packs, weapon limits, and level-based encounters. The genre is a mainstay of esports and has been linked to debates over video game violence, as well as to research on spatial cognition and attention.

Subgenres

  • Arena shooters — fast-paced, movement-focused multiplayer (e.g. Quake, Unreal Tournament)
  • Tactical shooters — emphasis on realism and planning
  • Hero shooters — distinct characters with unique abilities
  • Looter shooters — emphasis on randomized equipment acquisition
  • Battle royale games frequently use the first-person perspective
