Preface to the Poems Composed at the Orchid Pavilion

The Preface to the Poems Composed at the Orchid Pavilion (Chinese: 蘭亭集序; pinyin: Lántíngjí Xù), commonly known as the Lanting Xu ("Orchid Pavilion Preface"), is a piece of Chinese calligraphy work and prose written in 353 AD by the calligrapher Wang Xizhi (303–361). It is widely regarded as the greatest work of Chinese calligraphy and a masterpiece of Chinese literature.

Background

On the third day of the third lunar month in the ninth year of the Yonghe era (353 AD), Wang Xizhi hosted a gathering of 41 literati at the Orchid Pavilion near Shaoxing, Zhejiang, during the Shangsi Festival. The guests played a drinking game: cups of wine were floated down a winding stream, and where a cup stopped, the guest nearest had to compose a poem or drink. Twenty-six poems were composed, and Wang Xizhi, slightly drunk, improvised the preface to the collection.

Content

The preface, 324 characters long, begins by celebrating the beauty of the surroundings and the joy of the gathering, but turns melancholic, reflecting on the transience of life:

"Life and death are great affairs indeed. How sad it is! ... The causes that move us today will be viewed by later generations with the same sighs."

The work moves from delight to a meditation on mortality and the pain that future readers will look back on the gathering just as the author looked back on earlier generations.

Legacy

The original manuscript is lost — legend says it was buried with Emperor Taizong of Tang, who prized it above all calligraphy. Surviving copies, such as the Shenlong version attributed to Feng Chengsu, preserve its elegant running script (xingshu), with its fluid character variations, including multiple distinct forms of the character 之.
