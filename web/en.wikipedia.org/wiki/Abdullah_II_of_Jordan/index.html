Abdullah II bin Al-Hussein (born 30 January 1962) is King of the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan. He ascended the throne on 7 February 1999 following the death of his father, King Hussein, who had named him crown prince only weeks earlier, replacing his uncle Prince Hassan. Abdullah is a member of the Hashemite dynasty, which claims descent from the Prophet Muhammad, and is the 41st-generation direct descendant according to family tradition.

Early life and military career

Abdullah was educated in Jordan, England and the United States, and trained at the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst. He served as a career officer in the Jordanian Army, commanding armoured units and the Jordanian Special Forces, and reached the rank of major general before taking the throne.

Reign

His reign has emphasised economic liberalisation, tourism and technology, and Jordan's close strategic partnership with the United States. Jordan weathered the Arab Spring with limited reforms, absorbed huge refugee flows from Iraq and Syria, and faced the strain of the Gaza war after 2023. In 2004 Abdullah issued the Amman Message, a declaration promoting a moderate, tolerant interpretation of Islam. His son Hussein is crown prince.
