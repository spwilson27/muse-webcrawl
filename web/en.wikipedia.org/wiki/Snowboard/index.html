Snowboard

TypeWinter sports equipment
Invented1965 (Snurfer); modern form 1977
Used forSnowboarding

A snowboard is a board used in the sport of snowboarding, on which the rider stands with both feet attached by bindings, sideways to the direction of travel — as in surfing and skateboarding, the activities from which the sport developed.

Most snowboards consist of a wood core sandwiched between fibreglass layers, with a sintered or extruded plastic base and steel edges. Key dimensions are length, waist width, sidecut radius, flex and camber profile.

Types

  • Freestyle — short, light, twin-tipped boards for terrain parks and tricks.
  • Freeride — longer, stiffer, directional boards for all-mountain and powder riding.
  • Alpine/carving — narrow, stiff boards used with hard boots for high-speed carved turns.
  • Splitboard — a board that separates into two ski-like halves for backcountry touring.

History

Sherman Poppen's "Snurfer" (1965) is the direct ancestor; Jake Burton Carpenter and Tom Sims developed modern bindings and mass-market boards in the late 1970s. Snowboarding became an Olympic sport at Nagano in 1998.
