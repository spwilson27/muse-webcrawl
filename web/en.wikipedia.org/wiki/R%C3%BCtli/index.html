Rütli

Rütli (German pronunciation: [ˈryːtli]) is a mountain meadow on Lake Lucerne, in the Seelisberg municipality of the Swiss canton of Uri. It is the site of the traditional date of the founding of the Old Swiss Confederacy. According to tradition, representatives of the three original cantons (Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden) swore the Rütli Oath there to assist each other, an event associated with the Federal Charter of 1291.

The oath scene was famously dramatized in Friedrich Schiller's 1804 play William Tell. The meadow became a national shrine: it was purchased by the Swiss Society for Public Utility in 1859 and given to the Confederation on condition that it remain undeveloped.

Modern significance

The Rütli is a place of pilgrimage for Swiss patriots; the Swiss National Day celebrations of 1 August are traditionally held there, with an address by the President of the Swiss Confederation. In 1940, during the Second World War, General Henri Guisan assembled the senior officers of the Swiss army at the Rütli to order resistance against any invasion, an event known as the Rütli Report.
