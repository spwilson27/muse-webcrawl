Ip Man 2 (Chinese: 葉問2:宗師傳奇) is a 2010 Hong Kong biographical martial arts film directed by Wilson Yip and starring Donnie Yen as Ip Man, the Wing Chun master who taught Bruce Lee. A sequel to Ip Man (2008), it is loosely based on Ip Man's life after he moves to Hong Kong in 1949.

Plot

Having fled Foshan, Ip Man settles in Hong Kong and struggles to establish a Wing Chun school, facing indifference from students, poverty, and hostility from established local masters. He proves himself in a rooftop challenge against the masters' council led by Hung Chun-nam (Sammo Hung), earning the right to teach. When a brutish British boxer, Taylor "The Twister" Milos, insults Chinese martial arts during an exhibition and kills Hung in the ring, Ip Man challenges him and wins, restoring pride to the Chinese martial arts community. The film ends with a young Bruce Lee arriving to request training.

Reception and legacy

The film was a commercial success in Hong Kong and China and was nominated for multiple Hong Kong Film Awards, winning for action choreography by Sammo Hung. It consolidated Donnie Yen's portrayal of Ip Man as iconic and was followed by Ip Man 3 (2015) and Ip Man 4: The Finale (2019). Like its predecessor, it takes substantial liberties with the historical record, inventing the Twister rivalry and dramatising Ip's early Hong Kong years.
