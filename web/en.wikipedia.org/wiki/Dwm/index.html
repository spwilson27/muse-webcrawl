dwm is a minimalist dynamic tiling window manager for the X Window System, developed by the suckless.org community. It is written in C and is notable for its extremely small codebase — kept deliberately under about 2,000 lines of code — and for being configured by editing its source code and recompiling.

Design

dwm arranges windows automatically in tiled layouts: a master area and a stacking area, with monocular ("monocle") and floating modes also available. Windows are organised with tags rather than fixed workspaces, so a window can appear on several views. A built-in status bar displays tags and the title of the focused window, and dwm is controlled almost entirely from the keyboard.

Influence

dwm's simplicity inspired numerous other window managers and forks, including awesome (which began as a fork), xmonad's tiling concepts in the broader culture of tiling WMs, and many user-patched variants maintained through the suckless patch system. It exemplifies the suckless philosophy of simple, minimal, hackable software.
