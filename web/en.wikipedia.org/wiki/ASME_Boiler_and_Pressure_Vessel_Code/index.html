ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code

The ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code (BPVC) is a standard published by the American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) that regulates the design, fabrication and inspection of boilers and pressure vessels. First published in 1914, it is one of the most widely used engineering codes in the world and is legally adopted by most U.S. states and Canadian provinces, as well as many other jurisdictions.

PublisherASME (American Society of Mechanical Engineers)
First published1914
SubjectBoiler and pressure vessel design, fabrication, inspection

History

The code was prompted by a series of catastrophic boiler explosions in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, notably the 1905 Grover Shoe Factory disaster in Brockton, Massachusetts. ASME formed a committee to develop uniform rules for steam boiler construction; the first edition, covering steam boilers, appeared in 1914 and was formally adopted a year later.

Sections

The BPVC is organised into sections, including:

  • Section I — Rules for Construction of Power Boilers
  • Section II — Materials (Parts A–D)
  • Section III — Rules for Construction of Nuclear Facility Components
  • Section IV — Rules for Construction of Heating Boilers
  • Section V — Nondestructive Examination
  • Section VIII — Rules for Construction of Pressure Vessels (Divisions 1, 2 and 3)
  • Section IX — Welding, Brazing, and Fusing Qualifications
  • Section XI — Rules for Inservice Inspection of Nuclear Power Plant Components

Use

Equipment built to the code is marked with ASME certification marks (such as the U stamp for pressure vessels) after inspection by an Authorised Inspector. New editions are issued on a regular cycle, and code cases and interpretations supplement the published rules between editions.
