Intolerable Acts

The Intolerable Acts, sometimes called the Coercive Acts, were a series of five punitive laws passed by the British Parliament in 1774 in response to the Boston Tea Party. Intended to reassert authority over Massachusetts and punish Boston for the destruction of taxed tea, they instead united the Thirteen Colonies against Britain and became a major catalyst of the American Revolution.

The acts

The Boston Port Act closed the port of Boston until the East India Company was compensated for the destroyed tea. The Massachusetts Government Act revoked the colony's charter, brought its government under royal control and restricted town meetings. The Administration of Justice Act allowed royal officials accused of capital offences to be tried in Britain rather than in Massachusetts. The Quartering Act expanded the authority to house British troops in occupied buildings. A fifth measure, the Quebec Act — not aimed at the thirteen colonies but grouped with them by colonists — extended Quebec's boundaries and granted religious toleration to Catholics, alarming Protestant colonists.

Response

Rather than isolating Massachusetts, the Acts prompted intercolonial solidarity: supplies were sent to Boston, and in September 1774 the First Continental Congress met in Philadelphia, adopting boycotts and a declaration of rights. Within months, armed conflict began at Lexington and Concord in April 1775.
