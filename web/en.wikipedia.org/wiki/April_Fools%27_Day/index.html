April Fools' Day

April Fools' Day or All Fools' Day is an annual custom on 1 April consisting of practical jokes and hoaxes. Jokesters often expose their actions by shouting "April Fools!" at the recipient. Mass media can be involved with these pranks, which may be revealed as such the following day.

Date: 1 April
Type: Cultural / humorous
Observances: Practical jokes, hoaxes

Origins

The origins of April Fools' Day are uncertain. Proposed explanations include references in Chaucer's Canterbury Tales (1392), the switch from the Julian to the Gregorian calendar in France in 1582 (under which New Year's Day moved from late March/1 April to 1 January), and ancient spring festivals of hilarity such as the Roman Hilaria.

Modern pranks

Newspapers, broadcasters, and websites have staged famous hoaxes, including the BBC's 1957 report of Swiss spaghetti trees and various product announcements by technology companies intended as jokes.
