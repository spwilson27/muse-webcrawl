Stop Making Sense is a 1984 American concert film featuring a live performance by the American rock band Talking Heads. Directed by Jonathan Demme, it was shot over four nights at Hollywood's Pantages Theatre in December 1983, and is widely considered one of the greatest concert films ever made.

Production

The film avoids the conventions of earlier concert movies: there are few audience cutaways, no interviews, and the camera work favours long takes. The performance is carefully staged as a theatrical arc — frontman David Byrne begins alone on a bare stage with an acoustic guitar and a boombox for "Psycho Killer," and the band, stage set, and lighting grow number by number until the full nine-piece touring ensemble is performing "Burning Down the House" and "Life During Wartime." Byrne's progressively bigger business suit, culminating in the famous oversized "big suit," became one of the most recognisable images in popular music.

Reception and legacy

Critics praised the film's energy and precision; it has a lasting reputation as a peak document of the band's live power. A restored 40th-anniversary version was released by A24 in 2023, bringing the surviving band members together publicly for the first time in decades. The accompanying soundtrack album spent over two years on the Billboard charts.
