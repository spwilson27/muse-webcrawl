National Hunt racing, also known as jumps racing, is a form of horse racing particular to France, Great Britain, and Ireland, in which horses race over obstacles: either hurdles (smaller, brush-topped obstacles) or steeplechase fences (larger, more solid obstacles, often with water jumps or open ditches). The season traditionally runs through the winter months, when softer ground suits jumping.

Origins

The sport traces its origins to Ireland in the mid-18th century, when riders matched their hunters cross-country. A famous 1752 contest between Cornelius O'Callaghan and Edmund Blake was raced over four and a half miles between the church steeples of Buttevant and Doneraile in County Cork — the origin of the term "steeplechase". Racing over obstacles was codified in Britain in the 19th century under the National Hunt Committee, whose name the sport retained.

Principal races

The highlights of the British season are the Cheltenham Festival in March, featuring the Cheltenham Gold Cup and the Champion Hurdle, and the Grand National at Aintree, a handicap chase over 4 miles 2½ furlongs and 30 fences, first run in 1839. Other major events include the King George VI Chase at Kempton Park and the Irish Grand National at Fairyhouse.

Types of race

National Hunt races comprise steeplechases, hurdle races, and National Hunt flat races ("bumpers") for inexperienced jumpers. Most races are handicaps, in which horses carry weights assigned to equalise their chances. Jump horses are typically older and more durable than flat racers, and stars such as Arkle, Desert Orchid, and Kauto Star became household names in Britain and Ireland.
