Điện Biên is a province in the Northwest region of Vietnam, bordering Laos to the west and China's Yunnan province to the north. Its capital is the city of Điện Biên Phủ.

History

The province is best known internationally as the location of the Battle of Điện Biên Phủ in 1954, in which Viet Minh forces under Võ Nguyên Giáp defeated the French Union garrison, precipitating the end of the First Indochina War and the Geneva Accords. Điện Biên was established as a separate province in 2004, when it was split from Lai Châu Province.

Geography and demographics

The province is mountainous, with the Mường Thanh valley as its agricultural heartland, famed for Điện Biên rice. Ethnic Thái people form the largest group, alongside Hmong, Khmu and Kinh (ethnic Vietnamese) communities.

Economy

Agriculture, including rice, coffee and tea, dominates the economy. Tourism centred on the battlefield memorials of Điện Biên Phủ is growing.
