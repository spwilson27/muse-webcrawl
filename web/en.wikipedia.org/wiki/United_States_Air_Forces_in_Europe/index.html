The United States Air Forces in Europe – Air Forces Africa (USAFE-AFAFRICA) is a major command of the United States Air Force and the air component for both United States European Command (EUCOM) and United States Africa Command (AFRICOM). Headquartered at Ramstein Air Base in Germany, it directs air operations across Europe and Africa and provides combat-ready air forces to NATO.

History

USAFE traces its lineage to the activation of the Eighth Air Force in January 1942, whose heavy bombers flew from England during the Second World War. In August 1945 the headquarters became United States Air Forces in Europe. During the early Cold War USAFE units took part in the Berlin Airlift (1948–49), and through the following decades the command maintained fighter, reconnaissance, and airlift forces in Britain, Germany, Italy, Spain, and Turkey as NATO's principal American air deterrent. Its aircraft flew combat operations during the 1986 strike on Libya, the 1991 Gulf War, the Balkan campaigns of the 1990s (including Operation Allied Force over Kosovo in 1999), and the 2011 intervention in Libya.

Present day

In 2012 USAFE merged with Third Air Force, and in 2013 it combined with Air Forces Africa to form USAFE-AFAFRICA, a single command serving two geographic combatant commands. Principal flying units include the 48th Fighter Wing (F-35A/F-15E) at RAF Lakenheath, the 52nd Fighter Wing at Spangdahlem, the 31st Fighter Wing at Aviano, the 86th Airlift Wing at Ramstein, and the 100th Air Refueling Wing at RAF Mildenhall.
