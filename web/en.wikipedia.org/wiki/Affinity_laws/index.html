Affinity laws

The affinity laws (also called the fan laws or pump laws) are a set of relationships used in hydraulics and turbomachinery to express how changes in rotational speed or impeller diameter affect the performance of centrifugal pumps, fans, and compressors.

The laws

For a change in shaft speed N, with the same impeller diameter: flow rate Q varies directly with speed (Q ∝ N); pressure or head H varies with the square of speed (H ∝ N²); and power P varies with the cube of speed (P ∝ N³). Analogous relationships hold for changes in impeller diameter at constant speed, with flow proportional to diameter cubed in the most precise formulations and power to the fifth power.

Use

The laws allow engineers to predict performance at off-design conditions and are the physical basis for the large energy savings achieved by variable-frequency drives: because power falls with the cube of speed, a modest reduction in pump or fan speed produces a much larger reduction in energy consumption. The laws assume geometrically similar operation and become less accurate when efficiency changes significantly or the fluid is highly viscous.
