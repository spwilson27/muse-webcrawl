Walter Ulbricht

Born: 30 June 1893, Leipzig, German Empire
Died: 1 August 1973 (aged 80), Groß Dölln, East Germany
Office: First Secretary of the SED (1950–1971); Chairman of the State Council (1960–1973)

Walter Ernst Paul Ulbricht (30 June 1893 – 1 August 1973) was a German communist politician who led East Germany from 1950 until 1971. As First Secretary of the Socialist Unity Party (SED) and later Chairman of the State Council, he shaped the German Democratic Republic (GDR) into a Soviet-aligned one-party state, oversaw its economic policies, and authorised the construction of the Berlin Wall in 1961.

Early career

Born in Leipzig into a working-class family, Ulbricht trained as a cabinetmaker and joined the Social Democratic Party in 1912, then the Communist Party of Germany (KPD) in 1920. He rose in the KPD apparatus, became a Reichstag deputy, and fled to exile after the Nazi seizure of power in 1933, spending time in Paris and Moscow, where his closeness to Stalin's apparatus defined his career. During the Second World War he broadcast propaganda to German troops and worked with the National Committee for a Free Germany.

Leader of East Germany

Returning to Berlin in April 1945 with the "Ulbricht Group", he organised the communist administration in the Soviet occupation zone under the famous instruction that it "must look democratic, but we must control everything". He engineered the forced merger of the KPD and SPD into the SED (1946), became its effective leader at the founding of the GDR in 1949, and crushed the workers' uprising of 17 June 1953 with Soviet tanks. To stop the exodus of citizens to the West, he ordered the building of the Berlin Wall on 13 August 1961, after publicly denying any such intention ("No one has the intention of building a wall"). His later "New Economic System" reforms introduced limited market mechanisms but were rolled back, and his ambitions strained relations with Moscow.

Fall and death

In May 1971, under Soviet pressure, Ulbricht was replaced as party leader by Erich Honecker, retaining only the ceremonial chairmanship of the State Council until his death on 1 August 1973.
