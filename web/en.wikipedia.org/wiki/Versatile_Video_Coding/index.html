Versatile Video Coding

Versatile Video Coding (VVC), also known as H.266 and MPEG-I Part 3, is a video compression standard finalised in July 2020 by the Joint Video Experts Team of ITU-T and ISO/IEC. It is the successor to High Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC/H.265).

Features

VVC was designed for a wide range of applications — broadcast, streaming, video conferencing, gaming and immersive media — targeting about 50% bitrate reduction over HEVC at the same visual quality. It supports resolutions up to 16K, 360-degree video, HDR, screen content coding and adaptive resolution switching, using tools such as larger coding tree units with more partition shapes, improved intra prediction, affine motion compensation and adaptive loop filtering.

Licensing and adoption

Patent licensing is administered through pools including Access Advance and Via LA (which merged their HEVC/VVC programmes). Adoption in consumer devices has been gradual, with encoders such as Fraunhofer's VVenC and decoders such as VVdeC developed to support the ecosystem.
