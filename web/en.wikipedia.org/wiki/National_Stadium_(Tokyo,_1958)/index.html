The National Stadium (Japanese: 国立競技場, Kokuritsu Kyōgijō) in Tokyo, officially the National Stadium of Japan, was a multi-purpose stadium in Kasumigaoka, Shinjuku, Tokyo. Completed in 1958 on the site of the former Meiji Shrine Outer Garden Stadium, it served as the main stadium for the 1964 Summer Olympics and was for decades the principal venue for Japanese athletics and football. It was demolished in 2015 to make way for the new National Stadium built for the Tokyo 2020 Olympic and Paralympic Games.

History

The stadium was constructed for the 1958 Asian Games, with a design by Mitsuo Katayama that expanded the earlier Meiji Jingu Gaien Stadium. For the 1964 Tokyo Olympics it hosted the opening and closing ceremonies and the athletics events, and witnessed famous moments including Abebe Bikila's second consecutive marathon gold medal, run through the stadium finish, and the lighting of the cauldron by Yoshinori Sakai, born on the day of the Hiroshima bombing.

Major events

  • 1958 Asian Games — the stadium's inaugural international event.
  • 1964 Summer Olympics — main Olympic stadium.
  • 1991 World Championships in Athletics — scene of Mike Powell's long jump world record of 8.95 m, breaking Bob Beamon's 23-year-old mark.
  • Intercontinental Cup / Toyota Cup — hosted the annual club football match from 1980 to 2001.
  • Emperor's Cup and J.League Cup finals — the traditional home of Japanese cup football finals, including the New Year's Day Emperor's Cup final.

The stadium also hosted concerts by major Japanese and international artists and served as a home venue for the Japan national football team. Its last event was held on 31 May 2014, and demolition was completed in 2015.

Successor

The new Japan National Stadium, designed by Kengo Kuma after the original Zaha Hadid design was scrapped over cost concerns, was built on the same site and opened in December 2019 as the centrepiece of the Tokyo 2020 Games.
