Costa Concordia

Costa Concordia
Class: Concordia-class cruise ship
Operator: Costa Cruises
Launched: 2 September 2005
In service: 2006–2012
Fate: Ran aground and capsized, 13 January 2012; scrapped 2015–2017

Costa Concordia was a Concordia-class cruise ship operated by Costa Crociere (Costa Cruises), a subsidiary of Carnival Corporation. Built by Fincantieri at Sestri Ponente, Genoa, she entered service in July 2006 and was one of the largest ships in the Costa fleet, with a gross tonnage of 114,147 and capacity for about 3,780 passengers.

The disaster

On the evening of 13 January 2012, during a Mediterranean cruise with 4,252 people aboard, Costa Concordia deviated from her planned route to perform a close “sail-by salute” to the island of Giglio, Tuscany. At 21:45 she struck a rock outcrop (Le Scole), tearing a gash of about 50 metres in her port side. Flooding disabled propulsion and electrical power; the ship drifted and grounded on a rocky ledge just outside Giglio Porto, capsizing with a list of about 70 degrees.

Evacuation was chaotic and the order to abandon ship came more than an hour after the impact. Captain Francesco Schettino left the vessel while passengers remained aboard and was recorded being ordered back by Coast Guard officer Gregorio de Falco in an exchange that became notorious in Italy. In all, 32 people died. Schettino was convicted in 2015 of multiple manslaughter, causing a shipwreck, and abandoning ship, and sentenced to 16 years in prison; the conviction was upheld by Italy's Court of Cassation in 2017.

Salvage

The wreck became the subject of one of the largest maritime salvage operations in history. In September 2013 the ship was rolled upright in a “parbuckling” operation led by Titan Salvage and Micoperi. Re-floated in July 2014, she was towed to Genoa, where dismantling and scrapping were completed by 2017. Total costs of the disaster and salvage are estimated at around US$2 billion.
