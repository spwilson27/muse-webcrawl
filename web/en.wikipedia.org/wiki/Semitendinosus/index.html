Semitendinosus muscle

The semitendinosus is a muscle in the back of the thigh; it is one of the three hamstring muscles, together with the semimembranosus and the biceps femoris. It is named for its very long tendon of insertion, which makes up a conspicuous portion of the muscle's length — in fact its tendon accounts for roughly half of its length.

OriginIschial tuberosity
InsertionPes anserinus (superomedial surface of the tibia)
ArteryPerforating arteries
NerveSciatic nerve (tibial division), L5, S1, S2
ActionsFlexion of knee, extension of thigh, medial rotation of leg

Structure

The semitendinosus arises from the inferomedial impression of the upper part of the ischial tuberosity, in common with the long head of the biceps femoris. Its muscle belly descends on the medial side of the back of the thigh and ends a little below the middle of the thigh in a long, rounded tendon. The tendon curves around the medial condyle of the tibia and inserts into the upper part of the medial surface of the tibia as part of the pes anserinus, together with the sartorius and gracilis tendons. A characteristic tendinous inscription crosses the muscle belly obliquely.

Function

Acting across both hip and knee, the semitendinosus extends the thigh at the hip joint and flexes the leg at the knee; when the knee is flexed, it rotates the leg medially. It also helps stabilize the pelvis.

Clinical significance

The semitendinosus tendon is commonly harvested, alone or with the gracilis tendon, as an autograft for anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction. Hamstring strains frequently involve the posterior thigh muscles during sprinting.
