Stock (clothing)

A stock, also called a stock tie, is a close-fitting cloth collar or neckcloth formerly worn by men, and still worn today in certain formal, military and equestrian contexts. It consists of a wide band of fabric wrapped around the neck and fastened at the front or back.

Stocks originated in the 17th century as part of military dress, initially a leather collar designed, in lore, to encourage a soldier to keep his head up and eyes to the front; leather stocks remained part of many armies' uniforms into the 19th century and gave US Marines the nickname "Leathernecks". For civilian dress, stocks of linen, muslin or silk became fashionable in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, fastening with a buckle or pin at the back.

The stock survives today principally in equestrian dress, where a white stock tie is worn with the shadbelly coat in dressage and in hunting dress, secured with a stock pin; it is said also to serve traditionally as an emergency bandage or horse bandage. Clerical and legal dress in some traditions also retains stock-like bands.
