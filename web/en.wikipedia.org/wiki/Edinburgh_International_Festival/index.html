Edinburgh International Festival

GenrePerforming arts festival (opera, music, theatre, dance)
DatesThree weeks in August each year
LocationEdinburgh, Scotland
Founded1947
FounderRudolf Bing and Audrey Mildmay

The Edinburgh International Festival is an annual festival of performing arts in Edinburgh, Scotland, held over three weeks in August. By invitation from the festival director, leading companies and artists in classical music, opera, theatre, and dance perform each year. It is the original and flagship event of the Edinburgh festivals, the collection of arts festivals that together transform the city each summer.

History

The festival was founded in 1947 by Rudolf Bing, general manager of Glyndebourne Opera, with civic leaders including Lord Provost Sir John Falconer and the singer Audrey Mildmay. Its founding aim, in the aftermath of the Second World War, was to provide "a platform for the flowering of the human spirit" and to reunite Europe through culture. Edinburgh was chosen because its venues had survived the war largely intact. The first festival featured the Vienna Philharmonic under Bruno Walter.

The festival today

Performances are staged at venues including the Usher Hall, Festival Theatre, Royal Lyceum, The Hub, and the Queen's Hall. The festival opens each August alongside the Edinburgh Festival Fringe — which began informally in 1947 when uninvited theatre groups performed on the festival's margins — the Royal Edinburgh Military Tattoo, and the city's book and art festivals. The closing fireworks concert over Edinburgh Castle became a signature finale.
