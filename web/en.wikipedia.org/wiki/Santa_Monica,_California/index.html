Santa Monica, California

Santa Monica
County: Los Angeles County, California
Population: approx. 93,000 (2020 census)
Incorporated: November 30, 1886
Known for: Santa Monica Pier, beaches, Silicon Beach

Santa Monica is a coastal city in western Los Angeles County, California, United States. Situated on Santa Monica Bay and bordered by Los Angeles on three sides, it is a popular resort and residential city known for its beaches, mild climate, and the Santa Monica Pier.

History

The Tongva people inhabited the area for centuries. The city was founded in 1875 by Senator John P. Jones and Robert Baker and incorporated in 1886. The Looff Hippodrome and pleasure piers made it a celebrated seaside resort in the early 20th century, and U.S. Route 66 famously terminated at the pier. Douglas Aircraft was headquartered in Santa Monica, and the city later became a centre of health and fitness culture around Muscle Beach.

Modern Santa Monica

Today Santa Monica combines affluent residential neighbourhoods with major commercial districts including Downtown, the Third Street Promenade, and Montana Avenue. It is a hub of the "Silicon Beach" technology economy and a leader in municipal sustainability and rent control policies. The Metro E Line connects it to downtown Los Angeles. Tourism, driven by the pier, Palisades Park, and its wide beaches, remains central to the economy.
