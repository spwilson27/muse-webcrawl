Lake Debo

Lake Debo (French: Lac Débo) is a lake in the central part of Mali, formed by the seasonal flooding of the Niger River in the Inner Niger Delta. It is the largest lake in Mali, with its size varying greatly between the wet and dry seasons.

Geography

During the annual flood, the Niger's waters fill Lake Debo and the surrounding floodplains, creating a vast wetland that supports fisheries, rice farming, and transhumant herding. At maximum extent the lake covers roughly 160 square kilometres, shrinking substantially in the dry season.

Ecology

The lake and delta are an important habitat for migratory birds and for the African manatee. The Inner Niger Delta is listed as a Ramsar wetland of international importance.
