Blue laws
Also known asSunday laws, Sunday closing laws
PurposeRestrict or ban certain activities on Sundays
Common restrictionsRetail sales, alcohol sales, hunting, car sales
OriginColonial America; earlier Sabbath legislation in Europe

Blue laws, also known as Sunday laws, are laws that restrict or ban some or all Sunday activities, particularly to promote the observance of a day of rest. Such laws may restrict shopping or ban the sale of certain items on specific days, most often Sundays in the western world. Blue laws are enforced in parts of the United States and Canada as well as some European countries, particularly in Austria, Germany, Switzerland, and Norway, which restrict most retail activity on Sundays.

History

Sunday laws have roots in ancient Sabbath observance and in Roman and medieval European legislation. In colonial America, colonies enacted detailed codes regulating Sunday conduct; the term "blue laws" is popularly associated with the strict codes of colonial Connecticut, supposedly printed on blue paper, though this origin is disputed. Samuel Peters popularised the term in his 1781 General History of Connecticut.

United States

The U.S. Supreme Court upheld Sunday closing laws in McGowan v. Maryland (1961), finding that their present purpose was the secular one of providing a uniform day of rest rather than religious establishment. Bergen County, New Jersey, is noted for retaining some of the strictest remaining blue laws in the United States, restricting the sale of clothing, furniture and electronics on Sundays. Various states repealed many restrictions during the late twentieth century, though alcohol and motor-vehicle sales restrictions persist in several jurisdictions.

Canada

In Canada, the federal Lord's Day Act of 1906 was struck down in R v Big M Drug Mart Ltd (1985), in which the Supreme Court of Canada held that the Act infringed freedom of religion under the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms.

Europe

Many European countries retain Sunday trading restrictions. Germany's Ladenschlussgesetz historically kept most shops closed on Sundays; Norway and Austria maintain broadly similar regimes, with exemptions for tourist areas, petrol stations and small shops.
