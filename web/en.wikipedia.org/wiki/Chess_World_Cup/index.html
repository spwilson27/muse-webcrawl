Chess World Cup

The Chess World Cup is a major knockout chess tournament organised by FIDE, held roughly every two years. Beginning in its current form in 2005, it features a large field (128 players in recent editions) playing mini-matches of classical games followed, if tied, by rapid and blitz tiebreaks. The top finishers qualify for the Candidates Tournament, which decides the challenger for the World Chess Championship.

History

Winners have included Levon Aronian, Gata Kamsky, Boris Gelfand, Vladimir Kramnik, Viswanathan Anand's contemporaries such as Sergey Karjakin, and Magnus Carlsen, who won in 2023. A Women's Chess World Cup was introduced in 2021.
