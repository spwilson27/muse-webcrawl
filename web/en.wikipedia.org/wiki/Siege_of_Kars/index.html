Siege of Kars

The Siege of Kars was a siege of the Ottoman fortress city of Kars in north-eastern Anatolia during the Crimean War, lasting from June to 28 November 1855. An Ottoman garrison, advised by British officers under General William Fenwick Williams, held out against a Russian army under General Nikolay Muravyov for five months before starvation forced surrender. The defence became famous in Britain, where Williams was celebrated as "Williams of Kars."

Background

Kars guarded the road from the Caucasus into Anatolia. Russian forces in the Caucasus theatre of the Crimean War sought to capture it to threaten Erzurum and divert Ottoman strength from the Crimea. The Ottoman garrison, demoralised after earlier defeats at Gyumri (Alexandropol) in 1853 and Kurukdere in 1854, was reorganised in 1855 by Williams, the British commissioner attached to the Army of Anatolia, who strengthened the earthworks and imposed discipline.

The siege

Muravyov invested the city in June 1855 with about 25,000–30,000 men against a garrison of roughly 15,000 effectives. The main Russian assault, launched on 29 September 1855, attacked the heights around the city (the "English redoubts" and Turkish positions) and was repulsed with heavy losses — the Russians lost several thousand men. Thereafter Muravyov settled into a close blockade. Attempts at relief failed: Omar Pasha's army, diverted to operations from Sukhumi in Mingrelia, could not reach Kars in time.

Surrender and aftermath

By late November, food was exhausted and cholera and starvation ravaged the defenders. Williams negotiated honourable terms, and the garrison surrendered on 28 November 1855, marching out with the honours of war; Williams and the Ottoman officers were treated courteously by Muravyov. News of the fall of Kars reached the peace negotiations at Paris; under the Treaty of Paris (1856), Russia returned Kars to the Ottoman Empire in exchange for the return of Sevastopol and other conquered territory to Russia. Williams received a baronetcy and the freedom of the City of London.
