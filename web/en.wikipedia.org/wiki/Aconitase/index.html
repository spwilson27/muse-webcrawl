Aconitase

Aconitase (aconitate hydratase; EC 4.2.1.3) is an enzyme that catalyses the stereo-specific isomerisation of citrate to isocitrate via cis-aconitate in the tricarboxylic acid (Krebs) cycle, a non-redox-active process. It is an iron–sulfur protein containing a [4Fe–4S] cluster.

Reaction and structure

Aconitase first dehydrates citrate to the enzyme-bound intermediate cis-aconitate, then rehydrates it in the opposite sense to yield isocitrate. One iron atom of the [4Fe–4S] cluster directly coordinates the substrate's hydroxyl and carboxyl groups, acting as a Lewis acid in the reaction. Loss of the labile iron atom inactivates the enzyme.

Isozymes and iron regulation

Humans have two aconitases: a mitochondrial form (ACO2) operating in the Krebs cycle and a cytosolic form (ACO1). Cytosolic aconitase has a second life as iron regulatory protein 1 (IRP1): when iron is scarce, it loses its cluster and instead binds iron-responsive elements in the messenger RNAs of ferritin and the transferrin receptor, coordinating the regulation of cellular iron uptake and storage.

Inhibition

Aconitase is inhibited by fluoroacetate, a toxin found in some plants, which is converted to fluorocitrate and blocks the enzyme — a classic example of "lethal synthesis". Superoxide can also damage the cluster, inactivating the enzyme under oxidative stress.
