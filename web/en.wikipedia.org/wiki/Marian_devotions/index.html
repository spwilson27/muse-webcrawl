Marian devotions

Marian devotions are external pious practices directed to the person of Mary, mother of Jesus, by members of certain Christian traditions. They are performed in Catholicism, High Church Lutheranism, Anglo-Catholicism, Eastern Orthodoxy and Oriental Orthodoxy, but generally rejected in other Christian traditions such as Reformed Christianity. Devotions are held to be expressions of love and veneration for the Virgin Mary and are not considered worship (latria), which in Catholic theology is reserved for God alone; the veneration given to Mary is termed hyperdulia.

Marian devotions include various forms of prayer, hymns, acts of consecration, pilgrimages to shrines, and the wearing of sacramentals such as scapulars and medals. They developed over the centuries alongside Mariology and have been encouraged by successive popes, notably in documents such as Marialis Cultus (1974).

Forms of devotion

Prayers

The most widespread Marian prayer is the Rosary, a meditative devotion combining vocal prayer (the Hail Mary, the Lord's Prayer and the Glory Be) with meditation on the mysteries of the lives of Christ and Mary. Other major prayers include the Angelus, the Memorare, the Salve Regina and the Litany of Loreto. The Hail Mary itself derives from the greetings of the Annunciation and Visitation narratives in the Gospel of Luke.

Acts of consecration

Consecration to Mary, popularised by Saint Louis de Montfort in his True Devotion to Mary, involves entrusting oneself to Jesus through Mary. Popes have also consecrated the world and particular nations to the Immaculate Heart of Mary, notably Pius XII in 1942 and John Paul II in 1984.

Pilgrimages and shrines

Major Marian pilgrimage sites include Lourdes in France, Fátima in Portugal, Guadalupe in Mexico, Częstochowa in Poland, Aparecida in Brazil and Knock in Ireland. Many are associated with reported Marian apparitions.

Sacramentals

Devotional objects include the Brown Scapular, the Miraculous Medal (revealed, according to tradition, to Saint Catherine Labouré in 1830), and statues and images of Mary, which may be crowned with papal approval (canonical coronation).

Feast days

The liturgical calendar includes solemnities and feasts such as the Immaculate Conception (8 December), the Assumption (15 August), Mary, Mother of God (1 January), the Annunciation (25 March) and Our Lady of Sorrows (15 September). May and October are traditionally months dedicated to Mary.

In Eastern Christianity

In Eastern Orthodoxy, devotion to the Theotokos ("God-bearer") is expressed through hymns such as the Akathist, the Paraklesis service, and icons, which occupy a central place in Orthodox piety.

Protestant views

Most Protestant traditions reject Marian devotions, holding that prayer should be directed to God alone and viewing such practices as lacking scriptural warrant, though Anglo-Catholics and some Lutherans retain a number of them.
