Burlington House is a building on Piccadilly in Mayfair, London. It was originally a private Palladian mansion, and was expanded in the mid-19th century after being purchased by the British government. It is best known as the home of the Royal Academy of Arts and several of Britain's leading learned societies.

History

The house takes its name from the Earls of Burlington. A house on the site was begun around 1664, and Richard Boyle, 3rd Earl of Burlington — the architect-earl famous for promoting Palladianism — remodelled it in the early 18th century with the assistance of Colen Campbell and William Kent. In 1854 the government bought the house to accommodate learned societies.

Occupants

The courtyard buildings house the Royal Society of Chemistry, the Geological Society of London, the Linnean Society of London, the Royal Astronomical Society, and the Society of Antiquaries of London, alongside the Royal Academy in the main block.

Architecture

The Piccadilly frontage, completed in the 1870s to designs by Sydney Smirke, presents a unified Italianate composition around a courtyard entered through an archway. The Royal Academy's galleries were substantially remodelled for its 250th anniversary in 2018, linking Burlington House with Burlington Gardens.
