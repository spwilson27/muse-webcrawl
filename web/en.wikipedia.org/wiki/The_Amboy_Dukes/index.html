The Amboy Dukes

Origin: Detroit, Michigan, United States
Years active: 1964–1975
Genres: Psychedelic rock, garage rock, hard rock, acid rock
Best known member: Ted Nugent

The Amboy Dukes were an American rock band formed in Detroit, Michigan, in 1964. They are best known for their 1968 hit single "Journey to the Center of the Mind" and as the launching pad for guitarist Ted Nugent, who became the band's dominant figure and eventually its sole constant member.

History

The band took its name from Irving Shulman's 1947 novel The Amboy Dukes, about a Brooklyn street gang. After relocating to Detroit, the group signed with Mainstream Records and released its self-titled debut album in 1967. The follow-up, Journey to the Center of the Mind (1968), produced the title track, a psychedelic rock classic that reached the top 20 of the Billboard Hot 100. Ironically, the song's drug-implying lyrics were written by guitarist Steve Farmer while lead guitarist Nugent, who later became an outspoken anti-drug campaigner, has said he did not understand their meaning at the time.

Later years

Through the early 1970s the band underwent continual line-up changes, released albums such as Migration (1969), Marriage on the Rocks/Rock Bottom (1970), and, as "Ted Nugent and the Amboy Dukes," Survival of the Fittest (1971), Call of the Wild (1973), and Tooth, Fang & Claw (1974). In 1975 Nugent dropped the Amboy Dukes name and began his solo career. The original group was inducted into the Michigan Rock and Roll Legends Hall of Fame and reunited briefly for a performance in Detroit in 2009.
