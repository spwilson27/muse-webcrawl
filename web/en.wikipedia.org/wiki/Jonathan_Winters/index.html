Jonathan Winters

Born: Jonathan Harshman Winters III, November 11, 1925, Dayton, Ohio, U.S.
Died: April 11, 2013 (aged 87), Montecito, California, U.S.
Occupation: Comedian, actor, author, artist
Years active: 1949–2013

Jonathan Harshman Winters III (November 11, 1925 – April 11, 2013) was an American comedian, actor, and artist. A master of improvisational comedy, he created a repertory of characters—including the feisty grandmother Maude Frickert—and pioneered the use of props and free-association in stand-up. He was a major influence on generations of comedians, notably Robin Williams.

Career

Winters served in the U.S. Marine Corps in the Pacific during World War II, then began in radio in Ohio before moving to New York and national television in the 1950s. He became a fixture on variety and talk shows, hosted The Jonathan Winters Show, and recorded best-selling comedy albums such as The Wonderful World of Jonathan Winters (1960). His film roles include It's a Mad, Mad, Mad, Mad World (1963), The Loved One (1965), and The Russians Are Coming, the Russians Are Coming (1966).

Later work

Winters won an Emmy Award for the sitcom Davis Rules (1991) and voiced Papa Smurf in The Smurfs films late in life. He played Mork's son Mearth, appearing alongside Robin Williams, in the final season of Mork & Mindy. He received the Kennedy Center's Mark Twain Prize for American Humor in 1999. Winters was also an accomplished painter and author of short stories, and spoke candidly about his struggles with bipolar disorder.
