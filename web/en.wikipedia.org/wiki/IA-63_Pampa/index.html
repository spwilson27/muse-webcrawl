The FMA IA-63 Pampa is a two-seat advanced jet trainer with light combat capability, produced in Argentina by the Fábrica Militar de Aviones (now FAdeA, Fábrica Argentina de Aviones). Developed with assistance from the German firm Dornier, the Pampa is based conceptually on the Dassault/Dornier Alpha Jet and serves as the principal advanced trainer of the Argentine Air Force.

Development

The program began in the late 1970s when the Argentine Air Force sought a replacement for its Morane-Saulnier MS.760 Paris trainers. Dornier provided design assistance, and the prototype first flew on 6 October 1984. Deliveries to the IV Air Brigade at El Plumerillo began in 1988. Production was interrupted by Argentina's economic difficulties in the 1990s and by the privatization of FMA to Lockheed Martin (as Lockheed Martin Aircraft Argentina) in 1995; the factory was renationalized in 2009 and production resumed.

Variants

  • IA-63 Pampa — initial production trainer.
  • Pampa II — upgraded aircraft with a more powerful TFE731-40 engine and improved avionics.
  • Pampa III — modernized variant with a glass cockpit, new mission computer, and virtual training systems, first delivered in the late 2010s.
  • AT-63 — proposed armed attack version marketed for export.

Design

The Pampa is a shoulder-wing monoplane with tandem seating, the rear seat raised for visibility, powered by a single Honeywell TFE731 turbofan. It is subsonic, with a maximum speed around Mach 0.8, and can carry light armament — a 30 mm podded cannon and underwing stores — for weapons training and light attack missions. Despite repeated export campaigns, the type has been operated only by Argentina.
