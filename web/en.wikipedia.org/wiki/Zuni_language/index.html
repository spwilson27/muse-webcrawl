Zuni language

The Zuni language (endonym: Shiwi'ma) is spoken by the Zuni people of western New Mexico and eastern Arizona. It is a language isolate with no demonstrated relatives, with approximately 9,500 speakers, most of them in and around Zuni Pueblo.

Classification

Proposed links to Penutian, Tanoan, and other families have all been rejected for lack of evidence, so Zuni remains classified as an isolate. The language has been spoken in the Zuni region for millennia and contains vocabulary reflecting the people's long agricultural and ceremonial history.

Features

Zuni has a modest consonant inventory including ejectives and long vowels, and a verb complex that marks subject and object pronominally. Unlike many neighbouring Pueblo languages it is not tonal. A practical orthography is used in tribal schools, and immersion and language-revitalization programmes work to transmit the language to children, making Zuni one of the more vital Indigenous languages of the Southwest.
