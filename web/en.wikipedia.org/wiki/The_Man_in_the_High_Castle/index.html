The Man in the High Castle is an alternate history novel by Philip K. Dick, published in 1962. Set in 1962, fifteen years after the Axis powers won the Second World War, it depicts a United States partitioned between Imperial Japan, which holds the Pacific coast, and Nazi Germany, which holds the east, with a neutral buffer zone in the Rockies.

Plot

Interwoven storylines follow characters in Japanese-occupied San Francisco, including antiques dealer Robert Childan, factory worker Frank Frink, and his ex-wife Juliana, who travels to Colorado after encountering The Grasshopper Lies Heavy — a banned novel-within-the-novel by Hawthorne Abendsen, the "man in the high castle", which imagines an Allied victory. Many characters consult the I Ching, which Dick himself used while plotting the book.

Reception and adaptation

The novel won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 1963 and is considered a landmark of the alternate-history genre. Amazon adapted it into a television series that ran for four seasons (2015–2019).
