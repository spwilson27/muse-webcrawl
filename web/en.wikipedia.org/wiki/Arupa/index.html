Arupa

Arūpa (Pali: arūpa; Sanskrit: arūpya) is a term in Buddhism and other Indian religions meaning "formless" or "incorporeal." It is used in two principal contexts: the formless realms (Pali: arūpaloka) of Buddhist cosmology, and the formless attainments (arūpa-jhāna), the four meditative absorptions that lie beyond the four form jhānas.

The formless realms

In Buddhist cosmology the universe is divided into three realms (the traidhātuka): the desire realm (kāmadhātu), the form realm (rūpadhātu), and the formless realm (arūpadhātu). The formless realm comprises four planes of existence in which beings have no material form at all and consist entirely of mind. From lowest to highest they are: the sphere of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatana), the sphere of infinite consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatana), the sphere of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana), and the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana), the highest state of existence in samsara. Beings are reborn in these realms as the result of attaining the corresponding formless meditations in a previous life, and their lifespans are measured in tens of thousands of kalpas — yet they remain impermanent and within the cycle of rebirth.

The formless attainments

The four formless attainments are meditative absorptions in which the meditator successively transcends perception of form: first by attending to infinite space, then to infinite consciousness, then to nothingness, and finally to a state where perception is so attenuated it can be called neither perception nor non-perception. According to the early texts, the Buddha learned the first two attainments from his teachers Āḷāra Kālāma and Uddaka Rāmaputta before his enlightenment, and surpassed them. The formless attainments are considered worldly meditations — conducive to rebirth in the formless realms but not, by themselves, to liberation, which requires insight (vipassanā).

Other uses

In Abhidharma analysis, "arūpa" also classifies phenomena that are non-material, and the term appears in Hindu and Jain cosmological contexts for incorporeal states or beings.
