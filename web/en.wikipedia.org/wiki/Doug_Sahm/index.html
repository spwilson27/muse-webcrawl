Doug Sahm

Douglas Wayne Sahm (November 6, 1941 – November 18, 1999) was an American musician, singer-songwriter and multi-instrumentalist from Texas. A child prodigy on steel guitar, fiddle and mandolin, he became best known as the founder and leader of the Sir Douglas Quintet, and later of the Texas Tornados. His music blended rock and roll, country, blues, conjunto and Tejano influences, and he is regarded as a founding figure of Tex-Mex rock.

Early life

Born in San Antonio, Texas, Sahm performed on radio as a child and by age nine had played on stage with Hank Williams. He released singles as a teenager under names such as Little Doug.

Sir Douglas Quintet

In 1964 Sahm formed the Sir Douglas Quintet with keyboardist Augie Meyers. Managed to look like a British Invasion band, they scored hits with "She's About a Mover" (1965) and "Mendocino" (1969). The group's sound fused Vox organ-driven rock with Texas border music.

Later career

Sahm recorded solo albums in the 1970s, including Doug Sahm and Band (1973) with guest appearances by Bob Dylan and Dr. John. In 1989 he co-founded the Texas Tornados with Freddy Fender, Flaco Jiménez and Augie Meyers, winning a Grammy for Best Mexican-American Performance. He died of a heart attack in Taos, New Mexico, in 1999.
