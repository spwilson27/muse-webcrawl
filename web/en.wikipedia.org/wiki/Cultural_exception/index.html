Cultural exception

The cultural exception (French: exception culturelle) is a political concept holding that cultural goods and services should be treated differently from ordinary commodities in trade, so that governments may subsidise and protect domestic cultural production. It is most closely associated with France.

History

France championed the principle during the 1993 Uruguay Round of GATT negotiations, resisting the inclusion of audiovisual services in free-trade liberalisation; the resulting standoff nearly derailed the talks. France maintains measures such as broadcast quotas for French-language and European works and extensive film subsidies administered through the CNC.

Cultural diversity

In the 2000s the language of "cultural exception" was largely reframed as the promotion of "cultural diversity", embodied in the UNESCO Convention on the Protection and Promotion of the Diversity of Cultural Expressions (2005), which affirms states' right to adopt cultural policies. Critics argue the doctrine serves as disguised protectionism.
