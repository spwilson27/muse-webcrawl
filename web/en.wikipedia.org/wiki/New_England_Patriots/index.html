The New England Patriots are a professional American football team based in the Greater Boston area. The Patriots compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member club of the American Football Conference (AFC) East division. The team plays its home games at Gillette Stadium in Foxborough, Massachusetts. Founded in 1959 as the Boston Patriots of the American Football League (AFL), the team joined the NFL in the 1970 merger and changed its name to New England in 1971.

Early history

The Boston Patriots were a charter member of the AFL, reaching the 1963 AFL Championship game (losing to the San Diego Chargers). After joining the NFL, the renamed New England Patriots reached their first Super Bowl in the 1985 season, losing Super Bowl XX to the Chicago Bears, and returned in 1996 under coach Bill Parcells and quarterback Drew Bledsoe, losing Super Bowl XXXI to the Green Bay Packers. Robert Kraft bought the team in 1994, preventing a proposed move to St. Louis.

The Kraft–Belichick–Brady era

In 2000 Kraft hired Bill Belichick as head coach, and the team drafted quarterback Tom Brady in the sixth round (199th overall). When Bledsoe was injured early in the 2001 season, Brady took over and led the Patriots to an upset victory over the St. Louis Rams in Super Bowl XXXVI. It was the beginning of the most successful dynasty in modern NFL history.

Dynasty achievements

Between 2001 and 2019 the Patriots won six Super Bowls (2001, 2003, 2004, 2014, 2016, and 2018 seasons), appeared in nine, and won 17 AFC East titles. Highlights include back-to-back titles in 2003–04, the 16–0 regular season of 2007 (ending with a loss to the New York Giants in Super Bowl XLII), the comeback from 28–3 down against the Atlanta Falcons in Super Bowl LI — the largest comeback in Super Bowl history — and Super Bowl LIII, a 13–3 defensive victory over the Los Angeles Rams. Brady won three league MVP awards and four Super Bowl MVPs; Belichick became the only head coach with six Super Bowl wins.

Rivalries and controversies

The Patriots' fiercest rivalries in the era were with the Indianapolis Colts (the Brady–Manning rivalry), the New York Jets, and the Baltimore Ravens. The dynasty was also marked by the "Spygate" scandal (2007, videotaping opponents' signals) and "Deflategate" (2015, underinflated footballs in the AFC Championship), resulting in fines, lost draft picks, and a four-game suspension for Brady.

Post-Brady years

Brady departed for Tampa Bay in 2020. After several declining seasons, Belichick and the Patriots parted ways in January 2024 after 24 seasons; Jerod Mayo succeeded him, followed by Mike Vrabel in 2025, as the team rebuilt around young quarterback Drake Maye.
