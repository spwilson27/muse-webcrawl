Southern Hemisphere

The Southern Hemisphere is the half of Earth that is south of the Equator. It contains all or parts of five continents (Antarctica, Australia, most of South America, a third of Africa, and a small portion of Asia in island Southeast Asia), four oceans (Atlantic, Indian, Pacific, and Southern), and most of Oceania. Due to the tilt of Earth's rotation relative to its orbital plane, summer in the Southern Hemisphere occurs from late December to late March, when the Northern Hemisphere experiences winter.

Land area~48.6 million km² (about 32.7% of Earth's land)
SurfaceAbout 80.9% water
PopulationRoughly 800–850 million (about 10–11% of world population)

Geography and climate

The Southern Hemisphere has proportionally far more water than land compared to the Northern Hemisphere, which gives it a generally more maritime, milder climate at equivalent latitudes, with fewer extremes of temperature. The temperate zone is relatively narrow, consisting of southern South America, southern Africa, Australia, and New Zealand. The roaring forties and furious fifties — strong westerly winds of the Southern Ocean — are a famous feature of its mid-latitude weather.

Tropical cyclones in the Southern Hemisphere rotate clockwise (as seen from above), opposite to the Northern Hemisphere, because of the Coriolis effect. The same effect causes the sun to cross the northern part of the sky and shadows to move counterclockwise on sundials, and makes the Moon appear "upside down" compared to northern views.

Population and culture

Only about a tenth of the world's people live south of the Equator, with the largest populations in Brazil, Indonesia, and southern Africa. Seasons are reversed relative to the north, so Christmas in Australia and New Zealand is a summer holiday, and school years and sporting seasons are often structured differently. Astronomical highlights include the Southern Cross, the Magellanic Clouds, and the galactic centre of the Milky Way passing high overhead in winter.
