Humboldt University of Berlin

The Humboldt University of Berlin (German: Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin) is a public research university in Berlin, Germany. Founded in 1810 as the University of Berlin on the initiative of the liberal Prussian educational reformer Wilhelm von Humboldt, it became the model for the modern research university, uniting teaching and research.

History

The university's "Humboldtian model" influenced higher education worldwide. Its scholars have included Hegel, Marx, Einstein, Planck and many other leading figures; dozens of Nobel laureates have been affiliated with it. After 1949 it lay in East Berlin and was renamed for Wilhelm and Alexander von Humboldt; the Free University was founded in the west in response. Following German reunification the university was restructured and is today one of Germany's Universities of Excellence.

Organisation

The university has around 35,000 students across faculties including law, life sciences, humanities and social sciences, with campuses at Mitte, Nord and Adlershof. Its main building is the former palace of Prince Heinrich on Unter den Linden.
