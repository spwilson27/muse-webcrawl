Wire obstacle

In the military science of fortification, a wire obstacle is a defensive obstacle made of barbed wire, concertina wire or other wire entanglements. Wire obstacles are used to slow, channel or stop attacking infantry, to protect positions, and to deny access to defended areas. They are often combined with minefields, trenches and observed fire to maximise their effect.

Types

Common forms include concertina wire coils, the double-apron fence, low wire entanglements (such as the "knife rest"), and tanglefoot — wire strung at ankle height among stakes. Modern concertina wire, made of barbed tape spiralled into coils, can be deployed rapidly from vehicles and is difficult to cut or cross.

History

Barbed wire, invented in the 19th century for agricultural fencing, became a defining feature of trench warfare in the First World War, where deep belts of wire protected the trenches of both sides. Wire obstacles remain a standard element of field fortifications and are also used in riot control and perimeter security.
