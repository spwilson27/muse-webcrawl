Bougainville Island
Largest island of the Solomon archipelago
Autonomous Region of Bougainville, Papua New Guinea
Main towns: Buka, Arawa, Buin

Bougainville Island is the main island of the Autonomous Region of Bougainville, which is part of Papua New Guinea. Geographically it is the largest island in the Solomon Islands archipelago. The island is volcanic, mountainous, and densely forested, with Mount Balbi its highest point at about 2,715 metres. It is named after the French navigator Louis Antoine de Bougainville, who reached it in 1768.

History

Colonised by Germany in the nineteenth century and administered by Australia after the First World War, Bougainville saw heavy fighting between Allied and Japanese forces in the Second World War. After Papua New Guinea's independence in 1975, disputes over the giant Panguna copper mine and secessionist grievances erupted into a decade-long civil war (1988–1998) between the Bougainville Revolutionary Army and the PNG government, costing many thousands of lives.

Status

The 2001 Bougainville Peace Agreement granted autonomy and promised a referendum on independence. In the 2019 referendum, about 98 per cent of voters chose independence; the outcome is subject to ratification by Papua New Guinea's parliament, and negotiations over the island's political future continue.

Economy

Cocoa and copra are the main exports; the Panguna mine, once among the world's largest copper mines, has been closed since 1989, though its reopening is periodically debated.
