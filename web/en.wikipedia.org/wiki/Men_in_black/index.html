Men in black

In popular culture and ufology, men in black (MIB) are mysterious men, usually said to wear black suits and sunglasses, who allegedly visit or threaten people who have witnessed unidentified flying objects, attempting to silence them or suppress evidence. The reports emerged in the 1950s and became a staple of UFO conspiracy theories, and later of popular entertainment.

Origins

The lore is closely associated with Albert K. Bender, founder of the International Flying Saucer Bureau, who in 1953 claimed to have been visited by three men in black who warned him to stop investigating UFOs; his story was popularised by Gray Barker's 1956 book They Knew Too Much About Flying Saucers. Later accounts described the visitors as behaving oddly — speaking in monotone, appearing unfamiliar with everyday objects, and sometimes seeming not quite human — leading to speculation that they were government agents, aliens in disguise, or supernatural beings.

In popular culture

The concept inspired the comic book The Men in Black (1990) by Lowell Cunningham and the hugely successful Men in Black film franchise beginning in 1997, in which the MIB are a secret organisation policing extraterrestrial activity on Earth.
