The Great Indian Peninsula Railway (GIPR) was a railway company established in 1849 that constructed and operated the first passenger railway line in India. On 16 April 1853, its first train ran from Bori Bunder (later Victoria Terminus, now Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus) in Bombay to Thane, a distance of 34 km (21 mi), hauled by three locomotives and carrying about 400 passengers in 14 carriages.

History

The company was incorporated by an Act of the British Parliament on 1 August 1849 and entered into a contract with the East India Company to build an experimental line near Bombay and, subsequently, routes toward the interior. The line was extended to Kalyan and up the Western Ghats by the great inclines at Bhor Ghat and Thal Ghat, opening through routes toward Pune and beyond. By 1870 the GIPR connected Bombay with Calcutta (via the East Indian Railway at Allahabad/Jabalpur) and with Madras, forming the backbone of the Indian railway network.

Later years

The company's assets were purchased by the state in 1900, though it continued to operate the system under contract until 1925, when the government took over management directly. On 5 November 1951 the GIPR, together with several other systems, was merged into the newly created Central Railway zone of Indian Railways.

Legacy

Its Bombay headquarters and terminus, Victoria Terminus (now Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus), completed in 1887, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most famous Gothic Revival railway stations in the world.
