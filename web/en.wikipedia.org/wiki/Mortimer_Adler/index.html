Mortimer Jerome Adler (December 28, 1902 – June 28, 2001) was an American philosopher, educator, and encyclopedist. A proponent of Aristotelian and Thomistic philosophy and of liberal education through the study of the "Great Books", Adler spent his career arguing that education in a democracy should aim at developing every citizen's capacity to think, and that the same curriculum of classic works belongs to all students.

Life and career

Adler dropped out of school at 14 to work as a copy boy, but after taking night classes he entered Columbia University; denied a bachelor's degree over a swimming requirement, he nevertheless went on to earn a doctorate in psychology there in 1928 and later received the degree. At the University of Chicago, under president Robert Hutchins, he helped launch the Great Books movement. For Encyclopædia Britannica he served as editor-in-chief of the Great Books of the Western World (1952) and its Syntopicon, an index of the "great ideas", and later chaired the board of editors of the 15th edition of the Encyclopaedia Britannica.

Works and ideas

His bestseller How to Read a Book (1940; revised 1972 with Charles Van Doren) teaches "inspectional", "analytical", and "syntopical" reading and remains in print. In The Paideia Proposal (1982) he advocated a single, non-tracked school curriculum combining didactic instruction, coaching, and Socratic seminar. Raised in a secular Jewish family, Adler engaged Thomism throughout his life and was received into the Roman Catholic Church shortly before his death.
