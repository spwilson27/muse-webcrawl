Republicanism in the United Kingdom

Republicanism in the United Kingdom is the political movement that seeks to replace the United Kingdom's monarchy with a republic, typically with an elected head of state. Support for a republic has fluctuated over time; opinion polls generally find that a majority of Britons support retaining the monarchy, though support fell following the death of Queen Elizabeth II and among younger age groups republican sentiment is stronger.

History

England was briefly a republic — the Commonwealth of England — following the execution of Charles I in 1649, under Oliver Cromwell; the monarchy was restored in 1660. Republican ideas persisted among radicals of the 18th and 19th centuries, including supporters of Thomas Paine and the Chartists, and surged again during the scandals and expense of the early reign of Queen Victoria. Irish nationalism developed its own republican tradition.

Modern movement

The principal campaign organisation is Republic, founded in 1983, which argues that the monarchy is undemocratic, expensive, and incompatible with meritocracy. Republican views are found across parties, including within the Labour Party, the Scottish National Party, Plaid Cymru and the Green Party, though abolition is not official policy for most. Prominent historical republicans include Tony Benn and Jeremy Corbyn.

Alternatives proposed

Proposals typically envisage a parliamentary republic with a ceremonial, directly elected president, modelled on Ireland or Germany, with the monarch's constitutional functions transferred to that office.
