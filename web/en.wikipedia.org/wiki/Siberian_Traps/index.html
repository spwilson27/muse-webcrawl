Siberian Traps

The Siberian Traps (Russian: Сибирские траппы) are a large region of volcanic rock, a large igneous province, in Siberia, Russia. The massive eruptive event that formed the traps is one of the largest known volcanic events of the last 500 million years, and coincided with the Permian–Triassic extinction event about 252 million years ago.

Formation

"Trap" derives from Swedish trappa, stair, for the step-like hills of basalt. Eruptions – flood basalts plus intrusive sills into coal- and evaporite-bearing sediments – released huge volumes of CO₂, methane and halogens, apparently driving global warming, ocean anoxia and acidification linked to the "Great Dying", Earth's severest mass extinction, in which most marine species died.

Geography

The traps underlie much of the Central Siberian Plateau, including the Putorana Plateau, a UNESCO World Heritage site. The Norilsk region hosts world-class nickel-copper-palladium deposits associated with trap intrusions.
