Kozak sequence

The Kozak consensus, (gcc)gccRccAUGG, flanking the start codon (AUG shown in bold position)

The Kozak sequence is a short nucleotide motif in eukaryotic messenger RNA that surrounds the start codon and plays a major role in the initiation of translation. It is named after the American biochemist Marilyn Kozak, who identified the consensus by comparing the sequences around the initiation codons of hundreds of vertebrate mRNAs in the 1980s.

The consensus

The vertebrate consensus is commonly written (gcc)gccRccAUGG, where AUG is the start codon (with A numbered +1), R is a purine (A or G) three bases upstream (−3), and G follows the start codon at +4. The two most critical positions are the purine at −3 — most often adenine — and the guanine at +4. A start codon in a "strong" Kozak context is efficiently recognised by the scanning ribosome; an "adequate" context has only one of the two key features, and a "weak" context has neither, in which case ribosomes may skip the first AUG and initiate downstream, a phenomenon called leaky scanning.

Mechanism

In the scanning model championed by Kozak, the 40S ribosomal subunit, loaded with initiation factors and the initiator tRNA, enters at the capped 5′ end of the mRNA and scans along the leader until it encounters an AUG in a favourable context. Interactions between the sequence and components of the pre-initiation complex — including the anticodon loop of the initiator tRNA and 18S rRNA — stabilise arrest at the correct start site, after which the 60S subunit joins and elongation begins.

Variations and applications

Different groups of eukaryotes show variations: invertebrates such as Drosophila favour a related consensus (C/A)AA(A/C)ATG, and plants and yeasts have their own preferences, generally poorer in GC. The Kozak sequence is routinely engineered into expression vectors and synthetic genes to maximise protein production, and point mutations that weaken the Kozak context of a human gene — historically termed "Kozak sequence mutations" — have been implicated in diseases such as thalassemia, as in variants affecting the β-globin gene. The motif is distinct from the prokaryotic Shine–Dalgarno sequence, which base-pairs directly with the ribosome.
