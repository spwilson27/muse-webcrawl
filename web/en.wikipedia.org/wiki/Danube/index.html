Danube

The Danube flowing through Vienna with St. Stephen's Cathedral in the distance

The Danube is the second-longest river in Europe, after the Volga. It rises in the Black Forest of Germany and flows about 2,850 kilometres southeast, passing through or bordering ten countries — Germany, Austria, Slovakia, Hungary, Croatia, Serbia, Bulgaria, Romania, Moldova and Ukraine — before emptying into the Black Sea through the Danube Delta.

The Danube passes through four national capitals — Vienna, Bratislava, Budapest and Belgrade — more than any other river in the world. It has been a vital trade and transport route since ancient times and today forms part of a navigable corridor linking the North Sea to the Black Sea via the Rhine-Main-Danube Canal.

Course

The Danube begins at the confluence of the Brigach and Breg streams at Donaueschingen in Germany. Its major tributaries include the Inn, Drava, Sava, Tisza, Morava and Prut. Near its mouth it splits into the Danube Delta in Romania and Ukraine, a vast wetland and UNESCO World Heritage site rich in birdlife.

History and culture

The river marked the northern frontier (limes) of the Roman Empire for centuries. In the 19th century it inspired Johann Strauss II's famous waltz 'The Blue Danube'. The Iron Gates gorge, where the river cuts between the Carpathian and Balkan mountains on the Serbia-Romania border, is a dramatic highlight of its course.
