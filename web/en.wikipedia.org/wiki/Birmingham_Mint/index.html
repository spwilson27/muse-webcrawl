The Birmingham Mint was a mint and metal-working company in the Jewellery Quarter of Birmingham, England. Founded by Ralph Heaton II as Heaton's Mint, it became for a time the largest privately owned mint in the world, striking coins for countries around the globe.

History

Ralph Heaton began striking coins in 1850, initially producing copper coinage and tokens. Under his sons, notably Ralph Heaton III, the firm supplied coinage to British colonies and foreign governments — including France, Italy, China, and many others — and produced blanks and finished coins under contract for the Royal Mint. Coins from the mint often carry the mint mark "H".

Later years

In the twentieth century the company diversified into medals, tokens, casino chips, and commemorative issues. Facing financial difficulties, the mint closed in 2003. Its historic Icknield Street building, a notable piece of industrial architecture, was later converted to residential and commercial use.
