CharacteristicsImmediacy, interactivity, hyperlinks, multimedia

Online journalism, or digital journalism, is the reporting of news published over the Internet rather than through print or broadcast media. It emerged in the 1990s as news organisations launched websites, and it has since become the dominant distribution channel for most news outlets.

Features

Online stories can be updated continuously, linked to sources and archives through hyperlinks, enriched with video, audio, data visualisations, and interactive graphics, and discussed by readers in comments or on social media. The 24-hour cycle rewards speed, while analytics give editors detailed audience data unavailable in print.

Impact on the industry

The shift online undermined classified-advertising revenue and print circulation, forcing closures and consolidation, while enabling digital-native outlets and new formats such as blogs, live blogs, podcasts, and newsletters. Funding models include digital subscriptions and metered paywalls, pioneered at scale by The New York Times from 2011.

Challenges

Online journalism is associated with concerns about misinformation, clickbait, the speed–accuracy trade-off, and dependence on search and social platforms for traffic.
