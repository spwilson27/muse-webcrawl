Dal Lake

Dal is a lake in Srinagar, the summer capital of Jammu and Kashmir, India. It is an urban lake, the second largest in the region, covering about 18 km², and is integral to tourism and recreation, famous for its houseboats and shikara boats. It is often called the "Jewel in the crown of Kashmir".

Geography

The lake lies at an elevation of about 1,583 m, with an average depth of around 1.4 m and a maximum depth of about 6 m. It is surrounded by the Zabarwan mountain range and the Mughal gardens of Nishat Bagh, Shalimar Bagh and Chashme Shahi along its shore. A causeway divides it into basins, and floating gardens growing vegetables — known as rad — and lotus flowers in summer are characteristic features.

History and use

Dal Lake was a resort of Mughal emperors and later of the British, who moored houseboats on it because they were not permitted to own land; thousands of houseboats and shikaras still ply the lake, supporting a large tourism economy. Since the late 20th century the lake has suffered from encroachment, siltation and eutrophication, prompting restoration projects by the Lakes and Waterways Development Authority.
