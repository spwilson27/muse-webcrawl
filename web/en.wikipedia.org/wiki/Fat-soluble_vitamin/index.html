Fat-soluble vitamin

Fat-soluble vitamins are vitamins that dissolve in fat and are absorbed from the diet along with dietary lipids. The four fat-soluble vitamins are A, D, E and K. Unlike water-soluble vitamins, they can be stored in the liver and fatty tissues for long periods, so they need not be consumed daily — but excessive intake, especially of vitamins A and D, can cause toxicity (hypervitaminosis).

The four vitamins

  • Vitamin A (retinol and carotenoid precursors) — essential for vision, immune function and cell growth; deficiency causes night blindness.
  • Vitamin D (calciferol) — regulates calcium and phosphate metabolism and bone health; produced in skin exposed to sunlight; deficiency causes rickets and osteomalacia.
  • Vitamin E (tocopherols) — an antioxidant protecting cell membranes; deficiency is rare and mainly neurological.
  • Vitamin K — required for blood clotting and bone proteins; also produced by gut bacteria.

Absorption and storage

Absorption occurs in the small intestine and depends on bile salts and pancreatic enzymes; disorders of fat absorption, such as cystic fibrosis or coeliac disease, can cause deficiencies. Because the body stores these vitamins, megadose supplements carry a greater risk of accumulation and toxicity than for water-soluble vitamins.
