Band-e Kaisar

Remains of the Band-e Kaisar at Shushtar, Iran

The Band-e Kaisar (Persian: بند قیصر, "Caesar's dam") was a Roman-era arch bridge and weir at Shushtar in southwestern Iran, built in the 3rd century CE. It is considered the easternmost Roman bridge and Roman dam, and the first structure in Iran to combine a bridge with a dam. Its Persian name preserves the memory of its construction by Roman soldiers and engineers.

History

After the Sasanian king Shapur I defeated the Romans at the Battle of Edessa in 260 CE, capturing the emperor Valerian, the Roman prisoners were put to work on major hydraulic projects in Khuzestan. At Shushtar they built the Band-e Kaisar across the Karun River: a combined bridge-dam about 500 metres long that raised the river's level and diverted water into the irrigation canal system of the Shushtar Historical Hydraulic System, supplying the city and surrounding fields. The structure, built of sandstone ashlar bonded with mortar and iron clamps, remained in use into the 19th century, though progressively damaged by floods; its last arches collapsed in 1885. The surviving remains form part of the Shushtar Historical Hydraulic System, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2009.
