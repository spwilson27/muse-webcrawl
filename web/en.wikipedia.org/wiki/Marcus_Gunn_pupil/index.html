Marcus Gunn pupil

A Marcus Gunn pupil, also called a relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD), is a clinical sign in which the pupil of an affected eye paradoxically dilates when a bright light is swung from the normal eye to the affected eye during the swinging flashlight test. It is named after the Scottish ophthalmologist Robert Marcus Gunn.

Mechanism

The sign indicates damage to the afferent limb of the pupillary light reflex — the retina or optic nerve — on the affected side. Because the defective eye senses less light, the consensual and direct responses are weaker when it is stimulated; when the light swings to it, both pupils dilate slightly instead of constricting because the brain perceives a relative dimming.

Causes

Common causes include optic neuritis (classically in multiple sclerosis), severe ischaemic or compressive optic neuropathy, retinal detachment, and extensive retinal disease. A Marcus Gunn pupil is not caused by cataract or other media opacities, which do not significantly impair the afferent signal. It is an important objective finding in neuro-ophthalmological examination.
