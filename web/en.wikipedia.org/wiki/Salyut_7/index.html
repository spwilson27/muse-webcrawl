Salyut 7 (Russian: Салют-7) was a Soviet space station in low Earth orbit from April 1982 to February 1991. It was the last station of the Salyut programme and the backup to the near-identical Salyut 6, with two docking ports allowing simultaneous visits by a crewed Soyuz and a Progress cargo craft.

Operations

Ten crews visited the station aboard Soyuz T spacecraft. Notable missions included the record-setting 237-day flight of Leonid Kizim, Vladimir Solovyov and Oleg Atkov in 1984, and Svetlana Savitskaya's second flight, during which she became the first woman to perform a spacewalk. In February 1985 the station lost power and froze; the Soyuz T-13 crew of Vladimir Dzhanibekov and Viktor Savinykh performed a celebrated manual docking and repair in June 1985, an event dramatised in the 2017 film Salyut-7.

End of mission

The final crew departed in 1986, ferrying equipment to the new Mir station. Salyut 7 was boosted to a storage orbit but decayed earlier than planned, re-entering over South America on 7 February 1991; debris fell near Capitan Bermudez, Argentina, without injuries.
