Scree

Scree is a collection of broken rock fragments at the base of a cliff or other steep rocky slope, accumulated through periodic rockfall. Individual rock fragments are called talus, and the sloping deposit itself is often called a talus slope. Scree forms by mechanical weathering — especially freeze–thaw action — that breaks angular fragments from the rock face above.

Formation and character

Scree slopes typically rest at the angle of repose, around 35 degrees for loose angular material. Larger fragments travel further and collect toward the base of the slope, producing a natural sorting known as reverse grading. Over time scree may be cemented, vegetated and stabilised, or buried to become breccia in the geological record.

Ecology and mountaineering

Scree habitats support specialised plants and animals adapted to unstable, free-draining ground. For climbers and walkers, scree is notoriously laborious to ascend but can allow rapid, controlled descents ("scree running") where local ethics and erosion concerns permit.
