Centuria

Centuria (Latin: [kɛn̪ˈt̪ʊ.ri.a], plural centuriae) was a unit in the Roman army, originally consisting of 100 men, and later about 80 men, commanded by a centurion. Six centuriae formed a cohort, and ten cohorts formed a legion.

Military role

The centuria was the basic tactical and administrative unit of the legion. Its centurion was assisted by an optio (second-in-command), a signifer (standard-bearer) and a tesserarius (guard commander). Soldiers of a centuria shared a barrack block and camped together; the first centuria of the first cohort (the primus pilus's unit) was double strength in the Imperial period.

Political meaning

In Roman politics, the comitia centuriata (Centuriate Assembly) was an assembly of the Roman people organised into voting units also called centuriae, based on wealth classes under the Servian organisation. Its 193 centuries elected senior magistrates such as consuls and praetors, voted on laws and declared war, with voting weighted heavily toward the wealthiest classes.
