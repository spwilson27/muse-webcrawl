Jan Žižka z Trocnova a Kalicha (c. 1360 – 11 October 1424) was a Czech general and a contemporary and follower of Jan Hus, and a Radical Hussite who led the Taborites. Žižka is considered a Czech national hero and one of the most innovative military commanders in history: he was never defeated in battle, despite eventually losing sight in both eyes.

Military career

Žižka served as a mercenary, possibly fighting at the Battle of Grunwald (1410) against the Teutonic Order. After the First Defenestration of Prague in 1419 he became a leader of the Hussite forces defending Bohemia against successive anti-Hussite crusades launched by King Sigismund. Blind in one eye from youth and losing the other to an arrow wound in 1421, he continued to direct his armies from a cart.

Tactics

Žižka's genius lay in turning peasant levies into a disciplined army built around the vozová hradba, or war wagon: chains of armoured wagons crewed by crossbowmen, handgunners, and flailmen that could form a mobile fortress on the march and a devastating platform for counterattack. His victories included Sudoměř (1420), Vítkov Hill (1420), Kutná Hora (1421–22), and Malešov (1424).

Death and legacy

Žižka died of plague during a campaign in Moravia in 1424. His followers, calling themselves the Orphans, continued under Prokop the Great. A monumental equestrian statue of Žižka on Vítkov Hill in Prague — one of the largest bronze equestrian statues in the world — commemorates him.
