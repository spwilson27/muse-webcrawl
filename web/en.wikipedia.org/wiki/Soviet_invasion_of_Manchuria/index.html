Soviet invasion of Manchuria

Date9 August – 2 September 1945
LocationManchuria, Inner Mongolia, northern Korea, Sakhalin, Kuril Islands
ResultDecisive Soviet victory
BelligerentsSoviet Union, Mongolia vs. Empire of Japan

The Soviet invasion of Manchuria, formally known in the Soviet Union as the Manchurian strategic offensive operation, began on 9 August 1945 when the Soviet Union invaded the Japanese puppet state of Manchukuo in Manchuria. It was the last major campaign of the Second World War and the largest Soviet operation of the Pacific War.

Background

At the Yalta Conference in February 1945, Joseph Stalin agreed that the Soviet Union would enter the war against Japan within three months of Germany's surrender, in return for territorial concessions including southern Sakhalin and the Kuril Islands. Germany surrendered in May; shortly after midnight on 9 August — three months to the day after Victory in Europe Day in the USSR — Soviet forces attacked.

The offensive

The operation was commanded by Marshal Aleksandr Vasilevsky and employed around 1.5 million men, more than 26,000 artillery pieces, and some 5,500 tanks and self-propelled guns, organised in three fronts: the Transbaikal Front under Marshal Rodion Malinovsky advancing from Mongolia across the Greater Khingan mountains and desert; the 1st Far Eastern Front under Marshal Kirill Meretskov attacking from the Maritime Province through difficult forested terrain; and the 2nd Far Eastern Front along the Amur River.

Opposing them, the Japanese Kwantung Army had been stripped of its best units for service elsewhere and numbered perhaps 700,000–1,000,000 men, many under-trained and poorly equipped. Soviet forces advanced with startling speed — armour covered hundreds of kilometres in days despite monsoon rains — and airborne troops seized airfields at Harbin, Mukden and Changchun ahead of the ground advance. By 19–20 August the Kwantung Army had effectively surrendered; isolated resistance continued in places until early September.

Other operations

Simultaneously, Soviet forces invaded South Sakhalin and, beginning on 18 August, landed in the Kuril Islands — the battle for Shumshu being among the fiercest fighting of the campaign. Soviet troops also advanced into northern Korea, halting at the 38th parallel in accordance with the agreement with the United States.

Aftermath

Soviet casualties were roughly 12,000 killed; Japanese losses were far higher, and some 600,000 Japanese soldiers were taken prisoner, many transported to labour camps in Siberia, where some remained into the 1950s. The campaign destroyed Japan's last major field army on the Asian mainland and, together with the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, contributed to the Japanese decision to surrender. Manchuria was handed over to the Republic of China in 1946, though Soviet forces first removed substantial industrial equipment.
