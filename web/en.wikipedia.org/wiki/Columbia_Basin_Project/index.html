Grand Coulee Dam, the water source of the Columbia Basin Project

The Columbia Basin Project (also called the Columbia Basin Reclamation Project) is a large irrigation and hydroelectric development in central Washington state in the United States. Operated by the Bureau of Reclamation, it draws water from the Columbia River behind Grand Coulee Dam and distributes it across the Columbia Plateau, irrigating roughly 671,000 acres of farmland in Grant, Adams, Franklin, and Walla Walla counties.

History

Proposals to irrigate the semi-arid Columbia Basin date to the late 19th century. The project was authorised during the Great Depression as part of the public works programme that built Grand Coulee Dam (completed 1941–42). Irrigation deliveries began in earnest after the Second World War, with the first water delivered to farmland in 1948 and major canal systems completed through the 1950s.

Infrastructure

Water is pumped from Franklin D. Roosevelt Lake up into Banks Lake, an equalising reservoir in the Grand Coulee itself, then flows south through the Main Canal, the West and East Low canals, and the Potholes Canal. The system includes the Potholes Reservoir and O'Sullivan Dam. Principal crops grown with project water include potatoes, apples, wheat, alfalfa, corn, and wine grapes.

Environmental issues

The project transformed large areas of shrub-steppe habitat and created new wetlands around reservoirs and wasteways that now support migratory waterfowl. It has also been debated in relation to Columbia River salmon, groundwater decline in the Odessa aquifer, and drainage water quality.
