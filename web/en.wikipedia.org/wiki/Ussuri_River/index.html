Ussuri River


The Ussuri near Khabarovsk

The Ussuri River (Russian: Уссури; Chinese: 乌苏里江, Wūsūlǐ Jiāng) is a river of about 897 km in East Asia. Rising in the Sikhote-Alin mountains of Primorsky Krai, Russia, it flows north to join the Amur at Khabarovsk. For most of its course it forms the border between Russia and China's Heilongjiang province, and its basin, around 193,000 km², includes Lake Khanka, into which its tributary the Sungacha flows.

History

The Ussuri became a border river under the 1860 Convention of Peking, by which Qing China ceded the lands east of the river ("Outer Manchuria") to the Russian Empire. Border tensions culminated in the 1969 Sino-Soviet border conflict, when Chinese and Soviet forces fought over Zhenbao (Damansky) Island in the river — clashes that brought the two nuclear powers close to war. A final boundary settlement was reached in the 1990s–2000s, dividing contested islands between the two countries.

Geography and ecology

The river is frozen roughly from November to April and is prone to summer flooding. Its wetlands and the Khanka basin are important for migratory birds, including red-crowned cranes, and it supports fisheries of salmon, sturgeon and Amur carp.
