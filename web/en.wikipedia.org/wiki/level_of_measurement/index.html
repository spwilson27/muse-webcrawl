Level of measurement

Level of measurement or scale of measure is a classification that describes the nature of information within the values assigned to variables. Psychologist Stanley Smith Stevens proposed the best-known classification with four levels: nominal, ordinal, interval, and ratio.

Stevens's typology

ScalePropertiesExample
NominalCategories onlyEye colour, jersey numbers
OrdinalCategories with orderRace finishing places, Likert items
IntervalOrder and equal intervals, no true zeroTemperature in °C, calendar dates
RatioAll properties plus a true zeroMass, length, income

Statistical implications

The level of measurement determines which statistics are meaningful: modes suit nominal data; medians suit ordinal data; means and standard deviations suit interval and ratio data. Stevens's scheme has been debated — critics argue it is too restrictive, while it remains standard in psychology and social-science methodology.
