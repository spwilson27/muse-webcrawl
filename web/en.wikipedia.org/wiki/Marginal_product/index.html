Marginal product

In economics, the marginal product of an input (or factor of production) is the change in output resulting from employing one additional unit of that input, holding all other inputs constant. If the output and the input are infinitely divisible, the marginal product is the partial derivative of the production function with respect to that input.

Definition

The marginal product of a variable input, such as labour, is defined as:

MP = ΔQ / ΔL

where ΔQ is the change in quantity of output produced and ΔL is the change in the quantity of the input used. When the input is capital, the analogous quantity is the marginal product of capital (MPK); for labour it is the marginal product of labour (MPL).

Diminishing marginal returns

The law of diminishing marginal returns states that, beyond some point, adding successive units of a variable input to a fixed input will cause the marginal product of the variable input to decline. This behaviour is commonly divided into three stages: increasing marginal returns, diminishing marginal returns, and negative marginal returns, in which total output actually falls as more of the input is added.

Marginal revenue product

Multiplying the marginal product by the price of the output gives the value of the marginal product; multiplying by marginal revenue gives the marginal revenue product (MRP). Under competitive conditions, profit-maximising firms hire an input up to the point where its marginal revenue product equals its marginal cost, a condition at the heart of the marginal productivity theory of distribution associated with economists such as John Bates Clark.
