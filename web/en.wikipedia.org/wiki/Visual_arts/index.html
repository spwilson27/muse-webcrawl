Visual arts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The visual arts include painting, sculpture, photography and many other media.

The visual arts are art forms such as painting, drawing, printmaking, sculpture, ceramics, photography, video, filmmaking, design, crafts and architecture. Many artistic disciplines, such as performing arts, conceptual art and textile arts, also involve aspects of the visual arts as well as arts of other types.

Also included within the visual arts are the applied arts, such as industrial design, graphic design, fashion design, interior design and decorative art.

History

The visual arts have ancient origins in prehistoric cave paintings and carvings, and have developed through the art of ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome, medieval and Renaissance Europe, and the many traditions of Asia, Africa, Oceania and the Americas. In Europe, an aesthetic theory centred on imitating nature developed during the Renaissance, and the hierarchy of genres placed history painting and portraiture among the most prestigious forms.

Modern and contemporary art

From the late 19th century, movements such as Impressionism, Cubism, Surrealism and Abstract Expressionism transformed the visual arts, challenging traditional notions of representation. Current usage of the term "visual arts" includes fine art as well as applied or decorative arts and crafts, but this was not always the case: before the Arts and Crafts Movement in Britain and elsewhere at the turn of the 20th century, the term "artist" had for some centuries often been restricted to a person working in the fine arts, such as painting, sculpture or printmaking.

Education and training

Training in the visual arts has traditionally been through variations on the apprentice and workshop systems, and later through art schools and university programmes. Visual artists work in studios, exhibit in galleries and museums, and increasingly use digital tools for image-making and design.
