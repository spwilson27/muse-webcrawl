William Richard "Billy" West (born April 16, 1952) is an American voice actor, musician, and comedian. He is best known for voicing multiple lead roles on the animated series Futurama — including Philip J. Fry, Professor Farnsworth, Dr. Zoidberg, and Zapp Brannigan — and for voicing Stimpy on The Ren & Stimpy Show and both Doug Funnie and Roger Klotz on Nickelodeon's Doug.

Early career

West was born in Detroit and raised in Roslindale, Boston. After studying at Berklee College of Music, he worked as a musician and comedian before joining Boston radio station WBCN in 1980, performing character voices and song parodies. He later became a cast member on The Howard Stern Show, where his impressions of Larry Fine, Marge Schott, and others became popular features.

Voice acting

West's breakthrough in animation came with Nickelodeon's first Nicktoons in 1991: he voiced Doug Funnie and Roger Klotz in Doug and Stimpy — and later Ren, after John Kricfalusi's departure — in The Ren & Stimpy Show. In film, he took over the voices of Bugs Bunny and Elmer Fudd in Space Jam (1996) and voiced characters in numerous Warner Bros. productions. In 1999 he began his most celebrated role, supplying four principal voices on Matt Groening's Futurama, a performance frequently cited among the finest in modern voice acting; Fry's voice, West has said, is essentially his own voice at age 25.

Other work

West is also a guitarist and leads the band Billy West and The Grief Counselors. He voiced the Red M&M in Mars commercials for many years and the robot in Project G.e.e.K.e.R. In later years he voiced roles in Disenchantment and appeared in documentary projects on voice acting, including I Know That Voice. In 2020 he stepped back from the role of the Red M&M. West has served as a prominent advocate for voice actors' recognition within the entertainment industry.
