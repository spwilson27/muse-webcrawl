Sweeps

In United States and Canadian broadcasting, sweeps are periods during which television stations and networks schedule programming designed to attract the largest possible audience, because local stations' viewing data are measured and used to set advertising rates. The name comes from the way Nielsen Media Research traditionally processed its ratings diaries, "sweeping" across the country from west to east.

How sweeps work

Nielsen conducts sweeps four times a year, in February, May, July and November, in the largest television markets. During these weeks, networks traditionally air special episodes of series, high-profile guest stars, miniseries, made-for-television films and heavily promoted stunt episodes ("very special episodes"), while local stations run sensational news segments and contests to boost their ratings. The results determine local advertising rates for the following months.

Decline of the sweeps system

The introduction of people meters and, later, continuous electronic measurement in major markets has reduced the importance of the traditional diary-based sweeps. Networks have responded by spreading original programming more evenly through the year rather than concentrating it in sweeps months, and some have experimented with year-round premieres and summer original series.
