The plates of Nephi, in the belief system of the Latter Day Saint movement, are a set of metal plates—part of the larger collection of golden plates—that, according to the Book of Mormon, were engraved and kept by the prophet Nephi and his descendants over many centuries. The Book of Mormon is presented as an abridgment and translation of these and other ancient records.

Small and large plates

The plates of Nephi are described as coming in two sets. The small plates of Nephi contain primarily a spiritual and religious history of the Nephites and cover the period from Lehi's departure from Jerusalem (around 600 BC) to roughly the second century BC. The large plates of Nephi contain a more secular and political history, including the reigns of kings and accounts of wars and contentions.

According to the narrative, Nephi began keeping the small plates about thirty years after his family left Jerusalem (see 2 Nephi 5:28–34), after receiving a commandment from God, though he wrote that he did not initially know the reason for making a second record. Later writers on the small plates explain that the plates were made "for a wise purpose" known to God—a reason that became clear to Joseph Smith's contemporaries when the first 116 pages of the translation (the "lost manuscript," derived from the large plates via Mormon's abridgment) were stolen in 1828.

Contributors

The small plates passed from writer to writer, generally from father to son or brother to brother. Their recorders include Nephi, his brother Jacob, Jacob's son Enos, Enos's son Jarom, Jarom's son Omni, and Omni's sons Amaron, Chemish, and Abinadom, and Chemish's son Amaleki. Amaleki, having no children, delivered the small plates to King Benjamin around 130 BC, after which they were kept with the other royal records.

The historical books of the current Book of Mormon that derive from the small plates are First Nephi, Second Nephi, Jacob, Enos, Jarom, and Omni. Mormon's "Words of Mormon" serves as a bridge explaining how the small plates were incorporated into his abridgment of the large plates.

Role in the publication of the Book of Mormon

Because the lost 116-page manuscript had contained Mormon's abridgment covering roughly the same period as the small plates, Joseph Smith was instructed, according to the Doctrine and Covenants (section 10), not to retranslate that portion but instead to translate the small plates, which covered the same era with greater emphasis on religious matters. This is why the modern Book of Mormon begins with the small-plates material and jumps to Mosiah after the Words of Mormon.
