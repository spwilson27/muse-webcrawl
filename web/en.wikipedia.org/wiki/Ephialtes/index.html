Ephialtes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ephialtes (Greek: Ἐφιάλτης, "nightmare") may refer to several figures and concepts, most famously Ephialtes of Trachis, the Greek who betrayed his homeland at the Battle of Thermopylae in 480 BC.

Ephialtes of Trachis

According to Herodotus, Ephialtes, son of Eurydemus, a native of Malis, informed the Persian king Xerxes I of a mountain path (the Anopaea path) that bypassed the Greek position at the pass of Thermopylae, hoping for a reward. Guided by this path, Persian troops outflanked the Spartan king Leonidas and his force, leading to their destruction. The Amphictyonic Council set a price on Ephialtes' head, and he was later killed by Athenades of Trachis. His name became a byword for treachery in Greek culture — in Modern Greek, efiáltis means "nightmare" — and he is popularly depicted in works such as the film 300.

Other uses

The name is also borne by Ephialtes of Athens, a 5th-century BC democratic reformer who, in 462/461 BC, stripped the Areopagus council of most of its powers, a milestone in the development of Athenian democracy; he was assassinated shortly afterwards. In Greek mythology, Ephialtes was one of the Aloadae, giant sons of Poseidon who attempted to storm Olympus. In psychiatry, "ephialtes" was an older term for nightmare disorder.
