Sepiida

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sepiida is the order of cephalopods commonly known as cuttlefish, containing over 120 described species. Together with the bobtail squids (Sepiolida, sometimes included within Sepiida) they form a group of ten-armed decapod cephalopods characterised by an internal chambered shell, the cuttlebone, used to control buoyancy.

Biology

Cuttlefish possess a broad, flattened body bordered by narrow fins, eight arms, and two long retractable feeding tentacles. Their eyes have distinctive W-shaped pupils. The cuttlebone, unique among living cephalopods, is a gas-filled, finely chambered aragonite structure that the animal uses to adjust buoyancy by varying the ratio of gas to liquid in the chambers.

Sepiids are masters of camouflage: specialised skin cells called chromatophores, iridophores, and leucophores allow them to change colour and texture rapidly, despite being colour-blind themselves. They also produce ink and, in many species, complex body-pattern displays for communication and courtship. Most species live one to two years, grow quickly, and die after spawning.

Classification

The order contains the large family Sepiidae (true cuttlefish), including the genera Sepia, Metasepia (flamboyant cuttlefish) and Sepiella. Molecular studies have reshaped the taxonomy, and some classifications merge Sepiida with the bobtail squid order Sepiolida into an expanded Sepiida.
