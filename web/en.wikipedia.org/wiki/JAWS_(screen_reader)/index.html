JAWS ("Job Access With Speech") is a screen reader for Microsoft Windows developed by Freedom Scientific, a company of the Vispero group. It allows blind and vision-impaired users to read the screen either with a text-to-speech output or by a refreshable Braille display. JAWS is one of the most widely used screen readers, particularly in employment and education settings in North America.

History

JAWS was originally released in 1989 for MS-DOS by Ted Henter, a former motorcycle racer who had lost his sight in a car accident, working with developer Bill Joyce under the company Henter-Joyce. A Windows version, JAWS for Windows, followed in 1995. Henter-Joyce merged with Arkenstone and the Blazie Engineering companies in 2000 to form Freedom Scientific. In 2018 Freedom Scientific's parent combined with Optelec under the Vispero brand.

Features

JAWS intercepts information from the operating system and applications and presents it as synthesized speech or Braille. It supports all standard Windows applications, web browsing in major browsers, and document creation in office suites. Its scripting language allows advanced users and third parties to customize JAWS behaviour for applications that are not natively accessible, a feature that has made it popular in call centres and other specialised workplaces. Features include OCR of scanned documents and images (Convenient OCR), voice profiles for the bundled Eloquence and Vocalizer synthesizers, and remote access support for Citrix and Remote Desktop environments.

Reception and market position

Periodic surveys by WebAIM of screen reader users have historically ranked JAWS among the most commonly used primary screen readers on desktop systems, competing with the free open-source NVDA and platform-bundled tools such as VoiceOver. JAWS is sold under annual subscription or perpetual licensing models, with separate home and professional editions.
