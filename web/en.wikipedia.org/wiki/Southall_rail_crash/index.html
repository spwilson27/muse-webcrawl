Southall rail crash

The Southall rail crash occurred on 19 September 1997, when an InterCity 125 high-speed passenger train operating from Swansea to London Paddington, operated by Great Western Trains, failed to slow down in response to warning signals and collided with a freight train crossing its path just east of Southall station in west London. Seven people died and 139 were injured.

Accident

The passenger train's driver missed the preliminary warning and warning aspects of signals and approached a red signal at high speed. The Automatic Warning System (AWS) in the driver's cab, which would normally have sounded an alarm, was isolated (switched off), and the driver had failed to react to the signal sequence. The train passed the signal at danger and struck the wagons of a freight train, operated by English Welsh & Scottish Railway, that was crossing the main line into a siding. The leading power car and coaches were derailed and wreckage was strewn across the tracks.

Aftermath

The crash, coming shortly after privatisation of British Rail, raised intense scrutiny of rail safety and of the fragmentation of responsibility for safety standards. Great Western Trains was prosecuted and fined £1.5 million for breaches of health and safety law, having allowed the train to operate with its AWS isolated. The public inquiry, chaired by Professor John Uff and reporting in 2000, criticised the safety culture of the company and made recommendations about driver management, training, and safety systems.

The disaster, together with the Ladbroke Grove rail crash two years later, accelerated the introduction of the Train Protection & Warning System (TPWS) across the network and increased pressure for a fully automatic train protection system. A memorial garden was created near the site for the victims.
