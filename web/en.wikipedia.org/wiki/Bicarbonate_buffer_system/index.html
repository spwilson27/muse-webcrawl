Bicarbonate buffer system

ComponentsCarbonic acid (H2CO3) / bicarbonate (HCO3−)
FunctionMaintains blood pH near 7.4
Key enzymeCarbonic anhydrase
Regulated byLungs (CO2 excretion) and kidneys (HCO3− handling)

The bicarbonate buffer system is an acid–base homeostatic mechanism involving the balance of carbonic acid (H2CO3), bicarbonate ion (HCO3−), and carbon dioxide (CO2) in order to maintain pH in the blood and duodenum, among other tissues, to support proper metabolic function. Catalyzed by carbonic anhydrase, carbon dioxide reacts with water to form carbonic acid, which in turn rapidly dissociates to form a bicarbonate ion and a hydrogen ion:

CO2 + H2O ⇌ H2CO3 ⇌ HCO3− + H+

As with any buffer system, the pH is balanced by the presence of both a weak acid (H2CO3) and its conjugate base (HCO3−) so that any excess acid or base introduced to the system is neutralized.

Physiological role

In the blood, the bicarbonate buffer system is the principal extracellular buffer. Because one component of the system is a gas, carbon dioxide, the system operates as an open buffer: changes in ventilation alter the partial pressure of CO2 and therefore shift the equilibrium within minutes, while the kidneys adjust bicarbonate reabsorption and excretion over hours to days. The relationship is described clinically by the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation, in which arterial pH depends on the ratio of bicarbonate concentration to dissolved carbon dioxide.

Mechanism

When an acid enters the blood, it reacts with bicarbonate to form carbonic acid, which is converted to CO2 and water; the excess CO2 is exhaled by the lungs. When a base enters, carbonic acid donates a hydrogen ion to neutralize it, producing bicarbonate, which the kidneys can excrete. Failure of this system results in acid–base disorders such as respiratory or metabolic acidosis and alkalosis.

In the duodenum

Bicarbonate secreted by the pancreas and by glands in the duodenal wall also neutralizes acidic chyme arriving from the stomach, protecting the intestinal mucosa and providing the alkaline environment needed by intestinal digestive enzymes.
