Poincaré map

In mathematics, particularly in dynamical systems, a Poincaré map (also called a first return map) is the intersection of a periodic orbit in the state space of a continuous dynamical system with a lower-dimensional subspace, called the Poincaré section. The map records where trajectories cross the section, reducing the study of a continuous flow to that of a discrete map.

Definition

Given a flow and a surface of section transverse to the flow, the Poincaré map P sends each point x of a periodic orbit's intersection with the section to the point where the trajectory next returns to the section. Fixed points of P correspond to periodic orbits of the flow, and the stability of an orbit can be read off from the eigenvalues of the linearised map.

History and applications

The technique is named after Henri Poincaré, who introduced it in his work on the three-body problem and celestial mechanics. Poincaré maps are now standard tools in nonlinear dynamics, used to reveal chaos, quasiperiodicity, and bifurcations in systems ranging from driven oscillators to fluid flows, where full solutions cannot be obtained analytically.
