The Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum is a museum in Phnom Penh, the capital of Cambodia, chronicling the Cambodian genocide. The site is a former secondary school which was used as Security Prison 21 (S-21; Khmer: ស-២១) by the Khmer Rouge regime from 1975 until its fall in 1979, and was used as a secure prison to detain, torture and kill prisoners. "Tuol Sleng" means "Hill of the Poisonous Trees" or "Strychnine Hill".

S-21 under the Khmer Rouge

Between 1976 and 1979, an estimated 20,000 people were imprisoned at Tuol Sleng and were ultimately killed. It was one of between 150 and 196 torture and execution centres established by the Khmer Rouge, and at any given time the prison held between 1,000 and 1,500 prisoners. Prisoners were routinely beaten, tortured and forced to confess to fabricated crimes before being taken for execution, mostly to the killing fields of Choeung Ek outside the city. The prison was directed by Kang Kek Iew, known as Comrade Duch, who was later convicted of crimes against humanity by the Khmer Rouge Tribunal in 2010.

When Vietnamese forces liberated Phnom Penh in January 1979, only a handful of prisoners were found alive. The Khmer Rouge had meticulously documented prisoners with photographs and forced confessions, records that now form a central part of the museum.

The museum

The buildings at Tuol Sleng have been preserved as they were found in 1979. The museum displays the cells, torture instruments, and thousands of prisoner photographs. Along with Choeung Ek, it is a principal site of memory and education about the genocide in which approximately 1.5 to 2 million Cambodians died between 1975 and 1979. The archives of the museum were inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World register in 2009.
