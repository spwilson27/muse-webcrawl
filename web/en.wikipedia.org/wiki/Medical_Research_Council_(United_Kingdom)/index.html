The Medical Research Council (MRC) is a publicly funded body responsible for co-ordinating and funding medical research in the United Kingdom. Founded in 1913, it is one of the nine research councils that form UK Research and Innovation (UKRI).

History

The MRC began as the Medical Research Committee, established in 1913 to administer funds under the National Insurance Act, initially concentrating on tuberculosis research. It received a Royal Charter in 1920 and became the Medical Research Council. During the twentieth century it supported landmark work including the development of penicillin's therapeutic use, the first randomized controlled trial (of streptomycin, 1948), and research linking smoking to lung cancer.

Research

The MRC funds research through grants to universities, through its own institutes and units, and through large centres. Its flagship institute is the Laboratory of Molecular Biology (LMB) in Cambridge, founded in 1947, whose scientists have won numerous Nobel Prizes for work including the structure of proteins and DNA sequencing. Other major establishments include the MRC Harwell Institute and the London Institute of Medical Sciences. In 2018 the MRC became part of UKRI.

Notable achievements

Research supported by the MRC has led to the development of MRI scanning, monoclonal antibodies, DNA fingerprinting's underlying science, and the discovery of the influenza virus. More than 30 Nobel laureates have been associated with MRC-funded research.
