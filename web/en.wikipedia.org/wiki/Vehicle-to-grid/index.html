Vehicle-to-grid

Vehicle-to-grid (V2G) describes a system in which plug-in electric vehicles (PEVs) sell demand response services to the grid. Demand services are either delivering electricity to the grid or reducing the rate of charging. Such vehicles include battery electric vehicles (BEVs), plug-in hybrids (PHEVs), and hydrogen vehicles.

The concept rests on the observation that most vehicles are parked for the great majority of the day, while their batteries hold substantial stored energy. Aggregated into a virtual power plant, a fleet of vehicles can provide grid services comparable to dedicated storage installations.

Services

  • Peak shaving and load levelling: discharging during periods of high demand and charging when demand (and prices) are low.
  • Frequency regulation: rapid small adjustments of charge and discharge to stabilise grid frequency — one of the most valuable V2G services in early pilot studies.
  • Spinning reserves and backup: stored energy available on short notice.
  • Renewable energy integration: absorbing surplus solar and wind generation and releasing it later.

Technology

V2G requires a bidirectional charger and communication between the vehicle, the charging equipment, and an aggregator or grid operator. The CHAdeMO standard supported bidirectional power flow early on; the CCS standard added it through the ISO 15118-20 specification. Smart charging (V1G), in which only the timing and rate of charging is controlled, is a simpler precursor.

Challenges

Barriers include battery degradation from extra cycling, the cost of bidirectional chargers, regulatory and interconnection rules, and the need to guarantee that vehicles retain enough charge for their owners' travel needs. Pilot projects in Denmark, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, the United States, and Japan have tested the technical and commercial models.
