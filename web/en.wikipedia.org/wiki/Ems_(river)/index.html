The Ems (German: Ems; Dutch: Eems) is a river in northwestern Germany, 371 kilometres (231 mi) long. It flows through the states of North Rhine-Westphalia and Lower Saxony, rising in the Senne near Hövelhof on the slopes of the Teutoburg Forest and emptying into the Dollart Bay estuary of the North Sea near Emden, where its lower course forms part of the border with the Netherlands.

Course

From its source the Ems flows through Rheda-Wiedenbrück, Gütersloh, Warendorf, Telgte (near Münster), Greven, Emsdetten, Rheine and Lingen before crossing the Emsland through Meppen, Haren, Lathen, Papenburg and Leer to Emden and the sea. Its principal tributaries include the Werse, Hase and Leda.

Navigation and industry

The river is navigable for much of its length and is linked to the Ruhr by the Dortmund–Ems Canal. The lower Ems at Papenburg is the site of the Meyer Werft shipyard, from which large cruise ships are conveyed down the river to the sea with the aid of the Ems storm-surge barrier (Emssperrwerk) near Gandersum, completed in 2002. Plans to deepen the river have been controversial because of ecological damage to the tidal flats.
