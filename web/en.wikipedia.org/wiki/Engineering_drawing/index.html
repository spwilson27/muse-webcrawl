Engineering drawing

An engineering drawing is a type of technical drawing that discloses in full the requirements for manufacturing or constructing engineered items. It is a graphical language governed by standardised conventions for layout, projection, dimensioning, symbols, and tolerances, so that a part can be made exactly as intended by any competent manufacturer without further explanation. Engineering drawings are legal documents: the drawing, not the designer's informal intent, defines the product for contractual purposes.

Views and projection

Objects are usually represented in orthographic projection, with front, top, and side views arranged according to first-angle projection (used in most of Europe and Asia, per ISO standards) or third-angle projection (standard in the United States, per ASME Y14). Section views reveal internal features, auxiliary views show inclined surfaces in true shape, and pictorial views such as isometric drawings may be added for clarity.

Dimensioning and tolerancing

Dimensions define size and location, and each carries an explicit or general tolerance because no manufacturing process is exact. Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T) refines this by specifying permissible variation in form, orientation, location, and runout of features, referenced to datums. Surface finish symbols, thread notes, and material and heat-treatment specifications complete the manufacturing definition.

Standards and production

International practice is governed by ISO technical drawing standards and, in the United States, the ASME Y14 series (notably Y14.5 for dimensioning and tolerancing). Drawings carry a title block recording the part name and number, scale, material, revision, and approvals, together with a parts list on assembly drawings. Historically produced by hand on drawing boards with drafting machines, drawings today are usually generated from 3D CAD models in software such as AutoCAD, SolidWorks, and CATIA, although the conventions of the drawing sheet remain essentially those of the manual era.
