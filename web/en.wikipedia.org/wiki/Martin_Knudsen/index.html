Martin Knudsen

Martin Hans Christian Knudsen
Born: February 15, 1871, Hasmark, Denmark
Died: May 27, 1949 (aged 78), Copenhagen, Denmark
Nationality: Danish
Fields: Physics, oceanography
Known for: Knudsen number, Knudsen effusion, Knudsen layer

Martin Hans Christian Knudsen (February 15, 1871 – May 27, 1949) was a Danish physicist and oceanographer whose name is attached to several fundamental concepts in rarefied gas dynamics and physical oceanography.

Physics

Knudsen studied the flow of gases at very low pressures, where the mean free path of molecules becomes comparable to the size of the container. The Knudsen number (Kn) — the ratio of molecular mean free path to a representative physical length scale — distinguishes continuum flow from molecular flow regimes. He also described Knudsen effusion, Knudsen diffusion in porous media, and the Knudsen layer adjacent to surfaces, and he developed the Knudsen absolute manometer for measuring very low pressures.

Oceanography

As leader of Denmark's hydrographic work and a key figure in the International Council for the Exploration of the Sea, Knudsen developed the standard methods for defining and measuring the salinity and chlorinity of seawater; his Knudsen titration and hydrographical tables were used worldwide for decades. He also took part in the Ingolf expeditions to Icelandic and Greenland waters.

Career

Knudsen was a professor at the University of Copenhagen and served as its rector. He received numerous honours, including election to foreign academies and the Alexander Agassiz Medal.
