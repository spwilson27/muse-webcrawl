Nusaybin (Kurdish: Nisêbîn; ancient Nisibis) is a town and district of Mardin Province in south-eastern Turkey, situated on the border with Syria, directly across from the Syrian city of Qamishli. It stands on the Jaghjagh River at the foot of the Tur Abdin hills, on the edge of the Mesopotamian plain, and has a predominantly Kurdish population, with Arab and Syriac Christian minorities.

History

Nusaybin is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, attested as Našibīna in Assyrian sources of the ninth century BCE. As Nisibis it was a major fortress and trading city on the frontier between the Roman Empire and Persia, repeatedly fought over and changing hands. In 363 CE, after the failed Persian expedition of the emperor Julian, the emperor Jovian ceded Nisibis to Sasanian Persia, and its Christian population was evacuated. The city was an early centre of Syriac Christianity: Saint Jacob of Nisibis was its first bishop in the fourth century, and the School of Nisibis became one of the great theological schools of the Church of the East; the poet-theologian Ephrem the Syrian taught there before the Persian takeover. Nusaybin later passed through Arab, Seljuk, Mongol and Ottoman rule. In the twentieth century the border drawn between Turkey and French-mandate Syria split it from its twin city of Qamishli.

Recent events

During the Kurdish–Turkish conflict, Nusaybin saw heavy fighting in 2015–2016 between the Turkish military and Kurdish militants affiliated with the PKK; a long curfew was imposed and large parts of several neighbourhoods were destroyed, displacing many residents. Reconstruction has proceeded slowly since. Landmarks include the church and tomb of Saint Jacob (Mor Yakup) and the Zeynel Abidin mosque complex.
