St Martin's Lane

St Martin's Lane is a street in the City of Westminster in London, England. It runs from the Seven Dials area in the north to Charing Cross in the south, passing through the heart of London's Theatreland in the West End. The street takes its name from the church of St Martin-in-the-Fields, which stands at its southern end near Trafalgar Square.

St Martin's Lane looking south towards Charing Cross

History

The lane follows the line of an old track that connected the villages of Charing and St Giles. It developed during the 17th century as the surrounding area was built up, and by the 18th century it had become a fashionable address associated with artists, furniture makers and tradesmen serving the West End. The street was home to a number of notable cabinet-makers and upholsterers, and later became known for its theatres and places of entertainment.

Theatres

Several well-known theatres stand on or just off St Martin's Lane, including the London Coliseum, home of English National Opera, the Duke of York's Theatre, the Noël Coward Theatre and the Wyndham's Theatre on the adjoining Charing Cross Road. The presence of so many venues has made the street one of the centres of London's theatrical life.

Notable buildings

The street also contains the Salisbury public house, a listed Victorian pub noted for its ornate interior, and a number of bookshops and cafés serving theatregoers. At its southern end the lane opens towards Trafalgar Square and the National Gallery.
