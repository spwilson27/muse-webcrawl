Rocket propellant

Rocket propellant is the reaction mass of a rocket: material stored in the vehicle, usually in tanks, that is ejected or undergoes a chemical reaction to produce thrust. In chemical rockets the propellant consists of a fuel and an oxidiser that burn in a combustion chamber, producing hot gas that expands through a nozzle. Propellants are characterised principally by their specific impulse, density, storage requirements and toxicity.

Types

Liquid propellants include cryogenic combinations such as liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen (high performance, difficult storage) and storable pairs such as RP-1 kerosene with liquid oxygen, or hypergolic combinations like monomethylhydrazine with nitrogen tetroxide, which ignite on contact. Solid propellants — composites such as ammonium perchlorate composite propellant (APCP) or double-base formulations — are simpler and storable but cannot be throttled or stopped once ignited. Hybrid rockets combine a solid fuel with a liquid or gaseous oxidiser.
