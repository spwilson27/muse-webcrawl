Ahmed Kathrada

Ahmed Mohamed Kathrada
Born: 21 August 1929, Schweizer-Reneke, Transvaal, Union of South Africa
Died: 28 March 2017 (aged 87), Johannesburg, South Africa
Political party: African National Congress
Known for: Anti-apartheid activism; Rivonia Trial; Robben Island prisoner

Ahmed Mohamed Kathrada (21 August 1929 – 28 March 2017), sometimes known by the nickname "Kathy", was a South African politician and anti-apartheid activist. A close associate of Nelson Mandela and Walter Sisulu, he was one of the eight men convicted at the Rivonia Trial of 1963–64 and spent 26 years in prison, most of them on Robben Island.

Early activism

Kathrada was born in Schweizer-Reneke in the western Transvaal to Indian immigrant parents. Barred from local schools by segregation, he was educated in Johannesburg, where he joined the Transvaal Indian Youth Congress at age 12 and campaigned in the 1946 Passive Resistance campaign. He later worked with the ANC Youth League and the Congress movement, was a defendant in the 1952 Defiance Campaign trials and the 1956 Treason Trial (all accused were acquitted), and went underground after the ANC was banned in 1960.

Rivonia and imprisonment

Arrested at Liliesleaf Farm in Rivonia in July 1963, Kathrada was convicted of sabotage in June 1964 and sentenced to life imprisonment. He served 18 years on Robben Island and a further term at Pollsmoor Prison, studying by correspondence and earning several degrees while incarcerated. Released in October 1989, he was elected to the ANC's National Executive Committee in 1991.

Later life

After South Africa's first democratic elections in 1994 Kathrada served as a Member of Parliament and as political adviser to President Mandela. He chaired the Robben Island Museum Council and established the Ahmed Kathrada Foundation in 2008 to promote non-racialism and constitutional values. In 2016 he publicly called on President Jacob Zuma to resign amid corruption scandals. He died in Johannesburg in March 2017 and received an official funeral, at which Zuma's absence was widely noted.
