Butte is a consolidated city-county in southwestern Montana, United States, and the seat of Silver Bow County. Founded as a mining camp in the 1860s, it grew into one of the largest and most notorious boomtowns of the American West, built on gold, then silver, and above all copper.

Mining history

Butte's hill produced immense copper wealth, earning it the title "the Richest Hill on Earth". The Anaconda Copper Mining Company, associated with copper kings such as Marcus Daly and William A. Clark, dominated the city, which became a stronghold of organised labour and the International Union of Mine, Mill and Smelter Workers. Mining shifted to open pits in the 1950s with the Berkeley Pit; when it closed in 1982 it filled with acidic, metal-contaminated water, becoming part of one of the largest Superfund sites in the United States.

Culture

Butte retains a large historic uptown district, a strong Irish-American heritage celebrated on St. Patrick's Day and at the Montana Folk Festival, and landmarks including the "Our Lady of the Rockies" statue on the Continental Divide. The daredevil Evel Knievel was a native son, honoured annually at Evel Knievel Days.
