Sun Ce (175–200), courtesy name Bofu, was a Chinese military general, politician, and warlord who lived during the late Eastern Han dynasty of China. He was the eldest son of Sun Jian, a general who served under the warlord Yuan Shu. After Sun Jian's death, Sun Ce endured a period of service under Yuan Shu before breaking away in 195 to conquer the territories in the Jiangdong region south of the Yangtze River, laying the foundations of the state of Eastern Wu in the subsequent Three Kingdoms period.

Conquest of Jiangdong

Using his father's old troops — lent to him by Yuan Shu in exchange for the Imperial Seal Sun Jian had found in the ruins of Luoyang — Sun Ce crossed the Yangtze in 195 and defeated a string of local warlords and governors, including Liu Yao, Yan Baihu and Wang Lang. His rapid, striking campaigns earned him the nickname "the Little Conqueror" (Xiao Bawang). By 200 he controlled the commanderies of Wu, Kuaiji, Danyang, Yuzhang and Lujiang, and had recruited able officers such as Zhou Yu, Taishi Ci, Cheng Pu and Huang Gai.

Death and succession

In 200, while preparing a campaign northward, Sun Ce was ambushed and gravely wounded by assassins — retainers of Xu Gong, a former governor he had executed. He died shortly afterwards at the age of 25, entrusting his seals and his forces to his younger brother Sun Quan, with the famous charge that Sun Quan was better suited to ruling than to conquering. Sun Quan went on to found Eastern Wu and declare himself emperor in 229, posthumously honouring Sun Ce as Prince Huan of Changsha.

In popular culture

Sun Ce is a major character in the 14th-century historical novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms and appears as a playable character in the Dynasty Warriors video game series, where he is depicted wielding tonfas.
