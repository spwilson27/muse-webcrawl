The Theatre was an Elizabethan playhouse in Shoreditch, London, built in 1576 by the actor-manager James Burbage. It is generally regarded as the first permanent, purpose-built public playhouse in England, and the model for the great Elizabethan amphitheatres that followed, including the Curtain, the Rose, the Swan, and the Globe.

History

Burbage, a joiner by trade, leased land in Shoreditch — outside the City of London's jurisdiction, where the authorities disapproved of plays — and erected a polygonal, open-air timber theatre with galleries around a yard. It opened in 1576 with Burbage's company, Leicester's Men. From 1594 it was the home of the Lord Chamberlain's Men, the company of William Shakespeare, who was a sharer and its principal playwright; many of Shakespeare's early plays were first performed there.

The Globe connection

After James Burbage's death in 1597, a dispute with the ground landlord, Giles Allen, over renewal of the lease made the site untenable. On the night of 28 December 1598, the Burbage brothers Cuthbert and Richard, with a company of workmen, dismantled The Theatre's timbers and carried them across the Thames to Bankside, where they were reused in building the Globe Theatre in 1599. Excavations in 2008 uncovered part of The Theatre's foundations in Shoreditch.
