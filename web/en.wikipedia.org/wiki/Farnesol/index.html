Farnesol

Farnesol is a natural 15-carbon organic compound which is an acyclic sesquiterpene alcohol. Under standard conditions, it is a colorless liquid. It is hydrophobic, and thus insoluble in water, but miscible with oils. Its molecular formula is C15H26O.

Occurrence and uses

Farnesol is present in many essential oils including citronella, neroli, cyclamen, rose, and tuberose. It is used in perfumery to emphasize the odors of sweet, floral perfumes, and as a flavoring ingredient. It is produced by plants and by some animals, including humans, as an intermediate in the mevalonate pathway — farnesyl pyrophosphate is a precursor in the biosynthesis of sterols and other terpenes.

Biological roles

Farnesol is a quorum-sensing molecule in the pathogenic yeast Candida albicans, where it inhibits the transition from yeast to filamentous (hyphal) growth and influences biofilm formation. It has also been studied for antibacterial, antifungal, and potential anticancer properties, and it functions as a semiochemical in some insects.

Farnesol's name derives from the farnese acacia tree (Vachellia farnesiana), from whose flowers the compound was historically extracted.
