Ducasse de Mons

The Ducasse de Mons, also known as the Doudou, is a traditional folk festival held each year in Mons, Belgium, on Trinity Sunday (the Sunday after Pentecost). The festival centres on the re-enactment of the combat between Saint George and a dragon, known as the Lumeçon, and on the procession and descent of the shrine (châsse) of Saint Waltrude (Waundru), the city's patron saint. In 2005 UNESCO proclaimed the Ducasse de Mons a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, and it was inscribed in 2008 on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, together with the Ducasse d'Ath, as the "Ducasses of Mons and Ath".

The festival

Festivities begin on Saturday with the ceremonial descent of the shrine of St Waltrude from the collegiate church. On Sunday morning the golden coach, the Car d'Or, carries the shrine in a grand procession through the city, accompanied by guilds, brotherhoods, and figures representing the history of Mons. In the afternoon, tens of thousands of spectators gather in the Grand-Place for the Lumeçon: Saint George, on horseback and then on foot, fights the dragon — an 11-metre-long wicker beast whose tail the crowd tries to pluck for good luck — aided by traditional characters including the Chins-Chins, devils, leaf-men (hommes de feuilles), and wild men. The dragon is finally slain to cheers of "Et les Montois ne périront pas!" ("And the people of Mons shall not perish!").

History

The procession dates to 1349, when the relics of St Waltrude were carried through Mons to ward off the plague. The combat of Saint George is documented from the fifteenth century and was organized by the Brotherhood of Saint George. The festival has continued, with interruptions during wars and in 2020–2021, for more than six centuries and is the principal celebration of the city's identity.
