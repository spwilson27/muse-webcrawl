Mystery religions

Mystery religions, mystery cults, or sacred mysteries were religious schools of the Greco-Roman world for which participation was reserved to initiates (mystai). The main characterisation is the secrecy associated with particulars of initiation and ritual practice, which may not be revealed to outsiders.

By means of ecstatic dance, music, and secret rites, initiates sought a transformative experience and hoped for a better afterlife. Famous mysteries include the Eleusinian Mysteries of Demeter and Persephone, the Dionysian Mysteries, the Orphic Mysteries, and the cults of Isis, Mithras, and Cybele.

Influence

The mysteries flourished in late antiquity and influenced early Christianity and Gnosticism before being suppressed in the 4th and 5th centuries CE.
