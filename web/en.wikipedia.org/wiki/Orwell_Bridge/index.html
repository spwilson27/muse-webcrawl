Orwell Bridge

The Orwell Bridge over the River Orwell

Carries: A14 road

Crosses: River Orwell

Locale: Ipswich, Suffolk, England

Total length: 1,287 m (4,222 ft)

Longest span: 190 m (620 ft)

Opened: 17 December 1982

The Orwell Bridge is a concrete box girder bridge just south of Ipswich in Suffolk, England. It carries the A14 road over the River Orwell estuary between Wherstead (west) and Warren Heath (east). Opened to road traffic in 1982, the bridge forms part of the Ipswich southern bypass and is a key link on the route between the Midlands and the port of Felixstowe, one of Britain's busiest container ports.

Design and construction

The bridge is 1,287 metres (4,222 ft) long with a main span of 190 metres (620 ft) and nineteen spans in total. Construction began in October 1979 and was completed in 1982 at a cost of about £16.6 million. The designer was Sir William Halcrow and Partners, and the structure was built using the balanced cantilever method, with hollow pre-stressed concrete box sections.

Operation and closures

The bridge carries around 60,000 vehicles per day. Because of its exposed position high above the estuary, it is liable to closure to high-sided vehicles, and occasionally to all traffic, during high winds. Such closures cause severe congestion in Ipswich, and various measures have been proposed to keep the bridge open or manage traffic during closures, including reduced speed limits and the use of the bridge by one lane in each direction.
