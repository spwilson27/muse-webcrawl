Magdeburg Centuries

The Magdeburg Centuries is an ecclesiastical history, divided into thirteen volumes, each covering one century of the Christian church from the first to the thirteenth century. It was published in Basel between 1559 and 1574 by a group of Lutheran scholars led by Matthias Flacius Illyricus, and working in Magdeburg — hence the authors' collective name, the Centuriators of Magdeburg.

Purpose

The Centuries were written to demonstrate that throughout the history of the church, true (that is, proto-Protestant) Christianity had survived in opposition to the corruptions of the papacy, which the authors identified with Antichrist. Each century treated systematically under headings covering the church's spread, doctrine, rites, heresies and councils.

Method and influence

The work was a landmark of historical scholarship: the Centuriators assembled enormous quantities of primary material, including unpublished manuscripts, and organised it in a consistent framework. It provoked a massive Catholic response, the Annales Ecclesiastici of Cardinal Caesar Baronius, and together the two works founded modern church historiography.
