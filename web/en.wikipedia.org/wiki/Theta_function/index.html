Theta function

Theta functions
Type: special functions of several complex variables
Introduced by: Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi (1829, Fundamenta Nova)
Notation: θ(z; τ), with nome q = eπiτ
Closely related: elliptic functions, modular forms, the heat equation, Riemann surfaces

In mathematics, theta functions are special functions of several complex variables, first systematically studied by Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi. They are power series in a variable traditionally written as a "nome" q, whose coefficients are themselves exponential in a complex parameter. Theta functions are fundamental in the theory of elliptic functions and modular forms, and they reappear throughout number theory, mathematical physics, and the theory of Abelian varieties.

Definition

The prototypical Jacobi theta function is

θ(z; τ) = Σn=−∞∞ exp(πin2τ + 2πinz),

where τ lies in the upper half-plane. Four theta functions with characteristics, traditionally θ1, θ2, θ3, θ4, are obtained by shifting z by half-periods and by half-integer shifts in the summation index. The function θ3 in particular, evaluated at z = 0, gives the theta constant whose powers appear in the expansions of modular forms.

Properties

Theta functions are quasi-periodic in z: shifting z by a period changes θ by a simple exponential factor, which is exactly the property needed to build doubly periodic (elliptic) functions as quotients of theta functions. Under the modular transformation τ → −1/τ, a theta function transforms into a multiple of another theta function — a consequence of the Poisson summation formula. This identity is also the solution formula for the one-dimensional heat equation: the theta function is essentially the heat kernel on a circle, a fact Jacobi exploited, and theta functions accordingly satisfy the heat equation in (z, τ).

Jacobi's triple product identity expresses θ as an infinite product, from which flow many classical identities, including the representations of partitions in number theory. The derivatives of theta functions at z = 0 — the "theta constants" and their derivatives — generate the ring of modular forms for certain congruence subgroups.

Generalisations and applications

Riemann generalised theta functions to several variables using the period matrix of a compact Riemann surface; the resulting Riemann theta function vanishes precisely on an associated theta divisor and is central to the theory of Jacobians and Abelian varieties. Theta functions appear in the analytic theory of partitions (Hardy–Ramanujan–Rademacher), in string theory and conformal field theory, in the construction of error-correcting codes and sphere packings via theta series of lattices, and in quantum mechanics, where they describe wavefunctions on a torus.
