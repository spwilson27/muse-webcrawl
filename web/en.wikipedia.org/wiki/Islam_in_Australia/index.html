Islam in Australia

Islam in Australia is the country's second-largest religion. According to the 2021 census, about 813,000 people, or 3.2% of the population, identified as Muslim. Australian Muslims come from a wide variety of ethnic backgrounds, the largest groups being Lebanese, Turkish, Afghan, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, and Indonesian.

History

Early contacts

Muslim fishermen and traders from Makassar, in what is now Indonesia, visited northern Australia for centuries before European settlement, trading for trepang (sea cucumber) with Aboriginal peoples and leaving influences on Yolngu culture and language.

Cameleers

In the 19th century, Muslim cameleers — mostly from British India (including present-day Pakistan) and Afghanistan, collectively called "Afghans" — were brought to work in the arid interior, playing a crucial role in exploration and in building infrastructure such as the Overland Telegraph Line. They built some of Australia's first mosques, including in Adelaide (1888) and Broken Hill.

Post-war immigration

Following World War II, larger-scale Muslim immigration began, first from Turkey under a 1967 agreement, then from Lebanon during and after the Lebanese Civil War, and later from many other countries, as well as through refugee programs. The community grew further with Australian-born generations.

Demographics

Most Australian Muslims live in Sydney and Melbourne, particularly in areas such as Lakemba and Auburn in Sydney. The community is majority Sunni, with Shia, Ahmadiyya, and other minorities. There are over 150 mosques and many Islamic schools in Australia.

Organisations

The Australian Federation of Islamic Councils (AFIC), founded in 1964, is a peak body. The Grand Mufti of Australia is a senior religious figurehead; the role has been held by figures including Taj El-Din Hilaly and Ibrahim Abu Mohamed.

Controversies

The community has faced public debate and episodes of Islamophobia, notably after the September 11 attacks and the 2005 Cronulla riots, as well as controversies involving sermons by high-profile clerics. The 2014 Lindt Cafe siege in Sydney was perpetrated by a lone extremist.
