Treaty of Alexandropol

The Treaty of Alexandropol was a peace treaty signed on 3 December 1920 between the First Republic of Armenia and the Grand National Assembly of Turkey (the Turkish national movement), ending the Turkish–Armenian War. It was signed in Alexandropol (modern Gyumri).

Terms

Under the treaty, Armenia renounced the Treaty of Sèvres and its claims to Western Armenia, ceded the Kars region and other territory to Turkey, accepted a drastic reduction of its armed forces, and agreed to drop demands for an independent Armenian state in eastern Anatolia. The Armenian territory left by the settlement amounted to roughly half of the republic's pre-war extent.

Fate

The treaty was never ratified: on the very day of signature, Soviet forces entered Yerevan and the Armenian government that signed it fell. The Soviet Armenian and Soviet Russian governments repudiated it. The Turkish–Soviet frontier was instead settled by the Treaty of Moscow (1921) and the Treaty of Kars (1921), which broadly confirmed the territorial outcome while annulling Alexandropol formally. The border drawn then remains the modern Armenia–Turkey frontier.
