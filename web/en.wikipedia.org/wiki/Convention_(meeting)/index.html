Convention (meeting)

A convention is a meeting of individuals or groups who assemble at an arranged place and time in order to discuss or engage in some common interest. The most common conventions are based upon industry, profession, or fandom. Trade conventions typically focus on a particular industry or industry segment, while fan conventions are organized around shared cultural interests such as science fiction, anime, or gaming.

Types

  • Political conventions — gatherings of political parties to select candidates and adopt platforms, such as the national nominating conventions held in the United States before presidential elections.
  • Trade conventions and expositions — industry events where companies exhibit products and professionals network; often held in large convention centers.
  • Professional and academic conferences — meetings for researchers and practitioners to present papers and exchange findings.
  • Fan conventions — celebrations of popular culture featuring panels, costumes, and guest appearances.
  • Religious conventions — large assemblies of a faith community for worship, instruction, and organization.

Organization

Conventions are commonly organized by professional associations, societies, or commercial event companies. Large events may draw tens of thousands of attendees and require dedicated convention centers with exhibition halls, meeting rooms, and adjacent hotel capacity. Programming typically includes keynote speeches, panel discussions, workshops, exhibits, and social events.

The word "convention" also carries the broader meaning of an established practice or norm, but in the meeting sense it dates to usages describing formal assemblies of delegates, ecclesiastical bodies, and representative gatherings.
