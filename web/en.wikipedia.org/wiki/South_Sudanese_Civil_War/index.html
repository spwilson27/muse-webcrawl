South Sudanese Civil War
Date: December 2013 – February 2020 (peace agreement 2018)
Location: South Sudan
Belligerents: Government forces (SPLA) vs. SPLM-IO and allies

The South Sudanese Civil War was a conflict in South Sudan between forces loyal to President Salva Kiir and those loyal to his former deputy Riek Machar. It began in December 2013, only two years after South Sudan's independence from Sudan, amid a political power struggle within the ruling SPLM that erupted into fighting in the capital, Juba, and took on ethnic dimensions between Dinka and Nuer communities.

Course of the war

Fighting spread across the country, particularly in Unity, Jonglei, and Upper Nile states. Massacres, widespread sexual violence, and attacks on civilians were documented by the United Nations and human rights groups. A 2015 peace agreement collapsed in July 2016 when fighting resumed in Juba. A "revitalised" peace agreement was signed in September 2018, and a unity government was formed in February 2020.

Humanitarian impact

Studies estimated the death toll at around 380,000 or more, including from war-related famine and disease. Millions were displaced internally or fled to neighbouring countries, and famine was declared in parts of Unity State in 2017 — the first famine declared anywhere in the world in six years.
