Contemporary hit radio

Contemporary hit radio (abbreviated CHR) is a radio format that is common in many countries focusing on playing current and recurrent popular music as determined by the Top 40 music charts. The term "contemporary hit radio" was coined in the early 1980s by Radio & Records magazine to designate Top 40 stations which continued to play hits from all musical genres as popular music became more fragmented.

Format

CHR stations play a short playlist of current hit songs in heavy rotation, interspersed with presenter talk, jingles, competitions, news and entertainment segments. The format targets a young demographic, principally listeners aged roughly 12 to 34. Sub-formats include mainstream CHR, rhythmic contemporary (CHR/dance and urban-leaning playlists), and adult CHR, which leans toward adult contemporary crossovers.

History

The format descends from the Top 40 format developed in the United States in the 1950s by broadcasters such as Todd Storz and Gordon McLendon. During the late 1970s and early 1980s, many Top 40 stations on AM declined as music listening shifted to FM. Trade publications introduced the CHR designation to distinguish stations still committed to a broad, chart-based playlist from album-oriented rock and adult contemporary formats. CHR enjoyed a major revival on FM in the 1980s, exemplified by stations such as WHTZ (Z100) in New York and KIIS-FM in Los Angeles.

Programming

CHR playlists are driven by sales, streaming and airplay chart data. Songs are categorised by expected familiarity and rotated according to daypart. Morning shows typically emphasise personality and talk, while other dayparts emphasise music sweeps and contesting.
