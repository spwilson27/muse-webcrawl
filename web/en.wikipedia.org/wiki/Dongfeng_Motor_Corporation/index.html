Dongfeng Motor Corporation Ltd. (Chinese: 东风汽车集团有限公司; "Dongfeng" means "east wind") is a Chinese state-owned automobile manufacturer headquartered in Wuhan. Founded in 1969 as the Second Automobile Works in Shiyan, it is one of China's "Big Four" state carmakers and a major producer of commercial vehicles, with annual sales in the millions.

History

The company was established in the mountains of Hubei under Mao-era "Third Front" policy, building trucks — including military vehicles — in dispersed workshops. Reorganised as Dongfeng ("East Wind", from a Mao poem) in the reform era, it formed landmark joint ventures: with Citroën (1992), Honda, Nissan (a comprehensive partnership in 2003), Renault, and the former PSA Group (in which it took a stake in 2014, later part of Stellantis).

Operations

Dongfeng sells vehicles under its own marques — including Aeolus (Fengshen), the premium EV brand Voyah, and the off-road Mengshi line — and builds cars for its foreign partners in plants across China. Its Hong Kong–listed arm is Dongfeng Motor Group. Like its peers, Dongfeng has invested heavily in electric vehicles and exports, including military trucks supplied to the People's Liberation Army.
