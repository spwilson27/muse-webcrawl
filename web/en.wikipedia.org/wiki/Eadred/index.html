Eadred (also Edred; c. 923 – 23 November 955) was King of the English from 26 May 946 until his death in 955. He was the younger son of Edward the Elder and his third wife Eadgifu, and the grandson of Alfred the Great. He succeeded his elder brother Edmund I, who was killed in 946, and his reign was dominated by the final conquest of the Viking kingdom of York, which brought all of England under a single ruler for the first time on a lasting basis.

Reign

When Eadred came to the throne, Northumbria was wavering in its allegiance and accepted the Norse king Olaf Sihtricson, and later Erik Bloodaxe, as king of York. Eadred responded with a devastating raid into Northumbria in 948, burning the minster at Ripon, and after years of shifting control the Northumbrians finally expelled Erik — who was killed in 954, traditionally at Stainmore — and submitted definitively to Eadred. From 954 England remained united under the West Saxon dynasty.

Eadred suffered throughout his reign from a serious illness — possibly a digestive disorder — which contemporary accounts describe as so severe that he could scarcely eat, and which was probably the cause of his death at about the age of 32. Government was therefore conducted largely through his leading counsellors, notably his mother Eadgifu, the ealdorman Æthelstan Half-King, and the great churchmen Archbishop Oda and Dunstan, abbot of Glastonbury, whom Eadred strongly patronised. He was notably generous to the monasteries, granting land to Glastonbury, Abingdon and other houses and laying foundations for the monastic reform movement that flourished under his successors.

Death and succession

Eadred died unmarried and childless at Frome, Somerset, on 23 November 955 and was buried in the Old Minster, Winchester. He was succeeded by his nephew Eadwig, the elder son of Edmund I.
