Rajas

OriginIndian philosophy (Samkhya, Yoga, Ayurveda)
NatureOne of the three gunas
QualitiesActivity, passion, movement, attachment
Associated colourRed

Rajas (Sanskrit: रजस्) is one of the three gunas (qualities or tendencies) in Hindu philosophy, particularly in the Samkhya school. The three gunas are sattva (goodness, harmony, purity), rajas (passion, activity, movement) and tamas (darkness, inertia, ignorance). All three are present in every person and object in varying proportions.

Characteristics

Rajas is the quality of energy, change and striving. It is associated with desire, ambition, restlessness, attachment to action and its fruits, and the drive that motivates human endeavour. When rajas predominates in a person, they may be energetic and passionate but also agitated, anxious and prone to craving and frustration.

In the Bhagavad Gita

The Bhagavad Gita (especially chapter 14) describes rajas as born of desire and attachment, binding the soul through attachment to action. Food, occupations and even forms of knowledge and happiness are classified in the Gita as rajasic when they are stimulating, restless or pursued for reward.

In Ayurveda

In Ayurvedic practice, an excess of rajas in the mind is treated through calming routines, meditation and a sattvic diet, aiming to restore balance among the gunas.
