Versailles

Versailles is a commune in the Yvelines department of Île-de-France, about 19 km south-west of Paris. It is world-famous for the Palace of Versailles, the seat of French royal power under Louis XIV, and gave its name to the Treaty of Versailles of 1919.

Palace

What began as Louis XIII's hunting lodge was transformed by Louis XIV from 1661 into Europe's grandest palace, with the Hall of Mirrors, the Royal Chapel, and vast gardens designed by André Le Nôtre featuring the Grand Canal, fountains and the Orangerie. The court moved there in 1682. The palace remained the centre of power until the French Revolution in 1789, when the royal family was forced back to Paris.

Town

The town grew up to serve the court, laid out on avenues radiating from the palace. Versailles hosted the signing of treaties ending the American Revolutionary War (1783) and the proclamation of the German Empire in the Hall of Mirrors (1871), as well as the 1919 peace treaty after the First World War.

Today

With about 85,000 inhabitants, Versailles is an affluent administrative and university city; the palace and gardens, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, receive several million visitors each year.
