Christchurch (Māori: Ōtautahi) is the largest city in the South Island of New Zealand and the seat of the Canterbury Region. Lying on the Canterbury Plains near the Pacific coast, just north of Banks Peninsula, it is known as the "Garden City" for its many parks and tree-lined residential streets, and for the Avon River / Ōtākaro that winds through its centre.

History

The area was settled by Māori from about 1250–1300. Organised European settlement began in 1850 when the "First Four Ships" brought Canterbury Association settlers; the city was named after Christ Church, Oxford. It became a city by royal charter on 31 July 1856, making it officially the oldest established city in New Zealand.

Earthquakes

A magnitude 7.1 earthquake struck in September 2010, followed by a devastating magnitude 6.3 quake on 22 February 2011 that killed 185 people, destroyed much of the central city including the ChristChurch Cathedral spire, and caused widespread liquefaction. A long rebuild followed, producing new precincts, the transitional Cardboard Cathedral, and a redesigned central city.

Geography and culture

Christchurch serves as the gateway to Antarctica, hosting Antarctic programmes of several nations at its airport. Attractions include Hagley Park, the Botanic Gardens, punting on the Avon, and the nearby port town of Lyttelton.
