West Gate Bridge


The cable-stayed West Gate Bridge over the Yarra River.

The West Gate Bridge is a steel box girder cable-stayed bridge in Melbourne, Victoria, Australia. It spans the Yarra River just north of its mouth into Port Phillip Bay and is a vital link between the city centre and the western suburbs and beyond to Geelong. The bridge is about 2.58 kilometres long, with a main span of 336 metres, and carries ten lanes of traffic on the West Gate Freeway.

Collapse and construction

The bridge is remembered for one of Australia's worst industrial disasters: on 15 October 1970, during construction, a 112-metre span collapsed, killing 35 workers and injuring 18. A royal commission found serious errors in design and construction. The bridge was redesigned and eventually completed, opening on 15 November 1978. A memorial park beneath the bridge commemorates the workers who died, and an annual memorial service is held on the anniversary.

The bridge today

The West Gate Bridge is among the busiest road corridors in Australia, carrying well over 200,000 vehicles on busy days. Between 2009 and 2011 it underwent a major strengthening and widening programme that added a fifth lane in each direction. Flags of Australia and the Aboriginal flag fly from its twin pylons, and the bridge is a prominent landmark of Melbourne's skyline.
