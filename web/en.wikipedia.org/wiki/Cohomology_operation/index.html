Cohomology operation

A cohomology operation is a natural transformation between cohomology functors.

In mathematics, specifically in algebraic topology, a cohomology operation is a family of maps between cohomology groups of spaces that is natural with respect to continuous maps: applying the operation and then mapping, or mapping and then applying the operation, gives the same result. Cohomology operations detect structure invisible to the ordinary cohomology groups themselves and are central to many computations.

Examples

The best-known examples are the Steenrod squares and Steenrod powers, operations on mod-2 and mod-p cohomology introduced by Norman Steenrod in the 1940s, which satisfy axioms (naturality, the Cartan formula for products, and the Adem relations) that let them be computed explicitly. Other examples include the Bockstein homomorphism connecting integral and mod-p cohomology, Pontryagin squares, and cup products themselves viewed as operations.

Formal definition

Formally, a cohomology operation of type (n, q; G, H) assigns to every space X a function Hn(X; G) → Hq(X; H) commuting with maps induced by continuous functions. Operations of all types form algebras—for mod-p cohomology, the Steenrod algebra—which organise the totality of stable primary operations.

Applications

Steenrod operations were decisive in problems such as the Hopf invariant one problem and the classification of exotic spheres via the Adams spectral sequence, whose E2 term is built from the Steenrod algebra. Secondary cohomology operations arise when primary operations vanish and provide subtler invariants.
