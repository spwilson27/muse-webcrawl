Riboflavin

Riboflavin (vitamin B2)
Chemical formulaC17H20N4O6
AppearanceYellow-orange crystalline solid
SolubilitySlightly soluble in water
Dietary sourcesDairy products, eggs, meat, leafy green vegetables, enriched grains
DeficiencyAriboflavinosis

Riboflavin, also known as vitamin B2, is a water-soluble vitamin, one of the B vitamins, essential for energy production and cellular function in humans and other animals. It is the central component of the coenzymes flavin mononucleotide (FMN) and flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD), which serve as electron carriers in a wide range of oxidation–reduction reactions, including the citric acid cycle and the electron transport chain.

Biochemistry

Flavoproteins containing FMN or FAD participate in the metabolism of carbohydrates, fats and amino acids, in the production of red blood cells, and in the activation of other vitamins such as vitamin B6 and folate. Riboflavin is intensely yellow and fluoresces under ultraviolet light; it is sensitive to light and breaks down on exposure, which is why milk is often packaged in opaque containers.

Health

Deficiency (ariboflavinosis) causes sore throat, redness and swelling of the mouth and tongue (glossitis), cracks at the corners of the mouth (angular cheilitis), inflamed skin and light sensitivity. It is uncommon in developed countries, occurring mostly alongside other B-vitamin deficiencies. Riboflavin is used as the food colouring E101, and high-dose riboflavin has been studied for migraine prevention. Excess intake is excreted in urine, turning it bright yellow, and no tolerable upper intake level has been set because of low toxicity.
