Hurricane Patricia

Hurricane Patricia was the most powerful tropical cyclone on record worldwide in terms of maximum sustained winds, reaching 215 mph (345 km/h), and one of the most intense ever measured by barometric pressure (872 mbar). It formed in the eastern Pacific in October 2015 and underwent explosive rapid intensification before making landfall in Jalisco, Mexico, on 23 October 2015.

Meteorological history

Patricia developed from a tropical disturbance near the Gulf of Tehuantepec on 20 October 2015. In an environment of very warm sea surface temperatures and low wind shear, associated with a strong El Niño, it intensified with extraordinary speed: in about 24 hours its winds increased by roughly 120 mph, one of the fastest intensification rates observed. Hurricane Hunter aircraft measured a minimum pressure of 872 mb, the lowest in the Western Hemisphere on record.

Landfall and impact

Patricia made landfall near Emiliano Zapata, Jalisco, as a Category 4/5 hurricane on 23 October. Fortunately it struck a relatively sparsely populated stretch of coast and weakened rapidly over the Sierra Madre mountains. Damage in Mexico was serious locally — flooding, mudslides and destroyed homes — but mass casualties were avoided thanks to evacuations. In total, the storm was associated with around 13 deaths and roughly US$460 million in damage. Its remnants brought heavy rain to Texas.

Records

Patricia surpassed Typhoon Haiyan (2013) in measured sustained winds and remains the strongest tropical cyclone ever reliably recorded globally by wind speed.
