Achene

An achene is a type of simple dry fruit produced by many species of flowering plants. Achenes are monocarpellate (formed from one carpel) and indehiscent (they do not open at maturity to release the seed). The single seed nearly fills the pericarp (fruit wall) but is attached to it only at one point.

Examples

Familiar examples include the "seeds" on the outside of a strawberry — the true fruits, while the fleshy part is swollen receptacle tissue — and the fruits of the aster family such as sunflower and dandelion. The dandelion achene bears a pappus of fine hairs that forms the familiar parachute for wind dispersal.

Related fruit types

Achenes modified with wings are called samaras (as in maple and elm, though these arise from two carpels). A cypsela is the achene-like fruit of the Asteraceae derived from an inferior ovary; a utricle is an achene with a loose, bladder-like wall, as in some amaranths. Grain fruits of grasses, in which the seed coat is fused to the pericarp, are called caryopses.
