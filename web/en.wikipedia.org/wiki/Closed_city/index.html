Closed city

A closed city is a settlement with travel and residency restrictions, so that visitors require special authorisation to enter. Closed cities are most associated with the Soviet Union, which established a large system of them after the Second World War around sensitive military, nuclear, and industrial facilities; many remain closed or partially restricted in Russia today, where they are known formally as ZATOs (zakrytye administrativno-territorialnye obrazovaniya, "closed administrative-territorial formations").

Soviet and Russian closed cities

Soviet closed cities housed nuclear weapons research and production, uranium enrichment, submarine and aerospace work, and other classified programmes. They were often omitted from maps or given codenames based on a nearby larger city — for example, Arzamas-16 (now Sarov) and Chelyabinsk-65 (now Ozyorsk). Residents enjoyed superior supplies and services in exchange for strict secrecy and limits on movement and contact with foreigners. After the fall of the USSR, many cities were renamed and some opened, but around forty ZATOs continue to exist under Russian law, with entry controlled by permit.

Other countries

Analogous restricted areas exist elsewhere: China has closed facilities and towns connected with its nuclear programme, such as the former Plant 404 site; North Korea maintains restricted zones; and military towns and nuclear sites in the United States during the Manhattan Project, such as Los Alamos and Oak Ridge, were effectively closed cities in their early years.
