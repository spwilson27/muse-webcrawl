Studenica Monastery (Serbian: Манастир Студеница) is a 12th-century Serbian Orthodox monastery situated 39 km southwest of Kraljevo, in central Serbia. Founded in 1190 by Stefan Nemanja, founder of the medieval Serbian state, it is one of the largest and richest Serbian monasteries and the mother church of the Nemanjić dynasty's endowments.

Architecture and art

The complex centres on the Church of the Virgin (Bogorodičina crkva), built in the Raška school style combining Romanesque exterior forms with Byzantine spatial concepts, clad in polished white marble. Its frescoes, painted in the early 13th century under the patronage of Saint Sava, include a celebrated monumental Crucifixion, and are considered masterpieces of Byzantine art. The complex also includes the King's Church (1314), famed for its frescoes, and the smaller Church of St. Nicholas.

History

Stefan Nemanja retired to Studenica as the monk Simeon, and his relics were later brought here by his son, Saint Sava, who became the monastery's abbot and wrote its typikon. Studenica remained a major spiritual and cultural centre through the Ottoman period despite repeated attacks, and it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1986.
