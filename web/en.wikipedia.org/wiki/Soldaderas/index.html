Soldaderas


Soldaderas were women camp followers and fighters in the Mexican Revolution.

Soldaderas were women who accompanied armies during the Mexican Revolution (1910–1920), providing food, nursing, and logistical support, and in many cases fighting as soldiers themselves. Also known in popular imagery as Adelitas, they are an enduring symbol of the Revolution.

Roles

Armies of the period had little organised supply, so soldaderas foraged, cooked, carried water and ammunition, cared for the wounded, and buried the dead. Some women joined voluntarily; others followed husbands or were taken by force when troops passed through their villages. A number rose to command troops, often disguising themselves as men, including figures such as Petra Herrera ("Pedro Herrera") and Ángela Jiménez.

Legacy

The image of the soldadera — cartridge belts crossed over her chest, riding on troop trains — became iconic in revolutionary photography, corridos, and later film. Historians have increasingly documented the breadth of women's participation, from cooks and nurses to combatants and officers.
