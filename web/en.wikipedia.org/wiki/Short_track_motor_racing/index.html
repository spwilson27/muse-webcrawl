Short track motor racing

Short track motor racing is a form of automobile racing on an oval track measuring less than one mile (1.6 km), most commonly 1/4, 3/8, 1/2, or 5/8 mile. It is the grassroots foundation of stock car racing in the United States, with thousands of local paved and dirt tracks hosting weekly programs.

Characteristics

Because lap distances are short, races feature constant traffic, frequent contact, and heavy braking, making driver skill in handling lapped cars crucial. Tracks vary in banking, surface (asphalt, concrete, or clay), and shape. Common divisions include late models, modifieds, sprint cars, midget cars, street stocks, and figure-8 racing.

Role in motorsport

Many NASCAR stars, including Dale Earnhardt, Jeff Gordon, and Kyle Larson, began at short tracks. Notable American short tracks include Bristol Motor Speedway (0.533 mi, a NASCAR venue that is unusually short for the national series), Martinsville Speedway (0.526 mi), Bowman Gray Stadium, Eldora Speedway (dirt), and Knoxville Raceway. Sanctioning bodies range from NASCAR's Advance Auto Parts Weekly Series to independent promoters and series such as the World of Outlaws for dirt sprint cars.
