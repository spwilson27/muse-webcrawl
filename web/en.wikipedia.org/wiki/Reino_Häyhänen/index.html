Reino Häyhänen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Reino Häyhänen (14 May 1920 – 17 February 1961) was a Soviet intelligence officer of Finnish descent who operated in the United States under the cover name Eugene Nikolai Maki and the codename VICTOR. He is best known for defecting to the West in 1957 and exposing the Hollow Nickel Case, which led to the arrest of his partner, the Soviet illegal resident Rudolf Abel.

Career

Häyhänen was recruited by Soviet state security during the Second World War and, after service as an NKVD/KGB officer in Finland and Estonia, was prepared for an illegal posting in the United States. He entered the country in 1952 posing as a Finnish-born American labourer and settled in the New York area, where he acted as a support agent and courier for Abel's spy network, communicating through dead drops and hollow coins used to conceal microfilm.

Defection

Recalled to Moscow in 1957 and fearing punishment for his drinking and mishandling of funds, Häyhänen instead presented himself at the United States Embassy in Paris and defected. He revealed the hollow nickel — a specially machined coin containing a microfilmed message that had baffled the FBI since 1953 — and identified Abel, who was arrested in Brooklyn and later exchanged for the captured U-2 pilot Francis Gary Powers in 1962. Häyhänen testified at Abel's trial and afterwards lived under FBI protection; he died in a car accident on the Pennsylvania Turnpike in 1961, in circumstances sometimes described as suspicious.
