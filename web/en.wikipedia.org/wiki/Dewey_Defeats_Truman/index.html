Dewey Defeats Truman

"Dewey Defeats Truman" was the famously incorrect front-page headline of the Chicago Tribune on Wednesday, November 3, 1948, the day after the United States presidential election. The newspaper proclaimed Republican Thomas E. Dewey the winner over the incumbent president, Harry S. Truman — who had in fact won the election in one of the greatest upsets in American political history.

Background

Every major poll and most pundits expected Dewey, governor of New York, to defeat Truman comfortably; pollsters such as Elmo Roper had stopped polling weeks before the election, assuming the result was settled. The Tribune, facing an early deadline because of a printers' strike, went to press relying on its Washington correspondent and on incomplete early returns, and the headline was set before the true trend emerged. Some 150,000 copies were printed before the error was corrected in later editions.

The photograph

Two days later, on November 5, Truman stopped at St. Louis Union Station on his way back to Washington and was handed a copy of the paper. Beaming, he held it up for photographers; the resulting image — the president grinning beneath the banner headline announcing his defeat — became one of the most famous photographs in American politics and a permanent cautionary tale about polling and premature calls.
