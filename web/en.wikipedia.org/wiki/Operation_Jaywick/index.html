Operation Jaywick

Operation Jaywick was a special operation undertaken in the Second World War by Z Special Unit, a joint Allied special forces unit of Australian, British and other personnel. In September 1943, a party of commandos sailed from Australia aboard a captured Japanese fishing vessel, the MV Krait, penetrated to Singapore Harbour, and used limpet mines carried by canoe to sink or damage Japanese shipping before escaping safely back to Australia.

Background and planning

The operation was proposed and led by British officer Ivan Lyon of the Gordon Highlanders, who had escaped Singapore after its fall in 1942. Jaywick was organised by the Services Reconnaissance Department (SRD), the Australian cover name for Allied special operations. The team trained at Camp X on Z Experimental Station near Cairns, Queensland. It took its name from "Jaywick" brand toilet paper, then jokingly noted as the only thing the Japanese advance had not overcome, and the Krait was named after a small venomous snake.

The raid

The Krait, crewed by 14 men and disguised as a local fishing boat, left Exmouth Gulf on 2 September 1943 and sailed to within sight of Singapore. On the night of 24–25 September, three two-man crews paddled collapsible canoes (folboats) into the harbour area and attached limpet mines to Japanese vessels. In the early hours of 26 September the mines exploded: Allied accounts credited the raid with sinking or seriously damaging seven ships totalling some 37,000 tons, though Japanese records indicate a smaller number of vessels. All six canoeists were recovered by the Krait, which returned to Exmouth on 19 October without loss.

Aftermath

Unaware of the source of the attack, Japanese authorities blamed local resistance and launched the "Double Tenth" reprisals of 10 October 1943 against civilian internees and Peranakan Chinese residents of Singapore, in which many were tortured and killed. Lyon himself was killed in the follow-up raid, Operation Rimau, in 1944. The Krait is preserved at the Australian National Maritime Museum in Sydney.
