William Carlos Williams

William Carlos Williams (September 17, 1883 – March 4, 1963) was an American poet and physician associated with modernism and Imagism. He practised medicine in Rutherford, New Jersey, for over forty years while writing some of the most influential American poetry of the 20th century.

Poetry

Williams championed an American idiom rooted in local speech and things — "no ideas but in things." Famous short poems include "The Red Wheelbarrow" and "This Is Just to Say." His epic poem Paterson (1946–58), centred on Paterson, New Jersey, and the long poem sequence Spring and All (1923) are landmarks. Pictures from Brueghel (1962) won the Pulitzer Prize posthumously.

Other work

He wrote plays, novels (the Stecher trilogy), short stories such as "The Use of Force," essays and an autobiography, and mentored younger poets including Allen Ginsberg. His variable foot and triadic stanza in late poems such as "Asphodel, That Greeny Flower" influenced generations.
