National Geodetic Survey (NGS)
Type: U.S. federal agency
Parent: National Ocean Service, NOAA
Predecessor traditions: Survey of the Coast (1807)

The National Geodetic Survey (NGS) is a United States federal agency within the National Ocean Service of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). It defines and manages the National Spatial Reference System, the coordinate framework that provides latitude, longitude, height, scale, gravity and orientation throughout the United States.

History

NGS traces its origins to the Survey of the Coast, authorized in 1807 under President Thomas Jefferson and led by Ferdinand Hassler — often described as the first scientific agency of the U.S. federal government. The organization later became the U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey, and in 1970 its geodetic functions were reorganized into what is now the National Geodetic Survey.

Work

The agency maintains the CORS network of continuously operating GPS reference stations, produces aeronautical survey data for airports, maps the national shoreline, and provides tools such as OPUS for processing GPS observations. Its reference datums, including NAD 83 and NAVD 88, underpin surveying, mapping, engineering and geographic information systems across the country, and NGS has been developing modernized datums to replace them.
