The Aquitanian language was spoken in ancient times in the region between the western Pyrenees and the Garonne river — roughly modern Gascony and the Basque Country — before and during the Roman occupation. It is known almost entirely from personal and divine names preserved in Latin inscriptions of the 1st to 3rd centuries AD.

Relationship to Basque

Scholars such as Koldo Mitxelena showed that Aquitanian name elements (e.g. Andere "lady", Nescato "girl", Cison "man") closely match archaic Basque words, leading to the widely held view that Aquitanian was an ancestor or close relative of the Basque language — the only surviving pre-Indo-European language of Western Europe.

Attestation

Around 400 inscriptions, mostly funerary stelae written in Latin, contain Aquitanian names. No connected Aquitanian text survives, and the language's grammar remains largely unknown.
