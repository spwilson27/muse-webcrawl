Viennese cuisine

Viennese cuisine is the cooking style of Vienna, Austria, shaped by the city's history as capital of a multi-ethnic empire. It is famous for pastries and coffee-house culture, and is often said to be the only major cuisine named after a city rather than a country or region.

Dishes

The emblematic dish is Wiener schnitzel — breaded, pan-fried veal cutlet, protected by law when called "Wiener". Other staples include Tafelspitz (boiled beef), goulash of Hungarian origin, and dumplings from Bohemian influence. Desserts include Sachertorte, Apfelstrudel, Kaiserschmarrn and Dobos torte.

Coffee houses

The Viennese coffee house, recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage, serves coffee varieties such as the Melange alongside newspapers and marble tables, a tradition dating to the late 17th century.

Heuriger

Wine taverns (Heuriger) in districts like Grinzing serve new wine from Vienna's own vineyards with buffet food.
