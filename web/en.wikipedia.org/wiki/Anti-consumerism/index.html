Anti-consumerism

Anti-consumerism is a sociopolitical ideology that opposes consumerism, the continual buying and consuming of material possessions. Anti-consumerism is concerned with the private actions of business corporations in pursuit of financial and economic goals at the expense of public welfare, especially in matters of environmental protection, social stratification and ethics in the governing of a society.

Origins

Anti-consumerism originated from criticism of the consumption of wealth in capitalist economies. Thorstein Veblen's The Theory of the Leisure Class (1899) described "conspicuous consumption", while the Frankfurt School and later writers such as Guy Debord — in The Society of the Spectacle (1967) — analysed how mass consumption and advertising shape modern society. In the 1990s the movement gained wider visibility through Naomi Klein's No Logo (1999) and the magazine Adbusters.

Activism

Anti-consumerist activism includes Buy Nothing Day, an international day of protest against consumerism held on the day after US Thanksgiving (Black Friday), culture jamming — the subversion of advertising messages — and campaigns for voluntary simplicity, degrowth and freeganism. Critics of anti-consumerism argue that consumer goods have raised living standards and that anti-consumerist movements can be elitist.
