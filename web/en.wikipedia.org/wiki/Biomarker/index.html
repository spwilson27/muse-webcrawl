Biomarker

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A laboratory technician analysing biomarker samples in multi-well plates
A laboratory technician analysing biomarker samples in multi-well plates

A biomarker (biological marker) is a measurable characteristic that indicates some biological state or condition — a molecule, gene, image or physiological value used to detect or monitor disease, predict prognosis or measure response to treatment. Examples span medicine: blood glucose and HbA1c in diabetes, Troponin in heart attack, PSA in prostate screening, HER2 in breast cancer and amyloid PET in Alzheimer's disease. Regulators (FDA–NIH's BEST glossary) classify biomarkers as diagnostic, prognostic, predictive, monitoring, response, susceptibility and safety types.

Validation

A biomarker's path from discovery to clinical use is littered with failures — statistical overfitting in omics studies, unrepresentative cohorts, assay drift. The field's standards (analytical validity, clinical validity, clinical utility) and cautionary tales (the Duke genomics scandal) shaped modern biomarker science; qualification programmes at the FDA and EMA formalise contexts of use.

Frontiers

Liquid biopsies (circulating tumour DNA), multi-cancer early-detection blood tests, digital biomarkers from wearables and speech, and proteomic clocks of ageing extend the concept beyond the lab bench — with screening's eternal arithmetic (sensitivity, prevalence, overdiagnosis) deciding which survive into practice.
