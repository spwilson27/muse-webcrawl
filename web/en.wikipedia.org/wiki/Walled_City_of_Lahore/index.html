Walled City of Lahore

The Walled City of Lahore (Urdu: اندرونِ شہر لاهور, "Inner City of Lahore"), historically known as the Old City, is the historic core of Lahore, Pakistan. It is located in the north-western portion of the modern city and is enclosed by a city wall, around 2.5 kilometres of which survive in part. During Mughal times, the city was the capital of the Punjab and one of the great cities of northern India.

History

Lahore became a major city under the Ghaznavids in the 11th century and reached its height under the Mughals, especially under Akbar, who made it a capital from 1585 to 1598. The Mughals built or rebuilt the city wall and its 13 gates, and added the Lahore Fort, Badshahi Mosque, and numerous havelis and bazaars. After the decline of Mughal power, the city passed through Sikh rule under Ranjit Singh (1799–1849) and then British rule. The British demolished most of the wall in 1857, replacing parts of it with gardens and the Circular Road.

Gates

The 13 gates included Akbari Gate, Bhati Gate, Delhi Gate, Kashmiri Gate, Lahori Gate, Masti Gate, Mochi Gate, Mori Gate, Roshnai Gate, Shah Alami Gate, Sheranwala Gate, Taxali Gate, and Yakki Gate. Six gates survive, most notably Delhi Gate and Roshnai Gate, which have been restored.

Architecture and culture

The Walled City contains an extraordinary density of historic buildings: Mughal and Sikh-era mosques, gurdwaras, temples, havelis, and the Royal Trail along which Mughal emperors travelled from Delhi Gate to the Fort. Its bazaars, narrow lanes (gali), and traditional food streets make it a centre of Lahori culture. Restoration efforts led by the Walled City of Lahore Authority, including work along the Royal Trail, have drawn international attention.
