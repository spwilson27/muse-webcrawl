Salvatore Quasimodo

Born: 20 August 1901, Modica, Sicily, Italy

Died: 14 June 1968 (aged 66), Naples, Italy

Occupation: Author, poet, translator

Notable awards: Nobel Prize in Literature (1959)

Salvatore Quasimodo (Italian: [salvaˈtoːre kwaˈziːmodo]; 20 August 1901 – 14 June 1968) was an Italian poet and novelist. In 1959 he won the Nobel Prize in Literature "for his lyrical poetry, which with classical fire expresses the tragic experience of life in our own times". Along with Giuseppe Ungaretti and Eugenio Montale, he was one of the foremost Italian poets of the 20th century.

Biography

Quasimodo was born in Modica, Sicily, the son of a railway stationmaster. He spent his childhood in small Sicilian towns. In 1908 his family moved to Messina, where his father had been sent to help after the earthquake that had devastated the city. The impressions of natural disaster made a lasting impression on the young poet.

He studied engineering in Rome but abandoned his studies for financial reasons and took jobs as a technical draughtsman while studying Latin and Greek. In 1929 he moved to Florence, where his brother-in-law Elio Vittorini introduced him to the circle of the literary journal Solaria, which published his first poems.

Work

Quasimodo's first collection, Acque e terre ("Waters and Lands"), appeared in 1930. He became a leading figure of the Hermetic movement in Italian poetry, characterised by concise, obscure and introspective verse. His later work, especially after the Second World War, turned towards social and political themes, as in Giorno dopo giorno (1947) and La vita non è sogno (1949).

He was also a distinguished translator of Greek and Roman classics, Shakespeare, Neruda and others, and taught Italian literature at the Giuseppe Verdi Conservatory in Milan.

Death

Quasimodo died of a cerebral haemorrhage in Naples in 1968, during a literary congress. He is buried in the Cimitero Monumentale in Milan.
