Atlantic Canada

Provinces:New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island
Population:approx. 2.4 million (2021)
Largest city:Halifax, Nova Scotia

Atlantic Canada, also called the Atlantic provinces or the Atlantics, is a region of Eastern Canada comprising the provinces located on the Atlantic coast: New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island.

As of 2021 the combined population of the four provinces was about 2.4 million. The term "Maritime provinces" refers to the three provinces of New Brunswick, Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island, excluding Newfoundland and Labrador.

History

The region was among the first in North America settled by Europeans — Norse explorers around 1000 AD, then French Acadians and British settlers. Newfoundland was Britain's oldest overseas colony claim and joined Confederation only in 1949, the last province to do so.

Economy

Historically based on fishing, forestry, shipbuilding and agriculture, the regional economy now includes offshore oil and gas (Newfoundland), tourism, aerospace and information technology. The region receives federal equalization support and has experienced population growth driven by immigration since the late 2010s.
