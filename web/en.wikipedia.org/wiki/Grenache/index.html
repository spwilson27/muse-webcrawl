Grenache

Grenache (Spanish: Garnacha) is one of the most widely planted red wine grape varieties in the world. Originating in Spain — probably in Aragon — it ripens late, so it suits the hot, dry climates of southern France, Spain, Sardinia (where it is called Cannonau), and parts of the New World such as Australia and California.

Characteristics

The vine is vigorous and hardy, resistant to wind and drought, but the thin-skinned berries are susceptible to oxidation and disease. Grenache wines are typically high in alcohol, low in tannin and acidity, and noted for red-fruit flavours — strawberry, raspberry, cherry — with notes of spice and herbs. The variety is prized for old-vine examples, especially in Spain's Priorat and Australia's Barossa and McLaren Vale.

Uses

Grenache is a cornerstone of southern Rhône blends, dominating Châteauneuf-du-Pape and forming the "G" of the GSM blend (Grenache–Syrah–Mourvèdre). It is also widely used for rosé wines and for fortified vins doux naturels such as Banyuls and Maury. White (Grenache blanc) and grey (Grenache gris) mutations are also cultivated.
