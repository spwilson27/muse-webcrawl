The Texas Historical Commission (THC) is the state agency for historic preservation in Texas. It serves as the state's State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) under federal preservation law, and works to identify, preserve, and interpret historic and cultural resources — buildings, sites, cemeteries, archeological resources, and museum collections — across the state.

History

The agency was created by the Texas Legislature in 1953 as the Texas State Historical Survey Committee, initially focused on an inventory of historic sites and an expanding marker programme begun in the 1930s. It received its current name in 1973. Over time its responsibilities grew to include architectural review, archeology, historic courthouse preservation, and the operation of state historic sites.

Programmes

The commission manages the Official Texas Historical Markers programme, with more than 17,000 markers erected statewide covering subjects from county histories to notable individuals, plus designations such as Recorded Texas Historic Landmarks and State Antiquities Landmarks. Its Texas Historic Courthouse Preservation Program, launched in 1999, provides matching grants to counties to restore historic courthouses. The THC operates a network of state historic sites — including San Felipe de Austin, Washington-on-the-Brazos, the Sam Bell Maxey House, and the French Legation — and promotes heritage tourism through the Texas Heritage Trails Program. It also administers federal and state historic preservation tax credits and consults on projects affecting historic properties.
