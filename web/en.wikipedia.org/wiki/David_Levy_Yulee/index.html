David Levy Yulee (born David Levy; 12 June 1810 – 10 October 1886) was an American politician and attorney from Florida. He is generally regarded as the first person of Jewish ancestry to be elected and serve as a United States senator, representing Florida from its admission to the Union in 1845.

Career

Born in St. Thomas to a Sephardic Jewish family, Levy emigrated to Florida as a youth, studied law and became a planter and territorial politician, serving as Florida's delegate to Congress and lobbying successfully for statehood. In the Senate he chaired the Committee on Naval Affairs. He added the surname Yulee later in life. A supporter of secession, he left the Senate in 1861 and served in the Confederate Congress; after the Civil War he was imprisoned for several months at Fort Pulaski.

Railroads

Yulee's Florida Railroad, completed in 1860 between Fernandina and Cedar Key, was the first cross-peninsula railroad in Florida. Yulee, Florida, and Levy County are named in his honour. His legacy is debated because he was a slaveholder and secessionist, and in 2020 Fernandina Beach removed a statue of him from public display near the railroad depot.
