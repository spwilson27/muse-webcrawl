Gneiss

Gneiss is a common, widely distributed type of metamorphic rock formed by high-temperature and high-pressure metamorphic processes acting on formations composed of igneous or sedimentary rocks. It is distinguished by gneissic banding — alternating lighter bands rich in quartz and feldspar and darker bands rich in mica and amphibole — produced by the segregation of minerals under directed pressure.

Formation and varieties

Gneiss forms under conditions approaching the melting point of rock, deep in the roots of mountain belts. Gneiss derived from granite is called orthogneiss; that derived from sedimentary rock, paragneiss. Varieties include banded gneiss, augen gneiss (with large lens-shaped feldspar crystals, from German Augen, "eyes") and migmatite, which has partially melted. Gneisses make up much of the ancient continental shields; the Acasta Gneiss of Canada, at about 4 billion years old, is among the oldest known rocks on Earth.

Uses

Because of its durability and attractive banding, gneiss is used as a dimension and ornamental stone, for flooring, worktops and building facades.
