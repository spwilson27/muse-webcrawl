Epicenity

Epicenity (from Latin epicoenus, "common to both genders," from Greek epikoinos) is the lack of gender distinction, often reducing the emphasis on the masculine or feminine gender of a word or referent. In linguistics, an epicene word has the same form for male and female referents; in broader usage, the term describes styles, names or presentations that are not marked as either masculine or feminine.

In language

Languages with grammatical gender may possess epicene nouns. In Latin and Greek, some nouns are "common" — usable with masculine or feminine agreement according to the referent. In modern languages, epicene pronouns and titles avoid specifying gender: English speakers may use singular "they" or titles such as "flight attendant" in place of gendered alternatives. Romance languages have seen proposals for epicene forms, such as the French iel, and the Spanish suffix -e.

Names and culture

Epicene given names — such as Alex, Taylor or Robin in English — can be borne by people of any gender. The concept is also applied to fashion and design intended to be gender-neutral, and in biology to structures that are common to both sexes.
