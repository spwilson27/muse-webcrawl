A5 road (Wales)

A5 in Wales
Historic London–Holyhead route
Engineered in large part by Thomas Telford
Key places: Llangollen, Betws-y-Coed, Bangor, Anglesey.

The A5 road in Wales is the Welsh section of the historic London to Holyhead trunk road. Built and improved in the early nineteenth century to speed mail and government communication with Ireland, it is closely associated with the engineer Thomas Telford. In Wales the road runs from the Shrewsbury side through Llangollen, Corwen, Betws-y-Coed and the Ogwen Valley to Bangor, then crosses Anglesey to the ferry port of Holyhead.

History

After the Act of Union with Ireland, Parliament wanted a reliable route from London to Dublin via Holyhead. Telford’s road used gentler gradients than earlier tracks and required major works including embankments, cuttings and bridges. Two celebrated structures belong to the wider Holyhead route: the Menai Suspension Bridge, which carried the road to Anglesey, and the Stanley Embankment to Holy Island. In Snowdonia the road threads narrow valleys where Telford’s retaining walls and alignment remain visible.

Route and character

Unlike the modern A55 North Wales Expressway, which now carries most fast traffic to Holyhead, the A5 remains a largely single-carriageway scenic and local route. It passes through the Dee Valley and Eryri (Snowdonia), serving walkers, climbers and villages as well as through traffic. Places such as Betws-y-Coed and Capel Curig make it a gateway for tourism in the national park.

Heritage

The Welsh A5 is valued as an early example of state-planned road engineering. Milestones, tollhouses, bridges and Telford’s Waterloo Bridge at Betws-y-Coed survive as part of its landscape. Although no longer the fastest route to Ireland ferries, it remains one of Britain’s most historically resonant roads.
