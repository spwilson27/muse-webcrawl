Photomask

A photomask carries the pattern for one layer of an integrated circuit.

A photomask is an opaque plate with transparent areas that allow light to shine through in a defined pattern. Photomasks are used in photolithography to transfer circuit patterns onto semiconductor wafers in the production of integrated circuits. A typical mask consists of a fused silica (quartz) substrate coated with a thin chrome layer in which the pattern has been etched, protected by a thin film called a pellicle that keeps dust particles out of the focal plane.

Use in lithography

Each mask corresponds to one layer of a chip design. In a wafer stepper or scanner, light passes through the mask and a projection lens reduces its image — historically by 4× or 5× — onto a photoresist-coated wafer. A complete chip may require dozens of masks, and a full mask set for an advanced node can cost millions of dollars. Masks for extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography are reflective rather than transmissive, using multilayer mirrors with an absorber pattern.

Fabrication

Mask patterns are written from design data by electron-beam or laser mask writers, then etched, inspected, and repaired, since even nanometre-scale mask defects can print onto every wafer. Techniques such as phase-shift masks and optical proximity correction deliberately distort mask patterns so the printed wafer image matches the intended design at feature sizes near the wavelength of light.
