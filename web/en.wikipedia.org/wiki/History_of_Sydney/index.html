History of Sydney

The history of Sydney, Australia's largest city, extends from tens of thousands of years of Aboriginal occupation of the harbour region to the founding of the first British colony in Australia in 1788 and the city's growth into a global financial and cultural centre.

Aboriginal Sydney

The Sydney Basin has been inhabited by Aboriginal peoples for at least 30,000 years. At the time of European arrival, the Eora peoples — including the Gadigal, whose country included the southern shore of the harbour — lived by fishing, hunting and gathering, and left rock engravings, shell middens and scarred trees across the region.

The penal colony

Captain James Cook charted the coast in 1770, naming Botany Bay. The First Fleet under Captain Arthur Phillip arrived in January 1788 and, finding Botany Bay unsuitable, established the settlement at Sydney Cove in Port Jackson, naming it for the Home Secretary, Lord Sydney. The early colony nearly starved; a smallpox epidemic in 1789 devastated the Eora. The township grew around The Rocks and the Tank Stream, and under Governor Lachlan Macquarie (1810–1821) gained substantial public buildings. Convict transportation to New South Wales ended in 1840.

Growth of a colonial capital

The gold rushes of the 1850s, though centred elsewhere, swelled Sydney's population and commerce; suburbs spread along rail and tram lines. The city rivalled Melbourne in finance and culture. Federation of the Australian colonies in 1901 made Sydney the capital of New South Wales but not of the new nation. The Sydney Harbour Bridge opened in 1932, a landmark of Depression-era public works.

Post-war metropolis

After 1945 mass immigration from Britain and continental Europe, and later from Asia and the Middle East, transformed Sydney into a multicultural city of millions. The Sydney Opera House, designed by Jørn Utzon, opened in 1973 and became an international symbol. Sydney hosted the 2000 Olympic Games, widely judged a triumph, and continued expanding westward into one of the largest metropolitan areas in the world by area, while grappling with housing affordability and infrastructure demands.
