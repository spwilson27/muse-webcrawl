Equestrian statue of Bartolomeo Colleoni

Artist: Andrea del Verrocchio
Year: 1480s (cast 1493)
Medium: Bronze
Location: Campo Santi Giovanni e Paolo, Venice

The equestrian statue of Bartolomeo Colleoni is a Renaissance sculpture in Campo Santi Giovanni e Paolo, Venice, Italy, executed by Andrea del Verrocchio in the 1480s. It portrays the condottiero Bartolomeo Colleoni (1400–1475), a captain-general of the Republic of Venice, who left a substantial bequest to the Republic on condition that a statue of himself be placed in the Piazza San Marco.

History

The Venetian Senate circumvented Colleoni's condition by interpreting "Piazza San Marco" as the campo before the Scuola Grande di San Marco, adjacent to the church of Santi Giovanni e Paolo, rather than St Mark's Square itself. Verrocchio won the competition for the monument in 1483, modelling the horse and rider in his Florentine workshop; he died in 1488 before the bronze was cast, and the work was completed by Alessandro Leopardi, who signed the horse's girth-strap. The statue was unveiled in 1496 on a tall pedestal designed by Leopardi.

Description

Unlike Donatello's more measured statue of Gattamelata, Verrocchio's rider sits tensely in the saddle, turning in a dynamic pose that conveys energy and command. The horse walks with one foreleg raised. The monument is regarded as one of the masterpieces of Renaissance sculpture and among the finest equestrian statues since antiquity. During the Second World War it was removed and hidden for safekeeping, and it was restored in the 2000s.
