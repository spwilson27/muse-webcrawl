Passing (gender)

In gender studies, passing refers to a transgender or gender-nonconforming person being perceived by others as the gender they present or identify as, rather than the sex they were assigned at birth — for example, a transgender woman being routinely regarded as a cisgender woman.

Discussion

Passing can affect safety, access to work and housing, and freedom from harassment, and many trans people seek it through clothing, voice, mannerisms, hormone therapy or surgery; the ability to pass is sometimes called "passing privilege". At the same time, the concept is debated within trans communities: some view passing as a practical necessity, others criticise the pressure to conform to cisgender norms, and still others argue the goal should be acceptance regardless of perceptibility. The term also has a wider history, notably for racial passing, and belongs to the sociology of stigma developed by Erving Goffman.

Being involuntarily recognised as trans is sometimes called being "clocked" or "read".
