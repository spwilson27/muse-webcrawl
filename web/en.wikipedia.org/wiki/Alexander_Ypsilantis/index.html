Alexander Ypsilantis

Alexander Ypsilantis (Greek: Αλέξανδρος Υψηλάντης; 1792 – 1828) was a Greek national hero, a senior officer in the Imperial Russian cavalry, and leader of the Filiki Eteria, the secret society that launched the Greek War of Independence in 1821.

Early life and military career

Ypsilantis was born in Constantinople into a prominent Phanariot family; his father, Constantine Ypsilantis, had been hospodar of Moldavia and Wallachia. The family fled to Russia, where Alexander joined the Imperial Guards. He distinguished himself in the Napoleonic Wars, losing his right arm at the Battle of Dresden in 1813, and rose to the rank of major general, serving as aide-de-camp to Tsar Alexander I.

The 1821 uprising

In 1820 Ypsilantis accepted leadership of the Filiki Eteria. On 6 March 1821 he crossed the Prut River into Moldavia with a small force, the Sacred Band, hoping Russian support and a general Balkan revolt would follow. His appeal "Fight for Faith and Fatherland" is regarded as the opening act of the Greek revolution. Defeated at the Battle of Drăgășani in June 1821, he fled into Austria, where he was imprisoned; released in 1827, he died in Vienna the following year.

Legacy

Although his Danubian campaign failed militarily, it ignited the revolt in the Peloponnese that ultimately won Greek independence. Ypsilantis is commemorated in Greece as a national hero, and the Michigan city of Ypsilanti is named after his brother Demetrios.
