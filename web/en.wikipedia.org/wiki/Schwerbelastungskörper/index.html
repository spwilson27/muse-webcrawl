Schwerbelastungskörper

The Schwerbelastungskörper (German: "heavy load body") is a gigantic concrete cylinder in the Tempelhof district of Berlin, built in 1941–42 to test whether Berlin's sandy soil could bear the weight of the monumental buildings planned for Adolf Hitler's redesign of the capital as "Welthauptstadt Germania".

Construction and purpose

Designed under Albert Speer's Generalbauinspektion, the block is about 21 metres in diameter and 14 metres high above ground, with foundations extending some 18 metres down, and weighs about 12,650 tonnes. It simulated the ground pressure of one of the four piers of a colossal triumphal arch planned nearby. Instruments measured the settlement of the block into the soil; it sank about 19 cm over the following years, more than the few centimetres considered acceptable, casting doubt on the feasibility of the arch without deep foundations.

After the war

The block proved too massive to demolish safely amid surrounding housing, and attempts to blow it up were abandoned. It survived as a relic of Nazi planning and was used for civil engineering research into soil mechanics. In 1995 it was placed under landmark protection, and an information pavilion and viewing platform now explain its history to visitors.
