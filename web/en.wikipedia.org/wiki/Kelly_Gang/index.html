Kelly Gang

Kelly Gang
Members: Ned Kelly, Dan Kelly, Joe Byrne, Steve Hart
Active: 1878–1880, Victoria, Australia
Fate: killed or captured at Glenrowan, June 1880

The Kelly Gang was an Australian bushranger gang led by Ned Kelly, active in north-eastern Victoria from 1878 to 1880. Its other members were Ned's younger brother Dan Kelly, Joe Byrne and Steve Hart. The gang's two years on the run — marked by the killing of three policemen at Stringybark Creek, two audacious bank robberies, and a final siege in homemade armour at Glenrowan — made them the most famous outlaws in Australian history.

Outbreak

The gang formed in the aftermath of the "Fitzpatrick incident" of April 1878, in which a constable alleged he had been attacked at the Kelly home. Fleeing arrest, the four became outlaws; in October 1878 they ambushed a police camp at Stringybark Creek, killing three troopers. The Victorian government declared them outlaws under the Felons Apprehension Act, and large rewards were posted.

Robberies and the Jerilderie Letter

The gang robbed banks at Euroa (December 1878) and Jerilderie (February 1879), where Ned dictated his 8,000-word "Jerilderie Letter" denouncing police persecution of poor Irish selector families — now regarded as a foundational Australian document. In June 1880, after murdering police informer Aaron Sherritt, the gang took hostages at the Glenrowan Inn, intending to derail a police train. Ned, wearing bulletproof armour made from plough mouldboards, was captured; Dan Kelly, Byrne and Hart died in the siege. Ned Kelly was hanged at Melbourne Gaol on 11 November 1880.

Legacy

The gang remains deeply polarising in Australian culture — murderers to some, folk heroes resisting injustice to others — and has inspired novels, films and Sidney Nolan's celebrated Kelly paintings.
