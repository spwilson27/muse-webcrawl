Operation Spring

Operation Spring was an offensive operation of the Second World War conducted by II Canadian Corps during the Normandy campaign. The plan was intended to create, through a holding attack, pressure on the German forces facing the Canadian sector so that the strategically decisive American-led Operation Cobra could develop momentum in the west. It was launched together with Cobra on 25 July 1944.

Background

After capturing Caen in Operations Charnwood, Goodwood and Atlantic, Canadian forces faced strong German defences along Verrières Ridge, south of the city, which protected the road to Falaise. Operation Spring aimed to seize the ridge and nearby villages.

The operation

The attack, launched before dawn on 25 July, made some initial gains but faltered in the face of well-prepared German positions, counterattacks by SS panzer units, and poor weather that muddied the ground. The Black Watch (Royal Highland Regiment) of Canada suffered devastating losses in an attack toward Verrières village — of some 325 men who advanced, only about 15 answered the evening roll call, making it one of the costliest single actions for a Canadian battalion in the war.

Aftermath

Although Spring failed to take its objectives and cost roughly 1,500 Canadian casualties, it succeeded in its strategic purpose of fixing German armoured reserves in the east while Operation Cobra broke through in the American sector, beginning the collapse of the German position in Normandy.
