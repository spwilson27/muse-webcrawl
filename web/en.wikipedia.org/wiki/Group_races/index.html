Group races

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Group races, also known as Pattern races, are the highest level of thoroughbred horse races in Europe. They are administered by the European Pattern Committee and are categorised as Group 1, Group 2, or Group 3 according to the quality of competition they attract. A similar system of Graded stakes races operates in North America, and the International Federation of Horseracing Authorities maintains a worldwide list of top-level races.

History

The European Pattern system was introduced in 1971 to provide a structured programme of high-quality races and to identify championship-level competition across countries. It was designed by representatives of Britain, France, Ireland, Italy, and Germany. Races are reviewed annually, and may be promoted or demoted depending on the ratings of their recent runners.

Classification

LevelDescription
Group 1Championship races of the highest international quality, such as the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe and the Derby.
Group 2Major races just below championship level, often used as trials for Group 1 events.
Group 3Important domestic races of high quality.
ListedRaces below Group level but above handicap/class company.

Group 1 races generally carry no penalties for previous wins and are run at level weights (with allowances for age and sex), so the best horse should win. To retain its status, a race must maintain an average official rating for its first four finishers above a set threshold over a rolling period. Black type in sales catalogues derives from Pattern and Listed races, so group status strongly affects the value of breeding stock.

Worldwide

Part I countries of the International Cataloguing Standards Book — including Australia, Japan, and the major South American racing nations — run Group races whose status is recognised internationally. Famous Group 1 races include the Melbourne Cup, the Japan Cup, and the Dubai World Cup.
