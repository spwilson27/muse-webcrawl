Frederick McCubbin

Frederick McCubbin (25 February 1855 – 20 December 1917) was an Australian artist, art teacher and prominent member of the Heidelberg School of Australian impressionism. His large narrative paintings of pioneer life in the Australian bush — above all the triptych The Pioneer (1904) — became foundational images of Australian national identity.

Born: 25 February 1855, Melbourne, Victoria
Died: 20 December 1917, South Yarra, Melbourne
Movement: Heidelberg School, Australian impressionism
Notable works: Down on His Luck (1889); The Pioneer (1904)

Life

McCubbin was born in Melbourne, the son of a baker, and worked in the family business, as a coach painter and as a solicitor's clerk while studying art at night at the National Gallery of Victoria Art School under Eugene von Guérard and George Folingsby. In 1886 he became drawing master at the Gallery school, a post he held for the rest of his life, teaching generations of Australian artists. With Tom Roberts, Charles Conder and Arthur Streeton he painted at the artists' camps at Box Hill and Heidelberg in the late 1880s, producing the sunlit bush landscapes shown in the celebrated 9 by 5 Impression Exhibition of 1889.

Major works

McCubbin's best-known paintings tell melancholy stories of the selector's struggle: Down on His Luck (1889) shows a dejected swagman in the bush; The Pioneer (1904), now in the National Gallery of Victoria, unfolds across three panels the life of a settler family from clearing the land to a prosperous city glimpsed beyond the founder's grave. Other major works include Lost (1886/1907), On the Wallaby Track (1896) and The Letter. Late in life, influenced by Turner and a 1907 trip to England, his palette brightened in harbour and garden scenes around his home at Macedon and South Yarra.

Legacy

McCubbin died of heart failure in 1917. His bush pioneer imagery, widely reproduced in Australian schools and homes, helped define how the young federation imagined its origins; the McCubbin name is also carried by the Melbourne suburb and by generations of his students.
