United States Army officer rank insignia

United States Army officer rank insignia are the devices worn by commissioned officers of the United States Army to denote their grade. Insignia are worn on the shoulder straps or sleeves depending on the uniform, with company-grade officers wearing bars, field-grade officers wearing oak leaf clusters, and general officers wearing stars.

Grades

Company grades are second lieutenant (one gold bar), first lieutenant (one silver bar) and captain (two silver bars). Field grades are major (gold oak leaf), lieutenant colonel (silver oak leaf) and colonel (silver eagle). General officers wear from one to five silver stars; the five-star grade of General of the Army has not been held since the mid-20th century. Chaplains and medical officers wear branch-specific insignia in place of or alongside rank devices.

History

The insignia system evolved from devices introduced during the 19th century; the oak leaf and bar system was largely standardised around the First World War, and the "pinks and greens" tradition influenced the modern Army Green Service Uniform.
