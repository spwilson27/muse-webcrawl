Divisor function

Divisor function
Field: Number theory
Notation: σx(n), d(n), τ(n)

In mathematics, and specifically in number theory, a divisor function is an arithmetic function related to the divisors of an integer. When referred to as the divisor function, it counts the number of divisors of an integer n.

Definition

The divisor function σx(n) is defined as the sum of the x-th powers of the positive divisors of n: σx(n) = Σd|n dx. Special cases include σ0(n) = d(n), the number of divisors, and σ1(n) = σ(n), the sum of divisors.

Examples

For n = 12, divisors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12, so d(12) = 6 and σ(12) = 28. Prime numbers p have d(p) = 2 and σ(p) = p + 1.

Properties

Divisor functions are multiplicative but not completely multiplicative. They appear in the study of perfect numbers, Riemann zeta function identities, and Dirichlet series: Σ σa(n)/ns = ζ(s)ζ(s−a).

Growth

The average order of d(n) is log n, though individual values fluctuate widely; highly composite numbers have unusually many divisors.
