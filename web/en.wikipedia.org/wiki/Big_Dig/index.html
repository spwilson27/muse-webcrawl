Big Dig

Central Artery/Tunnel Project ("Big Dig")
LocationBoston, Massachusetts, United States
Construction1991–2007
Cost~$14.6 billion (over $21 billion incl. interest)
Key structuresThomas P. O'Neill Jr. Tunnel, Ted Williams Tunnel, Leonard P. Zakim Bunker Hill Bridge

The Big Dig, officially the Central Artery/Tunnel Project (CA/T Project), was a megaproject in Boston, Massachusetts, that rerouted the Central Artery of Interstate 93 into the 1.5-mile (2.4 km) Thomas P. O'Neill Jr. Tunnel under downtown Boston, extended Interstate 90 to Logan International Airport via the Ted Williams Tunnel, and built the Leonard P. Zakim Bunker Hill Memorial Bridge over the Charles River.

[Image: The cable-stayed Leonard P. Zakim Bunker Hill Bridge at night]

History

The elevated Central Artery, opened in the 1950s, carried far more traffic than designed and cut through downtown neighbourhoods. Planning for its replacement began in the 1970s under Massachusetts officials including Frederick P. Salvucci. Construction began in 1991; the project was substantially complete in 2006 and finished in December 2007.

Cost and problems

Originally estimated at about $2.8 billion, the project is widely cited as the most expensive highway project in U.S. history. Severe leaks were discovered in 2004, and in July 2006 a concrete ceiling panel fell in the I-90 connector tunnel, killing a motorist, Milena Del Valle; investigations blamed faulty epoxy anchors on ceiling bolts, and the project's managers and suppliers paid large settlements. The demolished elevated highway's corridor became the Rose Kennedy Greenway.

Legacy

Despite its cost overruns, the project removed an elevated highway from downtown Boston, reconnected neighbourhoods to the waterfront, and substantially increased traffic capacity through the city.
