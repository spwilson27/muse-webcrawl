Prostatectomy

Prostatectomy is the surgical removal of all or part of the prostate gland. This operation is done for benign conditions that cause urinary retention, as well as for prostate cancer and for other cancers of the pelvis. A radical prostatectomy removes the entire prostate together with the seminal vesicles and surrounding tissue, and is a principal treatment option for localized prostate cancer.

Types and approaches

Simple prostatectomy removes only the inner, enlarged part of the gland and is used for severe benign prostatic hyperplasia. Radical prostatectomy for cancer may be performed through an open retropubic or perineal approach, laparoscopically, or with robotic assistance, which has become common in many countries. Nerve-sparing techniques attempt to preserve the neurovascular bundles responsible for erectile function.

Risks and outcomes

Common consequences include urinary incontinence and erectile dysfunction, which may improve over months following surgery. Outcomes for cancer control are generally good for organ-confined disease, and surgery is compared in trials with radiation therapy and active surveillance for low-risk disease.
