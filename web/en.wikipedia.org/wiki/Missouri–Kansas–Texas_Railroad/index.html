Missouri–Kansas–Texas Railroad

The Missouri–Kansas–Texas Railroad (reporting mark MKT), commonly called the Katy, was a major American railroad operating in the south-central United States. Chartered in 1865 as the Union Pacific Railway, Southern Branch, it was the first railway to enter Texas from the north, crossing Indian Territory in 1870–72.

History

Renamed for its initials M-K-T, the Katy built and acquired lines linking Kansas City and St. Louis with Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston and San Antonio. It carried cattle, cotton, grain and passengers, and its "Katy Flyer" and "Texas Special" trains were well known.

After periods of receivership and reorganisation, the railroad was merged into the Missouri Pacific Railroad in 1988, which itself became part of Union Pacific. Parts of its right of way in Missouri now form the Katy Trail State Park, one of the longest rail trails in the United States.
