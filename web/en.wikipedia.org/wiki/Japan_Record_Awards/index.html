The Japan Record Awards (日本レコード大賞, Nihon Rekōdo Taishō) is a major annual music awards show in Japan, presented by the Japan Composer's Association and broadcast on TBS. First held in 1959, the ceremony honours outstanding achievements in the Japanese music industry, and its Grand Prix is considered one of the most prestigious prizes in Japanese popular music.

History

The first Japan Record Awards were held in 1959, when the Grand Prix went to Hiroshi Mizuhara's "Kuroi Hanabira". In the enka era the award was dominated by singers such as Hibari Misora, while from the late 1970s pop and rock acts increasingly prevailed. The ceremony was long staged on New Year's Eve but moved to December 30 in 2006 to avoid clashing with NHK's Kōhaku Uta Gassen.

Categories

The top honour is the Grand Prix (大賞), chosen from among the winners of the Excellent Work Awards. Other categories include Best New Artist (chosen from the New Artist Award winners), Best Vocal Performance, Best Album, honours for composition, arrangement, and lyrics, and special and lifetime-achievement awards.

Notable winners

Multiple Grand Prix winners include Ayumi Hamasaki, who won three consecutive awards (2001–2003), and EXILE, who won four times. In 2016 the awards attracted controversy over reports that the Grand Prix had been influenced by commercial considerations, which the organisers denied.
