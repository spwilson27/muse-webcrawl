Baseball positions

In the sport of baseball, each of the nine players on the fielding team occupies a defensive position. The positions are conventionally identified by numbers used in scoring: 1 (pitcher), 2 (catcher), 3 (first baseman), 4 (second baseman), 5 (third baseman), 6 (shortstop), 7 (left fielder), 8 (center fielder) and 9 (right fielder).

The battery

The pitcher (1) stands on the pitcher's mound and delivers the ball to the catcher (2), who crouches behind home plate. Together they are known as the battery.

Infield

The infield comprises the first baseman (3), second baseman (4), shortstop (6) and third baseman (5), positioned around the diamond of bases. Infielders field ground balls, cover bases for force plays and relays, and turn double plays.

Outfield

The left fielder (7), center fielder (8) and right fielder (9) patrol the outfield, catching fly balls and preventing batted balls from reaching the fence. The center fielder, usually the fastest, covers the largest area and takes priority on balls hit between outfielders.

Shifts and strategy

Modern analytics have led teams to reposition fielders according to a batter's tendencies, a practice known as shifting; rules introduced in Major League Baseball in 2023 placed limits on extreme infield shifts.
