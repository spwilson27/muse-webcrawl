Overview

The North Antelope Rochelle Mine is a surface coal mine in the Powder River Basin of northeastern Wyoming, south of Gillette. Operated by Peabody Energy, it has frequently been the largest coal mine in the United States — and among the largest in the world — by annual production, extracting low-sulfur sub-bituminous coal from thick seams.

History

The mine was formed in 1999 by combining the North Antelope and Rochelle mines. Powder River Basin coal became highly prized after clean-air legislation increased demand for low-sulfur fuel, and rail lines of the BNSF and Union Pacific carry the coal to power plants across the United States.

Operations

Mining uses draglines and truck-and-shovel fleets to remove overburden and extract coal seams tens of feet thick. At its peak in the mid-2010s the mine produced more than 100 million short tons of coal per year; output has since declined with falling demand for thermal coal. Reclamation of mined land proceeds alongside extraction.
