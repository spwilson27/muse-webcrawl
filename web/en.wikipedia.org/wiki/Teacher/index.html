Teacher

A teacher, also called a schoolteacher or formally an educator, is a person who helps students acquire knowledge, competence, and virtue through the practice of teaching. Teachers work in schools and other educational settings, guiding learning through instruction, lesson planning, assessment, and mentorship.

Roles and duties

Informally, the role of teacher may be taken on by anyone — for example, when showing a colleague how to perform a task. In formal education, teaching is a profession carried out by trained practitioners whose duties typically include preparing lessons and materials, delivering instruction, assessing student progress, maintaining classroom discipline, communicating with parents and guardians, and continuing their own professional development.

Teachers specialise by level (early childhood, primary, secondary, tertiary) and by subject (mathematics, languages, sciences, arts, physical education, and so on). Beyond instruction, teachers often serve as mentors and role models, and may take on pastoral and extracurricular responsibilities.

Training and qualifications

Most jurisdictions require teachers in public schools to hold a degree and a teaching qualification or licence, obtained through initial teacher education that combines subject study, pedagogy, and supervised classroom practice. Requirements vary widely between countries; UNESCO and the OECD collect comparative data on teacher training, salaries, and working conditions.

Professional issues

Teaching is among the largest professions worldwide. Issues affecting teachers include class size, workload, pay relative to other graduate professions, teacher shortages in particular subjects and regions, occupational stress, and the defence of academic freedom. Teachers' unions and professional associations represent teachers in many countries. World Teachers' Day is observed annually on 5 October.
