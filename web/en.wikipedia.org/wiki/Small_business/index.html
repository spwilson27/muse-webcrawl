Small business

Small businesses are privately owned corporations, partnerships, or sole proprietorships that have fewer employees and lower annual revenue than a regular-sized business or corporation. Businesses are classified as "small" in terms of being able to apply for government support and qualify for preferential policies, which varies depending on the country and industry.

Definitions

In the United States, the Small Business Administration (SBA) defines a small business by industry-specific standards, commonly based on employee count (often up to 500 employees for manufacturing) or average annual receipts. The European Union defines small enterprises as those with fewer than 50 employees and annual turnover or balance sheet total not exceeding €10 million, with micro-enterprises below 10 employees. Australia defines a small business as one employing fewer than 15 people, while other jurisdictions use similar headcount or revenue thresholds.

Economic role

Small businesses constitute the great majority of firms in most economies and are a major source of employment and new job creation. They include independent retailers, tradespeople, professional practices, restaurants, small manufacturers, and technology start-ups. Because they are often locally rooted, small businesses anchor neighbourhood economies and provide goods and services tailored to local needs. At the same time, failure rates are high: a substantial fraction of new small businesses close within their first five years, with access to finance, cash flow, and competition from larger firms among the common challenges.

Support and finance

Governments commonly support small businesses through loan guarantee schemes, tax incentives, simplified regulation, training, and procurement preferences. In the United States, the SBA, founded in 1953, guarantees loans, offers counselling through Small Business Development Centers, and supports disaster recovery lending. Small businesses typically finance themselves through owners' savings, bank loans, credit lines, trade credit, and increasingly through online lenders and crowdfunding platforms.

Small business and franchising

Many small businesses operate as franchises, allowing independent owners to trade under an established brand and business model in exchange for fees and royalties. Franchising lowers some risks of starting a business while limiting the owner's independence, and it is especially common in food service, retail, and personal services.
