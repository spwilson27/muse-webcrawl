Community health

Community health workers deliver preventive and basic care where people live.

Community health refers to the health status of a defined group of people and the actions and conditions, both private and public, that promote, protect, and preserve their health. As a field of public health, it focuses on the health of entire populations within a geographic or social community rather than on individual clinical care alone.

Scope

Community health practice includes immunisation programmes, maternal and child health services, health education, sanitation and safe water, screening for diseases such as tuberculosis and HIV, and the control of environmental hazards. It addresses the social determinants of health, including housing, income, nutrition and education.

Community health workers

Community health workers are lay members of communities who are trained to provide basic health services and link residents to formal health systems. Programmes in countries such as Ethiopia (Health Extension Workers), Brazil (Family Health Strategy) and India (ASHAs) have demonstrated improvements in child survival, vaccination coverage and management of chronic disease.

Assessment and planning

Community health needs assessments use epidemiological data and community consultation to identify priorities. Frameworks such as Mobilizing for Action through Planning and Partnerships (MAPP) guide local health departments in the United States through assessment, planning, implementation and evaluation cycles.
