Zulfikar Ali Bhutto

Zulfikar Ali Bhutto (5 January 1928 – 4 April 1979) was a Pakistani lawyer, diplomat, and politician who served as the fourth President of Pakistan from 1971 to 1973 and then as the ninth Prime Minister, from 1973 until his removal in a military coup in 1977. He founded the Pakistan People's Party (PPP) in 1967 and is often referred to by his supporters as Quaid-e-Awam ("Leader of the People"). Bhutto remains one of the most consequential and controversial figures in Pakistan's history.

Early career

Born into a wealthy Sindhi landowning family, Bhutto was educated at the University of California, Berkeley, and at Oxford, and was called to the bar at Lincoln's Inn. Entering politics in the 1950s, he held a series of ministerial posts under Presidents Iskander Mirza and Ayub Khan, becoming foreign minister in 1963. As foreign minister he strengthened ties with China and adopted a harder line toward India, resigning after the 1965 war and the Tashkent Declaration, which he publicly criticised.

Rise to power

Bhutto founded the PPP on a populist, socialist platform summarised by the slogan Roti, Kapra aur Makan ("bread, clothing, and housing"). In the 1970 general election the PPP won a majority in West Pakistan. Following the Bangladesh Liberation War and Pakistan's defeat in December 1971, Yahya Khan resigned and Bhutto became president and chief martial law administrator. His government negotiated the Simla Agreement with India (1972), secured the return of prisoners of war, obtained recognition of Bangladesh, and promulgated the Constitution of 1973, under which he became prime minister.

Government and fall

Bhutto's administration nationalised major industries, banks, and schools, introduced land reforms, hosted the 1974 Islamic Summit in Lahore, and initiated Pakistan's nuclear weapons programme. His rule also saw authoritarian measures, including the creation of the Federal Security Force and action against the opposition in Balochistan. After disputed elections in March 1977 and mass protests by the Pakistan National Alliance, General Muhammad Zia-ul-Haq seized power on 5 July 1977. Bhutto was tried for allegedly ordering the murder of a political opponent, convicted in a controversial trial, and hanged on 4 April 1979. His daughter Benazir Bhutto later twice served as prime minister.
