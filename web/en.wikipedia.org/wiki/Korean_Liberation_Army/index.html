The Korean Liberation Army (Hangul: 광복군, Gwangbokgun) was the armed force of the Provisional Government of the Republic of Korea, formally established in Chongqing, China, on 17 September 1940. It united Korean independence fighters in China under a single command for the struggle against Japanese colonial rule.

Activities

The army absorbed Korean units that had fought alongside Chinese forces, including the Korean Volunteer Army elements in northern China and independence forces formerly active in Manchuria. It cooperated with Chinese Nationalist forces, conducted propaganda and intelligence work behind Japanese lines, and encouraged Korean soldiers conscripted into the Japanese army to defect. In 1943 Korean units served with British forces in the Burma–India theatre, and in 1945 the Liberation Army worked with the American Office of Strategic Services (OSS) on the "Eagle Project", training agents for infiltration into Korea—an operation overtaken by Japan's sudden surrender in August 1945.

Disbandment and legacy

After liberation, its members returned to Korea as individuals, as the US military government did not recognise the Provisional Government and the army was dissolved in 1946. The Republic of Korea's constitution and official history honour the Liberation Army as a forerunner of the South Korean armed forces, whose founding is commemorated as tracing its roots to the independence movement.
