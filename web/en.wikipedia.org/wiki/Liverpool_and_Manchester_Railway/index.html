Liverpool and Manchester Railway

Opened15 September 1830
Length31 miles (50 km)
EngineerGeorge Stephenson

The Liverpool and Manchester Railway (L&MR) was the first inter-city railway in the world. It opened on 15 September 1830 between the Lancashire cities of Liverpool and Manchester, carrying both passengers and freight with locomotive-hauled trains on a timetabled service.

Construction

Promoted by merchants wanting cheaper transport than canals for raw cotton and finished goods, the line was engineered by George Stephenson. Works included the Sankey Viaduct, a tunnel under Liverpool, and crossing the bog of Chat Moss. The 1829 Rainhill Trials selected Stephenson's Rocket as the locomotive design.

Opening and legacy

At the opening, MP William Huskisson was struck by Rocket and died — the first widely reported railway fatality. The line was an immediate commercial success, profits exceeding expectations, and it became the model for railway development worldwide. It merged into the Grand Junction Railway in 1845. Much of the route remains in use today.
