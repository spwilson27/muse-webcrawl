Zither

Zither
Classification: String instrument, chordophone
Related: Guzheng, koto, dulcimer, autoharp

The zither is a class of stringed musical instruments whose strings are stretched over a resonating body and are plucked, strummed, or occasionally bowed or struck. In organology the term covers a broad family — including the Chinese guzheng, Japanese koto, and Korean geomungo — but in common Western usage it refers chiefly to the Alpine concert zither of Austria and southern Germany.

Concert zither

The concert zither has a flat, shallow wooden body carrying typically 30 to 45 strings. Five melody strings pass over a fretted fingerboard and are stopped with the left hand while being plucked with a plectrum worn on the right thumb; the remaining strings provide accompaniment and bass and are plucked or strummed with the other fingers. Anton Karas's zither score for the 1949 film The Third Man, including the famous "Harry Lime Theme", brought the instrument worldwide fame.

History

Instruments of the zither family are ancient: tube and board zithers appear in China more than two thousand years ago, and the Greek word kithara is a linguistic ancestor of both "zither" and "guitar". The Alpine zither developed in the 19th century from folk instruments such as the Scheitholt, reaching its standard concert form through makers and virtuosi in Vienna and Munich.

Variants

Related instruments include the chord zither and autoharp, which use chord bars to simplify accompaniment; the bowed psaltery; the hammered dulcimer, a struck zither; and long Asian zithers played on the floor or a stand. The term "zither" is also applied to the Baltic psalteries such as the Latvian kokles and Finnish kantele.
