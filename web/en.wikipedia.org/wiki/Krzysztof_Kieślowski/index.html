Krzysztof Kieślowski

Krzysztof Kieślowski (27 June 1941 – 13 March 1996) was a Polish film director and screenwriter. He is known internationally for The Double Life of Veronique (1991) and the Three Colours trilogy (1993–1994), and in Poland for his ten-part television series Dekalog (1989), based on the Ten Commandments. Kieślowski received numerous awards, including the Cannes Film Festival Jury Prize (1988), a FIPRESCI Prize, and an Academy Award nomination for Best Director for Three Colours: Red (1994).

Life and career

Born in Warsaw, Kieślowski grew up in several small towns as his tubercular father moved between sanatoria. After several attempts he was admitted to the renowned Łódź Film School, graduating in 1968. He began his career making documentaries for Polish television — including Workers '71, I Was a Soldier and From the Point of View of a Night Porter — before moving to feature films with Personnel (1975), The Scar (1976) and Camera Buff (1979), which won the grand prize at the Moscow Film Festival. His work of the late 1970s and early 1980s was associated with the "Cinema of Moral Anxiety" movement.

Blind Chance (1981, released 1987) explored fate and political choice through three alternative versions of one man's life. No End (1985) began his collaborations with composer Zbigniew Preisner and co-screenwriter Krzysztof Piesiewicz. A Short Film About Killing and A Short Film About Love (both 1988), expanded from the Dekalog series, brought him wide European acclaim. In the 1990s he worked in France: The Double Life of Veronique and the Three Colours trilogy — Blue (1993), White (1994) and Red (1994) — exploring the French revolutionary ideals of liberty, equality and fraternity. He announced his retirement after Red and died of a heart attack in Warsaw in 1996, aged 54.
