Human head


Lateral view of the human head and neck.

In human anatomy, the head is at the top of the human body. It supports the face and is maintained by the skull, which itself encloses the brain.

Structure

The human head consists of a fleshy outer portion, which surrounds the bony skull. The brain is enclosed within the skull. There are 22 bones in the human head. The head rests on the neck, and the seven cervical vertebrae support it. The human head typically weighs between 2.3 and 5 kilograms (5.1 and 11.0 lb). Over 98% of humans fit into the typical size range of the head, ranging from the 1st to the 99th percentile.

Components

The head contains sensory organs: the eyes, ears, nose, and mouth, the latter of which conveys information to the brain via nerves such as the trigeminal nerve, facial nerve, and the optic nerve. The skin of the head is richly supplied with nerves, and the scalp is covered by hair in most people.

Cultural significance

For humans, the front of the head (the face) is the main distinguishing feature between different people, due to its easily discernible features, such as eye and hair colors, shapes of the sensory organs, and the wrinkles. Identification of people in social situations is usually by the face, and damage to the brain can cause severe impairment.
