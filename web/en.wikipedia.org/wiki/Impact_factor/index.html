Impact factor

Impact factors are used to compare journals within a field.

The impact factor (IF) or journal impact factor (JIF) of an academic journal is a scientometric measure of the yearly mean number of citations of articles published in the last two years in a given journal, as indexed by Clarivate's Web of Science. It is frequently used as a proxy for the relative importance of a journal within its field; journals with higher impact factor values are given the status of being more important, or carrying more prestige in their respective fields, than those with lower values.

Calculation

In a given year, the two-year journal impact factor is the ratio between the number of citations received in that year for publications in the journal published in the two preceding years and the total number of "citable items" published in the journal during the two preceding years. If a journal published 100 citable items in 2022–2023 and those items received 400 citations in 2024, its 2024 impact factor would be 4.0.

History

The impact factor was devised by Eugene Garfield, the founder of the Institute for Scientific Information (ISI). Impact factors are calculated yearly starting from 1975 for journals listed in the Journal Citation Reports (JCR), now published by Clarivate. A five-year impact factor is also reported.

Use and criticism

Librarians use impact factors to guide subscription decisions, and researchers often consider them when choosing where to submit work. However, the metric is widely criticised: citation distributions within journals are highly skewed, so the mean reflects the performance of a few highly cited papers; values are not comparable across fields; editorial practices such as publishing many review articles or encouraging self-citation can inflate the figure; and using a journal-level metric to judge individual articles or researchers is statistically unsound. The San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA, 2013) recommends against using journal impact factors to assess individual researchers.
