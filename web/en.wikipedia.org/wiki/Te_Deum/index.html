Te Deum

The Te Deum (Latin: "Thee, O God, we praise") is a Latin Christian hymn of praise, also called the Ambrosian Hymn or A Song of the Church. Traditionally attributed to Saint Ambrose and Saint Augustine, who were said to have composed it improvisationally at Augustine's baptism in 387, it is now generally thought to date from the fourth century and may be the work of Nicetas of Remesiana.

Text and use

The hymn opens with praise of God the Father, continues with the Church's acclamation of Christ, and closes with psalm verses of supplication. It is sung at the end of Matins on Sundays and feasts in the Roman Catholic Church, in the Anglican service of Morning Prayer, and on occasions of public thanksgiving such as coronations, canonizations, and the end of the year.

Musical settings

The Te Deum has been set to music by many composers, including Handel, Haydn, Mozart, Berlioz, Verdi, Bruckner, Dvořák, Charpentier (whose setting's prelude is famous as the Eurovision theme), and Arvo Pärt.
