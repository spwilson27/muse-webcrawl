Sonja Henie

Sonja Henie
Born: 8 April 1912, Kristiania (Oslo), Norway
Died: 12 October 1969, en route to Oslo
Sport: Figure skating
Honours: 3× Olympic champion (1928, 1932, 1936); 10× World champion

Sonja Henie (8 April 1912 – 12 October 1969) was a Norwegian figure skater and film star. She is regarded as one of the greatest and most influential figure skaters in history, winning three Olympic gold medals, ten consecutive World Championships (1927–1936) and six consecutive European Championships.

Skating career

Henie made her Olympic debut at the first Winter Games in 1924, aged eleven. She revolutionised the sport by introducing elements of dance and ballet, choreographed routines, short skirts and white boots — transforming figure skating into a graceful performance art. Her ten straight world titles remain unmatched in ladies' singles.

Film and show career

Turning professional in 1936, Henie moved to the United States, starred in a series of popular Hollywood musical films for 20th Century Fox (beginning with One in a Million, 1936), and headlined the touring ice revue "Sonja Henie's Hollywood Ice Revue". She became one of the wealthiest self-made women of her time and a naturalised US citizen in 1941.

Legacy

Henie died of leukaemia in 1969 aboard a flight from Paris to Oslo. She was inducted into the World Figure Skating Hall of Fame, and her art collection formed part of the Henie Onstad Kunstsenter near Oslo.
