Adobe Photoshop is a raster graphics editor developed by Adobe Inc. for Windows and macOS. It is the industry standard for digital image processing and editing: it supports multi-layer composition, masks, alpha compositing, colour models including RGB, CMYK, Lab and greyscale, and a vast array of filters and adjustment tools. The name has become a generic verb — "to photoshop" — for digital image manipulation.

History

Photoshop began in 1987 when Thomas Knoll, a Michigan PhD student, wrote a program called Display to show greyscale images on a monochrome monitor; his brother John, working at Industrial Light & Magic, urged its development into a full image editor. The brothers demonstrated the renamed Photoshop to Adobe, which licensed it in 1988 and released version 1.0 on 19 February 1990, initially for Macintosh only. Layers arrived in version 3.0 (1994), adjustment layers and a free-transform tool in 4.0, editable type and the History palette in 5.0, Camera Raw support in the 2000s, Content-Aware Fill in CS5 (2010), and neural/AI features — including the generative fill of 2023 — in recent versions. Since 2013 Photoshop has been sold by subscription as part of Adobe Creative Cloud.

Features

Photoshop edits images as stacks of layers with blending modes and opacity; selections, channels, paths and smart objects support non-destructive workflows. It includes tools for retouching (clone stamp, healing brushes), painting, vector shapes and text, 3D and video editing (in extended versions), and automation by actions and scripting. Its native PSD format preserves layers and is supported across the graphics industry.

Cultural impact

Photoshop transformed photography, advertising and publishing, and its capacity for seamless manipulation has fuelled recurring debates over authenticity in journalism, fashion imagery and evidence. Adobe has repeatedly asked that the trademark not be used as a verb, without success.
