Saratoga Springs, New York

Saratoga Springs is a city in Saratoga County, New York, United States. The population was about 28,500 at the 2020 census. The city is known for its mineral springs, its history as a fashionable resort, and the Saratoga Race Course, one of the oldest thoroughbred horse-racing tracks in the United States. Its name reflects the presence of mineral springs in the area, which have made it a popular destination for over two centuries.

History

The area's springs were long used by the Mohawk and other Indigenous peoples. In 1771 the British officer William Johnson was brought to a spring for its reputed healing properties. After the American Revolutionary War — the pivotal Battles of Saratoga were fought nearby in 1777 — the settlement grew into a spa town. Gideon Putnam laid out the village in the early nineteenth century, and grand hotels lined Broadway as visitors came to "take the waters."

Horse racing began in 1863, and the Saratoga Race Course opened in 1864. Gambling, first legal and later suppressed, shaped the town's fortunes; grand casino buildings such as the Canfield Casino survive in Congress Park. In the twentieth century the city reinvented itself with the Saratoga Performing Arts Center (opened 1966), summer home of the New York City Ballet and the Philadelphia Orchestra, and Skidmore College relocated to the city.

Attractions

Saratoga Spa State Park preserves the mineral springs, bathhouses, geysers and reflecting pool. The Saratoga racing season each summer, highlighted by the Travers Stakes, draws tens of thousands of visitors.
