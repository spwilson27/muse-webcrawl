TRPV1
Also known as: Capsaicin receptor, VR1
Family: TRP channel
Gene: TRPV1 (human)
Activated by: Heat >43°C, capsaicin, acid

The transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily V member 1 (TRPV1), also known as the capsaicin receptor and vanilloid receptor 1, is a protein that in humans is encoded by the TRPV1 gene. It was the first isolated member of the TRPV group of TRP channels.

Function

TRPV1 is a non-selective cation channel in sensory neurons that detects noxious heat (above about 43 °C), protons (acid), and vanilloid compounds such as capsaicin, the pungent ingredient of chilli peppers. Activation produces a burning sensation and triggers pain signalling and neurogenic inflammation. The channel is sensitised by inflammatory mediators, lowering its threshold in injured tissue.

Discovery and significance

TRPV1 was cloned in 1997 by a team led by David Julius, who used capsaicin to identify the gene. Julius shared the 2021 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for discoveries of temperature and touch receptors. Cryo-electron microscopy structures of TRPV1 revealed how capsaicin and toxins bind.

Clinical relevance

High-dose capsaicin patches exploit TRPV1 desensitisation to treat neuropathic pain. TRPV1 antagonists were pursued as analgesics but many caused hyperthermia in trials. Endogenous activators include anandamide and other endovanilloids.
