Knafeh

Type: Dessert

Region: Levant and Middle East

Main ingredients: Shredded pastry or semolina, cheese, sugar syrup, pistachio

Knafeh (also spelled kunafa, kanafeh, or kunefe) is a traditional Middle Eastern dessert made with spun pastry called kadayif, soaked in a sweet sugar-based syrup called attar, and typically layered with cheese or other fillings such as clotted cream or nuts, depending on the region.

History

Knafeh is believed to have originated in the Levant, with the city of Nablus especially famous for its version made with white-brined Nabulsi cheese. The dessert spread throughout the former Ottoman Empire and remains popular across the Arab world, Turkey, Greece, and the Balkans.

Varieties

Common varieties include khishneh (rough, with long noodle-like threads), na'ama (fine, made with semolina dough), and Turkish künefe, which is served hot with unsalted cheese and topped with ground pistachio. It is often eaten for breakfast or as a dessert, sometimes sandwiched in a sesame bread roll.
