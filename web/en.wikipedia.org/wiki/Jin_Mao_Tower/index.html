The Jin Mao Tower (Chinese: 金茂大厦; literally "Golden Prosperity Building") is an 88-storey skyscraper in the Lujiazui financial district of Pudong, Shanghai, China. Completed in 1999, it rises 421 metres (1,381 ft) including its spire and was the tallest building in China until the Shanghai World Financial Center surpassed it in 2007. It remains an emblem of Shanghai's rapid economic rise in the 1990s.

Design

Designed by the Chicago office of Skidmore, Owings & Merrill under Adrian Smith, the tower's postmodern form draws on traditional Chinese architecture, with setbacks echoing a tiered pagoda. The design incorporates the lucky number 8 throughout: 88 floors, dedicated on 8/28/1988, and proportions based on segments of eight. The building's structural system uses a reinforced-concrete core and steel outriggers engineered to withstand typhoon winds and earthquakes.

Uses

The lower floors contain offices and a shopping mall, while the Grand Hyatt Shanghai hotel occupies floors 53 to 87, arranged around a dramatic 33-storey atrium. An observation deck on the 88th floor offers panoramic views over the Huangpu River and The Bund. In 2016 a glass-bottomed skywalk without guardrails opened on the 88th floor, allowing harnessed visitors to walk outside the building's crown.
