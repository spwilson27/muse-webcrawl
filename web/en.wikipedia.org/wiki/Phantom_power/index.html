Phantom power

Phantom power, in the context of professional audio equipment, is a method of transmitting DC electric power through microphone cables to operate microphones that contain active electronic circuitry. It is best known as a convenient power source for condenser microphones, though many active direct boxes and other devices also use it. The most common standard supplies +48 volts DC, often labelled P48, applied equally to both signal conductors of a balanced line so that it is invisible ("phantom") to equipment that does not use it.

How it works

In a balanced microphone connection (pins 2 and 3 of an XLR connector), phantom power applies the same positive DC voltage to both signal wires through matched resistors, with the return path via the cable shield (pin 1). A balanced dynamic microphone sees no potential difference between its two signal wires and is unaffected, while a condenser microphone extracts the voltage between the signal pair and ground to power its impedance converter and polarise its capsule.[1] The standard derives from a system developed for the Norwegian Broadcasting Corporation in the 1960s and was standardised by DIN and later IEC 61938.

Compatibility and caveats

Most modern equipment tolerates phantom power, but it can damage some vintage ribbon microphones if a faulty or unbalanced cable shorts one leg, and unbalanced sources should not be connected to phantom-powered inputs. Related but distinct systems include 12-volt T-power (A–B powering) formerly used in film sound, and low-voltage "plug-in power" for consumer electret microphones. Phantom power is also used to drive the electronics in active direct boxes and some small-diaphragm measurement microphones.
