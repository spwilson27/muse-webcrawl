Saul Bass

Saul Bass (May 8, 1920 – April 25, 1996) was an American graphic designer and filmmaker, celebrated for revolutionising film title sequences and for designing some of the most enduring corporate logos of the twentieth century. His minimalist, kinetic title designs for directors such as Alfred Hitchcock, Otto Preminger and Martin Scorsese transformed opening credits into an art form.

Film work

Bass's breakthrough came with Preminger's Carmen Jones (1954) and The Man with the Golden Arm (1955), whose jagged paper-cut arm of a heroin addict announced a modernist sensibility. He designed the spiralling titles for Hitchcock's Vertigo (1958), the fragmented grids of North by Northwest (1959), and the disquieting sequences of Psycho (1960), for which he also storyboarded sequences and claimed a hand in the shower scene's design. Other landmarks include West Side Story, Anatomy of a Murder, It's a Mad, Mad, Mad, Mad World, the racing footage of Grand Prix, and later work for Scorsese on Goodfellas, Cape Fear and Casino. His short film Why Man Creates (1968) won an Academy Award.

Logo design

With his firm Saul Bass & Associates he created identities including the AT&T "bell" and later globe logos, United Airlines' "tulip," Continental Airlines' jetstream mark, the Bell System logo, Dixie, Quaker Oats, and the Girl Scouts emblem. His logos were notable for exceptional longevity — many survived, with minor updates, for decades.
