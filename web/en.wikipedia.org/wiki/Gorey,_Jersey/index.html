Gorey, Jersey

LocationEast coast of Jersey, Channel Islands
ParishesSt Martin and Grouville
Known forMont Orgueil Castle, harbour

Gorey (Jèrriais: Gouôrray) is a village on the east coast of Jersey, in the Channel Islands. It straddles the border between the parishes of St Martin and Grouville, and is dominated by Mont Orgueil Castle, which overlooks its harbour. It is one of the island's three main harbours.

History

The harbour and castle made Gorey a strategically important settlement from the medieval period. Mont Orgueil, built in the early 13th century after Jersey's separation from mainland Normandy, defended the island's eastern approach. In the 19th century Gorey was a centre of oyster fishing, with a large fleet working banks between Jersey and France, and later of shipbuilding and tourism.

Landmarks

Mont Orgueil Castle is open to the public and offers views across the Royal Bay of Grouville to the French coast. Gorey Pier hosts restaurants and the departure point for boat trips. The village is a stop on Jersey's bus network and lies near the end of the railway-era Gorey branch line, closed in 1929.
