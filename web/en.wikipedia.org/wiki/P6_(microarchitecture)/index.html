P6 (microarchitecture)


Launched: 1995 (Pentium Pro). Products: Pentium Pro, Pentium II, Pentium III, Pentium M, Core.

The P6 microarchitecture is the sixth generation of Intel's x86 microarchitecture, launched with the Pentium Pro processor in November 1995. It introduced out-of-order execution, register renaming, and speculative execution to Intel's mainstream line, translating x86 instructions into RISC-like micro-operations.

Design

P6 decodes variable-length x86 instructions into fixed micro-ops, which are dispatched to reservation stations and executed out of order on multiple execution units, then retired in program order through a reorder buffer. The Pentium Pro also pioneered an on-package L2 cache running at full core speed. Later members added features such as MMX (Pentium II) and SSE (Pentium III).

Descendants

The design lineage continued through the Pentium II and Pentium III, and — after the NetBurst detour of the Pentium 4 — was revived and refined in the Pentium M and Intel Core processors designed in Haifa, Israel. Its descendants, via the Core microarchitecture, underpin Intel's subsequent Nehalem and later designs, making P6 arguably the most influential x86 microarchitecture.

Significance

P6's aggressive dynamic execution delivered a large performance jump, especially on 32-bit code, and established the template — decode to micro-ops, execute out of order — followed by essentially all high-performance x86 processors since, including those of AMD.
