Dermatome

A dermatome is an area of skin that is mainly supplied by afferent nerve fibres from the dorsal root of a single spinal nerve. There are eight cervical nerves, twelve thoracic nerves, five lumbar nerves, and five sacral nerves; with the exception of the first cervical nerve (C1), each supplies a characteristic dermatome.

Clinical significance

Dermatome maps allow clinicians to localize lesions of the spinal cord and nerve roots: loss of sensation or pain in a single dermatome suggests damage to the corresponding spinal nerve. A well-known example is shingles (herpes zoster), in which reactivated varicella-zoster virus travels down one sensory nerve and produces a painful rash confined to a dermatome. Dermatomes overlap to a variable degree, so neighbouring regions can differ between published maps (such as the Keegan and Garrett and Foerster maps), which were derived from different methods.

Related terms

Dermatomes are the cutaneous counterpart of myotomes (groups of muscles supplied by one spinal root) and should not be confused with the regions supplied by peripheral nerves, which combine fibres from several roots. In embryology, "dermatome" also names the part of the somite that gives rise to the dermis.
