Western African Ebola virus epidemic

The Western African Ebola virus epidemic (2013–2016) was the most widespread outbreak of Ebola virus disease in history. It caused major loss of life and socioeconomic disruption in the region, mainly in Guinea, Liberia, and Sierra Leone. The first cases were recorded in Guinea in December 2013; the disease later spread to neighbouring Liberia and Sierra Leone, with smaller outbreaks in Nigeria and Mali and secondary cases in the United States and Europe.

Scale

The World Health Organization (WHO) and respective governments reported a total of 28,616 suspected, probable, and confirmed cases and 11,310 deaths before the end of the epidemic was declared in June 2016, making it many times larger than all previous Ebola outbreaks combined.

Response

In August 2014 the WHO declared the outbreak a Public Health Emergency of International Concern. International agencies, governments, and non-governmental organisations mounted a large response involving treatment centres, contact tracing, safe burial teams, and community engagement. Clinical trials conducted during the epidemic produced the first licensed Ebola vaccine, rVSV-ZEBOV, shown to be highly effective in a ring vaccination trial in Guinea in 2015.

Causes and spread

The outbreak is thought to have begun with zoonotic transmission from a wild animal, possibly a fruit bat, to a young child in the village of Méliandou, Guinea. Transmission between people occurred through direct contact with the blood or body fluids of infected people, and burial practices involving contact with corpses amplified spread.
