Albert Mehrabian (born 17 November 1939) is an Iranian-American psychologist and Professor Emeritus of Psychology at the University of California, Los Angeles. He is best known for his publications on the relative importance of verbal and nonverbal messages in communication, and for the Pleasure–Arousal–Dominance (PAD) model of emotion and temperament.

The 7–38–55 rule

Mehrabian's experiments in the 1960s, reported in his book Silent Messages (1971), examined how listeners judge a speaker's feelings when words, tone of voice, and facial expression are inconsistent. From two studies he derived the widely quoted formula: feelings and attitudes are communicated 7% by words, 38% by tone of voice, and 55% by facial expression.

Mehrabian has repeatedly stressed that this formula applies only to the communication of feelings and attitudes in ambiguous situations, not to communication in general — a qualification often lost when the "7–38–55 rule" is cited in business and self-help literature as a claim about all communication.

Other work

His research ranged over nonverbal communication, environmental psychology — including how physical environments affect mood and behaviour — and temperament theory. The PAD model describes emotional states along three dimensions: pleasure (valence), arousal, and dominance, and Mehrabian extended it into a framework for describing personality temperament. He also developed psychometric scales for traits such as trait arousability and sensitivity to rejection.
