The Samaññaphala Sutta is the second discourse (DN 2) of the Digha Nikaya of the Pali Canon. Its title means "The Fruits of the Contemplative Life", and it is one of the most important early Buddhist texts on ethics, meditation and the monastic path.

Summary

In the discourse, King Ajatasattu of Magadha asks the Buddha what visible, immediate fruit a contemplative life produces — just as craftspeople enjoy the fruits of their trades. The Buddha first recounts unconvincing answers the king had received from six rival teachers, a valuable early record of non-Buddhist Indian philosophies, and then gives his own graduated answer: the joy of renunciation, the practice of virtue, sense restraint, mindfulness, the four jhanas, insight, and finally liberation.

Significance

The sutta's stock passage on the gradual training is repeated throughout the Pali Canon, and the text is also known for Ajatasattu's confession of having murdered his father, King Bimbisara.
