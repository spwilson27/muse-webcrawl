Kensington

Kensington is a district in the Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea in central London. It is an affluent area characterised by grand Victorian terraced houses, garden squares, and major cultural institutions. Kensington is home to Kensington Palace, Kensington Gardens, and the museum district of South Kensington.

Geography and character

Kensington lies immediately west of Hyde Park. Kensington High Street is the district's main commercial thoroughfare, while areas such as Kensington Square — one of London's oldest garden squares — and the streets around Kensington Palace are among the most expensive residential addresses in the United Kingdom. Notting Hill lies to the north, Earl's Court to the south-west, and Chelsea to the south.

History

The manor of Kensington is recorded in the Domesday Book as Chenesitone. It remained a rural village until the late 17th century, when William III purchased Nottingham House — enlarged into Kensington Palace by Christopher Wren — making the area fashionable with courtiers and aristocrats. Rapid development followed in the 19th century, producing the stucco terraces and squares that define the district today. Kensington was the birthplace of Queen Victoria, who was born at Kensington Palace in 1819.

Landmarks

Major landmarks include Kensington Palace, the Royal Albert Hall and Albert Memorial (in South Kensington, by Kensington Gardens), the Natural History Museum, the Victoria and Albert Museum, the Science Museum, and Imperial College London. The district is served by High Street Kensington, Gloucester Road, and South Kensington Underground stations.
