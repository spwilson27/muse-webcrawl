Wishbone formation

The wishbone formation, also known simply as the bone, is an offensive formation in American football. The formation is a variation of the T-formation, and consists of three running backs: the quarterback lines up under center, with the fullback directly behind him and two halfbacks split behind and to either side of the fullback. The resulting backfield alignment resembles the shape of a wishbone.

History

The wishbone was developed in 1968 by University of Texas assistant coach Emory Bellard, who adapted the formation while seeking a way to combine elements of the split-T option offense. Head coach Darrell Royal adopted it partway through the 1968 season, and Texas went on a long winning streak, winning national championships in 1969 and 1970. The offense, powered by the triple option, spread rapidly through college football: Barry Switzer's Oklahoma teams won multiple national titles running the wishbone in the 1970s and 1980s, and Bear Bryant adopted it at Alabama.

Description

The wishbone is a run-oriented formation built around the triple option, in which the quarterback reads the defense and chooses between handing off to the fullback up the middle, keeping the ball, or pitching to a trailing halfback. The formation requires a disciplined offensive line and a quarterback who is a capable runner, but offers limited passing capability, since only two wide receivers are typically on the field and the backs are aligned close to the formation.

Decline

By the 1990s most major college programs had abandoned the wishbone in favor of more balanced pro-style and spread offenses, as defensive speed and scheme adjustments reduced its effectiveness. Variants survived in the flexbone offenses run for many years by the service academies, and the formation still appears occasionally as a short-yardage package.
