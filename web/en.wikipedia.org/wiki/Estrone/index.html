Estrone

IUPAC name3-Hydroxyestra-1,3,5(10)-trien-17-one
FormulaC18H22O2
Molar mass270.37 g/mol
Other namesE1; oestrone

Estrone (E1), also spelled oestrone, is a steroid, a weak estrogen, and one of the three major endogenous estrogens, the other two being estradiol (E2) and estriol (E3). Estrone, as well as the other estrogens, is synthesised from cholesterol and secreted mainly from the gonads, though they can also be formed from adrenal androgens in adipose tissue.

Biological role

Estrone is the predominant circulating estrogen in postmenopausal women, in whom it is produced mainly by aromatisation of androstenedione in peripheral tissues such as fat. It can be converted reversibly to estradiol by 17β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase, and it serves as a circulating reservoir for the more potent estradiol. Estrone sulfate, its major conjugated form, has a long half-life and acts as a stable pool from which active estrogens can be regenerated in target tissues.

History

Estrone was the first estrogen to be isolated and crystallised: in 1929 Edward Doisy in the United States and Adolf Butenandt in Germany independently isolated it from the urine of pregnant women. Butenandt later received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1939 in part for his work on sex hormones.

Clinical use

Estrone has been available as a medication, for instance as estrone sulfate in esterified estrogen preparations used in menopausal hormone therapy. Measurement of estrone and estrone sulfate levels is occasionally used in the assessment of estrogen production in postmenopausal women and in some endocrine disorders.
