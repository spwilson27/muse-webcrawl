Scuderia Ferrari

Scuderia Ferrari is the racing division of the Italian luxury car manufacturer Ferrari and the name of the Formula One team it fields. Founded by Enzo Ferrari in 1929 in Modena as a racing team for Alfa Romeo cars, it began building cars under its own name in 1947. Based in Maranello, it is the oldest surviving and most successful Formula One team, having competed in every season since the championship began in 1950.

Formula One record

Ferrari has won a record number of Constructors' Championships (16) and its drivers have won 15 Drivers' Championships, including five consecutive titles with Michael Schumacher between 2000 and 2004. Other Ferrari world champions include Alberto Ascari, Juan Manuel Fangio, Mike Hawthorn, Phil Hill, John Surtees, Niki Lauda, Jody Scheckter and Kimi Räikkönen, the team's most recent drivers' champion in 2007.

Identity

The team's racing red colour, rosso corsa, its prancing horse emblem — adopted from First World War fighter pilot Francesco Baracca — and its devoted fans, the tifosi, make it one of the most recognisable brands in sport. Scuderia Ferrari also supports the Ferrari Driver Academy and customer racing programmes in GT competition.
