Fort King was a United States Army fort in north central Florida, near present-day Ocala in Marion County. Established in 1827 and named for Colonel William King, a former commander of the 4th Infantry Regiment, it was built beside the agency for the Seminole Indians and became one of the most important military posts during the Second Seminole War.

History

The fort guarded the Fort King Road between Fort Brooke (Tampa) and Fort Marion (St. Augustine) and served as the administrative centre for the Seminole Agency under agent Wiley Thompson. In December 1835, the war leader Osceola killed Thompson outside the fort, an event coordinated with the Dade Massacre the same day — together these ignited the Second Seminole War (1835–1842), during which Fort King served as a major US Army headquarters and supply depot under generals including Duncan Clinch and Thomas Jesup.

Later history

Abandoned by the Army in 1843, the site became the nucleus of the town of Ocala, serving briefly as Marion County's first courthouse. Archaeological work in the 20th century identified the fort's outline, and the site was designated a National Historic Landmark in 2004. A reconstructed blockhouse and interpretive centre now stand at the Fort King National Historic Landmark park.
