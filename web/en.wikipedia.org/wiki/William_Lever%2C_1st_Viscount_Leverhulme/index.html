William Hesketh Lever, 1st Viscount Leverhulme (19 September 1851 – 7 May 1925) was an English industrialist, philanthropist and politician. He co-founded Lever Brothers, whose Sunlight Soap pioneered branded, packaged consumer goods and modern advertising, and built the model village of Port Sunlight for his workers. His company later became part of Unilever.

Lever Brothers

Lever began in his family's wholesale grocery business in Bolton. In 1884 he and his brother James launched Sunlight Soap, manufactured from vegetable oils rather than tallow, and marketed with unprecedented advertising and branding. To secure production he built a factory on the Wirral from 1888, surrounded by the garden village of Port Sunlight, with cottages, schools and amenities for employees.

Expansion and politics

Lever expanded internationally, acquiring plantations and companies across Europe, America, Australia and Africa, and diversified into brands such as Lifebuoy and Lux. He sat as a Liberal MP for the Wirral from 1906 to 1910, was raised to the peerage as Baron Leverhulme in 1917 and made a viscount in 1922, taking his title from his name and his wife's birthplace, Hulme.

Legacy

Lever endowed the Leverhulme Trust, a major funder of research and education, and left collections that formed the Lady Lever Art Gallery at Port Sunlight. In 1929, four years after his death, Lever Brothers merged with the Dutch Margarine Unie to form Unilever.
