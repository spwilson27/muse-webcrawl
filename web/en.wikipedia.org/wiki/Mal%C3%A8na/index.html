Malèna is a 2000 Italian romantic drama film written and directed by Giuseppe Tornatore, starring Monica Bellucci in the title role and Giuseppe Sulfaro as the boy who adores her. Set in a small Sicilian town during the Second World War, the film is a coming-of-age story told through the eyes of a twelve-year-old who becomes obsessed with Malèna Scordia, the town's most beautiful woman, whose life is destroyed by gossip, envy, and war. Ennio Morricone's score was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Score, and John Seale's cinematography was also Oscar-nominated.

Plot

In 1940, in the fictional Sicilian town of Castelcutò, thirteen-year-old Renato Amoroso falls hopelessly in love with Maddalena "Malèna" Scordia, whose husband is away at war. The town's men lust after her and its women spread vicious rumours. When word arrives that her husband has been killed, Malèna is left unprotected: shunned, impoverished, and denied work, she is driven first into the hands of a lawyer who exploits her, and finally, after the Allied occupation, into prostitution with German and then American soldiers. When the town is liberated, its women drag her into the square, beat her, and cut off her hair. She leaves Castelcutò. A year later her husband — alive, having lost an arm — returns, and the couple come back to the town, walking through the square with dignity as Renato, who has quietly helped them reunite, whispers his only words to her: "Good luck, Mrs Malèna."

Themes and reception

The film is a study of beauty as a curse, of the cruelty of gossip and communal hypocrisy, and of adolescent longing, filtered through Tornatore's characteristic nostalgia. It was an international commercial success and established Bellucci internationally, though critics were divided: many praised the cinematography, score, and central performance while some found its treatment of its heroine sentimental or voyeuristic. The American release by Miramax was cut by several minutes; the full Italian cut runs about 109 minutes.
