Jubilee Line Extension

The Jubilee line extension is the extension of the Jubilee line of the London Underground from Green Park to Stratford, via Southwark, Canary Wharf, and the Greenwich Peninsula. Opened in stages during 1999, it was the largest expansion of the Underground in a generation, costing about £3.5 billion.

Background

The extension was promoted to serve the redeveloped London Docklands, particularly the Canary Wharf financial district, and the regeneration areas of south and east London. Authorized by Acts of Parliament in the early 1990s, construction began in 1993.

Route and stations

The extension added or rebuilt eleven stations, including Westminster, Waterloo, Southwark, London Bridge, Bermondsey, Canada Water, Canary Wharf, Heron Quays, North Greenwich, Canning Town, and Stratford. Several stations, designed by leading architects, are noted for their spacious architecture; Canary Wharf station, designed by Foster and Partners, was voted one of Londoners' favourite buildings.

Opening and significance

The line opened in three stages in 1999, fully entering service in time for the Millennium Dome at North Greenwich. It introduced platform screen doors at the new underground stations and remains essential infrastructure for Docklands and events at The O2 arena.
