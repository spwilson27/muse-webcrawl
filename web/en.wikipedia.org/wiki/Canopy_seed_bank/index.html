Canopy seed bank

A canopy seed bank or aerial seed bank is the store of seeds held in the canopy of a plant, rather than in the soil. It is characteristic of serotinous species, whose woody fruits or cones remain closed on the plant for years and open to release seed after fire — a trait called serotiny.

Ecology

Canopy seed banks are common in fire-prone ecosystems such as Australian banksia and hakea shrublands, South African fynbos proteas, and North American lodgepole pine forests. Storing seeds above ground protects them from seed predators and soil pathogens, and fire cues mass release onto a nutrient-rich, competition-free ash bed ideal for germination.

Contrast with soil seed banks

Unlike a soil seed bank, which can accumulate seeds over many seasons, a canopy bank's size reflects the plant's reproductive output since the last fire, making populations vulnerable if fires come too frequently (before plants mature) or too rarely (as seeds age and plants senesce).
