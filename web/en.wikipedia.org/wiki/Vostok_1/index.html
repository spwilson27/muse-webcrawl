Vostok 1

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Vostok 1 re-entry module.

Vostok 1 was the first spaceflight of the Soviet Vostok programme and the first human spaceflight in history. On 12 April 1961, cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin completed a single orbit of Earth in 108 minutes, launching from Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan aboard a rocket derived from the R-7 intercontinental ballistic missile.

Gagarin's call sign was Kedr (Siberian pine). Famously, he is reported to have said 'Poyekhali!' ('Let's go!') at liftoff. Because engineers were unsure how weightlessness would affect a pilot, the capsule's manual controls were locked, with an override code sealed in an envelope. At the end of the orbit the service module initially failed to separate cleanly, causing a violent tumble before the cables burned through. Gagarin ejected from the capsule at about 7 km altitude and landed by parachute near Engels, in Saratov Oblast — a detail kept secret for years, since aviation records rules technically required a pilot to land with his craft.

Aftermath

Gagarin became an international celebrity and a hero of the Soviet Union. 12 April is celebrated as Cosmonautics Day in Russia and as the International Day of Human Space Flight. The flight opened the Space Race era of crewed competition that culminated in the Apollo program.
