Skeet shooting

Skeet shooting is a recreational and competitive shooting sport in which participants use shotguns to break clay targets mechanically flung into the air from fixed stations at high speed from a variety of angles. The sport developed in the United States in the 1920s as a form of practice for bird hunting and as an off-season alternative to trap shooting.

Field layout

A skeet field has two target-throwing houses — a "high house" on the left and a "low house" on the right — facing each other across a semicircle of seven shooting stations, with an eighth station midway between the houses. Targets fly along a fixed path crossing the centre of the field. Shooters move through the stations, shooting singles and doubles (two targets launched simultaneously from opposite houses) at the middle stations.

American and Olympic skeet

In American skeet, governed by the National Skeet Shooting Association, targets travel at a set speed and the gun may be pre-mounted. Olympic (international) skeet, governed by the ISSF, uses faster targets, a variable delay of up to three seconds after the shooter calls for the target, and requires the gun to be held in a low ready position until the target appears. Skeet has been an Olympic event since 1968; women's skeet was added in 2000.

Equipment

Shooters typically use over-and-under or semi-automatic shotguns, most often 12 gauge, with open chokes suited to the short ranges involved. Targets are standard clay discs about 110 mm in diameter.
