Bel Air, Harford County, Maryland

Bel Air is a town and the county seat of Harford County, Maryland, United States. Located about 25 miles (40 km) northeast of Baltimore, it is the commercial and governmental centre of the county and identifies itself as "The Heart of Harford." The town had a population of about 10,661 at the 2020 census. It should not be confused with the census-designated place of the same name in Allegany County, nor with Bel Air South, the larger adjacent unincorporated community.

History

The town grew from a land grant known as Scott's Old Fields. In 1782 the Maryland General Assembly chose the site as the seat of Harford County, and the settlement, first called Scott's Bluff, was renamed Belle Aire, then Bel Air. A courthouse was built on Main Street, around which the town developed as a market and coach stop on the road between Baltimore and Pennsylvania. Bel Air was incorporated in 1874. The Maryland and Pennsylvania Railroad ("Ma and Pa") served the town for much of the twentieth century, and after the Second World War Bel Air grew rapidly as a suburb of Baltimore, surrounded by sprawling subdivisions while retaining a small historic core.

Geography and government

Bel Air covers about 3.1 square miles in the Piedmont region of northeastern Maryland, near the fall line. It is governed by an elected board of town commissioners. The Harford County courthouse, public schools including Bel Air High School, and the Upper Chesapeake Medical Center make it a regional centre. The town's most famous historical native is the actor Edwin Booth, brother of John Wilkes Booth, who was born at Tudor Hall near Bel Air.
