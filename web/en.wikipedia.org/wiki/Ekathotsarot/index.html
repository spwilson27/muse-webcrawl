Ekathotsarot

Reign: 1605 – 1620

Kingdom: Ayutthaya (Siam)

Predecessor: Naresuan

Successor: Si Saowaphak

Died: 1620, Ayutthaya

Ekathotsarot (Thai: เอกาทศรถ, pronounced [ʔēː.kāː.tʰót.sā.rót]; died 1620), known in his lifetime as Sanphet III, was the King of Ayutthaya from 1605 to 1620 and the younger brother of King Naresuan the Great.

Early life and campaigns

Ekathotsarot was the son of King Maha Thammaracha and Queen Wisutkasat. Known as the "White Prince" (in contrast to his elder brother, the "Black Prince"), he campaigned alongside Naresuan in the wars that freed Ayutthaya from Burmese domination, and was at his brother's side at the famous elephant duel of Nong Sarai in 1593. He succeeded peacefully on Naresuan's death during a campaign in 1605.

Reign

Whereas Naresuan's reign had been one of near-constant war, Ekathotsarot's was comparatively peaceful and prosperous, devoted to reorganising the kingdom's administration, law and finances. He expanded foreign trade: relations were developed with the Dutch, who received permission to open a factory, and with the English East India Company; letters were exchanged with Prince Maurice of Orange, and the first Siamese embassy reached Europe in these years. Japanese traders and mercenaries under Yamada Nagamasa also grew influential at his court.

Death and succession

Ekathotsarot died in 1620, reportedly grief-stricken after the death of his son and heir, Prince Suthat, who had been poisoned amid court intrigue. He was succeeded briefly by his younger son Si Saowaphak, who was soon deposed and executed, the throne passing to Songtham.
