The Aegis Combat System is an American integrated naval weapons system developed by the Missile and Surface Radar Division of RCA, now produced by Lockheed Martin. It uses powerful computer and radar technology to track and guide weapons to destroy enemy targets, and forms the core of the United States Navy's air and missile defence capability.

Components

The heart of the system is the AN/SPY-1, a passive electronically scanned array radar able to track hundreds of targets simultaneously. Aegis integrates the radar with a command-and-decision system, a weapons control system, and vertical launching system (VLS) cells firing Standard Missile family interceptors (SM-2, SM-3 and SM-6), Evolved Sea Sparrow missiles and Tomahawk cruise missiles. The name Aegis refers to the shield of Zeus and Athena in Greek mythology.

History

Development began in the 1960s in response to the threat of Soviet saturation anti-ship missile attacks. The first Aegis ship, the cruiser USS Ticonderoga, was commissioned in 1983; the system was later installed on the Arleigh Burke-class destroyers, which were designed around it. Aegis ships were involved in the 1988 shootdown of Iran Air Flight 655 by USS Vincennes, an incident attributed in part to crew misinterpretation of Aegis data under stress.

Ballistic missile defence

The Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense System uses the SM-3 interceptor to destroy ballistic missiles outside the atmosphere, and Aegis-equipped ships have also performed an anti-satellite intercept. A land-based variant, Aegis Ashore, has been deployed in Romania and Poland. Allied operators include Japan, South Korea, Spain, Norway and Australia.
