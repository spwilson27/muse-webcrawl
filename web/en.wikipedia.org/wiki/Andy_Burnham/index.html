Andrew Murray Burnham (born 7 January 1970) is a British politician who has served as Mayor of Greater Manchester since 2017. A member of the Labour Party, he was Member of Parliament (MP) for Leigh from 2001 to 2017 and served in Gordon Brown's cabinet as Chief Secretary to the Treasury (2007–2008), Secretary of State for Culture, Media and Sport (2008–2009), and Secretary of State for Health (2009–2010). Twice a candidate for the Labour leadership (2010 and 2015), he became one of the most prominent advocates of English devolution, earning the media nickname "King of the North".

Early life and parliamentary career

Burnham was born in Aintree, Liverpool, and grew up in Culcheth, Cheshire. He read English at Fitzwilliam College, Cambridge, then worked as a researcher for MP Tessa Jowell and for the NHS Confederation and the Football Task Force before being elected to Parliament for Leigh in 2001. He rose through junior ministerial posts at the Home Office and Department of Health, entering the Cabinet in 2007. As Culture Secretary he oversaw the digital switchover process and commissioned the Taylor Report on Hillsborough; as Health Secretary he defended the NHS against market reforms and set up the public inquiry into failings at Mid Staffordshire NHS Foundation Trust.

Leadership bids and shadow cabinet

Burnham stood for the Labour leadership in 2010, finishing fourth, and served as Shadow Health Secretary under Ed Miliband, campaigning against the coalition government's Health and Social Care Act. In the 2015 leadership election he finished second to Jeremy Corbyn, then served as Shadow Home Secretary before resigning in 2016 to seek the newly created Greater Manchester mayoralty.

Mayor of Greater Manchester

Burnham won the first mayoral election in May 2017 with 63% of the vote and was re-elected in 2021 with 67% and again in 2024. His tenure has centred on taking buses back under public control through the "Bee Network" franchising scheme — the first outside London — integrating public transport, tackling rough sleeping through the "A Bed Every Night" programme, and pressing for greater devolved powers, culminating in the 2023 "trailblazer" devolution deal. In October 2020 he won national attention by resisting the government's imposition of Tier 3 COVID-19 restrictions on Greater Manchester without additional financial support. He has campaigned for justice for victims of the Hillsborough disaster, having spoken at the 2009 memorial service where the crowd's reaction helped spur the Hillsborough Independent Panel, and he co-founded the campaign for "Hillsborough Law" duty-of-candour legislation.
