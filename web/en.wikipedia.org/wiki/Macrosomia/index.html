Macrosomia

Macrosomia
SpecialtyObstetrics, neonatology
DefinitionBirth weight above 4,000–4,500 g

Macrosomia, also called large for gestational age in some contexts, describes a newborn with an excessive birth weight. Definitions vary, but the term is commonly applied to babies weighing more than 4,000 grams (8 lb 13 oz) at birth, regardless of gestational age; a threshold of 4,500 grams is also used. The condition is associated with an increased risk of complications for both mother and baby during delivery.

Risk factors

The best-established risk factor is maternal diabetes, including gestational diabetes, in which elevated maternal glucose crosses the placenta and stimulates fetal insulin production and growth. Other risk factors include maternal obesity, excessive gestational weight gain, prolonged pregnancy beyond 42 weeks, multiparity, a previous macrosomic infant, and genetic or syndromic overgrowth disorders such as Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome.

Complications

Macrosomia increases the risk of labour dystocia and shoulder dystocia, in which the baby's shoulders become lodged after delivery of the head; this can cause brachial plexus injury or fracture of the clavicle or humerus. Maternal risks include caesarean delivery, postpartum haemorrhage and perineal trauma. Newborns are at higher risk of hypoglycaemia, polycythaemia and jaundice.

Diagnosis and management

Prenatal diagnosis by clinical examination and ultrasound is imprecise; estimated fetal weights commonly deviate by 10–15% from actual birth weight. Management focuses on glycaemic control in diabetic mothers, consideration of induction of labour near term in selected cases, and planning for possible caesarean delivery where the estimated weight is very high.
