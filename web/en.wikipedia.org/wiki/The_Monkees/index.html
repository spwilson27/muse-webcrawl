The Monkees

The Monkees were an American pop rock band formed in Los Angeles in 1966 for the television series of the same name. The quartet — Micky Dolenz, Michael Nesmith, Peter Tork and Davy Jones — became one of the best-selling acts of the late 1960s.

OriginLos Angeles, California, U.S.
GenresPop rock, bubblegum pop
Years active1966–1971; reunions thereafter
LabelsColgems, RCA, Rhino

Career

Cast by producers Bob Rafelson and Bert Schneider, the group starred in The Monkees (1966–68) while session musicians initially played on their records. Hits including "Last Train to Clarksville" and "I'm a Believer" topped the charts. Rebelling against their manufactured image, the members fought for and won control of their music on the album Headquarters (1967). They later starred in the cult film Head (1968).

Legacy

Though dismissed by some critics as the "Pre-Fab Four", the Monkees' music and show have been reassessed warmly; the band reunited repeatedly, and their influence on pop television and music video is widely acknowledged.
