Dallas Love Field (IATA: DAL) is a city-owned public airport located six miles northwest of downtown Dallas, Texas. It was Dallas's main airport until the opening of Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport in 1974, and it is now the corporate headquarters and a major hub of Southwest Airlines.

History

Love Field was established in 1917 as a U.S. Army Air Service training field and named after First Lieutenant Moss L. Love, killed in a flying accident in 1913. It became a municipal airport in 1927. On 22 November 1963, Air Force One landed at Love Field with President John F. Kennedy; hours later, after Kennedy's assassination, Lyndon B. Johnson was sworn in as president aboard the aircraft at the airport.

The Wright Amendment

To protect the new DFW Airport, the Wright Amendment of 1979 restricted flights from Love Field to destinations within Texas and a handful of neighbouring states, and later limited through-ticketing. The restrictions were gradually relaxed (the Shelby Amendment of 1997 added further states) and fully repealed effective October 2014, after which Southwest and other carriers expanded nonstop service nationwide. A compromise agreement caps the airport at 20 gates.

Facilities

Love Field has two runways and a single terminal with 20 gates, rebuilt under the Love Field Modernization Program completed in 2014. Southwest Airlines dominates operations, with additional service from Delta Air Lines and Alaska Airlines. The Frontiers of Flight Museum is located on the airport grounds.
