Federal Project Number One

A poster for the Federal Theatre Project, one of the five divisions of Federal Project Number One.

Federal Project Number One, also referred to as Federal One, was a group of five New Deal projects in the United States that provided jobs for unemployed artists, musicians, actors, and writers during the Great Depression. It was created in 1935 under the Works Progress Administration (WPA), directed by Harry Hopkins, and formed part of the Second New Deal.

The five projects

  • Federal Art Project (FAP) – employed visual artists to create murals, paintings, sculpture, and posters.
  • Federal Music Project (FMP) – supported orchestras, bands, and music education.
  • Federal Theatre Project (FTP) – staged plays and revived popular theatre across the country.
  • Federal Writers' Project (FWP) – produced the American Guide Series of state guidebooks and collected folklore and oral histories, including narratives of formerly enslaved people.
  • Historical Records Survey (HRS) – inventoried and preserved historical documents and archives.

Leadership and legacy

Each project was led by a national director — Holger Cahill (art), Nikolai Sokoloff (music), Hallie Flanagan (theatre), Henry Alsberg (writers), and Luther Evans (records). Federal One employed tens of thousands of cultural workers and produced an enduring body of public art, performances, and publications. It provoked controversy over alleged political content and government patronage of the arts, and most of its components were wound down by 1939–1943 as war mobilisation approached.
