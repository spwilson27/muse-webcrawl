Gag reflex

Also calledPharyngeal reflex
Afferent nerveGlossopharyngeal (CN IX)
Efferent nerveVagus (CN X)

The gag reflex, also called the pharyngeal reflex, is a protective contraction of the back of the throat triggered by an object touching the roof of the mouth, the back of the tongue, the tonsils, or the upper throat, helping to prevent foreign objects from entering the airway or being swallowed.

Reflex arc

Sensory input travels via the glossopharyngeal nerve (cranial nerve IX) to the medulla, and the motor response is carried mainly by the vagus nerve (cranial nerve X), producing elevation of the soft palate and constriction of the pharyngeal muscles.

Clinical significance

Clinicians test the gag reflex as part of the neurological examination of the lower cranial nerves; its absence may indicate nerve damage, while an exaggerated reflex can complicate dental work, dentures, and some medical procedures. A proportion of healthy people have no demonstrable gag reflex, so it is interpreted together with other findings.
