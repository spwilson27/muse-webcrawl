Joseph (Genesis)

Joseph (Hebrew: יוֹסֵף, Yosef) is a central figure in the Book of Genesis, the eleventh of the twelve sons of Jacob and the first son of Rachel. His story — chapters 37 to 50 of Genesis — recounts his betrayal by his brothers, his rise from slavery and prison to become vizier of Egypt, and his reconciliation with his family, which brings Jacob's household to Egypt and sets the stage for the Exodus narrative.

The coat and the sale into slavery

Favoured by Jacob, who gave him a richly ornamented robe (traditionally, a "coat of many colours"), and hated by his brothers for his dreams of dominion over them, Joseph was seized at Dothan, stripped of his robe, and sold to Ishmaelite (Midianite) traders for twenty pieces of silver. The brothers deceived Jacob with the bloodied robe, and Jacob mourned inconsolably.

In Egypt

In Egypt Joseph served Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh, and was falsely accused by Potiphar's wife and imprisoned. In prison he interpreted the dreams of Pharaoh's cupbearer and baker, and two years later was summoned to interpret Pharaoh's dreams of seven fat and seven lean cows, and seven full and seven thin ears of grain. Joseph predicted seven years of plenty followed by seven of famine and proposed a programme of storage; Pharaoh appointed him over Egypt, giving him the name Zaphenath-Paneah and Asenath as wife, by whom he had Manasseh and Ephraim.

Reconciliation and death

During the famine Joseph's brothers came to Egypt to buy grain. After testing them — most stringently over his younger brother Benjamin — Joseph revealed himself, forgave them ("ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good"), and brought the whole family to settle in Goshen. Before his death at the age of 110, Joseph made the Israelites swear to carry his bones up from Egypt when God visited them — a charge fulfilled in the Exodus and recorded as completed in the Book of Joshua. In later tradition Joseph is a patriarch of the tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh, and a revered figure in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, where the twelfth surah of the Qur'an, Yusuf, retells his story.
