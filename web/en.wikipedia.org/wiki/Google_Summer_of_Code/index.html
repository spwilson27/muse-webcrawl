Google Summer of Code

Google Summer of Code, often abbreviated to GSoC, is an international annual program in which Google awards stipends to contributors who successfully complete a free and open-source software coding project during the summer. The program is open to anyone aged 18 or over; it was open only to students until 2022, when eligibility was expanded to all newcomers to open source.

History

The program was founded in 2005 by Google co-founder Sergey Brin and its open-source team, inspired by similar initiatives such as the Linux Fund. In its first year about 400 students worked with mentoring organisations; the program has since grown to roughly a thousand or more contributors and around two hundred open-source organisations annually, including the Apache Software Foundation, Python Software Foundation, Wikimedia, and many smaller projects.

Structure

Open-source organisations apply to mentor, publish project ideas, and review contributor proposals. Accepted contributors work remotely with mentors over a coding period of about twelve weeks (longer projects may run up to 22 weeks), passing midterm and final evaluations before receiving their stipend, which is sized by project length and adjusted by country. Since its inception the program has introduced well over 20,000 new contributors to open-source development and produced millions of lines of publicly available code.
