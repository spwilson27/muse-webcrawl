French Foreign Legion

Legionnaires wearing the traditional white kepi.

The French Foreign Legion (French: Légion étrangère) is an elite corps of the French Army, established by King Louis Philippe on 10 March 1831. It is unique in permitting foreign nationals to enlist; recruits serve initially under an assumed identity (anonymat) and may apply for French citizenship after three years of service, or immediately if wounded in action ("Français par le sang versé" — French by spilled blood).

History

Created partly to absorb foreign refugees and reduce their presence in metropolitan France, the Legion fought in Algeria, Mexico — where its stand at the Battle of Camarón (1863) became its defining legend — the Franco-Prussian War, both World Wars, Indochina (including Dien Bien Phu), Algeria, and numerous modern operations in Africa, Afghanistan, and Mali. After Algerian independence its headquarters moved from Sidi Bel Abbès to Aubagne, near Marseille.

Organisation and traditions

Today the Legion numbers around 9,000 legionnaires from roughly 140 countries, organised into infantry, cavalry, engineer, and parachute regiments, including the 2nd Foreign Parachute Regiment (2e REP) based in Corsica. Its traditions include the white kepi, the slow marching pace of 88 steps per minute, the motto Legio Patria Nostra ("The Legion is our homeland"), and the annual commemoration of Camarón Day on 30 April.
