Servomotor


An industrial servomotor with encoder (placeholder image)

A servomotor (or servo motor) is a rotary or linear actuator that allows precise control of angular or linear position, velocity and acceleration. Unlike an ordinary motor, it forms part of a closed-loop servomechanism: a position sensor (typically an encoder or resolver) feeds back the output position to a controller, which continually corrects the motor's drive so that the output matches the commanded position.

Operation

A servomotor system consists of a motor, a feedback device, an amplifier or drive, and a controller implementing control algorithms such as PID. Industrial servomotors are usually AC synchronous or brushless DC machines offering high torque, rapid response and accurate positioning; smaller DC "hobby servos" — used in radio-controlled models and robotics — receive a pulse-width-modulated signal that sets a shaft angle over a limited range, using an internal potentiometer for feedback.

Applications

Servomotors are used wherever precise motion is needed: CNC machine tools, industrial robots, camera autofocus and stabilisation, printing and packaging machinery, aircraft control surfaces (historically among the earliest servomechanisms, in autopilots and ship steering engines), and animatronics.
