Franz Joseph I (German: Franz Joseph I.; 18 August 1830 – 21 November 1916) was Emperor of Austria, King of Hungary and ruler of the other states of the Habsburg monarchy from 2 December 1848 until his death in 1916. His reign of nearly 68 years was the longest of any Austrian or Hungarian ruler and among the longest in European history.


Franz Joseph I in later life

Accession and early reign

Franz Joseph came to the throne during the revolutions of 1848, when his uncle Ferdinand I abdicated and his father renounced his rights. The young emperor, guided by Prince Schwarzenberg, restored order with the help of the army; the revolutionary leader Lajos Kossuth's Hungary was subdued with Russian assistance in 1849. The first decade of the reign was neo-absolutist.

Wars and the Compromise

Austria lost Lombardy to France and Piedmont in 1859 and was defeated by Prussia in the Austro-Prussian War of 1866, ending Austrian leadership in Germany. In response Franz Joseph agreed the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867, creating the Dual Monarchy of Austria-Hungary, and was crowned King of Hungary in Budapest.

The emperor married his cousin, Duchess Elisabeth of Bavaria — the celebrated "Sisi" — in 1854. Their private life was marked by tragedy: their son Crown Prince Rudolf died at Mayerling in 1889, and Elisabeth was assassinated in Geneva in 1898.

Later reign and First World War

Franz Joseph became the patriarchal symbol of the multinational empire, famous for his iron work ethic and conservative devotion to duty. After the assassination of his heir, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914, he signed the ultimatum to Serbia and the declaration of war that set off the First World War. He died at Schönbrunn in November 1916, in the middle of the war, and was succeeded by his grandnephew Charles I, the last Habsburg emperor.
