Rudolf Ivanovich Abel (Russian: Рудольф Иванович Абель; 11 July 1903 – 15 November 1971), born William August Fisher, was a Soviet intelligence officer. Operating under the deep-cover identity of an artist in Brooklyn, New York, he directed a Soviet spy ring in the United States during the early Cold War. His arrest in 1957, trial, and subsequent exchange in 1962 for the captured American U-2 pilot Francis Gary Powers became one of the best-known espionage episodes of the era.

Fisher was born in Newcastle upon Tyne to Russian émigré parents who were associates of Lenin. The family returned to Russia in 1921, and Fisher joined Soviet intelligence in 1927. He served in signals and clandestine work before and during the Second World War.

Illegal residency in the United States

In 1948 Fisher entered the United States on a false passport and set up as a painter and photographer in Brooklyn under the name Emil Robert Goldfus. He ran a network of agents passing American secrets to Moscow, communicating through dead drops, hollow coins, and coded messages. His undoing came when his assistant, Reino Häyhänen, defected to the Americans in 1957 and revealed the espionage apparatus, including a hollow nickel containing a microfilmed message that the FBI had recovered years earlier from a Brooklyn newsboy.

Arrest, trial, and exchange

Arrested by the FBI in June 1957, Fisher gave his name as "Rudolf Abel" — in fact the name of a deceased colleague, which served as a signal to Moscow that he had been captured but had not cooperated. Defended by attorney James B. Donovan, he was convicted of conspiracy to commit espionage and sentenced to 30 years in prison. He remained silent throughout.

On 10 February 1962, Abel was exchanged at the Glienicke Bridge in Berlin for Francis Gary Powers, whose U-2 spy plane had been shot down over the Soviet Union in 1960. The exchange, depicted in the 2015 film Bridge of Spies, was negotiated by Donovan. Abel returned to the USSR, worked in KGB training, and died in Moscow in 1971.
