Public holidays in the Republic of Ireland

Public holidays in Ireland are governed principally by the Organisation of Working Time Act 1997. There are ten public holidays; when one falls on a weekend, employees are entitled to a day off in lieu, an additional day's pay, or an extra day of annual leave, though the holiday itself is not automatically moved.

Good Friday is not a public holiday, though many offices close and it was historically a day of alcohol-sale restriction until 2018. Saint Brigid's Day, added in 2023, is Ireland's first public holiday named after a woman, honouring both the saint and the Celtic festival of Imbolc. The term "bank holiday" survives from the Bank Holidays Act 1871, which applied when Ireland was part of the United Kingdom.
