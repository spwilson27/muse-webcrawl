Rugby Club Toulonnais, commonly known as RC Toulon or simply Toulon, is a French professional rugby union club based in Toulon in Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur. Founded in 1908 by the merger of local clubs, it competes in the Top 14, the top division of French rugby. Its home ground is the Stade Mayol, and its supporters are famous for the pre-match pilou-pilou chant.

History

Toulon won the French championship in 1931, 1987, and 1992 before entering a turbulent period that included relegation to Pro D2 in 2000 for financial reasons. The club's fortunes were transformed after 2006, when businessman Mourad Boudjellal became president and recruited international stars to the club.

Under coach Bernard Laporte, Toulon assembled a celebrated multinational squad — including Jonny Wilkinson, Bryan Habana, Drew Mitchell, Matt Giteau, and Bakkies Botha — and won three consecutive European Cups (Heineken Cup / European Rugby Champions Cup) in 2013, 2014, and 2015, adding the Top 14 title in 2014 to complete a historic double. Toulon became the first club to win three straight European titles. In 2023 the club won the EPCR Challenge Cup.

Honours

  • French champions (Top 14): 1931, 1987, 1992, 2014
  • European Rugby Champions Cup: 2013, 2014, 2015
  • EPCR Challenge Cup: 2023
  • Yves du Manoir Challenge: 1934, 1970

Notable players

Club legends include Christian Carrère, Éric Champ, Jérôme Gallion, Thierry Louvet, and André Herrero from earlier eras, and in the modern era Jonny Wilkinson, Felipe Contepomi, Juan Martín Fernández Lobbe, and Steffon Armitage.
