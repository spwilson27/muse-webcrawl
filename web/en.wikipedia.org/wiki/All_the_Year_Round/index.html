All the Year Round was a British weekly literary magazine founded and owned by Charles Dickens, published between 1859 and 1895. It succeeded Dickens's earlier journal Household Words, which he abandoned after a dispute with its publishers, taking much of its staff and readership with him. The title came from Shakespeare's Othello: "The story of my life, / From year to year…" — with the motto "A weekly journal. Conducted by Charles Dickens."

Serialised fiction

The magazine is remembered above all for serialising major novels. Its first numbers carried Dickens's A Tale of Two Cities (1859), and it later published his Great Expectations (1860–61), which Dickens substituted to rescue flagging sales, and Wilkie Collins's sensation novels The Woman in White (1859–60) and The Moonstone (1868). Other contributors included Elizabeth Gaskell, Edward Bulwer-Lytton, Charles Reade, Anthony Trollope and Sheridan Le Fanu. Dickens's own The Uncommercial Traveller essays appeared in its pages, and the Christmas numbers sold in huge quantities.

After Dickens

Dickens edited the journal until his death on 9 June 1870; his son Charles Dickens Jr. succeeded him as editor and proprietor, and the magazine continued until 1895. Unusually, contributions were anonymous, appearing under the authority of the "conductor" alone — a practice that frustrated contributors such as Gaskell but reinforced the journal's single editorial voice.
