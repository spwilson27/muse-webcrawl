Battle of Gravelotte

The Battle of Gravelotte, known in Germany as the Battle of Gravelotte–St. Privat, was fought on 18 August 1870 during the Franco-Prussian War, west of Metz in Lorraine. It was the largest battle of the war: the Prussian-led German armies under King Wilhelm I and Helmuth von Moltke attacked the French Army of the Rhine under Marshal François Bazaine in its strong defensive positions and, at heavy cost, forced it back into the fortress of Metz, where it was besieged and eventually compelled to surrender.

Background

Following the battles of Mars-la-Tour (16 August) and the earlier frontier engagements, Bazaine's army was retreating on Metz and took up a strong position along a ridge running from the Moselle at Rozerieulles north through Gravelotte, Amanvillers and St. Privat to Roncourt. Moltke intended to pin the French frontally with the First Army while the Second Army swung round their northern flank. The battle developed prematurely when Steinmetz's First Army attacked across the steep Mance ravine at Gravelotte into the teeth of French Chassepot fire.

The battle

The fighting at Gravelotte was a costly stalemate of frontal assaults; the Prussian infantry suffered terribly crossing open slopes against the superior French rifle, losing thousands in the Mance ravine, which German soldiers called the "Grave of the Guard" after the Prussian Guard's disastrous evening attack at St. Privat. The decisive action came on the German left, where the Saxon XII Corps worked around Roncourt and turned the French right. With enfilading artillery fire sweeping St. Privat and the Guard advancing in support, Canrobert's VI Corps was driven from the village; Bazaine, who never committed the Imperial Guard reserve, ordered a general retreat into Metz during the night.

Aftermath

Gravelotte cost the Germans more men than any other battle of the war, but strategically it sealed the fate of France's principal field army: some 180,000 men were shut up in Metz, besieged until their surrender on 27 October 1870. With Bazaine neutralised, Napoleon III's relief army was trapped at Sedan on 1 September, ending the Second Empire.
