Ammonium laureth sulfate

Ammonium laureth sulfate (ALES) is an organic compound used as an anionic surfactant and detergent in personal care products such as shampoos, shower gels and facial cleansers. Structurally it is the ammonium salt of ethoxylated lauryl sulfate: a dodecyl (lauryl) chain extended by a variable number of ethoxy groups and terminated by a sulfate group. Commercial ALES is a mixture rather than a single pure compound, since both the alkyl chain length and the degree of ethoxylation are averages.

Properties and production

ALES is manufactured by ethoxylating lauryl alcohol (from coconut or palm kernel oil, or synthetic feedstocks), then sulfating the product and neutralising it with ammonia. The ethoxylation makes it milder and more soluble than its non-ethoxylated counterpart, ammonium lauryl sulfate, and it produces abundant foam even in hard water. It is closely analogous to sodium laureth sulfate (SLES), differing only in the counter-ion; products use the ammonium form in some formulations for solubility and mildness characteristics.

Safety

Regulatory and expert reviews, including the Cosmetic Ingredient Review, consider ALES safe as used in rinse-off cosmetics when formulated to be non-irritating; like other sulfates it can irritate skin and eyes in concentrated or prolonged contact. Manufacture by ethoxylation can leave trace amounts of 1,4-dioxane, which producers reduce by vacuum stripping; persistent internet claims that laureth sulfates are potent carcinogens are not supported by regulatory assessments. ALES is readily biodegradable.
