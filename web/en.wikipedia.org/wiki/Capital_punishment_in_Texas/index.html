Capital punishment in Texas

JurisdictionTexas, United States
MethodLethal injection
LocationHuntsville Unit, Huntsville
StatusIn use

Capital punishment is a legal penalty in the U.S. state of Texas for murder under certain circumstances, classified as capital murder. Since the U.S. Supreme Court restored the death penalty in 1976, Texas has executed far more prisoners than any other state — over 580 people, roughly a third of all executions in the United States in the modern era. Executions are carried out by lethal injection at the Huntsville Unit in Huntsville.

History

Hangings were the original method of execution in Texas; from 1924 the state used the electric chair, with counties handing execution duties to the state. Following the national moratorium after Furman v. Georgia (1972), Texas enacted a new statute under the "guided discretion" model approved in Gregg v. Georgia (1976), in which juries answer "special issues" including the defendant's future dangerousness. The first execution under the new law was that of Charlie Brooks Jr. in December 1982, also the first lethal injection execution in the United States.

Capital offences and process

Capital murder includes murder of a peace officer or firefighter on duty, murder during certain felonies (such as robbery or burglary), murder of a child under 15, multiple murders, and murder for hire. Texas is notable for the "law of parties", under which accomplices can be sentenced to death for murders committed by others in some circumstances. Clemency rests with the Governor of Texas, acting on the recommendation of the Texas Board of Pardons and Paroles; the governor may grant a single 30-day reprieve without a recommendation.

Death row

Condemned men are housed at the Polunsky Unit near Livingston and women at the Mountain View Unit in Gatesville. Harris County (Houston) has historically sent more defendants to death row than any other Texas county.
