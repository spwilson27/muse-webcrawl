General Packet Radio Service (GPRS) is a packet-oriented mobile data standard on the 2G cellular communication network global system for mobile communications (GSM). GPRS was established by the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) and is an extension of GSM that enables "always-on" packet data connections, billed by volume of data transferred rather than connection time.

Technical overview

GPRS adds packet-switched data support to the circuit-switched GSM network by introducing Serving GPRS Support Nodes (SGSN) and Gateway GPRS Support Nodes (GGSN) to route data between mobile stations and external packet networks such as the Internet. By aggregating up to eight radio timeslots, GPRS offers theoretical data rates up to 171.2 kbit/s, though real-world throughput was typically closer to 40–60 kbit/s.

History and legacy

Commercial GPRS services began around 2000–2001 and popularised mobile email, WAP browsing and MMS before the arrival of 3G. The later Enhanced Data Rates for GSM Evolution (EDGE) standard roughly tripled data rates and is sometimes described together with GPRS as 2.5G/2.75G technology. GPRS remained widely used for machine-to-machine communication long after consumer traffic migrated to 3G and 4G.
