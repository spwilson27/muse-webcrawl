Ghaggar River

The Ghaggar in Haryana

The Ghaggar is an intermittent river in India and Pakistan that flows during the monsoon rains. It rises in the Siwalik Hills of Himachal Pradesh and flows some 320 kilometres through Haryana and Rajasthan before dissipating in the sands of the Thar Desert; in wet years its floodwaters reach the dry channel known in Pakistan as the Hakra River.

Historical significance

Numerous sites of the Indus Valley Civilisation, such as Kalibangan, Banawali and Rakhigarhi, lie along the Ghaggar–Hakra channel. Many scholars identify the paleochannel with the Sarasvati River of the Rigveda, a once-mighty river that dried up as its Himalayan tributaries were captured by the Yamuna and Sutlej systems, likely by around 2000 BCE, though the identification and chronology remain debated.

Modern river

Today the Ghaggar is a seasonal stream used for irrigation, with dams and barrages at several points. Its floods can be destructive in Haryana and Punjab during heavy monsoons.
