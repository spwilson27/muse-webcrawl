Feature creep

Feature creep is the excessive ongoing expansion or addition of new features in a product, especially in computer software and consumer electronics, beyond the original design. Closely related to scope creep in project management, it can make products unnecessarily complex, costly and difficult to use, and can delay or destabilise development.

Causes

Feature creep arises when new capabilities are added incrementally without revisiting the product's overall design: marketing departments seeking to match competitors feature-for-feature, vocal customers requesting additions, developers adding technically interesting functions, or managers unwilling to say no to any stakeholder. In video games, the phenomenon is also known as "feature bloat"; software so overloaded is sometimes called bloatware.

Effects and control

Consequences include missed deadlines, increased maintenance burden, degraded performance, cluttered interfaces and poor reliability. Common countermeasures are firm requirements baselines, change-control processes, prioritisation frameworks, prototyping to test whether features are actually used, and deliberate product strategies that value simplicity. The design aphorism "perfection is achieved not when there is nothing more to add, but when there is nothing left to take away" is frequently quoted in this context.
