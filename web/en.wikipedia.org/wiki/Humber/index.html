Humber

The Humber is a large tidal estuary on the east coast of Northern England, formed where the Rivers Trent and Ouse meet at Trent Falls. Some 64 km long from Trent Falls to the North Sea at Spurn Head, it drains a catchment of about 24,000 km² — roughly a fifth of England's land area — and is the largest source of freshwater flowing from Britain into the North Sea.

Geography

The estuary separates the East Riding of Yorkshire on the north bank from Lincolnshire on the south. Major settlements include Hull near the confluence, and the ports of Grimsby and Immingham on the south bank. Extensive mudflats, saltmarshes and reedbeds make the Humber internationally important for wading birds and migratory wildfowl, protected as a Ramsar site and Special Protection Area.

Crossings and history

Trade through the Humber powered the medieval prosperity of Hull. The estuary is now crossed by the Humber Bridge (opened 1981, once the world's longest single-span suspension bridge) near Hull and by rail and road bridges far upstream at Goole and Keadby. Defences against its powerful tides and storm surges — and careful management of dredged shipping channels — remain central to the region's economy.
