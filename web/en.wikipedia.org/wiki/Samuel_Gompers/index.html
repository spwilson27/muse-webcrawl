Samuel Gompers (born Gumpertz; January 27, 1850 – December 13, 1924) was a British-born American cigar maker and labour union leader. He founded the American Federation of Labor (AFL) in 1886 and served as its president for nearly four decades, until his death — interrupted only in 1895 — making him the most influential figure in the American labour movement of his era.

Early life

Gompers was born in London to a Dutch-Jewish family of cigar makers and emigrated to New York City in 1863. He worked as a cigar maker from boyhood and rose through Cigarmakers' International Union Local 144, becoming deeply read in labour economics and, briefly, Marxist theory. Under his leadership the cigarmakers built strong benefit systems — unemployment, sick and death benefits — that became his model for durable unionism.

The AFL and "pure and simple" unionism

The AFL was a federation of autonomous national craft unions, organised by trade rather than by industry. Gompers advocated what critics called "pure and simple" or "business" unionism: concentrating on immediate, practical goals — higher wages, shorter hours, better conditions — through collective bargaining and the strike, summarised in his answer to the question of what labour wants: "More." He resisted tying the federation to a political party or to socialist transformation, preferring to reward labour's friends and punish its enemies within the existing parties. He also opposed unrestricted immigration, supported Chinese exclusion, and was often criticised for the AFL's exclusion of Black workers, women and the unskilled.

Later career

During the First World War Gompers mobilised labour behind the war effort, serving on the Council of National Defense and afterwards at the Paris Peace Conference, where he chaired the commission that created the International Labour Organization. He opposed Prohibition and the post-war open-shop drive. Gompers died in San Antonio in December 1924 shortly after returning from a Pan-American labour conference in Mexico City, and was succeeded at the AFL by William Green.
