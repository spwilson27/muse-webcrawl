Curt Siodmak

Curt Siodmak
Born: 10 August 1902, Dresden, Germany
Died: 2 September 2000, California, USA
Occupation: Novelist, screenwriter
Known for: The Wolf Man (1941), Donovan's Brain

Curt Siodmak (1902–2000) was a German-American novelist and screenwriter, younger brother of director Robert Siodmak. Fleeing Nazi Germany, he became a leading writer of Universal horror and science-fiction films.

Career

In Germany he wrote novels and worked on films such as Menschen am Sonntag (1930). In Hollywood he scripted The Invisible Man Returns (1940), The Wolf Man (1941) — inventing much modern werewolf lore, including the full-moon transformation and silver bullets — Frankenstein Meets the Wolf Man and I Walked with a Zombie (1943).

Donovan's Brain

His 1942 novel Donovan's Brain, about a disembodied brain controlling others telepathically, was repeatedly filmed and became a science-fiction classic. Later work included The Magnetic Monster (1953), which he also directed, and memoirs Even a Man Who Is Pure in Heart.
