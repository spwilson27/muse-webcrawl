O'Nan–Scott theorem

The O'Nan–Scott theorem is a fundamental result in group theory describing the maximal subgroups of the symmetric groups. It classifies finite primitive permutation groups according to the structure of their socles, dividing them into a small number of types. The theorem was developed independently from work of Michael O'Nan and Leonard Scott in the late 1970s and became a central tool in the application of the classification of finite simple groups to permutation group problems.

Statement and types

In the modern formulation (as presented by Liebeck, Praeger, and Saxl), a finite primitive permutation group falls into one of a few classes based on its action and socle: affine type, where the socle is an elementary abelian regular normal subgroup; almost simple type, where the socle is a nonabelian simple group; and product action, diagonal, and twisted wreath types, where the socle is a direct power of a simple group. The theorem underlies the standard description of the maximal subgroups of symmetric and alternating groups.

Applications

The O'Nan–Scott theorem is used throughout finite group theory and combinatorics, for example in the study of maximal subgroups of almost simple groups, the classification of primitive groups of small degree, and problems in graph theory where automorphism groups act primitively on vertices.
