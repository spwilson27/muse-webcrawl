Knot garden

A knot garden is a formal garden design in which low, clipped hedges of evergreen plants — traditionally box — are planted in intricate, interlacing patterns resembling knots or embroidery, usually within a square frame and often with coloured gravels filling the spaces between hedges.

History

Knot gardens were popular in Tudor and Elizabethan England, adapted from Italian and French Renaissance parterres. They were best viewed from the raised walks and upper windows of the house. Herbs such as lavender, rosemary, germander and hyssop were used alongside box, making the garden fragrant as well as ornamental.

Design

Patterns were published in gardening books of the 16th and 17th centuries and ranged from simple geometric interlaces to elaborate heraldic designs. Many historic houses in England retain or have recreated knot gardens.
