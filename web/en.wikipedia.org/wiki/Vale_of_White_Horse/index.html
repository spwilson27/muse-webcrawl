Vale of White Horse

The Vale of White Horse is a local government district and a valley in Oxfordshire, England, lying between the Berkshire Downs to the south and the River Thames to the north. It takes its name from the Uffington White Horse, a famous prehistoric chalk hill figure carved into the downs above the village of Uffington. The district's council offices are in Abingdon-on-Thames, and its main towns include Wantage and Faringdon.

Geography

The Vale is a flat clay valley drained by the River Ock, overlooked by the chalk escarpment of the Berkshire Downs. Along the ridge run the ancient Ridgeway track and a chain of landmarks: Uffington Castle (an Iron Age hillfort), Dragon Hill — by legend where Saint George slew the dragon — and the Neolithic long barrow of Wayland's Smithy.

History

The White Horse itself, about 110 metres long, is dated to the late Bronze Age or early Iron Age and has been scoured (cleaned) by local custom for centuries — a tradition recorded as early as the 18th century. Wantage, at the foot of the downs, was the birthplace of Alfred the Great (born AD 849). The Vale was part of Berkshire until the 1974 local government reorganisation transferred it to Oxfordshire; the district was created the same year.
