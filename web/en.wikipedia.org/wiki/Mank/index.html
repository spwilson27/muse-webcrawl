Mank is a 2020 American biographical drama film directed by David Fincher, based on a screenplay by his late father Jack Fincher. It follows screenwriter Herman J. "Mank" Mankiewicz (played by Gary Oldman) in 1930s Hollywood as he races — bedridden after a car accident, his leg in plaster — to finish the script of Citizen Kane (1941) for Orson Welles. Shot in black-and-white and styled to evoke the films of its era, Mank also explores Mankiewicz's friendship with actress Marion Davies and his disillusionment with studio politics, including the 1934 California gubernatorial campaign against Upton Sinclair.

Plot

In 1940, Mankiewicz is installed at a ranch in Victorville, California, supervised by producer John Houseman and assisted by secretary Rita Alexander, to write the first draft of Kane without credit negotiations clouding the work. Flashbacks trace his wit-fuelled career at MGM under Louis B. Mayer, his welcome at William Randolph Hearst's San Simeon estate, and his growing disgust at the studios' fake newsreels deployed against Sinclair — experiences that feed into the portrait of Charles Foster Kane.

Production

Jack Fincher wrote the script in the 1990s; David Fincher originally intended to film it after The Game (1997) with Kevin Spacey and Jodie Foster. The project was revived at Netflix and announced in July 2019, filming from November 2019 to February 2020 in Los Angeles. Erik Messerschmidt shot digitally in black-and-white; Trent Reznor and Atticus Ross composed a period-style score using 1940s instruments and recording techniques.

Reception and awards

The film received generally favourable reviews, with praise for its direction, cinematography, production design, and Oldman's performance. At the 93rd Academy Awards it led all films with ten nominations and won Best Cinematography and Best Production Design. It was also nominated for the Golden Globe for Best Motion Picture – Drama.
