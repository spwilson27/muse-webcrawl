Chichimeca

RegionArid northern Mexico (Gran Chichimeca)
PeoplesGuachichiles, Zacatecos, Pames, Jonaz, Guamares, Caxcanes
LanguagesVarious, mostly Uto-Aztecan and Oto-Manguean

Chichimeca was the name the Nahua peoples of central Mexico applied generically to a range of semi-nomadic peoples who inhabited the arid north of modern-day Mexico and the southwestern United States, a region later called La Gran Chichimeca.

History

The term carried a connotation of "barbarian" in Mexica usage, though the Chichimeca peoples included skilled hunters, traders and, in groups such as the Caxcan, agricultural villagers. During the Chichimeca War (1550–1590), peoples such as the Guachichiles and Zacatecos resisted Spanish expansion along the silver routes of Zacatecas, one of the longest and costliest conflicts of the Spanish conquest.

Legacy

The conflict was ended largely by negotiation, gifts and resettlement — a "peace by purchase." Descendant communities, including the Chichimeca Jonaz and Pame, survive in the modern states of Guanajuato, Querétaro and San Luis Potosí.
