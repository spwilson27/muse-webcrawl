Participatory management

Participatory management, also known as employee involvement or participative decision-making, is a management approach in which employees at all levels of an organisation are encouraged to contribute ideas and share in decision-making processes that affect their work. It contrasts with strictly hierarchical, top-down management styles.

Forms

Participation ranges along a spectrum: consultative arrangements such as suggestion schemes and quality circles; substantive participation through self-managing teams and joint committees; representative participation via works councils and employee directors, as in German codetermination (Mitbestimmung); and full financial or ownership participation through cooperatives and employee stock ownership plans (ESOPs).

Rationale and evidence

Supporters argue that involving employees improves the quality of decisions by drawing on front-line knowledge, raises commitment and job satisfaction, and reduces resistance to change. Critics note that participation can slow decisions, blur accountability, or become tokenistic if management retains all real authority. Research in organisational behaviour generally finds positive associations between genuine participation and performance, moderated by organisational culture and the type of decisions shared.
