Real ale

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Real ale is the name coined by the Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA) for beer brewed from traditional ingredients, matured by secondary fermentation in the container from which it is dispensed, and served without the use of extraneous carbon dioxide. In practice this usually means cask ale: beer conditioned in the cask and pulled to the glass by a hand pump (beer engine) or by gravity.

Characteristics

Because the beer is unpasteurised and contains live yeast, it continues to mature in the cellar and must be vented, conditioned and served at cellar temperature (about 11–13 °C). It has gentler, natural carbonation than keg beer and a short drinking window once tapped — typically a few days — so cellarmanship is part of the product's quality.

CAMRA and the revival

CAMRA was founded in 1971 in response to the dominance of a few large brewers selling filtered, pasteurised keg bitter. Its campaigning — including the Good Beer Guide — is credited with preserving Britain's cask-ale tradition and inspiring consumer beer movements worldwide. The term "real ale" is now also applied to bottle-conditioned beers.
