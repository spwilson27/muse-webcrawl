Steven Soter

Steven Soter (born May 1943) is an American astrophysicist who holds positions at New York University and the American Museum of Natural History. He is a research associate in the museum's Department of Astrophysics and a resident research associate at NYU.

Soter received his bachelor's degree in astronomy and physics from UCLA in 1965 and his PhD in astronomy from Cornell University in 1971. At Cornell he worked with Carl Sagan. Together with Ann Druyan, he was one of the three writers of the award-winning 1980 television series Cosmos, narrated by Sagan. He also co-wrote the 1997 science-fiction drama film Contact, based on Sagan's novel.

Soter is known for his work on the definition of "planet". In 2006 he proposed that the International Astronomical Union adopt a criterion based on a "planetary discriminant" — a measure of a body's ability to clear its orbital neighbourhood of other objects. Under his scheme, Pluto does not qualify as a planet, a position the IAU effectively adopted at its 2006 General Assembly, which led to Pluto's reclassification as a dwarf planet. He also led the Natural History magazine series "Reflections on a Mote of Dust".

He is married and lives in New York City.
