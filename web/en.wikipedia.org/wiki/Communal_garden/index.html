Communal garden
TypeShared green space
RelatedCommunity garden, allotment

A communal garden is a piece of land cultivated and maintained collectively by a group of people, typically the residents of a housing development, street, or neighbourhood, rather than by a single household. The term overlaps with community gardening, though "communal" often denotes shared space attached to a specific group of dwellings, such as the garden squares of Georgian and Victorian terraces.

Forms

In London, communal gardens include the private garden squares enclosed by railings and accessible only to surrounding residents, many of them protected by nineteenth-century legislation. Elsewhere, communal gardens may be attached to apartment blocks, co-housing schemes, schools, or workplaces, and can mix ornamental planting, play space, and food growing.

Benefits and management

Shared gardens are credited with improving residents' wellbeing, providing wildlife habitat, and fostering social cohesion. They are usually run by a residents' association or management company under rules covering access, maintenance charges, and permitted activities.
