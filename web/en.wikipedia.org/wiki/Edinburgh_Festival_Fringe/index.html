Edinburgh Festival Fringe

TypePerforming arts festival
FrequencyAnnual, each August
LocationEdinburgh, Scotland
First held1947

The Edinburgh Festival Fringe (often called simply the Fringe) is the world's largest performing arts festival, held every August in Edinburgh, Scotland. Over roughly three weeks, thousands of shows — theatre, comedy, dance, circus, cabaret, musicals, opera and spoken word — are staged in hundreds of venues across the city. Uniquely, the Fringe is an open-access festival: anyone who can secure a venue may take part, with no selection process.

History

The Fringe began in 1947, when eight theatre groups turned up uninvited to perform alongside the inaugural Edinburgh International Festival and staged their own shows on its "fringe." The name stuck, and the Festival Fringe Society was formed in 1958 to coordinate the growing event. Comedy grew to dominate from the 1980s, and the Fringe became a famous launchpad for performers — companies and acts associated with early breakthroughs include Beyond the Fringe (1960), Rowan Atkinson, and many winners of the Edinburgh Comedy Award (formerly the Perrier Award).

Scale

In recent pre-pandemic years the Fringe has featured well over 3,000 shows and sold more than two million tickets. Together with Edinburgh's other August festivals it forms the largest annual arts gathering in the world. Major venue operators include the Pleasance, Assembly, Gilded Balloon and Underbelly.

Criticism

The festival faces recurring criticism over the cost of participation, performer accommodation prices, low pay, and its impact on the city's housing. Initiatives have promoted fair work and mental-health support for artists.
