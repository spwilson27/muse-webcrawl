Bengt Strömgren

Bengt Georg Daniel Strömgren (21 January 1908 – 4 July 1987) was a Danish astronomer and astrophysicist, best known for his work on the ionisation of interstellar hydrogen around hot stars — the concept now called the Strömgren sphere — and for developing the Strömgren photometric system used to classify stars.

Life and career

Strömgren was born in Gothenburg, Sweden, to Elis Strömgren, professor of astronomy and director of the Copenhagen University Observatory, and grew up in Copenhagen. He published his first paper as a teenager and received his doctorate from the University of Copenhagen in 1929, at age 21. He succeeded his father as professor of astronomy in 1940.

During a stay at the Yerkes Observatory and later as director of Yerkes and the McDonald Observatory (1951–1957), he worked on stellar interiors and chemical composition. He returned to Denmark and was a founding figure of the European Southern Observatory. He also served as president of the International Astronomical Union (1970–1973).

Scientific contributions

In 1939 Strömgren showed theoretically that the ultraviolet radiation of a hot star ionises the surrounding interstellar hydrogen out to a fairly sharply bounded radius — the Strömgren sphere — explaining the structure of H II regions. His photometric system (uvbyβ) uses intermediate-band filters to measure temperature, surface gravity and metal content of stars, and remains widely used. He also contributed to the theory of stellar evolution and to establishing the chemical composition of the Sun and stars.

Honours

Strömgren received the Bruce Medal (1959) and the Henry Norris Russell Lectureship (1965). The asteroid 1846 Bengt is named after him.
