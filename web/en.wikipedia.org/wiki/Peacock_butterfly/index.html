Peacock butterfly

Scientific nameAglais io (Linnaeus, 1758)
FamilyNymphalidae
Wingspan50–55 mm
RangeEurope and temperate Asia to Japan
Larval foodplantCommon nettle, hop

The European peacock (Aglais io), more commonly known simply as the peacock butterfly, is a colourful butterfly found in Europe and temperate Asia as far east as Japan. It is the only member of its species group in much of its range. The butterfly has a wingspan of about 5 cm and is unmistakable for the large blue-and-black eyespots on its rusty-red wings.

Appearance

The upperside is a rich reddish brown, with a large eyespot at the tip of each forewing and on each hindwing, coloured black, blue, yellow and lilac. The underside is a mottled dark brown and black, giving excellent camouflage among dead leaves. Males and females look similar, females being slightly larger.

Defence

The eyespots are believed to startle or deflect predators. When threatened, the butterfly also flashes its wings and produces a hissing sound by rubbing its wings together. Experiments have shown that peacocks with eyespots survive attacks by birds better than those whose spots have been painted out.

Life cycle

Adults emerge from hibernation in spring, hibernating in hollow trees, outbuildings and woodpiles. Females lay batches of up to several hundred eggs on the underside of common nettle leaves. The black, spiny caterpillars feed communally in a silk web before dispersing to pupate. In most of the range there is one generation a year, with new adults emerging from July and feeding heavily before hibernation; a partial second generation occurs in warm southern areas.
