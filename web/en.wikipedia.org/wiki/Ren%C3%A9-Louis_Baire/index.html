René-Louis Baire

Born21 January 1874, Paris, France
Died5 July 1932, Chambéry, France
NationalityFrench
Known forBaire category theorem, Baire space, Baire functions
FieldMathematics (analysis, topology)

René-Louis Baire (21 January 1874 – 5 July 1932) was a French mathematician famous for the Baire category theorem, which helped to generalize and prove the results of his doctoral thesis on the discontinuity of functions. He was among the founders of the descriptive theory of functions and an important figure in the French school of real analysis.

Life

Baire studied at the École Normale Supérieure and taught at lycées and at the universities of Montpellier and Dijon. His health was fragile throughout his life, and he retired early, dying in Chambéry in 1932.

Contributions

In his 1899 thesis, Baire introduced the classification of functions by pointwise limits of continuous functions (Baire classes) and proved the Baire category theorem: a complete metric space cannot be expressed as a countable union of nowhere dense sets. The theorem became a cornerstone of functional analysis, and Baire spaces and Baire measurable functions are named after him.
