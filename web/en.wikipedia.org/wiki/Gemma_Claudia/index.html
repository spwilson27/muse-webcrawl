Gemma Claudia

Gemma Claudia — Roman onyx cameo, c. AD 49. Five-layer sardonyx, 15.2 × 12 cm. Kunsthistorisches Museum, Vienna.

The Gemma Claudia is a very large ancient Roman cameo, carved around AD 49 in five-layered onyx (sardonyx). It depicts members of the Julio-Claudian dynasty and is now in the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna. Together with the Gemma Augustea, it is among the most famous works of Roman glyptic art.

Description

The cameo (about 15.2 cm high and 12 cm wide) shows two pairs of imperial figures on either side of a display of captured arms and military standards. On one side stand the emperor Claudius and his fourth wife Agrippina the Younger; facing them are Agrippina's parents, Germanicus and Agrippina the Elder. The composition celebrates the marriage of Claudius and Agrippina in AD 49 and the dynastic legitimacy it proclaimed.

The central trophy of arms includes shields, helmets, and an eagle; the layered stone is exploited so that the figures emerge in white against darker strata.

Technique and provenance

Cameos of this scale and quality were products of the imperial court workshops. The Gemma Claudia was documented in early modern collections before entering the Habsburg imperial collections in Vienna, where it remains a highlight of the Antikensammlung.
