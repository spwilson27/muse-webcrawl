GPT-3

Generative Pre-trained Transformer 3 (GPT-3) is a large language model developed by OpenAI and released in June 2020. An autoregressive model based on the transformer architecture, GPT-3 has 175 billion parameters — more than ten times its predecessor GPT-2 — and demonstrated that a sufficiently large language model, trained on hundreds of billions of words, could perform a wide range of natural-language tasks from examples given in a prompt, without task-specific fine-tuning.

The paper and few-shot learning

GPT-3 was introduced in the paper "Language Models are Few-Shot Learners" (Brown et al., 2020). Instead of being fine-tuned on labelled data for each task, the model is given a text prompt containing a task description and a few examples ("few-shot" prompting; "zero-shot" with none, "one-shot" with one) and continues the text appropriately. It achieved strong results in translation, question answering, arithmetic, unscrambling words, and writing news-like articles which human judges found hard to distinguish from human writing. The model was trained on a filtered mixture of Common Crawl, WebText2, books, and Wikipedia totalling about 300 billion tokens.

Release and applications

OpenAI made GPT-3 available only through a commercial API rather than releasing its weights, citing both business reasons and misuse concerns; Microsoft licensed exclusive access to the underlying model in September 2020. The API powered hundreds of applications — chatbots, code completion, copywriting, and games such as AI Dungeon — and the code-writing variant Codex later underlay Muse. In 2022, fine-tuned descendants (InstructGPT and GPT-3.5) trained with reinforcement learning from human feedback carried the technology to mass audiences through ChatGPT.

Criticism and limitations

GPT-3 was criticised for its energy-hungry training run, its reproduction of biases and toxic text present in web data, its tendency to produce fluent but false statements, and its lack of grounding in the physical world or persistent memory. Its release also sharpened debates about open versus closed release of powerful AI models, synthetic media, and the economics of very large-scale machine learning.
