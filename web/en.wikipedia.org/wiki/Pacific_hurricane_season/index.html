Pacific hurricane season

The Pacific hurricane season is the period of the year when tropical cyclones form most frequently in the northeastern and north central Pacific Ocean. In the eastern North Pacific, the season officially runs from 15 May to 30 November; in the central North Pacific, it runs from 1 June to 30 November. These dates are a convention bounding the period in which the great majority of Pacific tropical cyclones occur, although storms occasionally form outside them.

Seasonal characteristics

Activity peaks in late August and September. On average, the eastern Pacific produces about 15 named storms per season, of which about eight become hurricanes and four become major hurricanes (Category 3 or stronger on the Saffir–Simpson scale). The central Pacific averages about four to five tropical cyclones per year, many of which cross in from the eastern basin.

Seasonal forecasts and records

Seasonal outlooks are issued before each season by the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration and Mexico's Servicio Meteorológico Nacional. The most active season on record in the eastern Pacific was 1992, with 24 named storms, while the 2015 season produced the basin's strongest hurricane, Patricia, and the highest accumulated cyclone energy in the central Pacific.
