Blackwater Estuary

RiverRiver Blackwater

The Blackwater Estuary is the estuary of the River Blackwater in Essex, England, on the east coast of Great Britain. It stretches from the town of Maldon, where the Chelmer and Blackwater Navigation joins the tidal river, eastwards past Tollesbury and Bradwell-on-Sea to the North Sea near Mersea Island. The estuary is one of the largest areas of intertidal flats and saltmarsh on the Essex coast and is of international importance for wildfowl and wading birds.

Geography

The estuary is a broad, shallow inlet characterised by extensive mudflats, saltmarsh, shingle spits and tidal creeks. At low tide large areas of mud are exposed, feeding grounds for dunlin, redshank, curlew, shelduck and dark-bellied brent geese, the latter wintering in internationally important numbers. Mersea Island lies at the mouth of the estuary, separated from the mainland by the Strood channel. The northern shore includes the Dengie peninsula, while the southern shore near Bradwell is the site of the decommissioned Bradwell nuclear power station.

Conservation

Much of the estuary is designated a Site of Special Scientific Interest, a Special Protection Area and a Ramsar wetland of international importance. The Essex Wildlife Trust and RSPB manage reserves around the estuary, including at Old Hall Marshes near Tolleshunt D'Arcy, one of the largest areas of coastal grazing marsh and saltmarsh in Essex. The estuary also supports a traditional native oyster fishery, for which Tollesbury and West Mersea are noted.

History

The estuary has been important since Roman times; the Roman Saxon Shore fort of Othona stood near Bradwell. Salt production in the area is recorded from the Iron Age in the form of the "Red Hills" found around the Essex coast. The Battle of Maldon in 991 AD, commemorated in the famous Old English poem, was fought on the causeway to Northey Island in the estuary.
