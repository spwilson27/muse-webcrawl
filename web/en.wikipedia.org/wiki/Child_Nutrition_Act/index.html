Child Nutrition Act

Child Nutrition Act of 1966
Enacted11 October 1966
Signed byPresident Lyndon B. Johnson
Administered byU.S. Department of Agriculture (FNS)
Key programmesSchool Breakfast Program; Special Milk Program; WIC (added 1972)

The Child Nutrition Act of 1966 is a United States federal law that expanded the government's role in feeding schoolchildren. Signed by President Lyndon B. Johnson as part of the War on Poverty, it built on the National School Lunch Act of 1946 by establishing the School Breakfast Program and strengthening standards for school meals.

Provisions

The Act authorized federal assistance for school breakfasts — initially a pilot — targeting schools in poor areas, expanded the Special Milk Program, funded equipment for food service in low-income schools, and strengthened nutritional requirements and state administrative support. It declared that the nation's children need adequate nutrition for learning and development.

Later amendments

Amendments in 1972 created the Special Supplemental Food Program for Women, Infants, and Children (WIC) as a pilot under the Act. The law is periodically reauthorized together with the school lunch statutes; the Healthy, Hunger-Free Kids Act of 2010 was a major reauthorization that raised nutritional standards for school meals.
