Rebecca

Rebecca is a 1938 Gothic novel by the English author Daphne du Maurier. The novel depicts an unnamed young woman who impetuously marries a wealthy widower, only to discover that he and his household are haunted by the memory of his late first wife, the title character.

Plot summary

While working as a companion to a wealthy American woman on holiday in Monte Carlo, the narrator encounters the wealthy Englishman Maxim de Winter, whose wife Rebecca died a year earlier in a boating accident. After a whirlwind courtship, they marry and return to his mansion, Manderley, in Cornwall. There the narrator finds herself constantly compared, unfavourably, to the beautiful, accomplished Rebecca, a comparison encouraged by the housekeeper Mrs Danvers. The mystery of Rebecca's death is eventually revealed: she was terminally ill and had provoked Maxim into shooting her, making her death look like an accident.

Reception and adaptations

The novel was an immediate success and has never been out of print. It was adapted into the 1940 film Rebecca directed by Alfred Hitchcock, which won the Academy Award for Best Picture, as well as numerous stage, radio and television versions, including a 2020 Netflix film. The opening line, "Last night I dreamt I went to Manderley again", is among the most famous in English literature.
