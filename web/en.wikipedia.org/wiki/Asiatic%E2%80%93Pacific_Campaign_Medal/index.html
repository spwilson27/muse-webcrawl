The Asiatic–Pacific Campaign Medal is a United States military award of the Second World War, created by Executive Order of President Franklin D. Roosevelt on 6 November 1942. It was awarded to members of the U.S. Armed Forces who served in the Asiatic-Pacific Theater of Operations between 7 December 1941 and 2 March 1946.

Design

The medal was designed by Thomas Hudson Jones, with the reverse designed by Adolph Alexander Weinman. The obverse depicts troops landing on a beach amid palm trees, with a battleship, aircraft carrier, and submarine in the background and an aircraft overhead, and the words "ASIATIC PACIFIC CAMPAIGN". The ribbon features a yellow field representing the sands of Pacific beaches and the rising sun of Japan, with stripes of red, white, and blue and thin imperial colors reflecting both sides of the conflict.

Service requirements

Service members qualified by serving on permanent assignment within the theater, as passengers or in temporary duty for 30 consecutive or 60 non-consecutive days, or in combat against the enemy. Battle stars were worn for participation in designated campaigns, and the Arrowhead device denoted participation in amphibious assault landings. A similar medal, the American Campaign Medal and the European–African–Middle Eastern Campaign Medal, covered other theaters.
