Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore

Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore is a United States national lakeshore on the northeastern shore of Lake Michigan in Michigan's Leelanau Peninsula. Authorised in 1970 and administered by the National Park Service, it covers about 71,000 acres including the North and South Manitou Islands.

Location: Leelanau and Benzie counties, Michigan
Established: 21 October 1970
Governing body: National Park Service

Features

The lakeshore is named for a large perched dune resembling a sleeping bear, tied to an Anishinaabe legend of a mother bear and her cubs, commemorated also by North and South Manitou Islands. Highlights include the Dune Climb, the Pierce Stocking Scenic Drive, the Glen Haven historic village and 35 miles of mainland shoreline.

Geology

The dunes were formed by glacial deposits and wind action after the retreat of the Laurentide Ice Sheet; some perched dunes rise more than 100 metres above Lake Michigan.
