The Multinational Force in Lebanon (MNF) was an international peacekeeping force of American, French, Italian and (from 1983) British troops deployed to Beirut during the Lebanese Civil War, following the 1982 Israeli invasion of Lebanon.

First deployment (MNF I)

The first force landed in August 1982 to supervise the evacuation of Palestine Liberation Organization fighters from Beirut under a US-negotiated agreement. Having completed the evacuation, it withdrew in September 1982 — days before the Sabra and Shatila massacre of Palestinian civilians by Phalangist militiamen in areas controlled by Israel, which prompted the force's hurried return.

Second deployment (MNF II)

A larger force returned in late September 1982 with the stated mission of supporting the Lebanese government and armed forces and providing a stabilising presence. As Lebanon's civil war factions increasingly viewed the MNF as a party to the conflict — particularly after US naval gunfire supported the Lebanese Army at Suq al-Gharb in September 1983 — its positions came under attack. On 23 October 1983, suicide truck bombers struck the US Marine barracks at Beirut International Airport and the French paratrooper barracks almost simultaneously, killing 241 American servicemen and 58 French paratroopers, the deadliest single attack on US Marines since Iwo Jima. The multinational force was withdrawn by the spring of 1984.

Legacy

The Beirut barracks bombings, attributed to the Islamic Jihad Organization and linked to the emerging Hezbollah movement and Iran, profoundly influenced American debates about intervention in the Middle East and the risks of peacekeeping missions without a clear mandate or local consent.
