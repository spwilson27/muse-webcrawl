Nördlingen

Nördlingen (Swabian German: Nerle) is a town in the district of Donau-Ries in Swabia, Bavaria, Germany, with a population of roughly 20,000. It is famous as one of the few towns in Germany whose complete medieval city walls — with towers and a walkable covered parapet — survive intact, and for its location inside the Nördlinger Ries, a giant meteorite impact crater.

History

Nördlingen grew from an Alemannic settlement and a royal estate mentioned in 898. A free imperial city from 1215, it prospered on trade and its annual fair (the Pfingstmesse), and on agriculture from the fertile Ries basin. The town was the scene of two major battles of the Thirty Years' War: the First Battle of Nördlingen in 1634, a decisive victory for Imperial and Spanish forces over the Swedes, and a second battle in 1645. Nördlingen lost its imperial-city status in 1803 and passed to Bavaria. It was bombed in the Second World War, in April 1945, but its medieval centre largely survived and the old town is now a protected heritage area.

Sights

The late-Gothic St. George's Church dominates the town; its 90-metre tower, the "Daniel," is staffed by watchmen who still call out the traditional cry "So, G'sell, so!" every half hour at night. The circular wall can be walked for most of its roughly 2.7 kilometres. The Rieskrater Museum explains the impact event that formed the crater some 15 million years ago, when an asteroid about a kilometre wide struck at the site of the town; houses in Nördlingen are famously built in part from suevite, a rock containing shocked quartz and tiny diamonds created by the impact. The town hosts the Stabenfest, one of Germany's oldest children's festivals, and in Japanese popular culture it served as a model for a town in the anime Attack on Titan.
