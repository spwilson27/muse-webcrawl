Oasisamerica

Oasisamerica is a cultural region of pre-Columbian North America covering parts of the present-day southwestern United States and northwestern Mexico, including areas of Arizona, New Mexico, Colorado, Utah, Chihuahua, and Sonora. The term was proposed by the anthropologist Paul Kirchhoff as a counterpart to his better-known concept of Mesoamerica.

Cultures

Unlike the largely nomadic peoples of neighbouring Aridoamerica, the peoples of Oasisamerica adopted agriculture — especially maize, beans, and squash, introduced from Mesoamerica — and built settled farming communities in the desert river valleys. Major traditions of the region include the Ancestral Puebloans (Anasazi), the Hohokam, famed for their extensive irrigation canals, the Mogollon, the Fremont, and the Pataya. These cultures built pueblos and cliff dwellings, produced distinctive pottery and turquoise work, and traded extensively with Mesoamerica.

Decline

Between roughly 1200 and 1450 CE many of the great centres were abandoned, a transformation attributed to drought, resource depletion, and social change; the descendants of these peoples include the modern Pueblo peoples of the Rio Grande and Hopi mesas.
