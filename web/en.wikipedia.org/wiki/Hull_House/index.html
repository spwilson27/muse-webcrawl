Hull House

Hull House was a settlement house in Chicago, Illinois, co-founded in 1889 by Jane Addams and Ellen Gates Starr. Located on the Near West Side of Chicago at 800 South Halsted Street, it was situated amid a dense immigrant neighbourhood. Hull House became the flagship of the American settlement movement and a centre of social reform, research and education.

History

Addams and Starr leased the Charles J. Hull mansion, built in 1856, and opened the house to provide services to recently arrived European immigrants. The complex eventually grew to thirteen buildings offering a kindergarten, day nursery, night school for adults, clubs for older children, an art gallery, a public kitchen, a book bindery, a music school, a gymnasium, a labour museum and meeting space for unions and reform organisations.

Impact

Residents of Hull House gathered data on housing, sanitation and working conditions that informed landmark reforms in child labour, occupational safety, public health and immigrant protection. Addams and her colleagues, including Florence Kelley and Julia Lathrop, helped shape early social work in the United States. The house remained in operation until 2012; the original mansion and dining hall survive as the Jane Addams Hull-House Museum.
