X Japan (Japanese: エックス・ジャパン) is a Japanese heavy metal band formed in Chiba in 1982 by drummer and pianist Yoshiki and vocalist Toshi. Originally named simply X, the band achieved breakthrough success in the late 1980s and became one of the most influential acts in Japanese rock, credited as a founder of the visual kei movement with their flamboyant costumes, makeup, and hairstyles.

History

After building a following on the independent circuit, X signed to CBS/Sony and released the major-label debut Blue Blood in 1989, followed by Jealousy (1991), which topped the Oricon chart. Their 1993 album Art of Life consists of a single orchestral metal track nearly 29 minutes long. In 1992 the band renamed itself X Japan after discovering an American band with the name X. Guitarist Hide and bassist Taiji were central figures; Taiji left in 1992, and the band disbanded in 1997 after Toshi's departure. Hide died in 1998, an event that drew tens of thousands of mourning fans. X Japan reunited in 2007 with guitarist Sugizo (of Luna Sea) joining officially in 2009, releasing the single "I.V." and performing internationally, including a show at Madison Square Garden in 2014.

Legacy

With record sales reported in the tens of millions, X Japan is one of the best-selling Japanese musical acts ever. Yoshiki's combination of speed metal, balladry, and classical piano — and the band's dramatic aesthetic — shaped generations of Japanese rock and metal acts.
