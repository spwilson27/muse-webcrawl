Pu-erh tea


A compressed cake of pu-erh tea

Pu-erh or Pu'er tea is a variety of fermented tea traditionally produced in Yunnan Province, China. It takes its name from the town of Pu'er in southern Yunnan. Pu-erh is made from the leaves of Camellia sinensis var. assamica and undergoes microbial fermentation after the leaves are dried and rolled, a process that can continue for years and is said to improve the tea's flavour with age.

Types

Two main types are produced. Raw (sheng) pu-erh is sun-dried and compressed, then aged naturally over years or decades. Ripe (shou or shu) pu-erh, developed in 1973 by the Kunming Tea Factory, undergoes an accelerated "wet piling" (wo dui) fermentation that mimics long ageing in a matter of weeks or months.

Forms and storage

Pu-erh is commonly compressed into discs (bing), bowls (tuo), bricks, squares and melon shapes to aid transport and ageing. It is traditionally stored in warm, humid conditions; compressed cakes are wrapped in paper and often kept in bamboo tongs of seven cakes.

Market and collecting

Aged pu-erh commands high prices and is collected and traded like wine; speculation in pu-erh led to a market bubble that collapsed in 2007. Factors affecting price include the mountain or village of origin, wild versus plantation trees, vintage, and storage history. Since 2008, pu-erh tea has been a protected geographical indication product in China.

Brewing

Pu-erh is usually brewed in the gongfu style in a small teapot or gaiwan, with short successive infusions after an initial rinse of the leaves.
