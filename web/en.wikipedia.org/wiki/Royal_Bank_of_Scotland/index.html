Royal Bank of Scotland

The Royal Bank of Scotland (RBS) is a major retail and commercial bank in Scotland, founded in Edinburgh in 1727. It is part of the NatWest Group, formerly the Royal Bank of Scotland Group.

History

Granted a royal charter in 1727, RBS became known for innovations such as the overdraft. By 1874 it had expanded across Scotland. Its parent group grew rapidly under Fred Goodwin, acquiring NatWest in 2000 and leading the 2007 takeover of ABN AMRO. During the 2008 financial crisis the group was rescued by the UK government, which took a majority stake. The group was renamed NatWest Group in 2020; the Royal Bank of Scotland continues as a retail brand, and it is one of the banks authorised to issue Scottish banknotes.
