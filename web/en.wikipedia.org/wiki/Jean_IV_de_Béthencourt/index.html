Jean IV de Béthencourt

Jean IV de Béthencourt
Born: 1362, Grainville-la-Teinturière, Normandy
Died: 1425
Known for: Conquest of the Canary Islands

Jean IV de Béthencourt (1362–1425) was a French explorer and nobleman who led the first European conquest of the Canary Islands. In 1402 he sailed from La Rochelle with Gadifer de la Salle, landed on Lanzarote, and established settlements there and on Fuerteventura and El Hierro.

Conquest of the Canaries

Béthencourt pledged allegiance to Henry III of Castile and received the title King of the Canary Islands as a Castilian vassal. The expedition is chronicled in Le Canarien, compiled by his chaplains. Conquest of the remaining islands was completed later by other Castilian captains. He introduced settlers from Normandy, and in tradition he is associated with bringing crops and the first colonisation of the archipelago.

Later life

Béthencourt returned to Normandy, where he died in 1425; his nephew Maciot de Béthencourt governed and eventually sold rights in the islands.
