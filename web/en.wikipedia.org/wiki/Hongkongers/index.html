Hongkongers


Hong Kong's skyline on Hong Kong Island

Hongkongers, also known as Hong Kongers, Hong Kong citizens, and Hong Kong people, are people who originate from or live in Hong Kong. The term usually refers to legal residents of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, and it may also refer to people who hold a cultural identity connected to Hong Kong, including members of the Hong Kong diaspora.

Demographics

Hong Kong has a population of about 7.3 million. The overwhelming majority—around 92 per cent—are of Han Chinese descent, mostly of Cantonese (Taishanese and other Yue) and Hakka origin, with significant Teochew, Shanghainese, and Fujianese communities. Ethnic minorities include Filipinos, Indonesians, South Asians (Indians, Pakistanis, Nepalese), and Westerners. The main languages are Cantonese, English, and Mandarin.

Identity

Surveys by institutions such as the University of Hong Kong have tracked how residents identify themselves, with respondents variously describing themselves as "Hongkongers," "Chinese," or both. A distinctive local identity developed over the colonial period and after the 1997 handover, shaped by the Cantonese language, local popular culture (including Cantopop and Hong Kong cinema), and the city's separate legal and economic systems under the "one country, two systems" framework.

Diaspora

Waves of emigration occurred before the 1997 handover and again after 2019, with large Hong Kong communities in Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, the United States, and Taiwan. Under the British National (Overseas) visa scheme introduced in 2021, many Hongkongers relocated to the United Kingdom.
