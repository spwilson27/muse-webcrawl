Roald Dahl (13 September 1916 – 23 November 1990) was a British novelist, short-story writer and screenwriter of Norwegian descent. Born in Wales to Norwegian immigrant parents, he served in the Royal Air Force during the Second World War, in which he was seriously injured in a crash in Egypt, and began writing after being posted to Washington, D.C. He became one of the world's best-selling children's authors, famed for darkly comic stories in which child heroes triumph over grotesque, often villainous adults.

Children's books

Dahl's first children's book, The Gremlins (1943), grew out of RAF folklore. His breakthrough came with James and the Giant Peach (1961) and Charlie and the Chocolate Factory (1964), the tale of Charlie Bucket and the eccentric chocolatier Willy Wonka. Later classics include Fantastic Mr Fox (1970), Danny, the Champion of the World (1975), The BFG (1982), The Witches (1983) and Matilda (1988). Many were illustrated by Quentin Blake, whose scratchy drawings became inseparable from Dahl's prose. His books have sold more than 300 million copies and have been repeatedly adapted for film, stage and television.

Adult fiction and screenwriting

For adults Dahl wrote macabre short stories, collected in volumes such as Someone Like You (1953), Kiss Kiss (1960) and Tales of the Unexpected, the latter adapted as a television series. He wrote the screenplays for the James Bond film You Only Live Twice (1967) and Chitty Chitty Bang Bang (1968), and the film of Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory (1971). His autobiographical volumes Boy (1984) and Going Solo (1986) recount his schooldays, his work for Shell in Africa, and his wartime flying.

Life and controversies

Dahl was educated at Repton School, whose harshness coloured his portrayal of adults and institutions. He married the American actress Patricia Neal in 1953; their family life was marked by tragedy, including the death of their daughter Olivia from measles encephalitis in 1962, after which Dahl campaigned for vaccination. His private remarks, including antisemitic statements in interviews, have drawn sustained criticism, and his estate apologised for them in 2020. In 2023 revised editions of his books, with some passages altered by the publisher, provoked widespread debate about editing classic texts.

Legacy

Dahl wrote in a small hut, "Gipsy House" in Great Missenden, Buckinghamshire, in pencil on yellow paper, and the village now hosts the Roald Dahl Museum and Story Centre. His work remains a fixture of childhood reading in the English-speaking world and far beyond, celebrated for its invented words, subversive humour and sympathy for children against pompous authority.
