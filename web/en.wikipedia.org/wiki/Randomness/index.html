Randomness

Randomness, in common parlance, is the apparent lack of pattern or predictability in events. A random sequence of events, symbols or steps has no order and does not follow an intelligible pattern or combination. Individual random events are by definition unpredictable, but the probability distribution of many events can often be predicted — for example, in the tossing of dice or coins.

In mathematics and statistics, randomness is formalised through probability theory and concepts such as random variables and stochastic processes. Randomness is used in science, cryptography, gaming and statistical sampling. The question of whether physical processes such as radioactive decay are truly random, as opposed to merely unpredictable, is a longstanding topic in physics and philosophy.
