Dynamic pressure

A pitot tube compares total and static pressure to measure dynamic pressure and hence airspeed.

Dynamic pressure (denoted q or Q) is the kinetic energy per unit volume of a fluid flow. For incompressible flow it is given by:

q = ½ ρ v²

where ρ is the fluid density and v is the flow speed. Dynamic pressure has the units of pressure (pascals in SI) and appears in Bernoulli's principle, where the sum of static pressure, dynamic pressure and hydrostatic pressure is constant along a streamline in steady flow.

Applications

In aerodynamics, dynamic pressure governs the magnitude of aerodynamic forces: lift and drag are dynamic pressure multiplied by a reference area and a dimensionless coefficient. Aircraft airspeed indicators work by measuring dynamic pressure with a pitot-static system. In rocketry, "max q" is the point of maximum dynamic pressure during ascent, when aerodynamic stress on the vehicle peaks; launch commentators often announce it. Wind loading on buildings and bridges is likewise calculated from the dynamic pressure of the wind.

Compressible flow

For compressible flow, dynamic pressure is defined more generally—often as the difference between total (stagnation) pressure and static pressure—and its relation to speed involves the Mach number. At low speeds the incompressible formula above is an adequate approximation.
