Charles Archibald Hahn (September 14, 1880 – January 21, 1955) was an American sprinter and one of the dominant track athletes of the early twentieth century. Nicknamed "the Milwaukee Meteor", he won three gold medals at the 1904 Summer Olympics in St. Louis and a further gold medal at the 1906 Intercalated Games in Athens.

Career

Hahn studied at the University of Michigan, where he won collegiate sprint titles and was recruited for his speed after Michigan coach Keene Fitzpatrick saw him win a Canadian championship race. At the 1904 Olympics he won the 60 metres, 100 metres, and 200 metres — the latter in an Olympic record of 21.6 seconds that stood for 28 years. He remains one of the few athletes to have won the 100 m and 200 m double at the same Games in that era.

At the 1906 Intercalated Games in Athens, Hahn successfully defended his 100 metres title, becoming the first sprinter to repeat as champion over the distance at consecutive international games; his double-title feat was not matched until Carl Lewis in 1988. Hahn also set early world-leading times indoors and won United States national titles in the 100 yards and 220 yards.

Later life

After retiring from competition, Hahn became one of the first professional track coaches in the United States, coaching at several colleges and for many years at the University of Virginia, where he also wrote one of the earliest American instructional books on sprinting, How to Sprint (1925). He was inducted into the USA Track and Field Hall of Fame in 1974, and into the University of Michigan Athletic Hall of Honor.
