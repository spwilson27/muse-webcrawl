Temptation of Christ

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The temptation of Christ is a biblical narrative detailed in the Gospels of Matthew, Mark, and Luke. After being baptized by John the Baptist, Jesus fasted for forty days and nights in the Judaean Desert, during which Satan appeared to him and attempted to tempt him.

The three temptations

In Matthew and Luke, the Devil presents three challenges: (1) to turn stones into bread to relieve hunger; (2) to throw himself from the pinnacle of the Temple in Jerusalem, testing God's protection; and (3) to worship Satan in exchange for all the kingdoms of the world, shown from a high mountain. Jesus refuses each temptation, answering with quotations from Deuteronomy. Afterwards, angels come and minister to him.

Interpretation

The episode is traditionally read as a model of resisting temptation and as a recapitulation of Israel's wilderness wanderings, which also lasted forty years. The fast is commemorated in the Christian season of Lent. The traditional site is associated with the Mount of Temptation (Jebel Quruntul) near Jericho, site of a Greek Orthodox monastery.

In art

The temptations have been depicted frequently in Christian art, literature (including Milton's Paradise Regained), and film.
