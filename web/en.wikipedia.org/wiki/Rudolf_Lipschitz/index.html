Rudolf Lipschitz

Rudolf Otto Sigismund Lipschitz
Born: 14 May 1832, Königsberg, Prussia
Died: 7 October 1903, Bonn, Germany
Fields: Mathematics
Known for: Lipschitz continuity

Rudolf Otto Sigismund Lipschitz (1832–1903) was a German mathematician who made contributions to analysis, number theory, differential geometry and mechanics. He is chiefly remembered for the Lipschitz condition on functions, a fundamental hypothesis in the theory of differential equations.

Life and work

Lipschitz studied at Königsberg and Berlin, where he was a student of Dirichlet and Ohm, earning his doctorate in 1853. After teaching at Breslau, he moved in 1862 to the University of Bonn, where he spent the rest of his career and built up its mathematics seminar; Felix Klein was among those who studied and habilitated under him there.

Mathematics

The Lipschitz condition — that a function f satisfies |f(x) − f(y)| ≤ L|x − y| for some constant L — guarantees the uniqueness of solutions to initial value problems, and forms half of the Picard–Lindelöf theorem. Functions satisfying it are called Lipschitz continuous or Lipschitz functions. Lipschitz's research also spanned quadratic forms (where the algebras he studied were later developed into Clifford algebras), differential invariants, and the n-body problem. His textbook Lehrbuch der Analysis appeared in 1877–1880.
