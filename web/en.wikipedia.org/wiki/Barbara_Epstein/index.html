Barbara Epstein

Barbara Epstein (née Zimmerman; August 30, 1928 – June 16, 2006) was an American editor and a co-founder of The New York Review of Books. Born in Boston and educated at Radcliffe College, she began her career at Doubleday, where as a young editor she championed The Diary of Anne Frank for American publication, and later worked at Dutton, McGraw-Hill and Partisan Review.

The New York Review of Books

During the New York newspaper strike of 1962–63, Epstein, her husband Jason Epstein, Robert B. Silvers and Elizabeth Hardwick founded The New York Review of Books, whose first issue appeared in February 1963. Epstein and Silvers served as its co-editors for the rest of her life. She was known for her exacting line editing, her management of the paper's operations, and for shaping its mixture of literary criticism and political reportage by writers such as Susan Sontag, Joan Didion and Mary McCarthy.

Legacy

Epstein died of lung cancer in New York City in 2006, aged 77. Obituaries credited her as an indispensable, if less publicly visible, half of the Review's founding editorial partnership; the paper she built became one of the most influential intellectual publications in the English-speaking world.
