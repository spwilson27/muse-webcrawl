The Cuban exile refers to the mass emigration of Cubans from the island following the Cuban Revolution of 1959, when Fidel Castro's government came to power. Over the ensuing decades, well over a million Cubans left for the United States, Spain, and other countries, creating one of the most politically influential diasporas in the world.

Waves of exile

The first exiles (1959–1962), sometimes called the "golden exiles", were disproportionately drawn from Cuba's upper and middle classes. About 14,000 unaccompanied children were sent to the United States under Operation Pedro Pan. The Camarioca boatlift (1965) and the twice-daily Freedom Flights (1965–1973) brought several hundred thousand more. In 1980, the Mariel boatlift carried about 125,000 Cubans to Florida in a few months, including prisoners and psychiatric patients released by the Cuban government. Economic collapse after the Soviet Union's fall triggered the 1994 balsero crisis, in which tens of thousands attempted the sea crossing on rafts.

Political influence

The exile community, centred on Miami, long formed a powerful lobby supporting the US embargo against Cuba and opposing normalisation with Havana; exile organisations backed the Bay of Pigs invasion of 1961 and later legislative measures such as the Helms–Burton Act. Attitudes have gradually diversified among younger generations.
