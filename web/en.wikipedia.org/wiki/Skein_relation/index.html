Skein relation

The three local configurations of a crossing used in a skein relation

Skein relations are a mathematical tool used to study knots in knot theory. A skein relation gives a linear relation between the values that a knot invariant assigns to three links which differ only in one small region of their diagrams, where the crossings in that region can take three possible forms: a positive crossing, the same crossing with the strands crossing the other way (a negative crossing), and the crossing removed (the strands joined without crossing).

Definition

Given a link invariant F, a skein relation has the form of a linear equation relating F(L+), F(L−) and F(L0), the values on the three links that agree everywhere except at the chosen crossing. Repeated application of the relation allows a complicated knot to be reduced, step by step, to a collection of unknots, whose value is known by normalisation. This provides a recursive algorithm for computing the invariant.

Examples

The best-known skein relations include those of the Conway polynomial, which John Horton Conway discovered in the late 1960s; the HOMFLY polynomial, which satisfies a two-variable relation that generalises both the Alexander and Jones polynomials; and the Jones polynomial itself, defined through values in a bracket-type expansion. Louis Kauffman's bracket polynomial is computed with a skein-type recursion. Skein relations also underlie the definition of skein modules in three-manifold topology and appear in the study of quantum invariants and the Temperley–Lieb algebra.
