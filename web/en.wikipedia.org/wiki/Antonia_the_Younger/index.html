Antonia the Younger

Born: 31 January 36 BC, Athens
Died: 1 May AD 37, Rome
Spouse: Nero Claudius Drusus
Children: Germanicus, Livilla, Claudius

Antonia Minor (31 January 36 BC – 1 May AD 37), also known as Antonia the Younger, was a daughter of the triumvir Mark Antony and Octavia the Younger, and a niece of the first Roman emperor, Augustus. She was the mother of the general Germanicus and the emperor Claudius, and the paternal grandmother of the emperor Caligula.

Life

Antonia was raised in Rome by her mother Octavia after her father's suicide in 30 BC. In 16 BC she married Nero Claudius Drusus, the younger brother of the future emperor Tiberius. Drusus died in 9 BC during a campaign in Germania, and Antonia, though still young, never remarried.

She presided over one of the most influential households in Rome, raising not only her own children but also, for a time, her grandson Caligula. She enjoyed the confidence of Augustus and of Tiberius, whose household she supplied with crucial information; according to Suetonius, her disclosure of Sejanus's conspiracy in AD 31 helped bring about the prefect's fall.

Death and honours

When Caligula succeeded Tiberius in AD 37, he granted Antonia extensive honours, but relations between grandmother and grandson deteriorated quickly. She died on 1 May AD 37, shortly after Caligula's accession; ancient sources suggest she may have taken her own life after being treated harshly by him. Claudius, on becoming emperor, restored her memory, struck coins in her honour, and had her birthday observed as a public holiday.
