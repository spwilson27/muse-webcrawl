Spinning reserve

Spinning reserve is the unused generating capacity that is already online and synchronised ("spinning") with an electric power grid, available to begin supplying power within a short interval — typically within ten minutes — in response to a sudden increase in demand or the unexpected outage of a generator or transmission line. It is a component of the grid's operating reserve.

Function

Grid operators must balance supply and demand continuously; the loss of a large generating unit instantly creates a shortfall that depresses grid frequency. Spinning reserve covers this contingency because the contributing units are already rotating in step with the grid and need only increase their output. Reliability standards commonly require that the total operating reserve equal at least the output of the largest single generator in the system (the "N−1" criterion), with a substantial fraction held as spinning reserve.

Related categories

Non-spinning (supplemental) reserve comes from units that are offline but can start and reach full output within about ten to thirty minutes. Replacement reserve can be deployed over a longer period, around thirty to sixty minutes, to restore the faster reserves. Frequency-responsive reserves, including the inherent inertial response of rotating machines and increasingly fast response from battery storage, act within seconds.

Economics

Holding spinning reserve has an opportunity cost, since a generator held partly unloaded forgoes energy sales, so reserve capacity is often procured through ancillary-services markets in which generators are paid for availability. Large battery installations now compete to supply these services, responding in well under a second.
