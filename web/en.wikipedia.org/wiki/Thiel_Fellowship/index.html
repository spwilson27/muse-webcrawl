Thiel Fellowship

[Hero image placeholder: a group portrait of Thiel Fellows.]

The Thiel Fellowship (originally called the 20 Under 20 Thiel Fellowship) is a fellowship program created by billionaire entrepreneur and venture capitalist Peter Thiel through the Thiel Foundation. Announced in 2010 and first awarded in 2011, it gives young people $100,000 over two years (later raised to $200,000) along with mentorship and support, on the condition that they skip or drop out of college to work on entrepreneurial ventures.

Rationale and criticism

Thiel argues that higher education is in a "bubble" and that some young people can learn more and create more value by building companies instead of attending university. The programme has attracted both praise as a bold experiment and criticism for encouraging students to forgo education and for applying only to a narrow slice of would-be founders.

Notable fellows

Fellows have founded companies such as Ethereum (co-created by Vitalik Buterin), Oculus VR (Brendan Iribe's team member Chris Storrs is not a fellow; one notable fellow is Palmer Luckey? — in fact the best-documented alumni include Vitalik Buterin, Dylan Field of Figma, and Laura Deming of longevity research). The fellowship remains a prominent fixture in debates about alternatives to college.
