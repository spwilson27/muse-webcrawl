Estancia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An estancia is a large, private plot of land used for farming or raising cattle or sheep — the Spanish American equivalent of a ranch — found mainly in Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, southern Brazil (where it is called an estância), and Spanish-speaking regions of the southern United States historically. Estancias in the pampas of Argentina and Uruguay were historically estates of land, often thousands of hectares, worked by gauchos and owned by a landholding family (the estanciero).

History

The estancia originated in the Spanish colonial period, when large land grants (mercedes de tierra) were given to settlers for stock-raising in the sparsely populated grasslands of the Río de la Plata. Early estancias were semi-frontier outposts, exposed to raids, where cattle descended from colonial herds were rounded up for hides, tallow, and jerked beef (tasajo) rather than fresh meat. During the 19th century, as salting plants (saladeros) and later refrigeration opened export markets for beef, the estancia became a modern, fenced, capitalist enterprise: wire fencing, pedigree Shorthorn, Hereford, and Aberdeen Angus cattle, alfalfa pastures, and windmills transformed the pampas. Great estancieros became a powerful rural oligarchy in Argentine politics and society.

Organisation

A classic estancia was organised around the casco — the main house, often a substantial manor with outbuildings, chapel, and parkland — with a hierarchy of staff: the mayordomo (manager), capataz (foreman), and peones and gauchos who did the mounted work of herding, branding (yerra), and breaking horses. Sheep estancias were especially characteristic of Patagonia, where vast wool estates, some exceeding a million acres in company hands, were established in the late 19th century.

Modern era

Land reform pressures, subdivision among heirs, and mechanisation shrank many estates in the 20th century, but the estancia remains an emblem of Argentine and Uruguayan rural identity. Many historic estancias now operate as hotels and tourist lodges (estancias turísticas), offering horseback riding, polo, and traditional asado barbecues. The word is also used for rural estates in the Philippines, a legacy of Spanish rule there.
