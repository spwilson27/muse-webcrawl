The tower of Freemasons' Hall on Great Queen Street

Freemasons' Hall in London is the headquarters of the United Grand Lodge of England and the Supreme Grand Chapter of Royal Arch Masons, and a meeting place for many Masonic lodges in the London area. The present building, on Great Queen Street in Covent Garden, is the third Masonic hall on the site.

History

The first hall was built in 1775–76 to designs by Thomas Sandby. The current building was erected between 1927 and 1933 as a memorial to the more than 3,000 Freemasons who died on active service in the First World War; it was originally known as the Masonic Peace Memorial. It is a Grade II* listed building.

Architecture and use

Built in a restrained Art Deco style around a steel frame clad in Portland stone, the hall contains the Grand Temple, seating around 1,700, with a ceiling mosaic and large bronze doors, plus some twenty other lodge rooms, a library, and a museum of Freemasonry open to the public. The building is frequently used as a filming location, standing in for Thames House in the television series Spooks, among many other productions.
