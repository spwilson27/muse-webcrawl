Augustus of Prima Porta

Year1st century AD (c. 20 BC type)
MediumWhite marble
Dimensions2.03 m (6 ft 8 in) tall
LocationVatican Museums, Rome
Discovered20 April 1863, Villa of Livia, Prima Porta

The Augustus of Prima Porta (Italian: Augusto di Prima Porta) is a full-length portrait statue of Augustus, the first emperor of Rome. The statue was discovered on 20 April 1863 during archaeological excavations directed by Giuseppe Gagliardi at the Villa of Livia, owned by Augustus's consort Livia Drusilla, in Prima Porta, near Rome. It is now held in the Braccio Nuovo of the Vatican Museums.

Description

Augustus is shown in military dress (a muscled cuirass and cloak), barefoot, raising his right arm in the adlocutio pose of an orator addressing troops. The pose recalls Polykleitos' Doryphoros, blending Classical Greek idealism with distinctly Roman portraiture. A small figure of Cupid riding a dolphin at his right leg alludes to the Julian family's claimed descent from Venus.

The breastplate

The cuirass is carved in relief with a famous scene: a Parthian returning the military standards (aquilae) captured from Crassus at the Battle of Carrhae in 53 BC, which Augustus recovered by diplomacy in 20 BC. Surrounding figures include personifications of conquered peoples, the earth goddess Tellus below, and celestial deities above — a visual programme presenting Augustus as divinely sanctioned bringer of peace, the Pax Augusta.

Dating and polychromy

The statue is generally dated to the early 1st century AD; it may be a marble copy of a bronze original made around 20 BC. Traces of pigment show the statue was originally painted in vivid colours, and modern reconstructions with restored polychromy have been exhibited.
