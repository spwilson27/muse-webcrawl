National Youth Poet Laureate

Programme: Urban Word / Youth Poet Laureate programme
Established: 2017 (first national title)
First laureate: Amanda Gorman
Eligibility: Winners of local Youth Poet Laureate titles

The National Youth Poet Laureate is a title held by a young poet in the United States who demonstrates skill in poetry and spoken word, together with leadership, civic engagement and social justice work. The programme grew out of local Youth Poet Laureate competitions run in cities across the country, coordinated by the literary arts organisation Urban Word.

History

The first National Youth Poet Laureate was named in 2017: Amanda Gorman of Los Angeles, who had been the inaugural Youth Poet Laureate of Los Angeles and went on to read her poem "The Hill We Climb" at the inauguration of President Joe Biden in January 2021, becoming the youngest inaugural poet in U.S. history. Gorman's prominence brought national attention to the programme.

Selection

Regional and local laureates, chosen by partner organisations in dozens of cities, compete for the national title through portfolios of poetry and civic work. The national laureate undertakes a tour of readings and workshops, publishes work, and serves as an ambassador for youth literacy and civic expression.
