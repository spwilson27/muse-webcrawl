The Blossoms

The Blossoms were an American girl group formed in Los Angeles in the 1950s. Although they rarely achieved fame under their own name, the Blossoms were among the most successful backing vocalists in pop history: their voices appear on hundreds of hit records, frequently uncredited, and lead singer Darlene Love became one of the defining voices of Phil Spector's "Wall of Sound".

Career

Formed at Fremont High School in Los Angeles as the Dreamers, the group — built around Darlene Love, Fanita James and Jean King — sang backup for artists throughout the late 1950s before coming to the attention of producer Phil Spector. As Spector's house backing singers they appeared on records by the Crystals, Bob B. Soxx & the Blue Jeans and others; indeed, the Crystals' 1962 chart-topper "He's a Rebel" was actually recorded by the Blossoms with Love on lead vocal, though released under the Crystals' name. Love's solo releases for Spector, including "(Today I Met) The Boy I'm Gonna Marry", established her as one of the great voices of the era, and she also recorded the seasonal standard "Christmas (Baby Please Come Home)" in 1963.

The Blossoms worked steadily as the television house band singers on Shindig! in the mid-1960s, performed a residency behind Elvis Presley in his 1968 Comeback Special era, and backed artists from Sam Cooke and Duane Eddy to the Beach Boys, Sonny & Cher and Tom Jones. Later line-ups sang on records by John Lennon, Barbra Streisand and many others.

Recognition

For decades the group's contributions went largely unacknowledged by the industry. The 2013 Oscar-winning documentary 20 Feet from Stardom brought Love and her fellow backing singers to wide public attention, and Darlene Love was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2011.
