Sutlej

The Sutlej (also spelled Satluj) is the longest of the five rivers that gave the Punjab its name ("land of five rivers"). It flows from Lake Rakshastal near Mount Kailash in Tibet, westward through the Himalayas in Himachal Pradesh, then southwest across the Punjab plains of India and Pakistan, before joining the Chenab system to form the Panjnad and ultimately the Indus. Its total length is about 1,450 km.

Course and projects

In India the river is dammed at the Bhakra Dam, one of the world's highest gravity dams, whose Gobind Sagar reservoir supports irrigation and hydroelectric power; the Nathpa Jhakri project is another major hydropower scheme on its upper course. In its lower course the river forms part of the India–Pakistan border.

History

Under the Indus Waters Treaty of 1960, the waters of the Sutlej, Beas and Ravi — the "eastern rivers" — were allocated to India. In antiquity the river (the Greek Hyphasis region and Vedic Shutudri) is associated with the Ghaggar-Hakra system; some scholars link its former course to the lost Sarasvati river.
