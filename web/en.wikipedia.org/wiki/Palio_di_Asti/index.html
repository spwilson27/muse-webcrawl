Palio di Asti

The Palio di Asti is a traditional bareback horse race held in Asti, Piedmont, Italy, every year on the third Sunday of September. It is among the oldest palio races in Italy, with roots in the thirteenth century, predating the better-known Palio di Siena. The prize is the palio itself, a richly decorated banner of crimson velvet dedicated to the city's patron, Saint Secundus of Asti.

History

The race is traditionally said to have originated in 1275, when the men of Asti, after defeating their rivals from Alba, ran the race defiantly around Alba's walls. References to an annual race in honour of San Secondo appear in medieval chronicles and statutes. The race was run, with interruptions, through the centuries—around the city's streets and later on a course in Piazza Alfieri—before being suspended and then revived in 1929 under the Fascist regime, suppressed again after disputes, and definitively restored in 1967.

The modern race

Twenty-one participants—the rioni (districts), borghi (boroughs) of Asti, and nearby comuni (municipalities)—compete. Three heats of seven horses each qualify the first three finishers of each heat for the nine-horse final, run over three laps of the triangular course in Piazza Campo del Palio. Horses are ridden bareback by jockeys (fantini) in the colours of their district. The festivities include a historical procession in medieval costume, flag-throwing displays (sbandieratori), and neighbourhood dinners on the eve of the race.
