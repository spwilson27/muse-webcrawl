General classification in the Tour de France

The general classification of the Tour de France ranks riders by their cumulative finishing times over all stages of the race. The leader of the general classification wears the famous yellow jersey (French: maillot jaune), introduced in 1919 so spectators could easily identify the race leader.

How it works

Each rider's times for every stage are added together; the rider with the lowest total time leads. Time bonuses may be awarded at stage finishes and intermediate sprints, reducing a rider's aggregate. If riders are tied on time, fractions of a second from individual time trials and then stage placings are used to separate them.

History

The first Tour in 1903 was decided on time, and the classification has remained the race's principal competition. Four riders share the record of five overall victories: Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault and Miguel Indurain. Lance Armstrong was stripped of seven titles for doping, leaving those editions without an official winner.

Other classifications

The general classification is distinct from the points classification (green jersey), the mountains classification (polka-dot jersey), and the young rider classification (white jersey), though strong general classification riders frequently contest several of them.
