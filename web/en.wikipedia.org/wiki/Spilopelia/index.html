Spilopelia is a genus of doves in the pigeon family Columbidae. Its two species, the spotted dove and the laughing dove, were formerly placed in the genus Streptopelia but were separated on the basis of molecular phylogenetic studies and differences in morphology and vocalisation.

Species

  • Spotted dove (Spilopelia chinensis) – widespread in Asia from India to China and Southeast Asia, and introduced to Australia, New Zealand, Hawaii, and California. It is recognised by the black neck patch studded with white spots that gives the genus its name (spilos, "spot"; peleia, "dove").
  • Laughing dove (Spilopelia senegalensis) – a small, long-tailed dove of sub-Saharan Africa, the Middle East, and South Asia, named for its bubbling, laughter-like call.

Description

Species of Spilopelia are slender, long-tailed doves of open country, gardens, and scrub. They feed mainly on seeds and grains taken from the ground, often in pairs or small flocks, and build flimsy stick nests in trees or on buildings. Their calls are soft cooing phrases repeated rhythmically.

Taxonomy

The genus was introduced by the Swedish zoologist Carl Jakob Sundevall in 1873. Genetic analyses of doves and pigeons in the early 2000s showed that the spotted and laughing doves form a distinct lineage separate from the typical turtle doves of Streptopelia, prompting their transfer to the resurrected genus Spilopelia.
