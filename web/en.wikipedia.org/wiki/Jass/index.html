Jass

[Image: Cards used in Swiss Jass]

Jass is a family of trick-taking card games, most closely associated with Switzerland, where it is regarded as the national card game. The most common form, Schieber, is played by four players in partnerships with a 36-card pack. Jass games are also played in the neighbouring regions of Austria, Germany, France, and Italy, and by Swiss emigrant communities abroad.

History

Jass is believed to have originated in the Netherlands in the late eighteenth century and to have been brought to Switzerland by Protestant mercenaries returning from Dutch service. The earliest Swiss references date from the 1790s. The game spread throughout the country and became so ingrained in Swiss culture that dedicated television programmes, notably Samschtig-Jass (Saturday Jass) on SRF, have run since 1968.

Gameplay

In Schieber, each player is dealt nine cards. The player to the dealer's right chooses the trump suit or opts for one of two special contracts (top-down, Obenabe, or bottom-up, Undenufe), or "pushes" the choice to their partner. Card rankings are unusual: in the trump suit the jack (the Buur) and the nine (the Näll) are the two highest cards. Points are scored from card values and from melds (sequences and four of a kind) announced at the first trick, with a game typically played to 1,000 or 2,500 points.

Variants

Many regional variants exist, including Coiffeur, Differenzler (in which players predict their own score), and Molotow. Jass terminology has entered Swiss German colloquial speech.
