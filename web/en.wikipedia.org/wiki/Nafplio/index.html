Nafplio (Greek: Ναύπλιο) is a coastal town in the Peloponnese in Greece. It is the capital of the regional unit of Argolis and an important tourist destination. Founded in antiquity, the town was ruled in turn by Byzantines, Franks, Venetians, and Ottomans, and served as the first capital of the modern Greek state from 1828 to 1834, when Athens became the capital.

Nafplio's old town, crowned by the Venetian Palamidi fortress and the fortified islet of Bourtzi in the harbour.

History

According to Greek mythology, the town was founded by Nauplius, son of Poseidon. In the Middle Ages it passed through Byzantine, Frankish, and Catalan hands before Venice acquired it in 1388; the Venetians fortified it and called it Napoli di Romania. The Ottomans took it in 1540, Venice retook it in 1686 under Francesco Morosini — building the great Palamidi fortress in 1711–1714 — and the Ottomans recaptured it in 1715. During the Greek War of Independence, Greek forces seized the town in 1822 after a long siege, and Ioannis Kapodistrias, the first governor of Greece, made it his capital; he was assassinated here in 1831. King Otto arrived in Nafplio in 1833 before the capital moved to Athens the following year.

Landmarks

The Palamidi fortress, reached traditionally by 999 steps, dominates the town. The small castle of Bourtzi sits on an islet in the harbour. The old town preserves Venetian and Ottoman architecture around Syntagma Square, and the Archaeological Museum of Nafplio displays finds from nearby Mycenaean sites; the town is a convenient base for visiting Mycenae and Epidaurus.
