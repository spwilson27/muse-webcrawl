Massacre at Huế
LocationHuế, South Vietnam
DateFebruary–March 1968
ContextTet Offensive, Vietnam War

The Huế massacre was a series of murders and mass executions committed by the Viet Cong and People's Army of Vietnam during their occupation of Huế in the Tet Offensive of 1968, in the Vietnam War. Thousands of South Vietnamese civilians and officials were killed and buried in mass graves.

Events

During the roughly month-long occupation of the city, occupation forces conducted house-to-house searches using prepared lists, targeting civil servants, teachers, religious figures, and others deemed enemies of the revolution. Victims were shot, bludgeoned, or buried alive; mass graves were discovered over the following years.

Estimates and debate

Estimates of victims range from about 2,800 to 6,000. The massacre became a major element of American and South Vietnamese accounts of the war, while its scale and responsibility have remained subjects of historical debate.
