Dame Julie Andrews (born Julia Elizabeth Wells, 1 October 1935) is an English actress, singer, and author. A child actress and singer, she rose to prominence originating Eliza Doolittle in My Fair Lady (1956) and Guenevere in Camelot (1960) on Broadway.

Career

Passed over for the film of My Fair Lady, Andrews made her film debut as the title character in Walt Disney's Mary Poppins (1964), winning the Academy Award for Best Actress. She followed it with The Sound of Music (1965), one of the highest-grossing films of all time, as Maria von Trapp. Later roles ranged from Thoroughly Modern Millie and Victor/Victoria (1982) — directed by her husband Blake Edwards — to voice work as Queen Lillian in Shrek and the narrator Lady Whistledown in Bridgerton.

A botched throat operation in 1997 severely damaged her celebrated four-octave singing voice. Andrews has since focused on writing children's books, often with her daughter Emma Walton Hamilton, and on directing and narration.

Honours

She was made a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 2000, received the AFI Life Achievement Award, a Kennedy Center Honor, and is one of the few performers with Academy, Emmy, Grammy, and Golden Globe wins.
