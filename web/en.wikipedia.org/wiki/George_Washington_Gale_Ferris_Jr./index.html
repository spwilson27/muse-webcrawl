George Washington Gale Ferris Jr.

Born 14 February 1859, Galesburg, Illinois, U.S.
Died 22 November 1896 (aged 37), Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Occupation Civil engineer
Known for Inventing the Ferris wheel

George Washington Gale Ferris Jr. (February 14, 1859 – November 22, 1896) was an American civil engineer best known for creating the original Ferris wheel for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, Illinois.

Early life and education

Ferris was born in Galesburg, Illinois, the son of George Washington Gale Ferris Sr. and Martha Edgerton Ferris. In 1864 the family sold their dairy farm and moved to Nevada, eventually settling in Carson City. Ferris attended schools in Nevada and California, and in 1875 entered the California Military Academy in Oakland, where he learned horsemanship. He graduated in 1881 from Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute in Troy, New York, with a degree in civil engineering, and began his career in the railroad industry.

Career

Ferris specialized in bridge building and structural steel work. In Pittsburgh he founded G.W.G. Ferris & Co., a firm that tested and inspected metals for railroads and bridge builders. By the early 1890s he had built bridges across the Ohio River at Cincinnati and elsewhere, and was a respected steel engineer.

The Ferris wheel

For the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, the fair's planners wanted a landmark structure to rival the Eiffel Tower of the Paris Exposition of 1889. Ferris proposed a giant rotating observation wheel. Despite skepticism, his design was approved, and he and his partners raised the money to build it. The wheel was 264 feet (80 meters) high — about 26 stories — with 36 cars, each the size of a streetcar, capable of holding 60 people; in all it could carry 2,160 passengers at once. Its 45-foot axle was the largest single piece of forged steel then made in the United States.

The wheel opened on June 21, 1893, and was an immediate success, carrying hundreds of thousands of riders during the fair, but Ferris profited little from it. Litigation over the wheel and related concessions consumed his later years. After the fair the wheel was moved to St. Louis for the 1904 Louisiana Purchase Exposition and was eventually demolished with dynamite in 1906.

Death and legacy

Ferris died in 1896 at the age of 37 in Pittsburgh's Mercy Hospital of typhoid fever (reported in some accounts as kidney disease). His name lives on in the generic term "Ferris wheel" for large observation wheels worldwide.
