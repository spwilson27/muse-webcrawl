Head of navigation

Head of navigation is the farthest point upstream on a river that can be navigated by ships or boats.

The head of navigation is the farthest point above the mouth of a river that can be navigated by ships or boats. Above this point, navigation is blocked by natural obstacles such as rapids, waterfalls, shoals, or the narrowing and shallowing of the channel. The head of navigation typically coincides with the fall line on rivers flowing from uplands to a coastal plain.

Significance

Heads of navigation were historically important sites for settlement: goods had to be unloaded and transferred between water and land transport (a break-in-bulk point), and the falls that blocked navigation provided water power for mills. Many cities were founded at such points, including Richmond, Virginia, at the falls of the James River; Trenton, New Jersey, on the Delaware; and Augusta, Georgia, on the Savannah River. In the United States the legal concept also matters, since navigability in law determines federal jurisdiction over waterways and title to riverbeds.

Artificial extension

Canals, locks and dredging can extend the head of navigation upstream. The construction of canals around falls — such as the Welland Canal bypassing Niagara Falls — shifted historic break-in-bulk points and the cities that depended on them.
