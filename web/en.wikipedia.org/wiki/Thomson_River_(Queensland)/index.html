The Thomson River is a perennial river in central-west Queensland, Australia. It forms part of the Lake Eyre basin, one of the largest internal drainage systems in the world. The river flows through the town of Longreach and, at its junction with the Barcoo River north-west of Windorah, forms Cooper Creek.

Course

The Thomson rises in the high country west of the Great Dividing Range, north of the settlement of Muttaburra, and flows generally south-westward through Longreach, Stonehenge and Jundah. Like other rivers of the Channel Country, it is characterised by a network of braided channels and waterholes that fill during the seasonal floods of the summer monsoon and contract during the long dry season. Its confluence with the Barcoo River creates Cooper Creek, which in flood years reaches Lake Eyre in South Australia.

History

The country along the river is the traditional land of Aboriginal peoples including the Iningai. The river was named in honour of Sir Edward Deas Thomson, Colonial Secretary of New South Wales, by explorers who traversed the district in the mid-19th century. The explorer William Landsborough passed through the region in 1862 while searching for Burke and Wills. Longreach, founded in 1887, became the major service town of the river's middle reaches.

Ecology and use

The river's permanent waterholes are important refuges for fish, waterbirds and other wildlife in an arid landscape, and support pastoral grazing on the surrounding Mitchell grass plains. The Thomson River weir at Longreach supplies the town's water, and the river is a popular site for fishing and camping.
