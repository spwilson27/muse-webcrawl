The Thief of Bagdad (1924 film)

DirectorRaoul Walsh
StarringDouglas Fairbanks
Released1924
Production designWilliam Cameron Menzies

The Thief of Bagdad is a 1924 American silent swashbuckler film directed by Raoul Walsh and starring Douglas Fairbanks, who also produced and conceived the story. Drawing on One Thousand and One Nights, it follows the carefree thief Ahmed, who falls in love with the Caliph's daughter and, to win her, undertakes a quest for the rarest treasure in the world.

Production

Fairbanks built enormous stylised sets of Baghdad — among the largest of the silent era — designed by William Cameron Menzies, whose art direction produced a dreamlike, geometric Arabian Nights world. The film's special effects, including the flying carpet, the winged horse and the cloak of invisibility, were state-of-the-art, and its budget of roughly two million dollars made it one of the most expensive films of its time.

Legacy

The film is regarded as one of Fairbanks's finest vehicles and a landmark of fantasy cinema; it was selected for the United States National Film Registry in 1996. Its success inspired the 1940 British Technicolor remake of the same name, which in turn influenced Disney's Aladdin.
