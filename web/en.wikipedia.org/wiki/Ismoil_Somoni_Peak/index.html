Ismoil Somoni Peak is the highest mountain in Tajikistan, rising 7,495 metres (24,590 ft) in the Academy of Sciences Range of the Pamir Mountains. It was the highest point of the Soviet Union and was formerly known as Stalin Peak and later Communism Peak before being renamed in 1998 in honour of Ismoil Somoni (Ismail Samani), founder of the Samanid dynasty.

History

The peak's height and position were established by Soviet surveys in the late 1920s, correcting earlier confusion with Peak Garmo. It was first climbed in September 1933 by Soviet mountaineer Yevgeniy Abalakov during a Tajik–Pamir expedition. The standard route approaches from the Fedchenko Glacier region via the Bivachny Glacier, and the mountain is a classic objective of the "Snow Leopard" award for climbing the five 7,000-metre peaks of the former Soviet Union.

Geography

The peak lies in northwestern Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous Region near the border with Kyrgyzstan, surrounded by some of the longest valley glaciers outside the polar regions, including the Fedchenko Glacier.
