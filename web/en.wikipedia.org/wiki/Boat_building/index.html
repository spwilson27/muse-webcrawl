Boat building

[Image: A wooden boat under construction on frames in a workshop]

Boat building is the design and construction of boats and their systems. It is one of the oldest branches of engineering, with evidence of hollowed-out log canoes dating back to the Stone Age. Boat building ranges from the one-off construction of individual craft by amateurs and small yards to the serial production of fibreglass and metal boats.

History

The oldest known boats are dugout canoes made around 10,000 years ago; plank-built boats appeared in ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia. Viking shipwrights perfected clinker (lapstrake) construction, while carvel construction, with planks laid edge to edge over frames, became standard in the Mediterranean. Industrialisation introduced iron, steel, plywood, and from the 1950s fibreglass (GRP), which transformed recreational boat production.

Construction methods

Traditional methods include clinker and carvel planking over steam-bent or sawn frames. Modern methods include stitch-and-glue plywood construction popular with amateurs, cold-moulding with epoxy-saturated veneers, strip planking, aluminium welding, and fibreglass moulding for production boats.

Design

Boat design is governed by naval architecture: hull form determines stability, resistance and seaworthiness; displacement, planing and semi-displacement hulls serve different speeds and loads. Small-craft builders traditionally work by eye and by inherited moulds, "building by the rules of thumb" refined over generations.
