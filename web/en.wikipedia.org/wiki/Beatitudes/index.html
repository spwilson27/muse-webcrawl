Beatitudes

The Beatitudes are sayings of Jesus of Nazareth that open the Sermon on the Mount in the Gospel of Matthew (Matthew 5:3–12); a shorter set appears in the Sermon on the Plain in Luke (Luke 6:20–23), there accompanied by corresponding "woes." Each saying begins "Blessed are…" (Greek makarios) and pronounces a blessing on a group — often an unlikely one — together with the reason or reward. The name comes from the Latin beatus, "blessed," with which each saying begins in the Vulgate.

The sayings in Matthew

Matthew's eight (or nine) Beatitudes bless the poor in spirit ("for theirs is the kingdom of heaven"), those who mourn, the meek ("for they shall inherit the earth"), those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, the merciful, the pure in heart ("for they shall see God"), the peacemakers ("for they shall be called children of God"), and those persecuted for righteousness' sake. Luke's version addresses the poor, the hungry, those who weep, and the hated, and balances them with woes against the rich, the full, those who laugh, and the well-spoken-of.

Interpretation

The Beatitudes have been read in many ways: as an ethic for the coming kingdom, as entrance requirements for discipleship, as consolation for the afflicted, and — in the contrast between Matthew's "poor in spirit" and Luke's "poor" — as evidence of the evangelists' different emphases. They are among the most quoted of Jesus' teachings and are commemorated at the Church of the Beatitudes on the Mount of Beatitudes overlooking the Sea of Galilee, the traditional site of the sermon. The passage has inspired extensive commentary from Augustine and Aquinas to modern theologians, as well as musical settings and works such as Olivier Messiaen's meditations.
