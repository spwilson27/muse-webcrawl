Calabi–Yau manifold

Calabi–Yau manifold
Named afterEugenio Calabi and Shing-Tung Yau
ClassCompact Kähler manifold with trivial canonical bundle
Key propertyAdmits a Ricci-flat metric

A Calabi–Yau manifold is a special type of complex manifold that is Kähler and has a vanishing first Chern class, which by Yau's proof of the Calabi conjecture guarantees the existence of a Ricci-flat metric. Calabi–Yau manifolds are central objects in algebraic geometry and in string theory.

Definitions

Several closely related definitions are used: a compact Kähler manifold with trivial canonical bundle; a manifold with SU(n) holonomy; or a Ricci-flat Kähler manifold. Important examples in complex dimension three include the quintic threefold in complex projective 4-space.

Role in string theory

In superstring theory, spacetime is modelled as the product of four-dimensional Minkowski space with a tiny six-dimensional Calabi–Yau manifold. The geometry of the compact space determines the particle spectrum and interactions of the resulting four-dimensional physics, making the classification of Calabi–Yau threefolds a major research area.

Mirror symmetry

Calabi–Yau manifolds come in mirror pairs: two topologically distinct manifolds can yield identical physics, exchanging their Hodge numbers. Mirror symmetry, predicted by physicists and developed mathematically by Kontsevich and others, has produced striking enumerative geometry results, such as counting rational curves on the quintic threefold.
