Birjand (Persian: بیرجند) is a city in the Central District of Birjand County, South Khorasan province, Iran, serving as capital of both the province and the county. It lies in eastern Iran, near the border with Afghanistan, and is known for its exports of saffron, barberry, jujube and hand-knotted carpets.

History

The area around Birjand has been inhabited since ancient times, and the city appears in historical records from the time of the Persian geographer Yaqut al-Hamawi (13th century). The city developed as a trading post on the routes between Persia, Afghanistan and the Indian subcontinent. In the late Safavid and Qajar eras it was governed by the Alam family, whose scion, Asadollah Alam, served as prime minister of Iran under Mohammad Reza Pahlavi. The Shokatiyeh School in Birjand, founded in the early 20th century, was the third modern school in Iran.

Geography and climate

Birjand sits at an elevation of about 1,490 metres in a valley surrounded by mountains, in an arid region with a cold desert climate: hot, dry summers and cold winters, with low annual rainfall. The city is served by Birjand International Airport and is connected by road to Mashhad and Zahedan.

Economy and culture

South Khorasan is Iran's leading saffron-producing region, and Birjand trades in saffron, barberries (zereshk), jujubes and carpets. The Akbarieh Garden and Mansion, a Qajar-era complex in the city, is inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage "Persian Garden" listing. The University of Birjand, founded in 1975, and Birjand University of Medical Sciences are the city's main higher-education institutions.
