The Communist Party of Vietnam (CPV; Vietnamese: Đảng Cộng sản Việt Nam) is the founding and sole legal party of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam. Founded by Hồ Chí Minh in Hong Kong on 3 February 1930 through the unification of rival Vietnamese communist organisations, it led the August Revolution of 1945, the defeat of France at Điện Biên Phủ in 1954, and the victory over South Vietnam and the United States in 1975, followed by national reunification in 1976.

History

Operating for much of its early life as the Indochinese Communist Party, and from 1951 as the Vietnam Workers' Party in the North, the party resumed the name Communist Party of Vietnam at its Fourth Congress in 1976. At its Sixth Congress in 1986, facing economic crisis, it adopted the Đổi Mới ("renovation") reforms, introducing a "socialist-oriented market economy" while retaining the party's monopoly on political power.

Organisation

The party's supreme body is the National Congress, held every five years, which elects a Central Committee; the Central Committee elects a Politburo and a General Secretary, the country's most powerful office. Leadership is conventionally balanced among the "four pillars": the General Secretary, the President, the Prime Minister, and the Chair of the National Assembly. Membership stands at over five million. The party directs the state, the Vietnam People's Army, and mass organisations through the Vietnamese Fatherland Front.

Ideology

Official ideology is Marxism–Leninism combined with "Hồ Chí Minh Thought." Since Đổi Mới the party has legitimised extensive private enterprise and foreign investment while maintaining that Vietnam remains in a transitional stage toward socialism.
