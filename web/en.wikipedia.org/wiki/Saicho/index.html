Saichō (最澄, 767–822), posthumously known as Dengyō Daishi, was a Japanese Buddhist monk credited with founding the Tendai school of Buddhism in Japan, based on the Chinese Tiantai tradition centred on the Lotus Sutra. His monastery of Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei trained many of the figures who shaped later Japanese Buddhism, including the founders of Pure Land, Zen and Nichiren schools.

Life

Born Mitsu no Obito Hirono in Ōmi Province, Saichō was ordained at Tōdai-ji in Nara at 14 and soon withdrew to Mount Hiei, where he built a hermitage in 788 that became Enryaku-ji. His writings attracted the attention of Emperor Kanmu, who was moving the capital to Kyoto nearby.

In 804 Saichō sailed to Tang China as part of an official embassy, studying Tiantai doctrine at Mount Tiantai under Daosui and Xingman, as well as esoteric (mikkyō), Zen and Pure Land teachings. He returned in 805 with texts and initiations and established Tendai in Japan. He spent his later years in a long campaign to win imperial recognition for a separate Mahayana ordination platform on Mount Hiei — freeing his school from the Nara establishment — which was granted a week after his death in 822. In 866 he received the posthumous title Dengyō Daishi from Emperor Seiwa.

Legacy

Enryaku-ji grew into one of the great centres of Japanese religion, and Saichō's broad, Lotus-centred synthesis — including the idea that all beings possess Buddha-nature — provided the matrix from which Hōnen, Shinran, Eisai, Dōgen and Nichiren all emerged in the Kamakura period.
