Massively multiplayer online role-playing game

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A massively multiplayer online role-playing game (MMORPG) is a video game that combines aspects of a role-playing video game and a massively multiplayer online game. As in other RPGs, the player assumes the role of a character, often in a fantasy or science-fiction world, and takes control over many of that character's actions. MMORPGs are distinguished by their large, persistent worlds, hosted on the publisher's servers, in which thousands of players interact simultaneously and the game world continues to exist and evolve while the player is away.

History

The genre descends from text-based multi-user dungeons (MUDs) of the late 1970s and 1980s. The first graphical MMORPGs appeared in the 1990s: Meridian 59 (1996), Ultima Online (1997) and EverQuest (1999) established the model of subscription-based persistent worlds: character creation, levelling by gaining experience points, quests, player-versus-environment (PvE) combat, player-versus-player (PvP) combat, crafting and guilds. World of Warcraft (2004) brought the genre to the mainstream, peaking at over 12 million subscribers and defining the theme-park style of MMORPG design, in contrast to open, player-driven "sandbox" games such as EVE Online (2003).

Common features

Most MMORPGs share themes of progression, social interaction and an in-game economy. Players group into parties and guilds to attempt dungeons and raids requiring coordinated teams. Character classes fill complementary roles — tank, healer and damage-dealer (the "holy trinity"). Developers sustain the game through expansions, seasonal events and updates ("live service"), while players trade items in auction houses that sometimes mirror real economies.

Business models

The genre was built on monthly subscriptions, but most titles later shifted to free-to-play models supported by microtransactions and cash shops, or to buy-to-play. Games such as Final Fantasy XIV and World of Warcraft continue to combine box sales with subscriptions.

Culture and research

MMORPGs have been studied as social environments and virtual economies, and problems of gaming addiction have been discussed in relation to their reward structures. They have also been used for research, famously when epidemiologists analysed the accidental "Corrupted Blood" plague in World of Warcraft in 2005 as a model of epidemic spread.
