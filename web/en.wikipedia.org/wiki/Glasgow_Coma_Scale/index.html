Glasgow Coma Scale

The Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) is a clinical scale used to reliably measure a person's level of consciousness after a brain injury. It was devised by Graham Teasdale and Bryan Jennett of the University of Glasgow and first published in 1974 in The Lancet.

Elements of the scale

The scale assesses three aspects of responsiveness: eye-opening response, verbal response, and motor response. Each component is scored separately, and the sum ranges from 3 (deep unconsciousness) to 15 (fully awake).

Interpretation

Scores of 13–15 are generally considered mild brain injury, 9–12 moderate, and 8 or below severe. A score of 8 or less is commonly used as a threshold for intubation, since the patient may be unable to protect their airway.

History and use

The GCS is used in intensive care, emergency medicine, and trauma assessment worldwide. A paediatric version, the Paediatric Glasgow Coma Scale, modifies the verbal component for young children. In 2018 the Glasgow Coma Scale – Pupils score (GCS-P) was introduced, incorporating pupil reactivity.
