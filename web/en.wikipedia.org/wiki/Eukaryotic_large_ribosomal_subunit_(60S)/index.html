Eukaryotic large ribosomal subunit (60S)

60S ribosomal subunit
Part of: Eukaryotic 80S ribosome
Composition: 28S, 5.8S and 5S rRNAs; about 47 ribosomal proteins (in humans)
Function: Peptide bond formation (peptidyl transferase)
Assembled in: Nucleolus and cytoplasm

The eukaryotic large ribosomal subunit, designated 60S (where S is the Svedberg unit of sedimentation), is the larger of the two subunits of the eukaryotic 80S ribosome. Together with the 40S small subunit, it carries out translation of messenger RNA into protein.

Composition and structure

In humans, the 60S subunit contains three ribosomal RNAs—the 28S, 5.8S and 5S rRNAs—and about 47 ribosomal proteins. X-ray crystallography and cryo-electron microscopy have revealed its structure in atomic detail, showing that the catalytic heart of the ribosome is made of RNA: the peptidyl transferase centre, which forms peptide bonds between amino acids, is a ribozyme.

Function

The large subunit binds transfer RNAs at its A (aminoacyl), P (peptidyl) and E (exit) sites, catalyses peptide bond formation, and provides the tunnel through which the growing polypeptide chain exits. The 60S subunit joins the 40S subunit on an mRNA to form the complete 80S ribosome during translation initiation. Factors such as eIF6 prevent premature subunit joining; export of pre-60S particles from the nucleus and their final maturation involve numerous assembly factors, including the AAA-ATPase Rea1/Mdn1.
