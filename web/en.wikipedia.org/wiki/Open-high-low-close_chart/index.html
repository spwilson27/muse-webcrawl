Open-high-low-close chart

Each bar summarises one period's open, high, low and close.

An open-high-low-close chart (also abbreviated as an OHLC chart) is a type of chart typically used in technical analysis to illustrate movements in the price of a financial instrument over time. Each vertical bar represents the price range (the highest and lowest prices) over one unit of time, e.g. one day or one hour, with tick marks showing the opening and closing prices.

Construction

The top of each vertical line is the highest price traded during the period and the bottom is the lowest price. A short horizontal tick protruding to the left marks the opening price, and a tick protruding to the right marks the closing price. If the close is above the open, the bar is often coloured black or green; if below, red. The four values — open, high, low, close — give the chart its name and constitute the standard summary of a trading period (a "bar").

Use

OHLC bars compress a period's trading into a compact symbol readable at a glance across hundreds of periods. Traders read bar shapes for signs of momentum and indecision, and OHLC data is the input to most technical indicators and to the related candlestick chart, which encodes the same four values with a filled body between open and close.

Variants

  • HLC chart — omits the opening price
  • OHLC average — the mean of the four values, used as a representative price
  • Volume bars — often plotted in a panel beneath the price panel
