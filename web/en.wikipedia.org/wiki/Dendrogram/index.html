Dendrogram

A dendrogram showing nested clusters produced by hierarchical clustering

A dendrogram is a tree diagram used to illustrate the arrangement of clusters produced by hierarchical clustering. The name comes from the Greek déndron, "tree", and grámma, "drawing".

Structure

In a dendrogram, individual data points (or items) start at one end as leaves. As the clustering algorithm proceeds, the most similar items or groups are joined by branches; the height at which two branches merge represents the distance or dissimilarity at which the clusters were combined. Cutting the dendrogram at a chosen height yields a partition of the data into a corresponding number of clusters.

Applications

Dendrograms are widely used in computational biology to display evolutionary relationships as phylogenetic trees and gene-expression clustering, as well as in data mining, image segmentation and other fields where hierarchical structure must be visualized. Variants include cladograms, in which branch lengths may not correspond to distances.
