Paper industry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The paper industry, or pulp and paper industry, comprises companies that manufacture paper, paperboard, and other cellulose-based products from wood, recycled fibre, and other raw materials. It is a global industry dominated by North American, northern and eastern European, and East Asian companies. World production of paper and paperboard is roughly 400 million tonnes per year, and China and the United States are the largest producers.

Manufacturing

Paper production typically takes place in integrated pulp and paper mills. Wood is debarked and chipped, then converted to pulp by mechanical or chemical processes such as the kraft process. The pulp is washed, bleached, and refined, then formed into a sheet on a paper machine (commonly a Fourdrinier machine), pressed, dried on steam-heated cylinders, and wound onto reels. Recovered paper is repulped and deinked for use in newsprint, packaging, and tissue grades.

Products

  • Printing and writing papers (newsprint, office paper, coated papers)
  • Packaging papers and paperboard (containerboard, cartonboard)
  • Tissue and hygiene products
  • Specialty papers (filter paper, banknote paper, thermal paper)
  • Dissolving pulp and other cellulose derivatives

Industry structure

Major international producers include International Paper, UPM, Stora Enso, Sappi, Smurfit Westrock, Mondi, Nine Dragons Paper, and Oji Paper. The industry is capital intensive and cyclical, closely tied to demand for packaging, which has grown with e-commerce even as graphic paper demand declines in developed markets.

Environmental issues

The industry is a major consumer of water and energy and has historically been associated with water pollution from pulping and bleaching effluents, dioxin emissions from chlorine bleaching, and greenhouse gas emissions. Modern mills increasingly use elemental-chlorine-free bleaching, closed water systems, biomass energy from bark and black liquor, and certified forestry fibre. Recycling rates for paper exceed 50% in many regions, and recycled fibre accounts for roughly half of the industry's raw material.

History

Papermaking originated in China, traditionally credited to Cai Lun in 105 CE, and spread through the Islamic world to Europe. Industrial production began in the 19th century with the Fourdrinier machine, wood-pulp processes, and later the kraft process, which remains dominant.
