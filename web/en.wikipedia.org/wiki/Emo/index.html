Emo

Emo (short for "emotional hardcore") is a rock music genre and subculture characterised by emotional, often confessional lyrics. It emerged in the mid-1980s in the Washington, D.C. hardcore punk scene, with bands such as Rites of Spring and Embrace — led by figures including Guy Picciotto and Ian MacKaye — turning hardcore's intensity inward toward personal, introspective themes.

History

In the 1990s, bands such as Sunny Day Real Estate, Jawbreaker, Jimmy Eat World and the Promise Ring developed the sound in indie-rock directions. Emo broke into the mainstream in the 2000s with the success of My Chemical Romance, Fall Out Boy, Panic! at the Disco, Dashboard Confessional and Paramore, and became associated with a distinctive fashion of dark clothing, tight jeans, studded belts and side-swept fringes. The subculture was also the subject of media controversy and moral panic in several countries. A wave of "emo revival" bands emerged in the 2010s, and emo rap later carried the aesthetic into hip hop.

Characteristics

Emo songs typically address heartbreak, nostalgia, alienation, grief and self-loathing, delivered with dramatic dynamic shifts between quiet verses and cathartic choruses. Critics and fans long debated which bands count as "real" emo, a dispute that became a defining feature of the genre's discourse.
