Milton Keynes is a city in Buckinghamshire, England, about 50 miles (80 km) north-west of London. It was designated a new town on 23 January 1967, combining the existing towns of Bletchley, Wolverton and Stony Stratford with numerous villages, in order to relieve housing congestion in London. Taking its name from the existing village of Milton Keynes in the area, the new town is the largest settlement in Buckinghamshire and one of the fastest-growing urban areas in the United Kingdom. It was granted city status in 2022 as part of the Platinum Jubilee Civic Honours.

Central Milton Keynes, showing the city's distinctive grid-road layout.

History

The area that became Milton Keynes has been settled since the Bronze Age; finds include a hoard of Bronze Age jewellery known as the Milton Keynes Hoard and the remains of a Roman villa. In the 1960s the government decided to build a series of new towns around London. The designated area of 21,883 acres (8,855 ha) was chosen in 1967, with a target population of 250,000. The Milton Keynes Development Corporation, chaired by Lord Campbell of Eskan ("Jock" Campbell), oversaw development until it was wound up in 1992.

Grid roads and redways

The town's master plan by chief architect Derek Walker used a distinctive grid of distributor roads roughly 1 kilometre apart, separating through traffic from local streets. The grid roads are known by names such as the H (horizontal) and V (vertical) roads — for example the V6 Grafton Street and H5 Portway — and are characterised by numerous roundabouts. A separate network of paths for pedestrians and cyclists, the "redways" (surfaced in red tarmac), runs alongside and beneath the grid roads.

Geography

Milton Keynes lies in the valley of the River Great Ouse, which forms its northern boundary, and the River Ouzel, which flows through the eastern side. The Grand Union Canal passes through the town. Although designed as a low-density urban area, much of its land is parkland managed by the Parks Trust, including Willen Lake and Campbell Park.

Economy

Milton Keynes is a major employment centre, home to the headquarters of companies including the UK arm of Santander, Network Rail (at the Quadrant:MK), the Open University, and the Red Bull Racing Formula One team. The centre:mk and intu (now Midsummer Place) shopping centres form one of the largest retail complexes in the UK.

Culture and sport

The city's football club, Milton Keynes Dons F.C., plays at Stadium MK; the city also has the Milton Keynes Theatre, the MK Gallery, and Bletchley Park — the wartime codebreaking centre, now a museum — within its boundaries. The Concrete Cows, a 1978 sculpture by Liz Leyh, have become an informal emblem of the city.
