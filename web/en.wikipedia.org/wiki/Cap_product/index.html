Cap product

In algebraic topology, the cap product is a bilinear operation that pairs a cohomology class with a homology class to produce a homology class of lower degree. Introduced by Eduard Čech and Hassler Whitney in the 1930s, it is fundamental to Poincaré duality. For a space X, a cohomology class φ ∈ Hp(X; R) and a homology class a ∈ Hq(X; R) with q ≥ p, the cap product φ ⌢ a lies in Hq−p(X; R).

Definition

At the chain level, for a singular q-simplex σ: Δq → X and a cochain φ, the cap product is defined by evaluating φ on the "front face" of σ (the first p vertices) and retaining the "back face" (the remaining q − p vertices) as a chain, with signs determined by a chosen convention. The operation descends to homology and cohomology classes, being independent of the representatives chosen.

Properties

The cap product is natural with respect to continuous maps and satisfies an associativity-like identity with the cup product: for cohomology classes φ, ψ and a homology class a, (φ ⌣ ψ) ⌢ a = φ ⌢ (ψ ⌢ a) up to sign conventions. For the fundamental class [M] of a closed oriented n-manifold M, cap product with [M] gives the Poincaré duality isomorphism Hp(M) → Hn−p(M).
