AIM-9 Sidewinder

The AIM-9 Sidewinder is a short-range air-to-air missile developed by the United States Navy and now built by RTX Corporation (Raytheon). Entering service in 1956, it was the world's first effective heat-seeking guided missile and remains in production in advanced variants more than six decades later. Its name comes from the sidewinder rattlesnake, which senses warm-blooded prey.

Design

The Sidewinder homes on infrared emissions, primarily from an aircraft's engines. Its simplicity — sometimes described as an "electronic cigar" of modular sections — made it cheap and reliable. Distinctive features include rollerons on the rear fins for gyroscopic stability and a passive infrared seeker. Later models gained all-aspect capability (the AIM-9L), infrared counter-countermeasures, and, in the AIM-9X, an imaging infrared seeker, thrust vectoring, and compatibility with helmet-mounted cueing systems and the F-35.

Combat history

The Sidewinder's first combat use came in 1958 during the Second Taiwan Strait Crisis. It was used extensively in the Vietnam War, the Falklands War, the Gulf War, and many other conflicts, accounting for hundreds of aerial victories. Captured and copied examples inspired the Soviet K-13 and other missiles. Over 110,000 Sidewinders have been produced for dozens of countries.
