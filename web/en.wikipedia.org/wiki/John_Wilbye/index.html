John Wilbye (baptised 7 March 1574 – September 1638) was an English composer of madrigals, considered among the finest of the English madrigal school.

Life and works

The son of a tanner, Wilbye was taken into the household of the Kytson family at Hengrave Hall in Suffolk, where he served as resident musician for most of his life, teaching and composing for the family. He published two books of madrigals, in 1598 and 1609, and contributed "The Lady Oriana" to Thomas Morley's The Triumphs of Oriana (1601).

His madrigals — including "Draw on, Sweet Night", "Flora gave me fairest flowers", "Sweet honey-sucking bees" and "Adieu, sweet Amaryllis" — are prized for their sensitive word-setting, rich harmony and expressive intensity, and show the influence of Luca Marenzio filtered through a distinctly English lyricism. Wilbye published comparatively little, but the quality of his two sets has kept them in the repertoire of choirs ever since. On the death of his patron Lady Elizabeth Kytson in 1628 he retired to Colchester, where he died in 1638.
