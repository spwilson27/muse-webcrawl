The Stuart Restoration was the return of Charles II as king of England, Scotland, and Ireland in 1660, ending the Interregnum that began with the execution of Charles I in 1649. It restored the monarchy, the House of Lords, and the established Church of England after eleven years of republican rule under the Commonwealth and Oliver Cromwell's Protectorate.

Background and settlement

After Cromwell's death in 1658 and the collapse of his son Richard's Protectorate, General George Monck marched on London and arranged free elections. A Convention Parliament invited Charles to return, and his Declaration of Breda promised a general pardon (embodied in the Act of Indemnity and Oblivion), liberty of conscience, and settlement of land disputes. Charles entered London on 29 May 1660, his 30th birthday.

Cultural era

The Restoration also names a cultural period: theatres — closed under the Puritans — reopened, now with actresses on stage; Restoration comedy flourished; and the Royal Society was founded in 1660. The era was shadowed by the Great Plague of 1665 and the Great Fire of London in 1666.
