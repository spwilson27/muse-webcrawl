Nachman of Breslov (Hebrew: רבי נחמן מברסלב), also known as Rebbe Nachman (1772–1810), was the founder of the Breslov Hasidic movement. A great-grandson of the Baal Shem Tov, founder of Hasidism, Nachman developed a distinctive path that combined rigorous Torah study and Kabbalistic practice with joy, simple prayer, personal confession to God (hitbodedut), and storytelling.

Life

Born in Medzhybizh, Nachman married young and settled in Osman (Husiatyn). He made a pilgrimage to the Land of Israel in 1798–99, visiting Safed and Tiberias. In 1802 he moved to Bratslav (Breslov), Ukraine, which gave the movement its name. Suffering from tuberculosis, he moved in his final year to Uman, choosing to be buried there among the victims of the 1768 Haidamak massacre; he died there in 1810.

Teachings and works

His magnum opus, Likutei Moharan ("Collected Teachings of Our Teacher, Rabbi Nachman"), was compiled by his chief disciple Nathan of Breslov (Noson Sternhartz), who also recorded his biography and the thirteen mystical Tales (including "The Lost Princess"). Nachman's famous saying — "the whole world is a very narrow bridge, and the main thing is not to fear at all" — was set to music and became widely known. He promised that whoever visited his grave, gave charity and recited the ten psalms of the Tikkun HaKlali would be granted spiritual rectification.

Legacy

Uniquely, Breslov Hasidim never appointed a successor rebbe, remaining "the Hasidim of the dead rebbe" guided by his writings. Tens of thousands of pilgrims visit his tomb in Uman each Rosh Hashanah in fulfilment of his invitation to spend the festival with him.
