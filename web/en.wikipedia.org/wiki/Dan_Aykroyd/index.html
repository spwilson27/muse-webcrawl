Dan Aykroyd, Canadian-American actor and comedian.

Daniel Edward Aykroyd CM OOnt (born July 1, 1952) is a Canadian-American actor, comedian, screenwriter, and producer. Aykroyd was a writer and an original member of the "Not Ready for Prime Time Players" cast on the NBC sketch comedy series Saturday Night Live (1975–1979).

Career

During his tenure on Saturday Night Live, Aykroyd appeared in a recurring series of sketches, including the Coneheads and the Blues Brothers. For his work on the show, he received five Primetime Emmy Award nominations, winning for Outstanding Writing for a Variety Series in 1977. After his departure, he has since appeared in over 80 films.

With John Belushi, Aykroyd created the musical act The Blues Brothers, which led to the hit film The Blues Brothers (1980). His other major film credits include Trading Places (1983), Ghostbusters (1984) — which he co-wrote with Harold Ramis and in which he played Dr. Raymond Stantz — Spies Like Us (1985), Dragnet (1987), The Great Outdoors (1988), Coneheads (1993), and My Girl (1991). He was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for his role as Boolie Werthan in Driving Miss Daisy (1989).

Business ventures

Aykroyd co-founded the House of Blues chain of music venues and the Crystal Head Vodka brand. He maintains an interest in the paranormal, which informed his creation of Ghostbusters with Ramis.

Personal life

Aykroyd was born in Ottawa, Ontario, and grew up in the capital region. He studied criminology and sociology at Carleton University but dropped out before completing his degree. He has been married to actress Donna Dixon since 1983.
