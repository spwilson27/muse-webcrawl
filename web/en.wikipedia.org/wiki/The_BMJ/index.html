The BMJ is a weekly peer-reviewed medical trade journal, published by the BMJ Group, which is wholly owned by the British Medical Association (BMA). Founded in 1840 as the Provincial Medical and Surgical Journal, it became the British Medical Journal in 1857 and was renamed The BMJ in 2014. It is one of the world's oldest general medical journals and one of the most influential, alongside The Lancet, JAMA and The New England Journal of Medicine.

Content and policy

The journal publishes original research, clinical reviews, education articles, news, and editorials on health policy and medical politics. It has been a prominent campaigner on issues such as tobacco control, climate change and health, and transparency in research. In 1995 it launched one of the first major journal websites, bmj.com, and it has pioneered policies including mandatory sharing of clinical trial data and an influential patient involvement programme requiring authors to state how patients contributed to their work.

Notable episodes

The BMJ published the 1998 Andrew Wakefield paper linking the MMR vaccine to autism — work it later investigated and in 2011 described as an "elaborate fraud", helping to document the paper's retraction. Its annual Christmas issue, featuring light-hearted but peer-reviewed research, has become a well-known tradition.
