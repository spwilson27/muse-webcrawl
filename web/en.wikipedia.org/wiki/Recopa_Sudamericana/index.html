Recopa Sudamericana

The Recopa Sudamericana, known also as Recopa, is an annual international club football competition organized by CONMEBOL. It is contested between the champions of the two premier South American club competitions: the Copa Libertadores and the Copa Sudamericana. It is analogous to a super cup in other confederations.

History

A precursor competition, the Supercopa Masters, and an earlier Recopa Sudamericana were held in 1989, won by Nacional of Uruguay. The competition was revived in its modern form in 2003, when Olimpia of Paraguay won the title. Since then it has generally been played over two legs, home and away, early in the calendar year following the finals of the parent tournaments.

Format

The Recopa is contested over two legs, with the Copa Libertadores champion typically hosting the second leg. If the aggregate score is level, the tie may be decided by extra time and penalties. The competition grants the winner the informal claim of being the champion of champions of South American club football.

Records

Argentine club Boca Juniors is among the most successful teams in the competition's history, having won the title multiple times. Brazilian and Argentine clubs have dominated the honors list.
