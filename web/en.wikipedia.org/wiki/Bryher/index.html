Bryher

Bryher was the pen name of Annie Winifred Ellerman (2 September 1894 – 28 January 1983), a British novelist, poet, memoirist, and patron of modernist literature and film. The heiress to a shipping fortune, she used her wealth to finance writers, journals, and filmmakers.

Life and work

Bryher took her name from one of the Isles of Scilly, which she loved. She was the lifelong companion of the poet H.D. (Hilda Doolittle), whom she met in 1918; the two travelled widely and supported each other's work. Bryher wrote historical novels such as The Player's Boy, poetry, and memoirs including The Heart to Artemis, an account of her early life.

Film and patronage

With H.D. and Kenneth Macpherson she founded the film journal Close Up (1927–1933), a pioneering English-language magazine of film theory, and the POOL Group, which made the avant-garde film Borderline (1930). During the 1930s she helped Jewish refugees escape Nazi Germany, and she funded psychoanalysis and publishing ventures for many artists.
