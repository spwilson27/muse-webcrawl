The Anti-Revolutionary Party (Dutch: Anti-Revolutionaire Partij, ARP) was a Protestant Christian-democratic political party in the Netherlands, existing from 1879 to 1980. It is often considered the first modern, organised political party in Dutch history.

History

The party grew from the movement led by Guillaume Groen van Prinsterer and was organised as a mass party by Abraham Kuyper, who became prime minister in 1901. Its ideology opposed the principles of the French Revolution in favour of Reformed Christian principles, championing "sphere sovereignty" and pillarisation of Dutch society.

Merger

In 1980 the ARP merged with the Catholic People's Party and the Christian Historical Union to form the Christian Democratic Appeal (CDA).
