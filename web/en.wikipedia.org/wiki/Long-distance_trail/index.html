Long-distance trail

[Image: A waymarked long-distance trail crossing upland country]

Overview

A long-distance trail (also a long-distance footpath, trekking trail or grande randonnée) is a long recreational trail mainly through rural areas used for hiking, backpacking, cycling or horse riding. There is no universal minimum length; most definitions start around 50 km, while the most famous — the Appalachian Trail, the Camino de Santiago routes, the E-paths of Europe, the Te Araroa of New Zealand — run to thousands of kilometres.

Traditions

Britain's National Trails (beginning with the Pennine Way, 1965), France's GR network with its red-and-white waymarks, Spain's pilgrim Caminos, Germany's Rennsteig and Japan's Tokai Nature Trail illustrate distinct national styles: waymarked public rights of way, refuge-to-refuge hut systems, and pilgrimage routes. Thru-hiking (walking a trail end-to-end in one journey) is a distinct subculture with its own vocabulary and support economy of trail angels, hostels and resupply points.
