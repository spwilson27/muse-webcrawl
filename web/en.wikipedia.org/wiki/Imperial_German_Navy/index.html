The Imperial German Navy (German: Kaiserliche Marine) was the navy of the German Empire, created from the North German Confederation's navy in 1871 and existing until the end of the First World War in 1918. Under Kaiser Wilhelm II and Grand Admiral Alfred von Tirpitz, it expanded rapidly from a coastal defence force into one of the world's largest battle fleets, triggering an Anglo-German naval arms race that heightened tensions before 1914.

Build-up

The Navy Laws of 1898 and 1900, championed by Tirpitz, authorised a powerful fleet of battleships intended, under his "risk theory", to be strong enough that Britain would not dare attack it. Germany's principal force, the High Seas Fleet, was based at Wilhelmshaven and Kiel. Germany also pioneered submarine warfare with its U-boats.

First World War

During the First World War the High Seas Fleet fought the British Grand Fleet at the Battle of Jutland (1916), the largest naval battle of the war, but spent much of the conflict in port while U-boats waged an unrestricted campaign against Allied shipping. In October–November 1918, sailors' mutinies at Kiel, sparked by a planned final fleet sortie, helped trigger the German Revolution and the fall of the monarchy. Most of the fleet was interned at Scapa Flow and scuttled by its crews in June 1919.
