Mycobacterium

Domain: Bacteria
Phylum: Actinomycetota
Class: Actinomycetes
Order: Mycobacteriales
Family: Mycobacteriaceae
Genus: Mycobacterium

Mycobacterium is a genus of over 190 species in the phylum Actinomycetota, assigned its own family, Mycobacteriaceae. This genus includes pathogens known to cause serious diseases in mammals, including tuberculosis (Mycobacterium tuberculosis) and leprosy (Mycobacterium leprae) in humans.

Characteristics

Mycobacteria are aerobic, non-motile bacteria that are characteristically acid-fast, owing to the waxy mycolic acids in their cell walls, which resist decolorisation by acid alcohol during staining. They are usually slow-growing, sometimes taking weeks to form colonies in culture. Their cell walls make them resistant to many disinfectants and antibiotics, complicating treatment.

Pathogenic species

The M. tuberculosis complex causes tuberculosis, one of the deadliest infectious diseases worldwide. M. leprae and M. lepromatosis cause leprosy. So-called nontuberculous mycobacteria, such as the M. avium complex, can cause opportunistic infections, particularly in immunocompromised people.

Classification

Traditionally, mycobacteria have been divided into rapidly growing and slowly growing species. Modern phylogenetic analyses have proposed splitting the genus, but the name Mycobacterium remains widely used in clinical practice for the medically important species.
