Val Lewton

Val Lewton (born Vladimir Kalmanovich Leventon; May 7, 1904 – March 14, 1951) was a Ukrainian-born American film producer, novelist and screenwriter best known for producing a series of low-budget psychological horror films for RKO Pictures in the 1940s, including Cat People (1942) and I Walked with a Zombie (1943).

Early life

Lewton was born in Yalta and emigrated with his family to the United States as a child. He studied at Columbia University and worked as a journalist and pulp-fiction writer, publishing novels under several pen names in the 1930s.

Career

He worked for MGM, notably as an assistant to David O. Selznick, contributing to films such as A Tale of Two Cities (1935). In 1942 RKO hired him to head a unit making cheap horror films intended to compete with Universal Pictures. Given small budgets, evocative titles and little interference provided he stayed on budget, Lewton favoured suggestion, shadow and ambiguity over explicit monsters.

The RKO films

Working with directors Jacques Tourneur, Mark Robson and Robert Wise, Lewton produced Cat People, I Walked with a Zombie, The Leopard Man, The Seventh Victim, The Ghost Ship, The Curse of the Cat People, The Body Snatcher, Isle of the Dead and Bedlam. Many are now regarded as classics of psychological horror.

Later work and legacy

After leaving RKO he produced films for Paramount, Universal and MGM, including Please Believe Me and My Own True Love. He died of a heart attack in 1951. Critics and filmmakers including Martin Scorsese have cited his atmospheric approach as a major influence.
