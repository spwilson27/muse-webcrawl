David D. Burns (born September 19, 1942) is an American psychiatrist and adjunct professor emeritus in the Department of Psychiatry and Behavioral Sciences at the Stanford University School of Medicine. He is best known for his popular book Feeling Good: The New Mood Therapy (1980), which introduced cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) techniques to a mass audience and has sold several million copies. Burns was an early student and popularizer of the work of Aaron T. Beck, the founder of cognitive therapy.

Education and career

Burns received his undergraduate degree from Amherst College and his medical degree from Stanford University School of Medicine, and completed his psychiatric residency at the University of Pennsylvania, where he worked closely with Beck at the Center for Cognitive Therapy. He subsequently served on the faculties of the University of Pennsylvania and Harvard Medical School before returning to Stanford.

Feeling Good and bibliotherapy

Feeling Good presents the principles of cognitive therapy for depression in self-help form, centred on identifying and correcting distorted thinking patterns — such as all-or-nothing thinking, overgeneralization, mental filtering, and catastrophizing — which Burns presents as the "ten forms of twisted thinking". Research on bibliotherapy, including studies of patients assigned to read Feeling Good, has found measurable benefits for mild to moderate depression, and the book is among the most frequently recommended self-help titles by mental-health professionals in the United States. Burns followed it with The Feeling Good Handbook (1989), When Panic Attacks (2006), and Feeling Great (2020), the last of which presents his TEAM-CBT approach.

TEAM-CBT

Burns developed TEAM-CBT, an elaboration of cognitive therapy organized around four components: Testing (routine outcome and alliance measurement), Empathy, Agenda setting (including the identification and reduction of resistance), and Methods, a catalogue of dozens of techniques drawn from cognitive therapy and other schools. He has promoted the approach through workshops, the Feeling Good Institute, and a weekly podcast.
