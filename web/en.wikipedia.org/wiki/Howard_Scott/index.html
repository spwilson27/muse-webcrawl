Howard Scott

Howard Scott (April 1, 1890 – January 1, 1970) was an American engineer and the founder of the Technocracy movement, which proposed replacing politicians and businesspeople with scientists and engineers who would manage the economy on the basis of energy accounting rather than money.

Early life and the Technical Alliance

Scott worked in various engineering and construction jobs and cultivated an image as a technically trained reformer, though later journalists questioned his credentials. In 1919 he organised the Technical Alliance in New York, a group of scientists, engineers and architects — at one point including the sociologist Thorstein Veblen — that undertook an "energy survey" of North America to document waste in the industrial system.

Technocracy Incorporated

During the Great Depression Scott's ideas briefly became a national sensation. In 1932 he delivered a famous, poorly received speech at New York's Hotel Pierre, and press scrutiny soon damaged his reputation. He nevertheless founded Technocracy Incorporated in 1933 and led it for the rest of his life, promoting a continental administrative scheme called the "Technate", in which citizens would receive energy certificates instead of wages. The movement attracted a devoted membership — known for grey uniforms and salmon-coloured cars — but never achieved political power.

Death and legacy

Scott died in 1970, still head of the organisation. Technocracy's notions of expert rule and energy-based accounting have continued to attract interest as an early, idiosyncratic response to industrial crisis.
