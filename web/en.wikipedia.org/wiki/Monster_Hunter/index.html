Monster Hunter is a Japanese media franchise centred on a series of fantasy-themed action role-playing video games developed and published by Capcom. The first game in the series debuted in 2004 for the PlayStation 2. Players take the role of a Hunter, slaying or trapping large monsters across various landscapes as part of quests given to them by locals, protecting settlements from the creatures, and studying them. As part of its core gameplay loop, players use loot gained from slaying monsters, gathering resources, and quest rewards to craft improved weapons, armour, and other items that allow them to face more powerful monsters.

Gameplay

The core games are mission-based: hunters accept quests from a hub village or city, prepare equipment and items, and travel to open areas where they track and fight a target monster, ideally studying its behaviour and targeting weak points. Combat is deliberate and animation-driven, with a roster of weapon classes — including Great Sword, Long Sword, Bow, Hammer, and the transforming Charge Blade — each with distinct movesets. Games emphasise cooperative multiplayer for up to four players, as well as feline companions called Palicoes (and, in later games, rideable Palamutes).

History and reception

The series first found mass popularity in Japan on the PlayStation Portable, where Monster Hunter Freedom Unite (2008) became a cultural phenomenon, and later on Nintendo 3DS. Monster Hunter: World (2018), the first entry built for home consoles and PC worldwide, became Capcom's best-selling game of all time, with more than 20 million copies sold; Monster Hunter Rise (2021) followed on Nintendo Switch and other platforms, and Monster Hunter Wilds launched in 2025. The franchise has sold more than 100 million units in total, making it Capcom's second-best-selling series after Resident Evil. Adaptations include anime, manga, a trading card game, and a 2020 live-action film directed by Paul W. S. Anderson.
