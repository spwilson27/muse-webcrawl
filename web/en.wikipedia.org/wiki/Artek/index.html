Artek

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Stool 60, designed by Alvar Aalto in 1933

Artek is a Finnish furniture company founded in Helsinki in 1935 by architect Alvar Aalto and his wife Aino Aalto, visual arts promoter Maire Gullichsen, and art historian Nils-Gustav Hahl. The company's name is a combination of the words "art" and "technology", reflecting its founding manifesto: "to sell furniture and to promote a modern culture of habitation by exhibitions and other educational means."

History

Artek was founded to market and produce the furniture, lighting, and textiles designed by the Aaltos, many of which used the bent birch "L-leg" construction patented by Alvar Aalto in 1933. The company organized exhibitions and helped shape Finnish modernism, and it opened a flagship store in central Helsinki.

In 2013, Artek was acquired by the Swiss furniture company Vitra. Artek continues to produce classic Aalto designs alongside work by contemporary designers.
