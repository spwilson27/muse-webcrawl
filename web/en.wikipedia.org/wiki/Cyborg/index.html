Cyborg

A cyborg (short for "cybernetic organism") is a being with both organic and biomechatronic body parts. The term was coined in 1960 by Manfred Clynes and Nathan S. Kline in an article about adapting humans for space travel.

Concept

Unlike a robot or android, a cyborg begins as a living organism enhanced by integrated artificial components that extend its abilities or restore lost function. Medical technologies such as pacemakers, cochlear implants, and prosthetic limbs are often described as cyborg technologies, and people who use them have sometimes been called cyborgs.

In culture and theory

Cyborgs are a staple of science fiction, from Darth Vader to the Borg of Star Trek. Donna Haraway's 1985 essay "A Cyborg Manifesto" used the cyborg as a metaphor for transcending dualisms in feminist theory. Contemporary artists and "biohackers" such as Neil Harbisson have explored cyborg identity in real life.
