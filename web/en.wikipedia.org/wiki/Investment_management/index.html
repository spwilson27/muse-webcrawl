Investment management

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Portfolio managers monitoring investments

Investment management (also called asset management) is the professional management of securities (shares, bonds and other financial assets) and other assets (such as real estate) on behalf of investors, in order to meet specified investment goals. Investors may be institutions (insurance companies, pension funds, corporations, charities) or private individuals, served directly or through pooled vehicles such as mutual funds, exchange-traded funds and hedge funds.

Activities

Investment managers conduct asset allocation, portfolio strategy, security selection, trade execution and ongoing monitoring and reporting. The industry distinguishes active management, which seeks to outperform a benchmark index, from passive management, which tracks an index at low cost.

Industry

The largest investment managers worldwide include BlackRock, Vanguard and Fidelity Investments, each managing trillions of US dollars in assets. Fees are typically charged as a percentage of assets under management. The industry is heavily regulated in most jurisdictions, with fiduciary duties owed to clients.
