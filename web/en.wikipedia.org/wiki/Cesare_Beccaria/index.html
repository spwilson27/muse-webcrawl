Cesare Beccaria

Cesare Bonesana di Beccaria, Marquis of Gualdrasco and Villareggio (15 March 1738 – 28 November 1794) was an Italian criminologist, jurist, philosopher, economist and politician, widely considered one of the greatest thinkers of the Age of Enlightenment.

Cesare Beccaria
Born: 15 March 1738, Milan
Died: 28 November 1794, Milan
Known for: On Crimes and Punishments (1764)
Field: Criminology, law, economics

On Crimes and Punishments

His treatise Dei delitti e delle pene (1764), published anonymously at age 26, condemned torture and the death penalty and argued that punishment should be proportionate, swift, certain and aimed at deterrence rather than retribution. It profoundly influenced criminal-law reform across Europe and the founders of the United States, and is foundational to classical criminology and utilitarian penal theory associated with Jeremy Bentham.

Later life

Beccaria also wrote on economics, held public office in Milan, and was grandfather of novelist Alessandro Manzoni.
