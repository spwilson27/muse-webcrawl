The Coppa Italia ("Italy Cup") is the top domestic cup competition in Italian football. Organised by Lega Serie A, it was first held in 1922 and has been played annually since 1958, after an interrupted early history. Juventus is the most successful club with 15 titles, followed by Roma and Inter Milan with nine each.

History

The first edition in 1922 was won by Vado, a small Ligurian club whose triumph remains one of the great upsets in Italian football. The competition was discontinued after 1926 and revived in 1935–36, running until 1943 before wartime interruption. It resumed permanently in 1958. The winners receive the coccarda, a roundel in the Italian tricolour that the holders may wear on their shirts the following season.

Format

The modern tournament involves 44 clubs from Serie A, Serie B and a small number from Serie C. It is a single-elimination knockout tournament in which top Serie A teams enter in later rounds; ties are one-off matches until two-legged semi-finals, and the final is a single match, traditionally held at the Stadio Olimpico in Rome. The winner qualifies for the UEFA Europa League league phase and for the Supercoppa Italiana.

Notable finals

Memorable finals include Roma's repeated successes in the 1980s, Parma's three victories around the turn of the century, and Napoli's 2020 win over Juventus on penalties behind closed doors during the COVID-19 pandemic. Juventus extended its record by beating Atalanta 1–0 in the 2024 final.
