Greater Sunda Islands

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Greater Sunda Islands are four tropical islands situated within the Indonesian Archipelago, in the Pacific Ocean. The group comprises Borneo, Java, Sulawesi, and Sumatra. Together with the Lesser Sunda Islands to their southeast, they make up the Sunda Islands archipelago.

Most of the Greater Sunda Islands lie within the territory of Indonesia; Borneo is divided among Indonesia, Malaysia, and Brunei, and the island of Timor (in the Lesser Sundas) is divided between Indonesia and East Timor.

The islands

  • Borneo — the third-largest island in the world (about 748,000 km²), divided between Indonesian Kalimantan, Malaysian Sabah and Sarawak, and Brunei.
  • Sumatra — about 473,000 km², lying along the Sunda Strait and the Strait of Malacca.
  • Java — about 139,000 km² but the most populous island in the world, home to more than 150 million people and Indonesia's capital region.
  • Sulawesi — about 180,000 km², recognisable by its distinctive K-shaped outline.

Geology and nature

The islands sit on the Sunda Shelf, part of the continental shelf of Southeast Asia that was exposed during glacial periods, allowing land migrations of Asian fauna. West of the Wallace Line, their rainforests host orangutans, tigers (on Sumatra), rhinoceroses, and elephants; Sulawesi, east of the line's deep-water straits, has a more distinctive fauna of its own.
