The Meuse (French: [møz]; Dutch: Maas) is a major European river rising in France and flowing through Belgium and the Netherlands before draining into the North Sea. Its 925 km course passes the cities of Verdun, Sedan, Charleville-Mézières, Namur, Liège, Maastricht, and Rotterdam's port region. The river gave its name to the Meuse department in France, to the Maas valley art style of the Middle Ages (Mosan art), and — via its Latin name Mosa — is among Europe's oldest continuously used river names.

Course

The Meuse rises on the Langres plateau in Haute-Marne, flows north through Lorraine, cuts a steep, wooded valley through the Ardennes in Belgium, receives the Sambre at Namur and the Ourthe at Liège, then enters the Netherlands, where it merges with distributaries of the Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta before reaching the sea at the Hollands Diep.

History

The river valley was bitterly contested in both world wars: the Battle of Verdun (1916) raged along its banks, German forces crossed the Meuse at Sedan and Dinant in May 1940, and the river formed a key line during the Battle of the Bulge. The Treaty of Maastricht (1992), founding the European Union, was signed in a city on its banks. Catastrophic floods in July 2021 struck the Meuse basin in Belgium and the Netherlands.

Navigation

Canals and locks make the Meuse navigable for barges deep into France, and its lower course forms part of one of Europe's busiest inland waterway corridors serving the port of Rotterdam and the Liège industrial basin.
