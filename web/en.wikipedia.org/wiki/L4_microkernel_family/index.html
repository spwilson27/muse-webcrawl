L4 microkernel family

The L4 microkernel family is a family of second-generation microkernels originating in the work of Jochen Liedtke at GMD in Germany in the 1990s. L4 kernels are designed around fast inter-process communication (IPC) and minimal kernel functionality, with services such as device drivers running in user space.

History and members

Liedtke's original L4 demonstrated that IPC could be made fast enough to make microkernels practical, countering earlier criticisms of systems such as Mach. Members of the family include L4Ka::Pistachio, the seL4 kernel (formally verified for correctness in 2009), Fiasco.OC, and OKL4, which shipped in large numbers of mobile devices. The L4 concepts influenced later commercial embedded operating systems.
