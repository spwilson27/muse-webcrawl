Semper fidelis

Semper fidelis is a Latin phrase that means "always faithful" or "always loyal". In the United States it is best known as the motto of the United States Marine Corps, usually shortened to Semper Fi. The phrase has been used as a motto by many families, military units, schools, and cities since the Middle Ages.

United States Marine Corps

The Marine Corps adopted "Semper Fidelis" as its official motto in 1883, replacing earlier mottos such as "Fortitudine" and "By Sea and by Land". John Philip Sousa composed the march "Semper Fidelis" in 1888, which became the official march of the Marine Corps. "Semper Fi" is used by Marines as a greeting, a sign-off, and an expression of shared identity and commitment to the Corps and fellow Marines.

Other users

The motto has been borne by numerous other organizations. It was the motto of the city of Lwów (now Lviv) in Poland, inscribed on its coat of arms, and is associated with the U.S. Coast Guard's related motto "Semper Paratus" ("always ready"). Several British, French, Swiss, and other European regiments and families have used it since at least the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, including the city of Exeter in England, whose motto it has been since at least 1660.

Variants

Related Latin mottos of loyalty and readiness include semper paratus ("always ready"), semper eadem ("always the same"), and fidelis et gratus. The phrase is also the name of a march by Sousa — see "Semper Fidelis" (march).
