Duxelles

Duxelles is a finely chopped mixture of mushrooms or mushroom stems, onions or shallots, herbs, and black pepper, sautéed in butter and reduced to a paste. When cream is added, it is known in French classical cooking as duxelles à la crème.

History

Duxelles is said to have been created by the 17th-century French chef François Pierre La Varenne (1615–1678) and named after his employer, Nicolas Chalon du Blé, marquis d'Uxelles. Many classical cookbooks list duxelles as the standard stuffing for champignons farcis and as a component of other preparations.

Uses

Duxelles is a key component of beef Wellington, where it coats the beef fillet before wrapping in pastry. It is also used as a stuffing for vegetables, a filling for savoury pastries, and a flavouring base for sauces and soups. Reducing the mixture thoroughly is essential so that excess moisture does not make pastry soggy.
