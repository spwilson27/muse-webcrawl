Personal watercraft

A personal watercraft (PWC) is a recreational watercraft that the rider sits or stands on, rather than inside of, as in a boat. PWC are propelled by an internal pump-jet driven by an engine, steered by redirecting the jet nozzle, and typically carry one to three people. Leading brands include Kawasaki's Jet Ski, Yamaha's WaveRunner, and Bombardier's Sea-Doo — brand names often used generically.

History

The first commercially successful PWC was Kawasaki's stand-up Jet Ski, released in 1973, developed from prototypes by Clayton Jacobson II. Sit-down models introduced in the late 1980s broadened the market to families and touring, and PWC became one of the most popular categories of recreational boat in the 1990s.

Design and operation

The jet pump takes in water and expels it at high pressure; because there is no external propeller, PWC can operate in shallow water but have no steering or braking effect without throttle, a handling quirk addressed in newer models by electronic braking and reverse systems. Modern PWC use four-stroke engines, are 3–4 metres long, and can exceed 100 km/h; electric models have also appeared.

Safety and regulation

Many jurisdictions require operator licensing or minimum ages, life jackets, and safety lanyards (kill switches). Concerns over noise, emissions, wake damage, and collisions with wildlife and swimmers have led to restrictions on some waterways; two-stroke models have largely given way to cleaner four-strokes.

Uses

Beyond recreation, PWC are used by lifeguards and marine patrols for rescue work, often towing rescue sleds, and feature in freestyle and racing competitions.
