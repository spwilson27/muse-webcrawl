The Imperial City (Vietnamese: Hoàng thành; chữ Hán: 皇城) is a fortified palace complex within the Citadel of Huế, the former imperial capital of Vietnam. Built from 1804 under Emperor Gia Long on geomantic principles modelled in part on Beijing's Forbidden City, it was the seat of the Nguyễn dynasty until the abdication of Bảo Đại in 1945.

Layout

The square, moated Imperial City contains within it the still more restricted Forbidden Purple City, once reserved to the emperor and his household. Major structures include the Ngọ Môn (Meridian Gate) crowned by the Five Phoenix Pavilion, the throne hall of Supreme Harmony (Thái Hòa), the Miếu temples honouring Nguyễn ancestors, the pavilions of Hiển Lâm, and the Diên Thọ residence of the queen mothers. A massive flag tower flies the Vietnamese flag above the ramparts.

History

The complex suffered severe damage in 1947 during the First Indochina War and again in the Battle of Huế in the 1968 Tet Offensive, when many buildings were destroyed. Since inscription on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993 as part of the Complex of Huế Monuments, extensive (and sometimes debated) restoration has rebuilt gates, temples and palace halls, and the site is one of Vietnam's principal tourist destinations.
