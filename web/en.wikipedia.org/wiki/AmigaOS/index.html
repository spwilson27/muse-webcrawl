AmigaOS is the proprietary native operating system of the Amiga personal computer, first released in 1985 with the Amiga 1000. It combines a preemptive-multitasking kernel called Exec, a DOS component (AmigaDOS, derived from TRIPOS and written partly in BCPL), and the Workbench graphical user interface driven by the Intuition windowing system.

Features

For its era AmigaOS was unusually advanced: it offered preemptive multitasking, inter-process communication via message ports, shared libraries, device-independent I/O, and a speech synthesiser, all running in as little as 256 KB of RAM. The OS was split between the Kickstart ROM (Exec, Intuition, graphics libraries) and Workbench disks. Versions progressed from 1.x (blue-and-orange Workbench) through 2.0 (1990, a major GUI overhaul) and 3.0/3.1 with the AGA chipset machines.

After Commodore

Commodore's bankruptcy in 1994 scattered the Amiga's ownership, but development continued: Haage & Partner produced AmigaOS 3.5 and 3.9, and Hyperion Entertainment developed the PowerPC-native AmigaOS 4 (from 2006) for machines such as the AmigaOne and Sam series. Successor and compatible systems include MorphOS and the open-source AROS Research Operating System. Protracted legal disputes over AmigaOS rights between Hyperion, Amiga Inc. and Cloanto lasted for decades; Cloanto also maintains the 68k line (3.X), and Hyperion released AmigaOS 3.2 in 2021.

Legacy

AmigaOS retains a dedicated hobbyist community, with new 68k and PowerPC hardware (including FPGA recreations such as the Vampire accelerators) and software still produced decades after the platform's commercial peak.
