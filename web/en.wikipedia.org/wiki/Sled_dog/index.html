Sled dog

A sled dog is a dog trained and used to pull a land vehicle in harness, most commonly a sled over snow. Sled dogs have been used in the Arctic for thousands of years, and were vital for transport in Alaska, northern Canada, Greenland, Siberia and Svalbard until the introduction of snowmobiles and aircraft.

Breeds used include the Siberian Husky, Alaskan Malamute, Canadian Eskimo Dog, Greenland Dog and the purpose-bred Alaskan husky. Today sled dogs are best known from racing events such as the Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race and the Yukon Quest. A team is led by one or more lead dogs, with swing, team and wheel dogs arranged behind.

History

Archaeological evidence suggests dogs were used for traction in Siberia over 9,000 years ago. Sled dogs famously delivered diphtheria antitoxin to Nome, Alaska in the 1925 serum run, an event commemorated by the Iditarod.
