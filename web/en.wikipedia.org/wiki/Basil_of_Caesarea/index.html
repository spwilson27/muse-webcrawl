Basil of Caesarea, also called Saint Basil the Great (c. 330 – 1 January 379), was a Greek bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia and one of the most influential Christian theologians of the fourth century. Together with his brother Gregory of Nyssa and his friend Gregory of Nazianzus he forms the trio of the Cappadocian Fathers.

Life and work

Educated at Caesarea, Constantinople and Athens, Basil embraced monastic life and wrote rules that became the foundation of Eastern Orthodox monasticism. As bishop from 370 he defended Nicene orthodoxy against Arianism, articulated the doctrine of the Holy Spirit in On the Holy Spirit, and gave the classic formula of Trinitarian theology: one ousia (essence) in three hypostases (persons).

Legacy

Basil founded the Basiliad, a complex of hospital, poorhouse and hospice outside Caesarea often regarded as the first hospital. His Hexaemeron homilies on creation, his liturgy, and his address to young men on reading pagan literature remain widely read. He is venerated as a saint in both Eastern and Western Christianity and is a Doctor of the Church.
