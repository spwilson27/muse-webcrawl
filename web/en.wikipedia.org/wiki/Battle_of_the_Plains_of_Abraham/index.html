Battle of the Plains of Abraham

The Battle of the Plains of Abraham, also known as the Battle of Quebec, was a pivotal battle in the Seven Years' War, fought on 13 September 1759 on a plateau just west of the walls of Quebec City in New France. British forces under Major-General James Wolfe defeated the French under the Marquis de Montcalm; both commanders were mortally wounded. The battle decided the fate of Canada.

Background

Wolfe's expedition had besieged Quebec through the summer of 1759 without forcing a decision, and his assault at Montmorency had failed. On the night of 12–13 September, British troops scaled the cliffs at L'Anse-au-Foulon in silence and deployed on the plateau — farmland named for its former owner, Abraham Martin — cutting Montcalm off from his supply line.

The battle

Montcalm, fearing the British would entrench and bring up guns, attacked at once with a mixed force of regulars and militia. The British held their fire until the French were within about 40 yards, then delivered two crushing volleys and charged with the bayonet and Highlanders' broadswords. The French army broke within about fifteen minutes to an hour of fighting. Wolfe died on the field; Montcalm died the following morning in the city.

Aftermath

Quebec surrendered on 18 September. Although the French won a rematch at Sainte-Foy in 1760, British naval supremacy secured the conquest, and the Treaty of Paris (1763) ceded Canada to Britain. The battle remains foundational — and contested — in the historical memory of both English and French Canada.
