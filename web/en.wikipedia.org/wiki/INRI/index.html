INRI

INRI is the abbreviation for the Latin inscription Iēsus Nazarēnus, Rēx Iūdaeōrum ("Jesus the Nazarene, King of the Jews"), which in the New Testament of the Christian Bible was written on a sign (the titulus crucis) placed above Jesus at his crucifixion.

Biblical account

According to the Gospel of John (19:19–20), Pontius Pilate wrote a title and put it on the cross: "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews." The title was written in Hebrew (Aramaic), Latin and Greek, and many of the Jews read it because the place where Jesus was crucified was near the city. The chief priests protested, asking that it be changed to "This man said, I am King of the Jews," but Pilate answered, "What I have written I have written."

The inscription in the four Gospels

All four canonical Gospels mention the inscription, though with slight variations: Matthew gives "This is Jesus, the King of the Jews"; Mark gives "The King of the Jews"; Luke gives "This is the King of the Jews"; and John gives the fullest form, "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews". The Latin initials derive from John's version as rendered in the Vulgate.

Usage in art

The letters INRI appear on the titulus in a vast number of depictions of the Crucifixion in Western Christian art, usually on a small plaque or scroll above the head of Christ on the cross. In Eastern Orthodox tradition the corresponding Greek initials INBI (Iēsous ho Nazōraios ho Basileus tōn Ioudaiōn) are sometimes used, as is the Slavonic IНЦI.
