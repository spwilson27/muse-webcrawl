Ciudad Lineal

Ciudad Lineal ("Linear City") is a district in eastern Madrid, Spain, with a population of over 200,000. Its name derives from the Linear City project of the Spanish urban planner Arturo Soria y Mata, an ambitious 19th-century scheme for a city built along a single transport spine.

The Linear City concept

Soria proposed in 1882 that cities should be organised around a wide central avenue (roughly 500 metres across the developed band) carrying railways, tramways and utilities, with housing and gardens behind it, so that every home would be close to both transport and countryside. His slogan was "ruralise urban life, urbanise rural life". A construction company, the Compañía Madrileña de Urbanización (founded 1894), built a fragment — about 5 km — northeast of Madrid, along today's Calle de Arturo Soria, lined originally with single-family houses and gardens.

Today

The area was absorbed into Madrid in 1949. Most of the original villas have been replaced by apartment blocks, but the district remains comparatively green, and the main avenue preserves Soria's name. Ciudad Lineal is one of Madrid's 21 districts, comprising the neighbourhoods of Ventas, Pueblo Nuevo, Quintana, Concepción, San Pascual, San Juan Bautista, Colina, Atalaya and Costillares. The concept influenced Soviet linear-city planning and continues to be cited by advocates of transit-oriented development; the Saudi "The Line" project revived the term in the 2020s.
