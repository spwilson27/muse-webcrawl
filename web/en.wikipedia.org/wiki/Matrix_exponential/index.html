Matrix exponential

In mathematics, the matrix exponential is a matrix function on square matrices analogous to the ordinary exponential function. For an n × n matrix X, it is defined by the convergent power series

eX = I + X + X²/2! + X³/3! + ⋯

The matrix exponential is a central tool in the theory of Lie groups, where it maps a Lie algebra to its corresponding Lie group, and in the solution of systems of linear differential equations.

Properties

The matrix exponential is always invertible, with inverse e−X. Its determinant satisfies det(eX) = etr(X). If X and Y commute, then eX+Y = eXeY; in general this fails, and the relationship is governed by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula. Exponentials of skew-Hermitian matrices are unitary, and exponentials of Hermitian matrices are positive definite.

Computing the matrix exponential

Methods of computation include diagonalization (or the Jordan normal form), the Padé approximation combined with scaling and squaring — the standard approach in numerical software — and solving the associated differential equation. A well-known survey by Cleve Moler and Charles Van Loan, "Nineteen Dubious Ways to Compute the Exponential of a Matrix" (1978, updated 2003), catalogues the pitfalls of the many candidate algorithms.

Applications

For a system of linear ordinary differential equations ẋ = Ax with initial condition x(0) = x₀, the solution is x(t) = etAx₀. The matrix exponential also appears in the exponential map of Riemannian geometry, in quantum mechanics through unitary evolution operators, and in control theory.
