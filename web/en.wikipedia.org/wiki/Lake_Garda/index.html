Lake Garda

Lake Garda is the largest lake in Italy, with a surface area of about 370 square kilometres, located in northern Italy between the regions of Lombardy, Veneto and Trentino-Alto Adige, at the southern foot of the Alps. It was formed by glaciers at the end of the last ice age.

Garda's mild microclimate supports olive groves, lemon houses (limonaie) and vineyards (Bardolino, Lugana) unusual at this latitude (46°N). Towns such as Sirmione — with its Roman villa ruins and Scaliger castle — Desenzano, Riva del Garda and Malcesine ring the shore; wind sports thrive on the northern end's thermal winds (Ora and Pelèr). The lake was a battleground of the Italian Wars of Independence and a refuge of the fascist Salò Republic (1943-45).
