West Texas Intermediate

West Texas Intermediate (WTI), also known as Texas light sweet, is a grade of crude oil used as a benchmark in oil pricing. It is described as light because of its relatively low density, and sweet because of its low sulfur content.

Properties

WTI has an API gravity of around 39.6 and specific gravity of about 0.827, with sulfur content of roughly 0.24–0.34%. It is refined mainly in the Midwest and Gulf Coast regions of the United States.

Trading

The futures contract for WTI trades on the New York Mercantile Exchange (NYMEX), owned by CME Group, with delivery at Cushing, Oklahoma, a major pipeline and storage hub. Along with Brent Crude and Dubai Crude, WTI is one of the principal global oil price benchmarks.

On 20 April 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic demand collapse, the May 2020 WTI futures contract settled at a negative price (−$37.63 per barrel) for the first time in history, as traders paid to avoid taking physical delivery amid full storage at Cushing.
