Secondary traumatization

Secondary traumatization is a process in which an individual develops trauma-like symptoms through indirect exposure to another person's traumatic experiences—typically by hearing detailed accounts of them. It is studied especially among helping professionals, and among family members and close others of trauma survivors.

Related concepts

The term overlaps with several related constructs. Vicarious traumatization describes enduring changes in a clinician's worldview and beliefs resulting from cumulative empathic engagement with clients' trauma. Secondary traumatic stress refers to PTSD-like symptoms—intrusion, avoidance and arousal—arising from indirect exposure. Compassion fatigue, popularised by Charles Figley, combines secondary traumatic stress with burnout in caregiving roles.

Populations affected

Those at risk include psychotherapists, emergency responders, medical staff, social workers, journalists and interpreters covering traumatic material, and partners and children of people with post-traumatic stress disorder, in whom "secondary" symptoms may mirror those of the survivor.

Prevention

Mitigation strategies include supervision and consultation, balanced caseloads, professional training about trauma exposure, organisational support, and self-care. Evidence for specific interventions is still developing.
