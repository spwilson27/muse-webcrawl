NGC 5128

Designations: NGC 5128, Centaurus A, Caldwell 77

Constellation: Centaurus

Distance: ~10–16 million light-years

Type: Lenticular / peculiar galaxy

NGC 5128, better known as Centaurus A, is a galaxy in the constellation Centaurus, discovered by James Dunlop in 1826. At a distance of roughly 10–16 million light-years, it is one of the closest active galaxies to Earth and the fifth-brightest galaxy in the sky, although it is well placed only for southern-hemisphere observers.

Characteristics

Centaurus A has a distinctive morphology: a bright elliptical-like body bisected by a warped dark dust lane, evidence that it resulted from the merger of an elliptical galaxy with a smaller spiral. Its centre hosts a supermassive black hole of tens of millions of solar masses and an active galactic nucleus that launches relativistic jets visible in radio and X-ray wavelengths, extending over a million light-years. It was one of the first extragalactic radio sources identified, and remains a key target for studying jet physics and galaxy mergers.

Observations

The galaxy has hosted observed supernovae, including SN 1986G. In 2007 the Pierre Auger Observatory reported a correlation between the arrival directions of the highest-energy cosmic rays and nearby active galaxies such as Centaurus A, though subsequent analyses complicated the picture. The galaxy is the dominant member of the Centaurus A subgroup of the M83 group of galaxies.
