Radziwiłł family

Nesvizh Castle in present-day Belarus, a Radziwiłł residence.

The Radziwiłł family (Polish: Radziwiłłowie; Lithuanian: Radvilos; Belarusian: Радзівілы) is a Polish–Lithuanian noble family that rose to prominence in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania in the 15th century and became one of the wealthiest and most powerful magnate families of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.

History

The family traces its origins to the Lithuanian boyar Kristin Astikas and his son Radvila Astikas, from whose name the family name derives. In 1547 Barbara Radziwiłł married Sigismund II Augustus, the last Jagiellonian king, and the family received the title of Prince of the Holy Roman Empire. At their height the Radziwiłłs held vast estates — including Nesvizh, Olyka, Biržai, and Słuck — maintained private armies, and occupied the highest offices of state, including voivodeships and the grand hetmanship of Lithuania.

Notable members

Notable members include Mikołaj "the Black" Radziwiłł, a leading promoter of the Calvinist Reformation; his son Mikołaj Krzysztof "the Orphan" Radziwiłł, known for his pilgrimage to the Holy Land; Janusz Radziwiłł, grand hetman during the Deluge; and Karol Stanisław "Panie Kochanku" Radziwiłł, the wealthiest magnate of the 18th-century Commonwealth.

Legacy

The family's palaces and collections, including the Nesvizh Castle complex (now a UNESCO World Heritage Site), preserve a major part of the cultural heritage of Lithuania, Poland, and Belarus. See also Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and magnate.
