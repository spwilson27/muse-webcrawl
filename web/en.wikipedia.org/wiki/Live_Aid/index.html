Live Aid

Live Aid was a dual-venue benefit concert held simultaneously at Wembley Stadium in London and John F. Kennedy Stadium in Philadelphia on 13 July 1985. Organised by Bob Geldof and Midge Ure to raise funds for the Ethiopian famine, it was watched by an estimated 1.9 billion people in 150 countries—nearly 40% of the world's population at the time.

Background

Geldof and Ure had previously organised the charity supergroup Band Aid, whose single "Do They Know It's Christmas?" (1984) raised millions for famine relief. Live Aid scaled the idea into a sixteen-hour "global jukebox" linked by satellite.

The concert

Performers included Queen, U2, David Bowie, The Who, Elton John, Paul McCartney, Led Zeppelin (reunited), Madonna, and Phil Collins—who famously played at both venues on the same day, flying the Concorde to Philadelphia. Queen's 21-minute Wembley set is frequently cited as one of the greatest live performances in rock history.

Legacy

Live Aid established the template for large-scale charity concerts and was followed twenty years later by Live 8 (2005). The Wembley finale and Queen's set were recreated in the 2018 film Bohemian Rhapsody.
