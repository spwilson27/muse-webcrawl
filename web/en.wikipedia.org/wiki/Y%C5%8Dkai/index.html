Yōkai (Japanese: 妖怪, "strange apparition") are a class of supernatural entities and spirits in Japanese folklore. The word is composed of the kanji for "bewitching; attractive; calamity" and "spectre; apparition; mystery; suspicious". Yōkai range from the malevolent to the mischievous, and occasionally bring good fortune to those who encounter them. They often possess animal features (such as the kappa, a turtle-like creature, and the tengu, often depicted with wings), otherworldly features, or human-like appearances; still others resemble everyday objects, as in the tsukumogami, tools and utensils that come alive after a hundred years.

History

Belief in strange phenomena is documented from Japan's earliest chronicles; the term yōkai itself came into wide use in the Edo period (1603–1868), when printed picture books and woodblock prints spread their images. The artist Toriyama Sekien published influential catalogues of yōkai in the 18th century, sometimes inventing new creatures based on wordplay. The night parade of one hundred demons (hyakki yagyō), a procession of monsters through the streets at night, is a recurring theme in picture scrolls.

Types

Well-known yōkai include the oni, ogre-like demons with horns and clubs; the kappa, a water imp said to lure people into rivers; the tengu, mountain goblins associated with martial arts; shape-shifters such as the kitsune (fox) and tanuki (raccoon dog); and snow women (yuki-onna). The boundary between yōkai and other categories such as yūrei (ghosts of the dead) and kami (deities) is fluid, and scholars such as folklorist Kunio Yanagita have debated their classification.

In modern culture

The manga artist Shigeru Mizuki revived popular interest in yōkai after the Second World War with works such as GeGeGe no Kitarō, depicting them as charming rather than terrifying. Yōkai now pervade Japanese media — anime, video games such as the Yo-kai Watch franchise, and films by Studio Ghibli — and are a major theme of tourism in places such as Sakaiminato, Mizuki's home town.
