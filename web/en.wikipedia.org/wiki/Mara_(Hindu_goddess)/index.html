Mara (Hindu goddess)

Mara, also known as Jurai, is a Hindu goddess of death and disease — particularly smallpox and epidemics — worshipped in parts of Assam and northeastern India. She belongs to the family of village disease goddesses of Hindu folk religion, comparable to Shitala, the smallpox goddess of northern India, and to Mariamman in the south.

Worship

In Assamese folk practice, Mara/Jurai is propitiated during outbreaks of pox and epidemic illness, with offerings intended to cool the goddess's fever and persuade her to withdraw the disease from the village. Devotees associate her arrival with the hot season, and rituals may include songs, offerings of uncooked food, and visits to her shrines by the afflicted seeking her grace. As with Shitala, worship historically intertwined with inoculation and, later, vaccination practices.

Context

Disease goddesses like Mara reflect a pattern in South Asian folk Hinduism in which epidemic illness is personified as a goddess who both sends and removes the affliction, and whose worship provides a ritual framework for communities facing epidemics. She is distinct from the Buddhist demon Māra, the tempter of the Buddha, despite the similar name.
