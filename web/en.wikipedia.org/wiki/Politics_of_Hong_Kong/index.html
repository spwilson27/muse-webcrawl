Politics of Hong Kong


Legislative Council Complex, Tamar

The politics of Hong Kong takes place in a framework of a political system dominated by its constitutional document, the Basic Law of Hong Kong, its own legislature, the Chief Executive as the head of government and of the Special Administrative Region, and a politically constrained multi-party system. Hong Kong has been a special administrative region of China since the handover of sovereignty in 1997, under the principle of "one country, two systems".

Government

The Chief Executive is selected by an Election Committee dominated by pro-Beijing interests and appointed by the Central People's Government of China. The Legislative Council (LegCo) has 90 members: since electoral changes in 2021, 20 are directly elected in geographical constituencies, 30 represent functional constituencies, and 40 are chosen by the Election Committee. The judiciary is independent in form and retains the common law system inherited from British rule.

Political camps and recent history

Politics was long divided between the pro-democracy camp, which sought universal suffrage promised in the Basic Law, and the pro-Beijing camp. Major episodes include the 2014 Umbrella Movement, the 2019–2020 protests sparked by an extradition bill, the imposition of the Hong Kong national security law in 2020, and the 2021 electoral overhaul requiring candidates to be "patriots". Pro-democracy parties have since disbanded or been marginalized, opposition figures imprisoned or exiled, and turnout in subsequent elections has fallen sharply.
