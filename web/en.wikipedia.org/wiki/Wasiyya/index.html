Wasiyya

Waṣiyya (Arabic: وصية) is an Islamic legal term for a will or bequest — a disposition of property to take effect after the testator's death. In Islamic jurisprudence it is a recommended (and in some circumstances obligatory) act, closely regulated by the rules of inheritance.

Rules

Classical jurists generally hold that a bequest may not exceed one third of the deceased's estate after debts and funeral expenses, and that a bequest in favour of an heir is invalid unless the other heirs consent after death — principles derived from the Qur'an (2:180–182) and hadith, including the famous ruling given to Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ. The remaining two thirds (or the whole estate if there is no valid bequest) passes by the fixed shares of farāʾiḍ to the heirs.

Applications

The wasiyya is used to provide for non-heirs — charities, mosques, distant relatives excluded from inheritance, or non-Muslim family members — and a "wasiyya wājiba" (obligatory bequest), recognised in the law of several modern Muslim-majority states, provides for orphaned grandchildren whose parent predeceased the grandparent. The term also appears in a spiritual sense, as in a scholar's or ruler's final counsel and testament.
