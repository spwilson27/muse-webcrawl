Ignatius of Loyola (Basque: Ignazio Loiolakoa; Spanish: Ignacio de Loyola; born Íñigo López de Oñaz y Loyola; c. 1491 – 31 July 1556) was a Spanish Catholic priest and theologian who, with Peter Faber and Francis Xavier, founded the religious order of the Society of Jesus (Jesuits) and became its first Superior General in 1540. He is the author of the Spiritual Exercises, a widely influential work of Christian meditation, and is venerated as a saint in the Catholic Church.

Early life and conversion

Ignatius was born into a Basque noble family at the castle of Loyola in Azpeitia. He pursued a military career and was gravely wounded at the siege of Pamplona in 1521, when a cannonball shattered his legs. During a long convalescence, reading the only books available — a life of Christ and lives of the saints — he underwent a spiritual conversion and resolved to abandon his former life of soldiering and courtly ambition.

Pilgrimage and study

In 1522 Ignatius spent months in prayer at Manresa, experiences that formed the basis of the Spiritual Exercises. He made a pilgrimage to Jerusalem in 1523, and on his return studied at Barcelona, Alcalá, and Salamanca — where his preaching attracted the suspicion of the Inquisition — before moving to Paris in 1528. At the University of Paris he gathered a circle of companions, including Francis Xavier and Peter Faber.

Foundation of the Society of Jesus

On 15 August 1534, Ignatius and six companions took vows of poverty, chastity, and obedience at Montmartre, Paris. Pope Paul III approved their institute in 1540 with the bull Regimini militantis Ecclesiae. Ignatius was elected the first Superior General in 1541 and directed the order from Rome for the rest of his life, composing its Constitutions and sending missionaries to Asia, Africa, and the Americas. The Jesuits became a leading force in the Catholic Counter-Reformation and in education, founding colleges across Europe.

Death and canonization

Ignatius died in Rome on 31 July 1556. He was beatified in 1609 and canonized on 12 March 1622 by Pope Gregory XV, together with Francis Xavier, Teresa of Ávila, Isidore the Farmer, and Philip Neri. His feast day is 31 July. His remains lie in the Church of the Gesù in Rome.
