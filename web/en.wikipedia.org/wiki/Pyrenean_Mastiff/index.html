Pyrenean Mastiff

OriginSpain (Aragon)
HeightMales 77–81 cm; females 72–75 cm
Weight60–90 kg
CoatThick, double, white with coloured patches

The Pyrenean Mastiff (Spanish: Mastín del Pirineo) is a large breed of livestock guardian dog from the Aragonese Pyrenees in northeastern Spain. For centuries the dogs accompanied transhumant flocks of sheep moving between the Pyrenees and the Ebro valley, protecting them from wolves and bears.

Characteristics

It is a very large, powerfully built dog with a massive head, a deep chest, and a thick double coat that is predominantly white with well-defined patches of grey, black, brown, or brindle, and a dark mask. Despite its size it is calm, docile, and patient with its family, though wary of strangers and protective of its flock or territory.

History

The decline of transhumance in the 20th century nearly brought the breed to extinction; fanciers revived it from the 1970s onward, and a breed club was founded in 1977. The breed is recognised by the Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI) in Group 2.
