Syunik Province

Country: Armenia
Capital: Kapan
Area: approx. 4,506 km²
Population: approx. 140,000
Governor (marzpet): head of the provincial administration

Syunik (Armenian: Սյունիք) is the southernmost province (marz) of Armenia. It borders the Vayots Dzor Province to the north, Azerbaijan (including the Nakhchivan exclave) to the west and east, and Iran to the south, along the Aras River and the Meghri pass. Its capital and largest city is Kapan; other towns include Goris, Sisian, Kajaran, and Meghri. With an area of about 4,500 km² it is the second-largest Armenian province, though sparsely populated, with roughly 140,000 inhabitants.

Geography

Syunik is mountainous, dominated by the Zangezur Mountains, including Mount Kaputjugh (3,905 m), the highest peak in the province and of the Lesser Caucasus range in Armenia. The Vorotan and Voghji rivers cut deep gorges through the province. Historically the region — also known as Zangezur — was a stronghold of Armenian princes (the Siunia dynasty), and it retains a dense heritage of medieval churches and fortresses.

Economy and sights

Mining dominates the economy: the Kajaran copper-molybdenum deposit, worked by the Zangezur combine, is among the largest molybdenite deposits in the world, and mining also takes place at Kapan. The province is a strategic transport corridor — the main Armenia–Iran road and the (long-proposed) railway pass through Meghri.

Tourist landmarks include the 9th-century Tatev Monastery, reached since 2010 by the Wings of Tatev, one of the world's longest reversible aerial tramways (5.7 km), the cave dwellings and stone pyramids of Old Khndzoresk, the Bgheno-Noravank and Vorotnavank monasteries, Ukhtasar's petroglyphs, and the Shaki waterfall. The province's position made it central to post-2020 debates over a transport corridor across southern Armenia linking Azerbaijan to Nakhchivan.
