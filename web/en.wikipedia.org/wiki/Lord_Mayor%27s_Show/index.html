Lord Mayor's Show

LocationCity of London
First held13th century (modern form 1757)
DateSecond Saturday in November

The Lord Mayor's Show is one of the best-known annual events in London and one of the longest-established, dating back to the 13th century. It is held each November to mark the inauguration of the new Lord Mayor of the City of London, in which the Lord Mayor travels in procession to swear allegiance to the Crown.

History

When King John granted the City a charter in 1215 allowing it to elect its own mayor, he stipulated that the new mayor must present himself to the monarch for approval. For centuries the journey was made by barge on the River Thames; the modern land parade dates from 1757, when the gilded State Coach — still used today — was commissioned.

The procession

The modern show is a parade of some three miles involving thousands of participants: livery companies, military bands, charities, businesses and community groups, together with the traditional figures of Gog and Magog, giant wicker effigies carried since the reign of Henry V. The Lord Mayor rides in the State Coach to the Royal Courts of Justice, returning to Mansion House for a banquet.
