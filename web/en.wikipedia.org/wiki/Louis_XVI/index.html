Louis XVI (Louis-Auguste; French: [lwi sɛːz]; 23 August 1754 – 21 January 1793) was the last king of France before the fall of the monarchy during the French Revolution. He was executed by guillotine. The son of Louis, Dauphin of France, and grandson of Louis XV, he ascended the throne in 1774 and inherited a kingdom in deep financial crisis.

Early reign and reform attempts

Louis appointed a series of reforming ministers — Turgot, Necker, and Calonne — who attempted to overhaul taxation and reduce the royal debt, swollen by French support for the American Revolution. The resistance of the privileged orders and the parlements defeated successive reform programmes. Facing bankruptcy, Louis summoned the Estates-General in 1789 for the first time since 1614.

Revolution

The Estates-General transformed itself into the National Assembly; the storming of the Bastille (14 July 1789) and the October Days, when a crowd marched on Versailles and forced the royal family to Paris, destroyed the king's remaining freedom of action. Louis accepted the Constitution of 1791, becoming a constitutional monarch, but his attempted escape — the Flight to Varennes in June 1791 — convinced many that he was conspiring with foreign powers against the Revolution. War with Austria and Prussia in 1792 radicalised politics; the monarchy was abolished after the insurrection of 10 August 1792.

Trial and execution

Tried by the National Convention under the name "Citizen Louis Capet", Louis was found guilty of treason and sentenced to death by a narrow majority. He was guillotined on 21 January 1793 in the Place de la Révolution. His wife, Marie Antoinette, was executed nine months later. Their son, recognised by royalists as Louis XVII, died in prison in 1795. Louis XVI was canonised by neither church nor state, but was widely regarded by royalists as a martyr; the Bourbon monarchy was restored in 1814 under his brother, Louis XVIII.

Personality

Intelligent, well-read, and personally devout, Louis was an accomplished locksmith and keen hunter but indecisive in crisis and poorly served by court factions. Historians continue to debate whether any king could have preserved the monarchy amid the fiscal and political collapse of 1789.
