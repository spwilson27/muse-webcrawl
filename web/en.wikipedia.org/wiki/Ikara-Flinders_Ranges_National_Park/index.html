Ikara-Flinders Ranges National Park

Ikara-Flinders Ranges National Park
Location: South Australia, Australia
Nearest town: Hawker
Area: about 950 km²
Established: 1945 (as Flinders Ranges National Park)

Ikara-Flinders Ranges National Park is a protected area in the Flinders Ranges of South Australia, roughly 430 kilometres north of Adelaide. The park's centrepiece is Wilpena Pound (Ikara), a large natural amphitheatre of mountains that holds deep significance for the Adnyamathanha people, its traditional owners.

Geography

The park covers rugged quartzite and sandstone ranges, gorges such as Brachina and Bunyeroo, and creek beds lined with river red gums. Wildlife includes yellow-footed rock wallabies, emus, wedge-tailed eagles and numerous reptiles.

History

The area was first protected in 1945. In 2016 the park was renamed to include the Adnyamathanha word Ikara, meaning "meeting place", recognising Wilpena Pound's cultural importance.

Tourism

Walking trails, scenic drives and the Wilpena Pound Resort make the park one of South Australia's best-known inland destinations.
