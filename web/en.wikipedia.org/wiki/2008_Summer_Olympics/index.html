The 2008 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXIX Olympiad and branded as Beijing 2008, were held from 8 to 24 August 2008 in Beijing, China. The Games were China's first Olympics and a coming-out showcase for the country, with record spending — around US$44 billion — on venues such as the "Bird's Nest" National Stadium and the "Water Cube" aquatics center.

Highlights

American swimmer Michael Phelps won gold in all eight of his events, breaking Mark Spitz's record of seven golds at a single Games and becoming the most decorated Olympian of all time. Jamaican sprinter Usain Bolt shattered the world records in the 100 m and 200 m and the 4×100 m relay in electrifying fashion. Host China topped the gold-medal table for the first time with 48 golds, while the United States led the overall medal count. A record 87 nations won medals.

Controversies

The run-up to the Games brought international protests over China's human-rights record and Tibet, disruptions along the torch relay, and concern about Beijing's air pollution, which prompted factory shutdowns and traffic restrictions. Later investigations led to numerous doping disqualifications from retested samples, and questions persisted about the age of some Chinese gymnasts.

Legacy

Beijing later became the first city to host both the Summer and Winter Olympics, staging the Winter Games in 2022. The 2008 Games are widely regarded as a landmark in China's emergence as a global power and in the scale of Olympic spectacle.
