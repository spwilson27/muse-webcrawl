Mohammad Hatta

Born12 August 1902, Bukittinggi, Dutch East Indies
Died14 March 1980 (aged 77), Jakarta, Indonesia
Office1st Vice President of Indonesia (1945–1956); 3rd Prime Minister of Indonesia (1948–1950)
Known forCo-proclaimer of Indonesian independence with Sukarno; "Father of Indonesian Cooperatives"

Mohammad Hatta (12 August 1902 – 14 March 1980) was an Indonesian statesman, nationalist and economist who served as the first vice president of Indonesia. Together with Sukarno, he proclaimed Indonesian independence on 17 August 1945, and the two — the Dwitunggal, or duumvirate — are regarded as the founding fathers of the republic. Hatta also served as prime minister (1948–1950) and was a leading theorist of Indonesian cooperative economics, earning the title "Father of Indonesian Cooperatives" (Bapak Koperasi Indonesia).

Early life and nationalism

Hatta was born in Bukittinggi, West Sumatra, into a Minangkabau family with a religious background. A brilliant student, he went to the Netherlands in 1921 to study at the Rotterdam School of Commerce, remaining for a decade. There he chaired the Indonesian students' association Perhimpoenan Indonesia, was tried by a Dutch court in 1928 for anti-colonial agitation (and acquitted, delivering a celebrated defence speech, "Indonesia Free"), and became a leading voice of the non-cooperative independence movement. His writings on economics and nationalism, including support for cooperative enterprise, shaped the movement's programme.

Road to independence

Returning home in 1932, Hatta led the "new PNI" (Partai Nasional Indonesia Pendidikan). The colonial government exiled him to Boven-Digoel in New Guinea (1935) and then to Banda Neira (1936–1942) for his activism. Freed during the Japanese occupation, he worked with Sukarno in negotiating with the Japanese and preparing for independence. On 17 August 1945, two days after Japan's surrender, Sukarno read the Proclamation of Indonesian Independence, which both men signed; Hatta became vice president the next day.

Vice presidency and later life

During the Indonesian National Revolution Hatta was a key diplomat and administrator; he issued Vice-Presidential Edict No. X (October 1945) enabling political parties, and as prime minister and defence minister led the emergency government after his capture and release during the second Dutch offensive. He headed the Indonesian delegation at the Round Table Conference in The Hague (1949) that secured Dutch recognition of sovereignty. Frustrated by Sukarno's turn toward Guided Democracy and the marginalisation of the vice presidency, Hatta resigned on 1 December 1956. In retirement he wrote his memoirs, criticised corruption and authoritarianism, and remained a respected elder statesman until his death in Jakarta in 1980. He was declared a National Hero of Indonesia, and in 2012 he and Sukarno were jointly named Proclaimer Heroes.
