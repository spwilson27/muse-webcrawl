Maid café

A maid café (Japanese: メイド喫茶 or メイドカフェ) is a type of cosplay restaurant originating in Japan in which servers dress in maid costumes and treat customers as masters and mistresses of a private household rather than as café patrons. The first permanent maid café, Cure Maid Café, opened in Akihabara, Tokyo, in 2001. Maid cafés have since become a fixture of Japanese otaku culture and have spread to cities in Asia, North America, and Europe, often appearing at anime conventions and as permanent establishments.


Waitresses in maid costume greet customers at an Akihabara maid café.

Costume and atmosphere

Servers wear a stylised French maid outfit, typically a knee-length black or pastel dress with a white apron, petticoat, headdress, and accessories such as ribbons, gloves, and stockings. Men's cafés use butler costumes in the equivalent butler cafés. Décor tends toward the cute (kawaii) with pastel interiors. The customer experience is performative: guests are welcomed home with greetings such as "Welcome home, Master" (okaerinasai, goshujin-sama), and food may be decorated at the table with ketchup drawings accompanied by playful "spells" intended to make the meal delicious.

Services

Beyond food and drink — omurice, parfaits, and tea are staples — maid cafés sell interactions such as photographs with a maid, games, live song and dance performances, and seasonal events. Customers commonly pay a seating charge in addition to menu prices. Rules typically prohibit touching the staff, asking for personal contact details, and photographing staff except through the café's paid photo services.

History and spread

Maid costumes were already popular in anime, manga, and video games of the 1990s, and temporary maid cafés appeared at events before Cure Maid Café established the format permanently in Akihabara in 2001. Large chains such as @Home Café (formerly @Ho~mu Café) expanded the concept, and television coverage in the mid-2000s brought mainstream attention. Maid cafés have since opened in Taiwan, China, South Korea, Thailand, Canada, the United States, and elsewhere, frequently as part of anime and Japanese-culture scenes.

Variants

Related concepts include butler cafés aimed at female customers, tsundere cafés, cat-girl and other character cafés, and school- or shrine-maiden-themed establishments, collectively known as cosplay or concept cafés.
