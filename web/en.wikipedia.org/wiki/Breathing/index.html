Breathing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Breathing (ventilation) is the mechanical process of moving air into and out of the lungs so that gases can be exchanged: oxygen taken in for cellular respiration, carbon dioxide expelled. It is driven chiefly by the diaphragm, the dome-shaped muscle beneath the lungs, assisted by the intercostal muscles between the ribs.

Mechanics

On inhalation the diaphragm contracts and flattens, expanding the chest cavity; pressure falls and air flows in. Exhalation at rest is passive — the diaphragm relaxes and elastic recoil pushes air out — while exercise recruits abdominal and accessory muscles. Breathing is controlled automatically by centres in the brainstem that sense blood CO₂ (via pH), adjusting rate and depth to hold blood chemistry in homeostasis; adults at rest breathe about 12–20 times a minute.

Gas exchange itself occurs across the alveoli. Disorders of breathing range from asthma and pneumonia to sleep apnoea, and failure of breathing is treated with mechanical ventilation — the technology that filled intensive-care units during the COVID-19 pandemic.
