Sha Tin Racecourse

Sha Tin Racecourse is one of the two racecourses for horse racing in Hong Kong, located in Sha Tin in the New Territories. It is managed by the Hong Kong Jockey Club and, together with the older Happy Valley Racecourse, forms the centre of one of the world's most successful racing jurisdictions.

History

The course was built under the chairmanship of Sir Michael Clinton and opened on 7 October 1978, with some 35,000 racegoers in attendance. It was constructed largely on reclaimed land on Tide Cove, overlooked by the Lion Rock hills. When it opened, Sha Tin gave the Jockey Club a modern venue capable of hosting international-quality racing and much larger crowds than the cramped Happy Valley track.

Facilities

The grandstand can accommodate around 85,000 spectators. The course has a turf track of about 1,900 metres in circumference and an inner all-weather (dirt) track of about 1,555 metres. The venue includes extensive stabling, a racing laboratory, an equine hospital and a racecourse swimming pool for horses. Sha Tin was also the site of the equestrian events of the 2008 Beijing Olympic Games, for which a temporary venue was built adjacent to the racecourse.

Major races

Sha Tin hosts Hong Kong's flagship international meeting, the Hong Kong International Races each December, comprising the Hong Kong Cup, Hong Kong Mile, Hong Kong Sprint and Hong Kong Vase. It also stages the Queen Elizabeth II Cup, the Champions Mile, the Chairman's Sprint Prize and the Hong Kong Derby, the most prestigious local race for four-year-olds.
