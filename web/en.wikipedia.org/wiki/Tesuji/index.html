Tesuji

Tesuji (手筋) is a Japanese term used in the board game of Go for a clever, tactically skillful move — a "skillful finesse of stones." A tesuji is typically a local tactical play that achieves a specific purpose, such as capturing stones, connecting one's own stones, cutting the opponent's stones, making life for a group, or killing an opponent's group. The word derives from te (hand/move) and suji (line or method).

Types of tesuji

There are many named kinds of tesuji. Among the best known are:

  • Snapback — a move that allows one's stones to be captured so that the capturing stones can themselves be recaptured.
  • Ladder (shicho) — a sequence of ataris driving a group diagonally toward the edge of the board.
  • Net (geta) — a loose encirclement that prevents escape even without tight atari.
  • Under the stones (ishinoshita) — a sacrifice play exploiting a shortage of liberties.
  • Cross-cut tesuji and placement tesuji (oki) — moves played on key points inside an opponent's shape.

Usage

The concept of tesuji contrasts with honte, the "proper move," and with crude or heavy moves lacking tactical refinement. Collections of tesuji problems, known as tsumego when they concern life and death, are a standard part of Go study. The term is also used more loosely for skillful tactical sequences in related games such as shogi.
