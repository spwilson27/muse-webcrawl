West Virginia v. EPA, 597 U.S. 697 (2022), is a landmark decision of the United States Supreme Court concerning the Environmental Protection Agency's authority to regulate greenhouse gas emissions from power plants under the Clean Air Act. The Court held that the EPA lacked authority to adopt the "generation shifting" approach of the 2015 Clean Power Plan, and in doing so gave canonical form to the major questions doctrine.

Background

The Obama-era Clean Power Plan set state-level emissions targets that could be met partly by shifting electricity generation from coal to gas and renewables, rather than only by improvements at individual plants. The plan was stayed by the Supreme Court in 2016, replaced by the Trump administration's Affordable Clean Energy rule, and that replacement was itself vacated by the D.C. Circuit in 2021. A coalition of states led by West Virginia, together with coal companies, pursued the case even though no rule currently imposed the challenged approach.

Decision

Chief Justice Roberts's majority opinion held that in "extraordinary cases" of economic and political significance, an agency claiming an unheralded power must point to "clear congressional authorization", which Section 111(d) of the Clean Air Act did not supply for system-wide generation shifting. Justice Gorsuch's concurrence elaborated the doctrine's constitutional grounding, while Justice Kagan's dissent argued the Court had stripped the EPA of the authority Congress had given it to respond to climate change. The decision significantly constrained subsequent U.S. climate regulation of the power sector and has been applied in other challenges to agency action.
