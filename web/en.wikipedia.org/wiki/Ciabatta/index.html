Ciabatta

[Image: Ciabatta loaves with a crisp crust and open, holey crumb]

Overview

Ciabatta is an Italian white bread made with wheat flour, water, salt, yeast and olive oil, known for its crisp crust and large, irregular interior holes. The name means "slipper" in Italian, describing the loaf's flat, elongated shape. It was created in 1982 by baker Arnaldo Cavallari in Adria, in the Veneto region, as an Italian answer to the popularity of French baguettes for sandwiches.

Characteristics and use

Ciabatta dough is very wet compared with most bread doughs, producing the characteristic open crumb; a preferment (biga) is often used. The bread is the classic base for panini. Its youth makes it unusual among famous breads — most are centuries old.
