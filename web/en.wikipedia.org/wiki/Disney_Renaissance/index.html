Disney Renaissance

The Disney Renaissance was a period from 1989 to 1999 during which Walt Disney Feature Animation returned to producing critically and commercially successful animated films based predominantly on well-known stories, much as in its golden and silver ages. The films were largely musical adaptations with Broadway-style songs.

Disney Renaissance
Period: 1989–1999
Studio: Walt Disney Feature Animation
Key films: The Little Mermaid, Beauty and the Beast, Aladdin, The Lion King

The films

  • The Little Mermaid (1989)
  • The Rescuers Down Under (1990)
  • Beauty and the Beast (1991) — first animated film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture
  • Aladdin (1992)
  • The Lion King (1994) — the highest-grossing traditionally animated film of all time
  • Pocahontas (1995), The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1996), Hercules (1997), Mulan (1998), Tarzan (1999)

Background and causes

After Walt Disney's death the studio's films declined in the 1970s–80s. New management under Michael Eisner, Frank Wells and Jeffrey Katzenberg, the hiring of songwriters Howard Ashman and Alan Menken, new talent such as Glen Keane and John Musker, and technologies like the CAPS digital ink-and-paint system revived the studio. Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988) had already renewed interest in animation.

Legacy

The Renaissance restored Disney's prestige, grossed billions, won numerous Academy Awards for its songs, and inspired rival studios to expand animation. Its end is placed around 1999–2000 as computer animation (notably Pixar) rose and hand-drawn returns fell.
