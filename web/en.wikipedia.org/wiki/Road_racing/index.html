Road racing

TypeMotorsport / running / cycling discipline
SurfaceClosed public roads
Famous eventsIsle of Man TT, North West 200, Monaco Grand Prix

Road racing is a general term for racing on closed public roads rather than on purpose-built circuits. It is used in several sports: motorcycle road racing on real-road courses such as the Isle of Man TT and the North West 200; motor racing on street circuits such as Monaco; foot racing in road-running events from 5 km races to marathons; and bicycle road racing, the dominant form of competitive cycling.

Motorcycle road racing

"Real road racing" survives mainly in Ireland, Northern Ireland and the Isle of Man, where riders race on ordinary roads lined with walls, hedges and houses at speeds exceeding 200 mph on the fastest courses. Events such as the TT, Ulster Grand Prix and Tandragee 100 are celebrated for their history and atmosphere but are among the most dangerous in world sport.

Running and cycling

In athletics, road racing means mass-participation and elite races on tarmac, culminating in the marathon. In cycling, road races range from one-day classics such as Paris–Roubaix to stage races led by the Tour de France.
