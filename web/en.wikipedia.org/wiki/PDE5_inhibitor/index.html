PDE5 inhibitor

A phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor (PDE5 inhibitor) is a drug used to block the degradative action of cGMP-specific phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5) on cyclic GMP in the smooth muscle cells lining blood vessels. These drugs dilate blood vessels and are used principally in the treatment of erectile dysfunction and pulmonary arterial hypertension.

Mechanism of action

Sexual stimulation or nitric oxide release activates guanylate cyclase in smooth muscle, raising cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP), which relaxes smooth muscle and increases blood flow. PDE5 normally breaks down cGMP; inhibitors such as sildenafil prolong and amplify the signal, enhancing erection in the corpus cavernosum or lowering pulmonary vascular resistance.

Drugs in the class

  • Sildenafil (Viagra; Revatio for pulmonary hypertension) — the first PDE5 inhibitor, approved in 1998.
  • Tadalafil (Cialis; Adcirca) — noted for its long half-life (~17.5 hours).
  • Vardenafil (Levitra).
  • Avanafil (Stendra) — a later, more selective agent.
  • Others, such as udenafil and mirodenafil, are marketed in some countries.

History

Sildenafil was originally developed by Pfizer scientists in Sandwich, England, as a candidate treatment for angina and hypertension (compound UK-92,480). In early trials its effect on angina was modest, but male participants reported a striking side effect — improved erections — redirecting development toward erectile dysfunction. It was approved by the FDA in March 1998 and became one of the best-known pharmaceutical products in the world.

Side effects and contraindications

Common adverse effects include headache, flushing, nasal congestion, dyspepsia, and visual disturbances (a blue tinge with sildenafil, due to weak inhibition of retinal PDE6). PDE5 inhibitors are contraindicated with nitrates, because the combination can cause a dangerous fall in blood pressure. Caution is also advised with alpha blockers and in patients with severe cardiovascular disease.

Other uses

Beyond erectile dysfunction and pulmonary hypertension, PDE5 inhibitors have been studied for benign prostatic hyperplasia (tadalafil is approved for this indication), Raynaud's phenomenon, altitude sickness, and various experimental applications.
