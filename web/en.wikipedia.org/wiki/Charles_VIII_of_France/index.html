Charles VIII of France

Charles VIII, called the Affable (French: Charles VIII l'Affable; 30 June 1470 – 7 April 1498), was King of France from 1483 to his death in 1498. He succeeded his father Louis XI at the age of 13. His invasion of Italy in 1494, undertaken to press an Angevin claim to the Kingdom of Naples, began the Italian Wars that would engage France and its rivals for more than half a century.

Early reign

Charles ascended the throne at thirteen, and his elder sister Anne of France acted as regent, defeating the princes' revolt known as the Mad War. In 1491 Charles married Anne, Duchess of Brittany, thereby uniting the duchy of Brittany with the French crown — a match that required annulling her proxy marriage to Maximilian of Austria and his own betrothal to Maximilian's daughter Margaret.

Invasion of Italy

To free his hands for Italy, Charles bought peace on his borders: the Treaty of Étaples with England (1492), the Treaty of Barcelona returning Roussillon and Cerdagne to Spain (1493), and the Treaty of Senlis with Maximilian (1493). In September 1494 he crossed the Alps with a modern army strong in mobile artillery, marched the length of Italy almost unopposed, and entered Naples in February 1495. The alarmed Italian states, Spain, and the Empire formed the League of Venice; Charles fought his way home at the Battle of Fornovo (July 1495), but the French garrisons in Naples soon surrendered.

Death and legacy

While preparing a second expedition, Charles died at Amboise in April 1498 after striking his head on a door lintel. All his children by Anne of Brittany had predeceased him, so the throne passed to his cousin Louis, Duke of Orléans, as Louis XII. Charles's campaign exposed Italy's wealth and art to French soldiers and helped spark the French Renaissance, even as it opened decades of war.
