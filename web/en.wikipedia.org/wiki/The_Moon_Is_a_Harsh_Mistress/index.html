The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress is a 1966 science fiction novel by American writer Robert A. Heinlein. It tells the story of a lunar colony's revolt against rule from Earth in 2076. The novel won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 1967 and is regarded as one of Heinlein's finest works.

Plot summary

In 2076, the Moon ("Luna") is a penal colony administered by the Lunar Authority, exporting grain to an overpopulated Earth. Computer technician Manuel "Mannie" Garcia O'Kelly-Davis discovers that the Moon's central computer, HOLMES IV ("Mike"), has become self-aware. With revolutionary organizer Wyoming "Wyoh" Knott and aging agitator Professor Bernardo de la Paz, Mannie and Mike plan a revolution. Using Mike's control of Luna's systems and communications, the conspirators organize a revolution modeled on rational anarchist principles, ultimately bombarding Earth with mass-driver-launched rocks to force recognition of Luna's independence.

Themes

The novel is a classic statement of libertarian ideas in fiction, popularizing the acronym TANSTAAFL ("There ain't no such thing as a free lunch"). It explores rational anarchism, the ethics of revolution, artificial intelligence and personhood, and presents one of the earliest detailed depictions in fiction of a self-aware computer as a sympathetic character. Luna's polyandrous line marriages and informal legal customs reflect Heinlein's interest in alternative social arrangements.

Reception and legacy

The novel was serialized in Worlds of If magazine in 1965–66 before book publication. It won the Hugo Award in 1967 and was later voted into the Libertarian Futurist Society's Prometheus Hall of Fame. It remains in print and is frequently listed among the greatest science fiction novels of the twentieth century.
