Gothic rock (sometimes called goth rock or simply goth) is a style of rock music that emerged from post-punk in the United Kingdom in the late 1970s. The first post-punk bands which shifted towards dark music with gothic overtones include Siouxsie and the Banshees, Joy Division, Bauhaus, and the Cure.

The genre itself was defined as a separate movement from post-punk by bands such as Bauhaus, whose 1979 single "Bela Lugosi's Dead" is often cited as the first gothic rock record. Gothic rock gave rise to a broader goth subculture that included clubs, fashion, and publications in the 1980s.

Characteristics

Gothic rock typically features a combination of driving, post-punk rhythms with dark, introspective lyrics drawing on gothic literature, romanticism, and horror imagery. Common musical elements include prominent bass lines, reverb-heavy guitar, synthesizers, and baritone vocals. Themes frequently explore death, decay, the supernatural, and melancholy.

History

Origins (1979–1982)

The term "gothic rock" was coined by music producer Tony Wilson to describe Joy Division's sound. Bauhaus's "Bela Lugosi's Dead" (1979) is widely regarded as the genre's founding release. The Batcave club in London, opened in 1982, became a focal point of the emerging scene.

Expansion (1980s)

Bands such as The Sisters of Mercy, Fields of the Nephilim, The Mission, and Christian Death carried the genre through the 1980s, while offshoots including deathrock developed in the United States and dark wave and ethereal wave emerged in Europe.
