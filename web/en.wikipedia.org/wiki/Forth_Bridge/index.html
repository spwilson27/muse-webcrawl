Forth Bridge

The Forth Bridge is a cantilever railway bridge across the Firth of Forth in eastern Scotland, between South Queensferry and North Queensferry. Opened in 1890, it was the world’s longest cantilever bridge span and the first major steel structure in Britain; it remains an icon of Scotland and a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 2015).

Forth Bridge
Carries: Railway (Edinburgh–Aberdeen line)
Opened: 4 March 1890
Designers: John Fowler, Benjamin Baker
Total length: 2,467 m
Main spans: 2 × 521 m

Construction

After the Tay Bridge disaster of 1879, the Forth Bridge was designed conservatively for strength, using over 53,000 tonnes of steel and some 6.5 million rivets. Its three great double cantilevers, coated in distinctive red paint, took seven years and the lives of dozens of workers to build.

Today

About 200 trains cross daily. The bridge sits beside the 1964 Forth Road Bridge and the 2017 Queensferry Crossing. The saying “like painting the Forth Bridge” long meant a never-ending task, though a modern coating applied in 2002–2011 is expected to last decades.
