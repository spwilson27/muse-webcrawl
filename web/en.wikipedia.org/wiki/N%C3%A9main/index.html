Nemain (also spelled Nemhain, Neamhain, or Neman) is a goddess of war in Irish mythology, associated with the frenzy, panic, and havoc of battle. Her name is generally interpreted as meaning "frenzy" or "venom". She is one of the trio of war goddesses often grouped with Badb and Macha, and is closely linked with — and sometimes treated as an aspect of — the Morrígan.

Role in the tales

In the Ulster Cycle, particularly the Táin Bó Cúailnge, Nemain's shrieking and supernatural terror sow confusion among armies: her battle-frenzy is said to cause warriors to mistake their own comrades for enemies and fall upon one another. Medieval glossaries describe her as the wife of Neit, a god of war, and call her "the venomous one".

Like her fellow war goddesses, Nemain is connected with crows and ravens on the battlefield and with prophecy of doom for warriors. Scholars have compared her to figures such as the Norse valkyries and to Gaulish war deities attested in inscriptions.
