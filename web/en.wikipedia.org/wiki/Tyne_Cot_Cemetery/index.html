Tyne Cot Cemetery

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tyne Cot Commonwealth War Graves Cemetery and Memorial to the Missing is a burial ground for the dead of the First World War in the Ypres Salient on the Western Front. It is the largest cemetery for Commonwealth forces in the world, in terms of burials, and is located near Zonnebeke in Belgium.

History

The name "Tyne Cot" is said to have been given by the Northumberland Fusiliers to a barn that stood on the Passchendaele–Broodseinde road, which resembled typical Tyneside workers' cottages. The ground was captured by the 3rd Australian Division on 4 October 1917 during the Battle of Passchendaele.

The cemetery

Tyne Cot contains 11,965 burials, of which 8,369 are unidentified. The memorial, designed by Sir Herbert Baker, commemorates 34,959 British and New Zealand soldiers who died in the Salient and have no known grave. The Cross of Sacrifice stands on the site of a German blockhouse, at the request of King George V, who visited the cemetery in 1922.

Notable burials

Among those commemorated are Victoria Cross recipients including Private John James Collis and Lewis McGee. A modern visitor centre interprets the battles of 1917 for the many visitors each year.
