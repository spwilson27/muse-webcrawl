Georgian cuisine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Georgian cuisine consists of cooking traditions, techniques and practices of Georgia, a country in the Caucasus at the crossroads of Europe and Asia. It is noted for its use of walnuts, pomegranate, fresh herbs such as coriander and tarragon, chilli and garlic sauces, and a variety of cheese-filled breads. Georgian meals are traditionally served as a communal feast called a supra, presided over by a toastmaster (tamada) and accompanied by polyphonic singing and Georgian wine.

Signature dishes

Khachapuri, cheese-filled bread, is the national dish; its regional variants include the boat-shaped, egg-topped Adjaruli from Adjara and the round Imeruli and Megruli from western Georgia. Khinkali are large spiced meat dumplings, originally from the mountain regions, eaten by hand. Other staples include pkhali (chopped vegetables with walnut paste), mtsvadi (grilled meat skewers), eggplant rolls with walnut filling, the fiery condiment ajika, and churchkhela, strings of nuts dipped in thickened grape juice.

Wine

Georgia is one of the oldest wine-producing countries in the world, with evidence of viticulture going back some 8,000 years. Wine is traditionally fermented and aged in large clay vessels called qvevri buried underground — a method inscribed on UNESCO's heritage list.

Regional variation

Eastern regions such as Kakheti favour hearty meat dishes and wheat breads, while western regions such as Imereti and Samegrelo use more cornmeal, sulguni cheese, walnuts and hotter spices.
