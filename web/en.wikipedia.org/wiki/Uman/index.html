Uman

Uman (Ukrainian: Умань) is a city in Cherkasy Oblast, Ukraine, located in the east of the historic region of Podolia. It is the administrative centre of Uman Raion and had a population of roughly 82,000 before the full-scale Russian invasion of 2022. The city stands on the Umanka River, about 210 kilometres south of Kyiv.

History

Uman was first mentioned in 1616 as a fortified settlement of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth on the frontier with the Crimean Tatars. In the eighteenth century it became the property of the Potocki family. In 1768 the city was the scene of a massacre during the Koliivshchyna uprising, when Haidamak rebels killed thousands of Poles and Jews. Following the Second Partition of Poland in 1793, Uman passed to the Russian Empire and became a district town in Kyiv Governorate.

Sofiyivka Park

Uman's best-known landmark is Sofiyivka Park, an arboretum and landscape park founded in 1796 by Count Stanisław Szczęsny Potocki as a gift for his wife Sofia. Designed in the English landscape style with lakes, waterfalls, grottoes and pavilions illustrating scenes from Greek mythology, it covers about 180 hectares and is one of Ukraine's most visited parks and one of the Seven Wonders of Ukraine.

Jewish pilgrimage

Uman is the burial place of Rebbe Nachman of Breslov (1772–1810), founder of the Breslov Hasidic movement, who spent his final months in the city and asked to be buried there. Every year tens of thousands of Hasidic pilgrims, mostly from Israel, travel to Uman for the Rosh Hashanah kibbutz, a gathering at the Rebbe's grave that has become one of the largest Jewish pilgrimages in the world; it continued, in reduced form, even during the war with Russia.

Second World War and recent history

In August 1941, during the Battle of Uman, large Soviet forces were encircled by the German army near the city, and under the occupation the Jewish community was annihilated. In the Russo-Ukrainian War, Uman suffered a deadly missile strike on a residential building in April 2023 that killed 23 people, including children.
