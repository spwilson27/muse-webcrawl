Personalization

Personalization consists of tailoring a service, product, or experience to a specific individual or group based on known data about them. It is widely used in marketing, e-commerce, media, and software, where it stands in contrast to one-size-fits-all mass production. Related concepts include customization (in which the user actively specifies preferences) and mass customization in manufacturing.

Applications

On the web and in mobile apps, personalization uses browsing history, purchase records, location, and demographic data to select content: examples include recommender systems for films and products, personalized search results, targeted advertising, and individually tailored email marketing. In retail and manufacturing, personalization ranges from made-to-order goods to products bearing the customer's name.

Criticism

Personalization raises concerns about privacy and data collection, the creation of filter bubbles in which users are shown only content matching their existing views, and algorithmic discrimination. Regulations such as the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation impose conditions on the profiling of individuals. Research also notes a "personalization–privacy paradox": consumers value tailored experiences but are uneasy about the data collection that enables them.
