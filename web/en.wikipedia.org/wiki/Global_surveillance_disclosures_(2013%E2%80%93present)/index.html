Global surveillance disclosures (2013–present)

DateJune 2013 – present
SourceEdward Snowden
AgenciesNSA, GCHQ and partners
MediaThe Guardian, The Washington Post, Der Spiegel and others

The global surveillance disclosures began in June 2013 when former United States National Security Agency (NSA) contractor Edward Snowden leaked a large cache of classified documents to journalists Glenn Greenwald, Laura Poitras and Ewen MacAskill. Published initially by The Guardian and The Washington Post, the documents revealed extensive programmes of mass surveillance conducted by the NSA, the British Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ) and their partners in the Five Eyes intelligence alliance.

Revelations

Among the most significant disclosures was PRISM, a programme under which the NSA collected data from major internet companies, and the bulk collection of telephone metadata from millions of customers of the carrier Verizon. Other reports described the tapping of undersea fibre-optic cables under the GCHQ programme Tempora, the monitoring of the mobile phones of world leaders including German Chancellor Angela Merkel, and the XKeyscore system for searching global internet activity. Snowden fled to Hong Kong and then to Moscow, where he was granted asylum, and was charged in the United States under the Espionage Act.

Reactions and impact

The disclosures provoked a worldwide debate over privacy, civil liberties and state security. Technology companies moved to encrypt user traffic by default, several governments opened inquiries, and in 2015 the United States passed the USA Freedom Act, ending the bulk collection of domestic telephone metadata. Courts in several jurisdictions ruled elements of mass surveillance unlawful. The revelations remain a landmark in the public understanding of the scale of signals intelligence in the internet era.
