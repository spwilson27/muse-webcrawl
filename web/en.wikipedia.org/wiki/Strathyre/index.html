Strathyre

Strathyre (Scottish Gaelic: Srath Eadhair) is a village in the Stirling council area of Scotland. It lies in the valley of the River Balvag, at the head of Loch Lubnaig, on the A84 road between Callander and Lochearnhead, within the Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park.

Strathyre at the northern end of Loch Lubnaig.

History and surroundings

The village grew as a stopping place on the road north through the Trossachs and was once served by the Callander and Oban Railway, which closed in 1965; the trackbed is now part of National Cycle Route 7 and the Rob Roy Way long-distance path. Nearby Ben Ledi is a popular hill walk, and the surrounding forests are managed by Forestry and Land Scotland with waymarked trails.

Strathyre hosts a primary school, village shop, and inns, and is a base for walking, cycling, and fishing on Loch Lubnaig. The name is also associated with the air "Strathyre", composed by the 19th-century Scottish fiddler and bandleader J. Scott Skinner era tradition of strathspey tunes — the original air is attributed to Captain Simon Fraser's collection era of Highland music.
