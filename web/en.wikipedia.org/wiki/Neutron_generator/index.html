Neutron generator

Neutron generators are neutron source devices which contain compact linear particle accelerators and produce neutrons by fusing hydrogen isotopes together. The fusion reactions take place as the device accelerates deuterium (D), tritium (T), or a mixture of the two into a metal hydride target which also contains D, T, or a mixture. The D–T reaction yields 14.1 MeV neutrons and the D–D reaction yields 2.45 MeV neutrons. Unlike radioactive neutron sources and nuclear reactors, neutron generators can be switched off completely, are compact, and produce a nearly monoenergetic neutron output.

Design

The commonest design is the sealed tube neutron generator: a Penning or radio-frequency ion source produces deuterium/tritium ions, which are accelerated by a potential of roughly 100–200 kV onto a metal target (often titanium or scandium hydride) loaded with the fusion fuel. As the target is bombarded it also replenishes ("self-loads") with the incoming ions. Tube lifetime is limited by target depletion and gas clean-up, and ranges from hundreds to thousands of operating hours.

Larger beam line systems, used in laboratories, separate the ion source, acceleration column, and target and can reach outputs above 1012 neutrons per second. Commercial sealed tubes typically produce 106–1010 neutrons per second, continuous or pulsed.

Applications

  • Oil and gas well logging — pulsed neutron tools measure formation porosity and fluid type through casing.
  • Neutron activation analysis — prompt gamma analysis of bulk materials such as coal, cement, and ores.
  • Security screening — interrogation of cargo and luggage for explosives and special nuclear materials.
  • Research and detector calibration — a clean source of monoenergetic neutrons.
  • Medical isotope production and materials studies — niche uses where a reactor is impractical.

Safety

Because tritium is radioactive, sealed tubes are manufactured, transported, and disposed of under regulatory control, though the quantities are small. When switched off, a generator emits no neutrons, a major safety advantage over isotopic sources such as californium-252.
