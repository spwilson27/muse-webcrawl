Income statement

An income statement, also known as a profit and loss statement (P&L), statement of operations, or statement of earnings, is one of the core financial statements of a company. It reports the company's revenues, expenses, gains, and losses over a specified period of time — usually a fiscal quarter or year — and shows the resulting net income or net loss. Unlike the balance sheet, which is a snapshot at a point in time, the income statement covers a period. Together with the balance sheet and the cash flow statement, it forms the basis of financial statement analysis.

Structure

A typical multi-step income statement proceeds as follows:

  • Revenue (sales) — amounts earned from selling goods or services.
  • Cost of goods sold (COGS) — direct costs of producing what was sold; revenue minus COGS gives gross profit.
  • Operating expenses — selling, general and administrative expenses (SG&A), research and development, and depreciation and amortisation; gross profit minus operating expenses gives operating income.
  • Non-operating items — interest income and expense, gains and losses on asset sales and investments.
  • Income before tax, less income tax expense, gives net income — the "bottom line".
  • Earnings per share (EPS) — net income attributable to common shareholders divided by shares outstanding, reported as basic and diluted.

Formats

In a single-step format, all revenues are totalled and all expenses subtracted in one step. The multi-step format separates operating from non-operating items and highlights intermediate measures such as gross profit and operating income. Under IFRS (IAS 1), expenses may be classified by nature (materials, labour, depreciation) or by function (cost of sales, distribution, administration).

Key measures

Analysts derive figures such as gross margin, operating margin, and net profit margin from the income statement. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation) is a widely used non-GAAP proxy for operating cash performance. Because the income statement is prepared on the accrual basis, net income differs from cash generated; the cash flow statement reconciles the two.

Standards and presentation

Public companies must file income statements with regulators (for example on SEC Form 10-K and 10-Q in the United States). Items such as discontinued operations are presented separately, and "other comprehensive income" — gains and losses bypassing net income, such as certain currency translation and pension adjustments — appears in the statement of comprehensive income.
