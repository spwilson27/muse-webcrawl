Seabee

Seabees are members of the United States Naval Construction Forces. Their name derives from the initials "CB" for Construction Battalion, and their motto is Construimus, Batuimus — "We Build, We Fight". Created in the opening months of World War II, Seabees build and fight in support of naval and Marine Corps operations worldwide.

History

Rear Admiral Ben Moreell founded the Construction Battalions after the attack on Pearl Harbor, recruiting experienced construction workers at advanced ages and pay grades. More than 325,000 men served in World War II, building airfields, ports, roads, and hospitals across the Pacific and Atlantic theaters, famously on islands such as Guadalcanal and Okinawa. Seabees later served in Korea, Vietnam, Iraq, and Afghanistan, as well as in disaster-relief missions.

Organization

Today the Seabees' homeport on the West Coast is Naval Base Ventura County (Port Hueneme), with a second center at Gulfport, Mississippi. Units include Naval Mobile Construction Battalions (NMCBs), Amphibious Construction Battalions, and Underwater Construction Teams. Seabee ratings include builder, steelworker, equipment operator, construction electrician, utilitiesman, construction mechanic, and engineering aide.

Culture

The Seabee emblem — a flying bee wearing a sailor's cap and carrying a machine gun, wrench, and hammer — was designed by Frank Iafrate in 1942. March 5, the anniversary of their founding, is celebrated as the Seabee birthday.
