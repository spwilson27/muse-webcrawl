Álmos

Álmos (c. 820 – c. 895) was, according to Hungarian chronicles, the first head of the federation of the seven Magyar tribes and the father of Árpád, founder of the Árpád dynasty. His figure stands at the boundary of legend and history: the medieval Gesta Hungarorum recounts that his mother Emese dreamed of a turul bird, from which his name — derived from the Hungarian word álom, "dream" — was explained.

Life and tradition

The anonymous notary of King Béla (author of the Gesta Hungarorum) relates that the seven Magyar chieftains elected Álmos as their leader in Etelköz and swore a blood oath to follow him and his descendants. Álmos led the Magyars westward from the steppe but, according to tradition, did not enter the Carpathian Basin himself: he was killed or ritually sacrificed in Transylvania, and his son Árpád led the conquest of the new homeland around 895.

Historicity

Constantine VII's De administrando imperio, written in the 10th century, mentions Árpád but not Álmos, and modern historians debate which of the dual offices of early Magyar leadership — the sacral kende or the military gyula — Álmos may have held. He remains a central figure of Hungarian foundation mythology.
