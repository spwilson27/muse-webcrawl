Tell Beit Mirsim

Tell Beit Mirsim is an archaeological site in the southern Levant, in the hills southwest of Hebron. Excavated by William F. Albright between 1926 and 1932, it became a type-site for the pottery chronology of Bronze and Iron Age Palestine. Albright identified it with biblical Debir (Kiriath-Sepher), an identification later debated.

LocationWest Bank, near Hebron
ExcavatorWilliam F. Albright
PeriodsEarly Bronze – Iron Age II

Excavations

Albright's four seasons established a stratigraphic sequence (Strata A–J) from the Early Bronze Age to the late Iron Age. The well-published pottery assemblages helped anchor the chronology of the region. Finds included Bronze Age tombs, Iron Age houses and fortifications, and evidence of destruction in the late 6th century BC associated with the Babylonian campaigns.

Identification

Albright equated the tell with Debir, though some scholars have proposed Eglon or other sites; the debate remains unresolved.
