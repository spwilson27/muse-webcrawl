Conscientização

Conscientização is a Portuguese term, usually rendered in English as "critical consciousness" or "conscientization", denoting the development of an awareness of social and political contradictions and the capacity to act against oppressive structures. It is central to the philosophy of Brazilian educator Paulo Freire (1921–1997).

Freire's pedagogy

Freire introduced the concept in his literacy work with Brazilian peasants and developed it in Pedagogy of the Oppressed (1968). He contrasted conscientização with the "banking model" of education, in which teachers deposit information into passive students. Instead, dialogue-based "problem-posing" education leads learners to perceive their situation critically—to move from a "magical" or naive consciousness to a critical one—and to reflect and act upon the world in order to transform it, a unity of reflection and action Freire called praxis.

Influence

The idea shaped liberation theology, popular education movements in Latin America and Africa, and later critical pedagogy in the work of scholars such as Henry Giroux and Peter McLaren. Related formulations, such as Frantz Fanon's analysis of colonised consciousness, predate and parallel Freire's. The term is sometimes criticised for vagueness or for presuming the educator knows in advance what learners should conclude.
