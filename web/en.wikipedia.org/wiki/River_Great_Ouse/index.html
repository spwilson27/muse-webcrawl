The River Great Ouse is a river in East Anglia, England, and one of the longest rivers in the United Kingdom at about 230 km (143 miles). It rises in Northamptonshire and flows east through Buckinghamshire, Bedfordshire, Cambridgeshire and Norfolk, entering the Wash at King's Lynn. It is not to be confused with the Yorkshire Ouse or the Sussex Ouse.

Course

The Great Ouse rises near Syresham, south of Brackley, and flows through Buckingham, Stony Stratford, Newport Pagnell, Olney, Bedford, St Neots, Huntingdon and St Ives before entering the Fens. In the low fenland its course has been repeatedly straightened and embanked: below Earith the combined waters are split between the Old West River and the artificial New Bedford River (Hundred Foot Drain), built in the 17th century under Cornelius Vermuyden as part of the drainage of the Fens, with the parallel Old Bedford River and the tidal sluice at Denver. The river passes Downham Market and reaches the sea at King's Lynn.

Tributaries and fenland drains

Chief tributaries include the River Cam, joining at Pope's Corner near Ely; the Lark, Little Ouse and Wissey, joining the tidal river near Denver; and the Nar, which joins at King's Lynn. The cathedral city of Ely stands on a former island of higher ground beside the river.

Navigation and flooding

The river is navigable to Bedford, linked by locks, and connected over the Middle Level navigations to the River Nene. Together with its embanked fenland channels it forms one of the most heavily engineered river systems in Britain, protected by the Denver Sluice and the Ouse Washes — a washland between the Old and New Bedford Rivers that stores floodwater and is an internationally important wetland for wintering wildfowl.
