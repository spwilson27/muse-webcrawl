Act of Succession (Denmark)

The Act of Succession (Danish: Tronfølgeloven) is a part of the constitutional law of Denmark governing succession to the Danish throne. Adopted on 27 March 1953 alongside the new Constitution, it replaced the male-only succession of the 1853 act and made female succession possible, which allowed Princess Margrethe — later Queen Margrethe II — to become heir presumptive ahead of her uncle.

Provisions

The Act limits succession to the legitimate descendants of King Christian X and Queen Alexandrine, requires the monarch to belong to the Evangelical Lutheran Church, and requires parliament's consent for a dynastic marriage, failing which the person and their descendants lose succession rights. Following a referendum in 2009, the Act was amended to introduce absolute primogeniture, so the monarch's eldest child succeeds regardless of sex; the change first affects the generation of King Frederik X's children, headed by Crown Prince Christian.
