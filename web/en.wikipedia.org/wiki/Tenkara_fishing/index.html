Tenkara fishing

Tenkara (Japanese: テンカラ, literally "from the skies" / "from the heavens") is a traditional Japanese method of fly fishing used for catching mountain stream fish such as yamame, iwana and amago. It uses a very long telescoping rod, a fixed length of line tied to the rod tip, and a fly — with no reel.

Equipment

Tenkara rods are typically 3.2–4.5 m (10.5–15 ft) long and collapse into short sections for carrying in the mountains. Lines are either tapered furled lines or level fluorocarbon, roughly the length of the rod, with a short tippet. Traditional flies (kebari) are simple soft-hackle patterns, often tied with the hackle slanting forward (sakasa kebari).

Technique

Because the line is fixed, casting range is limited to rod plus line length, which suits small mountain streams. The light line lands softly and can be held off the water to give a natural drift; anglers manipulate the fly with pulses and pauses. Fish are played by raising the rod, and landed by hand or net.

History and spread

Tenkara developed over centuries among commercial anglers in Japan's mountain valleys. Introduced to the United States in 2009 by Tenkara USA, it has since gained an international following prized for its simplicity and minimal gear.
