Band-e Amir National Park

LocationBamyan Province, Afghanistan
AreaAbout 59,000 hectares
Established2009 (Afghanistan's first national park)
FeaturesChain of six deep-blue travertine lakes

Band-e Amir National Park is a national park in Bamyan Province, central Afghanistan, established in 2009 as the country's first national park. It protects a chain of six intensely blue lakes in the Hindu Kush, separated by natural dams of travertine — a form of limestone deposited by the mineral-rich spring water that fills the lakes.

Geography

The lakes — Band-e Gholaman, Band-e Qambar, Band-e Haibat, Band-e Panir, Band-e Pudina and Band-e Zulfiqar — lie at roughly 3,000 metres elevation. The travertine dams, which in places rise many metres above the downstream lake, are built up by algae and bacteria that precipitate calcite from the water, a process similar to that seen at Pamukkale in Turkey and Huanglong in China. The largest lake, Band-e Haibat, is about 490 hectares in area.

Conservation and tourism

The park was created with assistance from the Wildlife Conservation Society and international donors, and is managed by Afghanistan's National Environmental Protection Agency. Before the return of the Taliban to power in 2021, Band-e Amir was the country's most popular domestic tourist destination, drawing thousands of Afghan visitors each year; local legend attributes the lakes' creation to Ali, son-in-law of the Prophet Muhammad. Wildlife recorded in the park includes ibex, urial, wolves and the Afghan snowfinch.
