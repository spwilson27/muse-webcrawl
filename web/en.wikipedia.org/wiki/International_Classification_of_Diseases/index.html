The International Classification of Diseases (ICD) is a globally used medical classification system published by the World Health Organization (WHO). It is designed to promote international comparability in the collection, processing, classification and presentation of mortality and morbidity statistics. Diseases, disorders, injuries, symptoms and other health conditions are assigned alphanumeric codes, so that health data can be recorded, analysed and shared consistently between countries and over time.

History

The ICD descends from the Bertillon Classification of Causes of Death, adopted by the International Statistical Institute in 1893 under the French statistician Jacques Bertillon. Successive revisions added morbidity coding. The WHO assumed responsibility with ICD-6 in 1949, the first version to include causes of morbidity as well as mortality. ICD-8 followed in 1965 and ICD-9 in 1975.

ICD-10 was endorsed by the World Health Assembly in 1990 and came into use in member states from 1994; its codes run from A00 to Z99 and contain roughly 14,000 codes, expandable in national clinical modifications such as the United States' ICD-10-CM.

ICD-11 was adopted by the World Health Assembly in May 2019 and came into effect on 1 January 2022. It is the first fully digital revision, designed for electronic health records, with about 17,000 unique codes and more than 120,000 codable terms. New content includes chapters on conditions related to sexual health, traditional medicine, and gaming disorder among disorders due to addictive behaviours; antimicrobial resistance and codes for rare diseases were also substantially expanded.

Structure

ICD-11 is organised into 28 chapters, including certain infectious and parasitic diseases; neoplasms; diseases of the blood; endocrine, nutritional and metabolic diseases; mental, behavioural or neurodevelopmental disorders; diseases of the nervous system; diseases of the circulatory system; diseases of the respiratory system; diseases of the digestive system; symptoms and abnormal findings; injury and external causes; and factors influencing health status. Coding uses a stem-code and extension-code ("post-coordination") model allowing clusters of codes to describe a condition in detail.

Uses

The ICD underpins national mortality statistics, hospital discharge data, health insurance reimbursement (including diagnosis-related groups), epidemiological research and global burden-of-disease estimates. In the United States, procedure coding for inpatient care uses the companion ICD-10-PCS system, while ICD codes for diagnoses drive billing in most health systems.

Related classifications

The WHO Family of International Classifications also includes the International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF) and the International Classification of Health Interventions (ICHI). The American Psychiatric Association's DSM coordinates its diagnoses with ICD codes.
