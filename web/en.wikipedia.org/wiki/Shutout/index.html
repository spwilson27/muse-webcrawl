Shutout

In team sports, a shutout (abbreviated SHO or SO) is a game in which one team prevents the other from scoring any points or goals at all. Shutouts are most commonly associated with baseball, ice hockey, and association football (where the corresponding term in British English is a "clean sheet"), but can occur in almost any team sport.

Baseball

In baseball, a shutout is credited to a pitcher who allows no runs in a complete game; combined shutouts, in which two or more pitchers combine without allowing a run, are also recorded but not credited to an individual. Walter Johnson holds the Major League Baseball career record with 110 shutouts, and the 2010s saw a marked decline in complete-game shutouts as bullpen usage increased. A game in which neither team scores until late innings is sometimes called a "pitchers' duel"; a shutout of unusual dominance, with few hits and many strikeouts, is measured by metrics such as Bill James's "game score".

Ice hockey

In ice hockey, a shutout is credited to a goaltender who stops every shot and plays the entire game; if goaltenders are substituted, the team is credited with a combined shutout. Martin Brodeur holds the NHL regular-season record with 125 career shutouts, and Terry Sawchuk held the record (103) for decades before him. A scoreless game through regulation that ends 1–0 in overtime or a shootout still counts as a shutout for the winning goaltender.

Association football

In soccer, goalkeepers and defenders are credited with a clean sheet when their team concedes no goals. Clean sheets are a standard statistic in fantasy football and goalkeeping awards such as the Premier League Golden Glove.

Other sports

Shutouts occur in American football (rare in the modern NFL), lacrosse, rugby, and bandy. A double shutout, in which neither team scores, is possible in sports permitting ties, such as soccer and (historically) hockey before shootouts.
