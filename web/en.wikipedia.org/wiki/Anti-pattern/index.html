Anti-pattern

Anti-pattern
DefinitionA common response to a recurring problem that is usually ineffective and counterproductive
Term coined1995, attributed to Andrew Koenig
Key textAntiPatterns (Brown et al., 1998)
FieldsSoftware engineering, architecture, project management

An anti-pattern in software engineering, project management and business processes is a common response to a recurring problem that is usually ineffective and risks being highly counterproductive. The term, coined in 1995 by Andrew Koenig and inspired by the Gang of Four's design patterns book, was popularised by the 1998 book AntiPatterns by William Brown and co-authors.

Whereas a design pattern documents a proven good solution, an anti-pattern documents a solution that looks attractive — and is therefore repeatedly adopted — but produces negative consequences that outweigh any benefits. Studying anti-patterns allows practitioners to recognise, refactor and avoid them.

Structure

Following the format of AntiPatterns, an anti-pattern description generally identifies the problematic solution, the forces that lead teams to adopt it, its symptoms and consequences, and a refactored solution that resolves the underlying problem. Anti-patterns are classified into software design, software architecture and project management categories, among others.

Examples

  • God object (the Blob) — a single class that concentrates most of the system's responsibilities.
  • Spaghetti code — unstructured, tangled control flow that resists comprehension and change.
  • Golden hammer — using one familiar tool or technology for every problem ("when all you have is a hammer, everything looks like a nail").
  • Premature optimization — optimising before profiling has identified a real bottleneck.
  • Cargo cult programming — ritual inclusion of code whose purpose is not understood.
  • Death march — a project whose schedule and staffing make failure predictable but which continues regardless.
  • Analysis paralysis and design by committee — management anti-patterns of excessive deliberation and diluted responsibility.
  • Reinventing the wheel — duplicating an existing, adequate solution.

Related concepts

Anti-patterns are the negative counterparts of design patterns and are related to code smells — surface indications that may signal a deeper problem — though a smell is a symptom rather than a documented, recurring solution. In organisational writing, similar catalogues exist for management and process anti-patterns.
