Flip-flops

Flip-flops
TypeOpen-toed sandal
MaterialsRubber, foam, plastic, leather
Also known asThongs (AU/NZ), jandals (NZ), havaianas (BR)

Flip-flops are a type of light sandal, typically worn as casual footwear. They consist of a flat sole held loosely on the foot by a Y-shaped strap that passes between the first and second toes and around both sides of the foot. This footwear style dates back to ancient Egypt, and modern rubber flip-flops became popular worldwide after the Second World War.

History and names

Sandals with a toe-post design appear in many ancient cultures; the Japanese zori influenced the cheap rubber versions produced in the post-war period. Flip-flops are called "thongs" in Australia, "jandals" in New Zealand, and "slops" in South Africa. In Brazil, the brand Havaianas became a generic name for the style. The English name is onomatopoeic for the slapping sound the sole makes against the heel when walking.

Use and health

Flip-flops provide minimal support and protection, and podiatrists link heavy long-term use to foot and gait problems, though they are convenient for beaches, showers and casual wear, and they help prevent some fungal infections in communal washing areas.
