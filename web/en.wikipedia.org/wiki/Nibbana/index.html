Nibbana

Nibbāna (Pali; Sanskrit: Nirvāṇa) is the ultimate goal of the Buddhist path: the complete cessation of suffering (dukkha) and of rebirth in saṃsāra, attained through the extinction of greed, hatred, and delusion.

Meaning

The word suggests a “blowing out” — as of a flame — of the fires of craving. In the early texts Nibbāna is described as the unconditioned, deathless, and highest bliss, characterised more by negation than by positive description. One who attains it in life is an arahant; at death, the arahant's final Nibbāna (parinibbāna) ends rebirth entirely.

In the traditions

Theravāda Buddhism emphasises Nibbāna as liberation from saṃsāra, while Mahāyāna traditions reinterpret it: Nibbāna and saṃsāra are said to be ultimately non-dual, and the bodhisattva vows to aid all beings rather than enter final cessation alone. The concept has been compared, often misleadingly, with notions of heaven or annihilation; classical Buddhism rejects both eternalism and annihilationism.
