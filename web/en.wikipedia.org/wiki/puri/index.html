Puri

The Jagannath Temple in Puri, Odisha.

Puri is a coastal city and a municipality in the state of Odisha in eastern India, situated on the Bay of Bengal, about 60 kilometres (37 mi) south of the state capital Bhubaneswar. It is one of the original Char Dham pilgrimage sites for Hindus, and the city is famous for its historic Jagannath Temple and for the annual Ratha Yatra, or chariot festival, in which the deities Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra are drawn in massive wooden chariots through the streets.

History

Puri has been known by several names over the centuries, including Purushottama Kshetra and Shankha Kshetra. The present Jagannath Temple was begun in the 12th century, traditionally attributed to the Eastern Ganga king Anantavarman Chodaganga. The city came under Mughal, Maratha, and then British rule, and became a popular seaside resort during the colonial period.

Geography and culture

Puri is a district headquarters and one of Odisha's most visited tourist destinations, known for its sandy beach on the Bay of Bengal, its appliqué and palm-leaf craft traditions, and the nearby Sun Temple at Konark and Chilika Lake. The Ratha Yatra draws over a million pilgrims in some years; the English word "juggernaut" derives from Jagannath's chariot procession. The city also hosts the Puri Beach Festival and a large community of traditional painters producing pattachitra art.
