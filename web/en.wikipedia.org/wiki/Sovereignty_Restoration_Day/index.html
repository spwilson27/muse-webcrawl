Sovereignty Restoration Day

Sovereignty Restoration Day (Hawaiian: Lā Hoʻihoʻi Ea) is a commemoration in Hawaii held on the last Sunday of July, recalling 31 July 1843, when British Rear-Admiral Richard Thomas restored the sovereignty of the Kingdom of Hawaii after a five-month unauthorised seizure of the islands by Captain Lord George Paulet of HMS Carysfort.

Background

In February 1843 Paulet, acting on complaints of British subjects, forced King Kamehameha III to cede the islands provisionally to Britain. When Admiral Thomas, Paulet's superior, arrived on 26 July, he repudiated the seizure and formally restored the kingdom on 31 July 1843. On that occasion Kamehameha III spoke the words that became Hawaii's motto: Ua Mau ke Ea o ka ʻĀina i ka Pono — "the sovereignty of the land is perpetuated in righteousness." A Honolulu park, Thomas Square, commemorates the admiral.

Observance

The day was celebrated as a national holiday in the kingdom. After the overthrow of the monarchy in 1893 it fell out of official observance, but it has been revived since the late 20th century by the Hawaiian sovereignty movement and community events, and in 2022 Hawaii formally recognised Lā Hoʻihoʻi Ea in state law as a day of commemoration.
