Water treatment

Water treatment is any process that improves the quality of water to make it appropriate for a specific end-use. The end use may be drinking, industrial water supply, irrigation, river flow maintenance, water recreation or many other uses, including being safely returned to the environment. Water treatment removes contaminants and undesirable components, or reduces their concentration so that the water becomes fit for its desired end-use.

Drinking water purification

Conventional treatment of surface water typically involves: screening to remove debris; coagulation and flocculation, in which chemicals such as alum destabilise fine particles so they clump together; sedimentation, where the flocs settle out; filtration through sand or membranes; and disinfection, most commonly with chlorine, chloramine, ozone or ultraviolet light, to kill pathogens. A disinfectant residual is usually maintained through the distribution system. Groundwater may require fewer steps but can need removal of iron, manganese, hardness or naturally occurring contaminants such as arsenic.

Wastewater treatment

Wastewater treatment converts sewage and industrial effluent into an effluent that can be returned to the water cycle with acceptable impact. Stages include preliminary screening, primary settling, secondary biological treatment (such as the activated sludge process) that removes dissolved organic matter, and sometimes tertiary treatment to remove nitrogen, phosphorus or micropollutants, followed by disinfection. Sludge from the process is digested, dewatered and disposed of or reused.

Industrial and other uses

Industrial water treatment includes boiler feedwater treatment, cooling water treatment and process water purification, using methods such as ion exchange, reverse osmosis, deionisation and chemical dosing. Point-of-use devices, such as domestic filters and water softeners, treat small volumes at the tap.
