Sarah Josepha Hale (née Buell; 24 October 1788 – 30 April 1879) was an American writer, activist, and editor. As editor of Godey's Lady's Book for forty years, she was one of the most influential figures in nineteenth-century American publishing. She is also remembered as the author of the nursery rhyme "Mary Had a Little Lamb" and as the tireless campaigner who persuaded Abraham Lincoln to proclaim Thanksgiving a national holiday.

Career

Widowed in 1822, Hale turned to writing to support her five children, publishing the novel Northwood in 1827. In 1828 she became editor of the Ladies' Magazine in Boston, and in 1837 she moved to Philadelphia to edit Godey's Lady's Book, which reached a circulation of about 150,000 — the most widely read magazine of its era.

Advocacy

Hale promoted education for women, opposed altering the traditional role of women in the home while championing their intellectual development, and raised funds to complete the Bunker Hill Monument and to preserve George Washington's Mount Vernon estate. From 1846 to 1863 she wrote to successive presidents urging a national Thanksgiving day; Lincoln issued the proclamation in 1863 during the Civil War.
