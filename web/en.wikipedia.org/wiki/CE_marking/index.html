CE marking

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The CE marking is an administrative marking that indicates conformity with health, safety, and environmental protection standards for products sold within the European Economic Area (EEA). The CE marking is also found on products sold outside the EEA that have been manufactured to EEA standards. This makes the CE marking recognizable worldwide even to people who are not familiar with the European Economic Area.

The CE mark is the manufacturer's declaration that the product meets EU standards for health, safety, and environmental protection. It is not a quality indicator or a certification mark, but rather a mark that a product can be legally sold in the EEA, whether it was manufactured there or elsewhere.

History

The marking was introduced under the "New Approach" to technical harmonisation laid down from 1985 onward, principally through directives adopted by the European Union and through Council Decision 93/465/EEC of 1993, which formalised the affixing and use of the mark. The letters "CE" are an abbreviation of the French phrase Conformité Européenne, meaning "European conformity".

Rules and obligations

Products that are covered by one or more New Legislative Framework directives — including toys, electrical equipment, machinery, medical devices, and construction products — must bear the mark before they can be placed on the EEA market. By affixing the CE marking, the manufacturer takes sole responsibility for the conformity of the product with all applicable requirements. Depending on the product category, conformity assessment may involve self-declaration or the involvement of a notified body.

Appearance

The mark consists of the stylised letters "C" and "E" in a prescribed proportional grid, and must be at least 5 millimetres in height unless the product is too small. It must be visible, legible, and indelible.
