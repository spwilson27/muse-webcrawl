Bakhsh

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A bakhshi of Khorasan performing with a dutar at a village gathering]

A bakhsh (Persian: بخشی; also romanised bakhshi) is a traditional bard and folk musician of greater Khorasan — northeastern Iran, Turkmenistan and parts of Afghanistan — who performs epic poetry, heroic tales and religious narratives while accompanying himself on the long-necked lute called the dutar (or, in Turkmen tradition, the dutar and gyjak).

Tradition

The word derives from a Central Asian term for a shaman-healer and singer, and the bakhsh historically combined the roles of musician, storyteller and healer at weddings, circumcisions and seasonal feasts. Performances may last whole nights, alternating sung verse with spoken narration drawn from epics such as the Gorogly (Köroğlu) cycle, the romances of Leyli and Majnun, and Persian classics like Ferdowsi's Shahnameh.

Recognition

Iran's bakhshi music of northern Khorasan was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2011, and Turkmen dutar making and bakhshi performance art were inscribed in 2021.
