East Lothian

East Lothian
Country: Scotland
Administrative centre: Haddington
Area: 679 km²
Population: about 112,000

East Lothian (Scots: East Lowden) is one of Scotland's 32 council areas, and a historic county also known as Haddingtonshire. It lies on the south shore of the Firth of Forth, east of Edinburgh, bordered by Midlothian and the Scottish Borders. Its county town is Haddington, and its largest town is Musselburgh.

The Bass Rock, off North Berwick, is home to a vast colony of northern gannets.

Geography

East Lothian combines a fertile coastal plain—long farmed for grain and potatoes—with the Lammermuir Hills in the south. The River Tyne flows through Haddington to the sea at Belhaven. The coast is dotted with sandy beaches and golf links; Muirfield, Gullane, North Berwick and Dunbar make the county famous as Scotland's "Golf Coast". Islands in the Firth of Forth include the Bass Rock, Craigleith and Fidra.

History

The area was part of the kingdom of Northumbria before passing to Scotland in the 10th century. It saw battles at Dunbar (1296 and 1650) and Prestonpans (1745), where Jacobite forces defeated a government army. Tantallon and Dirleton castles guarded the coast. The salt pans of Prestonpans, coal mining, and fishing at Dunbar were historic industries; John Muir, founder of the American national parks movement, was born in Dunbar in 1838.

Towns

Principal settlements include Musselburgh, Tranent, Prestonpans, Haddington, North Berwick, Dunbar and East Linton. Many residents commute to Edinburgh by rail and road.
