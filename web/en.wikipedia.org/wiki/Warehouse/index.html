Warehouse

A warehouse is a building for storing goods. Warehouses are used by manufacturers, importers, exporters, wholesalers, transport businesses and customs authorities, and are usually large plain buildings in industrial parks on the outskirts of cities, towns or villages.

Functions

Warehouses store raw materials, packing materials, spare parts, components and finished goods in the supply chain. Modern warehouses use pallet racking, loading docks, forklifts and warehouse management systems; goods may arrive by truck, rail, sea or air and are stored until needed for production or distribution. Facilities serving e-commerce order fulfilment are often called fulfilment centres or distribution centres.

History

Storage buildings date to antiquity; Roman horrea stored grain and goods in cities and ports. The great dockside warehouses of ports such as Liverpool, Manchester and Hamburg in the industrial era were built in distinctive load-bearing masonry styles. In the twentieth century, single-storey steel-frame construction, the shipping container and automation — including conveyor systems and automated storage and retrieval — transformed warehouse design.

Types

Common types include general merchandise warehouses, refrigerated (cold storage) warehouses, bonded warehouses (where customs duties are deferred), and bulk storage for liquids and commodities.
