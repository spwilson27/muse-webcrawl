Milwaukee

Downtown Milwaukee and the Quadracci Pavilion of the Milwaukee Art Museum.

Milwaukee is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Wisconsin and the county seat of Milwaukee County. With a population of about 577,000 at the 2020 census, it ranks among the 35 largest cities in the United States. Located on the western shore of Lake Michigan at the confluence of the Milwaukee, Menomonee, and Kinnickinnic rivers, the city is the economic and cultural center of the Milwaukee metropolitan area, home to roughly 1.6 million people.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Potawatomi, Menominee, and Ho-Chunk, long inhabited the area; the name derives from an Algonquian word often rendered as "gathering place by the water." French missionaries and fur traders arrived in the 17th and 18th centuries. Solomon Juneau, Laurent Solomon, and Byron Kilbourn founded rival settlements — Juneautown, Walker's Point, and Kilbourntown — which united as the city of Milwaukee in 1846 after the "Bridge War" of 1845.

Industry and culture

Nineteenth-century German immigration shaped Milwaukee's identity as America's brewing capital, home to Pabst, Schlitz, Blatz, and Miller — the last surviving as Molson Coors, maker of Miller Lite. The city also became a center of machinery and manufacturing and, later, of Harley-Davidson motorcycles. Milwaukee is known for Summerfest, one of the world's largest music festivals, its Milwaukee Brewers (MLB) and Milwaukee Bucks (NBA) teams, and the Milwaukee Art Museum, whose Santiago Calatrava–designed pavilion opened in 2001.
