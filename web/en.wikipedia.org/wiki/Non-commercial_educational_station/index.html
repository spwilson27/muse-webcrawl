Non-commercial educational station

A non-commercial educational station (NCE station) is a type of radio or television station in the United States that does not accept traditional commercial advertising in order to generate revenue and is licensed to a non-profit educational organisation. NCE stations are authorised by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) and include most public broadcasting and college radio stations.

Regulation

In the FM band, the frequencies from 88.1 to 91.9 MHz are reserved exclusively for NCE stations in the United States. NCE licensees may not broadcast advertisements, although they may air underwriting announcements that acknowledge donors or sponsors in restrained terms — identifying the supporter but omitting prices, promotions, slogans of a sales nature, or calls to action. Funding comes from listener contributions, institutional support from universities or school boards, grants (notably from the Corporation for Public Broadcasting), and underwriting.

History

The FCC reserved FM channels for education in 1945 and television channels in 1952, laying the groundwork for what became public broadcasting. The Public Broadcasting Act of 1967 created the Corporation for Public Broadcasting, and networks such as National Public Radio (founded 1970) and PBS supplied national programming to local NCE licensees. Religious broadcasters and community radio stations also operate extensively as NCE stations.
