North-south position

The north-south position is a ground grappling position in martial arts and combat sports such as Brazilian jiu-jitsu, judo and submission wrestling. The dominant practitioner lies on top of the opponent's chest facing toward the opponent's legs — head pointing "south" while the opponent's head points "north" — pinning the opponent's shoulders and head with the chest and arms while the opponent lies supine beneath them.

Technique

From the north-south position the top player controls the opponent's upper body and head, often gripping the belt, trousers or collar, and keeps their hips low to prevent the opponent from recovering guard. The position is related to side control and is sometimes considered a variation of it (in judo it corresponds to kami-shiho-gatame, the "upper four quarters hold"). Although few direct finishes come from the position itself, it is a strong platform for attacks.

Submissions and transitions

  • North-south choke — a choke applied with the arm wrapped around the opponent's neck, finished by sprawling the hips; popularised at the highest levels of Brazilian jiu-jitsu by Marcelo Garcia.
  • Kimura and armbar attacks — the opponent's arms are exposed as they push against the top player's body.
  • Transitions — the top player can move to side control, knee-on-belly, mount, or take the back if the opponent turns.

Escapes

The bottom player typically escapes by framing against the top player's hips, bridging and shrimping to recover guard or coming up to their knees.
