Kitora Tomb

The Kitora Tomb (キトラ古墳, Kitora Kofun) is an ancient burial mound in Asuka, Nara Prefecture, Japan, dating to the late 7th or early 8th century. It is renowned for its vivid wall paintings, among the oldest surviving mural art in Japan.

Paintings

The stone chamber's walls depict the Four Deities of Chinese cosmology — the Azure Dragon of the east, White Tiger of the west, Vermilion Bird of the south and Black Tortoise of the north — from which the tomb's name derives. The ceiling carries an astronomical star chart, one of the oldest in East Asia, and the walls also bore paintings of the twelve zodiac animals with human bodies.

Conservation

The murals, threatened by mould and deterioration, were removed from the chamber for conservation in the 2000s and 2010s and are exhibited at a dedicated facility near the site.
