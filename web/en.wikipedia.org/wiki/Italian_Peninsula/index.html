Italian Peninsula

The Italian Peninsula, famously shaped like a boot, between the Adriatic and Tyrrhenian seas.

The Italian Peninsula (Italian: Penisola italiana), also known as the Apennine Peninsula, is a peninsula extending from the southern Alps in the north to the central Mediterranean Sea in the south. Nicknamed lo Stivale ("the Boot") for its characteristic shape, it comprises much of Italy as well as the microstates of San Marino and Vatican City.

Geography

The peninsula is bounded by the Ligurian and Tyrrhenian seas to the west, the Ionian Sea to the southeast, and the Adriatic Sea to the east. The Apennine Mountains form its spine, running the length of the peninsula, while the Po Valley separates it from the Alps in the north. Major sub-peninsulas include Salento (the "heel"), Gargano (the "spur"), and Calabria (the "toe").

Political divisions

Almost the entire peninsula belongs to the Italian Republic. The independent enclaves of San Marino and Vatican City lie within it, and small parts of the geographic peninsula's northeastern fringe are sometimes assigned to Slovenia and Croatia. Together with the islands of Sicily and Sardinia and the surrounding archipelagos, the peninsula forms the core of the Italian geographic region.
