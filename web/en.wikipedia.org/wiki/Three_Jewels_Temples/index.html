The Three Jewels Temples (Korean: Sambo-sachal) are the three principal Buddhist temples of Korea, each representing one of the "Three Jewels" of Buddhism: the Buddha, the Dharma (teachings), and the Sangha (community).

The temples

Tongdosa in Yangsan represents the Buddha, because it enshrines relics of the Buddha — a robe, bowl, and bone fragments — rather than a Buddha statue in its main hall. Haeinsa on Mount Gaya represents the Dharma, as it houses the Tripitaka Koreana, the complete Buddhist scriptures carved on more than 80,000 wooden printing blocks. Songgwangsa in Suncheon represents the Sangha, having produced many eminent monks and serving as a major centre of Seon (Zen) practice.

Significance

Together the three temples are among the most important pilgrimage destinations for Korean Buddhists, and Haeinsa's Tripitaka Koreana depositories are inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
