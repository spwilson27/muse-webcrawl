Inclusive language

Inclusive language is language that seeks to avoid expressions that are perceived to exclude, marginalise, or insult particular groups of people, often in relation to gender, race, disability, age, or sexuality. Style guides issued by governments, universities, publishers, and professional bodies commonly include recommendations for inclusive usage.

Examples

Recommendations include using gender-neutral job titles (such as "firefighter" rather than "fireman", and "chair" or "chairperson" rather than "chairman"), using the singular "they" or recasting sentences to avoid generic masculine pronouns, preferring person-first language for disability ("person with a disability") in some contexts, and avoiding terms considered stereotyping or outdated.

Debate

Advocates argue that language shapes social attitudes and that inclusive terms foster equal treatment. Critics argue that some prescriptions are unnecessary, euphemistic, or an imposition on natural language change, and disputes over particular terms have featured in broader cultural and political debates.
