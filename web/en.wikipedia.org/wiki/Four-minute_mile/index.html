Four-minute mile

First achieved6 May 1954
AthleteRoger Bannister
Time3:59.4
VenueIffley Road Track, Oxford

A four-minute mile is the completion of a mile run (1,760 yards, or 1,609.344 metres) in four minutes or less. It was first achieved on 6 May 1954 by English athlete Roger Bannister, in 3:59.4, at the Iffley Road Track in Oxford, England.

The barrier

Through the 1940s the world record crept toward four minutes under Swedish rivals Gunder Hägg and Arne Andersson, stalling at 4:01.3. Many commentators speculated the barrier might be a physiological limit. Bannister, a medical student training on limited schedules, planned the attempt with pacemakers Chris Brasher and Chris Chataway.

Aftermath

Australian John Landy broke the record just 46 days later. The feat became a cultural shorthand for breaking a supposedly impossible barrier, and sub-four-minute miles are now routine among elite male middle-distance runners; the current world record stands below 3:44.

Women's running

The women's equivalent milestones came later; Diane Leather was the first woman under five minutes in 1954, and the women's mile record has since fallen well under 4:10.
