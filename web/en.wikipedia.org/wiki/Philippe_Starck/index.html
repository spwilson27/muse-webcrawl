Philippe Patrick Starck (born 18 January 1949) is a French industrial architect and designer known since the start of his career in the 1980s for his wide range of designs, including interior design, architecture, household objects, furniture, boats and other vehicles. He is one of the most famous and prolific designers of the late 20th and early 21st centuries.

Career

Starck first attracted attention in 1982 when he redesigned the private apartments of President François Mitterrand at the Élysée Palace, and with his interiors for the Café Costes in Paris (1984). He went on to design interiors for Ian Schrager's boutique hotels, including the Royalton and Paramount in New York and the Delano in Miami, helping to define the "boutique hotel" aesthetic of the 1990s.

Product design

Starck's products combine sculptural form with mass-market manufacture. His best-known object is the Juicy Salif lemon squeezer for Alessi (1990), a cast-aluminium squeezer on tall legs that became an icon of postmodern design despite Starck's own remark that it was "not meant to squeeze lemons" but "to start conversations". Other celebrated works include the Louis Ghost chair for Kartell (2002), a baroque armchair moulded in transparent polycarbonate, and designs for Duravit, Flos, Kartell and many others.

Architecture and later work

Starck's architectural projects include the Asahi Beer Hall in Tokyo (1989), topped by a giant golden flame, and numerous hotels and restaurants worldwide. He has also designed motor yachts, including the A and Venus, and has promoted "democratic design" — improving quality while lowering prices — and ecological design, arguing for products designed to last and be shared rather than owned.
