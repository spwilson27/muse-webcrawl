Juan José Gerardi Conedera

Juan José Gerardi Conedera (27 December 1922 - 26 April 1998) was a Guatemalan Roman Catholic bishop and human rights defender. He was murdered two days after presenting the Recovery of Historical Memory (REMHI) report documenting atrocities committed during Guatemala's civil war.

Life

Gerardi was ordained a priest in 1946 and worked in rural parishes before becoming bishop of Verapaz in 1967. In 1974 he was appointed bishop of Santa Cruz del Quiché, the diocese at the heart of the Ixil region. As the army's counterinsurgency campaign intensified, he protested the massacres of indigenous villagers; after an attempt on his life and threats against the clergy, he closed the diocese in 1980 and was forced into exile in Costa Rica. He returned in 1982 and later served as auxiliary bishop of the Archdiocese of Guatemala.

REMHI report

After the 1996 peace accords ended the 36-year civil war, Gerardi directed the archdiocese's Recovery of Historical Memory project (REMHI), which gathered thousands of testimonies. Its report, Guatemala: Nunca Más ("Never Again"), presented on 24 April 1998, documented some 55,000 human rights violations and attributed the great majority to the army and state-backed paramilitaries.

Murder and trial

On the night of 26 April 1998, two days after the report's launch, Gerardi was beaten to death with a concrete block in the garage of his parish house in Guatemala City. After a long and controversial investigation, in 2001 three military men - Colonel Byron Lima Estrada and his son Captain Byron Lima Oliva, and army specialist Obdulio Villanueva - were convicted of the murder, along with Father Mario Orantes, who received a lesser sentence as an accomplice; key convictions were upheld on appeal. The case remains a landmark in Guatemala's struggle against impunity.
