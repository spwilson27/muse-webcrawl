Pachysandra

Pachysandra is a genus of five species of evergreen perennials and subshrubs in the boxwood family, Buxaceae. The species are native to eastern Asia and to southeastern North America. The name derives from the Greek pachys ("thick") and andra ("male"), referring to the thick stamens of the flowers.

Species and distribution

The most familiar species are Pachysandra terminalis (Japanese spurge), native to Japan and northern China, and Pachysandra procumbens (Allegheny spurge), native to the southeastern United States. Asian species also include P. axillaris and P. stylosa.

Cultivation and use

Pachysandra terminalis is widely planted as a shade-tolerant groundcover, spreading by rhizomes to form a dense carpet of glossy, toothed leaves about 20–30 cm tall, with small white flower spikes in spring. Popular cultivars include 'Green Carpet' and the variegated 'Variegata'. It is valued for covering ground beneath trees where grass fails, but it can spread aggressively, is susceptible to boxwood blight and Volutella blight, and is considered invasive in parts of North America, where the native P. procumbens is increasingly recommended as an alternative.

Description

Plants are monoecious, with male and female flowers in the same spike-like inflorescence, the males above with conspicuous thick white stamens. The fruit is a small drupe, seldom produced in cultivation. Leaves are alternate or clustered at stem tips, leathery, and coarsely toothed.
