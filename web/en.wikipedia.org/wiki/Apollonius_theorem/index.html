Apollonius's theorem

In geometry, Apollonius's theorem relates the length of a median of a triangle to the lengths of its sides. It states that "the sum of the squares of any two sides of any triangle equals twice the square on half the third side, together with twice the square on the median bisecting the third side".

Statement

For a triangle with sides a, b, c and a median m drawn to side a, the theorem gives: b2 + c2 = 2m2 + 2(a/2)2, equivalently b2 + c2 = 2m2 + a2/2.

The theorem is a special case of Stewart's theorem, and is equivalent to the parallelogram law when the triangle is doubled to form a parallelogram. It is named after the ancient Greek geometer Apollonius of Perga.
