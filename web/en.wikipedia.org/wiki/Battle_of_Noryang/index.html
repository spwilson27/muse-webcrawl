Battle of Noryang

The Battle of Noryang, fought on 16 December 1598, was the final major naval battle of the Imjin War (Japanese invasions of Korea, 1592–1598). A combined Joseon Korean and Ming Chinese fleet under Admiral Yi Sun-sin and Chen Lin attacked the withdrawing Japanese fleet in the Noryang Strait. The allies won a major victory, but Admiral Yi was killed in the fighting.

Background

Following the death of Toyotomi Hideyoshi in September 1598, Japanese forces in Korea began a general withdrawal. Yi Sun-sin, restored to command after the near-catastrophe of Chilcheollyang, had rebuilt the Korean navy and won at Myeongnyang in 1597. In December 1598 the allied fleet moved to blockade the Japanese garrison at Suncheon, whose commander Konishi Yukinaga sought escape by sea, aided by a relief fleet under Shimazu Yoshihiro.

The battle

Attacking before dawn on 16 December, the allied fleet surprised the Japanese in the strait between Namhaedo and the mainland. Korean panokseon and turtle ships and Ming war junks used cannon fire and fire arrows to devastating effect. During the pursuit and fighting near the end of the battle, Yi Sun-sin was struck by an arquebus ball and died; he is said to have ordered his death concealed so as not to break his men's morale. Roughly half the Japanese fleet was destroyed, though Shimazu and Konishi escaped.

Legacy

Noryang ended major fighting in the Imjin War; the remaining Japanese forces evacuated Korea by the end of 1598. Yi Sun-sin is revered in Korea as a national hero, and the battle is a fixture of Korean historical memory and cinema.
