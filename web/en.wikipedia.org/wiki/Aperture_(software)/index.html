Aperture (software)

Aperture was a professional image organiser and editor developed by Apple Inc. for Mac OS X. Announced in 2005, it was aimed at photographers working with large numbers of digital images, especially RAW files, and competed with Adobe Photoshop Lightroom.

Features

Aperture provided non-destructive editing, so adjustments were stored as instructions rather than permanently altering original files. It offered tools for importing, rating, keywording and searching photographs; Loupe and light-table views for comparing images; and adjustment brushes, curves and colour controls. Libraries could be backed up in “vaults” on external drives.

History

Version 2, released in 2008, improved performance and lowered the price. Aperture 3 followed in 2010 with features such as Faces and Places, borrowed from iPhoto. In 2014 Apple announced that development of Aperture and iPhoto would end in favour of the Photos app; Aperture was removed from sale in 2015, and later macOS versions dropped compatibility.
