Annandale-on-Hudson is a hamlet in the town of Red Hook in Dutchess County, New York, United States, located on the east bank of the Hudson River. It is best known as the home of Bard College.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Mohican people. The hamlet takes its name from Annandale, the estate of the Scottish-born merchant John Bard, who with his wife Margaret founded St. Stephen's College in 1860 — the institution that later became Bard College. An earlier name for the settlement was Cedar Hill.

Bard College

Bard College, a private liberal arts college, dominates the hamlet. Its campus includes the Richard B. Fisher Center for the Performing Arts, designed by Frank Gehry, and the Levy Economics Institute. The college's presence gives the small hamlet an unusually rich cultural life, including the Bard Music Festival and summers at the Spiegeltent.

Geography and transport

Annandale-on-Hudson lies along River Road (County Route 103), roughly 100 miles north of New York City. The nearest Amtrak station is at Rhinecliff, a few miles south. The Saw Kill stream flows through the hamlet into the Hudson.
