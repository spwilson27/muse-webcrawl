Vergence

Vergence is the simultaneous movement of both eyes in opposite directions to obtain or maintain single binocular vision. When the eyes rotate towards each other (convergence) the visual axes intersect at a nearer point, as when looking at an object close to the face; when they rotate away from each other (divergence), the axes point towards a more distant target, up to parallel for optical infinity. Vergence is one of the disconjugate (disjunctive) eye movements, contrasting with conjugate version movements in which both eyes move in the same direction.

Types and control

Vergence is driven by several components: fusional vergence, which eliminates retinal disparity to fuse the two images; accommodative vergence, linked to focusing the lens through the near triad (accommodation, convergence and pupillary constriction); proximal vergence, cued by awareness of nearness; and tonic vergence, the resting posture of the eyes. The AC/A ratio quantifies the convergence produced per unit of accommodation and is clinically important in evaluating strabismus. Vergence magnitude is measured in prism dioptres, and the midpoint of eye rotation is described relative to Listing's plane.

Disorders and applications

Failure of vergence causes or contributes to double vision (diplopia), eye strain and reading difficulty. Recognised disorders include convergence insufficiency — difficulty sustaining convergence for near work, with symptoms of fatigue and words moving on the page — convergence excess, and divergence insufficiency. Treatments include prism glasses, orthoptic exercises (vision therapy), and surgery in selected cases. In virtual and augmented reality, the vergence–accommodation conflict — eyes converge on virtual objects at varying depths while the lenses focus on a fixed screen — is a major source of visual discomfort.
