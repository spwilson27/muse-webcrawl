Juz'

Arabicجزء (juzʾ)
Meaning"Part" or "portion"
Number in Quran30
Further divisionEach juzʾ = 2 aḥzāb; each ḥizb = 4 quarters

A juzʾ (Arabic: جزء, plural ajzāʾ) is one of thirty roughly equal parts into which the Quran is divided. The division is not thematic but quantitative, made to facilitate completing a recitation of the entire Quran over a set period — most commonly one juzʾ per day across the thirty days of Ramadan.

Structure

Each juzʾ is subdivided into two aḥzāb (singular ḥizb), and each ḥizb into four quarters (rubʿ), giving a fine-grained scheme used in printed muṣḥafs to guide daily and weekly recitation. Juzʾ boundaries often fall in the middle of a surah. The divisions are marked in most editions with marginal symbols and are commonly referenced by their opening words — for example, the first juzʾ is called Alīf Lām Mīm, the sixteenth Qāla al-malaʾ, and the last, thirtieth juzʾ is widely known as Juzʾ ʿAmma after its opening word.

Use

The thirty-juzʾ scheme structures memorisation schedules, Ramadan taraweeh prayers (in which many mosques aim to complete the Quran over the month), and the layout of most printed Qurans.
