Parícutin

Parícutin (or Volcán de Parícutin) is a cinder-cone volcano in Michoacán, Mexico, famous for having been observed from its birth in a cornfield on 20 February 1943 until the end of its eruption in 1952. It is often counted among the seven natural wonders of the world and is one of the youngest volcanoes on Earth.

Eruption

Farmer Dionisio Pulido witnessed the ground crack open in his field near the village of Parícutin. Within a day the cone was 50 metres high; within a week, over 150 metres. Lava flows buried the villages of Parícutin and San Juan Parangaricutiro — the church tower of San Juan still rises from the hardened lava — and ash fell as far as Mexico City. No one was killed directly. The cone eventually reached 424 metres above the surrounding plain (2,808 m above sea level).

Significance

Parícutin gave volcanologists their first opportunity to document the complete life cycle of a volcano, and it remains a classic case study in the Michoacán–Guanajuato volcanic field. The site is now a popular hiking and horseback destination.
