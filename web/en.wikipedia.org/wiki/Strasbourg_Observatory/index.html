Strasbourg Observatory

The Strasbourg Observatory dome

The Strasbourg Observatory (French: Observatoire astronomique de Strasbourg) is an astronomical observatory in Strasbourg, France, associated with the University of Strasbourg. It was founded in 1881, when the city was part of the German Empire, and is notable both for its historic refracting telescope and, since the 1970s, as the home of one of the world's leading astronomical data centres.

History

Following the Franco-Prussian War, the German imperial government established a new university and observatory in Strasbourg. The observatory was inaugurated in 1881 and equipped with a 49 cm (19-inch) refractor built by Repsold and Reinfelder & Hertel, which was among the largest refractors in the world at the time and remains one of the largest in France. The building's great dome and meridian room survive as important examples of 19th-century observatory architecture; the site also houses a planetarium.

Centre de données astronomiques de Strasbourg

The observatory hosts the Centre de données astronomiques de Strasbourg (CDS), founded in 1972 under the direction of Jean Delhaye and developed by later directors including Pierre Lantos and Daniel Egret. The CDS operates services used worldwide by astronomers, including the SIMBAD astronomical database, the VizieR catalogue service, and the Aladin interactive sky atlas. Because of this role, Strasbourg is often described as a world capital of astronomical data.

Research

Modern research groups at the observatory work on stellar populations, galaxies, interstellar matter and high-energy astrophysics, and contribute to survey and space missions. The observatory's historical collections, including instruments and a museum of astronomy, are preserved on site.
