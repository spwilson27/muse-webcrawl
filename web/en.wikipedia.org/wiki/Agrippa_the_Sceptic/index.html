Agrippa the Sceptic

Agrippa the Sceptic was a Pyrrhonist philosopher who probably lived in the 1st or 2nd century CE. He is known almost exclusively through the writings of Sextus Empiricus and Diogenes Laertius.

Agrippa is credited with formulating the five modes (tropoi) of scepticism: disagreement, infinite regress, relativity, hypothesis, and circular reasoning. These modes are designed to show that any claim can be opposed by an equally strong counter-claim, leading the sceptic to suspend judgement (epoché).

The three modes attributed to the earlier Aenesidemus together with Agrippa's five — sometimes extended to the 'Agrippa's trilemma' of regress, circularity and dogmatic assumption — became the central argument-form of ancient scepticism and were revived in early modern philosophy.
