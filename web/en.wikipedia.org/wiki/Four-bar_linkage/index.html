Four-bar linkage

The four-bar linkage, also called a four-bar mechanism, is the simplest closed-chain movable linkage. It consists of four rigid bodies, called bars or links, connected in a loop by four revolute joints. One link is fixed to the ground (the frame), one is the input link or crank that rotates, one is the output link, and the fourth, the coupler, connects the moving links; a point on the coupler traces a curve called a coupler curve.

Grashof condition

The Grashof condition predicts the rotation behaviour of the links based on their lengths. If s is the shortest link, l the longest, and p and q the other two, then when s + l ≤ p + q the linkage is a Grashof linkage and at least one link can make a complete revolution. Depending on which link is fixed, a Grashof four-bar can act as a crank-rocker (shortest link a crank), a double crank or drag link (shortest link fixed), or a double rocker (shortest link the coupler, both side links oscillating). If s + l > p + q, no link can rotate fully.

Applications

Four-bar linkages are ubiquitous in machinery: they appear in bicycle suspensions, windscreen wipers, adjustable lamps, excavator arms, locking pliers, and prosthetic knee joints. Famous special cases include the parallel linkage used in drafting machines, the Chebyshev linkage, and the Watt linkage used in vehicle suspensions. When one revolute joint is replaced by a prismatic joint, the result is the slider-crank mechanism used in reciprocating engines.
