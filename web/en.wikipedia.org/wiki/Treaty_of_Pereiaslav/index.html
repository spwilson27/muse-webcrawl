Treaty of Pereiaslav

The Treaty of Pereiaslav (also called the Pereiaslav Agreement) was concluded in January 1654 during the Pereiaslav Council, when the Cossack Hetmanate under Hetman Bohdan Khmelnytsky swore an oath of allegiance to Tsar Alexis of Russia. The agreement was made in the course of the Khmelnytsky Uprising against the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.

Background

Since 1648 Khmelnytsky's Cossacks had been at war with Poland. Seeking a powerful protector, the Hetman turned to Orthodox Russia. At the council in Pereiaslav on 18 January 1654, Cossack officers and representatives of Ukrainian towns swore loyalty to the Tsar, and in March 1654 the "March Articles" set out conditions that preserved broad Cossack autonomy in exchange for Russian military support.

Interpretations

The nature of the agreement has been contested ever since: Russian and later Soviet historiography often described it as a "reunification" of Ukraine with Russia, while Ukrainian historians have typically characterized it as a military alliance or a personal union that Russia progressively violated by curtailing Hetmanate autonomy over the following century.

Consequences

The treaty led directly to the Russo-Polish War of 1654–1667, ended by the Truce of Andrusovo, which divided Ukraine along the Dnieper River between Russia and Poland. The 300th anniversary of the treaty was elaborately celebrated in the Soviet Union in 1954, the occasion on which Crimea was transferred from the Russian SFSR to the Ukrainian SSR.
