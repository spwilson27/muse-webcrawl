Financial Services Authority

The Financial Services Authority (FSA) was a quasi-judicial body accountable for the regulation of the financial services industry in the United Kingdom between 2001 and 2013. It was founded as the Securities and Investments Board in 1985.

History

The FSA unified previously fragmented regulators into a single "super-regulator" under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. Following criticism of its performance during the financial crisis, the government announced its abolition; on 1 April 2013 its functions were split between the Financial Conduct Authority and the Prudential Regulation Authority.

Role

The FSA authorised and supervised banks, insurers, and investment firms, enforced market rules, and supervised the listing of securities.
