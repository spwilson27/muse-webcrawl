Kathleen Hanna (born 12 November 1968) is an American singer, musician, and feminist activist. She is the frontwoman of the punk band Bikini Kill and a pioneering figure of the riot grrrl movement of the early 1990s. She later founded the electro-punk group Le Tigre and the band The Julie Ruin.

Early life

Hanna was born in Portland, Oregon, and grew up in Calverton, Maryland. Her mother introduced her to feminist literature, including works by Gloria Steinem. She attended The Evergreen State College in Olympia, Washington, where she studied photography and began performing spoken-word pieces; she also co-founded an independent art gallery, Reko Muse, that hosted early riot grrrl meetings.

Bikini Kill and riot grrrl

In 1990 Hanna formed Bikini Kill with Tobi Vail, Kathi Wilcox, and Billy Karren. The band became synonymous with riot grrrl, a feminist punk movement that encouraged girls to start bands, publish zines, and speak out about sexual violence, incest, and inequality. Hanna was famed for writing "SLUT" on her stomach during performances and for demanding "girls to the front" at shows. The band released albums including Pussy Whipped (1993) and Reject All American (1996) before disbanding in 1997; they later reunited for tours beginning in 2019. Hanna is also credited with suggesting the title of Nirvana's "Smells Like Teen Spirit."

Later work

Recording as Julie Ruin, Hanna released a lo-fi solo album in 1998. She then formed Le Tigre with Johanna Fateman and JD Samson, blending punk politics with electronic dance music on songs such as "Deceptacon." With The Julie Ruin she released Run Fast (2013) and Hit Reset (2016). The 2013 documentary The Punk Singer chronicled her career and her struggle with Lyme disease, which had forced her to step back from touring. Her memoir, Rebel Girl: My Life as a Feminist Punk, was published in 2024.
