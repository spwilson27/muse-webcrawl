Waikato (Māori pronunciation: [ˈwaikato]) is a region of the upper North Island of New Zealand. It covers the Waikato District, Waipā District, Matamata-Piako District, South Waikato District and Hamilton City, as well as Hauraki, Coromandel Peninsula, the northern King Country, much of the Taupō District, and parts of Rotorua District. It is governed by the Waikato Regional Council.

Geography

The region is named after the Waikato River, New Zealand's longest, which flows for 425 kilometres from Lake Taupō through the Waikato Plains to the Tasman Sea at Port Waikato. The fertile plains, formed from river and volcanic deposits, support some of the country's richest dairy farming. To the east lie the Kaimai and Coromandel ranges; to the west, the Hakarimata and Pirongia ranges. Hamilton, the region's main city and New Zealand's fourth-largest urban area, sits on the river in the middle of the plains.

History

The Waikato is the homeland of the Waikato-Tainui iwi and was the centre of the Kīngitanga (Māori King Movement), founded at Pukawa in 1858 with Pōtatau Te Wherowhero as the first Māori king. Refusal to sell land and the assertion of Māori sovereignty led to the Invasion of the Waikato (1863–64), the largest campaign of the New Zealand Wars, after which more than half a million hectares of Māori land were confiscated (raupatu). In 1995 Waikato-Tainui became the first iwi to settle its historical claims with the Crown, receiving an apology and $170 million in redress.

Economy

Dairy farming is the backbone of the regional economy — Waikato is home to Fonterra's largest processing sites and the dairy research centre at Ruakura — alongside forestry, thoroughbred horse breeding (the Matamata-Cambridge district), hydroelectric generation on the Waikato River, tourism (including the Waitomo Caves and Hobbiton Movie Set), and the University of Waikato in Hamilton.
