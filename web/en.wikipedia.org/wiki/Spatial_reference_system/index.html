Spatial reference system

A spatial reference system (SRS) or coordinate reference system (CRS) is a framework used to precisely measure locations on the surface of the Earth as coordinates. It is thus the foundation of most geographic information systems (GIS) and other geospatial applications. Assigning data a spatial reference system is called spatial referencing or georeferencing.

Components

A complete coordinate reference system combines:

  • a coordinate system — the axes and units in which coordinates are expressed (for example, latitude and longitude in degrees, or easting and northing in metres);
  • a datum — a model of the Earth's shape (an ellipsoid or spheroid) positioned relative to the actual Earth, together with a prime meridian, which realises the coordinate system on the ground.

Reference systems may be geographic (angular coordinates on an ellipsoid, such as WGS 84 used by GPS), projected (positions on a flat map produced by a map projection, such as UTM or national grids), vertical (heights relative to a geoid or datum), or compound, combining horizontal and vertical components. Transforms and conversions between systems are essential when integrating datasets.

Identifiers

Standardised identifiers make reference systems interchangeable between software. The best known are the codes of the EPSG Geodetic Parameter Dataset (maintained by the International Association of Oil & Gas Producers' geomatics committee): EPSG:4326 denotes WGS 84 latitude/longitude and EPSG:3857 the Web Mercator used by online maps. Systems are also routinely encoded as WKT (Well-Known Text) or PROJ strings in data formats and standards from the Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC) and ISO 19111.

Why it matters

Coordinates are meaningless without their reference system: the same pair of numbers can place a point hundreds of metres apart under different datums, and errors in spatial referencing have produced misaligned maps, mislocated infrastructure and failed data integration. Modern practice increasingly uses dynamic reference frames that account for tectonic motion over time.
