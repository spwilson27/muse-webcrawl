Württemberg

Württemberg is a historical region in southwestern Germany, centred on Stuttgart and the Neckar river, which forms the eastern part of the modern state of Baden-Württemberg. It developed from a medieval county into a duchy (1495), an electorate (1803), and a kingdom (1806–1918), then a free state of the Weimar Republic.

History

The House of Württemberg traces its origins to the 11th century, taking its name from Wirtemberg Castle near Stuttgart. Count Eberhard I raised Württemberg to a duchy in 1495. The region was devastated during the Thirty Years' War, expanded greatly under Napoleon, and became a kingdom under Frederick I in 1806. After the First World War it became the Free People's State of Württemberg within Germany.

Geography and culture

The region includes the Swabian Jura (Schwäbische Alb), the Black Forest's eastern foothills, and wine-growing Neckar and Rems valleys. Its people speak Swabian dialects, and Stuttgart became a centre of the automotive industry, home to Daimler and Porsche.
