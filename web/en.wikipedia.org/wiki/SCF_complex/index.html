SCF complex

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The SCF complex (SKP1–CUL1–F-box protein) is a multi-protein E3 ubiquitin ligase complex that catalyses the ubiquitination of proteins destined for proteasomal degradation. It is one of the best-characterised members of the cullin–RING ubiquitin ligase family and plays a central role in the control of the cell cycle and many other cellular processes.

Structure

The core of the complex consists of the scaffold protein cullin 1 (CUL1), the RING-box protein RBX1 (also called ROC1), which recruits the ubiquitin-conjugating (E2) enzyme, and the adaptor protein SKP1. SKP1 binds an interchangeable substrate-recognition subunit, an F-box protein, which determines which target proteins the complex ubiquitinates. Dozens of F-box proteins exist in humans, including SKP2, β-TrCP (FBXW1) and FBXW7, giving the SCF family a wide range of substrates.

Function

SCF complexes mark key regulatory proteins for destruction by the 26S proteasome, including cyclins, cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors such as p27Kip1, and signalling proteins such as β-catenin and IκB. Substrates frequently must be phosphorylated before they are recognised by their F-box protein, linking ubiquitination to signalling pathways. The activity of the complex is regulated by neddylation of the cullin subunit and by the COP9 signalosome.

Deregulation of SCF components is implicated in cancer: FBXW7 is a tumour suppressor that is frequently mutated, while SKP2 is often overexpressed in tumours.
