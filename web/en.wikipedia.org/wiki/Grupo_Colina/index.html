Grupo Colina

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Grupo Colina (the Colina Group) was a Peruvian military death squad active during the presidency of Alberto Fujimori in the early 1990s. Composed of members of the Peruvian Army drawn from the Army Intelligence Service (SIE), the group carried out assassinations, kidnappings, and forced disappearances of people suspected of links to the Shining Path and MRTA insurgencies. It operated under the knowledge of the National Intelligence Service (SIN), headed by Vladimiro Montesinos.

Operations

The group is best known for two massacres. In the Barrios Altos massacre of 3 November 1991, gunmen burst into a party in the Barrios Altos neighbourhood of Lima and killed 15 people, including an eight-year-old boy, apparently mistaking the gathering for a Shining Path meeting. In the La Cantuta massacre of July 1992, a professor and nine students from La Cantuta university were abducted and murdered; their burned remains were later discovered in clandestine graves. The group was named "Colina" after the Peruvian military hero Francisco Bolognesi — reportedly a code name taken from a hill (colina) or an acronym.

Trials and legacy

An initial prosecution in the mid-1990s was halted by an amnesty law passed by Fujimori's congress in 1995. After Fujimori's fall in 2000, the Inter-American Court of Human Rights ruled in the Barrios Altos case (2001) that the amnesty laws were incompatible with the American Convention and without legal effect. Trials resumed, and in 2009 Fujimori himself was convicted of ordering the Barrios Altos and La Cantuta killings and sentenced to 25 years in prison. Members of Grupo Colina, including its commander Santiago Martín Rivas, also received long sentences. The case became a landmark in international human rights law on amnesties for grave crimes.
