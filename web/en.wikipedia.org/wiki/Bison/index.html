A bison (pl.: bison) is a large bovine in the genus Bison within the tribe Bovini. Two extant species are recognised: the American bison (Bison bison), found only in North America, and the European bison (Bison bonasus, also called the wisent), found in Europe and the Caucasus. Though colloquially called "buffalo" in North America, bison are only distantly related to the true buffalo (the wild water buffalo and African buffalo).

Description

Bison are among the largest terrestrial animals in North America and Europe. American bison bulls can weigh up to about 900 kg (2,000 lb) and stand up to 1.8 m (6 ft) at the shoulder hump. Both species have massive heads with curved horns, shaggy coats, and a pronounced shoulder hump of muscle over elongated vertebrae. They are grazers, historically migrating in large herds across grasslands.

History and conservation

American bison once numbered in the tens of millions across the Great Plains and were central to the cultures and economies of many Indigenous peoples. Market hunting and a deliberate policy of extermination during the 19th century reduced the population to a few hundred by 1884. Conservation efforts by ranchers, the American Bison Society, and governments rebuilt numbers; today about 500,000 exist, mostly in commercial herds, with smaller conservation herds in parks such as Yellowstone. The American bison was named the national mammal of the United States in 2016.

The European bison was hunted to extinction in the wild by the early 20th century — the last wild wisent was killed in the Caucasus in 1927 — but survived in zoos and was reintroduced, notably to Białowieża Forest in Poland and Belarus; several thousand now live in free-ranging herds.

Behaviour

Bison are social, forming herds that merge during the summer rut, when bulls bellow and fight for mates. Despite their bulk they are agile, can run at speeds around 55 km/h (35 mph), and are known for unpredictable behaviour around humans.
