Timothy Douglas White (born August 24, 1950) is an American paleoanthropologist and professor of integrative biology at the University of California, Berkeley. He is best known for leading the Middle Awash research project in Ethiopia's Afar Depression and for his work on Ardipithecus ramidus, and he played a major role in the analysis of the "Lucy" skeleton from Hadar.

Career

White studied at the University of California, Riverside, and earned his PhD in biological anthropology from the University of Michigan in 1977, working with Milford Wolpoff. As a postdoctoral researcher he worked with the Leakeys in Kenya and with Donald Johanson on the Hadar fossils, helping clean and describe Lucy. He joined the Berkeley faculty in 1977 and became co-director of its Human Evolution Research Center.

Discoveries

White's Middle Awash team has described a near-complete sequence of hominin evolution from one valley, including Ardipithecus kadabba (5.2–5.8 million years), Australopithecus garhi (2.5 million years, found near early stone tools and butchered bones), Homo erectus from Bouri, and the Herto skulls of early Homo sapiens (c. 160,000 years), among the oldest known remains of anatomically modern humans. His most celebrated find, announced in full in 2009 after 15 years of reconstruction, is "Ardi", a 4.4-million-year-old Ardipithecus ramidus skeleton that reshaped ideas about the earliest hominins.
