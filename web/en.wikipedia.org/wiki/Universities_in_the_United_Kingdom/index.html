Universities in the United Kingdom

Universities in the United Kingdom have generally been instituted by royal charter, papal bull, act of parliament, or an instrument of government under the Further and Higher Education Act 1992 or the Higher Education and Research Act 2017. The United Kingdom has more than 160 higher education institutions with university status, including some of the oldest and most highly ranked universities in the world. Degree-awarding powers and university title are regulated, and most universities are largely publicly funded, with students contributing through tuition fees supported by government loans.

History

The University of Oxford, founded in the twelfth century, is the oldest university in the English-speaking world, followed by the University of Cambridge, founded in 1209. Scotland's ancient universities—St Andrews, Glasgow, Aberdeen, and Edinburgh—were founded in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries. In the nineteenth century, new civic institutions such as University College London and Durham University broadened access beyond the ancient universities. The "red brick" civic universities of the industrial cities followed, and after the Robbins Report of 1963 the sector expanded rapidly with the "plate glass" universities. In 1992 the polytechnics were granted university status, creating a unified sector.

Organisation and admissions

Undergraduate admissions are handled largely through UCAS, and students typically apply to study a single subject for a three-year bachelor's degree in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, or a four-year degree in Scotland. Universities are autonomous institutions, though publicly funded teaching and research are overseen by bodies such as the Office for Students in England and UK Research and Innovation. The Russell Group represents 24 research-intensive universities, while other mission groups represent different parts of the sector.

Rankings and reputation

British universities regularly appear near the top of international rankings, with Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial College London, and UCL among the most prominent. Higher education is also a significant British export, attracting hundreds of thousands of international students each year.
