The Royal Banner of Scotland, also known as the Lion Rampant, is the personal banner of the monarch of Scotland. It displays a red lion rampant, with blue tongue and claws, on a gold field, surrounded by a red double border (tressure) decorated with fleurs-de-lis. Historically it was the banner of the King of Scots, and tradition attributes its first use to William the Lion in the 12th century, though its first recorded appearance is on the seal of Alexander II in 1222.

Use

Unlike the national flag of Scotland — the blue-and-white Saltire — the Royal Banner is the personal property of the monarch, and its use by others is restricted. Since the Union of the Crowns in 1603, the lion rampant has formed part of the royal arms of the United Kingdom, appearing in the first and fourth quarters of the Royal Standard as used in Scotland (where the Scottish quarter takes precedence). The banner is flown above the Palace of Holyroodhouse and Balmoral Castle when the monarch is in residence.

Legal status

An Act of the Parliament of Scotland in 1672 made it an offence for any subject to use the lion rampant without authority, a restriction still enforced by the Court of the Lord Lyon, Scotland's heraldic authority. Despite this, the Lion Rampant is widely and informally used by Scottish football and rugby supporters as a symbol of Scottish identity — a usage the Lord Lyon has generally tolerated for individual, non-commercial use.
