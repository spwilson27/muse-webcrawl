Polyphenism

FieldDevelopmental biology, ecology
DefinitionMultiple phenotypes from one genotype via environmental cues
ExamplesCaste in ants, seasonal forms in butterflies

Polyphenism is the phenomenon where two or more distinct phenotypes are produced by the same genotype, with the environment determining which phenotype develops. It is a striking form of phenotypic plasticity, often producing discrete alternatives rather than a continuous range.

Examples

In ants and termites, genetically identical larvae develop into workers, soldiers, or reproductives depending on nutrition and pheromones. The butterfly Bicyclus anynana produces large eyespots in the wet season and cryptic forms in the dry season. Some nematodes develop either a normal mouth or a predatory morph depending on crowding and food.

Mechanism

Environmental cues — temperature, photoperiod, diet, density — are transduced by hormonal systems, especially ecdysteroids and juvenile hormone in insects, switching development between alternative pathways. Inducible defenses in Daphnia, which grow helmets in response to predator cues, are another well-studied case.

Evolutionary significance

Polyphenisms allow a single genotype to exploit multiple niches or respond to seasonal conditions, and they illustrate how environmental regulation of development can drive evolutionary novelty.
