Daggerboard

Daggerboard
TypeRetractable keel / centreboard variant
MotionSlides vertically in a trunk
UseDinghies, catamarans, windsurfers

A daggerboard is a retractable centreboard used in sailing craft that slides vertically, like a dagger, through a slot (the daggerboard trunk or case) in the hull. When lowered it provides lateral resistance that prevents the boat from being pushed sideways by the wind; when raised it reduces drag and lets the boat operate in shallow water or be beached.

Comparison with centreboards

Unlike a pivoting centreboard, which swings up into its case, a daggerboard moves straight up and down and usually must be lifted fully out or partly raised manually. Because it does not pivot, a daggerboard struck by an underwater obstacle does not swing back, which can damage the trunk, but the design is simpler and can be more hydrodynamically efficient.

Applications

Daggerboards are common on sailing dinghies such as the Laser/ILCA and Sunfish (which uses a daggerboard-like board), on beach catamarans, and in early windsurfing boards. High-performance racing yachts and foiling boats also use daggerboard-like appendages, sometimes curved, that generate lift.
