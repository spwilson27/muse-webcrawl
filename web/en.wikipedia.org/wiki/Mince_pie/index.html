A mince pie (also called a mincemeat pie in North America, and a fruit mince pie in Australia and New Zealand) is a sweet pie of English origin filled with mincemeat, a mixture of chopped dried fruit, distilled spirits and spices, and historically suet and meat. Traditionally served during the Christmas season, modern recipes are usually meat-free, though suet (beef or vegetarian) is still commonly included. Mince pies can vary in size from bite-sized party portions to full pies.

Mince pies are a traditional part of the British Christmas.

History

The ancestors of the mince pie date to the medieval period, when returning crusaders brought back Middle Eastern dishes combining meat, fruit and spices. Early English "Christmas pyes" were large oblong pies containing a mixture of minced meat — mutton, beef or game — with suet, dried fruits such as raisins, currants and prunes, and spices including cloves, mace, black pepper and saffron. The oblong shape was said to represent the manger, and the three spices the gifts of the Magi. During the Commonwealth period under Oliver Cromwell, mince pies were sometimes portrayed as a symbol of Catholic idolatry, and popular legend (not well supported by evidence) holds that they were banned. Over the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the meat content dwindled and the pies shrank to the small, round, sweet form familiar today.

Mincemeat

Modern mincemeat typically contains dried fruits (raisins, currants, sultanas), candied peel, apples, brown sugar, suet, spices such as cinnamon, nutmeg and mixed spice, and brandy or rum. It is matured for several weeks before use. Vegetarian versions replace beef suet with vegetable suet.

Customs and folklore

Folklore holds that mince pies should be eaten in silence, that one should make a wish with the first pie of the season, and that eating one pie on each of the twelve days of Christmas brings luck. In tradition, a mince pie and a glass of sherry or mulled wine are left out for Father Christmas on Christmas Eve. Stirring the mincemeat clockwise is said to bring good luck.
