Assassination of Shinzo Abe

Shinzo Abe, the former prime minister of Japan, was assassinated on 8 July 2022 while delivering a campaign speech in Nara, Japan. He was shot with a homemade firearm by Tetsuya Yamagami, a former member of the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force. Abe was the longest-serving prime minister in Japanese history and the first former Japanese prime minister to be assassinated since the 1930s.

The shooting

Abe was speaking in support of a Liberal Democratic Party candidate ahead of the House of Councillors election, scheduled for 10 July. At about 11:30 a.m., the gunman approached from behind and fired twice. The first shot missed or failed to take effect; the second struck Abe in the neck and chest. He was airlifted to Nara Medical University Hospital, where he was pronounced dead at 5:03 p.m. from blood loss. Yamagami was arrested at the scene.

Motive

Yamagami told investigators he held a grudge against the Unification Church, to which his mother had made large donations that bankrupted the family, and that he targeted Abe because he believed Abe had promoted the church in Japan. The killing prompted intense public scrutiny of the church's fundraising practices and of its ties to Japanese politicians, and contributed to a sharp fall in support for the government of Fumio Kishida.

Aftermath

World leaders expressed shock and condolences. Japan held a state funeral for Abe on 27 September 2022, an event that proved controversial domestically. Yamagami was indicted on murder and firearms charges in January 2023. The assassination led to reviews of VIP protection by Japanese police and debate over gun control and homemade weapons.
