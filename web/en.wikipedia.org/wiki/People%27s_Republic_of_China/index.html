The People's Republic of China (PRC), commonly known as China, is a country in East Asia. With a population exceeding 1.4 billion, it is the world's second-most populous country after India, and the third or fourth largest by area. China is a unitary one-party state governed by the Chinese Communist Party.

Geography

China borders 14 countries and encompasses landscapes from the subarctic forests of Manchuria to the deserts of Inner Mongolia, the Tibetan Plateau — the world's highest — and the subtropical south. The Yangtze and Yellow rivers are among the longest on Earth.

History

Chinese civilisation, one of the world's oldest, was unified under the Qin dynasty in 221 BC and ruled by successive dynasties for two millennia. The last dynasty, the Qing, fell in 1912, giving way to the Republic of China. After the Chinese Civil War, Mao Zedong proclaimed the PRC in Beijing on 1 October 1949, while the Republic retreated to Taiwan. Reform and opening up after 1978 under Deng Xiaoping unleashed rapid economic growth, lifting hundreds of millions out of poverty and making China the world's second-largest economy and largest manufacturer and exporter.

Politics and society

The PRC is administered through provinces, autonomous regions and direct-controlled municipalities, plus the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macau. Its leadership, most recently under Xi Jinping, has emphasised national rejuvenation while the state maintains extensive control over media, the internet and civil society. China claims Taiwan as part of its territory, a position disputed by Taiwan's government.
