Single-reed instrument

A single-reed instrument is a woodwind instrument that produces sound using a single reed – a thin strip of cane (or synthetic material) fastened to a mouthpiece – which vibrates against the mouthpiece when air is blown, periodically opening and closing the air column.

Single-reed instrument
ClassificationWoodwind, aerophone (Hornbostel–Sachs 422.2)
ExamplesClarinet, saxophone, chalumeau, tárogató

Instruments

The principal modern single-reed instruments are the clarinet family and the saxophone family. Historical and folk examples include the chalumeau, xaphoon, aulochrome, and various folk reedpipes. Although the saxophone has a brass body, it is classed as woodwind because of its reed.

Acoustics

The reed acts as a pressure-controlled valve. The clarinet's cylindrical bore makes it overblow at the twelfth (odd harmonics), while the saxophone's conical bore overblows at the octave.
