The Dow Chemical Company was an American multinational chemical corporation headquartered in Midland, Michigan. Founded in 1897 by chemist Herbert Henry Dow to extract bromine from the brine deposits beneath Midland, it grew through the 20th century into one of the largest chemical companies in the world, producing plastics, chemicals and agricultural products. Its products included polystyrene (Styrofoam), polyethylene, epoxy resins, silicones (through the Dow Corning joint venture), herbicides and the Saran line of resins and films.

History

Herbert Henry Dow developed an economical process for extracting bromine from brine and founded the company in 1897, later expanding into chlorine, caustic soda and magnesium — the latter making Midland a key supplier of the light metal during the Second World War. The company diversified after the war into consumer products and overseas production. It was a major manufacturer of napalm and Agent Orange for the U.S. military during the Vietnam War, which brought sustained public protest. In 2001 Dow acquired Union Carbide, inheriting liabilities connected with the 1984 Bhopal disaster at a Union Carbide India plant; Dow maintained that the legal settlement predated its ownership.

DowDuPont and Dow Inc.

In December 2015 Dow and DuPont announced a merger of equals, completed in August 2017 to form DowDuPont. The combined company then split into three independent public companies in 2019: Corteva (agriculture), DuPont de Nemours (specialty products) and Dow Inc., which took over the materials science business — plastics, industrial intermediates and coatings — and the Midland headquarters. Dow Inc. is a component of the Dow Jones Industrial Average.
