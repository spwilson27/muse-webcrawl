The Jiaohe ruins (Chinese: 交河故城; pinyin: Jiāohé gùchéng) are the remains of a ruined ancient city on a steep, leaf-shaped plateau between two river valleys, about 10 km west of Turpan in Xinjiang, China. Founded by the Jushi people by the 2nd century BC, Jiaohe was for centuries a frontier stronghold on the northern route of the Silk Road, and it is considered one of the best-preserved earthen ruined cities in the world.

History

Jiaohe was the capital of the Anterior Jushi kingdom. The Han dynasty repeatedly contested the region with the Xiongnu — the "five sieges of Jushi" — and after 60 BC established garrisons and military farms there. The site was for a time the seat of the Han's Anxi Protectorate. Under the Tang dynasty (from 640 AD) Jiaohe was a county seat of the Anxi Protectorate, governing the Turpan basin; the Buddhist monk Xuanzang passed through in 629. Uyghur rulers of Qocho (Gaochang) later controlled the city. Jiaohe was finally abandoned in the 14th century, destroyed during the Mongol-era wars of the Chagatai Khanate.

The site

Unusually, the city was not built up but carved down: its buildings were excavated from the loess plateau itself, with streets cut as trenches and houses hollowed from the earth, supplemented by rammed-earth and mud-brick construction. The plateau, 30 m above the rivers, provided natural defence — the city had no walls. A broad main street runs south–north past the remains of a government office quarter, residential districts, workshops, and a large Buddhist temple complex with a central stupa. Excavations by Aurel Stein and later Chinese archaeologists recovered documents, coins and artefacts; in 2014 Jiaohe was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the "Silk Roads: the Routes Network of Chang'an-Tianshan Corridor" listing.
