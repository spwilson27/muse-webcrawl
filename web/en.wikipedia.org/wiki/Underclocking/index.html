Underclocking

Underclocking, also known as downclocking, is the practice of modifying a computer component's timing settings — typically those of a processor or graphics card — so that it runs at a lower clock rate than its rated speed. It is the opposite of overclocking.

Purpose

Underclocking is used to reduce power consumption, heat output and fan noise. A slower processor generates less heat and can be cooled more quietly or even passively, which is valuable in home-theatre PCs, embedded systems and devices operating in hot environments. It also extends battery life in laptops and can improve stability in systems with marginal cooling or ageing components.

Methods

Clock rates can be lowered through BIOS or UEFI settings, operating-system power-management tools, or manufacturer utilities, often together with undervolting (reducing the supply voltage), which compounds the power savings. Many modern processors underclock themselves automatically when idle — technologies such as Intel SpeedStep and AMD Cool'n'Quiet dynamically vary frequency and voltage with workload.

Drawbacks

The trade-off is reduced performance. Underclocking is therefore most common in machines whose workloads are light, or where silence, efficiency or longevity matter more than speed.
