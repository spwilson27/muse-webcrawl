Menin

Menin is a protein that in humans is encoded by the MEN1 gene. Menin is a putative tumor suppressor associated with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1 syndrome). In vitro studies have shown menin interacts with FANCD2 and the transcription factor JunD, among many other proteins. Menin is a nuclear scaffold protein that regulates gene expression and maintains genomic integrity.

Gene and structure

The MEN1 gene is located on chromosome 11q13 and encodes a 610-amino-acid protein. Menin is ubiquitously expressed and localizes predominantly to the nucleus. It contains nuclear localization signals near its C-terminus and binds chromatin-associated protein complexes, including the MLL1/MLL2 histone methyltransferase complexes that place activating H3K4 trimethylation marks.

Function

Menin acts as a scaffold that coordinates transcriptional regulation, cell division, and DNA repair. It represses JunD-mediated transcription while activating the expression of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors such as p27 (Kip1) and p18 (INK4c). Through its association with MLL complexes, menin helps maintain expression of HOX genes and other developmental regulators. Loss of menin function leads to uncontrolled proliferation in endocrine tissues.

Clinical significance

Germline mutations in MEN1 cause multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1, an autosomal dominant syndrome characterized by tumors of the parathyroid glands, the anterior pituitary, and the endocrine pancreas. Somatic MEN1 mutations also occur in sporadic endocrine tumors. In acute leukemias with MLL rearrangements, the menin–MLL interaction is required for leukemic transformation, and small-molecule inhibitors of this interaction have been developed as targeted therapies.
