The Battle of Jenin took place between 1 and 11 April 2002 in the Jenin refugee camp in the West Bank, during Israel's Operation Defensive Shield in the Second Intifada. Israeli forces fought Palestinian militants who had heavily booby-trapped the camp; the battle became one of the most controversial episodes of the intifada.

Background

Israel launched Operation Defensive Shield after a wave of suicide bombings, including the Passover massacre in Netanya. Jenin, from which Israel said many bombers had come, was a principal target. Some 200 Palestinian fighters from Fatah, Hamas and Islamic Jihad prepared defences in the camp, planting explosives in streets and houses.

Battle

Israeli infantry, supported by tanks and helicopter gunships, met unexpectedly fierce resistance. On 9 April thirteen Israeli reservists were killed in a single ambush in a booby-trapped alley. Thereafter the IDF made extensive use of armoured bulldozers to clear paths and demolish buildings from which fire was coming, flattening much of the Hawashin quarter. Organized resistance ended by 11 April.

Aftermath and controversy

Early Palestinian claims of a massacre of hundreds prompted international outcry; subsequent investigations by the United Nations and Human Rights Watch found that 52 Palestinians had been killed, a substantial proportion of them combatants, and found no evidence of a massacre, though HRW and Amnesty documented possible war crimes including the use of human shields. Twenty-three Israeli soldiers died. The battle remains a potent symbol in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.
