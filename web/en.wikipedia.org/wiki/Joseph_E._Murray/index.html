Joseph Edward Murray (1919–2012) was an American surgeon who performed the first successful human kidney transplant. On December 23, 1954, at the Peter Bent Brigham Hospital in Boston, Murray transplanted a kidney between identical twins, Richard and Ronald Herrick, thereby avoiding the problem of immune rejection.

Career

Trained as a plastic surgeon, Murray's wartime experience with skin grafts for burned airmen led him to study the biology of graft rejection. His team's later work with immunosuppression enabled transplants between unrelated donors. In 1990 he shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with E. Donnall Thomas "for their discoveries concerning organ and cell transplantation in the treatment of human disease".
