Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates

Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates, named after Martin Kruskal and George Szekeres, are a coordinate system for the Schwarzschild geometry of general relativity. They are notable for covering the entire maximally extended spacetime manifold, including the regions inside the black hole and the "other side" of the wormhole (the white hole and parallel exterior region), whereas ordinary Schwarzschild coordinates break down at the event horizon.


A Kruskal diagram. Light cones are everywhere at 45°, and the two hyperbolae are the singularities.

Definition

For the exterior region, the new coordinates T and X are defined from the Schwarzschild time t and tortoise coordinate r* by X = cosh(t/4M)·√(r/2M − 1) er/4M and T = sinh(t/4M)·√(r/2M − 1) er/4M, so that T² − X² = (1 − r/2M) er/2M. In these coordinates the metric is regular at the horizon r = 2M, and radial light rays are straight lines at ±45°, making the causal structure transparent.

The four regions

The Kruskal diagram divides spacetime into four regions: I, the exterior of the black hole (our universe); II, the black hole interior, where the singularity at r = 0 is a spacelike surface in the future; III, a second, parallel exterior region ("another universe"); and IV, the white hole interior, bounded by a past singularity. Regions I and III are connected at the moment of time symmetry by the Einstein–Rosen bridge, a non-traversable wormhole.

History

The coordinates were found independently by Kruskal and Szekeres in 1960, building on earlier work; the resulting maximal analytic extension revealed the full structure implicit in Schwarzschild's 1916 solution.
