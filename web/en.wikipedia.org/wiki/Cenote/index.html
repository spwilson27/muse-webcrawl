Cenote

A cenote is a natural pit, or sinkhole, resulting when a collapse of limestone bedrock exposes groundwater. The term originated on the Yucatán Peninsula of Mexico, where the ancient Maya commonly used cenotes for water supplies, and occasionally for sacrificial offerings.

Formation and types

Cenotes form by dissolution of porous limestone and collapse of cavern roofs. They range from fully open, lake-like pools to vertical-walled waterholes and completely underground caverns connected to extensive flooded cave systems, such as those linked to the Chicxulub crater rim ("Ring of Cenotes").

Maya significance

At Chichen Itza, the Sacred Cenote was a place of pilgrimage and offerings to the rain god Chaac; gold, jade and human remains have been recovered from it. Today cenotes are popular for swimming, snorkelling and cave diving.
