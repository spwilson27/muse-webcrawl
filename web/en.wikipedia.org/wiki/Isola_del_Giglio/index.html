Isola del Giglio (Italian pronunciation: [ˈiːzola del ˈdʒiʎʎo]; English: Giglio Island) is an Italian island and comune in the Tyrrhenian Sea, part of the Tuscan Archipelago, off the coast of Tuscany in the province of Grosseto. The island is one of seven that form the archipelago, lying within the Arcipelago Toscano National Park.

Geography

Giglio is about 16 km (10 mi) from the mainland port of Porto Santo Stefano. The island is mostly granite, rising to Monte della Pagana (496 m). Its settlements are Giglio Porto on the east coast (the ferry port), Giglio Castello (a medieval hilltop village within walls, the municipal seat), and Giglio Campese on the north-west coast, known for its sandy bay and offshore rock stack, the Faraglione.

History

The island was known in antiquity as Igilium; granite from Giglio was quarried for Roman buildings. In medieval times it passed among the Aldobrandeschi counts, Pisa, and later the Medici Grand Duchy of Tuscany. In 1558 the islanders and Spanish troops repelled an attack associated with the Barbary corsair Dragut. The feast of the island's patron, San Mamiliano, is celebrated on 15 September.

Costa Concordia

On 13 January 2012, the cruise ship Costa Concordia struck a rock off Giglio Porto and capsized within sight of the island, with the loss of 32 lives. Islanders took in thousands of survivors. The wreck was righted in a record parbuckling salvage operation in September 2013 and towed to Genoa for scrapping in July 2014 — a story widely credited with raising the island's international profile.

Economy

Tourism, winemaking (Ansonaco wine), and fishing dominate the local economy; former pyrite and granite mines are closed.
