Nutrition facts label

The nutrition facts label (also known as the nutrition information panel, and by other names) is a standardised label required on most packaged food in many countries, showing the product's content of energy, macronutrients, and key micronutrients. In the United States it is regulated by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and appears under the heading "Nutrition Facts".

United States

Comprehensive labelling in the US followed the Nutrition Labeling and Education Act of 1990 (NLEA), with the uniform label becoming mandatory in 1994. The label lists serving size and servings per container, calories, total fat, saturated fat, trans fat, cholesterol, sodium, total carbohydrate, dietary fiber, sugars, and protein, followed by selected vitamins and minerals. Amounts are also expressed as a % Daily Value, based on a 2,000-calorie reference diet.

2016 redesign

In May 2016 the FDA announced the first major overhaul of the label in over twenty years. The new design features a larger, bolder typeface; calories displayed much more prominently; updated serving sizes reflecting actual consumption; a separate line for added sugars; and a revised list of required nutrients (vitamin D and potassium added; vitamins A and C made voluntary). Large manufacturers were required to adopt the new label by January 2020, with smaller manufacturers following a year later.

Other countries

Most countries require some form of nutrition declaration. The European Union mandates a table of energy, fat, saturates, carbohydrate, sugars, protein, and salt per 100 g or 100 ml. Front-of-pack schemes vary: the United Kingdom uses voluntary "traffic light" colours, while France's Nutri-Score and Chile's black warning octagons summarise overall healthfulness. Canada's "Nutrition Facts" table is bilingual and closely parallels the US format, with its own 2016-era revisions.
