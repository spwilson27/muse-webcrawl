Ballets Russes

The Ballets Russes was an itinerant ballet company founded in 1909 by the Russian impresario Sergei Diaghilev. Based in Paris but never performing in Russia, it became one of the most influential ballet companies of the twentieth century, transforming ballet into a collaborative art uniting choreography, music, and design.

History

Diaghilev recruited dancers from the Imperial Ballet of St Petersburg, including Anna Pavlova, Tamara Karsavina, and Vaslav Nijinsky. Early seasons presented Russian classics and new works by choreographer Michel Fokine, such as The Firebird (1910) and Petrushka (1911), with scores by Igor Stravinsky. Nijinsky's choreography for The Rite of Spring (1913), with Stravinsky's dissonant score, provoked a famous near-riot at its Paris premiere.

Collaborations

The company commissioned scores from composers including Debussy, Ravel, Prokofiev, and Poulenc, and designs from artists such as Léon Bakst, Pablo Picasso, Henri Matisse, and Coco Chanel. After Diaghilev's death in 1929 the company dissolved, but its dancers and choreographers — among them George Balanchine — carried its style across Europe and the Americas.

Legacy

The Ballets Russes redefined the status of ballet music, elevated the male dancer, and set the template for the modern dance company. Successor troupes under the names Ballets Russes de Monte Carlo and Original Ballet Russe toured widely in the 1930s and 1940s.
