CDC 6600

Manufacturer: Control Data Corporation
Released: 1964
Designer: Seymour Cray
Performance: ~3 MFLOPS

The CDC 6600 was a mainframe computer manufactured by Control Data Corporation, delivered in 1964. Generally regarded as the first successful supercomputer, it was roughly three times faster than the preceding record holder, IBM's 7030 Stretch.

Design

Designed by Seymour Cray's team in Chippewa Falls, Wisconsin, the 6600 used silicon transistors, a compact freon-cooled chassis, a single central processor with multiple functional units operating in parallel, and ten small peripheral processors that offloaded input/output, allowing the CPU to devote itself to computation. It ran at a 10 MHz clock with a 60-bit word.

Legacy

About 100 were sold at around $7 million each. The 6600 established CDC's dominance in scientific computing and set Cray on the path to founding Cray Research.
