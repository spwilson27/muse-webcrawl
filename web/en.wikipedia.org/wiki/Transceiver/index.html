A transceiver is an electronic device that is a combination of a radio transmitter and a receiver, hence the name. It can both transmit and receive radio waves using a common antenna, usually in half-duplex mode, in which the unit either transmits or receives at a given moment, though full-duplex transceivers operating on separate frequencies also exist.

Radio

Transceivers became standard in amateur radio, military communications, and mobile radio because combining the two functions shares components such as the local oscillator and antenna, reducing size, cost, and frequency error. Modern handheld and mobile radios, including those used by emergency services and in aviation, are almost universally transceivers. The term is also used for units performing modulation and demodulation, as in a modem.

Telephony and networking

In telephony, a handset combines a microphone (transmitter) and earpiece (receiver) in a single unit. In computer networking, transceivers connect a device to a medium: Ethernet transceivers handle collision detection and conversion between signals, and pluggable optical transceivers (such as SFP, SFP+, and QSFP modules) convert between electrical signals and light in fibre-optic links. Radar transponders on aircraft — which reply to interrogations — are a related combination device.
