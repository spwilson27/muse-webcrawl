The Treaty of Lahore of 9 March 1846 was a peace treaty marking the end of the First Anglo-Sikh War. The treaty was concluded between the British East India Company and the Sikh Empire, represented by the young Maharaja Duleep Singh and his council.

Background

After the death of Maharaja Ranjit Singh in 1839, the Sikh Empire fell into political turmoil. In December 1845 the Khalsa army crossed the Sutlej River, beginning the First Anglo-Sikh War. After defeats at Mudki, Ferozeshah, Aliwal and the decisive Battle of Sobraon in February 1846, British forces advanced on Lahore, and the Sikh durbar sought terms.

Terms

Under the treaty, the Sikhs ceded the valuable Jalandhar Doab — the territory between the Beas and Sutlej rivers — to the East India Company. They agreed to pay a war indemnity of 15 million rupees; because the Lahore treasury could not pay in full, the hill territories between the Beas and Indus rivers, including Kashmir and Hazara, were ceded in lieu of much of the balance. The Sikh army was reduced in size, a British resident was installed at Lahore, and British troops occupied the city until the end of 1846.

Aftermath

Days later, by the Treaty of Amritsar (16 March 1846), the British sold Kashmir to Gulab Singh, a Dogra chief, for 7.5 million rupees, creating the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir. The supplementary Treaty of Bhyrowal in December 1846 effectively placed the Sikh state under British control during Duleep Singh's minority. Dissatisfaction with these arrangements contributed to the Second Anglo-Sikh War of 1848–49, which ended with the annexation of the Punjab.
