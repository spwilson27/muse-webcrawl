Tourism in Georgia is a major and rapidly growing sector of the economy of Georgia, the country at the eastern end of the Black Sea in the Caucasus. Visitors are drawn by the combination of High Caucasus mountain scenery, an ancient wine-making culture, medieval churches and cave cities, Black Sea resorts, and the old town of the capital, Tbilisi. Tourism receipts and employment grew sharply in the 2010s, aided by visa liberalisation — citizens of many countries can enter visa-free for up to a year — and by low-cost flights to Tbilisi, Kutaisi, and Batumi.

Destinations

Tbilisi's Old Town, the Narikala fortress, sulphur baths, and the Mtkvari riverfront anchor the capital's appeal. The mountain region of Kazbegi (Stepantsminda) is famous for the Gergeti Trinity Church beneath Mount Kazbek (5,047 m), while remote Svaneti draws trekkers to its medieval defensive towers at Mestia and Ushguli, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Kakheti in the east is Georgia's wine heartland — the country claims 8,000 years of winemaking in clay qvevri vessels — and the cave monasteries and cities of Vardzia, Uplistsikhe, and Davit Gareji are among the leading historical sites. On the Black Sea, Batumi in Adjara is the principal resort, with a botanical garden, boulevard, and casino tourism.

Activities and infrastructure

Skiing is centred on Gudauri and Mestia's Hatsvali and Tetnuldi resorts, and hiking routes cross the Greater Caucasus in Tusheti, Khevsureti, and the national parks of Borjomi-Kharagauli and Kazbegi. Georgia markets itself on cuisine (khachapuri, khinkali), polyphonic singing, and the supra feast tradition. Tourism was hit hard by the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020–21 before recovering strongly, with visitor numbers again approaching record levels by the mid-2020s.
