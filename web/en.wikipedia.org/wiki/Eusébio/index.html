Eusébio da Silva Ferreira (25 January 1942 – 5 January 2014), nicknamed the "Black Panther", the "Black Pearl", or "O Rei" ("The King"), was a Portuguese footballer who played as a striker. He is considered one of the greatest players of all time as well as Benfica's best player ever. Born in Portuguese Mozambique, he spent most of his career with Benfica in Lisbon, scoring prolifically and helping the club to dominate Portuguese football and win the European Cup in 1962.

Club career

Eusébio joined Benfica in 1961 from Sporting Clube de Lourenço Marques. In his first full season he helped Benfica retain the European Cup, scoring twice in the 5–3 final victory over Real Madrid in 1962. Over fifteen seasons with the club he won eleven Primeira Liga titles and five Taça de Portugal trophies, and finished as the European Cup's top scorer on several occasions. He won the Ballon d'Or in 1965 and the European Golden Shoe in 1968 and 1973, the first player to win the latter award. In total he scored more than 470 goals in official matches for Benfica. After leaving in 1975 he played briefly in North America and Mexico before retiring in 1979.

International career

Eusébio earned 64 caps for the Portugal national team and scored 41 goals. His finest tournament was the 1966 FIFA World Cup in England, where he finished as top scorer with nine goals — including four in the quarter-final against North Korea — and led Portugal to third place, its best World Cup finish.

Legacy

Eusébio was named by Pelé in the FIFA 100 list of the world's greatest living players in 2004 and was voted among the top ten players of the 20th century in major polls. A statue of him stands outside Benfica's Estádio da Luz. Following his death from heart failure in January 2014, Portugal declared three days of national mourning, and in 2015 his remains were transferred to the National Pantheon in Lisbon.
