The Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor is an American single-seat, twin-engine, supersonic all-weather stealth fighter aircraft developed for the United States Air Force (USAF). Designed as an air superiority fighter, it also has ground attack, electronic warfare and signals intelligence capabilities.

Development

The F-22 emerged from the USAF's Advanced Tactical Fighter (ATF) programme. The Lockheed-led YF-22 was selected over the Northrop YF-23 in 1991. Production was capped at 187 operational aircraft, with the last delivered in 2012, largely because of high cost and the absence of a clear peer air-to-air threat at the time.

Design

The Raptor combines stealth shaping and materials, supercruise (supersonic flight without afterburners, up to about Mach 1.5), thrust vectoring nozzles for extreme agility, and integrated avionics with sensor fusion. It is powered by two Pratt & Whitney F119 turbofan engines. Maximum speed exceeds Mach 2.

Armament

For air-to-air missions the F-22 carries six AIM-120 AMRAAMs and two AIM-9 Sidewinders in internal bays, plus a 20 mm M61A2 Vulcan cannon. For ground attack it can carry JDAMs or Small Diameter Bombs internally, with additional stores on external hardpoints at the cost of stealth.

Operational history

The F-22 reached initial operational capability in December 2005. Its combat debut came in September 2014, striking Islamic State targets in Syria. Export of the aircraft is prohibited by US federal law, making the USAF its only operator.
