Min'yō

Min'yō (Japanese: 民謡, "folk song") is a genre of traditional Japanese folk music and song, originally sung by working people — farmers, fisherfolk, pack-horse drivers and others — to accompany labour, festivals and celebrations.

Characteristics

Min'yō songs are strongly regional, with thousands of local variants; well-known examples include "Sōran Bushi" of Hokkaido fishermen and "Esashi Oiwake". Performance commonly features the shamisen, shakuhachi, taiko drums and the transverse flute (shinobue), with a distinctive vocal technique using melisma and a characteristic pitch ornament. Songs often accompany bon odori dancing at summer festivals.

Modern practice

In the twentieth century min'yō became an organised art with teachers, competitions and professional singers, and it has influenced Japanese popular music, including crossover performers who blend folk song with jazz and rock.
