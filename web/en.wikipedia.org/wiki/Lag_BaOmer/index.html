Lag BaOmer

Lag BaOmer (Hebrew: ל״ג בעומר, "the 33rd [day] of the Omer"), also called Lag LaOmer, is a Jewish holiday falling on the 33rd day of the Counting of the Omer, which occurs on the 18th day of the Hebrew month of Iyar. In the Gregorian calendar it falls in late April or May. The day is traditionally a break in the semi-mourning customs observed during the Omer period, and is celebrated with bonfires, outings, weddings, haircuts, and — in Israel — a mass pilgrimage to the tomb of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai at Meron.

Origins

The name derives from the Hebrew letters lamed (30) and gimel (3), together 33. According to the Talmud, during the Omer period a plague killed 24,000 students of Rabbi Akiva — traditionally understood as punishment for their lack of mutual respect — and the deaths ceased on Lag BaOmer, making it a day of joy within the mourning season. The day is also associated with the hillula (anniversary of death, celebrated as a day of joy) of the second-century sage Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, to whom tradition attributes the Zohar, the central work of Kabbalah.

Customs

The mourning restrictions of the Omer — no weddings, haircuts, or live music in many communities — are lifted on Lag BaOmer (permanently in some traditions, for the day only in others). Children traditionally play with bows and arrows outdoors, a custom variously explained as recalling the rainbow sign after the Flood, the weapons of Bar Kokhba's rebels, or Rabbi Shimon's teaching. In Israel, bonfires are lit across the country and schoolchildren hold parades and outings. The first haircut of a three-year-old boy (upsherin) is often held on this day, notably at Meron.

Meron pilgrimage

Hundreds of thousands of pilgrims gather annually at the tomb of Shimon bar Yochai in Meron in the Galilee, the largest annual mass gathering in Israel. In 2021 a crowd crush at the site killed 45 people, the deadliest civilian disaster in Israel's history, prompting subsequent reforms of the event's crowd management.
