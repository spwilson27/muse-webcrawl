Pipe (fluid conveyance)

Steel pipes ready for installation in an industrial plant.

A pipe is a tubular section or hollow cylinder used to convey substances that can flow — liquids, gases, slurries and powders. Pipes form the backbone of plumbing, heating, water and sewer systems, oil and gas pipelines, and process plant of every kind. A system of pipes, valves, fittings and pumps for a particular application is called piping.

Materials and manufacture

Historical pipes were made of wood, clay and lead — the word "plumbing" derives from Latin plumbum, lead. Modern pipes are made from steel (welded or seamless), cast and ductile iron, copper, concrete, and plastics including PVC, CPVC, polyethylene and PEX. Plastic pipe has displaced metal in much domestic plumbing because it is cheap, light and corrosion-resistant; steel remains standard for high pressure and temperature.

Sizing

Pipe sizes are standardised. In North America, nominal pipe size (NPS) refers not to the actual diameter but to a schedule of sizes whose outside diameter is fixed for each nominal size while wall thickness varies by "schedule" number; metric systems use DN (diamètre nominal). Tube, by contrast, is sized by its actual outside diameter.

Flow

Flow through pipes is described by the principles of fluid mechanics: pressure drop from friction is commonly estimated with the Darcy–Weisbach equation, and regimes range from laminar to turbulent flow depending on the Reynolds number. See also pipeline transport.
