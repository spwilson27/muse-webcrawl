Westernisation

Westernisation (or Westernization) is the process by which societies adopt, voluntarily or under pressure, aspects of Western culture, technology, institutions, law, lifestyle and values. It has accompanied European colonialism, global trade and, in the modern era, globalisation and the spread of media and consumer culture.

Historical examples

Peter the Great's reforms westernised the Russian military, administration and court in the early 18th century. Japan deliberately westernised during the Meiji Restoration, importing legal, military and industrial models. In the Ottoman Empire and later Turkey, Tanzimat reforms and the Kemalist programme introduced Western legal codes, dress and the Latin alphabet.

Debates

Westernisation is debated as both modernisation and cultural loss. Critics associate it with imperialism and homogenisation; others distinguish selective adaptation — adopting technology and institutions while retaining local identity — from wholesale cultural replacement. Related terms include Americanisation, applied to the influence of the United States.
