Shōji

A shōji (障子) is a door, window or room divider in traditional Japanese architecture consisting of translucent paper (washi) stretched over a wooden lattice frame. Shōji slide on tracks and admit soft, diffuse light while preserving privacy.

Design

The frame is typically made of lightweight wood such as cedar or cypress, joined in a grid pattern (kumiko), over which washi paper is pasted. The paper filters sunlight and can be replaced when torn. Sliding shōji free up floor space because they do not swing open like hinged doors.

History

Paper screens appeared in Japan after paper-making arrived from China, and shōji became standard fittings in the shoin-style residences of the medieval period. They remain common in Japanese homes today, sometimes using plastic or reinforced papers for durability, and have influenced minimalist interior design worldwide.
