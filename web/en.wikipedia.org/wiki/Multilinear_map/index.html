Multilinear map

In linear algebra, a multilinear map is a function of several variables that is linear in each variable separately. Precisely, a multilinear map is a function

f : V1 × ⋯ × Vn → W,

where V1, …, Vn and W are vector spaces (or modules over a commutative ring), with the property that the function is linear in each variable when all the others are held fixed. A multilinear map of one variable is simply a linear map, and a multilinear map of two variables is a bilinear map.

Examples

  • The determinant of a square matrix is a multilinear function of its rows (or columns); when required to be alternating as well, it is unique up to scale.
  • The cross product in three-dimensional space is a bilinear map.
  • An inner product is a bilinear map (sesquilinear in the complex case).
  • The multiplication of tensors and the tensor product itself are characterized by universal multilinear maps.

Properties

A multilinear map is called alternating if it vanishes whenever two of its arguments are equal, which implies it changes sign under exchange of any two arguments (skew-symmetry). Multilinear maps that are both symmetric or alternating in all variables give rise to symmetric and exterior algebras respectively. The universal property of the tensor product states that multilinear maps out of a product of spaces correspond exactly to linear maps out of the tensor product of those spaces.

Coordinate representation

After choosing bases, a multilinear map between finite-dimensional spaces can be represented by a multidimensional array of scalars — a tensor — whose components transform according to the multilinear structure under changes of basis. This is the viewpoint from which the word "tensor" in physics and differential geometry derives: a tensor of type (r, s) is a multilinear map taking r dual vectors and s vectors to a scalar.
