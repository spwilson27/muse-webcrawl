Meantone temperament

Meantone temperaments are musical temperaments — systems of tuning — in which the interval of a fifth is narrowed (tempered) in order to bring major thirds closer to the acoustically pure ratio of 5:4. The average (mean) tone between the major and minor whole tones gives the system its name. Meantone was the dominant keyboard temperament in Europe from the early 16th century until the early 18th century.

Quarter-comma meantone

The best-known form is quarter-comma meantone, described by Pietro Aron in 1523 and by Gioseffo Zarlino. In it, each fifth is narrowed by one quarter of a syntonic comma, making four stacked fifths (reduced by two octaves) exactly equal to a pure major third. Eight major thirds are then perfectly in tune, but the remaining thirds and fifths are badly out of tune — including the notorious "wolf fifth" — so the system works only within a limited range of keys, roughly from E♭ to G♯ major.

Other fractions

Alternative meantone systems temper the fifth by other fractions of the syntonic comma, such as one-fifth, two-sevenths, or one-sixth comma, trading the purity of the thirds against a wider usable key range. The limiting case of 1/11-comma meantone approximates well temperament, while 1/3-comma meantone yields pure minor thirds. As music became more harmonically adventurous, meantone was gradually displaced by well temperaments and eventually by equal temperament.
