Taps

Taps is a bugle call sounded at dusk, during flag ceremonies and at military funerals in the United States. The 24-note melody, by far the most famous American bugle call, is also sounded at memorial services, wreath-laying ceremonies and by scout groups at the close of camp.

Origins

Taps derives from the earlier "Tattoo" and the French sonnerie "Extinguish lights". Its present form is credited to Union Army Brigadier General Daniel Butterfield, who in July 1862, with his bugler Oliver Willcox Norton at Harrison's Landing, Virginia, revised an earlier call into the melody now known during the American Civil War. Captain John C. Tidball is said to have ordered it played at a soldier's funeral in place of the customary rifle volley, fearing the shots might be mistaken for enemy fire, and the practice spread rapidly through both armies.

Use

By 1891 Taps was specified in US Army infantry drill regulations for funerals, and it is now played at an estimated thousands of ceremonies daily, notably at Arlington National Cemetery and at the daily retreat ceremony on military bases. Several sets of words are sung to the tune, beginning "Day is done, gone the sun". Girl Guides and Girl Scouts worldwide close gatherings with the related call.
