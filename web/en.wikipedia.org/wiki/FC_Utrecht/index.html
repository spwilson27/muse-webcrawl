Football Club Utrecht is a Dutch professional football club based in Utrecht, founded in 1970 through the merger of DOS, Elinkwijk, and Velox. Utrecht plays in the Eredivisie and is notable as the only club from outside the traditional "big three" never to have been relegated from the Dutch top flight since its foundation, and for winning the KNVB Cup three times, most famously in 2003 and 2004.

History

The merger club inherited the professional licence of DOS, Dutch champions in 1958, and began life at Stadion Galgenwaard in 1970. The early years were difficult financially, and the club went through a period of threatened bankruptcy in the early 1980s, surviving in part through the sale of players. In 1985 Utrecht nonetheless won its first KNVB Cup, beating Helmond Sport in the final.

The club's most successful era came under coach Frans Adelaar and then Foeke Booy, when Utrecht won consecutive KNVB Cups in 2003 and 2004, beating Feyenoord and FC Twente in the finals, and added the Johan Cruyff Shield in 2004 by defeating Ajax. These years featured combative, well-supported sides and celebrated players such as Dirk Kuyt, who emerged at Utrecht before moving to Feyenoord and Liverpool. In later seasons the club became a regular competitor for European play-off places, qualified for the UEFA Europa League group stage in 2010 after a famous play-off victory over Celtic, and in 2016 reached another cup final, losing to Feyenoord.

Stadium

Stadion Galgenwaard, Utrecht's home since the club's foundation, was extensively rebuilt between 2000 and 2004 and holds about 23,750 spectators. The Bunnikside, the stand behind one goal named for the village where many of the club's hard-core supporters originate, is known for its atmosphere.

Honours

  • KNVB Cup: 1984–85, 2002–03, 2003–04
  • Johan Cruyff Shield: 2004
  • Eredivisie (as DOS): 1957–58

Supporters and rivalries

Utrecht's supporters have a long-standing rivalry with Ajax of nearby Amsterdam, matches between the two being among the most intensely policed fixtures in Dutch football. The club also has a noted history of supporter unrest in the 1970s and 1980s, after which fan projects and the rebuilding of the stadium modernised its supporter culture.
