The Nürburgring is a motor racing complex around the village of Nürburg in the Eifel mountains of western Germany. It is famous above all for the Nordschleife ("North Loop"), a punishing 20.8-kilometre circuit through forested hills that three-time Formula One world champion Jackie Stewart nicknamed "The Green Hell".

History

The original circuit opened in 1927 and hosted the German Grand Prix. The Nordschleife's length, blind corners, and huge elevation change made it the most demanding of all racing circuits; Niki Lauda's near-fatal crash in 1976 ended its use for Formula One. A shorter, safer Grand Prix circuit opened in 1984 on the site, later hosting the European and Luxembourg Grands Prix as well as German rounds.

The Nordschleife today

The Nordschleife remains in daily use for manufacturer testing, the Nürburgring 24 Hours race, VLN/NLS endurance series, and public "touristenfahrten" sessions in which anyone may drive the circuit in a road car for a toll. Lap times on the loop have become a benchmark for performance cars, and the facility also hosts the Rock am Ring music festival.
