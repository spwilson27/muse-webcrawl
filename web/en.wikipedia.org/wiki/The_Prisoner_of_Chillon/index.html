The Prisoner of Chillon (1816) is a narrative poem by Lord Byron. Longer than some of his other works, at 392 lines, the poem tells the story of François Bonivard (1493–1570), a Genevois monk and politician who was imprisoned in the castle of Chillon from 1532 to 1536 because he opposed the Duke of Savoy's rule over Geneva.

Inspiration

Byron was inspired to write the poem after visiting Château de Chillon in June 1816 with Percy Bysshe Shelley, during the "Year Without a Summer". He saw the castle dungeon where Bonivard had been chained to a pillar, and was shown a pillar with a worn depression said to have been made by Bonivard's pacing.

The poem

The poem is written in the first person from the prisoner's perspective. It describes his long imprisonment, the deaths of his two brothers (also imprisoned in the castle), his despair, and his eventual, ambiguous release. Byron took liberties with the historical facts: Bonivard in reality had two brothers who were not imprisoned with him, and his imprisonment lasted about four years, not the much longer stretch implied in the poem.

The poem is notable for its themes of endurance, faith, freedom, and the psychological effects of isolation and confinement.
