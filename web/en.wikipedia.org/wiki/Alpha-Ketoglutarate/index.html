Alpha-Ketoglutarate

α-Ketoglutaric acid
IUPAC name2-Oxopentanedioic acid
FormulaC5H6O5
Molar mass146.10 g/mol
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid
Other names2-Ketoglutaric acid, α-ketoglutarate, AKG, 2-oxoglutarate

α-Ketoglutaric acid (2-oxopentanedioic acid) is an organic compound with the formula HO2CCH2CH2COCO2H. It is a keto acid, formally derived from glutaric acid by replacement of a methylene group with a carbonyl. Its conjugate base, alpha-ketoglutarate (AKG), is one of the most important intermediates in cellular metabolism: it is a key compound of the Krebs cycle (citric acid cycle) and a central link between carbohydrate and amino-acid metabolism.

Role in the Krebs cycle

In the citric acid cycle, alpha-ketoglutarate is formed by the oxidative decarboxylation of isocitrate, catalysed by isocitrate dehydrogenase with production of NADH. It is then converted to succinyl-CoA by the alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase complex, a second oxidative decarboxylation that also produces NADH and is a major control point of the cycle. Together these steps release the two carbon atoms that entered the cycle as part of acetyl-CoA (as CO2) in fully oxidised form.

Nitrogen metabolism

Alpha-ketoglutarate is the carbon skeleton onto which ammonia is fixed in the biosynthesis of amino acids. Reductive amination by glutamate dehydrogenase yields glutamate, and transamination reactions transfer amino groups between alpha-ketoglutarate/glutamate and many other keto acids and amino acids. It also accepts excess nitrogen during amino-acid catabolism, and it serves as a co-substrate for a large family of dioxygenase enzymes, including the prolyl hydroxylases that regulate the hypoxia-inducible factors (HIF) and enzymes involved in epigenetic demethylation of DNA and histones.

Uses

Salt forms such as calcium and ornithine alpha-ketoglutarate have been investigated as dietary supplements and in clinical nutrition, for example in wound healing, burns, and the preservation of muscle mass. Commercial production is by chemical synthesis or by fermentation with microorganisms such as Yarrowia lipolytica.
