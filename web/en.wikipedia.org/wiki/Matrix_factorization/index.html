Matrix factorization

A matrix M approximated as the product W·H of two lower-rank factor matrices.

In the mathematical discipline of linear algebra, a matrix decomposition or matrix factorization is a factorization of a matrix into a product of matrices. There are many different matrix decompositions; each finds use among a particular class of problems. The term also denotes, more loosely in machine learning, techniques that approximate a (possibly sparse) data matrix by the product of two or more lower-rank matrices.

Classical decompositions

  • LU decomposition — expresses a matrix as the product of a lower and an upper triangular matrix; used to solve linear systems.
  • QR decomposition — an orthogonal matrix times an upper triangular matrix; central to least-squares problems.
  • Cholesky decomposition — for symmetric positive-definite matrices, a lower triangular matrix times its transpose.
  • Eigendecomposition and the singular value decomposition (SVD) — factor a matrix using its eigenvectors, or orthogonal matrices and a diagonal matrix of singular values.
  • Polar decomposition — an orthogonal matrix times a positive-semidefinite matrix.

Factorizations for data analysis

In statistics and machine learning, factorization methods extract latent structure from data. Principal component analysis is based on the SVD. Non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) constrains the factors to be non-negative, giving parts-based representations used in text mining, image analysis, and audio processing. In recommender systems, matrix factorization models — popularized during the Netflix Prize (2006–2009), notably by Simon Funk's SVD approach — represent users and items by vectors of latent factors and predict missing ratings from their dot products; variants add biases, implicit feedback, and regularization, and are trained by stochastic gradient descent or alternating least squares.
