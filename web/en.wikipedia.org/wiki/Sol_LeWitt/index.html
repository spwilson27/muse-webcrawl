Sol LeWitt


Born: 9 September 1928, Hartford, Connecticut
Died: 8 April 2007 (aged 78), New York City
Movement: Conceptual art, Minimalism
Known for: Wall drawings, modular structures

Solomon "Sol" LeWitt (1928–2007) was an American artist linked to various movements, including conceptual art and minimalism. He came to fame in the late 1960s with his wall drawings and "structures" (a term he preferred to "sculptures"), but was prolific in a wide range of media including drawing, printmaking, photography, painting, installation, and artist's books.

Conceptual art

In his 1967 essay "Paragraphs on Conceptual Art" for Artforum, LeWitt wrote that "the idea becomes a machine that makes the art". His wall drawings, begun in 1968, exist as written instructions or diagrams that can be executed by others, separating conception from execution; over 1,200 have been installed in museums and collections worldwide. His modular open-cube structures similarly derive complex forms from simple, pre-determined systems.

Career

LeWitt worked as a graphic designer for architect I. M. Pei and at the Museum of Modern Art in New York alongside colleagues such as Dan Flavin and Lucy Lippard. He taught at several schools and co-founded Printed Matter, the artist's-book organisation, in 1976. A major retrospective of his wall drawings opened at MASS MoCA in 2008 for a 25-year run.
