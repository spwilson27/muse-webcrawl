The Zulu Social Aid and Pleasure Club is an African American parading krewe and benevolent society in New Orleans, Louisiana, famous for its parade on Mardi Gras morning and for handing out decorated coconuts, the most coveted throws of the New Orleans Carnival season. Organised in 1909, Zulu grew out of the traditions of Black benevolent societies that provided burial insurance and mutual aid to their members.

Origins

The club traces its ancestry to a group of labourers known as the Tramps, who marched in 1901. After attending a musical comedy whose skit celebrated a Zulu king, members adopted the name and pageantry of the Zulu of southern Africa; the first King Zulu, William Story, reigned in 1909. The organisation was incorporated on September 20, 1916, formalising its social-aid functions.

Coconuts

Zulu's coconut throws began around 1910, when painted coconuts were a cheap alternative to the glass necklaces of other krewes. Following injury concerns, coconuts were periodically banned until Louisiana's 1988 "coconut law" provided liability protection; today the glittered, hand-painted coconuts are usually handed down from floats.

Kings and characters

Each year Zulu elects a king, most famously Louis Armstrong in 1949. The parade's traditional characters include the Big Shot, Witch Doctor, Ambassador, Mr. Big Stuff, Mayor, Province Prince, and Governor, each with distinctive costumes.
