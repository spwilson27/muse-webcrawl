Small form-factor pluggable transceiver

The small form-factor pluggable transceiver (SFP) is a compact, hot-pluggable network interface module used for telecommunications and data communications. SFP modules connect a switch, router or other network device to fibre-optic or copper cabling, supporting standards such as Gigabit Ethernet, Fibre Channel and SONET at various wavelengths and distances.

Defined by a multi-source agreement rather than a formal standards body, the SFP was followed by enhanced versions: SFP+ (up to about 10 Gbit/s), and the QSFP family for 40–400 Gbit/s. Modules contain a laser transmitter and photodetector for fibre variants, an EEPROM with identification data, and diagnostics in DOM/DDM-capable versions.

Applications

SFPs allow the same electrical port to serve different media and link lengths simply by swapping modules — see Ethernet.
