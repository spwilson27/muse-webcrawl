History of Georgia (country)

The history of Georgia (the country in the Caucasus) stretches from some of the earliest evidence of wine-making and metalworking to an ancient Christian kingdom, a medieval golden age, centuries of Persian and Ottoman pressure, Russian annexation, and independence restored in 1991.

Ancient and early medieval

The western kingdom of Colchis and eastern Iberia (Kartli) figure in Greek myth (the Golden Fleece) and in Roman and Persian rivalry. Georgia adopted Christianity in the 330s, among the earliest states to do so, and developed its own alphabet and church. Arab rule from the 7th century was centered on the emirate of Tbilisi, while western principalities came under Byzantine influence.

Golden age and decline

The Bagrationi dynasty unified the Georgian lands under Bagrat III (1008) and David IV "the Builder" (r. 1089–1125), who expelled the Seljuks at Didgori (1121). Under Queen Tamar (r. 1184–1213) Georgia reached its greatest extent and cultural flowering, celebrated in Shota Rustaveli's epic The Knight in the Panther's Skin. Mongol invasions in the 13th century and Timur's raids ended the golden age; the kingdom fragmented into Kartli, Kakheti, and Imereti, contested by Ottoman and Safavid empires.

Russian rule and independence

Seeking protection, Kartli-Kakheti accepted a Russian protectorate (Treaty of Georgievsk, 1783) and was annexed outright in 1801; the rest of Georgia followed during the 19th century. A Democratic Republic of Georgia existed 1918–1921 before Soviet conquest made it a union republic. Georgia declared independence from the USSR on 9 April 1991, then endured civil conflict and wars in Abkhazia and South Ossetia, the Rose Revolution of 2003, and the Russo-Georgian War of 2008, after which Russia recognized the two breakaway regions. Georgia has since pursued European integration, gaining EU candidate status in 2023 amid continuing domestic political tensions.
