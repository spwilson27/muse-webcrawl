Scania (Swedish: Skåne, pronounced [ˈskôːnɛ]) is the southernmost of the historical provinces (landskap) of Sweden. Occupying the southern tip of the Scandinavian Peninsula, it corresponds roughly to modern Skåne County. Its largest city is Malmö, Sweden's third-largest city, connected to Copenhagen by the Øresund Bridge; other important towns include Lund, Helsingborg and Kristianstad.

History

Scania was Danish territory for centuries — the eastern third of the Danish kingdom — and its medieval cathedral city of Lund was the seat of the archbishop of all Scandinavia. Under the Treaty of Roskilde in 1658, Denmark ceded Scania (together with Blekinge and Halland) to Sweden after the Dano-Swedish War; the transfer was confirmed in 1660 and cemented by the Battle of Lund in 1676, the bloodiest battle in Scandinavian history. A period of "Swedification" followed, including the founding of Lund University in 1666.

Geography and identity

The province is characterised by fertile plains in the south and west — among Sweden's best farmland — and forested ridges in the north. Scanians maintain a distinct regional identity, with a dialect (Scanian) historically closer to Danish, a regional flag combining the Scandinavian cross with the province's griffin arms, and local traditions such as the goose dinner on St Martin's Eve. The region is sometimes called Sweden's breadbasket and gateway to the continent.
