UN number

TypeSubstance identifier
Administered byUN Committee of Experts on the Transport of Dangerous Goods
RangeUN0001 – UN3600+ (four digits)

UN numbers are four-digit numbers that identify dangerous goods, hazardous substances and articles — such as explosives, flammable liquids, and toxic substances — in the framework of international transport. They are assigned by the United Nations Committee of Experts on the Transport of Dangerous Goods.

Usage

UN numbers appear on placards and orange plates fixed to trucks, rail tank cars, and shipping containers carrying hazardous materials, allowing emergency responders to identify a substance quickly. Well-known examples include UN1203 for gasoline (petrol), UN1993 for flammable liquids not otherwise specified, UN2814 for infectious substances affecting humans, and UN3480 for lithium ion batteries. Where a substance falls under a generic or "not otherwise specified" entry, the UN number is paired with a proper shipping name.

Relationship to other systems

UN numbers underpin international regulations including the ADR for road transport in Europe, the IMDG Code for sea transport, and IATA dangerous goods rules for air transport. In North America, NA numbers (NA00xx) issued by the US Department of Transportation supplement UN numbers for domestic shipments.
