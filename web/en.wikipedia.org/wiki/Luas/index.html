Luas (Irish for "speed") is a tram/light rail system in Dublin, Ireland. It opened in 2004 with two unconnected lines, the Red Line and the Green Line, which were finally linked in December 2017 by the Luas Cross City extension across the River Liffey.

Lines

The Red Line runs from Tallaght and Saggart in the western suburbs through the city centre to The Point in the Docklands. The Green Line runs from Broombridge through the city centre to Brides Glen in the south, partly along the alignment of the former Harcourt Street railway line. Extensions have progressively lengthened both lines; the system carries tens of millions of passengers annually.

Rolling stock and operation

Luas trams are Alstom Citadis vehicles, initially 26- and 40-metre units, later extended and supplemented by longer 55-metre trams on the Green Line. Services are operated by Transdev under contract to Transport Infrastructure Ireland and the National Transport Authority, and use the Leap Card integrated ticketing system.

History

Dublin's original tram network had been dismantled by 1949. Plans for a modern light-rail system were developed in the 1990s, construction began in 2001, and the first passengers were carried in June and July 2004 on the Green and Red lines respectively. The Cross City project, opened in 2017, finally united the two lines.
