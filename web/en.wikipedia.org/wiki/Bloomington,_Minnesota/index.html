Bloomington is a city in Hennepin County, Minnesota, United States, lying on the north bank of the Minnesota River above its confluence with the Mississippi. It is the fifth-largest city in Minnesota and one of the principal suburbs of Minneapolis–Saint Paul. Bloomington is home to the Mall of America, the largest shopping mall in the United States by number of stores, which opened in 1992 on the former site of Metropolitan Stadium.

History

The area was inhabited by the Dakota people before European settlement; the 1851 Treaty of Traverse des Sioux opened the land to settlers, who named the township after Bloomington, Illinois. Bloomington remained agricultural until the post–Second World War suburban boom transformed it. Metropolitan Stadium, home of the Minnesota Twins and Vikings, stood in the city from 1956 until 1981, when the teams moved to the Metrodome in Minneapolis and the site was eventually redeveloped as the Mall of America.

Geography and economy

The southern part of the city is dominated by the Minnesota River valley bluffs and the Minnesota Valley National Wildlife Refuge. Bloomington hosts a large concentration of hotels — among the largest outside the Twin Cities downtowns — owing to its proximity to the Mall of America and to Minneapolis–Saint Paul International Airport, which borders the city to the northeast. Major employers include health, hospitality and technology firms.
