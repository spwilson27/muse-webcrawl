Oromo (Afaan Oromoo) is an Afro-Asiatic language belonging to the Cushitic branch. It is the most widely spoken Cushitic language and one of the most widely spoken languages in Africa, with more than 35 million native speakers, principally the Oromo people of Ethiopia and northern Kenya. It serves as an official working language in the Ethiopian region of Oromia and is used in education, media and administration there.

Oromo was formerly referred to by outsiders as "Galla", a term now considered pejorative and no longer used. The language is written in a modified Latin alphabet called Qubee, formally adopted in 1991.

History

Under the Ethiopian Empire, the public use and publication of Oromo were heavily restricted, and during the Derg regime the language was banned from education, mass media and public life. Restrictions were lifted after 1991, when the Qubee alphabet was adopted and Oromo entered widespread use in schools, newspapers, radio and television in Oromia.

Phonology

Oromo has a five-vowel system with phonemic vowel length, and a consonant inventory including ejective and implosive sounds. Long consonants (gemination) are also meaningful. Oromo is a tonal language in a limited sense, with pitch patterns interacting with grammatical case marking.

Grammar

Oromo is a subject–object–verb (SOV) language. Nouns are inflected for case, including nominative, accusative, genitive, dative, instrumental, locative and ablative forms, and verbs are heavily inflected for person, aspect, mood and voice. Grammatical gender (masculine/feminine) is marked on nouns and agreement targets.

Dialects

Oromo comprises a continuum of varieties, commonly grouped into Western, Central (including the widely understood Mecha/Wollega varieties), Eastern (Harar) and Southern (Borana) clusters, with a high degree of mutual intelligibility among most varieties.

Writing system

Qubee represents long vowels and geminate consonants by doubling letters, and uses digraphs such as ch, dh, ny, ph and sh. Historically, Oromo was also written in the Ge'ez script and in Arabic-based orthographies.
