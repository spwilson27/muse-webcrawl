Caruncle (botany)

The caruncle is a small fleshy outgrowth on some seeds.

In botany, a caruncle is a small, fleshy appendage found on the seeds of some flowering plants, typically near the hilum or micropyle. It is a type of elaiosome: a structure rich in lipids and proteins that attracts ants and other animals involved in seed dispersal.

Function

Ants carry caruncle-bearing seeds to their nests, consume the nutritious appendage, and discard the intact seed in waste areas, a dispersal strategy called myrmecochory. The discarded seed is often planted in nutrient-rich refuse, improving its chance of germination while the embryo itself remains unharmed.

Occurrence

Caruncles occur in a number of plant families, notably the spurge family (Euphorbiaceae); castor bean seeds, for example, bear a conspicuous caruncle. Similar seed appendages with different developmental origins include arils and strophioles.
