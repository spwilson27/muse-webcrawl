Money (novel)

Money: A Suicide Note is a 1984 novel by British author Martin Amis. It is widely regarded as one of Amis's best works and one of the finest novels of the 1980s, regularly appearing on lists of the greatest English-language novels of the twentieth century.

The novel is narrated by John Self, a British director of television commercials who travels to New York to make his first feature film, a project called Good Money (also called Bad Money). Self is a grotesque consumer — addicted to alcohol, junk food, pornography, and spending — and the novel satirizes the money-obsessed culture of the early 1980s on both sides of the Atlantic as Self is manipulated by his producer, Fielding Goodney, and comprehensively defrauded. A fictionalized "Martin Amis" appears as a character in the novel, hired to rewrite the screenplay, allowing the real Amis to play with questions of authorship and narrative reliability. The "suicide note" of the subtitle is deliberately ambiguous.

The novel was adapted as a two-part BBC television drama in 2010, starring Nick Frost as John Self.
