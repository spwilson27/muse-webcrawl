The Acropolis Museum (Greek: Μουσείο Ακρόπολης) is an archaeological museum in Athens, Greece, housing the finds from the Acropolis of Athens. Opened in 2009, it stands on the southeastern slope of the Acropolis rock, about 300 metres from the Parthenon, and replaced the small 19th-century museum on the rock itself.

Building

The museum was designed by Swiss-American architect Bernard Tschumi in collaboration with Greek architect Michael Photiadis, winning an international competition in 2001. The glass-and-concrete building is raised on columns above an excavated ancient Athenian neighbourhood, parts of which are visible through glass floors and were integrated into the museum's lowest level. The top-floor Parthenon Gallery is rotated to align exactly with the Parthenon itself, visible through panoramic glazing.

Collections

Displays are arranged chronologically and topographically: Archaic sculpture (including the Moschophoros and the korai) from the slopes and the pre-Parthenon sanctuary; finds connected with the Erechtheion (including original Caryatids, one having been removed to London in the 19th century); and, in the Parthenon Gallery, the surviving frieze, metopes and pedimental sculpture of the Parthenon arranged around a full-scale representation of the temple's core. Empty spaces and plain casts in the gallery mark the Parthenon sculptures held by the British Museum, whose return Greece continues to seek.

History

A first museum on the Acropolis opened in 1874. Plans for a larger museum were debated from the 1970s, driven by conservation needs and by Greece's campaign for the reunification of the Parthenon sculptures. After several competitions and delays — including litigation over nearby buildings — the new museum was inaugurated on 20 June 2009 and has since ranked among Greece's most-visited museums.
