Double acting ship

A double acting ship is a type of icebreaking ship designed to run ahead in open water and thin ice, but turn around and proceed stern-first in heavier ice in order to improve icebreaking capability. The concept, developed in Finland by Aker Arctic, takes advantage of the fact that a ship's stern fitted with azimuthing podded propulsion can break ice more efficiently than a conventional bow, because the propellers wash broken ice away and reduce friction on the hull.

Design

Double acting ships typically have a flared, ice-optimised stern and a conventional fuel-efficient bow for open-water sailing. Early examples include the Finnish oil tankers Tempera and Mastera, delivered in 2002–2003, and later Arctic shuttle tankers and LNG carriers developed for Russian Arctic projects. The design allows cargo ships to operate independently in moderate ice without icebreaker escort while retaining good open-water performance.
