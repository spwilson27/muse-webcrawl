Jordan Grand Prix was a Formula One constructor that competed from 1991 to 2005. Founded by Irish businessman Eddie Jordan, the team was based at Silverstone and became known for its bright yellow liveries, rock-and-roll image and ability to outperform its budget.

History

Jordan entered Formula One in 1991 after success in the junior categories, where the team had run future stars including Ayrton Senna and Damon Hill. The 1991 Jordan 191, powered by Ford engines, is often cited among the most beautiful Formula One cars ever made; that season the team gave Michael Schumacher his Grand Prix debut at Spa. Jordan took its first victory at the chaotic 1998 Belgian Grand Prix, where Hill led home a one-two finish. Heinz-Harald Frentzen won twice in 1999, taking Jordan to third in the constructors' championship, its best result. A final surprise win came with Giancarlo Fisichella at the rain-shortened 2003 Brazilian Grand Prix.

Sale and legacy

Declining sponsorship led Jordan to sell the team to the Midland Group in 2005. The Silverstone outfit subsequently raced as Midland, Spyker, Force India, Racing Point and, from 2021, Aston Martin. The team launched the Formula One careers of drivers including Schumacher, Rubens Barrichello, Eddie Irvine and Ralf Schumacher.
