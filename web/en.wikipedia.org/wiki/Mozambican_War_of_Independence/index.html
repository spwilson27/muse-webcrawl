The Mozambican War of Independence (1964–1974) was an armed conflict between the guerrilla forces of FRELIMO (Frente de Libertação de Moçambique) and the Portuguese military in the colony of Portuguese Mozambique. It formed part of the wider Portuguese Colonial War.

IMAGE: FRELIMO guerrilla fighters marching through the bush in northern Mozambique during the liberation war

Course of the war

FRELIMO, founded in 1962 under Eduardo Mondlane and based in Tanzania, launched its first attacks in Cabo Delgado province in September 1964. After Mondlane's assassination in 1969, Samora Machel took command. FRELIMO established "liberated zones" with schools and clinics in the north, while Portugal deployed tens of thousands of troops and pursued strategic-hamlet programmes. The war ended not on the battlefield but in Lisbon: Portugal's Carnation Revolution of April 1974 brought down the Estado Novo regime, and the new government negotiated the Lusaka Accord (September 1974), leading to Mozambique's independence on 25 June 1975.

Aftermath

Machel became the first president of independent Mozambique. The country soon descended into the Mozambican Civil War (1977–1992) between the FRELIMO government and RENAMO.
