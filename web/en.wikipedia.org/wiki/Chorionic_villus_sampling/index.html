Chorionic villus sampling

Chorionic villus sampling (CVS) is a form of prenatal diagnosis in which a sample of chorionic villi is taken from the placenta for genetic testing. Unlike amniocentesis, which is performed later, CVS is usually carried out between the 10th and 13th weeks of pregnancy.

Procedure

The procedure is performed either transcervically or transabdominally under ultrasound guidance, collecting a small sample of placental tissue. Because chorionic villi are fetal in origin, their chromosomes can be analyzed for conditions such as Down syndrome (trisomy 21), other chromosomal abnormalities, and specific single-gene disorders when a targeted test is indicated.

Risks and limitations

CVS carries a small risk of miscarriage, commonly quoted at roughly 0.5–1% above background in older literature, with more recent studies suggesting lower procedure-related losses in experienced hands. A limitation is confined placental mosaicism, in which the placental karyotype does not match that of the fetus, sometimes requiring follow-up amniocentesis.
