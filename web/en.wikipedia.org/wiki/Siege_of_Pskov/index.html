Siege of Pskov

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Pskov (August 1581 – February 1582) was the final major engagement of the Livonian War, in which the army of Stephen Báthory, King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania, besieged the great Russian fortress city of Pskov but failed to take it.

Background

After successful campaigns in 1579 (Polotsk) and 1580 (Velikiye Luki), Báthory marched on Pskov in 1581 to force Ivan IV to concede Livonia. The city, one of the largest fortresses in Russia, was defended by Prince Ivan Shuisky with a garrison of some 7,000 soldiers and armed townsmen, ringed by massive stone walls and over thirty towers.

The siege

Báthory's army — perhaps 50,000 Polish, Lithuanian, Hungarian and German troops — arrived in August 1581. Two great assaults in September and November were beaten back with heavy losses; a mine warfare and artillery bombardment failed to breach the defences, a relief raid by Russian cavalry cut Polish supply lines, and the besiegers suffered severely in the winter cold. The papal legate Antonio Possevino mediated negotiations at Yam-Zapolsky while the siege dragged on.

Aftermath

By the Truce of Yam-Zapolsky (January 1582) Ivan IV ceded all of Livonia and Polotsk to Poland-Lithuania, while Báthory abandoned the siege and returned captured Russian fortresses. Pskov's resistance had nonetheless saved northwestern Russia from deeper invasion and worn out Báthory's capacity to continue the war.
