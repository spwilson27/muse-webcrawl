The Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO) is an Australian federal government agency responsible for scientific research. Founded in 1926, it is one of the world's largest and most diverse research organisations, with sites across Australia and research spanning agriculture, health, energy, climate, manufacturing, minerals, and information technology.

CSIRO is Australia's national science agency.

History

CSIRO's origins lie in the Advisory Council of Science and Industry (1916) and the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR, 1926). Ian Clunies Ross, its influential chairman from 1949, oversaw its re-establishment as CSIRO in 1949 and its expansion into areas beyond primary industry.

Notable achievements

CSIRO inventions and discoveries include the mathematics underlying Wi-Fi local area networking (its WLAN patent became famous in litigation in the 2000s), the Hendra virus vaccine for horses, plastic (polymer) banknotes developed with the Reserve Bank and Note Printing Australia, the insect repellent Aerogard, radio astronomy advances including work at the Parkes Observatory, and major contributions to myxomatosis and rabbit biocontrol. CSIRO also operates national facilities including the Australia Telescope National Facility and the Marine National Facility.
