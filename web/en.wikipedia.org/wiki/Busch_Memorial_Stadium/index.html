Busch Memorial Stadium, often called Busch Stadium II, was a multi-purpose sports stadium in St. Louis, Missouri, that served as the home of the St. Louis Cardinals baseball team from 1966 to 2005. It replaced Sportsman's Park and was in turn replaced by the current Busch Stadium, which was built partly on its footprint.

Design and history

Part of the downtown redevelopment era that also produced the Gateway Arch (completed 1965), the stadium was a near-circular "cookie-cutter" bowl designed for both baseball and football. Its most distinctive feature was a ring of 96 arches around the top of the facade, echoing the Arch itself. It opened on May 12, 1966, with the Cardinals hosting the Atlanta Braves, and hosted the 1966 MLB All-Star Game weeks later.

The playing surface was natural grass until 1970, when artificial turf was installed; grass returned in 1996. The stadium hosted World Series in 1967, 1968, 1982, 1985, 1987, and 2004, including the Cardinals' championships of 1967 and 1982. It was also home at various times to the football Cardinals and, in 1995, the first season of the St. Louis Rams.

Demolition

The final game was played on October 19, 2005, a loss to the Houston Astros in the National League Championship Series. The stadium was demolished by implosion and wrecking ball in late 2005, and the new Busch Stadium opened on the site adjacent in April 2006.
