The Metamorphosis

The Metamorphosis (German: Die Verwandlung) is a novella by Franz Kafka, first published in 1915. It is one of Kafka's best-known works and a landmark of twentieth-century literature.

Plot

Travelling salesman Gregor Samsa wakes one morning to find himself transformed into a huge insect. Unable to work, he becomes a burden on his family, who at first care for him but gradually grow resentful and ashamed, confining him to his room. His sister Grete, initially his caretaker, eventually declares that they must get rid of "it." Gregor, wounded after his father drives him back with apples, dies; the family feels relief and looks forward to a fresh start.

Interpretation

The novella has been read as an exploration of alienation, guilt, family obligation, and the absurd. Its famous opening sentence and deadpan treatment of an impossible premise have made it a canonical text, widely taught and repeatedly adapted for stage and screen.
