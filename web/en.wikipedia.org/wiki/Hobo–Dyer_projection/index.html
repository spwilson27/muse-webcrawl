Hobo–Dyer projection

The Hobo–Dyer map projection is an equal-area cylindrical map projection commissioned in 2002 by Bob Abramms and Howard Bronstein of ODT Maps and designed by the cartographer Mick Dyer. A modification of the 1910 Behrmann projection, it uses standard parallels at 37.5° north and south, giving land masses a somewhat less vertically stretched appearance than the better-known Gall–Peters projection, whose standard parallels lie at 45°.

Characteristics

As a cylindrical equal-area projection, the Hobo–Dyer preserves the relative sizes of areas anywhere on the map, so continents appear in their true proportions — Africa, for example, appears much larger relative to Europe and North America than on the Mercator projection. Shapes are distorted, increasingly toward the poles, as with all cylindrical projections. The map is often printed double-sided, with a Pacific-centred, south-up view on the reverse, emphasising that "north up" is a convention.

Use and reception

The projection was promoted as a tool for education about global fairness and development, and was adopted by some non-governmental organisations and development agencies for world maps in reports and classrooms. Supporters argue that equal-area maps counter the visual over-emphasis on wealthy northern countries produced by the Mercator projection; critics note that all flat maps distort something, and that shape distortion in equal-area cylindrical maps can itself mislead.
