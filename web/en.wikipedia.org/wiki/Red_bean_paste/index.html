Red bean paste

Image placeholder: red bean paste (anko).

Red bean paste is a paste made from adzuki beans, used in East Asian and Southeast Asian cuisines as a filling or topping for sweet dishes. In Japanese it is called anko, in Korean pat, and in Chinese hóngdòu shā or dòushā. It is prepared by boiling the beans, sweetening them with sugar, and mashing or sieving them into a paste.

Types

In Japanese cuisine, tsubuan is a chunky paste in which the beans are only lightly mashed, while koshian is a smooth paste passed through a sieve to remove the skins. Chinese versions may be enriched with oil and flavoured, and are used in mooncakes and steamed buns. Korean pat fills rice cakes, breads and the shaved-ice dessert patbingsu.

Uses

Red bean paste fills countless confections: dorayaki and taiyaki in Japan, tangyuan and zongzi in China, and injeolmi and songpyeon in Korea. It is also eaten as a dessert soup, spread on toast, and used in modern fusion desserts and drinks.
