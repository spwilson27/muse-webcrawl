Æthelthryth

Saint Æthelthryth Also known as: Etheldreda, Audrey
Born: c. 636, Exning, Suffolk
Died: 23 June 679, Ely, Cambridgeshire
Venerated in: Roman Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodox Church, Anglican Communion
Feast day: 23 June
Æthelthryth, foundress and first abbess of Ely.

Æthelthryth (c. 636 – 23 June 679), also known as Etheldreda and by the shortened form Audrey, was an East Anglian princess, a Fenland and Northumbrian queen, and the foundress and first abbess of the monastery at Ely in Cambridgeshire. She is a saint in the Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church and the Anglican Communion.

Æthelthryth was the daughter of King Anna of East Anglia. Despite two marriages — first to Tondberct, chief of the South Gyrwe, and then to Ecgfrith, king of Northumbria — she is said to have preserved her virginity, having taken a vow of perpetual virginity in her youth. Her insistence on remaining chaste eventually drove her to leave the Northumbrian court.

Foundation of Ely

Around 672 Æthelthryth returned to the Isle of Ely, her dowry land, and founded a double monastery of monks and nuns on the site of the present Ely Cathedral. She ruled it as abbess until her death from a tumour on the neck in 679 — an affliction she reportedly regarded as penance for having worn fine necklaces in her youth. The word "tawdry" is traditionally said to derive from "Saint Audrey", whose feast-day fair sold cheap lace necklaces.

Veneration

The principal account of her life is in Bede's Ecclesiastical History of the English People, which praises her virtues and preserves a hymn in her honour. When her body was exhumed sixteen years after her death it was found incorrupt, and her shrine at Ely became a major pilgrimage site until its destruction in the Reformation.
