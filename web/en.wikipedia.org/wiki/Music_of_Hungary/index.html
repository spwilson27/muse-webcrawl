Music of Hungary

The music of Hungary includes Hungarian folk music, Roma music, classical music and popular music. Hungary has produced internationally celebrated composers such as Franz Liszt, Béla Bartók, Zoltán Kodály and György Ligeti, and its folk traditions have been central to its national identity.

Folk music

Hungarian folk songs employ distinctive pentatonic scales and rhythmic styles. The verbunkos, originally music for military recruiting, and the csárdás dance shaped the virtuoso style of nineteenth-century Roma bands, whose performances in cafés and at weddings carried Hungarian melodies across Europe. Bartók and Kodály, who began collecting peasant songs with the phonograph in 1905, distinguished this authentic folk tradition from the popular urban "Gypsy music" style.

Classical music

Franz Liszt, founder of the Academy of Music in Budapest that now bears his name, drew on verbunkos idioms in his Hungarian Rhapsodies. Ferenc Erkel created Hungarian national opera with works such as Hunyadi László and Bánk bán. In the twentieth century Bartók and Kodály fused folk sources with modernism, while Ligeti, György Kurtág and Péter Eötvös became leading figures of the postwar avant-garde. Renowned performers include conductors Georg Solti and Antal Doráti and pianists András Schiff and Zoltán Kocsis.

Popular music

During the socialist era, rock bands such as Illés, Metró and Omega achieved wide popularity; Omega's "Gyöngyhajú lány" was later adapted as the Scorpions' "White Dove." Since the 1970s the táncház (dance house) movement has revived folk music and dance among urban youth, and world-music acts such as Muzsikás have carried Hungarian folk music abroad.
