Charterparty

Charterparty
TypeMaritime contract
PartiesShipowner and charterer
Main typesVoyage charter; time charter; bareboat (demise) charter
Standard formsGENCON; NYPE; ASBATANKVOY
EtymologyFrom Latin carta partita, "divided charter"

A charterparty (sometimes charter party) is a maritime law contract between a shipowner and a charterer for the hire of a ship or the carriage of cargo. The term derives from the medieval practice of writing the contract in duplicate on one sheet, torn in two along an indented line — carta partita, a "divided charter" — so the halves could later be matched to prove authenticity.

Most world trade moves under charterparties

Types

  • Voyage charter — the ship is hired for a specific voyage between named ports; the owner pays operating costs and is paid freight, while loading and discharge time (laytime) and demurrage are fixed by contract.
  • Time charter — the ship is hired for a period; the charterer directs its commercial employment and pays bunkers and port charges, while the owner crews and maintains it and receives hire, usually per day.
  • Bareboat (demise) charter — the charterer takes possession of the ship itself, providing crew and assuming most owner responsibilities for the charter period.

Terms and standard forms

Charterparties set out the ship's description, cargo and trading limits, freight or hire rates, laytime and demurrage, bunker arrangements, and allocation of risks such as war or ice. Most are concluded on printed standard forms issued by organizations such as BIMCO — the GENCON form for voyage charters, the New York Produce Exchange (NYPE) form for time charters, and ASBATANKVOY for tankers — heavily amended by negotiated rider clauses.

Relation to bills of lading

When cargo is shipped under a charterparty, bills of lading issued for the cargo may incorporate the charterparty's terms by reference; as between owner and charterer the charterparty governs, while a third-party holder of the bill of lading may be bound by incorporated terms such as arbitration clauses. Disputes under charterparties are frequently resolved by maritime arbitration, notably in London under LMAA terms.
