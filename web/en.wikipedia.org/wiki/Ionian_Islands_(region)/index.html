The Ionian Islands region (Greek: Περιφέρεια Ιονίων Νήσων) is one of the thirteen administrative regions of Greece. It comprises the islands of the northern and central Ionian Sea; the island of Kythira, though geographically one of the Ionian Islands, is administered as part of the Attica region. The regional capital is Corfu.

Administration

The region was established in the 1987 administrative reform and is subdivided into five regional units: Corfu, Ithaca, Cephalonia, Lefkada and Zakynthos. Under the Kallikratis reform of 2011, the region became a fully self-governed entity with an elected regional governor and council; local government was further reorganised by the Kleisthenis I reform in 2019, which split several island municipalities.

Economy

Tourism dominates the regional economy, with Corfu, Zakynthos and Cephalonia receiving large numbers of visitors each summer. Agriculture — olive oil, currants and wine — and fisheries remain significant, and the region hosts the Ionian University, headquartered in Corfu.
