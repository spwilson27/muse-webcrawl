The Bloody Island massacre (also called the Clear Lake massacre) took place on 15 May 1850, when a detachment of the United States Army under Captain Nathaniel Lyon killed a large number of Pomo people on an island at the northern end of Clear Lake in California.

Background

The attack was mounted in retaliation for the killing of two notoriously abusive settlers, Charles Stone and Andrew Kelsey, by enslaved Pomo labourers. The soldiers attacked the island village of Bo-no-po-ti, whose inhabitants had no connection to the killings, and killed men, women and children indiscriminately.

Legacy

The massacre is one of the best-documented episodes of the California genocide. A California Historical Landmark marks the site, and the Elem Pomo Nation holds an annual memorial run and ceremony. See also California Indian Wars.
