Extraterrestrial intelligence

Extraterrestrial intelligence (ETI) refers to hypothetical intelligent extraterrestrial life. No such life form has ever been observed, and the question of whether or not other intelligent life exists remains the subject of scientific inquiry, philosophical debate and popular speculation. The search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI) uses scientific methods, principally radio telescopes and optical telescopes, to detect electromagnetic signals that might indicate the presence of a technological civilization.

The search

Modern SETI began in 1959 with a paper by Giuseppe Cocconi and Philip Morrison suggesting that radio telescopes were sensitive enough to detect transmissions from civilizations orbiting other stars. In 1960 Frank Drake conducted Project Ozma, the first modern SETI experiment, and in 1961 he formulated the Drake equation, which estimates the number of active, communicative extraterrestrial civilizations in the Milky Way galaxy. The equation multiplies factors including the rate of star formation, the fraction of stars with planets, the number of habitable planets per system, and the fraction of civilizations that develop detectable technology.

The Fermi paradox

The apparent contradiction between the high estimates for the probability of extraterrestrial civilizations and the lack of evidence for them is known as the Fermi paradox, after physicist Enrico Fermi, who in 1950 asked, "Where is everybody?" Proposed resolutions include the rarity of intelligent life (the Rare Earth hypothesis), the Great Filter, the zoo hypothesis, and the possibility that interstellar distances and timescales simply make contact unlikely so far.

Communication and consequences

Efforts to communicate deliberately, known as METI or active SETI, have sent messages such as the Arecibo message (1974) toward star clusters. The scientific community debates the wisdom of broadcasting Earth's presence. No confirmed signal of extraterrestrial intelligence has ever been detected; the famous "Wow! signal" of 1977 remains unexplained but has never repeated.
