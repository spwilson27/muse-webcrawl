Space-Based Infrared System

The Space-Based Infrared System (SBIRS) is a United States Space Force system of satellites and ground facilities providing persistent infrared surveillance for missile warning, missile defence, technical intelligence and battlespace awareness. It succeeded the Defense Support Program and is being succeeded in turn by the Next Generation Overhead Persistent Infrared (Next Gen OPIR) programme.

Components

The constellation combines satellites in geosynchronous orbit (SBIRS GEO), sensors hosted on satellites in highly elliptical orbits (SBIRS HEO), and associated ground stations and data-processing centres. Infrared sensors detect the heat plumes of missile launches within seconds, cueing warning networks. Lockheed Martin built the GEO satellites; the first, USA-230 (GEO-1), was launched in May 2011, with a constellation of six GEO spacecraft and multiple HEO payloads ultimately fielded.

History

SBIRS was long troubled by cost growth and delays, triggering repeated Nunn–McCurdy breaches in the 2000s and leading the Air Force to restructure the programme and at times consider a simpler alternative. Despite its troubled development, the operational system substantially improves on its predecessor's sensitivity and coverage.
