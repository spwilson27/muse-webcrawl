Levantine cuisine

Levantine cuisine is the traditional cuisine of the Levant, the region of the eastern Mediterranean encompassing modern Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Palestine, Israel, Cyprus, and parts of southern Turkey such as Hatay. It is a cuisine shaped by millennia of trade and empire — Phoenician, Roman, Arab, Ottoman and French influences have all left their mark — and it forms one of the most influential branches of Middle Eastern cooking.

Characteristics

Levantine cooking emphasizes fresh vegetables, legumes, grains, olive oil, lemon juice, garlic, and aromatic herbs and spices such as parsley, mint, sumac, za'atar, allspice, and cinnamon. Meals frequently begin with mezze, an assortment of small dishes — dips, salads, and stuffed items — shared communally and eaten with flatbread, classically pita. Grilled meats, especially lamb and chicken, are central to main courses, while rice and bulgur wheat serve as staple accompaniments.

Notable dishes

  • Hummus — a dip of mashed chickpeas blended with tahini, lemon juice, and garlic.
  • Falafel — deep-fried balls of ground chickpeas or fava beans with herbs.
  • Tabbouleh — a finely chopped parsley salad with bulgur, tomato, and mint.
  • Baba ghanoush / moutabal — smoky eggplant dips with tahini.
  • Kibbeh — bulgur and minced meat, eaten raw, baked, or fried.
  • Shawarma — marinated meat roasted on a vertical spit and shaved into wraps.
  • Fattoush — a bread salad with sumac-dressed vegetables and toasted pita.
  • Knafeh — a cheese pastry soaked in sweet syrup, associated with Nablus.

Beverages and sweets

Strong coffee — cardamom-scented Arabic coffee or Turkish coffee — anchors hospitality, alongside mint or sage tea and arak, an anise-flavoured spirit that turns milky when diluted. Desserts rely on syrup-soaked pastries such as baklava, stuffed dates and semolina cakes such as ma'amoul, and the pistachio-garnished rice pudding muhallebi.

Influence

Through the Lebanese and Syrian diaspora, Levantine dishes spread across the Americas, Australia, and Europe during the twentieth century; hummus and falafel in particular became global supermarket staples. In 2022 UNESCO inscribed elements of the region's food culture on its heritage lists, and the cuisine remains a touchstone of identity for the peoples of the Levant.
