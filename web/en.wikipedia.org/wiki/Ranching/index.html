Ranching is the practice of raising grazing livestock such as cattle, sheep, and horses over large areas of land known as rangeland or ranchland. A ranch is an area of land used for this purpose, typically including a ranch house and support buildings.

History

Ranching developed from the Spanish mesta system of livestock management, brought to the Americas by Spanish colonists in the 16th and 17th centuries. The traditions of the Mexican vaquero shaped the cowboy culture of the American West. Large-scale open-range ranching boomed in the late 19th century with the arrival of railroads, and was transformed by barbed wire and the severe winter of 1886–87.

Types of ranching

Cattle ranching is the most familiar form, but ranches may also raise sheep, horses, goats, and exotic species on game ranches. Dude ranches cater to tourists. In Argentina and Uruguay, cattle are raised on estancias; in Australia, enormous sheep and cattle stations cover the outback.

Ranch work

Core tasks include roundups, branding, castrating, vaccinating, calving, fencing, and moving herds between pastures. The work is traditionally done on horseback, though ATVs and helicopters are now common.

Modern ranching and the environment

Modern ranching faces scrutiny over greenhouse-gas emissions, water use, and overgrazing, but well-managed grazing is also promoted for grassland conservation, wildfire fuel reduction, and carbon sequestration.
