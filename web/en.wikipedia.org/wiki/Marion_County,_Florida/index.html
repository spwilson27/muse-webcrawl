Marion County is a county in north central Florida, United States. Its county seat and largest city is Ocala. The county was created in 1844 from parts of Alachua, Orange and Hillsborough counties and named for General Francis Marion, the "Swamp Fox" of the American Revolutionary War.

Geography and history

The county covers about 1,660 square miles, much of it within the Ocala National Forest — the southernmost national forest in the continental United States — and includes Silver Springs, one of Florida's oldest tourist attractions and the headwaters of the Silver River. The Ocala area grew up around Fort King, an Army post established in 1827 during the Seminole conflicts.

Horse capital

Marion County markets itself as the "Horse Capital of the World," a title grounded in its limestone-rich soil and hundreds of thoroughbred farms that have produced Kentucky Derby and Triple Crown winners, including Needles (1956) and Affirmed (1978). The Florida Thoroughbred industry is centred here, and the enormous World Equestrian Center opened in Ocala in 2021. The Villages, the vast retirement community, extends into the county's southern corner.
