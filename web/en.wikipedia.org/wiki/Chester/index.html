Overview

Chester is a cathedral city and the county town of Cheshire, England, on the River Dee close to the border with Wales. Founded by the Romans as the fortress of Deva Victrix around AD 79, it is one of the best-preserved walled cities in Britain, with a near-complete circuit of medieval walls built on Roman foundations.

History

Deva was one of the largest Roman fortresses in Britain, garrisoned by the Legio II Adiutrix and later the Legio XX Valeria Victrix; its amphitheatre is the largest known in Roman Britain. In the Middle Ages Chester was a major port on the Dee before silting shifted trade to Liverpool. The city preserves extensive black-and-white timber-framed buildings and its unique two-tiered medieval shopping galleries, the Rows.

Landmarks

Chester Cathedral, begun around 1092, combines Norman and Gothic fabric. Other landmarks include Chester Castle, the Eastgate Clock — reputedly one of the most photographed clocks in England — the racecourse at the Roodee, among the oldest in England, and Chester Zoo, one of the largest zoos in the United Kingdom.
