Cold case

A cold case is a crime, or suspected crime, that has not yet been fully resolved and is not the subject of a current criminal investigation, but for which new information could emerge from new witness testimony, re-examined archives, new or retained physical evidence, as well as fresh activities of the suspect. A case is considered "cold" when all investigative leads available to investigators have been exhausted.

Definition

There is no universal time threshold after which a case becomes cold; the term is applied when a case, typically a homicide or missing-person investigation, remains unsolved and inactive for an extended period. Many police forces maintain dedicated cold case units or periodic case-review processes. In the United States, the National Institute of Justice has funded cold case initiatives, noting that tens of thousands of unsolved homicides accumulate each decade.

Methods of resolution

DNA and forensic genealogy

Advances in DNA profiling have solved many cold cases by matching retained biological evidence against offender databases such as CODIS. Since 2018, investigative genetic genealogy — comparing crime-scene DNA against public genealogy databases — has identified suspects in decades-old cases, most famously the Golden State Killer, Joseph James DeAngelo, arrested in 2018 and convicted in 2020.

Other techniques

  • Re-interviewing witnesses or soliciting new tips through media appeals.
  • Re-analysis of fingerprints against enlarged automated databases (AFIS).
  • Reviewing case files with fresh investigators who may spot overlooked leads.
  • Developments such as a suspect's confession or an accomplice coming forward.

Notable examples

  • The Golden State Killer case, solved in 2018 through forensic genealogy.
  • The identity of "The Boy in the Box" (Joseph Augustus Zarelli), established in 2022 after 65 years.
  • The Bear Brook murders, where genealogy identified both victims and perpetrator Terry Peder Rasmussen.

In popular culture

Cold cases feature in television series such as Cold Case (2003–2010) and Cold Case Files, and in numerous true-crime podcasts, some of which have generated real investigative leads.
