Sanada clan

Home province: Shinano Province

Founder: Sanada Yukitaka

Crest: Rokumonsen (six coins)

Famous members: Sanada Masayuki, Sanada Yukimura

The Sanada clan (真田氏, Sanada-shi) was a Japanese samurai clan of Shinano Province (present-day Nagano Prefecture) that rose to prominence during the Sengoku period. Its most celebrated members are Sanada Masayuki and his son Sanada Yukimura (Nobushige), remembered as one of Japan's most legendary warriors.

History

Under Sanada Yukitaka, the clan became vassals of Takeda Shingen. After the Takeda fell in 1582, Masayuki skilfully manoeuvred among the great powers — the Tokugawa, Uesugi, Hōjō and Toyotomi — preserving the clan's lands around Ueda Castle. At the sieges of Ueda in 1585 and 1600, Sanada forces famously repelled much larger Tokugawa armies.

Before the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600, the family famously split: Masayuki and Yukimura sided with the Western Army while the elder son Nobuyuki joined the Tokugawa, ensuring the clan's survival whichever side won. After the Western defeat, Masayuki and Yukimura were exiled to Kudoyama.

Yukimura and Osaka

In 1614–1615 Yukimura escaped to serve Toyotomi Hideyori at the Siege of Osaka. His defence of the Sanadamaru outwork and his final charge against Tokugawa Ieyasu at the Battle of Tennōji made him a folk hero, often called "the last hero of the Sengoku period".

Edo period

Nobuyuki's line retained Matsushiro domain in Shinano until the Meiji Restoration, when the Sanada became counts in the kazoku peerage.
