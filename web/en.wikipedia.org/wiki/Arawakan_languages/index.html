The Arawakan languages (also called Maipurean) form one of the largest indigenous language families of South America, once spread from Central America and the Caribbean to Argentina. They include Wayuu, the most widely spoken indigenous language of Colombia and Venezuela, and Garifuna, an Arawakan language of Central America's Caribbean coast with African and Carib influences.

History

Arawakan-speaking peoples expanded along the great river systems of Amazonia and into the Antilles, where their Taíno language gave words such as canoe, hammock, hurricane and barbecue to world languages. Many Arawakan languages are now endangered or extinct following colonisation.

Classification

The family is usually divided into northern and southern branches; scholars debate the inclusion of several languages and its deeper relations within a proposed Macro-Arawakan stock.
