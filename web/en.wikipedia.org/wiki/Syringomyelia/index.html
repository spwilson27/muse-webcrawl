Syringomyelia

Syringomyelia is a generic term referring to a disorder in which a cyst or cavity forms within the spinal cord. Often, syringomyelia is used as a generic term before an etiology is determined. This cyst, called a syrinx, can expand and elongate over time, destroying the spinal cord. The damage may result in pain, paralysis, weakness, and stiffness in the back, shoulders, and extremities.

Causes

The most common cause is a Chiari malformation, in which cerebellar tissue protrudes into the foramen magnum and disturbs cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) flow. Other causes include spinal cord tumours, trauma, meningitis and arachnoiditis, and tethered cord syndrome. A related form, hydromyelia, involves dilatation of the central canal itself.

Symptoms

Symptoms typically develop slowly and often begin with a characteristic "cape-like" loss of pain and temperature sensation over the shoulders, arms, and back, caused by damage to spinothalamic fibers crossing in the centre of the cord — while light touch and proprioception, carried in the dorsal columns, are initially preserved ("dissociated sensory loss"). Later signs include hand weakness and wasting, spasticity of the legs, scoliosis, chronic pain, and bladder or bowel dysfunction. Because pain sensation is lost, burns and injuries to the hands may go unnoticed.

Diagnosis and treatment

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the spine demonstrates the syrinx and any associated Chiari malformation. Management addresses the underlying cause: posterior fossa decompression for Chiari-related cases, removal of tumours, or shunting of the syrinx cavity when it progresses. Many small, incidental syrinxes are simply monitored.
