Del

[Image: The nabla symbol]
The symbol ∇, called nabla or del

Del, or nabla, is a differential operator in vector calculus represented by the symbol ∇ (an inverted triangle, from the shape of a Greek harp, nabla). Treated formally as a vector of partial derivative operators, del is used to define the gradient, divergence and curl of fields, and appears in the Laplacian and other operators central to mathematical physics. The notation was developed by William Rowan Hamilton and popularized by Oliver Heaviside and J. Willard Gibbs in the 1880s.

Definition

In three-dimensional Cartesian coordinates, del is defined as ∇ = (i ∂/∂x + j ∂/∂y + k ∂/∂z). Applied to a scalar field φ, the "product" ∇φ is the gradient; the dot product ∇·v with a vector field is its divergence; and the cross product ∇×v is its curl. The combination ∇·∇ is the Laplace operator ∇². Del is not a true vector — it does not commute freely, and its "products" obey the rules of differentiation, including product rules for scalar and vector fields.

Uses

Del notation compactly expresses the fundamental equations of electromagnetism (Maxwell's equations), fluid dynamics (Navier–Stokes), gravitation and quantum mechanics, where the Hamiltonian of a particle is written with the Laplacian. Extensions into curvilinear coordinates and the covariant derivative adapt the operator to non-Cartesian geometry and general relativity.
