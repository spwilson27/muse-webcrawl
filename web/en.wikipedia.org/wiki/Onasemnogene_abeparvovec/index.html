Onasemnogene abeparvovec

Onasemnogene abeparvovec
Trade name: Zolgensma
Type: Gene therapy (AAV9 vector)
Route: Intravenous infusion, single dose

Onasemnogene abeparvovec, sold under the brand name Zolgensma, is a gene therapy used to treat spinal muscular atrophy (SMA), a severe genetic disorder caused by mutations in the SMN1 gene. It was developed by AveXis and is marketed by Novartis.

Mechanism

The therapy uses an adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) vector to deliver a functional copy of the human SMN1 gene into motor neuron cells. It is given as a single intravenous infusion, intended to halt the progressive loss of motor neurons that causes muscle weakness and atrophy in SMA, a leading genetic cause of infant death.

Approval and cost

Zolgensma was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in May 2019 for children under two years of age, with approvals in the European Union and other countries following. At its US launch price of about US$2.1 million, it was widely reported as the most expensive single-dose drug in the world, prompting debate about pricing of gene therapies.

Safety

Important risks include liver injury: patients receive corticosteroids around the time of infusion and liver function is monitored. Other reported issues include low platelet counts and elevated troponin. Screening for pre-existing antibodies to AAV9 is required before treatment.
