Planning

Planning is the process of thinking regarding the activities required to achieve a desired goal. It involves the creation and maintenance of a plan, such as psychological aspects that require conceptual skills. Planning is a fundamental property of intelligent behaviour and a core function of management in organisations of every kind, as well as a part of everyday individual life.

Overview

A plan typically sets out goals, the actions needed to reach them, the resources required, responsibilities, and a timetable. Planning may be formal or informal, strategic or operational, long-term or short-term. In management theory, planning is often listed as the first of the classic functions — planning, organising, leading, and controlling — because the other functions depend on the objectives and courses of action that planning establishes.

Types of planning

  • Strategic planning — long-range planning by senior management that defines an organisation's mission and overall direction.
  • Tactical and operational planning — shorter-term plans that translate strategy into departmental targets and day-to-day schedules.
  • Urban and regional planning — the design and regulation of land use in towns, cities, and regions (see urban planning).
  • Economic planning — government direction of economic activity, ranging from indicative planning to the central planning of command economies.
  • Automated planning — in artificial intelligence, the generation of action sequences by software agents.
  • Personal planning — time management, financial planning, and life planning by individuals.

The planning process

Common models of planning describe a cycle: analyse the current situation, set objectives, generate and evaluate alternative courses of action, choose and implement a course, then monitor results and revise. Planning theorists note a persistent tension — plans must be specific enough to guide action yet flexible enough to survive contact with changing circumstances. Critics of elaborate planning point to the "planning fallacy", the systematic underestimation of time, cost, and risk, while supporters observe that the value of planning often lies in the thinking it forces rather than in the document it produces.

Neuroscience and psychology

In cognitive psychology and neuroscience, planning is one of the executive functions, involving the prefrontal cortex. Tests such as the Tower of London and Tower of Hanoi tasks measure the ability to order sub-goals and foresee consequences. Damage to frontal systems characteristically impairs planning while leaving many other abilities intact.
