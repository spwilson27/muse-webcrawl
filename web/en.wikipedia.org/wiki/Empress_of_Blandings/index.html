Empress of Blandings

The Empress of Blandings is a fictional pig, a prize-winning Berkshire sow in the Blandings Castle stories of P. G. Wodehouse. She belongs to Lord Emsworth, the absent-minded ninth earl, whose one true passion in life is his pig. The Empress is first mentioned in the short story "Pig-hoo-o-o-o-ey" (1927) and appears throughout the Blandings novels and stories.

Character

The Empress is a large black Berkshire sow, repeatedly described as resembling a streamlined elephant. She has won first prize in the Fat Pigs class at the Shropshire Agricultural Show three years in succession, a record of which Lord Emsworth is immensely proud. Her chief attendants and admirers include the head pigman George Cyril Wellbeloved and his successors.

Role in the stories

As the object of Lord Emsworth's adoration, the Empress is a frequent target of schemes: rivals attempt to steal her, sabotage her diet, or substitute inferior pigs, while Emsworth's domineering sisters and his villainous neighbour Sir Gregory Parsloe-Parsloe — owner of the rival pig Pride of Matchingham — plot against her. Her feeding, especially the famous pig call "Pig-hoo-o-o-o-ey", is central to several plots. In Heavy Weather (1933) and Full Moon (1947) the theft or threatened theft of the Empress drives much of the comedy.
