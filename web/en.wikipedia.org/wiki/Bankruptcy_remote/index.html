Bankruptcy remote

Bankruptcy remote, in structured finance, describes a legal structure in which a special purpose entity (SPE) or special purpose vehicle (SPV) is designed so that it is highly unlikely to be affected by the bankruptcy of its sponsor or parent company, and unlikely to file for bankruptcy itself. Bankruptcy remoteness allows assets transferred to the entity — for example, mortgage loans in a securitisation — to be insulated from the creditors of the originator, protecting investors in the securities the entity issues.

Techniques

  • True sale — assets are sold to the SPV in a transaction intended to be respected as a sale rather than a secured loan, removing them from the sponsor's bankruptcy estate.
  • Independent directors — the SPV's board includes directors independent of the sponsor whose consent is required for a voluntary bankruptcy filing.
  • Non-petition covenants — transaction parties agree not to commence involuntary bankruptcy proceedings against the SPV.
  • Separateness covenants — the entity keeps separate books, accounts and premises, holds itself out as separate, and does not commingle assets.
  • Limited purpose and indebtedness — organisational documents restrict the entity's business and its ability to incur other debt.

Limits

Bankruptcy remoteness is a matter of probability, not certainty. Courts may order substantive consolidation of an SPV with its parent in exceptional circumstances, and fraudulent-transfer law can unwind transfers. Rating agencies typically require legal opinions on these issues before rating securities issued by an SPV.
