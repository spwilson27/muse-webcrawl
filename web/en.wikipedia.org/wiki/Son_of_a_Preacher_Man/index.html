Son of a Preacher Man

"Son of a Preacher Man" is a song written by American songwriters John Hurley and Ronnie Wilkins, most famously recorded by British singer Dusty Springfield in 1968 for her album Dusty in Memphis. The song reached number 9 on the UK Singles Chart and number 10 on the Billboard Hot 100, and is regarded as one of Springfield's signature recordings.

Background

Hurley and Wilkins wrote the song with Aretha Franklin in mind; Atlantic's Jerry Wexler instead offered it to Springfield for her Memphis sessions with the Memphis Cats and the Sweet Inspirations. The lyric tells of "Billy Ray", the preacher's son who steals time with the narrator when her father visits parishioners. Franklin later recorded her own version (1970), reaching the US top 20 in turn.

Reception and legacy

The recording is frequently listed among the greatest songs of all time — Rolling Stone placed it in its 500 Greatest Songs list. A new generation discovered it through Quentin Tarantino's film Pulp Fiction (1994), whose soundtrack propelled the Dusty in Memphis track back into charts worldwide. The song has been covered by artists including Elvis Presley (recorded 1968), Janis Joplin (live), Linda Ronstadt and Katey Sagal.
