Goole is a port town and civil parish on the River Ouse in the East Riding of Yorkshire, England. It is 19 miles (31 km) south of York and 28 miles (45 km) west of Hull. The town has a population of about 19,500.

Goole is the most westerly port in Yorkshire, being about 45 miles (72 km) from the North Sea. It is also Yorkshire's principal inland port, handling significant quantities of cargo each year despite its distance from the sea.

History

The name Goole derives from the Middle English goule, meaning a channel or stream. The Dutch civil engineer Cornelius Vermuyden diverted the River Don northwards to the River Ouse in 1626–1629 to drain the marshy areas around Hatfield Chase, which changed the geography of the region.

The modern town was founded when the Aire and Calder Navigation Company obtained an Act of Parliament in 1820 to build a canal from Leeds to Goole, together with docks at Goole. The canal opened in 1826, and the company built much of the town, including streets, housing and the distinctive "salt and pepper pot" water towers. Coal from the Yorkshire coalfield was the principal cargo, exported through the port, and a system of "Tom Pudding" compartment boats carried coal along the canal to be hoisted directly into ships by hydraulic hoists.

The port

The Port of Goole is operated by Associated British Ports. It handles around two million tonnes of cargo annually, including containers, forest products, steel, and bulk cargoes. Eight enclosed docks are connected to the canal and river system. The port is unusual in being so far inland yet remaining tidal.

Transport

Goole railway station is on the Pontefract Line and the Hull and Doncaster branch, with services to Leeds, Hull and Doncaster. The M62 motorway passes just north of the town, linking it to Hull in the east and Leeds and Manchester in the west.

Landmarks

The twin water towers, nicknamed the "salt and pepper pots," are the town's best-known landmarks; the older tower, built in 1926, was once the largest of its type in Britain. The parish church of St John the Evangelist was built in 1843–1848 to serve the growing port community.
