Norman Fairclough

[Image: Norman Fairclough]

Norman Fairclough (born 1941) is a British linguist and professor emeritus at Lancaster University. He is one of the founders of critical discourse analysis (CDA), an approach to studying language as a form of social practice, particularly its role in power relations and ideology.

Work

Fairclough's book Language and Power (1989) introduced his three-dimensional model of discourse analysis, distinguishing the text itself, discursive practice (production and interpretation), and social practice. His subsequent works include Discourse and Social Change (1992), Critical Discourse Analysis (1995), New Labour, New Language? (2000), a study of the rhetoric of Tony Blair's government, and Analysing Discourse (2003).

Concepts

He is associated with notions such as the "marketization" of public discourse, the conversationalization of institutional language, orders of discourse, and the dialectical-relational approach to CDA, which examines discourse as an element of social processes that both shapes and is shaped by other elements such as power and institutions. With Isabela Fairclough he wrote Political Discourse Analysis (2012), applying argumentation theory to political texts.

Influence

Fairclough's framework has been widely used across linguistics, media studies, education, and political science; it is commonly contrasted with the discourse-historical approach of Ruth Wodak and the socio-cognitive approach of Teun van Dijk.
