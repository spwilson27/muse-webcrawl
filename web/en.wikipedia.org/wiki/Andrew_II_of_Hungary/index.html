Andrew II, King of Hungary and Croatia (1205–1235).

Andrew II (Hungarian: II. András; c. 1177 – 26 October 1235), also known as Andrew of Jerusalem, was King of Hungary and Croatia from 1205 to his death in 1235. The younger son of Béla III, he contested the throne with his brother King Emeric for years before succeeding him.

Reign

Andrew's rule was marked by lavish grants of royal estates and revenues to his followers — his "new institutions" — which weakened the crown and provoked the barons and lesser nobles. In 1217–18 he led the Hungarian contingent on the Fifth Crusade to the Holy Land, earning his epithet "of Jerusalem", though the campaign achieved little. Resistance to his policies culminated in the Golden Bull of 1222, a charter often compared to Magna Carta, which guaranteed the rights of the nobility and the Church, limited royal largesse, and even granted a right of resistance against unlawful royal acts. Andrew's wife Gertrude of Merania was assassinated by discontented nobles in 1213, an event dramatized in József Katona's play Bánk bán.

Legacy

Andrew was succeeded by his son Béla IV, who sought to reverse his father's grants and rebuild royal authority before the Mongol invasion. Andrew's daughter Elizabeth became one of the most beloved saints of medieval Europe.
