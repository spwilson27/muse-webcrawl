Dystopian fiction

Dystopian fiction is a subgenre of speculative fiction that explores social and political structures in a dark, nightmare world. The term dystopia was coined as an antonym of utopia.

Dystopian fiction
Genre: Speculative fiction
Themes: Totalitarianism, surveillance, environmental disaster

History

Early examples include Yevgeny Zamyatin's We (1921), Aldous Huxley's Brave New World (1932) and George Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four (1949). The genre expanded again in young-adult fiction with The Hunger Games and Divergent.

Themes

Common themes include oppressive government control, propaganda, dehumanisation through technology, environmental collapse, and an individual protagonist struggling against the system, often as a warning.
