E-commerce in China

E-commerce in China is the largest e-commerce market in the world. China surpassed the United States in online retail sales in 2013 and has since grown to account for a uniquely high share of total retail sales, driven by near-universal mobile payment adoption through platforms such as Alipay and WeChat Pay.

Major platforms

The market is dominated by Alibaba Group's marketplaces Taobao and Tmall, JD.com's direct-retail model, and Pinduoduo's group-buying social commerce, with newer growth in livestreaming commerce and short-video platforms such as Douyin and Kuaishou. The annual Singles' Day shopping festival on 11 November, popularized by Alibaba in 2009, became the world's largest online shopping event.

Characteristics

Chinese e-commerce is characterized by mobile-first shopping, integration of social media, messaging and payment "super-apps", extensive logistics networks such as Cainiao, rapid delivery, and the early mass adoption of livestream selling and social group buying. Cross-border e-commerce and rural e-commerce programs have been promoted by government policy, while regulatory scrutiny of platform monopolies intensified from 2020.
