Mohammed Nadir Shah

Mohammed Nadir Shah (9 April 1883 – 8 November 1933) was King of Afghanistan from 1929 until his assassination in 1933. A member of the Musahiban branch of the Barakzai dynasty, he came to power after the civil war that ended the brief rule of Habibullah Kalakani.

Early life and career

Nadir was born in Dehradun, British India, into an exiled branch of the Afghan royal family. Returning to Afghanistan after 1901, he rose in the army, served as minister of war under King Amanullah Khan, and distinguished himself in the Third Anglo-Afghan War of 1919. After a falling-out with Amanullah he withdrew abroad, serving as ambassador to France and then living in exile on the French Riviera.

Reign

When the reformist Amanullah was overthrown in 1929 by the Tajik bandit leader Habibullah Kalakani, Nadir returned with his brothers, raised a tribal army from the frontier regions, and captured Kabul in October 1929. Kalakani was executed, and Nadir was proclaimed king.

Nadir Shah's reign was deliberately cautious: he rolled back some of Amanullah's rapid social reforms to conciliate conservative and religious forces, convened a loya jirga that adopted a new constitution in 1931, and relied heavily on his brothers — the Musahiban — who dominated the government. He pursued modest modernisation of the army, administration, banking and education while maintaining Afghanistan's neutrality and improving relations with Britain and the Soviet Union.

Assassination and legacy

On 8 November 1933 Nadir Shah was shot dead at a school graduation ceremony in Kabul by Abdul Khaliq, a young Hazara whose family had grievances against the regime. He was succeeded by his 19-year-old son, Mohammed Zahir Shah, who reigned for forty years. Nadir Shah is remembered as the founder of the Musahiban dynasty, whose rule lasted until 1973.
