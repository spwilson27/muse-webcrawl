Spaghetti and meatballs

CourseMain course
Place of originUnited States (Italian-American)
Main ingredientsSpaghetti, tomato sauce, meatballs

Spaghetti and meatballs is an Italian-American dish consisting of spaghetti noodles, tomato sauce and meatballs. Although inspired by Italian cuisine, the combination as commonly served — large meatballs in a marinara-style sauce over spaghetti — was developed in the United States by Italian immigrants in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries and is not a traditional dish in Italy, where meatballs (polpette) are typically smaller and served by themselves.

Origins

Italian immigrants arriving in America found meat far cheaper and more abundant than in Italy, so meatballs grew larger and meat became a centrepiece rather than a garnish. At the same time, canned tomatoes — available year-round — made tomato sauce a constant, and spaghetti was among the few familiar Italian pastas widely sold in the United States. Restaurants in Italian neighbourhoods of New York and other cities combined the three into an affordable, filling plate, and the dish became a defining staple of Italian-American "red sauce" cooking. The U.S. National Pasta Association published an early recipe for spaghetti and meatballs in the 1920s.

Preparation

Meatballs are usually made from ground beef (often mixed with pork or veal), breadcrumbs, egg, grated Parmesan or Pecorino, garlic and parsley, and are either pan-fried, baked, or braised directly in tomato sauce. The sauce is typically a simple tomato, garlic and basil or oregano sauce. The dish is served over spaghetti cooked al dente and finished with grated cheese.

In Italy

Regional Italian dishes offer partial precedents — for example, pasta al forno in southern Italy may contain tiny meatballs — but serving large meatballs on spaghetti with tomato sauce is generally regarded in Italy as an American invention, a fact sometimes cited as an example of how diaspora cuisines evolve.
