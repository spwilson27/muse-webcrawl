The Symphony No. 5 in C minor, Op. 67, is a symphony composed by Ludwig van Beethoven between 1804 and 1808. It is one of the best-known compositions in classical music and one of the most frequently played symphonies in the repertoire, famous above all for its distinctive opening four-note motif — short-short-short-long — which permeates the work. The symphony has been widely interpreted as an embodiment of struggle and triumph, a narrative of "fate knocking at the door", and it marks the transition from Beethoven's middle ("heroic") period style.

Composition and premiere

Beethoven worked on the Fifth Symphony intermittently from 1804, alongside other projects, while his deafness was worsening. It was premiered at a marathon benefit concert at the Theater an der Wien on 22 December 1808, which also featured the premiere of the Sixth Symphony, the Fourth Piano Concerto and parts of the Mass in C. The symphony is dedicated to Prince J. F. M. Lobkowitz and Count Andreas Razumovsky. E. T. A. Hoffmann's celebrated 1810 review hailed the work as a pinnacle of instrumental music.

Music

The first movement, Allegro con brio in C minor, is built almost entirely from the opening motif (three repeated Gs and a held E♭). The second movement, Andante con moto in A♭ major, is a lyrical set of variations on two alternating themes. The third movement, Scherzo: Allegro, returns to C minor and leads without pause — in a revolutionary transition passage featuring the motif over hushed timpani — into the triumphant C-major finale, Allegro, which introduces trombones, piccolo and contrabassoon to the symphony orchestra for the first time in this context. The finale recalls the scherzo before its final blaze of C-major chords.

Reception and cultural impact

The symphony's motif coincided with the Morse code for "V" (•••−), and it was adopted as a symbol of Allied victory ("V for Victory") during the Second World War, opening BBC broadcasts to occupied Europe. It has been quoted, parodied and adapted across popular culture, from disco (Walter Murphy's "A Fifth of Beethoven", 1976) to countless films and advertisements.
