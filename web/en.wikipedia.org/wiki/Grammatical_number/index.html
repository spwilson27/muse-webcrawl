Grammatical number

In linguistics, grammatical number is a grammatical category of nouns, pronouns, adjectives, and verb agreement that expresses count distinctions. The most common distinction, found in English and many other languages, is between singular (one) and plural (more than one).

Types of number

Many languages make finer distinctions. The dual marks exactly two, as in Classical Arabic, Slovene, and Sorbian. Some languages have a trial (exactly three) or a paucal (a few). Ancient Greek had singular, dual, and plural. Number may be marked on the noun itself, on modifiers, or through verb agreement, and in some languages, such as Chinese and Japanese (outside of pronouns and certain nouns), number marking is generally optional or absent.

Marking

English forms the plural of most nouns with the suffix -s or -es, with irregular plurals such as children, men, and sheep. Other languages use reduplication, internal vowel change, classifiers, or separate plural words. In verb agreement, English distinguishes third-person singular -s from other forms.
