O Come, O Come, Emmanuel

"O come, O come, Emmanuel" is a Christian hymn for Advent and Christmas. It is a metrical paraphrase of the O Antiphons, a series of plainchant antiphons used at Vespers in the days leading up to Christmas in the Western Christian tradition. The text is based on a Latin version dating to the early 18th century, and was translated into English in the mid-19th century, most famously by John Mason Neale in 1851.

Text

Each stanza addresses Christ by one of the titles drawn from the O Antiphons—Emmanuel, Wisdom, Lord of Might (Adonai), Rod of Jesse, Key of David, Dayspring, and Desire of Nations—and is followed by the refrain:

Rejoice! Rejoice! Emmanuel
Shall come to thee, O Israel.

The opening stanza begins: "O come, O come, Emmanuel, and ransom captive Israel, that mourns in lonely exile here, until the Son of God appear." The hymn expresses longing for the coming of the Messiah and is among the most widely sung Advent hymns in English-speaking churches. Its melody, often called Veni Emmanuel, is derived from a 15th-century French processional chant.
