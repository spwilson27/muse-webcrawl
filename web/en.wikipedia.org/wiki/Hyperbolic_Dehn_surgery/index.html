Hyperbolic Dehn surgery

In mathematics, hyperbolic Dehn surgery is an operation by which one can obtain further hyperbolic 3-manifolds from a given cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold. Hyperbolic Dehn surgery exists only in dimension three and is one feature that distinguishes hyperbolic geometry in three dimensions from higher dimensions.

Definition

Let M be a finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold with cusps, each cusp homeomorphic to a torus crossed with a half-line. A Dehn surgery on a cusp consists of removing a neighbourhood of the cusp and gluing in a solid torus along a chosen slope, a simple closed curve on the cusp torus. The surgery is determined by the slope, often written as a rational number p/q or as a pair of coprime integers.

Thurston's theorem

William Thurston proved that, with only finitely many exceptions for each cusp, the resulting manifold is again hyperbolic, and that as the surgery coefficients tend to infinity the filled manifolds converge geometrically to the original cusped manifold. This theorem was a key step in Thurston's geometrization programme: it implies that all but finitely many Dehn surgeries on a hyperbolic knot in the 3-sphere yield hyperbolic manifolds, and it underlies constructions such as the proof that the figure-eight knot complement is hyperbolic. The number of exceptional (non-hyperbolic) surgeries on a single cusp is bounded; for the figure-eight knot there are exactly ten exceptional slopes.

Significance

Because every closed orientable 3-manifold can be obtained by Dehn surgery on a link in the 3-sphere (the Lickorish–Wallace theorem), hyperbolic Dehn surgery provides a way to generate and study an enormous variety of hyperbolic manifolds from a small number of cusped ones.
