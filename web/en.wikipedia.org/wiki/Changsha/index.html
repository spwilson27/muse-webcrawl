Changsha (simplified Chinese: 长沙; traditional Chinese: 長沙) is the capital and most populous city of Hunan Province in south-central China. Located on the lower reaches of the Xiang River, it has a history of more than 3,000 years and was the capital of the Changsha Kingdom in the Han dynasty. The Han-era tombs at Mawangdui, excavated in the 1970s, yielded the remarkably preserved body of Xin Zhui and silk manuscripts of the Tao Te Ching.

History

Changsha was heavily damaged in the Sino-Japanese War: the deliberate Wenxi Fire of 1938, set by retreating Chinese forces, destroyed most of the city, and three battles for Changsha followed between 1939 and 1944. Mao Zedong began his political career in Changsha, where he studied and taught; the young Mao statue on Orange Isle is a modern landmark. Today Changsha is a major centre for media and entertainment (Hunan Television), construction machinery, and a growing technology sector.

Geography and culture

The city lies on the Xiang River plain west of the Mufu Mountains, with Yuelu Mountain rising on the west bank. It is famous for Hunan cuisine — fiery dishes flavoured with fresh chillies — and for stinky tofu sold at its night markets.
