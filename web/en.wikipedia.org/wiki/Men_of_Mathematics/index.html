Men of Mathematics: The Lives and Achievements of the Great Mathematicians from Zeno to Poincaré is a 1937 book of biographies of famous mathematicians, written by Scottish-American mathematician and historian E. T. Bell (1883–1960), a professor at the California Institute of Technology. After a brief introductory chapter on three ancient mathematicians, the book is devoted to the lives of about three dozen mathematicians who flourished in the seventeenth through nineteenth centuries.

The book was a popular success and inspired many young people, including John Forbes Nash Jr., Julia Robinson, and Freeman Dyson, to become research mathematicians. It was never intended to be strictly accurate historically and contains several romanticised accounts of its subjects.

Contents

The chapters are arranged roughly chronologically. Following an introduction on Zeno, Eudoxus, and Archimedes, the book profiles figures including Descartes, Fermat, Pascal, Newton, Leibniz, the Bernoulli family, Euler, Lagrange, Laplace, Monge, Fourier, Poncelet, Gauss, Cauchy, Lobachevsky, Abel, Hamilton, Galois, Sylvester, Weierstrass, Boole, Hermite, Kronecker, Riemann, Kummer, Dedekind, Poincaré, and Cantor.

Reception

Reviewers praised the book's readability and drama. Later historians of mathematics have criticised Bell for inaccuracies, including his influential but fabricated account of Évariste Galois's tragic final night, based partly on his repeated account of Galois frantically writing down his mathematical testament before his fatal duel — a story scholars have shown to be largely myth.
