Inbox zero

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image placeholder: empty email inbox]

Inbox zero is a time-management and email-management approach popularised by productivity writer Merlin Mann in talks and articles from 2007. The "zero" refers not merely to having no messages in the inbox, but to the amount of time one's brain spends thinking about the inbox: the inbox should be emptied regularly so it stops functioning as an unmanaged to-do list.

Method

Mann proposed processing mail in batch sessions with a small set of possible actions for every message — delete, delegate, respond, defer, or do — so each item leaves the inbox on first handling. The approach borrows from David Allen's Getting Things Done methodology and treats the inbox as a temporary staging area rather than a filing system. Later software, such as the Mailbox app and Gmail's scheduling and snoozing features, built the workflow into products.

Criticism

Critics argue that an obsessive focus on an empty inbox can itself become a distraction, that email volume in most jobs is driven by organisational culture rather than personal technique, and that alternative strategies — checking mail rarely, using filters, or simply tolerating a large inbox — may be healthier. The term has nonetheless entered general business vocabulary for an empty, fully processed inbox.
