Georeferencing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Georeferencing is the process of assigning real-world coordinates to each pixel of a raster image or to the features of spatial data, so that the data can be aligned with other geographic information within a geographic information system (GIS). The term is commonly used in geographic information science to describe the process of associating a physical map, aerial photograph, or satellite image with locations in geographic space.

Process

In the standard workflow, a user selects ground control points (GCPs) — identifiable features such as road intersections or building corners — whose coordinates are known from maps, GPS survey, or reference imagery. A mathematical transformation (for example a first-order polynomial/affine transformation, or higher-order "rubber-sheeting" methods) is fitted to map the image's row/column coordinates onto geographic coordinates. The residual error, often summarised as root-mean-square error (RMSE), measures the quality of the fit.

Georeferencing normally includes choosing a spatial reference system: a geodetic datum (such as WGS 84), a map projection, and units. The resulting output can be stored with an accompanying "world file" for raster images, or embedded in formats such as GeoTIFF.

Geocoding and related terms

Georeferencing is related to, but distinct from, geocoding, which converts textual addresses or place names into coordinates. Georeferencing of historical maps is an important technique in digital humanities and historical GIS, enabling old cartography to be overlaid on modern data. Orthorectification extends georeferencing by also correcting relief displacement and sensor-model distortions in aerial and satellite imagery.

Applications

  • Digitising and integrating scanned paper maps into GIS databases.
  • Aligning drone and aerial photography for surveying and land management.
  • Historical GIS: comparing land use, coastlines, and urban growth over time.
  • Location-based archival description in libraries and museums (georeferencing museum specimens and photographs).
