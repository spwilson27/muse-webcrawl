Agulhas Current

OceanIndian Ocean
TypeWarm western boundary current
Named forCape Agulhas, South Africa
[Image: Map of the Agulhas Current flowing down the east African coast]
Map of the Agulhas Current flowing down the east African coast

The Agulhas Current is the warm western boundary current of the south-western Indian Ocean. It flows south-westward along the east coast of southern Africa from about 27°S to the vicinity of Cape Agulhas near 40°S, carrying warm tropical water from the Mozambique Channel and the waters east of Madagascar toward the Cape.

Course and behaviour

Fed by the Mozambique Channel Current and the East Madagascar Current, the Agulhas hugs the narrow continental shelf off Mozambique and South Africa, reaching surface speeds among the fastest of any ocean current. South of Cape Agulhas most of its water turns back east into the Indian Ocean as the Agulhas Return Current — the Agulhas retroflection — while some leaks into the Atlantic in the form of large warm-core rings shed at the retroflection.

Significance

The current strongly influences the climate of South Africa's east coast, keeping it warmer and wetter than the Atlantic-facing west coast cooled by the Benguela Current. Agulhas leakage is studied as a variable part of the global overturning circulation, and the current's steep waves, formed where it meets opposing winds and swells off the Wild Coast, are a long-standing hazard to shipping. The name comes from Cape Agulhas, from the Portuguese agulhas ("needles"), referring either to the compass needle's behaviour or to sharp offshore rocks.
