Trinity Laban Conservatoire of Music and Dance

The Laban Building in Deptford, London

Trinity Laban Conservatoire of Music and Dance is a music and dance conservatoire based in London, England. It was formed in 2005 by the merger of two older institutions: Trinity College of Music, founded in 1872, and the Laban Dance Centre, founded by the movement theorist Rudolf Laban.

History

Trinity College of Music began in 1872 as the Church Choral Society, offering classes in church music, and developed into one of Britain's principal music examination boards and teaching institutions. The Laban Centre grew out of Rudolf Laban's work on movement analysis; Laban had settled in England after fleeing Nazi Germany, and his school eventually became one of Europe's leading centres for contemporary dance training.

The merger created the United Kingdom's first conservatoire combining music and dance, encouraging collaboration between the disciplines.

Campus

The Faculty of Music is housed at King Charles Court, part of the Old Royal Naval College in Greenwich, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Faculty of Dance is based at the Laban Building in Deptford, designed by architects Herzog & de Meuron and opened in 2003; the building won the RIBA Stirling Prize for architecture.

Teaching and performance

Trinity Laban offers undergraduate and postgraduate degrees in classical music, jazz, musical theatre and dance, along with junior programmes and community outreach. Performance venues include Blackheath Halls, which the institution manages, and the theatres within its own buildings.
