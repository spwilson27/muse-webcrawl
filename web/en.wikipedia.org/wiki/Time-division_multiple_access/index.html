Time-division multiple access

Time-division multiple access (TDMA) is a channel access method for shared-medium networks. It allows several users to share the same frequency channel by dividing the signal into different time slots: each user transmits in rapid succession, one after the other, each using its own assigned slot, so a single channel can carry many simultaneous conversations.

Applications

TDMA is best known as the access method of 2G GSM mobile-phone systems, where each radio channel is divided into eight time slots, and it was also used in IS-136/D-AMPS and Personal Digital Cellular. Related time-division techniques underlie time-division multiplexing in telephony and time slots in satellite and military links.

Advantages and disadvantages

TDMA lets a transceiver listen and transmit on the same frequency without a duplexer, and handsets transmit only part of the time, saving battery. It requires tight synchronisation and wastes capacity in unused guard times; bursty traffic is handled less flexibly than in packet-based schemes.
