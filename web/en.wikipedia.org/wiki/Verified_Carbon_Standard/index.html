Verified Carbon Standard

The Verified Carbon Standard (VCS) is a greenhouse-gas crediting programme operated by the non-profit organisation Verra. Founded in 2005 (originally as the Voluntary Carbon Standard) by business and environmental groups including the International Emissions Trading Association and the World Business Council for Sustainable Development, it became the world's largest voluntary carbon-crediting programme, certifying projects in forestry, renewable energy, energy efficiency, waste management and other sectors.

How it works

Projects are developed under approved methodologies, validated and periodically verified by independent accredited auditors, and registered in Verra's registry. Each verified tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent avoided or removed generates one Verified Carbon Unit (VCU), which can be traded in the voluntary carbon market and retired by buyers seeking to offset their emissions. Requirements address additionality (the reductions must exceed business-as-usual), permanence, leakage, and avoidance of double counting.

Criticism and reform

The programme — particularly its forest-conservation (REDD+) credits — has faced heavy criticism. A major 2023 investigation by journalists and researchers concluded that many rainforest offset credits certified under the scheme likely overstated deforestation baselines and represented little real climate benefit; Verra disputed the analysis but subsequently overhauled its REDD+ methodology, introducing jurisdictional baselines set by Verra itself. The episode fuelled a broader debate over the integrity of voluntary carbon markets, prompting new governance initiatives for credit quality and corporate claims.
