Thomas Townshend, 1st Viscount Sydney (24 February 1733 – 30 June 1800) was a British politician who served twice as Home Secretary, under the Earl of Shelburne in 1782–83 and under William Pitt the Younger from 1783 to 1789. The city of Sydney, Australia, is named after him.

Career

Townshend entered Parliament in 1754 and held a succession of junior offices, including a lordship of the Treasury and the paymastership of the forces. As Home Secretary he was responsible for colonial affairs during the final phase of the American War of Independence and its aftermath, including the peace negotiations with the United States and the planning of the penal settlement at Botany Bay in New South Wales.

New South Wales and Sydney

In 1786 Townshend approved the plan to establish a convict settlement in Australia. Governor Arthur Phillip named the cove where the First Fleet anchored in 1788 "Sydney Cove" in his honor, and the settlement that grew there became the city of Sydney. Townshend was created Baron Sydney in 1783 and Viscount Sydney in 1789.

Later life

He retired from the Home Office in 1789 and died at his home, Frognal House in Sidcup, in 1800.
