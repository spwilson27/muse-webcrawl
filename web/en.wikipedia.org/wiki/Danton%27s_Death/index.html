Danton's Death

AuthorGeorg Büchner
Original titleDantons Tod
Written1835
First performance1902
GenreDrama

Danton's Death (German: Dantons Tod) is a drama in four acts by Georg Büchner, written in 1835 when the author was twenty-one. It dramatises the fall of Georges Danton, a leading figure of the French Revolution, during the Reign of Terror, culminating in his execution in April 1794.

Plot

The play contrasts the pleasure-loving, weary Danton, who has come to doubt the Revolution's course of violence, with the ascetic, incorruptible Maximilien Robespierre. As the Committee of Public Safety moves against him, Danton refuses to flee or to organise resistance, and is arrested, tried before the Revolutionary Tribunal and guillotined together with his friends, including Camille Desmoulins.

Composition and reception

Büchner wrote the play in about five weeks in early 1835, drawing extensively on historical sources, from which he quoted nearly verbatim in places. Because of censorship it was published only in an expurgated version, and it was not performed until 1902. It is now regarded as one of the most important German plays of the 19th century, notable for its episodic structure, its large cast of minor characters and its bleak view of history as an unstoppable mechanism.
