Gun Control Act of 1968

The Gun Control Act of 1968 (GCA or GCA68) is a U.S. federal law that regulates the firearms industry and firearms ownership. Signed into law by President Lyndon B. Johnson on October 22, 1968, the Act primarily resulted in the prohibition of certain categories of individuals from purchasing firearms, restrictions on the sale of firearms across state lines, and licensing requirements for firearms dealers, importers, and manufacturers.

Background

The Gun Control Act was the culmination of decades of debate over federal firearms regulation. Its predecessor, the National Firearms Act of 1934, had regulated machine guns, short-barreled rifles and shotguns, and other specified weapons. The assassinations of President John F. Kennedy in 1963 (with a mail-order rifle), Martin Luther King Jr. and Robert F. Kennedy in 1968, galvanized congressional action. President Johnson pressed Congress for stronger legislation in the wake of these killings.

Provisions

The Act broadly prohibits certain categories of persons, including convicted felons, fugitives from justice, unlawful users of controlled substances, persons adjudicated as mentally defective or committed to mental institutions, illegal aliens, persons dishonorably discharged from the armed forces, persons who have renounced United States citizenship, and persons subject to certain restraining orders, from shipping, transporting, receiving, or possessing firearms.

The GCA also prohibited the importation of "surplus military firearms" and of firearms not "generally recognized as particularly suitable for or readily adaptable to sporting purposes," subject to limited exceptions. It required federal licensing of firearms dealers, manufacturers, and importers, who became known as Federal Firearms Licensees (FFLs), and generally banned the interstate sale of handguns except between licensees. The Act also restricted the sale of firearms through the mail, a practice that had previously allowed Lee Harvey Oswald to obtain the rifle used to assassinate President Kennedy.

Amendments and legacy

The Act has been amended several times, most notably by the Firearm Owners Protection Act of 1986, which loosened some restrictions while banning civilian ownership of machine guns manufactured after May 19, 1986. The Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act of 1993 added a background-check requirement, later implemented through the National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS).
