Wind power by country describes the deployment of wind energy in individual nations. By the mid-2020s global installed wind capacity exceeded 1 terawatt, supplying roughly 7–8% of the world's electricity. China is by far the largest market, accounting for around 40% or more of global cumulative capacity, followed by the United States, Germany, and India. Several European countries obtain very high shares of their electricity from wind: Denmark has led the world with wind shares exceeding half of its electricity consumption in some years, with Ireland, Portugal, Spain, and Germany also achieving shares well above the global average.

Offshore wind

Offshore wind is concentrated in China and northern Europe. The United Kingdom, Germany, the Netherlands, Denmark, and Belgium pioneered large arrays in the North Sea; China overtook the UK in total offshore capacity in the early 2020s. The United States, Taiwan, Vietnam, South Korea and Japan have been developing their first utility-scale projects.

Growth and policy

Growth has been driven by falling turbine costs, feed-in tariffs, renewable portfolio standards, and competitive auctions. Many countries set records for instantaneous wind penetration, with grids in Denmark, Ireland (EirGrid), and South Australia sometimes running on more than 100% wind relative to demand, exporting the surplus or curtailing output.
