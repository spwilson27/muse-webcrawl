Treaty of Berlin (1878)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Treaty of Berlin
Signed13 July 1878
LocationBerlin, German Empire
SignatoriesUnited Kingdom, Austria-Hungary, France, Germany, Italy, Russia, Ottoman Empire
PurposeRevise the Treaty of San Stefano; settle the Balkans after the Russo-Turkish War

The Treaty of Berlin was signed on 13 July 1878 as the concluding act of the Congress of Berlin, convened by Chancellor Otto von Bismarck to revise the Treaty of San Stefano, which Russia had imposed on the Ottoman Empire after the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878. Britain and Austria-Hungary had objected to San Stefano's creation of a large Russian-aligned Bulgarian state.

Terms

The treaty recognised the full independence of Romania, Serbia, and Montenegro, and a much-reduced autonomous Principality of Bulgaria; Eastern Rumelia was made a separate autonomous Ottoman province, and Macedonia was returned to direct Ottoman rule. Austria-Hungary received the right to occupy and administer Bosnia and Herzegovina (while they formally remained Ottoman), Russia retained southern Bessarabia and gains in the Caucasus (Kars, Ardahan, and Batum), and Britain acquired Cyprus under a separate convention with the Sultan. Greece was promised boundary rectification, realised in 1881 with the cession of Thessaly.

Consequences

The settlement frustrated Bulgarian national aspirations — the enlarged Bulgaria of San Stefano remained a national ideal — and left grievances that contributed to the Balkan Wars of 1912–1913. The Austro-Hungarian occupation of Bosnia set the stage for the annexation crisis of 1908 and, ultimately, for the Sarajevo assassination of 1914. Bismarck's role as "honest broker" confirmed Germany's centrality in European diplomacy, while Russia's humiliation pushed it toward the Franco-Russian alliance of the 1890s.
