Rehabilitation Act of 1973

The Rehabilitation Act of 1973 is a United States federal law, codified as 29 U.S.C. § 701 et seq. It replaced the Vocational Rehabilitation Act, extending and revising the authorization of grants to states for vocational rehabilitation services, and emphasised services to people with the most severe disabilities. The Act also contains several provisions prohibiting discrimination on the basis of disability.

Section 504

The Act's best-known provision, Section 504, states: "No otherwise qualified individual with a disability in the United States … shall, solely by reason of her or his disability, be excluded from the participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under any program or activity receiving Federal financial assistance." Section 504 was a landmark civil rights protection and a model for the later Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990. Implementing regulations were issued only in 1977, after protests by disability activists including a sit-in at federal offices in San Francisco.

Other sections

Section 501 prohibits disability discrimination in federal employment and requires affirmative action; Section 503 imposes similar obligations on federal contractors; Section 508, added in 1986 and strengthened in 1998, requires federal electronic and information technology to be accessible to people with disabilities.
