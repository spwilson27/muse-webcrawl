Purpura

Purpura are red or purple discoloured spots on the skin, mucous membranes or organs caused by bleeding underneath the surface. Unlike rashes from dilated vessels, purpura do not blanch under pressure. Individual spots measure roughly 3 mm to 1 cm; smaller spots are called petechiae and larger ones ecchymoses.

Causes

Purpura result from disorders of platelets (such as immune thrombocytopenic purpura), blood vessel fragility or inflammation (vasculitis, including IgA vasculitis, formerly Henoch–Schönlein purpura), coagulation defects, infections such as meningococcal disease, scurvy and certain drugs. Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura is a serious disorder involving microvascular clotting.

Evaluation

Assessment includes history, examination, full blood count, coagulation studies and sometimes skin biopsy; new widespread purpura with fever is a medical emergency.
