Hell Gate Bridge

Crosses: Hell Gate, East River
Locale: New York City (Queens–Bronx, via Wards Island)
Design: Through arch bridge
Longest span: 298 m (978 ft)
Opened: 9 March 1917
Designer: Gustav Lindenthal

The Hell Gate Bridge, originally the New York Connecting Railroad Bridge or The East River Arch Bridge, is a steel through-arch railroad bridge in New York City. It carries Amtrak's Northeast Corridor and freight traffic across the Hell Gate, a strait of the East River, between Astoria in Queens and Wards Island in the Bronx.

Design and construction

The bridge was designed by the engineer Gustav Lindenthal. Its main span of 978 feet (298 m) was, at completion, the longest steel arch bridge in the world, a record it held until surpassed by the Bayonne Bridge in 1931. The arch is flanked by two masonry towers and is approached by long viaducts, including a reinforced-concrete arch viaduct across Wards and Randalls Islands; the total length of the crossing is about 5.2 km (17,000 ft).

Construction began in 1912 under the New York Connecting Railroad, a joint venture of the Pennsylvania Railroad and the New Haven Railroad. The bridge was dedicated on 9 March 1917 and opened to rail traffic the following month, completing a through route between New England and the Pennsylvania Railroad system via the New York Penn Station tunnels.

Legacy

The Hell Gate Bridge served as the model for the Sydney Harbour Bridge in Australia, which is similar in design but larger. The bridge's original paint colour, a deep red, has become well known in New York. It remains a vital, heavily used rail link and an icon of early 20th-century American civil engineering.
