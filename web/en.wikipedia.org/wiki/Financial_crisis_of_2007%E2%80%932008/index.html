Financial crisis of 2007–2008

The financial crisis of 2007–2008, also known as the global financial crisis (GFC), was a severe worldwide economic crisis triggered by the collapse of a housing bubble in the United States, predatory and subprime lending, and complex securitised products whose risks were mispriced. It led to bank failures, bailouts, the Great Recession and the European debt crisis.

Period2007–2009 (acute phase Sep–Oct 2008)
TriggersU.S. housing bubble, subprime mortgages, MBS/CDOs
Key eventsBear Stearns rescue, Lehman bankruptcy, AIG bailout, TARP

Background and causes

Low interest rates, rising house prices, lax underwriting and securitisation spread mortgage risk through mortgage-backed securities and collateralised debt obligations blessed by credit rating agencies. Investment banks operated with extreme leverage in a lightly regulated shadow banking system.

Course

Defaults rose in 2007; BNP Paribas froze funds in August 2007 and Northern Rock suffered a bank run. In 2008 Bear Stearns was sold to JPMorgan, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac entered conservatorship, Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy on 15 September 2008, AIG was bailed out, and Congress passed the $700 billion Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP). Money market funds "broke the buck" and global trade collapsed.

Aftermath and reforms

The Great Recession raised U.S. unemployment to 10%; millions lost homes to foreclosure. Responses included near-zero interest rates, quantitative easing, the Dodd–Frank Act (2010), Basel III capital rules, and in Europe a sovereign debt crisis centred on Greece.
