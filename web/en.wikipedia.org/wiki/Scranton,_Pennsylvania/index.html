Scranton, Pennsylvania

Scranton is a city in and the county seat of Lackawanna County, Pennsylvania, United States. With a population of about 76,000 at the 2020 census, it is the largest city in Northeastern Pennsylvania and the sixth-largest city in the state. It lies in the valley of the Lackawanna River, in the heart of the historic anthracite coal region.

History

The area was settled by Europeans in the late 18th century and known successively as Slocum Hollow and Scranton City; it took its name from the Scranton brothers, George W. and Selden T. Scranton, who in 1840 founded the iron works that became the Lackawanna Iron and Steel Company. Scranton grew explosively on anthracite coal mining and railroads — the Delaware, Lackawanna and Western Railroad had its headquarters there — and became one of America's leading industrial cities, attracting waves of Welsh, Irish, German, Italian, Polish and other immigrants to the mines and mills.

In 1878 Scranton became the county seat of the newly created Lackawanna County. The city was the site of the Scranton general strike of 1877, part of the Great Railroad Strike, and of the 1902 Anthracite Coal Strike, whose settlement was brokered by President Theodore Roosevelt. Scranton was an early centre of electric traction — its streetcar system (1886) was among the first in the United States to operate successfully, earning it the nickname "The Electric City".

Decline and revitalisation

Anthracite demand collapsed after the Second World War, and the Knox Mine disaster of 1959 — when the Susquehanna River broke into underground workings near Pittston, killing twelve miners — effectively ended deep mining in the valley. Steel and textiles also left; the city's population fell from a peak of about 143,000 in 1930 to under 72,000 around 2010, before modest recovery. Since the 1990s, downtown revitalisation has centred on heritage tourism (the Steamtown National Historic Site, the Electric City Trolley Museum), higher education (the University of Scranton, Geisinger Commonwealth School of Medicine) and healthcare employment.

Culture and notability

Scranton is the hometown of U.S. President Joe Biden, who was born there in 1942 and whose family home in the Green Ridge neighbourhood is a noted site. To television audiences the city is best known as the setting of the American version of the sitcom The Office (2005–2013), whose fictional Dunder Mifflin paper company brought sustained tourist interest, commemorated by banners and a mural downtown. Local landmarks include the Lackawanna County Courthouse Square, Nay Aug Park, the Houdini Museum, and the Montage Mountain ski area.

Geography and climate

Scranton occupies the Lackawanna River valley between the Moosic Mountains to the east and West Mountain to the west. It experiences a humid continental climate with warm summers and cold, snowy winters. Major highways include Interstate 81, which links the city to Syracuse and Harrisburg, and Interstate 380/84 eastward toward New York City.
