Kalinago Territory

The Kalinago Territory, formerly known as the Carib Territory or Carib Reserve, is a 3,700-acre (15 km²) district on the eastern (windward) coast of the Caribbean island country of Dominica. Established by the British colonial government in 1903, it is the collective homeland of the Kalinago people (Island Caribs), one of the last remaining communities of the Caribbean's Indigenous peoples, and one of the few formally recognised Indigenous territories in the region.

Governance

Land in the Territory is held communally, without individual title. Residents elect a Kalinago Chief and a Kalinago Council, who manage local affairs in parallel with the national government of Dominica. The chief also sits within national political processes as an advocate for the Kalinago.

Culture

About 3,000 Kalinago live in the Territory's eight hamlets, including Bataca and Sineku. The Kalinago Barana Autê ("Kalinago village"), a cultural centre opened in 2006, presents traditional architecture, canoe building (gommier dugouts), basket weaving from larouma reed, cassava processing and dances to visitors. The community works to revive the Kalinago language, now largely replaced by Dominican Creole and English, and maintains festivals such as Kalinago Week each September.

History

The Kalinago resisted European colonisation from Dominica's rugged interior for centuries after the island's other peoples were displaced. The reserve's boundaries were settled after the 1930 "Carib War" incident and subsequent commissions confirmed Kalinago land rights; the community was severely hit by Hurricane Maria in 2017, which destroyed most buildings in the Territory.
