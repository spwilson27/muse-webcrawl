Stokes' theorem


Field: Vector calculus, differential geometry
Relates: Surface integral of curl to line integral on boundary
Named for: George Gabriel Stokes

Stokes' theorem states that the integral of the curl of a vector field over a surface equals the line integral of the field around the boundary curve of that surface. In symbols, ∫∫S (∇ × F) · dS = ∫∂S F · dr.

Generalised form

In the language of differential forms, the theorem says the integral of the exterior derivative of a form ω over a manifold M equals the integral of ω over the boundary of M. This single statement unifies the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green's theorem, the classical Kelvin–Stokes theorem and the divergence theorem.

History

Versions of the result were communicated by William Thomson (Lord Kelvin) to George Gabriel Stokes in 1850; Stokes set it as an examination question at Cambridge in 1854, and it has borne his name ever since.
