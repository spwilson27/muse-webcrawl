FieldLinguistics (semantics)

Aspectual classification is the grouping of verbs and verb phrases according to their inherent temporal structure, known as lexical aspect or Aktionsart. The most influential scheme is that of the philosopher Zeno Vendler, who in his 1957 paper "Verbs and Times" distinguished four classes of English verbs based on whether they denote situations that are static or dynamic, punctual or durative, and bounded (telic) or unbounded (atelic).

Vendler's four classes

States are static and durative: know, love, own. Activities are dynamic, durative and unbounded: run, swim, push a cart. Accomplishments are dynamic, durative and bounded, unfolding towards an inherent endpoint: build a house, run a mile. Achievements are dynamic, punctual and bounded, occurring in an instant: win the race, notice, reach the summit.

Diagnostics and extensions

The classes are distinguished by tests such as compatibility with the progressive, with duration adverbials ("for an hour" versus "in an hour"), and with verbs like stop and finish. Later scholars, notably David Dowty, recast the classes in formal semantics; others have added categories such as semelfactives (cough, knock) and degree achievements (cool, widen). Aspectual classification is important both for linguistic theory and for natural-language processing tasks such as temporal reasoning.
