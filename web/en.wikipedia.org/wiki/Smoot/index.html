Smoot

The smoot is a nonstandard, humorous unit of length created in October 1958 as a fraternity prank at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. One smoot equals 5 feet 7 inches (1.702 m), the height of Oliver R. Smoot, the MIT student whose body was used to measure the Harvard Bridge.

Origin

In 1958, members of the Lambda Chi Alpha fraternity, needing to measure the length of the Harvard Bridge (which connects Boston and Cambridge across the Charles River) for a pledge activity, repeatedly laid the shortest pledge, Oliver Smoot, end to end along the bridge, marking each increment in paint. The bridge was found to be 364.4 smoots "plus or minus one ear." The markings were repainted by successive pledge classes and became a beloved Cambridge landmark; when the bridge was rebuilt in the 1980s, the smoot markings were preserved, and the sidewalk slabs were even scored at smoot intervals. Cambridge police reportedly used the markings to indicate locations at accident scenes.

Oliver Smoot

Oliver Reed Smoot (born 1940) went on to a career in standards and measurement: he served as chairman of the American National Standards Institute (ANSI) and as president of the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) — an irony widely noted given his role as a living unit of measure. He is also a cousin of Nobel laureate George Smoot.

In culture

The smoot is recognized by Google's calculator and Google Earth as an optional unit, and it is commemorated by a plaque on the bridge. MIT celebrates the smoot as a symbol of its hacking (prank) culture, and "plus or minus one ear" remains a joking reference to measurement uncertainty.
