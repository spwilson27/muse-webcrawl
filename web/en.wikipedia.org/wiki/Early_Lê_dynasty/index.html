Early Lê dynasty
980–1009
Capital: Hoa Lư
Government: Monarchy
First emperor: Lê Đại Hành
Last emperor: Lê Long Đĩnh

The Early Lê dynasty (Vietnamese: Nhà Tiền Lê) was a Vietnamese dynasty that ruled the kingdom of Đại Cồ Việt from 980 to 1009. It was founded by the general Lê Hoàn, who took the throne after the child emperor of the Đinh dynasty proved unable to lead the country against an invasion by Song dynasty China.

The temple of Lê Đại Hành at the ancient capital of Hoa Lư.

Founding

In 979 Emperor Đinh Tiên Hoàng and his eldest son were assassinated, leaving the six-year-old Đinh Toàn on the throne. When the Song court prepared to invade in 980, the dowager empress Dương Vân Nga and the court persuaded the commander-in-chief Lê Hoàn to take the throne himself. As emperor Lê Đại Hành, he reorganised the army and in 981 defeated the Song invasion on land and at the Battle of Bạch Đằng River, forcing the Song to recognise Đại Cồ Việt.

Reign of Lê Đại Hành

Lê Đại Hành (r. 980–1005) consolidated the state founded by the Đinh, campaigned successfully against Champa in 982—sacking its capital Indrapura—and maintained tributary relations with Song China while preserving independence. He promoted agriculture, held the first royal ploughing ceremony, improved roads and canals, and kept the capital at Hoa Lư.

Decline and fall

After Lê Đại Hành's death in 1005, his sons fought a succession war. Lê Long Việt held the throne for only three days before being murdered by his brother Lê Long Đĩnh, whose cruel and dissolute rule (1005–1009) discredited the dynasty. When Long Đĩnh died in 1009, the court elevated the palace guard commander Lý Công Uẩn, who founded the Lý dynasty and moved the capital to Thăng Long (modern Hanoi), ending the Early Lê after just three emperors and three decades.
