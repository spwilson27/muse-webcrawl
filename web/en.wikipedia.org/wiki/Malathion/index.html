Malathion

Malathion is an organophosphate insecticide that acts as an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor. It is widely used in agriculture, public health pest control, residential landscaping, and mosquito eradication programs. Its chemical formula is C10H19O6PS2.

Properties and mechanism

Malathion is a relatively low-toxicity insecticide for mammals because it is rapidly detoxified by carboxylesterase enzymes that insects lack in comparable quantity. In the body it is converted to malaoxon, a much more potent cholinesterase inhibitor. Impurities such as isomalathion, which can form during storage at high temperatures, significantly increase its toxicity and have been responsible for poisoning incidents among spray workers.

Uses and regulation

Malathion is applied to food crops, stored grains, and ornamental plants, and is used in aerial spraying campaigns against fruit flies and mosquitoes. A low-dose preparation (Ovide) is used as a pediculicide for treating head lice. Regulatory agencies have periodically reviewed its safety; in 2015 the International Agency for Research on Cancer classified malathion as "probably carcinogenic to humans" (Group 2A), a classification debated by other regulators, and its use is restricted in a number of jurisdictions.
