Pechengsky District is an administrative and municipal district (raion) of Murmansk Oblast in the far northwest of Russia, on the Kola Peninsula bordering Norway and Finland. Its administrative centre is the urban locality of Nikel.

History

The district corresponds to the historical Pechenga (Finnish: Petsamo) region. The Pechenga Monastery, founded in 1533 by St Tryphon, was an important centre of Russian Orthodox mission in the north. The territory belonged to Finland between 1920 and 1944, giving Finland a corridor to the Barents Sea, but was ceded to the Soviet Union after the Continuation War.

Economy

The district's economy is dominated by nickel mining and smelting around Nikel and Zapolyarny, based on rich copper-nickel deposits. The smelter at Nikel long caused cross-border air pollution affecting Norway before its closure in 2020. The port of Pechenga on the Barents Sea and the Borisoglebsky border crossing with Norway are also significant.
