Western Federation of Miners

The Western Federation of Miners (WFM) was a radical American labour union representing hard-rock miners in the western United States and Canada. Founded in Butte, Montana, in 1893 after the suppression of earlier miners' unions, it became one of the most militant unions in American history and a precursor of industrial unionism.

History

The WFM led bitter struggles including the Leadville strike of 1896–97 and the Colorado Labor Wars of 1903–04, in which Governor James Peabody deployed the state militia, suspended habeas corpus and deported union members. In 1905 the federation's leaders, including "Big Bill" Haywood, helped found the Industrial Workers of the World in Chicago. Haywood, Charles Moyer and George Pettibone were famously tried in 1907 for the assassination of former Idaho governor Frank Steunenberg and acquitted with Clarence Darrow defending.

Later years

Internal conflict with the IWW, wartime repression and competition from the AFL weakened the union. In 1916 it renamed itself the International Union of Mine, Mill, and Smelter Workers ("Mine Mill"). Expelled from the CIO in 1950 during the McCarthy era over alleged communist leadership, Mine Mill merged into the United Steelworkers in 1967.
