Freelancer

A freelancer (also freelance worker) is a self-employed person who is not committed to a particular employer long-term, and who typically works for multiple clients on a per-job or per-project basis. Common fields for freelancing include writing, journalism, graphic design, programming, translation, consulting, photography, and film production.

Etymology

The term derives from the medieval mercenary "free lance" — a knight whose lance (weapon) was not sworn to any particular lord and was available for hire. The word was popularized by Sir Walter Scott in his novel Ivanhoe (1820) and shifted to figurative, non-military work by the mid-19th century.

Working arrangements

Freelancers are typically classified as independent contractors rather than employees. They set their own rates (hourly, daily, per-piece, or per-project), issue invoices, and are responsible for their own taxes, insurance, and retirement saving. Work is often obtained through personal networks, agencies, or online marketplaces and platforms that match freelancers with clients.

Advantages and disadvantages

Freelancing offers flexibility in schedule, workplace, and choice of projects, and the potential for varied work. Its drawbacks include income instability, lack of employer-provided benefits (paid leave, health insurance, pensions), the burden of self-employment taxes and administration, and isolation. Legal protections for freelancers vary widely by jurisdiction; some cities and countries have enacted "freelance isn't free" laws requiring written contracts and timely payment.

Scale

Surveys in the United States have estimated that tens of millions of Americans perform freelance work each year, contributing over a trillion dollars in earnings to the economy. Similar growth in independent work has been documented across Europe and Asia, driven by internet platforms and the broader gig economy.
