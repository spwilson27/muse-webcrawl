Alsace wine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Alsace wine or Alsatian wine (French: Vin d'Alsace; German: Elsass-Wein) is produced in the Alsace region in France and is primarily white wine. Along with Austria and Germany, it produces some of the most noted dry Rieslings in the world as well as highly aromatic Gewürztraminer wines. Wines are produced under three Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée (AOC) designations: Alsace AOC for white, rosé and red wines; Alsace Grand Cru AOC for white wines from certain classified vineyards; and Crémant d'Alsace AOC for sparkling wines.

Geography and climate

The vineyards run in a narrow strip along the eastern foothills of the Vosges mountains in north-eastern France, sheltered by the mountains from Atlantic rain, making Alsace one of the driest and sunniest wine regions of France. The complex geology of the Vosges foothills — granite, limestone, schist, sandstone and volcanic soils — contributes to the diversity of styles. The scenic Alsace wine route (Route des vins d'Alsace), established in 1953, links the main wine villages over about 170 kilometres.

Grape varieties and labelling

Unusually for France, Alsace wines are generally labelled by grape variety. The "noble grapes" entitled to Grand Cru production are Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot gris and Muscat. Other varieties include Sylvaner, Pinot blanc, Auxerrois and Chasselas; Pinot noir is the region's only red variety. Most Alsace wines are fermented dry, though late-harvest dessert wines are made as Vendange tardive and, from botrytis-affected grapes, Sélection de Grains Nobles. The tall, slender green bottle known as the flûte d'Alsace is legally prescribed for most wines of the region.

History

Viticulture in Alsace dates to Roman times. The region's history of alternating French and German sovereignty — most recently annexation by Germany in 1871 and 1940, and return to France in 1918 and 1945 — is reflected in its Germanic grape varieties and place names. Alsace received its AOC status in 1962, and the Grand Cru appellation followed in 1975, with 51 classified lieux-dits.
