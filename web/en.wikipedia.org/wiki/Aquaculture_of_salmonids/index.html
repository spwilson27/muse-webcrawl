Aquaculture of salmonids

The aquaculture of salmonids is the farming and harvesting of salmonid fishes under controlled conditions for commercial purposes. Salmonids include salmon and trout, principally Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar), and Pacific species such as Chinook and coho salmon, along with rainbow trout. Farmed salmon now account for well over half — roughly 70 percent — of the salmon eaten worldwide, with annual production of Atlantic salmon alone exceeding 2.7 million tonnes. The largest producers are Norway and Chile, followed by the United Kingdom (Scotland), Canada, and the Faroe Islands.

Production cycle

Production begins in freshwater hatcheries, where broodstock are spawned and eggs incubated. Juveniles (fry, then parr) are raised in tanks or raceways for 10–16 months until they undergo smoltification, the physiological change preparing them for seawater. Smolts of roughly 60–100 grams are transferred to floating net pens (sea cages) in sheltered coastal waters, where they are fed formulated pellets for 14–24 months until reaching a harvest weight of 3–6 kilograms. Increasingly, producers keep fish longer in land-based recirculating aquaculture systems (RAS) — or complete the whole cycle on land — to reduce sea lice exposure and environmental impacts.

Feeds

Salmon feeds were historically rich in fishmeal and fish oil from wild forage fish; improved formulations using plant proteins and oils, insect meal, and algal oils have reduced the fish-in/fish-out ratio substantially, though feed remains the industry's largest cost and a major share of its carbon footprint. Pigments such as astaxanthin are added to give farmed flesh the pink colour wild salmon derive from krill and shrimp.

Environmental and health issues

The industry is criticised for sea lice infestations that can spread to wild salmon, escapes of farmed fish that interbreed with wild populations, local pollution from uneaten feed and faeces, disease outbreaks (such as infectious salmon anaemia, which devastated Chilean production in 2007–2010), and antibiotic use in some jurisdictions. Chile's industry in particular has been scrutinised for antibiotic volumes and for harmful algal bloom losses. Mitigations include lice treatments and cleaner fish (lumpfish, wrasse), closed-containment and offshore pens, fallowing, area-based management, and vaccination, which has nearly eliminated antibiotic use in Norway. Certification schemes such as the Aquaculture Stewardship Council (ASC) set standards for certified farms.

Economics

Salmon farming supports tens of thousands of jobs in coastal communities and is a flagship export for Norway (where salmon is among the country's largest exports after oil and gas) and Chile. The sector is consolidated among large companies — Mowi, SalMar, and Lerøy (Norway), and AquaChile and others — and has attracted debate over resource-rent taxation, such as Norway's "salmon tax" on aquaculture profits introduced in 2023.
