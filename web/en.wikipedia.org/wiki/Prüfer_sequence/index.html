Prüfer sequence

In combinatorial mathematics, a Prüfer sequence (also called a Prüfer code or Prüfer numbers) of a labelled tree is a unique sequence of length n − 2 associated with a tree having vertices labelled 1 through n. The sequence was first used by Heinz Prüfer in 1918 to give a new proof of Cayley's formula.

Construction

Given a tree, repeatedly delete the leaf (degree-1 vertex) with the smallest label and record the label of its only neighbour, until only two vertices remain. The recorded labels form the Prüfer sequence. The process is reversible: from any sequence of length n − 2 over the labels, one can reconstruct the unique corresponding tree, by repeatedly attaching the smallest label not present in the remaining sequence to the sequence's first element.

Consequences

Because the correspondence is a bijection between labelled trees on n vertices and sequences of length n − 2 with entries from an n-element set, there are nn−2 labelled trees — Cayley's formula. In a tree's Prüfer sequence, each vertex appears exactly deg(v) − 1 times, which also makes the sequences useful for generating random labelled trees uniformly and for counting trees with specified degree sequences.
