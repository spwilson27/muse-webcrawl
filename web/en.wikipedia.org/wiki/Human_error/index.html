Human error

Human error is an action or decision that was not intended, or that led to an outcome which was not intended by the actor. It is a central concept in safety science, human factors and reliability engineering, where it is studied as a cause — and often a symptom — of accidents in aviation, medicine, industry and transport.

Classification

Psychologist James Reason distinguished between slips and lapses (errors in executing an intention) and mistakes (errors in the intention itself, arising from flawed knowledge or rules). Reason's "Swiss cheese model" holds that accidents occur when latent organisational weaknesses align with active failures by operators.

Management

Modern safety management emphasises that blaming individuals is less effective than designing systems — checklists, automation, forcing functions and clear procedures — that make errors less likely or catch them before harm results. Human reliability analysis attempts to quantify the probability of error for risk assessments.
