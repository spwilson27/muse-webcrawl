Haworth (pronounced HOW-ərth) is a village in the City of Bradford metropolitan borough of West Yorkshire, England, in the Pennines, 3 miles (5 km) southwest of Keighley and 10 miles (16 km) west of Bradford. It is famous as the home of the Brontë sisters — Charlotte, Emily, and Anne — who wrote most of their celebrated novels while living at Haworth Parsonage.

History

The village developed around the woollen textile trade, with handloom weaving in cottages giving way to mills in the 19th century; its steep cobbled Main Street, climbing the hillside, retains its Victorian character. Conditions in the industrial village were harsh: an 1850 report by Babbage for the General Board of Health found an average age of death of about 26 and infant mortality comparable to the worst London districts, owing largely to contaminated water and poor sanitation.

The Brontës

The Reverend Patrick Brontë became perpetual curate of Haworth in 1820 and moved his family into the parsonage beside St Michael and All Angels' Church. There his daughters wrote Jane Eyre (Charlotte), Wuthering Heights (Emily), and The Tenant of Wildfell Hall (Anne). All three — and their brother Branwell — died young and are associated with the church, where most of the family is buried in a vault beneath the floor. The parsonage is now the Brontë Parsonage Museum, run by the Brontë Society (founded 1893), and the surrounding moorland, including Penistone Hill and the ruin at Top Withens — a supposed inspiration for Wuthering Heights — is known as "Brontë Country".

Transport and attractions

Haworth station is on the Keighley and Worth Valley Railway, a preserved heritage steam railway frequently used as a film location (notably in The Railway Children). The village draws many thousands of literary tourists each year, with shops, tea rooms, and the Black Bull inn — where Branwell Brontë was a regular — along Main Street.
