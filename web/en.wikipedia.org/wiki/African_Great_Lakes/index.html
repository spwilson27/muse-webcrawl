African Great Lakes

The African Great Lakes (Swahili: Maziwa Makuu) are a series of lakes constituting the part of the Rift Valley lakes in and around the East African Rift. They include some of the largest and deepest lakes in the world. The region around the lakes, which includes parts of Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Zambia, Malawi, and Mozambique, is sometimes called the African Great Lakes region and is one of the most densely populated parts of Africa.

The lakes

Lake Victoria, shared by Tanzania, Uganda, and Kenya, is Africa's largest lake by surface area (about 68,800 km²) and the world's largest tropical lake; it is a source of the Nile River. Lake Tanganyika, shared by Tanzania, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Burundi, and Zambia, is the world's longest freshwater lake (about 673 km) and second deepest, reaching about 1,470 m. Lake Malawi (also called Lake Nyasa) lies between Malawi, Mozambique, and Tanzania. Other major lakes include Lake Turkana, the world's largest permanent desert lake; Lake Albert; Lake Edward; and Lake Kivu, one of the African Great Lakes on the border of Rwanda and the DR Congo.

Geography and geology

The lakes lie in the East African Rift, a divergent tectonic plate boundary where the African Plate is splitting into the Somali and Nubian plates. Rift valleys formed basins that filled with water over millions of years. The region's lakes drain into three great river systems: Victoria and the smaller western rift lakes ultimately feed the Nile; Tanganyika drains westward into the Congo River system; and Malawi drains south into the Zambezi.

Ecology

The lakes are famous for extraordinary biodiversity, above all their cichlid fishes, which have undergone spectacular adaptive radiation—hundreds of species evolved within individual lakes. Lake Victoria alone historically held more than 500 endemic cichlid species. The lakes support major fisheries that provide food and livelihoods for millions. The introduction of the Nile perch into Lake Victoria in the 1950s, along with pollution, overfishing, and invasive species such as water hyacinth, caused severe ecological disruption and the extinction or decline of many native cichlids.

History

The Great Lakes region has been home to complex societies for centuries, including the kingdoms of Buganda, Bunyoro, Rwanda, and Burundi. In the nineteenth century, European explorers such as John Hanning Speke, Richard Burton, David Livingstone, and Henry Morton Stanley reached the lakes while searching for the source of the Nile. The colonial era divided the region among British, German, and Belgian rule, and the arbitrary borders drawn then still shape regional politics.

Economy and population

More than 100 million people live in the Great Lakes region. Fishing, agriculture (coffee, tea, cotton), and cross-border trade are major economic activities. Port cities such as Kisumu (Kenya), Mwanza (Tanzania), and Entebbe (Uganda) serve lake transport networks. The region has also been affected by conflict, including the Rwandan genocide of 1994 and wars in the eastern Congo.
