Creation and annihilation operators

Creation operators and annihilation operators are mathematical operators with widespread applications in quantum mechanics, notably in the study of quantum harmonic oscillators and many-particle systems. An annihilation operator (usually denoted a) lowers the number of particles in a given state by one, and a creation operator (a†) increases the particle number by one.

Harmonic oscillator

Paul Dirac introduced the method in his work on quantum theory in the late 1920s: for the harmonic oscillator, combinations of position and momentum define ladder operators that step between energy eigenstates, so that the Hamiltonian takes the compact form Ĥ = ħω(a†a + ½), where a†a is the number operator whose eigenvalues count the quanta of excitation. The operators satisfy the commutation relation [a, a†] = 1.

Second quantisation and fields

In quantum field theory, generalisations of these operators create and destroy particles in each mode of a field, and the entire state of a field can be built by applying creation operators to the vacuum state (Fock space). For bosons, creation and annihilation operators obey commutation relations, allowing unlimited occupation of a state; for fermions they obey anticommutation relations, enforcing the Pauli exclusion principle that no state can hold more than one particle. The formalism underpins quantum optics, condensed matter physics and particle physics, where every interaction can be described as particles being created and annihilated.
