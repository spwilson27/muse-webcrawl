John Nelder

Born8 October 1924, Brushford, Somerset, England
Died7 August 2010 (aged 85), Luton, Bedfordshire, England
Known forGeneralized linear models; Nelder–Mead method
FieldsStatistics

John Ashworth Nelder FRS (8 October 1924 – 7 August 2010) was a British statistician known for his contributions to experimental design, analysis of variance, computational statistics, and statistical theory. Together with Robert Wedderburn he developed the framework of generalized linear models (GLMs), presented in their influential 1972 paper, which unified a wide range of statistical models including logistic regression, Poisson regression and classical linear regression under a single theory.

Career

Nelder was educated at Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge, where he read mathematics. He spent much of his career at Rothamsted Experimental Station, where he headed the statistics department. There he worked extensively on the design and analysis of agricultural experiments and played a central role in the development of the statistical computing package Genstat.

With J. A. Mead he devised the Nelder–Mead method (also called the downhill simplex method), a widely used numerical algorithm for optimisation in multidimensional spaces, published in 1965. He also contributed, with Peter McCullagh, the standard textbook Generalized Linear Models (1983; second edition 1989).

Honours

Nelder was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1981 and was awarded the Guy Medal in Gold of the Royal Statistical Society in 2005. He served as president of the Royal Statistical Society in 1985–86.
