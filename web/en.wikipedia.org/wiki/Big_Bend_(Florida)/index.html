The Big Bend is a geographic and demographic region of the U.S. state of Florida, where the Florida Panhandle transitions into the Florida Peninsula along the Gulf of Mexico. It is generally considered to include the coastal counties from Wakulla County west of Tallahassee down through Levy County, encompassing a sparsely populated, heavily forested coastline.

Geography

Unlike most of Florida's Gulf shore, the Big Bend coast has almost no sandy barrier-island beaches; instead it is a broad, shallow, low-energy shoreline of salt marsh, tidal creeks, and oyster bars, underlain by limestone karst. Rivers such as the Suwannee, Steinhatchee, Aucilla, and St. Marks cross the region, and numerous first-magnitude springs feed freshwater into the marshes. Large areas are protected, including the Big Bend Seagrasses Aquatic Preserve and several national wildlife refuges.

Communities and economy

Small towns such as Cedar Key, Steinhatchee, Perry, and St. Marks anchor the region; forestry, commercial and recreational fishing (notably bay scallops), and nature-based tourism dominate the economy. The area's low elevation and curving coastline make it vulnerable to hurricane storm surge, as seen with Hurricane Idalia (2023) and Hurricane Helene (2024), which made landfall in the Big Bend.
