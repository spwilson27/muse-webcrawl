The Parliament of New South Wales, formally the Legislature of New South Wales, is the bicameral legislative body of the Australian state of New South Wales (NSW). It consists of the Governor of New South Wales (representing the Crown) and two houses: the Legislative Council (the upper house) and the Legislative Assembly (the lower house). It is the oldest parliament in Australia, with origins in the advisory Legislative Council appointed in 1824, and it sits at Parliament House on Macquarie Street, Sydney — the oldest public building in the city.

History

A nominated Legislative Council began advising the Governor in 1824 and was expanded and partly elected under the Australian Constitutions Act 1842. Responsible government was established in 1856 with a fully elected Legislative Assembly and a nominated Council; the Council became directly elected only in 1978, by proportional representation across the whole state. Milestones include universal male suffrage (1858), votes for women (1902), and the first woman member, Millicent Preston-Stanley (1925). The Parliament building itself incorporates the old Sydney Hospital's "Rum Hospital" wing.

Composition and powers

The Legislative Assembly has 93 members elected from single-member districts by preferential (instant-runoff) voting for fixed four-year terms. The Legislative Council has 42 members elected by proportional representation for eight-year terms, with half facing election every four years. NSW has fixed four-year parliamentary terms, with elections held on the fourth Saturday in March. As a state parliament under the Australian federal system, it has plenary power over state matters — health, education, transport, police, and land — subject to the Australian Constitution and the Commonwealth's enumerated powers. Money bills originate in the Assembly; the Council acts as a house of review and cannot amend money bills.
