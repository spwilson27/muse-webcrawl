William Miller (15 February 1782 – 20 December 1849) was an American Baptist preacher whose proclamation that Jesus Christ would return to Earth by 1844 launched the Millerite movement, one of the most influential religious movements of nineteenth-century America.

Early life

Miller was a Massachusetts-born farmer who settled in Low Hampton, New York. He served as a captain in the War of 1812, fighting at the Battle of Plattsburgh. Formerly a deist, he returned to Baptist faith and from about 1816 devoted himself to intensive study of the Bible, using a concordance and a day-year interpretation of prophecy.

The Millerite movement

From Daniel 8:14 ("unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed") Miller calculated that Christ would return around 1843. He began preaching publicly in 1831, and promoters such as Joshua Vaughan Himes spread his message — "Millerism" — through camp meetings and mass-circulation papers like Signs of the Times. When 1843 passed, followers fixed on 22 October 1844, following Samuel Snow's "seventh-month" calculation.

The Great Disappointment and aftermath

The failure of the prophecy on 22 October 1844 became known as the Great Disappointment, devastating tens of thousands of believers. Miller admitted his error and never set another date, dying in 1849. From the movement's remnants arose several Adventist churches, most prominently the Seventh-day Adventist Church (organised 1863), which reinterpreted 1844 as the start of Christ's heavenly "investigative judgment".
