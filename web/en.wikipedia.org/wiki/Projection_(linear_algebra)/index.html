Projection (linear algebra)

In linear algebra and functional analysis, a projection is a linear transformation P from a vector space to itself such that P² = P; applying the map twice gives the same result as applying it once, so P is idempotent. The image of P is the subspace onto which vectors are projected, and every vector decomposes into a component in that image and a component in the kernel of P.

Orthogonal projection

In an inner product space, an orthogonal projection sends each vector to its nearest point in a subspace; the difference between the vector and its projection is orthogonal to the subspace. Orthogonal projections are self-adjoint, have operator norm at most 1, and underlie the least-squares method: the projection of a data vector onto the column space of a matrix gives the best approximation used in linear regression.

Examples and uses

Everyday examples include projecting a three-dimensional point onto a coordinate plane by deleting one coordinate, and the shadow cast by an object. Projections appear throughout mathematics and physics — in quantum mechanics, observables and state preparation are described by projection operators, and in statistics the "hat matrix" of regression is a projection matrix.
