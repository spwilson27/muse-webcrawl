Five-number summary

The five-number summary is a set of descriptive statistics that provides information about a dataset. It is a summary statistic containing five values: the sample minimum (smallest observation), the lower quartile or first quartile (Q1), the median (the middle value), the upper quartile or third quartile (Q3), and the sample maximum (largest observation). It may also be thought of as a helpful summary of the distribution of data points, because it provides insight into the range, midspread, and skewness of the dataset.

Components

The five numbers split the ordered data into four sections each containing roughly a quarter of the observations. The distance between Q1 and Q3 is the interquartile range (IQR), a robust measure of spread. The median indicates the central tendency of the data, and comparing the distances from the median to the quartiles and extremes gives an indication of symmetry or skew.

Box plots

The five-number summary is the basis of the box plot, popularised by the statistician John Tukey in his 1977 book Exploratory Data Analysis. In a box plot, a box spans the quartiles, a line marks the median, and "whiskers" extend to the extremes (or, in modified box plots, to the most extreme values within 1.5 IQR of the box, with outliers drawn individually). The summary is especially useful for comparing multiple datasets side by side, and because it relies on order statistics it is resistant to the influence of outliers, though different software packages use slightly different conventions for computing quartiles.
