Conformal symmetry

Conformal symmetry is the invariance of a physical system under conformal transformations — transformations that preserve angles but not necessarily lengths, including translations, rotations, dilations (scalings) and special conformal transformations. A theory with this symmetry is a conformal field theory.

Conformal group

In d-dimensional spacetime the conformal group extends the Poincaré group by dilations and special conformal transformations. In two dimensions the local conformal algebra becomes infinite-dimensional (the Virasoro algebra), making 2D conformal field theories especially tractable and important in string theory and statistical mechanics.

Occurrences

Conformal symmetry appears at critical points of phase transitions (where systems become scale invariant), in massless gauge theories, in the near-horizon and holographic contexts of the AdS/CFT correspondence, and classically in Maxwell's electrodynamics in four dimensions. Breaking of scale invariance in quantum theory produces the conformal (trace) anomaly.
