Gemeindebau

Gemeindebau (plural: Gemeindebauten) is a German word for a residential building erected and owned by a municipality, used especially for the famous municipal housing estates of Vienna, Austria. Vienna's programme of publicly owned housing — begun in the "Red Vienna" era after the First World War — made the city one of the world's largest landlords; today roughly 220,000 municipal flats in some 1,800 Gemeindebauten house around half a million people, about a quarter of the city's population.

Red Vienna programme

After 1918, Vienna's Social Democratic administration under mayor Jakob Reumann and finance councillor Hugo Breitner tackled a severe housing crisis by taxing luxury goods and property to finance a vast building programme. Between 1923 and 1934 the city built some 65,000 flats, typically arranged as perimeter-block "superblocks" around generous green courtyards. The estates integrated laundries, baths, libraries, kindergartens, clinics and shops, embodying a socialist vision of collective welfare. Famous examples include the Karl-Marx-Hof (1927–1930, architect Karl Ehn), over a kilometre long with some 1,300 flats; the Reumannhof; the Sandleitenhof; and the Karl-Seitz-Hof.

Later development

Building resumed after the Second World War and continued through the postwar decades in modernist styles. Unlike social housing in many countries, Viennese Gemeindebauten are open to a broad range of incomes, have no fixed income limits once tenancy begins, and rents are kept low — features often credited with the city's social mix and comparatively affordable housing. Together with limited-profit housing associations, municipal housing shapes the majority of Vienna's rental stock and is studied worldwide as a model of social housing.
