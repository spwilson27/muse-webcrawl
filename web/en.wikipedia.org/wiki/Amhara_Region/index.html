The Amhara Region (Amharic: አማራ ክልል, Āmara Kilil) is a regional state in northwestern Ethiopia and the homeland of the Amhara people. Its capital and largest city is Bahir Dar, on the southern shore of Lake Tana. It is the second-most populous region of Ethiopia and covers much of the country's historic northern highlands.

Geography

The region spans the northwestern Ethiopian Highlands, including the Simien Mountains, where Ras Dashen (4,550 m), Ethiopia's highest peak, stands. Lake Tana, the country's largest lake and the source of the Blue Nile, lies in the region; the Blue Nile Falls (Tis Issat) are nearby. The region borders Tigray to the north, Afar to the east, Oromia to the south, Benishangul-Gumuz to the west and Sudan to the northwest.

History

The Amhara highlands were the heartland of the Ethiopian Empire for centuries: the medieval capitals, the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), the castles of Gondar and the island monasteries of Lake Tana are all in the modern region. The present regional state was established in 1995 under Ethiopia's ethnic-federal constitution. Since 2023 the region has been affected by armed conflict between federal forces and the Fano militia, following the earlier spillover of the Tigray War into Amhara territory.

Demographics and economy

The population is overwhelmingly Amhara and Amharic-speaking, with Awi, Oromo and other minorities. Most inhabitants are Ethiopian Orthodox Christians. The economy is predominantly subsistence agriculture — teff, wheat, barley and livestock — with growing urban centres at Bahir Dar, Gondar, Dessie and Debre Markos. Tourism centres on Lalibela, Gondar, the Simien Mountains National Park and Lake Tana's monasteries.
