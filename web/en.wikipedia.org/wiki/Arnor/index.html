Arnor

Map of Arnor in north-western Middle-earth

Arnor is a fictional kingdom in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. It was the northern kingdom of the Dúnedain in exile, founded at the end of the Second Age by Elendil, counterpart to Gondor in the south, which was ruled by his sons. Arnor's history — its division into three rival realms, its long war with the Witch-kingdom of Angmar, and its eventual restoration — is recounted in the appendices to The Lord of the Rings and in Unfinished Tales.

History

Arnor was founded in S.A. 3320 by Elendil the Tall, who landed in the north after the Downfall of Númenor. Its capital was Annúminas beside Lake Evendim. The realm covered much of Eriador, between the Misty Mountains and the Blue Mountains, including the lands later known as the Shire. Elendil and his son Isildur were killed in the War of the Last Alliance, and the northern line of kings slowly dwindled.

In T.A. 861, after the death of King Eärendur, disputes among his sons split Arnor into three kingdoms: Arthedain, Cardolan and Rhudaur. The fragmentation fatally weakened the north. From about T.A. 1300 the Witch-king of Angmar waged war on the successor states; Rhudaur fell under his sway, Cardolan was devastated by war and plague, and Arthedain finally fell in T.A. 1974, its last king, Arvedui, perishing in a shipwreck in the Ice Bay of Forochel.

The surviving Dúnedain of the north became the Rangers, a wandering folk led by Chieftains descended from the royal line, who guarded Eriador in secret. After the War of the Ring, Aragorn II Elessar refounded Arnor as part of the Reunited Kingdom, rebuilding Annúminas and ruling the north from there in part of each year.

Geography

Arnor's heartland lay around Lake Evendim, the Hills of Evendim and the North Downs. Its chief cities were Annúminas and later Fornost Erain on the North Downs. Great roads — the East Road and the Greenway (North-South Road) — crossed the realm, guarded by fortresses such as Weathertop (Amon Sûl), which held one of the principal palantíri.
