Integrated Data Store

Integrated Data Store (IDS) was one of the first database management systems, developed in the early 1960s by Charles Bachman at General Electric. It implemented the network database model, in which records are organised as sets linking owner and member record types, and strongly influenced the CODASYL DataBase Task Group specifications of 1969–1971.

Design

IDS stored records with direct pointers between related records, allowing applications to navigate from one record to associated records with high performance for the hardware of the time. Bachman described the programming model in his 1973 Turing Award lecture, "The Programmer as Navigator". A later version, IDS/II, ran on Honeywell systems after GE's computer business was sold.

Legacy

CODASYL-standardised systems modelled on IDS, such as IDMS, were widely used on mainframes in the 1970s. The navigational approach was later largely displaced by relational databases, though IDS is regarded as a foundational system in database history.
