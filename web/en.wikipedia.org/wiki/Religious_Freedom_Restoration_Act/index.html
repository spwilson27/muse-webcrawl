Religious Freedom Restoration Act

The Religious Freedom Restoration Act of 1993 (RFRA) is a United States federal law enacted to restore the strict-scrutiny test for laws that substantially burden a person's exercise of religion. It was passed in response to the Supreme Court's decision in Employment Division v. Smith (1990), which had held that neutral, generally applicable laws need only pass rational-basis review even if they burden religious practice.

Provisions and litigation

RFRA provides that the government may substantially burden religious exercise only if the burden furthers a compelling governmental interest and is the least restrictive means of doing so. In City of Boerne v. Flores (1997), the Supreme Court held RFRA unconstitutional as applied to state and local governments, so it now binds only the federal government; many states have enacted their own "state RFRAs". The federal Act figured prominently in Burwell v. Hobby Lobby (2014), in which the Court held that closely held for-profit corporations could claim exemption from a contraceptive-coverage mandate.
