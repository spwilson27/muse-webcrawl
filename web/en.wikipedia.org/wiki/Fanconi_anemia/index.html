Fanconi anemia
SpecialtyHaematology, medical genetics
InheritanceMostly autosomal recessive
Named afterGuido Fanconi

Fanconi anemia (FA) is a rare genetic disease characterised by progressive bone marrow failure, congenital abnormalities, and a greatly increased risk of cancer, particularly acute myeloid leukaemia and squamous cell carcinomas. It is named after the Swiss paediatrician Guido Fanconi, who described it in 1927.

Cause

FA results from mutations in any of more than 20 genes (FANCA–FANCW and associated genes) whose proteins cooperate in the Fanconi anemia pathway, which repairs DNA interstrand crosslinks. FANCA mutations are the most common. The disease is usually autosomal recessive, though rare X-linked and dominant forms exist. Cells from FA patients show characteristic chromosome breakage when exposed to crosslinking agents such as diepoxybutane or mitomycin C, which is the standard diagnostic test.

Signs and treatment

Findings can include short stature, thumb and radial abnormalities, skin pigmentation changes (café-au-lait spots), microcephaly, and kidney malformations, though some patients have no visible anomalies. Bone marrow failure typically appears in childhood. The only cure for the haematological problems is haematopoietic stem cell transplantation; androgens and growth factors can provide temporary benefit. Lifelong cancer surveillance is required.
