Kong: Skull Island is a 2017 American monster film directed by Jordan Vogt-Roberts and written by Dan Gilroy, Max Borenstein and Derek Connolly. A reboot of the King Kong franchise, it is the second film in Legendary Pictures' MonsterVerse. The film stars Tom Hiddleston, Samuel L. Jackson, John Goodman, Brie Larson, Jing Tian, Toby Kebbell, John Ortiz, Corey Hawkins, Jason Mitchell, Shea Whigham, Thomas Mann, Terry Notary and John C. Reilly.

Plot

In 1973, at the close of the Vietnam War, the U.S. government organisation Monarch secures funding for an expedition to the uncharted Skull Island in the South Pacific, escorted by a helicopter squadron led by Lieutenant Colonel Preston Packard. Seismic charges dropped to map the island provoke Kong, a 100-foot-tall ape, who destroys the helicopters. The scattered survivors — including tracker James Conrad and photojournalist Mason Weaver — discover the island's indigenous Iwi people and Hank Marlow, an American pilot stranded since 1944, and learn that Kong protects the island from subterranean predators known as Skullcrawlers. After a final confrontation in which Kong kills the largest Skullcrawler, the survivors escape, while Packard is killed by Kong.

Production

Principal photography ran from October 2015 to March 2016 in Hawaii, Australia's Gold Coast and Vietnam, including locations in Quang Binh province that received a tourism boost from the production. Kong was realised through motion capture and keyframe animation, standing significantly taller than in earlier films so that he could later plausibly fight Godzilla.

Reception

The film grossed $568.6 million worldwide against a budget of $185 million and received generally positive reviews praising its visuals, action and 1970s period style; it was nominated for Best Visual Effects at the 90th Academy Awards. A post-credits scene sets up the sequel Godzilla: King of the Monsters (2019), and Kong later returned in Godzilla vs. Kong (2021).
