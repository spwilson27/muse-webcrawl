Minerva Reefs

Location: South Pacific Ocean, south of Tonga and Fiji
Features: North Minerva Reef and South Minerva Reef
Status: Submerged atolls; sovereignty claimed by Tonga, disputed by Fiji

The Minerva Reefs (Tongan: Teleki Tokelau and Teleki Tonga) are a group of two submerged atolls in the South Pacific Ocean, located roughly 500 kilometres southwest of Tongatapu, Tonga, and a similar distance southeast of Fiji. North Minerva and South Minerva are ring-shaped reefs with lagoons that provide a well-known anchorage for yachts travelling between Tonga, Fiji, and New Zealand, though the reefs offer no dry land at high tide except for small sand cays and the remains of a beacon tower on South Minerva.

Republic of Minerva

The reefs became briefly famous in 1972, when a group organized by American libertarian Michael Oliver attempted to create a new country, the Republic of Minerva, by proclaiming independence and beginning to construct an artificial island on the reef. Tonga responded by asserting its claim to the reefs, sending an expedition that removed the Minervan flag and formally annexing the territory; the claim was recognized by the South Pacific Forum. The micronation attempt collapsed.

Dispute between Tonga and Fiji

Fiji also claims the reefs, and the overlapping claims — important for the associated maritime zones and seabed resources — have periodically caused friction, including a 2010–2011 standoff in which navigational beacons erected by one side were removed by the other. The dispute remains a live issue in Tonga–Fiji relations, and the reefs are also valued as a marine ecosystem and a hazard noted on charts for the wrecks they have claimed.
