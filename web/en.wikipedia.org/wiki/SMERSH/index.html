SMERSH (Russian: СМЕРШ) was the umbrella name for several independent counter-intelligence organisations in the Soviet Union during the Second World War. The name is an acronym of the Russian phrase Smyert shpionam (Смерть шпионам, "Death to Spies"), a slogan attributed to Joseph Stalin.

History

SMERSH was created on 14 April 1943, when the counter-intelligence directorates of the NKVD were transferred to the defence and naval commissariats. The main SMERSH directorate, under Viktor Abakumov, answered directly to Stalin. Its tasks included detecting enemy agents and "anti-Soviet elements" within the Red Army, screening Soviet soldiers and civilians returning from German captivity or occupied territory, and protecting the rear of the fronts.

SMERSH operatives worked at every level of the armed forces down to regiments and were credited by Soviet sources with neutralising thousands of German agents, though they were also feared for arrests and summary punishments of Soviet servicemen accused of cowardice, desertion or collaboration. In 1945 SMERSH units were involved in the search for Adolf Hitler's remains in Berlin and in the capture of senior Nazi personnel and documents.

Dissolution and legacy

In May 1946 SMERSH was abolished and its functions returned to the Ministry of State Security (MGB), with Abakumov becoming minister. The organisation became famous in the West through Ian Fleming's James Bond novels, in which SMERSH — anachronistically portrayed as still active — served as the villains of several early books, before Fleming replaced it with the fictional SPECTRE.
