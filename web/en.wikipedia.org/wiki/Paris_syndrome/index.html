Paris syndrome

Paris syndrome
Japanese: パリ症候群 (Pari shōkōgun)
Specialty: Psychiatry
Symptoms: Hallucinations, delusions, anxiety, derealisation, tachycardia
Causes: Severe culture shock; disillusionment with Paris
Population affected: Predominantly Japanese tourists

Paris syndrome (Japanese: パリ症候群, Pari shōkōgun) is a sense of extreme disappointment or shock experienced by some individuals when visiting Paris, who feel that the city does not live up to the romanticised image they held of it. The condition is regarded as a severe form of culture shock, and is particularly associated with Japanese tourists. The term was coined by the psychiatrist Hiroaki Ota, a Japanese doctor working in Paris, who described the phenomenon in the 1980s.

Symptoms

Reported symptoms include acute delusional states, hallucinations, feelings of persecution, derealisation, depersonalisation, anxiety, and psychosomatic manifestations such as dizziness, sweating, and tachycardia. In severe cases sufferers have required hospitalisation, and the Japanese embassy in Paris has operated a 24-hour helpline, repatriating a small number of tourists each year.

Causes

Explanations emphasise the gap between the idealised Paris of Japanese popular media — elegant, wealthy, uniformly beautiful — and the realities of a large modern city: language barriers, unfamiliar manners, less deferential service than Japanese visitors expect, and fatigue from travel. Individuals with a history of psychiatric illness appear more vulnerable.

Related phenomena

Paris syndrome is often compared with Jerusalem syndrome, in which visitors to Jerusalem experience religious-themed delusions, and with Stendhal syndrome, a psychosomatic response to great works of art, first described in Florence.
