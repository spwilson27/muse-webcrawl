Kakuma
Country: Kenya
County: Turkana
Known for: Kakuma refugee camp (est. 1992)

Kakuma is a town in Turkana County, northwestern Kenya. It is best known as the site of the Kakuma refugee camp, established in 1992 to host refugees fleeing the Second Sudanese Civil War — including the "Lost Boys of Sudan" — and since grown into one of the world's largest and longest-standing refugee settlements.

Refugee camp

The camp and the adjacent Kalobeyei settlement host well over 200,000 refugees and asylum seekers from South Sudan, Somalia, Ethiopia, Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and other countries. It is administered with the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) and Kenyan authorities, and has developed a substantial informal economy, schools and services despite chronic shortages of water, funding and durable solutions. The name Kakuma is said to derive from a Swahili phrase meaning "nowhere", reflecting the area's remote, arid setting.

Geography

Kakuma lies in an arid lowland west of Lake Turkana, near the Lodwar–Lokichoggio road, with high temperatures and scarce water supplied from seasonal rivers and boreholes.
