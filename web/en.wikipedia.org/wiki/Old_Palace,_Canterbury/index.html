Old Palace, Canterbury

The Old Palace in Canterbury, Kent, is the residence of the Archbishop of Canterbury when in the city. Located within the precincts of Canterbury Cathedral, adjacent to the cathedral itself, it stands on the site of the medieval archiepiscopal palace.

History

Archbishops lived on the site from the early medieval period; the great hall of Archbishop Lanfranc's palace received Thomas Becket shortly before his murder in 1170. Much of the medieval palace was demolished in the 1640s during the Commonwealth period. The present building incorporates surviving medieval fragments and was substantially rebuilt in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, notably under Archbishop Frederick Temple.

Use

The Old Palace serves as the Canterbury home of the Archbishop and as a venue for meetings and events; every ten years it hosts bishops gathering for the Lambeth Conference.
