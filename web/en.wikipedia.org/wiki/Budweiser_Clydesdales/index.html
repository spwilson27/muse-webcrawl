Budweiser Clydesdales

The Budweiser Clydesdale hitch, an American advertising icon since 1933.

The Budweiser Clydesdales are a group of Clydesdale horses used for promotions and commercials by the Anheuser-Busch brewing company, brewers of Budweiser beer. The tradition began in 1933, when August A. Busch Jr. presented a six-horse hitch of Clydesdales to his father to celebrate the repeal of Prohibition; the hitch then famously delivered a case of beer to former governor Al Smith in New York.

The hitch

Several teams ("hitches") are kept in the United States and travel tens of thousands of miles a year for parades, fairs and sporting events. A hitch consists of eight horses pulling a turn-of-the-century red beer wagon, driven by handlers in green and gold livery, with a Dalmatian — historically the coach dog of brewery dray teams — riding beside the drivers. Individual horses must meet strict standards: bay colour, four white stockings, a white blaze, black mane and tail, at least 18 hands high and around 1,800–2,300 pounds.

Breeding and fame

The primary breeding farm is Warm Springs Ranch near Boonville, Missouri, with a famous stable at the Anheuser-Busch brewery in St. Louis. The Clydesdales are best known to the general public for their long-running series of Super Bowl television commercials, frequently ranked among the most popular of the game.
