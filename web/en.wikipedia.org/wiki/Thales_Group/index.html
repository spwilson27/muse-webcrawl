Thales Group

Thales S.A.
Industry: Aerospace, defence, electronics
Founded: 1893 (as Compagnie Française Thomson-Houston)
Headquarters: Meudon, France
Revenue: approx. €20 billion (2024)
Employees: approx. 80,000

Thales Group is a French multinational company that designs, develops and manufactures electrical systems as well as devices and equipment for the aerospace, defence, transportation and security sectors. The company is headquartered in Meudon, a suburb of Paris, and operates in more than 60 countries. It is partly owned by the French state and is consistently ranked among the world's ten largest defence contractors.

History

The company's origins lie in the Compagnie Française Thomson-Houston, founded in 1893 as the French subsidiary of the American Thomson-Houston Electric Company. After a series of mergers and nationalizations the electronics group adopted the name Thomson-CSF in 1968. Following privatization in 1998 and the acquisition of the British firm Racal Electronics in 2000, the group was renamed Thales in December 2000, after the Greek philosopher Thales of Miletus.

Operations

Thales activities span five principal areas: avionics (flight decks, in-flight entertainment), space (through the Thales Alenia Space joint venture with Leonardo, builder of satellites and orbital infrastructure), defence electronics (radars such as the Ground Master family, optronics, sonar and electronic warfare), digital identity and security (the former Gemalto business, acquired in 2019, covering payment cards, SIM cards, e-passports and cybersecurity), and surface transportation (railway signalling and fare collection systems).

In 2023 Thales agreed to sell its rail signalling business to Hitachi Rail, a transaction completed in 2024, in order to concentrate on its three core markets of defence, aerospace and digital identity/security. The group employs roughly 80,000 people; approximately half of its revenue comes from military contracts.

Controversies

The company has been involved in several long-running legal cases, most prominently the investigations in France and South Africa connected with the 1999 South African arms deal, in which Thales's local subsidiary was accused of bribery; proceedings against former South African president Jacob Zuma and the company continued for many years.
