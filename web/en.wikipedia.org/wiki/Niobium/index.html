Niobium is a chemical element with symbol Nb and atomic number 41. It is a light grey, crystalline, ductile transition metal often found in the minerals pyrochlore and columbite. Its name comes from Niobe, daughter of Tantalus in Greek mythology, reflecting its similarity to tantalum.

History

Niobium was identified in 1801 by the English chemist Charles Hatchett in an ore from Connecticut; he named it columbium. Confusion with tantalum lasted decades until Heinrich Rose and others established it as a distinct element, and the name niobium was officially adopted in 1949.

Properties

Niobium is soft and ductile when pure, resists corrosion, and becomes a superconductor at cryogenic temperatures (critical temperature 9.2 K, the highest of the elemental superconductors).

Applications

Most niobium is used as ferroniobium in high-strength low-alloy steels for pipelines, cars and construction. Niobium-titanium and niobium-tin superconductors are used in MRI magnets and particle accelerators; niobium alloys serve in jet engines and the metal is used in jewellery and coinage.
