Essex is a ceremonial county in the East of England, bordering Suffolk, Cambridgeshire, Hertfordshire, Greater London and Kent across the Thames Estuary, with a long North Sea coastline. Its name derives from the East Saxons, whose kingdom emerged in the early Middle Ages.

History

Colchester (Camulodunum) was Roman Britain's first capital and is among England's oldest towns; it was targeted in Boudica's revolt of AD 60–61. Essex later became a royal Saxon kingdom before absorption into Wessex. In 1381 the Peasants' Revolt began partly in the county. Large areas of marshland were drained, and Harwich and Tilbury developed as ports.

Geography and economy

The county mixes London commuter towns, new towns such as Basildon and Harlow, seaside resorts like Southend and Clacton, and rural districts around Dedham Vale ("Constable Country" after painter John Constable). London Stansted Airport and the ports of Harwich and London Gateway are major employers.
