S-unit

An S-unit is a unit of measure of biological catalytic activity, defined in the field of enzyme kinetics for certain glycoside hydrolases. The unit is named after its origin in studies of the sweet-potato β-amylase system and related work: one S-unit is the amount of enzyme that liberates a defined quantity of reducing sugar — measured as maltose equivalents — per unit time under specified conditions.

Definition and use

In the classical Sumner tradition of enzyme units, activity was often expressed by the amount of product formed under a standard assay (for example, the quantity of enzyme that produces 1 mg of maltose from starch in a fixed time at a given temperature and pH). The S-unit belongs to this family of practical, assay-defined units developed before the adoption of the international unit (U, µmol/min) and the SI katal. Such units persist in some industrial and food-science literature — for example in specifications of amylase preparations used in starch processing — where assay conditions are standardised by trade practice.

Relation to standard units

Because an S-unit is tied to particular assay conditions (substrate, temperature, pH, and reaction time), it cannot be converted to katals without reference to those conditions. Under the standard assay, conversion factors were published by manufacturers and in methods compendia so that results could be compared with activity expressed in international units.
