The Battle of Mount Longdon was fought between 11 and 12 June 1982 during the Falklands War. It was one of the decisive night battles for the mountain defences west of Stanley, in which the British 3rd Battalion, the Parachute Regiment (3 PARA) captured the strongly held Argentine position on Mount Longdon.

Background

Mount Longdon, a long rocky ridge west of the Falkland capital, formed the northern anchor of Argentina's defensive ring around Stanley. It was held by the Argentine 7th Infantry Regiment, reinforced with machine guns, mortars and minefields. As part of Brigadier Julian Thompson's coordinated night assault on 11–12 June — alongside attacks on Two Sisters and Mount Harriet — 3 PARA under Lieutenant Colonel Hew Pike was ordered to take Longdon.

The battle

The British attack began silently but was compromised when a paratrooper stepped on a mine, alerting the defenders. There followed many hours of close-quarters fighting among the rocks, bunker by bunker. Sergeant Ian McKay, who single-handedly attacked a machine-gun position that was pinning down his platoon, was killed and received a posthumous Victoria Cross. By dawn on 12 June the summit was in British hands, though Argentine artillery fire continued to cause casualties.

Aftermath

The battle cost 3 PARA 23 killed — the heaviest British battalion losses of the war — while Argentine deaths were estimated at 31 or more, with many taken prisoner. The fall of Mount Longdon, combined with the capture of the other hills, broke the Stanley defences; the Argentine garrison surrendered two days later, on 14 June 1982.
