Independent school (United Kingdom)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An independent school in the United Kingdom is a school that is independent of national or local government control in its finances and governance. Independent schools are funded principally by tuition fees paid by parents, supplemented in some cases by endowments, donations, and investment income. Roughly 2,500 such schools educate around 600,000–700,000 pupils — about 7 percent of schoolchildren in England, with higher proportions in the later secondary years.

Terminology

The most prestigious independent secondary schools are traditionally called public schools — a historical term (the schools were "public" in being open to fee-paying pupils from anywhere, as opposed to private tutoring), strongly associated with a group that includes Eton, Harrow, Winchester, Rugby, and Westminster. Independent primary-age schools are often called preparatory ("prep") schools. The sector's main representative body is the Independent Schools Council.

Regulation and debate

Independent schools must register with the government and are inspected for compliance with standards, but they need not follow the National Curriculum, and their teachers need not hold qualified teacher status. Many are charities, conferring tax advantages that have long been politically contested; critics argue the sector entrenches privilege, pointing to its disproportionate presence among entrants to elite universities and professions, while defenders cite parental choice, educational diversity, bursaries, and partnerships with state schools.
