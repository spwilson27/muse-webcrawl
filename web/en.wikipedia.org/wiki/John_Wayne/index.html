John Wayne

Born: Marion Robert Morrison, May 26, 1907, Winterset, Iowa, U.S.
Died: June 11, 1979 (aged 72), Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Occupation: Actor, filmmaker
Years active: 1926–1979

John Wayne (born Marion Robert Morrison; May 26, 1907 – June 11, 1979), nicknamed "the Duke", was an American actor and filmmaker who became a popular icon through his starring roles in films made during the Golden Age of Hollywood, especially in Western and war movies. His career flourished from the silent era of the 1920s through the American New Wave, as he appeared in a total of 179 film and television productions.

Early life

Wayne was born in Winterset, Iowa, and grew up in Southern California. He lost a football scholarship to the University of Southern California after a bodysurfing accident, and began working for the Fox Film Corporation as a prop boy and extra. Director John Ford befriended him and gave him small roles; his first leading part came in Raoul Walsh's The Big Trail (1930), for which the studio gave him the stage name John Wayne.

Career

After a decade of B-Westerns, Wayne's breakthrough came with Ford's Stagecoach (1939), which made him a mainstream star. He went on to headline such classics as Red River (1948), The Quiet Man (1952), The Searchers (1956), Rio Bravo (1959), The Man Who Shot Liberty Valance (1962), and True Grit (1969), for which he won the Academy Award for Best Actor. His final role was in The Shootist (1976), playing an ageing gunfighter dying of cancer—a part with strong parallels to his own life.

Legacy

Wayne remains an enduring symbol of rugged American individualism and masculinity. The American Film Institute ranked him thirteenth on its list of the greatest male screen legends of classic American cinema. He was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1980, and Orange County's airport was renamed John Wayne Airport in his honour.
