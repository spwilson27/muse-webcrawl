Annihilation of Caste

Annihilation of Caste is an undelivered speech written in 1936 by B. R. Ambedkar, the Indian social reformer, jurist and principal architect of the Indian Constitution. The speech was prepared for the annual conference of the Jat-Pat Todak Mandal, a Hindu reformist organisation in Lahore, which cancelled the event after finding the text too radical in its criticism of Hinduism.

Argument

Ambedkar argued that caste is not merely a division of labour but a "division of labourers" into a graded hierarchy, sustained by religious sanction. He held that genuine reform required destroying the sacredness of the Hindu scriptures (shastras) that legitimise caste, and promoted inter-caste marriage as the true solvent of the system. He concluded: "You must destroy the religion of the Shrutis and the Shastras. Nothing else will avail."

Gandhi's response

Mahatma Gandhi responded in his journal Harijan, defending varna while opposing untouchability. Ambedkar printed a rejoinder in the second edition (1937), and the exchange became a classic debate in Indian political thought.

Legacy

The text remains a foundational work of anti-caste literature and is widely read in Dalit studies and Indian political science.
