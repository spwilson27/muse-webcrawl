The abdomen (colloquially called the belly, tummy, midriff, tuckus, or stomach) is the front part of the torso between the thorax (chest) and pelvis, in humans and in other vertebrates. In arthropods it is the posterior tagma of the body.

Contents

The abdomen contains most of the organs of the digestive system, including the stomach, small and large intestines, pancreas, liver, and gallbladder. It also houses the kidneys, spleen, and the great blood vessels — the abdominal aorta and inferior vena cava. These organs are held together loosely by connective tissue and by the membrane known as the peritoneum.

Muscles

The abdominal wall consists of layered muscles including the rectus abdominis, the external and internal obliques, and the transversus abdominis, which support the trunk and assist in breathing, posture, and movement.

Clinical significance

Abdominal pain may arise from many causes, ranging from indigestion and infection to appendicitis and an abdominal aortic aneurysm. Physical examination of the abdomen is divided into four quadrants or nine regions for diagnostic purposes.
