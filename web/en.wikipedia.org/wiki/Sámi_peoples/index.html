The Sámi peoples are the distinct regional and linguistic groups that together make up the Sámi people, the indigenous inhabitants of Sápmi in northern Europe. The plural form is used especially by scholars and by Sámi political organisations to stress the diversity of the ten traditionally recognised Sámi groups, each with its own language, costume patterns and territory.

The groups

The groups, roughly from west and north to east, are the Southern Sámi, Ume Sámi, Pite Sámi, Lule Sámi and Northern Sámi (the largest, in Norway, Sweden and Finland), and in Finland and Russia the Inari Sámi, Skolt Sámi, Akkala Sámi (whose language is now extinct), Kildin Sámi and Ter Sámi. Nine of the languages remain living but all are endangered to some degree; Northern Sámi is spoken by the great majority of Sámi language speakers.

Terminology

The Sámi parliaments and the Saami Council use both "Sámi people" (singular, for the nation as a whole in political and legal contexts) and "Sámi peoples" (plural, acknowledging internal diversity, as in the Nordic Sámi Convention negotiations). The older exonym "Lapps" is now considered derogatory and has been replaced in all four states' official usage.
