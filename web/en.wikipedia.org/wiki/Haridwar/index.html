Haridwar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Haridwar is a city and municipal corporation in the Haridwar district of Uttarakhand, India. Situated where the Ganges leaves the foothills of the Himalayas and enters the plains, it is one of the seven holiest places (Sapta Puri) in Hinduism and a major pilgrimage centre.

Religious significance

Haridwar hosts the Kumbh Mela, held in rotation with Prayagraj, Nashik and Ujjain; the 2021 Haridwar Kumbh drew millions of pilgrims. The most sacred spot is Har Ki Pauri ghat, where the evening Ganga aarti ceremony is performed daily. Important temples include Mansa Devi and Chandi Devi on hills overlooking the city, and the Daksha Mahadev temple at Kankhal.

Geography and economy

The city lies at the head of the Upper Ganges Canal, historically a major irrigation work. Besides pilgrimage tourism, Haridwar has grown as an industrial city since the development of the State Industrial Development Corporation estates, hosting pharmaceutical and engineering firms. The nearby town of Rishikesh and the Rajaji National Park are closely associated with it.
