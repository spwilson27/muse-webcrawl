The Industrial Areas Foundation (IAF) is a United States-based network of local faith- and community-based organisations devoted to broad-based community organising. Founded in Chicago in 1940 by Saul Alinsky, together with figures including the businessman Marshall Field III and Bishop Bernard Sheil, it is regarded as the founding institution of modern community organising.

Approach

The IAF builds local organisations out of existing institutions — congregations, schools, unions, and civic groups — rather than around single issues or charismatic leaders. Professional organisers train volunteer leaders, conduct "relational meetings" and house meetings to identify issues and develop leaders, and then take collective public action directed at government and corporate decision-makers. The method prizes local dues-paying independence, non-partisanship, and the "iron rule": never do for others what they can do for themselves.

History

Alinsky's first project, the Back of the Yards Neighborhood Council in Chicago (1939), became the model for the IAF. After Alinsky's death in 1972, his protégé Edward Chambers became executive director and systematised the IAF's training, expanding the network across the United States and, through affiliates, to Canada, the United Kingdom (Citizens UK), Germany, and Australia. IAF organisations have campaigned on living wages, affordable housing, public education, health care, immigration, and infrastructure, and are credited with early living-wage victories and large-scale housing programmes such as the Nehemiah housing in Brooklyn.

Notable affiliates

Affiliates have included Communities Organized for Public Service (COPS) in San Antonio, led for many years by Ernesto Cortés Jr., and the Metro IAF network on the US East Coast. Barack Obama's early work as a community organiser in Chicago was influenced by the Alinsky tradition, though Obama worked for a different organising network.
