Kindia is the fourth largest city in Guinea, lying about 137 km northeast of the capital Conakry. It is the capital of Kindia Region and of Kindia Prefecture, and a major transport and trading hub connecting the coast with Guinea's interior and with Sierra Leone.

History and economy

Kindia developed as a railway town on the Conakry–Kankan line and grew into the principal market centre for the surrounding agricultural region, sometimes called the "city of citrus fruits" for its production of oranges, bananas, pineapples, and mangoes. Bauxite mining near the town — the Compagnie des Bauxites de Kindia operation at Débélé — is a major employer, exporting ore through the port of Conakry. The city is predominantly inhabited by the Susu and Malinké peoples.

Geography

Kindia sits at the foot of the Fouta Djallon highlands' western escarpment, near Mount Gangan, whose cliffs and waterfalls — including the Voile de la Mariée (Bride's Veil) falls on the Samou River — are popular attractions. The Institut de Recherche Agronomique de Guinée maintains research stations in the area, and the Pasteur Institute of Guinea operates a research centre at Kindia.
