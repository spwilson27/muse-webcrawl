James Wolfe

Major General James Wolfe (2 January 1727 – 13 September 1759) was a British Army officer, known for his leadership of British forces in the capture of Quebec in the Seven Years' War, during which he was killed in the Battle of the Plains of Abraham.

Early life and career

Wolfe was born in Westerham, Kent, the son of Lieutenant General Edward Wolfe. He joined the army at thirteen and saw action in Flanders during the War of the Austrian Succession. He distinguished himself at the Battle of Culloden in 1746, though accounts differ on his role in the aftermath.

Quebec campaign

In 1758 Wolfe served under Jeffery Amherst at the Siege of Louisbourg. Promoted to major general, he was given command of the expedition against Quebec in 1759. After months of stalemate, he surprised the French under Louis-Joseph de Montcalm by landing troops below the Heights of Abraham, forcing a battle on 13 September 1759.

Death

Wolfe was wounded three times during the battle and died on the field, reportedly uttering "Now, God be praised, I will die in peace" upon hearing of the French retreat. Montcalm was also fatally wounded. Wolfe became a British national hero; his death was famously depicted by Benjamin West in The Death of General Wolfe.
