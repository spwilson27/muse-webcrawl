An-Nisa (Arabic: النساء, "The Women") is the fourth chapter (surah) of the Quran, with 176 verses (ayat). It is a Medinan surah — revealed after the migration to Medina — and takes its name from its extensive discussion of women, family law and inheritance. It is one of the principal sources of Islamic family and social legislation.

Contents

The chapter addresses the rights of orphans and women, rules of marriage (including the much-discussed verse 4:3 on polygyny and verse 4:34), the division of inheritance with specified shares for male and female relatives (verses 4:11–12 and 4:176), and regulations concerning prayer, warfare, relations with the People of the Book, and the hypocrites (munafiqun) of Medina. Verse 4:157 addresses the crucifixion of Jesus, stating the mainstream Islamic position that he was not killed or crucified, but that it was made to appear so.

Themes

Commentators note the surah's emphasis on protecting vulnerable members of society — orphans, women and the weak — and on building a just community order in the young Medinan state. Its legal passages have generated a large body of jurisprudence (fiqh) and remain central to debates about gender and law in Islam.
