Van der Pol oscillator

The van der Pol oscillator is a non-conservative oscillator with nonlinear damping, described by a second-order differential equation. It evolves in time according to:

d²x/dt² − μ(1 − x²)dx/dt + x = 0

where x is the position coordinate, t is time, and μ is a scalar parameter indicating the strength of the nonlinearity and damping. The oscillator is named after the Dutch physicist Balthasar van der Pol, who proposed it in the 1920s while working at Philips on electrical circuits containing vacuum tubes.

Properties

Unlike a simple harmonic oscillator, the van der Pol oscillator exhibits a stable limit cycle: trajectories in phase space converge to a closed orbit regardless of their starting conditions (except at the unstable origin). When |x| is small, the damping term is negative, so the system gains energy and oscillations grow; when |x| is large, the damping is positive and energy is dissipated. The balance between these regimes produces self-sustained oscillations of nearly constant amplitude.

For μ = 0 the equation reduces to simple harmonic motion. For small μ the limit cycle is nearly circular and the oscillations are almost sinusoidal. For large μ the oscillator becomes a relaxation oscillator, with slow build-up phases followed by rapid jumps.

History and applications

Van der Pol originally developed the equation to model oscillations in triode vacuum-tube circuits. He observed stable limit cycles and, when the circuit was driven near the limit cycle frequency, entrainment phenomena. The oscillator has since become a canonical example in nonlinear dynamics and has been applied to fields as varied as circuit theory, seismology, and biology — including models of the heartbeat and of neuronal firing, such as the FitzHugh–Nagumo model, which generalizes the van der Pol system.

Forced oscillator

When an external periodic driving term is added, the forced van der Pol oscillator can display frequency locking, quasiperiodicity, and chaos, making it a standard testbed for the study of synchronization.
