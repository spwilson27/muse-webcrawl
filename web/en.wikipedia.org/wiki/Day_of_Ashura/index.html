Day of Ashura

Ashura (Arabic: عَاشُورَاء) is the tenth day of Muharram, the first month of the Islamic calendar. It is a major day of commemoration in Islam. For Shia Muslims, Ashura marks the martyrdom of Husayn ibn Ali, the grandson of the Prophet Muhammad, at the Battle of Karbala in 61 AH (680 CE), and is observed with mourning rituals and processions. For Sunni Muslims, the day is traditionally associated with events such as Moses and the Israelites being saved from Pharaoh at the Red Sea, and is commonly marked by voluntary fasting.

Historical background

On 10 Muharram 61 AH, Husayn ibn Ali and a small party of his family and companions were killed by the forces of the Umayyad caliph Yazid I at Karbala in present-day Iraq, after refusing to pledge allegiance to Yazid. The event is central to Shia identity and theology, symbolizing resistance to tyranny and injustice.

Observance

Shia commemorations during Muharram culminate on Ashura with majlis (gatherings recounting the tragedy), processions, recitations of elegies, and in some communities ritual chest-beating and other expressions of grief. Pilgrims gather in Karbala, which hosts one of the largest annual gatherings in the world. The following fortieth day, Arbaeen, is also widely observed.

Sunni tradition, based on hadith reporting that the Prophet Muhammad fasted on Ashura after finding the Jews of Medina fasting in commemoration of Moses, recommends fasting on the ninth and tenth, or tenth and eleventh, days of Muharram.
