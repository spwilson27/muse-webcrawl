W. A. J. "Alan" Watson (27 October 1933 – 7 November 2018) was a Scottish legal scholar, one of the world's leading authorities on Roman law, comparative law and legal history. He is best known for developing the theory of legal transplants — the idea that most changes in law occur through the borrowing of rules and institutions from other legal systems.

Career

Watson was educated at the University of Glasgow and held chairs at the University of Oxford and the University of Edinburgh, where he founded the Centre for Legal History. In 1989 he moved to the United States, becoming Ernest and Ruby Morgan Professor at the University of Georgia School of Law. He was also a leading scholar of the law of slavery in antiquity.

Major works

His many books include Legal Transplants: An Approach to Comparative Law (1974), The Evolution of Law (1985), Roman Slave Law (1987), and a translation of Justinian's Digest which he edited. His thesis that law develops largely autonomously through borrowing, often out of keeping with the society it governs, generated wide scholarly debate.
