Dollar diplomacy

Dollar diplomacy is the term applied to the foreign policy of the United States under President William Howard Taft (1909–1913) and his Secretary of State Philander C. Knox, which sought to further American commercial and financial interests abroad — especially in Latin America and East Asia — through government support for loans and investments rather than through military force alone.

Policy

The policy encouraged American bankers to extend loans to countries such as Nicaragua, Honduras and China, and to participate in international railway consortia. In practice it was backed at times by military intervention, as in Nicaragua, where American supervision of customs revenues followed defaults. Critics coined the phrase to suggest that dollars, rather than statesmanship, were doing the talking.

Legacy

President Woodrow Wilson repudiated the term while retaining elements of the practice. The phrase remains in use for foreign policies conducted primarily through economic leverage.
