The politics of Iraq take place within a federal parliamentary republic established under the Constitution of 2005, adopted after the 2003 invasion of Iraq overthrew Saddam Hussein's Ba'athist regime. The President is head of state, the Prime Minister heads the government, and legislative authority rests with the Council of Representatives.

The Council of Representatives meets in Baghdad.

Muhasasa system

Iraqi politics since 2003 have been structured by muhasasa ta'ifiyya, an informal ethno-sectarian apportionment of offices: the presidency is conventionally held by a Kurd, the premiership by a Shia Arab, and the speakership of parliament by a Sunni Arab, with ministries distributed among parties along communal lines. The system is credited with preventing any group's monopoly of power but widely blamed for corruption, patronage, and weak public services.

Federalism and the Kurdistan Region

The constitution recognises the Kurdistan Region as a federal region with its own parliament, government (KRG), and security forces (Peshmerga). Disputes between Baghdad and Erbil over oil exports, budgets, and contested territories such as Kirkuk remain a recurring source of tension.

Recent developments

Mass protests in 2019 (the Tishreen movement) denounced corruption and the muhasasa system, forcing the resignation of Prime Minister Adel Abdul Mahdi. After the October 2021 elections and a year-long political deadlock marked by armed clashes between rival Shia factions, Mohammed Shia' Al Sudani formed a government in October 2022 backed by the Coordination Framework coalition.
