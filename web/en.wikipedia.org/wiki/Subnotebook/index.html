Subnotebook

A subnotebook, also called an ultraportable, is a class of laptop computer that is smaller and lighter than a typical notebook, sacrificing some features — particularly the optical drive and some ports — for portability. Subnotebooks generally have screens between about 10 and 13 inches and weigh under roughly 1.5 kilograms, while retaining a full-size or near-full-size keyboard and a full desktop operating system.

History

Early examples included the Toshiba Libretto and the Sony VAIO PictureBook series of the 1990s, and the IBM ThinkPad 701 with its folding "butterfly" keyboard. The category was reshaped by the MacBook Air (2008) and by Intel's Ultrabook specification (2011), which standardised thin, light, fast-waking designs. The low-cost netbooks of the late 2000s are generally treated as a related but distinct category because of their reduced performance and smaller keyboards.
