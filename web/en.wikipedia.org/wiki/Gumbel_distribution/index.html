In probability theory and statistics, the Gumbel distribution (also known as the type-I generalized extreme value distribution) is used to model the distribution of the maximum (or the minimum) of a number of samples of various distributions. It is named after Emil Julius Gumbel (1891–1966), who described the distribution in papers on extreme values.

Definitions

The probability density function of the Gumbel distribution is

f(x) = (1/β) · exp(−(z + e−z)), where z = (x − μ)/β.

Here μ is the location parameter (the mode of the distribution) and β is the scale parameter. The cumulative distribution function is F(x) = exp(−exp(−(x − μ)/β)). The mean is μ + βγ, where γ ≈ 0.5772 is the Euler–Mascheroni constant, and the variance is π²β²/6.

Properties and applications

The Gumbel distribution is the limiting distribution of the maximum of a sequence of independent random variables from many common distributions, a result formalised in the Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko theorem of extreme value theory. It is therefore widely used in hydrology to model extreme events such as annual maximum flood levels and rainfall, in engineering for extreme wind speeds, and in survival analysis. In machine learning, the Gumbel distribution is used in the Gumbel–softmax trick for differentiable sampling from categorical distributions.

Related distributions

If the random variable X follows a Gumbel distribution, then −X follows the Gumbel distribution for minima. The Gumbel distribution is a special case of the generalized extreme value distribution, and is related to the logistic and Gompertz distributions.
