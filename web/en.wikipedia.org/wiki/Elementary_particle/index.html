In particle physics, an elementary particle or fundamental particle is a subatomic particle that is not composed of other particles. The Standard Model organizes them into fermions (matter particles) and bosons (force carriers).

Fermions

Six quarks (up, down, charm, strange, top, bottom) combine into protons, neutrons, and other hadrons. Six leptons include the electron and its neutrino, plus muon and tau families. Antiparticles mirror each fermion.

Bosons

Gauge bosons mediate forces: photons (electromagnetism), W and Z bosons (weak interaction), and gluons (strong interaction). The Higgs boson, discovered at CERN in 2012, is a scalar boson associated with the mechanism giving particles mass. The graviton, carrier of gravity, remains hypothetical.

History

The electron (1897), photon concept, proton, and neutron anchored early theory; particle accelerators revealed hundreds of hadrons in the mid-20th century, prompting the quark model (1964) and the Standard Model's completion.

Beyond the Standard Model

Open questions — dark matter, neutrino masses, matter–antimatter asymmetry — motivate searches for supersymmetric partners, sterile neutrinos, and other new particles.
