Yuan Zhen (Chinese: 元稹; Wade–Giles: Yüan Chen; 779 – September 2, 831), courtesy name Weizhi (微之), was a Chinese poet and politician of the middle Tang dynasty. He is known for his work in both the old-style and new-style poetry movements and as a key figure, along with his friend Bai Juyi, in the New Yuefu (新樂府) movement, which sought to use poetry to address social and political issues.

Life and works

Yuan Zhen passed the imperial examinations at a young age and served in a variety of official posts, weathering the factional struggles of the Tang court, including exile to provincial posts. He briefly served as chancellor under Emperor Muzong in 822. His close friendship and poetic exchanges with Bai Juyi were celebrated; the two are often paired as "Yuan–Bai" (元白).

His best-known work is the chuanqi (tales of the marvelous) story Yingying's Biography (鶯鶯傳, Yingying zhuan), a tale of a young scholar's love affair and abandonment of the heroine Cui Yingying, which influenced later Chinese drama, notably the celebrated play Romance of the Western Chamber (西廂記). His autobiographical "Poem of Grief" (遣悲懷) lamenting his deceased wife Wei Cong remains famous.
