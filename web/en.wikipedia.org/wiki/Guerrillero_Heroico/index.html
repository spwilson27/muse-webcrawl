Guerrillero Heroico

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Guerrillero Heroico ("Heroic Guerrilla Fighter") is an iconic photograph of Marxist revolutionary Ernesto "Che" Guevara taken by Cuban photographer Alberto Korda. It was captured on 5 March 1960, in Havana, Cuba, at a memorial service for victims of the La Coubre explosion. By the end of the 1960s the image had become a ubiquitous symbol of revolution and counterculture; Maryland Institute College of Art has called it "the most famous photograph in the world".

The photograph

Korda, staff photographer for the newspaper Revolución, photographed Guevara, then 31, as he appeared briefly on the speakers' platform at the funeral march; using a Leica camera with a 90 mm lens, he shot two frames before Guevara withdrew. The published crop concentrated on Guevara's face — his beret with its star, flowing hair and gaze fixed on some distant point. Korda's paper did not use the picture, but he kept a print on his studio wall for years.

Spread and impact

After Guevara's capture and execution in Bolivia in 1967, Italian publisher Giangiacomo Feltrinelli obtained the image from Korda and turned it into a best-selling poster. Reproduced on murals, banners, T-shirts, album covers and advertisements, the photograph became perhaps the most reproduced image in history — a fate Korda, a committed revolutionary who never received royalties under the embargo, largely welcomed, though he later sued to stop its use in a vodka advertisement.

Legacy

A monumental version of the image, rendered in steel by sculptor Enrique Ávila, overlooks the Plaza de la Revolución in Havana. Critics have noted the paradox of an anti-capitalist icon becoming a global commercial brand emptied of its original meaning.
