California wine production has a rich viticulture history since 1680 when Spanish Jesuit missionaries planted Vitis vinifera vines native to the Mediterranean region in their established missions to produce wine for religious services. In the 1770s, Spanish missionaries continued the practice under the direction of the Father Junípero Serra, who directed the planting of the first vineyard at Mission San Juan Capistrano.

History

Commercial winemaking expanded during the Gold Rush era, with pioneers such as Agoston Haraszthy importing European vine varieties. The industry was devastated by phylloxera in the late 19th century and by Prohibition (1920–1933), after which it slowly rebuilt. International recognition came with the 1976 "Judgment of Paris", in which California Cabernet Sauvignon and Chardonnay defeated top French wines in blind tastings.

Geography and production

California produces roughly four-fifths or more of all American wine, and if it were a country it would rank among the world's largest wine producers. Wine grapes are grown from Mendocino County in the north to Temecula in the south, and in the vast Central Valley, which supplies much of the volume production. The state has over 140 American Viticultural Areas (AVAs) recognising distinct growing conditions, and its Mediterranean climate, with coastal cooling from fog and ocean breezes, suits a wide range of grape varieties.
