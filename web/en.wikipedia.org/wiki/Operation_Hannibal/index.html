Operation Hannibal

ObjectiveEvacuation of German troops and civilians from East Prussia, Pomerania and the Baltic states
Date23 January – 8 May 1945
Executed byKriegsmarine
OutcomeAn estimated 800,000–900,000 civilians and 350,000 soldiers evacuated

Operation Hannibal was a German naval operation involving the evacuation by sea of German troops and civilians from Courland, East Prussia, and the Polish Corridor from mid-January to May 1945 as the Red Army advanced during the East Prussian and East Pomeranian offensives and subsidiary operations.

The operation

Grand Admiral Karl Dönitz ordered the evacuation on 23 January 1945. Hundreds of vessels of every type — ocean liners, freighters, naval auxiliaries, and fishing boats — ferried refugees from ports such as Pillau, Danzig, and Gotenhafen westward to Germany and German-occupied Denmark. In terms of the numbers of people moved in a short period, it was one of the largest emergency evacuations by sea in history, exceeding the scale of the Dunkirk evacuation.

Losses

The operation was marred by some of the worst maritime disasters of all time. The liner Wilhelm Gustloff, torpedoed by a Soviet submarine on 30 January 1945, sank with an estimated 9,000 or more lives lost — the largest loss of life in a single ship sinking in history. The General von Steuben (February) and Goya (April) were also torpedoed with thousands of deaths each.
