Wilmington is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Delaware and the county seat of New Castle County. It lies at the confluence of the Christina River and Brandywine Creek, near where the Christina flows into the Delaware River. The city is part of the Philadelphia–Camden–Wilmington metropolitan area.

History

The area was first settled by Swedish colonists who built Fort Christina in 1638, the first Swedish settlement in North America. The Dutch took over New Sweden in 1655, and the English in 1664. The town was chartered as Willingtown in the 1730s and renamed Wilmington, likely after Spencer Compton, Earl of Wilmington. In the 19th century the Brandywine's mills — flour mills and the DuPont gunpowder works — made the city an industrial center, and shipbuilding boomed during the Civil War and both world wars.

Economy

Wilmington is a national center for the credit card and banking industries, owing to Delaware's business-friendly financial laws, and is famous for the Delaware Court of Chancery: a majority of Fortune 500 companies are incorporated in Delaware, many with nominal headquarters in the city. Major employers have included DuPont, JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America and Capital One.

Geography and climate

The city covers about 17 square miles on the fall line between the Piedmont and the Atlantic Coastal Plain. It has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers and moderately cold winters.

Government and education

Wilmington has a mayor–council government. It is home to Delaware State University's city campus, Wilmington University locations, and the Delaware College of Art and Design. Amtrak's Wilmington station serves the Northeast Corridor.
