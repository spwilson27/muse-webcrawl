Republic of Iraq

The Republic of Iraq is the official name of Iraq, a country in West Asia bordered by Turkey, Iran, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Syria, with a short coastline on the Persian Gulf. The republic was proclaimed on 14 July 1958, when the Hashemite monarchy was overthrown in a military coup led by Abd al-Karim Qasim and Abdul Salam Arif. Iraq's capital and largest city is Baghdad.

History of the republic

The 14 July Revolution of 1958 ended the monarchy; King Faisal II and his family were killed and Qasim became prime minister. Qasim was himself overthrown in 1963, and after further coups the Ba'ath Party seized power in 1968. Saddam Hussein became president in 1979 and ruled until the 2003 invasion of Iraq by a United States-led coalition. His rule encompassed the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), the Gulf War (1990–91) after the invasion of Kuwait, and a decade of sanctions.

Politics since 2003

A new constitution adopted in 2005 established Iraq as a federal parliamentary republic, with a president as head of state and a prime minister as head of government; the legislature is the Council of Representatives. The Kurdistan Region enjoys constitutional autonomy. Iraq subsequently endured insurgency and sectarian civil conflict, and from 2014 fought a major war against the Islamic State, which had seized Mosul and much of the north and west, before its territorial defeat in 2017.

Economy

Iraq possesses some of the world's largest proven oil reserves, and petroleum dominates exports and government revenue. The country is a founding member of OPEC. Agriculture in the Tigris–Euphrates valley, once the heartland of ancient Mesopotamia, remains significant but is under pressure from drought and upstream damming.
