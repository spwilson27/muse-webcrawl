Sakanoue no Tamuramaro (坂上 田村麻呂, 758 – June 17, 811) was a court noble and general of the early Heian period of Japan. He is best known as the commander who subdued the Emishi peoples of northern Honshū and as the first general to hold permanently the office of Sei-i Taishōgun — the title later shortened to "shogun".

Career

Tamuramaro served the Emperor Kanmu. After earlier commanders failed against the Emishi chieftain Aterui, Tamuramaro was appointed to lead the campaign in 801 and built fortresses in the Kitakami valley (including Isawa Castle). In 802 Aterui surrendered and was executed despite Tamuramaro's reported plea to spare him. Kanmu rewarded Tamuramaro with appointment as Sei-i Taishōgun in 801 (reconferred in 803) and later as Dainagon, one of the highest court offices.

Legacy

Tradition credits Tamuramaro with founding the famous Kiyomizu-dera temple in Kyoto (a more modest version of the story has him endowing its hall). He died in 811 and was buried at Shōgun-zuka (the "General's Mound") east of Kyoto, from which, legend says, his spirit guards the capital. His campaigns are commemorated in the Tamuramaro legend cycle of northern Japan, and the Tanabata festivals of the Tōhoku region are sometimes linked to his Emishi wars.
