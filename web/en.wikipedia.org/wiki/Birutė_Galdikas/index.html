Birutė Marija Filomena Galdikas, OC (born May 10, 1946) is a Canadian anthropologist, primatologist, conservationist, ethologist, and author. She is a professor at Simon Fraser University in British Columbia. Well known in the field of primatology, Galdikas is recognised as a leading authority on orangutans. Prior to her fieldwork, little was known about the dietary and behavioural ecology of this elusive species.

Galdikas is one of the "Trimates" (or "Leakey's Angels"), three women researchers recruited by the palaeoanthropologist Louis Leakey to study the great apes in their natural habitats, alongside Jane Goodall, who studied chimpanzees, and Dian Fossey, who studied gorillas.

Early life and education

Galdikas was born in Wiesbaden, Germany, to Lithuanian parents who emigrated to Canada when she was a child. She grew up in Toronto and studied psychology, biology and anthropology at the University of British Columbia and UCLA, where she earned her doctorate in anthropology in 1978.

Work in Borneo

In 1971 Galdikas arrived at Tanjung Puting Reserve in Indonesian Borneo and established Camp Leakey, beginning one of the longest continuous studies of any mammal by a single researcher. Her work documented orangutan social organisation, diet, reproduction and their long interbirth intervals. She also rehabilitated orphaned and ex-captive orangutans for release to the wild. In 1986 she co-founded Orangutan Foundation International to support habitat protection, as Borneo's forests came under pressure from logging and oil-palm plantations.

Recognition

Galdikas has received numerous honours, including appointment as an Officer of the Order of Canada, Indonesia's Kalpataru environmental award, and the Tyler Prize-adjacent recognition of her conservation work. Her memoirs, including Reflections of Eden (1995), brought orangutan conservation to a wide audience.
