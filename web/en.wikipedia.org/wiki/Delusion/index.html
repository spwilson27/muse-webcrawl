Delusion

A delusion is a false fixed belief that is not amenable to change in light of conflicting evidence. As a pathology, it is distinct from a belief based on false or incomplete information, confabulation, dogma, illusion, hallucinations, or other misleading perceptual effects. Delusions are a symptom of many mental disorders, particularly psychotic disorders such as schizophrenia.

Definition

The DSM-5 defines delusions as "fixed beliefs that are not amenable to change in light of conflicting evidence." Karl Jaspers proposed three criteria: certainty (held with subjective conviction), incorrigibility (not changeable by compelling counterargument or proof), and impossibility or falsity of content. Modern definitions exclude beliefs widely shared within a person's culture or subculture.

Types

Common themes include persecutory delusions (belief that one is being targeted or harmed), grandiose delusions (inflated self-worth or powers), delusions of reference (neutral events carrying personal significance), erotomanic delusions, somatic delusions, and jealous delusions. Delusions are further classed as bizarre or non-bizarre, and as mood-congruent or mood-incongruent in affective disorders.

Causes and treatment

Delusions occur in schizophrenia, delusional disorder, bipolar disorder, severe depression, dementia and delirium, and can be induced by drugs. Treatment addresses the underlying condition, often with antipsychotic medication and psychological therapy; cognitive behavioural therapy for psychosis targets the distress and conviction attached to delusional beliefs.
