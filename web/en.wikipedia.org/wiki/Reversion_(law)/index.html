Reversion (law)

In property law, a reversion is the future interest retained by a grantor after conveying a smaller estate than the grantor owns. When a landowner who holds a fee simple absolute conveys a lesser estate — such as a life estate or a term of years — the interest that remains in the grantor, and that will become possessory when the lesser estate ends, is the reversion.

Characteristics

A reversion arises by operation of law rather than by express reservation: whenever a grantor transfers less than their whole interest without disposing of the remainder, the residue is a reversion. Unlike a remainder, which is created in favour of a third party, a reversion is always retained by the transferor or their heirs. Reversions are transferable, devisable, and descendible, and historically were distinguished from remainders by the rules governing their creation and by the doctrine of seisin.

Examples

If O, owning land in fee simple, grants "to A for life", O retains a reversion in fee simple, which becomes possessory on A's death. Similarly, a landlord who grants a lease holds a reversion (the "leasehold reversion") during the term, entitling the landlord to resume possession at the end of the tenancy and to enforce the lease covenants.
