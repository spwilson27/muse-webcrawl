Forrester, pioneer of real-time computing and founder of system dynamics.

Jay Wright Forrester (July 14, 1918 – November 16, 2016) was an American computer engineer, management scientist, and systems scientist. He spent his career at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where he led the development of the Whirlwind I computer, contributed to the SAGE air-defense system, and is credited with the invention of magnetic-core memory. He later founded the field of system dynamics, applying feedback-control concepts to social, economic, and urban systems.

Early life and education

Forrester was born on a cattle ranch near Anselmo, Nebraska. He studied electrical engineering at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln, graduating in 1939, and went on to MIT, where he earned a master's degree in electrical engineering in 1945 and joined George Valley and others in the Servomechanisms Laboratory. Under Gordon S. Brown he studied feedback control systems, an intellectual thread that ran through all of his later work.

Whirlwind and SAGE

During the Second World War Forrester worked on servomechanisms for radar antennas and gun mounts. In 1944 he became head of the Whirlwind project, originally conceived as a flight simulator for the U.S. Navy, which evolved into one of the first digital computers designed for real-time operation. Whirlwind's demands for fast, reliable memory led Forrester to develop magnetic-core memory, which became the standard form of random-access computer memory for two decades. The Whirlwind technology fed directly into the SAGE air-defense network and into MIT's Lincoln Laboratory.

System dynamics

In 1956 Forrester moved to MIT's Sloan School of Management, where he began building computer models of industrial and social systems. His books Industrial Dynamics (1961), Urban Dynamics (1969), and World Dynamics (1971) applied feedback-loop simulation to supply chains, city growth, and global resource limits. The world model developed by his group underlay the Club of Rome's The Limits to Growth (1972), which brought system dynamics worldwide attention and controversy.

Awards and legacy

Forrester received the IEEE Medal of Honor in 1972 and the National Medal of Technology in 1989, and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame. He founded the System Dynamics Society's predecessor organizations and continued writing and lecturing into his nineties. He died in Concord, Massachusetts, in 2016 at the age of 98.
