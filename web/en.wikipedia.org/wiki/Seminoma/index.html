Seminoma

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Seminoma — a germ cell tumour of the testicle (or, rarely, other sites), highly curable when detected early.

A seminoma is a germ cell tumour of the testicle or, less commonly, of the mediastinum, retroperitoneum, or other extragonadal sites. It is one of the most treatable and curable cancers, with survival rates exceeding 95% in early stages, because it is extremely sensitive to radiation therapy and chemotherapy. Seminomas account for roughly half of all testicular germ cell tumours and typically occur in men between the ages of 25 and 45 — slightly older, on average, than patients with non-seminomatous tumours.

Presentation and diagnosis

The usual presentation is a painless testicular mass noticed by the patient. Diagnosis begins with physical examination and scrotal ultrasound, followed by radical inguinal orchiectomy, which both establishes the histology and serves as primary treatment. Microscopically, seminoma consists of uniform sheets of large clear cells divided by fibrous septa infiltrated with lymphocytes. Serum tumour markers — particularly β-hCG (mildly raised in a minority of cases) and LDH — and cross-sectional imaging of the abdomen and chest are used for staging; AFP should be normal in a pure seminoma.

Treatment

Management depends on stage. Stage I disease is often managed with active surveillance, adjuvant carboplatin, or (historically) radiotherapy to the retroperitoneum. Advanced seminoma is treated with cisplatin-based chemotherapy, such as the BEP regimen, and post-chemotherapy residual masses are generally observed or assessed by PET imaging because they frequently contain only necrotic tissue. The ovarian counterpart of seminoma is the dysgerminoma.
