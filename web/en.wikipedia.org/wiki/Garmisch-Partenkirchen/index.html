Garmisch-Partenkirchen is a ski town in Bavaria, southern Germany, formed by the merger of the two towns of Garmisch and Partenkirchen. It lies near the border with Austria at the foot of the Zugspitze, at 2,962 metres Germany's highest mountain. The merged municipality is the administrative centre of the district of Garmisch-Partenkirchen in Upper Bavaria.

History

Partenkirchen originated as the Roman town of Partanum on the trade route from Venice to Augsburg. Garmisch was first mentioned around the year 800. For centuries the two settlements developed separately on either side of the Partnach River: Partenkirchen to the east and Garmisch to the west.

In 1935, in preparation for the 1936 Winter Olympics, the two towns were merged by order of the Nazi regime over the objections of local residents. The combined town hosted the 1936 Winter Olympic Games, which introduced alpine skiing as an Olympic discipline. The Olympic ski stadium, with its large ski jump, remains a landmark and hosts the annual New Year's ski jumping competition as part of the Four Hills Tournament.

After the Second World War, the town became a major recreation centre for the United States military in Germany, and it hosts institutions such as the George C. Marshall European Center for Security Studies.

Geography and climate

Garmisch-Partenkirchen lies in a broad valley basin of the Loisach River, surrounded by the Ammergau Alps, the Ester Mountains and the Wetterstein range, which includes the Zugspitze and the dramatic Partnach Gorge (Partnachklamm). The climate is relatively wet for its altitude, with cold winters that make it one of Germany's most reliable winter sports areas.

Tourism and sport

The town is among Germany's best-known mountain resorts. Skiing takes place on the Zugspitze glacier and in the Garmisch-Classic area, which includes the Kandahar downhill course, a regular stop on the FIS Alpine Ski World Cup circuit. In summer, the region is popular for hiking, climbing and mountaineering. A cog railway and cable cars carry visitors to the summit of the Zugspitze.

Transport

The town is connected by rail to Munich and to Innsbruck via Mittenwald, and by road to the A95 autobahn from Munich. Local transport includes the Zugspitze cog railway (Zugspitzbahn) operated by Bayerische Zugspitzbahn Bergbahn AG.
