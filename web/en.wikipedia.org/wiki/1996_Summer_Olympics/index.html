1996 Summer Olympics

Muhammad Ali lights the Olympic flame at the opening ceremony in Atlanta.

The 1996 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXVI Olympiad and branded as the Centennial Olympic Games, were held in Atlanta, Georgia, United States, from 19 July to 4 August 1996. Marking the hundredth anniversary of the first modern Olympics in Athens, they featured a record 197 nations and 10,318 athletes competing in 271 events across 26 sports. Atlanta won the right to host in 1990, defeating Athens, which many had expected to be awarded the centenary Games.

Opening ceremony and highlights

The opening ceremony's emotional climax came when a trembling Muhammad Ali, suffering from Parkinson's disease, lit the Olympic cauldron. On the track, Michael Johnson completed an unprecedented 200 m / 400 m double, setting a world record of 19.32 seconds in the 200 m that stood for twelve years, while Carl Lewis won his ninth career gold medal in the long jump. Donovan Bailey won the 100 m in a world record 9.84 seconds, and Marie-José Pérec of France completed the women's sprint double. In gymnastics, the United States women's team — the "Magnificent Seven" — won the country's first team gold, sealed by Kerri Strug's vault on an injured ankle.

Centennial Olympic Park bombing

On 27 July, during the Games, a pipe bomb exploded in Centennial Olympic Park, killing one spectator and injuring 111 others; a cameraman also died of a heart attack. Security guard Richard Jewell, who had spotted the device and helped clear the area, was initially treated as a suspect before being exonerated. The actual bomber, Eric Rudolph, was arrested in 2003 and later pleaded guilty. The Games continued after a brief suspension of festivities.

Medal table and legacy

The United States topped the medal table with 44 gold medals and 101 medals overall, ahead of Russia and Germany. The Atlanta Games were notable as the first Olympics since 1968 staged without government financing, relying instead on sponsorship and television revenue; they were profitable but also criticised for excessive commercialisation. The main stadium was subsequently converted into Turner Field for baseball.
