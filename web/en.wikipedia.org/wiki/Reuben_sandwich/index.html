The Reuben sandwich is a North American grilled sandwich composed of corned beef, Swiss cheese, sauerkraut, and Russian dressing or Thousand Island dressing, grilled between slices of rye bread. It is associated with kosher-style delicatessens but, because it contains both meat and cheese, it is not kosher. The Reuben is one of the most famous sandwiches in American cuisine and is widely regarded as a classic of the delicatessen tradition.

History

Omaha origin

One account holds that the sandwich was created in Omaha, Nebraska, by Reuben Kulakofsky (sometimes rendered Kulakovsky or Kay), a Lithuanian-born Jewish grocer. According to this story, Kulakofsky devised the sandwich during a weekly poker game held at the Blackstone Hotel sometime between 1920 and 1935; the hotel's owner, Charles Schimmel, liked it so much that he put it on the menu, naming it for Kulakofsky. A descendant of Kulakofsky's family has maintained that the original Reuben used a special "Reuben sauce" and was served on pumpernickel or dark rye.

New York origin

A competing claim credits Arnold Reuben, owner of Reuben's Delicatessen in New York City. In this version, dating to about 1914, Reuben created the sandwich for actress Marjorie Rambeau (or, in another telling, for Annette Seelos), who asked for something unusual; the sandwich allegedly combined Virginia ham, turkey, Swiss cheese, coleslaw and Russian dressing, and was called the "Reuben Special." Food historians have noted that the earlier New York sandwich differs substantially from the modern corned-beef Reuben.

Variations

Several regional and thematic variations exist:

  • Rachel sandwich — substitutes pastrami or turkey for the corned beef, and coleslaw for the sauerkraut.
  • Grouper Reuben — a Florida variation using fried grouper.
  • Reuben egg rolls — the sandwich fillings wrapped in an egg-roll wrapper and deep-fried, popularized in the Midwest.
  • Tempeh or vegetarian Reubens — replace the corned beef with tempeh, seitan, or vegetables.

The "Reuben" theme also appears in pizzas, potato skins, and other dishes that combine the signature flavors of corned beef, sauerkraut, Swiss cheese, and dressing.

Cultural significance

The Reuben is a fixture of American delicatessens and diners and has been celebrated in popular culture. March is sometimes promoted by Omaha civic groups as a time to celebrate the sandwich's hometown claim, and the Blackstone Hotel in Omaha—where the poker-game legend is set—remains associated with the sandwich's origin story. The Reuben frequently appears in rankings of America's favorite sandwiches.
