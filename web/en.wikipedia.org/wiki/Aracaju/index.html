Aracaju

Aracaju is the capital and largest city of Sergipe, the smallest Brazilian state, in the country's northeast. Founded in 1855 as a planned capital — one of Brazil's first planned cities, laid out on a grid — it replaced the colonial capital São Cristóvão. The name comes from Tupi ará acaiú, often interpreted as "cashew tree of the parrots." Aracaju has a population of roughly 670,000 and is known in Brazil for its quality of life, its orderly beachfront at Atalaia, and its São João (June festival) celebrations.

History

Provincial president Inácio Joaquim Barbosa ordered the transfer of Sergipe's capital to the port settlement at the mouth of the Sergipe River in 1855, both for economic reasons (a better port for sugar exports) and to strengthen his political position. The city was engineered by Sebastião Basílio Pirro on a rational grid of straight streets — unusual in an era of organically grown colonial towns. Aracaju grew with the sugar trade and, in the 20th century, with oil, potash, and salt industries in the region.

Tourism and culture

The Orla do Atalaia, a roughly 6-kilometre beachfront promenade, is considered one of the most complete in Brazil, with sports courts, lakes, a kart track, craft fairs, and the Tamar Project oceanarium (Projeto Tamar), where sea turtles are studied and displayed. The city's giant crab sculpture on the Passarela do Caranguejo honours its beloved crab-eating culture. In June, the Forró Caju festival draws hundreds of thousands for forró music and quadrilha dancing, anchoring Sergipe's claim (with neighbouring towns) to be the "country of forró." Excursions run to the canyons of the São Francisco River at Canindé de São Francisco (Xingó Canyon) and to the colonial towns of São Cristóvão and Laranjeiras.
