The Northern District (Hebrew: מחוז הצפון, Mehoz HaTzafon; Arabic: منطقة الشمال, Minṭaqat ash-Shamāl) is one of Israel's six administrative districts. It covers the Galilee, the Jezreel and Beit She'an valleys, the Sea of Galilee basin, and — in Israeli administration — the Golan Heights, captured from Syria in 1967 and annexed in 1981, a move not recognized internationally.

Geography

The district is Israel's greenest and most rural: the Galilee's hills, Mount Meron (the highest point in Israel within the pre-1967 lines), the Hula Valley, and the Jordan River headwaters at Mount Hermon. Major cities include Nazareth, Safed (Tzfat), Tiberias, Acre (Akko), Afula, Nahariya, Carmiel and Kiryat Shmona. The district seat is Nof HaGalil (formerly Nazareth Illit).

Demographics

The Northern District is the only district in Israel where Arab citizens form a majority or near-majority of the population (roughly 54% Arab, including Druze and Circassian communities, alongside Jewish towns, kibbutzim and moshavim). Nazareth is Israel's largest Arab city and an important Christian pilgrimage centre; Safed is one of the four holy cities of Judaism and a centre of Kabbalah.

Administration

Districts in Israel are administrative arms of the Ministry of the Interior rather than elected regional governments; the district is divided into sub-districts (Safed, Kinneret, Jezreel, Akko and Golan) and dozens of municipalities, local councils and regional councils.
