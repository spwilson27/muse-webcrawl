Clathrate gun hypothesis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The clathrate gun hypothesis proposes that rises in sea temperature can trigger the abrupt release of methane from methane clathrate (hydrate) deposits under the ocean floor and in permafrost. Because methane is a potent greenhouse gas, such releases could cause rapid further warming — a positive feedback compared to firing a gun: once triggered, the process cannot be stopped.

Methane escaping from degrading subsea hydrates has been proposed as a driver of abrupt past warming.

Origins

The idea grew from work in the 1980s–2000s on ice-core records: James Kennett and colleagues proposed that destabilisation of marine methane hydrates explained the rapid warmings that punctuated the last glacial period, including Dansgaard–Oeschger events. The hypothesis was also invoked for the Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum (about 56 million years ago) and, more speculatively, for end-Permian mass extinction scenarios.

Mechanism

Methane hydrates are stable only within a zone of low temperature and high pressure. Warming ocean water can thin this stability zone from above; slope failures could expose hydrates and release methane suddenly. Much methane released in deep water is oxidised to CO2 by microbes before reaching the atmosphere, which limits — but does not eliminate — the effect, and oxidation also contributes to ocean acidification and deoxygenation.

Modern assessments

The IPCC Sixth Assessment Report concludes that a catastrophic clathrate-gun release is very unlikely this century: most assessments project only gradual hydrate responses to warming over centuries to millennia, and present-day methane emissions from hydrates are estimated to be small compared with other sources. Research focuses instead on vulnerable shallow Arctic deposits, such as those beneath the East Siberian Arctic Shelf, where observations of methane ebullition remain debated. Permafrost carbon feedback more broadly is regarded as a significant but slower amplifier of climate change.
