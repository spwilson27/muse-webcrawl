High Arctic relocation

The High Arctic relocation (French: La délocalisation du Haut-Arctique) took place during the Cold War, when the Government of Canada relocated 92 Inuit from Nunavik (northern Quebec) and Pond Inlet to the High Arctic in 1953 and 1955. The relocatees founded the communities of Resolute Bay and Grise Fiord on Cornwallis and Ellesmere Islands.

Background

The federal government gave several reasons for the move: reducing welfare dependence and over-hunting around Inukjuak, returning Inuit to a traditional hunting lifestyle, and — controversially — asserting Canadian sovereignty in the High Arctic during the Cold War. Families were promised abundant game and the option to return after two years; the promised return was not honoured, and families separated between the two settlements were unable to reunite for years.

Hardship and redress

The relocatees endured extreme cold and darkness, unfamiliar game, and inadequate supplies, scavenging at the Resolute airbase dump. A 1990s Royal Commission on Aboriginal Peoples report and subsequent inquiries criticised the relocation. The federal government created a $10 million trust fund in 1996, and in August 2010 the Minister of Indian Affairs formally apologised to the relocatees on behalf of the Government of Canada.
