Antiplatelet drug

Antiplatelet drugs decrease platelet aggregation and inhibit thrombus formation, mainly in the arterial circulation.

An antiplatelet drug (antiaggregant), also known as a platelet agglutination inhibitor or platelet aggregation inhibitor, is a member of a class of pharmaceuticals that decrease platelet aggregation and inhibit thrombus formation. They are effective in the arterial circulation, where anticoagulants have relatively less effect, and are widely used in the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease — myocardial infarction, stroke and stent thrombosis.

Classes

  • Irreversible cyclooxygenase (COX-1) inhibitors — aspirin, which blocks production of thromboxane A2 for the life of the platelet.
  • P2Y12 receptor blockers — thienopyridines such as clopidogrel, prasugrel and ticlopidine, and the reversible agent ticagrelor; these block the ADP receptor on the platelet surface.
  • Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors — abciximab, eptifibatide and tirofiban, used intravenously in acute coronary syndromes and during coronary intervention.
  • Phosphodiesterase inhibitors — dipyridamole and cilostazol, which raise platelet cAMP.
  • Thrombin receptor (PAR-1) antagonists — vorapaxar.

Uses

Low-dose aspirin is used long-term after myocardial infarction and ischaemic stroke. Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT), usually aspirin combined with a P2Y12 blocker, is standard after coronary stent implantation and in acute coronary syndromes to prevent stent thrombosis. Antiplatelet agents are distinguished from anticoagulants such as warfarin and heparin, which act on the coagulation cascade and are preferred for venous thromboembolism and atrial fibrillation.

Adverse effects

The principal risk is bleeding, including gastrointestinal haemorrhage (a particular concern with aspirin, which also damages the gastric mucosa) and intracranial bleeding. Thienopyridines can rarely cause thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, and some patients exhibit "clopidogrel resistance" owing to CYP2C19 genetic variants that impair activation of the prodrug.
