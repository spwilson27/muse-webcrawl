Edmund John Millington Synge (16 April 1871 – 24 March 1909) was an Irish playwright, poet, writer, and collector of folklore. A key figure in the Irish Literary Revival, he was a co-founder, with W. B. Yeats and Lady Gregory, of the Abbey Theatre. He is best known for the play The Playboy of the Western World, whose reception provoked riots at the Abbey in 1907.

Life

Synge was born in Rathfarnham, near Dublin, into a wealthy Anglo-Irish Protestant family. He studied at Trinity College Dublin and the Royal Irish Academy of Music, training as a musician before turning to literature. In Paris in 1896 he met W. B. Yeats, who famously urged him to go to the Aran Islands and "express a life that has never found expression." Synge spent parts of five summers on the islands, learning Irish and absorbing the speech, stories, and way of life that supplied the material and language of his plays. His prose account, The Aran Islands, was published in 1907.

Plays

Synge's one-act tragedy Riders to the Sea (1904), about an Aran island family destroyed by drowning, is often called the greatest one-act play in English. His comedies In the Shadow of the Glen (1903) and The Well of the Saints (1905) used a stylized Hiberno-English dialogue drawn from the speech of the western peasantry. The Playboy of the Western World (1907), in which a young man is lionized by a Mayo village for claiming to have killed his father, was denounced as a slander on Irish womanhood and sparked disturbances — the "Playboy riots." His last play, Deirdre of the Sorrows, was left unfinished at his death and completed by Yeats and Synge's fiancée, Molly Allgood (the actress Maire O'Neill).

Death and legacy

Synge suffered from Hodgkin's disease and died in Dublin on 24 March 1909, aged 37. Though his career lasted barely a decade, his six plays transformed Irish drama, and his distinctive dramatic language influenced generations of playwrights. Yeats's poem "In Memory of Major Robert Gregory" and the elegy "On Mr. Synge" commemorate him, and the annual Synge Summer School in County Wicklow is named in his honor.
