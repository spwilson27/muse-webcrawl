Summer squash
SpeciesMainly Cucurbita pepo
Common typesZucchini, yellow squash, pattypan, crookneck
HarvestedImmature, while rind is soft and seeds undeveloped

Summer squash is a group of squashes harvested while immature, while the rind is still tender and edible — in contrast to winter squash, which is harvested mature with a hard rind. Most summer squashes are varieties of Cucurbita pepo, native to the Americas, and include zucchini (courgette), yellow squash, crookneck squash, and pattypan (scallop) squash.

Cultivation and use

Summer squash plants are typically bushy rather than vining and produce fruit prolifically through the warm season; fruits are picked young, often within days of flowering, because they grow rapidly and become seedy and coarse if left. The blossoms are also eaten, stuffed or battered and fried in Mexican and Italian cuisines.

Nutrition and cooking

Summer squash is low in calories, high in water content, and a source of vitamin C, vitamin B6, and manganese. It is eaten raw in salads or cooked by grilling, sautéing, roasting, or baking, and is a staple of dishes such as ratatouille.
