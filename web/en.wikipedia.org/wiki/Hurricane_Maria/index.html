Hurricane Maria was a devastating Category 5 hurricane that struck the northeastern Caribbean in September 2017, most catastrophically Dominica and Puerto Rico. It is regarded as the worst natural disaster in Puerto Rico's recorded history and the deadliest Atlantic hurricane since Jeanne in 2004.

Impact on Puerto Rico

Maria made landfall near Yabucoa on 20 September 2017 as a high-end Category 4, knocking out the island's entire electrical grid and causing flooding and destruction across the territory. The power restoration effort was the largest in US history. An independent study by George Washington University estimated 2,975 excess deaths in Puerto Rico in the six months following the storm, a figure the government eventually adopted.

Aftermath

The federal response drew sustained criticism, and the disaster accelerated out-migration from Puerto Rico to the US mainland. The name Maria was retired and replaced with Margot.
