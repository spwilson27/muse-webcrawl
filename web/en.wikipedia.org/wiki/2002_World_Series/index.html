The 2002 World Series was the championship series of Major League Baseball's 2002 season, the 98th edition of the World Series. The American League champion Anaheim Angels defeated the National League champion San Francisco Giants in seven games to win the first championship in the Angels' 42-year history. It was the first World Series contested entirely in California since 1989, and the first in which both teams were wild-card entries.

Series summary

The series was an offensive showcase: the two teams combined for a record 85 runs. Barry Bonds, playing in his only World Series, hit .471 with four home runs. The turning point came in Game 6, when the Giants led 5–0 in the seventh inning and stood eight outs from the title; the Angels rallied for three runs in the seventh and three more in the eighth — spurred on by the crowd's "Rally Monkey" — to win 6–5.

Game 7

In the deciding game, rookie John Lackey pitched five strong innings and Garret Anderson's three-run double in the third inning proved decisive in a 4–1 Angels victory. Third baseman Troy Glaus was named Series MVP after hitting .385 with three home runs.

Legacy

The championship was the Angels' only World Series appearance and title to date. Manager Mike Scioscia's team became the first wild-card club from the American League to win the Series.
