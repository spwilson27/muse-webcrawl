Vatnajökull

Vatnajökull (Icelandic pronunciation: [ˈvahtnaˌjœːkʏtl̥], "Water Glacier"), also known as the Vatna Glacier, is the largest ice cap in Iceland and one of the largest in Europe by volume. It covers about 7,700 square kilometres — roughly 8% of Iceland — in the southeast of the country, and its ice averages about 380 metres thick, reaching nearly 1,000 metres at its thickest.

Geology and volcanoes

Beneath the ice lie several active volcanic systems, including Grímsvötn, Bárðarbunga, and Öræfajökull, whose summit Hvannadalshnjúkur is Iceland's highest peak at 2,110 metres. Subglacial eruptions can melt vast quantities of ice and release catastrophic glacial outburst floods called jökulhlaups, as in the 1996 Gjálp eruption, which flooded the Skeiðarársandur plain and damaged the ring road.

Setting

The ice cap is the centrepiece of Vatnajökull National Park, established in 2008 and later expanded to become one of Europe's largest national parks, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2019. Numerous outlet glaciers, including Breiðamerkurjökull above the Jökulsárlón lagoon, descend from the cap. Like most Icelandic glaciers, Vatnajökull is retreating rapidly with climate warming.
