Prince Henry the Navigator

Prince Henry the Navigator (Portuguese: Infante Dom Henrique, 1394–1460) was a Portuguese prince whose patronage of voyages along the west coast of Africa in the 15th century is regarded as central to the beginning of the European Age of Discovery.

Life and voyages

A son of King John I, Henry was Grand Master of the Order of Christ, whose funds supported expeditions. From Sagres and Lagos his sailors progressively rounded Cape Bojador (1434), reached Cape Verde, Sierra Leone and the Gulf of Guinea, and colonised Madeira and the Azores. The voyages sought a sea route to India, gold, and the legendary Christian kingdom of Prester John.

Legacy

Although Henry never sailed on the expeditions himself, later historians styled him "the Navigator". The so-called School of Sagres is now considered partly legendary, but his funding model and cartographic enterprise shaped Portuguese expansion.
