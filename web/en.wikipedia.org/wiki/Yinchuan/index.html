Yinchuan (Chinese: 银川, "silver river") is the capital of the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region in north-central China. Situated on the fertile Ningxia plain west of the Yellow River and east of the Helan Mountains, it has historically been a frontier city on routes between China proper, Mongolia, and the Hexi Corridor.

History

The area was long contested between Chinese dynasties and steppe peoples. In the 11th century, Yinchuan became Zhongxing, the capital of the Tangut-led Western Xia dynasty, whose imperial tombs — a group of large earthen pyramids — still stand at the foot of the Helan Mountains west of the city. The Mongols destroyed Western Xia in 1227. Under the Ming and Qing the city was a garrison town; in 1958 it became the capital of the newly created Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region.

Geography and economy

Yinchuan has a continental steppe climate with cold, dry winters and hot summers. Although arid, the surrounding plain is irrigated by Yellow River water through a canal system dating back two millennia, making Ningxia a noted producer of rice, goji berries (wolfberries), and wine grapes — the Helan Mountain foothills are one of China's most acclaimed wine regions. The city's economy also includes energy and chemicals, textiles (cashmere), and tourism centred on the Western Xia tombs, the Helan Mountain rock art, and the Sand Lake and Shapotou desert attractions nearby.

Culture

Roughly a quarter to a third of the region's population is Hui, and Yinchuan's culture blends Han Chinese and Hui Muslim traditions, visible in its mosques, halal cuisine, and the Nanguan Mosque. The city hosts the China–Arab States Expo, reflecting Ningxia's role in Belt and Road trade outreach.
