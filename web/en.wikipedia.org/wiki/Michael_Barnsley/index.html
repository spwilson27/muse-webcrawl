Michael Fielding Barnsley (born 1946) is a British mathematician, researcher and entrepreneur known for his work in fractal geometry, in particular iterated function systems (IFS) and fractal image compression. His book Fractals Everywhere (1988) was a landmark popularisation of the subject.

Work

Barnsley received his doctorate in theoretical chemistry from the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 1972 and taught in chemistry before turning to mathematics. At the Georgia Institute of Technology he developed the theory of iterated function systems as a unifying language for fractals, showing how complex natural-looking images could be generated from a handful of affine transformations via the collage theorem. The "Barnsley fern", generated by four simple maps, became one of the most famous examples of a mathematically generated natural form.

In 1987 he founded Iterated Systems Incorporated to commercialise fractal image compression, a technology that encodes images as IFS transformations; he holds numerous patents in the field. He later joined the Australian National University in Canberra, where he continued research on fractals, super-fractals and their applications.
