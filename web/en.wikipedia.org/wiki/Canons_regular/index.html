Canons regular

Canons regular are priests (and in some cases lay religious) living in community under a rule of life (regula in Latin) and generally vowed to public celebration of the liturgy of the hours and the pastoral care of souls. They are distinguished from secular canons, who do not take religious vows or live under a rule, and from monks, whose lives are primarily contemplative.

History

The canonical life developed from communities of clergy attached to cathedrals and collegiate churches. The reforming councils of the 11th century, notably the Lateran synod of 1059, encouraged canons to adopt a common life modelled on the early Church. Most orders of canons regular follow the Rule of Saint Augustine, a brief rule attributed to St Augustine of Hippo.

Orders

Major orders and congregations of canons regular include:

  • The Canons Regular of St. Augustine (Augustinians), a confederation of congregations including the Lateran canons.
  • The Premonstratensians (Norbertines), founded by St Norbert of Xanten in 1120 at Prémontré.
  • The Canons Regular of the Holy Cross (Crosiers).
  • The Canons Regular of Prémontré and numerous Augustinian congregations of canons and canonesses.

Unlike monks, canons regular have traditionally engaged in parish ministry, education and the care of pilgrims, combining the common life with active pastoral work. Canonesses regular, female counterparts living under the same rules, founded many schools and hospitals in medieval Europe.
