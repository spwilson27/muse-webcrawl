Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari Province

Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari Province (Persian: استان چهارمحال و بختیاری, Ostān-e Chahārmaḥāl-o Bakhtīārī) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran. Its capital is the city of Shahr-e Kord. Located in the southwestern part of the country, in the central Zagros Mountains, it covers an area of about 16,332 square kilometres and had a population of roughly 950,000 at the 2016 census. The province is known for its high elevation — it is sometimes called the "roof of Iran" — and as a principal homeland of the Bakhtiari people.

Geography

The province lies in the Zagros range, with peaks exceeding 4,000 metres, including Zard Kuh (4,221 m), the source of both the Karun and Zayanderud rivers. The Karun, Iran's largest river by discharge, and the Zayanderud, which flows to Isfahan, both rise in the province, making it one of Iran's most important water-producing regions. Its high altitude gives it a cold mountain climate with heavy winter snow, and it contains notable wetlands such as Choghakhor and Gandoman.

People and culture

The name combines Chaharmahal ("four districts") — the Chaharmahal region around Shahr-e Kord — with Bakhtiari, the territory of the Bakhtiari tribe, a Lurish-speaking people historically known for seasonal nomadic pastoralism (transhumance) between summer quarters (sardsir) in this province and winter quarters in Khuzestan. The Bakhtiari seasonal migration, undertaken with flocks over high mountain passes, is one of the great traditional migrations of the Middle East and has been the subject of famous documentaries such as Grass (1925). The province is also noted for its handicrafts, including felt-making, carpets, and the traditional chogha cloak worn by Bakhtiari men.

Administrative divisions

The province comprises ten counties, including Shahr-e Kord, Borujen, Farsan, Lordegan, Ardal, Kuhrang, Kiar, Ben, Saman, and Khanmirza. Major towns include Shahr-e Kord, Borujen, Farsan, and Lordegan.

Economy

Agriculture, animal husbandry, and trout farming are major activities; the province is an important producer of almonds and of cold-water fish. Dams on the Karun river system, several of them among Iran's largest hydroelectric projects, lie along its borders.
