Stockfish (chess)

Stockfish is a free and open-source chess engine, widely considered the strongest conventional chess engine in the world. It is available for multiple platforms and communicates via the Universal Chess Interface (UCI) protocol, allowing it to be used with most chess graphical interfaces. Stockfish has won numerous editions of the Top Chess Engine Championship (TCEC) and the computer chess championships held on Chess.com, typically defeating its principal rival Leela Chess Zero in superfinals.

History

Stockfish began as a fork of the open-source engine Glaurung, by Tord Romstad, released in 2008; the name comes from Norwegian: stockfish is dried cod, a nod to the Norwegian and Italian (Marco Costalba) origins of its early authors. Joona Kiiski became a principal developer. Unusually, Stockfish is developed through the distributed "Fishtest" framework, in which volunteers donate computing time to statistically test proposed code changes; only changes that demonstrate improvement across thousands of test games are merged.

Technology

Stockfish uses alpha–beta search with extensive pruning and extensions, bitboard board representation, and a handcrafted evaluation historically built from piece-square tables and positional features. In 2020 the project adopted NNUE (Efficiently Updatable Neural Networks) — small neural networks, originally developed for shogi, that can be evaluated incrementally during search — which brought a large gain in playing strength while retaining the engine's speed. Stockfish routinely searches tens of millions of positions per second on multi-core hardware and is typically rated well above 3500 Elo on computer rating lists, far beyond any human player.

Uses

Because it is free and open source (GPL-licensed), Stockfish powers the analysis features of major sites such as Lichess and is embedded in countless apps and electronic boards. It is used by players of all levels for game analysis and opening preparation, and its opening books and endgame evaluations have influenced modern chess understanding.
