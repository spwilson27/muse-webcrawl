A science fiction convention (or con) is a gathering of fans of science fiction and fantasy, typically organised by fans on a non-profit basis. Conventions range from small local events of a few hundred attendees to gatherings of tens of thousands, and feature programme panels, guest-of-honour talks, a dealers' room, art show, filking, gaming and a costume masquerade.

History

Fans met at the first World Science Fiction Convention (Worldcon) in New York in 1939, held alongside the World's Fair. Worldcon became an annual, travelling event and the home of the Hugo Awards, voted by its members. Regional conventions spread across the United States and internationally after the Second World War, developing the traditions of fanzines, fan funds and costuming.

Types

Traditional literary conventions emphasise books and author panels; media conventions focus on film and television guests; and large commercial events such as San Diego Comic-Con draw over a hundred thousand attendees around popular culture generally. Specialised cons cover anime, gaming, furry fandom and individual franchises such as Star Trek.

Culture

Conventions developed a distinctive participatory culture — attendees are "members" rather than spectators — with institutions including room parties, hall costumes, gripe sessions and the ceremonial awarding of the Hugos and other fan-voted honours.
