Grumman AA-1

The Grumman American AA-1 series is a family of American two-seat, single-engine, low-wing light aircraft. The original design, the BD-1, was created by Jim Bede; it was subsequently developed by Grumman American Aviation as the AA-1 Yankee (later Yankee Clipper), first flown in the late 1960s.

The AA-1 is notable for its bonded aluminium honeycomb construction, sliding canopy and sporty handling derived from its fighter-like design heritage. Later variants include the AA-1A Trainer, the AA-1B Trainer/Tr-2, and the AA-1C Lynx and T-Cat, with progressively more powerful Lycoming engines. Production ran from the late 1960s until the late 1970s, with later manufacture by Gulfstream American.

The type developed a reputation for demanding stall and spin characteristics, leading to placards and, in some markets, restrictions on intentional spinning.
