Connacht Rugby is one of the four professional provincial rugby union teams in Ireland, representing the western province of Connacht and governed by the Connacht Branch of the IRFU, founded in 1885. The team is based in Galway and plays at the Sportsground (Dexcom Stadium). Connacht competes in the United Rugby Championship and in European competition (the Champions Cup and Challenge Cup).

History

Historically the weakest-resourced of the Irish provinces, Connacht was threatened with disbandment as a professional entity in 2003, a proposal reversed after protests. Long regarded as a development province, the team transformed under coach Pat Lam, culminating in the fairytale 2015–16 season: Connacht finished the Pro12 regular season near the top and defeated Glasgow and Leinster in the play-offs, beating Leinster 20–10 in the final at Murrayfield to win their first major trophy. The province's supporters, known as the Connacht Clan, adopt the motto of the region's fighting tradition; the team's crest features the eagle-and-sword arms of the province.

Identity

Connacht's catchment covers counties Galway, Mayo, Sligo, Leitrim, and Roscommon. Development structures include an academy, sub-academies, and links to clubs and schools across the province, as well as the domestic club scene in the Connacht leagues.
