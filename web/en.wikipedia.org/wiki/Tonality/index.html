Tonality

Tonality is the arrangement of pitches and/or chords of a musical work in a hierarchy of perceived relations, stabilities, attractions, and directionality. In this hierarchy, the single pitch or triad with the greatest stability is the tonic.

Characteristics

Tonal music is built on major and minor scales, functional harmony, and cadences that resolve tension toward the tonic. Key signatures and modulation — temporary shifts of tonic — structure much Western music from about 1600 to 1900, the "common practice period."

History

Tonality crystallized in the Baroque era from earlier modal practice; theorists such as Jean-Philippe Rameau codified its chord progressions in the 18th century. In the late 19th century, composers like Wagner stretched tonal syntax to its limits, and in the early 20th century Schoenberg and others embraced atonality as a reaction.

Other systems

The term is sometimes extended to any system of pitch organization with a central reference tone, including some non-Western modal traditions.
