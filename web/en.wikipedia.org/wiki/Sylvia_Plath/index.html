Sylvia Plath

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A poet's desk in Devon in winter: a typewriter, jars of preserved specimens in Ariel's manuscript pages, snow against the window]

Sylvia Plath (27 October 1932 – 11 February 1963) was an American poet, novelist and short-story writer. She is credited with advancing the genre of confessional poetry and is best known for two of her published collections, The Colossus and Other Poems (1960) and Ariel (1965), and also The Bell Jar, a semi-autobiographical novel published shortly before her death in 1963.

A brilliant Smith College student whose first suicide attempt at 20 became the basis of The Bell Jar, Plath married the poet Ted Hughes in 1956. In the last months of her life, separated and alone with two small children in a freezing London winter, she wrote the incandescent poems of Ariel — 'Daddy', 'Lady Lazarus', 'Ariel' — at dawn before the children woke. She killed herself at 30; the posthumous Collected Poems won the Pulitzer Prize in 1982.
