DR-DOS (in the context of CP)

DR-DOS is an operating system in the lineage of CP/M, developed by Digital Research (DRI). As an operating system in the CP family — descending from Gary Kildall's CP/M for 8-bit microcomputers — DR-DOS carried the CP/M tradition into the IBM PC world, where it competed directly with Microsoft's MS-DOS.

History

After CP/M-86 lost the IBM PC operating-system contest to PC DOS, Digital Research developed Concurrent DOS and DOS Plus before releasing DR DOS 3.31 in 1988, a fully MS-DOS-compatible single-user system. Version 5.0 (1990) added the ViewMAX graphical file manager and disk compression, and DR DOS 6.0 (1991) competed feature-for-feature with MS-DOS 5.0, including task switching via TaskMAX. Microsoft responded with MS-DOS 6.0, and DR DOS lost market share amid controversy over compatibility checks in Windows betas (the "AARD code").

Novell acquired Digital Research in 1991 and renamed the product Novell DOS 7. The line later passed to Caldera, which released the source of some components (OpenDOS), and then to Lineo and DeviceLogics. A notable legal outcome: Caldera's antitrust suit against Microsoft over DR-DOS was settled in 2000 for an estimated $280 million.

Legacy

DR-DOS survives in embedded systems and in the FreeDOS-era ecosystem as a historically important descendant of CP/M. The Enhanced DR-DOS project continued development of the kernel as an open-source continuation.
