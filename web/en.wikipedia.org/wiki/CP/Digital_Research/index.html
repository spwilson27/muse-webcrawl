Digital Research, Inc. (DRI) was an American software company founded by Gary Kildall in 1974 to market his CP/M operating system. In the late 1970s CP/M became the dominant operating system for microcomputers, and Digital Research was for a time the leading microcomputer software company in the world.

Digital Research was headquartered in Pacific Grove and later Monterey, California.

History

Kildall had written CP/M in 1974 for Intel's development systems and founded DRI (originally Intergalactic Digital Research) with his wife Dorothy to license it. The company grew with the S-100 bus microcomputer market, adding MP/M (a multi-user CP/M), CP/NET, compilers including PL/I, and later CP/M-86 for 16-bit machines.

DRI's fortunes turned when IBM chose Microsoft's system for the IBM PC in 1981 — the famous episode in which IBM's negotiators failed to conclude a licensing deal with DRI (the details remain disputed). DRI later produced DR-DOS, a well-regarded MS-DOS competitor, and the GEM graphical environment. Novell acquired Digital Research in 1991, and its assets passed to Caldera in 1996; Caldera subsequently released the CP/M source materials for hobbyist use.
