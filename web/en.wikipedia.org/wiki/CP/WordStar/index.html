WordStar on CP/M

WordStar, released by MicroPro International in 1978, was the dominant word processor of the CP/M era and one of the first commercially successful application programs for microcomputers. Written originally for the Intel 8080 under CP/M, it later became closely associated with the Zilog Z80 machines that formed the bulk of the CP/M installed base.

History

WordStar grew out of MicroPro's earlier WordMaster editor and SuperSort utility. Rob Barnaby, its principal author, implemented it in assembly language. Because CP/M terminals varied widely, WordStar shipped with an extensive installation program that patched the code for dozens of different terminals and printers — an unusual degree of hardware independence for the time.

Interface

WordStar is remembered for its "diamond" cursor-key scheme, in which Ctrl-E/S/D/X moved the cursor up, left, right, and down, and for its system of prefix menus (Ctrl-K for block and file operations, Ctrl-Q for quick movements, Ctrl-P for print controls). Dot commands embedded in the text controlled formatting. The keyboard conventions were so widely learned that many later editors offered WordStar-compatible modes, and the interface remains available in modern descendants and clones.

Decline

Although ported to MS-DOS in 1982, WordStar adapted slowly to changing expectations; competitors such as WordPerfect overtook it in the later 1980s. It remains an emblem of the CP/M period described in the history of operating systems.
