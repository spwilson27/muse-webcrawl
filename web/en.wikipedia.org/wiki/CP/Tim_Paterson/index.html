Tim Paterson (in the context of CP)

Born: 1 June 1956
Occupation: Computer programmer
Known for: Author of 86-DOS (QDOS)

Tim Paterson (born 1 June 1956) is an American computer programmer best known as the author of 86-DOS, the operating system that Microsoft bought and adapted into MS-DOS and IBM PC DOS. His work stands in direct relation to CP/M: 86-DOS was written with an application programming interface closely modeled on Digital Research's CP/M so that software could be ported easily from CP/M to the new 16-bit world.

86-DOS and CP/M

While employed at Seattle Computer Products (SCP), Paterson developed the 8086 processor card for the S-100 bus. Because Digital Research's CP/M-86 was delayed, Paterson wrote his own operating system in 1980, in about six weeks, calling it QDOS ("Quick and Dirty Operating System"), soon renamed 86-DOS. He deliberately made its API resemble CP/M's — including system calls and program structure — so that existing CP/M applications could be translated mechanically, though the underlying code and file system (FAT, which he designed) were new.

Microsoft licensed 86-DOS in December 1980 and bought it outright in July 1981 for $50,000, providing it to IBM as PC DOS. Paterson later worked at Microsoft (1981–82 and again in the late 1980s/1990s) and ran his own company, Falcon Technology (Paterson Technology). The resemblance between 86-DOS and CP/M became a long-running subject of debate and litigation-adjacent controversy, including claims about Gary Kildall's role and later source-code comparisons.
