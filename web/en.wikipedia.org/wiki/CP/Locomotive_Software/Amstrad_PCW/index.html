Amstrad PCW

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Amstrad PCW series is a range of personal computers sold by British company Amstrad between 1985 and 1998. "PCW" stands for Personal Computer Word-processor, and the machines were marketed primarily as dedicated word processors: each system was sold as a complete package including a monitor, keyboard, printer, and the LocoScript word-processing software written by Locomotive Software.

Models

The first models, the PCW8256 and PCW8512, launched in 1985 at prices far below rival systems. They were based on the Zilog Z80 processor, ran CP/M Plus, and used 3-inch floppy disks. Later models included the PCW9512 (1987) with a daisywheel printer, the PcW9256 and PcW10, and the PcW16 of 1996, which used a custom graphical operating system called Rosanne. Total sales of the series exceeded several million units, and in Britain the PCW was for a time the most common machine used for word processing in homes and small businesses.

Software

LocoScript was bundled with every machine and was later expanded with companions such as LocoMail, LocoFile, and LocoSpell. Because the machines also ran CP/M, a sizeable library of business software was available, including ports of WordStar and dBase, Supercalc, and Mallard BASIC.

Legacy

The PCW introduced many first-time users — including journalists, academics, and writers — to word processing, and remained in use in some offices well into the 2000s.
