CP/Amstrad CPC

Developer: Digital Research (CP/M); Locomotive Software (bundled implementation)
Platform: Amstrad CPC, PCW and ZX Spectrum +3
Bundled versions: CP/M 2.2 (CPC 464/664), CP/M Plus (CPC 6128, PCW)
Licence: Proprietary

CP/M on the Amstrad CPC refers to the versions of Digital Research's CP/M operating system supplied with Amstrad's CPC and PCW home and small-business computers during the 1980s. Because the CPC's native operating environment was Locomotive BASIC in ROM, CP/M was supplied on disc and widened the machine's appeal by giving access to a large library of business software.

Versions supplied

The Amstrad CPC 464 with the DDI-1 disc interface, and later the CPC 664, were supplied with CP/M 2.2. The CPC 6128 and the PCW 8256/8512 word processors shipped with CP/M Plus (CP/M 3.0), which supported the machines' banked memory of 128 KB (or 256/512 KB on the PCW). Amstrad's implementations were produced in cooperation with Locomotive Software, authors of the CPC's BASIC. CP/M Plus on the 6128 supported a 61 KB transient program area and the GSX graphics extension.

Software

Software available under CP/M on the Amstrad machines included WordStar, dBase II, SuperCalc, Multiplan, Protext, and the languages Turbo Pascal, C and Mallard BASIC (the latter bundled with the PCW). The PCW series, marketed as dedicated word processors running LocoScript, could also boot CP/M Plus, which was a significant part of their popularity with small businesses and writers in the United Kingdom. On the CPC, CP/M discs used Amstrad's own disc formats, and utilities such as FORMAT, DISCKIT and PIP were provided on the system discs.

Legacy

The inclusion of CP/M helped sell the disc-based CPC and PCW models into offices and homes, and the PCW became one of the best-selling dedicated word processors in Britain. Emulators and archives preserve the Amstrad CP/M system discs and software library.
