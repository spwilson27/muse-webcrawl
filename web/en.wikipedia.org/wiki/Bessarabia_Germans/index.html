Bessarabia Germans

The Bessarabia Germans (German: Bessarabiendeutsche) were an ethnic German group who lived in Bessarabia, a region between the Dniester and Prut rivers (today largely in Moldova and partly in Ukraine), from 1814 until their resettlement in 1940.

History

Following the Russo-Turkish War and the Treaty of Bucharest (1812), the Russian Empire acquired Bessarabia from the Ottoman vassal of Moldavia. Tsar Alexander I invited German colonists, mainly from the Duchy of Warsaw and southwestern Germany, to settle the steppe lands, granting them land, tax privileges and self-administration. They founded some 150 villages and developed prosperous farming communities, growing wheat, maize and wine grapes.

Resettlement and aftermath

After the Soviet occupation of Bessarabia in 1940, under the terms of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, nearly all of the roughly 93,000 Bessarabia Germans were resettled to the German Reich under the slogan Heim ins Reich ("Home into the Reich"), and most were settled in occupied Poland. After 1945 many fled or were expelled westward. Their descendants maintain cultural associations in Germany preserving the group's dialect, dress and traditions.
