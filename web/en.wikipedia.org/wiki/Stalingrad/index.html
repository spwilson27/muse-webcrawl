Stalingrad

Volgograd (Stalingrad) Country: Russia
Federal subject: Volgograd Oblast
Former names: Tsaritsyn (1589–1925), Stalingrad (1925–1961)
Population: ~1 million
Location: West bank of the Volga River

Stalingrad is the former name (1925–1961) of the Russian city of Volgograd, on the west bank of the Volga River. Founded in 1589 as the fortress of Tsaritsyn, it was renamed in honour of Joseph Stalin during his rule and renamed Volgograd ("Volga city") in 1961 as part of de-Stalinization. The name Stalingrad remains famous worldwide as the site of the decisive Second World War battle.

The Motherland Calls (1967) on Mamayev Kurgan, the battle's memorial height

Battle of Stalingrad

From August 1942 to February 1943, Nazi Germany and its allies fought the Soviet Union for control of the city in the war's largest and bloodiest battle. The Soviet counteroffensive, Operation Uranus, encircled the German Sixth Army, which surrendered on 2 February 1943. With total casualties estimated near two million, the battle marked the turning point of the war on the Eastern Front.

The city today

Rebuilt after near-total destruction, Volgograd is a major industrial and transport centre. The city is officially designated a Hero City. Since 2013, on certain anniversaries the name "Hero City Stalingrad" is used ceremonially. Mamayev Kurgan, crowned by the 85-metre The Motherland Calls statue, commemorates the battle.
