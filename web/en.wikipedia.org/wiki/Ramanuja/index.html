Ramanuja (c. 1017–1137 CE), also known as Ramanujacharya, was a Hindu theologian, philosopher, and social reformer, and the most important exponent of the Vishishtadvaita ("qualified non-dualism") school of Vedanta. He systematised the devotional theology of the Tamil Alvar saints and gave the Sri Vaishnava tradition its classical philosophical foundation.

Life

Born in Sriperumbudur near modern Chennai, Ramanuja studied in Kanchipuram under the Advaita teacher Yadavaprakasha, with whom he clashed over the interpretation of the Upanishads. He came under the influence of the Vaishnava acharya Yamunacharya of Srirangam, whom he is said never to have met in life, and eventually succeeded to the leadership of the Srirangam temple community. Tradition recounts that, learning the secret Tirumantra from his guru Thirukkacchi Nambi at great personal risk, Ramanuja climbed the temple tower at Thirukkostiyur and proclaimed it aloud to everyone below, accepting damnation for himself so that all might be saved — the episode that defines his reputation for compassion.

Philosophy

Against Shankara's pure monism, Ramanuja argued that Brahman is real, the world is real, and individual souls are eternally distinct yet inseparably dependent on God, as the body is to the soul (the sharira-atma bhava doctrine). God (Narayana/Vishnu) is the inner ruler of all conscious selves and of matter. Liberation comes through loving devotion (bhakti) and total self-surrender (prapatti). His Sri Bhashya, a commentary on the Brahma Sutras, remains the foundational text of Vishishtadvaita, and he is credited with opening temple worship and religious instruction to people excluded by caste — reforms at the root of Sri Vaishnavism's later division and enduring influence across South India.
