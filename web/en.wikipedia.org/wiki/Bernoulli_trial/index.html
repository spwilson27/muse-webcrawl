Bernoulli trial

A coin toss is the canonical example of a Bernoulli trial.

In the theory of probability and statistics, a Bernoulli trial (or binomial trial) is a random experiment with exactly two possible outcomes, "success" and "failure", in which the probability of success is the same every time the experiment is conducted. It is named after Jacob Bernoulli, the 17th-century Swiss mathematician.

Definition

Mathematically, a Bernoulli trial is modelled by a random variable that takes the value 1 with probability p (success) and the value 0 with probability q = 1 − p (failure). The probability mass function is

P(X = 1) = p,    P(X = 0) = 1 − p

The expected value of such a variable is p and its variance is p(1 − p).

Examples

Examples of Bernoulli trials include flipping a coin (heads or tails), asking a voter whether they will vote "yes" in a referendum, determining whether a manufactured part is defective, and whether a newborn child is a boy or a girl.

Sequences of trials

A sequence of independent Bernoulli trials with the same success probability p is called a Bernoulli process. The number of successes in n trials follows the binomial distribution, while the number of trials needed to obtain the first success follows the geometric distribution.
