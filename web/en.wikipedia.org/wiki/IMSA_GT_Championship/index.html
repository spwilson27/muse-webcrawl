The IMSA GT Championship was a North American sports car racing series organized by the International Motor Sports Association (IMSA). First held in 1971 under founder John Bishop, it ran until 1998, when it was succeeded by the American Le Mans Series and related championships.

History

The series began with GT classes for production-based cars and grew to include the spectacular GTP prototype class in the 1980s, featuring manufacturers such as Porsche, Nissan, Toyota and Jaguar. Famous events included the 24 Hours of Daytona and the 12 Hours of Sebring.

Legacy

After a split in the late 1990s, American sports car racing fragmented before reuniting under the IMSA SportsCar Championship in 2014, which revived the IMSA GT name in its GT classes.
