Mälaren

Lake Mälaren
Location: Sweden (Svealand)
Surface area: about 1,140 km²
Max depth: about 64 m
Islands: about 1,200
Outflow: Norrström to the Baltic Sea

Mälaren is the third-largest freshwater lake in Sweden, covering roughly 1,140 square kilometres west of Stockholm. At the city's eastern edge it connects through locks and channels to Saltsjön, part of the Baltic Sea, and the lake forms the historical heartland of the Swedish region of Svealand.

Geography

The lake is complex and fjord-like, with numerous bays, peninsulas, and about 1,200 islands. Major towns on its shores include Västerås, Eskilstuna, Södertälje, and Uppsala (on a northern bay via the river Fyris). Its average depth is modest, around 13 metres, with a maximum near 64 metres.

History

In the Viking Age, Mälaren was still an inlet of the Baltic; post-glacial land uplift gradually cut it off, making it a freshwater lake by roughly the twelfth century. The trading town of Birka on Björkö island and nearby Adelsö were major early medieval centres, and the lake remained a key transport artery into modern times.

Use

Mälaren supplies drinking water to around two million people and is used for shipping, fishing, and recreation; protecting its water quality is a major regional concern.
