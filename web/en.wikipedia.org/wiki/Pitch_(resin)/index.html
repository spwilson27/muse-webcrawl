Pitch (resin)

Pitch
MaterialViscoelastic polymer / tar derivative
SourcesPine resin, wood tar, coal tar, petroleum
AppearanceBlack, glossy, brittle solid
BehaviourFlows very slowly at room temperature

Pitch is a viscoelastic polymer which can be natural or manufactured, derived from petroleum, coal tar, or plants. Pitch produced from petroleum is also called bitumen or asphalt, while plant-derived pitch, made by heating wood or resin in the absence of air, is a form of tar. Pitch has been used since antiquity as a waterproofing and adhesive material.

Production

Traditional pitch was made by the destructive distillation of pine wood or by boiling down pine tar. Birch-bark pitch, made by Neanderthals and early modern humans at least tens of thousands of years ago, is among the earliest known synthetic materials, used to haft stone tools. Industrial pitch is today produced mainly as a residue of coal-tar distillation and petroleum refining.

Uses

Pitch was historically essential in shipbuilding for caulking seams and waterproofing hulls and rigging — hence the expression "pitch black" and the naval use of hot pitch. It has also been used for roofing, paving, torches, and as an adhesive and sealant. Modern applications include carbon-fibre precursors, electrodes for aluminium smelting, and coatings.

Viscoelastic behaviour

Although it shatters like a solid when struck, pitch flows as a very viscous liquid over long periods. This is famously demonstrated by the pitch drop experiment at the University of Queensland, begun in 1927, in which drops of pitch fall from a funnel roughly once a decade; the viscosity of pitch has been measured at roughly 230 billion times that of water.
