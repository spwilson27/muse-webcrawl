Trinità dei Monti

The Trinità dei Monti (French: La Trinité-des-Monts) is a Roman Catholic church in Rome, Italy, famous for its commanding position at the top of the Spanish Steps above the Piazza di Spagna. It is a titular church and remains closely associated with France, whose kings funded its construction and whose French religious community has historically administered it.

History

The site was donated in 1494 by King Charles VIII of France to the Minim friars founded by Saint Francis of Paola. Construction of the present church began in 1502 in late Gothic style, and the twin bell towers that give the façade its distinctive silhouette were added in the 16th century, attributed to designs involving Giacomo della Porta and Domenico Fontana. In front of the church stands the Obelisco Sallustiano, an ancient Roman imitation of an Egyptian obelisk, erected there in 1789.

Interior and setting

The interior contains notable works of art, including frescoes and paintings by Daniele da Volterra, among them a celebrated Deposition. The church is linked to the Spanish Steps by its position on the Pincian Hill, and the adjoining convent historically served French religious orders; the French school of the Sacré-Cœur remains located nearby.
