Chuy Region (Kyrgyz: Чүй облусу, Chüy oblusu; Russian: Чуйская область) is the northernmost region (oblast) of Kyrgyzstan. It surrounds the capital city of Bishkek, which is administered separately as a city of republican significance, and takes its name from the Chu River that flows through the fertile Chuy Valley.

Geography

The region occupies the Chuy Valley and the surrounding mountain ranges, including the Kyrgyz Ala-Too to the south and the Chu-Ili ranges to the northwest. It borders Kazakhstan to the north and west, Talas Region to the southwest, Jalal-Abad Region to the south, and Issyk-Kul Region to the southeast. The Chuy Valley is one of the most agriculturally productive and densely populated areas of the country, irrigated by the Chu River and its tributaries.

History

The valley was an important corridor of the northern Silk Road, with medieval settlements such as Burana (site of the Burana Tower, capital of the Karakhanids at Balasagun) flourishing here. The region formed part of the Russian Empire and later the Kirghiz SSR; the surrounding oblast was reorganised several times during the Soviet period and re-established in its present form in 1990.

Economy

Agriculture dominates the regional economy, with sugar beet, wheat, maize, vegetables and livestock raised on irrigated land. Industry is concentrated near the capital, including food processing, construction materials and the Kant cement works. Tokmok, the region's largest town, was an important Soviet-era industrial centre.

Administrative divisions

The region is divided into eight districts: Alamüdün, Chuy, Jayyl, Kemin, Moskva, Panfilov, Sokuluk and Ysyk-Ata, together with the city of Tokmok. The regional administration is seated in Bishkek.
