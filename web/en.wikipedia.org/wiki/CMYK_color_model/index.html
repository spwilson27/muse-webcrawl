CMYK color model

The CMYK color model (also known as process color, or four color) is a subtractive color model, based on the CMY color model, used in color printing, and is also used to describe the printing process itself. The abbreviation CMYK refers to the four ink plates used: cyan, magenta, yellow, and key (black).

How it works

The CMYK model works by partially or entirely masking colors on a lighter, usually white, background. The ink reduces the light that would otherwise be reflected. Such a model is called subtractive because inks "subtract" the colors red, green and blue from white light: cyan ink absorbs red, magenta absorbs green and yellow absorbs blue.

Why black ink is added

In theory, equal parts of cyan, magenta and yellow combine to make black, but in practice real pigments are imperfect and produce a muddy dark brown. Adding a separate black ink (the "key" plate, so named because in four-color printing the black plate was aligned — "keyed" — to the others) gives deeper shadows, sharper text, and lower ink consumption and cost.

Comparison with RGB

The CMYK gamut is smaller than the additive RGB gamut used by displays, so vivid screen colors — especially bright greens, blues and oranges — may print duller unless converted carefully. Professional workflows use ICC color profiles to manage conversion between the two spaces.

Halftoning

Because press inks cannot vary in intensity continuously, intermediate tints are simulated by halftone dots of varying size, traditionally printed at different screen angles for each plate to avoid moiré patterns.
