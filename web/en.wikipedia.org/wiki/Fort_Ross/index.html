Fort Ross

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Ross (Russian: Форт-Росс, originally Fort Russ) is a former Russian establishment on the coast of present-day Sonoma County, California. Founded in 1812 by the Russian-American Company, it was the southernmost settlement of Russian North America, established to supply food to Alaskan colonies and to support the sea otter fur trade.

History

The settlement, built with the local Kashaya Pomo people, centred on a stockaded quadrangle with blockhouses, a chapel, and officials' houses; the Rotchev House (c. 1836) is the only surviving original building. Russian, Native Alaskan, and Californian workers farmed, ranched, and built ships — the first ships constructed in California. With sea otter populations depleted and agriculture unprofitable, the company sold the post in 1841 to John Sutter.

Today

Fort Ross is now a California State Historic Park, with reconstructed buildings including the chapel, and is a California Historical Landmark and National Historic Landmark district.
