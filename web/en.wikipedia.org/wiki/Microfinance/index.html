Microfinance is a category of financial services provided to low-income individuals and small businesses that lack access to conventional banking. Its best-known product is microcredit — very small loans, often of a few hundred dollars — extended without traditional collateral, frequently to groups of borrowers who guarantee one another's repayment.

History

Informal lending circles and cooperatives existed for centuries, but the modern movement is generally dated to the 1970s, when economist Muhammad Yunus began lending small sums to poor villagers in Jobra, Bangladesh. In 1983 he founded the Grameen Bank, whose group-lending model — five-member borrowing circles, weekly meetings, and a strong focus on women clients — achieved repayment rates above 95 per cent and was replicated worldwide. Yunus and the bank jointly received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2006. The United Nations declared 2005 the International Year of Microcredit.

Services and models

  • Microcredit — small working-capital loans to micro-entrepreneurs such as market traders and farmers.
  • Group lending and village banking — social collateral substitutes for physical collateral.
  • Microsavings and microinsurance — deposit accounts and low-premium insurance products for poor households.
  • Mobile money — services such as M-Pesa in Kenya brought microfinance onto mobile phones, sharply reducing costs.

Debate and evidence

Microfinance was widely promoted as a near-panacea for poverty, but randomised evaluations in the 2010s found more modest average effects: expanded business activity and consumption smoothing, with little evidence of transformative income gains on average. Critics have also pointed to high interest rates, over-indebtedness crises (notably in Andhra Pradesh in 2010), and the commercialisation of the sector, exemplified by the controversial 2007 IPO of Mexico's Compartamos Banco. Supporters respond that access to finance has intrinsic value for managing risk and seizing opportunities, even where average income effects are small.
