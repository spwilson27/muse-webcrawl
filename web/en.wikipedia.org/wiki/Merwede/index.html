Merwede

The Merwede is a Dutch river in the Rhine–Meuse delta. Originally the name applied to a long stretch of the lower Rhine, but today it refers to the section between Woudrichem, where the Waal and the Maas (Afgedamde Maas) meet, and the point near Papendrecht and Dordrecht where it splits into the Noord and the Oude Maas.

Course

At Woudrichem the Merwede begins as the Boven Merwede ("Upper Merwede"), flowing past Gorinchem and Hardinxveld-Giessendam. Near Dordrecht it becomes the Beneden Merwede ("Lower Merwede") before dividing around the island of Dordrecht. The Nieuwe Merwede, a canal dug in the 19th century, diverts much of the discharge southward to the Hollands Diep, while the Merwede itself carries heavy inland shipping traffic toward Rotterdam.

History

The name derives from an older form meaning a broad water between wooded banks. The river's course was reshaped by the St. Elizabeth's flood of 1421 and by subsequent engineering works, including the digging of the Nieuwe Merwede channel between 1868 and 1870.
