Society of Antiquaries of London

The Society of Antiquaries of London (SAL) is a learned society "charged by its Royal Charter of 1751 with 'the encouragement, advancement and furtherance of the study and knowledge of the antiquities and history of this and other countries'." It is based at Burlington House, Piccadilly, London.

History

The Society traces its origins to informal meetings of antiquaries at the Mitre Tavern in 1707. It was formally constituted in 1717–18, with Peter Le Neve as its first president, and received a Royal Charter from George II in 1751. It is the second-oldest antiquarian society in the English-speaking world, after its Edinburgh counterpart was founded later in the century; a college of antiquaries had existed earlier under Elizabeth I and James I.

Fellowship and activities

Fellows are elected and may use the post-nominal letters FSA. The Society maintains a major archaeological and historical research library, an antiquarian museum collection, and publishes the Antiquaries Journal and the journal Archaeologia, which has appeared since 1770. It hosts lectures and campaigns on heritage issues.

Its rooms at Burlington House are shared with other learned societies, including the Royal Academy of Arts, the Geological Society and the Royal Society of Chemistry.
