Kei car

Kei cars carry distinctive yellow licence plates in Japan.

Kei cars (Japanese: 軽自動車, keijidōsha, "light automobile") are a Japanese category of small vehicles, including passenger cars, vans and pickup trucks, built to comply with strict size and engine regulations in exchange for tax and insurance benefits. They are the smallest highway-legal passenger vehicles in Japan.

Regulations

Under the current rules, in force since 1998, a kei car may be at most 3.4 m long, 1.48 m wide and 2.0 m high, with an engine of no more than 660 cc; manufacturers observe a voluntary power cap of 64 PS (47 kW). Private kei cars bear yellow licence plates with black characters.

History and market

The class was created in 1949 to encourage affordable motorisation after the war, with engine limits rising over the decades from 150 cc through 360 cc and 550 cc to 660 cc in 1990. Kei cars regularly account for around a third of new car sales in Japan. Popular models include the Honda N-BOX, Suzuki Wagon R and Daihatsu Tanto; kei trucks such as the Suzuki Carry are staples of farming and small business. The category is almost entirely domestic, though some models have been exported or built under licence abroad.
