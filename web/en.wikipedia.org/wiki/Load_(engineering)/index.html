Load (engineering)

A load, in engineering, is a force or system of forces applied to a structure, machine or component. Loads may be static or dynamic, may act at a point or be distributed over an area or volume, and they produce stresses, strains and deformations that engineers must design the system to withstand safely.

Structural loads

  • Dead loads — the permanent weight of the structure itself and fixed attachments.
  • Live loads — variable loads from occupancy, vehicles, furniture and stored materials.
  • Environmental loads — wind, snow, rain, earthquake (seismic) forces, thermal expansion and soil or water pressure.

Design codes such as the Eurocodes and ASCE 7 specify load combinations in which factored dead, live and environmental loads are combined to check both strength and serviceability.

Other senses

In mechanical engineering a load may be any resistance a machine works against; in electrical engineering the "load" is the device or circuit consuming power from a source, characterised by its impedance. In computing, load refers to the demand placed on a system, measured for example by CPU load averages.
