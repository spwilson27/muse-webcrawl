Dehydration

Dehydration
SymptomsThirst, dry mouth, reduced urine output, dizziness, headache, fatigue
CausesInsufficient fluid intake, vomiting, diarrhoea, fever, sweating, excessive urination
TreatmentOral rehydration therapy; intravenous fluids in severe cases

In physiology, dehydration is a lack of total body water, with an accompanying disruption of metabolic processes. It occurs when free water loss exceeds free water intake, usually because of exercise, disease, or high environmental temperature. Mild dehydration can be treated by drinking water; severe dehydration is a medical emergency that can lead to hypovolemic shock, organ failure and death.

Signs and symptoms

Early signs include thirst, dry mucous membranes, decreased urine output and dark-coloured urine. As fluid loss progresses, symptoms may include headache, dizziness, rapid heartbeat, low blood pressure, confusion and reduced skin turgor. Losses of around 5–6% of body water can cause significant symptoms, and losses of 15% or more are often fatal without treatment.

Causes

Dehydration may result from inadequate fluid intake, or from fluid losses through vomiting and diarrhoea (a leading cause of death in young children worldwide, particularly in cholera and other diarrhoeal diseases), fever, excessive sweating, burns, and uncontrolled diabetes.

Treatment

The mainstay of treatment is replacement of water and electrolytes, ideally with oral rehydration solution containing water, glucose and salts. Severe cases require intravenous fluids and monitoring.
