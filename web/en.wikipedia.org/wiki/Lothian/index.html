Lothian (Scots: Lowden; Scottish Gaelic: Lodainn) is a region of the Scottish Lowlands, lying on the southern shore of the Firth of Forth and bounded principally by the Pentland Hills and the Lammermuir Hills. It is traditionally divided into three historic counties — West Lothian, Midlothian and East Lothian — and contains Scotland's capital city, Edinburgh.

History

The name is traditionally linked to the legendary King Lot (Loth) of Arthurian romance, said to have ruled the territory. In the early medieval period the area formed part of the territory of the Gododdin and later of the Anglian kingdom of Northumbria, before passing definitively to the Scottish crown in the tenth and eleventh centuries. With Edinburgh as its capital, Lothian became the political and commercial heartland of the Kingdom of Scotland.

From 1975 to 1996 Lothian was one of Scotland's nine local government regions, administered by Lothian Regional Council. On the 1996 reorganisation it was replaced by the unitary authorities of Edinburgh, West Lothian, Midlothian and East Lothian, though the name survives in bodies such as NHS Lothian, Lothian and Borders Police (until 2013), and Lothian Buses.

Geography

The region extends from the shale fields and former mining towns of West Lothian, through the Edinburgh conurbation and the coalfield villages of Midlothian, to the rich farmland and golfing coastline of East Lothian, sometimes called "Scotland's Golf Coast". Notable towns include Livingston, Linlithgow, Bathgate, Dalkeith, Penicuik, Musselburgh, Haddington, North Berwick and Dunbar.

Transport and culture

Edinburgh Airport, the Edinburgh City Bypass and the Queensferry Crossing serve the region, and Edinburgh Waverley is Scotland's second-busiest railway station. Lothian hosts the Edinburgh Festival Fringe and the Edinburgh International Festival, the world's largest performing-arts gathering, as well as historic sites including Linlithgow Palace, Rosslyn Chapel and Tantallon Castle.
