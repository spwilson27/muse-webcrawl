The Siege of Jerusalem in 597 BC was the first capture of Jerusalem by Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon. After the death of King Jehoiakim, his young son Jehoiachin surrendered the city to the Babylonian army in March 597 BC, ending the siege without the city's destruction.

Background

Judah had become a Babylonian vassal after Nebuchadnezzar's victory over Egypt at Carchemish (605 BC) and his first campaign into the Levant. Jehoiakim rebelled after an Egyptian success against Babylon, and Nebuchadnezzar marched on Jerusalem; Jehoiakim died during the crisis, leaving the eighteen-year-old Jehoiachin to surrender after a reign of three months.

Deportation

Nebuchadnezzar plundered the treasures of the Temple and royal palace and deported the king, the royal family, court officials, warriors, and skilled craftsmen — the biblical account gives figures of 7,000–10,000 exiles — to Babylon. Among the deportees was the prophet Ezekiel. Nebuchadnezzar installed Jehoiachin's uncle Mattaniah, renamed Zedekiah, as vassal king. Zedekiah's later rebellion brought the second, catastrophic siege of 589–587 BC, in which Jerusalem and its Temple were destroyed.

Sources

The event is recorded in the Hebrew Bible (2 Kings 24) and corroborated by the Babylonian Chronicles, which date the capture of "the city of Judah" precisely, and by ration tablets from Babylon listing provisions for Jehoiachin and his sons in exile.
