Libra (unit)

The libra (Latin for "scales" or "balance") was a Roman unit of weight. It is the origin of the abbreviation lb used for the pound, and of the £ sign for the pound sterling, which descends from a stylised letter L for libra. The Roman libra weighed approximately 328.9 grams (about 11.6 ounces) and was divided into 12 unciae (ounces).

History

The libra was the fundamental weight standard of the Roman world and remained influential throughout medieval Europe. Local pounds derived from it — such as the various pounds used in England, France, Spain, and Portugal — diverged considerably in mass over the centuries. In the Iberian world the libra survived into the modern era as a metric-era folk unit: the Spanish libra of about 460 grams and other regional variants persisted in everyday commerce until metrication.

Legacy

Beyond the pound symbol and abbreviation, the libra survives in the names of currencies historically called pounds in several languages, and in the astrological sign Libra, the scales. The French livre and Italian lira likewise derive their names from the libra.
