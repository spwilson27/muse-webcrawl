Battle of Näfels

The Battle of Näfels was fought on 9 April 1388 between the forces of the Old Swiss Confederacy, principally the canton of Glarus, and an army of the Habsburg Duke Albert III of Austria. It was the decisive battle of the Swiss-Habsburg wars of the 1380s and confirmed Glarus's membership in the Confederacy.

Background

Glarus had joined the Confederacy in 1352, but Habsburg officials continued to claim jurisdiction over the valley. In 1388 an Austrian army invaded over the Kerenzerberg pass and plundered the lower valley as far as Näfels and Mollis.

The battle

The small Glarner force fell back fighting before the much larger Austrian host, then turned on the heights above Näfels. Rolling boulders down on the enemy and attacking from the slopes — reinforced during the day by men from Schwyz and Uri — the Swiss broke the Austrians, who fled across the Linth plain; many drowned when the bridge at Weesen collapsed under the fugitives.

Aftermath

Näfels secured Glarus permanently for the Confederacy and, with the earlier victory at Sempach (1386), ended Habsburg attempts to crush the Swiss by force. The battle is still commemorated annually by the Näfelser Fahrt, a procession on the first Thursday of April in the canton of Glarus.
