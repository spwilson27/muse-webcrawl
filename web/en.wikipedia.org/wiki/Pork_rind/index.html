Pork rind

Pork rind is the culinary term for the skin of a pig, cooked and eaten as a food. It can be rendered, fried in fat, or roasted to produce crisp, puffy snacks known as chicharrones in Spanish-speaking countries, pork scratchings in the United Kingdom, and cracklings in the southern United States.

Preparation

For commercial pork rinds, the skin is first rendered of fat and dehydrated into hard pellets, then deep-fried at high temperature so that it puffs dramatically, and finally seasoned — commonly with salt, barbecue, chilli, or vinegar flavourings. British scratchings are made from shank rind cooked until crisp and are traditionally sold in pubs, often with a characteristic layer of fat and occasional hair.

Culinary uses

Beyond snacking, fried rinds garnish dishes such as the Filipino sisig and lechon kawali, Mexican stews, and Brazilian feijoada accompaniments (torresmo). Being virtually carbohydrate-free, pork rinds became popular in low-carbohydrate and ketogenic diets, and ground rinds are used as a breadcrumb substitute.
