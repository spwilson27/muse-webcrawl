Nerve conduction study

A nerve conduction study (NCS) is a medical diagnostic test used to evaluate the function and ability of motor and sensory nerves to conduct electrical impulses. It is commonly performed together with electromyography (EMG), with which it is often grouped as "EMG/NCS" testing.

Procedure

Surface electrodes are placed on the skin over a nerve or the muscle it supplies. A brief electrical pulse stimulates the nerve at known points, and the resulting response is recorded. Measurements include latency, conduction velocity and the amplitude of the compound muscle action potential (CMAP) and sensory nerve action potential (SNAP).

Uses

NCS helps diagnose entrapment neuropathies such as carpal tunnel syndrome, peripheral polyneuropathies (for example in diabetes), radiculopathies, plexopathies and traumatic nerve injuries, distinguishing demyelinating from axonal damage.
