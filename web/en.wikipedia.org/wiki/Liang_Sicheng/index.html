Liang Sicheng

Liang Sicheng (20 April 1901 – 9 January 1972) was a Chinese architect and architectural historian, known as the father of modern Chinese architecture. He was the son of Liang Qichao, a notable political reformer, and husband of architect Lin Huiyin.

Career

Educated at the University of Pennsylvania, Liang returned to China and founded the architecture department at Northeastern University in 1928 and later at Tsinghua University. With Lin Huiyin, he surveyed and documented hundreds of historic Chinese timber buildings, including the Foguang Temple (857 CE), the oldest surviving wooden structure in China, which they identified in 1937. His A History of Chinese Architecture was the first comprehensive work of its kind.

Preservation advocacy

After 1949 Liang advocated for preserving Beijing's city walls and historic fabric while planning the new capital, a plan that was rejected; the walls were later demolished. He helped design the national emblem of the People's Republic of China and the Monument to the People's Heroes.
