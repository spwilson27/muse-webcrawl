Siku (instrument)

Siku
Other namesZampoña (Spanish); antara
ClassificationAerophone; pan flute / panpipes
RegionCentral Andes: Bolivia, Peru, northern Chile and Argentina, Ecuador
MaterialBamboo or cane tubes (traditionally also stone, bone, metal)

The siku (Quechua: siku; Aymara: siku; Spanish: zampoña) is a traditional Andean panpipe composed of one or two rows of hollow tubes of graduated length bound together, each tube stopped at one end and sounded by blowing across its open top. It is one of the emblematic instruments of the music of the Altiplano of Bolivia and Peru, with pre-Columbian antecedents more than a thousand years old, including panpipes depicted—and preserved—in Moche and Nazca cultures.

Construction and playing

Siku tubes are traditionally made from a light bamboo-like cane (zongo or chuki), lashed with thread and cross-sticks. In the best-known system the scale is split between two complementary halves: the ira ("leader", with six tubes) and the arca ("follower", with seven). In communal performance two players interlock their parts—each playing alternate notes—in a technique analogous to hocket; tuning is typically based on an A–G diatonic or pentatonic scheme. Instruments come in families of sizes, from the small ch'uli through the standard malta to the deep toyo, whose longest tubes exceed a metre, and are played in ensembles (sikuri) with drums such as the wankara.

Cultural context

Sikuri ensembles are central to Aymara and Quechua festivals in the southern Andes, notably the Fiesta de la Candelaria in Puno and the Arica carnival in northern Chile. In the later twentieth century the siku, together with the quena and charango, became a hallmark of the Andean folk revival and of nueva canción, carried worldwide by groups such as Inti-Illimani and Los Kjarkas; the instrument's sound reached global audiences through Andean-influenced popular music. Bolivia asserts the siku as part of its cultural patrimony, and large sikuri gatherings are a feature of civic celebration in La Paz and El Alto.
