The 2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami occurred at 00:58:53 UTC on 26 December 2004, when a megathrust earthquake of magnitude 9.1–9.3 ruptured the fault where the Indian Plate subducts beneath the Burma Plate off Sumatra. The quake — among the largest ever recorded, with a rupture roughly 1,200–1,300 km long — displaced the seabed and generated a basin-wide tsunami that devastated coastlines around the Indian Ocean.

Impact

Waves reaching 30 metres (about 100 feet) in places killed an estimated 228,000 people in 14 countries, making it one of the deadliest natural disasters in recorded history. Indonesia's Aceh province was worst hit, followed by Sri Lanka, India's Tamil Nadu coast, and Thailand's resort areas, where thousands of foreign tourists were among the dead. Communities as far away as Somalia were struck hours later. Millions were displaced, and fishing fleets, ports, railways and entire villages were destroyed.

Response

The international humanitarian response was one of the largest ever mounted, with roughly US$14 billion pledged in aid. The disaster exposed the absence of a tsunami warning system in the Indian Ocean; an Indian Ocean Tsunami Warning System was subsequently established under UNESCO's Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission. In Aceh, the disaster helped catalyze the 2005 peace accord ending the long-running separatist insurgency.
