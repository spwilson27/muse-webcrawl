Westerlund 1

Westerlund 1 (abbreviated Wd1), sometimes called the Ara Cluster, is a compact young star cluster, or super star cluster, in the Milky Way galaxy about 3.5–5 kiloparsecs away, in the constellation Ara. It was discovered by Bengt Westerlund in 1961 during an infrared survey of the Zone of Avoidance, but was largely ignored for years because of heavy interstellar extinction in its direction.

Properties

Westerlund 1 is the most massive compact young cluster known in the Milky Way, with an estimated mass of about 63,000 solar masses and an age of roughly 3.5 to 5 million years. It contains an exceptionally rich population of evolved massive stars, including Wolf–Rayet stars, red and yellow supergiants and hypergiants, luminous blue variables, and the magnetar CXO J164710.2-455216.

Notable stars

The cluster hosts several of the largest and most luminous stars known, including Westerlund 1-26 (W26), a red supergiant or hypergiant with a radius over 1,500 times that of the Sun, and W243, a luminous blue variable. The concentration of such evolved stars makes Westerlund 1 a key laboratory for studying the late stages of massive stellar evolution.
