Ardabil (Persian: اردبیل) is a city in northwestern Iran and the capital of Ardabil Province. Located on a high plateau near Mount Sabalan and the Caspian region, it is an ancient city known for its silk and carpet trade, its mineral springs, and its association with the Safavid dynasty, which originated here.

The Sheikh Safi al-Din Khanegah and Shrine Ensemble

History

Ardabil is believed to be of great antiquity, and local tradition associates its founding with the Sasanian era. The city was conquered by Arab Muslim forces in the 7th century. In the 13th and 14th centuries, Sheikh Safi al-Din, founder of the Safaviyya Sufi order, made Ardabil a center of religious influence; his descendants founded the Safavid Empire in 1501 under Shah Ismail I, who was born in Ardabil. The city was contested between the Safavids and the Ottoman Empire and later occupied by Russian forces during the Russo-Persian wars.

Landmarks

The Sheikh Safi al-Din Khanegah and Shrine Ensemble, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010, is one of the finest examples of Iranian and Islamic architecture, containing the tomb of Sheikh Safi al-Din and of Shah Ismail I. The city also preserves the Friday Mosque and numerous historical bridges and baths.

Ardabil carpet

The city lends its name to the Ardabil Carpet, one of the oldest dated Islamic carpets (1539–40), now in the Victoria and Albert Museum in London; a second carpet from the same commission is in Los Angeles. Woven for the mosque of Sheikh Safi al-Din, it is considered a masterpiece of Persian carpet weaving.

Climate and economy

Ardabil has a cold, dry climate with cool summers that attract domestic tourists. The region is known for honey, dairy products, carpets, and the nearby Sareyn hot springs.
