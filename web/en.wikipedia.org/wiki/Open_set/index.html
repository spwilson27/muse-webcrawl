Open set

In mathematics, an open set is a set that, intuitively, does not include its boundary: around every point of the set there is a small region entirely contained in the set. Open sets are fundamental to topology, real analysis, and geometry, because they underlie the definitions of continuity, limits, and convergence.

In Euclidean space

A subset U of Euclidean space is open if, for every point x in U, there exists a positive radius r such that the ball of radius r centered at x lies entirely within U. Examples include open intervals such as (0, 1) on the real line and open disks in the plane. The empty set and the whole space are both open; arbitrary unions of open sets are open, and finite intersections of open sets are open.

In topology

In general topology the concept is taken as primitive: a topology on a set is a collection of subsets declared to be open, satisfying the union and finite-intersection properties above. In metric spaces this agrees with the ball-based definition. A set whose complement is open is called closed; some sets are both open and closed ("clopen"), and many are neither.

Uses

Open sets allow the definition of continuous functions — the preimage of every open set is open — and of neighborhoods, interior points, and open covers used in compactness arguments. In functional analysis and differential geometry, domains of functions are typically taken to be open sets.
