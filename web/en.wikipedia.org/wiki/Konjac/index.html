Konjac

Konjac
SpeciesAmorphophallus konjac
OriginEast and Southeast Asia
Key productsKonnyaku, shirataki noodles, glucomannan fibre

Konjac (Japanese: 蒟蒻, konnyaku) is a plant, Amorphophallus konjac, grown in East and Southeast Asia for its large starchy corm, and the name of the food made from it. The corm is dried and ground into a flour rich in glucomannan, a highly viscous soluble dietary fibre that absorbs many times its weight in water.

Food uses

Konjac flour mixed with an alkaline solution (typically lime water) sets into a firm, grey, gelatinous cake called konnyaku, a traditional Japanese food eaten in dishes such as oden and sukiyaki. Extruded into noodles, it becomes shirataki, nearly calorie-free noodles popular in low-carbohydrate diets. Konjac is also used in fruit jellies, as a vegan substitute for gelatin, and as a thickener (food additive E425).

Health and safety

Glucomannan is marketed as a dietary supplement for weight loss and cholesterol reduction; the European Food Safety Authority has authorised a weight-loss claim under specific conditions. Mini-cup konjac jellies were banned in several countries after choking deaths, and glucomannan tablets must be taken with ample water because the fibre can swell and obstruct the throat.
