Sir William Phips (2 February 1651 – 18 February 1695) was a shipwright, ship's captain, treasure hunter and naval officer from New England who became the first royally appointed governor of the Province of Massachusetts Bay, serving during the Salem witch trials.

From shipwright to treasure hunter

Born on the Maine frontier, one of a very large family, Phips learned shipbuilding in Boston and went to sea. Backed by the Duke of Albemarle and other investors, in 1687 he located and salvaged the wreck of the Spanish treasure ship Nuestra Señora de la Concepción, lost off Hispaniola in 1641, recovering some 34 tons of silver and gold. His share made him wealthy, and he was knighted by James II — among the first American-born colonists to receive a knighthood.

Military command and governorship

In 1690 Phips captured Port Royal in Acadia and led the unsuccessful expedition against Quebec during King William's War. His advocacy secured the new Massachusetts charter of 1691, and he arrived in May 1692 as governor.

Salem witch trials

Within weeks of his arrival, Phips established the special Court of Oyer and Terminer to try accused witches at Salem. As the trials spiralled — nineteen people hanged and one pressed to death — criticism grew, including from Increase Mather. In October 1692 Phips dissolved the court and later barred spectral evidence, effectively ending the executions; he subsequently pardoned remaining suspects. Beset by political quarrels, he was recalled to London, where he died in February 1695 while preparing his defence.
