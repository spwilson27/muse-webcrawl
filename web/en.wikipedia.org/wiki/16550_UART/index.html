16550 UART

The 16550 UART (universal asynchronous receiver-transmitter) is an integrated circuit designed for implementing the interface for serial communications, developed by National Semiconductor. It became the standard serial-port chip of the IBM PC compatible world, used for RS-232 ports connecting modems, mice and other peripherals. Its defining improvement over the earlier 8250 and 16450 was a pair of 16-byte FIFO buffers.

History and the FIFO fix

At the higher baud rates arriving with fast modems, the single-byte buffering of the 8250/16450 forced the CPU to service an interrupt for every character, and characters were lost when the processor was busy. The original 16550 (1987) had a bug in its FIFO; the revised 16550A fixed it, and the 16550A became ubiquitous in 1990s PCs. The FIFOs let the chip accumulate up to 16 characters (with programmable trigger levels of 1, 4, 8 or 14 bytes) before interrupting, dramatically reducing overruns and CPU load.

Legacy

Later variants (16650, 16750, 16950) enlarged the FIFOs to 32, 64 and 128 bytes and raised maximum speeds. The 16550A's register set became a de facto standard: operating systems and virtual machines still present emulated 16550-compatible UARTs, and its I/O-port assignments for COM1–COM4 remain a fixture of PC architecture even though physical serial ports have largely vanished from consumer machines.
