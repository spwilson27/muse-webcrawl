Christopher Plantin (c. 1520 – July 1, 1589) was a French-born printer and publisher who founded the Plantin Press in Antwerp, one of the most famous printing houses of the Renaissance. His publications, produced on an industrial scale unmatched in 16th-century Europe, helped make Antwerp a centre of humanist learning.

Life and career

Trained as a bookbinder in Paris, Plantin settled in Antwerp in the 1540s. By the 1560s his workshop operated dozens of presses with hundreds of workers, issuing classical texts, liturgical books, botanical works and maps in multiple languages. He became printer to the King of Spain, which funded his monumental Biblia Regia — the eight-volume Antwerp Polyglot Bible (1568–1573) in Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek, Latin and Syriac, edited by Benito Arias Montano.

Legacy

The press was continued by his son-in-law Jan Moretus and became the Officina Plantiniana. The original Antwerp workshop survives as the Plantin-Moretus Museum, a UNESCO World Heritage Site preserving two of the world's oldest printing presses. The Plantin typeface, cut for his editions, remains a standard book face.
