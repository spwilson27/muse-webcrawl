Fort Yates is a city in Sioux County, North Dakota, United States. It is the county seat of Sioux County and the tribal headquarters of the Standing Rock Sioux Tribe. The population was 176 at the 2020 census.

History

Fort Yates was founded in 1874 as a United States Army post adjacent to a Hunkpapa Lakota settlement. It was named for Captain George Yates, an officer killed at the Battle of the Little Bighorn. The post was decommissioned in 1903, and the community remained as an administrative center for the Standing Rock Reservation.

Sitting Bull

The Hunkpapa Lakota leader Sitting Bull was killed at his camp near Fort Yates in December 1890 by Indian police during an attempted arrest. He was originally buried at the fort; a monument at the site commemorates him.

Geography

The city sits on the west bank of the Missouri River, within Lake Oahe's reach, in south-central North Dakota near the South Dakota border.
