Harris Treaty

Treaty of Amity and Commerce (Harris Treaty)
Signed: 29 July 1858, near Edo, Japan
Parties: United States and Tokugawa shogunate
Negotiator: Townsend Harris

The Harris Treaty, formally the Treaty of Amity and Commerce between the United States and Japan, was signed in 1858 by US consul-general Townsend Harris. It followed Perry's Convention of Kanagawa (1854) and opened Japanese ports to American trade.

Terms

The treaty opened Kanagawa (Yokohama), Nagasaki, Niigata, Hyōgo (Kobe) and other ports, allowed American residence and extraterritoriality, fixed low tariffs Japan could not control, and permitted a US minister to reside in Edo. Similar unequal treaties with European powers followed.

Impact

Trade disruption, gold outflow and resentment of foreign privilege weakened the Tokugawa shogunate, contributing to its fall in the Meiji Restoration. Japan renegotiated the unequal treaties in the 1890s.
