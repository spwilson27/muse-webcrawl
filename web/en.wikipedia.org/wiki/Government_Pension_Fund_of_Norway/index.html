The Government Pension Fund of Norway (Norwegian: Statens pensjonsfond) comprises two entirely separate Norwegian sovereign wealth funds: the Government Pension Fund Global (GPFG), widely known as the "Oil Fund", and the smaller Government Pension Fund Norway. The GPFG is among the largest sovereign wealth funds in the world, built from Norway's petroleum revenues.

Government Pension Fund Global

Established in 1990 as the Government Petroleum Fund and renamed in 2006, the fund receives the state's cash flow from petroleum activities. It is invested entirely abroad — in equities, fixed income, real estate, and renewable energy infrastructure — to avoid overheating the Norwegian economy and to shield it from oil price volatility. The fund owns on average around 1.5% of all listed companies worldwide. Its spending is guided by Norway's fiscal rule, which limits the structural non-oil budget deficit to the fund's expected real return, conventionally estimated at 3%.

Ethics

An independent Council on Ethics recommends exclusions of companies involved in severe environmental damage, human rights violations, production of certain weapons, coal, or tobacco. Excluded companies are publicly listed.

Government Pension Fund Norway

The domestic fund, successor to the National Insurance Scheme Fund of 1967, is managed by Folketrygdfondet and invested in Norwegian and Nordic assets.
