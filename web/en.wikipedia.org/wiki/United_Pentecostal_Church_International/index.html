United Pentecostal Church International

The United Pentecostal Church International (UPCI) is a Oneness Pentecostal Christian denomination headquartered in Weldon Spring, Missouri, United States. Formed in 1945 by the merger of the Pentecostal Church, Incorporated and the Pentecostal Assemblies of Jesus Christ, it is the largest Oneness (Apostolic) Pentecostal organisation in the world, reporting roughly 5.5 million constituents in some 200 countries and more than 40,000 churches worldwide, including about 4,900 churches in North America.

Beliefs

The UPCI teaches the Oneness of God — that God is absolutely one, manifested as Father, Son and Holy Spirit, rather than a Trinity of persons — and that the name of Jesus embodies the fullness of the Godhead. It holds that salvation includes repentance, water baptism by immersion "in the name of Jesus Christ" (Acts 2:38) and receiving the Holy Spirit with the initial evidence of speaking in tongues. The denomination also maintains distinctive holiness standards of dress and conduct, including modesty in clothing and (historically) women not cutting their hair, though emphasis varies between congregations.

Organisation and activities

The church is governed by a General Superintendent — David K. Bernard has held the office since 2010 — together with a General Board and district organisations. It operates Urshan University and Urshan Graduate School of Theology in Missouri, supports hundreds of missionaries through its Global Missions division, and publishes through the Pentecostal Publishing House. Annual events include the North American Youth Congress, which draws tens of thousands of young people.

The UPCI traces its roots to the Pentecostal movement that emerged from the Azusa Street Revival of 1906 and the "New Issue" of Oneness teaching from 1913 onward, which led Oneness ministers to separate from the Trinitarian Assemblies of God in 1916.
