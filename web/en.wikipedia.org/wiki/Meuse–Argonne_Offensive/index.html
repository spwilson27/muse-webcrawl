Meuse–Argonne Offensive

Date: 26 September – 11 November 1918
Location: Argonne Forest and Meuse valley, France
Result: Allied victory; contributed to the Armistice
US forces: c. 1.2 million (AEF, First Army)
Commander: Gen. John J. Pershing

The Meuse–Argonne Offensive (also known as the Battle of the Argonne Forest) was a major part of the final Allied offensive of the First World War, fought from 26 September 1918 until the Armistice of 11 November 1918. It was the largest battle in the military history of the United States, involving some 1.2 million American soldiers of the American Expeditionary Forces (AEF) under General John J. Pershing, alongside French forces.

Background

The offensive formed the American sector of Marshal Ferdinand Foch's Hundred Days Offensive. The AEF First Army attacked between the river Meuse to the east and the dense Argonne Forest to the west, aiming to cut the vital Sedan–Mézières railway supplying the German front. German defenders held a series of fortified lines forming part of the Hindenburg Line system.

Course of the battle

The attack opened with a massive artillery preparation of some 2,700 guns. Initial gains slowed amid logistical chaos, autumn rain and fierce resistance; the fighting included the famous episode of the "Lost Battalion" of the 77th Division, cut off in the forest in early October. Captain Harry S. Truman, later US President, served as an artillery battery commander, and Corporal Alvin C. York won fame for his actions near Châtel-Chéhéry. After a reorganisation in mid-October, the Americans broke through the German positions, and by early November were advancing towards Sedan. The offensive ended with the Armistice on 11 November 1918.

Casualties and legacy

American casualties were roughly 122,000, including about 26,000 killed, making it the deadliest battle in US history up to that time. The Meuse-Argonne American Cemetery near Romagne, with more than 14,000 graves, is the largest American military cemetery in Europe.
