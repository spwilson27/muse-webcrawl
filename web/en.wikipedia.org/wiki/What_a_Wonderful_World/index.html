Song by Louis Armstrong

Released: 1967

"What a Wonderful World" is a pop ballad written by Bob Thiele (under the pseudonym "George Douglas") and George David Weiss. It was first recorded by American jazz trumpeter and vocalist Louis Armstrong in 1967 and released as a single. Intended as an antidote for the increasingly racially and politically charged climate in the United States, the song was initially not promoted in the US, where it sold fewer than 1,000 copies, but it became a major success in the United Kingdom, reaching number one on the UK Singles Chart.

History

The song was written during the summer of 1967, a time of political turmoil in the United States. Weiss recalled that Thiele hoped Armstrong would record it because of his ability to bring people of different races together. Armstrong recorded the song in Las Vegas late one night in August 1967, backed by an orchestra arranged by Artie Butler. The recording session was reportedly interrupted by United Artists president Larry Newton, who attempted to stop it.

Because ABC Records president Larry Newton disliked the slow ballad, the label did little to promote it in the United States, and it initially peaked at only number 116 on the Bubbling Under Hot 100 chart. In the United Kingdom, however, it was released on the HMV label and reached number one, making Armstrong, at 66, the oldest male artist to top the UK Singles Chart — a record he held until 2009.

Resurgence

The song gained renewed popularity in 1988 after being featured in the film Good Morning, Vietnam. The single was re-released and climbed to number 32 on the US Billboard Hot 100. It was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1999. The song describes everyday wonders — trees of green, red roses, blue skies, white clouds, and the bright blessed day — concluding that the singer keeps thinking to himself, "what a wonderful world."

Covers

The song has been covered by many artists, including Eva Cassidy, Israel Kamakawiwo'ole (in a medley with "Over the Rainbow"), Joey Ramone, Tony Bennett, and k.d. lang.
