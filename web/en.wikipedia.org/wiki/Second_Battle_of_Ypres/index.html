Second Battle of Ypres
Date22 April – 25 May 1915
LocationYpres, Belgium
ResultInconclusive; German gains limited

The Second Battle of Ypres was fought from 22 April to 25 May 1915 for control of the Flemish town of Ypres in western Belgium during the First World War. It is notorious for the first large-scale use of poison gas by the German Army on the Western Front.

Course

On 22 April, German forces released chlorine gas against French colonial and Canadian positions north of Ypres, opening a gap, but reserves and improvised protection (including urine-soaked cloths) helped hold the line. Sub-battles followed at Gravenstafel, St. Julien, Frezenberg, and Bellewaarde. Canadian troops' stand at St. Julien became a celebrated episode; John McCrae wrote "In Flanders Fields" after the death of a friend during the battle.

Casualties and outcome

Allied casualties were roughly 70,000 and German about 35,000. The Ypres salient shrank but the town remained in Allied hands. Gas warfare prompted rapid development of gas masks by both sides.
