NEET

A NEET is a person who is "not in education, employment, or training". The acronym was first used in the United Kingdom in the late 1990s in government reporting on 16- to 24-year-olds, and its use has since spread to other countries, including Japan, South Korea, Canada and various European states, though age ranges and definitions vary.

Usage and policy

NEET rates are monitored as an indicator of youth disengagement from the labour market and education system, and governments run programs aimed at re-engaging NEET young people. In Japan the term overlaps with social phenomena such as freeters and hikikomori, while in some online subcultures "NEET" has been adopted as a self-descriptor. The concept is distinct from unemployment statistics because NEETs include those not actively seeking work.
