Auckland Castle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Auckland Castle, also known as Auckland Palace, is a castle in Bishop Auckland, County Durham, England. For nearly 900 years it was the principal country residence of the Bishops of Durham, the "Prince Bishops" who wielded quasi-royal power in the County Palatine of Durham. It is a Grade I listed building set in a large deer park above the River Wear.

History

A manor house existed on the site by the 12th century; Bishop Hugh de Puiset built the great hall and chapel in the late 1100s. The castle was defended for the bishops during medieval conflicts, seized briefly by the Scots, and later remodelled by successive bishops, including major Gothic Revival work by James Wyatt for Bishop Shute Barrington around 1795. During the Civil War it was sold and its chapel demolished, but it was restored to the bishops at the Restoration.

The Auckland Project

In 2012 the Church Commissioners sold the castle and its famous set of Zurbarán paintings, Jacob and His Twelve Sons, to financier Jonathan Ruffer, who founded The Auckland Project to regenerate the town. The castle reopened in 2019 after extensive restoration as a museum telling the story of the Prince Bishops, and the surrounding development now includes a Spanish Gallery, a Faith Museum and a mining art gallery.
