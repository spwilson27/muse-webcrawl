Gene Autry

Orvon Grover "Gene" Autry (29 September 1907 – 2 October 1998) — American singer, songwriter, actor, musician, rodeo performer, and baseball owner; "The Singing Cowboy".

Orvon Grover "Gene" Autry (September 29, 1907 – October 2, 1998), nicknamed the Singing Cowboy, was an American singer, songwriter, actor, musician, rodeo performer, and baseball owner. Autry is regarded as the most important figure in the history of the singing cowboy film genre, starring in 93 films between 1934 and 1953.

Career

Autry rose to fame as a recording and radio artist in the early 1930s — discovered, by legend, after Will Rogers heard him singing while Autry worked as a telegraph operator. His recording of "That Silver-Haired Daddy of Mine" was a major hit. Signed to Republic Pictures, he became Hollywood's first singing cowboy star, with films such as Tumbling Tumbleweeds (1935) and his own television series, The Gene Autry Show (1950–1955). He is also remembered for recording holiday standards including "Here Comes Santa Claus", "Frosty the Snowman", and "Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer", the last a number-one hit in 1949.

Later life and business

Autry became a wealthy broadcasting and entertainment magnate, owning radio and television stations and the California Angels (later Anaheim Angels) baseball team, which he founded in 1961 and owned until 1997. His number 26 was retired by the Angels in his honour.

Legacy

Autry is the only entertainer with five stars on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, one for each category (film, television, music recording, radio, and live performance). The Autry Museum of the American West in Los Angeles, which he founded, opened in 1988. He was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1969.
