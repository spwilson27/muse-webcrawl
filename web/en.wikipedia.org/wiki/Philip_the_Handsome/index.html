Philip the Handsome (22 July 1478 – 25 September 1506), also known as Philip the Fair and as Philip I of Castile, was the first Habsburg king of Castile. He was Duke of Burgundy and ruler of the Burgundian Netherlands from 1482, and became King of Castile in 1504 through his marriage to Joanna, heiress of the Catholic Monarchs.

Early life

Philip was born in Bruges, the son of Archduke Maximilian of Austria (later Emperor Maximilian I) and Mary of Burgundy. On his mother's death in 1482 he inherited the Burgundian lands in the Low Countries. His upbringing in Flanders made him culturally Burgundian and Francophone, and his court at Mechelen was one of the most brilliant in Europe.

Marriage and Castile

In 1496 Philip married Joanna of Castile, daughter of Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile, as part of a double alliance that also married his sister Margaret to Joanna's brother John. After the deaths of John and of Isabella (1504), Joanna inherited Castile, and Philip was proclaimed king alongside her. Relations with his father-in-law Ferdinand, who wished to govern Castile himself, were bitter. By the Treaty of Villafáfila (1506) Ferdinand withdrew to Aragon, leaving Philip and Joanna to rule Castile.

Death

Philip's reign in Castile lasted only a few months. He fell ill after drinking cold water following a game of pelota and died at Burgos on 25 September 1506, probably of typhoid fever; rumours of poison were widespread. His sudden death unhinged Joanna, whose subsequent mental decline led to her confinement at Tordesillas. Philip's eldest son eventually inherited both the Habsburg and Spanish lands as Charles V, uniting an empire of unprecedented extent.
