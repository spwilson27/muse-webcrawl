Ghoti

"Ghoti" — a constructed spelling of "fish"

Ghoti is a creative respelling of the word fish, used to illustrate the irregularities of English orthography. The spelling is constructed as follows:

  • gh, pronounced [f] as in enough;
  • o, pronounced [ɪ] as in women;
  • ti, pronounced [ʃ] as in nation.

Giving the pronunciation "fish" (/fɪʃ/). The respelling is often attributed to the Irish playwright George Bernard Shaw, a vocal supporter of spelling reform, but there is no evidence that Shaw actually invented it; the earliest known attribution of the idea dates to 1855, in a letter by Charles Ollier, and variants circulated in the 19th century.

Discussion

Linguists point out that ghoti is a deliberate sleight of hand: the spellings chosen only produce those sounds in particular positions in a word (for example, gh is pronounced /f/ only at the end of a word or syllable, and ti is pronounced /ʃ/ only before a vowel). Taken at face value, ghoti would more plausibly be read as something like "goaty". Nonetheless, the example remains a popular illustration in debates over English spelling reform.
