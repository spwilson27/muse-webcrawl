Crankset

The crankset (in the US) or chainset (in the UK) is the component of a bicycle drivetrain that converts the reciprocating motion of the rider's legs into rotational motion used to drive the chain or belt, which in turn drives the rear wheel. It consists of one or more chainrings attached to the cranks — the arms to which the pedals attach — mounted on an axle (spindle) carried by the bottom bracket.

Components

Cranks are most commonly 170–175 mm long on adult bikes; longer cranks give more leverage but reduce ground clearance. Chainrings historically came in triples (three rings) for wide gear range, but modern road and mountain bikes favour doubles, compact doubles (50/34 teeth) or single-ring ("1x") setups used with wide-range cassettes. The ring pattern used to mount rings to the crank spider is described by its bolt circle diameter (BCD).

Standards and history

Cranks were once fixed to square-taper spindles with cotter pins, later cotterless designs, then splined interfaces such as Shimano's Octalink and ISIS, and now predominantly two-piece designs with an integrated spindle and external bottom bracket bearings, or press-fit shells. Materials range from steel and aluminium to carbon fibre. Chainring teeth are now shaped and ramped to aid shifting, and single-ring systems use narrow-wide tooth profiles and clutch derailleurs to retain the chain.
