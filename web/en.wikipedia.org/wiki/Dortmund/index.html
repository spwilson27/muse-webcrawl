Dortmund (German pronunciation: [ˈdɔʁtmʊnt]) is the largest city in the Ruhr region and the third-largest city in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, with a population of around 590,000. Founded around 882, it grew into a free imperial city and Hanseatic trading centre, then became one of Germany's great industrial cities on the strength of coal, steel, and beer. Since the late 20th century it has reinvented itself around services, logistics, technology, and education.

Signal Iduna Park, Germany's largest football stadium, is home to Borussia Dortmund and its famous "Yellow Wall" south stand.

History

Dortmund was first mentioned around 882 as "Throtmanni." It became a free imperial city in the Holy Roman Empire and a member of the Hanseatic League, prospering from trade. Industrialisation in the 19th century transformed it: deep coal mines, steelworks such as Hoesch, and major breweries (Dortmunder Export lager took its name from the city) made it an industrial powerhouse. The city was heavily bombed in the Second World War — its centre was about 98% destroyed — and rebuilt in the postwar decades. The last collieries and steel plants closed by the early 2000s.

Today

Modern Dortmund is home to TU Dortmund University, the technology park Technologiepark Dortmund, the German Football Museum, and the Dortmund U-Tower arts centre in a former brewery. The football club Borussia Dortmund (BVB), founded in 1909, plays at Signal Iduna Park, Germany's largest stadium, and is one of Europe's best-supported teams. The city is also a major logistics hub, with one of Europe's largest canal ports.
