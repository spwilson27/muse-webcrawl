Government and binding theory

Government and binding theory (GB) is a theory of syntax and a phrase structure grammar within generative linguistics, developed principally by Noam Chomsky in his 1981 lectures published as Lectures on Government and Binding and refined in Barriers (1986).

GB replaced the earlier extended standard theory with a modular architecture: instead of many construction-specific rules, a small number of interacting subsystems — X-bar theory, case theory, theta theory, binding theory, bounding theory, control theory, and government theory — constrain a single level of representation, with "move α" as the only transformational rule. The framework rests on universal principles with parameters that vary between languages (the principles and parameters approach).

Its best-known component, binding theory, states conditions on the distribution of anaphors (such as himself), pronouns, and referring expressions. GB was the dominant model in generative syntax through the 1980s and early 1990s before being superseded by Chomsky's minimalist program, which preserved many of its insights while seeking to eliminate its more intricate machinery.
