Good Samaritan law

Good Samaritan laws offer legal protection to people who give reasonable assistance to those who are injured, ill or in peril, protecting rescuers from liability for ordinary negligence in many jurisdictions.

Good Samaritan law
Field: Tort law
Named for: Parable of the Good Samaritan
Variants: Duty-to-rescue laws in parts of Europe

Principles

In the United States all 50 states have some form of Good Samaritan statute, typically requiring that aid be given voluntarily, without expectation of payment, and without gross negligence. Many jurisdictions extend protection to administering naloxone or using an AED. The name derives from the parable told in the Gospel of Luke.

Duty to rescue

Common-law systems generally impose no duty to rescue strangers, while many civil-law countries (e.g. France, Germany) criminalise failure to assist in emergencies. China's 2017 provisions likewise protect rescuers nationally.
