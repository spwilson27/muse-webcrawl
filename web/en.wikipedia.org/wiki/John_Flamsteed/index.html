Overview

John Flamsteed (1646–1719) was an English astronomer and the first Astronomer Royal. Appointed by Charles II in 1675 on the recommendation of a royal commission (prompted by the longitude problem and, in legend, by a French proposal), he founded the Royal Observatory at Greenwich, whose site he laid out and where he observed for over forty years, producing the great star catalogue Historia Coelestis Britannica.

The catalogue controversy

Flamsteed, a perfectionist, quarrelled bitterly with Isaac Newton and Edmond Halley, who, impatient for his lunar data and star positions, published an unauthorised edition of his catalogue in 1712; Flamsteed later bought up and burned most copies. His own edition, completed posthumously by his widow Margaret and assistants (1725), listed some 3,000 stars with unprecedented accuracy and assigned the "Flamsteed numbers" still used for many stars (e.g. 61 Cygni).
