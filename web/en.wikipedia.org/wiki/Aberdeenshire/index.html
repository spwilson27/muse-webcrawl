Aberdeenshire (Scots: Aiberdeenshire; Scottish Gaelic: Siorrachd Obar Dheathain) is one of the 32 council areas of Scotland and a historic county in the north-east, taking its name from the city of Aberdeen, which is separate from the council area.

Geography

The area stretches from the Cairngorm mountains — including part of Cairngorms National Park — through fertile farmland (the Howe) to a long North Sea coast with fishing towns such as Fraserburgh, Peterhead and Stonehaven. Rivers include the Dee, Don, Deveron and Ythan.

Economy and heritage

Agriculture (including Aberdeen Angus cattle and whisky distilling), fishing, energy services and tourism dominate. Aberdeenshire is famous for its density of castles — over 260, including Balmoral, Craigievar and Dunnottar — marketed as Scotland's Castle Trail, and for towns like Kemnay.
