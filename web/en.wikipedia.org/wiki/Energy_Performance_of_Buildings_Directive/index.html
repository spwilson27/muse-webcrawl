Energy Performance of Buildings Directive

The Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD) is European Union legislation setting the framework for improving the energy efficiency of buildings, which account for roughly 40% of EU energy consumption and over a third of its energy-related greenhouse gas emissions.

History

The first EPBD (Directive 2002/91/EC) required member states to set minimum energy performance standards, certify buildings' energy performance when built, sold, or rented, and inspect boilers and air-conditioning systems. A recast (Directive 2010/31/EU) required all new buildings to be "nearly zero-energy" by 2021 (2019 for public buildings). Amendments in 2018 added long-term renovation strategies and electromobility provisions.

The 2024 recast

Directive (EU) 2024/1275, adopted under the "Fit for 55" package and the Renovation Wave, requires all new buildings to be zero-emission from 2030 (2028 for public buildings), introduces minimum energy performance standards for the worst-performing existing buildings, phases in life-cycle carbon reporting, and obliges member states to publish national building renovation plans. Solar installations must be deployed on suitable new and renovated buildings where technically and economically feasible.

Energy performance certificates

The best-known output of the directive is the Energy Performance Certificate (EPC), rating buildings on a scale (commonly A to G) with recommendations for improvement. Certificates must be shown in advertisements and given to buyers and tenants.
