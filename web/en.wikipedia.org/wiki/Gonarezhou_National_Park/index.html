Gonarezhou National Park

Gonarezhou National Park is a national park located in south-eastern Zimbabwe. It is situated in a relatively remote corner of Masvingo Province, south of Chimanimani along the Mozambique border. The park is Zimbabwe's second-largest, covering some 5,053 square kilometres, and its name means "place of many elephants" in Shona.

Landscape and wildlife

The park protects wide floodplains of the Save, Runde and Mwenezi rivers, mopane woodland, and the dramatic red sandstone Chilojo Cliffs along the Runde. It is home to one of Africa's larger elephant populations, along with lion, leopard, cheetah, African wild dog, buffalo, hippo, nyala and more than 400 bird species. The black and white rhinoceros have been reintroduced.

Conservation

Gonarezhou forms part of the Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park, linking it with South Africa's Kruger National Park and Mozambique's Limpopo National Park. Since 2009 the Frankfurt Zoological Society has co-managed the park with the Zimbabwe Parks and Wildlife Management Authority through the Gonarezhou Conservation Trust, substantially reducing poaching and restoring wildlife numbers.
