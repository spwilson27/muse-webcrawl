Hirotugu Akaike

Hirotugu Akaike (赤池 弘次, Akaike Hirotsugu, 5 November 1927 – 4 August 2009) was a Japanese statistician best known for formulating the Akaike information criterion (AIC), a widely used measure for model selection in statistics. Working at the Institute of Statistical Mathematics in Tokyo, he spent much of his career developing practical methods for time series analysis and statistical modeling.

Life and career

Akaike was born in Fujinomiya, Shizuoka Prefecture. He studied mathematics at the University of Tokyo, graduating in 1952, and joined the Institute of Statistical Mathematics, where he would remain for his entire career, eventually serving as its director general from 1986 to 1994. His early work concerned the statistical modelling of industrial processes, including the vibration of railway cars and the control of ship manoeuvring.

Akaike information criterion

In work presented in the early 1970s and published in 1973–74, Akaike proposed the AIC as a criterion for choosing between competing statistical models. The criterion, AIC = 2k − 2 ln(L̂), balances the goodness of fit of a model (measured by its maximised log-likelihood) against its complexity (the number of estimated parameters k). Models with lower AIC values are preferred. Grounded in information theory and the Kullback–Leibler divergence, the AIC became one of the most cited contributions in statistics and is standard practice in fields from ecology to economics.

Other contributions

Akaike also contributed to time series analysis, including methods for fitting autoregressive models, the final prediction error criterion, and Bayesian modelling approaches that led to the Bayesian information criterion variant ABIC. He promoted a pragmatic, goal-oriented philosophy of statistics, emphasising that models should be judged by their predictive usefulness.

Honours

Among many honours, Akaike received the Kyoto Prize in Basic Sciences in 2006 and was named a Person of Cultural Merit by the Japanese government. In 2017 Google commemorated the 90th anniversary of his birth with a Doodle. He died in 2009 at the age of 81.
