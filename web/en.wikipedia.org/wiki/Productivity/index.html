Productivity

Productivity is the efficiency of production of goods or services, expressed by some measure. Measurements of productivity are often expressed as a ratio of an aggregate output to a single input or an aggregate input used in a production process, i.e. output per unit of input, typically over a specific period of time. The most common example is (aggregate) labour productivity, for example GDP per worker.


Labour productivity measures output per worker or per hour worked.

Measurement

Labour productivity growth is a key driver of rising living standards, since it allows average incomes to grow without more hours being worked. Multifactor (or total factor) productivity measures the portion of output growth not explained by growth in inputs such as labour and capital, and is attributed to technology, organisation, and efficiency. National statistical agencies such as the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics publish productivity series for economies and industries.

Determinants

Productivity depends on physical capital, technology, human capital (education and skills), management practices, infrastructure, and institutions. Economists have long debated episodes of productivity slowdown, such as that experienced in many advanced economies after the 1970s and again after the mid-2000s, and the apparent paradox that new digital technologies did not immediately show up in measured productivity.
