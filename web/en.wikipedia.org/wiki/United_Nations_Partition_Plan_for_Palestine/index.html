The United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine was a proposal by the United Nations, adopted as General Assembly Resolution 181 (II) on 29 November 1947, that recommended the termination of the British Mandate for Palestine and the partition of the territory into independent Arab and Jewish states, with Jerusalem placed under a special international regime (corpus separatum).

Background and terms

The plan followed the work of the UN Special Committee on Palestine (UNSCOP), appointed after Britain announced it would give up the Mandate. The proposed Jewish state received about 56 per cent of the territory, including the coastal plain and the Negev desert; the Arab state about 43 per cent; and the Jerusalem–Bethlehem area was to be internationalised. The two states were to be joined in an economic union and come into existence after a transitional period.

Responses and aftermath

The Jewish Agency accepted the plan, while the Arab Higher Committee and the Arab states rejected it as unjust and illegal. Civil war between the Jewish and Arab communities in Palestine began the day after the vote. Britain ended the Mandate on 14 May 1948; Israel declared independence the same day, and the ensuing 1948 Arab–Israeli War rendered the partition map obsolete: Israel emerged controlling more territory than allotted, the West Bank came under Jordanian rule and Gaza under Egyptian administration, and hundreds of thousands of Palestinian Arabs became refugees — events at the heart of the continuing Israeli–Palestinian conflict.
