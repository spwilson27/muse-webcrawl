Circassia

Circassia is a historical region and a country in the North Caucasus along the northeast shore of the Black Sea. It is the traditional homeland of the Circassian (Adyghe) people, who were largely expelled from the region in the 19th century following the Russian conquest of the Caucasus.

Geography

Historical Circassia stretched from the Taman Peninsula in the west to the area of present-day North Ossetia in the east, bounded by the Black Sea to the south and the Kuban River to the north. Today the territory lies mainly within the Russian federal subjects of Krasnodar Krai, Adygea, Karachay-Cherkessia, Kabardino-Balkaria and Stavropol Krai.

History

The Circassians, among the oldest indigenous peoples of the North Caucasus, formed a federation of tribal principalities governed by customary law known as Adyghe Xabze. From the late 18th century, the Russian Empire expanded into the region, leading to the Russo-Circassian War, which ended in 1864 with the mass expulsion and deaths of much of the Circassian population — events many Circassians and some scholars describe as genocide. A large diaspora now lives in Turkey, Jordan, Syria and elsewhere.

Within Russia, Circassians are concentrated in the republics of Adygea, Kabardino-Balkaria and Karachay-Cherkessia. Circassian national symbols include the green flag with twelve gold stars and three crossed arrows.
