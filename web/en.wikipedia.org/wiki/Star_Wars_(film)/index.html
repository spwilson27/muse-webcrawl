Star Wars (later retitled Star Wars: Episode IV – A New Hope) is a 1977 American epic space opera film written and directed by George Lucas, produced by Lucasfilm and distributed by 20th Century-Fox. It stars Mark Hamill, Harrison Ford, Carrie Fisher, Peter Cushing, Alec Guinness, David Prowse, James Earl Jones, Anthony Daniels, Kenny Baker and Peter Mayhew.

Plot

A long time ago in a galaxy far, far away, the Galactic Empire rules through fear and is completing the Death Star, a battle station capable of destroying a planet. Princess Leia, a leader of the Rebel Alliance, hides the station's plans in the droids R2-D2 and C-3PO, who escape to the desert planet Tatooine. There they are bought by moisture farmer Owen Lars and his nephew Luke Skywalker. Luke meets the hermit Obi-Wan Kenobi, a former Jedi Knight, who tells him of the Force and gives him his father's lightsaber. After stormtroopers murder Luke's family, the group hires smuggler Han Solo and his co-pilot Chewbacca to rescue the imprisoned Leia. Obi-Wan is killed duelling Darth Vader, but Luke, guided by Obi-Wan's spirit and trusting the Force, destroys the Death Star during the Rebel assault.

Production

Lucas conceived the film after completing American Graffiti (1973), drawing on Flash Gordon serials, Akira Kurosawa's The Hidden Fortress, and Joseph Campbell's writings on the hero's journey. Filming began in Tunisia in March 1976 and continued at Elstree Studios in England; the production was troubled by weather, malfunctioning props and creative conflict. Lucas founded Industrial Light & Magic to create the film's unprecedented special effects. John Williams's orchestral score, performed by the London Symphony Orchestra, became one of the most famous in cinema history.

Release and reception

Initially released in only 32 theatres, the film became a phenomenal blockbuster, at the time the highest-grossing film ever made, and it is credited with launching the era of summer blockbusters and revitalising special-effects filmmaking. It won six Academy Awards, including Best Visual Effects and Best Original Score. The film spawned the Star Wars franchise, beginning with the sequels The Empire Strikes Back (1980) and Return of the Jedi (1983). In 1989 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry. A Special Edition with altered scenes and enhanced effects was released in 1997.
