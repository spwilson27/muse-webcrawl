José Onuchic

José Nelson Onuchic is a Brazilian-American physicist and theoretical chemist known for his work on the theory of protein folding, including the energy landscape theory and the principle of minimal frustration. He is the Harry C. and Olga K. Wiess Professor of Physics and Astronomy at Rice University and co-director of the Center for Theoretical Biological Physics (CTBP).

Education and career

Onuchic studied physics and engineering at the University of São Paulo and earned his doctorate from the California Institute of Technology. He spent much of his career at the University of California, San Diego, before moving to Rice University, where he co-directs the Center for Theoretical Biological Physics, a Physics Frontiers Center supported by the National Science Foundation.

Research

Working with Peter Wolynes and others, Onuchic helped develop the energy landscape theory of protein folding, which describes folding as diffusion on a funnel-shaped energy landscape shaped by evolution to be minimally frustrated. His group has applied statistical mechanics and computational methods to protein folding and function, electron transfer in biological systems, gene regulatory networks, cancer biology, and the physical organization of chromosomes and genomes, including work connecting genome structure to folding dynamics.

Honors

Onuchic is a member of the United States National Academy of Sciences, a fellow of the American Physical Society, and a member of the Brazilian Academy of Sciences. He received the 2012 Max Delbrück Prize in Biological Physics from the American Physical Society.
