The Hornbill Festival is an annual cultural festival held from 1 to 10 December in the state of Nagaland in north-eastern India. Organised by the Government of Nagaland, it brings together the state's Naga tribes at the Naga Heritage Village in Kisama, about 12 km from the capital Kohima, to celebrate and preserve their distinct traditions of dance, music, dress, food, and craft.

The festival is named after the great hornbill, a bird prominent in Naga folklore and symbolism. It was first held in 2000 to encourage inter-tribal interaction and to promote Nagaland's cultural heritage and tourism.

Events

Each of Nagaland's recognised tribes maintains a traditional hut, or morung, at the heritage village, and performs dances and songs in ceremonial dress. The programme includes a traditional archery and wrestling, indigenous games, a chilli-eating contest centred on the fiery Naga king chilli, craft and food stalls, and the Hornbill International Rock Contest, which draws bands from across India and abroad. The festival closes on 10 December, Nagaland's Statehood Day celebrations having opened the season.

Significance

The Hornbill Festival has become Nagaland's flagship tourism event, drawing large numbers of domestic and international visitors each December and anchoring a wider season of tribal festivals across the state.
