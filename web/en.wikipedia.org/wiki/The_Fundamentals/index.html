The Fundamentals: A Testimony to the Truth is a set of ninety essays published between 1910 and 1915 in twelve volumes, defending orthodox Protestant beliefs against liberal theology, higher criticism of the Bible, Darwinian evolution, and other movements. Financed by California oil magnates Lyman and Milton Stewart, some three million copies were distributed free of charge to pastors, missionaries, students and other Christian workers. The publication is generally regarded as the founding document of Christian fundamentalism, a term coined in 1920 by the Baptist editor Curtis Lee Laws, who described those ready "to do battle royal for the Fundamentals".

Contents

The essays were written by 64 authors from the United States, Canada and Great Britain, including prominent conservatives such as B. B. Warfield of Princeton Seminary, James Orr of Glasgow, and R. A. Torrey. Five doctrines became especially associated with the movement:

  1. The inerrancy of Scripture.
  2. The virgin birth and deity of Jesus Christ.
  3. The substitutionary atonement of Christ's death.
  4. The bodily resurrection of Christ.
  5. The authenticity of the miracles recorded in the Bible (in some formulations, the future second coming of Christ).

History and influence

Lyman Stewart, co-founder of the Union Oil Company, underwrote the project with some $300,000. The volumes were published by the Testimony Publishing Company of Chicago and sent free to roughly three million recipients. Although the essays were often moderate in tone — some authors accepted an old earth and a limited role for evolution — the series lent its name to a more separatist and militant movement that crystallised in the 1920s, in the controversies surrounding the Scopes Trial of 1925 and the fundamentalist–modernist disputes within the mainline denominations.
