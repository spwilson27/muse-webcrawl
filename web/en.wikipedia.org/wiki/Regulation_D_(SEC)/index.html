Regulation D (SEC)

Regulation D (Reg D) is a regulation of the United States Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), adopted under the Securities Act of 1933, that provides exemptions from the registration requirements of federal securities law for certain private offerings of securities.

Rules

The principal exemptions are found in Rules 504 and 506. Rule 506(b) permits an issuer to raise an unlimited amount from an unlimited number of accredited investors and up to 35 non-accredited investors, provided there is no general solicitation. Rule 506(c), added under the JOBS Act, permits general solicitation if all purchasers are accredited investors and the issuer takes reasonable steps to verify that status. Rule 504 covers smaller offerings, generally up to $10 million in a 12-month period. Issuers relying on Regulation D must file a notice on Form D with the SEC. Securities sold under Regulation D are restricted securities and cannot be freely resold.
