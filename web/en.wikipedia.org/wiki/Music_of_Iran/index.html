Music of Iran

The music of Iran (Persian music) encompasses music produced in the territories of Iran and the Persianate world. With roots reaching back thousands of years — musical instruments appear in Elamite and Sasanian art — Persian art music is built on a modal system of melodic frameworks called dastgah.

The tar, one of the principal instruments of Persian classical music.

The dastgah system

Persian classical music is organised into twelve modal systems: seven dastgahs and five subsidiary avaz. The complete repertoire, the radif, consists of several hundred short melodic movements called gushehs, memorised by master musicians and used as the basis for improvisation. A performance typically moves from a slow, non-metric improvisation through composed pieces (tasnif) and virtuosic rhythmic sections known as chaharmezrab and reng.

The radif of the master Mirza Abdollah was inscribed by UNESCO in 2009 on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Instruments

Principal instruments include the tar and setar (long-necked lutes), the santur (hammered dulcimer), the kamancheh (spike fiddle), the ney (end-blown flute), the daf and tombak (percussion), and the oud. The vocal tradition, avaz, sets classical Persian poetry — Rumi, Hafez, Saadi — to improvised melody.

History

Under the Sassanian Empire, court musicians such as Barbad were celebrated. After the Islamic conquest, music theory flourished with scholars such as al-Farabi and Safi al-Din. The modern system crystallised in the Qajar era of the 19th century, when the radif was codified.

Popular and modern music

Before the 1979 revolution, Iran had a thriving pop industry with stars such as Googoosh, Dariush, and Ebi. After the revolution, public music performance was severely restricted, and much of the industry moved to Los Angeles ("Tehrangeles"). Within Iran, permitted traditional and revolutionary music continued, and an underground rock and rap scene later developed. Noted classical vocalists include Mohammad-Reza Shajarian, regarded as the greatest master of Persian traditional singing, and instrumentalists such as tar player Hossein Alizadeh and kamancheh player Kayhan Kalhor.
