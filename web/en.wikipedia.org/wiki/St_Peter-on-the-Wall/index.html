Chapel of St Peter-on-the-Wall

The Chapel of St Peter-on-the-Wall is an Anglo-Saxon chapel standing on the seawall at Bradwell-on-Sea in Essex, England. Built around 654 AD by St Cedd, a missionary from Lindisfarne, on the wall of the Roman Saxon Shore fort of Othona, it is among the oldest largely intact churches in England and remains in regular use.

History

According to Bede's Ecclesiastical History, Cedd came south from Northumbria in 653 to evangelise the East Saxons at the request of King Sigeberht the Good. He built the church using stone and Roman brick taken from the ruined fort. After Cedd's death from plague in 664, the chapel served the Saxon community; it later fell out of ecclesiastical use and was for centuries used as a barn. It was restored and reconsecrated in 1920.

Description

The simple rectangular nave, built of stone rubble and Roman tiles, measures about 15 metres by 8 metres. The original apse was demolished in the medieval period. The interior is austere, with small round-headed windows cut high in the walls. The chapel sits alone on the seawall at the end of a track, a mile from the village, facing the Blackwater estuary.

Today

Each July the Bradwell Pilgrimage draws walkers to the chapel, continuing a tradition started in the 20th century. The nearby Othona Community, founded in 1946, is named after the Roman fort.
