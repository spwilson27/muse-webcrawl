Anatoly Alexeyevich Karatsuba (31 January 1937 – 28 September 2008) was a Soviet and Russian mathematician working in the fields of analytic number theory, p-adic analysis, and the theory of algorithms. He is best known for the Karatsuba algorithm, the first sub-quadratic method for multiplying large numbers and a foundational result in the theory of fast algorithms.

Early life and education

Karatsuba was born in Grozny in 1937. He studied at the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of Moscow State University, graduating in 1959. In 1962 he defended his candidate's dissertation under the supervision of Nikolay Korobov, and in 1966 he obtained the Doctor of Sciences degree. He spent much of his career at the Steklov Institute of Mathematics.

The Karatsuba algorithm

In 1960 Karatsuba discovered that the product of two n-digit numbers can be computed with only about nlog23 ≈ n1.585 elementary operations, rather than the n2 required by the classical schoolbook method. The result was a direct refutation of a conjecture by Andrey Kolmogorov, who had believed the quadratic lower bound to be optimal; Kolmogorov was reportedly so struck by the result that he published it under Karatsuba's name in the Proceedings of the USSR Academy of Sciences in 1962.

The discovery launched the field of fast arithmetic algorithms and led, via the work of Toom and Cook, to the Schönhage–Strassen algorithm and modern FFT-based multiplication.

Other work

Karatsuba made numerous contributions to analytic number theory, including estimates of character sums, the method of trigonometric sums, and work on the Riemann zeta function.
