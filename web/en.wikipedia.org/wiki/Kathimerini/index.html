Kathimerini (Greek: Η Καθημερινή, meaning "The Daily") is a daily newspaper published in Athens, Greece. Founded in 1919 by Georgios Vlachos, it is regarded as Greece's newspaper of record and has traditionally been associated with liberal-conservative and pro-European positions.

History

Kathimerini was established in the aftermath of the First World War and became a leading voice of the anti-Venizelist and later conservative camp. Publication was suspended during the Greek military junta (1967–1974) after the paper refused to submit to censorship; it resumed after the restoration of democracy. Ownership passed through the Vlachos family to the Kathimerini publishing group; in the 2010s the paper's ownership changed amid Greece's debt crisis.

Editions

An English-language edition, founded in 2002, was distributed for years as an insert with the International Herald Tribune (later the International New York Times) in Greece and Cyprus, and continues online. The Sunday edition is noted for in-depth features, and the paper also publishes the financial newspaper and cultural supplements.
