Qissa Khwani massacre

The Qissa Khwani massacre took place on 23 April 1930 in the Qissa Khwani Bazaar of Peshawar, British India, when troops of the British Indian Army opened fire on unarmed demonstrators of the Khudai Khidmatgar movement. It was one of the defining episodes of the nonviolent Pashtun resistance led by Abdul Ghaffar Khan.

Background

The Khudai Khidmatgar ("Servants of God"), known as the Red Shirts, was a nonviolent movement opposing British rule in the North-West Frontier Province. When its leaders, including Abdul Ghaffar Khan, were arrested in April 1930, crowds gathered in protest in Peshawar's historic storytellers' bazaar.

The shooting

Armoured cars drove into the crowd and troops fired on the demonstrators for an extended period. Official figures put the death toll at around 20; Indian sources estimated 200–400 killed. Notably, platoons of the Royal Garhwal Rifles refused orders to fire on the unarmed crowd, an act of defiance for which the soldiers were severely punished. The massacre drew international attention and strengthened the independence movement.
