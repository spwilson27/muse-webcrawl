Du'a

In Islam, duʿāʾ (Arabic: دعاء, "invocation" or "supplication") is the act of calling upon God with requests, praise or appeals for help. It is a central devotional practice, distinct from the ritual prayer (ṣalāh), and may be performed at any time, in any language, though many believers use memorised Arabic formulas drawn from the Qur'an and hadith.

Practice

Supplicants traditionally raise their open hands to shoulder level, palms upward, and wipe their faces with their hands at the end. Du'a is considered the essence of worship in a well-known hadith, and the Qur'an enjoins believers to call upon God, promising a response. Supplication is especially emphasised during Ramadan, on the Night of Decree (Laylat al-Qadr), after obligatory prayers, and while travelling or fasting.

Forms and collections

Canonical collections of supplications include al-Nawawi's Kitab al-Adhkar and, in Shia Islam, works such as Mafatih al-Jinan, which contains the famous Du'a Kumayl. Sufi traditions developed litanies (awrad) and communal sessions of invocation closely related to dhikr.
