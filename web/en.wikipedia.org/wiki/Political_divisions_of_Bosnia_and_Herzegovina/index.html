Political divisions of Bosnia and Herzegovina

The political divisions of Bosnia and Herzegovina were created by the Dayton Agreement, which recognized the country as a federation of two entities: the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and Republika Srpska, plus the self-governing Brčko District. The agreement was signed in 1995, ending the Bosnian War.

Structure

The state-level government is weak by design, with most powers reserved to the entities. The country is led by a tripartite Presidency, with one Bosniak, one Croat, and one Serb member, and a bicameral Parliamentary Assembly.

Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina

The Federation, covering roughly 51% of the territory, is itself divided into ten cantons, each with its own government, assembly, and extensive powers over education, police, and health. The cantons are: Una-Sana, Posavina, Tuzla, Zenica-Doboj, Bosnian-Podrinje, Central Bosnia, Herzegovina-Neretva, West Herzegovina, Sarajevo, and Canton 10 (Herzeg-Bosnia).

Republika Srpska

Republika Srpska, covering roughly 49% of the territory, has a unitary government and is divided into municipalities (opštine) without an intermediate regional tier in administrative law, though it recognizes regions for statistical purposes. Its capital is Banja Luka, while Sarajevo is its official, de jure seat.

Brčko District

The Brčko District in the northeast is a self-governing administrative unit held in condominium by both entities but administered independently under international supervision, a status formalized by arbitration in 1999–2000.

Municipalities

Below the entity and canton levels, Bosnia and Herzegovina is divided into municipalities (općina / општина) and cities (grad), numbering around 140 in total. The capital, Sarajevo, lies within the Federation and is itself composed of four municipalities.
