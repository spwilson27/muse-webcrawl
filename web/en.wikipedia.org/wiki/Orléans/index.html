Orléans is a city in north-central France, about 120 kilometres southwest of Paris. It is the prefecture of the department of Loiret and the capital of the region of Centre-Val de Loire, situated on a bend of the river Loire. The city is forever associated with Joan of Arc, who lifted its siege in 1429.

History

Founded as Cenabum, a Gallic stronghold of the Carnutes, the city was destroyed by Julius Caesar in 52 BC and rebuilt under the Roman emperor Aurelian, from whom its present name derives (Aurelianum). In 451 it resisted Attila the Hun. During the Hundred Years' War, English forces besieged Orléans from October 1428; the arrival of Joan of Arc in April 1429 and the relief of the city on 8 May was a turning point of the war, earning her the epithet "the Maid of Orléans". The city celebrates the Fêtes de Jeanne d'Arc every year.

Landmarks

The Gothic Orléans Cathedral (Sainte-Croix), rebuilt from the 13th to the 18th centuries, dominates the city; its stained glass recounts Joan of Arc's story. Other sights include the Renaissance Hôtel Groslot, the Maison de Jeanne d'Arc, and the Musée des Beaux-Arts. The historic centre, with its half-timbered houses, forms part of the Val de Loire UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Economy and transport

Orléans is a logistics hub close to Paris and home to pharmaceutical, cosmetics, and food industries, as well as the University of Orléans, founded in 1306. It lends its name to the cadet branch of the French royal house of Orléans and to the city of New Orleans in Louisiana.
