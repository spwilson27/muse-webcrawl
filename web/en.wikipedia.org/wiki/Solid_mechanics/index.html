Solid mechanics

Solid mechanics (also known as mechanics of solids) is the branch of continuum mechanics that studies the behaviour of solid materials, especially their motion and deformation under the action of forces, temperature changes, phase changes, and other external or internal agents. It provides the theoretical foundation for civil, aerospace, mechanical, and materials engineering, and for much of geophysics.

Deformation of a loaded beam — a classical problem of solid mechanics.

Fundamental concepts

Solid mechanics describes a body by fields such as displacement, strain, and stress. The stress tensor relates internal forces across imaginary surfaces within the material; the strain tensor measures local deformation relative to a reference configuration. The two are connected by a constitutive equation that encodes the material's response:

  • Elasticity — reversible deformation; in the linear case, Hooke's law relates stress and strain through elastic moduli such as Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio.
  • Plasticity — permanent deformation after a yield stress is exceeded, described by yield criteria (e.g. von Mises, Tresca) and flow rules.
  • Viscoelasticity and creep — time-dependent deformation found in polymers, and in metals at high temperature.
  • Fracture mechanics — the study of crack initiation and growth, using quantities such as the stress intensity factor and fracture toughness.

Equations and methods

The governing equations combine the balance of linear and angular momentum with the constitutive law and kinematic (strain–displacement) relations, subject to boundary conditions. Closed-form solutions exist for simple geometries — bars in tension, shafts in torsion, beams in bending, thick-walled cylinders — but most practical problems are solved numerically, above all by the finite element method. Classical strength-of-materials treatments trace back to Galileo's analysis of beams and the work of Euler, Bernoulli, Cauchy, and Saint-Venant.

Applications

The subject underlies the design of bridges, buildings, pressure vessels, aircraft structures, machine components, and biomedical implants, as well as the analysis of earthquakes, rock deformation, and ice-sheet flow.
