Time constant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In physics and engineering, the time constant, usually denoted by the Greek letter τ (tau), is the parameter characterising the response to a step input of a first-order, linear time-invariant system. The time constant is the time taken for the response to reach approximately 63.2% of its final (asymptotic) value in a rising response, or to decay to about 36.8% of its initial value in a falling response.

Examples

For an RC circuit the time constant is τ = RC; for an RL circuit it is τ = L/R. Thermal systems, radioactive decay and the discharge of a capacitor all display the same exponential character, with the half-life related to the time constant by t1/2 = τ ln 2. After about five time constants the response is within 1% of its final value, a rule used to estimate settling time in electronics and control engineering.
