Dicer is an endoribonuclease of the RNase III family that cleaves double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) and precursor microRNA (pre-miRNA) into short double-stranded fragments — small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) and mature microRNAs (miRNAs) — typically 21 to 25 base pairs long with two-nucleotide 3′ overhangs. These small RNAs are loaded into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), whose Argonaute proteins use them as guides to silence complementary target RNAs. Dicer is therefore an essential component of the RNA interference (RNAi) pathway.

Discovery

The enzyme was identified in 2001 by Emily Bernstein and colleagues in the laboratory of Gregory Hannon, working with extracts of the fruit fly Drosophila melanogaster; the name "Dicer" reflects its ability to dice long dsRNA into small fragments. Homologues were rapidly found across eukaryotes, including a single Dicer in mammals and multiple Dicer-like proteins in plants (DCL1–DCL4 in Arabidopsis), which divide the labour of producing different small-RNA classes.

Structure and mechanism

Human Dicer is a large (~220 kDa) multidomain protein. From N- to C-terminus it contains a DExD/H-box helicase domain, a DUF283 domain, a PAZ domain that binds the 3′ end of RNA, two RNase III domains that form the catalytic core and cut both strands of the substrate, and a dsRNA-binding domain. The distance between the PAZ domain and the RNase III active sites acts as a molecular ruler, setting the characteristic ~22-nucleotide length of the products. In mammals Dicer works with the dsRNA-binding partners TRBP and PACT, which aid substrate processing and RISC loading.

Products and function

In the canonical microRNA pathway, the nuclear Microprocessor complex (Drosha and DGCR8) first crops primary miRNA transcripts into ~70-nucleotide hairpin pre-miRNAs; after export to the cytoplasm, Dicer removes the loop, yielding the mature miRNA duplex. In the siRNA pathway, Dicer directly processes long dsRNA — from viruses, transposons or experimental triggers — into siRNAs. Loss of Dicer is lethal in mouse development, and conditional knockouts reveal roles in stem-cell maintenance, differentiation and neuronal function.

Clinical significance

Germline mutations in the human DICER1 gene cause DICER1 syndrome, a tumour predisposition syndrome associated with pleuropulmonary blastoma, cystic nephroma, multinodular goitre and other rare tumours, often involving second "hotspot" mutations in the RNase IIIb domain that skew cleavage toward one arm of the hairpin. Altered Dicer expression has also been reported in a variety of cancers and in viral infection, where many viruses encode suppressors of RNA silencing.
