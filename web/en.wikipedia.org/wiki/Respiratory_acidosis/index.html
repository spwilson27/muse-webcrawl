Respiratory acidosis

Respiratory acidosis is a state in which decreased ventilation (hypoventilation) increases the concentration of carbon dioxide in the blood and decreases the blood's pH (a condition generally called acidosis). Carbon dioxide is produced continuously as the body's source of energy, and accumulates rapidly if the lungs do not adequately expel it through alveolar ventilation. It can be acute or chronic.

Causes

Respiratory acidosis can result from diseases of the airways such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), central respiratory depression from drugs or brain injury, neuromuscular disorders such as myasthenia gravis, and chest wall abnormalities. Acute respiratory acidosis occurs in acute respiratory failure, while in chronic respiratory acidosis the kidneys compensate by retaining bicarbonate.

Symptoms

Symptoms depend on the rate and degree of the increase in carbon dioxide and may include headache, anxiety, blurred vision, restlessness, confusion, and in severe cases asterixis, decreased level of consciousness, and coma.
