Académie française

The Académie française is France's pre-eminent learned society for matters of the French language. Founded in 1635 by Cardinal Richelieu, it consists of forty members known as les immortels ("the immortals"), elected for life to numbered chairs. It is the senior of the five academies of the Institut de France and meets in the Mazarin Palace in Paris.

Role

The Académie's statutes charge it with fixing the usage of French and producing a dictionary — the first edition appeared in 1694 and a ninth has been in progress since the late 20th century. It issues opinions on spelling and grammar, resists (often symbolically) the influx of Anglicisms, and awards literary prizes, notably the Grand Prix du roman.

Membership and customs

New members are elected by existing academicians to a vacant numbered seat and received in a ceremony under the dome, wearing the famous green embroidered habit (habit vert) and carrying a ceremonial sword. Members have included many of France's greatest writers; the first woman, Marguerite Yourcenar, was elected only in 1980.
