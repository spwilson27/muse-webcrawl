Tumor

A tumor (also spelled tumour in British English) is an abnormal mass of tissue that results when cells divide and grow excessively, or fail to die when they should. Tumors can be benign (non-cancerous), premalignant, or malignant (cancerous). The word comes from the Latin tumor, meaning "swelling", one of the classic signs of inflammation described since antiquity. In modern medical usage, "tumor" generally refers to a neoplasm — a proliferation of genetically abnormal cells — rather than to swelling from other causes such as oedema or inflammation.

Classification

Benign tumors grow locally, are usually encapsulated, and do not invade surrounding tissue or spread to distant sites; examples include many lipomas (fatty tumors), fibroids (leiomyomas) and adenomas. Malignant tumors (cancers) invade adjacent tissues and may metastasize through the bloodstream or lymphatic system to form secondary tumors elsewhere in the body. Tumors are further classified by the tissue type from which they arise — for example carcinomas from epithelial tissue, sarcomas from connective tissue, leukaemias from blood-forming cells, and lymphomas from lymphoid tissue.

Causes

Neoplasms arise from accumulated genetic mutations affecting cell growth, division and death, driven by the interplay of inherited susceptibility, environmental carcinogens (such as tobacco smoke, ultraviolet radiation and certain chemicals), infections (for example human papillomavirus and hepatitis viruses), chronic inflammation and chance errors in DNA replication. Genes central to this process include oncogenes, which promote growth when activated, and tumor suppressor genes, which restrain it when functioning normally.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis typically involves imaging (such as CT, MRI or ultrasound), laboratory tests, and definitive examination of tissue by biopsy under histopathology. Treatment depends on tumor type, location and stage, and may include surgical removal, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted drugs and hormonal therapy, often in combination.
