The Battle of Dunkeld was fought on 21 August 1689 between Jacobite Highlanders and a government regiment of Covenanters, the Cameronian Regiment, in and around the town of Dunkeld, Perthshire. It formed part of the first Jacobite rising in support of the deposed James VII and II. The Cameronians' stubborn defence of the town broke the momentum of the rising after the Jacobite victory at Killiecrankie.

Background

At the Battle of Killiecrankie on 27 July 1689 the Jacobites under Viscount Dundee shattered a government army, but Dundee himself was killed in the moment of victory. Command passed to Alexander Cannon, who led the Highland army south toward Perth. To block him, the newly raised Cameronian Regiment — some 800 staunch Presbyterians recruited from the southwest — occupied Dunkeld, a town with no walls, dominated by its cathedral.

The battle

The Cameronians barricaded the streets and held Dunkeld House and the cathedral precinct. On 21 August a Jacobite force of perhaps 4,000–5,000 Highlanders attacked. Fighting raged from house to house for some sixteen hours; the Cameronians stripped lead from the roofs of Dunkeld House and the cathedral to cast bullets when ammunition ran short. Their colonel, William Cleland, was killed early in the action, and command passed to Captain George Munro. Exhausted and unable to dislodge the defenders, the Jacobites withdrew during the night, having suffered heavy casualties.

Aftermath

The defence of Dunkeld was a severe check to the Jacobite rising. Cannon's army melted away, and although fighting continued into 1690, the rising effectively collapsed after the government victory at Cromdale. The Cameronian Regiment went on to a long career in the British Army, later numbered the 26th Foot.
