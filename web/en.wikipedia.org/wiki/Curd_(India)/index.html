Curd (India)

Dahi set in a traditional earthenware bowl.

Curd, called dahi in Hindi and by related names across South Asia (doi in Bengali, thayir in Tamil, perugu in Telugu, mosaru in Kannada), is a traditional fermented dairy product of the Indian subcontinent, made by inoculating warm milk with a small amount of existing curd and allowing lactic acid bacteria to set it. It is a form of yogurt, though typically prepared with mixed household cultures rather than defined commercial strains.

Preparation and use

Milk — traditionally cow or buffalo milk — is boiled, cooled to lukewarm, mixed with a spoonful of starter curd and left undisturbed for several hours, often in an earthen pot. The result is a staple of everyday meals across India, eaten plain with rice and sugar or salt, whisked into raita, or seasoned as pachadi.

Dishes and drinks

Curd is the base of lassi (sweet or salted), chaas (spiced buttermilk, made by churning curd and removing the butter), curd rice — a comfort food of South India tempered with mustard seeds and curry leaves — and marinades for kebabs and biryanis. It features in shrikhand, mishti doi (sweetened, caramelized curd of Bengal) and many raitas.

Cultural significance

Curd holds ritual importance: a spoonful of curd and sugar is traditionally eaten before journeys and examinations for luck, and curd is offered in Hindu ceremonies. In Ayurveda it is classed as cooling and digestive, though its heaviness is debated.
