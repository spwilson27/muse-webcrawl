El Badi Palace (Arabic: قصر البديع, "Palace of the Incomparable") is a ruined palace in Marrakech, Morocco. Commissioned by the Saadian sultan Ahmad al-Mansur al-Dhahabi shortly after his accession in 1578, it was built with the wealth brought by the ransom and spoils of the Battle of the Three Kings and by the sultan's control of the trans-Saharan trade. Contemporary accounts describe it as one of the most splendid palaces of its age.

History

Construction began around 1578 and continued for many years, with the palace essentially complete by the early 1590s though work continued until al-Mansur's death in 1603. The palace served for royal receptions, diplomatic audiences and festivities. After the fall of the Saadian dynasty, the Alaouite sultan Moulay Ismail systematically stripped the palace in the late 17th century, transporting its marble, onyx and other precious materials to embellish his new capital at Meknes. The operation reportedly took over a decade, leaving only the massive pisé (rammed earth) walls and foundations.

Layout and decoration

The palace is organised around a vast rectangular courtyard, roughly 135 by 110 metres, with a large central water basin and sunken gardens. Pavilions stood on the eastern and western sides, including the domed Crystal Pavilion and the Audience Pavilion. The complex incorporated Italian marble — obtained, according to tradition, in exchange for an equal weight of Moroccan sugar — as well as onyx, gold leaf, ivory, carved cedarwood and elaborate zellij tilework. Its design shows affinities with Nasrid palace architecture such as the Alhambra in Granada.

Present day

Today the palace is a major tourist attraction in Marrakech. Its walls are famous as a nesting site for storks. The site includes a small museum housing the restored 12th-century minbar of the Koutoubia Mosque, and a section of the original Saadian-era hydraulic system and pavilions has been partially restored. The palace grounds host the annual Marrakech du Rire comedy festival.
