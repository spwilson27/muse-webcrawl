Karen Carpenter

Karen Anne Carpenter (March 2, 1950 – February 4, 1983) was an American singer and drummer who formed half of the sibling duo the Carpenters with her older brother Richard. Possessed of a distinctive three-octave contralto voice, she has been praised by fellow musicians and critics as one of the greatest singers of all time.

Early life

Carpenter was born in New Haven, Connecticut, and moved with her family to Downey, California, in 1963. She took up the drums in high school, displaying exceptional natural talent, and began singing with Richard's jazz-influenced groups. The Carpenters signed with A&M Records in 1969.

Career

The duo achieved enormous success in the 1970s with soft-pop hits including "(They Long to Be) Close to You" (1970), "We've Only Just Begun", "Rainy Days and Mondays", "Superstar", "Top of the World" and "Yesterday Once More". Karen initially sang from behind the drum kit before moving to centre stage as frontwoman. Her unreleased solo album, recorded in 1979–80 with producer Phil Ramone, was shelved by the label and finally released in 1996.

Death and legacy

Carpenter suffered for years from anorexia nervosa, at a time when the condition was little understood publicly. She died on February 4, 1983, at age 32, of heart failure caused by complications of the disease. Her death brought widespread attention to eating disorders. Rolling Stone ranked her among the 100 greatest singers of all time, and her recordings remain staples of adult-contemporary radio.
