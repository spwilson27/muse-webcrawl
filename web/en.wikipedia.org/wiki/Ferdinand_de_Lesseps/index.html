Ferdinand de Lesseps

Ferdinand Marie, Comte de Lesseps (19 November 1805 – 7 December 1894) was a French diplomat and canal developer, celebrated as the builder of the Suez Canal and later disgraced by the failed attempt to build the Panama Canal.

Suez Canal

While serving as a diplomat in Egypt, Lesseps formed a friendship with Sa'id Pasha, who as ruler of Egypt granted him a concession in 1854 to build a canal across the Isthmus of Suez. Lesseps raised capital through the Suez Canal Company, and construction ran from 1859 to 1869. The canal opened on 17 November 1869, transforming trade between Europe and Asia.

Panama scandal

In 1879 Lesseps championed a sea-level canal in Panama. His Panama Canal Company collapsed in 1889 amid disease, engineering failures, and cost overruns, in one of the largest bankruptcies of the century. In the ensuing Panama scandals, Lesseps and his son Charles were convicted of fraud and bribery (sentences later overturned on a technicality).
