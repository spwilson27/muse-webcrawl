Storegga Slide

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Storegga Slides (Norwegian: Storeggaskredet) are among the largest known submarine landslides. They occurred on the edge of Norway's continental shelf, in the Norwegian Sea, around 8,200 years ago (approximately 6225–6170 BCE). The collapse involved an estimated 2,400–3,500 km3 of sediment along some 290 km of the continental slope, roughly 100 km off the coast of More og Romsdal.

Tsunami

The slide triggered a very large tsunami in the North Atlantic Ocean. Deposits of the tsunami are found in Scotland, Norway, Iceland, Greenland, and the Faroe and Shetland Islands. Run-up heights have been estimated at more than 20 metres on Shetland and parts of Norway, and 3–6 metres on the Scottish mainland coast. The wave inundated Doggerland, the low-lying land bridge then connecting Britain to mainland Europe, and is thought to have devastated Mesolithic coastal communities; it may have contributed to the final separation of Britain from the continent.

Cause

The failure is attributed to the destabilization of gas hydrates and the accumulation of weak glacial sediments on the continental slope during and after the last ice age. Earthquakes or the dissociation of methane hydrates as ocean temperatures rose may have triggered the collapse. The Storegga area is now the site of the Ormen Lange gas field, whose development required careful assessment of slope stability.
