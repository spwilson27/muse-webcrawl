Merrie Melodies is an American animated comedy short film series produced by Warner Bros. from 1931 to 1969, during the golden age of American animation. It was originally produced by Harman-Ising until 1933, and then by Leon Schlesinger Productions until 1944, when Schlesinger sold his studio to Warner Bros. Together with its sister series Looney Tunes, Merrie Melodies introduced characters such as Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, Porky Pig, Elmer Fudd, Yosemite Sam, the Tasmanian Devil and Speedy Gonzales.

Origins and format

The series was conceived to showcase Warner Bros.' music catalog: each early Merrie Melodies short was built around a song from the studio's publishing holdings, which the cartoon effectively advertised. The title is a play on Walt Disney's Silly Symphonies. Originally Merrie Melodies were produced in colour (first two-colour, then three-strip Technicolor from 1934) while Looney Tunes remained black-and-white; by the mid-1940s both series were in colour and the practical distinction between them disappeared — the Merrie Melodies title card simply used blue rings and, eventually, different opening music, ending with "Merrily We Roll Along".

Legacy

The combined Looney Tunes/Merrie Melodies library comprises more than a thousand shorts. Many are regarded among the greatest cartoons ever made: titles such as What's Opera, Doc? (1957), directed by Chuck Jones and frequently voted the greatest cartoon of all time, were released under the Merrie Melodies banner. The shorts formed the basis of television packages, feature compilations and the 1996 film Space Jam.
