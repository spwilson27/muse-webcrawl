Singular homology

In algebraic topology, singular homology refers to the study of a certain set of algebraic objects (abelian groups) associated to a given topological space. Intuitively, singular homology counts the different ways n-dimensional "holes" can be detected in the space.

Construction

A singular n-simplex in a topological space X is a continuous map from the standard n-simplex Δn into X. The singular chain group Cn(X) is the free abelian group generated by all such maps. The boundary operator ∂n sends a simplex to the alternating sum of its restrictions to its faces, and satisfies ∂∘∂ = 0, giving a chain complex. The singular homology groups are then defined as the homology of this complex:

Hn(X) = ker(∂n) / im(∂n+1)

Properties

Singular homology is a functor from topological spaces to graded abelian groups. Homotopic maps induce the same map on homology, and homotopy-equivalent spaces have isomorphic homology groups. Singular homology satisfies the Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms and agrees with simplicial homology on spaces that admit triangulations, but it applies to arbitrary topological spaces.

Examples

H0(X) counts path components. The homology of a point is ℤ in degree 0 and vanishes above it. The circle S1 has H1 ≅ ℤ, reflecting its single loop, and the n-sphere Sn has Hn ≅ ℤ.
