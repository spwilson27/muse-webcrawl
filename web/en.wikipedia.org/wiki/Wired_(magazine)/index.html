Wired is an American magazine and website focused on how emerging technologies affect culture, the economy, and politics. Founded in 1993 by Louis Rossetto and Jane Metcalfe in San Francisco, it became the flagship publication of the digital revolution, known for its neon, heavily art-directed design and its optimistic "Wired" worldview.

History

The magazine's early contributors included Nicholas Negroponte and Stewart Brand, and its founding executive editor Kevin Kelly helped shape its techno-utopian tone. Wired Ventures also launched the HotWired website in 1994, an early commercial web property. Condé Nast acquired the print magazine in 1998 (separately from Wired News, which was reunified with the brand in 2006). Wired has won multiple National Magazine Awards and launched international editions in the UK, Italy, Japan, and elsewhere.

Influence

Wired coined or popularized terms such as "crowdsourcing" (2006) and championed concepts like the Long Tail, also the title of editor-in-chief Chris Anderson's 2006 book. Its coverage spans gadgets, science, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, and digital culture.
