Queensbridge Houses

Queensbridge Houses
Location: Long Island City, Queens, New York City
Opened: 1939–1940
Operator: New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA)
Units: 3,142 apartments in 96 buildings

The Queensbridge Houses is a public housing development in the Long Island City neighbourhood of Queens, New York City. Opened in 1939–1940 and operated by the New York City Housing Authority, it is the largest public housing development in the United States by number of units, comprising 3,142 apartments across 96 six-storey buildings on about 47 acres (19 hectares) between Vernon Boulevard and 21st Street, just south of the Queensboro Bridge from which it takes its name.

Design

The buildings were designed under the direction of chief architect William Lescaze in a modified Y shape, intended to let more sunlight and air into each apartment while reducing construction costs by eliminating corridors and using cheaper materials. The development is divided by 41st Avenue into two sections, the North Houses and the South Houses.

History

Queensbridge was built under the Works Progress Administration during the Great Depression, replacing a district of tenements. It was one of the earliest large NYCHA developments and was intended for working families. Over the following decades the development faced the disinvestment and crime problems common to American public housing, becoming notorious in the 1980s and 1990s, though residents and community organisations have worked on improvements since.

In popular culture

Queensbridge is famous as the childhood home of the rapper Nas, whose debut album Illmatic (1994) drew directly on his upbringing there. Other artists raised in Queensbridge include Mobb Deep's Havoc and Prodigy, Marley Marl, MC Shan, and Roxanne Shante, making the development a landmark in hip-hop history. The Queensbridge–Roxbury feud and the "Bridge Wars" of the 1980s are a noted episode in early hip-hop.
