Bharata (emperor)

Bharata (Sanskrit: भरत, Bharata) is a legendary emperor in Hindu, Jain, and other Indian traditions, celebrated as a great conqueror and righteous ruler. According to the Mahabharata and the Puranas, he was the son of King Dushyanta and Shakuntala, and the ancestor of the Kuru dynasty from whom the heroes of the Mahabharata descend. India is traditionally said to derive its Sanskrit name, Bhārata (भारत), from him — a derivation invoked in the Constitution of India, which names the country "India, that is Bharat."

In the Mahabharata and Puranas

The story of Bharata's parents — the love of Dushyanta and Shakuntala — is told in the Mahabharata's Adi Parva and was made famous by Kalidasa's play Abhijñānaśākuntalam. Bharata is said to have performed numerous sacrifices, conquered the whole earth, and ruled as a universal monarch (chakravartin). The Mahabharata itself takes its name from his descendants: it is the story of the "great [descendants] of Bharata." His nine sons were deemed unfit to rule, so Bharata adopted Bharadvaja's son Bhumanyu as his successor, a significant episode in dynastic lore.

In Jain tradition

In Jainism, Bharata is the eldest of the hundred sons of the first Tirthankara, Rishabhanatha (Adinatha), and the brother of Bahubali. He is revered as the first chakravartin (universal emperor) of the present time cycle. According to Jain accounts, after conquering the world Bharata became arrogant, and his later life is associated with renunciation and attaining liberation (moksha). The Jain and Hindu traditions differ in genealogy: the Jain Bharata is the son of Rishabha, while the Hindu Bharata of the Mahabharata is the son of Dushyanta — a reflection of distinct textual traditions sharing the same legendary name.

Name of India

The name Bhārata for the land is attested in ancient texts, including the Vishnu Purana, which describes the country north of the ocean and south of the Himalayas as Bhārata. Historians debate whether the name derives from this legendary king, from the ancient Bharata tribe mentioned in the Rigveda, or from both traditions converging over time.
