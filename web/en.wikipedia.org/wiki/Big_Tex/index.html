Big Tex is a 55-foot (16.8 m) tall cowboy figure that serves as the icon and official mascot of the State Fair of Texas, held annually at Fair Park in Dallas, Texas. Since his debut in 1952, the towering figure — dressed in jeans, a plaid shirt, boots, and a cowboy hat — has greeted fairgoers with the trademark salutation "Howdy, folks!"

Big Tex towering over Fair Park in Dallas.

History

The figure that became Big Tex began in 1949 as a giant Santa Claus built in Kerens, Texas, to promote the town's Christmas shopping season. The statue was later purchased by State Fair of Texas president R. L. Thornton for $750 and redesigned as a cowboy for the 1952 fair. Artist Jack Bridges gave the figure features said to be modeled partly on Thornton himself and on a local rancher, Doc Simmons.

Over the decades Big Tex has been periodically rebuilt and redressed. His wardrobe, supplied by the Dickies clothing company, has included jeans measured in yards of denim and a shirt of similar scale. A mechanized jaw allows the figure to appear to speak, with pre-recorded greetings voiced by longtime announcer Bill Bragg, who served as the voice of Big Tex for many years.

2012 fire and rebuilding

On October 19, 2012, the final Friday of that year's fair, an electrical fault in the figure's boot sparked a fire that consumed Big Tex almost entirely, leaving only the metal frame and hands. The loss was treated as a major civic event in Dallas; fair officials vowed to rebuild him "bigger and better." A redesigned Big Tex, slightly taller at 55 feet and with a more animated design, was unveiled at the opening of the 2013 fair.

Cultural significance

Big Tex is one of the most recognizable symbols of Dallas and of Texas fair culture. Meeting "under Big Tex" is a traditional rendezvous point during the fair, and his image appears on merchandise, posters, and promotional material statewide. In 2020 the figure briefly wore an oversized face mask during the COVID-19 pandemic, when the fair was held in reduced, drive-through form.
