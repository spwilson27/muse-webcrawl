Ndabaningi Sithole

Ndabaningi Sithole

Born: 21 July 1920, Nyamandhlovu, Southern Rhodesia

Died: 12 December 2000, Philadelphia, USA

Known for: Founding ZANU (1963)

Occupation: Politician, minister, author

Ndabaningi Sithole (21 July 1920 – 12 December 2000) was a Zimbabwean politician, Methodist minister, and author who founded the Zimbabwe African National Union (ZANU) in 1963, a leading organization in the struggle against white minority rule in Rhodesia.

Life and career

Educated in Southern Rhodesia and the United States (Andover Newton Theological School), Sithole became a teacher and minister. In August 1963 he and colleagues including Robert Mugabe split from Joshua Nkomo's ZAPU to form ZANU, and Sithole became its first president. He was imprisoned by the Rhodesian government from 1964 to 1974. In prison he wrote the novel The Polygamist.

After release he went into exile and, amid leadership disputes in Lusaka in 1975, lost control of ZANU to Mugabe. He returned under the 1978 Internal Settlement, served in the transitional government of Zimbabwe Rhodesia (1979), and later led the small ZANU–Ndonga party, sitting in parliament in independent Zimbabwe and spending periods in exile before his death in the United States.
