Bungalow

Bungalow
Type: House style
Storeys: One (or one and a half)
Origin: Bengal, India
Peak popularity: Early 20th century

A bungalow is a small house or cottage that is a single storey or has a second storey built into a sloping roof (usually with dormer windows). The term derives from the Hindi bangla, referring to houses from the Bengal region of India, adapted by the British during the colonial period into low, veranda-wrapped dwellings suited to hot climates.

Development

Bungalows spread through Britain, Australia, South Africa, and North America in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In the United States they became emblematic of the Arts and Crafts movement: the California bungalow and the Craftsman bungalow, popularized by pattern books and mail-order kit homes from companies such as Sears, featured low-pitched roofs, wide eaves, exposed rafters, and full-width porches.

Variations

Regional variants include the Chicago bungalow, usually of brick; the chalet bungalow, with a habitable loft; and raised bungalows in Australia and Canada. In the United Kingdom, bungalows remain popular particularly with retirees because of single-level living. Modern usage in India denotes any detached family house, often multi-storeyed, in contrast to the original usage.
