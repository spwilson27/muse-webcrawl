Micro Four Thirds system

The Micro Four Thirds system (MFT or M4/3) is a standard for mirrorless interchangeable-lens digital cameras and lenses released by Olympus and Panasonic in 2008. It uses the same 4/3-type image sensor as the earlier Four Thirds DSLR system — a sensor measuring 17.3 × 13.0 mm with a 2× crop factor — but eliminates the mirror box and optical viewfinder, enabling a shorter flange focal distance of 19.25 mm and smaller, lighter bodies and lenses.

History

Olympus and Panasonic announced the standard on 5 August 2008. The first camera was the Panasonic Lumix DMC-G1 (October 2008), the world's first mirrorless interchangeable-lens camera, followed by the Olympus PEN E-P1 in 2009, which revived the compact half-frame PEN styling. Other manufacturers joined the consortium, notably Blackmagic Design (Pocket Cinema Camera), DJI (for drone cameras), Sigma (lenses), Sharp and Xiaomi/YI.

Characteristics

Autofocus historically relied on contrast detection, with on-sensor phase detection added in later Olympus/OM System and Panasonic models (the Panasonic Lumix G9 II, 2023, was Panasonic's first MFT body with phase-detect AF). Because of the short flange distance, almost any legacy lens can be adapted. The system supports advanced video features — Olympus/OM System and Panasonic GH-series cameras such as the GH5 and GH6 became popular with videographers and content creators.

Brands

Camera bodies are made principally by OM System (formerly Olympus) and Panasonic (Lumix G series); lenses are offered by both, as well as by Sigma, Tamron, Samyang and others. As full-frame mirrorless systems grew, Micro Four Thirds remained notable for compact size and an extensive lens catalog.
