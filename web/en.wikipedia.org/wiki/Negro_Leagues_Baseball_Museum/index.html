The Negro Leagues Baseball Museum (NLBM) is a privately funded museum in Kansas City, Missouri, dedicated to preserving and celebrating the history of African-American baseball. Founded in 1990, it is located in the historic 18th and Vine district, a centre of African-American culture, sharing a complex with the American Jazz Museum.

History

The museum began in a one-room office in 1990, founded by a group including former Kansas City Monarchs player Buck O'Neil, who became its leading ambassador. It grew through several locations before opening in its current 10,000-square-foot home in 1997. O'Neil championed the museum until his death in 2006.

Exhibits

Exhibits trace Black baseball from its origins after the Civil War through the Negro leagues era — including the Negro National League founded by Rube Foster in 1920 in Kansas City — to Jackie Robinson's breaking of the colour line in 1947. A signature display, the Field of Legends, features life-sized bronze statues of stars including Satchel Paige, Josh Gibson, Cool Papa Bell, and O'Neil positioned on a miniature diamond. The museum collects photographs, artefacts, and oral histories and hosts educational programs and travelling exhibitions.
