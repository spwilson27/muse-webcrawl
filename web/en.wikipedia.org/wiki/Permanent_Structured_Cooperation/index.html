Permanent Structured Cooperation

PESCO

Established: 11 December 2017

Framework: Treaty of Lisbon, EU Common Security and Defence Policy

Participants: 26 of 27 EU member states

Projects: 60+ joint capability projects

Permanent Structured Cooperation (PESCO) is a framework of the European Union's Common Security and Defence Policy in which participating member states commit to jointly developing defence capabilities, increasing defence investment and making their armed forces more interoperable. It was activated in December 2017 by 25 member states — the first use of a Lisbon Treaty provision allowing willing members to integrate more deeply in defence.

Commitments and projects

Members accept 20 binding commitments, reviewed annually, covering defence spending, harmonisation of requirements and participation in joint projects. PESCO projects — more than 60 in successive waves — include a European medical command, military mobility (sometimes called "military Schengen"), maritime surveillance, cyber rapid response teams, and the Eurodrone-related and future combat air cooperation efforts. The European Defence Agency and the EU Military Staff support coordination; Denmark joined after its 2022 referendum abolishing its defence opt-out, leaving Malta as the only non-participant.

Significance

PESCO is seen as a step towards deeper EU defence integration, complemented by the European Defence Fund, though analysts debate its practical effect relative to NATO, which remains the cornerstone of territorial defence for most members.
