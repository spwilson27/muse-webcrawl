Cytomegalovirus

Cytomegalovirus (CMV) is a genus of viruses in the order Herpesvirales, in the family Herpesviridae, in the subfamily Betaherpesvirinae. Humans and other primates serve as natural hosts. The genus includes the species Human betaherpesvirus 5 (HCMV, human cytomegalovirus, HHV-5), which is the type species and the one that most commonly infects humans.

Infection

Infection with CMV is very common in humans worldwide. Most infections are asymptomatic in healthy people, although the virus can cause serious disease in immunocompromised individuals, such as organ transplant recipients and people with AIDS, and in newborns infected before birth. Congenital CMV infection is a leading infectious cause of hearing loss, intellectual disability, and other long-term neurodevelopmental problems in children.

After primary infection, CMV establishes lifelong latency in the host, principally in cells of the myeloid lineage, and can reactivate when immunity is impaired. The name derives from the characteristic enlargement (cytomegaly) of infected cells, which contain large intranuclear inclusions — historically described as "owl's eye" inclusions.

Structure and genome

Like other herpesviruses, CMV has an icosahedral capsid surrounded by a protein tegument and a lipid envelope studded with glycoproteins. Its double-stranded DNA genome is among the largest of the human herpesviruses, at roughly 235 kilobase pairs in HCMV, encoding more than 160 proteins. The virus was first isolated in tissue culture in the 1950s by several groups, including those of Margaret Smith, Thomas Weller, and Wallace Rowe.

Transmission and prevention

CMV spreads through contact with body fluids including saliva, urine, blood, breast milk, and genital secretions, and through organ transplantation and blood transfusion. Young children in group childcare settings are a common source of infection for pregnant women. No vaccine is licensed, although several candidates are in development; hygiene measures, screening of blood and organs, and antiviral drugs such as ganciclovir and valganciclovir are used for prevention and treatment in high-risk patients.
