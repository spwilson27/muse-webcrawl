West Syriac Rite

The West Syriac Rite, also called the Syro-Antiochian Rite, is a Christian liturgical rite that employs the Divine Liturgy of Saint James in Syriac. It is practised by the Syriac Orthodox Church, the Syriac Catholic Church, the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, the Malankara Mar Thoma Syrian Church and related churches in India and the Middle East.

History

The rite developed in the ancient Patriarchate of Antioch, one of the earliest centres of Christianity. After the Council of Chalcedon in 451, the non-Chalcedonian Syriac-speaking churches preserved and developed the Antiochene liturgical tradition in Syriac, centred on monasteries such as Mor Gabriel in Tur Abdin.

The West Syriac tradition is distinguished from the East Syriac Rite used historically by the Church of the East in Persia and Mesopotamia.

Characteristics

The principal anaphora is that of St James, though the Syriac tradition preserves more than seventy anaphoras attributed to various saints. Liturgical features include the use of the Syriac language alongside local vernaculars, distinctive chant, the sign of peace before the anaphora, and elaborate prayers of incense (sedro).

Use

Today the rite is celebrated in Syria, Turkey, Lebanon, Iraq, India (especially Kerala), and in diaspora communities in Europe, the Americas and Australia.
