Kalundborg is a Danish town on the west coast of the island of Zealand, at the head of Kalundborg Fjord. It is the main town of Kalundborg Municipality in Region Zealand and is notable for its remarkably well-preserved medieval old town, its large industrial harbour, and the Kalundborg Eco-industrial Park, a pioneering example of industrial symbiosis.

History

The town grew up around a castle founded by Esbern Snare in the twelfth century. Its landmark is the Church of Our Lady (Vor Frue Kirke), built around 1170–1190, a unique granite church with five spires — a tall central octagonal tower surrounded by four smaller ones — which collapsed in 1827 and was rebuilt. Kalundborg was an important staging point on the route to Funen and hosted royal assemblies in the Middle Ages.

Industry and symbiosis

Since the mid-twentieth century Kalundborg has hosted major plants including a large oil refinery, power station, and pharmaceutical production by Novo Nordisk and Novozymes (Novonesis), making it one of Denmark's largest industrial areas relative to its size. Beginning in the 1960s, local firms developed the Kalundborg Symbiosis, an exchange network in which one plant's waste — steam, water, gypsum, fly ash and biomass — becomes another's input, a model studied worldwide in industrial ecology.

Transport

Kalundborg station is the western terminus of the Northwest Line from Copenhagen via Roskilde and Holbæk, and ferry services have historically linked the port to Jutland.
