Pinch hitter

In baseball, a pinch hitter is a substitute batter who replaces a player in the batting order for a single plate appearance or for the remainder of the game. Pinch hitters are most often used late in games to replace a weak hitter — traditionally the pitcher in leagues without a designated hitter — with a stronger bat in a high-leverage situation.

Rules

Once replaced, the original player may not return to the game. The pinch hitter assumes the replaced player's place in the batting order and may stay in the game on defense, be replaced in turn, or exit after batting. A batter who enters and completes a plate appearance is charged with that appearance even if the substitution was announced before a pitch.

Strategy

Managers deploy pinch hitters to gain platoon advantages (a left-handed batter against a right-handed pitcher, or vice versa) or to use a specialist bench player. Pinch hitting is considered difficult: substitutes bat less often, enter cold, and historically hit for lower averages than regular starters. Career specialists such as Manny Mota, Lenny Harris — the all-time leader in pinch hits — and Matt Stairs, who holds the record for pinch-hit home runs, built reputations in the role.

Related terms

Analogous substitutes include the pinch runner, used for speed on the basepaths, and defensive replacements. The term "pinch hitter" has passed into general usage for any last-minute substitute.
