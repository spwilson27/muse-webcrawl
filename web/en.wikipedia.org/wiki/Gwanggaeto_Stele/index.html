Gwanggaeto Stele

The Gwanggaeto Stele is a large stone monument erected in 414 CE by King Jangsu of Goguryeo in honour of his father, King Gwanggaeto the Great. Standing near the king's tomb in present-day Ji'an, Jilin Province, China, it is one of the principal primary sources for the history of the ancient Korean kingdom of Goguryeo and for relations among the states of Northeast Asia in the 4th and 5th centuries.

Description

The stele is a single block of rough granite about 6.39 metres high, with inscriptions on all four sides totalling 1,775 Chinese characters in 44 lines. The text recounts the founding myth of Goguryeo, the accession and campaigns of Gwanggaeto — including his wars against Baekje, his subjugation of Buyeo and the Khitan, and his campaigns against the Wa (Japanese) forces — and regulations for the care of the royal tomb.

Historiographical significance

The stele was rediscovered in the late 19th century after centuries of neglect; a rubbing made in the 1880s brought it to scholarly attention. Its account — particularly the so-called sinmyo passage (辛卯年) describing Wa crossing the sea in 391 — has been at the centre of long-running scholarly and nationalist disputes between Korean, Chinese, and Japanese historians over the interpretation of early Korean-Japanese relations. In 2004 the stele and the associated Goguryeo tombs were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Capital Cities and Tombs of the Ancient Koguryo Kingdom.
