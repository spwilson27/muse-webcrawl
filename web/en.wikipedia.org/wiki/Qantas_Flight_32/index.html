Qantas Flight 32

Date4 November 2010
AircraftAirbus A380-842, VH-OQA "Nancy-Bird Walton"
RouteLondon Heathrow – Singapore – Sydney
Occupants469 (440 passengers, 29 crew)
Fatalities0
CauseUncontained failure of No. 2 engine

Qantas Flight 32 was a scheduled passenger flight from London to Sydney via Singapore that suffered an uncontained failure of one of its four engines on 4 November 2010, shortly after departing Singapore Changi Airport. The Airbus A380, the first delivered to Qantas and named Nancy-Bird Walton, returned to Singapore and landed safely with no injuries to the 469 people aboard, in what became a widely praised example of airmanship.

Incident

About four minutes after takeoff, at 7,000 feet over Indonesia's Batam Island, the inner left (No. 2) Rolls-Royce Trent 900 engine suffered an uncontained failure: its intermediate-pressure turbine disc burst, severing fuel and hydraulic lines, damaging the wing, the landing gear controls and one of the aircraft's two hydraulic systems, and leaving the crew with dozens of cascading ECAM warnings. Remarkably, the outer left engine could not be shut down normally after landing and had to be drowned by fire crews. Debris fell on Batam, damaging buildings, but no one on the ground was hurt.

Investigation and aftermath

The Australian Transport Safety Bureau determined that a misaligned counterbore in an oil feed stub pipe had caused an oil fire, which overheated and weakened the turbine disc until it burst. Rolls-Royce and Airbus issued inspections and software changes across the Trent 900 fleet, Qantas temporarily grounded its A380s, and the incident led to a large compensation settlement. The captain, Richard de Crespigny, became a prominent aviation author; the heavily repaired aircraft returned to service in April 2012.
