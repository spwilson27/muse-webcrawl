Nicodemus (Greek: Νικόδημος, Nikódēmos) is a New Testament figure who appears in the Gospel of John. He is described as a Pharisee and a member of the Sanhedrin, the Jewish ruling council, and is portrayed as a cautious sympathiser of Jesus who eventually helps to bury him.

In the Gospel of John

Nicodemus first appears in John 3, visiting Jesus by night — a detail that has given rise to the modern term "Nicodemite" for a secret believer. In their conversation Jesus speaks of being "born again" (or "born from above"), which Nicodemus famously misunderstands literally, and delivers the saying of John 3:16, "For God so loved the world…".

In John 7, Nicodemus intervenes in the Sanhedrin, urging his colleagues not to judge Jesus without a hearing. Finally, in John 19, after the crucifixion, Nicodemus joins Joseph of Arimathea in burying Jesus, bringing a large quantity — about seventy-five pounds — of myrrh and aloes.

Later tradition

An apocryphal Gospel of Nicodemus (also known as the Acts of Pilate), dating from late antiquity, expands on the Passion narrative and the Harrowing of Hell. Nicodemus is venerated as a saint in both Eastern and Western Christianity, commemorated among the Myrrhbearing women and men of Easter tradition. Rabbinic literature mentions a wealthy Jerusalemite named Naqdimon ben Gorion, whom some scholars have identified with Nicodemus, though the identification is uncertain.
