The Lorax (also known as Dr. Seuss' The Lorax) is a 2012 American animated musical fantasy comedy film produced by Illumination Entertainment and distributed by Universal Pictures. Directed by Chris Renaud and co-directed by Kyle Balda, it adapts Dr. Seuss's 1971 children's book of the same name. The film stars Danny DeVito as the Lorax, Ed Helms as the Once-ler, and Zac Efron and Taylor Swift as the framing-story teenagers Ted and Audrey.

Plot

In Thneedville, a walled town where everything is artificial and fresh air is sold in bottles by the O'Hare Air company, twelve-year-old Ted Wiggins seeks a real tree to impress Audrey, who paints Truffula trees on her house. His grandmother tells him to find the Once-ler, who lives beyond the town. Over several visits the recluse tells his story: as a young entrepreneur he arrived in a valley of Truffula trees, met the Lorax — a small orange creature who "speaks for the trees" — and, despite a promise, began chopping down trees to mass-produce his knitted "Thneeds". The business boomed, the forest fell, the animals left, and the Once-ler, ruined and regretful, watched the last Truffula tree cut down as the Lorax departed, leaving a stone bearing the word "UNLESS". The Once-ler gives Ted the last Truffula seed. Despite the efforts of air magnate Aloysius O'Hare to stop him, Ted plants the seed in the centre of Thneedville, inspiring the town to restore the forest.

Reception

The film grossed $349 million worldwide. Reviews were mixed: critics praised the animation and environmental message while criticising the expanded subplots and commercialised tie-ins — the film was marketed with dozens of licensed products, including an SUV advertising campaign that drew accusations of hypocrisy from environmentalists. The film's release date, 2 March, coincided with Dr. Seuss's 108th birthday and the United States' Read Across America Day.
