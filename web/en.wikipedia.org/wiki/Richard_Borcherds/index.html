Richard Borcherds

Richard Ewen Borcherds (born 29 November 1959) is a British mathematician currently working in quantum field theory. He is known for his work in lattice theory, group theory, and infinite-dimensional algebras, and for proving the monstrous moonshine conjectures.

Biography

Borcherds was born in Cape Town, South Africa, and educated at King Edward's School, Birmingham, and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he completed his PhD in 1985 under the supervision of John Horton Conway. He has held positions at Cambridge and at the University of California, Berkeley, where he is a professor.

Work

In 1992 he proved Conway and Norton's monstrous moonshine conjectures, which connect the Monster group — the largest sporadic simple group — with modular functions, by constructing the "moonshine module" using vertex operator algebras partly borrowed from string theory. He introduced Borcherds algebras (generalized Kac–Moody algebras) and the notion of vertex algebras in form usable by mathematicians. In 1998 he received the Fields Medal in large part for this work. He is a Fellow of the Royal Society.
