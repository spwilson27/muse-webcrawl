Partial order

In mathematics, especially order theory, a partial order on a set is a binary relation that is reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive. A set equipped with a partial order is called a partially ordered set or poset. Unlike a total order, a partial order need not compare every pair of elements: some pairs may be incomparable.

Examples

Standard examples include the divisibility relation on the natural numbers (a divides b), the subset relation on the subsets of a set, and the "ancestor of" relation in a family tree. Hasse diagrams depict posets by drawing only covering relations.

Terminology

A chain is a subset in which every pair is comparable; an antichain is a subset in which no two distinct elements are comparable. Maximal and minimal elements, greatest and least elements, and upper and lower bounds are defined analogously to those in total orders. Partial orders underpin lattice theory, scheduling and the semantics of concurrent computation.
