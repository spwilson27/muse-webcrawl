AllMusic (previously known as the All Music Guide and AMG) is an American online music database and guide. It catalogs more than three million album entries and 30 million tracks, as well as information on musical artists and bands. Founded in 1991 by popular-culture archivist Michael Erlewine, it predates the World Wide Web as a printed reference work and moved online in 1994.

History

AllMusic was launched as the All Music Guide by Michael Erlewine, a former blues musician and computer programmer, as a resource for music consumers. The first printed edition appeared in 1992. AMG's content was licensed to major online services including AOL and Yahoo!, and its database powered recommendation features in early digital music stores.

The company was acquired by Macrovision (later Rovi, then TiVo) in 2007–2008. The website was redesigned in 2013 with ratings, staff reviews, and credits drawn from the database.

Content

AllMusic's reviews are written by a staff of professional music writers and freelancers. Artists are covered with biographies, discographies, ratings, and "moods" and "themes" metadata. The database is widely used by record stores, streaming services, and musicians to verify credits.
