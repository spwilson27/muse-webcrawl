New Territories

The New Territories is one of the three main regions of Hong Kong, alongside Hong Kong Island and the Kowloon Peninsula. It comprises the area north of Boundary Street on the mainland up to the border with Shenzhen, together with more than 200 outlying islands including Lantau Island, Lamma Island and Cheung Chau. Covering about 952 square kilometres, it is the largest of Hong Kong's regions.

History

The New Territories was leased by Qing China to Britain for 99 years under the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory signed on 9 June 1898, effective 1 July 1898. Unlike Hong Kong Island and Kowloon, which were ceded in perpetuity, the lease expired on 30 June 1997 — the deadline that drove negotiations leading to the transfer of the whole of Hong Kong to China in 1997. The British takeover of the territory in April 1899 was resisted in the Six-Day War by local clan militias.

Development

Once largely rural — farmland, villages of the indigenous inhabitants, and market towns such as Tai Po, Yuen Long and Sha Tin — the New Territories was transformed from the 1970s by the new towns programme, which built large self-contained towns including Tsuen Wan, Sha Tin, Tuen Mun, Tai Po, Yuen Long, Fanling/Sheung Shui, Tseung Kwan O, Tin Shui Wai and Tung Chung. A majority of Hong Kong's population now lives in the New Territories. The region also contains Hong Kong's country parks, its highest peak Tai Mo Shan (957 m), and Hong Kong International Airport on Lantau.
