United States Department of Health and Human Services

The United States Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) is a cabinet-level executive branch department of the US federal government created to protect the health of the US people and provide essential human services. Its motto is "Improving the health, safety, and well-being of America."

Agencies

HHS operating divisions include the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the National Institutes of Health (NIH), the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), the Administration for Children and Families, and the Health Resources and Services Administration. It administers the Medicare and Medicaid programmes and is one of the largest grant-making departments in the federal government.

History

The department was created as the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare (HEW) in 1953. In 1980, the Department of Education was split off and the remainder renamed Health and Human Services. The department is led by the Secretary of Health and Human Services, a cabinet position.
