Detransition


Detransition as a life course topic

Detransition is the cessation or reversal of a gender transition, whether social (such as reverting to a previous name, pronouns or presentation), legal, or medical (such as stopping hormone therapy or seeking surgery to undo earlier procedures). People who detransition are sometimes called detransitioners. Reasons reported in research include external pressure from family, employers or society, discrimination, difficulty with the transition process, evolving self-understanding, and, for some, concluding that their gender dysphoria had other causes.

Terminology and prevalence

Detransition is distinct from regret: a person may stop or reverse transition steps without regretting having transitioned, and surveys of transgender populations generally find that most discontinuation of care is attributed to external factors rather than a change of identity. Estimates of prevalence are contested and depend heavily on definitions and samples; clinical follow-up studies of surgical regret report very low rates, while broader surveys that count any pause in social or medical transition report higher figures. Many people who pause or stop a transition later resume it ("retransition").

Research and debate

Research on detransition is limited, and the topic has become politically contested in debates over gender-affirming care, particularly for adolescents. Health-care providers and researchers have called for better long-term data and for support services for detransitioners that do not stigmatize either pathway. Some detransitioners describe unmet needs for medical and psychological support specific to their experience.
