Precision Time Protocol

IEEE 1588 is the standard defining the Precision Time Protocol (PTP), a protocol used to synchronise clocks throughout a packet-switched network such as Ethernet with far greater accuracy than the Network Time Protocol — typically to sub-microsecond precision.

Operation

PTP organises clocks into a master–slave hierarchy. The best master clock algorithm selects a grandmaster, and slaves measure the message transit delay using timestamped Sync, Follow_Up, Delay_Req and Delay_Resp messages, correcting for path delay. Hardware timestamping in network interfaces and PTP-aware ("transparent" and "boundary") clocks in switches compensate for queuing delays, enabling accuracies of tens of nanoseconds in well-engineered networks. The first version was published in 2002, IEEE 1588-2008 ("PTPv2") added major improvements, and a further revision appeared in 2019.

Applications

PTP is used in telecommunications (including mobile network synchronisation), electrical power systems (substation automation and phasor measurement), industrial automation, financial trading timestamping, broadcasting (SMPTE ST 2059) and test and measurement.
