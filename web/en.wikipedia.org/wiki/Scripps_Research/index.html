Scripps Research, previously known as The Scripps Research Institute (TSRI), is an American nonprofit medical research facility that focuses on research and education in the biomedical sciences. Headquartered above Torrey Pines in La Jolla, San Diego, California, with a second campus in Jupiter, Florida, the institute is consistently ranked among the world's most influential in biomedical research and is home to several thousand scientists, technicians and graduate students.

History

The institute traces its origins to the Scripps Metabolic Clinic founded in 1924 by philanthropist Ellen Browning Scripps. It evolved into a research centre within Scripps Memorial Hospital, became an independent institute in the 1950s under immunologist Frank Dixon, and grew rapidly during the long presidency of chemist Richard Lerner (from 1987). In 2004 it opened its Florida campus with state support. Peter Schultz became president and CEO in 2015.

Research

Scripps Research is known for an unusual integration of chemistry and biology. Highlights include the development of catalytic antibodies, K. Barry Sharpless's work on click chemistry (for which he shared the 2022 Nobel Prize in Chemistry — his second Nobel), major contributions to understanding antibody structure, the discovery of the pressure-sensing ion channels Piezo1/Piezo2 by Ardem Patapoutian (Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, 2021), and the translational work of its drug-discovery arm, Calibr. Its Kellogg School of Science and Technology runs a highly ranked graduate program in chemistry and biology, now part of the Scripps Research Graduate Program.
