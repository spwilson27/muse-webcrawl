Collegium system

The collegium system (Swedish: kollegialsystemet) was the central administrative organisation of Sweden in the 17th and 18th centuries, in which royal government departments — collegia (Swedish: kollegier) — were run collectively by boards of senior officials rather than by a single minister.

History

Systematised by Chancellor Axel Oxenstierna in the 1634 Instrument of Government, the system created five great collegia — the Chancellery, Treasury (Kammarkollegium), War Office (Krigskollegium), Admiralty and the Svea Court of Appeal as a judicial collegium — each headed by one of the great officers of the realm and deciding cases by majority vote with minuted deliberations.

Legacy

The collegial, rule-bound and comparatively uncorrupt style shaped Sweden's enduring agency model: small government ministries setting policy, semi-autonomous central agencies administering it, with a constitutional ban on ministerial interference in individual cases (ministerstyre).
