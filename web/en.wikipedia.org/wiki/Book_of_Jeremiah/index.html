The Book of Jeremiah is the second of the Latter Prophets in the Hebrew Bible and the second of the Major Prophets in the Christian Old Testament. It records the prophecies and biography of Jeremiah, who prophesied in the Kingdom of Judah from about 627 BC until after Jerusalem's fall to Babylon in 586 BC. It is the longest book of the Bible by word count.

Content

The book is not in chronological order. It contains oracles against Judah and its kings, calls to repentance, predictions of exile, symbolic actions, laments known as Jeremiah's "confessions", oracles against foreign nations, and the promise of a "new covenant" (Jeremiah 31:31–34). Chapters 30–33 are often called the "Book of Consolation". Jeremiah's scribe Baruch ben Neriah features prominently; the book recounts the burning of Jeremiah's scroll by King Jehoiakim and its rewriting (chapter 36).

Textual history

Jeremiah survives in two main versions: the longer Hebrew Masoretic Text and a shorter Greek Septuagint version (about one-eighth shorter, with the oracles against the nations placed differently). Dead Sea Scrolls fragments support the antiquity of both editions, suggesting the book was edited over a long period, likely by Deuteronomistic circles.
