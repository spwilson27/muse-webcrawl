Semiconductor device fabrication

Semiconductor device fabrication is the process used to manufacture semiconductor devices, typically integrated circuits such as microprocessors, memory chips and image sensors. It is a multiple-step photolithographic and chemical process during which electronic circuits are gradually created on a wafer made of pure semiconducting material, most commonly silicon.

Process overview

Fabrication takes place in highly controlled cleanrooms (fabs), because microscopic contamination can ruin devices. Major steps, repeated many times to build up the layers of a chip, include: wafer preparation and cleaning; thermal oxidation; photolithography, in which patterns are printed with ultraviolet or extreme-ultraviolet light; etching of exposed material; ion implantation and diffusion to dope regions; thin-film deposition (chemical and physical vapour deposition); chemical-mechanical planarisation; and metallisation to form interconnects. Advanced nodes may use several hundred individual steps over weeks or months.

Testing and packaging

After fabrication, wafers are electrically probed and die are sorted, then diced, packaged, and final-tested. Yields — the fraction of good die — determine the economics of the fab.

Industry

The industry divides between integrated device manufacturers and fabless design companies that outsource production to foundries such as TSMC, Samsung Foundry and GlobalFoundries. Leading-edge fabs cost tens of billions of dollars, and fabrication nodes are named by nominal feature sizes that have progressed from micrometres in the 1970s to a few nanometres in the 2020s — a trajectory described by Moore's law.
