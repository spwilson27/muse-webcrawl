Hernani

Hernani, ou l'Honneur Castillan is a drama in verse by the French writer Victor Hugo, first performed on 25 February 1830 at the Théâtre-Français in Paris. The stormy reception of its premiere — the famous "Battle of Hernani" — became a landmark in literary history, marking the triumph of Romanticism over the classical rules of French theatre.

Plot

Set in Spain in 1519, the play tells of the bandit Hernani, secretly a dispossessed nobleman, and his love for Doña Sol, who is also loved by her aged uncle Don Ruy Gómez de Silva and by the king, Don Carlos. Hernani, owing his life to Ruy Gómez, swears to die when his saviour sounds a horn. On the wedding day of Hernani and Doña Sol the horn is heard; faithful to his oath, Hernani poisons himself, and Doña Sol shares his fate.

The Battle of Hernani

Hugo deliberately broke the classical unities and conventions of the alexandrine, and his young supporters — led by Théophile Gautier, conspicuous in a red waistcoat — packed the theatre to cheer the play against heckling classicists. The clashes continued for dozens of performances and passed into legend as the moment Romantic drama conquered the French stage.

Adaptations

Giuseppe Verdi adapted the play as the opera Ernani (1844), one of his early successes.
