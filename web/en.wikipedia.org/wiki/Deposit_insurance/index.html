Deposit insurance
Purpose: Protect bank depositors
US agency: FDIC
US limit: $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank
Related: Bank run, FDIC

Deposit insurance is a measure implemented in many countries to protect bank depositors, in full or in part, from losses caused by a bank's inability to pay its debts when due. It is a component of a financial safety net.

United States

The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation was created in 1933 after widespread bank failures during the Great Depression. Coverage was raised over time, reaching $250,000 in 2008, a level later made permanent.

Around the world

The European Union requires coverage of €100,000 per depositor. Canada, the United Kingdom, Japan and many other states operate their own schemes with differing limits and funding models, usually financed by premiums paid by banks.

Moral hazard

Economists debate moral hazard: insured depositors have less incentive to monitor banks, which may encourage risk-taking, offset by regulation and supervision.
