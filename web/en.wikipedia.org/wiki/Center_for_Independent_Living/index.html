Center for Independent Living

The Center for Independent Living (CIL) is a non-profit organisation founded in Berkeley, California, in 1972 by disability rights activists including Ed Roberts, Phil Draper, and John Hessler. Recognised as the first centre for independent living in the United States, it pioneered the independent living model: services designed, delivered, and controlled by people with disabilities themselves, aimed at enabling disabled people to live in the community rather than in institutions.

History

The CIL grew out of the "Rolling Quads," a group of students with severe disabilities at the University of California, Berkeley, led by Ed Roberts, who had fought for accessible housing and attendant services through the Cowell Memorial Hospital programme. After graduating, Roberts and his peers concluded that the services they needed — personal assistance referral, wheelchair repair, peer counselling, accessible housing and transport advocacy — should be provided by a community-based organisation run by disabled people. With federal and foundation support, the CIL opened its doors in 1972 and became the model for hundreds of similar centres.

Services and influence

The CIL provides core independent living services: information and referral, independent living skills training, peer support, individual and systems advocacy, and transition services for people moving from institutions to community living or from school to adult life. Its advocacy helped secure curb cuts in Berkeley (among the first in the nation), accessible public transit, and passage of disability rights legislation, feeding into the movement that won Section 504 regulations and ultimately the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990. The Rehabilitation Act amendments of 1978 funded independent living centres across the United States on the Berkeley model, and the concept spread internationally.
