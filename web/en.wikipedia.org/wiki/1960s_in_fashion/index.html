1960s in fashion

Fashion in the 1960s was characterised by a break with the formal, conservative styles of the previous decade and the rise of youthful, experimental designs driven by London's boutiques, Paris couturiers and a new generation of consumers. The decade moved from the tailored elegance of the early years — epitomised by Jacqueline Kennedy's pillbox hats and Oleg Cassini suits — through the geometric miniskirts of the "Swinging London" mod scene, to the flowing, eclectic hippie styles of the late 1960s.

Mod fashions of the mid-1960s: short hemlines, bold geometry and boots.

Early 1960s

In the early 1960s, fashion remained influenced by 1950s haute couture: full-skirted and sheath dresses, stiletto heels, matching gloves and hats. The election of John F. Kennedy in 1960 made his wife Jacqueline Kennedy a global style icon; her pillbox hat, bouffant hair and clean-lined suits were widely copied. Men's fashion was dominated by narrow-lapel suits, skinny ties and the continental look popularised by the Rat Pack and Italian cinema.

Mid-1960s: mod and the miniskirt

By the middle of the decade, London designers such as Mary Quant popularised the miniskirt, whose hemline rose steadily above the knee. Quant's Chelsea boutique Bazaar, and the designs of John Stephen in Carnaby Street, fed the mod subculture: bold geometric patterns, PVC raincoats, white go-go boots, false eyelashes and Vidal Sassoon's sharp five-point haircut. Models such as Twiggy, Jean Shrimpton and Penelope Tree embodied the new androgynous, youthful ideal. In Paris, André Courrèges and Pierre Cardin offered a "space age" look of white boots, goggles and sculptural synthetic fabrics, inspired by the Space Race.

Late 1960s: hippie and counterculture

Towards the end of the decade, mainstream fashion absorbed the styles of the counterculture: bell-bottom jeans, tie-dyed and psychedelic prints, kaftans, Afghan coats, love beads, fringed leather and long hair for both sexes. Unisex dressing blurred earlier gender distinctions, while the "peacock revolution" brought colour and pattern into menswear. Space-age synthetics gave way in part to ethnic and handcrafted garments associated with the hippie movement and festivals such as Woodstock in 1969.

Legacy

The 1960s permanently shifted fashion's centre of gravity from couture salons to the street and the youth market, establishing ready-to-wear, boutique retailing and rapid trend cycles that still structure the industry.
