Ivindo River

The Ivindo River is the most important tributary of the Ogooué River in Gabon, Central Africa. It drains a vast basin of equatorial rainforest in the north-east of the country and forms part of Gabon's border with the Republic of the Congo and Cameroon.

CountryGabon
MouthOgooué River
Length~500 km (approx.)
Notable featuresKongou Falls, Ivindo National Park

Geography

The Ivindo rises near the border with Cameroon and the Republic of the Congo and flows south-west through dense rainforest before joining the Ogooué. Its course is interrupted by spectacular rapids and waterfalls, including the Kongou and Mingouli falls.

Conservation

Ivindo National Park, created in 2002 and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021, protects the river's middle course and its forests, which shelter forest elephants, gorillas, and the unique "bai" clearings where animals gather.
