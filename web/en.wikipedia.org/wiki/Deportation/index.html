Deportation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Deportation is the expulsion of a person or group from a territory. In its administrative sense it is the removal of a non-citizen by the host state for immigration violations, criminal convictions or security grounds — exercised under immigration law with varying procedural protection. In its historical and criminal senses, "deportation" also names forced population removals, including the Acadian expulsion (1755–64), Ottoman and Soviet deportations of whole peoples, and Nazi deportations to ghettos and extermination camps — the latter prosecuted as crimes against humanity.

Law and practice

States' power to expel aliens is broad in international law but limited by due process requirements, the prohibition of collective expulsion (in European and American human-rights law), non-refoulement (no return to persecution or torture), statelessness problems and family-life rights (ECHR Article 8 balancing). Modern controversies include deportation of long-resident offenders ("double punishment" critiques), accelerated removals and charter-flight controversies, offshore processing, and — in the United States — the scale and methods of interior enforcement and expedited removal, litigated across administrations. Deportation differs from extradition (surrender for prosecution elsewhere), exile (banishment of citizens, now generally prohibited) and voluntary departure schemes.
