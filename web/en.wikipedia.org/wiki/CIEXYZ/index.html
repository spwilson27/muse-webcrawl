CIE 1931 color space

The CIE 1931 color spaces were the first defined quantitative links between distributions of wavelengths in the electromagnetic visible spectrum and physiologically perceived colors in human color vision. The mathematical relationships that define these color spaces are essential tools for color management and are known collectively as the CIE XYZ color space, or CIEXYZ.

Tristimulus values

In the XYZ color space, any color is described by three tristimulus values X, Y, and Z, obtained by integrating the product of the spectral power distribution of a light source, the reflectance or transmittance of an object, and the color-matching functions of the CIE 1931 standard observer. The color-matching functions were derived from experiments by William David Wright (1928) and John Guild (1931), in which observers matched spectral colors with mixtures of three primaries.

The derived chromaticity diagram, plotted with coordinates x and y computed from X, Y, and Z, shows all chromaticities visible to the average person in a horseshoe-shaped region. The CIE 1931 space remains the foundation of modern colorimetry, from which later spaces such as CIELAB and CIELUV were derived.
