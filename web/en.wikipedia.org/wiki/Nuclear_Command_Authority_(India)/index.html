Nuclear Command Authority (India)

The Nuclear Command Authority (NCA) is the authority responsible for the command, control and operational decisions regarding India's nuclear weapons programme. It was formally established on 4 January 2003, following a decision of the Cabinet Committee on Security, when India also announced its official nuclear doctrine.

Structure

The NCA comprises a Political Council and an Executive Council. The Political Council is chaired by the Prime Minister of India and is the sole body that can authorise the use of nuclear weapons. The Executive Council, chaired by the National Security Adviser, provides inputs for decision-making by the Political Council and carries out the directives issued to it. Operational execution of authorised orders is entrusted to the Strategic Forces Command, which manages and administers India's strategic (nuclear) forces across the services.

Doctrine

The accompanying doctrine statement committed India to a policy of no first use of nuclear weapons, credible minimum deterrence, and massive retaliation in the event of a nuclear attack on India or Indian forces. It also retained the option of nuclear retaliation against a major chemical or biological attack, maintained strict civilian political control over release authority, and reaffirmed India's commitment to the goal of global nuclear disarmament. India's nuclear assets are therefore held under firm civilian command, with the Political Council at the apex.
