Myth of the clean Wehrmacht

Clean Wehrmacht myth
German: Mythos der sauberen Wehrmacht
Subject: War crimes of the Wehrmacht, 1935–1945
Promoted by: Former officers, veterans' groups (HIAG), memoirs
Countered by: Historians; Wehrmacht exhibition (1995)

The myth of the clean Wehrmacht (German: Mythos der sauberen Wehrmacht) is the false notion that the regular German armed forces (the Wehrmacht) were not involved in the Holocaust or other war crimes during the Second World War, and fought an honourable campaign, with atrocities attributable only to the Nazi Party's SS. Historians have thoroughly refuted the myth: the Wehrmacht participated extensively in war crimes, particularly on the Eastern Front.

Origins

The myth took shape during and immediately after the war. German generals' memoirs — notably Franz Halder's work for the US Army Historical Division and Erich von Manstein's Lost Victories — presented the officer corps as apolitical professionals who had opposed Hitler and known nothing of SS crimes. During the Cold War, Western willingness to rearm West Germany encouraged acceptance of this narrative.

Evidence against the myth

Documentation shows Wehrmacht involvement at every level: the Commissar Order and Barbarossa Decree of 1941 authorised the murder of Soviet political officers and suspended prosecution of crimes against civilians; army units cooperated closely with the Einsatzgruppen in mass shootings of Jews; and Wehrmacht policy deliberately starved millions of Soviet prisoners of war — some 3.3 million died in German captivity. Anti-partisan operations in the Balkans and the East killed vast numbers of civilians.

Public reckoning

The 1995 exhibition Vernichtungskrieg. Verbrechen der Wehrmacht 1941–1944 ("War of Annihilation: Crimes of the Wehrmacht"), organised by the Hamburg Institute for Social Research, brought documentary photographs and evidence to the German public, provoking intense debate and effectively ending the myth's hold on mainstream memory, though it persists in some popular culture and revisionist writing.
