Lean-to

Lean-to
TypeSimple structure; single-pitch (mono-pitch) roof
Also calledPent roof structure; appentice (historical)
UsesSheds, shelters, greenhouses, additions to houses and barns

A lean-to is a type of simple structure originally added to an existing building with the rafters "leaning" against another wall. Free-standing lean-to structures are generally used as shelters. A lean-to has a single-sloped — monopitch — roof. One of the commonest informal buildings, lean-tos are used for storage sheds, animal shelters, woodsheds and temporary camps.

In wilderness survival, a lean-to is an improvised shelter made by leaning branches, poles or a tarpaulin against a horizontal support, with the open side typically facing a fire. Such shelters shed wind and rain from one direction but retain heat poorly compared with enclosed designs.

Architecture

Attached lean-tos extend the floor space of houses, barns and industrial buildings cheaply, because one wall is shared and the roof requires only one slope. The English word was once rendered in broader senses: in medieval and early modern usage related terms such as appentice and penthouse described roofed additions of this kind. Lean-to roofs are also characteristic of the aisled sides of Gothic churches, where the sloped roof over an aisle leans against the clerestory wall.
