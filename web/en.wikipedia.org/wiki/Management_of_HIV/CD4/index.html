Management of HIV/AIDS

The management of HIV/AIDS normally includes the use of multiple antiretroviral drugs, known as antiretroviral therapy (ART), to control HIV infection. There are several classes of antiretroviral agents that act at different stages of the HIV life cycle. The use of multiple drugs that act on different viral targets is known as combination therapy; it suppresses viral replication to undetectable levels, restores and preserves immune function, reduces HIV-associated illness and death, and prevents onward transmission.

Antiretroviral classes

  • Nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) — e.g., tenofovir, emtricitabine, zidovudine.
  • Non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs) — e.g., efavirenz, rilpivirine, doravirine.
  • Protease inhibitors (PIs) — e.g., darunavir, atazanavir, usually boosted with ritonavir or cobicistat.
  • Integrase strand transfer inhibitors (INSTIs) — e.g., dolutegravir, bictegravir, raltegravir; the backbone of most modern first-line regimens.
  • Entry and fusion inhibitors — e.g., maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist), enfuvirtide.

Monitoring: CD4 count and viral load

Treatment is monitored with two laboratory measures: the CD4 T-cell count, an index of immune function, and the plasma HIV RNA viral load, which measures viral replication. A normal CD4 count is roughly 500–1,500 cells/mm³; counts below 200 cells/mm³ define AIDS and indicate high risk of opportunistic infections. Guidelines now recommend ART for all people living with HIV regardless of CD4 count, started as soon as possible after diagnosis. With sustained suppression, the CD4 count typically rises and the viral load becomes undetectable; a person with a sustained undetectable viral load does not sexually transmit HIV ("U=U").

Prevention

Management also includes prophylaxis against opportunistic infections at low CD4 counts, screening for comorbidities and co-infections such as tuberculosis and viral hepatitis, and preventive services: pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) for people at risk, harm reduction, and prevention of mother-to-child transmission.
