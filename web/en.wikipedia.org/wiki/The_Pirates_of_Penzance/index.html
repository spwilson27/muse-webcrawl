The Pirates of Penzance; or, The Slave of Duty is a comic opera in two acts, with music by Arthur Sullivan and libretto by W. S. Gilbert. It officially premiered at the Fifth Avenue Theatre in New York City on 31 December 1879, partly to secure the American copyright, the night after a single copyright performance in Paignton, England.

Plot

Frederic, a young man apprenticed to a band of tender-hearted pirates by mistake (his nurse Ruth confused "pilot" with "pirate"), is released from his apprenticeship on his 21st birthday. He falls in love with Mabel, daughter of Major-General Stanley, but discovers that because he was born on 29 February in a leap year, he has technically had only five birthdays and remains bound to the pirates until he is 84. The famous "Major-General's Song" ("I am the very model of a modern Major-General") is among the best-known patter songs in the repertoire.

Legacy

The opera remains one of the most popular Gilbert and Sullivan works, frequently revived, including a celebrated 1980s Central Park production that transferred to Broadway and was filmed in 1983.
