Nonverbal communication

Facial expressions and gestures are central channels of nonverbal communication

Nonverbal communication (NVC) is the transmission of messages or signals through a nonverbal platform such as eye contact (oculesics), body language (kinesics), social distance (proxemics), touch (haptics), voice (paralanguage), physical environments and appearance, and the use of objects and time (chronemics). Through NVC, people communicate a great deal of information about their emotions, attitudes and intentions, often unconsciously.

Forms

Kinesics

Kinesics is the study of body movements, gestures, posture and facial expressions. Facial expressions of basic emotions such as happiness, sadness, anger, fear, surprise and disgust are widely recognised across cultures, a universality argued by Paul Ekman. Gestures include emblems with direct verbal equivalents, illustrators that accompany speech, and regulators that manage conversation flow.

Proxemics

Proxemics, introduced by anthropologist Edward T. Hall, concerns the use of space. Hall distinguished intimate, personal, social and public distances, noting that preferred distances vary between cultures.

Paralanguage

Paralanguage refers to the vocal but non-lexical features of speech: tone, pitch, volume, tempo, and vocal qualities such as sighs and laughter. These features can reinforce, qualify or contradict the spoken words.

Haptics and chronemics

Haptics is communication through touch, which conveys intimacy, status and emotion. Chronemics studies how time is used in communication — punctuality, waiting times and the pace of interaction all carry cultural meaning.

Functions

Nonverbal signals can repeat, complement, substitute for, accent or contradict verbal messages. When verbal and nonverbal cues conflict, listeners tend to trust the nonverbal channel, a finding often linked (and sometimes over-generalised) to Albert Mehrabian's studies of feelings and attitudes.
