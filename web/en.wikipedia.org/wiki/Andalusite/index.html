Andalusite

Andalusite is an aluminium nesosilicate mineral with the chemical formula Al2SiO5. It is named after Andalusia in Spain, where it was first described. Andalusite is one of three polymorphs of aluminium silicate, the others being kyanite and sillimanite; which form crystallises depends on the temperature and pressure at which a metamorphic rock formed, making the trio important indicators of metamorphic conditions.

Properties and varieties

Andalusite is typically pinkish-red, white, grey or brown and strongly pleochroic, showing different colours when viewed from different directions. The variety chiastolite contains dark carbonaceous inclusions arranged in a cross shape in cross-section and has long been worn as an amulet. Transparent material is occasionally cut as a gemstone.

Occurrence and uses

Andalusite forms in low-pressure, medium- to high-temperature metamorphism of aluminium-rich sediments, commonly in contact aureoles around granite intrusions. Major deposits are mined in South Africa, France and elsewhere; because it converts to mullite at high temperature, andalusite is an important raw material for refractory bricks and ceramics used in furnaces and kilns.
