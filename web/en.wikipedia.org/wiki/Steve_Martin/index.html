Steve Martin

Stephen Glenn Martin (born August 14, 1945) is an American comedian, actor, writer, producer and musician. Known for his absurdist stand-up comedy, his many film roles and his accomplished banjo playing, he has been one of the most influential American entertainers since the 1970s.

Stand-up comedy

Born in Waco, Texas, and raised in Southern California, Martin worked as a teenager at Disneyland and Knott's Berry Farm, where he learned magic, juggling and banjo. After writing for The Smothers Brothers Comedy Hour (winning an Emmy in 1969), he developed a stand-up act that parodied show-business conventions — the catchphrases "Excuuuse me!" and "a wild and crazy guy", the novelty hit "King Tut" (1978) and albums such as Let's Get Small (1977) and A Wild and Crazy Guy (1978). A frequent host of Saturday Night Live, he played to stadium audiences before abandoning stand-up at its peak in 1981.

Film and writing

Martin's first starring film, The Jerk (1979), began a long screen career including Dead Men Don't Wear Plaid, The Man with Two Brains, All of Me, Roxanne (which he wrote), Planes, Trains and Automobiles, L.A. Story, Father of the Bride and Bowfinger. He has written novellas (Shopgirl), plays (including Waiting for Godot productions and the musical Bright Star, co-written with Edie Brickell) and the bestselling memoir Born Standing Up (2007).

Music

Martin is a serious bluegrass banjo player. His album The Crow (2009) won the Grammy Award for Best Bluegrass Album, and he has recorded and toured with the Steep Canyon Rangers and Edie Brickell. Since 2021 he has co-starred with Martin Short and Selena Gomez in the comedy series Only Murders in the Building.
