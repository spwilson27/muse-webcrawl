Martin Karplus (March 15, 1930 – December 28, 2024) was an Austrian-American theoretical chemist. He was the Theodore William Richards Professor of Chemistry at Harvard University and also directed a research group in Strasbourg, France. He was a co-recipient of the 2013 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, together with Michael Levitt and Arieh Warshel, "for the development of multiscale models for complex chemical systems."

Early life and education

Karplus was born in Vienna, Austria, into a Jewish family of physicians and academics. After the Anschluss in 1938, the family fled Austria, eventually settling in the United States. He studied at Harvard College, graduating in 1950, and completed his PhD at Caltech in 1953 under Linus Pauling.

Career

After postdoctoral work at Oxford, Karplus taught at the University of Illinois and Columbia University before joining Harvard in 1966. His early work concerned nuclear magnetic resonance; the Karplus equation, relating proton-proton coupling constants to molecular geometry, bears his name. From the 1970s onward he pioneered molecular dynamics simulations of proteins, and his group developed the CHARMM program for simulating macromolecules, which became one of the foundational tools of computational chemistry.

Nobel Prize

The 2013 Nobel citation recognized Karplus, Levitt, and Warshel for laying the foundations of methods that combine quantum mechanics with classical physics to model chemical processes in large molecules, enabling computer simulations of enzyme catalysis and protein dynamics.

Other interests

Karplus was also an accomplished photographer whose early color photographs of postwar Europe and other travels were exhibited in museums. His memoir, Spinach on the Ceiling, recounts his family's escape from Vienna and his scientific life.
