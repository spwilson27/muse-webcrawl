Harmonic major scale

In music theory, the harmonic major scale is a musical scale, also called the major flat six scale, that differs from the natural major scale by a flattened sixth degree. In C, its notes are C, D, E, F, G, A♭, B — combining the lower tetrachord of the major scale with the upper tetrachord of the harmonic minor scale.

Structure

The scale's interval pattern in semitones is 2, 2, 1, 2, 1, 3, 1. Its characteristic sound comes from the augmented second between the flattened sixth and the leading tone, which gives it a flavour reminiscent of harmonic minor while retaining a major tonic triad.

Harmony and usage

Chords derived from harmonic major include the minor-major seventh flavour on the sixth degree and augmented chords on III. The scale appears in classical music and in jazz improvisation, where it is valued for resolving strongly to the tonic while offering darker colour tones than the plain major scale.
