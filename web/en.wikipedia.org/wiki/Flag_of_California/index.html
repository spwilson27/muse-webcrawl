Flag of California

The flag of California, often called the Bear Flag, is the state flag of California. It consists of a white field with a red stripe along the bottom, a red star in the upper hoist corner, a grizzly bear walking on a patch of green grass in the centre, and the words "CALIFORNIA REPUBLIC" in red below the bear. It derives from the flag raised during the Bear Flag Revolt of 1846, when American settlers in Sonoma declared California independent of Mexico.

History

The original Bear Flag was hastily made in June 1846, reportedly from dress fabric, with a bear that early accounts said resembled a pig. The original flag was destroyed in the fires following the 1906 San Francisco earthquake. The modern design was adopted by the California State Legislature and signed into law by Governor Hiram Johnson in 1911 as the official state flag; the design was standardised in 1953, when the specifications — including the colours of the bear, based on the grizzly "Monarch" — were set in law. Monarch, one of the last wild California grizzlies, was captured in 1889 and displayed in San Francisco; the California grizzly is now extinct.

Symbolism

The lone red star echoes the "Lone Star" of Texas and an earlier California Lone Star flag of 1836. The bear symbolises strength and unyielding resistance; the red stripe and star represent courage. In a 2001 survey by the North American Vexillological Association, California's flag ranked among the more highly regarded U.S. state and Canadian provincial flags, praised for its distinctive bear.
