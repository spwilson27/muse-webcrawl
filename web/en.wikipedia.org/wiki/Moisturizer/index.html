Moisturizer

A moisturizer, or emollient, is a cosmetic or pharmaceutical preparation used to protect, moisturise, and lubricate the skin. Moisturizers are used both for everyday skin care and in the management of skin conditions characterised by dryness, such as eczema and psoriasis.

Mechanism of action

Moisturizers work through three broad classes of ingredients. Occlusives, such as petrolatum, form a hydrophobic film on the skin that reduces transepidermal water loss. Humectants, such as glycerin and urea, attract and hold water in the stratum corneum. Emollients, such as oils and fatty acids, fill gaps between skin flakes, improving smoothness and flexibility. Most commercial products combine all three classes in creams, lotions, or ointments of varying thickness.

Use and risks

Regular application after bathing is recommended for people with dry skin and eczema. Some ingredients, including fragrances and certain preservatives, can cause contact dermatitis or acne in susceptible individuals, and heavy use of paraffin-based products carries a fire risk because residues build up on clothing and bedding.
