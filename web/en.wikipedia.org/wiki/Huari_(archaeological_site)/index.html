Huari (archaeological site)


The extensive ruins of Huari near Ayacucho, Peru.

Huari or Wari is an archaeological site located near the village of Huari (near the modern city of Quinua), 25 kilometres (16 mi) northeast of Ayacucho in the Ayacucho Region of Peru. It was the capital city of the Wari culture, a pre-Inca people that flourished from about 600 to 1000 AD, and is presumed to have been a very large settlement dominated by large temple, dwelling, and storage structures. The ruins of the site are extensive — composed of terraces, stone structures, and underground mortuary chambers — spread across a large area.

History

Huari was the capital of the Wari state, often considered the first empire of the Andes, which controlled much of the Peruvian highlands and coast during the Middle Horizon period. The city may have housed as many as 40,000–70,000 inhabitants at its peak. Around AD 1000, after a period of protracted drought, the Wari state collapsed and the city was abandoned.

Archaeology

Important finds include the royal mortuary temple of D-shaped plan and the Vegachayuq Moqo sector, noted for its megalithic stone vessels. Excavations have uncovered fine polychrome pottery, offerings of turquoise, and evidence of large-scale craft production and storage of foodstuffs such as potatoes, maize, and camelid meat.
