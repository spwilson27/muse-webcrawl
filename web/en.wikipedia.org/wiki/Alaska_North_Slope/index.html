Alaska North Slope

The Alaska North Slope is the region of the US state of Alaska lying on the northern slope of the Brooks Range, draining into the Arctic Ocean. It comprises a treeless coastal plain of tundra, lakes and braided rivers underlain by continuous permafrost, roughly coincident with the North Slope Borough. The region is famous for its petroleum reserves: the Prudhoe Bay Oil Field, discovered in 1968, is the largest oil field in North America, and the area also contains the National Petroleum Reserve–Alaska and part of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge.

Geography and development

Major communities include Utqiaġvik (formerly Barrow), the northernmost town in the United States, Deadhorse and Prudhoe Bay. Oil production at Prudhoe Bay began in 1977 with the completion of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline to Valdez; output peaked in 1988 at about two million barrels per day and has since declined, spurring interest in fields such as Alpine, Kuparuk and the Willow project. The North Slope is also an important habitat for caribou herds, migratory waterfowl and polar bears, and debates over drilling in the Arctic Refuge coastal plain have run for decades.
