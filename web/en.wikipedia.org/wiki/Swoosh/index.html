The Swoosh is the logo of American sportswear company Nike, Inc., and one of the most valuable and widely recognized brand marks in the world. It was designed in 1971 by Carolyn Davidson, a graphic design student at Portland State University, who was paid $35 for her work by Nike co-founder Phil Knight, then an accounting instructor at the university.

Design and history

Knight needed a logo for the new shoe brand he and Bill Bowerman were launching as Blue Ribbon Sports became Nike — named after the Greek goddess of victory. Davidson produced several concepts; Knight chose the check-like swoosh, saying he would "grow to like it." The mark is often said to evoke a wing of Nike's statue, and a swooshing sense of movement and speed. It first appeared on footwear in 1972 at the US Olympic track and field trials.

In 1983 Knight gave Davidson a golden Swoosh ring set with a diamond and an envelope of Nike stock — later worth a fortune — in recognition of her contribution. The Swoosh has been used alone, without the Nike wordmark, since 1995, and adorns products from the Air Jordan line (alongside the Jumpman) to the kits of national football teams. Brand-valuation firms have estimated the mark itself to be worth many billions of dollars.
