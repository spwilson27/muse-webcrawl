Pyridostigmine

Pyridostigmine
ClassAcetylcholinesterase inhibitor
Trade nameMestinon, others
UsesMyasthenia gravis; reversal of neuromuscular blockade; nerve-agent pretreatment

Pyridostigmine is a medication used to treat myasthenia gravis and underactive bladder. It is a parasympathomimetic and a reversible cholinesterase inhibitor, structurally related to neostigmine but with a longer duration of action and fewer gastrointestinal side effects. It is available as the bromide salt, commonly under the brand name Mestinon.

Medical uses

In myasthenia gravis, pyridostigmine improves muscle strength by slowing the breakdown of acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction. It is also used, together with an anticholinergic such as glycopyrrolate, to reverse the effects of non-depolarising muscle relaxants after surgery, and to treat orthostatic hypotension in some patients.

Military use

Pyridostigmine bromide tablets were issued to soldiers as a pretreatment against the nerve agent soman, since prior reversible inhibition of cholinesterase protects a fraction of the enzyme from irreversible binding. Its role in Gulf War illness has been studied extensively but remains debated.
