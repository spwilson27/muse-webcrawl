Replica symmetry breaking

In the statistical mechanics of disordered systems, replica symmetry breaking (RSB) is a scheme for organising the replicas of a system when the replica-symmetric solution of the replica method proves unstable or unphysical. It was introduced by Giorgio Parisi in 1979 in his exact solution of the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model of a spin glass.

Background

The replica method computes the averaged free energy of a disordered system by replicating it n times and taking the limit n → 0. The simplest assumption is that all replicas are equivalent ("replica symmetric"), characterised by a single overlap parameter q. For the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick spin glass this solution gives a negative entropy at low temperature, signalling its failure; de Almeida and Thouless identified the line in the phase diagram below which it becomes unstable.

The Parisi scheme

Parisi proposed that the replicas divide into groups: replicas within a group have one overlap, replicas in different groups another, and the grouping is applied hierarchically, generating a function q(x) for x in [0,1]. Physically, the Gibbs measure decomposes into many pure states organised in an ultrametric tree, so that the overlap between two configurations depends only on how closely related their states are. The scheme yields a non-negative entropy and an exact solution of the model, later made mathematically rigorous in large part through the work of Michel Talagrand.

Applications

Replica symmetry breaking has been applied to neural networks, combinatorial optimisation, error-correcting codes, structural glasses and problems in theoretical computer science, wherever a complex energy landscape with many metastable states dominates the physics.
