Borama alphabet

The Borama alphabet, also known as the Gadabuursi script, is a writing system devised for the Somali language. It was invented around 1933 by Sheikh Abdurahman Sheikh Nuur, a teacher and religious figure of the Gadabuursi clan from the Borama area of present-day Somaliland, from which the script takes its name.

Characteristics

The Borama script is an alphabet written from left to right, with distinct letters for consonants and vowels; long vowels and consonant gemination can be marked. Sheikh Abdurahman taught the script locally, and it was used for correspondence, records, and religious instruction in the Borama region for several decades. It was one of several indigenous Somali scripts—alongside the Osmanya and Kaddare scripts—that competed to become the national orthography before Somalia officially adopted a Latin-based alphabet in 1972.
