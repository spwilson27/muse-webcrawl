Villa Tugendhat

Villa Tugendhat is a pioneering modernist house in Brno, Czech Republic, designed by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe and Lilly Reich and completed in 1930 for Fritz and Greta Tugendhat. It is regarded as a masterpiece of the International Style and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001.

Design

Built with a steel skeleton, the villa's main living floor is an open plan divided by an onyx wall and a macassar ebony screen, with floor-to-ceiling glass walls — two panels retractable into the floor — opening onto the garden. The "Barcelona chair" and "Brno chair" were designed for its interiors. Revolutionary amenities included air cooling and a photographic darkroom.

History

The Jewish Tugendhat family fled in 1938; the house was confiscated, used by the Gestapo and later as a dance school and rehabilitation centre. Meticulous restoration completed in 2012 returned it to its 1930 appearance as a museum.
