An international investment agreement (IIA) is a treaty between countries regarding cross-border investment, typically for the purpose of promoting and legally protecting foreign direct investment by nationals of one state in the territory of another. The most common form is the bilateral investment treaty (BIT); investment provisions also appear in free trade agreements and in multilateral instruments such as the Energy Charter Treaty.

Contents

IIAs typically define covered investments broadly (including enterprises, shares, debt and intellectual property) and grant investors substantive protections: fair and equitable treatment, full protection and security, national and most-favoured-nation treatment, and compensation for direct or indirect expropriation. Most allow investors to bring claims directly against host states through investor–state dispute settlement (ISDS), commonly under the ICSID or UNCITRAL rules, without exhausting local remedies.

History and reform

The first modern BIT was concluded between West Germany and Pakistan in 1959, and the network grew explosively in the 1990s to more than 3,000 treaties. Since the 2000s the regime has faced criticism over regulatory chill, inconsistent awards and arbitrator independence, prompting reform efforts: the European Union's investment court system, UNCITRAL Working Group III negotiations, new-generation treaties with right-to-regulate clauses, and withdrawals by some states from the Energy Charter Treaty and from BITs altogether.
