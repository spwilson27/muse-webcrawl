Anna Stepanovna Politkovskaya (30 August 1958 – 7 October 2006) was a Russian journalist, writer and human rights activist known for her opposition to the Second Chechen War and to Russian president Vladimir Putin. She reported from Chechnya for the newspaper Novaya Gazeta, documenting war crimes and human rights abuses by Russian federal forces, Chechen fighters and the Moscow-backed Chechen administration.

On 7 October 2006 she was shot and killed in the elevator of her apartment building in Moscow. Her murder attracted international attention and remains a symbol of the dangers faced by journalists in Russia. Five men were eventually convicted in connection with the killing, though those who ordered it have never been identified.

Early life and career

Politkovskaya was born in New York City to Soviet diplomats of Ukrainian origin working at the United Nations. She studied journalism at Moscow State University, graduating in 1980. She worked for the newspaper Izvestia and later for Obshchaya Gazeta, before joining Novaya Gazeta in 1999.

Reporting on Chechnya

From 1999 onward, Politkovskaya made dozens of trips to Chechnya, interviewing hospital staff, refugees, soldiers and civilians. Her books, including A Dirty War (2001), A Small Corner of Hell (2003) and Putin's Russia (2004), condemned the conduct of the war and accused the Kremlin of dismantling democratic freedoms. She also acted as a negotiator during the 2002 Moscow theatre hostage crisis and attempted to assist during the 2004 Beslan school siege, during which she fell seriously ill after drinking tea on a flight, an incident she believed was a poisoning attempt.

Death and investigation

Politkovskaya was found shot dead in the lift of her apartment block on Lesnaya Street in Moscow on 7 October 2006 — the birthday of Vladimir Putin. After several trials, in 2014 five men of Chechen origin were sentenced for carrying out the murder; former police officer Sergei Khadzhikurbanov and Lom-Ali Gaitukayev were identified as organizers. The European Court of Human Rights later found the Russian investigation inadequate in establishing who commissioned the killing.

Legacy

The Anna Politkovskaya Award was established in 2007 by the organization RAW in WAR to honour women human rights defenders. Her collected journalism was published posthumously as Is Journalism Worth Dying For? (2011).
