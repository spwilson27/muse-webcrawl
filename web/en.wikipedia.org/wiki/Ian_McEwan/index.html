Ian Russell McEwan (born 21 June 1948) is a British novelist and screenwriter. With a writing career spanning over five decades, he is one of Britain's most acclaimed writers; in 2008, The Times featured him on its list of "The 50 greatest British writers since 1945," and The Daily Telegraph ranked him 19th in its list of the 100 most powerful people in British culture.

McEwan began his career writing sparse, dark short stories — the collections First Love, Last Rites (1975) and In Between the Sheets (1978) earned him the nickname "Ian Macabre." His early novels The Cement Garden (1978) and The Comfort of Strangers (1981) continued this vein. He later moved toward broader historical and political subjects with The Innocent (1990), Black Dogs (1992), and Enduring Love (1997).

Major works

Amsterdam (1998) won the Booker Prize. His best-known novel, Atonement (2001), is widely considered his masterpiece; its 2007 film adaptation was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture. Later novels include Saturday (2005), On Chesil Beach (2007), Solar (2010), Sweet Tooth (2012), The Children Act (2014), Nutshell (2016), Machines Like Me (2019), and the autobiographical Lessons (2022).

Life

McEwan was born in Aldershot, the son of an army officer, and spent much of his childhood in East Asia, Germany, and Libya. He studied English at the University of Sussex and was among the first graduates of the University of East Anglia's creative writing programme, taught by Malcolm Bradbury and Angus Wilson.
