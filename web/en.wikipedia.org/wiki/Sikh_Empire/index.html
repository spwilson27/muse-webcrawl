Sikh Empire

The Sikh Empire (Punjabi: سکھ سلطنت, Sikkh Saltanat; also called the Punjab Empire or the Lahore State) was a regional power based in the Punjab region of the Indian subcontinent. It existed from 1799, when Maharaja Ranjit Singh, a Sikh of the Sukerchakia Misl, captured Lahore, to 1849, when it was annexed by the British East India Company after the Second Anglo-Sikh War. At its greatest extent in the early 19th century, the empire stretched from the Khyber Pass in the west to western Tibet in the east, and from Mithankot in the south to Kashmir in the north.

History

The empire grew out of the Sikh misls — sovereign confederacies that filled the vacuum left by the decline of the Mughal Empire in Punjab. Ranjit Singh united the misls, took Lahore in 1799 and was proclaimed Maharaja in 1801. He captured Multan, Kashmir and Peshawar, and built a modern army, the Fauj-i-Khas and the regular Khalsa army, trained by European officers such as Jean-François Allard and Jean-Baptiste Ventura, with a formidable artillery arm cast in Lahore's own foundries.

Decline and annexation

After Ranjit Singh's death in 1839 the empire descended into court intrigues and army panchayats. The British fought two hard wars against the Khalsa army — the First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46) and the Second (1848–49) — and annexed Punjab in March 1849 after the battles of Chillianwala and Gujrat. The last Maharaja, the child Duleep Singh, was deposed, and the Koh-i-Noor diamond passed to Queen Victoria.

Administration and society

Though a Sikh-ruled state, the empire was notably secular in its administration: Hindus and Muslims held the highest offices, cow slaughter was banned out of respect for Hindu sentiment, and mosques and temples received state patronage. Its population of roughly 3.5 to 12 million included a Muslim majority and large Hindu and Sikh minorities.
