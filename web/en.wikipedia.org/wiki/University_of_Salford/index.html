The University of Salford is a public university in Salford, Greater Manchester, England, about 1.5 miles west of Manchester city centre. Its origins lie in the Pendleton Mechanics Institute of 1850 and the Salford Working Men's College; it developed through the Royal Technical Institute and received university status by royal charter in 1967.

History

The institution grew out of 19th-century technical education serving Salford's industrial workforce. As the Royal Technical College, Salford, it became a College of Advanced Technology in the 1950s and a university in 1967 following the Robbins Report. In 1996 it merged with University College Salford, bringing in nursing and health studies.

Campuses

The main Peel Park campus occupies parkland beside the River Irwell, anchored by the Victorian Peel Building and the Maxwell Building. Since 2011 the university has also had a major presence at MediaCityUK at Salford Quays, alongside the BBC and ITV, housing media, journalism, film, and digital programs.

Academics

The university is organized into schools of arts, media and creative technology; science, engineering and environment; health and society; and the Salford Business School. It is noted for acoustics and audio engineering, built-environment research, robotics (including the Energy House test facility for energy-efficient housing), and prosthetics and orthotics.
