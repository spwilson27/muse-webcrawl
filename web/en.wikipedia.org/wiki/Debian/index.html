Debian (also known as Debian GNU/Linux) is a free and open-source Linux distribution developed by the Debian Project, which was established by Ian Murdock on August 16, 1993. The name is a portmanteau of Murdock's first name and that of his then-girlfriend (later wife) Debra Lynn.

Features

Debian is known for its stability, its strict adherence to free software principles set out in the Debian Free Software Guidelines (part of the Debian Social Contract), and its enormous package repository—over 59,000 packages in recent releases—managed by the APT package management system. It supports many hardware architectures and serves as the basis for numerous derivative distributions, most prominently Ubuntu, as well as Linux Mint, Raspberry Pi OS, Kali Linux, and SteamOS.

Releases

Stable releases are issued roughly every two years and are codenamed after characters from the Toy Story films, beginning with Buzz (1.1, 1996) and Rex (1.2), and continuing through Woody, Sarge, Etch, Lenny, Squeeze, Wheezy, Jessie, Stretch, Buster, Bullseye, Bookworm, and Trixie. Three branches are maintained simultaneously: stable, testing, and unstable (permanently codenamed Sid).

Organisation

The project is a volunteer, democratic organisation of about a thousand developers worldwide, governed by a constitution and led by an annually elected Debian Project Leader. Funding and asset holding are handled in part by Software in the Public Interest, a non-profit founded by the project.
