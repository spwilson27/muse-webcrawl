United States Army Central

United States Army Central (ARCENT) is the Army service component command of United States Central Command (CENTCOM), responsible for US Army land forces across the Middle East and Central Asia. It is headquartered at Shaw Air Force Base, South Carolina, with a forward headquarters at Camp Arifjan, Kuwait. ARCENT's lineage traces to the Third Army, the famous field army commanded by General George S. Patton Jr. in the Second World War, and it still uses the Third Army designation and "Patton's Own" heritage.

History

The Third Army was activated in France in November 1918, performed occupation duty after the Armistice, and was reactivated in 1932. Under Patton from 1944 it raced across France after the Normandy breakout, relieved Bastogne during the Battle of the Bulge, and drove into Germany and Czechoslovakia by war's end. Demobilised and reactivated several times during the Cold War, the headquarters was assigned in 1982 as the Army component of the new Central Command.

Southwest Asia wars

ARCENT directed Army ground operations in Operations Desert Shield and Desert Storm (1990–91), when General Norman Schwarzkopf's coalition ground offensive liberated Kuwait, with Third Army under Lieutenant General John Yeosock serving as the senior Army headquarters. It again served as the land component in the 2003 invasion of Iraq — as Coalition Forces Land Component Command (CFLCC) under Lieutenant General David McKiernan — and subsequently supported operations in Iraq and Afghanistan for two decades.

Today ARCENT oversees Army forces and exercises across the CENTCOM area of responsibility, including Operation Spartan Shield, theatre security cooperation with regional partners, and pre-positioned equipment stocks in Kuwait and Qatar.
