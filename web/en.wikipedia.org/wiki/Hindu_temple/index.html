Hindu temple

A Hindu temple (Sanskrit: mandira, "house") is a house of a Hindu deity (deva), a sacred place where Hindus worship through rituals such as puja. It is designed to bring together gods, human beings, and the structure of the universe, as laid down in the ancient texts on temple architecture known as the Shilpa Shastras.

Architecture

The temple plan is based on the vastu-purusha-mandala, a geometric square grid representing the cosmos. The innermost sanctum, the garbhagriha ("womb chamber"), houses the main deity's image (murti) and is typically surmounted by a tower — the curvilinear shikhara of the northern Nagara style or the pyramidal vimana of the southern Dravida style, whose gateways (gopurams) may rise higher still and are covered with sculpture. Surrounding elements include halls (mandapas), corridors for circumambulation (pradakshina), and temple tanks.

Worship

Unlike congregational worship in many religions, temple practice centres on individual or family darshan — seeing and being seen by the deity — and receiving blessings and prasad. Daily rituals conducted by priests include awakening the deity, bathing and dressing the image, offering food (naivedya), and waving lamps (aarti). Major temples host festivals that draw enormous crowds, such as the Ratha Yatra chariot procession at Puri.

History

Early worship centred on altars and small shrines; monumental stone temples developed from the Gupta period (4th–6th centuries CE), culminating in great complexes such as Khajuraho, Thanjavur, and Angkor Wat in Cambodia, the largest religious monument in the world.
