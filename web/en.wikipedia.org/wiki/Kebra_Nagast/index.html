The Kebra Nagast (Ge'ez: ክብረ ነገሥት, "The Glory of the Kings") is a 14th-century Ethiopian book, written in Ge'ez, that recounts the legendary origins of the Solomonic dynasty of Ethiopian emperors. It tells how the Queen of Sheba (Makeda) visited King Solomon in Jerusalem, bore his son Menelik I, and how Menelik brought the Ark of the Covenant to Ethiopia.

Contents

The work is a compilation of biblical commentary, history and legend, framed as a debate among 318 orthodox fathers. Its central narrative follows Makeda's journey to Solomon's court, her conversion to his God, the birth of Menelik, and the young king's own visit to Jerusalem. On his return, the Ark — carried by the first-born sons of Israel's elders who accompanied him — is brought to Aksum, transferring God's chosen status from Israel to Ethiopia. Later chapters trace the line of Ethiopian kings, discuss Christology, and end with prophecies and the triumph of Ethiopia.

History and significance

The book was compiled around 1321, probably by the Aksumite scribe Yeshaq, drawing on older Arabic, Coptic and Ethiopian sources. It served to legitimise the "restored" Solomonic dynasty that took power in 1270, a claim reflected in Ethiopia's 1955 constitution, which asserted the imperial line's descent from Menelik I. The Kebra Nagast remains revered by the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church and is also a sacred text for Rastafari, who regard Haile Selassie I as divine.

The text reached European readers through translations beginning with E. A. Wallis Budge's English version, The Queen of Sheba and Her Only Son Menyelek (1922).
