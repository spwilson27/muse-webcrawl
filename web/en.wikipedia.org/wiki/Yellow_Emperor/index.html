Yellow Emperor

The Yellow Emperor, also known by his Chinese name Huangdi (Chinese: 黃帝), is a legendary Chinese sovereign and cultural hero, counted among the mytho-historical Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors. Traditional accounts credit him with a reign from 2697 to 2597 BCE (or 2698–2598 BCE) and with inventing numerous foundations of Chinese civilization.

Legend

According to Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian, Huangdi united the tribes of the Yellow River plain after defeating the Flame Emperor at the Battle of Banquan and the rebel Chiyou at the Battle of Zhuolu. He is credited with inventing the Chinese calendar, writing, mathematics, wheeled vehicles, and boats, while his wife Leizu is said to have discovered sericulture. His minister Cangjie is traditionally credited with creating Chinese characters.

Legacy

Huangdi is revered as the ancestor of the Chinese people; the Chinese often describe themselves as "descendants of Yan and Huang" (炎黃子孫). The Huangdi Neijing (Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon), the foundational text of traditional Chinese medicine, is cast as a dialogue with him. His mausoleum in Shaanxi has been a site of state ceremonies honouring him as a founding ancestor, and in Taoism he was deified as a god associated with longevity and esoteric arts.

Historicity

Modern historians regard Huangdi as a legendary figure, probably a deified tribal leader around whom myths accumulated, with his cult rising to prominence during the Warring States and Han periods.
