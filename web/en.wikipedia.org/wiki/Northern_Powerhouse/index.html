The Northern Powerhouse is a proposal and government policy to rebalance the economy of the United Kingdom by boosting the economy of Northern England, particularly its core cities — Manchester, Liverpool, Leeds, Sheffield, Newcastle upon Tyne and Hull — through investment in transport, science, culture and devolved powers. The term was introduced by Chancellor of the Exchequer George Osborne in a speech in Manchester in June 2014.

Rationale

Osborne argued that the individual northern cities were each "collectively" strong but individually weaker than London, and that better connections between them could create a single economy acting as a counterweight to the capital — a "powerhouse". Key proposals included high-speed east–west rail links (dubbed "HS3", later Northern Powerhouse Rail), upgrades to road networks, devolution deals with elected metro mayors, and investment in universities and innovation.

Development

Devolution deals created combined authorities with elected mayors, first in Greater Manchester (2017) and later in the Liverpool City Region, Tees Valley, South Yorkshire, West Yorkshire and elsewhere. Transport for the North was established in 2018 as a statutory body to advise on strategic transport. Progress on Northern Powerhouse Rail was controversial; the government's 2021 Integrated Rail Plan scaled back planned east–west high-speed lines, drawing criticism from northern leaders, and later governments revisited the plans.

Criticism and legacy

Critics argued the initiative delivered more rhetoric than funding, concentrated on cities at the expense of towns, and lost momentum after Osborne left office in 2016. The related "levelling up" agenda of the Johnson government adopted similar goals. The Northern Powerhouse Partnership, a business-led advocacy body chaired by Osborne, continues to promote the concept.
