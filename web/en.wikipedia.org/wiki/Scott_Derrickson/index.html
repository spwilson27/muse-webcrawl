Scott Derrickson

Born: July 16, 1966, Denver, Colorado, U.S.
Occupation: Film director, producer, screenwriter
Years active: 1995–present
Known for: The Exorcism of Emily Rose, Sinister, Doctor Strange, The Black Phone

Scott Derrickson (born July 16, 1966) is an American filmmaker known primarily for horror films and for directing Marvel Studios' Doctor Strange (2016). Frequently collaborating with co-writer C. Robert Cargill, he has directed commercially successful genre films including The Exorcism of Emily Rose (2005), Sinister (2012), and The Black Phone (2021).

Career

Derrickson studied at Biola University and earned a master's degree from the USC School of Cinematic Arts. He began in direct-to-video horror (Hellraiser: Inferno, 2000) before his breakthrough with The Exorcism of Emily Rose, a courtroom-horror hybrid based loosely on the Anneliese Michel case that grossed over $144 million worldwide. He directed the 2008 remake The Day the Earth Stood Still with Keanu Reeves, then returned to low-budget horror with Sinister, starring Ethan Hawke, a major box-office success on a small budget. Doctor Strange (2016), starring Benedict Cumberbatch, grossed over $677 million; Derrickson left the sequel, Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness, over creative differences but remained an executive producer. The Black Phone (2021), adapted from a Joe Hill short story and again starring Hawke, was a critical and commercial hit, followed by The Gorge (2025) for Apple TV+.

Style and themes

A self-described Christian whose faith informs his interest in supernatural horror, Derrickson has spoken about horror as a genre suited to exploring good and evil. His films often centre on families, children in peril, and investigators confronting demonic or occult threats.
