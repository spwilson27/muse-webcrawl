Donald Winnicott

Donald Woods Winnicott (7 April 1896 – 25 January 1971) was an English paediatrician and psychoanalyst who was especially influential in the field of object relations theory and developmental psychology.

Donald Winnicott
Born: 7 April 1896, Plymouth, England
Died: 25 January 1971, London
Known for: Transitional object, good enough mother, holding environment, true self and false self

Life and work

Winnicott trained in medicine at Cambridge and St Bartholomew's Hospital, London, and worked for 40 years as a children's physician at Paddington Green Children's Hospital. He trained as a psychoanalyst, was analysed by James Strachey and Joan Riviere, and twice served as president of the British Psychoanalytical Society.

Concepts

Winnicott introduced influential concepts including the transitional object (such as a child's blanket or teddy bear), transitional phenomena, the holding environment, the good enough mother — arguing that ordinary devoted care, not perfection, supports healthy development — and the distinction between the true self and false self. His BBC broadcasts and books, including Playing and Reality (1971), brought developmental ideas to a wide public. His famous dictum held that "there is no such thing as a baby" — only a baby together with its caregiver.
