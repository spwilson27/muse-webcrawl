Adverb

Adverb
Part of speech: Modifier
Modifies: Verbs, adjectives, other adverbs, clauses
Examples: quickly, very, yesterday, however

An adverb is a word or phrase that modifies a verb, an adjective, another adverb, a determiner, a clause, or a whole sentence, typically expressing manner, place, time, frequency, degree, or certainty.

Functions and forms

In English, many adverbs are formed from adjectives by adding the suffix -ly (e.g. quickly), but many common adverbs, such as very, well, now and often, are not. Adverbs are comparatively invariable: in many languages they do not inflect, though some have comparative and superlative forms (faster, fastest).

Categories

Traditional grammar groups adverbs as conjunctive adverbs, adverbs of manner, place, time, frequency, degree and modality. Linguists note that "adverb" functions as a catch-all class in some languages.
