Transfer (law)

Transfer (law)
Field: Property law
Examples: Conveyance, assignment, negotiation
Related: Title, deed, novation

In law, a transfer is the passing of a right, title or interest in property — real or personal, tangible or intangible — from one person or entity to another, whether by act of the parties (sale, gift, assignment) or by operation of law (inheritance, bankruptcy, court order).

Forms

Land is transferred by conveyance, usually by deed and registration. Contractual rights are transferred by assignment; obligations generally require novation. Negotiable instruments transfer by negotiation — endorsement and delivery. Fraudulent transfer (fraudulent conveyance) law lets creditors void transfers made to defeat their claims, now codified in statutes such as the US Uniform Voidable Transactions Act.

Requirements

Valid transfer typically requires a competent transferor, identifiable subject matter, intent to transfer, and compliance with formalities such as writing under the Statute of Frauds or recordation to be effective against third parties. See Conveyancing and Assignment.
