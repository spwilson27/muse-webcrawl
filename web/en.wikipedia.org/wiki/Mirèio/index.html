Mirèio (in French, Mireille) is an epic poem in twelve cantos by the Provençal poet Frédéric Mistral, published in 1859. Written in Occitan, it is the masterpiece of the Félibrige movement for the revival of Provençal language and culture, and the work that established Mistral's European reputation, leading eventually to his Nobel Prize in Literature in 1904.

Plot

Mirèio is the daughter of a wealthy farmer of the Crau plain in Provence. She falls in love with Vincèn, a poor basket-weaver's son. Her father refuses the match and promises her to richer suitors. Desperate, Mirèio flees across the Camargue under the blazing sun to pray at the church of Les Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer for her father's consent. Struck down by the heat, she dies in the church in a vision of the Three Marys, in one of the most famous endings in Provençal literature.

Significance

Mistral published the poem with a facing French translation, and it was praised by Alphonse de Lamartine, who hailed its young author as a Homeric poet of Provence. Charles Gounod adapted it as the opera Mireille (1864). The poem remains the central text of modern Occitan literature.
