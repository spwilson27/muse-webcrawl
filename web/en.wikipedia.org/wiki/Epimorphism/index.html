Epimorphism

In category theory, an epimorphism (also called an epi) is a morphism f : X → Y that is right-cancellative: for all objects Z and all morphisms g1, g2 : Y → Z, if g1 ∘ f = g2 ∘ f then g1 = g2. Epimorphisms are categorical analogues of surjective functions, though the two notions do not coincide in every concrete category.

Examples

In the category of sets, epimorphisms are exactly the surjective functions. In the category of groups and of abelian groups, epimorphisms are also surjective homomorphisms. However, in the category of rings, the inclusion map ℤ → ℚ is an epimorphism despite not being surjective. In the category of topological spaces, epimorphisms are maps with dense image need not hold either; epimorphisms there are the surjective continuous maps, while in the category of Hausdorff spaces they include maps with dense image.

Related notions

The dual concept is a monomorphism (left-cancellative). A morphism that is both an epimorphism and a monomorphism is a bimorphism, which need not be an isomorphism. Stronger variants include regular, strong, and extremal epimorphisms, which arise in factorisation systems.
