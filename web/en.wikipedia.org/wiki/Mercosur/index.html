Mercosur

Mercosur, officially the Southern Common Market (Spanish: Mercado Común del Sur; Portuguese: Mercado Comum do Sul), is a South American trade bloc established by the Treaty of Asunción in 1991 and the Protocol of Ouro Preto in 1994. Its full members are Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, and Uruguay. Bolivia became a full member in 2024 after a prolonged accession process; Venezuela is a full member but has been suspended since 2016.

Purpose and structure

Mercosur's purpose is to promote free trade and the fluid movement of goods, people, and currency among its members. It is a full customs union in aspiration, with a common external tariff, though numerous exceptions persist. Its main decision-making bodies are the Common Market Council and the Common Market Group; the bloc's administrative secretariat is in Montevideo, Uruguay, and its parliament (Parlasur) also sits in Montevideo.

History

The bloc grew out of rapprochement between Brazil and Argentina in the 1980s following their return to democracy. The Treaty of Asunción (26 March 1991) brought in Paraguay and Uruguay. Associate members include Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Guyana, Panama, Peru, and Suriname. Mercosur has negotiated trade agreements with numerous partners, most prominently a long-negotiated association agreement with the European Union, negotiations for which were concluded in December 2024 after 25 years of talks.

Economy

Mercosur countries together have a combined population of roughly 270 million (excluding suspended Venezuela) and one of the largest economies of any trade bloc in the developing world, dominated by Brazil, the bloc's largest member.
