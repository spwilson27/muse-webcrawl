Engel's law

Proposed byErnst Engel (1857)
FieldEconomics

Engel's law is an economic observation stating that as household income rises, the proportion of income spent on food falls, even though absolute spending on food may increase. It was formulated by the German statistician Ernst Engel in 1857 from his study of Belgian working-class family budgets.

Implications

The relationship implies that the income elasticity of demand for food is less than one: food is a necessity. Engel's law is used in development economics as an indicator of a country's standard of living — a declining share of expenditure on food (the Engel coefficient) generally accompanies rising prosperity. It also helps explain structural change, since growing incomes shift demand toward manufactures and services.

Engel curves

The related Engel curve graphs household expenditure on a good against income. For necessities the curve rises with diminishing slope; for luxuries it steepens; for inferior goods it eventually declines.
