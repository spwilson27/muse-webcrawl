Ellas Otha Bates (December 30, 1928 – June 2, 2008), known professionally as Bo Diddley, was an American singer, guitarist and songwriter who played a key role in the transition from the blues to rock and roll. He influenced many artists, including Buddy Holly, Elvis Presley, the Beatles, the Rolling Stones and Jimi Hendrix, with his use of African rhythms and a signature beat — the "Bo Diddley beat" — that appears in numerous songs.

Career

Born in Mississippi and raised in Chicago, Bates studied violin before switching to guitar, inspired by John Lee Hooker. Signing with Chess Records' Checker subsidiary in 1955, he scored an immediate rhythm-and-blues hit with "Bo Diddley" backed by "I'm a Man". His performances — playing a distinctive rectangular "cigar box" guitar, backed by maracas player Jerome Green — bridged blues and the emerging rock and roll; his appearance on The Ed Sullivan Show in 1955 widened his audience, though a dispute over the song he performed led to a ban from the programme.

Musical style and legacy

The Bo Diddley beat — a five-accent clave-like rhythm — underpins songs by artists from Buddy Holly ("Not Fade Away") to U2 ("Desire"). Hits such as "Who Do You Love?", "Mona" and "Before You Accuse Me" became standards widely covered by other performers. Diddley was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 1998.
