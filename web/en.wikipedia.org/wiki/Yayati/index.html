Yayati

Yayati (Sanskrit: ययाति) is a king in Hindu mythology, described in the Mahabharata, the Bhagavata Purana and other texts as an ancestor of the Pandavas and Kauravas. He was a son of King Nahusha and a ruler of the Lunar dynasty (Chandravamsha). Yayati is best known for the curse of premature old age laid upon him and for exchanging his decrepitude with the youth of his son Puru.

Legend

Yayati married Devayani, daughter of Shukra, the preceptor of the asuras, and also took Sharmishtha, daughter of the asura king Vrishaparva, as a wife. When Devayani complained to her father about Yayati's infidelity, Shukra cursed the king with premature old age. Appealed to for mercy, Shukra allowed Yayati to exchange his old age with any man willing to take it on. Of Yayati's five sons — Yadu, Turvasu, Druhyu, Anu and Puru — only the youngest, Puru, born of Sharmishtha, agreed to accept his father's age.

Exchange of youth

Having enjoyed youth for a thousand years through Puru's sacrifice, Yayati at last recognised that desire is never quenched by indulgence, comparing it to fire fed with ghee, and returned the youth to Puru, taking back his old age. He crowned Puru as his successor, while the elder sons, who had refused him, received lesser portions and were cursed that their lines would not inherit the main kingdom. Yayati then retired to the forest and eventually ascended to heaven.

Descendants

Yayati's sons are the eponymous founders of great lineages: from Yadu descended the Yadavas, the clan of Krishna; from Puru descended the Pauravas, the line that leads through Bharata and Kuru to the heroes of the Mahabharata.
