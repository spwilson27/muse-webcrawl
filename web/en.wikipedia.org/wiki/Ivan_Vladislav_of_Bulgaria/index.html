Ivan Vladislav (Bulgarian: Иван Владислав) was the last ruler of the First Bulgarian Empire, reigning from August 1015 until his death in February 1018. The son of Aron, brother of Emperor Samuel, he came to power by murdering his cousin, Emperor Gavril Radomir, and spent his short reign in a losing struggle against the Byzantine emperor Basil II, "the Bulgar-slayer", whose conquest of Bulgaria was completed within months of Ivan Vladislav's death.

Background and accession

Ivan Vladislav owed his life to the cousin he later murdered: in 976, Samuel ordered the execution of his brother Aron and his family at Razmetanitsa, but Gavril Radomir intervened to save Ivan Vladislav. After Samuel's death in 1014 and the disaster at the Battle of Kleidion, Gavril Radomir succeeded him. In the autumn of 1015 Ivan Vladislav assassinated Gavril Radomir while hunting near Lake Ostrovo and seized the throne, also killing the Serbian prince Jovan Vladimir in 1016 through treachery, according to the Chronicle of the Priest of Duklja.

Reign and war with Byzantium

On taking power Ivan Vladislav sent envoys to Basil II offering a truce on terms of nominal submission, but the letter was widely seen as a ruse and the war continued. He fortified the remaining Bulgarian strongholds — the Bitola inscription of 1015/16 records his rebuilding of the fortress of Bitola and styles him "autocrat of the Bulgarians", born of a Bulgarian lineage. He sought an alliance with the Pechenegs north of the Danube, but the plan collapsed. Byzantine armies progressively captured strongholds including Ohrid's outer defences, and in early 1018 Ivan Vladislav besieged Dyrrhachium (modern Durrës), where he was killed in fighting before the walls in February 1018.

Aftermath and family

His widow, the Empress Maria, negotiated surrender terms with Basil II, securing favourable treatment for the imperial family. Most Bulgarian nobles submitted; a few, such as Ibatzes, resisted briefly in the mountains. Bulgaria was annexed to the Byzantine Empire, ending the First Bulgarian Empire until the restoration under the Asen dynasty in 1185. Ivan Vladislav's descendants entered Byzantine aristocracy; through his daughter Catherine he was the grandfather-in-law of Emperor Isaac I Komnenos, and the later Bulgarian tsar Peter Delyan claimed descent from his line. Ivan Vladislav Point on Rugged Island in Antarctica is named after him.
