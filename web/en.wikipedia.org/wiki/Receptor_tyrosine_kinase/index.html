Receptor tyrosine kinase

Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) are the high-affinity cell surface receptors for many polypeptide growth factors, cytokines, and hormones. Of the 90 unique tyrosine kinase genes identified in the human genome, 58 encode receptor tyrosine kinase proteins. RTKs have been shown to be key regulators of normal cellular processes and to play a critical role in the development and progression of many types of cancer.

Structure

Most RTKs are single subunit receptors with a single transmembrane domain. Each monomer has an extracellular ligand-binding region, a transmembrane helix, and a cytoplasmic region containing the protein tyrosine kinase domain plus additional regulatory regions. Ligand binding causes most RTKs to dimerise, bringing the kinase domains together so that each phosphorylates tyrosine residues on the other.

Signalling

The phosphorylated tyrosines serve as docking sites for signalling proteins containing SH2 or PTB domains, which activate downstream pathways such as the Ras–MAPK pathway, the PI3K–Akt pathway, and phospholipase C. Well-known families include the epidermal growth factor receptors (EGFR/ErbB), insulin receptors, platelet-derived growth factor receptors (PDGFR), vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFR), and fibroblast growth factor receptors (FGFR).

Clinical significance

Mutations that constitutively activate RTKs are found in many cancers. Drugs targeting RTKs include monoclonal antibodies such as trastuzumab and small-molecule kinase inhibitors such as imatinib.
