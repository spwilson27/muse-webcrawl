Čech cohomology

In mathematics, specifically algebraic topology, Čech cohomology is a cohomology theory based on the intersection properties of open covers of a topological space. It is named after the Czech mathematician Eduard Čech, who introduced related ideas in the 1930s in his work on higher homotopy groups and simplicial approximation.

Construction

Let X be a topological space and let 𝒰 = {Ui} be an open cover of X. The nerve of the cover is the simplicial complex whose vertices are the sets of the cover and whose simplices correspond to finite subcollections with nonempty intersection. Čech cochains with coefficients in a sheaf F assign to each finite intersection Ui₀ ∩ ⋯ ∩ Uip a section of F over that intersection, alternating in the indices. The coboundary operator combines restriction maps with alternating signs:

(δf)i₀…ip+1 = Σj (−1)j fi₀…îj…ip+1|Ui₀∩⋯∩Uip+1

Since δ² = 0, the cochains form a cochain complex, and its cohomology groups are the Čech cohomology groups of the cover. Taking the direct limit over all open covers (ordered by refinement) gives the Čech cohomology of the space itself, usually written Ȟq(X, F).

Relation to other theories

For paracompact spaces, Čech cohomology with constant coefficients agrees with singular cohomology. More generally, for a large class of spaces and sheaves, Čech cohomology coincides with sheaf cohomology defined via derived functors of the global sections functor; the comparison map is an isomorphism whenever the cover is acyclic for the sheaf in question, a criterion made precise by Leray's theorem on acyclic covers. On manifolds, Čech cohomology also matches de Rham cohomology through the standard comparison theorems.

Applications

Čech cohomology is a basic computational tool in algebraic geometry, where affine covers of schemes and quasi-coherent sheaves make the groups computable; for example, the cohomology of line bundles on projective space is computed this way. In complex analysis and complex geometry it is used to study the exponential sequence, line bundles and the classification of holomorphic vector bundles, and it underlies the Čech-to-derived spectral sequence relating local data to global cohomology.
