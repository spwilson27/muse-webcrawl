Valet parking

Valet parking is a parking service offered by some restaurants, hotels, stores and other businesses, in which an attendant — the valet — parks and retrieves the customer's vehicle, in contrast to self-parking, where the customer finds and occupies a space themselves. The customer leaves the car at a designated drop-off point near the entrance and receives a claim ticket; on departure the valet brings the vehicle back. The service is usually offered for a fee, sometimes validated or complimentary for guests and premium customers, and valets are traditionally tipped.

Operation

Valet services may be provided by venue employees or by third-party parking companies. Because valets park cars more densely — blocking in vehicles and holding all keys centrally — a valet lot can hold substantially more cars than a self-park lot of the same size, and the service allows sites with no on-site parking to offer convenient arrival. Major liability considerations include damage claims, theft and the obligation to verify claim tickets before releasing vehicles.

History and geography

The practice grew with automobile ownership in the United States in the early-to-mid 20th century; Herb Citrin, who began parking cars in Los Angeles in 1946, is often credited with popularising the term "valet parking". The service is most widespread in the United States, where tipping is customary, and in luxury hospitality worldwide. It is less common in much of Europe, partly because of a higher proportion of manual-transmission cars historically and differing tipping norms. At high-end venues valets may also be expected to handle special requests such as keeping gifts or flowers in the car. In some cities app-based "on-demand valet" services briefly flourished in the 2010s, sending valets to meet drivers at arbitrary locations.
