Romantic music is a stylistic movement in Western Classical music associated with the period from the early 19th century to the first decade of the 20th, part of the broader Romantic movement in art and literature. It emphasized emotion, individualism, imagination and the expression of inner feeling over the balance and formal clarity of the Classical period.

Characteristics

Romantic music expanded harmony with richer chromaticism, larger orchestras, wider dynamic range, and longer, more expansive forms — the symphonic poem, the character piece and the art song (Lied) flourished. Composers drew on literature, folklore, nature and national identity; programme music telling a story became central, as in Berlioz's Symphonie fantastique. Virtuoso performers such as Liszt and Paganini became celebrities.

Late Romanticism

In the later 19th century Wagner's operas transformed harmony and orchestration, while Brahms represented a more classicizing strand — a stylistic divide debated as the "War of the Romantics". National schools flourished from Russia to Bohemia and Scandinavia. By around 1910 the style shaded into modernism, though its works remain the core of the concert repertoire.
