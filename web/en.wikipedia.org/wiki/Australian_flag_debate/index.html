Australian flag debate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Australian flag debate is a question over whether the flag of Australia should be changed, particularly to remove the Union Jack from the canton and better represent Indigenous Australians and the country's independence. Periodic debate has accompanied the broader movement for an Australian republic.

Background

The current flag, a defaced Blue Ensign bearing the Commonwealth Star and the Southern Cross, was chosen in a 1901 design competition and confirmed by the Flags Act 1953. A 1998 amendment to the Act, introduced by the Howard government, requires that any change to the national flag be approved by a national vote of electors.

Arguments

Supporters of change, organised since the 1980s by groups such as Ausflag, argue that the flag is a colonial relic that fails to represent a multicultural, independent nation and is too easily confused with the flag of New Zealand and other ensign-based designs. Opponents, including the Australian National Flag Association, argue that the flag is a popular symbol under which Australians have served and died, that it records the nation's history and constitutional monarchy, and that change commands no majority support. Opinion polling has consistently shown a majority favouring retention of the current flag, though support for change tends to rise around republican debates.

Alternative designs

Proposed alternatives frequently retain the Southern Cross and the green and gold sporting colours, while some incorporate Aboriginal art motifs. The Aboriginal flag and Torres Strait Islander flag were recognised as official flags of Australia in 1995, a development some see as a partial answer to the representation argument.
