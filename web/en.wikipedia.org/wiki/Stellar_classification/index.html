Stellar classification

In astronomy, stellar classification is the classification of stars based on their spectral characteristics. Electromagnetic radiation from the star is split by a prism or diffraction grating into a spectrum displaying absorption lines, from which temperature, composition and luminosity class are inferred.

Morgan–Keenan system

The current system assigns types O, B, A, F, G, K, M (hottest to coolest), each subdivided 0–9, with luminosity classes I (supergiants) through V (main sequence) and VII (white dwarfs). The mnemonic is "Oh, Be A Fine Guy/Girl, Kiss Me". Brown dwarf classes L, T and Y extend the sequence to cooler objects.

History

Annie Jump Cannon and the Harvard Computers systematised spectral types in the Henry Draper Catalogue; Ejnar Hertzsprung and Henry Norris Russell related spectra to luminosity, producing the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram. William Wilson Morgan and Philip Keenan refined the two-dimensional system in 1943.
