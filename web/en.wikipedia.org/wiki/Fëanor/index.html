Fëanor

Fëanor (Quenya pronunciation: [fe.anor]) is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. He is a High Elf (Noldo) and the greatest craftsman and smith of the Elves, the maker of the Silmarils — three jewels containing the light of the Two Trees of Valinor. His name, given by his father Finwë, is Fëanáro, "Spirit of Fire"; he is also called Curufinwë.

History

Fëanor was the eldest son of Finwë, King of the Noldor, and his first wife Míriel, who died giving birth to him, exhausted by the magnitude of his spirit. Fëanor married Nerdanel and had seven sons, including Maedhros and Maglor.

After the Dark Lord Morgoth stole the Silmarils and slew Finwë, Fëanor swore the Oath of Fëanor — binding himself and his sons to recover the Silmarils at any cost. This oath led the Noldor to rebel against the Valar, leave Valinor, and return to Middle-earth. During the escape, Fëanor ordered the burning of the Telerin ships at Losgar, abandoning the host of Fingolfin. This betrayal, along with the earlier Kinslaying at Alqualondë, brought doom upon his house.

Fëanor died in Middle-earth shortly after landing, fighting against Morgoth's Balrogs at the Dagor-nuin-Giliath (Battle-under-Stars). His body burned away, consumed by his own fiery spirit, leaving no remains. The oath he swore continued to drive his sons through the First Age, shaping much of the tragedy of The Silmarillion.

Character and legacy

Fëanor is portrayed as supremely talented, proud, and fiery-tempered. Tolkien presents him as a figure of both great creation and great destruction: his craft produced marvels — the palantíri and the Tengwar script as well as the Silmarils — but his pride and oath caused catastrophic ruin. Critics have compared him to figures from mythology such as Prometheus and to the tragic hero.
