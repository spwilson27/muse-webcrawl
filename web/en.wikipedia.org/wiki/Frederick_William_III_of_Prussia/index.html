Frederick William III of Prussia

Frederick William III (German: Friedrich Wilhelm III.; 3 August 1770 – 7 June 1840) was King of Prussia from 16 November 1797 until his death in 1840. His reign spanned the Napoleonic Wars, the humiliation of Prussia's defeat in 1806, the era of reform that followed, and the Wars of Liberation in which Prussia helped defeat Napoleon.

Early reign and defeat

The son of Frederick William II, Frederick William III was cautious and indecisive by temperament. He kept Prussia neutral in the early Revolutionary Wars, but in 1806, under pressure to confront Napoleon, Prussia went to war and was crushed at the twin battles of Jena and Auerstedt. The royal family fled to Königsberg, and the Treaty of Tilsit (1807) stripped Prussia of half its territory.

Reform era

Defeat forced reform. Under ministers Stein and Hardenberg and military reformers Scharnhorst and Gneisenau, serfdom was abolished, municipal self-government introduced, the army rebuilt and the University of Berlin founded (1810). The king sanctioned these changes reluctantly but held to them. His wife, the popular Queen Louise, who died in 1810, became an enduring national symbol.

Wars of Liberation and later reign

In 1813 Frederick William issued the appeal An mein Volk ("To my people") — the first time a Prussian king addressed his subjects directly — and joined Russia against Napoleon. Prussian forces fought at Leipzig (1813) and Blücher's army helped decide Waterloo (1815). At the Congress of Vienna Prussia gained the Rhineland and part of Saxony, emerging as a leading German power.

His later reign was conservative: the promised constitution was never granted, the Carlsbad Decrees suppressed liberal movements, and in 1817 he imposed the Prussian Union of Churches, merging Lutheran and Reformed congregations. He died in Berlin in 1840 and was succeeded by his son Frederick William IV.
