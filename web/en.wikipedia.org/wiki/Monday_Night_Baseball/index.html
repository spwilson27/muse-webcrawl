Monday Night Baseball
NetworksNBC (1972–1975), ABC (1976–1989), ESPN (various)
GenreSports telecast

Monday Night Baseball was an American television program presenting live Major League Baseball games on Monday evenings. It began on NBC in 1972 as a supplement to the network's Saturday Game of the Week coverage, and from 1976 to 1989 became a prominent part of ABC Sports' schedule, produced in the style of ABC's successful Monday Night Football.

History

NBC first televised Monday night baseball periodically from 1972 through 1975. In 1976, ABC took over the Monday night package under executive producer Roone Arledge, initially with announcers Bob Prince, Bob Uecker and Warner Wolf, and later with Keith Jackson, Howard Cosell and Al Michaels in the booth. The program featured split-national coverage of two games in its early ABC years before settling on a single national game.

ABC's coverage ended after the 1989 season as Major League Baseball's television contracts were restructured. ESPN later revived the title for its own Monday night broadcasts, presenting Monday Night Baseball as part of its regular-season coverage from the 2000s onward, typically in the early part of the season.

Legacy

The program helped establish prime-time national baseball telecasts and featured memorable moments, including Reggie Jackson's famous 1977 "three-home-run" era performances covered by ABC's baseball team, though the Monday night slot never matched the ratings of its football counterpart.
