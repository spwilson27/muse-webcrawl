Piedmontese cuisine


Agnolotti del plin

Piedmontese cuisine is the cuisine of the Piedmont region of Italy, in the country's northwest. It is known for rich antipasti, stuffed pastas, braised meats, white truffles from Alba, fine wines such as Barolo and Barbaresco, and desserts including gianduja chocolate and panna cotta. Piedmont is home to the Slow Food movement, founded in Bra by Carlo Petrini in 1986.

Typical dishes

Well-known Piedmontese dishes include agnolotti and the small pinched agnolotti del plin; tajarin, a rich egg-yolk pasta; vitello tonnato, cold sliced veal with a tuna and caper sauce; bagna càuda, a hot dip of garlic, anchovies, and olive oil served with raw and cooked vegetables; brasato al Barolo, beef braised in Barolo wine; and fritto misto alla piemontese, a mixed fry of meats, offal, and vegetables. Raw meat preparations such as carne cruda all'albese are also characteristic.

Cheeses and sweets

Important cheeses include Castelmagno, Robiola, Bra, and Toma Piemontese. The region's confectionery traditions centre on hazelnuts from the Alta Langa, used in gianduja (a chocolate-hazelnut paste created in Turin), gianduiotti chocolates, and hazelnut cakes. Other desserts include bonet, a chocolate-amaretto custard, and peaches stuffed with amaretti.
