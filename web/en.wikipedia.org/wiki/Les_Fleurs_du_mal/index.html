Les Fleurs du mal (French pronunciation: [le flœʁ dy mal]; English: The Flowers of Evil) is a volume of French poetry by Charles Baudelaire, first published in 1857. It was important in the symbolist and modernist movements. The poems deal with themes relating to decadence and eroticism, and the collection is dedicated to the French poet Théophile Gautier.

The author and the publisher were prosecuted under the regime of the Second French Empire as an outrage aux bonnes mœurs ("an insult to public decency"). As a consequence of the prosecution, Baudelaire was fined 300 francs. Six poems were suppressed, and the ban on their publication was not lifted in France until 1949. These poems were later published as Les Épaves (The Wrecks) in Brussels in 1866.

Overview

The first edition, published on 25 June 1857, comprised 100 poems divided into five sections: Spleen et Idéal (Spleen and Ideal), Fleurs du mal (Flowers of Evil), Révolte (Revolt), Le Vin (Wine) and La Mort (Death). The second edition of 1861 removed the six condemned poems and added 35 new ones, organised into six sections with the addition of Tableaux parisiens (Parisian Scenes).

The poems explore the changing nature of beauty in the newly industrialised Paris of the nineteenth century: beauty is found in evil, in decay, in the artificial and in the diseased. Baudelaire juxtaposes the aspiration toward the ideal with the crushing weight of "spleen" — boredom, melancholy and ennui. Famous poems in the collection include "L'Albatros", "Correspondances", "Hymne à la Beauté", "Invitation au voyage" and "Une Charogne" (A Carcass).

Trial and censorship

Baudelaire was prosecuted in August 1857, shortly after the novelist Gustave Flaubert had been tried (and acquitted) for Madame Bovary. The prosecutor, Ernest Pinard, argued that thirteen poems were obscene or blasphemous. The court acquitted Baudelaire of blasphemy but condemned him for offending public morality, ordering six poems removed: "Lesbos", "Femmes damnées (Delphine et Hippolyte)", "Le Léthé", "À celle qui est trop gaie", "Les Bijoux" and "Les Métamorphoses du vampire". The condemnation was formally annulled by the Court of Cassation on 31 May 1949.

Influence

The volume is widely regarded as the most influential collection of French poetry of the nineteenth century. It shaped the work of Arthur Rimbaud, Paul Verlaine and Stéphane Mallarmé, and its themes and imagery influenced the Decadent movement and modernist literature in English through translations by writers such as Algernon Charles Swinburne and later Edna St. Vincent Millay and Richard Howard.
