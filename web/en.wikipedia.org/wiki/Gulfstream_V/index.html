Gulfstream V

The Gulfstream V (also known as the G-V) is a long-range, large-cabin business jet produced by Gulfstream Aerospace. Introduced in 1997, it was the first ultra-long-range business jet, capable of flying distances of up to about 6,500 nautical miles (12,000 km), enough to connect New York with Tokyo non-stop.

Development

Developed from the Gulfstream IV, the G-V received a stretched fuselage, a new wing, and more powerful Rolls-Royce BR710 turbofan engines. It first flew on 28 November 1995 and entered service in June 1997. The aircraft set numerous city-pair speed records and won the 1997 Collier Trophy. It was later developed into the Gulfstream G500 and G550, and a military version, the C-37A, serves with the United States armed forces as an executive transport.

Operators

Besides corporate and private operators, the Gulfstream V is flown by government and military users. The U.S. Air Force and U.S. Army operate it as the C-37A for senior leader transport, and modified versions have been used for airborne early warning (as Israel's Eitam, based on the G550 line) and research missions.
