Executive car

Executive car is a British car classification term for a large car that is equivalent in size to the European E-segment and American full-size classifications, though somewhat larger than a compact executive car. Executive cars are designed to carry business executives and senior managers in comfort, emphasising rear-seat space, ride quality, refinement and prestige, and are often used for chauffeur and corporate fleet duties.

Characteristics

Executive cars are typically four-door saloons (sedans) around five metres long, with six-cylinder, V8 or increasingly hybrid and electric powertrains, and interiors trimmed in leather and wood or aluminium. They sit below flagship luxury cars (F-segment) such as the Mercedes-Benz S-Class in price and status, but above mid-size models. In the European classification they occupy the E-segment, making them the second-largest mainstream car category.

Examples

Classic and current examples include:

  • Mercedes-Benz E-Class
  • BMW 5 Series
  • Audi A6
  • Jaguar XF
  • Volvo S90
  • Lexus ES and GS
  • Genesis G80
  • Historically: Rover 800, Ford Granada/Scorpio, Vauxhall Omega, Saab 9-5

In the United States, similar vehicles are classed as full-size or mid-size luxury cars depending on interior volume, while the term "executive car" is not commonly used.

Market

The executive segment has long been dominated by the German premium brands, whose cars are bought by both private owners and company-car fleets; in Britain, benefit-in-kind tax rules significantly shape demand, favouring low-emission and electric versions. Since the 2010s the segment has faced competition from luxury SUVs and from electric models such as the Tesla Model S, and several manufacturers have withdrawn traditional saloons from the class.
