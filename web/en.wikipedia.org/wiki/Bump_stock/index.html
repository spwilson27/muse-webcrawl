Bump stock

Bump stock
Type: Firearm accessory (gun stock)
Function: Uses recoil to rapidly re-actuate the trigger
US status: Federal ban (2019) struck down by Supreme Court in Garland v. Cargill (2024)

A bump stock or bump fire stock is a gun stock that can be used to assist in bump firing, a technique in which a semiautomatic firearm's recoil is harnessed so that the trigger is repeatedly actuated by a stationary finger, producing a rate of fire approaching that of automatic weapons while the trigger technically functions once per shot.

History and regulation

Bump stocks such as the Slide Fire were sold in the United States from around 2010 after the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) determined they were not machine guns under federal law. Following the 2017 Las Vegas shooting, in which the gunman used bump-stock-equipped rifles to kill 60 people, the ATF reversed course; a rule effective March 2019 reclassified bump stocks as machine guns, requiring their destruction or surrender. Several states also enacted bans.

Garland v. Cargill

On June 14, 2024, the U.S. Supreme Court held in Garland v. Cargill that a semiautomatic rifle equipped with a bump stock is not a "machinegun" under the National Firearms Act, invalidating the federal rule, though state-level prohibitions remained in place.
