Ribe

Ribe is a town in south-west Jutland, Denmark, in the municipality of Esbjerg. Founded in the early 8th century, it is the oldest existing town in Denmark and one of the oldest in Scandinavia. Ribe was an important trading port during the Viking Age and the seat of a diocese from the 10th century; its cathedral, Ribe Cathedral, dominates the town's well-preserved medieval centre.

History

Archaeological excavations show that a marketplace was established at Ribe around 700–710 AD, with craftsmen producing beads, bronze castings and other goods for trade across the North Sea. Ribe appears in written sources in connection with the missionary work of Ansgar in the 9th century, and a bishopric was founded there about 948.

In the Middle Ages Ribe was one of Denmark's most important towns and a royal residence; the castle of Riberhus was built in the 12th century. The town later declined as its harbour silted up and trade shifted. A famous episode is the career of Maren Spliid, burned as a witch in 1641, one of the last such executions in Denmark.

Sights

Today Ribe is a popular tourist destination, known for its cobbled streets, half-timbered houses, the Viking Centre (Ribe VikingeCenter), and the Wadden Sea Centre nearby, interpreting the tidal Wadden Sea, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
