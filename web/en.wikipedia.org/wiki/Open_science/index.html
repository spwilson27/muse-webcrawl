Open science is the movement to make scientific research — including publications, data, physical samples, and software — and its dissemination accessible to all levels of society, amateur or professional. Practised in the pursuit of transparency and accessibility, open science encompasses movements such as open access publishing, open data, open-source software and tools, open peer review, and citizen science.

Principles

Advocates argue that publicly funded research should be publicly available, that sharing data and methods improves reproducibility and speeds discovery, and that openness helps counteract problems such as publication bias and the replication crisis identified in psychology, biomedicine, and other fields in the 2010s. The FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) provide widely adopted guidelines for managing research data.

Practices

Open science practices include posting preprints on servers such as arXiv (founded 1991) and bioRxiv; publishing in open-access journals or depositing manuscripts in repositories; preregistering study designs; sharing code and materials; and publishing datasets with persistent identifiers. Funders and governments have increasingly mandated such practices, including the European Union's Horizon programmes and the UNESCO Recommendation on Open Science adopted by 193 member states in 2021.

Criticism and debate

Debates continue over article processing charges that can shift costs to authors, the risk of predatory publishing, the protection of sensitive data, and the extent to which openness alone can remedy deeper incentive problems in academic publishing and career evaluation.
