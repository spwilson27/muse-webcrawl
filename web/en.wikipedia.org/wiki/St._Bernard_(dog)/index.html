St. Bernard (dog)

The St. Bernard is a breed of very large working dog from the Western Alps in Italy and Switzerland. They were originally bred for rescue work by the hospice of the Great St Bernard Pass on the Italian-Swiss border. The monks of the hospice used the dogs to locate and rescue travellers overcome by snowstorms and avalanches in the dangerous mountain pass.

History

The breed descends from large farm dogs of the region, crossed over time with mastiff-type dogs brought to the Alps by the Romans and later invaders. By the 17th and 18th centuries, the hospice dogs had become famous for their path-finding ability, keen sense of smell, and success at finding buried travellers. The most celebrated of the rescue dogs was Barry, credited with saving more than 40 lives in the early 19th century.

The popular image of the St. Bernard carrying a small cask of brandy around its neck derives largely from Edwin Landseer's 1820 painting Alpine Mastiffs Reanimating a Distressed Traveller; the keg was an artistic embellishment rather than documented practice.

Characteristics

St. Bernards are giant dogs, typically standing 70–90 cm (28–35 in) at the shoulder and weighing between 64 and 120 kg (140 and 264 lb). They may have short or long coats, in shades of red with white, or white with red markings. The breed is known for a calm, patient, and gentle temperament, making it good with children, though its size, drooling, and short average lifespan (around 8–10 years) are important considerations for owners.
