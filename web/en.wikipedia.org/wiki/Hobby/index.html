Hobby

A hobby is a regular activity done for enjoyment, typically during one's leisure time, not as a profession and not for pay. Hobbies include collecting themed items and objects, engaging in creative and artistic pursuits, playing sports, or pursuing other amusements. Participation in hobbies encourages acquiring substantial skills and knowledge in that area.

History

The word hobby derives from the hobby horse, a toy horse and an earlier term for a small horse; a "hobby" came to mean a favourite pursuit that one "rides" enthusiastically. The pursuit of hobbies grew with industrialisation, which gave workers regulated leisure time; by the mid-19th century hobbies such as stamp collecting, gardening and model building were widely promoted as improving pastimes. The 20th century added hobbies centred on new technologies, from amateur radio to home computing and video games.

Types

Common categories include collecting (stamps, coins, books, vinyl records), making and tinkering (crafts, woodworking, electronics, model kits), outdoor recreation (hiking, fishing, gardening), games and sports played for pleasure, and observation and learning pursuits (birdwatching, amateur astronomy, genealogy). Online communities have transformed many hobbies by connecting enthusiasts worldwide.

Psychology

Research associates hobbies with lower stress, improved mood and better work performance; structured, challenging hobbies can produce the state of "flow" described by psychologist Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi. Hobbies may also develop into small businesses or change careers, blurring the line between amateur and professional; the word amateur itself originally meant a lover of the pursuit.
