Robotics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: An orange industrial robot arm on a factory production line]

Robotics is the interdisciplinary study and practice of the design, construction, operation, and use of robots: machines that sense their environment, compute decisions, and act in the physical world. It draws on mechanical and electrical engineering, computer science, and increasingly machine learning.

Industrial robots (the first, Unimate, installed at a General Motors plant in 1961) dominate manufacturing — welding, painting, assembly. Modern robotics extends to surgical robots, warehouse automation, drones, planetary rovers, domestic vacuum robots, and humanoid research platforms.

Key problems

Perception (computer vision, sensor fusion), localisation and mapping (SLAM), motion planning, and control under uncertainty are core challenges. Moravec's paradox observes that tasks easy for humans (walking, grasping varied objects) are hard for robots, while tasks hard for humans (chess, arithmetic) proved easy for computers.

Learning-based methods — reinforcement learning and imitation learning — increasingly complement classical control, and foundation models are being adapted to robot perception and planning.

Society

Robots raise questions of employment displacement, safety (collaborative 'cobots' work beside humans), military use (autonomous weapons debates), and elder care. The word 'robot' itself comes from Karel Čapek's 1920 play R.U.R., from the Czech robota (forced labour).
