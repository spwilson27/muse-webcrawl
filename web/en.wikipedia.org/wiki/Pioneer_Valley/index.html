Pioneer Valley

The Pioneer Valley is the colloquial and promotional name for the portion of the Connecticut River Valley that lies in Massachusetts, in the United States. It generally comprises the three counties of Hampden, Hampshire, and Franklin, with Springfield as its largest city and commercial centre, and the college towns of Amherst and Northampton giving the upper valley a distinct academic and cultural character.

Geography and communities

The valley follows the Connecticut River from the Vermont and New Hampshire border south to the Connecticut state line, where the river continues through Hartford. The "Five Colleges" — Amherst College, Hampshire College, Mount Holyoke College, Smith College, and the University of Massachusetts Amherst — anchor the region's identity as the "Knowledge Corridor", a name also applied to the combined Springfield–Hartford metropolitan region.

History and economy

English settlement began at Springfield in 1636; the region developed around river trade, tobacco and produce farming in its fertile floodplain, and later manufacturing along its mill streams. Today the valley mixes agriculture, higher education, healthcare, and heritage tourism, with attractions including the Connecticut River, the Mount Holyoke Range, and historic Deerfield.
