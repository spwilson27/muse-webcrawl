Nordic combined

Nordic combined is a winter sport in which athletes compete in both ski jumping and cross-country skiing. It has been part of the Winter Olympic Games since the first edition in 1924 and is governed internationally by the International Ski Federation (FIS).

Nordic combined
Governing body: FIS
Olympic debut: Chamonix 1924
Disciplines: Ski jumping, cross-country skiing

History

The sport originated in Norway in the 19th century, where soldiers trained in both jumping and cross-country racing. For much of its history it was dominated by Norwegians, Finns, and later Germans and Japanese. The Holmenkollen Ski Festival in Oslo has hosted combined events since the 1890s.

Formats

Under the modern Gundersen method, introduced in the 1980s, ski jumping points are converted into time handicaps: the jumping winner starts the cross-country race first, and the first athlete across the finish line wins overall. Events include the individual normal hill/10 km, large hill/10 km, and team events. A women's World Cup circuit began in 2020–21, although a women's Olympic event was not included for 2026.

Notable athletes

Eric Frenzel of Germany and Jarl Magnus Riiber of Norway are among the most successful competitors in World Cup history; Samppa Lajunen of Finland won three gold medals at the 2002 Salt Lake City Olympics.
