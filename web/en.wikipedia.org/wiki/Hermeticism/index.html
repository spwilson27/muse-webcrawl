Hermeticism

Hermeticism, or Hermetism, is a philosophical and religious tradition rooted in writings attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, a syncretic figure combining the Greek god Hermes and the Egyptian god Thoth.

Texts

The Corpus Hermeticum (1st–3rd centuries CE) and the Emerald Tablet present a cosmology of a divine Mind (Nous), emanation, the correspondence 'as above, so below', and the soul's ascent. Technical Hermetica covered astrology, alchemy and magic.

Influence

Translated by Marsilio Ficino in Renaissance Florence, Hermetic texts influenced Renaissance humanism, natural philosophy and early science, and later esotericism including Rosicrucianism, Freemasonry and modern occult movements.

Scholarship

Isaac Casaubon showed in 1614 that the Corpus dated from the early Christian era, not primordial Egypt, reshaping its authority.
