Marcus Joel Borg (11 March 1942 – 21 January 2015) was an American New Testament scholar, theologian and author. He was a prominent figure in liberal and progressive Christianity and a leading voice in the historical Jesus movement of the late twentieth century, as well as a fellow of the Jesus Seminar.

Career and thought

Borg studied at Concordia College, Union Theological Seminary and Oxford University, where he earned his doctorate. He taught for most of his career at Oregon State University, where he held the Hundere Endowed Chair in Religion and Culture until his retirement in 2007. His best-known book, Meeting Jesus Again for the First Time (1994), became a bestseller and introduced many readers to historical Jesus scholarship; later works included The Heart of Christianity (2003), Speaking Christian (2011) and, with John Dominic Crossan, The Last Week and The First Paul.

Borg distinguished the "pre-Easter Jesus" — the historical Jewish teacher and healer — from the "post-Easter Jesus" of Christian tradition, and contrasted an "earlier paradigm" of Christianity centred on belief and heaven with an "emerging paradigm" centred on transformation in this life. He described his own theology as panentheistic. Critics in evangelical circles disputed his reconstruction of Jesus and his treatment of the resurrection, while admirers credited him with making scholarship accessible and renewing faith for many readers. He died of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis in 2015.
