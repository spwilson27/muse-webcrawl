Gulf of Gdańsk

The Gulf of Gdańsk (Polish: Zatoka Gdańska; German: Danziger Bucht; Russian: Гданьский залив) is a southeastern bay of the Baltic Sea, bordered by Poland and the Kaliningrad Oblast of Russia. It is named after the port city of Gdańsk, which lies at its western shore near the mouth of the Vistula River.

Geography

The gulf extends from the Hel Peninsula in the west, which encloses the shallow Bay of Puck, to the Sambian Peninsula in the east. The Vistula enters the gulf through the Vistula Lagoon and by direct channels near Gdańsk. Major ports include Gdańsk and Gdynia in Poland and Kaliningrad and Baltiysk in Russia. The gulf's waters are brackish, with salinity lower than that of the open Baltic because of the Vistula's inflow.

History

The gulf was a centre of the amber trade since antiquity and later of the Hanseatic commerce of Danzig (Gdańsk). On 1 September 1939 the German battleship Schleswig-Holstein opened fire on the Polish garrison at Westerplatte in the gulf, an action conventionally regarded as the start of the Second World War in Europe.
