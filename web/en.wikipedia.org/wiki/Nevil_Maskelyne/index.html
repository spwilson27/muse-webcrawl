Overview

Nevil Maskelyne (1732–1811) was the fifth Astronomer Royal (1765–1811), a central figure in the longitude story and in practical astronomy. Sent to St Helena in 1761 to observe the transit of Venus, he there developed the lunar-distance method of finding longitude at sea, and on a voyage to Barbados in 1763 he trialled both lunars and John Harrison's H4 timekeeper.

Work

As Astronomer Royal Maskelyne founded the Nautical Almanac (first published 1766, for 1767), which tabulated lunar distances and made the method usable by ordinary navigators — it remained a standard tool for two centuries. He championed lunars over chronometers (and is cast as the villain in popular Harrison retellings, though he also supported Harrison's later awards), organised the 1774 Schiehallion experiment to "weigh the Earth", and fixed the Greenwich meridian's role through his almanac. He was a cousin by marriage of Clive of India and a keen correspondent across European science.
