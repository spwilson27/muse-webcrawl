Central Alaskan Yup'ik language

Central Alaskan Yup'ik
Native name: Yugtun
Region: southwestern Alaska
Speakers: roughly 10,000–19,000
Family: Eskimo–Aleut > Yupik

Central Alaskan Yup'ik (self-name Yugtun) is an Eskimo–Aleut language spoken in western and southwestern Alaska, from Norton Sound to Bristol Bay and along the lower Yukon and Kuskokwim rivers. It is the most widely spoken Alaska Native language and one of the few with a substantial population of child speakers, though it is still considered threatened.

Characteristics

Yup'ik is polysynthetic and ergative–absolutive, building long words from a base plus derivational and inflectional suffixes. It has several regional dialects, including General Central Yup'ik, Norton Sound, Hooper Bay–Chevak (Cup'ik) and Nunivak (Cup'ig). A practical orthography in the Latin alphabet was developed in the 1960s by linguists and native speakers at the University of Alaska, and Yup'ik is taught in bilingual programmes such as the Yup'ik immersion schools in the Yukon–Kuskokwim delta.
