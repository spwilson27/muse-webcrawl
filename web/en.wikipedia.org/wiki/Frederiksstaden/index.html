Frederiksstaden is a district of central Copenhagen, Denmark, laid out in the mid-eighteenth century and regarded as one of the finest Rococo complexes in Europe. It was founded by King Frederick V in 1749 to celebrate the 300th anniversary of the accession of the House of Oldenburg to the Danish throne.

Layout

The district was planned by court architect Nicolai Eigtved on a grid of straight streets running parallel and perpendicular to the harbour. Its focal point is the octagonal Amalienborg Palace Square, where four identical Rococo palaces face an equestrian statue of Frederick V by Jacques Saly. The short axis of the square runs from the harbour, past the statue, to Frederik's Church (the Marble Church); in the modern era it has been extended across the harbour to the Copenhagen Opera House, forming the so-called Frederik Axis.

Principal buildings

Besides Amalienborg, the present residence of the Danish monarch, Frederiksstaden contains the Marble Church, the Design Museum Denmark (originally Frederik's Hospital), the former Surgical Academy, and a uniform stock of aristocratic town houses in Eigtved's restrained Rococo manner. The district also includes the waterfront residences along Amaliegade and Esplanaden.

Heritage

Nearly all of Frederiksstaden's original buildings are protected, and the district remains an affluent residential and institutional quarter of the Danish capital.
