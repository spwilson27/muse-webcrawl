Seven Factors of Enlightenment

Satta bojjhaṅgā (Pali)
Tradition: Buddhism
Part of: Bodhipakkhiyādhammā (37 aids to awakening)

The Seven Factors of Enlightenment (Pali: satta bojjhaṅgā; Sanskrit: sapta bodhyanga) are mental qualities cultivated in Buddhist practice that lead to awakening (bodhi). They form part of the thirty-seven aids to enlightenment (bodhipakkhiyādhammā) and are frequently expounded in the Pali Canon, notably in the Bojjhaṅga Saṃyutta.

The seven factors

1. Mindfulness (sati) — awareness and recollection of the present moment and of wholesome states. 2. Investigation of phenomena (dhamma vicaya) — discerning analysis of experience and doctrine. 3. Energy (viriya) — effort and persistence. 4. Joy or rapture (pīti) — delight arising in meditation. 5. Tranquillity (passaddhi) — calm of body and mind. 6. Concentration (samādhi) — collectedness and unification of mind. 7. Equanimity (upekkhā) — balanced, even-minded observation.

Development

The factors unfold in sequence in meditation: mindfulness supports investigation, which arouses energy; energy gives rise to joy, joy settles into tranquillity, tranquillity supports concentration, and concentration matures into equanimity. The first three are considered "arousing" factors useful against sloth, while tranquillity, concentration, and equanimity are "calming" factors useful against restlessness; mindfulness balances both groups.

Healing tradition

In the Bojjhaṅga Suttas, the Buddha and senior disciples recite the seven factors to one another during illness, and recovery follows — a tradition continued today in the chanting of these texts for the sick in Theravada countries.
