The colossal squid (Mesonychoteuthis hamiltoni) is a species of squid in the family Cranchiidae. It is sometimes called the Antarctic squid or giant cranch squid and is believed to be the largest squid species in terms of mass. It is the only recognized member of the genus Mesonychoteuthis and is known from only a small number of specimens.

The colossal squid is distinguished from the giant squid (Architeuthis) by having sharp hooks on its arms and tentacles rather than suckers lined with small teeth, and by its mantle being wider and longer in mature specimens, although its tentacles are shorter relative to body size.

Distribution

The species occurs in the Southern Ocean, in cold waters surrounding Antarctica and extending as far north as the waters around New Zealand, South Africa and South America. It lives in deep water, generally at depths of about 300 to 2,200 metres, migrating to shallower waters at night.

Discovery and specimens

The species was first described in 1925 by Guy Coburn Robson, based on two partial specimens recovered from the stomach of a sperm whale. Few intact specimens have been recovered since. In February 2007, New Zealand fishermen aboard the vessel San Aspiring caught a large living specimen weighing an estimated 495 kilograms (1,091 lb) in the Ross Sea; it was taken to the Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa and thawed and studied in 2008.

Size

Based on beak measurements and partial remains, the colossal squid is estimated to reach a total length of 10 to 14 metres (33 to 46 ft), making it comparable in length to, and heavier than, the giant squid. Its eyes, up to 27 centimetres (11 in) across with an 8–9 cm pupil, are among the largest known in the animal kingdom.

Biology

The colossal squid is preyed upon by sperm whales, which in turn may hunt it actively; scarred suckers and hooks on whale skin suggest struggles between the two animals. Its diet is thought to include toothfish and other large fish as well as smaller squid. Little is known of its life history; it is thought to use bioluminescent bait on its tentacles to lure prey in the darkness of the deep ocean.
