The Alismataceae, the water-plantain family, is a family of flowering plants in the order Alismatales, comprising about 117 species of aquatic and marsh plants distributed worldwide, mainly in the Northern Hemisphere.

Description

Members are mostly perennial herbs rooted in mud in shallow water, with basal leaves that may be submerged, floating, or emergent. Flowers typically have three petals and three sepals, numerous stamens and carpels, and produce achenes or follicles as fruit. Well-known genera include Alisma (water plantains), Sagittaria (arrowheads), and Echinodorus, several of which are popular aquarium plants.

Ecology and uses

Species of Sagittaria produce edible tubers eaten by waterfowl and historically by humans, while some family members are invasive weeds of wetlands and waterways.
