The Expo Line is the oldest line of the SkyTrain rapid transit system in the Metro Vancouver region of British Columbia, Canada. Operated for TransLink by the British Columbia Rapid Transit Company, the driverless line runs from Waterfront station in downtown Vancouver through Burnaby and New Westminster to Surrey, with a branch to Production Way–University in Burnaby. It is named for Expo 86, the world exposition for which the system was built as a showcase for the automated transit technology developed in Ontario.

History

The initial line opened on December 11, 1985, in time for Expo 86, running from Waterfront to New Westminster. The SkyBridge, then the world's longest cable-supported transit-only bridge, carried the line across the Fraser River to Surrey in 1990, and extensions reached King George station in 1994. The Millennium Line, opened in 2002, originally shared trackage with the Expo Line; in 2016 the service patterns were reorganized so that the Expo Line took over the branch to Production Way–University. A further extension to Langley City along Fraser Highway has been under construction in the 2020s.

Route and fleet

The line runs elevated and underground on a mostly dedicated right-of-way, with trains automated under the same moving-block control system used across SkyTrain. Its fleet includes the original Mark I cars, Mark II and Mark III trains, and Mark V trains entering service in the mid-2020s. Stations such as Commercial–Broadway and Metrotown are among the busiest in the region.
