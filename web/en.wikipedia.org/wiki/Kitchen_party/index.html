Kitchen party

A kitchen party brings music, dancing and company into the warmest room of the house.

A kitchen party is an informal social gathering centred on music, storytelling, food, and drink, traditional in Atlantic Canada — particularly Newfoundland and Labrador, Cape Breton, and Prince Edward Island — and in parts of rural Scotland and Ireland from which many of its customs derive. Because the kitchen was the warmest room in the house, it naturally became the place where neighbours gathered through the winter.

Traditions

Guests crowd around the kitchen table while fiddlers, accordion players, and guitarists play reels and jigs; spoons and the "ugly stick" provide percussion, and step dancing may break out on the kitchen floor. Food is typically home-baked — tea, biscuits, and preserves — and the host's duty is that no one leaves hungry or without having been persuaded to sing or play. The Newfoundland band Great Big Sea popularised the phrase with their song "Excursion Around the Bay" and the album Kitchen Party by various artists; the expression "kitchen party" is also used fondly across Atlantic Canada for any crowded, convivial house party, and gave its name to a popular Canadian radio and television motif of East Coast hospitality.
