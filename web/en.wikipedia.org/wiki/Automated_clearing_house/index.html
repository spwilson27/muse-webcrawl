Automated clearing house

An automated clearing house (ACH) is a computer-based electronic network for processing transactions, usually domestic low-value payments, between participating financial institutions. It may support both credit transfers and direct debits. The ACH system is designed to process batches of payments containing numerous transactions, and it charges fees low enough to encourage its use for low-value payments.

Uses

ACH networks are used for a wide variety of payment transactions, including direct deposit of payroll, Social Security and other government benefits, consumer bills such as mortgages, loans, utilities, insurance premiums, rent and subscriptions, business-to-business payments, and person-to-person payments. In the United States, the network is operated by Nacha and processes tens of billions of transactions annually.

Operation

Transactions are submitted by an originating depository financial institution in batch files to an ACH operator, which sorts the entries and routes them to the receiving institutions. Settlement typically occurs one to three business days after submission, although Same Day ACH services introduced in 2016 allow faster settlement windows. Because processing is batched rather than real-time, ACH transfers are less expensive than wire transfers but slower than instant payment systems such as FedNow or the RTP network.

International systems

Similar systems operate around the world, including the SEPA system in Europe, Bacs in the United Kingdom, and the Canadian Payments Association's ACSS. These systems share the model of batch processing of low-value electronic payments between banks.
