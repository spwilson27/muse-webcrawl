Throne of God

Throne of God
TraditionsJudaism, Christianity, Islam
Key textsIsaiah 6; Ezekiel 1; Psalm 47; Revelation 4–5; Qur'an 2:255 (Ayat al-Kursi)
HebrewKisse Hakavod ("Throne of Glory")
Arabical-ʿArsh (العرش); al-Kursī (الكرسي)

The Throne of God is the reigning centre of God in the Abrahamic religions, depicted in scripture as a celestial seat from which God rules the universe. Prophetic visions of the throne appear throughout the Hebrew Bible, the New Testament, and the Qur'an, and the throne is a central image in Jewish, Christian, and Islamic theology and liturgy.

In the Hebrew Bible

The prophet Isaiah describes a vision of God "sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up", attended by seraphim crying "Holy, holy, holy" (Isaiah 6). Ezekiel's opening vision describes a wheeled throne-chariot — the merkabah — bearing "a likeness like the appearance of a man" amid fire and the four living creatures. The Psalms repeatedly proclaim that "God reigns over the nations; God sits on his holy throne" (Psalm 47:8), and Micaiah and Daniel also describe heavenly throne scenes, Daniel's vision of the "Ancient of Days" being especially influential.

In Christianity

In the New Testament, the Book of Revelation (chapters 4–5) gives the most elaborate description: a throne in heaven surrounded by twenty-four elders and four living creatures, with a sea of glass before it and the Lamb (Christ) at its centre. Jesus speaks of the Son of Man seated on "his glorious throne" at the last judgement (Matthew 25:31), and Christian theology distinguishes the shared throne of God and of the Lamb. The throne figures in hymns, creeds ("seated at the right hand of the Father"), and church architecture such as the bishop's cathedra.

In Islam

In the Qur'an, the Throne of God (al-ʿArsh) is described as borne by angels and as encompassing the heavens and the earth. The famous Throne Verse, Ayat al-Kursi (Qur'an 2:255), speaks of God's Kursī (footstool/throne) extending over the heavens and the earth. Islamic theologians have debated whether the throne is to be understood literally or metaphorically; the majority tradition affirms it as a created reality "without asking how".

In Judaism

The throne of glory (Kisse Hakavod) is a major theme of early Jewish mysticism, especially the Hekhalot literature and Merkabah ("chariot") mysticism built around Ezekiel's vision. In Jewish liturgy, the throne image recurs in the High Holy Day prayers, which picture God rising from the throne of judgement to the throne of mercy.
