Montenegrin (crnogorski, црногорски) is a South Slavic language, the official language of Montenegro. It is a standardised variety of the Shtokavian dialect of Serbo-Croatian, closely related to Serbian, Croatian, and Bosnian, with which it is mutually intelligible. Whether it constitutes a separate language is a matter of both linguistics and national identity, and the question has been politically contentious since Montenegro's independence in 2006.

History and status

Montenegro's 2007 constitution declared Montenegrin the official language of the state. A standard orthography was adopted in 2009–2010, and a grammar and dictionary followed. In 2017 the International Organization for Standardization assigned Montenegrin its own language code (cnr), separate from Serbian.

Features

The standard is based on the Ijekavian pronunciation of the East Herzegovinian dialect, as is the Serbian Ijekavian standard spoken in Montenegro. Montenegrin orthography added two letters not found in the Serbian alphabet — Latin ś and ź (Cyrillic с́ and з́) — to represent palatalised sounds heard in some Montenegrin speech. Both the Latin and Cyrillic alphabets are in official use, with Latin predominating in practice.

Usage

In Montenegro's census, residents variously declare their language as Montenegrin or Serbian, with the proportions shifting between censuses; a large plurality declared Serbian in 2011 and 2023, while Montenegrin was declared by a substantial minority and is the language of state institutions and education.
