Environmental criminology

FieldCriminology
Key conceptsRoutine activity theory, crime pattern theory, rational choice

Environmental criminology focuses on criminal patterns within particular built environments and analyzes the impacts of these external variables on people's cognitive behavior. It forms a part of criminology's positivist school in that it applies the scientific method to examine the society that causes crime.

Theories

The field encompasses routine activity theory, which holds that crime occurs when a motivated offender, a suitable target, and the absence of a capable guardian converge in time and space; crime pattern theory, which examines how offenders become aware of opportunities through their everyday activity spaces; and the rational choice perspective, which views offenders as weighing costs and benefits.

Applications

Environmental criminology underpins crime prevention through environmental design (CPTED) and situational crime prevention, using measures such as improved lighting, sight lines, and access control to reduce criminal opportunities.
