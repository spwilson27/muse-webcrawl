INF Clairefontaine

The Institut national du football de Clairefontaine, commonly referred to as INF Clairefontaine or simply Clairefontaine, is the national football centre of France, located at Clairefontaine-en-Yvelines, about 50 km southwest of Paris. It is one of the most renowned football academies in the world and serves as the base of the French national teams.

History

The centre was conceived by Fernand Sastre, president of the French Football Federation (FFF), and officially opened in June 1988 by François Mitterrand, President of France. The youth academy itself, the INF (Institut National du Football), had been founded in 1972 at Vichy and later moved to Clairefontaine. The estate's centrepiece is a château that houses the senior national team during training camps, its grounds famously decorated with photographs of France's World Cup-winning squads of 1998 and 2018.

Academy

Each year the academy selects a small intake of boys (and, since 2014, a parallel girls' programme in cooperation with the national youth setup) aged about 13 from the Île-de-France region, who live and train at Clairefontaine for three years while continuing school. Graduates include Thierry Henry, Nicolas Anelka, William Gallas, and Kylian Mbappé, among many other French internationals.
