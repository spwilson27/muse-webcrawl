Kingdom of Naples (Napoleonic)

The Kingdom of Naples under Napoleonic rule (1806–1815) was a client state of the French Empire created after Napoleon deposed the Bourbon king Ferdinand IV. It was ruled first by Joseph Bonaparte (1806–1808) and then by Joachim Murat (1808–1815).

History

French forces occupied Naples in early 1806 after Ferdinand fled to Sicily. Joseph abolished feudalism, reformed taxation and administration, and introduced the Napoleonic Code. Murat, who succeeded him, pursued a more autonomous course, modernising the army and administration while quietly negotiating with France's enemies. After Napoleon's fall, Murat's attempt to hold the kingdom ended with defeat at Tolentino in May 1815; the Bourbons were restored and the kingdom merged into the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies in 1816.

Administration

The regime reorganised the kingdom into provinces and districts on the French model, suppressed many religious orders, and confiscated and sold church lands, creating a new class of landowners.
