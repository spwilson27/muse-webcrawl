Monastiraki

Monastiraki (Greek: Μοναστηράκι, "little monastery") is a historic neighbourhood and flea-market district in the old town of Athens, Greece, at the foot of the Acropolis, just north of the ancient Agora. It is one of the principal shopping districts of the city, known for its flea market, its Ottoman- and Byzantine-era monuments, and its panoramic views of the Acropolis.

Monastiraki Square, with the Tzistarakis Mosque and the Acropolis in the background.

History

The area takes its name from a small monastery church, the Church of the Pantanassa, which stands on the main square and was once attached to a monastery. In Ottoman times the district was the commercial heart of Athens: the Tzistarakis Mosque, built in 1759 by the Ottoman voivode of Athens, Mustafa Agha Tzistarakis, stands on the square and today houses a museum of Greek folk pottery (an annex of the Museum of Greek Folk Art). Nearby on Adrianou Street stands the Tower of the Winds and the Roman Agora, as well as the Library of Hadrian, built by the Roman emperor Hadrian in AD 132.

The flea market

Monastiraki's flea market stretches along Pandrossou Street and the surrounding lanes around Avissinias Square. It grew up in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when refugee and antiques traders set up shop in the area, and today it mixes permanent antique and souvenir shops with stalls selling bric-a-brac, old books, vinyl records, military surplus, jewellery, leather goods and traditional instruments. The Sunday market is particularly crowded, and bargaining is customary.

Transport

The district is served by Monastiraki station, a busy interchange of Athens Metro Lines 1 and 3 with a station building dating to 1895, which also offers views over the ancient Agora and the Temple of Hephaestus.
