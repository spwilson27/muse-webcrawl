Haüyne

Haüyne
Category: Tectosilicate, sodalite group
Formula: (Na,Ca)₄₋₈Al₆Si₆(O,S)₂₄(SO₄,Cl)₁₋₂
Colour: Blue, white, grey, yellow, green, pink
Mohs hardness: 5–6

Haüyne, or hauynite, is a tectosilicate sulfate mineral of the sodalite group with a predominantly blue colour. It is a feldspathoid mineral occurring in silica-poor volcanic rocks such as phonolites, and is a component of lapis lazuli, in which it accounts for much of the prized blue colouring.

Occurrence

Haüyne was first described in 1807 from Vesuvius, Italy, and named for French crystallographer René Just Haüy. It forms dodecahedral crystals and granular masses in volcanic ejecta and lavas at localities including the Eifel district of Germany, the Roman volcanic province of Italy, and the Canary Islands.

Properties

The mineral is typically bright blue but may be white, grey, or greenish. It has a vitreous to greasy lustre, a hardness of 5 to 6, and is transparent to translucent. Gem-quality transparent blue crystals are rare and occasionally faceted for collectors.
