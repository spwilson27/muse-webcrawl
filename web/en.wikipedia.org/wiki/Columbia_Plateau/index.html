Columbia Plateau

Columbia Plateau
Location: Washington, Oregon, Idaho, U.S.
Area: about 260,000 km²
Geology: Columbia River flood basalts
Major rivers: Columbia, Snake

The Columbia Plateau is a geologic and geographic region in the Pacific Northwest of the United States, covering much of Washington, Oregon, and Idaho between the Cascade Range and the Rocky Mountains. It is formed principally by the Columbia River Basalt Group, one of the world's largest flood basalt provinces.

Geology

Between about 17 and 6 million years ago, enormous fissure eruptions poured out layered lava flows that buried the landscape to depths of up to several kilometres in places. During the last ice age, repeated Missoula Floods scoured channels and coulees across the plateau, creating the Channeled Scablands and depositing fertile wind-blown loess.

Geography and climate

The plateau is drained by the Columbia River and its tributary the Snake River, which cut deep gorges through the basalts. East of the Cascades the climate is semi-arid steppe, with sagebrush shrublands and bunchgrass; higher areas support ponderosa pine forest.

Economy

The loess soils of the Palouse support intensive dryland wheat farming, while irrigated areas supplied by projects such as the Columbia Basin Project produce potatoes, apples, and wine grapes. Hydroelectric dams on the Columbia and Snake provide power and navigation, and the region includes the Hanford Site and several Native American reservations, including those of the Yakama Nation, the Nez Perce, and the Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla.
