Benin

The flag of Benin. Cotonou is the seat of government; Porto-Novo is the capital.

Benin, officially the Republic of Benin, is a country in West Africa bordered by Togo to the west, Nigeria to the east, Burkina Faso to the north-west and Niger to the north-east, with a short coastline on the Bight of Benin to the south. Its capital is Porto-Novo, while Cotonou is the largest city and seat of government.

History

From the 17th to the 19th century the region was dominated by the Kingdom of Dahomey, a powerful Fon state centred on Abomey, known for its palace bas-reliefs, its army — including the women soldiers Europeans called the Dahomey Amazons — and its role in the Atlantic slave trade along the "Slave Coast". France seized the area in the 1890s, and Dahomey became part of French West Africa.

The country gained independence on 1 August 1960 as the Republic of Dahomey. After a period of coups, Mathieu Kérékou seized power in 1972 and proclaimed a Marxist-Leninist state. In 1975 the country was renamed Benin, after the Bight of Benin. Following economic crisis, a national conference in 1990 peacefully restored multiparty democracy — an often-cited model of democratic transition in Africa — and Nicéphore Soglo won the 1991 presidential election.

Geography and economy

Benin stretches from the Niger River in the north to the Atlantic coast in the south, passing through savanna and the Atakora mountains in the north-west. Agriculture employs much of the population; cotton is the leading export, and the port of Cotonou serves landlocked neighbours including Niger. The Pendjari and W national parks in the north protect elephants, lions and other West African wildlife.

Culture

Benin is recognised as the birthplace of Vodun (Voodoo), which is an officially recognised religion; the annual Vodun festival is held in Ouidah on 10 January. The country has more than fifty ethnic groups, including the Fon, Yoruba, Adja and Bariba. Beninese music, notably the singer Angélique Kidjo, has achieved international prominence, and the royal palaces of Abomey are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
