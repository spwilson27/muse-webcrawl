Oyster card

The Oyster card is a payment method in the form of a contactless smart card used on public transport in Greater London, England. Issued by Transport for London (TfL), it was first made available to the public in 2003.

Use

Passengers touch the card on yellow readers when entering and, for rail journeys, leaving the system. The card can hold pay-as-you-go credit and Travelcard season tickets, and is valid on the London Underground, buses, trams, the Docklands Light Railway, London Overground, the Elizabeth line, most National Rail services within London, and river services. Daily and weekly capping automatically limits charges to the price of the equivalent pass.

Technology and legacy

The system was developed under the TranSys consortium using MIFARE technology, later migrating to DESFire. From 2012, TfL also accepted contactless bank cards and mobile payments, which gradually overtook Oyster in usage, but the card remains widely used, particularly by visitors and those without bank cards. Its name was chosen to evoke the oyster shell's pearl, security, and the phrase "the world is your oyster".
