Via Dolorosa

The Via Dolorosa (Latin for "Sorrowful Way", often translated "Way of Suffering"; Arabic: طريق الآلام) is a processional route in the Old City of Jerusalem, today about 600 metres long, that commemorates the path Jesus is believed to have taken, carrying the cross, from his condemnation by Pontius Pilate to his crucifixion and burial. It is marked by the fourteen Stations of the Cross and ends at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, and it is a focus of Christian pilgrimage.

Pilgrims on the Via Dolorosa, Jerusalem

The route

The route begins in the Muslim Quarter near the site of the Antonia Fortress and the Monastery of the Flagellation, and winds westward through the Old City to the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in the Christian Quarter. Franciscan friars have led a weekly Friday procession along the Via Dolorosa since the 14th century, when the Franciscan Custody of the Holy Land took responsibility for guiding pilgrims.

Stations of the Cross

  1. Jesus is condemned to death
  2. Jesus takes up his cross
  3. Jesus falls the first time
  4. Jesus meets his mother
  5. Simon of Cyrene helps carry the cross
  6. Veronica wipes the face of Jesus
  7. Jesus falls the second time
  8. Jesus meets the women of Jerusalem
  9. Jesus falls the third time
  10. Jesus is stripped of his garments
  11. Jesus is nailed to the cross
  12. Jesus dies on the cross
  13. Jesus is taken down from the cross
  14. Jesus is laid in the tomb

The last five stations are inside the Church of the Holy Sepulchre. Several stations rest on tradition rather than scriptural accounts, and the precise route has shifted over the centuries; the modern route largely crystallised in the 18th and 19th centuries.
