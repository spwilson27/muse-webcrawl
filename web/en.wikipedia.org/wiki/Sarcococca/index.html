Sarcococca

Sarcococca (commonly called sweet box or Christmas box) is a genus of flowering plants in the box family, Buxaceae, native to eastern and southeastern Asia and the Himalayas. The genus comprises around 11–20 species of evergreen shrubs valued in gardens for their glossy foliage and intensely fragrant small winter flowers.

Description

Sarcococca species are monoecious evergreen shrubs with alternate, leathery, lanceolate leaves. The flowers are small, petal-less and inconspicuous but produce a powerful sweet scent in late winter; male flowers carry prominent stamens. They are followed by fleshy drupe-like fruits, black, red or white depending on the species.

Cultivation

Sweet box is prized as a shade-tolerant groundcover and low hedging plant for difficult dry shade beneath trees. Widely grown species include Sarcococca confusa, S. ruscifolia, S. hookeriana (Himalayan sweet box, including the compact form 'Humilis') and S. saligna. Plants prefer moist, well-drained soil but tolerate drought once established.

Etymology

The name combines the Greek sarx (flesh) and kokkos (berry), referring to the fleshy fruit.
