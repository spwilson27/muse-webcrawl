Baseball scorekeeping

A traditional scorecard grid

Baseball scorekeeping is the practice of recording the details of a baseball game as it unfolds. An official scorer, appointed for each professional game, records every play; fans and journalists also keep score using paper scorecards or electronic applications. The resulting record forms the basis of the box score and of player statistics.

Position numbering

Each defensive position is assigned a standard number used in recording plays:

NumberPosition
1Pitcher
2Catcher
3First baseman
4Second baseman
5Third baseman
6Shortstop
7Left fielder
8Center fielder
9Right fielder

Common notation

A ground out from the shortstop to first base is written "6-3". A strikeout is recorded as "K" (or a backwards K for a called third strike), a walk as "BB", a single as "1B", a double as "2B", a triple as "3B", and a home run as "HR". Fly outs and line outs are often noted with F or L followed by the fielder's number. Errors are marked E followed by the position number of the fielder charged.

History

Systematic scorekeeping developed in the mid-19th century alongside the organized game. Journalist Henry Chadwick is credited with popularizing the box score and many of the conventions still in use. Modern professional scorekeeping is supplemented by pitch-tracking systems that log the type, speed, and location of every pitch.
