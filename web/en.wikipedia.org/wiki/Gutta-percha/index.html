Gutta-percha

Gutta-percha
Source: Palaquium gutta tree latex
Region: Southeast Asia
Uses: Cable insulation, dentistry, golf balls

Gutta-percha is a type of natural latex produced from the sap of trees of the genus Palaquium, native to Southeast Asia. Unlike rubber, it is rigid at room temperature but becomes pliable when heated, and is biologically inert and electrically insulating.

History

Introduced to Europe in the 1840s, gutta-percha became essential as insulation for submarine telegraph cables, enabling the first transatlantic cables. It was also widely used for golf ball covers (the "guttie"), furniture, and jewellery.

Modern uses

Today gutta-percha is still used in dentistry as the standard filling material for root canal treatment (endodontics), in the form of gutta-percha points. Synthetic substitutes have replaced it in most industrial applications.

Properties

It is the trans-isomer of polyisoprene, making it harder and less elastic than natural rubber (the cis-isomer).
