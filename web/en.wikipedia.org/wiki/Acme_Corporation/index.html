Acme Corporation

The Acme Corporation is a fictional company appearing in numerous cartoons, films, and other media, most famously the Warner Bros. Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies shorts featuring Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner. Acme sells an extravagant catalogue of products — anvils, rocket-powered roller skates, instant tornado seeds, dehydrated boulders, earthquake pills — ordered by mail by the Coyote in his pursuit of the Road Runner; the devices invariably malfunction catastrophically, usually injuring the Coyote.

Name and origins

"Acme" comes from the Greek akmē (ἀκμή), "peak" or "prime", and was a popular name for real businesses in the early 20th century, partly because it appeared near the front of the alphabetically arranged Yellow Pages. Director Chuck Jones and the Warner Bros. animators adopted Acme as a running gag beginning around the Road Runner's debut in Fast and Furry-ous (1949), and the brand thereafter pervaded the studio's cartoons.

Appearances elsewhere

The name became a general pop-cultural shorthand for a generic company. Acme appears in Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988), where the Acme Corporation of Toontown and its murdered owner Marvin Acme drive the plot; in the film Good Burger; in Disney and Tiny Toon Adventures references; and in countless parodies. Real companies have also used the name, including the Acme Markets supermarket chain and the former Acme Brick Company, one of the largest US brick manufacturers.

Legal note

A widely circulated 1994 piece of humor, "Wile E. Coyote v. Acme Company", written by Ian Frazier for The New Yorker, is a mock legal complaint cataloguing the Coyote's injuries from defective Acme products; it is frequently mistaken for a real court filing.
