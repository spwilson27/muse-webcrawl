de Havilland

The de Havilland Aircraft Company Limited was a British aviation manufacturer established in late 1920 by Geoffrey de Havilland at Stag Lane Aerodrome in Edgware on the outskirts of London. It became known for innovative aircraft including the DH.60 Moth trainer, the Mosquito multirole combat aircraft of World War II, and the Comet, the world's first commercial jet airliner.

History

Geoffrey de Havilland had earlier designed aircraft for Airco, including the DH.4 bomber. His own company produced a long line of civil and military designs, among them the Tiger Moth trainer, the Dragon Rapide airliner, the Vampire jet fighter, and the Mosquito, built largely of wood.

Later years

The Comet's early fatal accidents, caused by metal fatigue, damaged the company, and in 1960 de Havilland was absorbed into Hawker Siddeley Aviation, later becoming part of British Aerospace. The de Havilland name survived in Canada through de Havilland Canada.
