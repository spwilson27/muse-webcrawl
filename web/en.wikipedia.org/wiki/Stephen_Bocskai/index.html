Stephen Bocskai

Stephen Bocskai (Hungarian: Bocskai István; 1 January 1557 – 29 December 1606) was a Hungarian nobleman who led a successful uprising against the Habsburg emperor Rudolf II and became Prince of Transylvania (1605–1606) and Prince of Hungary. His rebellion, the Bocskai uprising (1604–1606), secured important concessions for Hungarian Protestants and the Hungarian estates.

Early career

Bocskai was born in Kolozsvár (Cluj) into a family of the Hungarian lower nobility. He served at the Transylvanian court of his uncle Christopher Báthory and later of Sigismund Báthory, undertaking diplomatic missions to Prague and Vienna. He accumulated extensive estates in Bihar County and became one of the largest landowners of the region. During the Long Turkish War he fought the Ottomans, but Habsburg confiscations and forced Catholicisation in Royal Hungary turned him against the emperor.

The uprising

In 1604, after imperial troops seized his estates and Catholic forces took the Protestant church at Kassa, Bocskai raised the standard of revolt. His army, built around the Hajdú soldier-peasants whom he later settled and enfranchised with collective noble privileges, won a series of victories in Upper Hungary. The Hungarian Diet elected him prince of Hungary in April 1605, and the Transylvanian Diet elected him prince of Transylvania; the Ottoman sultan Ahmed I recognised him and sent him a crown.

Peace settlements and death

Rather than pursue a war on two fronts, Bocskai acted as mediator between the emperor and the sultan. The Peace of Vienna (23 June 1606) guaranteed the Hungarian estates their constitutional rights and religious freedom for Protestants in Royal Hungary, while the Peace of Zsitvatorok ended the Long War with the Ottomans. Bocskai died at Kassa shortly afterwards, on 29 December 1606 — some suspected poisoning. His settlement of the Hajdús in towns such as Hajdúböszörmény remains his most durable legacy.
