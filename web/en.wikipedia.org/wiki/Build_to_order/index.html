Build to order

Assembling a product to a customer's specification

Build to order (BTO), also known as make to order (MTO), is a production approach in which a product is not built until a confirmed order for it has been received. It is the opposite of the traditional build-to-stock approach, in which products are manufactured in anticipation of demand and held in inventory.

Advantages

Because production is triggered by actual demand, build-to-order firms hold less finished-goods inventory, reducing storage costs and the risk of unsold stock. BTO also makes mass customisation practical: each unit can be configured to the customer's requirements, as in Dell's made-to-order personal computers, which combined BTO with just-in-time component supply to offer customised machines at close to mass-production prices.

Disadvantages

Customers must wait for the product to be manufactured, which lengthens lead times compared with off-the-shelf purchase. The approach demands a flexible supply chain and efficient order processing, and is less suitable for very high-volume commodity products where economies of scale dominate.

Variants

Related strategies include assemble to order, in which standard components are held in stock and assembled only when an order arrives; engineer to order, in which the product is designed as well as built after the order; and configure to order, common in software and electronics, in which the customer selects options from a predefined set.
