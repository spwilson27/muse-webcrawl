Urban warfare

Urban warfare is warfare conducted in urban areas such as towns and cities. It differs from combat in open fields in its physical complexity — dense construction, subterranean spaces, and intermingled civilian populations constrain visibility, manoeuvre, and the use of heavy firepower. Militaries often refer to it by acronyms such as MOUT (military operations on urbanised terrain), FIBUA (fighting in built-up areas), or FISH (fighting in someone's house).

Characteristics

Built-up terrain channels movement along streets, where vehicles are vulnerable to ambush from rooftops and upper floors; buildings provide ready-made fortifications and concealment, and rubble can increase defensive advantage. Combat typically devolves to small infantry units fighting room-to-room, supported by armour, engineers, and snipers. Communications and situational awareness are degraded, logistics are difficult, and the presence of civilians raises acute legal and moral issues under the law of armed conflict. Urban battles are frequently among the costliest in modern war for both attackers and defenders.

Historical examples

  • Battle of Stalingrad (1942–43)
  • Battle of Berlin (1945)
  • Battle of Huế (1968)
  • Battle of Mogadishu (1993)
  • Battle of Fallujah (2004)
  • Battle of Aleppo (2012–16)
  • Battle of Mosul (2016–17)
  • Battle of Mariupol (2022)

Doctrine and training

Modern armies maintain dedicated urban training facilities — mock cities with movable walls, tunnels, and role-playing civilians — and have developed specialised tactics, breaching techniques, and equipment, including micro-drones and robots for reconnaissance. Because a majority of the world's population now lives in cities, strategists expect urban operations to remain a central feature of future conflict.
