The Third Man

The Third Man is a 1949 British film noir directed by Carol Reed, written by Graham Greene, and starring Joseph Cotten, Alida Valli, Orson Welles, and Trevor Howard. Set in postwar Vienna, divided between the Allied occupying powers, it is celebrated for its atmospheric use of black-and-white cinematography, its tilted camera angles, its locations among the city's bombed ruins and sewers, and Anton Karas's distinctive zither score.

Plot

American pulp Western writer Holly Martins arrives in Vienna at the invitation of his childhood friend Harry Lime, only to be told that Lime has died in a traffic accident. Suspicious of conflicting accounts — in particular of a mysterious "third man" seen at the scene — Martins investigates and discovers that Lime faked his death and runs a racket diluting stolen penicillin, with deadly consequences for patients. The story climaxes in a celebrated chase through the Vienna sewers and a famous final shot of Anna, Lime's lover, walking past Martins without a glance.

Legacy

The film won the Grand Prix at the 1949 Cannes Film Festival and the Academy Award for Best Cinematography. Welles's "cuckoo clock" speech is one of the most quoted passages in cinema. In 1999 the British Film Institute voted it the greatest British film of all time.
