Time ball

A time ball or timeball is a large painted wood or metal ball that is dropped at a predetermined time, principally to allow navigators aboard ships offshore to verify the setting of their marine chronometers. Accurate timekeeping by chronometer was essential for computing longitude.

History

The first time ball was erected at Portsmouth, England, in 1829 by its inventor, Royal Navy Captain Robert Wauchope; another followed at Greenwich in 1833, dropping at 1 p.m. About 160 time balls were installed at ports around the world. Observers watched the ball begin to fall — the exact instant marking the hour — and rated their chronometers accordingly.

Later use

Time balls were superseded by radio time signals and GPS, but several survive as landmarks, and the tradition inspired the illuminated ball drop held each New Year's Eve in New York's Times Square since 1907.
