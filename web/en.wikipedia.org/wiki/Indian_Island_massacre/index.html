Indian Island massacre

The Indian Island massacre (also known as the Tuluwat massacre) took place in the early morning of 26 February 1860, when a group of white settlers attacked Wiyot people gathered on Indian Island (Tuluwat) in Humboldt Bay, near Eureka, California. The Wiyot had just completed their week-long World Renewal ceremony and the men were away from the island; the attackers killed primarily women, children and elderly men, using hatchets and other weapons. Estimates of the dead range from about 80 to 200, and simultaneous attacks were carried out at other Wiyot sites around the bay.

Context and aftermath

The massacre occurred amid widespread violence against Native Californians during the settlement of the state's north coast. Despite local newspaper editorials condemning the killings and an account by the writer Bret Harte, who was driven from the area for his criticism, no one was ever prosecuted. Survivors were rounded up and removed, and the Wiyot population was devastated.

Commemoration and return of Tuluwat

In 2000 the Wiyot Tribe began purchasing parts of the island, and in 2019 the city of Eureka formally returned the remainder of its holdings on Tuluwat to the tribe — described as a landmark act of land return. The tribe has worked to restore the island's environment and revive the World Renewal ceremony.
