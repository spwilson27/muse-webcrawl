Chanel No. 5

Chanel No. 5 is a perfume created in 1921 by the French-Russian perfumer Ernest Beaux for the fashion house of Gabrielle "Coco" Chanel. Generally regarded as the most famous fragrance in the world, it was revolutionary for its abstract floral composition built around aldehydes, and for its stark, modernist bottle.

Creation

Chanel wanted a scent that was artificial and abstract — "a woman's perfume with the scent of a woman." Beaux presented a series of numbered samples; Chanel chose the fifth, reputedly saying the number was her lucky one, and the perfume launched on the fifth day of the fifth month of 1921. Its heavy use of aliphatic aldehydes gave it a sparkling, soapy-clean freshness unlike the simple floral soliflores then fashionable.

Composition

The parfum features top notes of aldehydes, neroli and ylang-ylang; a heart of jasmine from Grasse, May rose, iris and lily of the valley; and a base of sandalwood, vanilla, musk, civet and amber. Jacques Polge, Chanel's in-house perfumer from 1978, maintained and reinterpreted the formula across concentrations.

Cultural impact

No. 5's fame was cemented when Marilyn Monroe, asked what she wore to bed, replied "five drops of Chanel No. 5." The bottle is held in the collection of the Museum of Modern Art in New York, and Andy Warhol made it a subject of his silkscreen work. It remains one of the best-selling perfumes in the world and a symbol of luxury branding.
