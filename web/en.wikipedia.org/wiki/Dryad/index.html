Dryad

A dryad (Greek: Δρυάς, Dryás, from δρῦς drŷs, "oak") is a tree nymph or tree spirit in Greek mythology. Dryads were originally associated specifically with oak trees, but the term broadened to cover tree nymphs in general. They were depicted as beautiful young women, and were generally gentle, though they could punish mortals who harmed their trees.

Types

Greek tradition distinguished several kinds of tree nymphs. Hamadryads were bound to a single tree, with which they lived and died. Meliae were nymphs of ash trees, Daphnaeae of laurel, and Epimeliads protected fruit and apple trees as well as flocks. Unlike hamadryads, ordinary dryads were not inseparably tied to one tree.

Myths

Notable dryads include Eurydice, the wife of Orpheus, and Daphne, who was transformed into a laurel tree to escape Apollo. In the story of Erysichthon, a dryad's sacred oak was felled by the impious Thessalian king, who was cursed by Demeter with insatiable hunger and eventually devoured himself.

In later culture

Dryads became stock figures of pastoral poetry from antiquity through the Renaissance and beyond, and appear widely in modern fantasy literature, role-playing games, and video games, usually as guardians of forests.
