Hélène Cixous

Hélène Cixous (born 5 June 1937) is a French feminist writer, professor, philosopher, literary critic and playwright. Born in Oran, Algeria, to a Sephardic Jewish family, she came to France in 1955 and was educated at the Sorbonne, writing her doctoral thesis on James Joyce.

Career and thought

In 1968 Cixous helped found the experimental University of Paris VIII at Vincennes, where she established one of the first centres of women's studies in Europe. Her 1975 essay "The Laugh of the Medusa" (Le Rire de la Méduse) is a landmark of feminist theory, calling on women to write themselves into the text through what she termed écriture féminine ("feminine writing"). Together with Luce Irigaray and Julia Kristeva she is often grouped among the so-called "French feminists", though the three differ considerably.

Cixous has published dozens of books of fiction, essays and plays, including Dedans (1969), which won the Prix Médicis, works on Joyce, Clarice Lispector and Rembrandt, and a long collaboration with the Théâtre du Soleil and its director Ariane Mnouchkine, for whom she wrote plays including L'Histoire terrible mais inachevée de Norodom Sihanouk, roi du Cambodge and L'Indiade. She was a close friend of Jacques Derrida, who called her the greatest living writer in the French language.
