Extremophile

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An extremophile is an organism that thrives in conditions that are extreme for most life — very hot, cold, acidic, alkaline, salty, pressurised, dry or radioactive environments. Most extremophiles are microbes, especially Archaea, though some animals (tardigrades, Pompeii worms) tolerate remarkable extremes. Their study extends the known limits of life and informs the search for life on other worlds.

Types

Thermophiles and hyperthermophiles grow in hot springs and deep-sea vents, some above 100 °C; psychrophiles live in polar ice and deep oceans; acidophiles flourish below pH 3 (some near pH 0); halophiles colour salt lakes pink; piezophiles withstand the pressure of ocean trenches; and radioresistant Deinococcus radiodurans survives radiation doses thousands of times the human lethal dose.

Importance

Enzymes from extremophiles (extremozymes) work in industrial processes and laundry detergents, and Taq polymerase from the hot-spring bacterium Thermus aquaticus makes PCR possible. Extremophiles show that life can persist in subsurface rock, Antarctic valleys and possibly Martian or Europan environments, guiding astrobiology.
