Codex Vaticanus (designated by siglum B or 03 in the Gregory–Aland numbering) is a 4th-century Christian manuscript of a Greek Bible, containing the majority of the Greek Old Testament and most of the Greek New Testament. It is one of the four great uncial codices and, along with Codex Sinaiticus, one of the oldest and most important witnesses to the text of the Bible.

Description

The codex originally contained around 820 leaves of fine vellum, of which 759 survive. The text is written in uncial script in three columns per page, 40–44 lines per column. The manuscript ends abruptly at Hebrews 9:14; the concluding books, the Pastoral Epistles, Philemon and Revelation, are missing and were supplied in a 15th-century supplement.

History and importance

It has been housed in the Vatican Library since at least 1475. In the 19th century, scholars such as Constantin von Tischendorf studied it closely, and it became a foundation of modern textual criticism of the New Testament, representing the Alexandrian text-type.
