Dependent origination

Dependent origination
Palipaṭiccasamuppāda
Sanskritpratītyasamutpāda
TraditionBuddhism
Core formula"When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises."
[Image: The Bhavacakra (wheel of life) depicting the twelve nidānas of dependent origination]

Dependent origination or dependent arising is a key doctrine in Buddhism shared by all schools. It states that all dharmas (phenomena) arise in dependence upon other dharmas: "if this exists, that exists; if this ceases to exist, that also will cease to exist". The basic principle is that all things (dharmas, phenomena, principles) arise in dependence upon other things.

The twelve nidānas

The classic formulation lists twelve linked causes (nidānas) that sustain the cycle of rebirth (saṃsāra):

  1. Ignorance (avidyā)
  2. Volitional formations (saṃskāra)
  3. Consciousness (vijñāna)
  4. Name-and-form (nāmarūpa)
  5. Six sense bases (ṣaḍāyatana)
  6. Contact (sparśa)
  7. Feeling (vedanā)
  8. Craving (tṛṣṇā)
  9. Clinging (upādāna)
  10. Becoming (bhava)
  11. Birth (jāti)
  12. Ageing and death (jarāmaraṇa)

Interpretation

The doctrine underpins the Buddhist teaching of emptiness (śūnyatā): because phenomena arise dependently, they lack independent, inherent existence (svabhāva). In the Mahāyāna tradition, Nāgārjuna's Mūlamadhyamakakārikā identifies dependent origination with emptiness itself. Understanding dependent origination is said to break the chain of ignorance and craving that perpetuates suffering.
