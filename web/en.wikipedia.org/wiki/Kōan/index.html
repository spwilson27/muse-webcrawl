Kōan

Kōan
Chinese公案 (gōng'àn)
Japaneseこうあん
TraditionChan / Zen Buddhism (esp. Rinzai)
PurposeMeditative inquiry to provoke insight

A kōan (Japanese: 公案; Chinese: 公案, gōng'àn, literally "public case") is a story, dialogue, question or statement used in Zen Buddhist practice — especially in the Rinzai school — as an object of meditation. Kōans are typically paradoxical anecdotes about encounters between teachers and students, designed to exhaust conceptual thinking and provoke direct insight (kenshō).

Famous examples

Well-known kōans include "What is the sound of one hand clapping?" (associated with Hakuin), Zhaozhou's "Mu" (the monk who asked whether a dog has Buddha-nature, answered "Mu" — "no/not"), and "What was your original face before your parents were born?"

Collections and practice

Classic collections compiled in Song-dynasty China include the Blue Cliff Record (1125), the Gateless Barrier (Mumonkan, 1228) and the Book of Equanimity. In formal practice, a student works with an assigned kōan during zazen and presents their understanding to the teacher in private interview (dokusan/sanzen).
