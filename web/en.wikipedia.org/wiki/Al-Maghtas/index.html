Al-Maghtas (Arabic: المغطس, "the immersion" or "baptism site"), officially the Baptism Site "Bethany Beyond the Jordan", is an archaeological and pilgrimage site on the east bank of the Jordan River in Jordan, about 9 kilometres north of the Dead Sea. It is widely identified with the biblical "Bethany Beyond the Jordan" where John the Baptist lived and where Jesus was baptised. In 2015 the site was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Identification and archaeology

The Gospel of John places John's baptising at "Bethany beyond the Jordan" (John 1:28) and at Aenon near Salim. After the 1994 Israel–Jordan peace treaty made the mined border area accessible, excavations from 1996 onwards uncovered a complex of Byzantine and early Islamic remains on and around Jabal Mar-Elias (Elijah's Hill): churches, chapels, baptism pools, water systems, caves of monks and hermitages. A large Byzantine church complex at the river's edge at Wadi al-Kharrar, with marble steps descending to a baptismal pool fed by spring water, matched early pilgrim accounts describing the baptism site at this spot.

The remains — more than twenty churches, monasteries and caves in total — testify to continuous veneration from the Roman and Byzantine periods. The nearby Tell al-Kharrar is also traditionally associated with the ascension of the prophet Elijah.

Modern site

Al-Maghtas forms part of Jordan's Baptism Site Commission area, with a visitor centre, shelters and a modern Greek Orthodox church of St John the Baptist near the river. Successive popes — John Paul II in 2000, Benedict XVI in 2009 and Francis in 2014 — have visited or prayed at the site. The Catholic Church in Jordan celebrates an annual pilgrimage here in January. Access to the river at the baptism pools faces the Israeli-administered site of Qasr el Yahud across the water; the two are sometimes distinguished in debates over the precise historical location, though the eastern site is accepted by most churches and by UNESCO as Bethany Beyond the Jordan.
