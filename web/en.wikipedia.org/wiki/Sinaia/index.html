Sinaia is a town and mountain resort in Prahova County, Romania, situated in the Prahova Valley at the foothills of the Bucegi Mountains. Named after Sinaia Monastery, itself named for Mount Sinai, the town is famous for Peleș Castle, the former summer residence of the Romanian royal family, and for its ski slopes and hiking trails.

History

The town grew around Sinaia Monastery, founded in 1695 by Prince Mihai Cantacuzino. In the 19th century King Carol I built Peleș Castle (1873-1914), one of Europe's most beautiful castles, and the town became a fashionable resort for Romania's elite.

Landmarks

Peleș Castle and its smaller companion Pelișor Castle are major tourist attractions. Sinaia Monastery, the Bucegi Natural Park, and the Cota 1400 and Cota 2000 ski areas draw visitors year-round. The town is served by the railway between Bucharest and Brașov.

Geography

Sinaia lies at an elevation of about 800 metres, surrounded by beech and fir forests. The climate is alpine, with snowy winters making it one of Romania's principal winter sports destinations.
