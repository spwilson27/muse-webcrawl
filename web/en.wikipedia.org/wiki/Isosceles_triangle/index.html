Isosceles triangle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An isosceles triangle with its two equal sides marked

In geometry, an isosceles triangle is a triangle that has two sides of equal length. The two equal sides are called the legs and the third side is the base; the angle between the legs is the vertex angle and the angles adjacent to the base are the base angles.

Properties

By the base angles theorem, the base angles of an isosceles triangle are congruent (Euclid, Elements I.5, the pons asinorum), and conversely, a triangle with two congruent angles is isosceles. The altitude from the vertex angle bisects both the base and the vertex angle, and coincides with the perpendicular bisector of the base and the median to the base. Equilateral triangles (all three sides equal) are a special case of isosceles triangles under the inclusive definition.

Formulas

For an isosceles triangle with equal sides a and base b, the altitude is h = √(a² − b²/4) and the area is (b/4)√(4a² − b²).
