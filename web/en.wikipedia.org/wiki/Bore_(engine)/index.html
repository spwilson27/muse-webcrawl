Bore (engine)

Bore
Definition: Diameter of an engine cylinder
Related quantity: Stroke (piston travel)
Displacement: proportional to bore² × stroke × cylinders
Configurations: Oversquare, square, undersquare (long-stroke)

In a piston engine, the bore is the diameter of each cylinder. Together with the stroke — the distance the piston travels within the cylinder — and the number of cylinders, the bore determines the engine's displacement: displacement = (π/4) × bore² × stroke × number of cylinders.

Bore/stroke ratio

The ratio of bore to stroke characterizes an engine's design. Engines with a bore larger than the stroke are called oversquare or short-stroke; the larger bore allows bigger valves and higher engine speeds, favouring power, and such designs are common in high-performance and racing engines. Engines with a stroke longer than the bore are undersquare or long-stroke, tending to produce more torque at lower speeds and greater efficiency, typical of diesel and economy engines. A bore equal to the stroke is termed square. Increasing the bore of an existing engine ("boring out") is a common way of enlarging displacement during manufacture or rebuilding.

Steam and other engines

The term applies equally to steam engines and other reciprocating machinery, where cylinder bore and stroke define the swept volume and hence the engine's power potential for a given steam pressure and speed.
