The euro short-term rate (€STR) is a reference interest rate for the euro, administered and published by the European Central Bank (ECB). It reflects the wholesale euro unsecured overnight borrowing costs of banks located in the euro area, and it serves as the euro area's principal risk-free rate, succeeding EONIA.

Calculation

The €STR is computed from transaction-level data reported by the largest euro area banks under the ECB's Money Market Statistical Reporting (MMSR). For each day, the ECB takes the volume-weighted mean of overnight deposit rates, trimmed by removing the top and bottom 25 per cent of volumes, and publishes the rate at 08:00 CET on the following business day. Because it is based on actual transactions, it complies with the EU Benchmarks Regulation and the IOSCO principles for financial benchmarks.

History and transition

The ECB announced the new rate in 2017–2018 as part of the global reform of interest-rate benchmarks prompted by the LIBOR scandal. The €STR was first published on 2 October 2019. Its predecessor EONIA was recalibrated as €STR plus a fixed spread of 8.5 basis points and then discontinued on 3 January 2022. Market participants also use €STR to build fallback rates for EURIBOR-linked contracts, and an €STR-based overnight index swap (OIS) market has developed.
