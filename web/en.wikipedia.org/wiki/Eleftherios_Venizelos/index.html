Eleftherios Kyriakou Venizelos (23 August 1864 – 18 March 1936) was a Greek statesman and prominent leader of the Greek national liberation movement. He is noted for his contribution to the expansion of Greek territory and the modernisation of the Greek state, and served several terms as Prime Minister of Greece between 1910 and 1933. He is often described as the maker of modern Greece.

Early life and Crete

Venizelos was born in Ottoman-ruled Crete and trained as a lawyer. He became a leading figure in the Cretan struggle for union (enosis) with Greece, playing a central role in the Theriso revolt of 1905 and in the island's eventual incorporation into Greece in 1913.

Prime Minister and the Balkan Wars

Invited to Athens after the Goudi coup of 1909, Venizelos founded the Liberal Party, won the 1910 elections and embarked on constitutional, military and economic reforms. His diplomacy created the Balkan League, whose victories in the Balkan Wars of 1912–13 nearly doubled Greece's territory and population.

National Schism and First World War

Venizelos's insistence that Greece join the Entente in the First World War brought him into conflict with King Constantine I, producing the National Schism: Venizelos formed a rival government in Thessaloniki in 1916, and Greece eventually entered the war on the Allied side. At the Paris Peace Conference, Venizelos secured extensive territorial gains, codified in the Treaty of Sèvres (1920), but the subsequent Greco-Turkish War ended in the Asia Minor Catastrophe of 1922.

Later career and legacy

Venizelos negotiated the Treaty of Lausanne (1923) and, in his final premiership (1928–1932), pursued reconciliation with Turkey through the 1930 friendship treaty with Mustafa Kemal Atatürk. Exiled after a failed coup attempt in 1935, he died in Paris in 1936. Athens International Airport is named in his honour, and he remains one of the most celebrated figures in modern Greek history.
