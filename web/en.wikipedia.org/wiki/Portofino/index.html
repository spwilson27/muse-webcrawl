Portofino

Portofino is an Italian fishing village and holiday resort on the Italian Riviera, in the Metropolitan City of Genoa, Liguria. It is famous for its picturesque harbour lined with pastel-coloured houses and is a popular destination for yachting and celebrity tourism.

History

According to Pliny the Elder, the settlement was founded by the Romans and named Portus Delphini after the dolphins in the surrounding waters. In the Middle Ages it belonged successively to the Abbey of San Fruttuoso and the Republic of Genoa. From the late 19th century, British and European aristocrats began holidaying there, and in the 1950s it became a haunt of film stars and the international jet set.

Sights

Landmarks include the church of St. Martin (Divo Martino), the Castello Brown (a 16th-century fortress above the harbour, now a museum and events venue), the church of St. George said to hold relics of St. George, and a lighthouse at the end of the peninsula. The surrounding Portofino Regional Park and the Portofino Marine Protected Area protect the headland's Mediterranean scrub and marine life, and the abbey of San Fruttuoso is reachable on foot or by boat nearby.
