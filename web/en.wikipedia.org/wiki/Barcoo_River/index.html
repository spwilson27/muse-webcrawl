The Barcoo River is a river in western Queensland, Australia. Rising in the Warrego Range north of Tambo, it flows generally south-west through the towns of Blackall, Isisford and Jundah before joining the Thomson River near Windorah to form Cooper Creek, one of Australia's great inland river systems. The Barcoo forms part of the Lake Eyre Basin, one of the largest internal drainage systems in the world.

The Barcoo in the Channel Country of western Queensland.

Course and floods

Like other rivers of the Channel Country, the Barcoo is highly ephemeral: it may be a chain of waterholes for years and then, after monsoon rain far to the north, become a braided flood many kilometres wide. Floodwaters eventually reach Lake Eyre in South Australia in exceptional years. Towns on the river, such as Blackall — famous as the home of the Black Stump and of shearer Jack Howe — are built with the river's floods in mind.

History and culture

The river was named by explorer Edmund Kennedy in 1847 from an Aboriginal word reported to mean "ice" or "running water". The Barcoo gave its name to "Barcoo rot" (a scurvy-like condition of outback travellers) and to "Barcoo salute", the Australian bush gesture of brushing away flies. The Barcoo shire and the broader Barcoo district remain synonymous with the remote Queensland outback and its cattle stations.
