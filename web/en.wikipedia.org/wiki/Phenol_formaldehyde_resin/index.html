Phenol formaldehyde resin

Phenol formaldehyde resins (PF) are synthetic polymers obtained by the reaction of phenol or substituted phenols with formaldehyde. First commercialized as Bakelite by Leo Baekeland in 1907, they were the first plastics made entirely from synthetic components.

TypeThermosetting polymer
MonomersPhenol, formaldehyde
Trade nameBakelite

Types

Two main classes exist: novolacs, made with excess phenol under acidic conditions, which require a curing agent; and resoles, made with excess formaldehyde under alkaline conditions, which cure on heating alone.

Properties and uses

Cured PF resins are hard, heat-resistant, and electrically insulating. They are used in molded products, laminates such as Formica, adhesives for plywood, brake linings, and as binders in foundry sands and abrasives.
