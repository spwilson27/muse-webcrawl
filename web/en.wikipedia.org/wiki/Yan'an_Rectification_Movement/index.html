The Yan'an Rectification Movement (Chinese: 延安整風運動) was the first ideological mass movement initiated by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), conducted from 1942 to 1945 at the party's base in Yan'an. Led by Mao Zedong, it consolidated Mao's supremacy within the party, established "Mao Zedong Thought" as guiding doctrine, and set enduring patterns for later CCP campaigns.

Background

After the Long March, the CCP regrouped at Yan'an, where it absorbed tens of thousands of idealistic urban recruits. Mao sought to discipline both these newcomers and rival party factions — notably the Soviet-trained "Returned Students" associated with Wang Ming — and to sinify Marxism by adapting it to Chinese conditions.

The campaign

Beginning in February 1942, cadres studied a canon of twenty-two documents, including Mao's speeches "Rectify the Party's Style of Work" and "Oppose Stereotyped Party Writing," which attacked subjectivism, sectarianism and formalism. Participants wrote self-criticisms and underwent group struggle sessions. In its later "cadre examination" and "rescue" phases, administered under security chief Kang Sheng, the movement turned coercive: large numbers of cadres were accused of being spies, and many were persecuted, with some driven to suicide, before Mao halted the excesses and partial apologies were issued.

Legacy

The movement unified the party ideologically behind Mao on the eve of victory in the civil war, and its methods — study sessions, self-criticism, and struggle meetings — became templates for subsequent campaigns, including the Cultural Revolution.
