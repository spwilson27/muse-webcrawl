Normal series

In group theory, a normal series (also called a normal tower or subnormal series) of a group G is a chain of subgroups

1 = A₀ ⊲ A₁ ⊲ … ⊲ Aₙ = G

in which each subgroup Ai is normal in the next subgroup Ai+1. The quotient groups Ai+1/Ai are called the factors of the series.

Related notions

A composition series is a normal series that cannot be refined further — equivalently, one whose factors are all simple groups. The Jordan–Hölder theorem states that any two composition series of a finite group have the same length and the same factors up to order and isomorphism. A central series is a normal series whose factors lie in the centre of the corresponding quotients of G; groups possessing a central series reaching the trivial group are nilpotent, and those possessing a normal series with abelian factors are solvable.

Applications

Normal series are the main tool for breaking groups into simpler pieces: the derived series and lower central series define solvable and nilpotent groups, and the unsolvability of the general quintic follows from the fact that the alternating group A₅ is simple and non-abelian.
