HomePlug

HomePlug is the family of power-line communication (PLC) specifications developed by the HomePlug Powerline Alliance for networking devices over household electrical wiring. It allows data to be transmitted over existing AC power lines, so devices can be networked without new cabling.

Versions

The original HomePlug 1.0 specification (2001) offered speeds up to 14 Mbit/s. HomePlug AV (2005) raised theoretical throughput to 200 Mbit/s and targeted audio/video distribution; HomePlug AV2 (2012) extended this further using wider frequency bands, MIMO over the wiring and higher-order modulation, with gigabit-class physical-layer rates. Related specifications include HomePlug Green PHY, a low-power variant for smart-grid and Internet-of-things applications.

Use

HomePlug adapters are commonly sold in pairs that plug into wall sockets to extend Ethernet networks within homes. The alliance's certification programme promoted interoperability between vendors.
