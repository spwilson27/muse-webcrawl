Noise (signal processing)

In signal processing, noise is a general term for unwanted (and, in general, unknown) modifications that a signal may suffer during capture, storage, transmission, processing, or conversion. Sometimes the word is also used to mean signals that are random (unpredictable) and carry no useful information; even if they are not interfering with another signal.

Originally, the word "noise" referred only to audio signals; today it is used for the same phenomenon applied to any signal type, including images and video. The reduction of noise is covered in part under the heading of signal integrity.

Types of noise

Many different types of noise occur in signals:

  • Thermal noise (Johnson–Nyquist noise) — generated by the random thermal agitation of charge carriers in a conductor.
  • Shot noise — arising from the discrete nature of electric charge and photon arrival statistics.
  • Flicker noise (1/f noise, "pink noise") — noise whose power spectral density falls inversely with frequency.
  • Quantization noise — introduced when an analogue signal is converted to digital values with finite precision.
  • Gaussian noise — random noise whose values follow a normal distribution, common in thermal processes.
  • Impulse noise ("salt-and-pepper" noise in images) — sparse, high-amplitude corruptions.

Signal-to-noise ratio

The effect of noise is commonly quantified by the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), the ratio of the power of the desired signal to the power of the noise, often expressed in decibels. A higher SNR means a cleaner signal. Methods that reduce noise include filtering, shielding, averaging repeated measurements, and error-correcting codes in digital transmission.

Noise in images and audio

In photography and imaging, noise appears as random variation in brightness or colour, worsened at high sensor sensitivity (ISO). In audio, it is heard as hiss, hum, or crackle. Dedicated techniques — such as noise reduction algorithms — attempt to remove noise while preserving the underlying signal.
