Siltstone

Siltstone
TypeSedimentary rock
CompositionSilt-sized particles (1/256–1/16 mm), mainly quartz and feldspar
TextureClastic

Siltstone is a clastic sedimentary rock composed mainly of silt-sized particles. It is finer-grained than sandstone and coarser-grained than mudstone and shale. Silt particles range from 1/256 to 1/16 millimetre in diameter, corresponding to grades 4 to 8 on the phi (φ) scale.

Formation

Silt accumulates in relatively quiet depositional environments where currents are strong enough to carry silt but too weak to move sand — including floodplains, deltas, lake beds, and the deeper parts of marine shelves. When buried and compacted, the silt is lithified into siltstone, chiefly through compaction and cementation by silica or carbonate minerals.

Identification

Siltstone feels gritty when rubbed between the fingers or chewed (a traditional field test distinguishes its grit from the smooth feel of claystone), and it lacks the fissility of shale. It is commonly grey, brown, or reddish in colour and often shows fine lamination.
