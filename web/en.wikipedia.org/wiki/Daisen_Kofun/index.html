Daisen Kofun

The Daisen Kofun (大仙陵古墳), officially the mausoleum of Emperor Nintoku (Nintoku-tennō-ryō), is a keyhole-shaped burial mound (kofun) in Sakai, Osaka Prefecture, Japan. It is the largest tomb in Japan and one of the largest tombs in the world by area, traditionally attributed to the semi-legendary Emperor Nintoku, said to have reigned in the early 5th century.

Description

The mound is about 486 metres long and up to 36 metres high, surrounded by three concentric moats, and forms part of the Mozu cluster of tumuli. Seen from above, it has the distinctive keyhole shape — a square front and circular rear — characteristic of elite kofun. Construction is estimated to have taken many years and a workforce of thousands. The interior has never been scientifically excavated, as the Imperial Household Agency administers the site as an imperial mausoleum and restricts access; limited finds from the outer areas include haniwa figures, armour, and weapons.

Heritage status

In 2019 the Mozu-Furuichi Kofun Group, including the Daisen Kofun, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
