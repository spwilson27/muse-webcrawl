Gran Paradiso National Park

Gran Paradiso National Park (Italian: Parco nazionale del Gran Paradiso; French: Parc national du Grand Paradis) is Italy's oldest national park, established in 1922 from the former royal hunting reserve of King Victor Emmanuel II in the Graian Alps. Covering about 710 km² in the Aosta Valley and Piedmont, it is named for the Gran Paradiso massif (4,061 m), the only mountain entirely within Italy to exceed 4,000 metres. The park was created above all to protect the Alpine ibex, and its surviving herd became the source for reintroducing the species across the Alps.

History

In 1856 Victor Emmanuel II declared a royal reserve to save the ibex, then reduced to a few hundred animals in the area; his game guards and hunting lodges (such as the Orvieille) laid early foundations for protection. In 1919 the king donated the reserve to the state for a park, which parliament created in 1922. Wardens — many of them former royal guards — fought poaching through the war years, and from Gran Paradiso ibex were translocated to seed today's Alpine populations.

Landscape and wildlife

The park spans five main valleys around the massif, with glaciers, alpine meadows, larch and spruce forests and high lakes. Besides several thousand ibex it supports chamois, marmots, alpine hares, golden eagles and the reintroduced bearded vulture. Together with France's adjoining Vanoise National Park it forms one of Europe's largest protected areas.
