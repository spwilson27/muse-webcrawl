National League Championship Series

Sport: Baseball
League: Major League Baseball
First played: 1969
Format: Best of seven (since 1985)
Trophy: Warren C. Giles Trophy

The National League Championship Series (NLCS) is the semifinal round of the Major League Baseball postseason, determining the National League pennant winner, who advances to the World Series against the American League champion.

History and format

The NLCS began in 1969 when the National League split into East and West divisions. Initially a best-of-five series, it expanded to best-of-seven in 1985. Since the 1994 realignment and the introduction of the Wild Card and Division Series rounds, the NLCS matches the winners of the two National League Division Series.

Notable series

Memorable NLCS matchups include the 1986 series between the Mets and Astros, the 2004 Cardinals–Astros series, and the Cubs' 2016 victory that ended their 71-year pennant drought. The winner receives the Warren C. Giles Trophy, named for a former National League president, and an NLCS Most Valuable Player award is given each year.
