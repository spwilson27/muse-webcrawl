Lytham St Annes is a seaside town in the Borough of Fylde in Lancashire, England, formed in 1922 by the amalgamation of the two neighbouring resorts of Lytham and St Annes-on-Sea. It lies on the Fylde coast just south of Blackpool, at the mouth of the Ribble estuary.

History

Lytham is an old settlement recorded in the Domesday Book, long associated with the Clifton family of Lytham Hall. St Annes was a purpose-built Victorian resort founded in 1875. The twin towns developed a reputation as a genteel, leafy counterpoint to livelier Blackpool, with wide avenues, gardens and a promenade. Landmarks include the white Lytham Windmill (1805) on the Green, St Annes Pier and the statue of comedian Les Dawson, a long-time resident.

Golf

The town is internationally known for golf: the Royal Lytham & St Annes Golf Club, founded in 1886, is one of the courses on the rota for The Open Championship, and the town and neighbouring links host several championship courses, earning the area the nickname "England's Golf Coast."
