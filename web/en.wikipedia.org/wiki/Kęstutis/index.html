Kęstutis (c. 1297 – 15 August 1382) was a ruler of medieval Lithuania, Duke of Trakai, and, with his brother Algirdas, co-ruler of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania. One of the sons of Grand Duke Gediminas, Kęstutis governed the western lands of the duchy — Samogitia and the Trakai region — and spent his life defending them against the Teutonic Order, while Algirdas ruled in the east from Vilnius.

Career

The brothers' division of labour was remarkably stable: Algirdas expanded Lithuanian power into the Rus' principalities, while Kęstutis waged near-constant war with the Teutonic and Livonian Orders along the Nemunas frontier. German chronicles, though hostile, praised Kęstutis as a chivalrous and honourable enemy. In 1361 he was captured by the Order after the siege of Kaunas and held at Marienburg before escaping. Tradition credits his marriage to Birutė, a pagan priestess (vaidilutė) from Palanga, as a famous romantic abduction story of Lithuanian folklore.

Civil war and death

After Algirdas's death in 1377, power passed to his son Jogaila, whose secret Treaty of Dovydiškės (1380) with the Teutonic Order against Kęstutis provoked civil war. In 1381 Kęstutis seized Vilnius and was proclaimed Grand Duke, but in 1382 Jogaila and his brother Skirgaila captured Kęstutis and his son Vytautas during negotiations and imprisoned them in Kreva Castle, where Kęstutis died — probably murdered — days later. Vytautas escaped, and the struggle ultimately led to his recognition as Grand Duke under Jogaila's overlordship.
