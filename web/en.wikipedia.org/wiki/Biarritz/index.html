Biarritz (Basque: Miarritze) is a city on the Bay of Biscay, on the Atlantic coast in the Pyrénées-Atlantiques department in the French Basque Country in southwestern France. It is located 35 kilometres (22 mi) from the border with Spain. A luxurious seaside tourist destination since the 19th century, it is also known for its surfing culture.

History

Originally a whaling settlement, Biarritz was transformed after Empress Eugénie, wife of Napoleon III, built a palace on the beach in 1854 (now the Hôtel du Palais). European royalty, including Queen Victoria and Edward VII, made it a fashionable resort. The casino opened in 1901.

Surfing

Biarritz is considered the birthplace of surfing in Europe: in 1957 the American screenwriter Peter Viertel, in town for the filming of The Sun Also Rises, surfed on a board brought from California. The city hosts international surfing competitions, and nearby beaches such as the Côte des Basques are major surf spots.

Sights

Landmarks include the Rocher de la Vierge (Rock of the Virgin), a rocky outcrop topped by a statue of the Virgin Mary, erected in 1865 and reached by a footbridge; the lighthouse (Phare de Biarritz), built in 1834; the Orthodox church built for the Russian aristocracy; and the Museum of the Sea (aquarium). The city is also home to the professional rugby union club Biarritz Olympique.
