1894 Australian shearers' strike

The 1894 Australian shearers' strike was a major industrial dispute in colonial Australia, the second great shearers' strike after the 1891 strike. It arose when pastoralists, facing falling wool prices during an economic depression, sought to cut shearers' wages and impose a new shearing agreement.

Course of the strike

Shearers organised under the Amalgamated Shearers' Union refused the reduced rates, and strike camps formed across Queensland and New South Wales. As in 1891, colonial governments deployed police and military forces to protect non-union labour. The burning of the river steamer Rodney on the Darling River, which had been carrying non-union shearers, became the most notorious incident of the dispute.

Outcome

The strike eventually collapsed and pastoralists largely imposed their terms, but the defeats of 1891 and 1894 strengthened the labour movement's turn toward parliamentary politics through the emerging Labor parties, shaping Australian politics for decades.
