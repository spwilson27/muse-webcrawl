Leonard Nimoy

Leonard Simon Nimoy (March 26, 1931 – February 27, 2015) was an American actor, filmmaker, photographer, author, singer, and songwriter, best known internationally for playing Spock in the Star Trek franchise — a role he originated in the original television series (1966–1969) and reprised across films, animation, and the reboot series for nearly fifty years.

Star Trek

Cast by Gene Roddenberry as the half-Vulcan, half-human science officer of the USS Enterprise, Nimoy created many of the character's signatures, including the Vulcan neck pinch and the split-fingered Vulcan salute, which he adapted from a Jewish priestly blessing he had seen as a child; the accompanying phrase "Live long and prosper" entered global popular culture. He earned three Emmy nominations for the role. Nimoy directed two of the most successful films in the series, Star Trek III: The Search for Spock (1984) and Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home (1986), the latter co-written by him.

Other work

Beyond Star Trek, Nimoy starred in Mission: Impossible, hosted the documentary series In Search of..., and directed the hit comedy Three Men and a Baby (1987). He published two autobiographies, I Am Not Spock (1975) and I Am Spock (1995), volumes of poetry, and fine-art photography exploring identity and spirituality. He died in 2015 of complications from chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; his final public message, days before his death, read: "A life is like a garden. Perfect moments can be had, but not preserved, except in memory. LLAP."
