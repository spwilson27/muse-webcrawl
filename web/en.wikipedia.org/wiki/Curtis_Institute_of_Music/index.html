Curtis Institute of Music

Placeholder image: Curtis Institute of Music

The Curtis Institute of Music is a private conservatory in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Founded in 1924 by Mary Louise Curtis Bok, it is renowned for its policy of offering full-tuition scholarships to all admitted students, making admission exceptionally competitive. About 175 students are enrolled at any time, training in instrumental performance, composition, conducting, vocal studies, and opera.

History

Mary Louise Curtis Bok, heiress to the Curtis Publishing Company, founded the institute in 1924 and named it for her father, Cyrus Curtis. From 1928 onward, all students attended on full scholarship, a policy sustained by the founding endowment. The school is housed primarily in a former mansion on Rittenhouse Square, with the addition of Lenfest Hall in 2011.

Academics and admissions

Admission is by live audition and is based solely on artistic merit; acceptance rates are around 4–5 percent, among the lowest of any American school. The small student body allows a low student-to-faculty ratio and an emphasis on performance, including a student recital series and the Curtis Symphony Orchestra. Degrees offered include the Bachelor of Music, Master of Music in opera, and artist diplomas.

Notable faculty and alumni

Faculty have included pianist Rudolf Serkin, who also served as director, and violinist Ivan Galamian. Alumni include Lang Lang, Hilary Hahn, Yuja Wang, Leonard Bernstein, Gary Graffman, Peter Serkin, and members of many leading orchestras; a large share of principal players in top American orchestras are Curtis graduates.
