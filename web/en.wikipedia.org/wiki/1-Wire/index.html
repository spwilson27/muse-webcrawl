1-Wire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

1-Wire is a wired half-duplex serial communication bus system designed by Dallas Semiconductor (now part of Analog Devices) that provides low-speed data, signalling, and power over a single conductor, plus a ground return.

Overview

Each 1-Wire device carries a unique, factory-lasered 64-bit registration number — an 8-bit family code, a 48-bit serial number, and an 8-bit CRC — which serves as both its address on the network and, for identification devices, an electronic serial number. A bus master controls communication with precise time slots: data are transferred in pulses on the single wire, and devices can draw "parasite power" from the line itself, storing energy in an internal capacitor while the line is high.

Applications

1-Wire chips provide temperature sensing (the popular DS18B20 digital thermometer), real-time clocks, EEPROM and flash memory, battery monitoring, and secure authentication. The iButton — a 1-Wire chip sealed in a 16 mm stainless-steel coin-shaped can — is used for access control, transit fare keys, temperature logging, and asset tracking worldwide. Communication normally proceeds at about 16.3 kbit/s, with an overdrive mode around 142 kbit/s.
