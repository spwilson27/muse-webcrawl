Consortium

A consortium (from the Latin consortium, a partnership) is an association of two or more individuals, companies, organizations or governments (or any combination of these) that join together with the objective of participating in a common activity or pooling their resources for achieving a common goal. A consortium is generally less formal than a joint venture and does not usually involve the creation of a separate legal entity, although members may choose to incorporate one.

Characteristics

Consortia are common where a project is too large, complex or risky for a single participant — for example, in large construction and infrastructure projects, defence procurement, airline alliances, research programmes and technology standards development. Members typically remain independent outside the scope of the consortium agreement, and each member's liability is limited to the obligations set out in that agreement.

Examples

Well-known consortia include Airbus, formed by European aerospace manufacturers; the SEMATECH consortium of semiconductor companies; and numerous university and library consortia that pool purchasing power for journals and databases. In finance, a consortium of banks may jointly underwrite or lend on a large transaction, a practice known as syndication.
