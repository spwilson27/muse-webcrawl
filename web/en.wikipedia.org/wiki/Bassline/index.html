Bassline

A walking bassline in notation

A bassline (also spelled bass line) is the term used in many styles of popular music for the low-pitched instrumental part or line played by a rhythm section instrument such as the electric bass, double bass, cello, tuba, or keyboard (synthesizer, piano, or organ). The bassline supplies the harmonic foundation of the music while helping to drive the rhythm.

Function

In most styles, the bass player outlines the chord progression by emphasizing chord roots and fifths on strong beats, connecting them with passing tones, arpeggios, and scale fragments. A steady, rhythmically clear bassline locks with the drums to form the "groove" over which other instruments and vocals perform.

Styles

In jazz, the walking bass line moves in quarter notes through the chord changes. In Baroque music, the basso continuo provided a continuous bass foundation. Funk and R&B basslines often feature syncopation, octave jumps, and slap techniques, while in electronic dance music the bassline — frequently produced by synthesizers or the Roland TB-303 in acid house — can be the dominant melodic and rhythmic element of a track. Reggae and dub give the bassline unusual prominence, with simple, heavy lines carrying the song's identity.

Notable examples

Some basslines have become as recognizable as the songs' melodies, including the lines in "Come Together" by the Beatles, "Billie Jean" by Michael Jackson, and "Another One Bites the Dust" by Queen.
