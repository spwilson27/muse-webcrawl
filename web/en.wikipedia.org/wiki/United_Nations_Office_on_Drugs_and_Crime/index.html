The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) is a United Nations agency established in 1997 through the merger of the UN Drug Control Programme and the Centre for International Crime Prevention. It works to combat illicit drugs, transnational organized crime, corruption, and terrorism, and to strengthen criminal justice systems and the rule of law. Its headquarters are in Vienna, and its executive director also serves as director-general of the UN Office at Vienna.

Mandate

UNODC serves as the secretariat and guardian of key treaties: the three international drug control conventions, the UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime (the Palermo Convention, 2000) and its protocols on human trafficking, migrant smuggling, and firearms, and the UN Convention against Corruption (2003). It provides technical assistance—legal drafting, training of police, prosecutors, and judges, border management, prison reform, and alternative development for farmers growing illicit crops—and conducts research, including the annual World Drug Report and global studies on homicide and trafficking in persons.

Operations

The office is funded overwhelmingly by voluntary contributions, most of them earmarked for specific projects, a structure that has been both praised for flexibility and criticized for donor influence on priorities. Notable field work includes monitoring opium poppy cultivation in Afghanistan and Myanmar, maritime crime programmes combating piracy and smuggling in the Indian Ocean and the Gulf of Guinea, and container control programmes run jointly with the World Customs Organization.
