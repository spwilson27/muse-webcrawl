Rothamsted Research

Founded: 1843

Headquarters: Harpenden, Hertfordshire, England

Type: Agricultural research institute

Rothamsted Research, at Harpenden in Hertfordshire, England, is one of the oldest agricultural research institutions in the world. Founded in 1843 by John Bennet Lawes on his estate at Rothamsted, it is famous for its long-term field experiments, several of which have run continuously for more than 170 years, and for foundational contributions to agricultural chemistry and statistics.

History

Lawes, who had patented a process for making superphosphate fertiliser, established the Rothamsted Experimental Station in 1843 and hired the chemist Joseph Henry Gilbert; their partnership lasted 57 years. They began systematic field trials comparing fertilisers and crop rotations. The station was endowed by the Lawes Agricultural Trust in 1889. In the twentieth century the statistician Ronald Fisher worked at Rothamsted (1919–1933) and there developed the analysis of variance and the principles of experimental design, transforming both statistics and agricultural science. The insecticides pioneer Michael Elliott later synthesised the pyrethroid insecticides at Rothamsted.

Long-term experiments

The classical experiments still running include Broadbalk (winter wheat since 1843), Hoosfield (barley since 1852), and the Park Grass Experiment (since 1856), the oldest ecological experiment in the world, whose plots show how fertilisers and liming reshape grassland plant communities. Archived samples of grain, straw, and soil from every harvest — one of the world's great scientific archives — allow modern researchers to track pollution, isotope signatures, and even DNA changes across a century and a half. Rothamsted also operates a site at North Wyke in Devon, focused on grassland and livestock systems.

Modern work

Today the institute receives strategic funding from the UK's Biotechnology and Biological Sciences Research Council and researches sustainable crop production, soil health, pest and disease resistance, and reducing agriculture's environmental footprint. Its field release of genetically modified wheat trials in the 2010s drew public protest and debate.
