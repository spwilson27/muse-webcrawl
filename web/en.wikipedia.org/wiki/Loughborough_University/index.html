Loughborough University is a public research university in the market town of Loughborough, Leicestershire, England. It traces its origins to a technical institute founded in 1909, grew through Loughborough College of Technology, and received its royal charter in 1966, becoming the first technological university in the United Kingdom.

Campus and academics

The university occupies one of the largest single-site campuses in the UK, at 523 acres, and also operates a London campus on the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park. It is organised into schools covering engineering, science, design, business and economics, and social sciences and humanities, and is consistently ranked among the UK's top ten universities in national league tables.

Sport

Loughborough is renowned as the UK's leading university for sport: its teams have dominated the British Universities and Colleges Sport championships for decades, its Sports Technology Institute works with manufacturers and governing bodies, and many Olympic and Paralympic athletes train on campus with national institutes based there. Alumni include Sebastian Coe, Paula Radcliffe and Adam Peaty.
