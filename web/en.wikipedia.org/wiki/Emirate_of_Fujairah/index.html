The Emirate of Fujairah (Arabic: إمارة الفجيرة, romanized: Imārat al-Fujayrah) is one of the seven emirates that make up the United Arab Emirates. It is the only emirate situated entirely on the UAE's eastern coastline, on the Gulf of Oman, rather than on the Persian Gulf. Its capital and largest city is Fujairah.

Geography

Fujairah covers an area of about 1,166 square kilometres. Much of the emirate is mountainous, dominated by the Hajar Mountains, which separate it from the rest of the country and give it a higher rainfall than most of the UAE. The emirate's port, the Port of Fujairah, is a major bunkering and oil-storage hub, benefiting from its position outside the Strait of Hormuz.

History

The area was historically part of the Sharqiyin tribal territory. Fujairah became a separate emirate recognised by the British in 1952, the last of the Trucial States to be recognised, and it joined the United Arab Emirates at its formation in December 1971. Sheikh Hamad bin Mohammed Al Sharqi has ruled since 1974.

Economy

The economy rests on shipping, oil storage, free-zone trade, quarrying and tourism along the Gulf of Oman coast, including the beach resorts around Al Aqah and Dibba Al-Fujairah.
