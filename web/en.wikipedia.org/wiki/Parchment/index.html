Parchment is a writing material made from specially prepared untanned skins of animals — primarily calf, sheep and goat. Unlike leather, parchment is not tanned but limed, scraped and dried under tension, giving it a stiff, smooth, pale surface that takes ink well and can last for millennia. The finest parchment, made from calfskin (including stillborn or newly born calves), is called vellum. Parchment was the principal writing surface of Europe and the Near East from late antiquity until the spread of paper in the late Middle Ages, and it produced the surviving form of most classical literature.

History

Prepared skins were used for writing in Egypt and Mesopotamia from the second millennium BCE. Herodotus notes that Ionians called books "skins". Tradition credits Eumenes II of Pergamon (2nd century BCE) with perfecting parchment — whence its name, from pergamena — after an Egyptian embargo on papyrus. With the adoption of the codex in the 2nd–4th centuries CE, parchment displaced the papyrus roll: it could be folded, sewn, written on both sides, and erased and reused (palimpsests). Monastic scriptoria consumed vast quantities — a single great Bible such as Codex Amiatinus required the skins of some 500 calves.

Manufacture

The skin is soaked in water and then in a lime bath to loosen hair and flesh, dehaired and defleshed, then stretched taut on a frame (a herse) and scraped with a crescent-shaped knife (lunarium) while drying. The resulting sheet is whitened with chalk or pumice. Because it is dried under tension, parchment is hygroscopic and cockles with humidity; good storage is cool and stable.

Uses today

Parchment is still used for Torah scrolls, calligraphy, diplomas and certificates, luxury bookbinding, drumheads and banjo heads, and — notably — for the record copies of Acts of the UK Parliament, which were printed on vellum for durability (with interruptions and debates over cost, the practice has continued in modified form). Modern "vegetable parchment" and "parchment paper" for baking are cellulose products, not skin.
