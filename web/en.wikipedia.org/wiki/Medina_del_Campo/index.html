Medina del Campo

Medina del Campo
Province: Valladolid, Castile and León, Spain
Population: about 20,000

Medina del Campo is a town in the province of Valladolid, in the autonomous community of Castile and León, Spain. It stands on the Zapardiel River southwest of Valladolid.

History

In the 15th and 16th centuries Medina del Campo hosted some of Europe's most important trade fairs, where merchants from across the continent exchanged goods and credit, making the town a financial centre of the Spanish monarchy. Isabella I of Castile died in the town in 1504, and Saint Teresa of Ávila founded a convent there.

Landmarks

The Castillo de la Mota, a formidable brick fortress on a hill at the town's edge, served as a royal residence, prison — Cesare Borgia was held there — and archive. The Plaza Mayor and the collegiate church of San Antolín are also notable.
