Vittorio Pozzo

Vittorio Pozzo (1886–1968) was an Italian football manager who led the Italy national team to victory in two FIFA World Cups, in 1934 and 1938 — still the only manager to win two World Cups — as well as Olympic gold in 1936.

Career

Pozzo studied in England, where he developed a love of football, and became a central figure in early Italian football, co-founding the Torino side of the game. As national coach (principally 1929–1948) he devised the Metodo formation (2–3–2–3) and built his teams around discipline and patriotic motivation under Mussolini's Fascist regime. His Italy won the Central European International Cup and went on a long unbeaten run in the 1930s.

Legacy

Pozzo's back-to-back World Cup wins make him one of the most successful international managers in history, though his career is inseparable from the Fascist context in which those triumphs occurred. He later worked as a journalist. He died in Turin in 1968.
