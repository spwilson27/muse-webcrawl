Gongfu tea ceremony

The gongfu tea ceremony or kung fu tea ceremony (Chinese: 工夫茶; pinyin: gōngfū chá) is a method of making and serving tea originating in the Chaoshan region of Guangdong province and in Fujian, China, and practised widely in Taiwan. The term gongfu means "skill" or "effort" — tea made with skill and care — rather than referring to martial arts.

Method

The ceremony uses a small teapot (often Yixing clay) or a lidded gaiwan, filled generously with whole-leaf tea — traditionally oolong such as Tieguanyin — and very hot water. The leaves are steeped in many short infusions, each poured fully into small tasting cups, often through a fairness pitcher so that every cup tastes the same. The first rinse infusion may be discarded to "awaken" the leaves. Successive infusions of the same leaves are compared for aroma, colour and flavour.

Utensils and setting

A typical setup includes a tea tray with drainage, teapot or gaiwan, fairness pitcher, strainer, aroma and tasting cups, kettle, and tools for handling the leaves. The ceremony emphasises precision of temperature and timing, cleanliness, and hospitality toward guests, and is practised both informally at home and in teahouses across the Chinese-speaking world.
