Hydrofluoric acid is a solution of hydrogen fluoride (HF) in water. Although classified as a weak acid because it does not fully dissociate, it is one of the most hazardous acids in common use: it attacks glass and most metals, and fluoride ions released in contact with tissue penetrate deeply and can cause severe, sometimes fatal, systemic toxicity.

Properties and production

HF dissolves silica and silicates, the basis of glass etching and of its use in the semiconductor industry to clean and pattern silicon wafers; for this reason it must be stored in plastic (typically polyethylene or PTFE) containers. Industrially, hydrogen fluoride is produced by reacting fluorspar (calcium fluoride) with concentrated sulfuric acid.

Hazards and treatment

Unusually for an acid, dilute HF burns may initially be painless, with pain and tissue destruction developing hours later. Fluoride ions bind calcium and magnesium, so absorption through skin or by inhalation can cause dangerous hypocalcaemia, hypomagnesaemia and cardiac arrhythmias, and deep burns to even a small skin area can be lethal. First aid involves copious irrigation, topical or injected calcium gluconate (which precipitates the fluoride as insoluble calcium fluoride), and urgent hospital assessment with cardiac monitoring and blood electrolyte correction. HF is also used in petroleum alkylation, uranium processing, rust removal and the production of fluorocarbons.
