Extra 300

The Extra Flugzeugbau EA300, commonly called the Extra 300, is a two-seat German aerobatic monoplane capable of Unlimited category competition. It was designed in 1987 by German aerobatic pilot Walter Extra and built by the company he founded, Extra Flugzeugbau.

The Extra 300 has a welded steel tube fuselage covered in aluminium and fabric, a mid-mounted carbon-fibre composite wing, and is powered by a Lycoming AEIO-540 engine producing 300 horsepower (224 kW), driving a three- or four-bladed propeller. The aircraft is stressed to ±10 g and has an exceptionally high roll rate, making it a favourite in air shows and competitions such as the Red Bull Air Race.

Variants

Variants include the original two-seat Extra 300, the single-seat Extra 300S, the high-performance Extra 300SX, the four-place Extra 400 touring aircraft, and the Extra 330 series, including the Extra 330SC used in world championship competition. The Extra 300L is a popular two-seat version with a lowered wing for improved visibility.

Specifications (Extra 300S)

Length: 6.65 m; wingspan: 7.39 m; empty weight: about 627 kg; maximum speed: 407 km/h; roll rate: over 400 degrees per second; service ceiling: 4,900 m.
