Stress concentration

A stress concentration (also called a stress raiser or stress riser) is a location in an object where the stress is significantly greater than in the surrounding region. Stress concentrations occur where there is a sudden change in the geometry of the material, such as at holes, notches, grooves, fillets, threads, keyways or cracks, and also at inclusions or abrupt changes of section.

Stress concentration factor

The severity is measured by the stress concentration factor, the ratio of the maximum stress at the discontinuity to the nominal stress in the section. For an elliptical hole, the theoretical factor increases with the sharpness of the ellipse; for a circular hole in a wide plate it is about 3. In fatigue loading, the related fatigue stress concentration factor accounts for the material's notch sensitivity.

Design practice

Engineers reduce stress concentrations with generous fillet radii, relief grooves, avoiding abrupt section changes, and polishing surfaces. Undetected stress concentrations, such as at the square windows of the early de Havilland Comet airliners, have caused catastrophic fatigue failures.
