Bridgwater is a historic market town and civil parish in Somerset, England, on the edge of the Somerset Levels, along both banks of the River Parrett. The town was a major inland port and industrial centre, noted for its brick, tile and cellophane industries, and is the administrative centre of the Sedgemoor area. It lies about 28 miles (45 km) south-west of Bristol and 9 miles (14 km) from the coast at Bridgwater Bay.

History

Bridgwater is recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Brugie; its name is usually taken to derive from "the bridge belonging to Walter" (Walter of Douai, the Norman lord who held the manor). The town received a charter from King John in 1200 and grew as a port on the tidal Parrett, trading in wool, wine and later coal. During the English Civil War the town was a Royalist stronghold and was besieged and taken by Parliamentarian forces in 1645. In 1685 the Duke of Monmouth was proclaimed king in Bridgwater before his defeat at the nearby Battle of Sedgemoor, the last pitched battle fought on English soil; the subsequent Bloody Assizes were presided over by Judge Jeffreys. Bridgwater was the birthplace, in 1597, of Robert Blake, one of the Commonwealth's most important naval commanders; his statue stands in the town centre and the Blake Museum preserves his memory.

Industry

In the 19th century Bridgwater became a major centre of brick and tile manufacture, its products exported worldwide from the docks. In the 20th century the British Cellophane factory, opened in the 1930s, became a dominant employer until its closure in 2005. Today the local economy includes logistics, food processing and services supporting the construction of the Hinkley Point C nuclear power station nearby, for which Bridgwater provides accommodation and the campus of Bridgwater and Taunton College.

Carnival

Bridgwater is famous for its Guy Fawkes Carnival, held each year on the Saturday closest to 5 November and dating its origins to the town's celebrations after the Gunpowther Plot of 1605. The procession of illuminated carnival carts, built by local carnival clubs and among the largest illuminated processions in Europe, is accompanied by the traditional "squibbing" of hand-held fireworks in the town square. The carnival forms part of the circuit of Somerset carnivals.

Transport

Bridgwater railway station lies on the Bristol to Exeter line and is served by Great Western Railway and CrossCountry trains. The town is linked to the M5 motorway at junctions 23 and 24. The port, once extending into the town centre, declined in the 20th century; commercial traffic now uses the wharves at Dunball downstream.
