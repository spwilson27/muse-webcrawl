Siege of Toulouse (721)

The Battle of Toulouse of 721 was a victory of an Aquitanian Christian army led by Duke Odo of Aquitaine over an Umayyad Muslim army besieging the city of Toulouse, and led by the governor of Al-Andalus, Al-Samh ibn Malik al-Khawlani. The victory checked the Umayyad expansion northward from the Iberian Peninsula into Gaul for a time; Al-Samh died of his wounds shortly after the battle. The battle is sometimes dated to 9 June 721 and is known in Arabic sources as the "Battle of the Highway of the Martyrs" (Maʿarakat Balāṭ ash-Shuhadāʾ), a name later also applied to the Battle of Tours.

Background

After conquering Visigothic Hispania, Umayyad forces crossed the Pyrenees and seized Septimania, then advanced up the Garonne against Toulouse, the chief city of Aquitaine. Duke Odo, unable to hold the city alone, withdrew to gather reinforcements while the Umayyads settled down to a siege.

The battle

Odo returned with an army and fell on the besiegers, catching them unprepared between his forces and the city garrison, which sallied out at the same moment. The Umayyad camp was overrun and the army routed with heavy losses; Al-Samh was mortally wounded and died at Narbonne. Medieval chronicles give wildly exaggerated casualty figures, but the defeat was plainly severe.

Aftermath

The victory preserved Aquitaine's independence and gave the Franks a respite, though Umayyad raiding resumed and reached its height before the Battle of Tours (732). Odo's success at Toulouse is sometimes overshadowed by Tours but was the first major check to Muslim expansion in southwestern Gaul.
