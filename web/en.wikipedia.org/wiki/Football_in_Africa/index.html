Association football is the most popular sport in Africa. The sport was introduced to the continent by European colonists, soldiers, and missionaries in the late 19th century, and the first recorded matches took place in South Africa in the 1860s. The Confederation of African Football (CAF) was founded in Khartoum, Sudan, in 1957 by the national associations of Egypt, Sudan, Ethiopia, and South Africa, and the first Africa Cup of Nations was held the same year, won by Egypt.

International football

The Africa Cup of Nations (AFCON), held every two years, is the continent's premier national-team competition; Egypt is its most successful team with seven titles. African teams have competed at the FIFA World Cup since Egypt's appearance in 1934. The best World Cup performances by African teams are semi-final appearances by Morocco in 2022 — the first African (and Arab) team to reach the last four — following quarter-final runs by Cameroon (1990), Senegal (2002), and Ghana (2010). South Africa hosted the 2010 World Cup, the first on African soil, and Morocco is set to co-host the 2030 tournament.

Club football

CAF organises the CAF Champions League (first held in 1964) and the CAF Confederation Cup. Egypt's Al Ahly is the most successful club in African competition, with a record number of Champions League titles; other historically strong clubs include Zamalek (Egypt), TP Mazembe (DR Congo), Espérance (Tunisia), and Raja and Wydad (Morocco). Domestic leagues vary greatly in resources, and the continent's top players frequently move to European clubs.

Players

Africa has produced many of world football's outstanding players, including Roger Milla, Abedi Pele, George Weah — the only African winner of the Ballon d'Or (1995) and later President of Liberia — Didier Drogba, Samuel Eto'o, Yaya Touré, Mohamed Salah, and Sadio Mané. Women's football has also grown strongly, with Nigeria's Super Falcons the dominant force in the Women's Africa Cup of Nations and Asisat Oshoala among the game's leading players.

Challenges

African football faces persistent challenges including underfunded infrastructure, governance and corruption scandals within CAF and national federations, the early departure of talented players to Europe, and disputes over the scheduling of the Cup of Nations in the middle of the European club season.
