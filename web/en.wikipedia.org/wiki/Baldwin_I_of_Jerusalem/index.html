Baldwin I of Jerusalem

Baldwin I, first King of Jerusalem

PredecessorGodfrey of Bouillon (as ruler of Jerusalem)

Baldwin I (c. 1058 – 2 April 1118) was the first count of Edessa from 1098 to 1100 and the first king of Jerusalem from 1100 until his death. A younger brother of Godfrey of Bouillon, he joined the First Crusade in 1096, carved out the County of Edessa — the first Crusader state — and succeeded his brother in Jerusalem, where he was crowned king in Bethlehem on Christmas Day 1100.

Count of Edessa

During the crusade's march through Anatolia, Baldwin separated from the main army, moved into Cilicia and northern Syria, and was invited to Edessa by its Armenian ruler Thoros, who adopted him as heir. After Thoros was killed in a revolt in March 1098, Baldwin became sole ruler, founding the County of Edessa. He married Arda, an Armenian noblewoman, and consolidated his rule by alliances with local Armenian lords.

King of Jerusalem

On Godfrey's death in July 1100, Baldwin was invited to Jerusalem by Godfrey's supporters, defeating the rival claims of Patriarch Daimbert. He greatly expanded the kingdom: with Genoese and Venetian naval help he captured Arsuf, Caesarea, Acre (1104), Beirut, and Sidon, securing the coastal cities on which the kingdom depended for supplies and trade. He built castles such as Montreal in Oultrejordain to control the routes east of the Jordan, and campaigned repeatedly against Fatimid Egypt and towards Damascus.

Marriages and succession

Baldwin set aside his Armenian wife Arda and married Adelaide del Vasto, the wealthy widow of Roger I of Sicily, in 1113 — a bigamous union later annulled under church pressure. He died on campaign in Egypt in 1118 after eating fish from the Nile, leaving no children; the barons elected his cousin (or brother) Baldwin of Le Bourcq, count of Edessa, as Baldwin II.
