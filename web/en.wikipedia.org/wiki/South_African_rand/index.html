South African rand

ISO 4217 code: ZAR

Symbol: R

Subunit: Cent (1/100)

Central bank: South African Reserve Bank

Introduced: 14 February 1961

The South African rand (sign: R; code: ZAR) is the official currency of South Africa. It is divided into 100 cents. The rand is also legal tender in the Common Monetary Area member states of Namibia, Lesotho and Eswatini, whose own currencies are pegged at par to it. The name derives from the Witwatersrand ("ridge of white waters"), the gold-bearing ridge on which Johannesburg is built.

History

The rand was introduced on 14 February 1961, replacing the South African pound at a rate of 2 rand to 1 pound, shortly before the country became a republic. Initially worth about US$1.40, it traded above parity with the US dollar until the early 1980s. Political turmoil, sanctions and the 1985 debt crisis weakened it severely; a dual exchange-rate system (commercial and financial rand) operated until 1995. After 1994 the currency floated freely and became one of the most actively traded emerging-market currencies, though with high volatility: it crashed sharply in 2001, recovered strongly by 2004–05, and has periodically weakened during global risk sell-offs and domestic energy crises.

Coins and banknotes

Coins circulate in denominations of 10c, 20c, 50c, R1, R2 and R5. Banknotes are issued in R10, R20, R50, R100 and R200 denominations. The current note series features Nelson Mandela's portrait on the obverse and the "Big Five" animals on the reverse; an upgraded series with enhanced security features and accessibility elements was released from 2023.
