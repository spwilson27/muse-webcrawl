Bob Hope (born Leslie Townes Hope; 29 May 1903 – 27 July 2003) was an American comedian, actor, entertainer and producer with a career that spanned nearly 80 years, in vaudeville, on Broadway, in radio and films, and on television. He hosted the Academy Awards 19 times, more than any other host, and was famed for entertaining American troops overseas through the USO from the Second World War to the Gulf War.

Career

Hope emigrated from England to the United States with his family in 1908. After success in vaudeville and Broadway, his radio show became one of America's most popular. With Bing Crosby and Dorothy Lamour he made the hugely successful series of "Road to..." comedy films (1940–1962), including Road to Morocco and Road to Utopia. His comic persona — cowardly, boastful, wisecracking — and rapid-fire one-liners delivered with impeccable timing defined his style.

USO tours

Beginning in 1941, Hope performed for American service personnel in every major conflict through 1991, making him an enduring symbol of home-front support; Congress named him an honorary veteran in 1997.

Legacy

Hope received over 2,000 honours, including five special Academy Awards, the Presidential Medal of Freedom, and an honorary knighthood. He died aged 100 in 2003.
