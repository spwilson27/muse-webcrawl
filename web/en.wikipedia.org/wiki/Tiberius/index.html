Tiberius Claudius Nero (42 BC – AD 37) was Roman emperor from AD 14 until his death, succeeding his stepfather Augustus as the second emperor and ruling for 23 years. Before his accession he was one of Rome's most successful generals, conquering Pannonia, Dalmatia, Raetia, and parts of Germania.

Reign

Tiberius inherited a stable empire and a full treasury and largely preserved Augustus's settlement. His reign saw the trial and political destruction surrounding the death of his nephew and heir Germanicus (AD 19). Increasingly distrustful, he relied on the praetorian prefect Sejanus, withdrew in AD 26 to the island of Capri, and governed from there while treason trials (under the maiestas law) multiplied in Rome at Sejanus's instigation — until Sejanus's own spectacular fall and execution in AD 31. Ancient sources such as Tacitus, Suetonius, and Cassius Dio paint Tiberius as a gloomy, suspicious tyrant, though modern historians credit him with competent, frugal administration that left the empire prosperous and its finances strong (he left a large surplus in the treasury).

Death and succession

Tiberius died at Misenum in AD 37, aged 77. Rumours that he was smothered to hasten the succession are reported by the sources. He was succeeded by Germanicus's son Gaius (Caligula). Jesus of Nazareth was crucified during his reign, under the prefect Pontius Pilate — hence the Gospels' phrase "in the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar".
