Mont-de-Marsan (Gascon: Lo Mont de Marçan) is a commune and the prefecture of the Landes department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region of southwestern France. It stands at the confluence of the Midou and the Douze rivers, which join to form the Midouze, in the heart of the Landes forest, one of the largest maritime pine forests in Europe.

History

The town was founded in the 12th century, around 1133–1141, by Pierre de Lobanner, Viscount of Marsan, who built a castle where the rivers meet. It grew as a fortified river port, shipping wine, grain, and resin products down the Midouze toward Bayonne. The town was contested during the Hundred Years' War and the French Wars of Religion.

Economy and life

Mont-de-Marsan is a market and administrative centre for the surrounding agricultural and forestry region, known for foie gras, Armagnac, and maize production. The Fêtes de la Madeleine, held each July, are among the major summer festivals of southwestern France, with bull-related events, concerts, and street festivities.

Mont-de-Marsan Air Base (Base aérienne 118) is a major French Air and Space Force installation on the edge of the town, hosting fighter squadrons and the air force's experimentation centre. The town is also home to the Despiau-Wlérick museum of sculpture and the Stade Montois sports clubs, including a professional rugby union team.
