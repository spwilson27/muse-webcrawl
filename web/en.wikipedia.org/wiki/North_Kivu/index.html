North Kivu (French: Nord-Kivu) is a province in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. It covers about 59,483 square kilometres and has its capital at Goma, on the northern shore of Lake Kivu. The province borders Uganda and Rwanda to the east, and takes its name from Lake Kivu, which forms part of its eastern boundary.

Geography

The province includes part of the Rwenzori Mountains in the north and much of Virunga National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for its mountain gorillas. Lake Edward lies partly within the province. The terrain ranges from volcanic highlands near Goma — including the active volcano Mount Nyiragongo, which erupted in 1977, 2002 and 2021 — to tropical forest in the interior.

History

North Kivu was created as a separate province in the reorganisation of 1988, when the former Kivu region was split into North Kivu, South Kivu and Maniema. The region was heavily affected by the aftermath of the 1994 Rwandan genocide, when large numbers of refugees fled into the area, and by the First and Second Congo Wars. Armed conflict involving numerous militia groups, including the M23 movement, displaced millions of people in the province in subsequent decades.

Economy

The economy is largely agricultural, producing coffee, tea, beans, potatoes and quinine. The province also contains deposits of coltan, cassiterite and gold, whose extraction has been linked to the financing of armed groups. Tourism centred on Virunga's gorillas has been an important but conflict-sensitive source of revenue.
