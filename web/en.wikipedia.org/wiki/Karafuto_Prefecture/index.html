Karafuto Prefecture

Karafuto Prefecture (樺太庁, Karafuto-chō), commonly called South Sakhalin, was a prefecture of the Empire of Japan covering the southern half of Sakhalin Island, south of the 50th parallel. Japan acquired the territory from Russia under the Treaty of Portsmouth in 1905, ending the Russo-Japanese War, and administered it until the Soviet Union seized it in August 1945.

History

The Karafuto Agency was established in 1907 with its capital at Ōdomari (later Korsakov), moving to Toyohara (now Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk) in 1908. In 1943 Karafuto was elevated from a colonial agency to an ordinary prefecture of Japan proper (naichi). Settlers developed coal mines, fisheries, timber and paper industries, and by 1940 the population exceeded 400,000, mostly ethnic Japanese and Koreans, alongside the indigenous Ainu, Nivkh and Uilta peoples.

Evacuation and aftermath

The Soviet Union invaded South Sakhalin on 11 August 1945; fighting continued past Japan's surrender, and the territory was incorporated into the USSR as part of Sakhalin Oblast. Nearly all Japanese inhabitants were repatriated by 1949, though many ethnic Koreans remained stranded for decades. Japan formally renounced its claim in the San Francisco Peace Treaty of 1951.
