La Monte Young

La Monte Thornton Young (born October 14, 1935) is an American composer, musician and performance artist widely recognised as the first minimalist composer and one of the most influential figures in post-war avant-garde music. His works are built on sustained tones and drones in just intonation, prolonged durations and altered states of listening. With his wife, visual artist Marian Zazeela, he has created long-running sound-and-light environments known as Dream Houses.

Life and career

Born in Idaho and raised in Utah and California, Young studied at Los Angeles City College and UCLA, where he played saxophone in jazz bands and became absorbed in serialism, studying informally with Leonard Stein and encountering the work of Anton Webern. His Trio for Strings (1958), composed of long sustained tones separated by silences, is regarded as a founding document of musical minimalism. Moving to New York in 1960, he became central to the Fluxus circle; his Compositions 1960 — instruction pieces such as "draw a straight line and follow it" — influenced Yoko Ono and the conceptual art movement.

Theatre of Eternal Music

From 1962 Young led the ensemble the Theatre of Eternal Music (later the Dream Syndicate), whose members included Tony Conrad, John Cale, Angus MacLise and Marian Zazeela. The group developed improvisations over sustained drones tuned in just intonation — work that directly fed into the Velvet Underground's sound via Cale and Conrad. Disputes over credit and Young's control of the group's tapes later became notorious in experimental music history.

Later work

Young studied Hindustani vocal music with Pandit Pran Nath from 1970, which shaped his later vocal works. His monumental, still-unfinished piano work The Well-Tuned Piano (begun 1964) uses a radically retuned grand piano and performances lasting five hours or more. The Dream House installations, combining his sine-wave drones with Zazeela's coloured light sculptures, have run in New York (notably the long-term installation on Church Street maintained by the MELA Foundation) and internationally for decades.
