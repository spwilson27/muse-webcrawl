David Warren Brubeck (December 6, 1920 – December 5, 2012) was an American jazz pianist and composer. Often regarded as a foremost exponent of cool jazz, his work is characterised by unusual time signatures and the superimposition of contrasting rhythms, metres, and tonalities, partly influenced by his studies with the French composer Darius Milhaud.

Career

Brubeck formed the Dave Brubeck Quartet in 1951 with saxophonist Paul Desmond, touring American college campuses and building a devoted young audience. The quartet's 1959 album Time Out, an experiment in odd metres, was initially received cautiously by Columbia Records but became the first jazz album to sell a million copies. It contains "Take Five" — composed by Desmond in 5/4 time, the best-selling jazz single of all time — and Brubeck's own "Blue Rondo à la Turk".

Later life and honours

An advocate for racial integration, Brubeck cancelled tours rather than replace his African-American bassist Eugene Wright, and refused to perform for segregated audiences. In 1958 he toured Eastern Europe and Asia for the U.S. State Department as a cultural ambassador. He composed oratorios, ballets, and a mass, and continued performing into his nineties. Brubeck received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award, was designated a "Living Legend" by the Library of Congress, and received the Kennedy Center Honors in 2009. He died of heart failure on 5 December 2012, one day before his 92nd birthday.
