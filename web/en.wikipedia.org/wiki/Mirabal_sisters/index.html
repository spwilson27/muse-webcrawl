Mirabal sisters

The Mirabal sisters — Patria, Minerva, and María Teresa Mirabal — were three Dominican women who opposed the dictatorship of Rafael Trujillo and were assassinated on 25 November 1960. A fourth sister, Bélgica ("Dedé"), survived and devoted her life to preserving their memory. Known by their underground codename Las Mariposas ("The Butterflies"), they became enduring symbols of resistance to tyranny and of the fight against violence against women.

Activism and assassination

Minerva, the most politically outspoken, studied law and helped found the 14th of June Movement against the regime. The sisters and their husbands were repeatedly imprisoned and persecuted. On 25 November 1960, the three sisters were ambushed on a mountain road while returning from visiting their jailed husbands; they and their driver were beaten and strangled, and the car was pushed over a cliff to simulate an accident. Public outrage at the murders accelerated the collapse of the dictatorship; Trujillo was assassinated six months later.

Legacy

In 1999 the United Nations designated 25 November, the anniversary of the assassinations, as the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women. Julia Alvarez's novel In the Time of the Butterflies popularised their story internationally.
