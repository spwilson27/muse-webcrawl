The Meherrin River is a river in the U.S. states of Virginia and North Carolina, a tributary of the Chowan River. It is formed by the confluence of the North Meherrin River and the South Meherrin River in Lunenburg County, Virginia, and flows generally southeast through southern Virginia into North Carolina, where it joins the Chowan River near its mouth on Albemarle Sound.

The river takes its name from the Meherrin, an Iroquoian-speaking Native American people whose homeland lay along the river in what is now the Virginia–North Carolina border region. The Meherrin Indian Tribe is today based in Hertford County, North Carolina.

Course

The North and South forks rise in Charlotte and Halifax counties, Virginia, and unite near the Lunenburg–Mecklenburg county line. From there the river flows past the towns of Emporia, Virginia, where it is dammed to form a small reservoir, and crosses into North Carolina, passing near Murfreesboro before meeting the Chowan.
