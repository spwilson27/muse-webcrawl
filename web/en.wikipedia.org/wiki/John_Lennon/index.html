John Lennon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: John Lennon in December 1980, photographed in New York days before his death: long hair, round glasses, dark jacket]

John Winston Ono Lennon (9 October 1940 – 8 December 1980) was an English singer, songwriter and musician, co-founder of the Beatles — with Paul McCartney his songwriting partner in the most successful partnership in popular music — and thereafter a solo artist and peace activist whose songs 'Imagine' and 'Give Peace a Chance' became secular hymns.

With the Beatles (1960–70) Lennon wrote or co-wrote much of the group's defining work, from 'A Hard Day's Night' to 'Strawberry Fields Forever' and 'Come Together'. His solo career — Plastic Ono Band (1970), Imagine (1971) — exchanged Beatle polish for confession and slogan, in partnership with the artist Yoko Ono, whom he married in 1969.

Beatles and after

Born in Liverpool and raised by his Aunt Mimi, Lennon formed the Quarrymen at 16, met McCartney in 1957, and drove the Beatles' early bite: the wit, the sneer, the harmonica. Fame's machinery wounded him (his 1966 'more popular than Jesus' remark, made about modern fame, ignited American record-burnings); drugs, the avant-garde and Ono drew him out of the group by 1969. The bed-ins for peace, the Plastic Ono Band and the primal-scream therapy of his first solo album remade him as an activist-artist; the US government, frightened of his influence, spent 1971–75 trying to deport him.

Death and legacy

After five years as a househusband raising his son Sean, Lennon returned with Double Fantasy in November 1980. Three weeks later, on 8 December 1980, he was shot outside the Dakota building by Mark David Chapman, a deranged fan he had signed an autograph for that afternoon. The world's mourning was unprecedented for a pop musician; Strawberry Fields in Central Park, the Imagine mosaic, and Lennon's standing in every poll of songwriters keep his memory public property.
