DesignerJim Bede

VariantsBD-5B (propeller), BD-5J (jet)

The Bede BD-5 Micro is a series of small, single-seat homebuilt aircraft created in the early 1970s by American aircraft designer Jim Bede and produced by Bede Aircraft Corp in kit form. At roughly 13 feet (4 metres) in length, it is among the smallest crewed aircraft ever built, and its sleek, fighter-like appearance made it one of the most famous kitplanes in history.

Design and development

The BD-5 is a low-wing monoplane with a pusher propeller driven by an engine mounted inside the fuselage behind the pilot. The jet-powered BD-5J variant, powered by a Microturbo turbojet, became widely known through appearances including the James Bond film Octopussy and held a Guinness record as the world's smallest jet aircraft.

History

The BD-5 generated enormous enthusiasm and thousands of kit orders, but engine supply failures and the bankruptcy of Bede Aircraft in the mid-1970s meant many kits were never completed. Of the thousands sold, only a few hundred were finished and flown. The type also acquired a reputation for demanding handling, particularly in early examples with short wings.
