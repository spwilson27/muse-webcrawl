The Ann Alexander was an American whaleship from New Bedford, Massachusetts, famous for having been attacked and sunk by a sperm whale in the Pacific Ocean on 20 August 1851. The incident occurred only about thirty years after a similar attack sank the whaleship Essex in 1820, an event that later inspired Herman Melville's novel Moby-Dick (1851).

The sinking

On 20 August 1851, while cruising for sperm whales in the Pacific, the crew of the Ann Alexander lowered boats in pursuit of a large bull sperm whale. The whale turned on the boats, stoving and destroying two of them. After the men were picked up, the enraged whale attacked the ship itself, ramming it below the waterline and holing the hull so badly that the vessel filled and sank. The entire crew escaped in the remaining boats and were later rescued by another vessel; remarkably, no lives were lost.

Legacy

The episode was widely reported in newspapers of the day and was noted by Herman Melville, who commented on the curious coincidence that a second whaleship had been sunk by a sperm whale in the same region. The whale was reportedly captured some months later by another whaleship, with pieces of the Ann Alexander's timbers still embedded in its head.
