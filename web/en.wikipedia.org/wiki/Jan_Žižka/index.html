Jan Žižka z Trocnova a Kalicha (c. 1360 – 11 October 1424) was a Czech general and leader of the Hussites during the Hussite Wars. He is regarded as one of the most innovative military commanders of the Middle Ages and a Czech national hero. Despite losing sight in both eyes over the course of his career, he never lost a battle.

Early life

Žižka was born into the minor nobility at Trocnov in southern Bohemia. Little is certain about his youth; he lost one eye as a boy or young man. He served at the royal court in Prague, may have fought at the Battle of Grunwald (1410), and, radicalized by the execution of the reformer Jan Hus in 1415, took part in the First Defenestration of Prague in 1419 that opened the Hussite revolt.

Military career

As a captain of the radical Taborites, Žižka developed the tactics that made peasant armies a match for armoured knights: the vozová hradba, a moving fortress of war wagons chained together, manned by crossbowmen, handgunners, and flailmen, coordinated with field artillery and cavalry counterattacks. With these methods he defeated successive crusades against the Hussites, winning at Sudoměř (1420), Vítkov Hill (1420), Kutná Hora (1421–22), and elsewhere. He lost his remaining eye to an arrow at the siege of Rábí castle in 1421 but continued to command, reportedly having battle maps and reports described to him.

Death and legacy

Žižka died of plague near Přibyslav on 11 October 1424 while campaigning against the Utraquists, fellow Hussites of a more moderate faction. His followers took the name "Orphans" in mourning. Legend claims he asked that his skin be made into a drum so he could lead his men even in death. His equestrian statue in Prague, among the largest in the world, anchors the National Monument at Vítkov, and his name has been given to the Prague district of Žižkov.
