Siege of Namur (1692)

The siege of Namur, 25 May – 30 June 1692, was a major operation of the Nine Years' War. A huge French army personally commanded by Louis XIV, with the siege directed by Vauban, besieged the fortress-city of Namur in the Spanish Netherlands, one of the strongest positions in Europe, held for the Grand Alliance by a garrison under the Prince of Barbançon with fortifications supervised by the Dutch engineer Menno van Coehoorn. The town surrendered on 5 June and the citadel, after a heroic defence, on 30 June. It was the showcase siege of the age — Vauban against Coehoorn, the two greatest engineers of the era.

Background

Namur, at the confluence of the Sambre and Meuse and crowned by a rock citadel recently modernized by Coehoorn, blocked French movement between Flanders and the Meuse. Louis assembled roughly 60,000 men for the siege proper, with the Duc de Luxembourg's covering army of similar size warding off William III. Louis watched operations from a royal pavilion, accompanied by the court.

The siege

Vauban's attack, pressed with great quantities of artillery and relentless trench work despite torrential rain that turned the trenches to mud, carried the town's water-side defences, and the town capitulated on 5 June. The citadel, however, held out under Barbançon and Coehoorn in a celebrated defence; Coehoorn was wounded and briefly captured during a parley. An epic struggle for the outwork known as Fort William (the "coffre," or coffer) consumed the middle of June. Bombardment and the exhaustion of ammunition finally compelled the citadel's surrender on 30 June, the garrison marching out with the honours of war.

Aftermath

The fall of Namur was trumpeted across France as the summit of Louis's military glory; Racine and Boileau celebrated it in verse. Three years later William III retook Namur in the equally famous siege of 1695, reversing the verdict. The 1692 siege, minutely documented in plans and memoirs, remains a classic study in siege warfare and in the professional rivalry of Vauban and Coehoorn.
