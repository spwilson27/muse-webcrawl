The Battle of Atlanta was fought on 22 July 1864, just south-east of the city of Atlanta, Georgia, during the Atlanta Campaign of the American Civil War. Union forces under Major General William Tecumseh Sherman repelled a major Confederate assault ordered by General John Bell Hood, who had recently replaced Joseph E. Johnston in command of the Army of Tennessee.

Background

Sherman's armies had manoeuvred Hood back into Atlanta's defences. Major General James B. McPherson's Army of the Tennessee extended the Union left east of the city toward Decatur and the Georgia Railroad, threatening Atlanta's last supply line. Hood devised a bold plan: Lieutenant General William J. Hardee's corps would march around the Union flank overnight and attack McPherson from the rear and side at dawn.

The battle

The long march delayed Hardee's attack until the afternoon of 22 July, by which time the Federals had refused their flank. Fierce fighting erupted east of Atlanta; Confederate troops briefly captured guns and broke into the Union lines near the Troup Hurt house, but counterattacks restored the position. McPherson was killed while reconnoitring a gap in his lines — one of the highest-ranking Union officers to die in the war. Major General John A. Logan took command and rallied the army. A separate Confederate cavalry raid under Joseph Wheeler failed to destroy the Union supply depot at Decatur.

Aftermath

Confederate casualties were roughly 5,500 against about 3,700 Union. Hood's defeat did not end his offensive efforts — he attacked again at Ezra Church a week later — but Atlanta's fate was sealed as Sherman tightened the siege; the city fell on 2 September 1864, a blow that helped secure President Lincoln's re-election.
