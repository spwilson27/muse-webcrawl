Heighliner

A heighliner is a fictional starship type in Frank Herbert's Dune universe. Operated exclusively by the Spacing Guild, heighliners are enormous vessels — described as some 20 kilometres long — whose cavernous holds carry fleets of frigates, transports and cargo between star systems. They do not travel through space in the ordinary sense: a Guild Navigator, mutated and saturated with the spice melange, uses limited prescience to fold space with the ship's Holtzman-effect drives, moving the vessel instantaneously between points while guiding it to a safe arrival.

Guild heighliners are the backbone of interstellar transport in the Imperium.

Role in the universe

Because interstellar travel is a Guild monopoly, heighliners are neutral ground: no fighting is permitted aboard them, and all factions of the Imperium — the Emperor, the Great Houses of the Landsraad, and the Bene Gesserit — must pay Guild tariffs and rely on Guild Navigators to move armies, goods and people. This monopoly gives the Guild immense political leverage, though it never seeks direct rule. Passengers and ships pay by size and weight, and vessels dock internally within the heighliner's hold for the instantaneous jump.

Depictions

The heighliner appears in Herbert's first novel Dune (1965), carrying House Atreides from Caladan to Arrakis. It has been visualised in David Lynch's 1984 film Dune, the 2000 Sci Fi Channel miniseries, and Denis Villeneuve's Dune (2021), where the heighliner is a vast horizontal cylinder through which smaller ships pass, emerging instantaneously above Arrakis.
