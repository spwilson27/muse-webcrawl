Thimmamma Marrimanu is a banyan tree (Ficus benghalensis) located about 25 km from Kadiri in the Anantapur district of Andhra Pradesh, India, and roughly 120 km north-east of Bengaluru. It is renowned for having the largest tree canopy of any single tree in the world: its crown covers approximately 19,107 square metres (about 2 hectares, or nearly 5 acres), supported by more than a thousand aerial prop roots that have grown down into the soil to form secondary trunks. The tree was entered in the Guinness Book of World Records in 1989 as the largest tree specimen in the world.

In Telugu, marri means banyan and manu means tree; "Thimmamma" is the name of the woman with whom the tree is traditionally associated.

Legend

Local tradition holds that the tree grew from the spot where Thimmamma, a devout woman of the 15th–16th century, performed sati on the funeral pyre of her husband, Bala Veerayya, who is said to have died around 1434 (some accounts give later dates). A small temple dedicated to Thimmamma stands beneath the tree, and the site is a place of pilgrimage, particularly for childless couples, who believe that worshipping Thimmamma there will bless them with children. A festival is held at the temple on the day of Maha Shivaratri.

The tree

Like other great banyans — such as the Great Banyan in the Acharya Jagadish Chandra Bose Indian Botanic Garden near Kolkata — Thimmamma Marrimanu has expanded outward over centuries as its branches send down prop roots that thicken into new trunks. Its canopy area has been surveyed at about 19,107 m², exceeding that of the Kolkata tree, although the Great Banyan retains a larger number of prop roots. The tree suffered damage during cyclones in the region, and parts of the canopy have been lost over time, but it remains a living landmark maintained with support from the Andhra Pradesh forest and tourism authorities.
