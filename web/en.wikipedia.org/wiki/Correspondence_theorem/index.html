Correspondence theorem

In group theory, the correspondence theorem (also known as the lattice theorem, and variously the third and fourth isomorphism theorem) states that if N is a normal subgroup of a group G, then there is a bijection between the set of all subgroups A of G containing N and the set of all subgroups of the quotient group G/N.

Statement

The bijection is given by A ↦ A/N. It preserves inclusions, intersections, joins, indices, and normality: A is a normal subgroup of G if and only if A/N is a normal subgroup of G/N. Moreover, the quotient of two corresponding subgroups corresponds naturally, so that (B/N)/(A/N) ≅ B/A.

General versions

Analogous correspondence theorems hold throughout abstract algebra: for rings (subrings and ideals containing an ideal I versus those of the quotient ring), for modules (submodules containing a given submodule), and for fields in Galois theory, where the correspondence between intermediate fields and subgroups of the Galois group is the fundamental theorem of Galois theory.
