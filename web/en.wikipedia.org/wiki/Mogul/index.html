Mogul

A mogul is a powerful and influential person in a particular industry, most often used of leaders in business, media, film or technology. The word derives from the Mughal emperors of India: "Mogul" is an English variant of the Persian word for the Mughal dynasty, whose rulers were associated with immense wealth and power, so the term came to be applied figuratively to any dominant magnate.

The term "mogul" evokes the power of the Mughal emperors.

Usage

Familiar compounds include media mogul (for example Rupert Murdoch), movie mogul for the studio heads of Hollywood's golden age such as Louis B. Mayer and Jack Warner, real-estate mogul, and tech mogul. The word overlaps with tycoon, magnate and baron, but carries a particular suggestion of personal empire-building and hands-on control.

Other senses

In skiing, a mogul is one of the bumps of packed snow on a piste, negotiated in the freestyle discipline of mogul skiing, an Olympic event. In railway terminology, "Mogul" is the name for a steam locomotive with a 2-6-0 wheel arrangement in Whyte notation. The word should not be confused with the Muggles of popular fiction, nor with the historical Mughal Empire itself, from which the metaphor derives.
