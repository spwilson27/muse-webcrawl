UCSC Genome Browser

The UCSC Genome Browser is a web-based genome browser hosted by the University of California, Santa Cruz (UCSC). It provides access to genome sequence data and annotations for a wide collection of vertebrate and model-organism genomes, displayed as graphical "tracks" aligned to a reference assembly. It is one of the most widely used resources in genomics research.

History

The browser grew out of the Human Genome Project's need to display the draft human genome sequence. In 2000, UCSC graduate student Jim Kent wrote the GigAssembler program that assembled the public draft sequence just ahead of the project's deadline, and the first version of the Genome Browser went online in July 2000, offering free public access to the human genome. The site has since expanded to hundreds of assemblies for many species.

Features

Users can navigate to a gene or genomic region and view stacked annotation tracks covering genes, variation, conservation, gene expression, regulation, and clinical data. Companion tools include BLAT for fast sequence alignment searches, the Table Browser for database queries and downloads, and facilities for uploading custom tracks and sharing sessions.
