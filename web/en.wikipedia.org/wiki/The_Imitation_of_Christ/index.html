The Imitation of Christ

The Imitation of Christ (Latin: De Imitatione Christi) is a Christian devotional book written in Latin, composed between about 1418 and 1427 and traditionally attributed to Thomas à Kempis. It is a handbook of spiritual meditations reflecting the ideals of the Devotio Moderna movement, and is one of the most widely read devotional works in Christianity — often said to be, after the Bible, the most printed book in history.

Contents

The work is divided into four books: "Helpful Counsels of the Spiritual Life", "Directives for the Interior Life", "On Interior Consolation" — a dialogue between Christ and the disciple — and a book on the sacrament of the altar. Its teaching emphasises humility, contempt for worldly vanity, interior prayer and the imitation of Christ's life and Passion. Its famous opening quote adapts John 8:12: "He who follows Me shall not walk in darkness."

Influence

Circulating first among the Brethren of the Common Life, the book spread across Europe in manuscript and early print, and has been translated into more languages than almost any other Christian text. It influenced figures as diverse as Ignatius of Loyola, John Wesley and Thérèse of Lisieux, and remains in print in many editions today.
