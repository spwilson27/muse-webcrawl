Noodle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Fresh noodles being pulled by hand]

Noodles are a type of food made from unleavened dough rolled flat and cut, stretched or extruded into long strips or strings. They are a staple in many cultures and are made from wheat, Rice, buckwheat, starches and other flours, served in soups, stir-fried, or with sauces.

The world's oldest known noodles — millet noodles some 4,000 years old — were found at Lajia in China. Wheat noodles spread across Asia (lamian, udon, soba from buckwheat, Ramen) and, through independent and exchanged traditions, to Italy's Pasta. Instant noodles, invented in 1958, are a global commodity eaten in the tens of billions of servings annually.

1. Types

Types

East Asian wheat noodles (Chinese lamian, Japanese udon and somen), buckwheat soba, rice noodles (pho, pad thai, rice vermicelli), glass noodles from mung bean or sweet potato starch, and egg noodles differ in bite and use; Korean naengmyeon is served in icy broth.
