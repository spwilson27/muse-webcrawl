The Story Bridge is a heritage-listed steel cantilever bridge spanning the Brisbane River that carries vehicles, bicycles and pedestrians between northern and southern suburbs of the city of Brisbane, Queensland, Australia. It is the longest cantilever bridge in Australia, connecting Fortitude Valley to Kangaroo Point. The bridge is named after prominent public servant John Douglas Story.

History

The bridge was designed by John Bradfield, the engineer of the Sydney Harbour Bridge, and built between 1935 and 1940 as a Depression-era public works project. It was opened on 6 July 1940 by Queensland governor Sir Leslie Wilson; tolls were charged until 1947. The steel was fabricated in Brisbane by Evans Deakin. Three men died during construction. The bridge was heritage-listed in 1992.

Features

The bridge carries six lanes of traffic on the Bradfield Highway and has footpaths on both sides. It is floodlit at night, often in colours marking special events. Since 2005, guided "bridge climbs" have allowed visitors to climb to the top of the structure, making it one of Brisbane's best-known tourist attractions and a symbol of the city.
