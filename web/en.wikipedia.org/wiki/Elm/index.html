Elms are deciduous and semi-deciduous trees comprising the genus Ulmus in the family Ulmaceae. They are found throughout the Northern Hemisphere from Siberia to Indonesia, Mexico to Japan. Many species and cultivars are grown as ornamental street and park trees worldwide.

Description

Elms typically have rough, deeply furrowed bark and alternate, simple leaves with serrated margins and characteristically asymmetric bases. The hermaphrodite flowers appear before the leaves in early spring and are wind-pollinated; the fruit is a flat, papery samara containing a single seed, designed for wind dispersal.

Dutch elm disease

Elm populations in Europe and North America were devastated during the 20th century by Dutch elm disease, a fungal disease caused by Ophiostoma species and spread by elm bark beetles. Two pandemics killed the majority of mature elms in many regions, transforming landscapes. Breeding programmes have produced disease-resistant cultivars, and resistant species from Asia have been widely planted, though continued vigilance is required.

Uses

Elm wood is interlocking-grained and resistant to splitting and to decay when permanently wet, making it historically valued for wheel hubs, chair seats, water pipes, and boat building. Elms also support rich wildlife communities and have long held a place in mythology and literature.
