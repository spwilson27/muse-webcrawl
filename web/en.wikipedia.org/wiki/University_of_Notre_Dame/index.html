University of Notre Dame

The University of Notre Dame du Lac, known simply as Notre Dame, is a private Catholic research university in Notre Dame, Indiana, near South Bend. Founded in 1842 by the Reverend Edward Sorin and the Congregation of Holy Cross, it is one of the most prominent Catholic universities in the world, enrolling about 13,000 students.

Campus and traditions

The 1,265-acre campus is famed for its Gothic Revival architecture. Landmarks include the Main Building topped by the Golden Dome with its statue of the Virgin Mary; the Basilica of the Sacred Heart; the Grotto of Our Lady of Lourdes; and the Hesburgh Library, whose south wall bears the "Word of Life" mural, popularly known as "Touchdown Jesus" for its view over Notre Dame Stadium. Undergraduate life is organized around 32 residence halls rather than fraternities.

Academics

Notre Dame is organized into colleges of arts and letters, science, and engineering, the Mendoza College of Business, the School of Architecture (known for New Classical architecture), the Keough School of Global Affairs, and a law school. It is classified among universities with very high research activity.

Athletics

The Fighting Irish are famous for their independent football program, which has won eleven consensus national championships; most other sports compete in the Atlantic Coast Conference.
