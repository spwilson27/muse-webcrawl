Theaterplatz (Dresden)

Theaterplatz

Location: Innere Altstadt, Dresden, Saxony, Germany

Named for: The Semperoper (court theatre)

Surrounding buildings: Semperoper, Zwinger, Dresden Castle, Hofkirche, Semper Gallery

The Theaterplatz ("Theatre Square") is one of the most famous squares in Dresden, the capital of Saxony, Germany. It lies on the left bank of the Elbe, west of the Innere Altstadt (inner old town), and forms the forecourt of the Semperoper, Dresden's opera house, from which it takes its name.

Surroundings

The square is framed by several of Dresden's most important buildings: the Semperoper, designed by Gottfried Semper and rebuilt in its present form in 1871–78; the Zwinger palace with the Semper Gallery (home of the Old Masters Picture Gallery); Dresden Castle (Residenzschloss); and the Hofkirche, the Catholic court church, linked to the castle by a bridge over the square's eastern edge. The Augustus Bridge crosses the Elbe at its northern side, and the Brühl Terrace runs along the river nearby.

Monument

At the centre of the square stands the equestrian statue of King John of Saxony, executed by Johannes Schilling and unveiled in 1889. During the GDR period the square was renamed Dimitroffplatz (after Georgi Dimitrov) in 1946; it regained the name Theaterplatz after German reunification in 1990.

History

The buildings around the square were largely destroyed in the bombing of Dresden in February 1945. The Semperoper reopened in 1985, forty years after its destruction, and the square was progressively restored to its pre-war appearance during the reconstruction of the old town.
