Borwein integral

In mathematics, a Borwein integral is an integral whose unusual properties were first presented by mathematicians David Borwein and Jonathan Borwein in 2001. The integrals involve products of sinc functions whose values display a pattern that appears to continue indefinitely but suddenly fails — a famous cautionary example against trusting pattern recognition in computer algebra.

The pattern

Consider integrals over the positive real line of products of sinc(ax) for a sequence of denominators 1, 1/3, 1/5, 1/7, and so on. Integrating the product of the first several factors yields exactly π/2 each time, and this continues for denominators up to 1/13. When the factor with denominator 1/15 is included, however, the value drops slightly below π/2 — the first failure occurs when the sum of the reciprocals of the denominators first exceeds 2.

Explanation

The behaviour is explained by interpreting the integrals via Fourier transforms and convolutions of box functions: the result is π/2 as long as a certain support condition holds, which breaks exactly at the observed threshold. Related phenomena extend to other families of integrals and have been given a physical interpretation in terms of random walks.
