Algorave

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Algorave (a portmanteau of "algorithm" and "rave") is an event where electronic dance music and visuals are generated live from algorithms, usually using live coding techniques. Performers write and modify code on stage — projected on screens so the audience can watch — using environments such as TidalCycles, SuperCollider, Sonic Pi and Hydra, producing music that ranges from techno and breakcore to ambient.

History

The term was coined around 2011–2012 by British live coders Alex McLean and Nick Collins. Algorave grew out of the live coding and demoscene communities and the TOPLAP collective (founded 2004). Events spread internationally through the 2010s, with algoraves held in clubs, festivals and academic settings in Europe, the Americas, Asia and Australia, and a surge of streamed "virtual algoraves" during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Culture

Algorave events emphasise transparency and participation: the code is part of the performance, mistakes are part of the aesthetic, and the community promotes open-source tools and inclusive, safer dancefloor policies. A frequently repeated motto is that algorave performers "don't have much of a clue what they're doing".
