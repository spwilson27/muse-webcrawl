Æthelstan or Athelstan (c. 894 – 27 October 939) was King of the Anglo-Saxons from 924 to 927 and King of the English from 927 to his death. The son of Edward the Elder and grandson of Alfred the Great, he is regarded as the first king of England.

Reign

Raised at the Mercian court, Æthelstan was accepted as king in Mercia on his father's death in 924 and crowned in 925. In 927 he conquered the Viking kingdom of York, becoming the first Anglo-Saxon ruler of the whole of England, and styled himself in charters “king of all Britain”. He extracted submissions from Welsh and Scottish kings, and in 937 defeated a coalition of Scots, Vikings and Strathclyde Britons at the Battle of Brunanburh, celebrated in an Old English poem.

Government and church

Æthelstan centralised government, issued law codes, controlled coinage, and cultivated a learned court with European connections — his half-sisters married into continental royal families including the Ottonians. A noted relic collector and church patron, he promoted monastic reform that flowered under his successors. He faced down the Vikings who had long raided England.

Death and legacy

Æthelstan died at Gloucester in 939 and was buried at Malmesbury Abbey. Though overshadowed later by Alfred, modern historians rank him among the greatest Anglo-Saxon kings; England briefly fell apart on his death but was reunified by his half-brothers Edmund and Eadred, predecessors of later kings down to the age of William the Conqueror.
