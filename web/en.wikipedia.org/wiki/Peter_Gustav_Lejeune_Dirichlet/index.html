Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Johann Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet (13 February 1805 – 5 May 1859) was a German mathematician who made deep contributions to number theory (including creating the field of analytic number theory), the theory of Fourier series, and other topics in mathematical analysis. He is credited as being one of the first mathematicians to give the modern formal definition of a function.

Biography

Dirichlet was born in Düren, then part of the French Empire, into a family descended from the Belgian town of Richelette — hence the surname "Lejeune Dirichlet" ("le jeune de Richelette", the youngster from Richelette). Educated at the Gymnasium in Bonn and later in Paris, he studied under leading French mathematicians including Fourier and Laplace. After teaching in Paris, he moved to Breslau in 1827, Berlin in 1828, and in 1855 succeeded Carl Friedrich Gauss at Göttingen, where he remained until his death in 1859. He married Rebecka Mendelssohn, sister of the composer Fanny Mendelssohn and Felix Mendelssohn.

Contributions

In 1837 Dirichlet proved his celebrated theorem on prime numbers in arithmetic progressions, introducing Dirichlet characters and L-functions and thereby founding analytic number theory. His unit theorem describes the units of algebraic number fields; the Dirichlet approximation theorem and the pigeonhole principle (often called the Dirichlet box principle) are foundational in approximation theory and combinatorics. He also proved the first case of Fermat's Last Theorem for n = 5 and n = 14 (with Legendre), clarified the theory of quadratic forms, and gave rigorous convergence conditions for Fourier series in 1829.

Function concept and analysis

In an 1837 paper Dirichlet proposed the modern definition of a function: a variable y is a function of x if to every value of x in an interval there corresponds a definite value of y, regardless of whether the correspondence follows a formula. His example of a function equal to one constant on the rationals and another on the irrationals — the Dirichlet function — became a standard test case showing that functions need not be continuous or given by analytic expressions.

Legacy

Many concepts bear his name: the Dirichlet distribution, Dirichlet boundary conditions, the Dirichlet problem, Dirichlet series, Dirichlet convolution, and the Dirichlet kernel. A lunar crater and the asteroid 11665 Dirichlet are named after him.
