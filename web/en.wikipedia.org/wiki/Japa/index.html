Japa

Sanskritजप (japa)
MeaningMuttering, repeated recitation
TraditionsHinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism
AidJapa mala (108 beads)

Japa (Sanskrit: जप) is the meditative repetition of a mantra or a divine name. It is a practice found in Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism, in which the devotee repeats a sacred formula — aloud, in a whisper, or silently in the mind — often counting the repetitions on a string of prayer beads called a japa mala.

Etymology and method

The Sanskrit root jap- means "to utter in a low voice, repeat internally, mutter". Classical texts classify japa by its manner of utterance: vācika (spoken aloud), upāṃśu (whispered, considered more meritorious) and mānasa (mental repetition, held to be the most potent). Repetitions are usually counted in multiples of 108, the number of beads on a standard mala; devout practitioners may perform thousands of repetitions daily. Japa may be accompanied by visualisation of the deity, regulated breathing (pranayama), or ritual posture.

In Hindu traditions

Japa is one of the most widely recommended devotional practices (sadhana) in Hinduism. Famous mantras used include the Gayatri mantra, the Mahamrityunjaya mantra, "Om Namah Shivaya" for Shaivites, and the Hare Krishna maha-mantra in Gaudiya Vaishnavism, where congregational chanting (kirtana) and personal japa on tulsi beads are central. The Bhagavad Gita declares, "of sacrifices, I am the sacrifice of japa" (10.25), ranking it among the highest forms of worship accessible in the Kali Yuga.

In other traditions

In Buddhism, japa-like recitation appears in the repetition of the Buddha's name (nembutsu/nianfo in Pure Land practice) and in Tibetan Buddhist mantra recitation with a mala. In Jainism, the Navkar Mantra is repeated in japa. Sikhism emphasises nam japna, the repetition of the divine Name, as one of the three pillars of the faith. Analogous repetitive prayer exists in other religions, such as the Jesus Prayer in Eastern Christianity and dhikr in Islam.
