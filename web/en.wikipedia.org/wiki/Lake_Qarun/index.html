Lake Qarun

Lake Qarun (Arabic: بحيرة قارون, Buḥayrat Qārūn) is a brackish lake in the Faiyum Depression of Egypt, northwest of the city of Faiyum and about 80 km southwest of Cairo. It is the shrunken remnant of the ancient Lake Moeris, once a vast freshwater lake fed by the Nile, and today lies about 45 metres below sea level.

Geography

The modern lake covers roughly 202–243 km² and is about 40 km long and up to 8.5 km wide. Having no outlet, it loses water only by evaporation; drainage water from the irrigated fields of the Faiyum Oasis, carried by the Bahr Yusuf channel from the Nile, now sustains it. As a result its salinity has risen steadily and is now comparable to or greater than that of seawater, which has transformed its fishery toward salt-tolerant species such as mullet and tilapia stocked from the sea.

History

In the Middle Kingdom, pharaohs of the Twelfth Dynasty, especially Amenemhat III, undertook great hydraulic works in the Faiyum, and the regulated Lake Moeris served as a reservoir for Nile floodwater. Herodotus described the lake and the nearby "Labyrinth" mortuary temple at Hawara. The lake's Arabic name refers to Qarun (Korah), a figure of Quranic tradition said to have been swallowed by the earth with his treasures near this place.

Palaeontology and nature

The desert north of the lake, including the Wadi El Rayan and Wadi al-Hitan (Valley of the Whales) areas, is one of the world's richest Eocene fossil regions, famous for early whale fossils such as Basilosaurus and for fossil forests and the Faiyum primates. The lake itself is designated a protected area and an Important Bird Area, hosting large wintering populations of waterbirds, including flamingos and grebes.
