The Franciscans are a group of related mendicant religious orders within the Catholic Church, founded in 1209 by Saint Francis of Assisi. These orders include the Order of Friars Minor, the Order of Saint Clare, and the Third Order of Saint Francis. The distinctive feature of the Franciscan orders is their emphasis on poverty, humility, and preaching, inspired by the life of their founder, who renounced wealth to live among the poor.

History

Francis of Assisi, born Giovanni di Pietro di Bernardone in 1181 or 1182, was the son of a prosperous cloth merchant. After a religious conversion, he renounced his inheritance and embraced a life of poverty and prayer. In 1209 he gathered a small group of followers and travelled to Rome, where Pope Innocent III gave oral approval to their rule of life. The order grew rapidly across Europe, with friars preaching repentance and ministering to the poor and sick.

After Francis's death in 1226, tensions arose within the order over the interpretation of his rule of absolute poverty. These disputes eventually led to divisions, most notably between the Observants, who sought strict observance of the rule, and the Conventuals, who accepted a more moderate practice. In 1517 Pope Leo X formally separated the two branches. A third branch, the Capuchins, emerged in 1525 as a further reform movement emphasising austerity and solitude.

Branches

  • Order of Friars Minor (OFM) — the principal male branch, known as the Observants.
  • Order of Friars Minor Conventual (OFM Conv.) — a branch that historically held property in common.
  • Order of Friars Minor Capuchin (OFM Cap.) — founded in 1525, distinguished by pointed hoods.
  • Order of Saint Clare (Poor Clares) — the contemplative female branch, founded by Clare of Assisi in 1212.
  • Third Order — for lay people and secular clergy living the Franciscan spirit in the world.

Spirituality and influence

Franciscan spirituality centres on devotion to the humanity of Christ, especially his poverty, humility, and suffering. Franciscans popularised devotions such as the Christmas crib (nativity scene), first set up by Francis at Greccio in 1223, and the Stations of the Cross. The order produced many notable theologians and philosophers, including Bonaventure, Duns Scotus, and William of Ockham, and played a leading role in medieval universities, missions, and pastoral care. Franciscan missionaries travelled as far as China and the Americas. Today Franciscans serve in parishes, schools, hospitals, and social justice ministries around the world.
