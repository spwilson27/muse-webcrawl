Ohnesorge number

The Ohnesorge number (Oh) is a dimensionless number that relates the viscous forces to inertial and surface tension forces. It is named after Wolfgang von Ohnesorge, who introduced it in his 1936 doctoral thesis on the atomization of liquid jets.

The number is defined as:

Oh = μ / √(ρ σ L)

where μ is the dynamic viscosity of the liquid, ρ is its density, σ is the surface tension, and L is a characteristic length scale (typically the jet or droplet diameter).

Relation to other numbers

The Ohnesorge number can be expressed in terms of the Reynolds number (Re) and the Weber number (We):

Oh = √We / Re

Because it contains no velocity, the Ohnesorge number depends only on the fluid properties and the length scale, which makes it especially convenient for characterizing printing, spraying, and droplet formation processes.

Applications

The Ohnesorge number is widely used in inkjet printing, where printable fluids are often said to lie in the range 0.1 < Oh < 1 (equivalently, the inverse number Z = 1/Oh between 1 and 10). It is also used in the study of droplet impact, spray atomization, and the breakup of liquid jets, where it helps predict whether a jet will form uniform droplets or break up irregularly.
