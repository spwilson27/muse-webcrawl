Bar billiards

A bar billiards table with its mushroom skittles.

Bar billiards is a form of billiards that developed in the United Kingdom from the Russian game of bar biliards via the French/Belgian game Billard Russe. It is played on a distinctive small table with no side or corner pockets; instead, holes are set into the bed of the table, guarded by small skittles shaped like mushrooms. The game is traditionally played in pubs, and organised leagues survive mainly in southern and central England, particularly Oxfordshire, Berkshire, Kent and Sussex.

Equipment and play

The table (about 6 feet by 3 feet in traditional form) has nine holes: five across the centre scoring 10, 20, 30, 20 and 10 points, and four behind them scoring higher values, with the central "200 hole" guarded by the black skittle. Four skittles stand on the table — two white, one red and one black guarding the 200 hole. Players strike from one end only (the "D" end), and all shots are played from the same place on the table. Knocking over the black skittle loses the player's entire break and accumulated score for the frame in many rulesets; toppling a white skittle ends the break.

Scoring

Games are timed (historically by a coin-operated mechanism that drops a bar across the holes after a set period, around 15–20 minutes). Points are scored by potting balls in the holes and by cannons (striking both object balls with the cue ball). At the end of the game, the ball left on the table may be played for the 200 or 100 hole as a final shot. The sport's governing body in Britain is the All England Bar Billiards Association, founded in 1932, and county championships and a world championship (held in Jersey for many years) are contested.
