Mothers Against Drunk Driving (MADD) is a nonprofit organization in the United States, Canada and Brazil that seeks to stop drunk driving, support those affected by drunk driving, prevent underage drinking, and strive for stricter impaired-driving policies. Founded in 1980 in Sacramento, California, MADD is credited with reshaping public attitudes and laws concerning driving under the influence in the United States.

History

MADD was founded by Candy Lightner after her 13-year-old daughter, Cari, was killed in Fair Oaks, California, in May 1980 by a drunk driver who was a repeat offender and out on bail for a previous hit-and-run. Outraged by the lenient treatment of impaired drivers, Lightner began organizing other bereaved parents into a grassroots campaign. The group — originally "Mothers Against Drunk Drivers" — grew rapidly into a national movement.

Legislative achievements

MADD lobbied successfully for the National Minimum Drinking Age Act of 1984, which pressured states to raise the legal drinking age to 21. It later campaigned for the adoption of a 0.08 blood alcohol concentration (BAC) limit as the national standard for drunk driving, achieved nationwide by 2005, and for administrative license revocation laws, sobriety checkpoints, ignition interlock devices for convicted offenders, and "no refusal" enforcement policies. Drunk-driving deaths in the United States have fallen by roughly half since MADD's founding, a change to which the organization is widely credited as a major contributor.

Programs

MADD provides free victim services, including emotional support, assistance navigating the criminal justice system, and an annual candlelight vigil. Its prevention campaigns have included "Tie One On for Safety", the "Campaign to Eliminate Drunk Driving", and school programs against underage drinking. In recent years MADD has broadened its mission to include drugged driving and advocacy for passive vehicle alcohol-detection technology, such as the driver alcohol detection system mandated for study and rulemaking under the 2021 Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act.
