CHIPS (payments)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Clearing House Interbank Payments System (CHIPS) is a United States private clearing house and real-time final settlement payment system for large-value wire transfers. Operated by The Clearing House Payments Company, CHIPS is the main system for clearing large US dollar payments made between international banks, and it handles the great majority of US dollar cross-border payments.

CHIPS was established in 1970 by the New York Clearing House Association as an electronic replacement for paper cheque clearing between banks. Unlike Fedwire, which is operated by the Federal Reserve and settles payments gross in real time, CHIPS since 2001 has combined real-time final settlement with multilateral netting, allowing banks to settle very large values with relatively small liquidity balances.

Operation

Participants hold a shared prefunded account at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. Payment orders are matched, queued, and released against available balances throughout the day using a patented liquidity-optimisation algorithm; final settlement occurs when obligations are extinguished against the prefunded balance, making payments final and irrevocable in near real time.

Scale

CHIPS settles on the order of two trillion US dollars per day across several hundred thousand payments — a small fraction of Fedwire's transaction count but with comparable total value, and with far greater liquidity efficiency owing to its netting features.
