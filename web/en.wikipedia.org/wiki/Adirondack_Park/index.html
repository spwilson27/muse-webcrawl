Adirondack Park

The Adirondack Park is a publicly and privately protected area in northeastern New York State, United States, covering about 6.1 million acres (2.5 million hectares) — larger than several US national parks combined.

History

Created by the state in 1892 amid concerns about logging and water supplies, the park's public lands form the Adirondack Forest Preserve, protected as "forever wild" by Article 14 of the New York Constitution (1894). The Adirondack Park Agency (1971) oversees land-use planning for both public and private land.

Geography

The park contains the Adirondack Mountains, including Mount Marcy (the state's highest peak at 1,629 m), over 2,000 miles of trails, hundreds of lakes including Lake Placid and Saranac Lake, and about 130,000 year-round residents in 101 municipalities.
