Llanito

Llanito (also written Yanito) is a form of Andalusian Spanish heavily laced with words from English and other languages, spoken in the British overseas territory of Gibraltar. The word also denotes a native Gibraltarian. Llanito is Gibraltar's vernacular language and an important marker of Gibraltarian identity, though English is the territory's official language and the language of government and education.

Characteristics

Llanito is characterised by constant code-switching between Spanish and English, sometimes within a single sentence. Its vocabulary draws loans from Genoese, Hebrew, Maltese and Portuguese, reflecting Gibraltar's historically mixed population of Genoese, Jewish, Spanish, British and other settlers. Typical features include English nouns embedded in Spanish grammar, Spanish verbs adapted from English (such as wachar, "to watch"), and calques and local words like chuchurrío and faina.

Examples

A classic example is a sentence such as: "El autocar está en el stop, pero hay mucho traffic hoy" mixing Spanish and English. Expressions like "¡No te pongas nervous!" or referring to a kettle as el kettle are typical of everyday speech.

Status and culture

Although Spanish is widely understood, Gibraltarians often assert a distinct identity by speaking Llanito, especially informally. The dialect is celebrated locally in comedy, theatre and on the Gibraltar Broadcasting Corporation, and a Llanito dictionary has been published. Its survival is debated, as younger Gibraltarians increasingly favour English in formal registers, but it remains the everyday speech of many residents of the Rock.
