Clutha River

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Native name: Mata-Au
Location: Otago, South Island, New Zealand
Length: 338 km (210 mi)
Source: Lake Wānaka
Mouth: Pacific Ocean, near Balclutha

The Clutha River (Māori: Mata-Au, "surface current") is the second longest river in New Zealand and the largest by mean discharge. It flows south-southeast for 338 kilometres (210 mi) through Central and South Otago from Lake Wānaka in the Southern Alps to the Pacific Ocean, 75 kilometres south of Dunedin. It is the country's highest-volume river, with a mean flow of about 614 cubic metres per second.

Course

The river begins near the Haast Pass at the head of Lake Wānaka, and is properly called the Makarora River at its uppermost reach. From Lake Wānaka it flows through the Upper Clutha valley, past Cromwell—where it meets its main tributary, the Kawarau River—and through the Cromwell Gorge. It continues through the Roxburgh Gorge and past Alexandra, Roxburgh, and Balclutha, dividing around the island of Inch Clutha before reaching the sea at Molyneux Bay.

Hydroelectric power

Two large hydroelectric dams harness the river: the Clyde Dam (completed 1993), which created Lake Dunstan behind it, and the older Roxburgh Dam (1956). The flooding for Lake Dunstan submerged parts of old Cromwell and orchards in the Cromwell Gorge, a decision that remains controversial.

History

The river valley was a Māori travel and moa-hunting route long before European settlement. Gold was discovered near the Clutha at Gabriel's Gully in 1861, sparking the Otago gold rush; the river and its tributaries were extensively dredged, and the Roxburgh area became famous for stone fruit. The river's bright turquoise colour comes from glacial meltwater filtered through the headwater lakes.
