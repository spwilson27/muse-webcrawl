Park Avenue

Park Avenue
Location: Manhattan and the Bronx, New York City
Length: c. 11 miles (18 km)
Former name: Fourth Avenue
Known for: Grand Central Terminal, luxury residences, corporate headquarters

Park Avenue is a wide boulevard in the New York City boroughs of Manhattan and the Bronx, carrying northbound and southbound traffic in Manhattan. It is known for its landscaped median ("the malls"), its luxury apartment buildings, and the many corporate headquarters and flagship buildings along its length. South of Grand Central Terminal the road continues as Park Avenue South.

History

The street was originally part of Fourth Avenue and carried the tracks of the New York and Harlem Railroad. In the mid-19th century the railroad south of 42nd Street was placed in a tunnel, and the section above it was renamed Park Avenue in 1867 because of the greenery planted over the tracks. Grand Central Terminal, opened in 1913, was made possible by the electrification and covering of the rail yard, which created some of the most valuable real estate in the city. The grand apartment houses and hotels that followed — along with Lever House (1952) and the Seagram Building (1958), landmarks of modernist architecture — made Park Avenue synonymous with wealth and corporate power.

Route

Park Avenue runs from the Bowery (as Park Avenue South begins at Astor Place) north through Midtown and the Upper East Side, tunnels around Grand Central, crosses the Harlem River via the Park Avenue Bridge, and continues into the Bronx to Fordham Road. The Manhattan section's median is planted with seasonal flowers and, each December, illuminated fir trees sponsored by the Fund for Park Avenue.

In culture

"Park Avenue" is used in American English as a byword for affluence, in deliberate contrast to less wealthy districts of the city; the contrast between Park Avenue in Manhattan and the same street in the South Bronx has been a recurring theme in commentary on inequality in New York.
