Lord Kitchener Wants You is a 1914 advertisement by Alfred Leete which was developed into a poster. It depicts Herbert Kitchener, 1st Earl Kitchener, the British Secretary of State for War, pointing at the viewer and calling them to enlist in the British Army to fight for Britain in the First World War. The image first appeared on the cover of the magazine London Opinion on 5 September 1914, with the caption "Your Country Needs YOU". It was later adapted as an official recruitment poster with the text "Lord Kitchener Wants You".

Massively imitated, the poster has become one of the most enduring images of the First World War and one of the most famous posters ever produced. Its composition — a commanding figure, direct eye contact, and a pointing finger aimed at the passer-by — inspired many later designs, most famously the American Uncle Sam "I Want You" poster by James Montgomery Flagg (1917), and imitations in many other countries.

History

Leete's original drawing was produced at the request of Arthur Gunn, advertising manager of London Opinion. In March 1915 the Parliamentary Recruiting Committee issued it as Poster No. 100. Contrary to popular belief, the poster was not the most widely distributed of the war; its fame rests largely on its later iconic status, its countless parodies, and its reproduction on merchandise and in histories of propaganda.

Influence

The poster has been endlessly parodied and adapted for advertising, political campaigns, and satire, with the pointing figure replaced by everyone from politicians to cartoon characters, and the slogan altered to suit new messages — "…wants you" being instantly recognisable even when Kitchener's face is not used.
