Weatherization

Weatherization (American English) or weatherproofing (British English) is the practice of protecting a building and its interior from the elements — particularly sunlight, precipitation, and wind — and of modifying a building to reduce energy consumption and optimize energy efficiency.

Measures

Typical weatherization work begins with an energy audit, including a blower-door test to locate air leaks. Common measures include sealing bypasses and duct leaks, adding attic, wall, and floor insulation, insulating hot water pipes and tanks, installing storm windows or replacing windows, weatherstripping doors, and tuning or replacing inefficient furnaces and air conditioners. These measures lower heating and cooling bills, improve comfort and indoor air quality, and reduce moisture damage.

Programs

In the United States, the Weatherization Assistance Program (WAP), created in 1976 and administered by the Department of Energy, funds state and local agencies to weatherize the homes of low-income households at no cost. The program reports having served more than seven million homes, with average energy savings estimated at several hundred dollars per household per year. Evaluations have found benefit-cost ratios above one when health and safety benefits are included. Weatherization differs from general home maintenance in focusing specifically on the building envelope and energy systems.
