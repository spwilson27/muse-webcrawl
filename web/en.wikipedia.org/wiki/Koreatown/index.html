Koreatown

Korean-language signs in a Koreatown district

A Koreatown (Korean: 코리아타운) is a Korean ethnic enclave found in cities around the world, typically characterised by Korean-owned businesses, restaurants, grocery stores, churches and bilingual signage. Koreatowns developed as Korean immigrant communities formed following waves of emigration, particularly after the Korean War and after changes to United States immigration law in 1965.

Notable Koreatowns

Among the largest and best known are Koreatown in Los Angeles, the biggest in the United States by population; Koreatown in Manhattan, New York City, centred on West 32nd Street; Koreatown in Toronto, along Bloor Street; and districts in cities such as Chicago, Atlanta, Dallas and Sydney. In East Asia, Koreatowns exist in Japanese cities including Osaka's Tsuruhashi and Tokyo's Shin-Ōkubo, formed in part by the Zainichi Korean community.

Culture and economy

Koreatowns are centres of Korean cuisine, with barbecue restaurants, bakeries and markets, as well as karaoke (noraebang), video rental historically, beauty salons and churches, which often serve as community hubs for recent arrivals. They also function as gateways through which Korean popular culture, including K-pop and Korean television drama, reaches wider audiences.
