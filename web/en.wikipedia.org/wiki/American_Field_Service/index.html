The American Field Service (AFS) is a leader in intercultural learning and offers international exchange programs in more than 40 countries around the world through independent, nonprofit AFS organisations, each with its own network of volunteers, professionally staffed offices, and volunteer boards. It originated as a volunteer ambulance service in the First World War.

History

AFS was founded in April 1915 by A. Piatt Andrew, a former U.S. Assistant Secretary of the Treasury teaching at Harvard, who organised American volunteers to drive ambulances for the French Army, based at the American Hospital in Paris. By the end of the war more than 2,500 volunteers had served, evacuating hundreds of thousands of wounded soldiers; notable volunteer drivers included Ernest Hemingway, John Dos Passos, and E. E. Cummings. The service revived in the Second World War, with volunteers serving with Allied forces in Europe, Africa, and Asia.

Exchange programs

In 1946–1947, under director Stephen Galatti, the wartime ambulance drivers transformed AFS into a scholarship exchange program, initially bringing European secondary-school students to the United States to build understanding and prevent future wars. The program expanded into year, semester, and summer exchanges worldwide, placing students with host families and in local schools. Today AFS Intercultural Programs is an international network of partner organisations and holds consultative status with UNESCO.
