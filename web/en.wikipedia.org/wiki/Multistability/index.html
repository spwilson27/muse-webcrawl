Multistability

Multistability is the property of a system that can rest in more than one stable state under the same external conditions, so that its behaviour depends on its history. The simplest case, with two stable states, is called bistability.

In dynamical systems

In mathematics and physics, multistable systems possess several attractors separated by unstable boundaries; small perturbations or different initial conditions determine which state the system settles into, and hysteresis appears when parameters are swept back and forth. Examples include magnetic systems, lasers, and electronic circuits such as flip-flops, which exploit bistability to store one bit of information.

In biology and ecology

Gene regulatory networks can be multistable, letting identical cells adopt different fates. Ecosystems may show alternative stable states — for example, clear versus turbid shallow lakes — with abrupt regime shifts when conditions cross a threshold.

In perception

Perceptual multistability describes ambiguous stimuli, such as the Necker cube or binocular rivalry, where perception alternates spontaneously between competing interpretations although the stimulus does not change.
