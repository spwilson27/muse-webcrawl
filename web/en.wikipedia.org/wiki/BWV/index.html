BWV is the abbreviation of Bach-Werke-Verzeichnis ("Bach Works Catalogue"), the standard catalogue of the compositions of Johann Sebastian Bach, compiled by the German musicologist Wolfgang Schmieder and first published in 1950. A work's BWV number is the normal way of identifying it; for example, the Mass in B minor is BWV 232.

Organisation

Unlike opus numbers, BWV numbers are not chronological. Schmieder arranged the works by genre: cantatas (BWV 1–224), motets (225–231), masses and related liturgical works (232–243), Passions and oratorios (244–249), four-part chorales (250–438), songs and arias (439–524), works for organ (525–771), works for keyboard (772–994), works for lute (995–1000), chamber music and works for solo instruments (1001–1040), orchestral works (1041–1071), and canons and late contrapuntal works (1072–1128). Doubtful and spurious works were placed in an appendix, the BWV Anhang (Anh.). Individual movements of cantatas and other subdivisions are sometimes indicated with lowercase letters, as in BWV 232a.

Revisions

Schmieder's catalogue was prepared alongside the Neue Bach-Ausgabe, the new collected edition of Bach's works. A revised edition, commonly called BWV2a, appeared in 1990, renumbering nothing but reclassifying some works between the main catalogue and the appendix as scholarship advanced. A third edition, BWV3, edited by Christine Blanken, Christoph Wolff, and Peter Wollny, was published in 2022 by the Bach-Archiv Leipzig.

Well-known numbers

Frequently cited numbers include BWV 244 (St Matthew Passion), BWV 248 (Christmas Oratorio), BWV 846–893 (The Well-Tempered Clavier), BWV 1007 (Cello Suite No. 1), BWV 1046–1051 (Brandenburg Concertos), BWV 1068 (Orchestral Suite No. 3, containing the "Air"), and BWV 1080 (The Art of Fugue).
