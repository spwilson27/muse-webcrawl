Protected areas of China

China has an extensive system of protected areas, including nature reserves, forest parks, scenic areas, geological parks and, since reforms begun in 2015, a unified system of national parks. Protected areas cover roughly a fifth of China's land territory when all categories are counted.

History

China's first nature reserve, at Dinghu Mountain in Guangdong, was established in 1956. The network grew rapidly from the 1980s; by the late 2010s there were more than 2,700 nature reserves at national, provincial and local levels, covering about 15 per cent of the land area.

National park system

Following pilot schemes launched in 2015, China formally designated its first five national parks in October 2021: Sanjiangyuan (source of the Yangtze, Yellow and Lancang rivers), Giant Panda National Park, Northeast China Tiger and Leopard National Park, Hainan Tropical Rainforest National Park and Wuyishan National Park. The system aims to consolidate overlapping designations under a single management authority.

Notable areas

Famous reserves include Wolong and other Sichuan sanctuaries for the giant panda, Changbaishan on the Korean border, Jiuzhaigou, and the vast Chang Tang reserve on the Tibetan Plateau, home to chiru (Tibetan antelope) and wild yak. China also has numerous UNESCO Biosphere Reserves and World Heritage natural sites.
