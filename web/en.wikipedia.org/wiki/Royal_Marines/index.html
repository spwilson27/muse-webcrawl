The Royal Marines are the United Kingdom's amphibious special operations capable commando force, one of the five fighting arms of the Royal Navy. Formed in 1664 as the Duke of York and Albany's Maritime Regiment of Foot, they provide the UK's standing expeditionary and littoral strike capability and specialise in operating in extreme environments, including arctic and mountain warfare. Since 1943 Royal Marines have served as commandos, and all ranks complete the gruelling Commando Course at Lympstone to earn the green beret.

Organisation

The core of the Corps is UK Commando Force (formerly 3 Commando Brigade), comprising 40 Commando, 42 Commando and 45 Commando, together with 30 Commando (information exploitation), 43 Commando (fleet protection), 47 Commando (raiding), the Commando Logistic Regiment, artillery, engineer and air defence support, and the Special Boat Service (SBS), the UK's maritime special forces unit, drawn mainly from the Royal Marines.

History

Marines fought in virtually every British naval campaign, from Gibraltar (1704) and Trafalgar to Gallipoli, the Zeebrugge Raid (1918) and the Normandy landings. In 1982 Royal Marines formed the bulk of the land force that retook the Falkland Islands, famously "yomping" across East Falkland to Port Stanley. They have since served in Iraq, Sierra Leone and Afghanistan, and now form the core of the UK's Littoral Response Groups under the Future Commando Force programme.

Traditions

The Corps' cap badge combines the Globe and Laurel — reflecting worldwide service, granted after the capture of Belle Île in 1761 — with the Lion and Crown; its single battle honour is "Gibraltar". The quick march is "A Life on the Ocean Wave".
