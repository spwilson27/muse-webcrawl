The History of the Adventures of Joseph Andrews and of his Friend Mr. Abraham Adams, commonly known as Joseph Andrews, is a comic novel by English author Henry Fielding, published in February 1742. It is widely regarded as one of the first novels in English literature.

Origins as parody

The novel began as a parody of Samuel Richardson's bestselling epistolary novel Pamela; or, Virtue Rewarded (1740). Fielding had already attacked Richardson anonymously in Shamela (1741). In Joseph Andrews, the hero is the virtuous brother of Pamela: Joseph, a handsome footman, resists the amorous advances of his widowed mistress, Lady Booby, reversing Richardson's plot of a servant girl pursued by her master. After being dismissed, Joseph sets out on foot for London and then homeward, and the narrative quickly outgrows its parodic frame.

Plot

On the road, Joseph is robbed and beaten, then joined by his old mentor, the absent-minded and benevolent Parson Abraham Adams, and by the chambermaid Fanny Goodwill, whom Joseph loves. The trio's journey across the English countryside becomes a sequence of comic episodes in inns, country houses, and courtrooms, exposing the vanity, hypocrisy, and selfishness of the age. The plot is resolved through revelations about Joseph's and Fanny's parentage, allowing their marriage.

The "comic epic in prose"

In his preface, Fielding famously defined the work as a "comic epic poem in prose," distinguishing his fiction from both romance and burlesque and laying early theoretical foundations for the English novel. The novel's intrusive narrator, mock-heroic episodes, and moral interest in charity and good nature anticipate Fielding's masterpiece Tom Jones (1749).

Adaptations

The novel was adapted for the stage several times and filmed in 1977 as Joseph Andrews, directed by Tony Richardson.
