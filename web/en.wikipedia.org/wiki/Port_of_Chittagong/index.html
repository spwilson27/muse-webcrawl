Port of Chittagong

The Port of Chittagong (Chattogram Port) is the principal seaport of Bangladesh, located on the banks of the Karnaphuli River near its mouth on the Bay of Bengal. It handles more than 90 percent of the country's import and export trade and is one of the busiest ports in South Asia.

History

The anchorage at Chittagong has been used since ancient times and was visited by Arab, Persian, Chinese and European traders; the Portuguese established a settlement in the sixteenth century. The modern port was constituted under British administration in 1887 and expanded steadily, developing general-cargo jetties and, in the 2000s, the Chittagong Container Terminal and New Mooring Container Terminal.

Operations

The port handles several million TEU of containers annually, together with bulk cargo such as grain, fertiliser, cement clinker and petroleum products. It has long suffered from congestion and limited draught, prompting projects for additional terminals at Patiya and a deep-sea port at Matarbari.
