Google Glass

Google Glass is a line of smart glasses developed by Google (initially by its X research division) that displays information in front of the wearer's right eye through a small prism projector, controlled by voice commands, a touchpad on the frame, and head movements. First sold to developers and "Glass Explorers" in 2013 at US$1,500, the consumer version was withdrawn in 2015 amid privacy controversy; an Enterprise Edition aimed at workplaces followed in 2017 and 2019 before the line was discontinued in 2023.

Development and release

Glass was unveiled in 2012 with a promotional skydiving demonstration at Google I/O. The Explorer programme sold units to developers and selected applicants; a one-day public sale followed in 2014. The device ran a stripped-down Android, showing "timeline cards" for search, navigation, photos, messages and third-party "Glassware" apps, and was activated with the phrase "OK Glass".

Reception and privacy controversy

Glass drew intense criticism over privacy: its camera could record inconspicuously, prompting bans in some cinemas, casinos and bars, and the pejorative nickname "Glasshole" for inconsiderate users. Concerns also covered safety while driving, price and limited battery life. Google ended the Explorer programme in January 2015 and moved the project out of X.

Enterprise editions and legacy

Glass found a second life in manufacturing, logistics and medicine, where hands-free information has clear value. The Glass Enterprise Edition (2017) and Edition 2 (2019) improved performance and camera; Google ended sales in March 2023. Despite its commercial failure, Glass is credited with pioneering consumer augmented-reality wearables and shaping debates about cameras in public space.
