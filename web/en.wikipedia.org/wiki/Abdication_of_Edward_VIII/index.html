The abdication of Edward VIII was a constitutional crisis in the British Empire in 1936, in which King Edward VIII renounced the throne in order to marry Wallis Simpson, an American socialite who was divorced from her first husband and divorcing her second. Edward signed the Instrument of Abdication on 10 December 1936, Parliament enacted it the following day, and his brother Albert, Duke of York, succeeded him as George VI.

Background

Edward succeeded his father George V in January 1936. His relationship with Wallis Simpson was known to the British government and press but kept from the wider public. As Supreme Governor of the Church of England — which at the time opposed the remarriage of divorced people whose former spouses were still living — the King's position made the marriage constitutionally and religiously untenable in the eyes of Prime Minister Stanley Baldwin and the governments of the Dominions.

The crisis

In November 1936 Edward told Baldwin he intended to marry Simpson. Baldwin replied that the marriage would be unacceptable to the government and the Dominion governments; a morganatic marriage, under which Simpson would not become queen, was also rejected. Faced with the choice between the Crown and the marriage, Edward chose abdication. The crisis became public in early December, prompting frenzied press coverage and public debate.

Abdication and aftermath

On the evening of 11 December, the former king broadcast to the nation, explaining that he had found it impossible to carry the burden of kingship "without the help and support of the woman I love." He was created Duke of Windsor by George VI and married Simpson in France in June 1937. The couple lived mostly in exile in France; Edward's wartime posting was as Governor of the Bahamas. He remains the only British monarch to have voluntarily renounced the throne, and his reign of 326 days is among the shortest in British history.
