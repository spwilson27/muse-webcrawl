Benzoic acid is an organic compound with the formula C6H5COOH — the simplest aromatic carboxylic acid, consisting of a benzene ring attached to a carboxyl group. It is a colourless crystalline solid, slightly soluble in water and more soluble in hot water, that occurs naturally in many plants, notably in cranberries, prunes and cinnamon, and was first isolated from gum benzoin in the sixteenth century.

Production

Industrially, benzoic acid is produced mainly by the liquid-phase oxidation of toluene with air, using cobalt or manganese catalysts. Older routes included the partial hydrolysis of benzotrichloride and the decarboxylation of phthalic anhydride.

Uses

Benzoic acid and its salts — sodium benzoate (E211), potassium benzoate (E212) and calcium benzoate (E213) — are among the most widely used food preservatives, inhibiting moulds, yeast and many bacteria in acidic foods such as soft drinks, fruit juices, pickles and jams. The acid is also a precursor to benzoyl chloride, plasticisers such as benzoate esters, phenol (via oxidative decarboxylation), and sodium benzoate used in fireworks (whistling compositions) and in the treatment of urea-cycle disorders. In medicine, benzoic acid is a component of Whitfield's ointment for fungal skin infections.

Safety

Benzoic acid is of low acute toxicity and is metabolised in the liver to hippuric acid, which is excreted in urine. Regulatory bodies set an acceptable daily intake of up to 5 mg per kg of body weight. In soft drinks containing both benzoates and ascorbic acid, trace amounts of benzene can form under heat and light, which led to reformulation and monitoring programmes.

History

Benzoic acid was described by Nostradamus and others in the 1500s as a sublimate of gum benzoin; Scheele isolated it in 1775, and in 1832 Liebig and Wöhler determined its composition, work central to the development of structural organic chemistry.
