Lyme Regis is a town in west Dorset, England, on the English Channel coast at the Dorset–Devon border. Nicknamed the "Pearl of Dorset", it is renowned for the fossils found in its cliffs and beaches — part of the Jurassic Coast World Heritage Site — and for its harbour wall, the Cobb.

History

The town appears in the Domesday Book (1086) as a salt-making and fishing settlement; the suffix "Regis" was added after Edward I granted a royal charter in 1284. The Cobb, its artificial harbour, dates to at least the fourteenth century and was repeatedly rebuilt after storms. In 1685 the Duke of Monmouth landed at Lyme to begin his rebellion against James II. The town later featured in Jane Austen's novel Persuasion and in John Fowles's The French Lieutenant's Woman, whose film adaptation made the Cobb famous.

Fossils and Mary Anning

The Blue Lias cliffs around Lyme are rich in Jurassic marine fossils. In the early nineteenth century, local fossil collector Mary Anning made landmark discoveries here, including the first complete Ichthyosaurus skeleton (found with her brother Joseph in 1811–12), the first Plesiosaurus (1823), and an early pterosaur. Her work helped found modern palaeontology, and fossil hunting remains central to the town's identity and tourism.
