Lonicera is a genus of flowering plants in the honeysuckle family, Caprifoliaceae, comprising roughly 180 species of shrubs and twining vines native to the Northern Hemisphere, from North America and Europe to Asia. The genus is named after the German herbalist Adam Lonicer.

Description

Honeysuckles are arching shrubs or twining bines growing 1–10 metres tall. Their leaves are opposite, simple and oval, and most species bear sweetly scented, bilaterally symmetrical tubular flowers, often in pairs, followed by red, blue or black berries. Many species are deciduous, though some in warmer climates are evergreen.

Cultivation and ecology

Species such as Lonicera periclymenum (common honeysuckle or woodbine) and Lonicera japonica (Japanese honeysuckle) are widely grown as ornamental climbers for their fragrance. The flowers are pollinated by hawkmoths and bumblebees and are an important nectar source. Some introduced species, notably Japanese honeysuckle and Amur honeysuckle (L. maackii), have become invasive in North America and elsewhere. The berries of most species are mildly toxic to humans.
