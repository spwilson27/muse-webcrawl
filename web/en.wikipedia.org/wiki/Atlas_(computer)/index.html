Atlas (computer)

The Atlas was a joint development between the University of Manchester, Ferranti, and Plessey. First installed at Manchester University and officially commissioned in 1962, it was one of the world's first supercomputers, considered the most powerful computer in the world at that time. It pioneered many hardware and software concepts still in use today, including virtual memory with paging, which it called a "one-level store".

Developer: University of Manchester, Ferranti, Plessey

Release date: 1962

Units sold: 3 (plus Atlas 2 derivatives)

Predecessor: Manchester Mark 1 / Mercury

History

The project was led by Tom Kilburn at Manchester, building on experience with the Manchester Mark 1 and the Megacycle Machine (later the Mercury). The first Atlas was switched off in 1971; two others were installed, at the University of London and at the Atlas Computer Laboratory at Chilton near Oxford, which served as a national computing facility.

Hardware

Atlas used discrete germanium transistors, a 48-bit word length, and a core store of 16,384 words supplemented by a magnetic drum. Its virtual memory scheme combined core store and drum into a single address space of 220 words, with automatic page transfers handled by the hardware and the Supervisor program. The machine executed roughly 590,000 instructions per second.

Software

The Atlas Supervisor is regarded as one of the first recognizable modern operating systems: it scheduled jobs, allocated memory and peripherals, and handled interrupts, allowing the machine to run many programs nearly simultaneously. Programming languages included Atlas Autocode, ALGOL 60, and Fortran.

Legacy

Atlas introduced concepts—paging, virtual memory, spooling, interrupts, and an operating system "supervisor"—that became standard in computing. In 2022, the Atlas was commemorated on the 60th anniversary of its inauguration.
