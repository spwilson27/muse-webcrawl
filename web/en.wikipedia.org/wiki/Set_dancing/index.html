Set dancing

Set dancing is a form of Irish folk dance performed by four couples arranged in a square, or "set". It is distinct from céilí dancing: set dances are derived largely from French quadrilles brought to Ireland in the nineteenth century and adapted to Irish traditional music, whereas céilí dances derive from older native traditions and later formalisation.

Structure

Each named set — such as the Clare Lancers, the Caledonian, or the Connemara set — consists of several figures, typically three to six, danced to reels, jigs, polkas and hornpipes. Within a set, the couples are designated first and second top couples and first and second side couples, and movements are performed in a prescribed sequence led by these roles.

History and revival

Set dancing was the dominant form of social dance in rural Ireland through the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, but declined sharply in the mid-twentieth century under pressure from the Gaelic League's promotion of céilí dance and from Church disapproval of house dances. A major revival began in the 1980s, driven by teachers such as Connie Ryan, and set dancing is now enjoyed at workshops, festivals and social dances (seisiúns) throughout Ireland and the Irish diaspora.
