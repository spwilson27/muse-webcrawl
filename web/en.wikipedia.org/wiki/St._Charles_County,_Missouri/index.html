St. Charles County, Missouri

St. Charles County is located in the eastern part of the U.S. state of Missouri. As of the 2020 census, the population was approximately 405,000, making it one of the most populous counties in the state and one of its fastest-growing. The county seat is St. Charles. The county lies north of the Missouri River from St. Louis County and is part of the St. Louis metropolitan area.

History

St. Charles County was organized in 1812 and named after Saint Charles Borromeo, an Italian cardinal. The city of St. Charles served as Missouri's first state capital from 1821 to 1826. The county's early history is closely tied to French and Spanish colonial settlement and to the westward expansion of the United States; Daniel Boone settled in the county's Femme Osage district in his later years, and the Boone Home is now a historic site.

Geography

The county is bordered by the Mississippi River to the east, the Missouri River to the south, and the confluence of the two great rivers lies just beyond its southeastern corner. Its landscape ranges from river floodplains to rolling uplands in the west. Major communities include St. Charles, St. Peters, O'Fallon, Wentzville, and Lake St. Louis; O'Fallon and Wentzville rank among Missouri's fastest-growing cities.

Economy and transport

The county has a diversified economy including manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and retail. It is served by Interstates 70 and 64, US Route 61, and the Missouri River bridges connecting it to St. Louis County. The Spirit of St. Louis Airport and the nearby Lambert–St. Louis International Airport serve the region.
