Print on demand

Print on demand (POD) is a printing technology and business process in which book copies (or other documents) are not printed until the company receives an order, allowing prints of single copies or small quantities. While print-on-demand has also been used for other printed goods, the term is most closely associated with book publishing.

History

Print on demand became feasible with the development of high-quality digital printing in the 1990s. Lightning Source, founded in 1997 by the Ingram book distribution group, was a pioneer, enabling publishers to keep titles "in print" indefinitely without warehousing stock. Amazon's CreateSpace (now part of Kindle Direct Publishing) and Lulu brought POD services directly to self-publishing authors in the 2000s.

Advantages and disadvantages

POD eliminates the costs and risks of large print runs and inventory, lets backlist and niche titles remain available, and reduces waste from unsold copies. Per-unit printing costs are higher than offset printing, design options (trim sizes, paper, binding) may be restricted, and print quality historically lagged behind offset, though the gap has narrowed substantially.

Beyond books

The same model is applied to merchandise: t-shirts, mugs, posters, and other products printed to order by services integrated with online storefronts, enabling sellers to offer designs without holding inventory.
