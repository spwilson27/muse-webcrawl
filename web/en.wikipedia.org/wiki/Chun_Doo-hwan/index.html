Chun Doo-hwan

Chun Doo-hwan (전두환)
Born: 18 January 1931, Hapcheon, Korea
Died: 23 November 2021, Seoul, South Korea
President of South Korea: 1980–1988

Chun Doo-hwan (Korean: 전두환; 18 January 1931 – 23 November 2021) was a South Korean politician, army general, and military dictator who ruled South Korea from 1980 to 1988. He seized power in a military coup following the assassination of President Park Chung-hee in 1979.

Rise to power

A graduate of the Korea Military Academy, Chun built a powerful faction within the army known as Hanahoe. After Park's assassination on 26 October 1979, Chun, as head of the Defense Security Command, led the investigation and then staged the coup of December 1979, purging rivals. In May 1980 his regime extended martial law, closed universities, and suppressed protests.

Gwangju Uprising

Chun's rule is most notoriously associated with the Gwangju Uprising of May 1980, in which soldiers violently suppressed pro-democracy demonstrations in the city of Gwangju, killing hundreds of civilians by most scholarly estimates. The event remains deeply traumatic in modern Korean history and central to assessments of his presidency.

Presidency and aftermath

As president, Chun oversaw continued export-led economic growth and Seoul's successful bid for the 1988 Summer Olympics, but ruled through an authoritarian constitution. Mass protests in June 1987 forced democratization, and he stepped down in 1988 in favour of his ally Roh Tae-woo, the first directly elected president.

In 1996, Chun was convicted of treason and corruption and sentenced to death, later commuted to life imprisonment; he was pardoned in 1997 by President Kim Young-sam on the advice of president-elect Kim Dae-jung. He was also ordered to pay massive fines for amassing a fortune in bribes. Chun never apologized for Gwangju and died in 2021 without receiving a state funeral.
