Battle of Pydna

The Battle of Pydna was fought on 22 June 168 BC near Pydna in Macedonia, between the Roman Republic under the consul Lucius Aemilius Paullus and King Perseus of Macedon. The Roman victory ended the Third Macedonian War and the Antigonid dynasty, and is a classic demonstration of the Roman manipular legion's superiority over the Macedonian phalanx on broken ground.

The battle

The Macedonian phalanx initially drove the Romans back up a slope, but as it advanced over uneven ground its formation opened gaps. Roman maniples infiltrated the phalanx, where the long sarissa pikes were useless and Roman swordsmen slaughtered the pikemen at close quarters. The Macedonian cavalry and Perseus fled the field early. In about an hour the Macedonian army was destroyed.

Aftermath

Perseus was captured and displayed in Paullus's triumph at Rome. Macedonia was divided into four client republics and later annexed as a province. Pydna marked the effective end of the Hellenistic successor kingdoms as military rivals of Rome in the eastern Mediterranean.
