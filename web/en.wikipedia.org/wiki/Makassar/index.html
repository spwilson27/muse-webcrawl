Makassar

Makassar, formerly known as Ujung Pandang, is the capital of the Indonesian province of South Sulawesi and the largest city in eastern Indonesia. Situated on the southwest coast of the island of Sulawesi, facing the Makassar Strait, it is a major port and trading centre with a metropolitan population of several million people.

History

Makassar rose to prominence in the 16th and 17th centuries as the centre of the Sultanate of Gowa, a powerful maritime trading state whose port was a major entrepôt for the spice trade. The Dutch East India Company captured the fortress of Somba Opu in 1669 after the Makassar War, establishing Fort Rotterdam, which still stands in the city. The city was officially renamed Ujung Pandang in 1971 and reverted to Makassar in 1999.

Geography and economy

The city is a gateway to eastern Indonesia, with the Port of Makassar and Sultan Hasanuddin International Airport serving as major transport hubs. Landmarks include Losari Beach, Fort Rotterdam, and the floating mosque Amirul Mukminin. The local cuisine is known for dishes such as coto Makassar and konro. The predominant ethnic groups are the Makassarese and Bugis peoples.
