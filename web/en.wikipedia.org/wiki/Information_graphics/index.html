Infographic

Information graphics or infographics are visual representations of information, data, or knowledge intended to present complex material quickly and clearly. They combine charts, diagrams, maps, icons, illustrations, and minimal text so that patterns and comparisons can be grasped at a glance.

History

The roots of the infographic reach back to prehistoric cave paintings and early maps. Scientific visualisation advanced with pioneers such as William Playfair, who invented the bar chart and pie chart in the late 18th century; Florence Nightingale, whose polar-area diagrams dramatised mortality in the Crimean War; and Charles Joseph Minard, whose 1869 flow map of Napoleon's Russian campaign is often called the greatest statistical graphic ever drawn. In the 20th century, Otto Neurath's Isotype pictorial language and newspaper graphics departments — notably at USA Today — popularised visual explanation for mass audiences. Edward Tufte's books on information design, beginning with The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (1983), set modern standards of clarity and "data-ink" economy.

Types and uses

Common types include statistical infographics, timelines, process diagrams, geographic and map-based graphics, comparisons, and hierarchical charts. Infographics are used in journalism, education, science communication, government reports, marketing, and social media, where their visual format makes them highly shareable. Critics note that decorative design can distort data, and that simplified graphics may oversimplify complex subjects.
