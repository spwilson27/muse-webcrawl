Cape Breton Highlands National Park

Location: Cape Breton Island, Nova Scotia, Canada
Area: 948 km²
Established: 1936
Governing body: Parks Canada
Highest point: White Hill, 532 m

Cape Breton Highlands National Park is a Canadian national park on northern Cape Breton Island in Nova Scotia. Established in 1936, it was the first national park in Atlantic Canada. The park protects an area of highland plateau, deep river canyons and rugged coastline where the mountains meet the Gulf of St. Lawrence on the west and the Atlantic Ocean on the east.

Geography

The park covers about 948 square kilometres. The Cape Breton Highlands plateau dominates the interior, rising to White Hill, at 532 metres the highest point in Nova Scotia. Rivers have cut steep canyons through the plateau, and the two coasts differ markedly: the western Gulf coast is relatively sheltered, while the eastern Atlantic shore is exposed and rocky.

The famous Cabot Trail, a scenic highway loop of about 298 kilometres around the northern tip of the island, passes through the park and is one of Canada's best-known drives, celebrated especially for its autumn colours.

Ecology

The park lies at the meeting point of three forest regions — Acadian, boreal and taiga — giving it an unusual diversity of plant communities, including old-growth stands and rare arctic-alpine plants on the plateau barrens. Wildlife includes moose, black bear, lynx and bald eagles; North Atlantic right whales and pilot whales are sometimes seen offshore. The Atlantic salmon spawn in the park's rivers. The Canada lynx was reintroduced to the park in the 2000s.

Recreation

The park offers 26 hiking trails, ranging from the short Skyline Trail — known for sunset views over the Gulf and frequent moose sightings — to backcountry routes in the interior. There are several Parks Canada campgrounds, freshwater and saltwater beaches, and the Highlands Links golf course at Ingonish, designed by Stanley Thompson and consistently ranked among Canada's finest.

Acadian fishing villages such as Chéticamp on the west coast and Neil's Harbour on the east lie just outside the park boundaries and form part of its cultural landscape.
