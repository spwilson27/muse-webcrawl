Esports in South Korea

Esports in South Korea developed earlier and more extensively than anywhere else in the world, making the country widely regarded as the birthplace and historic centre of professional competitive gaming. Professional leagues have been broadcast on dedicated television channels since around 2000, top players enjoy celebrity status, and the scene is overseen by the Korea e-Sports Association (KeSPA), founded in 2000 with government support.

Origins

Several factors converged in the late 1990s: the 1997 Asian financial crisis pushed young people toward inexpensive entertainment; a government ban on Japanese cultural imports limited console gaming; and massive investment in broadband infrastructure made the PC bang (gaming café) ubiquitous. The release of StarCraft in 1998 was transformative — televised leagues such as the OnGameNet Starleague and MBCGame StarCraft League turned players like Lim "BoxeR" Yo-hwan into national celebrities.

StarCraft and Brood War era

Through the 2000s, professional Brood War teams were sponsored by major conglomerates such as Samsung, SK Telecom and KT. The era produced legendary rivalries and stars including Lee "Flash" Young-ho and Kim "Bisu" Taek-yong, and culminated for many fans in sold-out stadium finals. A 2010 match-fixing scandal involving the player Ma Jae-yoon badly damaged the scene's reputation.

League of Legends era

From 2012, League of Legends became dominant through the LCK (League of Legends Champions Korea). Korean teams won five consecutive World Championships from 2013 to 2017, and Lee "Faker" Sang-hyeok became the most famous esports player in the world. South Korea also achieved major success in Overwatch, winning the first two Overwatch World Cups. Esports was a demonstration event at the 2018 Asian Games in Jakarta, where South Korea won gold in League of Legends, and a full medal event at the 2022 Asian Games, where the Korean team again won gold.

Culture and issues

Esports is embedded in Korean popular culture, with dedicated arenas such as LoL Park in Seoul. The industry has also faced scrutiny over gruelling practice schedules, player welfare, gaming addiction debates and the short career spans of professionals.
