Probability density functions of the Rayleigh distribution for several σ.

In probability theory and statistics, the Rayleigh distribution is a continuous probability distribution for nonnegative-valued random variables. It arises as the distribution of the magnitude of a two-dimensional vector whose components are independent, identically distributed normal random variables with zero mean — for example, the distance from the origin of a point whose coordinates are Gaussian, or the amplitude of a signal with Gaussian noise in each quadrature.

Properties

The probability density function is f(x; σ) = (x/σ²) exp(−x²/(2σ²)) for x ≥ 0. The mean is σ√(π/2) ≈ 1.253σ, the mode is σ, and the variance is (4−π)/2 · σ². The Rayleigh distribution is a special case of the chi distribution with two degrees of freedom and is closely related to the Weibull distribution.

Applications

It is used to model wind speeds, wave heights in oceanography, the distribution of target impact distances, fading amplitudes in wireless communications (Rayleigh fading), and lifetimes in reliability analysis.
