Cosmetic surgery

[Image: Cosmetic surgery]

Cosmetic surgery is a subspecialty of plastic surgery concerned with elective procedures that alter or enhance a person's appearance. Unlike reconstructive surgery, which restores function or a normal appearance after injury, disease, or congenital conditions, cosmetic surgery is performed on otherwise healthy tissue primarily for aesthetic reasons.

Common procedures

The most commonly performed cosmetic surgical procedures include breast augmentation, liposuction, rhinoplasty (nose reshaping), blepharoplasty (eyelid surgery), abdominoplasty (tummy tuck), facelifts (rhytidectomy), and hair transplantation. Minimally invasive cosmetic treatments, such as botulinum toxin injections and dermal fillers, are often grouped with cosmetic surgery in industry statistics and are even more numerous.

History

Reconstructive techniques date back to ancient India, where Sushruta described nasal reconstruction, and developed substantially during the First and Second World Wars. Aesthetic surgery emerged as a distinct field in the early twentieth century, with pioneers such as John Peter Mettauer and Jacques Joseph. The introduction of silicone implants, liposuction in the 1970s, and laser techniques greatly expanded its scope and popularity.

Psychological aspects

Most patients report satisfaction after cosmetic surgery, but clinicians screen for conditions such as body dysmorphic disorder, in which surgery is unlikely to relieve distress. Unrealistic expectations and repeated procedures are recognised risks, and ethical guidelines recommend psychological assessment in some cases.
