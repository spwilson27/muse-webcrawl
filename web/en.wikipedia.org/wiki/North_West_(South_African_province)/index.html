North West (South African province)

North West (Tswana: Bokone Bophirima) is one of the nine provinces of South Africa. It was created after the end of apartheid in 1994 from parts of the former Cape Province and Transvaal, together with most of the territory of the former Bantustan of Bophuthatswana. Its capital is Mahikeng (formerly Mafikeng), while its largest city is Rustenburg.

Geography

The province lies on a flat inland plateau in the north-west of the country, bordering Botswana to the north and the provinces of Limpopo, Gauteng, Free State, and Northern Cape. The southern boundary is formed by the Vaal River. The Magaliesberg mountain range runs through the south-east of the province, and the Pilanesberg, an ancient volcanic complex, is a major game reserve. The climate is semi-arid, with hot summers and mild, dry winters.

Economy

Mining is the backbone of the provincial economy: the Bushveld Igneous Complex around Rustenburg hosts the world's richest platinum deposits, and gold is mined around Klerksdorp and Orkney. Agriculture is dominated by maize, sunflower seed, and cattle ranching; the province is sometimes called the "Texas of South Africa" for its ranching country. Tourism centres on the Sun City resort and the Pilanesberg National Park. See also provinces of South Africa, Rustenburg, and Mahikeng.
