The Central Bank of the United Arab Emirates (CBUAE; Arabic: مصرف الإمارات العربية المتحدة المركزي) is the central bank of the United Arab Emirates, headquartered in Abu Dhabi. It is responsible for monetary policy, the stability of the dirham, supervision of banks and other financial institutions, and management of the country's foreign reserves.

History

Before federation, the region used currencies including the Gulf rupee, the Saudi riyal, and the Qatar and Dubai riyal. A UAE Currency Board was created in 1973 and issued the dirham, but it lacked full central banking powers. The Central Bank was established under Union Law No. 10 of 1980, absorbing the Currency Board. Its mandate was comprehensively revised by Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2018, which strengthened its responsibility for financial stability, consumer protection, and combating financial crime, and it later took over regulation of the insurance sector from the former Insurance Authority.

Functions

The Central Bank issues the dirham, which has been pegged to the US dollar at 3.6725 dirhams since 1997; consequently UAE monetary conditions closely follow those of the US Federal Reserve. It licenses and supervises banks, finance companies, exchange houses, and payment systems, sets reserve and liquidity requirements, and acts as lender of last resort. It also manages the UAE's official reserves and operates the national payment infrastructure, and it maintains the Al Etihad Credit Bureau framework.

Organisation

The bank is headed by a governor and a board of directors appointed by federal decree. It maintains branches and cash centres across the emirates and a museum of currency at its Abu Dhabi headquarters.
