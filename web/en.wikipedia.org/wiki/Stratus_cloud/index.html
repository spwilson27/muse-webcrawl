Stratus cloud

AbbreviationSt
GenusStratus (layered)
Altitude0–2,000 m (surface to 6,500 ft)
AppearanceHorizontal, sheet-like, grey
PrecipitationDrizzle, light snow, ice prisms

Stratus clouds are low-level clouds characterised by horizontal layering with a uniform base, giving them their name from the Latin stratus, meaning layered. They form a dull grey, featureless sheet that can cover the whole sky, and when they form at ground level they are known as fog.

Formation

Stratus forms when a layer of air is cooled to its dew point or moistened until saturation, often under a temperature inversion that prevents vertical development. Advection of warm, moist air over a cooler surface, or the lowering of a cloud base, commonly produces stratus decks.

Weather

Stratus typically brings overcast, gloomy conditions and may produce light drizzle, snow grains or ice prisms, but not heavy rain. Thick, precipitating stratus is classified as nimbostratus, a separate mid-level genus. Stratus fractus are ragged shreds of stratus, often seen beneath rain clouds.
