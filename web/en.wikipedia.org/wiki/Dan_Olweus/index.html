Dan Olweus (18 April 1931 – 20 September 2020) was a Swedish-born Norwegian psychologist. He is widely regarded as a pioneer of research on bullying and victimisation among school children, and the developer of the Olweus Bullying Prevention Program (OBPP), one of the most widely used anti-bullying interventions in the world.

Career

Olweus began studying aggression and bullying among boys in Stockholm in the late 1960s and published his landmark work Aggression in the Schools: Bullies and Whipping Boys in 1978 (Swedish edition 1973). In 1983, following the suicides of three Norwegian boys linked to bullying, the Norwegian Ministry of Education launched a nationwide campaign, and Olweus led the accompanying large-scale intervention study in Bergen. The study reported reductions of roughly 50 percent in bully/victim problems, attracting worldwide attention.

Definition and the Olweus Bullying Prevention Program

Olweus defined bullying as aggressive behaviour characterised by three criteria: intentional harm-doing, repetition over time, and an imbalance of power between perpetrator and victim. His school-based program operates at school, classroom, individual and community levels, restructuring the school environment to reduce opportunities and rewards for bullying. Long based at the University of Bergen, Olweus received numerous honours, including the American Psychological Association's Award for Distinguished Contributions to the International Advancement of Psychology (2011).
