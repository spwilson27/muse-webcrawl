The Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists is a nonprofit media organisation addressing global security issues, best known as the keeper of the Doomsday Clock. Founded in 1945 in Chicago by former Manhattan Project scientists — principally biophysicist Eugene Rabinowitch and physicist Hyman Goldsmith, with the support of figures such as Albert Einstein and J. Robert Oppenheimer — the Bulletin began as a newsletter informing scientists and the public about the dangers of nuclear weapons in the aftermath of the bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

The Doomsday Clock

In 1947 the Bulletin introduced the Doomsday Clock on its cover, designed by artist Martyl Langsdorf: a symbolic clock face whose hands show how close, in the judgement of the Bulletin's Science and Security Board, humanity stands to catastrophe ("midnight"). Originally set at seven minutes to midnight, the clock has been moved backward and forward more than twenty times in response to nuclear developments, arms control agreements, and, since 2007, climate change and disruptive technologies. In January 2023 the clock was set at 90 seconds to midnight — the closest it had ever been — citing Russia's invasion of Ukraine and nuclear threats; in January 2025 it was moved to 89 seconds.

Publication

What began as a mimeographed newsletter became a magazine and then, in the 2010s, a primarily digital publication with a bimonthly print journal published in association with academic publishers. The Bulletin publishes analysis by scientists, policymakers, and journalists on nuclear weapons and disarmament, climate policy, biosecurity, artificial intelligence, and misinformation. Its board has included many Nobel laureates.
