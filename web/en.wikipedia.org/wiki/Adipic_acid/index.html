Adipic acid

IUPAC nameHexanedioic acid
FormulaC6H10O4
Molar mass146.14 g/mol
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid
Melting point152.1 °C

Adipic acid, or hexanedioic acid, is the organic compound with the formula (CH2)4(CO2H)2. From an industrial perspective, it is the most important dicarboxylic acid: millions of tonnes are produced annually, mainly as a precursor to the production of nylon 6,6. It occurs rarely in nature, but is known as the food additive E355.

Production

Adipic acid is produced industrially from a cyclohexane/cyclohexanone mixture ("KA oil", for ketone-alcohol oil), which is oxidized with nitric acid. The process releases nitrous oxide (N2O), a potent greenhouse gas, and manufacturers have installed abatement technology to reduce these emissions. Alternative "greener" routes, including biocatalytic production from renewable feedstocks, have been researched.

Uses

The dominant use of adipic acid is in the manufacture of nylon 6,6: it is polymerized with hexamethylenediamine to form the polyamide used in fibers, carpets, engineering plastics, and tire cord. It is also used in polyurethanes, plasticizers, adhesives, and as an acidulant in foods, where it gives a tart taste and is used in gelatins and baking powders.

Safety

Adipic acid is mildly irritating to skin, eyes, and mucous membranes. It is biodegradable and of low toxicity.
