Mount Hermon


Mount Hermon from the south.

Mount Hermon (Arabic: جبل الشيخ, Jabal al-Shaykh; Hebrew: הַר חֶרְמוֹן, Har Hermon) is a mountain cluster constituting the southern end of the Anti-Lebanon mountain range. Its summit straddles the border between Syria and Lebanon, at 2,814 metres (9,232 ft) above sea level — the highest point in Syria and in the Anti-Lebanon range.

The southern slopes of Mount Hermon extend to the Israeli-occupied Golan Heights, where the Mount Hermon ski resort is located. The mountain's snowmelt feeds the headwaters of the Jordan River, including the springs at Banias and Dan.

History and religion

Hermon has been a sacred mountain since ancient times, with numerous temples on its slopes; it marked the northern boundary of the Israelite territory in the Hebrew Bible and is associated by some traditions with the Transfiguration of Jesus and with the descent of the Watchers in the Book of Enoch. The Druze and other local communities also hold the mountain in reverence.

The summit hosts a United Nations buffer zone (UNDOF) position, among the highest permanently manned UN posts in the world.
