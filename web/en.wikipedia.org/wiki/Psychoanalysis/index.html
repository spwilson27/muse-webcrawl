Psychoanalysis

Psychoanalysis is a set of theories and therapeutic techniques for treating mental disorders by investigating the interaction of conscious and unconscious elements of the mind. It was founded by Sigmund Freud in the 1890s, growing out of his clinical work in Vienna with patients suffering from hysteria and neurosis. Psychoanalytic ideas — the unconscious, repression, transference, the Oedipus complex — profoundly influenced 20th-century psychology, psychiatry, literature and culture, and remain subjects of scientific controversy.

Theory

Freud's mature model divides the psyche into the id (instinctual drives), the ego (which mediates with reality) and the superego (internalised moral standards). Psychological symptoms arise from conflicts among these agencies, especially repressed childhood experiences and wishes. Development passes through oral, anal, phallic, latency and genital psychosexual stages; fixation at a stage was held to shape adult character.

Technique

Classical analysis involves frequent sessions over years, with the patient reclining on a couch and speaking freely (free association) while the analyst interprets dreams, slips and the transference — the patient's redirection of feelings about others onto the analyst. Interpretation aims to bring repressed material into consciousness, dissolving symptoms.

History and schools

Early collaborators Carl Jung and Alfred Adler broke with Freud (1911–1913) to found analytical psychology and individual psychology. Later schools include ego psychology, Kleinian object relations, self psychology and Lacanian psychoanalysis. The International Psychoanalytical Association, founded in 1910, remains the main accrediting body.

Criticism

Critics, notably Karl Popper, have charged psychoanalysis with unfalsifiability, and its clinical efficacy and the scientific status of its concepts remain debated. Nevertheless, modern psychodynamic therapy — shorter and more focused than classical analysis — has accumulated an evidence base for several conditions.
