Shear and moment diagram

Shear and bending moment diagrams are analytical tools used in structural engineering to determine the value of shear force and bending moment at a given point of a structural element, such as a beam. These diagrams help engineers design the element so that it can resist the applied loads without failure.

Conventions and construction

Diagrams are drawn along the length of the beam beneath a free-body diagram of the loading. The shear at a section equals the algebraic sum of vertical forces to one side of it; the bending moment equals the sum of moments about the section. Point loads produce sudden steps in the shear diagram, while uniformly distributed loads produce sloped shear lines and parabolic moment curves.

Relationships

The slope of the shear diagram at a point equals the negative of the distributed load intensity there, and the slope of the moment diagram equals the shear. The maximum bending moment in a simply supported beam typically occurs where the shear crosses zero.
