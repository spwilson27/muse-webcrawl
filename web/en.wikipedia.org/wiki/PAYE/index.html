PAYE

Pay As You Earn (PAYE) is the system used in the United Kingdom and some other countries, including Ireland and New Zealand, by which income tax and National Insurance contributions are deducted by employers from employees' wages before payment and remitted to the tax authority (HM Revenue and Customs in the UK). It was introduced in the UK in 1944, designed largely by Sir Cornelius Gregg, and spread the tax burden across the year instead of a single annual bill.

A payslip showing PAYE tax and National Insurance deductions

How it works

Each employee is assigned a tax code reflecting their tax-free personal allowance and other adjustments. The employer applies the code to cumulative pay through the tax year, deducting the appropriate tax each pay period and reporting payroll data to HMRC in real time (Real Time Information, introduced in 2013). At year end the employer issues a P60 summary; leavers receive a P45. Discrepancies are corrected through later tax codes or self-assessment.

Related schemes

PAYE also collects student loan repayments in the UK. Similar withholding systems exist worldwide, such as PAYG withholding in Australia and Lohnsteuer in Germany; the term PAYE is also used for systems in South Africa, Ireland and New Zealand.
