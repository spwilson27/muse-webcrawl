Dhritarashtra

Dhritarashtra (Sanskrit: धृतराष्ट्र, Dhṛtarāṣṭra) is a major figure in the Hindu epic Mahabharata. The blind elder son of Vichitravirya by Ambika, he became the de facto king of Hastinapura and father of the hundred Kauravas, whose rivalry with the Pandavas leads to the Kurukshetra War.

Life

Because Dhritarashtra was born blind — Ambika had closed her eyes during her union with the sage Vyasa — the crown passed to his younger brother Pandu. On Pandu's death he ruled as regent, and the succession dispute between his sons and Pandu's became the engine of the epic. Dhritarashtra is portrayed as torn between dharma and paternal love: he repeatedly acknowledges the Pandavas' rights and Duryodhana's faults, yet cannot bring himself to restrain his eldest son — consenting to the rigged dice game, the disrobing of Draupadi, and finally the war itself.

The war and aftermath

Throughout the eighteen days of battle, Dhritarashtra sits in Hastinapura receiving reports from his charioteer Sanjaya, who is granted divine sight by Vyasa; the epic's war books are framed as this narration, beginning with the king's famous opening question about his sons and the sons of Pandu assembled at Kurukshetra. After the war he lives for years at the victorious Yudhishthira's court, treated with honour but consumed by grief, before retiring to the forest with Gandhari, Kunti, and Vidura, where he dies in a forest fire — in some tellings, having at last embraced and forgiven.
