The Annuario Pontificio ("Pontifical Yearbook") is the annual directory of the Holy See and the Roman Catholic Church. It lists the pope and the departments of the Roman Curia, all cardinals, bishops, dioceses, religious orders, and the Holy See's diplomatic missions and corps, together with statistics on the worldwide Church.

History

A forerunner, the Notizie per l'Anno, was first published in Rome in 1716 by the Cracas press. From 1851 the Holy See itself compiled the volume, issued as Gerarchia cattolica; the title Annuario pontificio was adopted in 1860. Publication was interrupted in the 1870s and resumed in 1872.

Contents

The yearbook is the authoritative source for the official list of popes. In 2001 its editors famously deleted the question mark that had long stood against the legendary Pope Joan. The 2013 edition removed the title "Patriarch of the West" from the pope's styles under Benedict XVI's earlier decision, and later editions list "Pope Francis" with his titles in a new arrangement. The volume also gives the number of Catholics, priests, deacons and seminarians by diocese and globally.

Related publications

The Annuarium Statisticum Ecclesiae, compiled by the same office, presents the Church's statistics in analytical form, while the Acta Apostolicae Sedis is the Holy See's official gazette of acts and appointments.
