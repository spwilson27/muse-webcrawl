CONCACAF Gold Cup

The CONCACAF Gold Cup (Spanish: Copa Oro de la CONCACAF) is the main association football competition of the men's national football teams governed by CONCACAF, determining the continental champion of North America (which includes Central America and the Caribbean). It is held every two years. The Gold Cup was founded in 1991 as the successor to the CONCACAF Championship (1963–1989).

History

The first Gold Cup was held in the United States in 1991, and the United States won the inaugural final against Honduras on penalties. The tournament has since been hosted primarily in the United States, with editions co-hosted by Mexico and, more recently, Canada. In 2019 the field expanded from 12 to 16 teams, with qualification partly through the CONCACAF Nations League.

Guest teams from outside the confederation were invited in several early editions, including Brazil, Colombia, Peru, South Korea, South Africa, and Qatar, who participated in 2021 and 2023.

Results

Mexico and the United States have dominated the competition, winning nearly every edition between them. Mexico won the 2023 final 1–0 against Panama and defended the title in 2025, beating the United States 2–1 in the final in Houston.
