Extended edition

An extended edition (or extended cut) is a version of a film or other media work that is longer than the version originally released theatrically, typically created for home media release. Extended editions restore deleted scenes or add new footage, often with the involvement of the director.

Examples

Famous examples include the extended editions of The Lord of the Rings trilogy, each adding roughly 30 to 50 minutes to the theatrical running times, and extended versions of Kingdom of Heaven and the Hobbit films. Unlike a director's cut, which restores a director's preferred version, an extended edition may simply lengthen a film for fans without necessarily superseding the theatrical version.
