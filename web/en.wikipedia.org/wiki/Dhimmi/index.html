Dhimmi

Dhimmi
Context: Islamic law (sharia)
Status: Protected non-Muslim subjects
Tax: Jizya
Related: People of the Book, Jizya

Dhimmi is a historical term referring to non-Muslim subjects of a state governed by Islamic law who were granted protection and allowed to practice their religion in return for paying a tax called jizya and accepting certain legal restrictions.

Origins

The concept derives from dhimma, a pact of protection. Initially applied to Jews, Christians and Sabians — People of the Book — it was later extended in many places to Zoroastrians, Hindus and others.

Status and restrictions

Dhimmis were exempt from military service and zakat but typically had inferior legal status: testimony rules, dress codes and restrictions on building places of worship varied widely by time, place and ruler, ranging from relative tolerance to persecution.

Abolition

The Ottoman Empire formally abolished the status in the 19th-century Tanzimat reforms, notably the 1856 Hatt-ı Hümayun, as citizenship concepts replaced confessional categories.
