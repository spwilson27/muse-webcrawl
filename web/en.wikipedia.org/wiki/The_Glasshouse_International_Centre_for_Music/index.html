The Glasshouse International Centre for Music

Former name: Sage Gateshead (2004–2023)
Location: Gateshead Quays, Gateshead, England
Opened: 17 December 2004
Architect: Foster and Partners
Resident orchestra: Royal Northern Sinfonia

The Glasshouse International Centre for Music, known until 2023 as Sage Gateshead, is a concert venue and musical education centre located on the south bank of the River Tyne in Gateshead, North East England. Designed by Foster and Partners, it opened in 2004 as part of the regeneration of the Gateshead Quays area, facing the Baltic Centre for Contemporary Art and the Gateshead Millennium Bridge across the river.

Architecture

The building is enclosed under a single undulating stainless-steel and glass roof, beneath which sit three separate, self-contained performance "pods": Hall One, a 1,700-seat shoebox concert hall with variable acoustics; Hall Two (now Sage Two), a 450-seat ten-sided hall adaptable for many genres; and the Northern Rock Foundation Hall, a rehearsal and performance space home to the Royal Northern Sinfonia. The design, led by Spencer de Grey with acoustics by Arup Acoustics, cost about £70 million.

Music programme

The venue is the home of the Royal Northern Sinfonia, and hosts concerts across classical, folk, jazz, rock and popular music, as well as a large learning and participation programme of classes, choirs and ensembles for all ages, run with partners including the folk development agency Folkworks. In September 2023 the centre was renamed The Glasshouse International Centre for Music, ending a naming-rights association with the software company Sage Group; a new arena and conference centre planned nearby retained the Sage name instead.
