Gospel of Luke

The Gospel of Luke is the third and longest of the four canonical Gospels. Written around AD 80–110, it tells of Jesus's life, ministry, death and resurrection, and forms a two-volume work with the Acts of the Apostles.

Gospel of Luke
Chapters: 24
Trad. author: Luke the Evangelist
Companion volume: Acts of the Apostles

Content

Unique material includes the Annunciation, the detailed Nativity with shepherds, parables of the Good Samaritan (see Good Samaritan law) and the Prodigal Son, and an emphasis on prayer, women, the poor and outcasts. Luke presents Jesus as saviour of all peoples, tracing his genealogy to Adam.

Authorship

Tradition attributes it to Luke, companion of Paul; most scholars regard it as anonymous, written in polished Greek for a figure named Theophilus, using Mark and a sayings source shared with Matthew.
