Igor Stravinsky

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Igor Stravinsky conducting: round spectacles, baton raised, score of the Rite before him]

Igor Fyodorovich Stravinsky (17 June 1882 – 6 April 1971) was a Russian composer and conductor, later a naturalised citizen of France (1934) and the United States (1945). He is widely considered one of the most important and influential composers of the 20th century and a pivotal figure in modernist music.

Stravinsky shot to fame with three ballets commissioned by Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes: The Firebird (1910), Petrushka (1911) and The Rite of Spring (1913), whose savage rhythms and dissonance provoked a famous near-riot at its Paris premiere and permanently changed musical thinking. His later output passed through a neoclassical period (Pulcinella, the opera The Rake's Progress, 1951) and, in his seventies, an unexpected turn to serialism.
