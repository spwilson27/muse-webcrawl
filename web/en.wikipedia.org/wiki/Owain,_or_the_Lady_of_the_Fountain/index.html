Owain, or the Lady of the Fountain

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Owain, or the Lady of the Fountain (Welsh: Iarlles y Ffynnon, "The Lady of the Fountain"), also known as Owain and Lunet, is one of the Three Welsh Romances (Y Tair Rhamant) associated with the Mabinogion. It is an Arthurian romance relating to the Owain of Welsh tradition, and is closely related to Chrétien de Troyes's French romance Yvain, the Knight of the Lion.

Plot summary

The tale opens with Cynon fab Clydno telling the story of his defeat at the hands of a mysterious black knight who guards a magical fountain. Owain sets out to avenge the disgrace and, after defeating the knight, follows him to his castle, where he is trapped between portcullises. Lunet, the handmaiden of the widowed countess Laudine, gives Owain a ring of invisibility; he falls in love with Laudine and eventually marries her, taking over the defence of the fountain.

Years later, King Arthur arrives, and Owain departs with Gawain on knightly adventures, promising to return within three years. He forgets his promise, and Laudine rejects him. Distraught, Owain goes mad and lives in the wilderness until he is cured; he rescues a lion from a serpent, and the grateful lion becomes his companion. After further adventures, including aiding Lunet, Owain is reconciled with Laudine.

Origins and parallels

Like the other Two Romances, Geraint and Enid and Peredur, the tale survives in the White Book of Rhydderch and the Red Book of Hergest (14th century). Scholars debate how closely the Welsh tale depends on Chrétien's poem or whether both derive from a common source.
