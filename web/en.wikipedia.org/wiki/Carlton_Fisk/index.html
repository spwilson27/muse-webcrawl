Carlton Fisk

Born: December 26, 1947, Bellows Falls, Vermont
Position: Catcher
Teams: Boston Red Sox, Chicago White Sox
Hall of Fame: inducted 2000

Carlton Ernest Fisk (born December 26, 1947), nicknamed "Pudge" and "the Commander", is an American former professional baseball catcher who played 24 seasons in Major League Baseball for the Boston Red Sox and Chicago White Sox.

Career

Fisk won the American League Rookie of the Year award in 1972 and was an eleven-time All-Star. He is best remembered for his walk-off home run in the 12th inning of Game 6 of the 1975 World Series, in which he was filmed waving his arms to keep the ball fair as it struck the left-field foul pole at Fenway Park — one of the most iconic moments in baseball history.

Records and legacy

At his retirement in 1993, Fisk held the records for most games played by a catcher and most home runs by a catcher (351 of his 376 career homers). Both the Red Sox and White Sox retired his numbers (27 and 72 respectively). He was elected to the National Baseball Hall of Fame in 2000.
