Jury

Jury
DefinitionSworn body of citizens convened to decide facts in a trial
SizesTypically 12 in US/UK criminal trials; unanimity rules vary
[Image: Jurors seated in a jury box listening to testimony]

A jury is a sworn body of people (jurors) convened to hear evidence in a court and render an impartial verdict. Trial by jury is a hallmark of common-law systems, descending from medieval England, distributing the power to convict from the state to citizens.

Debates

Juries check state power and bring community judgment, but face criticism over competence in complex cases, representativeness and media influence. Many civil-law countries use mixed panels of judges and lay assessors instead.
