Separable polynomial


Distinct roots of a separable polynomial (placeholder image)

In mathematics, a separable polynomial is a polynomial over a field whose roots — in an algebraic closure, or equivalently in its splitting field — are all distinct. Equivalently, a polynomial P is separable if and only if it is coprime with its formal derivative P′, sharing no common root with it.

Properties

Every irreducible polynomial over a field of characteristic zero is separable, as is every irreducible polynomial over a finite field; such fields are called perfect. In positive characteristic p, an irreducible polynomial can fail to be separable only when it is a polynomial in xp — its derivative is then zero — as with xp − t over the rational function field Fp(t).

Separable extensions

Separable polynomials are the building blocks of separable field extensions: an algebraic element is separable if its minimal polynomial is separable, and an extension is separable if every element satisfies this. Separable extensions behave well under Galois theory — every finite separable extension has a primitive element — and the theory of inseparable extensions is largely confined to characteristic p, where purely inseparable extensions also arise.
