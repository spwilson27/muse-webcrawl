Casti connubii ("Of chaste wedlock") is an encyclical of Pope Pius XI, promulgated on 31 December 1930. It set out the Catholic Church's teaching on Christian marriage, affirming its sacramental character, indissolubility, and openness to children, and condemning contraception, abortion, and eugenic sterilization.

Context

The encyclical was issued in part as a response to the 1930 Lambeth Conference of the Anglican Communion, which had for the first time permitted the use of contraception within marriage in limited circumstances. Pius XI reaffirmed the traditional Catholic position that the primary purpose of marriage is the procreation and education of children, and that any deliberate frustration of the marital act's procreative end is intrinsically wrong. At the same time, the encyclical praised marital love and the mutual formation of the spouses as genuine goods of marriage, and it acknowledged that marital relations during periods of natural infertility were not sinful — an allowance later developed in teaching on natural family planning.

Contents

The document also condemned eugenics legislation then spreading in several countries, rejected "trial" and companionate marriage arrangements, defended the subordination of the wife within the household as then commonly taught while urging husbands to love their wives as Christ loved the Church, and encouraged civil authorities to support families economically. Its teaching was later restated by Humanae vitae (1968), in which Paul VI again upheld the prohibition of artificial contraception.
