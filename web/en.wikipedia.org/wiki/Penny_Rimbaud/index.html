Penny Rimbaud (born Jeremy John Ratter, 8 June 1943) is an English writer, poet, artist and musician, best known as the drummer, lyricist and co-founder of the seminal anarcho-punk band Crass, and of the long-running counter-cultural commune at Dial House in Essex.

Early life

Ratter was born in Northwood, Middlesex. After attending art college he became involved in experimental performance art, co-founding the EXIT art group in the late 1960s. In the early 1970s he settled at Dial House, a cottage in North Weald, Essex, which he developed into an open creative community and a centre for avant-garde and anarchist activity.

Crass

In 1977 Rimbaud, together with vocalist Steve Ignorant and others at Dial House, formed Crass, taking his stage name from the poet Arthur Rimbaud. The band combined punk music with an explicit anarchist programme, releasing records on their own Crass Records label, pioneering the pay-no-more-than pricing model and elaborate fold-out record sleeves, and articulating the "there is no authority but yourself" ethos that defined anarcho-punk. Rimbaud wrote most of the band's lyrics and manifestos. Crass disbanded in 1984, having planned the split in advance in reference to George Orwell's novel Nineteen Eighty-Four.

Later work

After Crass, Rimbaud continued to write, perform poetry and record, including work with the Last Amendment performance collective, jazz-influenced projects and collaborations with musicians such as Louise Elliot. His books include the autobiographical Shibboleth: My Revolting Life (1998) and the extended prose poem The Diamond Signature. He remains based at Dial House, which continues as a self-managed centre for creative and radical projects.
