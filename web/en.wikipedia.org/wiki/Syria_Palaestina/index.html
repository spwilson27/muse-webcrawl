Syria Palaestina (Latin: Provincia Syria Palaestina) was a Roman province established in 135 AD, following the suppression of the Bar Kokhba revolt. It was created by merging the former province of Judaea with neighbouring territories, including parts of Galilee and the coastal plain. The province endured under that name through late antiquity, until the Muslim conquest of the Levant in the 630s–640s.

History

After the disastrous Bar Kokhba revolt (132–136 AD), the emperor Hadrian renamed the province of Judaea Syria Palaestina. The name Palestine, from the Greek Palaistine used by Herodotus and others, derived ultimately from the Philistines who had inhabited the southern coast. Historians have long debated whether the renaming was intended as a punishment meant to sever the region's Jewish association; others argue the name was already in common administrative use.

Jerusalem was refounded as the Roman colony Aelia Capitolina, and Jews were barred from entering the city except on one day a year. The provincial capital remained the coastal city of Caesarea Maritima. The province was governed by a legate of consular rank, and two legions were stationed there: Legio VI Ferrata at Legio in Galilee and Legio X Fretensis at Jerusalem.

Late antiquity

Under the reforms of Diocletian and later emperors, the province's boundaries shifted repeatedly. Around 390 AD Syria Palaestina was divided into Palaestina Prima (with its capital at Caesarea), Palaestina Secunda (capital Scythopolis), and later Palaestina Salutaris (capital Petra). The region prospered in the Byzantine period, with major cities at Caesarea, Scythopolis, Gaza and Jerusalem, before passing to the Rashidun Caliphate after the Battle of Yarmouk in 636.
