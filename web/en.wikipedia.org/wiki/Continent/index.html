Continent

[Image: World map showing the seven continents in different colours]
World map showing the seven continents in different colours

A continent is any of several large geographical regions. Continents are generally identified by convention rather than any strict criteria. A continent could be a single landmass or a part of a very large landmass, as in the case of Asia and Europe. Due to this, the number of continents varies; up to seven or as few as four geographical regions are commonly regarded as continents. Most English-speaking countries recognize seven regions as continents: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania and South America.

The geological concept of a continent is quite different from the geographical one. In geology, a continent is defined as one of the Earth's major landmasses, including both dry land and continental shelves, and is associated with a continental plate. By this definition, the edges of the continental shelves are the true boundaries of continents.

Definitions and conventions

There is no single standard definition of a continent, and different cultures and scientists have different lists. The seven-continent model is taught in most English-speaking countries as well as in India, Pakistan, the Philippines, China and parts of Western Europe. A six-continent model that combines Europe and Asia as Eurasia is preferred in Russia, Eastern Europe and Japan, while a six-continent model combining North and South America is used in Spanish-speaking countries and parts of Southern Europe.

Criteria for identifying continents include geological criteria, geographical criteria based on landmass contiguity, and cultural criteria, under which Europe and Asia are treated separately despite forming one landmass.

Geology

Continents are composed of relatively light sialic rock (continental crust) that sits above denser simatic material, and they move over geological time through plate tectonics. Continental fragments and microcontinents, such as Madagascar, may be partly submerged. Zealandia, a mostly submerged mass of continental crust around New Zealand, has been proposed as an eighth continent.

Area and population

Asia is the largest continent by both land area (about 44.6 million square kilometres) and population (more than 4.7 billion people). Africa is second in both measures. Oceania is the smallest continent by land area, and Antarctica has no permanent human population, only rotating research station staff.
