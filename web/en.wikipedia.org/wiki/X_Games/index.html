The X Games is an annual action sports event controlled and arranged by ESPN (in partnership, since 2022, with majority owner MSP Sports Capital) that focuses on extreme sports. First held in 1995 as the Extreme Games, the summer event features competitions in skateboarding, BMX, motocross and related disciplines, while the Winter X Games (since 1997) feature snowboarding, skiing and snowmobile events. The X Games are widely credited with bringing action sports and their stars to a mainstream audience.

History

ESPN launched the Extreme Games in Newport and Providence, Rhode Island in 1995, renaming the event the X Games the following year. After early editions in San Diego and San Francisco, the summer games settled in Los Angeles (2003–2013), then moved to Austin, Texas (2014–2016), and subsequently to Minneapolis and venues in Southern California. The winter edition has been held in Aspen, Colorado, since 2002. Between 2010 and 2013, ESPN also ran international X Games stops in Europe, Asia and Brazil before refocusing on its American events. In 2022 ESPN sold a majority stake to MSP Sports Capital.

Notable moments and athletes

In 1999, Tony Hawk landed the first documented "900" (two-and-a-half rotations) in skateboarding history at X Games Five, in one of the most famous moments in action sports. Freestyle motocross pioneer Travis Pastrana dominated Moto X events in the late 1990s and 2000s and completed the first double backflip in competition in 2006. Snowboarder Shaun White became one of the most decorated winter athletes, and skateboarder Nyjah Huston accumulated a record haul of X Games medals. The games also helped drive the inclusion of skateboarding, BMX freestyle and sport climbing in the Olympic Games.

Events

Typical disciplines include skateboard street, park and vert; BMX street, park and dirt; Moto X freestyle and best trick; and, in winter, ski and snowboard slopestyle, big air and superpipe. Competitions are staged as media spectacles with live music and festival elements, distinguishing the X Games from traditional championships run by sports federations.
