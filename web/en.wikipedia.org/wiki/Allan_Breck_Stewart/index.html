Allan Breck Stewart (c. 1713 – c. 1791) was a Scottish soldier and Jacobite, best known as the prime suspect in the Appin Murder of 1752 and as a principal character in Robert Louis Stevenson's novel Kidnapped (1886).

Life

Stewart was fostered with the Stewarts of Appin in the Scottish Highlands. He fought for the British Army, deserted to join the Jacobite rising of 1745 and fought at Prestonpans, then escaped to France, where he served in the French service and acted as an agent and rent-collector for the exiled chief of Appin.

The Appin Murder

On 14 May 1752 Colin Roy Campbell of Glenure, the government factor on the forfeited Stewart estates, was shot dead in the Wood of Lettermore near Ballachulish. Allan Breck, who had been in the district and had threatened Campbell, was blamed and fled to France. In his absence James Stewart of the Glens was tried at Inveraray by a Campbell-dominated jury and hanged for the murder — a conviction widely regarded as a miscarriage of justice. Whether Stewart fired the shot has never been established.

In fiction

Stevenson made Allan Breck the dashing, quarrelsome companion of David Balfour in Kidnapped and its sequel Catriona, romanticising him as a proud Highland swordsman loyal to the exiled Stuarts. The real Stewart's later life in France is obscure; he is said to have served at farms and in military employment, and the date and place of his death are uncertain.
