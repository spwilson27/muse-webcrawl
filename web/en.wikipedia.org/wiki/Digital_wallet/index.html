Digital wallet

A digital wallet, also known as an e-wallet, is an electronic device, online service, or software program that allows one party to make electronic transactions with another party bartering digital currency units for goods and services. Money can be deposited in the digital wallet prior to any transactions, or a bank account or payment card can be linked to it.

Types

Device-based wallets such as Apple Pay, Google Pay and Samsung Pay store tokenised card credentials on a phone or wearable and pay via NFC contactless terminals or in apps. Online wallets such as PayPal hold account credentials for e-commerce checkout. Cryptocurrency wallets store the private keys controlling blockchain assets. Many wallets also store loyalty cards, tickets, boarding passes and identification.

Security

Transactions are commonly protected by tokenisation — substituting a device account number for the real card number — biometric authentication, and transaction-specific cryptograms, which can make wallet payments more secure than magnetic-stripe card payments.

Adoption

Digital wallets are widely used in mobile payment systems, especially in Asia with services such as Alipay and WeChat Pay, and have steadily displaced cash and physical cards for everyday payments in many countries.
