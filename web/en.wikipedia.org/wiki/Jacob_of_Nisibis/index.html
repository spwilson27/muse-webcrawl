Jacob of Nisibis

Saint Jacob of Nisibis
BornNisibis, Roman Mesopotamia (modern Nusaybin, Turkey)
Diedc. 337/338 or c. 350, Nisibis
Venerated inRoman Catholic Church; Eastern Orthodox Church; Oriental Orthodox Churches; Church of the East
Feast15 July (Roman Catholic); various dates in Eastern calendars

Jacob of Nisibis (Syriac: ܝܥܩܘܒ ܢܨܝܒܝܢܝܐ, Yaʿqôḇ Nṣîḇînāyâ; Greek: Ἰάκωβος τῆς Νισίβεως; died c. 350), also known as Jacob of Nusaybin, was the second bishop in the episcopal list of Nisibis, spiritual father of the renowned Syriac writer Saint Ephrem the Syrian. He is venerated as a saint in the Catholic, Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox churches.

Life

Jacob was raised in Nisibis, a frontier city of Roman Mesopotamia, and embraced the ascetic life in the desert before being made bishop of the city, traditionally in 308/309. He attended the First Council of Nicaea in 325, where he opposed Arianism. In Nisibis he founded the cathedral school and built the city's great church; the young Ephrem was among his disciples.

Syriac tradition, echoed by the historian Theodoret, credits Jacob's prayers with the deliverance of Nisibis during the Persian sieges by Shapur II in 337/338 and 350, when the city withstood repeated attacks — in one account the bishop, ascending the walls, prayed that a swarm of gnats and mosquitoes would afflict the besiegers' elephants and horses. He is also said to have travelled to find the remains of Noah's Ark on Mount Ararat. Numerous miracles, including raising the dead, are attributed to him in later hagiography.

Veneration

Jacob died at Nisibis and was buried in his church. The Church of Saint Jacob of Nisibis in modern Nusaybin, with its baptistery, is traditionally associated with his school and episcopate. A hymn and homilies once attributed to him are now generally assigned to other authors.
