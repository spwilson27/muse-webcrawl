Taoism

Taoism (also spelled Daoism) is a diverse tradition of Chinese philosophy and religion that emphasises living in harmony with the Tao (Chinese: 道; pinyin: Dào), the Way — the fundamental principle underlying the universe. It is one of the most influential traditions in East Asian culture, alongside Confucianism and Buddhism.

Origins

Taoism traces its origins to the semi-legendary sage Laozi and his text, the Tao Te Ching (Daodejing), and to the writings of Zhuang Zhou (the Zhuangzi), both from the Warring States period. In the 2nd century CE, Zhang Daoling founded the Way of the Celestial Masters, the first organised Taoist religious movement, and Taoism went on to develop temples, priesthoods, rituals, alchemy and meditation practices.

Beliefs and practices

Key Taoist concepts include wu wei (effortless action), ziran (naturalness), simplicity, compassion, humility and the balance of yin and yang. Religious Taoism encompasses the worship of deities such as the Jade Emperor and the Three Pure Ones, longevity practices, feng shui, qigong and internal alchemy (neidan), all aimed at health, longevity or immortality. Major schools include Quanzhen (Complete Perfection) and Zhengyi (Orthodox Unity).

Influence

Taoist ideas permeate Chinese medicine, martial arts such as tai chi, landscape painting, poetry and architecture, and have spread throughout Korea, Japan and Vietnam as well as to the West.
