De Haas–van Alphen effect

Named afterWander Johannes de Haas; Pieter Martinus van Alphen
Discovered1930 (in bismuth)
PhenomenonOscillations of magnetisation in a metal with varying magnetic field

The de Haas–van Alphen effect is a quantum mechanical phenomenon in which the magnetic susceptibility of a pure metal crystal oscillates periodically as the strength of an applied magnetic field increases. Discovered in 1930 by Wander Johannes de Haas and his student Pieter Martinus van Alphen, it arises from the quantisation of electron orbits into Landau levels.

Explanation

As the field changes, the spacing of Landau levels changes and the levels repeatedly pass through the Fermi energy, causing oscillations in the density of states and hence in the free energy and magnetisation. The oscillations are periodic in the inverse of the field, and the period is directly related to the extremal cross-sectional areas of the Fermi surface.

Applications

The effect is a principal experimental tool for mapping the Fermi surfaces of metals, since analysis of the oscillation frequencies (the Onsager relation) yields the geometry of the surface in momentum space. Related oscillatory phenomena include the Shubnikov–de Haas effect in electrical resistance.
