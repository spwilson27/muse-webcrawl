Constitution of Hawaii

The Constitution of Hawaii is the fundamental governing document of the U.S. state of Hawaii. Hawaii has had several constitutions: those of the Kingdom of Hawaii in 1840, 1852, 1864, and 1887 (the "Bayonet Constitution"), the constitution of the Republic of Hawaii in 1894, and the state constitution adopted in 1950.

State constitution

The 1950 constitutional convention drafted the document anticipating statehood, which followed in 1959 under the Admission Act. The constitution was substantially revised by the 1978 constitutional convention, which created the Office of Hawaiian Affairs, established term limits for state officials, and strengthened environmental protections, including a provision declaring a public trust in natural resources.

Notable features

Unusually among U.S. state constitutions, Hawaii's mandates a constitutional convention question every ten years and provides for citizen initiative in convention referenda only, not ordinary legislation. It also includes a strong right to privacy, expressly stated in Article I, and recognizes Hawaiian as an official state language alongside English.
