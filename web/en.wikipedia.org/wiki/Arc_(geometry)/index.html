Arc (geometry)

In mathematics, an arc (from Latin arcus, a bow) is a portion of a curve. Most commonly the term refers to an arc of a circle: any connected part of the circle's circumference.

Circular arcs

A circular arc is determined by its central angle. A chord through the endpoints divides the circle into two arcs: the smaller is the minor arc and the larger the major arc; a semicircle, with central angle 180°, divides the circle into two equal arcs. The length of an arc of radius r subtending a central angle θ (in radians) is s = rθ — see arc length. The region enclosed by an arc and the chord joining its endpoints is a circular segment; the region enclosed by an arc and two radii is a circular sector.

Other curves

The term extends to arcs of ellipses, parabolas, and other curves, and in topology an "arc" may mean a homeomorphic copy of a closed interval — a curve without self-intersections. On the celestial sphere and in navigation, arcs of great circles give the shortest paths between points on a sphere. See also circle, circular segment, and chord.
