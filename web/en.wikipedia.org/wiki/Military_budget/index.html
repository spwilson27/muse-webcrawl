Military budget

The largest military budgets in the world, led by the United States and China.

A military budget (also called a defence budget) is the portion of a government's budget allocated to funding the armed forces, including salaries, training, operations, maintenance, procurement of weapons and equipment, construction, and research and development. Military budgets are a central instrument through which states translate economic resources into military capability.

Global spending

According to the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI), world military expenditure reached approximately $2.44 trillion in 2023, rising further to about $2.72 trillion in 2024, the steepest increases since the end of the Cold War. The United States has consistently maintained the largest military budget in the world, accounting for roughly 37–40 percent of global spending, followed by China, Russia, Germany, and India.

Components

Military budgets are typically divided between personnel costs, operations and maintenance, procurement, and research, development, test and evaluation (RDT&E). In professional volunteer forces, personnel and operations often consume the majority of spending, while conscription-based forces may spend proportionally more on equipment.

Share of GDP

Military spending is often compared as a share of gross domestic product. NATO member states have adopted a guideline of spending at least 2 percent of GDP on defence, a target reaffirmed after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, with several members exceeding 3 or even 4 percent.
