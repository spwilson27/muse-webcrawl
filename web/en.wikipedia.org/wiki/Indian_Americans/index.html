Indian Americans are Americans with ancestry from India. Numbering over five million, they are one of the largest groups within the Asian American population and the largest subgroup of South Asian Americans. Indian immigration to the United States occurred in small numbers from the late nineteenth century, grew after the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 removed national quotas, and accelerated sharply from the 1990s with employment-based immigration in technology and medicine.

Demographics and socioeconomic profile

Indian Americans have the highest median household income of any major ancestry group in the United States and very high levels of educational attainment, with a large majority of adults holding at least a bachelor's degree. Major population centers include the New York–New Jersey region, the San Francisco Bay Area, Chicago, Houston, Dallas, Atlanta, and Seattle. A majority of Indian Americans are foreign-born, and many immigrated through the H-1B skilled worker program.

Culture and prominence

The community maintains Indian languages, festivals such as Diwali, and religious institutions while participating prominently in American public life. Indian Americans have led major technology companies, served in Congress and cabinet posts — including Vice President Kamala Harris, of part-Indian descent — and achieved prominence in medicine, academia, business, and the arts.
