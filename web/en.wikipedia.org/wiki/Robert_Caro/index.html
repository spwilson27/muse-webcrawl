Robert Caro

Born: October 30, 1935, New York City
Occupation: Biographer, journalist
Known for: The Power Broker; The Years of Lyndon Johnson
Awards: Two Pulitzer Prizes

Robert Allan Caro (born October 30, 1935) is an American journalist and author celebrated for his monumental biographies of Robert Moses and Lyndon B. Johnson. His work is distinguished by exhaustive archival research and interviews, and by its central theme: the nature and consequences of political power.

The Power Broker

After working as an investigative reporter at Newsday, Caro spent seven years writing The Power Broker: Robert Moses and the Fall of New York (1974), a 1,200-page biography of the master builder who reshaped New York through highways, bridges, and parks while remaining unelected. The book won the Pulitzer Prize for Biography and is routinely listed among the greatest nonfiction works of the twentieth century.

The Years of Lyndon Johnson

Caro then turned to Lyndon Johnson, intending a three-volume biography that grew into a projected five volumes: The Path to Power (1982), Means of Ascent (1990), Master of the Senate (2002), and The Passage of Power (2012). Master of the Senate won his second Pulitzer. To understand Johnson's Texas Hill Country roots, Caro and his wife and research partner, Ina Caro, moved to Texas for years, interviewing hundreds of witnesses. A fifth volume, covering Johnson's presidency and the Vietnam War, has been in progress for many years. His memoir of method, Working, appeared in 2019.
