Judas of Galilee

Activec. 6 CE
Known forRevolt against the census of Quirinius
RegionJudaea, Roman Empire

Judas of Galilee, also called Judas the Galilean, was a Jewish leader who led resistance to the Roman census conducted by Quirinius in 6 CE, when Judaea became a Roman province. He is known chiefly from the writings of the historian Josephus.

Revolt

When the Syrian legate Quirinius ordered a census of Judaea for taxation, Judas, together with a Pharisee named Zadok, urged the population to refuse registration, arguing that paying tribute to Rome amounted to slavery and that God alone was Israel's ruler. The revolt was suppressed, and Judas appears to have perished, though the sources do not give a clear account of his death.

The "fourth philosophy"

Josephus credited Judas with founding a "fourth philosophy" alongside the Pharisees, Sadducees and Essenes, characterized by an uncompromising commitment to liberty under God. The Zealot movement of later decades is often linked to this tradition, and Josephus names sons of Judas who were crucified decades later, as well as a descendant, Menahem, who led a faction during the revolt of 66 CE.

In the New Testament

Judas is mentioned in the Acts of the Apostles, in a speech attributed to Gamaliel, who recalls that "Judas the Galilean rose up in the days of the census and drew away some of the people after him; he too perished."
