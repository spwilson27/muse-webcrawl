Silicon Wadi

Silicon Wadi (Hebrew: סיליקון ואדי, from Arabic wadi, a valley or riverbed) is a nickname for the concentration of high-technology industry in Israel, especially along the coastal plain around Tel Aviv, Herzliya, Ra'anana, and extending to Haifa and Jerusalem. It is one of the world's foremost technology clusters, comparable in spirit to California's Silicon Valley, to which its punning name alludes.

Development

The cluster grew from electronics work for the Israel Defense Forces and companies such as Elron (founded 1962) and ECI Telecom. In the 1990s the government's Yozma programme seeded a venture capital industry, while large-scale immigration from the former Soviet Union supplied engineers. Intel, Microsoft, Google, Apple, and many other multinationals established R&D centres in Israel, alongside thousands of local start-ups — a density popularised by the phrase "Start-up Nation."

Characteristics

Strengths include cybersecurity, semiconductors (Intel's Fab 28 in Kiryat Gat), medical devices, agricultural and water technology, and enterprise software. Contributing factors cited by analysts include military R&D units such as Unit 8200, strong universities (the Technion, Weizmann Institute, Hebrew University, Tel Aviv University), and close ties to US capital markets, where many Israeli firms list on NASDAQ.
