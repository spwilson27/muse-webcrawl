Otago Peninsula

The Otago Peninsula is a long, hilly, indented finger of land that forms the easternmost part of Dunedin, New Zealand. Volcanic in origin, it forms one wall of the eroded valley that now forms Otago Harbour. The peninsula lies south-east of Otago Harbour and runs parallel to the mainland for some 30 km, and is joined to the mainland at the south end by a narrow isthmus about 1.5 km wide.

Suburbs of Dunedin extend along the peninsula's northern shore, and small communities dot its southern shore on the Pacific Ocean. The peninsula is renowned for its wildlife, including northern royal albatrosses at Taiari Head, yellow-eyed penguins, little penguins and New Zealand fur seals, making it a major ecotourism destination.

Communities on the peninsula include Macandrew Bay, Broad Bay, Portobello and Otakou, site of one of the earliest Māori settlements in the Otago region and a marae of Ngāi Tahu.
