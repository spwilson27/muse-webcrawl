Nymph

A nymph (Greek: νύμφη, nýmphē) in Greek mythology is a minor female nature deity, typically depicted as a beautiful maiden associated with a particular place or natural feature — a spring, grove, mountain or tree. Unlike gods, nymphs were generally mortal, though exceptionally long-lived.

Types of nymphs

Nymphs were classified by their domains: Naiads of fresh water, Nereids of the sea, Oreads of mountains, Dryads and Hamadryads of trees, Meliae of ash trees, and Oceanids of the great ocean. Well-known individual nymphs include Calypso, Daphne, Echo, Eurydice and Thetis.

Role in myth and culture

Nymphs appear throughout Greek myth as companions of gods such as Artemis, attendants of Dionysus, and objects of pursuit by gods and heroes; those who resisted, like Daphne, were sometimes transformed into trees or springs. Springs and caves sacred to nymphs (nymphaea) became sites of worship and later inspired Roman and Renaissance garden architecture. The figure of the nymph remained a central subject in classical art, poetry and ballet.
