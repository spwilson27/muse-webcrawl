CONAKAT

CONAKAT, the Confédération des associations tribales du Katanga ("Confederation of Tribal Associations of Katanga"), was a political party in the Belgian Congo and the independent Republic of the Congo, founded in 1958 by Moise Tshombe and Godefroid Munongo. It represented the interests of the Lunda and related peoples of southern Katanga and championed a federal Congo in which the mineral-rich Katanga province would retain substantial autonomy.

History

CONAKAT emerged from associations of "authentic" Katangese peoples that feared domination by the large numbers of Luba (Baluba) migrants who had come to Katanga to work in the mines. The party's rivalry with Jason Sendwe's BALUBAKAT, which represented the Baluba of northern Katanga, shaped the bitter politics of the province in the run-up to independence.

In the May 1960 elections CONAKAT won the provincial assembly of Katanga but performed less well nationally. When the Congo became independent on 30 June 1960 and the Force Publique mutinied, Tshombe, with Belgian support, declared the secession of the State of Katanga on 11 July 1960. CONAKAT became the ruling party of the secessionist state, which survived until January 1963, when United Nations forces ended the secession (see Congo Crisis).

Aftermath

Tshombe went into exile but returned in 1964 as prime minister of the reunified Congo, leading a new coalition (CONACO) before being dismissed by President Kasa-Vubu in 1965. CONAKAT itself disappeared into the new political order that followed Mobutu Sese Seko's 1965 coup.
