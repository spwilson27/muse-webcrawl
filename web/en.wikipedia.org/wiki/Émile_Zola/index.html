Émile Édouard Charles Antoine Zola (2 April 1840 – 29 September 1902) was a French novelist, journalist and playwright, the most important practitioner of the literary school of naturalism and a major figure in the political liberalisation of France.

Life and work

Zola was raised in Aix-en-Provence, where he befriended Paul Cézanne, and moved to Paris in 1858. His twenty-volume cycle Les Rougon-Macquart (1871–1893) traces the fortunes of a family under the Second Empire through novels including L'Assommoir, Nana and Germinal (1885), his celebrated novel of a coal-miners' strike. His essay The Experimental Novel (1880) set out the theory of naturalism: fiction as a quasi-scientific study of heredity and environment.

The Dreyfus affair

In January 1898 Zola published "J'Accuse…!", an open letter to the President of France accusing the army of wrongfully convicting the Jewish officer Alfred Dreyfus of treason. Convicted of libel, Zola fled to England for a year; the affair split France and ended with Dreyfus's full exoneration in 1906. Zola died in 1902 from carbon-monoxide poisoning from a blocked chimney, in circumstances some have suspected were not accidental. His remains were transferred to the Panthéon in 1908.
