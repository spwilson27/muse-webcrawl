Phrygia


Phrygia was an ancient kingdom and region in west-central Anatolia (modern Turkey), famous for kings Midas and Gordias and for the worship of Cybele.

In classical antiquity, Phrygia was a kingdom and later a region in the west-central part of Anatolia, in what is now Asian Turkey, centred on the Sangarios (Sakarya) River. The Phrygians, an Indo-European-speaking people, appear to have migrated from the Balkans into Anatolia around the end of the Bronze Age or during its collapse.

History

The Phrygian kingdom flourished in the 8th century BC, with its capital at Gordium. Its kings include the historical Midas (Mita of the Mushki in Assyrian records), remembered in Greek legend for the golden touch and the ass's ears, and Gordias, of the "Gordian knot" later cut by Alexander the Great. Rock-cut façades and tumulus burials survive at Gordium and at "Midas City" in the Phrygian highlands. Cimmerian invasions in the early 7th century BC destroyed Gordium and ended Phrygian power; the region then passed under Lydian, Persian (as part of the satrapy of Hellespontine Phrygia and Greater Phrygia), Macedonian, Galatian and Roman rule. Phrygia became a Roman province-name under the Empire, split into Phrygia Prima and Secunda (Salutaris and Pacatiana) in late antiquity.

Culture

Phrygia was the homeland of the Great Mother goddess Cybele (Matar), whose cult spread across the Greek and Roman world, and of the ecstatic music of the Phrygian mode, named for the region. The Phrygian cap became an enduring symbol of liberty. Phrygian language inscriptions in an alphabet related to Greek survive from the 8th century BC to the Roman era.
