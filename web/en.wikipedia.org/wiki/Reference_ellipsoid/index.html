Reference ellipsoid

Image placeholder: an oblate spheroid as a model of the Earth.

A reference ellipsoid (or reference spheroid) is a mathematically defined surface that approximates the geoid, the truer physical shape of the Earth or another planetary body. Because the geoid is irregular, a smooth ellipsoid of revolution — defined by its semi-major axis and flattening — is used as the reference surface for geodesy, cartography and navigation, on which latitude and longitude coordinates are computed.

Parameters

An ellipsoid of revolution is specified by the equatorial (semi-major) radius a and the polar (semi-minor) radius b, or equivalently by a and the flattening f = (a − b)/a. The widely used WGS 84 ellipsoid has a = 6,378,137 m and flattening of about 1/298.257, so the polar radius is roughly 21.4 km shorter than the equatorial radius.

Historical and modern ellipsoids

Different surveys historically fitted local ellipsoids — such as those of Bessel (1841), Clarke (1866, 1880) and Hayford — to their regions. Satellite geodesy enabled global best-fit ellipsoids such as GRS 80 and WGS 84, which underpin modern datums and the Global Positioning System.
