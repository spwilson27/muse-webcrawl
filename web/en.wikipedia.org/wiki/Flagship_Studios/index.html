Flagship Studios was an American video game developer founded in 2003 by Bill Roper together with Max Schaefer, Erich Schaefer, and David Brevik — leading members of the Blizzard North team responsible for the Diablo series — along with other former Blizzard staff. Based in San Francisco, the studio was best known for the action role-playing game Hellgate: London (2007) and the online game Mythos, developed by its Seattle satellite, Flagship Seattle.

History

The founders left Blizzard North in mid-2003 after a dispute with Blizzard's parent company, Vivendi, and formed Flagship Studios with backing from the Korean publisher HanbitSoft. Its first announced title, Hellgate: London, was a heavily anticipated action RPG set in a demon-invaded London, published in partnership with Namco Bandai and Electronic Arts and released on 31 October / 2 November 2007. The game launched with significant technical problems and an unusual hybrid free-to-play/subscription business model, and received mixed reviews.

Financial difficulties mounted in 2008; in July 2008 the studio laid off most of its staff and suspended operations. Intellectual property rights were contested between Flagship, HanbitSoft, and the studio's creditors: HanbitSoft took over Mythos and Hellgate for Asian markets, while the western Hellgate: London servers operated by Namco Bandai closed on 31 January 2009. Former staff went on to found studios including Runic Games (creators of Torchlight), many of them from the Mythos team at Flagship Seattle.
