Earned media

Earned media is media exposure for a brand, organisation, or individual that is generated by third parties rather than purchased or produced directly, such as press coverage, word of mouth, reviews, shares, mentions, and recommendations. In the widely used PESO model, it contrasts with paid media (advertising), owned media (channels a brand controls, such as its website), and shared media (social media activity).

Characteristics

Earned media is considered highly credible because editorial and peer endorsement is perceived as independent, but it cannot be bought or fully controlled: journalists and customers decide what to say and whether to cover a story at all. Public relations practice aims to generate earned media through news releases, events, and story pitching, while digital marketing emphasises content designed to be shared, reviewed, and discussed. Negative earned media — criticism, unfavourable reviews, or crisis coverage — can spread rapidly on social platforms.

Measurement

Quantifying earned media is contested. Advertising value equivalency (AVE), which assigns coverage the price of equivalent advertising space, is widely used but rejected as invalid by professional bodies such as AMEC, whose Barcelona Principles advocate outcome-based measurement such as audience reach, sentiment, and behaviour change instead.
