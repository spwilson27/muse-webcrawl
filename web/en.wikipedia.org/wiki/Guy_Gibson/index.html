Wing Commander Guy Penrose Gibson, VC, DSO & Bar, DFC & Bar (12 August 1918 – 19 September 1944) was a distinguished bomber pilot in the Royal Air Force during the Second World War. He was the first commanding officer of No. 617 Squadron, which he led in the "Dam Busters" raid (Operation Chastise) in 1943, resulting in the destruction of two large dams in the Ruhr valley in Germany. He was awarded the Victoria Cross, the highest award for gallantry in the face of the enemy that can be awarded to British and Commonwealth forces, for his leadership of the raid.

Early life and career

Gibson was born in Simla, India, and educated in England, including at St Edward's School, Oxford. He joined the RAF in 1936 and, after a spell flying fighters, moved to bombers. By 1942 he commanded No. 106 Squadron, flying Avro Manchesters and then Lancasters, and gained a reputation as a demanding and fearless leader who completed over 170 operations.

Operation Chastise

In March 1943 Gibson was chosen to form a special squadron—No. 617 Squadron—to attack the Möhne, Eder, and Sorpe dams using Barnes Wallis's "bouncing bomb" (Upkeep mine). On the night of 16–17 May 1943, Gibson led the first wave against the Möhne Dam, making his own attack and then circling to draw flak from the following aircraft. The Möhne and Eder dams were breached. Of the 133 aircrew who took part, 53 were killed. Gibson was awarded the Victoria Cross and became a national hero.

Death

After a publicity tour of North America and desk work, Gibson returned to operations with No. 627 Squadron. On the night of 19 September 1944, acting as master bomber for a raid on Rheydt, his de Havilland Mosquito crashed near Steenbergen in the Netherlands, killing Gibson and his navigator. The cause of the crash remains uncertain. He is buried alongside Squadron Leader James Warwick in Steenbergen Catholic cemetery.

Gibson's memoir, Enemy Coast Ahead, was published posthumously in 1946. He was portrayed by Richard Todd in the 1955 film The Dam Busters.
