Jane Porter (Tarzan)

Jane Porter (later Jane Clayton, Lady Greystoke) is a fictional character in Edgar Rice Burroughs's Tarzan series of novels, and in their many adaptations. She is the love interest and eventually the wife of Tarzan (John Clayton, Lord Greystoke) and the mother of his son, Korak. Burroughs created her as an intelligent, resourceful American heroine from Baltimore, Maryland.

Character biography

Jane is introduced in the first novel, Tarzan of the Apes (magazine publication 1912; book 1914), as the daughter of Professor Archimedes Q. Porter. Marooned on the African coast with her father's treasure-hunting expedition, she is rescued repeatedly by Tarzan and falls in love with him, though she initially returns to America and becomes engaged to William Cecil Clayton, Tarzan's cousin and (unbeknownst to all) the holder of Tarzan's inherited title. In The Return of Tarzan she finally marries Tarzan. In later novels she shares his adventures, is kidnapped with some regularity, and proves herself an able survivor in the jungle; in some later books she receives an immortality treatment, like Tarzan himself.

Portrayals

The first screen Jane was Enid Markey, opposite Elmo Lincoln in Tarzan of the Apes (1918). The most famous is Maureen O'Sullivan, who played Jane in six films opposite Johnny Weissmuller (1932–1942) — though contrary to popular belief she never says the line "Me Jane" in the famous exchange. Later Janes include Brenda Joyce, Vanessa Brown, Joanna Pettet, Jane Fonda (in a 1960s television episode context aside, the role passed through many hands), Bo Derek in Tarzan, the Ape Man (1981), Andie MacDowell (voice by Glenn Close) in Greystoke (1984), Jane March in Tarzan and the Lost City (1998), and Margot Robbie in The Legend of Tarzan (2016). In Disney's animated Tarzan (1999), Jane — voiced by Minnie Driver — is re-imagined as an English artist and naturalist's daughter.
