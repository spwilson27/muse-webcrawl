Case report

In medicine, a case report is a detailed report of the symptoms, signs, diagnosis, treatment and follow-up of an individual patient. Case reports may describe an unusual or novel occurrence — a previously unreported disease, an unexpected adverse drug reaction, or a new treatment approach — and are among the oldest forms of medical literature.

Role and limitations

Case reports sit low in the hierarchy of clinical evidence because they lack controls and statistical generalisability, but they are valuable for hypothesis generation, pharmacovigilance, and medical education. Famous reports include early descriptions of AIDS cases in 1981 and thalidomide embryopathy.

Structure

Typical sections follow the CARE guidelines: title, abstract, introduction, patient information, clinical findings, timeline, diagnostic assessment, therapeutic interventions, follow-up, and discussion, with informed patient consent. Journals devoted to the form include BMJ Case Reports and the Journal of Medical Case Reports.
