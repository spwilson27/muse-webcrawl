Code-switching

In linguistics, code-switching is the practice of alternating between two or more languages, or language varieties, within a single conversation or utterance. Multilingual speakers switch for emphasis, quotation, identity, audience design or because a concept is most available in another language.

Switching between languages mid-conversation

FieldSociolinguistics, psycholinguistics
TypesInter-sentential, intra-sentential, tag-switching

Types and constraints

Switches may occur between sentences (inter-sentential), within a clause (intra-sentential, sometimes called code-mixing), or as tags and fillers. Researchers such as Shana Poplack proposed grammatical constraints — the equivalence and free-morpheme constraints — while the Matrix Language Frame model of Carol Myers-Scotton holds that one language supplies the grammatical frame into which the other inserts content.

Social functions

John Gumperz distinguished situational switching (with changes of setting or participants) from metaphorical switching for rhetorical effect. Switching can signal solidarity, distance, expertise or group membership and is a normal, rule-governed feature of bilingual communities from Spanglish in the United States to Hinglish in India — not a sign of deficient language knowledge.
