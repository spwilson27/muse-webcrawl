Educational measurement is the science and practice of assigning numbers or categories to students' knowledge, skills, abilities and attitudes in order to make inferences and decisions — for classroom grading, university admissions, certification, programme evaluation and accountability. As a branch of psychometrics applied to education, it draws on statistics and measurement theory and is carried out by teachers, testing organisations and governments.

Concepts

The field revolves around the quality of scores and their interpretations. Reliability concerns the consistency of scores across items, occasions and raters; validity concerns whether evidence supports the intended interpretation and use of a score, a unified conception articulated by Samuel Messick that includes the social consequences of testing. Classical test theory models an observed score as a true score plus error, while item response theory — including the Rasch model — models the probability of a response as a function of the person's latent ability and the item's characteristics, enabling adaptive testing and equating of test forms.

Practice and issues

Large-scale assessments such as national examinations, the SAT and ACT, and international surveys (PISA, TIMSS) are prominent applications. Persistent issues include measurement invariance and fairness across groups, test anxiety and coaching effects, the narrowing of curricula under high-stakes testing ("teaching to the test"), and the balance between standardised testing and classroom-based assessment.
