Photosynthesis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A green leaf in bright sunlight, light streaming through it and showing its veins]

Photosynthesis is a biological process used by many cellular organisms to convert light energy into chemical energy, stored in sugars. In most cases oxygen is released as a waste product. Photosynthesis is largely responsible for producing and maintaining the oxygen content of Earth's atmosphere, and supplies most of the biological energy necessary for complex life on Earth.

Contents1. Process
2. Significance

Process

In plants, algae and cyanobacteria, the light reactions split water in chloroplasts, releasing oxygen and generating ATP and NADPH; the Calvin cycle then fixes carbon dioxide into sugars. The overall equation is commonly written: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.

Significance

Almost all life depends directly or indirectly on photosynthesis. Ancient cyanobacteria oxygenated the atmosphere in the Great Oxidation Event (~2.4 billion years ago), transforming Earth's environment and evolution.
