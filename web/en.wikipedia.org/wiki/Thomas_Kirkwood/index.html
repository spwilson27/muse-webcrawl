Thomas Burton Loram Kirkwood CBE FRS (born 6 July 1951, Durban, South Africa) is an English biologist and gerontologist best known for proposing the disposable soma theory of ageing in 1977. He spent much of his career at Newcastle University, where he was Professor of Medicine and directed the Institute for Ageing and Health.

Disposable soma theory

Kirkwood's theory explains ageing as an evolutionary trade-off: because resources are limited, natural selection favours allocating energy to reproduction over indefinite maintenance and repair of the body (the "soma"). The soma is thus effectively "disposable" once reproduction is secured, and the accumulation of unrepaired molecular and cellular damage causes ageing. The theory became one of the leading evolutionary accounts of senescence, alongside mutation accumulation and antagonistic pleiotropy.

Career and recognition

Kirkwood delivered the BBC Reith Lectures in 2001 on "The End of Age". He was elected a Fellow of the Academy of Medical Sciences and of the Royal Society, and was appointed CBE for services to medical science. His books include Time of Our Lives: The Science of Human Aging (1999).
