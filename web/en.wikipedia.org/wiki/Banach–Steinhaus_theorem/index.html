Banach–Steinhaus theorem


Pointwise boundedness implies uniform boundedness on a Banach space.

The Banach–Steinhaus theorem, or uniform boundedness principle, is one of the fundamental results of functional analysis, alongside the Hahn–Banach theorem and the open mapping theorem — the three are sometimes called the "three basic principles" of the subject. It was published in 1927 by Stefan Banach and Hugo Steinhaus, building on earlier work of Hans Hahn.

Statement

Let X be a Banach space and Y a normed vector space. Let F be a family of bounded linear operators from X to Y. If for every x in X the values {T(x) : T in F} form a bounded set in Y (pointwise boundedness), then the operator norms of the family are uniformly bounded: there is a constant M with ‖T‖ ≤ M for all T in F.

The proof is a classic application of the Baire category theorem: X, being complete, cannot be a countable union of nowhere dense sets, which forces the uniform bound. The completeness of the domain is essential — on incomplete normed spaces the conclusion can fail.

Consequences

  • Weak convergence — weakly convergent sequences in a normed space are norm-bounded.
  • Condensation of singularities — if the uniform bound fails, the set of points where the family is unbounded is a dense Gδ; this is used to prove that certain pathologies are generic, such as continuous functions whose Fourier series diverge at a point, or everywhere continuous nowhere differentiable functions.
  • Convergence of operators — if bounded operators Tn converge pointwise on a dense set and their norms are bounded, they converge pointwise everywhere.
  • Resonance theorem — the principle extends to families of continuous linear functionals and underlies the Banach–Steinhaus treatment of summation methods and Fourier series.

An immediate corollary concerns dual spaces: a weakly bounded subset of the dual of a normed space is norm bounded. Combined with the uniform boundedness of weakly convergent sequences, the theorem is a standard step in showing that Banach spaces are weakly sequentially complete in important cases and in proving results such as the pointwise convergence criteria for Fourier expansions.
