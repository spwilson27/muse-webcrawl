Geometric distribution

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In probability theory and statistics, the geometric distribution is either one of two discrete probability distributions describing a sequence of independent Bernoulli trials:

  • the probability distribution of the number X of trials needed to obtain the first success, supported on {1, 2, 3, ...}; or
  • the probability distribution of the number Y = X − 1 of failures before the first success, supported on {0, 1, 2, ...}.

Which of these is called "the" geometric distribution depends on convention; textbooks differ, so the support must be checked.

Probability mass function

If the probability of success on each trial is p, then for the number-of-trials version,

Pr(X = k) = (1 − p)k−1 p, for k = 1, 2, 3, ...

The mean of this distribution is 1/p and its variance is (1 − p)/p². For the number-of-failures version, Pr(Y = k) = (1 − p)kp, with mean (1 − p)/p.

Memorylessness

The geometric distribution is the only discrete distribution with the memoryless property: the probability of needing additional trials does not depend on how many failures have already occurred. It is the discrete analogue of the exponential distribution, and the distribution of trial counts arises naturally in modeling waiting times for rare events, reliability testing, and queues. The sum of independent geometric random variables with the same p follows a negative binomial distribution.
