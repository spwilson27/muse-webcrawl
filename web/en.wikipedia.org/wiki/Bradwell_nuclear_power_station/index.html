Bradwell nuclear power station (Bradwell A) was a Magnox nuclear power station located on the southern shore of the Blackwater Estuary on the Dengie Peninsula in Essex, England, near the village of Bradwell-on-Sea. It was among the first commercial nuclear power stations built in the United Kingdom.

History

Construction began in 1957 and the two graphite-moderated, carbon-dioxide-cooled Magnox reactors began supplying electricity to the National Grid in 1962. Together they generated about 242 megawatts in later life, downrated from the original design output. Cooling water was drawn from the Blackwater Estuary. The station operated for 40 years before generation ceased in March 2002, making it one of the longest-serving of the first Magnox fleet.

Defuelling was completed by 2005 and the site then entered decommissioning. Between 2011 and 2018 the reactor buildings were enclosed in weatherproof aluminium cladding, and in December 2018 Bradwell became the first UK nuclear site to enter "care and maintenance" — a passive state in which the sealed reactors are left for several decades to allow radioactivity to decay before final site clearance, planned for late in the 21st century. The distinctive cladding changed the site's appearance from twin concrete reactor blocks to a single long silver structure.

Proposed Bradwell B

The adjacent site was nominated in the government's 2011 National Policy Statement as potentially suitable for new nuclear development. China General Nuclear Power Group and EDF proposed building two UK HPR1000 ("Hualong One") reactors as "Bradwell B"; the design completed the UK Generic Design Assessment in 2022, but the project's future has remained uncertain amid political tensions over Chinese involvement in UK critical infrastructure.
