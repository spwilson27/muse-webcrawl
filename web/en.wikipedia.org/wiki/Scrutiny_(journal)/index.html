Scrutiny was a British literary journal founded in 1932 by L. C. Knights and F. R. Leavis, who edited it throughout its existence until it ceased publication in 1953. Published from Cambridge, it became the most influential vehicle of the Leavisite approach to literature: rigorous close reading, moral seriousness, and a sharply evaluative canon of English writing.

An issue of Scrutiny.

Programme and influence

Scrutiny attacked what its editors saw as the commercialisation of culture and the laxness of metropolitan literary journalism. It championed the poetry of Gerard Manley Hopkins and the mature work of D. H. Lawrence, revalued the Metaphysical poets and the "line of wit" in English verse, and severely criticised Bloomsbury figures and much popular fiction. Contributors included Q. D. Leavis, Denys Thompson, William Empson (early), and later critics shaped by the journal's ethos. Its methods flowed into English teaching in schools and universities through works such as Culture and Environment (1933).

Legacy

Though often attacked as narrow, puritanical or anti-democratic, Scrutiny transformed English studies by insisting that literary criticism be a disciplined, text-centred practice. The complete run was reissued by Cambridge University Press in 1963 with an index and retrospective.
