The Sega Saturn is a home video game console developed by Sega and released on 22 November 1994 in Japan, 11 May 1995 in North America, and 8 July 1995 in Europe. Part of the fifth generation of video game consoles, it was the successor to the successful Sega Genesis and competed with Sony's PlayStation and the Nintendo 64.

Hardware

The Saturn used a dual-CPU architecture centred on two Hitachi SH-2 processors running at 28.6 MHz, supported by additional graphics and sound processors. The complex design was powerful for 2D games but notoriously difficult to program for 3D. Games were distributed on CD-ROM, and the console offered internal backup memory, cartridge slots for memory expansion, and arcade-derived hardware lineage shared with Sega's Titan arcade board.

History and market performance

Sega's surprise early launch in North America in May 1995, months ahead of the announced date, alienated retailers and developers and left the console with few launch titles, while the PlayStation's lower price and strong third-party support won the market. The Saturn was more successful in Japan, supported by the hit Virtua Fighter series and Sega's arcade ports. Notable games include Nights into Dreams, Panzer Dragoon Saga, Sega Rally Championship, and a strong library of 2D fighting and shoot-'em-up titles.

Legacy

Commercial failure in the West contributed to Sega's heavy financial losses in the late 1990s and set the stage for the Dreamcast. The Saturn has since been reappraised by collectors and enthusiasts for its arcade conversions and Japanese library, though its complicated hardware long made accurate emulation difficult.
