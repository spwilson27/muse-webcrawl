Bathurst, New South Wales

Bathurst is a city in the Central Tablelands of New South Wales, Australia, about 200 kilometres west-northwest of Sydney. It is Australia's oldest inland settlement, proclaimed in 1815, and had a population of roughly 35,000 in the early 2020s. The city stands on the banks of the Macquarie River and serves as a regional centre for agriculture, education and manufacturing.

Central Bathurst, with its nineteenth-century courthouse.

History

The area is the traditional land of the Wiradjuri people. Surveyor George Evans reached the site in 1813 during the first European crossing of the Blue Mountains region, and Governor Lachlan Macquarie proclaimed the town on 7 May 1815, naming it after Henry Bathurst, 3rd Earl Bathurst, the British Secretary of State for War and the Colonies. The discovery of gold nearby at Ophir in 1851 — Australia's first payable gold find — sparked the first Australian gold rush and rapid growth.

Mount Panorama

Bathurst is internationally known for the Mount Panorama motor racing circuit, a 6.213 km public-road track that hosts the Bathurst 1000 touring car race each October, one of Australia's most famous sporting events, as well as the Bathurst 12 Hour endurance race.

Economy and education

The surrounding district produces fruit, vegetables, livestock and wine. The city is home to a campus of Charles Sturt University, and its historic centre preserves many gold-rush-era buildings, including the Bathurst Court House (1880).
