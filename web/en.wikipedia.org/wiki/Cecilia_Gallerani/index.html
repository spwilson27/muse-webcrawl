Cecilia Gallerani (1473–1536) was an Italian noblewoman and poet, best known as the mistress of Ludovico Sforza, Duke of Milan, and as the sitter of Leonardo da Vinci's painting Lady with an Ermine (c. 1489–1490), now in the Czartoryski Museum in Kraków, Poland.

Early life

Cecilia was born in Siena into a family of scholars and courtiers: her father, Fazio Gallerani, served in various diplomatic and financial posts for the Sforza. She received an education unusual for a woman of her time, learning Latin and literature, and was known at the Milanese court for her musicianship and poetry.

At the court of Milan

By the late 1480s Cecilia was the mistress of Ludovico Sforza, regent and later Duke of Milan. She bore him a son, Cesare, in 1491. Ludovico gave her properties including the Palazzo Carmagnola and the estate of Saronno. Around the time of Ludovico's marriage to Beatrice d'Este in 1491, Cecilia left the court. She subsequently married Count Ludovico Carminati de' Brambilla, with whom she had four children.

Lady with an Ermine

Leonardo painted Cecilia when she was about sixteen. She holds an ermine, an emblem both of purity and of Ludovico, who was a member of the Order of the Ermine and known to the court by the nickname l'Ermellino. The portrait is one of only four portraits of women by Leonardo and is considered a landmark of Renaissance portraiture for its three-quarter pose, the sitter's turn of the head, and the psychological animation of both woman and animal. In later life Cecilia corresponded with Isabella d'Este, who asked to borrow the portrait to compare it with works by Giovanni Bellini; Cecilia's reply of 1498 is a key documentary source for the painting.

Later life

Widowed, Cecilia maintained literary connections and a household noted for its conversation and music. She died in 1536.
