Book club

The term book club refers to two related things: a reading group — a circle of people who meet regularly to discuss a book they have all read — and a book sales club, a commercial scheme in which subscribers receive selected books by mail, usually at a discount and on a regular schedule.


A reading group in session

Reading groups

Reading groups have roots in nineteenth-century literary societies, women's clubs and workers' education circles. A typical modern group has between five and fifteen members who choose a book — usually a novel, sometimes non-fiction — read it over a month, and meet in homes, libraries, bookshops or cafés to discuss it. Libraries and bookstores frequently support groups with discussion guides and meeting space. Celebrity-led clubs have greatly enlarged the phenomenon: Oprah's Book Club (launched 1996) turned its selections into bestsellers, and television and online clubs such as Reese Witherspoon's and Richard and Judy's have had similar effects. The internet enabled virtual book clubs meeting by forum, video call or social media.

Book sales clubs

Commercial book clubs sell books by subscription or mail order. The model was pioneered by the Book-of-the-Month Club (founded 1926 in the United States) and the Literary Guild: members received a monthly selection chosen by an editorial board, which they could decline in favour of alternatives, often with introductory offers of several books for a nominal price. German publisher Bertelsmann built a vast international book-club business after the Second World War. The rise of discount bookselling and e-commerce in the 1990s and 2000s caused most general book clubs to shrink or close, though specialist and revived curated-subscription services persist.
