Alexandre Aleksandrovich Kirillov (born 9 May 1936) is a Russian and American mathematician known for his work in representation theory, topological groups, and Lie groups. As a student of Israel Gelfand at Moscow State University, he created the orbit method, one of the central ideas of modern representation theory.

Work

Kirillov's orbit method relates the irreducible unitary representations of a Lie group to coadjoint orbits in the dual of its Lie algebra, and his character formula expresses characters in terms of these orbits. He also contributed to the theory of nilpotent Lie groups, supergeometry, and mathematical education. His son, Alexander Kirillov Jr., is also a mathematician.

Career

Kirillov taught for many years at Moscow State University before moving to the United States, where he became a professor at the University of Pennsylvania.
