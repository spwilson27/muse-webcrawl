Sturmgeschütz IV

The Sturmgeschütz IV (StuG IV; Sd.Kfz. 167) was a German assault gun used during the Second World War. It combined the superstructure and 7.5 cm StuK 40 L/48 gun of the Sturmgeschütz III with the chassis of the Panzer IV tank, and was built by Krupp from late 1943 until the end of the war.

Development

The StuG IV arose after Allied bombing in November 1943 badly damaged the Alkett factory producing the StuG III. To maintain assault-gun production, the StuG III superstructure was adapted to the Panzer IV hull, available from Krupp. The resulting vehicle differed from the StuG III mainly in its hull, running gear, and driver's position, and it served in the same assault-gun and tank-destroyer roles on all fronts.

Production and service

Approximately 1,100 StuG IVs were produced between December 1943 and April 1945. Like the StuG III — the most-produced German fully tracked armoured fighting vehicle of the war — the StuG IV's low silhouette and powerful gun made it an effective defensive weapon and tank destroyer. Surviving examples are held by museums including the Polish Army Museum in Warsaw.
