Gaochang

Gaochang (Chinese: 高昌; Uyghur: Khoja), also called Karakhoja, is the site of an ancient oasis city on the northern edge of the Taklamakan Desert in Xinjiang, China, about 37 km southeast of Turpan. Built in the 1st century BC, it flourished on the Silk Road and was destroyed in war in the 14th century.

History

Initially a Han garrison town for the Jushi people, Gaochang became a kingdom under the Qu family (499–640), a centre of Buddhism and Manichaeism with Nestorian Christianity present. Tang China annexed it in 640 as Xizhou. After 843 Uyghurs established the Qocho Kingdom there, ruling until Mongol times.

Archaeology

German expeditions under Albert von Le Coq and others removed murals and manuscripts now in Berlin; Aurel Stein also visited. The Astana Cemetery nearby yielded documents and mummies that illuminate Silk Road life.
