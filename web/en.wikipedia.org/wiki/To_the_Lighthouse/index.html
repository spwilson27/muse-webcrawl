To the Lighthouse

To the Lighthouse
Author: Virginia Woolf
Published: 5 May 1927
Publisher: Hogarth Press
Genre: Modernist novel

To the Lighthouse is a 1927 novel by Virginia Woolf. Set in the Ramsay family's summer home on the Isle of Skye in Scotland, the novel follows visits to the house in 1910 and 1920, centred on the deferred trip to a nearby lighthouse, and is regarded as a landmark of modernist literature.

Structure

The novel is divided into three sections: "The Window", a long account of a single evening; "Time Passes", a compressed decade in which the First World War and family deaths occur largely offstage; and "The Lighthouse", the eventual completion of the journey ten years later.

Themes

With minimal plot and dialogue, Woolf uses stream of consciousness to explore perception, memory, grief, art — embodied in the painter Lily Briscoe — and the relationships between men and women modelled partly on Woolf's own parents.
