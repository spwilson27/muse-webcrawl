Mathieu group

In group theory, a Mathieu group is one of five sporadic simple groups discovered by Émile Mathieu in the 1860s and 1870s: M11, M12, M22, M23, and M24. They are multiply transitive permutation groups on sets of 11, 12, 22, 23, and 24 elements respectively.

Properties

M24, of order 244,823,040, is the automorphism group of the Steiner system S(5,8,24) and is related to the extended binary Golay code and the Leech lattice. M12 relates to the Steiner system S(5,6,12). The Mathieu groups were the first sporadic simple groups discovered and are exceptional in the classification of finite simple groups.

History

Mathieu introduced M11 and M12 in 1861 and the larger groups in 1873; their simplicity and existence were not fully settled until later work.
