Vaud (French: Vaud; German: Waadt) is a canton of Switzerland, in the French-speaking west of the country. Its capital and largest city is Lausanne, seat of the International Olympic Committee. Vaud stretches from Lake Neuchâtel in the north to Lake Geneva (Lac Léman) in the south, and from the Jura mountains in the west to the Bernese and Valais Alps in the east.

History

The region was inhabited in prehistory — its Neolithic lake dwellings are part of a UNESCO listing — and formed part of Roman Helvetia, with Aventicum (Avenches) as a major town. In the Middle Ages the territory passed between the house of Savoy and the prince-bishops of Lausanne. Bern conquered Vaud in 1536 and imposed the Protestant Reformation; Bernese rule lasted until the Vaudois revolution of 1798, supported by revolutionary France, created the short-lived Lemanic Republic. By Napoleon's Act of Mediation in 1803, Vaud entered the Swiss Confederation as an independent canton, with its green-and-white flag bearing the words Liberté et Patrie.

Geography and economy

The canton comprises the Swiss Plateau lakeshore, the Jura foothills with the Vallée de Joux watchmaking valley, and Alpine districts around Les Diablerets and Château-d'Œx. The terraced vineyards of Lavaux, between Lausanne and Vevey, are a UNESCO World Heritage Site; the Chasselas wines of the region are celebrated. Vaud hosts the University of Lausanne, the EPFL (Swiss Federal Institute of Technology) in Ecublens, the headquarters of Nestlé at Vevey, the resort towns of Montreux — home of the Montreux Jazz Festival — and numerous international sports federations around Lausanne.
