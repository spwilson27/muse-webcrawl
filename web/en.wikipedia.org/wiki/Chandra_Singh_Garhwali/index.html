Chandra Singh Garhwali

Chandra Singh Garhwali (25 December 1891 – 1 October 1979), born Chandra Singh Bhandari, was an Indian soldier in the Royal Garhwal Rifles of the British Indian Army, remembered as the hero of the Peshawar incident of 23 April 1930. Ordered to fire on unarmed Khudai Khidmatgar protesters in Peshawar during the Indian independence movement, Garhwali instead ordered his troops to cease fire and lay down their arms, declaring that he would not shoot unarmed people. He was court-martialled and sentenced to life imprisonment, of which he served about eleven years.

Early life and service

Garhwali was born in the village of Meason in the Pauri Garhwal district of what is now Uttarakhand. He enlisted in the Garhwal Rifles in 1914 and served in the First World War in Mesopotamia, later serving on the North-West Frontier. By 1930 he had risen to the rank of Havildar Major (company quartermaster havildar equivalent senior NCO) in the 2/18 battalion.

The Peshawar incident

On 23 April 1930, British troops were deployed in Peshawar's Qissa Khwani Bazaar to suppress the non-violent Khudai Khidmatgar ("Red Shirt") movement of Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan. When armoured cars drove into the crowd and other units fired, killing many demonstrators, Garhwali's company was ordered to open fire. According to the celebrated account, Garhwali commanded his men "Garhwali, cease fire!" and then ordered them to ground their weapons, saying he would not fire on unarmed brethren. The refusal is commemorated as a landmark in the history of the freedom struggle; some historians caution that details of the episode are partly legendary, but the court-martial and punishment of Garhwali and his comrades are documented.

Imprisonment and later life

Garhwali was court-martialled, stripped of rank and property, and sentenced to life imprisonment; he was released in 1941 after intervention by nationalist leaders and thereafter was kept under surveillance. After independence he joined the Communist Party of India, worked for peasant and ex-servicemen's causes in Garhwal, and contested elections unsuccessfully. He died in 1979. In 1994 the Indian government issued a postage stamp in his honour, and he remains a celebrated figure in Uttarakhand, where schools, roads and a museum bear his name.
