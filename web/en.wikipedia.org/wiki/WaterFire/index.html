WaterFire

ArtistBarnaby Evans
Year1994 (first installation)
TypeEnvironmental art installation / performance
LocationWoonasquatucket, Moshassuck and Providence Rivers, Providence, Rhode Island, U.S.
ElementsMore than 80 braziers of wood fires on the river surface, boats, music

WaterFire is an environmental art installation created by artist Barnaby Evans in Providence, Rhode Island. On scheduled evenings, more than eighty braziers are installed on the three rivers that pass through the downtown area of the city and are lit at sunset, burning through the night while tenders in boats move silently among them feeding the fires. Accompanied by a programme of world music played from speakers along the riverbanks, the event transforms the urban riverscape and draws large crowds to the city centre.

History

Evans first created Fire Walk, the precursor work, in 1994 as part of Providence's First Night celebrations. The first WaterFire proper followed in 1996, staged for the International Sculpture Conference and the Convergence International Arts Festival. The installation became a recurring feature of the city's calendar, organised by the non-profit WaterFire Providence, and is widely credited as a catalyst in the cultural and economic revitalisation of downtown Providence, alongside the uncovering and redevelopment of the city's rivers.

The event

A full WaterFire lighting extends along the Woonasquatucket River basin in Waterplace Park and up the Providence River. Volunteers known as fire tenders, dressed in black, ride in wooden boats to light and maintain the braziers, which are filled with cedar and pine. The fires are lit at sunset following a torch ceremony and burn until after midnight. Smaller "basin lightings" illuminate only the Waterplace Park basin. The event is free to the public and funded by sponsors and donors.

Recognition and other cities

WaterFire has received numerous awards for urban design and public art, and Evans has created related fire installations in other cities in the United States and abroad. It is frequently cited in the urban-planning literature as a successful example of "creative placemaking".
