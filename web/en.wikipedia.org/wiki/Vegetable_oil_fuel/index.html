Vegetable oil fuel

Vegetable oil fuel refers to the use of vegetable oils — either straight vegetable oil (SVO, also called pure plant oil) or waste vegetable oil (WVO) — as a fuel in diesel engines, either directly or after conversion. It is distinct from biodiesel, which is vegetable oil chemically processed by transesterification into a fuel with properties closer to petroleum diesel.

History

Rudolf Diesel demonstrated his engine running on peanut oil at the 1900 Paris Exposition, envisioning local plant fuels. Petroleum's cheapness displaced the idea until fuel crises and environmental interest revived it from the 1970s onward.

Use in engines

Vegetable oil is much more viscous than diesel, so direct use usually requires pre-heating the oil, a two-tank system that starts on diesel and switches to oil when warm, or engine modifications. Waste oil from fryers must be filtered and dewatered. Unmodified long-term use can cause injector coking, ring sticking and oil dilution.

Environmental and economic issues

Proponents cite renewability, carbon recycling by growing plants, and cheap waste feedstock. Critics note food-versus-fuel competition, land use, variable emissions, and legal/tax questions where road-fuel duty applies to vegetable oil.
