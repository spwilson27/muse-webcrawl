Ivory-billed woodpecker

Ivory-billed woodpecker
Scientific name: Campephilus principalis
Status: Critically endangered, possibly extinct

The ivory-billed woodpecker (Campephilus principalis) is a woodpecker native to the Southern United States and Cuba. One of the largest woodpeckers in the world, it is known for its ivory-coloured bill. It was listed as an endangered species in 1967 and is possibly extinct.

Description

The bird measures about 48–53 cm in length with a wingspan of around 76 cm. Males have a prominent red crest, while females have a black crest. Its plumage is glossy blue-black with white markings on the neck and back and extensive white on the wings.

Habitat and decline

The species inhabited old-growth bottomland hardwood forests and swamps of the southeastern United States. Extensive logging of these forests in the late 19th and early 20th centuries destroyed its habitat. The last widely accepted sighting in the United States was in Louisiana's Singer Tract in 1944.

Reported rediscovery

In 2004–2005, reported sightings in the Big Woods of Arkansas, published in Science, drew worldwide attention, but subsequent searches found no conclusive evidence, and the reports remain debated. The US Fish and Wildlife Service proposed declaring the species extinct in 2021.
