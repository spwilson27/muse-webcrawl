World energy supply and consumption

World energy supply and consumption refers to the global production and use of energy. Since the Industrial Revolution, annual consumption has steadily increased, reaching roughly 600 exajoules per year in the early 2020s. Energy production and consumption are central to the world economy and to greenhouse gas emissions, because the dominant sources remain fossil fuels: coal, oil and natural gas.

Primary energy supply

Total primary energy supply is dominated by oil (roughly 30%), coal (roughly 27%) and natural gas (roughly 24%), with hydropower, nuclear power and modern renewables — wind, solar, geothermal and biomass — making up the remainder. Renewable sources have grown rapidly since the 2010s, driven by steep cost declines in solar photovoltaics and wind turbines, but absolute fossil fuel use has continued to rise in most years.

Consumption by sector

Final energy is consumed mainly in industry, transport, buildings (residential and commercial) and agriculture. Electricity accounts for about a fifth of final energy use, a share that is growing as transport and heating electrify. Per-capita consumption varies enormously: residents of North America and the Gulf states use many times the world average, while hundreds of millions of people still lack access to electricity.

Trends and outlook

The International Energy Agency's World Energy Outlook scenarios explore pathways ranging from continued fossil-fuel growth to net-zero emissions by 2050. Under announced-pledges scenarios, global coal demand peaks in the 2020s, oil demand peaks around 2030, and solar becomes the largest single source of electricity during the 2030s.
