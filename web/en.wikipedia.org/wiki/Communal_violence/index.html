Communal violence

Communal violence involves conflict between groups identified by religion, ethnicity or other communal identities.

Communal violence is a form of violence perpetrated across ethnic or communal lines, in which the violent parties feel solidarity for their respective groups and victims are chosen based upon group membership. The term includes conflicts, riots and other forms of violence between communities of different religious faiths or ethnic origins.

Causes and dynamics

Scholars identify contributing factors including competition for political power and economic resources, rumours and provocative incidents, demographic anxieties, and the actions of political entrepreneurs who mobilise communal identities. Once violence begins, breakdown of state protection, segregation of neighbourhoods and cycles of revenge can sustain it.

Historical examples

Notable episodes include the Partition riots in India and Pakistan in 1947, Hindu–Muslim riots in post-independence India such as those in Gujarat in 2002, conflicts between Sinhalese and Tamils in Sri Lanka, Buddhist–Muslim violence in Myanmar, and sectarian violence between Sunni and Shia communities in Iraq and Pakistan. Communal violence has also occurred in Europe, Africa and Southeast Asia in varied forms.

Responses

Responses range from policing and curfews in the short term to peace committees, inter-communal civic networks, prosecutions and truth-telling in the longer term. Research by scholars such as Ashutosh Varshney suggests that cities with dense inter-ethnic civic associations are more resistant to riots than those where civic life is segregated.
