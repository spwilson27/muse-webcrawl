The British Rail Class 700 is an electric multiple unit passenger train built by Siemens Mobility at Krefeld in Germany for the Thameslink network. Part of the Desiro City family, the fleet comprises 60 eight-car Class 700/0 and 55 twelve-car Class 700/1 units, ordered under the Thameslink Programme to operate the expanded cross-London services. The first train entered service in June 2016.

Design

The trains are dual-voltage, operating on 25 kV AC overhead lines north of the Thames and 750 V DC third rail to the south, with automatic changeover at stations such as Farringdon. They were designed for high-capacity, metro-style operation through the central London core at up to 24 trains per hour, supported by automatic train operation in the central section. Interiors are fixed-formation with wide gangways, extensive standing space, real-time passenger information screens showing loading information, and standard-class accommodation with a small first-class section retained after initial controversy over its absence.

Service

Class 700s operate the full Thameslink network, linking destinations such as Bedford, Peterborough, Cambridge and Luton with Brighton, Gatwick Airport, Sevenoaks, Horsham and Rainham via London Bridge and St Pancras International. The fleet is owned by Cross London Trains and maintained by Siemens at depots at Hornsey and Three Bridges. The introduction of the fleet allowed older Class 319, 377 and 387 units to cascade to other operators.
