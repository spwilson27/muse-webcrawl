The California State Senate is the upper house of the California State Legislature, the lower house being the California State Assembly. The Senate has 40 members, each representing a district of about 980,000 residents — larger in population than a United States congressional district. Senators serve four-year terms, with half the seats contested every two years.

Composition and leadership

By the state constitution, the Lieutenant Governor of California is the President of the Senate and may cast tie-breaking votes, but day-to-day leadership rests with the President pro tempore, elected by the senators. Since Proposition 28 (2012), members are limited to 12 years of total legislative service. The Senate chamber in the Capitol is decorated in red, following the tradition of the British House of Lords, and bears the motto Senatoris est civitatis libertatem tueri ("It is a senator's duty to protect the liberty of the people").

History

The Senate first sat in 1849–1850. For much of California's history, Senate districts were based on counties, giving rural areas disproportionate weight; the United States Supreme Court's reapportionment decisions of the 1960s (Reynolds v. Sims applied to state legislatures) forced California to redraw districts by population in 1965. The chamber was the political base of powerful figures such as Hiram Johnson, who became governor and then a United States senator.

Functions

The Senate shares the power to pass legislation and the budget with the Assembly, and uniquely confirms many gubernatorial appointments. A two-thirds vote of both houses overrides a veto or places constitutional amendments before voters.
