Wholesale market

A wholesale market is a market where goods are sold in bulk to retailers, industrial or commercial users, or other wholesalers, rather than to final consumers. Wholesale markets concentrate supply and demand, set reference prices, and form a central link in the distribution chain between producers and retailers.

Produce markets

The term most often denotes large physical marketplaces for fresh food — such as Rungis outside Paris, the largest wholesale food market in the world; the Toyosu Market in Tokyo for seafood; and historic markets like London's New Covent Garden and Smithfield. Farmers and importers sell in bulk, typically in the early morning, to greengrocers, restaurants, caterers, and smaller distributors. Prices struck in major wholesale markets serve as benchmarks for the trade.

Other wholesale markets

By extension, "wholesale market" also describes bulk trading in electricity (generators selling to suppliers in wholesale power markets), finance (interbank and institutional trading), and telecommunications capacity. Such markets share the defining features of professional participants, large transaction sizes, and resale rather than final consumption.
