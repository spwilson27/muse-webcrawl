Discretionary income

Discretionary income is the amount of an individual's income that is left for spending, investing, or saving after all taxes and necessities have been paid. Necessities include items such as rent or mortgage payments, utilities, insurance, food, minimum loan payments, and other expenses required for maintaining a basic standard of living. Unlike disposable income, which is simply income after taxes, discretionary income accounts for the cost of necessities as well.

Definition and measurement

Discretionary income can be expressed as:

Discretionary income = Gross income − taxes − all compelled payments (bills, necessities)

Because the definition of "necessities" varies between households and analysts, estimates of discretionary income are less standardized than those of disposable income. Consumer research firms and economists nevertheless track it as an indicator of consumer purchasing power, since spending on non-essential goods and services — travel, entertainment, dining out, luxury goods — is funded from discretionary income.

Economic significance

Changes in aggregate discretionary income affect consumer spending patterns. During economic downturns, households typically cut discretionary spending first, so industries dependent on it tend to be more cyclical. Conversely, rising discretionary income is associated with increased spending on leisure, elective healthcare, education, and charitable giving, as well as higher rates of voluntary saving and investment.
