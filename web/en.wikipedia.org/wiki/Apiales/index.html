The Apiales are an order of flowering plants placed within the asterid group of dicotyledons. The order is best known for two large families: the Apiaceae (carrot family) and the Araliaceae (ginseng family).

Characteristics

Members of the Apiaceae are famous for their compound umbel inflorescences and include many familiar herbs and vegetables — carrot, celery, parsley, parsnip, coriander, cumin and fennel — as well as deadly poisons such as hemlock. Araliaceae are mostly woody plants, including ivy, ginseng and the umbrella tree. In the APG IV system the order contains seven families, the others being Griseliniaceae, Myodocarpaceae, Pennantiaceae, Pittosporaceae and Torricelliaceae.

Classification

Earlier systems such as Cronquist's placed the core families in the same order, though some treatments put Pittosporaceae elsewhere. Molecular phylogenetic work has confirmed the Apiales as a well-supported clade within the campanulids, closely related to the Dipsacales and Aquifoliales.
