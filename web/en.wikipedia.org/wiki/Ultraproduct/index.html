Ultraproduct

In model theory, the ultraproduct is a quotient of the direct product of a family of mathematical structures by an ultrafilter. Given structures Mi indexed by a set I and an ultrafilter U on I, the ultraproduct is the set of equivalence classes of tuples in the Cartesian product, where two tuples are equivalent if they agree at "almost all" indices, as measured by U.

Properties

By Łoś's theorem, a first-order sentence is true in the ultraproduct if and only if the set of indices where it is true in the factors belongs to the ultrafilter. When all the factors are the same structure, the ultraproduct is called an ultrapower. If the ultrafilter is principal (concentrated at a single index) the ultraproduct is isomorphic to that factor; non-principal ultrafilters yield genuinely new structures.

Uses

Ultraproducts are used to prove the compactness theorem of first-order logic, to construct nonstandard models of arithmetic and analysis, and in abstract algebra and functional analysis (for example, ultraproducts of Banach spaces and of finite fields, which produce pseudofinite fields).
