Zweigelt

Species: Vitis vinifera

Origin: Austria, 1922

Parentage: Blaufränkisch × St. Laurent

Zweigelt is a red wine grape variety created in 1922 by Friedrich (Fritz) Zweigelt at the federal viticulture institute in Klosterneuburg, Austria, by crossing Blaufränkisch with St. Laurent. It is the most widely planted red grape in Austria.

Characteristics

The variety ripens early, yields reliably and resists frost and disease better than many alternatives. Its wines are typically violet-red, fruity and soft, with cherry and berry flavours, and are drunk young as well as in blends and oak-aged styles; it is also used for rosé.

Name

Originally called Rotburger, the grape was renamed in honour of its breeder in 1975. Debate has since arisen over the name because of Zweigelt's involvement with the Nazi Party, and some producers have campaigned to restore the name Rotburger, so far without official change.
