The 32X is an add-on for the Sega Genesis video game console, released by Sega in November 1994. Code-named "Project Mars", it inserted into the cartridge slot of the Genesis and added two 32-bit Hitachi SH-2 processors, enhanced graphics capabilities and its own library of cartridge games.

History

Sega developed the 32X as a cheaper bridge to the 32-bit era, worried that the upcoming Sega Saturn would be too expensive for the American market. It launched at $159.99 with ports and originals such as Doom, Virtua Racing Deluxe, Virtua Fighter, Star Wars Arcade and Knuckles' Chaotix. Combined with the Sega CD, certain games could use a combined "Super 32X CD" format.

Failure

The 32X is regarded as a commercial failure. It launched only months before the Saturn, confusing consumers; third-party support dwindled quickly, and only 40 games were released. Sega discontinued it in 1996 to focus on the Saturn. The episode damaged Sega's reputation with retailers and consumers and is often cited among the worst console launches in industry history.
