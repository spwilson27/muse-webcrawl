Neues Museum

The Neues Museum ("New Museum") is a listed building on Museum Island in the historic centre of Berlin, Germany. Built from 1843 to 1855 to designs by Friedrich August Stüler, it was the second museum constructed on the island, intended to relieve the overflowing Altes Museum. Badly damaged in the Second World War, it stood as a ruin for decades before a celebrated restoration by David Chipperfield Architects; it reopened in 2009.

History

The museum originally housed the Egyptian collection, casts of classical sculpture, ethnographic and prehistoric collections, and prints and drawings, arranged as a universal survey of art and antiquity with richly painted themed halls. It closed in 1939 and suffered severe bomb damage in air raids of 1943 and 1945; the northwest wing and parts of the great stair hall were destroyed.

Restoration

After German reunification, the museum was rebuilt under the Museum Island masterplan. David Chipperfield's approach preserved the damaged fabric — including scarred brickwork and surviving fresco fragments — while inserting restrained new elements in matching forms; the destroyed northwest wing and southeast dome were reconstructed. The project won international acclaim, including the EU Prize for Contemporary Architecture (Mies van der Rohe Award) in 2011.

Collections

Today the Neues Museum houses the Egyptian Museum and Papyrus Collection and the Museum of Prehistory and Early History. Its star exhibit is the painted limestone bust of Queen Nefertiti (c. 1345 BC), discovered at Amarna and displayed in the north dome room. Other highlights include the Berlin Gold Hat, a Bronze Age ceremonial cone, and the skull of the Le Moustier Neanderthal.
