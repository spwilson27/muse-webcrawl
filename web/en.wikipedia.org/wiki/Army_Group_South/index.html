Army Group South

Army Group South was one of three army groups that invaded the Soviet Union in 1941.

Army Group South (German: Heeresgruppe Süd) was the name of several German Army Groups during the Second World War. Best known is the formation that took part in Operation Barbarossa, the invasion of the Soviet Union, and fought on the southern sector of the Eastern Front.

Campaigns

An Army Group South under Gerd von Rundstedt first fought in the invasion of Poland in 1939. Re-formed for Barbarossa in June 1941, again under Rundstedt, it advanced through Ukraine, fighting the great encirclement battle of Kiev (September 1941) before being checked at Rostov; Rundstedt was relieved after authorising a withdrawal.

In the summer of 1942 the army group split into Army Groups A and B for Case Blue: Army Group A drove toward the Caucasus while Army Group B advanced on Stalingrad, where the Sixth Army was destroyed in the winter of 1942–43. A re-formed Army Group South under Erich von Manstein counterattacked at Kharkov in February–March 1943 and fought the Battle of Kursk in July 1943, then retreated across Ukraine under pressure, being renamed Army Group North Ukraine in April 1944.

A final Army Group South was created in September 1944 from Army Group South Ukraine; it fought in Hungary (including the defence of Budapest) and Austria until the end of the war in May 1945.
