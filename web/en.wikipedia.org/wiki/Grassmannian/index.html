Grassmannian

The Grassmannian Gr(2, 3) parametrizes all 2-dimensional subspaces of a 3-dimensional space.

In mathematics, the Grassmannian Gr(k, V) is a space that parameterizes all k-dimensional linear subspaces of the n-dimensional vector space V. For example, the Grassmannian Gr(1, n) is the space of lines through the origin in V, so it is the projective space Pn−1. The Grassmannians are named in honour of Hermann Grassmann, who introduced the notion in the 1840s. They are important objects in algebraic geometry, representation theory, and mathematical physics.

Properties

The Grassmannian Gr(k, n) is a smooth projective algebraic variety of dimension k(n − k). It can be embedded into a projective space via the Plücker embedding, which maps a k-plane to the line spanned by the wedge product of a basis, expressed in Plücker coordinates. The image is cut out by a system of quadratic equations known as the Plücker relations.

dim Gr(k, n) = k(n − k)

Grassmannians are homogeneous spaces: the general linear group GL(n) acts transitively on them, and Gr(k, n) may be identified with the quotient GL(n) / P, where P is a parabolic subgroup. In the real and complex cases they also arise as quotients of orthogonal and unitary groups, giving them the structure of compact manifolds with rich topology. Their cohomology rings are described by Schubert calculus, in which Schubert varieties correspond to combinatorial data such as partitions and Young tableaux.

Generalizations

Related constructions include flag varieties, which parameterize chains of nested subspaces, and the affine Grassmannian, an infinite-dimensional variety central to the geometric Langlands programme. Grassmannians also appear in computer vision and signal processing, where collections of subspaces are treated as data points on the Grassmann manifold.
