Spit (landform)


A spit extending from the coast.

A spit or sandspit is a narrow coastal land formation, a type of bar or beach, that develops where a re-entrant occurs, such as at the mouth of a bay or river estuary, by the process of longshore drift. Sediment carried along the coast is deposited where the drift weakens, building a finger of sand or shingle that extends from the headland into open water.

Formation

Longshore drift moves beach material along the shore. Where the coastline changes direction abruptly, the drift continues in its original line, depositing sediment in deeper water and creating a spit. The far end of a spit often curves landward — a recurved tip — because waves refract around the end. If a spit grows across a bay, it may form a bar; a spit joined to an island forms a tombolo.

Examples

Well-known spits include Spurn Head on the Humber estuary in England, the Curonian Spit between Lithuania and Russia, Farewell Spit in New Zealand, and the Arabat Spit in the Sea of Azov, one of the longest in the world at about 110 kilometres.
