Central Alaskan Yup'ik (self-name Yugtun) is a Yupik language of the Eskimo–Aleut family, spoken by the Yup'ik people in western and southwestern Alaska, from Norton Sound south to the Alaska Peninsula and east to Prince William Sound. It is the most widely spoken indigenous language in Alaska, with roughly 10,000 speakers.

Dialects

The language has several dialects, including General Central Yup'ik, Norton Sound, Hooper Bay–Chevak (Cup'ik), Nunivak (Cup'ig) and Egegik. Hooper Bay–Chevak and Nunivak differ enough that their speakers use distinct self-names and orthographic conventions.

Grammar

Central Alaskan Yup'ik is polysynthetic: a single long word can express what English requires a whole sentence to say, by combining a root with derivational and inflectional suffixes. Its morphology was documented in detail by linguists working with the Alaska Native Language Center, and a standard orthography in the Latin alphabet was developed in the 1960s.

Revitalisation

Yup'ik is taught in immersion programmes such as the Ayaprun Elitnaurvik school in Bethel, and bilingual education is widespread in the Yukon–Kuskokwim Delta region.
