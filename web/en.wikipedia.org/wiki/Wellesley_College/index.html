Wellesley College is a private women's liberal arts college in Wellesley, Massachusetts, United States. Founded in 1870 by Henry and Pauline Durant and opened in 1875, it is one of the Seven Sisters colleges and is consistently ranked among the top liberal arts colleges in the United States.

Campus and academics

The 500-acre campus, designed in part by Frederick Law Olmsted Jr., overlooks Lake Waban and is noted for its landscape and Collegiate Gothic architecture. Wellesley enrols about 2,400 students and offers more than 50 departmental and interdepartmental majors, with cross-registration at MIT and other Boston-area institutions. Its endowment is among the largest of any liberal arts college.

Notable alumnae

Graduates include former U.S. Secretary of State Hillary Clinton and Madeleine Albright, journalist Diane Sawyer, and Chinese writer Bing Xin. Clinton delivered the commencement address in 1969 as the first student speaker in the college's history.
