Fresh cheese

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Rounds of fresh white cheese draining on a cloth-lined rack
Rounds of fresh white cheese draining on a cloth-lined rack

Fresh cheese is cheese that has not been aged or ripened — eaten within days of production, mild, moist and perishable. The category spans mozzarella and burrata, ricotta, cottage cheese, cream cheese, quark, paneer, queso fresco, feta-style brined whites (often classed semi-fresh), chèvre and mascarpone — the original form of all cheese, before affinage turned accident into art.

Making

Fresh cheeses are made by acidifying milk (with cultures, lemon or vinegar) or light renneting, draining the whey, and salting: paneer is pressed hot-acid curd; ricotta ('re-cooked') is reboiled whey protein; mozzarella is stretched in hot water (pasta filata). High moisture means short shelf life and gentle flavour — milk and salt forward, funk absent.

Place in cuisines

Fresh cheeses are everyday staples: queso fresco across Latin America, paneer through South Asia, quark in German baking, cottage cheese in Anglo diet culture, mozzarella at pizza's heart — and the burrata boom of the 2010s showed even the simplest cheese can become a luxury signifier.
