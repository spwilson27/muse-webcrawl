The Presbyterian Church in America (PCA) is the second-largest Presbyterian church body in the United States of America, after the Presbyterian Church (USA). It is a conservative, evangelical denomination in the Reformed tradition, organised in 1973 by congregations that separated from the Presbyterian Church in the United States over theological differences, including the ordination of women and perceived liberal trends.

Beliefs

The PCA subscribes to the Westminster Confession of Faith and catechisms as its doctrinal standard, subordinate to Scripture. It practices Presbyterian polity, with congregations governed by elders and connected through presbyteries and a general assembly.

History

The denomination held its first general assembly in Birmingham, Alabama, in December 1973. It later absorbed the Reformed Presbyterian Church, Evangelical Synod (1982) and maintains agencies for missions, education, and church planting, including Mission to the World and Covenant Theological Seminary.

Membership

The PCA reports roughly 1,900 congregations and missions and about 390,000 members, concentrated in the southeastern United States but present nationwide.
