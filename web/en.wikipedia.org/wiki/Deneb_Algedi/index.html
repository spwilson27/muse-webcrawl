Deneb Algedi, Bayer designation Delta Capricorni (δ Cap, δ Capricorni), is a multi-star system in the constellation of Capricornus. Its traditional name derives from the Arabic dhanab al-jady, meaning "the tail of the goat". With an apparent magnitude of about 2.8, it is the brightest star in Capricornus despite its delta designation.

Properties

Deneb Algedi lies about 39 light-years from the Sun. The primary is a white giant of spectral type A, an Am star with enhanced metallic spectral lines. It forms an eclipsing binary with a close companion in an orbit of just over one day; as the companion passes in front of and behind the primary, the system's brightness dips noticeably, varying between magnitudes 2.70 and 3.05. The primary is also a Delta Scuti-type pulsating variable. Two much fainter visual companions are listed in double-star catalogues but are probably not physically bound to the inner pair.

The star system's proximity and brightness make Deneb Algedi a useful reference in the otherwise faint constellation of the Sea-Goat, where it marks the easternmost (tail) point of the constellation's triangle.

Nomenclature

In 2016 the International Astronomical Union organised a Working Group on Star Names to catalogue and standardise proper names for stars; the name Deneb Algedi was approved for the primary component of the system. The star should not be confused with the far more luminous Deneb (Alpha Cygni), whose name is the source of a whole family of "Deneb-" star names, including Deneb Kaitos, Deneb Dulfim, and Denebola.
