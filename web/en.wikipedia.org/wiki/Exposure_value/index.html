Exposure value

Exposure value (EV) is a number that represents a combination of a camera's shutter speed and f-number such that all combinations that yield the same exposure have the same EV (for any fixed scene luminance). EV is defined so that a one-unit increase corresponds to a halving of the exposure — one "stop". The concept was developed by the German shutter manufacturer Friedrich Deckel in the 1950s.

Definition

Formally, EV is defined as EV = log2(N²/t), where N is the relative aperture (f-number) and t is the exposure time in seconds. Thus f/1.0 at 1 second corresponds to EV 0, and f/16 at 1/125 s corresponds to approximately EV 15, the value associated with bright sunlight (the "sunny 16 rule").

Uses

Exposure values are used in exposure meters and the APEX system of exposure calculation. For a given film speed (ISO), a meter reading in EV indicates the combination of aperture and shutter speed to use. At ISO 100, typical values range from EV −6 in very dim conditions to EV 16 in bright snow or beach scenes. EV also underlies the light value (LV) scale and the exposure compensation settings marked in EVs on cameras.
