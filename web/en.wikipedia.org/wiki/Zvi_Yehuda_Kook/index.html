Zvi Yehuda HaCohen Kook (Hebrew: צבי יהודה הכהן קוק; 23 April 1891 – 9 March 1982) was an Orthodox rabbi and religious Zionist leader, the son of Rabbi Abraham Isaac Kook, the first Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi of Mandate Palestine. As head of the Mercaz HaRav yeshiva in Jerusalem for three decades, he became the spiritual mentor of the religious Zionist settler movement, and his students founded Gush Emunim.

Life

Born in Žeimelis, Lithuania, Kook moved with his family to Jaffa in 1904 when his father became rabbi of the town. He studied with his father and at the Torat Hayim yeshiva in Jerusalem, and served as the administrator of his father's yeshiva after its founding. For decades he devoted himself to editing and publishing his father's extensive writings, many of which had remained in manuscript.

In 1952, after his father's death, he became Rosh Yeshiva of Mercaz HaRav and transformed it into the flagship institution of religious Zionism, attracting thousands of students from the Bnei Akiva youth movement.

Influence

Zvi Yehuda taught that the State of Israel was a divine instrument of redemption (atchalta de'geulah, "the beginning of redemption") and that settling the whole Land of Israel — including the territories captured in the Six-Day War of 1967 — was a religious commandment. His famous address on the eve of Independence Day in 1967, weeks before the war, in which he lamented the divided land, was later read by followers as prophetic.

His students — among them rabbis Zvi Tau, Shlomo Aviner, and Hanan Porat — led the settlement of the West Bank (Judea and Samaria), Gaza Strip, and Sinai after 1967 and founded Gush Emunim in 1974. His interpretations of his father's philosophy remain central to Hardal (Haredi-religious-nationalist) Judaism in Israel.
