The Palamidi Fortress (Greek: Παλαμήδι) is a fortress on the hill of Palamidi, 216 metres above the town of Nafplio in the Peloponnese, Greece. Built by the Venetians during their second occupation of the area (1686–1715), it is a vast and well-preserved castle in the Venetian style, and one of the major achievements of Venetian fortification architecture.

Construction

The fortress was built between 1711 and 1714 to designs of the engineers Giaxich and Lasalle, based on plans by the Provveditore Generale Agostino Sagredo. It consists of eight self-contained bastions, originally named for Venetian provveditori and saints (San Agostino, San Luca and others), later renamed for Greek heroes such as Miltiades, Leonidas, Phocion, Achilles, Themistocles and Epaminondas. Each bastion could be defended independently.

History

Completed only a year before the Ottoman reconquest, the fortress fell to the Turks in 1715 after its capture of the town. In November 1822, during the Greek War of Independence, Staikos Staikopoulos led a small force that scaled the walls by night and seized the fortress, a victory celebrated as a turning point for Nafplio, which became the first capital of modern Greece. In the 1830s–1840s the fortress served as a prison; the revolutionary hero Theodoros Kolokotronis was imprisoned in its Miltiades bastion in 1834.

Access

A monumental stairway of 999 steps (by tradition — the actual count is usually given as 857) climbs from the town to the gate; a road also reaches the eastern entrance. The fortress offers views over Nafplio, the Argolic Gulf and the island fortress of Bourtzi.
