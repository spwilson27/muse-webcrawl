Lead Belly

Huddie William Ledbetter (January 20, 1888 – December 6, 1949), better known as Lead Belly, was an American folk and blues singer notable for his strong vocals, virtuosity on the twelve-string guitar, and the folk standards he introduced, including "Goodnight, Irene", "Midnight Special" and "Cotton Fields".

Life and career

Born in Louisiana, Ledbetter worked as a musician in Texas, performed with Blind Lemon Jefferson, and served several prison terms, including at Angola Prison in Louisiana, where folklorists John and Alan Lomax recorded him in 1933. Pardoned and taken north by the Lomaxes, he recorded extensively and became influential on the folk revival.

Legacy

His songs were covered by the Weavers, Nirvana ("Where Did You Sleep Last Night"), Creedence Clearwater Revival and many others. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1988. See Blues and American folk music.
