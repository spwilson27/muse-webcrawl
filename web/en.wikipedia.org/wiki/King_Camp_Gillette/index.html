King Camp Gillette (January 5, 1855 – July 9, 1932) was an American businessman and inventor who invented the best-selling version of the safety razor with disposable stamped blades. He founded the company that became the Gillette brand of Procter & Gamble. Gillette's innovation — a cheap, thin, disposable double-edged steel blade — created the "razor and blades" business model in which the handle is sold cheaply and profits are made on repeat sales of the consumable blades, and made daily shaving at home practical for millions of men.

Early life

Gillette was born in Fond du Lac, Wisconsin, and raised in Chicago. His parents were inventors and patent agents, and after the Great Chicago Fire of 1871 the family moved to New York City. Gillette became a travelling salesman, selling, among other products, the products of the Crown Cork and Seal Company, where he worked for its founder William Painter. Painter — inventor of the disposable crown bottle cap — advised Gillette to invent something that, like the bottle cap, would be used once and thrown away, so that customers would keep coming back.

The safety razor

According to Gillette's own account, the idea came to him in 1895 while shaving with a dull straight razor: he envisioned a thin sheet of steel, ground to an edge on both sides, clamped in a simple holder — cheap enough to throw away when dull. Existing razors used heavy wedge blades requiring skilled stropping and honing. Making the idea work required machining very thin sheet steel to a fine edge, which experts initially considered impossible. In 1899–1901 the machinist William Emery Nickerson developed the necessary grinding and sharpening machinery, and Gillette founded the American Safety Razor Company in Boston in 1901, soon renamed the Gillette Safety Razor Company. His US patent (No. 775,134) was granted in November 1904.

Production began in 1903: the company sold 51 razors and 168 blades that year. In 1904 it sold 90,884 razors and 123,648 blades, aided by giving razors away or selling them at a loss through banks, hardware stores and mail order, and by contracts to supply the US armed forces in the First World War, which put a Gillette set in every American soldier's kit. By Gillette's death the company's razors and blades were sold worldwide, and his face appeared on blade packets.

Social thought

Gillette was also a Utopian socialist writer. In The Human Drift (1894) and later works he proposed replacing competitive capitalism with a single great corporation owned by the public, and imagined the population concentrated in an enormous city, "Metropolis", powered by Niagara Falls. He offered former president Theodore Roosevelt a million dollars to head his proposed corporation; Roosevelt declined. He spent much of his later fortune on these ideas and on land in California, and he died in Los Angeles in 1932 with his fortune much reduced by the stock market crash.
