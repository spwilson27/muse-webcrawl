The president of the Republic of Iraq is the head of state of Iraq. Under the 2005 Constitution, the president is described as the symbol of the country's unity and the guarantor of the constitution; the office is largely ceremonial, with real executive power exercised by the prime minister. The president is elected by a two-thirds majority of the Council of Representatives for a four-year term, renewable once, and carries out duties such as ratifying treaties and laws passed by parliament, issuing pardons on the recommendation of the prime minister, and formally tasking the prime-ministerial nominee of the largest parliamentary bloc with forming a government.

By an unwritten power-sharing convention (the muhasasa system) in place since 2005, the presidency is held by a Kurd, the premiership by a Shia Arab, and the speakership of parliament by a Sunni Arab.

History

Iraq became a republic on 14 July 1958, when the Hashemite monarchy was overthrown in a military coup. Muhammad Najib ar-Ruba'i served as chairman of the Sovereignty Council and first head of state, alongside Prime Minister Abd al-Karim Qasim. Presidents Abd al-Salam Arif (1963–1966) and his brother Abd al-Rahman Arif (1966–1968) followed. After the Ba'ath Party seized power in 1968, Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr became president; he was succeeded in 1979 by Saddam Hussein, under whom the presidency became the centre of an authoritarian state. Saddam was removed by the US-led invasion of 2003.

Post-2003 presidents

Under the interim and permanent constitutions, the presidency passed to Ghazi Mashal Ajil al-Yawer (2004–2005), then to Jalal Talabani (2005–2014), the first Kurdish president, followed by Fuad Masum (2014–2018), Barham Salih (2018–2022), and Abdul Latif Rashid, elected by parliament on 13 October 2022 after a year of political deadlock following the 2021 elections.

Powers

The president ratifies laws and treaties, promulgates legislation enacted by the Council of Representatives, issues special pardons (except for crimes involving corruption, terrorism, or international crimes), accredits ambassadors, and is ceremonially the commander of the armed forces for honorific purposes. The president must charge the nominee of the largest parliamentary bloc with forming a cabinet within fifteen days of election.
