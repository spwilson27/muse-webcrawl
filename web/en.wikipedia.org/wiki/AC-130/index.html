Lockheed AC-130

AC-130
Role: Ground-attack gunship
Manufacturer: Lockheed / Lockheed Martin
First flight: 1966 (AC-130A)
Status: In service (AC-130J)
Primary user: United States Air Force

The Lockheed AC-130 is a heavily armed, long-endurance ground-attack aircraft developed by Lockheed (now Lockheed Martin) from the C-130 Hercules transport. Operated by the United States Air Force's Air Force Special Operations Command, it provides close air support, air interdiction and force protection, and is famous for its side-firing cannons.

Development

The gunship concept was tested during the Vietnam War under Project Gunship II; the first AC-130A conversions flew combat missions in 1967. Successive variants — AC-130H Spectre, AC-130U Spooky II and the current AC-130J Ghostrider — added sensors, precision-guided munitions and heavier cannon armament.

Armament

Classic variants carried a mix of 20 mm, 40 mm and 105 mm cannon firing from the port side as the aircraft flies a pylon turn around a target. The AC-130J adds a 30 mm cannon, the 105 mm howitzer, and wing-mounted precision weapons including GBU-39 small diameter bombs and AGM-176 Griffin missiles.

Operational history

AC-130s have served in Vietnam, Grenada, Panama, the Gulf War, Somalia, the Balkans, Afghanistan, Iraq and Syria, typically operating at night in support of special operations forces. See also C-130 Hercules.
