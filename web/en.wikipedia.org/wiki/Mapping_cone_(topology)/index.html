Mapping cone (topology)

In mathematics, specifically algebraic topology, the mapping cone of a continuous map f : X → Y of topological spaces is a quotient space formed by attaching the cone over X to Y along the map f. It is denoted Cf. Intuitively, one takes the cylinder over X, collapses one end to a point (forming the cone) and glues the other end to Y via f.

Definition

Given f : X → Y, the mapping cone is the quotient of the disjoint union of the cone CX = (X × [0,1]) / (X × {0}) with Y, where each point (x, 1) of the cone is identified with the point f(x) in Y. Equivalently, it is the homotopy pushout of the diagram Y ← X → CX, and so it is the standard way of turning a map into a cofibration up to homotopy.

Properties

The mapping cone fits the map into a Puppe sequence, a long sequence of spaces and maps that continues with the mapping cone of the inclusion of Y into Cf, which is homotopy equivalent to the suspension of X. On homology, the reduced homology of the mapping cone computes the relative homology of the pair, and the map f induces an isomorphism on homology precisely when the reduced homology of its cone vanishes. Analogous constructions exist for chain complexes in homological algebra, where the mapping cone of a chain map underlies distinguished triangles in derived categories.
