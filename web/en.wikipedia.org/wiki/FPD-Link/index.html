FPD-Link

FPD-Link SerDes used for automotive camera connections

FPD-Link (Flat Panel Display Link) is a serial data interface standard originally developed by National Semiconductor and now owned by Texas Instruments. It was introduced in the 1990s to carry digital video from a graphics controller to a flat-panel display, transmitting parallel RGB video data over a small number of twisted pairs using low-voltage differential signalling (LVDS).

Technology

The first generation of FPD-Link serialised 21 bits of parallel data (18-bit colour plus sync signals) onto three differential pairs, with a fourth pair carrying the clock, dramatically reducing cable width compared with parallel TTL interfaces. Later generations embedded the clock in the data stream. The interface is closely related to, and broadly compatible with, the OpenLDI standard.

FPD-Link II and III

FPD-Link II moved to a single differential pair with an embedded clock and support for a DC-balanced code, finding use in automotive infotainment. FPD-Link III, introduced around 2010, added a bidirectional back channel over the same coaxial or shielded twisted-pair cable, allowing control data, touch input and I2C/GPIO signals to be sent from the display or camera back to the processor, together with power-over-coax capability. FPD-Link III links are widely used for surround-view and driver-assistance cameras in vehicles.
