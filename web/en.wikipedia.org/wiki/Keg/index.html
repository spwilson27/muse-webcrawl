Keg

Modern stainless-steel kegs used for draught beer.

A keg is a small barrel, traditionally made of wood and today usually of stainless steel or aluminium, used to store, transport and serve beverages, most notably draught beer. Unlike a cask, a keg is pressurised with carbon dioxide (or a nitrogen/CO₂ mix) that drives the beer out when a tap is opened.

Construction and sizes

Modern kegs are cylindrical vessels with a single valve at the top (the Sankey or similar fitting) through which they are both filled and emptied, via a spear reaching to the bottom. Common sizes include the full-size US barrel keg (15.5 US gallons, the "half barrel"), the quarter barrel ("pony keg", 7.75 gal), and the Cornelius keg (5 gal) popular with homebrewers. In Europe, 30-litre and 50-litre kegs are standard.

Use

Kegs are filled at the brewery, shipped to bars and restaurants, and connected to a draught system with cooling and gas supply. Because the beer is sealed and pressurised, it keeps longer than cask ale once tapped. Historically, small kegs also held gunpowder and nails ("keg" remains a unit term in those trades).
