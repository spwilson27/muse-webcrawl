The Vistula Spit (Polish: Mierzeja Wiślana; Russian: Балтийская коса, Baltiyskaya kosa; German: Frische Nehrung) is a long, narrow sand spit, or peninsula, stretching about 65 kilometres (40 mi) along the south-eastern shore of the Baltic Sea. It separates the Vistula Lagoon from the Gulf of Gdańsk and is divided between Poland in the south-west and the Kaliningrad Oblast of Russia in the north-east, with the border of the two countries crossing it.

Geography

The spit ranges from about 300 metres to 9 kilometres in width and consists of wind-built dunes, the highest of which is Wielbłądzi Garb ("Camel's Hump", 49.5 m) on the Polish side. Much of the spit is covered in pine forest planted in the 19th century to stabilise the moving sands. On the Polish side lie the resort towns of Krynica Morska, Stegna and Sztutowo; the Russian part, known as the Baltiysk Spit, is a restricted military zone containing the town of Baltiysk and one of Russia's largest air bases in the exclave.

History

Until 1945 the spit belonged to East Prussia. During the Second World War, the Stutthof concentration camp was located near the base of the spit on its landward side. In 1945 the frozen lagoon behind the spit served as an escape route for German civilians fleeing the Red Army. After the war, the Potsdam Agreement divided the spit between Poland and the Soviet Union.

In 2019–2022, Poland cut a shipping channel through its part of the spit — the Vistula Spit canal — so that ships could reach the port of Elbląg without transiting the Strait of Baltiysk in Russian waters.
