Lake Geneva

Lake Geneva (French: Lac Léman) is a crescent-shaped lake on the Rhône River at the northern foot of the Alps, shared by Switzerland (about 60%) and France (about 40%). It is Western Europe's largest lake by surface area — about 580 km² — and reaches about 310 metres deep (figures approximate).

The Rhône enters at the eastern end near Villeneuve and leaves at Geneva in the west. Cities on its shores include Geneva, Lausanne, Montreux and Évian-les-Bains; the Lavaux vineyard terraces on the north shore are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and Château de Chillon stands on an island near Montreux. The lake was formed by glacial action during the last ice age.
