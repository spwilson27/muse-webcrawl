Thomas Charles

Thomas Charles
Born: 14 October 1755, Llanfihangel Abercowin, Wales
Died: 5 October 1814, Bala, Wales
Occupation: Clergyman, educator

Thomas Charles (14 October 1755 – 5 October 1814) was a Welsh Calvinistic Methodist clergyman of great influence in promoting education in Wales, and one of the founders of the British and Foreign Bible Society. He is often known as Thomas Charles of Bala.

Early life and ministry

Charles was born at Llanfihangel Abercowin, near St Clears in Carmarthenshire, and educated at Llanddowror and Carmarthen Academy before studying theology at Jesus College, Oxford. Ordained in the Church of England, he served as a curate in Somerset and then at Llanymawddwy and Bala in Wales. His evangelical convictions brought him into conflict with church authorities, and in 1784 he joined the Calvinistic Methodists while continuing to exercise a wide ministry.

Sunday schools and the circulating schools

Disturbed by the illiteracy of the Welsh poor, Charles organised a system of circulating charity schools, in which a teacher stayed in a village for a limited period — often about three months — teaching children and adults to read Welsh, especially the Bible, before moving on. When the circulating schools proved insufficient, he adopted and greatly expanded the Sunday school model, personally training teachers and preparing Welsh-language instructional materials. His schools are credited with transforming literacy in Wales.

The Bible Society and Mary Jones

A famous episode in Charles's life concerns Mary Jones, a young girl who walked some 26 miles barefoot across the mountains to Bala in 1800 to buy a Welsh Bible from him with money she had saved for six years. Charles was so moved that he resolved that Wales, and then the world, needed an organisation to supply affordable Bibles. He raised the idea in London in 1802, and in 1804 the British and Foreign Bible Society was founded, with Charles as a leading figure.

Works and legacy

Charles produced the first Welsh biblical dictionary, Geiriadur Ysgrythyrol (1805), catechisms, and theological works, and he edited the periodical Y Drysorfa Ysbrydol. He played a central part in the ordination of the first Calvinistic Methodist ministers in 1811, which led to the formation of a separate denomination. His grave is at Llanycil, near Bala, and he is commemorated by a statue in the town.
