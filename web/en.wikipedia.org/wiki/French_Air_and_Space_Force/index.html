French Air and Space Force

The Dassault Rafale is the force's principal combat aircraft.

The French Air and Space Force (French: Armée de l'air et de l'espace) is the air and space force of the French Armed Forces. Formed in 1909 as the Service Aéronautique within the French Army, it became an independent service, the Armée de l'Air, in 1934 — making France one of the first countries to establish an independent air force. It was renamed the Air and Space Force in September 2020, reflecting the creation of a French Space Command in 2019.

Role and equipment

The force numbers roughly 40,000 personnel and operates about 500 aircraft. Its combat fleet centres on the Dassault Rafale multirole fighter, supplemented by Mirage 2000 variants, Airbus A330 MRTT tankers, A400M and C-130 transports, and E-2C and AWACS-type surveillance aircraft. The Strategic Air Forces operate the airborne leg of France's nuclear deterrent, with Rafales armed with ASMP-A supersonic cruise missiles.

Operations

French air power has been deployed extensively since the Cold War, including over the Balkans, Afghanistan, Libya (2011), Mali and the Sahel, and against the Islamic State in Iraq and Syria. The space component oversees military satellites for communications, observation, and signals intelligence.
