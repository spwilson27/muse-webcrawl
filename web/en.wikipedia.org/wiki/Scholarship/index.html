Scholarship

A scholarship is a form of financial aid awarded to students to support their education. It is generally awarded on the basis of criteria reflecting the values of the donor or founder, which may include academic merit, athletic ability, financial need, or membership of a particular group. Unlike a loan, a scholarship does not have to be repaid.

Types

Merit scholarships reward academic, artistic or athletic achievement; need-based scholarships take the financial circumstances of the student and their family into account. Other common categories include student-specific scholarships for particular demographic groups, career-specific scholarships tied to an intended field of study, and college-specific awards offered by individual institutions. Well-known international programmes include the Rhodes Scholarship at the University of Oxford, the Fulbright Program, and the Gates Cambridge Scholarship.

Sponsoring

Scholarships may be funded by governments, universities, employers, professional associations, charitable foundations and private individuals. Some awards cover only tuition, while "full-ride" scholarships also cover accommodation, books, travel and living expenses.

Application

Applicants typically submit academic transcripts, references and personal essays, and may be interviewed. Competition for prestigious scholarships is intense, and deadlines often fall many months before the start of the academic year.
