Bibracte, a Gallic oppidum (fortified settlement), was the capital of the Aedui and one of the most important hillforts in Gaul. It was situated near modern Autun in Burgundy, France, on Mont Beuvray, which rises to 821 metres and straddles the communes of Saint-Léger-sous-Beuvray, Glux-en-Glenne, and Larochemillay.

History

Bibracte was founded in the late 2nd century BC. At its height it covered roughly 200 hectares inside double ramparts of the murus gallicus type and housed perhaps 5,000 to 10,000 inhabitants. Julius Caesar mentions Bibracte repeatedly in his Commentarii de Bello Gallico: in 58 BC the Battle of Bibracte, fought nearby, saw Caesar defeat the migrating Helvetii, and in 52 BC Vercingetorix was proclaimed commander of the Gallic coalition at Bibracte. Caesar wintered there in 52–51 BC and wrote part of his commentaries at the site.

After the Roman conquest, the Aedui were granted the status of allies and brothers of the Roman people. Around 5 BC, the population was transferred to the new Roman city of Augustodunum (modern Autun), about 25 km away, and Bibracte was gradually abandoned, although cult activity continued at its temple on the summit for some time.

Archaeology

The site was identified with Mont Beuvray in the 19th century, and excavations were conducted by Jacques Gabriel Bulliot between 1867 and 1895, then continued by his nephew Joseph Déchelette, a pioneer of European protohistory. Excavations resumed in 1984 and continue as an international research programme. Finds include workshops of bronze-workers, enamellers and potters, Mediterranean imports such as wine amphorae and fine pottery, monumental public buildings, and one of the largest collections of Celtic coinage. The Museum of Celtic Civilisation at the foot of the mountain presents the results of the excavations.

Layout

The oppidum was organised along a main street running up the mountain, with distinct districts for aristocratic residences (including large Roman-style houses at the Parc aux Chevaux), artisan quarters, warehouses, and public spaces including a monumental fountain basin. Excavation has shown a deliberate, planned urban layout unusual among Gallic oppida.
