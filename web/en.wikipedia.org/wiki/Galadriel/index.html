Galadriel is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. She appears in The Lord of the Rings, The Silmarillion and Unfinished Tales. An Elf of the Noldor, she was one of the leaders of the rebellion against the Valar and came to Middle-earth in the First Age; by the Third Age she is the co-ruler, with her husband Celeborn, of the woodland realm of Lothlórien, and the bearer of Nenya, one of the three Elven Rings of Power.

In The Lord of the Rings

The Fellowship of the Ring rests in Lothlórien after escaping Moria. Galadriel shows Frodo and Sam her mirror, a basin of water in which visions of past, present and possible futures appear; she is offered the One Ring by Frodo and refuses it, declaring "I pass the test. I will diminish, and go into the West, and remain Galadriel." She gives each member of the Fellowship a gift — including three strands of her hair to Gimli and the Phial of Galadriel to Frodo — and her people repel three assaults from Dol Guldur during the War of the Ring. After Sauron's defeat she sails from the Grey Havens with the other Ring-bearers, her ancient exile finally lifted.

Character

Tolkien described Galadriel as a penitent rebel, the greatest of the Noldor except Fëanor, and a figure of wisdom, beauty and power; scholars have compared her both to the Virgin Mary and to figures such as the Lady of courtly romance. She is played by Cate Blanchett in Peter Jackson's film trilogies and by Morfydd Clark in the television series The Rings of Power.
