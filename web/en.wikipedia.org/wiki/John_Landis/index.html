John Landis

John David Landis (born August 3, 1950) is an American filmmaker known for comedy classics of the late 1970s and 1980s, including National Lampoon's Animal House, The Blues Brothers, Trading Places, and Coming to America, as well as the horror-comedy An American Werewolf in London and Michael Jackson's "Thriller" music video.

Career

Born in Chicago and raised in Los Angeles, Landis began in the mail room at 20th Century Fox and worked as a stuntman and assistant director in Europe, including on spaghetti westerns. His debut Schlock (1973) led to directing The Kentucky Fried Movie (1977) and then Animal House (1978), a landmark gross-out comedy that launched John Belushi, with whom he also made The Blues Brothers (1980).

Twilight Zone accident

During filming of Twilight Zone: The Movie in 1982, a helicopter crash killed actor Vic Morrow and two child actors, Myca Dinh Le and Renee Shin-Yi Chen. Landis and four others were tried for involuntary manslaughter and acquitted in 1987, though civil suits were settled for millions and the accident led to stricter on-set safety rules.

Landis later directed hits such as Spies Like Us, ¡Three Amigos!, and Beverly Hills Cop III, and the music videos "Thriller" (1983) and "Black or White" (1991) for Michael Jackson.
