Polypodiaceae

Polypodiaceae is a family of ferns. In the Pteridophyte Phylogeny Group classification of 2016 (PPG I), the family includes around 65 genera and an estimated 1,650 species, divided into six subfamilies. It is one of the largest families of ferns and includes many of the epiphytic ferns of the tropics.

Description

Members of the family are mostly terrestrial or epiphytic ferns with creeping or erect rhizomes covered in scales. The fronds are usually simple to pinnately compound, and the sori (clusters of sporangia) are typically round and lack an indusium, a feature that distinguishes many members of the family.

Taxonomy

The circumscription of Polypodiaceae has varied considerably. Older treatments were much narrower, while modern molecular phylogenetic studies support a broader family that absorbs groups formerly treated as separate families, such as the Grammitidaceae (grammitid ferns).

Distribution and ecology

The family is nearly cosmopolitan but is most diverse in the tropics. Many species grow as epiphytes on tree trunks and branches in humid forests, while others colonise rocks or soil. Well-known genera include Platycerium (staghorn ferns), Microsorum, Phlebodium and Polypodium.

Uses

Many polypodiaceous ferns are grown as ornamental plants, including staghorn ferns mounted on bark and the blue star fern (Phlebodium aureum).
