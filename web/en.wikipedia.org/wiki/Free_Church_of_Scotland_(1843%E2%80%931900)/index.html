Free Church of Scotland (1843–1900)

The Free Church of Scotland was a Scottish denomination which was formed in 1843 by a large withdrawal from the established Church of Scotland in a schism known as the Disruption of 1843. In 1900 the vast majority of the Free Church merged with the United Presbyterian Church to form the United Free Church of Scotland, though a minority continued under the Free Church name — the origin of the present-day Free Church of Scotland.

The Disruption

The schism arose from the "Ten Years' Conflict" over patronage and the spiritual independence of the Church: evangelical ministers objected to landowners presenting ministers to parishes against the wishes of congregations, and to civil courts overriding church discipline. On 18 May 1843, at the General Assembly in Edinburgh, some 450 ministers — roughly a third of the Church of Scotland's ministry, led by Thomas Chalmers — walked out and constituted the Free Church at Tanfield Hall.

Growth and work

Under Chalmers's leadership the Free Church built, within a few years, over 700 churches, hundreds of schools, and a theological college (New College, Edinburgh), funded voluntarily through the Sustentation Fund. It became known for energetic home and foreign missions, including work in India, Africa and among Jewish communities, and for its strong commitment to education. Its members built much of their communal life around evangelical preaching and Presbyterian discipline.

Union of 1900

Doctrinal relaxation, including acceptance of a declaratory statement on the Westminster Confession, paved the way for union with the United Presbyterian Church in 1900. A small minority refused to enter the union and retained the Free Church's name and, after a famous House of Lords judgement in 1904, much of its property, leading to parliamentary intervention to redistribute assets.
