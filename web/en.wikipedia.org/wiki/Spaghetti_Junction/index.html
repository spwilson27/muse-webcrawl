Spaghetti Junction

Spaghetti Junction is the popular name for the Gravelly Hill Interchange, junction 6 of the M6 motorway in Birmingham, England. Opened on 24 May 1972, its tangle of slip roads, flyovers and underpasses — said to resemble a plate of spaghetti — made it a byword for complex road junctions, and the nickname has since been applied to complicated interchanges worldwide.

Construction

The interchange covers about 30 acres (12 hectares), carries 18 routes on some 4 kilometres of slip roads, and is supported by 559 concrete columns. It weaves together the M6, the A38 and A38(M) Aston Expressway, local roads, two railway lines, three canals and the River Tame. Construction cost about £10.8 million and took four years.

Legacy

The name was coined by journalists at the Birmingham Evening Mail before the junction opened. Despite its fearsome reputation, the layout is signed conventionally, and it has become a local landmark and a symbol of Birmingham's motorway era.
