Lachlan Macquarie

Lachlan Macquarie
Born: 31 January 1762, Isle of Mull, Scotland
Died: 1 July 1824, London
Office: Governor of New South Wales (1810–1821)

Major General Lachlan Macquarie was a Scottish soldier and colonial administrator who served as the fifth Governor of New South Wales from 1810 to 1821. He is often called the "Father of Australia" for his role in transforming the penal colony into a prosperous free settlement.

Governorship

Macquarie arrived in Sydney after the Rum Rebellion, restoring order and discipline. He pursued an ambitious program of public works — roads, bridges, churches, and public buildings, many designed by the convict architect Francis Greenway — and founded towns including Liverpool, Windsor, and Richmond. He encouraged exploration, including the first crossing of the Blue Mountains in 1813, followed by a road over them to Bathurst.

Emancipists

Macquarie controversially advanced emancipists — convicts who had served their sentences — appointing them to public office and treating them as restored citizens, a policy that alienated wealthy free settlers and the military. Following criticism, including the Bigge reports, he resigned and returned to Britain in 1822.

Legacy

Many Australian places bear his name, including Macquarie University, Macquarie Street in Sydney, and the Macquarie River.
