The 1968 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XIX Olympiad, were held in Mexico City from October 12 to 27, 1968—the first Olympic Games staged in Latin America and in a Spanish-speaking country. At an altitude of 2,240 metres (7,350 feet), Mexico City's thin air profoundly affected endurance events while aiding explosive ones, producing an extraordinary harvest of world records.

Tlatelolco massacre

Ten days before the opening ceremony, on October 2, 1968, Mexican security forces fired on a student demonstration in the Plaza de las Tres Culturas in Tlatelolco, killing dozens—estimates range from 30 to several hundred. The massacre, part of Mexico's "Dirty War," shadowed the Games, though the IOC proceeded as planned.

Sporting highlights

Bob Beamon's long jump of 8.90 m shattered the world record by 55 cm and stood for nearly 23 years. Dick Fosbury won the high jump with his revolutionary "Fosbury flop." Al Oerter won his fourth consecutive discus gold. These were the first Games with systematic drug testing and sex verification.

The Black Power salute

After the 200 metres final, American sprinters Tommie Smith and John Carlos raised black-gloved fists on the podium during the U.S. anthem, protesting racial injustice; Australian silver medallist Peter Norman wore a human-rights badge in support. Smith and Carlos were expelled from the Games. The image became one of the most iconic in sports history.

Medal table

The United States topped the table with 45 gold medals, ahead of the Soviet Union's 29, with Japan third.
