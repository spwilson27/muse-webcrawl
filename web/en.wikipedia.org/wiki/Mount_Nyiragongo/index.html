Mount Nyiragongo is an active stratovolcano in the Virunga Mountains of the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, about 12 kilometres north of the city of Goma and Lake Kivu, within Virunga National Park. Its summit crater, some two kilometres wide, long contained one of the world's largest and most persistent lava lakes. Nyiragongo's lava is unusually fluid — a low-silica nephelinite — and can flow at speeds of up to 60 km/h, among the fastest-moving lava known, making its flank eruptions exceptionally dangerous.

Eruptions

In January 1977 the lava lake drained through flank fissures, killing at least 70 people in under an hour. On 17 January 2002, a major flank eruption sent two lava flows through Goma, destroying roughly 15% of the city, including part of the airport runway, leaving some 120,000 people homeless and killing about 250. Another eruption began on 22 May 2021, when fissures opened on the southeastern flank; lava again reached the outskirts and airport of Goma, destroying hundreds of homes and prompting the evacuation of much of the city's population of around two million, while the crater's lava lake subsequently began to refill.

Monitoring and hazards

The Goma Volcano Observatory monitors Nyiragongo under difficult conditions. Scientists also warn of the related — if distinct — hazard of Lake Kivu, whose dissolved carbon dioxide and methane could in principle be released catastrophically by major volcanic or seismic disturbance.
