Exclusion Crisis

The Exclusion Crisis ran from 1679 to 1681 in the reign of Charles II of England. It was driven by attempts in Parliament to pass an Exclusion Bill that would bar the king's brother and heir presumptive, James, Duke of York — a Roman Catholic — from succeeding to the thrones of England, Scotland, and Ireland.

Course

Amid fears inflamed by the fabricated Popish Plot of Titus Oates, Anthony Ashley Cooper, Earl of Shaftesbury, and supporters — who became known as Whigs — pushed exclusion in three parliaments; defenders of hereditary succession became known as Tories. Charles II dissolved successive parliaments, including the Oxford Parliament of 1681, and ruled without Parliament thereafter.

Outcome

Exclusion failed; James succeeded as James II in 1685, but his Catholicism and policies led to the Glorious Revolution of 1688, which settled succession on Protestant terms via the Bill of Rights 1689.
