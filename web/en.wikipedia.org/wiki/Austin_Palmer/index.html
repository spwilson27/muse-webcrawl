Austin Norman Palmer (1860–1940s) was an Australian-born American penmanship innovator who developed the Palmer Method of handwriting, a simplified, rapid business script taught in American schools from the 1890s into the mid-20th century. His system displaced the ornate Spencerian script in most classrooms and trained several generations of American schoolchildren.

Palmer demonstrating his muscular-movement drills.

Life and career

Palmer was born in Sydney, Australia, and emigrated to the United States as a young man. After teaching penmanship in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, he founded the A. N. Palmer Company, which published his manuals — beginning with Palmer's Guide to Business Writing (1894) — and operated correspondence courses and normal-school training for teachers. The company later became a major educational publisher of handwriting textbooks, atlases and spellers.

The Palmer Method

Against the finger-driven, shaded Spencerian hand, Palmer prescribed "muscular movement": writing powered by the forearm resting on the desk, producing a plain, slanting, unshaded script at commercial speed. Lessons relied on rhythmic drills — ovals and push-pull strokes — practised to counts, with posture, pen hold and paper position standardised. The method's simplicity suited mass public schooling and business-college training, and Palmer certificates became a common credential for teachers. From the 1950s, ballpoint pens and competing methods (notably Zaner-Bloser) eroded its dominance, and Palmer instruction faded as cursive itself declined in curricula.
