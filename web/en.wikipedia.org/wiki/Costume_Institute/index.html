Anna Wintour Costume Center

The Anna Wintour Costume Center is a department of the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York City devoted to the art of costume. It originated as the Museum of Costume Art, founded in 1937 by Irene and Aline Lewisohn, and was long known simply as the Costume Institute. In 1946 the collection was transferred to the Met, where it became a curatorial department. It houses one of the world's foremost collections of dress, with more than 33,000 garments and accessories spanning five continents and seven centuries.

History

The Museum of Costume Art was established in 1937 at the Lewisohns' laboratory theatre in order to give designers and students access to historical dress. After joining the Met in 1946 it staged exhibitions in the museum galleries while keeping its collection stored off-site. Diana Vreeland, as special consultant from 1972 until her death in 1989, mounted glamorous and influential exhibitions that raised the institute's international profile. The collection moved into dedicated spaces in the Met's basement level, and in 2014 the renovated costume center was renamed for Vogue editor-in-chief Anna Wintour, who had raised some $125 million for the institute; Lizzie and Jonathan Tisch gave $10 million toward the renovation.

Exhibitions and the Met Gala

Because textiles are fragile, the center normally presents temporary exhibitions rather than a permanent display. Landmark shows include Alexander McQueen: Savage Beauty (2011), one of the Met's most visited exhibitions ever, China: Through the Looking Glass (2015), Heavenly Bodies: Fashion and the Catholic Imagination (2018), and Camp: Notes on Fashion (2019). Each spring exhibition opens with the Met Gala, the Costume Institute's principal fundraising benefit, chaired since 1995 by Anna Wintour and regarded as fashion's most prominent red-carpet event.

Curators

Curators-in-charge have included Diana Vreeland (as consultant), Richard Martin, and Harold Koda. Since 2016 the curator in charge has been Andrew Bolton, whose exhibitions have drawn record attendance.
