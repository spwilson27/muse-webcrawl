Northeastern University

Northeastern University (NEU) is a private research university in Boston, Massachusetts. Founded in 1898 as an evening program of the Boston YMCA, it is best known for its cooperative education ("co-op") programme, one of the largest and oldest in the world, in which students alternate semesters of academic study with full-time professional work.

History

The university began with evening classes in law and commerce for working students and incorporated as Northeastern College in 1916, becoming Northeastern University in 1922. It established its main campus in the Fenway–Back Bay area of Boston and expanded through the twentieth century into a comprehensive research university.

Academics and co-op

Northeastern's cooperative education programme places students with thousands of employers worldwide, and most undergraduates complete at least two six-month co-ops before graduating. The university comprises colleges including D'Amore-McKim School of Business, the College of Engineering, Khoury College of Computer Sciences, Bouvé College of Health Sciences and the School of Law. In recent decades Northeastern has expanded into a global campus network with locations in cities including Toronto, London, Seattle and the San Francisco Bay Area. Its athletic teams are the Huskies.
