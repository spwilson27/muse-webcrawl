Persian, also known by its endonym Fārsī (فارسی), is a Western Iranian language of the Indo-Iranian branch of the Indo-European family. It is the official language of Iran, where it is called Farsi; of Afghanistan, where its variety is called Dari; and of Tajikistan, where it is called Tajik and written in the Cyrillic script. Persian has been a major literary and cultural language of the Islamic world and of a region — sometimes called Greater Persia — stretching from the Balkans to western China.

History

Persian descends from the language of the ancient province of Persis (Fars) in southwestern Iran. Old Persian, written in a cuneiform script, was the administrative language of the Achaemenid Empire (550–330 BC) and is known from royal inscriptions such as that of Darius I at Behistun. Middle Persian (Pahlavi) was the language of the Sasanian Empire. After the Muslim conquest of Persia in the 7th century, New Persian emerged, written in an adapted Arabic script and enriched by a large Arabic vocabulary, flowering from the 9th–10th centuries in the poetry of Rudaki, Ferdowsi — whose epic Shahnameh is a cornerstone of Persian identity — and later Rumi, Saadi and Hafez.

Grammar and vocabulary

Modern Persian is a subject–object–verb language with no grammatical gender and no case system for nouns; grammatical relations are shown by word order, prepositions and the ezāfe construction linking nouns to their modifiers. Verbs conjugate for person and tense with a compact set of stems. Roughly a quarter of everyday vocabulary, and more in learned registers, derives from Arabic; Persian in turn has lent many words to Turkish, Urdu, Hindi and other languages of the region, and to English (e.g. bazaar, caravan, jasmine, pajamas).

Varieties

The three national standard varieties — Farsi, Dari and Tajik — are mutually intelligible to a high degree, differing mainly in pronunciation, vocabulary borrowings (from French, Russian, English and Pashto respectively) and the Tajik use of Cyrillic. Colloquial spoken Persian differs noticeably from the formal written standard, a mild diglossia managed by all speakers.
