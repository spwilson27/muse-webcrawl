Daemonologie

Daemonologie, in full Daemonologie, In Forme of a Dialogue, Divided into three Books: By the High and Mighty Prince, James &c., is a book on demonology and witchcraft written and published in 1597 by King James VI of Scotland (later James I of England).

Written as a dialogue between the characters Philomathes and Epistemon, the work argues for the reality of witchcraft and the necessity of witch hunting, drawing on James's experiences in the North Berwick witch trials. Its three books discuss magic and necromancy, witchcraft and sorcery, and spirits and ghosts.

Influence

Republished in London in 1603 after James ascended the English throne, Daemonologie influenced English attitudes to witchcraft and is often cited as a source context for Shakespeare's Macbeth.
