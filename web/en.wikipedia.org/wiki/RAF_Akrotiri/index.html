RAF Akrotiri

Royal Air Force Akrotiri, commonly abbreviated to RAF Akrotiri (IATA: AKT, ICAO: LCRA), is a large Royal Air Force station on the Mediterranean island of Cyprus. It is located in the Western Sovereign Base Area, one of two areas of the Sovereign Base Areas of Akrotiri and Dhekelia, a British Overseas Territory on the island.

History

The station was constructed in the 1950s amid deteriorating British-Egyptian relations and the relocation of British forces from the Suez Canal zone. It opened in 1955–56 and grew into one of the RAF's most important overseas bases. During the Cold War it hosted Canberra and Vulcan bombers and U2 reconnaissance flights operated with American involvement. When Cyprus gained independence in 1960, the Sovereign Base Areas were retained by the United Kingdom.

Operations

Akrotiri has served as a staging post for operations in the Middle East, including support to operations over Iraq and Syria. From 2014, Tornado and later Typhoon aircraft based at Akrotiri flew strikes against ISIL as part of Operation Shader. The base also hosts the Cyprus Joint Services Health Unit, a support helicopter squadron (No. 84 Squadron, long associated with search and rescue), and facilities used by visiting units. The Red Arrows aerobatic team traditionally uses Akrotiri for winter training.

Facilities

The station sits on the Akrotiri Peninsula south of Limassol, with a single runway of roughly 2,750 metres. The surrounding area includes salt lakes important for flamingos and other wildlife. The base supports a significant community of service personnel and families.
