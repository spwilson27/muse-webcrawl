Synthetic fuel

Synthetic fuel, or synfuel, is a liquid fuel, or sometimes a gaseous fuel, obtained from synthesis gas (syngas), a mixture of carbon monoxide and hydrogen produced from feedstocks such as coal, natural gas, biomass or oil shale. Major production routes include coal liquefaction by the Bergius direct hydrogenation process and the indirect Fischer–Tropsch process, gas-to-liquids (GTL) conversion of natural gas, and newer power-to-liquid "e-fuel" routes that combine green hydrogen with captured carbon dioxide. Synthetic fuels can be engineered to match gasoline, diesel or jet fuel specifications and to be used in existing engines and infrastructure.

A Fischer–Tropsch plant converting syngas into liquid fuels.

History

Germany, lacking oil, industrialised coal liquefaction between the wars: Friedrich Bergius developed direct liquefaction and Franz Fischer and Hans Tropsch the indirect synthesis (1925). During the Second World War German synthetic plants supplied much of the Wehrmacht's fuel and nearly all its aviation gasoline, becoming prime Allied bombing targets in the 1944 oil campaign. Apartheid-era South Africa built the Sasol plants at Secunda and Sasolburg, still the world's largest coal-to-liquids complex. The 1970s oil crises spurred synfuel programmes in the United States (including the Synthetic Fuels Corporation, 1980–1986), while modern GTL plants such as Shell's Pearl GTL in Qatar (opened 2011) convert cheap natural gas to premium diesel and base oils.

Environmental issues

Coal-derived synfuels have roughly twice the lifecycle CO2 emissions of petroleum fuels unless coupled with carbon capture and storage, because the conversion itself rejects much of the feedstock's carbon. GTL diesel offers lower local pollutant emissions but modest climate benefit. By contrast, e-fuels made with renewable electricity, green hydrogen and captured CO2 are promoted for sectors hard to electrify, such as aviation, though they are energy-intensive and costly; Porsche and partners' Haru Oni pilot plant in Chile is a prominent example.
