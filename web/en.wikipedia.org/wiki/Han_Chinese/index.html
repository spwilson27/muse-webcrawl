Han Chinese

The Han Chinese (simplified Chinese: 汉族; traditional Chinese: 漢族; pinyin: Hànzú) are an East Asian ethnic group native to China. They are the world's largest ethnic group, making up about 18% of the global population and over 90% of the population of China. The Han are a diverse group unified by shared culture, ancestry and language (the Sinitic languages).

History

The Han trace their origins to the Huaxia peoples of the Yellow River valley in northern China. Their ethnonym derives from the Han dynasty (206 BCE – 220 CE), which is regarded as a golden age in Chinese history; the name of the dynasty itself came from the Han River and Liu Bang's fief of Hanzhong. During the Han dynasty, the people of China began to identify themselves as the "people of Han", and Chinese characters came to be called Hanzi.

Distribution

Han Chinese form the majority in mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan, and constitute significant diaspora communities worldwide, particularly in Southeast Asia (Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand), North America and Australia.

Culture and language

The Han speak the Sinitic (Chinese) languages, including Mandarin, Yue (Cantonese), Wu, Min, Hakka, Gan and Xiang, written with Chinese characters. Core cultural traditions include Confucian social values, ancestor veneration, and festivals such as the Lunar New Year and Mid-Autumn Festival.
