Neustadt an der Weinstraße (Palatinate German: Neischdadt) is a town in Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany. With around 53,000 inhabitants, it is the largest town called Neustadt in Germany. The town lies on the German Wine Route (Deutsche Weinstraße), at the edge of the Palatinate Forest (Pfälzerwald), about 25 km southwest of Ludwigshafen.

History

Neustadt was founded around 1200 as a planned "new town" by the counts palatine of the Rhine and received its town charter in the 13th century. The Gothic collegiate church (Stiftskirche) dominates the old town. Nearby Hambach Castle (Hambacher Schloss), overlooking the town from the village of Hambach, became a symbol of the German democratic movement as the site of the Hambach Festival of 1832.

Wine and culture

Neustadt is a centre of the Palatinate wine region. Each autumn the town hosts the German Wine Festival (Deutsches Weinlesefest), at which the German Wine Queen is crowned, and its wine parade is among the largest in Germany. The town's districts, including Diedesfeld, Hambach, Mußbach and Gimmeldingen, are noted wine villages.
