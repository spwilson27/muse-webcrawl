Myositis

Specialty: Rheumatology, neurology
Symptoms: Muscle weakness, pain, fatigue; skin rash in dermatomyositis
Causes: Autoimmune disease, infection, injury, medicines
Treatment: Corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, physiotherapy

Myositis is inflammation of the muscles. This can be caused by infection, injury, autoimmune disease, or as a side effect of certain medications. The inflammatory myopathies — a group of chronic, autoimmune muscle diseases — typically cause progressive weakness of the muscles closest to the trunk, such as those of the hips, thighs, shoulders and neck.

Types

The main types of inflammatory myopathy include dermatomyositis, which features a characteristic skin rash; polymyositis, which lacks the rash; inclusion body myositis, a slowly progressive form mostly affecting older adults; and immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy. Infectious myositis can be caused by viruses such as influenza, as well as bacteria and parasites.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms typically include muscle weakness that develops over weeks to months, fatigue after walking or standing, tripping or falling, and difficulty swallowing or breathing in severe cases. Blood tests often show elevated muscle enzymes such as creatine kinase.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines clinical examination, blood tests for muscle enzymes and autoantibodies, electromyography, MRI and muscle biopsy. First-line treatment is usually high-dose corticosteroids, often with immunosuppressive drugs; physiotherapy helps preserve muscle function.
