Delano, California

Delano is a city in Kern County, California, United States, in the San Joaquin Valley. It was founded in 1873 as a railroad town by the Southern Pacific Railroad and named for Columbus Delano, then Secretary of the Interior. Its population was about 51,000 at the 2020 census. Delano is an important agricultural centre, especially for table grapes, and is historically significant as a starting point of the 1965 Delano grape strike.

History

The town grew as a shipping point for the surrounding farms, which drew successive waves of immigrant labour, including Filipino, Mexican, Yemeni and Punjabi workers. On 8 September 1965, the predominantly Filipino Agricultural Workers Organizing Committee, led by Larry Itliong, struck the grape growers around Delano; the National Farm Workers Association of Cesar Chavez and Dolores Huerta joined the strike, which merged into the United Farm Workers. The five-year Delano grape strike and the accompanying national consumer boycott became landmarks of the American labour and Chicano movements, ending in 1970 with union contracts.

Geography and economy

Delano lies roughly 31 miles (50 km) north-northwest of Bakersfield at an elevation of about 315 feet (96 m). Agriculture — grapes, almonds, citrus, and other crops — dominates the local economy, supported by packing houses and cold-storage facilities. Two state prisons, Kern Valley State Prison and North Kern State Prison, are also major employers. The city is served by State Route 99 and is home to a campus of Bakersfield College.

Demographics

The population is majority Hispanic or Latino, with a substantial Filipino American community dating to the farm labour migrations of the early twentieth century. English and Spanish are widely spoken, along with Tagalog and other languages of more recent immigrant communities.
