Submission (combat sports)

A submission is a combat sports term for yielding to an opponent, and hence resulting in an immediate defeat. The submission — then called a "tap out" or "tapping out" — is often performed by clearly tapping the floor or the opponent with the hand or sometimes with the foot, to signal the opponent and possibly the referee of the submission. In some combative sports where the fighter has cornermen, the corner can also stop the fight by "throwing in the towel" (although that is not technically a submission). A technical submission may occur when a referee or doctor stops the fight because a fighter has sustained an injury or become unconscious while in a hold.

Categories

Joint locks

Joint locks isolate a limb and apply leverage beyond the joint's normal range of motion. Common examples include the armbar (hyperextending the elbow), the kimura and americana (shoulder locks), the kneebar, and ankle locks and heel hooks acting on the knee. Heel hooks are considered particularly dangerous and are restricted in many rule sets.

Chokeholds

Chokes end a fight by rendering the opponent unconscious. Blood chokes such as the rear naked choke, triangle choke and guillotine compress the carotid arteries and restrict blood flow to the brain; air chokes compress the trachea. A fighter who does not tap to a blood choke typically loses consciousness within seconds.

Compression and pain holds

Compression locks squeeze muscles against bone, and some holds — banned in most competition — manipulate small joints or the spine.

Submission grappling

Arts specialising in submissions include Brazilian jiu-jitsu, judo (whose ne-waza groundwork includes armlocks and strangles), sambo, and catch wrestling. Submission finishes are a defining feature of mixed martial arts, where they complement striking and knockouts as a way to win. Submission-only grappling events, decided solely by tap out with no points, have grown in popularity.

Safety

Because holds can injure quickly once fully applied, etiquette and rules require releasing a hold immediately upon a tap or a referee's intervention. Injuries occur when submissions are applied explosively or refused: a fighter who declines to submit to a joint lock risks torn ligaments or broken bones, while refusing a choke results in unconsciousness, usually without lasting harm if released promptly.
