Eight queens puzzle

The eight queens puzzle is the problem of placing eight chess queens on an 8×8 chessboard so that no two queens threaten each other — that is, no two share the same row, column or diagonal. It was first proposed in 1848 by the chess composer Max Bezzel.

Solutions

The puzzle has exactly 92 distinct solutions, of which only 12 are fundamental — the rest being obtained from these by rotation and reflection of the board. The first solutions were published in 1850, and the complete count was established in the following years. Franz Nauck is credited with publishing the first solutions, and Carl Friedrich Gauss studied the problem without finding all of them.

Generalisation: the n queens problem

The generalisation to an n×n board with n queens is known as the n queens problem. Solutions exist for all n except 2 and 3. The number of solutions grows extremely fast: for n = 27 there are 234,907,967,154,122,528 solutions. In 2017 the solutions for n = 27 were confirmed in a large parallel computation.

In computer science

The puzzle is a classic example used to teach programming techniques, especially backtracking algorithms; Edsger Dijkstra used it in 1971 as an illustration of structured programming and depth-first search with backtracking. It also serves as a benchmark problem for constraint programming and for exact-cover algorithms such as Knuth's Algorithm X.
