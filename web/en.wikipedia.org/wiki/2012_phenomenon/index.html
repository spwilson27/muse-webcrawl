2012 phenomenon

The 2012 phenomenon was a range of eschatological beliefs that cataclysmic or transformative events would occur on or around 21 December 2012. The date was regarded as the end of a 5,126-year-long cycle in the Mesoamerican Long Count calendar, used by the Maya civilisation, and festivities and fears coalesced around the end of the 13th b'ak'tun.

Beliefs

Proposed scenarios included the arrival of a "galactic alignment", a geomagnetic reversal, a collision with a hypothetical planet called Nibiru, and other doomsday events. Other interpretations, particularly in New Age circles, held that the date marked a positive spiritual transformation or a new era of consciousness.

Scholarly response

Scholars of Maya studies rejected the apocalyptic interpretations, noting that no Classic-period Maya text predicted the end of the world and that the date merely marked the end of a cycle, after which the calendar would continue. Astronomers likewise dismissed the proposed astronomical scenarios as pseudoscience; NASA published material debunking claims of a planetary collision or polar shift.

Aftermath

21 December 2012 passed without incident. The date was marked by ceremonies at Maya sites such as Chichén Itzá and Tikal, as well as by media coverage and the earlier blockbuster film 2012 (2009).
