The ATR 42 is a regional turboprop airliner built in France and Italy by ATR. The "42" in its name derives from its standard seating capacity of 42 passengers (later versions seat up to 50). Announced in 1981 alongside the consortium itself, the type made its first flight on 16 August 1984 and entered service with Air Littoral in December 1985.

Design

The ATR 42 is a high-wing, T-tail twin-turboprop with rear passenger boarding via an airstair door and a cargo door at the front. It is powered by two Pratt & Whitney Canada PW120-family engines (PW127XT in the latest -600 series) driving six-bladed Hamilton Standard propellers. Final assembly takes place in Toulouse, with fuselage and tail sections built by Leonardo at Pomigliano d'Arco near Naples.

Variants

The original ATR 42-300 was followed by the -320 with more powerful engines, the -500 (1995) with PW127 engines, new propellers and improved performance, and the current ATR 42-600 (2012) with a glass cockpit and PW127XT-M engines. A short take-off and landing version, the ATR 42-600S, capable of operating from runways as short as 800 metres, was launched with EWA Air as launch customer. The stretched ATR 72 shares the same type certificate and most systems.

Operators and safety

Major operators have included Air New Zealand Link, FedEx Feeder (cargo conversions), and regional carriers across Europe, Asia, and the Pacific. The type's record includes the 1987 crash of ATI Flight 460 and the 1994 American Eagle Flight 4184 accident in Indiana — though that involved the ATR 72 — which led to major changes in icing certification and de-icing boot design for the family.
