George H. W. Bush

George Herbert Walker Bush (June 12, 1924 – November 30, 2018) was an American politician, diplomat and businessman who served as the 41st president of the United States from 1989 to 1993.

Early life and career

Bush, son of Senator Prescott Bush, became one of the youngest naval aviators in World War II and was shot down over the Pacific in 1944. After Yale, he made a fortune in the Texas oil business, served as a Texas congressman, ambassador to the United Nations, envoy to China, and director of the CIA. As Ronald Reagan's vice president (1981–1989) he won the 1988 election defeating Michael Dukakis.

Presidency

Bush presided over the end of the Cold War, the fall of the Berlin Wall and peaceful German reunification, and led a broad coalition in the Gulf War (1991) liberating Kuwait from Iraq after its invasion. At home he signed the Americans with Disabilities Act and Clean Air Act amendments, but his breaking of his "read my lips: no new taxes" pledge amid recession hurt him politically. He lost the 1992 three-way election to Bill Clinton.

Later life

Bush's eldest son George W. Bush became the 43rd president. After leaving office Bush engaged in humanitarian work, including with Clinton after the 2004 tsunami and Hurricane Katrina. He died in Houston in 2018, aged 94, months after Barbara Bush, and received a state funeral.
