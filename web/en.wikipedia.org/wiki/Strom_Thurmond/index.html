Strom Thurmond

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

James Strom Thurmond Sr. (5 December 1902 – 26 June 2003) was an American politician who represented South Carolina in the United States Senate from 1954 to 2003 — a tenure of 47 years and 5 months that made him, at his retirement at age 100, the longest-serving and oldest senator in US history to that date (records since surpassed in length by Robert Byrd). He is remembered for two careers: as the 1948 presidential candidate of the segregationist States' Rights Democratic Party (“Dixiecrats”), winning four Deep South states against Truman; and as the Senate's embodiment of the South's resistance to civil rights, including the longest individual filibuster in Senate history (24 hours 18 minutes against the Civil Rights Act of 1957).

Strom Thurmond of South Carolina, the Senate's longest-serving member of his era.

Thurmond switched to the Republican Party in 1964 (over civil rights and national direction), becoming a founding figure of the modern conservative South; after the Voting Rights Act he moderated his practice — hiring Black staff, voting for the 1982 VRA extension and the King holiday — without renouncing his earlier record, an arc historians use to trace the region's political transformation. After his death it was publicly acknowledged by his family that he had fathered a daughter, Essie Mae Washington-Williams, with Carrie Butler, a Black teenager employed in his parents' household — a biracial daughter he had supported privately while publicly defending segregation, a revelation that framed his obituaries' moral accounting.
