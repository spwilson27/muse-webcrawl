Detroit

Detroit, Michigan
Founded: 1701
State: Michigan
Population: ~633,000 (2020 census, city); ~4.3 million metro
Nickname: Motor City, Motown
Related: Michigan, Detroit River

Detroit is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Michigan. It lies on the Detroit River opposite Windsor, Ontario, Canada, and is a major port on the Great Lakes system.

History

Founded in 1701 by French explorer Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac as Fort Pontchartrain du Détroit, the city grew as a fur-trading and shipping center. In the 20th century it became the center of the American automobile industry, home to Ford, General Motors and Chrysler — the Big Three.

Decline and bankruptcy

Postwar suburbanization, deindustrialization and racial tensions, including the 1967 uprising, drove steep population loss from a peak of about 1.85 million in 1950. In 2013 Detroit filed the largest municipal bankruptcy in U.S. history, exiting in 2014.

Culture

Detroit gave its name to Motown Records, founded by Berry Gordy in 1959, and to Detroit techno. Landmarks include the Renaissance Center, Belle Isle, the Detroit Institute of Arts and Eastern Market. Its sports teams include the Lions (NFL), Tigers (MLB), Pistons (NBA) and Red Wings (NHL).

Revival

Since the 2010s downtown and Midtown have seen investment, new residents and businesses, though challenges of poverty, services and neighborhood inequality remain.
