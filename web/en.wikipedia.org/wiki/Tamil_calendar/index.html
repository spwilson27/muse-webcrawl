Tamil calendar

The Tamil calendar is a sidereal solar calendar used by the Tamil people of India and Sri Lanka, and by the Tamil diaspora worldwide. It is also used for religious and agricultural purposes in Tamil Nadu, Puducherry, and among Tamil communities in Malaysia, Singapore and Mauritius.

Structure

The calendar has twelve months named Chithirai, Vaikasi, Aani, Aadi, Aavani, Purattasi, Aippasi, Karthigai, Margazhi, Thai, Masi and Panguni, each of 29 to 32 days, following the sun's passage through the twelve zodiac signs (rasi). The new year, Puthandu, falls in mid-April, on the first day of Chithirai, when the sun enters Mesha (Aries).

Cycles and timekeeping

Years follow a 60-year cycle (the Jupiter cycle) in which each year bears a unique name, such as Prabhava or Vibhava; the cycle repeats continuously. The day is divided into 60 nazhigai of 24 minutes each, and the calendar records astrological details — tithi, nakshatra (lunar mansion), yoga and karana — used to determine auspicious times (muhurtam) for ceremonies.

Use

Although the Gregorian calendar governs civil life, the Tamil calendar remains central to fixing temple festival dates, marriages and religious observances, and printed almanacs (panchangam) are published annually.
