Battle of Tel el-Kebir

The Battle of Tel el-Kebir (often spelled Tell El Kebir) was fought on 13 September 1882 between the Egyptian army led by Ahmed Urabi and a British expeditionary force under Lieutenant-General Sir Garnet Wolseley. The British victory ended the Urabi revolt and began the British occupation of Egypt, which lasted in various forms until the 1950s.

Background

Urabi, an Egyptian army officer who had led a nationalist revolt against the Khedive and foreign influence, fortified a strong position astride the road and railway to Cairo at Tel el-Kebir, with extensive earthworks, trenches and artillery. Wolseley, advancing from the Suez Canal zone, feinted against the Egyptian flank at Kassassin while preparing his main blow.

The battle

Wolseley's army of some 17,000 men made a difficult night approach march across the desert, guided by the stars, and assaulted the Egyptian lines at dawn before the defenders could fully man them. The position was carried in roughly an hour of fighting. Egyptian losses were estimated at around 2,000 killed; British casualties were fewer than 500, of whom 57 were killed. A cavalry pursuit followed, and Cairo was occupied the next day.

Aftermath

Urabi surrendered and was exiled to Ceylon. The Khedive Tewfik was restored under British control, beginning the "veiled protectorate": Egypt remained nominally Ottoman and ruled by its khedive, but British officials, backed by an army of occupation, directed its government for decades.
