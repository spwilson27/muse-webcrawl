Parque Batlle

LocationMontevideo, Uruguay
Former nameParque de los Aliados
Named forJosé Batlle y Ordóñez

Parque Batlle is a neighbourhood and public park in Montevideo, Uruguay. Named after José Batlle y Ordóñez, twice president of Uruguay, the area is best known as the city's sporting heart: it contains the Estadio Centenario, Uruguay's national stadium, along with an athletics track, velodrome, and other venues.

Landmarks

The Estadio Centenario, built for the first FIFA World Cup in 1930, dominates the park; its Art Deco tower, the Torre de los Homenajes, is a symbol of Montevideo. Nearby stand the Monumento a la Carreta, a bronze sculpture of an ox-cart by José Belloni, and the city's velodrome and shooting range.

The neighbourhood

Parque Batlle is an affluent residential area of tree-lined streets and embassies, bordered by Pocitos, Tres Cruces, and La Blanqueada.
