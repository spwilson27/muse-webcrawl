Castle in the Sky (Japanese: 天空の城ラピュタ, Hepburn: Tenkū no Shiro Rapyuta), also known as Laputa: Castle in the Sky, is a 1986 Japanese animated fantasy adventure film written and directed by Hayao Miyazaki. It was the first film produced by Studio Ghibli and was produced for Tokuma Shoten. The film follows a young boy and girl in the late 19th century attempting to keep a magic crystal from military and pirate groups while searching for a legendary floating castle. The film won the Animage Anime Grand Prix in 1986.

Plot

An airship carrying Sheeta, a young orphan girl held prisoner by government agent Muska, is attacked by air pirates led by Captain Dola. Sheeta escapes by falling from the airship and is caught, floating gently to earth thanks to the glowing crystal pendant she wears, by a boy named Pazu in a mining town. Pazu dreams of finding Laputa, the mythical floating island his father once photographed. Together the two children evade pirates and soldiers alike, and with the help of Dola's gang they reach Laputa — an abandoned, overgrown floating city guarded by robots, whose ancient technology Muska seeks to seize for world domination. In the climax, Sheeta and Pazu recite the forbidden "spell of destruction", sacrificing Laputa's heart to prevent its misuse; the city's ruins, held aloft by its great tree, drift away into the sky.

Production

The film's story was influenced by Jonathan Swift's Gulliver's Travels, which features the floating island of Laputa; Miyazaki also drew on Welsh mining towns he had visited during the miners' strike of 1984–85 for the design of Pazu's home. Castle in the Sky was produced by the newly founded Studio Ghibli, with Isao Takahata as producer and Joe Hisaishi composing the score, beginning a long series of collaborations with Miyazaki.

Reception and legacy

The film was a moderate success on release and has grown greatly in reputation, frequently ranking among the greatest animated films ever made. Its imagery — flying machines, ancient robots, and a garden-tended floating island — has been hugely influential on Japanese animation and popular culture. The robot soldier from the film stands as a statue on the roof of the Ghibli Museum in Mitaka, Tokyo.
