The Imam Ali Mosque (Arabic: حَرَم ٱلْإِمَام عَلِيّ), also known as the Imam Ali Shrine, in Najaf, Iraq, is the mosque housing the tomb of Ali ibn Abi Talib, cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet Muhammad and, in Shia Islam, the first Imam. It is one of the holiest sites in Shia Islam, visited by millions of pilgrims each year.

History

Ali was assassinated in Kufa in 661 and buried near Najaf; his grave was kept secret for fear of desecration until it was revealed under the Abbasid caliph Harun al-Rashid in the late 8th century. Successive shrines were built, destroyed by fire and rebuilt. The present structure dates largely to the Safavid shah Abbas I, who after 1623 rebuilt the shrine with its famed golden dome and extensive tilework; Nader Shah gilded the dome and minarets in 1742–43.

Pilgrimage

The shrine is the focal point of pilgrimage (ziyara) to Najaf, especially during Arbaeen commemorations, and stands beside the vast Wadi al-Salam cemetery. In August 2003 a car bomb outside the mosque killed Ayatollah Mohammed Baqir al-Hakim and scores of worshippers; the shrine also saw fighting during the 2004 Iraq conflict.
