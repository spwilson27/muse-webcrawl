Minerva

Minerva is the Roman goddess of wisdom, strategic war, art, trade and strategy, identified with the Greek goddess Athena. She was one of the Capitoline Triad alongside Jupiter and Juno.

Origins and worship

Minerva's name derives from the Etruscan goddess Menrva. Her principal temple stood on Rome's Capitoline Hill, and her festival, the Quinquatrus (19 March), was a holiday for artisans, teachers and schoolchildren. The owl of Minerva became a lasting symbol of wisdom in Western culture.

Mythology

Like Athena, Minerva was said to have sprung fully armed from the head of her father Jupiter. Myths describe her contest with Neptune for Athens, her transformation of Arachne into a spider, and her patronage of heroes such as Odysseus (Ulysses).
