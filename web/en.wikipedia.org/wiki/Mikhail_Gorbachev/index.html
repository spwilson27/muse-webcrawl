Mikhail Sergeyevich Gorbachev (2 March 1931 – 30 August 2022) was a Soviet and Russian politician who served as the eighth and final leader of the Soviet Union from 1985 until its dissolution in 1991. His policies of glasnost (openness and transparency) and perestroika (economic and political restructuring) loosened state control, unleashed public debate, and set in motion the revolutions of 1989 in Eastern Europe and the end of the Cold War.

Rise to power

Born to a peasant family in the Stavropol region, Gorbachev joined the Communist Party while studying law at Moscow State University and rose through the party apparatus, becoming regional first secretary in Stavropol in 1970 and a full Politburo member in 1980 under the patronage of Yuri Andropov. On the death of Konstantin Chernenko in March 1985, the Politburo elected him General Secretary, choosing the youngest member to revitalise a stagnating system.

Reforms and foreign policy

At home, Gorbachev introduced glasnost, released political prisoners such as Andrei Sakharov, permitted contested elections to a new Congress of People's Deputies (1989), and became the USSR's first (and only) President in 1990. Abroad, he withdrew Soviet forces from Afghanistan, signed the INF Treaty with Ronald Reagan (1987), and — in the policy nicknamed the Sinatra Doctrine — declined to use force to keep Eastern European governments in power, allowing the Berlin Wall to fall in November 1989 and German reunification the following year. He received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1990.

Collapse and later life

Reform spiralled beyond Gorbachev's control: national movements in the Baltic states and republics demanded independence, hardliners attempted a coup in August 1991, and on 25 December 1991 Gorbachev resigned as the Soviet Union dissolved, with Boris Yeltsin ascendant in Russia. Revered in the West, he was widely unpopular in Russia, blamed for the Union's collapse and the chaos that followed; his 1996 presidential bid won under 1% of the vote. He founded the Gorbachev Foundation and Green Cross International, and remained a commentator on Russian and world politics until his death in Moscow on 30 August 2022.
