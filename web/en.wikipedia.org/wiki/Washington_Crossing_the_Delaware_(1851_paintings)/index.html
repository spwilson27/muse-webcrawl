Washington Crossing the Delaware (1851 paintings)

Washington Crossing the Delaware is the title of three full-sized paintings by the German-American artist Emanuel Leutze depicting General George Washington's crossing of the Delaware River on the night of 25–26 December 1776, during the American Revolutionary War, before the Battle of Trenton.

The paintings

Leutze painted the first version in Düsseldorf in 1849–1850; it was damaged by fire in his studio and, though restored, was later destroyed in a bombing raid on Bremen in 1942. The second, best-known version was completed in 1851 (measuring about 149 × 255 inches) and is now the centrepiece of the American Wing of the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York. A third, smaller version hangs in the Minnesota Marine Art Museum; another small copy was long displayed in the White House.

Composition and accuracy

The image — Washington standing heroically in a Durham boat amid ice floes, the flag borne beside him — is one of the most famous in American art, though it contains deliberate inaccuracies: the crossing happened at night in a storm, the boats were larger ferry types, and the Stars and Stripes shown was not adopted until 1777. Leutze hoped the work would inspire liberal reformers in Germany as well as celebrate the American republic.
