Moroni (Arabic: موروني, Mūrūnī) is the largest city, federal capital, and seat of government of the Union of the Comoros, a sovereign archipelago nation in the Indian Ocean. It is located on the western coast of Grande Comore (Ngazidja), the largest of the Comoro Islands, at the foot of the active volcano Mount Karthala.

History

Moroni was founded by Arab and Swahili-speaking settlers, probably by the 10th century, as a trading port on the routes linking East Africa, Madagascar, and the Middle East. Its name is often derived from a local word meaning "at the place of fire," a reference to the looming Mount Karthala volcano. The old town grew around the port and its mosques, trading in spices and other goods. During the French colonial period Moroni was overshadowed administratively by Dzaoudzi on Mayotte, but in 1958 Moroni became the capital of the Comorian territory, and it remained the capital when the Comoros declared independence from France in 1975.

Geography and climate

Moroni has a tropical maritime climate with a hot, rainy season from November to April and a cooler, drier season from May to October. The city spreads along the coast beneath the 2,361-metre (7,746 ft) Mount Karthala, one of the world's largest active volcanoes, whose eruptions have periodically threatened the city, most notably in 2005 and 2006, when ash falls forced evacuations.

Landmarks and transport

Moroni's old medina contains narrow lanes of whitewashed coral-stone buildings, the old Friday mosque (Ancienne Mosquée de Vendredi) beside the harbour, and the newer Grande Mosquée du Vendredi with its prominent minaret. The National Museum of the Comoros displays the island's cultural and natural history, including coelacanth specimens. The city's port handles most of the country's trade, and international travel is served by Prince Said Ibrahim International Airport at Hahaya, about 24 km north of the city. Air Austral, Kenya Airways, and other regional carriers connect Moroni with East Africa, Madagascar, and the Gulf.
