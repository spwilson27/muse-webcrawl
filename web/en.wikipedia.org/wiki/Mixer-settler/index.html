Mixer-settler

A battery of mixer-settler stages in a solvent extraction plant

A mixer-settler is a class of mineral process equipment used in solvent extraction (liquid–liquid extraction). It consists of two stages: a mixer, in which two immiscible liquids are agitated together so that a solute transfers between them, and a settler, a calm basin where the phases separate by gravity.

Operation

The aqueous and organic phases are pumped into the mixing chamber, dispersed by an impeller, then flow to the settler where the lighter organic phase overflows a weir and the heavier aqueous phase underflows. Multiple mixer-settler stages are linked in series, usually in counter-current flow, to achieve high extraction and stripping efficiency.

Applications

Mixer-settlers are widely used in hydrometallurgy — for example in copper solvent extraction and electrowinning (SX-EW), uranium and rare-earth processing — and in nuclear fuel reprocessing. Advantages include simplicity and scalability; disadvantages include a large footprint and solvent inventory compared with extraction columns or centrifugal contactors.
