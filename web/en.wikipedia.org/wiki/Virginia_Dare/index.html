Virginia Dare

Born18 August 1587, Roanoke Colony
DiedUnknown
ParentsAnanias Dare and Eleanor White

Virginia Dare (born 18 August 1587) was the first English child born in a New World English colony. She was born into the Roanoke Colony on what is now Roanoke Island in Dare County, North Carolina. Her fate, along with that of the other Roanoke colonists, is unknown. Virginia Dare became a prominent figure in American myth and folklore, symbolising innocence, purity and new beginnings.

The baptism of Virginia Dare

Life

Virginia Dare was the daughter of Ananias Dare, a tiler and bricklayer, and Eleanor White, daughter of John White, the governor of the colony. She was baptised on 24 August 1587. When White returned to England for supplies, war with Spain delayed his return until 1590. On his arrival he found the settlement abandoned, with the word "CROATOAN" carved on a post — the only clue to the fate of the "Lost Colony", including his granddaughter. No trace of Virginia Dare was ever conclusively found, and theories about assimilation into local Native American tribes or death from starvation, disease or conflict remain unproven.

Legacy

Virginia Dare has been commemorated in place names (including Dare County, North Carolina), literature, poetry, a wine label, and the outdoor drama The Lost Colony performed on Roanoke Island since 1937. Her image was long used as an icon of the early English settlement of America.
