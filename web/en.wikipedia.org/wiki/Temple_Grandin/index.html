Mary Temple Grandin (born August 29, 1947) is an American scientist, inventor, and author. A professor of animal science at Colorado State University, she is best known for designing humane livestock handling facilities and for her advocacy and writing on autism, having been diagnosed as autistic herself. She is widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in both fields.

Early life and education

Grandin was born in Boston and diagnosed in early childhood at a time when autism was poorly understood. She did not speak until age three and a half, and credited supportive mentors and her mother's refusal to institutionalize her for her progress. Observing cattle relax in a squeeze chute at her aunt's Arizona ranch led her to build a "squeeze machine" that calmed her own sensory anxiety — an early example of her insight that her visual, detail-focused thinking resembled animal perception. She earned degrees in psychology and animal science, completing her PhD at the University of Illinois in 1989.

Livestock handling work

Grandin designed curved cattle corrals and chutes that exploit animals' natural circling behaviour and minimize visual distractions, balking, and stress. Her centre-track restrainer systems and handling guidelines have been adopted by major meat processors; by the late 1990s a large share of cattle in North America were handled in facilities she designed. She also developed objective animal-welfare auditing criteria — scoring vocalization, falls, and electric-prod use — that became industry standards for companies such as McDonald's and Wendy's.

Autism advocacy and writing

Grandin's memoir Emergence: Labeled Autistic (1986) was among the first first-person accounts of autism, and Thinking in Pictures (1995) popularized her description of visual thinking. She argues for recognizing different kinds of minds, for early intervention and skill-building, and for employment pathways for autistic people. The 2010 HBO film Temple Grandin, starring Claire Danes, won multiple Emmy Awards; Time named Grandin to its 2010 list of the 100 most influential people in the world.
