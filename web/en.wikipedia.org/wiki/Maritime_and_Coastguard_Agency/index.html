Maritime and Coastguard Agency

The Maritime and Coastguard Agency (MCA) is an executive agency of the United Kingdom Department for Transport. It is responsible for implementing the government's maritime safety policy, preventing the loss of life on the coast and at sea, coordinating search and rescue through His Majesty's Coastguard, and ensuring that ships meet UK and international safety standards.

History

The MCA was created in 1998 by merging the Coastguard Agency and the Marine Safety Agency. Its headquarters are in Spring Place, Southampton. The agency took over the functions of regulating shipping safety that had descended in part from the Marine Department of the Board of Trade and the survey work of bodies such as the Mercantile Marine Office.

Responsibilities

  • Search and rescue coordination through His Majesty's Coastguard, which coordinates responses to emergencies at sea and on the coast from a network of operations centres, and maintains volunteer Coastguard Rescue Teams.
  • Survey and inspection of UK-flagged ships and foreign ships in UK ports (port state control), ensuring compliance with safety and pollution-prevention standards.
  • Seafarer certification: examining and certifying officers and crews, and maintaining the UK Ship Register.
  • Counter-pollution work, responding to spills and maintaining the National Contingency Plan for maritime pollution.
  • Navigation safety, including hydrographic and wreck-related functions in liaison with other bodies.

His Majesty's Coastguard

HM Coastguard is one of the four UK category 1 emergency services reachable via 999. Since 2015, search and rescue helicopters have been operated by Bristow Helicopters under contract to the MCA, replacing the previous military provision by the Royal Navy and Royal Air Force. A second-generation contract (UKSAR2G) continues this arrangement.
