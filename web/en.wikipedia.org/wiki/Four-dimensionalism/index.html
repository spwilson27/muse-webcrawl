Four-dimensionalism

FieldMetaphysics, philosophy of time
Core claimObjects are extended in time as well as space
SynonymPerdurantism (temporal parts view)

Four-dimensionalism is an ontological position in philosophy holding that the existence of an object over time is analogous to its extension in space: just as an object has spatial parts in different places, it has temporal parts in different times, and the whole object is the sum of these parts spread across a four-dimensional spacetime "block".

Perdurantism and stage theory

The most common form of four-dimensionalism is perdurantism: objects perdure by having temporal parts at each moment they exist, as opposed to endurantism, on which objects are wholly present at each moment. A variant, stage theory, identifies an object with a single temporal stage related to others by counterpart relations. Philosophers associated with the view include W. V. O. Quine and David Lewis; Theodore Sider's Four-Dimensionalism (2001) is a major defense.

Arguments

Four-dimensionalists argue the view elegantly handles puzzles of change and temporary intrinsics — how an object can be bent at one time and straight at another — and fits naturally with the spacetime picture of special relativity.
