Chaplain

A chaplain is a member of the clergy, or in modern usage a trained spiritual-care provider, who serves in an institution outside a parish setting, such as a military unit, hospital, prison, school, university, police or fire department, seaport, sports team, or workplace. Chaplains minister to people of all faiths and of none within their institution, offering pastoral care, religious services, counselling, and support in crises, while respecting the beliefs of those they serve.

History

The word derives from the Latin cappellanus, originally the keeper of the cloak (cappa) of St Martin of Tours, a treasured relic carried into battle by the Frankish kings; the tent or chapel housing the relic, and then its guardian priests, gave rise to the terms "chapel" and "chaplain." Over the centuries chaplains served royal courts, noble households, and armies before the role spread to civilian institutions in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Types of chaplaincy

  • Military chaplains accompany armed forces, hold commissioned rank in many countries without bearing arms, and are protected as non-combatants under the Geneva Conventions.
  • Healthcare chaplains work in hospitals and hospices as part of interdisciplinary care teams, supporting patients, families, and staff.
  • Prison chaplains provide religious ministry and rehabilitation support within correctional institutions.
  • Educational chaplains serve schools and universities; some legislatures also appoint chaplains to open sessions with prayer.
  • Emergency-services and industrial chaplains support police, firefighters, mariners, and employees in sectors such as railways, aviation, and professional sport.

Training

Professional chaplains are usually ordained or endorsed by a faith community and increasingly complete graduate theological education and clinical pastoral education (CPE), with board certification available in countries such as the United States. Multi-faith and humanist chaplaincies have grown in recent decades to reflect the populations served.
