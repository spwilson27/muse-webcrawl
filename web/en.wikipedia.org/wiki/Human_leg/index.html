Human leg

The human leg is the entire lower limb of the human body, including the foot, thigh and even the hip or gluteal region. In common usage, however, "leg" usually refers only to the segment between the knee and the ankle, anatomically called the crus. The leg's primary functions are support, locomotion and balance in bipedal posture.

Structure

The thigh contains the femur, the longest and strongest bone in the body; the lower leg contains the tibia and fibula; and the foot comprises the tarsals, metatarsals and phalanges. Major muscle groups include the quadriceps and hamstrings of the thigh and the calf muscles (gastrocnemius and soleus). The leg is supplied mainly by the femoral and sciatic nerves and drained by the great saphenous vein, the longest vein in the body.

Clinical significance

Common problems include fractures, varicose veins, deep vein thrombosis and peripheral arterial disease. Leg length and proportions are important in anthropology and ergonomics.
