ComposerTerry Riley

In C is a musical piece composed by Terry Riley in 1964 for an indefinite number of performers. It consists of 53 short numbered melodic patterns, generally of one bar in length, which each performer repeats at will before moving on to the next, staying within a few patterns of the others, all anchored by a high C pulse traditionally played on piano or mallet instrument. The piece is widely regarded as the first minimalist composition and one of the most influential works of the 20th century.

Structure and performance

The one-page score gives patterns in C major (with one F-sharp figure creating gentle tension). Performances last anywhere from about twenty minutes to over an hour; ensembles have ranged from a handful of players to orchestras and rock bands. Because alignment is left to the performers, no two performances sound alike, though the gradual drift from pattern to pattern produces a characteristic shimmering, meditative texture.

Influence

In C helped launch musical minimalism, influencing Steve Reich, Philip Glass and generations of composers in classical, electronic and popular music. Notable recordings include the 1968 Columbia album and large-scale versions such as Africa Express's In C Mali (2015). Riley also suggested that ensembles may transpose the work, giving rise to performances "in" other keys.
