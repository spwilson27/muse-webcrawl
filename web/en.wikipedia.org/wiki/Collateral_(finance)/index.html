Collateral (finance)

In lending agreements, collateral is a borrower's pledge of specific property to a lender to secure repayment of a loan. The collateral serves to protect the lender against a borrower's default — that is, a borrower failing to pay the principal and interest under the terms of a loan obligation.

[ Image placeholder: diagram of a secured loan showing borrower, lender and pledged asset ]

Concept

If a borrower defaults, the lender may seize the collateral and sell it to recoup some or all of the amount originally loaned. Loans secured by collateral typically carry lower interest rates than unsecured loans because the lender's risk is reduced. Common examples include mortgages secured on real estate and car loans secured on the vehicle.

Marketable collateral

In financial markets, collateral — typically cash or government securities — is posted against derivatives and repurchase agreements. The market value of collateral is usually discounted by a "haircut" to protect against price fluctuations, and margin calls restore the required level when values fall.
