Agustín Cosme Damián de Iturbide y Arámburu (27 September 1783 – 19 July 1824) was a Mexican royalist general who achieved Mexican independence from Spain and briefly reigned as Agustín I, the first emperor of the First Mexican Empire.

Career

Born in Valladolid (now Morelia) to a wealthy Criollo family, Iturbide joined the royalist army and fought against the insurgents of Hidalgo and Morelos during the Mexican War of Independence. In 1821, alarmed by liberal reforms in Spain, he allied with the insurgent leader Vicente Guerrero under the Plan of Iguala, which promised independence, Catholicism, and equality. Their Army of the Three Guarantees entered Mexico City on 27 September 1821, and the Treaty of Córdoba confirmed independence.

Empire and downfall

After a popular demonstration, Congress proclaimed Iturbide emperor in May 1822. His reign quickly faltered amid fiscal crisis and conflict with Congress, which he dissolved. Antonio López de Santa Anna's Plan of Casa Mata rallied opposition, and Iturbide abdicated in March 1823 and went into exile in Europe. Unaware that Congress had decreed him a traitor, he returned in July 1824 and was captured and executed by firing squad at Padilla, Tamaulipas.

Legacy

Iturbide remains a contested figure: honored as the consummator of independence and designer of Mexico's first national flag, yet criticized as an opportunist and would-be monarch.
