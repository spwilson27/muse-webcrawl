Emperor Chūai

Emperor Chūai (仲哀天皇, Chūai-tennō) was the 14th legendary emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. Very little is known about his life and reign, which are recorded in the chronicles Kojiki (712) and Nihon Shoki (720). Historians consider him a legendary figure; conventional dates assign his reign to 192–200 AD.

Legendary narrative

According to the chronicles, Chūai was a son of the hero Yamato Takeru and nephew of Emperor Seimu. During a campaign against the Kumaso in Kyushu, his wife Jingū was possessed by deities who urged an expedition against the Korean kingdom of Silla. Chūai doubted the divine message and died suddenly; Jingū then concealed his death, completed the campaign in Korea, and returned to give birth to his son, the future Emperor Ōjin. Chūai's tomb (misasagi) is traditionally associated with the Ega no Naganu no nishi no misasagi in Fujiidera, Osaka.

Historicity

No firm evidence survives for Chūai's dates or deeds, and modern scholars treat the early emperors through at least the 5th century with caution. His traditional chronology was constructed centuries after the purported events.
