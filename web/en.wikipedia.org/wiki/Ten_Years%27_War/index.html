Ten Years' War


Date: 1868–1878
Location: Cuba
Parties: Cuban independence forces vs Spanish Empire
Result: Pact of Zanjón; independence not achieved

The Ten Years' War (1868–1878), also known in Cuba as the Great War, was the first of three wars fought by Cuban independence movements against Spanish colonial rule. It began on 10 October 1868 with Carlos Manuel de Céspedes's Grito de Yara, in which he freed his slaves and declared Cuban independence.

Course of the war

Insurgent generals such as Máximo Gómez and Antonio Maceo waged guerrilla warfare in eastern Cuba, but the rebels were hampered by internal divisions, racial tensions and lack of outside support. Spain deployed large numbers of troops and the conflict caused enormous loss of life and economic devastation.

Outcome

The war ended with the Pact of Zanjón in 1878, which promised reforms and amnesty but neither independence nor general abolition of slavery. Maceo's Protest of Baraguá rejected the settlement without independence; fighting resumed in later wars, culminating in 1895.
