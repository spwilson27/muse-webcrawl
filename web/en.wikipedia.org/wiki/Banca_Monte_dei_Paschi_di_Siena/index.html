Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena S.p.A., commonly known as BMPS or simply Monte dei Paschi, is an Italian bank. Founded in 1472 by the magistrates of the city-state of Siena as a mount of piety, it is often described as the oldest surviving bank in continuous operation in the world.

History

The bank was established as a monte di pietà to lend money to the poor at modest rates against pawned goods, an institution promoted by the Franciscans. It expanded during the Renaissance, survived the incorporation of Siena into the Grand Duchy of Tuscany, and became a cornerstone of the Sienese economy, famously supporting the Palio and local agriculture through its monte functions. In the 1990s it was converted into a joint-stock company and listed on the Milan stock exchange.

Expansion and crisis

Monte dei Paschi grew by acquiring regional banks and in 2008 purchased Banca Antonveneta for about €9 billion, a deal later criticised as overpriced. Exposure to Italian sovereign debt, non-performing loans, and the loss-making derivatives transactions known as Alexandria and Santorini pushed the bank into crisis. After failing European stress tests, it received a precautionary state recapitalisation in 2017, leaving the Italian government its majority shareholder.

Recent developments

A multi-year restructuring reduced non-performing loans and branches. In 2022 the bank completed a €2.5 billion capital increase. The Italian Treasury has since been reducing its stake in line with commitments to the European Commission, and Monte dei Paschi has pursued consolidation moves within Italian banking, including a 2025 exchange offer for Mediobanca.
