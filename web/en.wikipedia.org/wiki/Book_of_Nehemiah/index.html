Book of Nehemiah

The Book of Nehemiah is a book of the Hebrew Bible and the Christian Old Testament. Told largely in the first person, it recounts the rebuilding of the walls of Jerusalem by Nehemiah, a cupbearer to King Artaxerxes I of Persia, and the dedication of the city and the reforms enacted after the Jewish return from Babylonian exile.

Summary

Nehemiah, serving at the Persian court in Susa, hears that Jerusalem's walls lie in ruins and obtains the king's permission and letters of safe passage to go to Judah as governor. Despite opposition from neighboring officials such as Sanballat and Tobiah, the walls are rebuilt in 52 days. Ezra reads the Law of Moses to the assembled people, who confess their sins and enter a covenant. The book closes with lists of residents, the dedication of the wall, and Nehemiah's reforms concerning the Sabbath, tithes, and intermarriage.

Composition

In most Jewish and Christian traditions the book was combined with the Book of Ezra as a single work, Ezra–Nehemiah, likely compiled in the Persian or early Hellenistic period. Scholars regard the "Nehemiah memoir" chapters as based on Nehemiah's own account.
