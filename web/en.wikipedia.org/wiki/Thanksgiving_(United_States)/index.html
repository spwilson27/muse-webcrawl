Thanksgiving (United States)

Thanksgiving is a federal holiday in the United States celebrated on the fourth Thursday of November. It originated as a day of thanksgiving and harvest festival, and centres on a large meal, generally featuring roasted turkey, shared with family and friends. The holiday commemorates, in popular tradition, the harvest feast shared by the Plymouth colonists (the Pilgrims) and the Wampanoag people in 1621.

History

Days of thanksgiving were observed by English colonists and Native peoples in North America from the early 17th century. The 1621 Plymouth feast is the most famous prototype. George Washington proclaimed a national day of thanksgiving in 1789, and in 1863, during the Civil War, Abraham Lincoln proclaimed Thanksgiving a national holiday to be held each November. The date was fixed as the fourth Thursday of November by Congress in 1941.

Traditions

The centrepiece is the Thanksgiving dinner: roast turkey with stuffing, mashed potatoes, gravy, cranberry sauce, sweet potatoes and pumpkin pie. Other customs include the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York City (held since 1924), NFL and college football games, charitable food drives, and the presidential "pardoning" of a turkey. The following day, Black Friday, opens the Christmas shopping season.
