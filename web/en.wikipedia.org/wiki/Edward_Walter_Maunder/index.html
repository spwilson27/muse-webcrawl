Edward Walter Maunder

Edward Walter Maunder (12 April 1851 – 21 March 1928) was an English astronomer best remembered for his study of sunspots and the solar magnetic cycle, and for identifying the Maunder Minimum, a period of very low sunspot activity from about 1645 to 1715.

E. Walter Maunder
Born: 1851, London
Died: 1928
Workplace: Royal Observatory, Greenwich
Known for: Maunder Minimum, butterfly diagram

Career

Maunder worked at the Royal Observatory, Greenwich from 1873, measuring sunspots. With his wife Annie Maunder, a mathematician and photographer, he produced the sunspot "butterfly diagram" showing migration of sunspots toward the equator over the cycle.

Maunder Minimum

Building on Spörer's work, Maunder highlighted the near-absence of sunspots in the late 17th century, later named for him and linked (in modern research) to the Little Ice Age, though causation is debated. He also founded the British Astronomical Association in 1890.
