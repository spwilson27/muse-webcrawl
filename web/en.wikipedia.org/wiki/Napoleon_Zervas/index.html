Napoleon Zervas

Napoleon Zervas (Greek: Ναπολέων Ζέρβας; 17 May 1891 – 10 December 1957) was a Greek military officer and resistance leader during the Axis occupation of Greece in World War II. He founded and led the National Republican Greek League (EDES), the second-largest resistance organisation in occupied Greece after the communist-led EAM/ELAS, and later served as a government minister in post-war Greece.

Early career

Born in Arta in Epirus, Zervas volunteered for the Greek Army during the Balkan Wars and became a career officer associated with the Venizelist faction. Cashiered after the royalist victory of 1920 and again involved in the turbulent interwar politics of Greece, he was living in semi-retirement when Italy invaded Greece in 1940.

EDES and the Resistance

In September 1941 Zervas founded EDES (National Republican Greek League), a republican, anti-communist resistance movement based mainly in Epirus. Its military arm took to the mountains in the summer of 1942. In November 1942 EDES guerrillas, together with ELAS and a British SOE team, carried out Operation Harling, the destruction of the Gorgopotamos railway viaduct — one of the most celebrated sabotage actions of the war in Greece.

Relations between EDES and ELAS deteriorated into open civil conflict in 1943–44. Zervas's forces were also controversial for operations against the Cham Albanian minority in Thesprotia in 1944, which resulted in many deaths and the expulsion of most Chams from the region.

Post-war career

After liberation, Zervas founded the National Party of Greece, served as Minister of Public Order in 1947 and later as Minister of Public Works. His tenure as Minister of Public Order during the Greek Civil War was associated with harsh anti-communist measures. He died in Athens in 1957.
