Show hunter

A show hunter is a horse that competes in hunter classes at horse shows, judged on its manners, movement, conformation and jumping style rather than on speed or time. The discipline derives from the traditions of fox hunting: courses simulate fences found in the hunt field — brush, coops, gates and natural rails — and the ideal round is smooth, flowing and even-paced, as if the horse could carry a rider comfortably all day.

Judging

Judges reward a horse that jumps in good form with knees tucked and even, bascules over the fence, maintains a consistent rhythm and an obedient, relaxed manner, and shows quality, ground-covering gaits in the under-saddle (flat) phase. Faults include rails down, refusals, irregular pace, poor jumping form and bad manners such as bucking or excessive freshness.

Divisions

Hunter divisions are organised by fence height (from cross-rails and short stirrup for beginners up to high-performance hunters at 3'6"–4'), by rider age and experience, and sometimes by horse type; "hunter derbies" add natural obstacles and handy options. The separate hunt seat equitation classes judge the rider's position and effectiveness instead. In Britain and Ireland, related showing classes — including working hunter classes over rustic fences — judge heavier native types under similar principles.
