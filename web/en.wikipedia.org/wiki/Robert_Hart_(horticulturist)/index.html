Robert Hart (horticulturist)

Robert Adrian de Jauralde Hart (1 April 1913 – 7 March 2000) was an English pioneer of forest gardening in temperate climates. He created a model forest garden on a 0.12 acre (500 m²) plot at Wenlock Edge in Shropshire, and his book Forest Gardening: Cultivating an Edible Landscape (1991) helped popularise the concept worldwide.

Life

Hart was born in London and worked as a journalist with Reuters before moving with his brother Terence to Wenlock Edge in Shropshire after the Second World War. Motivated by poor health, veganism and a wish for self-sufficiency, the brothers began farming a smallholding. Robert Hart observed that a natural woodland is organised in layers and set out to imitate that structure with edible and useful plants.

The forest garden

From a conventional orchard of apples and pears, Hart developed a seven-layer system: a canopy of mature fruit trees; a low tree layer; a shrub layer (currants, gooseberries); a herbaceous layer; a rhizosphere of root crops; a ground-cover layer; and a vertical layer of climbers and vines. The garden, though tiny, produced abundant food with minimal labour and no artificial fertilisers, and became a demonstration site visited by thousands.

Works

Hart's writings include The Inviolable Hills (1968), Forest Farming (1976, with James Sholto Douglas, proposing the system for the tropics), and Forest Gardening (1991), revised with a foreword and further material in a 1996 edition. He also wrote on wider themes of ecology, non-violence and world government in Beyond the Forest Garden.

Legacy

Hart is regarded as a founding figure of the British permaculture and agroforestry movements. His Wenlock Edge garden influenced practitioners such as Martin Crawford, whose Agroforestry Research Trust continued research into temperate forest gardening.
