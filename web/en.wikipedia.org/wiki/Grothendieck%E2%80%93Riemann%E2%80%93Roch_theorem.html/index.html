Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem

[Image: Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem]

The Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem is a far-reaching generalisation of the Riemann–Roch theorem in algebraic geometry, proved by Alexander Grothendieck (with contributions by Armand Borel and Jean-Pierre Serre) in 1957. It relates the Chern character of a coherent sheaf on a smooth variety to the Todd class of the variety.

Statement

For a proper morphism f: X → Y of smooth quasi-projective varieties, the theorem asserts that ch(f!(E)) · td(Y) = f*(ch(E) · td(X)) in the Chow ring of Y, for any coherent sheaf E on X. Here f! is the derived pushforward in K-theory and ch is the Chern character.

Significance

The classical Riemann–Roch theorem for curves and the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem for smooth projective varieties are special cases. The result reframed Riemann–Roch as a statement about functoriality and led directly to the development of K-theory and, later, to the Atiyah–Singer index theorem.
