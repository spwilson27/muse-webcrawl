U.S. Route 66 or U.S. Highway 66 (US 66 or Route 66) was one of the original highways within the United States Numbered Highway System. It was established on 11 November 1926, with road signs erected the following year. The highway, which became one of the most famous roads in the United States, ran from Chicago, Illinois, through Missouri, Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas, New Mexico, and Arizona before ending in Santa Monica, California, covering a total of 2,448 miles (3,940 km).

Route 66 served as a primary route for migrants westward, especially during the Dust Bowl of the 1930s, and it supported the economies of the communities through which it passed. John Steinbeck dubbed it the "Mother Road" in The Grapes of Wrath (1939). The road became a symbol of American mobility and freedom, celebrated in the song "(Get Your Kicks on) Route 66" (1946) and the 1960s television series Route 66.

Decommissioning and legacy

Beginning in the 1950s, the Interstate Highway System progressively bypassed Route 66; the last section was replaced by Interstate 40 in Arizona in 1984, and US 66 was officially removed from the United States Highway System on 26 June 1985. Portions of the road that survive are now designated a National Scenic Byway and signed as "Historic Route 66", and the route remains a focus of preservation efforts, roadside Americana, and tourism. It also inspired the fictional Route 66 settings of Pixar's Cars (2006). (See also U.S. Route 101 for another storied American highway.)
