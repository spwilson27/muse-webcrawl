First Coalition

The First Coalition (1792–1797) was the first major concerted effort by the monarchies of Europe to contain and defeat Revolutionary France. Its members included Austria, Prussia, Great Britain, Spain, the Netherlands, Sardinia, and various other states, at different times between 1792 and 1797, during the War of the First Coalition.

Course of the war

France declared war on Austria in April 1792. After the French victory at Valmy (September 1792) halted the allied invasion, France went on the offensive, occupying the Austrian Netherlands and the Rhineland. The execution of Louis XVI in January 1793 and French expansion brought Britain, Spain, and the Dutch Republic into the coalition. Despite early successes, the coalition was undermined by divergent aims and by the rising military power of France under the levée en masse.

Prussia and Spain left the war by the Peace of Basel in 1795. The coalition finally collapsed after Napoleon Bonaparte's brilliant Italian campaign of 1796–97 forced Austria to sign the Treaty of Campo Formio in October 1797, leaving Britain alone at war with France.
