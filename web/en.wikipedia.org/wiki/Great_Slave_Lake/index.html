Great Slave Lake

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Great Slave Lake
Location: Northwest Territories, Canada
Depth: 614 m — deepest lake in North America
Area: about 27,200 km²

Great Slave Lake is the second-largest lake in Canada's Northwest Territories and the deepest lake in North America, at 614 metres. It lies on the Canadian Shield south of the Arctic Circle, its name an old and unhappy translation: it commemorates the Slavey people (Dehcho Dene), so called by their Cree rivals — a name modern usage increasingly questions, though it persists on the map.

The lake's East Arm is a landscape of Shield rock, islands and deep clear water proposed for protection as Thaidene Nëné National Park Reserve (established 2019, co-managed with the Łutsël K'é Dene First Nation). Yellowknife, the territorial capital, stands on its north shore among gold-mining history and houseboats frozen into the ice each winter; the Mackenzie River — Canada's longest — begins at the lake's western end, draining its basin to the Arctic Ocean.

Frozen for some eight months a year, the lake supports commercial whitefish fisheries, ice roads and a growing tourism of aurora-watching; its warming winters and shortening ice season are remaking northern life, in parallel with Baikal's changing ice far to the west.
