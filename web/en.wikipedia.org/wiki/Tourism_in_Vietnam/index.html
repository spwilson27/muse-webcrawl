Tourism in Vietnam is a significant and growing component of the Vietnamese economy. Following the Đổi Mới economic reforms of 1986 and the normalisation of international relations in the 1990s, Vietnam opened to international visitors, and arrivals grew from a few hundred thousand in the early 1990s to a record 18 million international visitors in 2019, alongside tens of millions of domestic trips each year.

Destinations

Northern Vietnam

The capital Hanoi attracts visitors with its Old Quarter, French colonial architecture, Hoan Kiem Lake, the Temple of Literature and the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum. Hạ Long Bay, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is famed for its emerald waters and thousands of limestone karsts and islets, typically visited on cruise boats. The mountain town of Sa Pa draws trekkers to terraced rice fields and ethnic minority villages.

Central Vietnam

The former imperial capital Huế preserves its citadel and royal tombs. Nearby Hội An is a UNESCO-listed trading port with lantern-lit streets and tailoring shops, and Da Nang is a fast-growing beach city near the Marble Mountains and the Ba Na Hills resort. Phong Nha-Kẻ Bàng National Park protects some of the world's largest caves, including Sơn Đoòng.

Southern Vietnam

Ho Chi Minh City (Saigon) offers colonial landmarks such as Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica and the Central Post Office, the War Remnants Museum, and day trips to the Củ Chi tunnels and the Mekong Delta. Beach destinations include Nha Trang, Mũi Né, and the island of Phú Quốc, which has developed into a major resort hub.

Trends and policy

Vietnam has progressively liberalised its visa policy, expanding visa exemptions and introducing an electronic visa system, in order to compete with regional destinations such as Thailand and Malaysia. The COVID-19 pandemic closed borders from 2020; Vietnam fully reopened to international tourism in March 2022, and arrivals have since recovered strongly, with the government targeting pre-pandemic and higher levels. Tourism contributes on the order of 9% of GDP including indirect effects, and the sector's development priorities include infrastructure, air connectivity and sustainable tourism in heritage areas.
