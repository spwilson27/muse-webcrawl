Wiston Cap

The Wiston Cap (1660)
Wiston Cap
Date1660
MediumEmbroidered wool and silk
LocationParham House, West Sussex
Associated withOliver Cromwell (traditionally)

The Wiston Cap is an embroidered skullcap dated 1660. It is a rare survival of a nightcap of the type worn by men of the 17th century. The cap is made of wool embroidered with silk in a pattern of flowers and leaves, and bears the badge of the House of Hanover. It is said to have originally belonged to Oliver Cromwell.

The cap is believed to have been given by Cromwell to Robert Parker, who lived at Wiston, Sussex. It passed through generations of the Parker family before it came to Robert Thomas Goff, owner of Wiston House, and subsequently came into the ownership of the Curzon family at Parham House in Sussex, where it is now exhibited.

Description

Museum-quality embroidered caps of this period are extremely rare in private collections; most surviving examples are held in major institutions, such as the Victoria and Albert Museum. Domestic caps were worn by men at home for warmth and informality, replacing the wig or hat, and were commonly decorated with intricate patterns of flora — often roses, carnations, and strawberries — in coloured silks and metal threads.

Provenance

According to family tradition, Oliver Cromwell, Lord Protector of the Commonwealth of England, Scotland and Ireland from 1653 until his death in 1658, presented the cap to Robert Parker of Wiston. Cromwell died in 1658, yet the cap is dated 1660, the year of the Restoration of the monarchy; whether the date refers to the year the cap was made, the year it was given, or the year it was adorned with the Hanoverian badge has been the subject of speculation. The badge of the House of Hanover is itself curious on an object associated with the Commonwealth period, Hanoverian succession to the British throne dating only from 1714.

The Wiston Cap remains a remarkable piece of 17th-century needlework, drawing interest for both its craftsmanship and its Cromwellian association.
