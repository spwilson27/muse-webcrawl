Kepler–Poinsot polyhedron

In geometry, a Kepler–Poinsot polyhedron is one of four regular star polyhedra. They may be obtained by stellating the regular convex dodecahedron and icosahedron, and they have regular polygonal faces and identical vertex figures, but unlike the Platonic solids they are not convex and their faces intersect.

The four solids

The four Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra are the small stellated dodecahedron, the great dodecahedron, the great stellated dodecahedron, and the great icosahedron. Johannes Kepler described the two stellated dodecahedra in 1619, and Louis Poinsot rediscovered all four in 1809, including the two Kepler had missed.

Properties

Because their faces pass through one another, Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra violate the usual Euler characteristic formula unless density is taken into account. They form, together with the five Platonic solids, the complete set of nine regular polyhedra.
