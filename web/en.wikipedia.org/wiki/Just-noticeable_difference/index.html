Just-noticeable difference

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: perception threshold diagram]

In the branch of experimental psychology focused on sense, sensation, and perception, the just-noticeable difference (JND), also known as the difference threshold, is the minimum level of stimulation that a person can detect 50 percent of the time.

Weber's law

Ernst Heinrich Weber observed that the JND for a stimulus is a constant proportion of the stimulus's initial intensity — a principle formalised as Weber's law and later incorporated by Gustav Fechner into psychophysics. For lifted weights the ratio is about 1:40; for brightness and loudness the ratios differ by modality.

Applications

JNDs are used in fields from audiology and vision science to marketing, where product changes are sometimes designed to fall below the JND (for example, small reductions in package size), while improvements are made large enough to exceed it.
