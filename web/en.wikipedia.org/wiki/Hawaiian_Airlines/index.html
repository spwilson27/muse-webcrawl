Hawaiian Airlines is a commercial U.S. airline and the flag carrier of the state of Hawaii. Founded in 1929 as Inter-Island Airways, it is the oldest American airline that has never had a fatal accident or lost a hull. Hawaiian operates flights between the Hawaiian Islands and to destinations in North America, Asia, and Oceania from its hub at Daniel K. Inouye International Airport in Honolulu.

History

The airline began inter-island sightseeing and passenger services with Sikorsky amphibious aircraft and later became a pioneer of jet service within the islands. It adopted the name Hawaiian Airlines in 1941. Over the decades it expanded to scheduled service to the U.S. mainland (beginning in 1985) and across the Pacific.

Merger with Alaska Airlines

In December 2023, Alaska Air Group announced it would acquire Hawaiian Airlines for about $1.9 billion including debt. The merger was completed in September 2024, with the two airlines continuing to operate under their separate brands while combining loyalty programmes and moving toward a single operating certificate.

Fleet

Hawaiian's fleet has included Airbus A330 and A321neo aircraft for long-haul routes, Boeing 717s for short inter-island flights, and Boeing 787 Dreamliners introduced in 2024. Its tail logo, "Pualani" (heavenly flower), depicts a Hawaiian woman with a hibiscus in her hair.
