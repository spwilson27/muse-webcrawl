El Zacatón

LocationAldama, Tamaulipas, Mexico
TypeWater-filled sinkhole (cenote)
Depth339 m (1,112 ft) total; water depth about 319 m
SystemSistema Zacatón

El Zacatón is a water-filled sinkhole near Aldama in the state of Tamaulipas, Mexico. It is the deepest known water-filled sinkhole in the world, with a total depth of about 339 metres. It is the largest of several sinkholes in the Sistema Zacatón karst system.

Features

The sinkhole is roughly circular, with steep limestone walls rising above a green, warm, mineral-rich lake whose surface lies about 20 metres below the rim. The water is slightly sulfurous and supports unusual floating islands of travertine and zacate grass, from which the sinkhole takes its name. Uniquely, these islands drift across the surface pushed by the wind.

Exploration

In 1994 the diver Jim Bowden descended to 282 metres, then a world depth record for a cave dive; during the same project his partner Sheck Exley died in an attempt. In 2007 the NASA-funded DEPTHX robotic submarine explored and mapped the sinkhole, reaching its bottom and producing the first detailed map; the robot's technology was intended to inform exploration of subsurface oceans on Europa.
