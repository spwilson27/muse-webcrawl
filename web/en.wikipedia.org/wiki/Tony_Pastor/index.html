Tony Pastor

Tony Pastor (born Antonio Pestritto; May 28, 1837 – August 26, 1908) was an American impresario, variety performer, and theatre owner who is known as the "father of vaudeville".

Pastor began performing as a child in minstrel and variety shows, singing comic songs and performing as a ringmaster in a circus. In 1865 he opened Tony Pastor's Opera House in the Bowery in New York City. Variety entertainment at the time had a rough, male-dominated reputation; Pastor's innovation was to clean it up — banning liquor, profanity, and risqué material — so that women and families would attend.

Legacy

The strategy worked: Pastor's theatres, eventually including a famous house on 14th Street, drew respectable middle-class audiences and launched the careers of many stars of the American stage, including Lillian Russell. His respectable variety format became the template for American vaudeville, which dominated popular entertainment into the early 20th century.
