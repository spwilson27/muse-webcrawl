Scorpius is a zodiac constellation located in the Southern celestial hemisphere, lying between Libra to the west and Sagittarius to the east. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. Its distinctive curved tail of stars makes it one of the most recognisable constellations.

Scorpius with its bright red heart star, Antares.

Notable features

The heart of the scorpion is marked by Antares (α Scorpii), a red supergiant whose name means "rival of Ares (Mars)" for its colour. The claws are formed by stars including Acrab and Dschubba, while the stinger at the tail's tip comprises Lesath and Shaula. Scorpius lies in a rich region of the Milky Way and contains numerous open and globular clusters, including the Butterfly Cluster (M6), the Ptolemy Cluster (M7), and globular clusters M4 and M80.

Mythology

In Greek mythology Scorpius is the scorpion sent by Gaia (or Apollo) that stung and killed the boastful hunter Orion; the two are placed on opposite sides of the sky, so that as Scorpius rises, Orion sets.
