History of the United Arab Emirates

The history of the United Arab Emirates (UAE) runs from Paleolithic settlement and the Bronze Age Magan civilisation, a trading partner of Mesopotamia, through the coming of Islam in the seventh century, Portuguese and then British influence, the era of the Trucial States, and the discovery of oil. On 2 December 1971 six emirates — Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain and Fujairah — formed the federation; Ras Al Khaimah joined in February 1972. Under Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, the UAE used oil wealth to become a global commercial and financial hub.

Dubai Creek was the region's pearling and trading centre long before oil.

Ancient period

Archaeology at Jebel Faya and elsewhere shows human presence over 100,000 years ago. The Bronze Age Umm Al Nar culture (c. 2600–2000 BC) and the land of Magan supplied copper and diorite to Sumer and the Indus Valley. Iron Age sites such as Mleiha and Ed-Dur thrived on caravan and maritime trade with Persia, India and, later, Rome.

Islam and the Portuguese

Islam arrived in AD 630; the Battle of Dibba (632–634) was among the decisive engagements of the Ridda Wars in southeastern Arabia. From 1507 the Portuguese seized coastal forts at Hormuz, Julfar and Khor Fakkan, dominating Gulf trade for a century until expelled with Persian and local help in the 1600s.

The Trucial States

Britain's campaigns against the Qawasim (Al Qasimi), whom it accused of piracy from Ras Al Khaimah and Sharjah, led to the General Maritime Treaty of 1820 and the Perpetual Maritime Truce of 1853 — hence "Trucial Coast". The pearling industry dominated the economy until its collapse in the 1930s amid the Great Depression and Japanese cultured pearls.

Oil and federation

Oil was struck in Abu Dhabi in 1958 (offshore) and 1960 (onshore), with exports from 1962; Dubai found oil in 1966. When Britain announced withdrawal in 1968, the rulers negotiated a federation; Sheikh Zayed of Abu Dhabi and Sheikh Rashid of Dubai were its chief architects. The UAE was proclaimed on 2 December 1971, now celebrated as National Day.

Modern UAE

Oil revenues funded infrastructure, education and sovereign wealth funds (notably ADIA). Dubai diversified into trade, aviation and tourism through Jebel Ali Port and Emirates airline. Khalifa bin Zayed succeeded his father Zayed in 2004; Mohamed bin Zayed became president in 2022. The UAE has pursued an assertive regional role, space missions (the Hope Mars probe, 2021) and economic diversification.
