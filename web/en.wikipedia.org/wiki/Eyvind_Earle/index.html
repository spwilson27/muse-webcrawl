Eyvind Earle (April 26, 1916 – July 20, 2000) was an American artist, author and illustrator, best known for the distinctive background painting and styling he brought to Walt Disney's animated film Sleeping Beauty (1959). His mature work is instantly recognisable for its precision, simplified forms, and an almost sculptural sense of light and mass in landscape.

Early life

Earle was born in New York City in 1916. His father, Ferdinand, was a painter who encouraged his son's drawing from an early age and sold his work; Earle held his first solo exhibition in France at the age of fourteen. The family moved frequently, and Earle spent time in Mexico and California. As a young man he bicycled across the United States, painting watercolours along the way to support himself.

Career at Disney

Earle joined Walt Disney Productions in 1951 as an assistant background painter and quickly rose to become one of the studio's most distinctive artists. He painted backgrounds for Peter Pan (1953) and Lady and the Tramp (1955), as well as the acclaimed short Toot, Whistle, Plunk and Boom (1953). For Sleeping Beauty, Walt Disney gave Earle an unprecedented degree of control over the film's look: Earle designed the backgrounds, much of the colour styling, and influenced the character design, drawing on medieval art, illuminated manuscripts, Persian miniatures and early Renaissance painting. The result was a film of unusual visual richness, although its painstaking production made it expensive and contributed to Earle leaving the studio before its release.

Later work

After Disney, Earle founded his own company producing animated commercials and title sequences. From the mid-1960s he devoted himself to fine art, producing meticulously detailed serigraphs and oil paintings of California landscapes, forests, deserts and coastlines. His paintings are characterised by towering trees, rhythmic rolling hills, deep shadows and a sense of hushed monumentality. He also wrote poetry and an autobiography, Horizon Bound on a Bicycle.

Legacy

Earle was named a Disney Legend in 2015, fifteen years after his death. His work for Sleeping Beauty is widely credited with shaping the visual ambition of later animated films, and his fine-art prints remain popular with collectors.
