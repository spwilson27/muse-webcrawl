The 1956 United States presidential election was the 43rd quadrennial presidential election, held on November 6, 1956. Incumbent Republican president Dwight D. Eisenhower defeated former Illinois governor Adlai Stevenson II of the Democratic Party in a rematch of the 1952 contest. Eisenhower's popular-vote margin was larger than in 1952, and he carried 41 states.

Campaign

Eisenhower ran on peace, prosperity and personal popularity after the Korean War armistice and several years of economic growth. Questions about his health followed a 1955 heart attack and 1956 abdominal surgery, but he remained broadly trusted. Stevenson campaigned on proposals including a halt to atmospheric nuclear testing and criticism of administration farm and foreign policies. Late crises in Hungary and the Suez reinforced Eisenhower's image as an experienced wartime leader.

Results

Eisenhower won about 57 percent of the popular vote to Stevenson's roughly 42 percent, with an electoral vote landslide of 457 to 73; one Alabama elector voted faithlessly for Walter Burgwyn Jones. Despite the presidential landslide, Democrats retained control of Congress, continuing the divided government that characterized much of Eisenhower's second term.
