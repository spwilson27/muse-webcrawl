Overview

Lake Managua, also called Lake Xolotlán, is a lake in western Nicaragua lying immediately north of the capital, Managua, which stands on its southern shore. It covers roughly 1,049 square kilometres and is the smaller of Nicaragua's two great lakes, the other being Lake Nicaragua (Cocibolca) to the southeast, to which it is connected by the Tipitapa River when water levels are high.

The lake sits in a volcanic landscape; the Momotombo volcano rises on its northern shore, and the small Momotombito island lies in the lake. Volcanic activity has periodically caused fish kills.

Pollution

Through much of the 20th century the lake received untreated sewage and industrial waste from Managua, making it one of Central America's most polluted lakes. A wastewater treatment plant opened in 2009 substantially reduced the flow of untreated sewage, and water quality has since been reported to improve, though legacy contamination remains a concern.
