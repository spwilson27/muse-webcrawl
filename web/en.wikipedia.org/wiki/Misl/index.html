Misl

A Misl (Punjabi: ਮਿਸਲ, from the Arabic misal, "example" or "file") was a sovereign state or warrior confederacy of the Sikh Confederacy in the Punjab during the 18th century. After the death of Banda Singh Bahadur in 1716 and decades of Mughal and Afghan persecution, Sikh fighting bands organised themselves into initially two, then eleven (later twelve) misls, which together formed the Dal Khalsa, the combined army of the Khalsa.

The twelve misls

The misls traditionally numbered twelve: the Ahluwalia, Bhangi, Dallewalia, Faizullahpuria (Singhpuria), Kanheya, Karora Singhia, Nakai, Nishanwalia, Ramgarhia, Shahid (Nihang), Sukerchakia and Phulkian misls. Their strength varied widely — from a few hundred horsemen to the Bhangi Misl's roughly 30,000 — and their combined cavalry was estimated at 70,000–100,000 at its height. The misls controlled most of the Punjab between the Indus and the Yamuna by the 1760s–1770s, collecting the rakhi protection tax and minting coinage in the name of the Gurus.

Organisation

Each misl was led by a sardar whose authority rested on personal following rather than heredity, and internally the misls practised an egalitarian ethos among their own horsemen. The Sarbat Khalsa, the assembly of the whole Khalsa at Amritsar, passed gurmatas (resolutions) binding on all misls, though rivalries between them were frequent.

Unification

In 1799 Ranjit Singh of the Sukerchakia Misl captured Lahore and, over the following decades, absorbed or subordinated the other misls west of the Sutlej, founding the unified Sikh Empire (1799–1849). The Phulkian states east of the Sutlej survived under British protection as princely states.
