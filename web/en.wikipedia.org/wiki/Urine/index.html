Urine

Urine

Produced by: Kidneys

Main components: Water (approx. 95%), urea, salts

Typical output: 1–2 litres per day (adult human)

Urine is a liquid by-product of metabolism in humans and many other animals. It is produced by the kidneys, which filter waste products from the blood, and is excreted through the urethra after storage in the urinary bladder. Urination is the primary method for excreting water-soluble waste products such as urea.

Composition

Urine is an aqueous solution of more than 3,000 compounds, about 95% water. Its chief dissolved solids are urea, creatinine, uric acid and inorganic salts of sodium, potassium and chloride. Its normal yellow colour comes from the pigment urobilin, and its pH averages around 6.2, though it varies with diet and health.

Formation

Blood is filtered in the glomeruli of the nephrons; water and solutes are then selectively reabsorbed along the tubules, and additional wastes are secreted, producing urine that passes via the ureters to the bladder. Hormones such as antidiuretic hormone (ADH) regulate its concentration.

Uses and analysis

Urinalysis is a fundamental diagnostic tool: glucose in urine can indicate diabetes, and proteins or blood cells may signal kidney disease. Historically, urine was used in tanning, dyeing, fertiliser production and the manufacture of gunpowder; modern research explores urine as a source of stem cells and as fertiliser in sustainable agriculture.
