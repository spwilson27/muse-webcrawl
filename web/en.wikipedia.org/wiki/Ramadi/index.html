Ramadi (Arabic: الرمادي, Ar-Ramādī) is a city in central Iraq, about 110 kilometres (68 miles) west of Baghdad on the Euphrates River. It is the capital of Al Anbar Governorate, the largest governorate in Iraq by area. Founded in 1869 during the Ottoman era, Ramadi grew as a transport hub on the route between Baghdad and the Syrian border, later serving as a junction for highways to Jordan and Syria.

History

The city was established by Midhat Pasha, the Ottoman governor of Baghdad, as an administrative centre in the fertile Euphrates valley. During the Iraq War (2003–2011), Ramadi was a focal point of the Sunni insurgency and the scene of intense fighting, including the 2004 and 2006 battles in which United States Marine and Army units fought alongside Iraqi forces. The "Anbar Awakening" tribal movement that began in the Ramadi area in 2006 helped turn local tribes against al-Qaeda in Iraq and is considered a turning point of the war.

In May 2015 the city fell to the Islamic State (ISIL) after Iraqi security forces withdrew, prompting a long counteroffensive. Iraqi government forces, backed by coalition air support, retook Ramadi in late December 2015, though much of the city was left in ruins by the fighting, booby traps and improvised explosive devices.

Geography and economy

Ramadi lies on the right bank of the Euphrates, near Lake Habbaniyah and the Ramadi Barrage, which regulates water flow to the lake and downstream channels. The surrounding region is agricultural, producing wheat, dates and vegetables, and the city historically hosted factories for glass and ceramics. The University of Anbar, founded in 1987, is headquartered in Ramadi.

Reconstruction

After 2016, large-scale rebuilding began with support from the Iraqi government and international agencies; residents returned as markets, schools and hospitals reopened, though clearing unexploded ordnance remained a prolonged task.
