F-14 Tomcat

The Grumman F-14 Tomcat is an American supersonic, twin-engine, variable-sweep wing, two-seat carrier-based fighter developed for the United States Navy. It served as the Navy's primary air superiority fighter and fleet air defence interceptor from 1974 to 2006.

F-14 Tomcat
Manufacturer: Grumman
First flight: 21 December 1970
Retired (US): 2006
Crew: 2; Speed: Mach 2.34

Design

Its AWG-9 radar and AIM-54 Phoenix missiles could engage multiple targets at over 100 miles. Variable geometry wings, twin tails and a spacious "tunnel" between engines define its silhouette; later F-14B/D models gained improved engines and strike capability.

Service

F-14s scored kills over Libya (1981, 1989), flew in the Gulf War and Afghanistan, and were exported only to Iran (1976), where they remain in service. Popular culture cemented its fame via the film Top Gun (1986). It was replaced by the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet.
