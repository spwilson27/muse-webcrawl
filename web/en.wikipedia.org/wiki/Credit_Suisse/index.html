Credit Suisse Group AG was a global investment bank and financial services firm founded and based in Switzerland. Headquartered at Paradeplatz in Zurich, it maintained offices in all major financial centres and was one of the nine global "bulge bracket" banks, providing services in investment banking, private banking, asset management and shared services. With UBS, it was one of the two dominant Swiss banks.

History

The bank was founded by Alfred Escher in 1856 as the Schweizerische Kreditanstalt (Swiss Credit Institution) to finance the development of Switzerland's railway system. It grew with Swiss industrialisation, expanded internationally in the 20th century — notably through its link with First Boston in investment banking — and adopted the Credit Suisse name corporately in the 1990s. It survived the 2008 financial crisis without a government bailout, unlike UBS.

Scandals and collapse

The final decade of the bank's independence was marked by repeated scandals and losses, including the "Suisse Secrets" data leak (2022), fines over money laundering and tax evasion, the collapses of Archegos Capital and Greensill Capital in 2021 (which cost the bank billions), and a spying affair involving senior executives. After years of restructuring failed to restore confidence, a crisis of trust in March 2023 — amid turmoil following the failure of Silicon Valley Bank — led to massive deposit outflows and a collapsing share price.

On 19 March 2023, Swiss authorities brokered an emergency takeover by UBS for CHF 3 billion, backed by government guarantees; the deal closed on 12 June 2023. The write-down of Credit Suisse's AT1 bonds to zero while shareholders received payment provoked international controversy and litigation. The legal merger of Credit Suisse AG into UBS AG was completed in 2024, ending the 167-year-old institution's independent existence.
