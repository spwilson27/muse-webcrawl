Diplomacy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Diplomacy is the practice of conducting negotiations between representatives of states or groups, concerning peace-making, trade, war, culture, economics and the environment. It is the principal instrument by which states pursue foreign policy without force, conducted by professional diplomats (ambassadors and envoys accredited to other states and international organisations) and political leaders. International treaties are usually negotiated by diplomats before endorsement by politicians.

History

Diplomatic practice is ancient — Amarna letters between Bronze Age kings, Greek proxenoi, Byzantine and Italian Renaissance resident embassies (a fifteenth-century innovation), and the congresses of Westphalia (1648) and Vienna (1815) codified the modern system of resident missions, precedence and multilateral conferences. The Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961) remains the framework treaty, guaranteeing diplomatic immunity: inviolability of missions and persons, balanced against the duty to respect host-state laws.

Forms

Diplomacy may be bilateral or multilateral (UN and conference diplomacy), public or secret, summit-level or working-level, and specialised — economic, cultural, public, digital and climate diplomacy among them. Track-two diplomacy uses academics and former officials informally. Institutions such as foreign ministries, permanent missions and special envoys conduct it; its failures are studied in crises from July 1914 to the present.
