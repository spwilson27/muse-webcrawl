Kākāpō

The kākāpō (Māori: kākāpō, "night parrot"; Strigops habroptila) is a species of large, flightless, nocturnal parrot endemic to New Zealand. It is the world's heaviest parrot, the only flightless one, and one of the longest-lived birds.

Description

Adults weigh between 0.95 and 4 kg and have moss-green plumage mottled with yellow and black, a distinctive facial disc of sensory bristles, and a sweet, musty odour. Unusually among parrots, kākāpō breed in a lek system: males gather at traditional display courts, dig bowl-shaped scrapes, and boom through the night to attract females, breeding only every two to four years when rimu trees mast.

Conservation

Once widespread, kākāpō were devastated by introduced predators — stoats, cats and rats — and by habitat loss. Since 1995 the Kākāpō Recovery Programme has managed every individual intensively on predator-free offshore islands. The population recovered from a low of 51 birds in 1995 to just over 240 by the mid-2020s; each bird is named and fitted with a radio transmitter. The hand-reared male Sirocco became a famous conservation ambassador.
