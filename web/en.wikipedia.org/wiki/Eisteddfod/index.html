Eisteddfod

An eisteddfod (Welsh: [əiˈstɛðvɔd], plural eisteddfodau) is a festival of Welsh literature, music and performance, traditionally a competition between poets and musicians (bards) presided over by a noble patron. The word derives from the Welsh eistedd ("sit") and fod ("being"), together "a sitting" or session. Eisteddfodau range from small village events to the National Eisteddfod of Wales, one of Europe's largest cultural festivals, held annually in the first week of August and conducted primarily through the medium of Welsh.

History

The first eisteddfod is traditionally dated to 1176, when Lord Rhys ap Gruffudd held a great gathering at Cardigan Castle at which poets and musicians competed for a seat of honour at his table. A notable early event was the Carmarthen eisteddfod of about 1450, organised by Gruffudd ap Nicholas, which produced a codification of Welsh poetic metres. In 1567–68 Elizabeth I commissioned eisteddfodau at Caerwys to license professional minstrels.

After a period of decline, the tradition was revived in the late 18th century, notably through the Gwyneddigion Society in London and the efforts of Edward Williams, better known as Iolo Morganwg, who founded the Gorsedd of the Bards in 1792 and attached its pageantry — some of it invented — to the eisteddfod movement. Provincial eisteddfodau were held through the 19th century, and the modern National Eisteddfod dates from 1861; the National Eisteddfod Association was formed in 1880.

The National Eisteddfod

The National Eisteddfod alternates between north and south Wales. Its ceremonies include the Crowning of the Bard (for a free-metre poem), the award of the Chair to the winning strict-metre poet (awdl), the Prose Medal, and the Daniel Owen Memorial Prize for the novel. Competitions span choral singing, solo recitation, folk dancing, brass bands, drama and the visual arts. The festival is organised by the Gorsedd of Bards, whose druids process in coloured robes — blue for bards, green for ovates and white for druids.

Other eisteddfodau

The Urdd National Eisteddfod, organised by the Welsh-language youth movement Urdd Gobaith Cymru since 1929, is among Europe's largest youth festivals. The Llangollen International Musical Eisteddfod, founded in 1947 to promote peace after the Second World War, hosts amateur choirs and folk dancers from around the world and awards the Choir of the World title. Local eisteddfodau are held in communities throughout Wales and in Welsh settlements abroad, notably in Patagonia, Argentina, where Welsh colonists arrived in 1865.
