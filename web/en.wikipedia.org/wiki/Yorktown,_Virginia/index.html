Yorktown is a census-designated place in York County, Virginia, United States, on the York River. Despite its small population — under 200 residents — it is of outsized historical importance as the site of the Siege of Yorktown in 1781, the last major land battle of the American Revolutionary War, where British General Charles Cornwallis surrendered to George Washington, effectively securing American independence. It is one corner of Virginia's Historic Triangle with Jamestown and Williamsburg.

History

Yorktown was founded in 1691 as the port of York, an official tobacco port for the colony, and prospered as a trading town. In the autumn of 1781, Cornwallis fortified the town after marching his army into Virginia. Washington's Franco-American army, supported by the French fleet under de Grasse, which defeated the Royal Navy at the Battle of the Chesapeake, besieged the position; after bombardment and the storming of two redoubts, Cornwallis surrendered on 19 October 1781. The town declined as a port in the following century and was again contested during the Civil War, when it was occupied and fortified during the 1862 Peninsula Campaign.

Sights

Much of the area is preserved within Colonial National Historical Park, which includes the Yorktown Battlefield, the Nelson House, the Moore House (where surrender terms were negotiated) and the Victory Monument, erected in 1881. The Colonial Parkway links Yorktown with Williamsburg and Jamestown. The American Revolution Museum at Yorktown, operated by the Jamestown-Yorktown Foundation, interprets the war and the siege. Nearby are Naval Weapons Station Yorktown and a Coast Guard training centre.
