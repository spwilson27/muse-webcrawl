Centre-right politics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Centre-right politics is a political position within the left–right spectrum between the centre and the right. Centre-right parties generally combine support for free markets, private property and limited government with respect for tradition and gradual change, and include conservative, Christian democratic and liberal-conservative parties. They are distinguished from the far right by commitment to liberal-democratic institutions, the rule of law and (usually) international cooperation.

Centre-right parties have been the principal governing force in much of post-war Europe — the German CDU/CSU, Britain's Conservatives for most of the modern era, and the parties of the European People's Party group — and in Latin America and parts of Asia. Their policy profile typically includes fiscal restraint, market-oriented reform, support for the welfare state in moderated form, firm law-and-order positions and Atlanticist or pro-European foreign policies, though national variants differ widely.
