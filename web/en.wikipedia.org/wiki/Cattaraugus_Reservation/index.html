The Cattaraugus Reservation is a Native American reservation of the federally recognized Seneca Nation of Indians, one of the Six Nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy, located in western New York State, United States. The reservation spans parts of three counties — Cattaraugus, Chautauqua, and Erie — along Cattaraugus Creek and the shore of Lake Erie, with the Seneca Nation's administrative centre at Irving.

History

The reservation's lands were confirmed to the Seneca under the Treaty of Canandaigua of 1794, signed between the United States and the Six Nations, and its present boundaries largely derive from the Treaty of Big Tree of 1797, in which the Seneca ceded most of their lands west of the Genesee River while reserving several tracts, including Cattaraugus. The Seneca Nation of Indians adopted a republican form of government in 1848, replacing hereditary chiefs with elected officials, and the Cattaraugus Reservation became one of its two principal territories, along with the Allegany Reservation.

Geography and population

The reservation has an area of roughly 21,618 acres (about 34 square miles / 87.5 km²). It stretches along both banks of Cattaraugus Creek from near Gowanda southwestward to the creek's mouth on Lake Erie at Irving. Communities on the reservation include Irving, Perrysburg (partly), and the area around Versailles. The population recorded by the United States Census has been in the range of about 2,400 residents, the great majority of whom are enrolled Seneca citizens.

Government and economy

The Seneca Nation's government alternates its capital between the Cattaraugus and Allegany territories; Nation administrative offices are located in Irving on the Cattaraugus Reservation. The Nation operates governmental, health, and cultural facilities on the reservation, including the Seneca Nation Health System and the Seneca-Iroquois National Museum nearby at Salamanca. The name "Cattaraugus" derives from a Seneca phrase often rendered "bad-smelling banks", referring to the odour of natural gas escaping along the creek.
