Human spaceflight

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Crew members aboard the International Space Station.

Human spaceflight (also called crewed spaceflight) is spaceflight with a crew or passengers aboard the spacecraft. It began on 12 April 1961, when Soviet cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin completed one orbit of Earth aboard Vostok 1. Since then more than six hundred people have flown in space, and humans have maintained a continuous presence in orbit aboard the International Space Station and Chinese stations since 2000.

Human spaceflight programs have been run by the Soviet Union and Russia (Soyuz), the United States (Mercury, Gemini, Apollo, the Space Shuttle and commercial crew vehicles), and China (Shenzhou). Crewed vehicles must keep their occupants alive with life-support systems, protect them during launch and re-entry, and in most designs return them safely to Earth.

Milestones

Key firsts include the first spacewalk by Alexei Leonov in 1965, the first Moon landing by Apollo 11 in 1969, the first space station (Salyut 1) in 1971, and the first privately developed crewed orbital vehicle, SpaceX's Crew Dragon, in 2020. Human spaceflight has also seen fatal accidents, including Soyuz 1, Soyuz 11, and the Challenger and Columbia shuttle losses, which killed twenty-one people in total across those four events.
