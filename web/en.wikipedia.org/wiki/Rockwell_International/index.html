Rockwell International

Rockwell International was a major American manufacturing conglomerate involved in aircraft, the space industry, defence and commercial electronics, automotive and truck components, printing presses and industrial automation. It was formed in 1973 by the merger of North American Rockwell and Rockwell Manufacturing, businesses rooted in enterprises founded by Willard Rockwell.

History

Through its North American Aviation heritage, Rockwell built the B-1 Lancer bomber and, as prime contractor, the Space Shuttle orbiters; its Rocketdyne division built the Shuttle's main engines, and its Collins radio businesses were leaders in avionics. At its peak in the 1980s Rockwell was among the largest defence contractors in the United States.

Break-up

In 1996–97 Rockwell sold its aerospace and defence businesses, including Rocketdyne and the former North American divisions, to Boeing. In 2001 the remaining company split into Rockwell Automation (industrial automation) and Rockwell Collins (avionics and communications), ending the Rockwell International name; Rockwell Collins was acquired by United Technologies in 2018 and became part of Collins Aerospace under RTX.
