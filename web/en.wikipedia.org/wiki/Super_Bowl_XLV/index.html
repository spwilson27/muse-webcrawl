Super Bowl XLV was the championship game of the 2010 NFL season, played on 6 February 2011 at Cowboys Stadium in Arlington, Texas. The Green Bay Packers defeated the Pittsburgh Steelers 31–25 to win their fourth Super Bowl and thirteenth NFL championship overall.

The game

Green Bay, the NFC's sixth seed, had won three road playoff games to reach the final. The Packers built a 21–3 lead: Aaron Rodgers threw touchdown passes to Jordy Nelson and Greg Jennings, and safety Nick Collins returned a Ben Roethlisberger interception 37 yards for a touchdown. Pittsburgh rallied to 21–17 and then 28–25 in the fourth quarter, but Green Bay's defence forced three turnovers in total — including a fourth-quarter fumble by Rashard Mendenhall — and Rodgers found Jennings for a second touchdown and a late field goal sealed the result. Rodgers completed 24 of 39 passes for 304 yards and three touchdowns and was named MVP.

Context and records

The victory returned the Lombardi Trophy — named for legendary Packers coach Vince Lombardi — to Green Bay for the first time since Super Bowl XXXI. Both franchises were among the NFL's most storied: Pittsburgh was seeking a record seventh Super Bowl win, while Green Bay's win made it the first sixth seed from the NFC to win the championship. The paid attendance of 91,060 (with temporary seating problems that left some ticket holders without seats, prompting litigation) fell just short of the Super Bowl record, but the Fox telecast averaged 111 million viewers, then the largest audience in American television history. The Black Eyed Peas headlined a halftime show guest-starring Usher and Slash; Christina Aguilera's flubbed line in the national anthem also drew wide notice.
