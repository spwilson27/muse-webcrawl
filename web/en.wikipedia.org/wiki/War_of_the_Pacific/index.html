ResultChilean victory; Chile annexes Tarapacá and Antofagasta

The War of the Pacific (Spanish: Guerra del Pacífico), also known as the Saltpeter War, was fought between Chile and a Bolivian–Peruvian alliance from 1879 to 1884 over control of the nitrate-rich Atacama Desert territories on the Pacific coast of South America.

Origins

The dispute centred on Bolivia's imposition of a tax on Chilean nitrate mining companies in Antofagasta, in defiance of an 1874 treaty. Chile occupied Antofagasta in February 1879; Peru, bound by a secret defensive alliance with Bolivia, refused to remain neutral, and Chile declared war on both countries on 5 April 1879.

Course

Chile won command of the sea after the capture of the Peruvian ironclad Huáscar at Angamos in October 1879. Chilean forces then invaded and occupied the Peruvian provinces of Tarapacá, Tacna and Arica, and after victories at Chorrillos and Miraflores occupied Lima in January 1881. Peruvian guerrilla resistance continued in the sierra until 1883.

Aftermath

By the Treaty of Ancón (1883) Peru ceded Tarapacá to Chile; Bolivia, by the Treaty of Valparaíso (1884), lost its entire coastline, becoming a landlocked country — a grievance that persists in Bolivian politics to this day.
