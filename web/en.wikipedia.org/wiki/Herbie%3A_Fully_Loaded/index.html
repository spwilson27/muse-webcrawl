Herbie: Fully Loaded is a 2005 American sports comedy film directed by Angela Robinson from a screenplay by Thomas Lennon, Robert Ben Garant, Alfred Gough, and Miles Millar. The film is the sixth and final installment in the Herbie film series, following the television film The Love Bug (1997), and is a loose sequel to Herbie Goes Bananas (1980). It stars Lindsay Lohan as Maggie Peyton, the daughter of a former NASCAR driver who buys Herbie — a sentient 1963 Volkswagen Beetle with racing number 53 — and discovers his magical abilities.

Plot

Maggie Peyton, a recent college graduate, is given money by her father Ray, a former NASCAR champion, to buy a car as a graduation present. At a junkyard she buys a battered Volkswagen Beetle — Herbie — for $75. The car soon reveals its personality and racing talents, helping Maggie win an impromptu street race against the arrogant champion Trip Murphy, who becomes determined to defeat and destroy the odd little car.

Concealing her racing from her protective father, Maggie races under a pseudonym with the help of her friend Kevin, a mechanic. Her success turns sour when she succumbs to showboating and Herbie, feeling betrayed after she agrees to race Trip's car, loses a demolition derby on purpose and is wrecked and towed. Maggie must win a local stock-car race to earn the money to recover Herbie, then reconciles with the car and, with her father's blessing, races Trip in a final showdown — winning with Herbie driving on the wall of the track.

Several real NASCAR drivers, including Jeff Gordon, make cameo appearances.

Production

The film used more than 100 Volkswagen Beetles in various states of repair and modification for Herbie's stunts and transformations, including versions prepared for street racing, demolition derby, and stock-car racing. Director Angela Robinson aimed to honour the original films, beginning the picture with a montage of clips from the earlier Herbie movies.

Reception

Herbie: Fully Loaded grossed $66 million in the United States and Canada and $144.1 million worldwide against a $50 million budget. Critics gave the film mixed reviews, generally praising Lohan's performance and the film's family-friendly energy while finding the plot formulaic. It holds a mid-range approval rating on review aggregators, with a warmer audience reception.
