United Auto Workers

The United Auto Workers (UAW), formally the International Union, United Automobile, Aerospace and Agricultural Implement Workers of America, is one of the largest trade unions in North America, representing workers in the United States (including Puerto Rico) and Canada. Founded in 1935 in Detroit, Michigan, the UAW rose to prominence organising the automobile industry and became a central force in American labour, civil rights and Democratic Party politics.

History

The union won recognition at General Motors after the Flint sit-down strike of 1936–37, one of the most consequential strikes in American labour history, and at Ford in 1941 after a long and sometimes violent campaign. Under president Walter Reuther (1946–1970) the UAW pioneered pattern bargaining, cost-of-living adjustments and employer-funded pensions and health care, embodied in the 1950 "Treaty of Detroit" contract with GM. Reuther also made the union a major supporter of the civil rights movement; the UAW helped fund and organise the 1963 March on Washington.

Decline and renewal

Membership peaked at about 1.5 million in 1979, then declined with automation, offshoring and deindustrialisation, falling below 400,000 by the late 2000s. The union made major concessions during the 2008–09 auto industry crisis, including transferring retiree health care to an independent trust (VEBA). From the 1970s the UAW diversified, organising university graduate students, legal services workers, casino dealers and other sectors.

Recent years

A federal corruption scandal in the late 2010s led to convictions of former officials and a government-supervised referendum that introduced direct election of officers. In 2023, under president Shawn Fain, the UAW conducted an unprecedented simultaneous "stand-up strike" against all three Detroit automakers — GM, Ford and Stellantis — winning record wage increases, restoration of cost-of-living adjustments and commitments on electric-vehicle plants. In 2024 the union won a landmark organising victory at Volkswagen's plant in Chattanooga, Tennessee, its first at a foreign-owned car factory in the South.
