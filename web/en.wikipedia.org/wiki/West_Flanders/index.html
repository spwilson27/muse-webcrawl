West Flanders (Dutch: West-Vlaanderen) is a province of Belgium in the Flemish Region. It is the westernmost province of the country, bordering the North Sea, the Netherlands and France, and the Belgian provinces of East Flanders and Hainaut. Its capital and largest city is Bruges.

Geography

The province includes Belgium's entire North Sea coastline (about 65 km), with resorts such as Ostend, Blankenberge, De Panne and Knokke-Heist, behind which lie flat polder landscapes reclaimed from the sea. Inland are the cities of Kortrijk, Roeselare and Ypres (Ieper). The Ypres Salient was the scene of some of the heaviest fighting of the First World War, including the battles of Passchendaele in 1917, and the countryside is dotted with Commonwealth war cemeteries and memorials such as the Menin Gate and Tyne Cot.

Economy and culture

The economy combines tourism on the coast, the port of Zeebrugge (part of the merged Port of Antwerp-Bruges), textiles and food processing around Kortrijk and Roeselare, and intensive agriculture. The West Flemish dialects spoken in the province differ noticeably from standard Dutch. Bruges's medieval centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the province is closely associated with Flemish cycling culture, including the Tour of Flanders, which finishes in nearby Oudenaarde and climbs hills on the provincial border.
