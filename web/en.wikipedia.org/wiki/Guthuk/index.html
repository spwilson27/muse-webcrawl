Guthuk

Guthuk
Type: Noodle soup
Origin: Tibet
Occasion: Guthor, eve of Losar
Key feature: Fortune dough balls

Guthuk is a traditional Tibetan noodle soup eaten on Guthor, the 29th day of the 12th lunar month, the eve of Losar (Tibetan New Year). It is a barley noodle soup made with hand-pulled dough bits and seasonal ingredients.

Tradition

Guthuk is famous for its playful fortune-telling dough balls: large dough balls are filled with symbolic items — such as wool (good luck), coal (bad character traits), chilli (sharp speech), or salt — and distributed among diners. The item a person finds is jokingly said to reveal their character or fortune for the coming year.

Ingredients

The soup typically contains nine ingredients, which may include meat, radish, cheese, peas, and hand-torn noodles, varying by household and region. Eating guthuk is part of rituals to expel evil and negativity before the new year.
