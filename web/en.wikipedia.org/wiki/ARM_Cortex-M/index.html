ARM Cortex-M

The ARM Cortex-M is a group of 32-bit RISC processor cores designed by Arm Holdings and licensed to manufacturers for use in microcontrollers and embedded systems. The family includes the Cortex-M0, M0+, M3, M4, M7, M23, M33, M52, M55, and M85, spanning from tiny low-power cores to high-performance cores with DSP, floating-point, and machine-learning extensions.

Architecture

Cortex-M processors implement the ARMv6-M, ARMv7-M, or ARMv8-M instruction set architectures, using the Thumb and Thumb-2 instruction encodings. They feature a nested vectored interrupt controller (NVIC) for fast, deterministic interrupt handling, optional memory protection units, and low-power sleep modes — characteristics suited to real-time embedded applications such as automotive controllers, industrial automation, consumer devices, and Internet of things products. Popular product families built on Cortex-M include STM32, ESP32-S series companions, and the RP2040 in the Raspberry Pi Pico (Cortex-M0+).
