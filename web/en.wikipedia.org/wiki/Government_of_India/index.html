The Government of India (ISO: Bhārata Sarakāra) is the national government of the Republic of India, situated in New Delhi, the capital of India. It is a federal union of states and union territories governed under a parliamentary system established by the Constitution of India, which came into force on 26 January 1950.

Executive

The executive consists of the President — the constitutional head of state, elected by an electoral college — the Vice President, and the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister, who exercises real executive power and is accountable to the Lok Sabha, the lower house of Parliament. The Union Council of Ministers administers through ministries and the civil services, headed by the Indian Administrative Service.

Legislature

Parliament comprises the President, the Rajya Sabha (Council of States, upper house) and the Lok Sabha (House of the People), whose 543 elected members are chosen by universal adult suffrage in single-member constituencies. States have their own legislatures and governments, with subjects divided between Union, State, and Concurrent Lists.

Judiciary

The independent judiciary is headed by the Supreme Court of India, with High Courts in the states and a hierarchy of subordinate courts. The Supreme Court is the final interpreter of the Constitution and guardian of fundamental rights, empowered by writs and judicial review.
