Make in India

Make in India is an initiative of the Government of India launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 25 September 2014 to encourage companies to develop, manufacture, and assemble products in India, and to incentivise dedicated investment in manufacturing. It aims to transform India into a global design and manufacturing hub.

Objectives

The programme's stated targets included raising the manufacturing sector's growth to 12–14% per year, increasing manufacturing's share of GDP to 25%, and creating 100 million additional manufacturing jobs by 2022 (later revised). It identified 25 focus sectors, including automobiles, electronics, pharmaceuticals, textiles, defence manufacturing, and renewable energy.

Policy measures

Make in India was accompanied by measures to improve the ease of doing business: streamlined industrial licensing, single-window clearances, digitised approvals, and liberalised foreign direct investment caps in sectors such as defence, railways, and insurance. Subsequent linked initiatives included Skill India, Digital India, Startup India, and the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes announced from 2020 to subsidise domestic output in sectors such as mobile phones and electronics.

Assessment

Supporters point to growth in electronics assembly — India became a major smartphone manufacturer and exporter — and rising FDI inflows. Critics note that manufacturing's share of GDP remained broadly around 16–17% rather than reaching 25%, and that job creation fell short of the original goals. The initiative remains a central slogan of Indian industrial policy, and its gear-and-lion logo, built from cogs and silhouetted as a walking lion, became a widely recognised emblem.
