Kingston, Jamaica

Kingston Country: Jamaica
Founded: 1692
Status: Capital and largest city
Population: about 937,700 (metropolitan area)
Port: Kingston Harbour, one of the world's largest natural harbours

Kingston is the capital and largest city of Jamaica, located on the south-eastern coast of the island. It faces a natural harbour protected by the Palisadoes, a long sand spit that connects the former town of Port Royal to the mainland. Kingston is the political, commercial and cultural centre of Jamaica and the birthplace of reggae music.

History

The city was founded in July 1692 after an earthquake destroyed the nearby port town of Port Royal; survivors camped on the Liguanea Plain, and the new town was laid out on a grid plan. Kingston grew as an agricultural and trading port and in 1872 replaced Spanish Town as the capital of Jamaica.

A devastating earthquake in January 1907 destroyed much of the city and killed around 1,000 people; rebuilding followed in reinforced concrete with height restrictions. After Jamaican independence in 1962 the city expanded rapidly northward into the residential districts of St Andrew, forming the Kingston Metropolitan Area.

Culture

Kingston is the heart of Jamaican music: reggae, ska, rocksteady, dub and dancehall all developed in its studios and sound-system dances. The Bob Marley Museum is housed in the singer's former home on Hope Road, and the neighbourhood of Trench Town, where Marley grew up, is a landmark of reggae history. The city is also home to the National Gallery of Jamaica and the University of the West Indies' Mona campus.

Geography and transport

The city sits on the Liguanea Plain with the Blue Mountains to the north-east. Kingston Harbour is among the largest natural harbours in the world, and the Port of Kingston is a major Caribbean transshipment hub. Norman Manley International Airport serves the city from the Palisadoes.
