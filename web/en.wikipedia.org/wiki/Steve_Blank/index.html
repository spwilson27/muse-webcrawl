Steve Blank

Steven Gary Blank (born 1953) is an American entrepreneur, educator and author based in Silicon Valley. He created the customer development method that launched the Lean Startup movement, and is regarded as one of the most influential figures in modern entrepreneurship education.

Career

Blank arrived in Silicon Valley in 1978 and worked at a succession of technology companies over 21 years, including ESL, Zilog, MIPS Computers, Convergent Technologies, Ardent Computer and SuperMac. He was a co-founder or executive at several startups, among them the games company Rocket Science Games and the customer-relationship software company E.piphany, whose 1999 IPO marked his retirement from operating roles. In total he participated in eight startups.

Customer development

Drawing on that experience, Blank concluded that startups fail not from lack of technology but from lack of customers, and that they should "get out of the building" to test hypotheses with customers before building products. He set out the method in The Four Steps to the Epiphany (2005) and, with Bob Dorf, The Startup Owner's Manual (2012). His Stanford and UC Berkeley classes, and the National Science Foundation's I-Corps programme built on his curriculum, taught the approach to thousands of founders and scientists; Eric Ries, one of his early students and investees, popularised the method under the "lean startup" banner.

Later work

Blank teaches at Stanford, Columbia and other universities, writes widely on innovation in large organisations and government, and co-created the "Hacking for Defense" course, which applies lean methods to national-security problems.
