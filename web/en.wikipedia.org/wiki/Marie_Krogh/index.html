Birte Marie Krogh (née Jørgensen; 25 December 1874 – 25 March 1943) was a Danish physician and physiologist. She was one of the first women to earn a medical doctorate in Denmark, and collaborated with her husband, Nobel laureate August Krogh, on research into pulmonary gas exchange.

Career

Marie Krogh qualified in medicine in 1907 and received her doctorate in 1914 with a dissertation on the diffusion of gases through the lungs. Her experimental work provided the evidence that oxygen passes from the alveoli into the blood by passive diffusion rather than by active secretion — a key point in the controversy with J. S. Haldane's school, resolved in the Kroghs' favour.

She later researched thyroid disorders and helped introduce insulin production to Denmark after accompanying August Krogh to Canada and the United States in 1922; their work led to the founding of the Nordisk Insulinlaboratorium. She also lectured on nutrition and was active in women's professional organisations.
