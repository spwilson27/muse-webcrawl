Laguiole

Laguiole (pronounced [laɡjɔl], locally [lajɔl]) is a commune in the Aveyron department in the Occitanie region of southern France, on the Aubrac plateau. It has given its name to two celebrated products: the Laguiole knife and Laguiole cheese.

Laguiole knife

The Laguiole knife is a traditional French folding pocket knife that originated in the village in the 1820s, evolving from the local shepherd's knife combined with influences from Spanish navaja knives brought by seasonal Catalan workers. Its hallmarks are a slender, curved handle, a narrow yatagan-style blade, and a decorated spring often topped with a bee or fly motif (la mouche). Originally made by village cutlers, production shifted largely to Thiers in the late nineteenth century; it revived in Laguiole itself from the 1980s, notably with the founding of Forge de Laguiole. Because "Laguiole" was never protected as a trademark for knives, generic knives of widely varying quality—many made abroad—are sold under the name, a long-running legal and commercial dispute.

Laguiole cheese

Laguiole cheese is a firm, pressed, uncooked cow's-milk cheese made on the Aubrac plateau from the milk of Aubrac and Simmental cows, matured from four months to over two years. It has held AOC/AOP status since 1961. It is closely related to Cantal and is traditionally used in aligot, the regional dish of mashed potatoes with cheese, cream, and garlic.

The village

Laguiole lies at about 1,000 m elevation on the Aubrac, a region of high pasture where cattle are still moved to summer grazing (transhumance) and housed in stone huts called burons. The village is also home to the renowned restaurant of the Bras family, long among France's most decorated. Winters are cold, and the area has a small ski station.
