Linear algebra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Linear algebra is the branch of mathematics concerning linear equations, linear maps (such as rotations and scalings) and their representation by vectors, vector spaces and matrices. It is a central, unifying subject in mathematics and is fundamental in nearly all its applications areas, including physics, engineering, computer graphics, statistics and machine learning.

Contents1. Core concepts
2. History

Core concepts

A vector space is a collection of vectors that can be added and scaled. A matrix represents a linear transformation between spaces; solving systems of linear equations, determinants, eigenvalues and eigenvectors are core topics. Eigenvectors — directions unchanged by a transformation — underlie everything from vibrating structures to Google's PageRank and principal-component analysis.

History

Systems of linear equations were solved in ancient China (the Nine Chapters) and by many later cultures. Determinants were studied by Leibniz and Cramer; matrix theory was systematised in the 19th century by Cayley, Grassmann and others, and vector spaces were axiomatised by Peano in 1888.
