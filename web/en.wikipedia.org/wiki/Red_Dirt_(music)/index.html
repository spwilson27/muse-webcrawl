Red Dirt (music)

Red Dirt
Stylistic originsCountry, folk, rock, Americana, Western swing, blues
Cultural origins1970s–1980s, Stillwater, Oklahoma
Key scenesOklahoma and Texas

Red Dirt is a genre of country music that gets its name from the colour of the soil found in Oklahoma. Although it is chiefly associated with Oklahoma, the closely related Texas country scene is often grouped under the same umbrella. Stillwater, Oklahoma, is considered the epicentre of Red Dirt music; the movement grew out of a farmhouse scene there known as "The Farm", where musicians gathered and jammed beginning in the late 1970s.

History

Bob Childers, who settled in Stillwater and mentored generations of younger songwriters, is widely called the godfather of Red Dirt music. The scene coalesced around Stillwater bars and songwriters such as Jimmy LaFave, Tom Skinner and the Red Dirt Rangers. In the 1990s, Cross Canadian Ragweed carried the sound to wider audiences, while Texas acts such as Pat Green, Robert Earl Keen and Cory Morrow built a parallel touring circuit of dancehalls and college towns defined by independence from Nashville's mainstream.

Characteristics

Red Dirt music blends country with folk, rock and roll, Southern rock, blues and Western swing. Songwriting tends to emphasise storytelling, place and working-class themes, and artists typically build followings through relentless regional touring rather than radio. Critics distinguish it from mainstream country by its rougher edges and closer kinship with Americana and outlaw country.
