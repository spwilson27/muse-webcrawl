Met Office Hadley Centre

The Met Office Hadley Centre — named in honour of George Hadley — is one of the United Kingdom's foremost centres for climate change research. Founded in 1990 and based at the Met Office in Exeter, it is partly funded by the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra) and provides scientific advice on climate science to the UK government.

Work

The Centre develops and runs the HadGEM and UKESM families of climate and Earth-system models, produces the HadCRUT global temperature record jointly with the Climatic Research Unit at the University of East Anglia, and publishes observational datasets such as HadSST (sea surface temperatures). Its scientists are prominent contributors to the assessment reports of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), and the Centre issues decadal predictions and detection-and-attribution studies of observed climate change.

History

The Centre was opened in 1990, with Sir John Houghton as a leading figure in its establishment, following growing scientific and political concern about global warming in the late 1980s. It moved with the Met Office to Exeter in 2003.
