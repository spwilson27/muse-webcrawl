Mission style

Mission style furniture and a Mission Revival building

Mission style is a design movement with distinct sub-styles in furniture and architecture that emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries in the United States.

Furniture

In furniture, Mission style is a style of furniture that originated in the late 19th century, emphasising simple horizontal and vertical lines and flat panels that accentuate the grain of the wood — most often quartersawn white oak — with minimal ornament. It is closely associated with the American Arts and Crafts movement and with designers such as Gustav Stickley, whose magazine The Craftsman popularised it.

Architecture

In architecture, the related Mission Revival style drew on the Spanish colonial missions of California and the Southwest, featuring stucco walls, arched openings, red tile roofs, and bell towers (espadañas). It was widely used for railway stations, hotels and theatres in the early 1900s.
