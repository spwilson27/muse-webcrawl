Student syndrome

Student syndrome
FieldProject management, behavioural psychology
Popularised byEliyahu M. Goldratt, Critical Chain (1997)
DefinitionDelaying work until the last possible moment before a deadline
Related conceptsProcrastination, Parkinson's law, planning fallacy

Student syndrome refers to the phenomenon in which a person will start to fully apply themselves to a task only immediately before a deadline. The term was popularised by Eliyahu M. Goldratt in his 1997 business novel Critical Chain, in the context of project management and the theory of constraints.

Description

Just as students often begin studying only on the night before an exam, project team members given generous task deadlines tend to waste the early safety margin and start in earnest only when the deadline becomes urgent. The result is that the built-in safety is consumed by delay rather than being available to absorb genuine problems: if anything unexpected happens once work finally starts, the task finishes late. Student syndrome thus defeats the purpose of padding estimates.

Countermeasures

Critical chain project management addresses the syndrome by removing safety from individual task estimates and pooling it into shared project buffers, with tasks treated as "relay races" in which each resource starts as early as possible. Other approaches include shorter milestones, timeboxing, and making progress visible. The phenomenon is related to Parkinson's law ("work expands so as to fill the time available for its completion") and to general procrastination.
