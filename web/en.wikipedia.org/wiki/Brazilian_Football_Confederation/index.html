Brazilian Football Confederation

Founded: 8 June 1914

CBF headquarters, Barra da Tijuca, Rio de Janeiro

The Brazilian Football Confederation (Portuguese: Confederação Brasileira de Futebol, CBF) is the governing body of association football in Brazil. Founded on 8 June 1914 as the Federação Brasileira de Sports, it organises the national championships — including the Campeonato Brasileiro Série A and the Copa do Brasil — and administers the Brazil national football teams at all levels, men's and women's.

History

The CBF joined FIFA in 1923 and is a founding member of CONMEBOL (1916). Under its administration the men's national team became the most successful in World Cup history, winning five titles (1958, 1962, 1970, 1994 and 2002). The confederation was led for almost a quarter of a century by Ricardo Teixeira (1989–2012), whose tenure ended amid corruption allegations that later engulfed several successors; former president José Maria Marin was convicted in the United States in connection with the FIFA corruption case. The CBF is headquartered in Barra da Tijuca, Rio de Janeiro.
