Urinary tract infection

A urinary tract infection (UTI) is an infection that affects a part of the urinary tract. When it affects the lower urinary tract it is known as a bladder infection (cystitis), and when it affects the upper urinary tract it is known as a kidney infection (pyelonephritis). Symptoms from a lower urinary tract infection include pain with urination, frequent urination, and feeling the need to urinate despite having an empty bladder.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms of a kidney infection include fever and flank pain, usually in addition to the symptoms of a lower UTI. Rarely, the urine may appear bloody. In the very young and the very old, symptoms may be vague or non-specific.

Cause and diagnosis

The most common cause of infection is the bacterium Escherichia coli, though other bacteria or fungi may sometimes be responsible. Risk factors include female anatomy, sexual intercourse, diabetes, obesity, and family history. Diagnosis is generally based on symptoms and testing of the urine for bacteria or white blood cells; urine culture can confirm the infecting organism.

Treatment

The mainstay of treatment is antibiotics, such as nitrofurantoin or trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole. Uncomplicated infections often resolve in a few days with treatment. Prevention measures for recurrent infections include hygiene practices, adequate fluid intake, and in some cases low-dose prophylactic antibiotics.
