Bugsy Siegel

Benjamin "Bugsy" Siegel (February 28, 1906 – June 20, 1947) was an American mobster who was a driving force behind the development of the Las Vegas Strip. Siegel, known as one of the most "infamous and feared gangsters of his day", was the founder and leader of the Bugs and Meyer Mob and a key figure in Murder, Inc. He hated the nickname "Bugsy", derived from slang for "crazy", which was never used to his face.

Early life and crime career

Born to poor Jewish immigrants in Williamsburg, Brooklyn, Siegel formed a street gang with Meyer Lansky that grew into bootlegging, gambling and contract-killing operations during Prohibition. With Lansky, Charles "Lucky" Luciano and others he helped build the National Crime Syndicate. In 1937 the syndicate sent him to California, where he infiltrated Hollywood unions and gambling ships and socialised with movie stars.

Las Vegas and the Flamingo

In the mid-1940s Siegel took over William J. Wilkerson's Flamingo Hotel project in Las Vegas, Nevada. Funded by syndicate money, construction ballooned in cost — commonly estimated from $1 million to $6 million — amid Siegel's extravagance and his contractor girlfriend Virginia Hill's suspected skimming. The Flamingo opened disastrously on 26 December 1946 in rain, with the casino losing money; after a brief closure it reopened in March 1947 and began to profit, but too late for Siegel.

Death

On 20 June 1947 Siegel was shot dead through the window of Virginia Hill's Beverly Hills home, struck by .30-calibre carbine rounds fired from outside. No one was ever charged; the murder is generally attributed to syndicate displeasure over the Flamingo's costs and missing money. The Warren Beatty film Bugsy (1991) dramatised his life. Within minutes of his death, syndicate representatives took control of the Flamingo, which went on to anchor the modern Las Vegas Strip.
