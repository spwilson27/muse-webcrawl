The Righteous Brothers are an American musical duo originally formed by Bill Medley and Bobby Hatfield in 1962 in Orange County, California. They were not brothers; the name was adopted after a fan at an early show exclaimed, "That's righteous, brothers!" Their dramatic, soulful harmonies exemplified the style known as "blue-eyed soul".

Career

Signing with Phil Spector's Philles label in 1964, they recorded "You've Lost That Lovin' Feelin'" (1964), produced by Spector and written by Spector, Barry Mann and Cynthia Weil. The record topped the charts in the US and UK and was later named by BMI as the most-played song of the twentieth century on American radio and television. Other hits include "Just Once in My Life", the chart-topping "(You're My) Soul and Inspiration" (1966), "Ebb Tide" and "Unchained Melody" (1965).

After solo years, the duo reunited in 1974 with the hit "Rock and Roll Heaven". "Unchained Melody" became a massive hit again in 1990 through its use in the film Ghost. Hatfield died in 2003, shortly after the duo's induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame; Medley revived the act in 2016 with singer Bucky Heard.
