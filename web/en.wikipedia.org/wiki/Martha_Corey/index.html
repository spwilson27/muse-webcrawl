Martha Corey

Martha Corey
Born: c. 1619–1621
Died: September 22, 1692, Salem, Massachusetts Bay Colony
Cause of death: Hanging
Known for: Victim of the Salem witch trials

Martha Corey (c. 1619/1621 – September 22, 1692) was an English-born American woman accused of witchcraft during the Salem witch trials of 1692. A respected, churchgoing member of the Salem Village community, her accusation shocked many because of her piety and standing. She was convicted and hanged on Gallows Hill.

Accusation and trial

Corey had openly expressed skepticism about the afflicted girls' accusations, which likely contributed to her own arrest in March 1692. During her examination she maintained her innocence and questioned the credibility of her accusers. Her husband, Giles Corey, initially defended her but was himself accused; he was famously pressed to death with heavy stones on September 19, 1692, after refusing to enter a plea.

Execution and legacy

Martha Corey was hanged on September 22, 1692, in the last group of executions of the trials, alongside Mary Eastey, Ann Pudeator, and others. In 1957 the Massachusetts legislature formally exonerated the remaining victims, including Corey, and she is commemorated at the Salem Witch Trials Memorial and the Proctor's Ledge Memorial.
