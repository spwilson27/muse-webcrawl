Union of the Crowns

The Union of the Crowns (Scottish Gaelic: Aonadh nan Crùintean) was the accession of James VI of Scotland to the thrones of England and Ireland as James I on 24 March 1603. It united the crowns of the separate kingdoms of Scotland and England in a personal union, though the two states remained legally and politically distinct until the parliamentary union of 1707.

Background

Under the Treaty of Perpetual Peace of 1502, James IV of Scotland married Margaret Tudor, daughter of Henry VII of England, and it was their descendant James VI who, on the death of the childless Elizabeth I, inherited the English throne as her nearest Protestant relative. James proclaimed himself "King of Great Britain" in 1604 and sought a fuller union, but both parliaments resisted; only the flag combining the crosses of St George and St Andrew (1606) and mutual naturalisation of subjects born after 1603 resulted.

Consequences

The union ended centuries of Anglo-Scottish warfare and gave the Stuart dynasty a composite monarchy, but separate parliaments, churches (Anglican England and Presbyterian Scotland), and legal systems generated tensions that erupted in the Bishops' Wars and the Wars of the Three Kingdoms in the 1640s. The crowns remained united thereafter, save for the Commonwealth interlude, until the Acts of Union 1707 created the Kingdom of Great Britain.
