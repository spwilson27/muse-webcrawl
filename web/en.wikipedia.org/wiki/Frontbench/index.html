In the Westminster system of government, a frontbench is the front row of seats in a legislative chamber, occupied by the leading politicians of the governing party and of the opposition. By extension, "the frontbench" refers collectively to the senior leadership team of a political party in parliament — for the government, its ministers, and for the opposition, its shadow cabinet and shadow ministers.

Usage

In the United Kingdom House of Commons, government ministers sit on the treasury bench to the Speaker's right, while the Leader of the Opposition and the shadow cabinet sit opposite. Members who hold frontbench positions are known as frontbenchers, as distinct from backbenchers, who hold no ministerial or spokesperson role. The term is used in many Commonwealth parliaments modelled on Westminster, including those of Australia, Canada, New Zealand and Ireland.

Frontbench teams are appointed by party leaders and are expected to maintain collective responsibility: government frontbenchers defend government policy, and opposition frontbenchers lead scrutiny of the corresponding departments and present their party's alternative policies.
