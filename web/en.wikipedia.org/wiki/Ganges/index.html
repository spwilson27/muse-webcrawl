Ganges

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Ganges at Varanasi at dawn, with ghats and temples along the west bank and boats on the wide brown river]
The Ganges at Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh.

The Ganges (also Ganga) is a trans-boundary river of Asia which flows through India and Bangladesh. About 2,525 km (1,569 mi) long, it rises in the western Himalayas in the Indian state of Uttarakhand, flows southeast across the Gangetic Plain of northern India, and empties into the Bay of Bengal through the vast Ganges Delta. The river is sacred in Hinduism and is worshipped as the goddess Ganga.

Course

The Ganges proper begins at Devprayag, where the Bhagirathi and Alaknanda rivers meet. It flows past Kanpur, Prayagraj (where it meets the Yamuna), Varanasi and Patna, before dividing into distributaries in West Bengal; the main branch enters Bangladesh as the Padma and joins the Brahmaputra and Meghna before reaching the sea.

Religious significance

Hindus regard the Ganges as the holiest of rivers; bathing in it is believed to cleanse sins, and its water is used in religious rituals. Cities such as Varanasi and Prayagraj host huge pilgrimages, including the Kumbh Mela, among the largest gatherings in the world.

Pollution

The river supports a population of more than 400 million people in its basin, and suffers severe pollution from industrial waste, sewage and agricultural runoff. Government programmes such as Namami Gange have aimed to clean it, with mixed results.
