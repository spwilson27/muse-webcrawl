Arab–Israeli conflict

The Arab–Israeli conflict is a long-running series of political tensions and military conflicts between Israel and its Arab neighbours, as well as between Israelis and Palestinians, rooted in competing claims to the land of historic Palestine. It has been one of the most enduring conflicts of the modern era.

Region: Middle East / Levant
Parties: Israel; Arab states; Palestinian groups
Origins: Late 19th–early 20th century

Background

The conflict's roots lie in the rise of Zionism and Arab nationalism, the British Mandate for Palestine, and the 1947 UN Partition Plan. Israel declared independence in 1948, and the ensuing war led to the displacement of hundreds of thousands of Palestinians (the Nakba) and Jewish migration from Arab countries.

Major wars

Major interstate wars included the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, the Suez Crisis (1956), the Six-Day War (1967), the War of Attrition, the Yom Kippur War (1973), and the Lebanon wars of 1982 and 2006. Peace treaties were signed with Egypt (1979) and Jordan (1994), and the Abraham Accords (2020) normalized relations with several Arab states. The Israeli–Palestinian dimension, including the intifadas, the Oslo Accords, and the Gaza conflicts, remains unresolved.
