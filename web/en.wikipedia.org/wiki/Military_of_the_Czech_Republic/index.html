Military of the Czech Republic

The Armed Forces of the Czech Republic (Czech: Ozbrojené síly České republiky) comprise the Czech Army (land forces), the Czech Air Force and special support units. They are responsible for the defence of the Czech Republic, a member of NATO since 1999 and of the European Union since 2004.

History

The modern force descends from the Czechoslovak Army and the Czechoslovak Legions of the First World War. After the peaceful dissolution of Czechoslovakia in 1993, the armed forces were divided between the Czech Republic and Slovakia. Conscription was abolished at the end of 2004, making the force fully professional.

Structure

The President of the Czech Republic is commander-in-chief, and the Ministry of Defence exercises civilian control. Czech units have served in Afghanistan, Iraq, Mali, Kosovo and the Baltic states, and the army operates equipment such as T-72 tanks, Pandur II armoured vehicles and L-159 light combat aircraft.
