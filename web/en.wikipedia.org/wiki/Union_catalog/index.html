Union catalog

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A union catalog (or union catalogue) is a combined library catalog describing the collections of several libraries in a single searchable database. By recording which library holds which items, a union catalog lets readers locate a book across a region or the whole world, and it underpins interlibrary lending, shared cataloguing, and cooperative collection management.

History and examples

Union catalogs began as printed volumes and card files: the British Museum's printed catalogue aggregated holdings beyond one collection, and in the United States the National Union Catalog, maintained by the Library of Congress, cumulated printed cards from hundreds of libraries. The computer era transformed the concept. WorldCat, maintained by OCLC since 1971 (as the Ohio College Library Center's shared system), is the world's largest union catalog, with hundreds of millions of bibliographic records and billions of holdings from libraries in over a hundred countries. Other major examples include COPAC and its successor Jisc Library Hub Discover in the United Kingdom, the German K10plus and the Karlsruhe Virtual Catalog, Australia's Trove, and the European aggregation Europeana for digital cultural heritage. Union catalogs of serials have been especially valuable, since no single library holds complete runs of most journals. Maintaining one requires shared cataloguing standards (such as MARC records and authority control) and persistent deduplication of records describing the same edition.
