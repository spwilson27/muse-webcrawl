Helen Frankenthaler

Born: 12 December 1928, New York City, U.S.
Died: 27 December 2011, Darien, Connecticut, U.S.
Education: Bennington College
Known for: Painting, printmaking
Movement: Abstract expressionism, Color Field painting

Helen Frankenthaler (December 12, 1928 – December 27, 2011) was an American abstract expressionist painter. She was a major contributor to the history of postwar American painting. Having exhibited her work for over six decades (early 1950s until 2011), she spanned several generations of abstract painters while continuing to produce vital and ever-changing new work.

Life and career

Frankenthaler was born in New York City and studied at Bennington College, where she was taught by Paul Feeley. Through the critic Clement Greenberg she met leading figures of the New York School, and in 1951 she had her first solo exhibition. After seeing the paintings of Jackson Pollock, she developed her signature "soak-stain" technique, pouring thinned paint directly onto unprimed canvas so that pigment soaked into the support.

Her breakthrough painting Mountains and Sea (1952), painted after a trip to Nova Scotia, pioneered the technique and directly influenced Morris Louis and Kenneth Noland, helping to launch Color Field painting. Frankenthaler later moved from oil to acrylic paint, which allowed brighter, more even fields of colour, and in the 1970s worked with large stained and brushed areas. She was also an accomplished printmaker, particularly in woodcut.

Recognition

Frankenthaler had major retrospectives at the Whitney Museum of American Art (1969) and the Museum of Modern Art (1989). She received the National Medal of Arts in 2001. She was married to the painter Robert Motherwell from 1958 to 1971. The Helen Frankenthaler Foundation, established in her will, supports the visual arts. She died in Darien, Connecticut, on December 27, 2011, aged 83.
