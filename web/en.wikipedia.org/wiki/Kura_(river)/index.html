The Kura is an east-flowing river south of the Greater Caucasus which drains the southern slopes of the Greater Caucasus east into the Caspian Sea. It also drains the north side of the Lesser Caucasus, while its main tributary, the Aras, drains the north side of those mountains south of it. Starting in northeastern Turkey, it flows through Turkey to Georgia, then to Azerbaijan, where it receives the Aras as a right tributary, and enters the Caspian Sea at Neftçala. The total length of the river is 1,515 kilometres.

Known in Georgian as Mt'k'vari and in Azerbaijani as Kür, the Kura is the largest river of the South Caucasus. It was known to the ancient Greeks and Romans as the Cyrus, from which the modern name derives.

Course

The Kura rises in the highlands of northeastern Turkey at over 2,700 metres, flows north-east through deep gorges into Georgia, where it passes the cave city of Vardzia and the town of Akhaltsikhe. It then turns east, flowing through Gori and Mtskheta before reaching Tbilisi, the Georgian capital, which straddles its banks. Entering Azerbaijan, the river crosses the dry Shirvan steppe, is impounded at the Mingachevir Reservoir — the largest body of water in Azerbaijan — and finally merges with the Aras (Araks) near Sabirabad before reaching the Caspian delta south of Neftçala.

Use and ecology

The Kura–Aras basin supports most of the agriculture of Georgia and Azerbaijan. The river is heavily used for irrigation and hydroelectric power, notably at Mingachevir, and its lower course was once navigable. Pollution from industry and agriculture, and heavy water abstraction, are major ecological concerns; the Kura delta is an important habitat for migratory birds, and the river historically supported valuable sturgeon fisheries.
