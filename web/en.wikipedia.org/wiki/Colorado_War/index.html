Colorado War

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The war was fought across the high plains of Colorado Territory.

The Colorado War was a series of conflicts fought between 1864 and 1865 in Colorado Territory and neighbouring regions between the United States Army, Colorado volunteers, and settlers on one side, and confederated forces of the Cheyenne, Arapaho, and Lakota peoples on the other. It followed the steady encroachment of miners and settlers onto lands guaranteed to the tribes by the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1851) and the contested Treaty of Fort Wise (1861).

Sand Creek massacre

Tensions escalated through 1864 as Governor John Evans issued proclamations authorising attacks on hostile Indians and Colonel John Chivington's Colorado volunteers sought battle. On November 29, 1864, Chivington's troops attacked a peaceful encampment of Cheyenne and Arapaho, under chiefs Black Kettle and White Antelope, at Sand Creek in southeastern Colorado, killing roughly 150–230 people, mostly women, children, and the elderly. The Sand Creek massacre shocked the nation and was condemned by congressional investigations.

Retaliation and outcome

In January and February 1865, Cheyenne, Arapaho, and Lakota warriors retaliated with raids along the South Platte River, twice attacking Julesburg, burning ranches and stage stations, and cutting the telegraph line to Denver. Federal troops campaigned on the plains through 1865, and fighting continued in the wider region in the Powder River Expedition and its aftermath. The war effectively ended with treaties in late 1865, but conflict on the central plains resumed in 1867–1869, ending with the defeat of the Dog Soldiers at Summit Springs in 1869.
