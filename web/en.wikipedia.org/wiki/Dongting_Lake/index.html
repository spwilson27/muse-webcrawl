Dongting Lake (Chinese: 洞庭湖) is a large, shallow freshwater lake in northeastern Hunan Province, China. It is a flood basin of the Yangtze River, into which it both receives floodwater and drains, so its surface area varies dramatically with the seasons — swelling during the summer monsoon and shrinking in winter. Historically the largest lake in China, it has contracted greatly over the past two centuries because of siltation and land reclamation, and is now generally ranked second in size behind Poyang Lake.

Dongting Lake expands greatly during the summer flood season.

Geography and ecology

The lake receives water from the Yangtze through distributary channels and from four major rivers of Hunan — the Xiang, Zi, Yuan, and Li. Its wetlands are of international importance, protected under the Ramsar Convention, and provide habitat for migratory waterbirds and the Yangtze finless porpoise. Junshan Island in the lake is famous for its yellow tea, and the Yueyang Tower on the lake's eastern shore is one of the celebrated towers of Chinese literature, immortalised by Fan Zhongyan's essay "Memorial to Yueyang Tower."

Shrinkage and flooding

In the mid-19th century Dongting covered roughly 6,000 km², but sediment carried in from the Yangtze and extensive poldering for farmland reduced it to under 3,000 km² by the late 20th century. The loss of flood storage contributed to devastating Yangtze floods, notably in 1998, after which the Chinese government adopted a policy of returning reclaimed farmland to the lake, partially restoring its area.
