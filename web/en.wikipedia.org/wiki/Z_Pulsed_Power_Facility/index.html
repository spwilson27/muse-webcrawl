The Z Pulsed Power Facility, informally known as the Z machine, is the largest high-frequency electromagnetic wave generator in the world and is operated by Sandia National Laboratories in Albuquerque, New Mexico. It is designed to test materials in conditions of extreme temperature and pressure, principally in support of nuclear weapons stockpile stewardship without underground testing, and to study inertial confinement fusion.

Operation

The facility stores energy in banks of Marx generators and releases it in a pulse lasting about 100 nanoseconds. Currents of more than 26 million amperes are passed through an array of fine tungsten wires, vaporising them into a plasma that implodes under its own magnetic field — a configuration called a Z-pinch. The collapse produces X-ray pulses of up to about 2.7 terawatts and temperatures exceeding 1.8 billion kelvin, briefly among the hottest places on Earth.

Research

Experiments at the Z facility have studied the behaviour of materials under extreme pressures, the physics of fusion plasmas, and astrophysical phenomena reproduced at laboratory scale. In 2014 researchers reported significant neutron production from magnetised liner inertial fusion (MagLIF) experiments.
