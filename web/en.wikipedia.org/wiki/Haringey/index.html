London Borough of Haringey

The London Borough of Haringey is a London borough in north London. It was created in 1965 by the amalgamation of the former municipal boroughs of Hornsey, Tottenham, and Wood Green. Haringey covers an area of about 11 square miles (29 km²) and had a population of roughly 264,000 at the 2021 census.

Geography

Haringey contrasts markedly from west to east. The western districts — Highgate, Muswell Hill, and Crouch End — are largely leafy, affluent residential suburbs built on rising ground. The east, including Tottenham and Wood Green, is flatter and more urban, following the valley of the River Lea, which forms the borough's eastern boundary.

Landmarks

The borough's best-known landmark is Alexandra Palace, opened in 1873 and the site of the world's first regular public television broadcasts by the BBC in 1936. Other notable sites include Bruce Castle Museum in Tottenham, the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium (opened 2019), and Highgate Cemetery.

Governance

Haringey London Borough Council administers the borough. It is divided into 21 wards, each electing two or three councillors. The borough contains the parliamentary constituencies of Hornsey and Friern Barnet, and Tottenham.

Demographics

Haringey is one of London's most ethnically diverse boroughs, with large Turkish, Kurdish, Greek Cypriot, Caribbean, African, and Eastern European communities; more than 180 languages are spoken locally.
