A Member of the European Parliament (MEP) is a person who has been elected to serve as a popular representative in the European Parliament, the directly elected legislative body of the European Union.

Election

MEPs are elected every five years by universal adult suffrage in all member states, using forms of proportional representation. Seats are allocated to member states by degressive proportionality, so smaller states elect more MEPs per capita than larger ones. The first direct elections were held in 1979; before that, members were appointed by national parliaments.

Role and work

MEPs sit in transnational political groups rather than national delegations, and most detailed legislative work takes place in parliamentary committees. Members divide their time between the Parliament's seats in Strasbourg and Brussels and their constituencies. MEPs vote on EU legislation, the EU budget, and international agreements, and they elect the President of the European Commission.

Privileges and pay

Since 2009 all MEPs have received the same salary from the EU budget, set at 38.5% of the salary of a judge at the European Court of Justice, and they enjoy parliamentary immunity for votes and opinions expressed in their official capacity.
