dBFS

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia   (Redirected from DBFS)

dBFS (decibels relative to full scale) is a unit of measurement for amplitude levels in digital systems, such as digital audio, which have a defined maximum peak level. The unit is similar to the units dBov and decibels relative to overload (dBO). The level of 0 dBFS is assigned to the maximum possible digital level; for example, in a 16-bit system, a signal whose sample values reach the largest representable magnitude is at 0 dBFS. All amplitude levels below the maximum are therefore negative numbers in dBFS.

A digital peak meter displaying levels in dBFS.

Definition

For a signal measured against the full-scale reference, the level in decibels is:

LdBFS = 20 log10( A / AFS )

where A is the signal amplitude and AFS is the full-scale amplitude. A convention question arises for RMS measurements: depending on whether the reference is taken as the RMS value of a full-scale sine wave or of a full-scale square wave, quoted RMS levels can differ by about 3 dB. Standards such as AES17 define measurement practice for digital audio equipment.

Analogue levels

dBFS is not directly comparable to analogue level units such as dBu or dBV, because the relationship depends on the headroom designed into the converter. By common convention in studio practice, an alignment level such as −18 dBFS (EBU) or −20 dBFS (SMPTE) corresponds to the nominal analogue operating level, leaving headroom above it before digital clipping at 0 dBFS.

Clipping and headroom

A signal that exceeds 0 dBFS cannot be represented and is clipped, producing distortion. Because reconstruction filtering in digital-to-analogue converters can create inter-sample peaks above the largest sample value, true-peak metering was developed to estimate the actual peak of the reconstructed waveform, which may exceed 0 dBFS even when no sample does.
