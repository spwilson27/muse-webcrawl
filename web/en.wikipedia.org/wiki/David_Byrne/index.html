David Byrne

David Byrne (born 14 May 1952) is a Scottish-American singer, songwriter, musician, and artist, founding member and frontman of the band Talking Heads. He has won Academy, Grammy, and Tony awards and is known for eclectic solo work and collaborations.


Born: 14 May 1952, Dumbarton, Scotland
Occupation: Singer, songwriter, artist
Associated acts: Talking Heads, Brian Eno, St. Vincent

Talking Heads

Born in Scotland and raised in Canada and Maryland, Byrne formed Talking Heads in New York in 1975 with Chris Frantz, Tina Weymouth, and Jerry Harrison. The band fused punk, funk, art rock, and world music on albums like Remain in Light (1980) and the concert film Stop Making Sense (1984). Byrne's oversized suit and anxious stage persona became iconic.

Solo work

Solo projects include the Oscar-winning score for The Last Emperor (1987, with Ryuichi Sakamoto and Cong Su), the label Luaka Bop, books such as How Music Works, and the hit Broadway show American Utopia (2019), filmed by Spike Lee.
