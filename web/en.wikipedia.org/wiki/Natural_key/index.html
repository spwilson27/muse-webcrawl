Natural key

Natural key
FieldDatabase design
Related conceptSurrogate key

A natural key (also known as a business key or domain key) is a type of unique key in a relational database formed from attributes that already exist in the real world and have a logical relationship to the rest of the data in the row. Unlike a surrogate key, which is generated by the database system solely to serve as an identifier, a natural key is meaningful outside the database: examples include a person's national identification number, a book's ISBN, or a country's ISO code.

Comparison with surrogate keys

Advocates of natural keys argue that they preserve the business meaning of data, avoid the need for additional indexes, and make queries simpler because joins on related tables can use values that humans recognise. Critics note that natural keys may change over time (for example, when a registration scheme is revised), may be long or composite and therefore inefficient as foreign keys, and may expose sensitive personal data if used widely. In practice, many database designers use surrogate keys as primary keys while enforcing natural keys with unique constraints.

Examples

  • A two-letter country code (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2) identifying a country.
  • The combination of flight number and departure date identifying a scheduled flight.
  • An employee's email address within a single organisation.
