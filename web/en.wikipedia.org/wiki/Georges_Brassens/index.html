Georges Brassens (22 October 1921 – 29 October 1981) was a French singer-songwriter and poet, regarded as one of France's most accomplished postwar lyricists. Accompanying himself on acoustic guitar, he sang literate, often irreverent songs that celebrated outcasts and mocked authority, convention, and hypocrisy.

Life

Born in the Mediterranean port of Sète, Brassens moved to Paris in 1940. During the war he was sent to Germany under the forced-labour scheme (STO) and deserted, hiding in Paris with Jeanne Le Bonnissec, whom he commemorated in song. Championed by the singer Patachou, he began performing in cabarets in 1952 and quickly rose to fame.

Work

His best-known songs include "Le Gorille", "Les Copains d'abord", "Chanson pour l'Auvergnat", "La Mauvaise Réputation", and "Le Parapluie", along with settings of poems by François Villon, Paul Verlaine, and Louis Aragon. He received the Grand Prix de Poésie of the Académie française in 1967. Brassens is buried in Sète, and a park in Paris near his former home bears his name.
