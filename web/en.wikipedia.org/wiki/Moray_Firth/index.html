Overview

The Moray Firth is a roughly triangular inlet of the North Sea in northeastern Scotland, the largest firth in Scotland, stretching from Duncansby Head and Fraserburgh to Inverness and the entrance of the Caledonian Canal at the head of the inner firth. Its name relates to the historic province of Moray.

Ecology and economy

The inner Moray Firth hosts one of the UK's two resident populations of bottlenose dolphins, viewable from Chanonry Point. The firth supports fisheries, the Beatrice and Moray East/West offshore wind farms in its outer reaches, and the Cromarty Firth oil-rig anchorage on its southern shore.
