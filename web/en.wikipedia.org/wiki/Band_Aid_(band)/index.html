Band Aid (band)

Band Aid
OriginLondon, England
GenresPop, Christmas music

Band Aid is a charity supergroup organised by Bob Geldof and Midge Ure in 1984 to raise money for famine relief in Ethiopia. The group recorded the single "Do They Know It's Christmas?", written by Geldof and Ure, which became one of the best-selling singles of all time in the United Kingdom and raised millions of pounds. Band Aid inspired the American counterpart USA for Africa ("We Are the World") and the Live Aid concerts of July 1985.

The 1984 recording

Shocked by BBC news reports by Michael Buerk on the Ethiopian famine, Geldof recruited many of the biggest British and Irish pop stars of the day — including Paul Young, Boy George, George Michael, Sting, Bono, Phil Collins, Duran Duran, Spandau Ballet, Status Quo and members of U2, Ultravox and Kool & the Gang — who recorded the song at Sarm West Studios in London on 25 November 1984. Released days later, it was the UK Christmas number one for five weeks and sold over a million copies in its first week.

Later incarnations

Band Aid II (1989) re-recorded the song with contemporary pop acts. Band Aid 20 (2004) marked the twentieth anniversary with a new generation of artists, including Coldplay, Robbie Williams and a rap contribution by Dizzee Rascal. Band Aid 30 (2014) adapted the lyrics to raise funds for the Ebola crisis in West Africa, featuring One Direction, Ed Sheeran and Adele among others. The lyrics have sometimes been criticised for their portrayal of Africa, and Geldof has defended the project by pointing to the money raised for relief.

Legacy

Proceeds are administered by the Band Aid Charitable Trust, which funds long-term development and emergency relief in Africa. The project established the template for the celebrity charity single.
