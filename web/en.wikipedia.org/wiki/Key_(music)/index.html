Key (music)

In music theory, a key is the group of pitches, or scale, that forms the basis of a musical composition in Western classical music, art music and popular music. A key consists of a tonic note and its corresponding chords; pieces are typically described as being "in" a major key or a minor key, such as C major or A minor, according to the scale from which their melodic and harmonic material is principally drawn.

Tonic and key signature

The tonic is the first degree of the scale and the tonal centre to which the music gravitates. In notation, the key determines the key signature — the set of sharps or flats placed after the clef — which specifies which lines and spaces are routinely altered. Major and minor keys sharing a key signature are called relative major and relative minor; keys with the same tonic but different signatures, such as C major and C minor, are called parallel keys.

Relationships between keys

Keys are organised by the circle of fifths, in which adjacent keys differ by one sharp or flat. Closely related keys share most of their pitches and are common destinations for modulation, the process of changing key within a piece. Movement to the dominant or relative major/minor is characteristic of classical forms such as sonata form, in which the exposition typically moves from tonic to dominant.

Key in practice

Composers historically associated particular keys with particular affects — the doctrine of key characteristics — though such associations are subjective. In equal temperament all keys are acoustically equivalent in interval structure, but instrumentation, vocal range and the mechanics of instruments still influence the choice of key in practice. Music that lacks a tonal centre is described as atonal.
