Georges Simenon

Georges Joseph Christian Simenon (13 February 1903 – 4 September 1989) was a Belgian writer, most famous among his 400 books for his 75 novels and 28 short stories featuring Chief Inspector Jules Maigret. One of the most prolific and widely read authors of the 20th century, his books have sold hundreds of millions of copies and been translated into dozens of languages.

Early life and pulp years

Simenon was born in Liège, Belgium. He left school at 15 and became a reporter for the Gazette de Liège, an apprenticeship that taught him speed, observation, and the life of the city's streets and police courts. Moving to Paris in 1922, he spent a decade writing popular fiction at extraordinary speed — by his own account some 200 pulp novels under about 17 pen names, alongside hundreds of short stories and articles.

Maigret

In 1931 he launched, under his own name, the series featuring Commissaire Jules Maigret of the Paris Police Judiciaire — a patient, pipe-smoking detective whose method is to understand rather than merely to deduce: to soak himself in the atmosphere of a place and its people until the truth emerges. The Maigret books, beginning with Pietr the Latvian, made Simenon famous and have been adapted repeatedly for film and television, with actors including Jean Gabin, Rupert Davies, Michael Gambon, and Rowan Atkinson in the role.

The "romans durs"

Alongside the Maigrets, Simenon wrote what he called his romans durs — "hard novels" — serious psychological works such as The Man Who Watched Trains Go By (1938), The Snow Was Dirty (1948), and The Cat (1967), which earned the admiration of writers including André Gide. He typically wrote a novel in about ten days, in a state of intense immersion, and famously worked to a fixed daily routine of chapters.

Later life

After the Second World War — during which his conduct and his dealings with the German-controlled film industry drew scrutiny, leading him to leave France — Simenon lived in the United States from 1945 to 1955, setting several novels there, then settled in Switzerland. He stopped writing fiction in 1972, thereafter dictating memoirs. He died in Lausanne in 1989.
