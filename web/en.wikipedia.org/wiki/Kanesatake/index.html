Kanesatake

Kanesatake (Mohawk: Kanehsatà:ke) is a Mohawk (Kanienʼkehá꞉ka) settlement on the north shore of the Lake of Two Mountains in southwestern Quebec, Canada, near the town of Oka. Its roughly 1,300 residents are descendants of Mohawk converts who settled the area in the early 18th century at the Sulpician mission of the Lake of Two Mountains. Uniquely among Indigenous communities in Canada, Kanesatake does not have formal reserve status under the Indian Act, a longstanding source of land disputes.

The Oka Crisis

Kanesatake became internationally known during the Oka Crisis of 1990, when the municipality of Oka moved to expand a golf course onto land including a Mohawk burial ground in the pine forest claimed by Kanesatake. Mohawks erected barricades; a police raid on 11 July 1990 left Sûreté du Québec Corporal Marcel Lemay dead, and the standoff escalated into a 78-day confrontation involving the Canadian Army at both Kanesatake and the nearby community of Kahnawake. The golf course expansion was cancelled, and the crisis became a watershed in Canadian–Indigenous relations, leading among other things to the Royal Commission on Aboriginal Peoples.

Land and governance

The community is governed by the Mohawk Council of Kanesatake, with an elected grand chief. Land claims negotiations with Canada over the original Sulpician seigneury grant — which the Mohawks maintain was to be held in trust for them, not owned — remained unresolved for decades.
