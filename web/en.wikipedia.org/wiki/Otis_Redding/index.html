Otis Redding

Otis Ray Redding Jr. (September 9, 1941 – December 10, 1967) was an American singer and songwriter. He is regarded as one of the greatest singer-songwriters in the history of American popular music and a seminal artist in soul music and rhythm and blues. Nicknamed the "King of Soul", Redding's style of singing gained inspiration from the gospel music that preceded the genre. His gruff, impassioned vocal style influenced many other soul artists of the 1960s.

Early life

Redding was born in Dawson, Georgia, and raised in Macon, where he sang in church and talent contests, influenced by Little Richard and Sam Cooke. He joined Johnny Jenkins's band, the Pinetoppers, as singer and driver.

Career

In 1962, during a recording session at Stax Records in Memphis for Jenkins, Redding recorded his own song "These Arms of Mine" in leftover studio time; it became his first hit. A string of acclaimed recordings followed for the Stax subsidiary Volt, including "Pain in My Heart", "Mr. Pitiful", "I've Been Loving You Too Long", and his original "Respect" (later a signature hit for Aretha Franklin). His 1965 album Otis Blue is regarded as a soul landmark. Redding wrote or co-wrote much of his material, often with guitarist Steve Cropper.

In June 1967 Redding gave a celebrated performance at the Monterey International Pop Festival, winning over a predominantly white rock audience. Three days before his death he recorded "(Sittin' On) The Dock of the Bay", co-written with Cropper, which in 1968 became his only number-one single on the Billboard Hot 100.

Death

On December 10, 1967, Redding's chartered Beechcraft H18 aircraft crashed into Lake Monona near Madison, Wisconsin, in rain and fog. Redding, aged 26, and four members of the Bar-Kays were killed; trumpeter Ben Cauley survived.

Legacy

Redding was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1989 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 1999. His songs have been widely covered, and Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest singers of all time.
