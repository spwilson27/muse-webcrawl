Great ShakeOut

The Great ShakeOut is an annual earthquake preparedness drill in which participants practice the "Drop, Cover, and Hold On" safety technique at a set time on a designated day, typically the third Thursday of October (International ShakeOut Day). What began in Southern California in 2008 has grown into the world's largest earthquake drill, with tens of millions of registered participants across the United States and in many other countries.

History

The first Great Southern California ShakeOut was held on November 13, 2008, organized by the Earthquake Country Alliance with the United States Geological Survey and other partners, and built around a scenario of a magnitude 7.8 earthquake on the southern San Andreas Fault. More than 5.4 million people participated. The drill expanded to other states and regions—including the central United States, the Pacific Northwest (Great ShakeOut of British Columbia and Cascadia), Japan, New Zealand, and elsewhere—and became an annual October event.

The drill

At the scheduled time (commonly 10:17 a.m. on October 17, matching the date), participants Drop to their hands and knees, Cover their head and neck under sturdy furniture or with their arms, and Hold On until the shaking stops, for one minute. Schools, businesses, government agencies, and individuals register in advance; many organizations use the drill to review full earthquake response plans, communications, and supplies. The ShakeOut is intended to raise awareness of earthquake risk and to encourage preparedness steps such as securing furniture and assembling emergency kits.
