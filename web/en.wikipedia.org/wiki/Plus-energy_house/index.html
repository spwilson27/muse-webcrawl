Plus-energy house

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A plus-energy house (German: Plusenergiehaus) is a building that produces more energy from renewable sources over the course of a year than it consumes from external sources. It achieves this by combining a very low energy demand — typically through passive house levels of insulation, airtightness and heat-recovery ventilation — with on-site generation, most often a rooftop photovoltaic system, sometimes supplemented by solar thermal collectors or a small wind turbine.

Concept

The plus-energy concept goes one step beyond the zero-energy building, whose annual balance is neutral. Surplus electricity is exported to the grid — the building effectively acts as a small power station — while at night or in winter the house draws power back, making the annual balance, rather than real-time self-sufficiency, the criterion. The term was popularised by the German architect Rolf Disch, whose Heliotrope rotating solar house (1994) and the Solar Settlement at Freiburg (completed in the 2000s) demonstrated plus-energy design at neighbourhood scale: the 59 homes of the Solar Settlement generate more electricity than they use.

Design features

  • Super-insulated, airtight envelope with minimal thermal bridges.
  • Heat-recovery ventilation and efficient appliances to cut demand.
  • Large, well-oriented photovoltaic arrays, often forming the roof surface.
  • Sometimes battery storage or an electric vehicle integrated into the balance.
