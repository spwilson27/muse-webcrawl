Jesus College, Oxford

Jesus College
Full name: Jesus College in the University of Oxford of Queen Elizabeth's Foundation
Founded: 1571
Founder: Elizabeth I (charter obtained by Hugh Price)
Location: Turl Street, Oxford

Jesus College is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England, founded in 1571. It was established under a charter of Queen Elizabeth I obtained by Hugh Price, a Welsh lawyer and clergyman, and long maintained a particularly strong connection with Wales, drawing many of its fellows and students from the Principality.

History

Price, treasurer of St Davids Cathedral, petitioned the Queen for a college "for the maintenance and education of students in the University". The college grew slowly during its first century, expanded under principals such as Jonathan Edwards in the late 17th century, and was reformed in the 19th century, when its Welsh character gradually broadened. Women were first admitted in 1974.

College life

The college occupies a compact site between Turl Street and Ship Street, arranged around three quadrangles, with a chapel dating from the 1620s. Notable alumni include the Welsh scholar Edward Lhuyd, the writer Lawrence of Arabia (T. E. Lawrence), Prime Minister Harold Wilson, and the historian Norman Davies. Its Welsh connection survives in traditions such as the college's Celtic studies links and its annual celebrations.
