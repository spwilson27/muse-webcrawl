Day of Judgment

The Day of Judgment (also Judgment Day, Last Judgment, or Doomsday) is a concept in the Abrahamic religions of a final divine assessment of humanity at the end of the world, in which the dead are raised and judged according to their deeds, determining their fate in the afterlife.

In Christianity

Christian eschatology teaches that Jesus Christ will return in glory to judge the living and the dead, an event confessed in the Nicene Creed. The Book of Revelation describes a great white throne, the opening of the books, and the separation of the saved and the damned. Michelangelo's The Last Judgment (1536–1541) on the altar wall of the Sistine Chapel is the most famous visual depiction.

In Islam

In Islam, Yawm al-Qiyāmah (the Day of Resurrection) and Yawm ad-Dīn (the Day of Judgment) are central doctrines. After resurrection, all people are judged by Allah; their deeds, recorded by angels, are weighed, and each person crosses the bridge of As-Sirāt to Paradise (Jannah) or falls to Hell (Jahannam).

In Judaism

Jewish tradition associates judgment with Rosh Hashanah, the annual "Day of Judgment" (Yom HaDin), and with a future messianic age in which the dead are resurrected and judged, as in Daniel 12.
