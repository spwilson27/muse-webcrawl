Old Three Hundred

The Old Three Hundred were the 297 grantees, comprising about 300 families, who purchased land in Stephen F. Austin's first colony in Mexican Texas between 1823 and 1825. They formed the founding Anglo-American settler population of Texas.

Background

Austin inherited his father Moses Austin's empresario contract from the Spanish, confirmed by the newly independent Mexican government. Settlers received leagues and labors of land along the Brazos, Colorado, and San Bernard rivers, with the capital at San Felipe de Austin. Grantees were required to be of good character, profess Catholicism (at least formally), and swear allegiance to Mexico.

Legacy

Members of the Old Three Hundred, including figures such as Jared Groce and William B. Travis-era settlers, became the social and economic elite of early Texas, and many of their descendants played roles in the Texas Revolution and the Republic of Texas.
