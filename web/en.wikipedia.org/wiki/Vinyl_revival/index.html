Vinyl revival

The vinyl revival, also known as the vinyl resurgence, is the renewed interest in and increased sales of vinyl records (phonograph records) that began in the mid-2000s, after the format had been largely displaced first by the compact disc in the 1980s and 1990s and then by downloads and streaming in the 2000s.

History

LP sales reached their low point in the mid-2000s, when many pressing plants had closed and major labels had abandoned the format. From around 2006–2007, sales began climbing year on year, driven by independent artists and labels, the launch of Record Store Day in 2008, and demand from younger listeners who had grown up with digital music. In several markets vinyl revenues surpassed those of CDs in the early 2020s, and in the United States vinyl outsold CDs in units in 2022 for the first time since the 1980s. Large retailers returned records to their shelves, new pressing plants opened, and pressing backlogs became a recurring industry problem.

Reasons

Explanations offered for the revival include the perceived warmth and quality of analogue sound, the tactile ritual of playing a record, large-format artwork and liner notes, collectability, support for artists through physical purchases, and nostalgia — including nostalgia among listeners too young to have used vinyl in its original era. Critics note that vinyl remains a small share of total recorded-music revenue compared with streaming, and raise environmental concerns about PVC production.
