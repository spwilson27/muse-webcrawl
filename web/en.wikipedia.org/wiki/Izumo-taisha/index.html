Izumo-taisha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The haiden (worship hall) of Izumo-taisha

Izumo-taisha (出雲大社), formally Izumo Ōyashiro, is one of the most ancient and important Shinto shrines in Japan, located in Izumo, Shimane Prefecture. It is dedicated to the kami Ōkuninushi-no-mikoto, famous in mythology for creating the land of Japan together with Sukunabikona and for the "transfer of the land" (kuniyuzuri) to the descendants of Amaterasu.

Mythology and festivals

In the tenth month of the traditional lunar calendar, Shinto tradition holds that the eight million kami gather at Izumo; the month is therefore called Kamiarizuki ("month when the gods are present") in Izumo but Kannazuki ("month without gods") elsewhere in Japan. The main festival honouring this gathering is held at the shrine.

Buildings

The shrine's honden (main hall), built in the taisha-zukuri style—the oldest shrine architectural style in Japan—stands 24 metres tall; records suggest an earlier structure may have been far taller. The huge sacred straw rope (shimenawa) of the kagura hall, weighing several tonnes, is one of the largest in Japan. Izumo-taisha is traditionally linked with matchmaking and good fortune in relationships (en-musubi).
