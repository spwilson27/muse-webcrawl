Cuckoo bee


Image placeholder: Cuckoo bee

Cuckoo bees are bees in several lineages that have evolved a kleptoparasitic lifestyle, laying their eggs in the nests of other bees rather than building and provisioning their own. The name refers to the similar behaviour of cuckoo birds. Well-known groups include the genera Nomada, Sphecodes, Coelioxys, and the cuckoo bumblebees of the subgenus Psithyrus.

Biology

Female cuckoo bees typically lack pollen-carrying structures (scopae) and are often less hairy and more wasp-like than their hosts. The female enters a host nest and lays an egg in a provisioned cell; her larva kills the host's egg or larva and consumes the stored pollen and nectar.

Evolution

Kleptoparasitism has evolved independently many times among bees; roughly 15 percent of bee species are cuckoos of other bees. Cuckoo bumblebees invade the colonies of social bumblebees and subdue or kill the host queen.
