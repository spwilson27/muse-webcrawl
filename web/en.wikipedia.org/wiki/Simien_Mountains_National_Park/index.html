Simien Mountains National Park is a national park in northern Ethiopia, in the Amhara Region. It protects part of the Simien Mountains, a dramatic landscape of jagged peaks, deep valleys and sheer escarpments formed by massive volcanic eruptions and millions of years of erosion. It includes Ras Dashen, the highest mountain in Ethiopia at about 4,550 metres. The park was established in 1969 and was among the first sites inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in 1978.

Wildlife

The park is home to several rare and endemic species. The gelada baboon lives in large troops on the high grasslands, grazing on the plateau while retreating to the cliffs at night. The Ethiopian wolf, the world's rarest canid, hunts rodents on the Afroalpine moorlands, and the Walia ibex, a wild goat found only in these mountains, climbs the steep crags. Other mammals include the klipspringer, hamadryas baboon and spotted hyena. Birds of prey such as the bearded vulture (lammergeier), which drops bones onto rocks to crack them, are a notable sight along the escarpments.

Geology and scenery

The Simien range is the eroded remnant of a vast shield volcano built up by flood basalt eruptions some 40 million years ago. Erosion has carved the lava plateau into pinnacles, canyons and precipices dropping more than 1,500 metres, giving views often compared to those of the Grand Canyon. The high plateau, much of it above 3,600 metres, supports Afroalpine vegetation including giant lobelias.

Conservation

The park was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 1996 because of declines in Walia ibex, Ethiopian wolf and other species, together with pressure from settlement, cultivation, grazing and civil unrest. Following improvements in management and wildlife numbers it was removed from the danger list in 2017. Tourism, centred on trekking routes from Debark and camps such as Sankaber, Chenek and Ambiko (the base for climbing Ras Dashen), provides income for local communities.
