Dental braces

Dental braces (also known as braces, orthodontic cases, or cases) are devices used in orthodontics that align and straighten teeth and help position them with regard to a person's bite, while also aiming to improve dental health. Braces also fix gaps in teeth, underbites, overbites, cross bites, open bites, deep bites, crooked teeth, and various other flaws of the teeth and jaw.

Components

Fixed braces consist of brackets bonded to the front of each tooth, an archwire threaded through the brackets that applies corrective force, and ligatures or self-ligating clips that hold the wire in place. Auxiliary elements may include elastic bands, springs, and headgear. Alternatives include lingual braces fitted behind the teeth and removable clear aligners.

Process

Treatment typically begins with records, X-rays, and impressions or digital scans. Brackets are bonded and wires inserted; the patient returns every few weeks for adjustments as teeth move gradually through bone remodeling — resorption on the pressure side and deposition on the tension side. Treatment commonly lasts from one to three years, after which retainers are worn to hold the teeth in their new positions.

History

Attempts to straighten teeth date to antiquity, but modern orthodontics began in the 18th and 19th centuries with Pierre Fauchard and others; Edward Angle, regarded as the father of modern orthodontics, developed standard appliances in the early 20th century.
