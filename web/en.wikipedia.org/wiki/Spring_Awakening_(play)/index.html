Spring Awakening (German: Frühlings Erwachen, subtitled A Children's Tragedy) is a play by the German dramatist Frank Wedekind, written in 1890–91 and first published in 1891. Set in provincial Germany in the 1890s, it depicts a group of adolescents confronting sexual awakening in a society whose parents and teachers refuse to speak honestly with them about sexuality. Long banned or censored for its frank treatment of puberty, masturbation, homosexuality, abortion, and suicide, the play is now regarded as a landmark of modern drama.

Plot

The central characters are the gifted, questioning Melchior Gabor; his anxious friend Moritz Stiefel, crushed by academic pressure; and the naïve fourteen-year-old Wendla Bergmann, whose mother refuses to explain conception. Melchior and Wendla's hayloft encounter leads to her pregnancy; her mother arranges a secret abortion that kills her. Moritz, expelled from school and unable to confess his failures to his parents, shoots himself. Melchior is sent to a reformatory, escapes, and in a graveyard encounters the ghost of Moritz and a mysterious Masked Man — often read as Wedekind's stand-in for life itself — who persuades him to live.

Performance history

Wedekind published the play at his own expense in Zurich in 1891, but it was not staged until 1906, when Max Reinhardt directed it at the Deutsches Theater in Berlin. Productions in the English-speaking world were repeatedly threatened with censorship; a 1917 New York performance was nearly shut down by the authorities. The play's episodic structure and expressionist techniques influenced twentieth-century theatre, and it has been translated and revived frequently since the 1960s.

Adaptations

The play was adapted into the rock musical Spring Awakening (2006), with music by Duncan Sheik and book and lyrics by Steven Sater, which won eight Tony Awards including Best Musical and has been revived internationally, including a notable 2015 production by Deaf West Theatre integrating American Sign Language.
