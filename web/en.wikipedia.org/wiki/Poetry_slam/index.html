Poetry slam

A poetry slam is a competitive art event in which poets perform spoken-word poetry before an audience and a panel of judges. While formats can vary, slams are often loud and lively, with audience participation, cheering and booing. The performances are judged on a numeric scale by randomly selected members of the audience.

History

Poetry slams were first conceptualised in Chicago in 1984 by Marc Kelly Smith, a poet and construction worker who disliked the decorum of traditional academic poetry readings. Smith began holding performance poetry events at the Get Me High Lounge and later at the Green Mill Cocktail Lounge, where the Uptown Poetry Slam became a long-running weekly event. Slams spread across the United States in the late 1980s and 1990s, and the first National Poetry Slam was held in 1990 in San Francisco.

Format and rules

Although rules vary by venue, common features include:

  • Poems must be the performer's own original work.
  • Performances are usually limited to about three minutes, with penalties for exceeding the time limit.
  • No props, costumes or musical accompaniment are allowed in traditional slam rules.
  • Five judges, chosen randomly from the audience, score each poem from 0 to 10; the highest and lowest scores are dropped and the middle three are summed.
  • Competition proceeds over several rounds, with the lowest-scoring poets eliminated.

Competitions

Major slam competitions include the National Poetry Slam, the Individual World Poetry Slam, the Women of the World Poetry Slam, and the Brave New Voices youth poetry festival. Slam poetry also became widely known through the television series Russell Simmons Presents Def Poetry.

Criticism

Critics of the slam format argue that judging by score encourages poets to write crowd-pleasing pieces with predictable emotional crescendos — sometimes derided as the "slam voice" — at the expense of quieter or more experimental work. Supporters counter that slams democratised poetry by returning it to a live, participatory audience.
