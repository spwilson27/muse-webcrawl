German battleship Tirpitz

ClassBismarck-class battleship
Launched1 April 1939
FateSunk 12 November 1944
Tirpitz in Norwegian waters

Tirpitz was the second of two Bismarck-class battleships built for Nazi Germany's Kriegsmarine during World War II. Named after Grand Admiral Alfred von Tirpitz, she was the sister ship of Bismarck. After commissioning in February 1941, Tirpitz was deployed to Norway, where her mere presence as a "fleet in being" tied down large Allied naval forces guarding the Arctic convoys to the Soviet Union.

Service

Tirpitz made few offensive sorties, including a bombardment of Allied installations on Spitzbergen in September 1943 (Operation Zitronella). She spent most of her career in Norwegian fjords, principally Altafjord and later Tromsø, protected by torpedo nets, smoke screens and anti-aircraft defences.

Attacks and sinking

The Royal Navy and RAF mounted repeated attempts to sink her. In September 1943 British X-class midget submarines damaged her in Operation Source. Fleet Air Arm carrier aircraft attacked in Operation Tungsten in April 1944. Finally, on 12 November 1944, RAF Lancasters of Nos. 9 and 617 Squadrons dropped 12,000 lb Tallboy bombs in Operation Catechism, capsizing the ship near Tromsø with the loss of roughly 950–1,200 of her crew.

Legacy

The wreck was salvaged and broken up after the war by a Norwegian-German company. A section of her armour plate is displayed at the Royal Navy Museum in Portsmouth, and she remains the subject of numerous books and memorials in Norway.
