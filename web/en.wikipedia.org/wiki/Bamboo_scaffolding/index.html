Bamboo scaffolding

Bamboo scaffolding on a Hong Kong high-rise
MaterialBamboo poles, nylon or rattan lashings
Regions of useChina, Hong Kong, South and Southeast Asia
ApplicationsConstruction, maintenance, festival stages

Bamboo scaffolding is a type of scaffolding made from bamboo poles lashed together with strips of bamboo bark, rattan, or nylon ties. It has been used in China for centuries and remains a hallmark of construction in Hong Kong, where it is erected even around skyscrapers, as well as in parts of mainland China, India, and Southeast Asia. Bamboo is light, strong in tension, cheap, fast-growing, and quick to assemble and dismantle.

Construction

Scaffolders — in Hong Kong a licensed trade — erect a lattice of vertical standards and horizontal ledgers of mature bamboo, braced diagonally and tied to the building. Joints are made with lashings rather than metal couplers, allowing the frame to be adjusted to curved or irregular façades. Types include suspended (trussed-out) scaffolds hung from upper floors for repairs and signboards, and full-height double-row scaffolds for new construction. Cantonese opera festival stages and the scaffolding towers used in the Cheung Chau Bun Festival are also traditionally built of bamboo.

Safety and regulation

Bamboo scaffolding performs surprisingly well in typhoons because its lashings flex rather than snap, but high-profile collapses and fires have prompted tighter codes of practice, training, and inspection requirements in Hong Kong. Authorities there have in recent years moved to widen the use of metal scaffolding in public works, citing safety and standardization, while the bamboo trade defends the craft's speed, adaptability, and sustainability.
