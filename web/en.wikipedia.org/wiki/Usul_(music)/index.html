Usul (music)

Usul (also spelled usul'; Turkish: usûl, from Arabic uṣūl, "principles/roots") are the rhythmic cycles used in Ottoman and Turkish classical music, as well as in related traditions across the Middle East. An usul is a repeating pattern of beats of varying length and stress, traditionally played on percussion instruments such as the kudüm, bendir, or daf, and serving as the rhythmic foundation over which melodies in a given makam are composed and performed.

Structure

An usul consists of a fixed sequence of strong and weak beats, counted in time units that range from two beats to very long cycles of over a hundred beats. The beats are conventionally represented by the syllables düm (a deep stroke, generally strong), tek (a lighter stroke), and variants such as te-ke for subdivided strokes. Shorter, simpler usuls include Düyek (8/8), Aksak (9/8, literally "limping"), and Türkü forms, while grand ceremonial and religious compositions may use long cycles such as Devr-i Kebir (28/4) or Bereket.

Function

Composers of Turkish art music choose an usul appropriate to the genre and mood of a piece: vocal forms such as the şarkı commonly use short cycles, whereas the Mevlevi Sufi ceremony (ayin) employs long, stately ones. The usul is memorised by performers as a pattern of strokes and is closely analogous to the tala of Indian classical music and to the rhythmic modes (īqāʿāt) of Arabic music.
