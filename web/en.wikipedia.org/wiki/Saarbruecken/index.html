Saarbrücken (German pronunciation: [zaːɐ̯ˈbʁʏkn̩]) is the capital and largest city of the German state of Saarland, situated on the river Saar at the French border. It is a centre for industry, transport and higher education in the SaarLorLux cross-border region.

History

The city grew around a castle first mentioned in 999 as castellum Sarabrucca and became the seat of the Counts of Nassau-Saarbrücken. Devastated in the Thirty Years' and Franco-Dutch wars and repeatedly annexed by France, it developed in the 19th century into a centre of coal mining and steel production, its boroughs united into a single city in 1909. The modern city was created in 1909 by merging Saarbrücken with St. Johann and Malstatt-Burbach. Saarbrücken followed the Saar territory's shifting status: League of Nations mandate, return to Germany in 1935, French Saar Protectorate after the Second World War — during which it was heavily bombed — and accession with Saarland to the Federal Republic in 1957.

Landmarks and institutions

Landmarks include the baroque Ludwigskirche (1762–1775) by Friedrich Joachim Stengel, the Saarbrücken Castle overlooking the river, and the Saarländisches Staatstheater. Saarland University, founded in 1948 under French auspices, is a major employer and a noted centre for computer science, hosting institutes of the Max Planck Society and the German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI). The city's football club, 1. FC Saarbrücken, was a founding presence of the protectorate era and once played in France's Ligue 2.
