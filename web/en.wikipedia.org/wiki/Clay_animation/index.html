Clay animation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Clay animation or claymation is a form of stop motion animation in which characters and environments are sculpted from a malleable substance such as plasticine. Each frame is photographed after minute adjustments to the models.

History

Clay figures appeared in trick films as early as 1908. The technique matured in the 1950s–1970s with Art Clokey's Gumby and Will Vinton's Oscar-winning work — Vinton coined the trademarked term "Claymation". British studio Aardman Animations brought clay animation to mass audiences with Wallace and Gromit, Chicken Run, and Shaun the Sheep.

Technique

Animators sculpt figures around wire armatures, often keeping drawers of replacement mouths and hands. Lighting heat and handling degrade figures, so multiple copies are made for long productions. Free-form "clay painting" and melting effects exploit the material's plasticity directly on camera.

Notable examples

  • Wallace and Gromit series (Aardman)
  • The Nightmare Before Christmas (puppet stop motion, clay-influenced)
  • Celebrity Deathmatch (MTV)
  • Coraline (Laika, puppet/clay hybrid)
