Epicenter

The epicenter (also spelled epicentre), or epicentrum in seismology, is the point on the Earth's surface directly above the hypocenter or focus, the point where an earthquake or an underground explosion originates. The word derives from the Neo-Latin noun epicentrum, the latinisation of the ancient Greek adjective ἐπίκεντρος (epikentros), "occupying a cardinal point", from ἐπί (epi) "on, upon, at" and κέντρον (kentron) "centre".

Surface damage

In most earthquakes, the epicenter is the point where the greatest damage takes place, but the length of the subsurface fault rupture may indeed be a much longer one, and damage can be spread across the entire zone of rupture. For example, in the magnitude 7.9 2002 Denali earthquake in Alaska, the epicenter was at the western end of the rupture, but the greatest damage occurred about 330 km (210 mi) away at the eastern end. Depth of the hypocenter below the epicenter is also a major factor in surface damage: shallow earthquakes generally cause more destruction than deep ones of equal magnitude.

Determination

Epicenters are located using the arrival times of seismic waves recorded at seismograph stations. Because P-waves travel faster than S-waves, the difference between their arrival times at a station gives the distance to the earthquake; intersecting the distance circles from three or more stations (a form of triangulation) fixes the epicenter. Modern networks compute locations within minutes and distribute them as latitude and longitude together with focal depth.

Epicentral distance

During an earthquake, seismic waves propagate spherically out from the hypocenter. The distance from a given point to the epicenter, measured along the surface, is called the epicentral distance, generally expressed in degrees for distant events. Seismic waves are attenuated with distance, which is why the area surrounding the epicenter typically experiences the strongest shaking, subject to local soil conditions and rupture directivity.

Usage in other contexts

By extension, "epicenter" is often used figuratively for the centre of any activity or crisis — such as the epicentre of an epidemic — although purists sometimes object that the word properly denotes a point above a centre rather than the centre itself; "ground zero" is the analogous term for the surface point above or below a nuclear explosion.
