Saka language

Saka
Family: Indo-Iranian > Iranian > Eastern Iranian
Region: Tarim Basin (Khotan, Tumshuq)
Era: c. 5th–10th centuries CE
Varieties: Khotanese, Tumshuqese

Saka is a variety of Eastern Iranian languages, attested in the ancient Buddhist kingdoms of Khotan and Tumshuq in the Tarim Basin (modern Xinjiang, China). It is the language of the Saka peoples of the region and is known principally from manuscripts in Brāhmī script dating from roughly the 5th to 10th centuries CE.

Varieties

Two varieties are distinguished: Khotanese, from Khotan in the south, comparatively well documented in religious translations, poems and documents; and Tumshuqese, from the northwest, known from a smaller corpus. Both died out as Turkic languages spread through the basin.

Texts

Important Khotanese texts include the Book of Zambasta, a long Buddhist poem, and secular documents such as letters and contracts, many recovered by Aurel Stein and now held in London and Paris collections.
