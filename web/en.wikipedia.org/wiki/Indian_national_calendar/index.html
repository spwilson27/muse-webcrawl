Indian national calendar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indian national calendar, also called the Saka calendar, is the official civil calendar used in India alongside the Gregorian calendar. It was adopted on 22 March 1957 (1 Chaitra 1879 Saka) following the report of the Calendar Reform Committee chaired by the physicist Meghnad Saha, set up in 1952 to examine India's many calendars and prepare an accurate unified calendar. Dates in the national calendar are printed in the Gazette of India, used in All India Radio news broadcasts, and appear in calendars issued by the Government of India.

Structure

The calendar is a solar calendar whose epoch is the Saka era, traditionally dated to 78 CE. Its year begins with the month of Chaitra, whose first day falls on 22 March in ordinary years and 21 March in leap years. Months have fixed lengths, aligned so that the calendar stays in step with the Gregorian calendar, to which it is permanently correlated: 1 Chaitra always corresponds to 22 (or 21) March.

Conversion

To convert a Gregorian date to the Saka year, subtract 78 from the Gregorian year for dates from 1 Chaitra to 31 December, and 79 for dates in January to mid-March before Chaitra begins. Leap years in the Saka calendar coincide with Gregorian leap years.

Usage

Although constitutionally the official civil calendar, the Saka calendar is little used in everyday life, where the Gregorian calendar and regional lunisolar calendars (for religious festivals) predominate. Its most visible uses are in government publications and official date announcements.
