Alba de Tormes is a municipality in the province of Salamanca, in the autonomous community of Castile and León, Spain. It stands on the River Tormes, about 20 kilometres south-east of the city of Salamanca.

History

The town gave its name to the House of Alba, one of Spain's grandest aristocratic families, whose dukes have held the title Duke of Alba de Tormes since the fifteenth century. The family's castle, of which the Torre del Homenaje survives, dominates the town. During the Peninsular War the Battle of Alba de Tormes (1809) saw a Spanish army defeated by French forces shortly after the Spanish victory at Tamames.

Saint Teresa of Ávila

Alba de Tormes is best known as the place where Saint Teresa of Ávila died in 1582. Her body is enshrined in the church of the Carmelite convent of the Annunciation, which she founded in 1571, and the town is a place of pilgrimage. Relics including her heart and an arm are preserved there.
