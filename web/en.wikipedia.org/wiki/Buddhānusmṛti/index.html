Buddhānusmṛti (Sanskrit: बुद्धानुस्मृति; Pali: buddhānussati), literally "recollection of the Buddha," is a Buddhist practice of meditating upon and bringing to mind the qualities of the Buddha. It is one of the classical "recollections" (anussati) taught in early Buddhist texts as a means of developing concentration, faith, and joy, and in Mahāyāna and East Asian traditions it developed into the central practice of Pure Land Buddhism: the recitation of the Buddha's name, known in Chinese as nianfo and in Japanese as nembutsu.

In early Buddhism and Theravāda

In the Pali Canon, buddhānussati is listed among the six (or ten) recollections, alongside recollection of the Dhamma, the Saṅgha, virtue, generosity, and deities, and among the forty meditation subjects of the Visuddhimagga. The practitioner recollects the Buddha's epithets — "Thus-gone, worthy, fully enlightened, perfect in knowledge and conduct, well-gone, knower of the world, unsurpassed trainer of persons to be tamed, teacher of gods and men, enlightened, blessed" (the iti pi so formula). Buddhaghosa teaches that because the Buddha's virtues are profound, the practice yields access concentration rather than full absorption, but gladdens the mind, suppresses the hindrances, and inclines the practitioner toward awakening; tradition also regards it as protective and conducive to a good rebirth.

In Mahāyāna and Pure Land Buddhism

Mahāyāna sutras expanded the practice into visualization of a Buddha's form and marks (as in the Pratyutpanna Sūtra and the Pure Land sutras' contemplations of Amitābha) and into vocal recitation of the Buddha's name. In China, masters such as Tanluan, Daochuo, and Shandao systematized nianfo as the accessible path for beings in the latter age of the Dharma. In Japan, Hōnen and Shinran made the nembutsu — "Namu Amida Butsu" ("I take refuge in Amida Buddha") — the sole practice of the Pure Land schools Jōdo-shū and Jōdo Shinshū, with Shinran emphasizing faith (shinjin) and the "other-power" of Amida's Primal Vow. Name-recitation is also integrated into Chan and Tiantai practice, and Tibetan Buddhism includes buddhānusmṛti within guru yoga and recitation of the Buddha's mantra and names.

Purposes attributed to the practice

Texts credit recollection of the Buddha with calming fear, generating serenity and faith, purifying unwholesome states, and — in Pure Land traditions — ensuring rebirth in Sukhāvatī, from which enlightenment is assured. Modern scholars read its development as a key case of the interaction between meditative and devotional currents in Buddhist history.
