Pratityasamutpada

Pratityasamutpada is a transliteration, without diacritics, of the Sanskrit pratītyasamutpāda (Pali: paṭiccasamuppāda), the Buddhist doctrine usually translated as dependent origination, dependent arising, or interdependent co-arising. It holds that no phenomenon exists independently or permanently; everything arises in dependence on causes and conditions, and ceases when those conditions cease.

Doctrine

The general formula states: "When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises. When this does not exist, that does not come to be; with the cessation of this, that ceases." The standard application of the formula is the twelvefold chain of nidanas — ignorance, formations, consciousness, name-and-form, sense bases, contact, feeling, craving, clinging, becoming, birth, and aging-and-death — which explains how beings remain bound to samsara, the cycle of rebirth, and how liberation is possible by uprooting ignorance.

Philosophical significance

Dependent origination is the middle way between eternalism and annihilationism. In the Madhyamaka school of Nagarjuna, it is identified with emptiness: to be dependently arisen is precisely to be empty of inherent existence. In Theravada commentarial tradition it is analysed through the twenty-four conditional relations of the Patthana. Modern interpreters also read the doctrine psychologically, as a description of how suffering is constructed moment by moment through craving and clinging.
