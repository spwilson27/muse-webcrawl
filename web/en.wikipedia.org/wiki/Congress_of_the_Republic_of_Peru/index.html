The Congress of the Republic of Peru (Spanish: Congreso de la República del Perú) is the unicameral legislative branch of the Peruvian government. It consists of 130 members (congressmen and congresswomen) elected for five-year terms by proportional representation across multi-member districts, sitting in the Legislative Palace in Lima.

History and powers

Peru's first constituent congress met in 1822. For much of the country's history the legislature was bicameral, with a Senate and a Chamber of Deputies; the 1993 constitution, adopted during the presidency of Alberto Fujimori after his 1992 self-coup dissolved the previous congress, established the current unicameral body. Congress enacts laws, approves the budget and treaties, oversees the executive, and may censure ministers or vacate the presidency. Its frequent conflicts with the executive have been a recurring feature of Peruvian politics, including the 2019 dissolution of Congress by President Martín Vizcarra and the impeachment crises of the following years. In 2024 a constitutional reform restored bicameralism, with a Senate and Chamber of Deputies to take effect from the 2026 general election.
