Batman (1989 film)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The 1989 bat symbol — a gold oval with a black bat silhouette — against darkness]
The 1989 bat symbol — a gold oval with a black bat silhouette — against darkness

Batman is a 1989 superhero film based on the DC Comics character Batman, directed by Tim Burton and starring Michael Keaton as Bruce Wayne/Batman and Jack Nicholson as the Joker. A dark departure from the camp 1960s television series, it was a cultural phenomenon — 'Batmania' — grossing over US$400 million and proving that comic-book films could anchor summer blockbuster seasons; it won the Academy Award for Best Art Direction.

Contents1. Production
2. Impact

Production

Warner Bros. developed a serious Batman for a decade; Burton, fresh from Beetlejuice, cast Keaton over fan protest (50,000 letters) and built Anton Furst's monumental Gothic-industrial Gotham at Pinewood. Prince contributed songs; Danny Elfman's score became the character's musical signature. Nicholson's deal — salary plus gross points — earned him a reported US$60 million or more, a landmark star contract.

Impact

Batman reshaped Hollywood marketing (the teaser poster with only the logo and date), revived superhero cinema's fortunes ahead of its 2000s dominance, and generated sequels — Burton's Batman Returns (1992), then Joel Schumacher's lighter pair — plus the acclaimed Batman: The Animated Series, which inherited its tone. Its darkness-versus-franchise tension remains the genre's defining negotiation.
