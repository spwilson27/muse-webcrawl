British Jews

British Jews (often referred to collectively as British Jewry or the Anglo-Jewish community) are British people of Jewish descent or converts to Judaism who identify as Jewish. The community numbers around 290,000 people, making the United Kingdom home to the fifth-largest Jewish population in the world.

History

Jews first came to England with the Norman Conquest in 1066, but were expelled by Edward I in 1290, the first permanent expulsion of Jews from a European kingdom. An unofficial resettlement began under Oliver Cromwell in the 1650s, and a small Sephardi community established itself openly in London. Large-scale Ashkenazi immigration followed between 1881 and 1914, as Jews fled pogroms in the Russian Empire; many settled in the East End of London, Leeds and Manchester. A further wave arrived in the 1930s fleeing Nazism, including the 10,000 children of the Kindertransport.

Demographics and religion

Most British Jews live in London — especially in Barnet, Hackney (Stamford Hill) and Golders Green — with significant communities in Manchester, Leeds, Glasgow and Brighton. Denominations range from the strictly Orthodox and the growing Haredi community, through the mainstream United Synagogue, to Masorti, Reform and Liberal movements. Representative bodies include the Board of Deputies of British Jews, founded in 1760.
