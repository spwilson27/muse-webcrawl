Malmsey

Malmsey
TypeFortified wine (Madeira)
GrapeMalvasia
RegionMadeira, Portugal
SweetnessSweet (richest style of Madeira)

Malmsey is the name given to the sweetest style of Madeira wine, made from Malvasia grapes. The name derives from Monemvasia, a Greek port from which sweet wines of the Malvasia type were originally shipped in the Middle Ages; "malmsey" became a generic English term for rich sweet Mediterranean wines before being attached to the Madeira style.

Production

On Madeira, Malmsey wines are produced mainly from the Malvasia Cândida and Malvasia Branca de São Jorge grape varieties. Fermentation is stopped by the addition of grape spirit, leaving high residual sugar, and the wine is then aged by the estufagem or canteiro heating processes that give Madeira its characteristic oxidised, caramelised flavours and extraordinary longevity. Vintage Malmseys (frasqueira) must be aged in cask for at least 20 years and can remain drinkable for a century or more.

Cultural references

Malmsey is famed in English literature and legend. In Shakespeare's Richard III, George, Duke of Clarence, is said to have been drowned in a butt of Malmsey wine in the Tower of London in 1478 — an execution method said to have been his own choice, though historians treat the detail as legend. The wine was also celebrated by Falstaff in Henry IV, Part 2, and was served at celebrations of American independence; Madeira was the wine used to toast the Declaration of Independence.
