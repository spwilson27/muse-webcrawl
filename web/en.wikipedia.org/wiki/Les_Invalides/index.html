Les Invalides (French: Hôtel des Invalides) is a complex of buildings in the 7th arrondissement of Paris containing museums and monuments relating to the military history of France, together with a hospital and retirement home for war veterans — the purpose for which it was founded by Louis XIV in 1670. Its gilded dome shelters the tomb of Napoleon Bonaparte.

History

Louis XIV ordered the construction of a home for aged and disabled soldiers who had previously been left to beg. Libéral Bruant designed the vast complex around a series of courtyards; the first veterans arrived in 1674. The soldiers' church, Saint-Louis-des-Invalides, was joined by the royal chapel — the Dôme des Invalides, designed by Jules Hardouin-Mansart and completed in 1706 — whose 107-metre golden dome remains one of the masterpieces of French Baroque architecture.

Napoleon's tomb

In 1840, under King Louis-Philippe, Napoleon's remains were returned from Saint Helena in the celebrated retour des cendres and eventually placed, in 1861, in a monumental tomb of red quartzite designed by Louis Visconti beneath the dome, surrounded by reliefs of his achievements and the tombs of other French commanders.

Museums and present use

The complex houses the Musée de l'Armée, one of the world's great military museums, founded in 1905, as well as the Musée des Plans-Reliefs and the Musée de l'Ordre de la Libération. The Institution Nationale des Invalides still cares for disabled veterans, and the site hosts national ceremonies honouring France's war dead; marshals such as Foch and Lyautey are also buried there.
