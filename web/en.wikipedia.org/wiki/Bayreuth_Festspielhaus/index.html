Bayreuth Festspielhaus

The Bayreuth Festspielhaus (Bayreuth Festival Theatre) is an opera house in the town of Bayreuth in Bavaria, Germany, built by the composer Richard Wagner and dedicated solely to the performance of his stage works. It stands on the "Green Hill" north of the town and hosts the annual Bayreuth Festival. Its revolutionary design — a single steeply raked amphitheatre of seats, no boxes, and a deep sunken orchestra pit entirely hidden from the audience — profoundly influenced theatre architecture and remains famous for its unique acoustics.

Design

Wagner adapted plans drawn up by Gottfried Semper for a Munich festival theatre. The fan-shaped auditorium provides every seat an unobstructed view, abolishing the social hierarchy of boxes and galleries. The orchestra sits in a deep pit beneath a curved hood (the "mystic abyss"), invisible to the audience, so that the sound emerges blended and disembodied while singers are never overwhelmed. The plain red-brick exterior and wooden interior were partly products of economy, but the acoustics they produce — a long, warm reverberation — are considered ideal for Wagner's music.

History

The foundation stone was laid on Wagner's 59th birthday, 22 May 1872. The house opened on 13 August 1876 with the first complete Ring des Nibelungen. It has remained the home of the festival ever since, with interruptions for the World Wars, and has undergone successive renovations, most recently a major restoration of the facade and technical facilities in the 2010s–2020s.
