Simply connected space

In topology, a simply connected space or 1-connected space is a topological space that is path-connected and has a trivial fundamental group: every loop can be continuously shrunk to a point within the space.

Simply connected space
FieldAlgebraic topology
ConditionPath-connected, π₁ = 0

Definition

A topological space X is simply connected if it is path-connected and every continuous map from the unit circle S¹ to X can be extended to the unit disk – equivalently, any two paths between the same points are homotopic, and the fundamental group π₁(X) is trivial.

Examples

Euclidean space ℝⁿ, spheres Sⁿ for n ≥ 2, convex sets and trees are simply connected. The circle S¹, the torus, the annulus and the punctured plane are not: loops winding around the hole cannot be contracted. The 2-sphere is simply connected even though it is not contractible (its higher homotopy groups are nontrivial).

Properties

Simply connected spaces play a central role in the theory of covering spaces – a simply connected covering is the universal cover. The Poincaré conjecture, proved by Grigori Perelman, states that every simply connected, closed 3-manifold is homeomorphic to the 3-sphere.
