Subtraction

Subtraction (signified by the minus sign, −) is one of the four basic arithmetic operations, together with addition, multiplication and division. It is the inverse operation of addition: subtracting a number from another finds the difference between them, or equivalently determines what must be added to the smaller quantity to obtain the larger. The result of a subtraction is called the difference; the number being subtracted is the subtrahend, and the number from which it is taken is the minuend.

Properties

Unlike addition, subtraction is neither commutative (a − b ≠ b − a in general) nor associative. It can be redefined as addition of the additive inverse: a − b = a + (−b), which is how the operation is extended beyond the natural numbers to integers, rational and real numbers, vectors and other algebraic structures. Subtraction of natural numbers is only partly defined — if the subtrahend exceeds the minuend, the difference requires negative numbers.

Algorithms

The standard written algorithm proceeds digit by digit from right to left in positional notation, "borrowing" (regrouping) from the next higher place when a digit of the minuend is smaller than the corresponding digit of the subtrahend. Alternative methods include the Austrian (additive) method and decomposition. In binary arithmetic, computers typically implement subtraction through two's complement addition, avoiding separate subtraction circuitry.

Notation and history

The minus sign was introduced into print in the late 15th century, appearing in Johannes Widmann's arithmetic of 1489, though it was used there to indicate deficit rather than as an operation. The symbol ÷ was once also used in some European traditions to denote subtraction. In many programming languages and spreadsheets, subtraction of dates yields a number of days, and subtracting sets in set theory denotes the relative complement.
