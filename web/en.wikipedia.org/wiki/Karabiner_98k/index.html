Karabiner 98k

The Karabiner 98 kurz ("carbine 98 short"), often abbreviated Kar98k or K98k, is a bolt-action rifle chambered for the 7.92×57mm Mauser cartridge. It was adopted on 21 June 1935 as the standard service rifle of the German Wehrmacht, and remained in front-line service throughout the Second World War.

Design

The Kar98k is a shortened development of the Karabiner 98b, itself derived from the Gewehr 98 of 1898. It uses Paul Mauser's controlled-feed bolt action, a five-round internal magazine loaded by stripper clips, and iron sights graduated to 2,000 metres. With a 600 mm (23.6 in) barrel and overall length of 1,110 mm, it was handier than its predecessors while retaining their accuracy and reliability.

Production and service

More than 14 million were produced by Mauser, Sauer, Steyr and other makers between 1935 and 1945. Sniper variants fitted with telescopic sights were prized, and after 1945 captured and newly produced Kar98ks served for decades in Yugoslavia, Israel, Norway and many other states; the design also influenced post-war sporting rifles. It remains one of the most widely recognised military rifles ever made.
