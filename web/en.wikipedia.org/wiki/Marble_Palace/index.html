The Marble Palace (Russian: Мраморный дворец) is one of the first Neoclassical palaces in Saint Petersburg, Russia, situated between the Field of Mars and the Palace Embankment on the Neva. Catherine the Great commissioned it in 1768 as a gift for her favourite, Count Grigory Orlov; its architect, Antonio Rinaldi, took seventeen years to complete it, and Orlov died two years before it was finished.

Architecture

The palace takes its name from its lavish use of some forty varieties of marble and granite — pink Tivdian marble on the piano nobile pilasters, grey Siberian and Ural stone, and lapis and agates in the interiors — making it one of the costliest buildings of Catherine's reign. Its restrained three-storey Neva façade, the grand marble staircase, and the Marble Hall survive largely intact. A wry tradition notes the palace's cornerstone inscription and the fact that Catherine bought it back from Orlov's heirs for her grandson, Grand Duke Constantine Pavlovich, whose descendants occupied it until 1917.

Later history

After the 1917 revolutions the palace housed government offices and, from 1937, the Leningrad branch of the Lenin Museum — complete with the armoured car from which Lenin addressed crowds in April 1917, long displayed in the courtyard. Since 1992 the Marble Palace has been a branch of the State Russian Museum, exhibiting the Ludwig Collection of international contemporary art and rotating exhibitions. In 1994 a bronze equestrian statue of Alexander III (by Paolo Troubetzkoy) was installed in its courtyard.
