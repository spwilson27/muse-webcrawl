Operation Paul Bunyan

Date: 21 August 1976

Location: Joint Security Area, Panmunjom, Korean Demilitarized Zone

Part of: Korean DMZ Conflict

Result: Tree felled; North Korea accepted UNC statement

Operation Paul Bunyan was an operation carried out by United States and South Korean forces on 21 August 1976 to cut down a poplar tree in the Korean Demilitarized Zone's Joint Security Area (JSA). It was a response to the Axe Murder Incident three days earlier, in which North Korean soldiers killed two United States Army officers, Captain Arthur Bonifas and First Lieutenant Mark Barrett, who had been part of a work party trimming the same tree because it blocked the view between UN Command observation posts.

Background

On 18 August 1976, a UN Command work party attempting to prune the tree was confronted by North Korean guards. In the ensuing confrontation, North Korean soldiers seized the work party's axes and killed Bonifas and Barrett. The incident provoked a crisis: the United States raised its forces in Korea to DEFCON 3 and planned a show of force that would complete the original task without giving North Korea grounds to claim a provocation.

The operation

Named after the giant lumberjack of American folklore, Operation Paul Bunyan sent a heavily armed convoy into the JSA at 7:00 a.m. on 21 August: engineers with chainsaws, backed by infantry, attack helicopters, B-52 bombers circling overhead from Guam, fighters, and artillery and the aircraft carrier Midway task force positioned in support. South Korean special forces soldiers accompanied the convoy, some with claymore mines strapped to their chests. The engineers felled the tree in 42 minutes, leaving a stump, while North Korean forces observed but did not intervene.

Aftermath

Later that day, North Korean leader Kim Il Sung sent a message through the Military Armistice Commission expressing regret that the incident had occurred — the closest North Korea came to an apology, and the first such statement since the Korean War armistice. The United States accepted the message as satisfactory. The incident led to changes in the JSA: the area was physically divided between the two sides, and armed contact between the parties was reduced. The tree stump was later replaced by a monument to the slain officers.
