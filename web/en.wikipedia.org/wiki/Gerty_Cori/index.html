Gerty Cori

Born: 15 August 1896, Prague, Austria-Hungary
Died: 26 October 1957, Glendale, Missouri, US
Fields: Biochemistry
Known for: Cori cycle; glycogen metabolism; glucose-1-phosphate ("Cori ester")
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (1947)

Gerty Theresa Cori (née Radnitz; August 15, 1896 – October 26, 1957) was a Bohemian-born American biochemist who, with her husband Carl Ferdinand Cori, elucidated how the body stores and uses carbohydrates. In 1947 they shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (with Bernardo Houssay) "for their discovery of the course of the catalytic conversion of glycogen," making her the first woman to win the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Life and career

Born in Prague, she earned her medical doctorate from the German Charles University in 1920, the year she married Carl Cori. The couple emigrated to the United States in 1922, working at the New York State Institute for the Study of Malignant Diseases in Buffalo, and moved in 1931 to Washington University in St. Louis. Despite her accomplishments, she was long held at low rank and pay — appointed full professor only in 1947, months before the Nobel announcement.

Scientific work

The Coris described the Cori cycle, in which lactate produced by muscle is converted back to glucose in the liver. They isolated glucose-1-phosphate (the "Cori ester"), discovered the enzyme glycogen phosphorylase, and worked out how glycogen is built and broken down. Gerty later identified the enzyme defects behind several glycogen storage diseases, founding the study of inherited metabolic disorders. The Cori crater on the Moon is named for her.
