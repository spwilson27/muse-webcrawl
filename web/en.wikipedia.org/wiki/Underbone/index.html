Underbone

An underbone is a type of motorcycle built on a single large-diameter tubular or pressed-steel frame that runs beneath the engine — the "underbone" — rather than the step-through frame of a scooter or the cradle frames of conventional motorcycles. The fuel tank is usually beneath the seat, giving a low, open step area in front of the rider.

Design and history

The archetype is the Honda Super Cub, introduced in 1958, which combined the underbone layout with a semi-automatic centrifugal clutch and became the best-selling motor vehicle in history. Unlike scooters, underbones have larger wheels, mid-mounted engines and chain drive, and many models have manual gearboxes operated by a heel-and-toe shifter.

Popularity

Underbones dominate personal transport in Southeast Asia, where models such as the Honda Wave, Yamaha Exciter and Suzuki Raider are ubiquitous, and racing series for modified underbones are popular in Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam and the Philippines.
