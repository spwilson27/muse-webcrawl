The 2010–11 Ivorian crisis was a political and military crisis in Côte d'Ivoire that followed the disputed presidential election of November 2010, intended to conclude the country's long division after the civil war of 2002–2007. Both incumbent Laurent Gbagbo and challenger Alassane Ouattara claimed victory, producing rival governments and a descent into armed conflict sometimes called the Second Ivorian Civil War.

The disputed election

The Independent Electoral Commission declared Ouattara the winner of the 28 November runoff with about 54 percent of the vote, a result certified by the United Nations and recognized by the African Union, ECOWAS, the EU, and the United States. The Constitutional Council, headed by a Gbagbo ally, annulled results from several northern regions and proclaimed Gbagbo the winner. Both men were sworn in, and the standoff paralysed the country; sanctions and the freezing of state accounts squeezed Gbagbo's government.

Armed conflict

Fighting intensified in late February 2011. Forces loyal to Ouattara advanced from the north, taking most of the country by the end of March, while the western town of Duékoué saw massacres of hundreds of civilians attributed to both sides. In Abidjan, Gbagbo's forces shelled pro-Ouattara districts and attacked UN peacekeepers; on 4 April, UN and French forces struck Gbagbo's heavy weapons under a Security Council mandate to protect civilians. On 11 April 2011, pro-Ouattara forces stormed the presidential residence and arrested Gbagbo.

Aftermath

Ouattara was inaugurated in May 2011. Gbagbo was transferred to the International Criminal Court in The Hague, charged with crimes against humanity; he was acquitted in 2019, with the acquittal upheld on appeal in 2021, and he returned to Côte d'Ivoire. The UN estimated about 3,000 people were killed and up to a million displaced during the crisis.
