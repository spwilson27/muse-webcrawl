The Shambala Preserve is an animal sanctuary in Acton, California, in the high desert north of Los Angeles. Operated by the Roar Foundation, it provides lifetime care for exotic animals — primarily big cats such as lions, tigers, leopards, servals and cougars — that were born in captivity and relinquished, confiscated, or rescued from private owners, circuses and the entertainment industry.

History

The preserve was founded by actress and animal-welfare activist Tippi Hedren, whose interest in big cats grew out of the making of the 1981 film Roar, filmed with dozens of untrained lions and tigers. Hedren established the Roar Foundation in 1983 to support the sanctuary, which took its name from Shambala, a mythical kingdom in Buddhist tradition said to be a place of peace and harmony.

Animals and care

At various times the preserve has housed more than fifty big cats, including animals that appeared in films and advertising and individuals seized by authorities from unlicensed owners. The sanctuary does not breed, sell or trade its animals, and the cats live out their lives in large compounds with pools, trees and enrichment. Public access is limited to scheduled, guided monthly safaris and special events, with proceeds supporting the animals' care.

Advocacy

Through the Roar Foundation, Hedren campaigned for decades for federal legislation restricting private ownership of big cats, efforts that contributed to the passage of the Big Cat Public Safety Act in the United States in 2022.
