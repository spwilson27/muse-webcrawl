Kavango–Zambezi Transfrontier Conservation Area

KAZA TFCA
Countries: Angola, Botswana, Namibia, Zambia, Zimbabwe
Area: about 520,000 km²
Established: treaty signed 18 August 2011
Component parks: 36 protected areas, incl. Chobe, Hwange, Kafue and the Okavango Delta

The Kavango–Zambezi Transfrontier Conservation Area (KAZA TFCA) is a very large transboundary conservation area in southern Africa, spanning parts of Angola, Botswana, Namibia, Zambia and Zimbabwe where the Kavango and Zambezi river basins meet. At roughly 520,000 km² it is among the largest terrestrial conservation areas in the world, about the size of France.

Geography and wildlife

KAZA links 36 national parks, game reserves and community conservancies, including Chobe National Park, Hwange National Park, Kafue National Park, the Okavango Delta and Victoria Falls. It protects the largest contiguous population of African elephants, estimated at over 220,000 animals, along with lions, buffalo, wild dogs and the seasonal zebra migrations of the region.

Establishment

The five partner states signed a treaty establishing KAZA on 18 August 2011 in Luanda, building on a 2006 memorandum of understanding. Its aims include harmonising conservation policy, removing barriers to wildlife movement, and developing nature-based tourism — including a proposed univisa to ease cross-border travel. Implementation is coordinated by a secretariat in Kasane, Botswana.
