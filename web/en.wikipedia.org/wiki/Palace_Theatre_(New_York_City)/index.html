The Palace Theatre is a Broadway theatre at 1564 Broadway, at the north end of Times Square in the Theater District of Manhattan in New York City. Designed by architects Kirchhoff & Rose, the theatre opened in 1913 and became the most famous venue of American vaudeville: for performers of the era, "playing the Palace" was the pinnacle of success.

Vaudeville era

Built by impresario Martin Beck and soon controlled by the Keith-Albee circuit, the Palace presented star-studded two-a-day vaudeville bills. Headliners included Sarah Bernhardt, Harry Houdini, W. C. Fields, Will Rogers, Fanny Brice, and the Marx Brothers. Judy Garland's celebrated 1951 and 1956 engagements helped revive the house as a venue for one-person shows and concerts, a role it reprised with stars such as Garland again in 1967, and later Liza Minnelli and Diana Ross.

Broadway era

After decades as a vaudeville and then movie palace (under RKO), the theatre was restored for legitimate theatre and reopened in 1966 with the original production of Sweet Charity. Subsequent hits included Applause with Lauren Bacall, La Cage aux Folles, The Will Rogers Follies, Disney's Beauty and the Beast and Aida, Legally Blonde, Priscilla, Queen of the Desert, An American in Paris and SpongeBob SquarePants.

Renovation

Between 2018 and 2024 the theatre closed for a major redevelopment in which the auditorium was lifted about 30 feet to make way for retail space below, as part of the TSX Broadway project, while its landmarked interior was restored. The theatre reopened in 2024 with the musical Tammy Faye.
