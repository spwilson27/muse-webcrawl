Yacht racing

Yacht racing
Governing body: World Sailing
First contested: 17th century (Netherlands and England)
Olympic sport: Since 1900 (as sailing)

Yacht racing is a sailing sport involving sailing yachts and larger sailboats, as distinguished from dinghy racing. It is composed of multiple yachts, racing head-to-head in fleet races, match races between two boats, or longer offshore distance races. The sport is governed internationally by World Sailing and conducted under the Racing Rules of Sailing.

Formats

Fleet racing pits many boats against each other around a course of marks, typically windward–leeward or triangular courses. Because boats differ in speed, keelboat racing often uses handicap systems such as IRC, ORC, or PHRF, or one-design classes in which all boats are identical. Match racing, used in the America's Cup, is a duel between two boats. Offshore racing covers long distances in open water, as in the Fastnet Race, the Sydney to Hobart Yacht Race, and round-the-world events such as The Ocean Race.

History

Organized yacht racing traces to 17th-century Holland and Restoration England, where Charles II raced on the Thames. The first yacht clubs appeared in the 18th century (Cork's Water Club, 1720), and the America's Cup began in 1851 when the schooner America won a race around the Isle of Wight. Sailing has been an Olympic sport since 1900.
