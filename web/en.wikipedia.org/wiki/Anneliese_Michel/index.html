Anneliese Michel (21 September 1952 – 1 July 1976) was a German woman who underwent Catholic exorcism rites during the year before her death. Diagnosed in her teens with epilepsy and treated in psychiatric care, she and her devout family came to believe she was demonically possessed.

Exorcism and death

In 1975, with the permission of the Bishop of Würzburg, two priests performed 67 exorcism sessions over about ten months. Michel stopped eating and drinking adequately, displayed extreme physical behaviour, and died of malnutrition and dehydration, weighing about 30 kg (66 lb).

Trial

Her parents and the two priests were tried for negligent homicide in 1978 and convicted, receiving suspended sentences. The case prompted the German bishops to revise exorcism guidelines and inspired the films The Exorcism of Emily Rose (2005) and Requiem (2006).
