Messiah Complex

A messiah complex (also called Christ complex or saviour complex) is a state of mind in which an individual holds a belief that they are destined to become a saviour. The term is also used more loosely to describe someone who believes they are responsible for saving or rescuing others, sometimes in a way that suggests grandiosity.

Description

The messiah complex is not a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5. It is most often discussed in connection with psychiatric conditions such as schizophrenia and bipolar mania, in which grandiose delusions can take a religious form; the sufferer may believe they are Jesus Christ, another messiah figure, or a chosen prophet. It has also been associated historically with the so-called Jerusalem syndrome, in which visitors to the city develop religious delusions.

Saviour complex

In popular usage, a "saviour complex" describes a psychological pattern in which a person feels compelled to help others, sometimes deriving self-worth from being needed, and may neglect their own needs or enable harmful behaviour in those they try to rescue. It is discussed in relation to codependency and helping professions.
