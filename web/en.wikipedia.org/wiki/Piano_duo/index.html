Piano duo

A piano duo is an ensemble of two pianists playing on two separate pianos, as distinct from a piano duet, in which two players share a single instrument. Two-piano music allows a fullness and antiphonal effect impossible at one keyboard and forms a small but distinguished corner of the chamber repertoire.

Repertoire

Original works include Mozart's Sonata for Two Pianos in D major, K.448, Brahms's Sonata for Two Pianos (later reworked as the Piano Quintet), Rachmaninoff's two suites, Debussy's En blanc et noir, and Bartók's Sonata for Two Pianos and Percussion. Two-piano concertos include Mozart's Concerto No. 10, Mendelssohn's concertos, and Poulenc's Concerto for Two Pianos.

Notable duos

Celebrated duo teams have included Josef and Rosina Lhévinne, Vitya Vronsky and Victor Babin, Arthur Gold and Robert Fizdale, Katia and Marielle Labèque, and the twin sisters Christina and Michelle Naughton.
