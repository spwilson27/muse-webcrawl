Rusa II (reigned c. 685–639 BC) was a king of Urartu, the Iron Age kingdom centred on Lake Van in the Armenian Highlands. The son of Argishti II, he is regarded as the last great king of Urartu, presiding over a period of renewed prosperity, extensive building works and diplomatic engagement with the Assyrian Empire.

Reign

Rusa II ascended the throne at a time when Urartu had been weakened by Cimmerian incursions and earlier Assyrian campaigns. He restored the kingdom's fortunes, reorganising its administration and founding new cities and fortresses. His great building projects included the city of Rusahinili ("city of Rusa", modern Toprakkale near Van), the fortress and city at Karmir Blur (Teishebaini) on the Ararat plain, the complex at Ayanis on the eastern shore of Lake Van, and Bastam in north-western Iran. These sites have yielded temples, palaces, granaries, wine cellars and large bronze hoards.

Relations with Assyria

Unlike his early predecessors, Rusa maintained broadly peaceful relations with Assyria. Assyrian royal inscriptions of Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal mention embassies from Rusa, and Urartian envoys appear at the Assyrian court. A famous letter reports Rusa's grief and death following news of an Assyrian victory, though the chronology of his final years remains debated. After his death, Urartu declined rapidly under Sarduri III and later kings, and the kingdom disappeared by the early 6th century BC.

Legacy

Excavations at Ayanis, Karmir Blur and Bastam have shown that Rusa II's reign was a final flourishing of Urartian art and state organisation, marked by monumental inscriptions — including a bilingual Assyrian-Urartian stele — inscribed bronze shields, helmets and large-scale irrigation works.
