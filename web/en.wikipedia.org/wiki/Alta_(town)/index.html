Alta is a town in Alta Municipality in Finnmark county, Norway. Situated at the head of Altafjorden at about 70° north latitude, it is one of the northernmost towns of its size in the world and a hub for the surrounding region.

History and sights

The area is known for the Rock art at Alta, a UNESCO World Heritage Site of petroglyphs dating from roughly 4200 BC to 500 BC. The town was largely destroyed at the end of the Second World War and rebuilt. Its modern landmark is the Northern Lights Cathedral (Nordlyskatedralen), consecrated in 2013, with a distinctive spiral tower clad in titanium.

Climate and activities

Despite its far-northern location, Alta has a relatively mild subarctic climate for its latitude, with long summer daylight and dark winters lit by the aurora borealis. It hosts the Finnmarksløpet, Europe's longest dog-sled race, and the Alta River is famous for salmon fishing and for its large slate quarries.
