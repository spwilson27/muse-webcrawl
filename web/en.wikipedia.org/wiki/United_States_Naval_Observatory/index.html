United States Naval Observatory

USNO
Established: 1830 (Depot of Charts and Instruments)
Location: Washington, D.C.

The United States Naval Observatory (USNO) is a scientific and military facility in Washington, D.C., that produces data for navigation and fundamental astronomy for the United States Navy and the United States Department of Defense. It maintains the master clock of the United States, providing precise time for GPS and military operations.

History

The observatory grew from the Depot of Charts and Instruments, established in 1830. Under Matthew Fontaine Maury it became a leading center of oceanography. In 1877 Asaph Hall discovered the moons of Mars, Phobos and Deimos, using its 26-inch "Great Equatorial" telescope. The observatory moved to its present site on Observatory Circle in 1893.

Time service

USNO operates an ensemble of atomic clocks whose combined output defines the USNO master clock, a primary realization of Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) for the Department of Defense. Its time signals support the Global Positioning System, whose ground control uses USNO time.

Vice President's residence

Since 1974, the superintendent's house at Number One Observatory Circle has served as the official residence of the Vice President of the United States.
