Principal curvature

In differential geometry, the two principal curvatures at a given point of a surface are the maximum and minimum values of the normal curvature at that point. They measure how the surface bends by different amounts in different directions at that point.

Definition

At each point p of a surface in three-dimensional Euclidean space, one may choose a unit normal vector. A normal plane through the normal contains infinitely many normal curves, whose curvatures form a function on directions. The maximum and minimum values of this function are the principal curvatures, κ1 and κ2, and the directions in which they occur — the principal directions — are always perpendicular to each other.

Properties

The principal curvatures are the eigenvalues of the shape operator (Weingarten map) at the point. Their product is the Gaussian curvature K = κ1κ2, and their arithmetic mean is the mean curvature H = (κ1 + κ2)/2. A point is called umbilical if the two principal curvatures are equal there, as happens everywhere on a sphere or a plane.

Principal curvature directions can become undefined or discontinuous at umbilical points; away from them, they form two orthogonal line fields on the surface whose integral curves are called lines of curvature.
