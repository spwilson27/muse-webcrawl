Dronabinol

Dronabinol is the international nonproprietary name (INN) for synthetic delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), the principal psychoactive constituent of cannabis. It is marketed under brand names including Marinol and Syndros and is used medically as an appetite stimulant and antiemetic.

Medical uses

Dronabinol is approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of nausea and vomiting associated with cancer chemotherapy in patients who have failed to respond adequately to conventional antiemetic treatments, and for anorexia associated with weight loss in patients with AIDS. It is also used off-label and in trials for conditions such as chronic pain and obstructive sleep apnea, though evidence for sleep apnea was considered insufficient by the FDA.

Pharmacology

Dronabinol acts as a partial agonist at cannabinoid CB1 and CB2 receptors. After oral administration it is absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract and metabolised in the liver, largely to 11-hydroxy-THC, an active metabolite that is itself psychoactive. Its onset is slower and its effects longer-lasting than smoked cannabis; plasma levels are variable because of extensive first-pass metabolism.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include dizziness, euphoria, drowsiness, confusion, dry mouth, nausea, and abdominal pain. At higher doses it may cause anxiety, paranoia, tachycardia, and orthostatic hypotension. Dronabinol has some potential for dependence and is a controlled substance in most jurisdictions.

History and legal status

Marinol capsules were first approved by the FDA in 1985 for chemotherapy-induced nausea and in 1992 for AIDS-related anorexia. In the United States, dronabinol in sesame-oil capsules (Marinol) was moved from Schedule II to Schedule III of the Controlled Substances Act in 1999, while the oral solution Syndros is Schedule II. It was originally developed and marketed by Unimed Pharmaceuticals and later sold by AbbVie.
