San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance

Founded: 1916 (as the Zoological Society of San Diego)

Type: Nonprofit conservation organisation

Headquarters: San Diego, California, U.S.

Operates: San Diego Zoo; San Diego Zoo Safari Park

The San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance is a nonprofit international conservation organisation based in San Diego, California. It operates the San Diego Zoo in Balboa Park and the San Diego Zoo Safari Park in Escondido, and conducts wildlife conservation, research and education programmes around the world. Until 2021 it was known as San Diego Zoo Global, and before that as the Zoological Society of San Diego.

History

The society was founded in 1916 by Dr Harry M. Wegeforth, who gathered animals left behind after the Panama–California Exposition to create the San Diego Zoo. Over the following decades the zoo became one of the largest and most visited in the world, pioneering cage-less, moated exhibits. The Wild Animal Park (now Safari Park) opened in 1972 as a large-scale breeding and conservation facility.

Conservation work

The Alliance's conservation arm, including its Institute for Conservation Research, works on reintroduction and recovery programmes for endangered species. Its emblematic success is the California condor, brought back from the brink of extinction through captive breeding. Other programmes include work on giant pandas, polar bears, mountain yellow-legged frogs and plant conservation through a native seed bank.
