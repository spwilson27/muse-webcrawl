Kahekili

Kahekili II, full name Kahekilinuiahumanu (c. 1737 – July 1794), was the twenty-second King (Moʻi) of Maui and, by conquest, ruler of Oʻahu. A powerful and feared aliʻi of the late pre-unification Hawaiian Islands, he was known for extensive tattooing — one side of his body was tattooed black from head to toe — and for his warlike campaigns.

Reign

Kahekili succeeded his brother Kamehamehanui as ruler of Maui, and in 1783 he conquered Oʻahu, installing his rule there after defeating the Oʻahu chief Kahahana. His empire came to include Maui, Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi and Oʻahu, making him the principal rival of Kamehameha I in the wars that eventually unified the islands. He is sometimes speculated to have been Kamehameha's biological father, though this is debated.

Death and succession

Kahekili died at Waikīkī in 1794. His brother Kaeokulani and his son Kalanikūpule attempted to hold his domains, but Kamehameha defeated Kalanikūpule at the Battle of Nuʻuanu in 1795, taking Oʻahu and completing his control of all the islands except Kauaʻi.

Legacy

Kahekili is remembered in Hawaiian tradition both as a formidable strategist and as a stern ruler; the practice of cliff-leaping at Kahekili's Leap (Puʻu Kekaʻa) on Maui is named for a legend associated with him.
