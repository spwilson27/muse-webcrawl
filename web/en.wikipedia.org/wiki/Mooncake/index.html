Mooncake
TypePastry
Place of originChina
Associated festivalMid-Autumn Festival
Main ingredientsWheat flour crust; lotus seed paste, red bean paste, salted egg yolk, nuts

A mooncake (月餅) is a Chinese baked pastry traditionally eaten during the Mid-Autumn Festival, when families gather to admire the full moon. Mooncakes are round — symbolising reunion and the Moon — and are typically given as gifts in ornate boxes to relatives, friends, and business associates. Rich and dense, a traditional mooncake is usually shared, cut into small wedges, and eaten with tea.

Traditional mooncakes

The most widespread style is the Cantonese mooncake: a tender, golden-brown baked crust made with syrup and lye water, stamped in a wooden mould with decorative characters for longevity or harmony. Classic fillings include lotus seed paste, sweet red bean paste, jujube paste, and mixed nuts and seeds ("five kernels"). A prized variant encloses one or more salted duck egg yolks, representing the full moon. Other regional styles include the flaky Suzhou-style savoury pork mooncake, Yunnan ham mooncakes, and the pale, shortcrust Teochew style.

Modern varieties

Contemporary adaptations include snow skin mooncakes, an unbaked style from Hong Kong with a soft glutinous-rice skin eaten chilled, as well as fillings such as chocolate, coffee, durian, green tea, custard, and ice cream. Low-sugar and mini mooncakes cater to health-conscious consumers, while luxury hotel and fashion-brand mooncake gift sets have become a major seasonal market across Asia.

History and legend

Moon-shaped cakes offered to the Moon date back many centuries in China, and mooncakes were well established by the Ming dynasty. A popular legend holds that mooncakes were used to smuggle secret messages coordinating the Han Chinese revolt against the Mongol-led Yuan dynasty at the end of the 14th century — a story associated with the founding of the Ming dynasty, though historians regard it as folklore.

Regional counterparts

Related festival cakes include Vietnamese bánh trung thu and Korean songpyeon (a different rice cake for Chuseok). Mooncakes are produced and sold in large quantities throughout East and Southeast Asia and in Chinatowns worldwide.
