Imperial Valley

The Imperial Valley is a valley in Imperial County in southeastern California, extending south into Baja California, Mexico. Lying largely below sea level in the Colorado Desert, it is one of the most productive agricultural regions in the United States thanks to irrigation water diverted from the Colorado River via the All-American Canal.

Geography

The valley forms the southern part of the Salton Trough and contains the Salton Sea, California's largest lake, which formed in 1905–1907 when Colorado River floodwaters breached an irrigation canal. Principal settlements include El Centro (the county seat), Brawley, Calexico and Imperial, with Mexicali across the Mexican border.

Agriculture and economy

With year-round sunshine and imported water, the valley produces a large share of the United States' winter vegetables, including lettuce and other leafy greens, as well as alfalfa, melons and dates. The region has a hot desert climate with summer temperatures regularly exceeding 43 °C (110 °F). Geothermal energy production around the Salton Sea is also significant.
