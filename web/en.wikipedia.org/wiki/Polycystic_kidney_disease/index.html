Polycystic kidney disease

Polycystic kidney disease (PKD) is a genetic disorder in which clusters of fluid-filled cysts develop within the kidneys, enlarging them and progressively impairing their function. It is one of the most common inherited diseases and a significant cause of kidney failure.

Forms

Autosomal dominant PKD (ADPKD) affects roughly 1 in 400 to 1 in 1,000 people and usually presents in adulthood. It is caused mainly by mutations in PKD1 (encoding polycystin-1, about 78–85% of cases) or PKD2 (polycystin-2), with PKD1 disease generally progressing faster, reaching kidney failure around the mid-50s on average. Autosomal recessive PKD (ARPKD), caused by mutations in PKHD1, is much rarer and presents in infancy or childhood, often with congenital hepatic fibrosis.

Features

Symptoms include flank pain, blood in the urine, urinary infections, kidney stones and high blood pressure. Cysts may also develop in the liver and pancreas. ADPKD is associated with intracranial (berry) aneurysms, whose rupture can cause subarachnoid haemorrhage, and with mitral valve prolapse and colonic diverticula.

Management

There is no cure, but blood-pressure control, hydration and treatment of infections slow progression. The vasopressin receptor antagonist tolvaptan can slow cyst growth and decline in kidney function in rapidly progressing ADPKD. End-stage disease is treated with dialysis or kidney transplantation.
