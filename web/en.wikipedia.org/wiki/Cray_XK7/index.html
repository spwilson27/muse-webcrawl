Cray XK7

Cray XK7
Manufacturer: Cray Inc.
Announced: 2011
Architecture: AMD Opteron CPUs with NVIDIA Tesla GPUs
Interconnect: Cray Gemini
Notable system: Titan (Oak Ridge)

The Cray XK7 is a supercomputer platform manufactured by Cray Inc. and announced in 2011. The XK7 is a hybrid architecture: each compute node combines a conventional AMD Opteron central processing unit with an NVIDIA Tesla graphics processing unit (GPU) used as an accelerator, connected by Cray's Gemini interconnect. The design was intended to deliver high performance per watt by offloading suitable parallel computation to the GPUs.

Architecture

XK7 blades mount multiple nodes, each pairing a 16-core AMD Opteron 6200-series ("Interlagos") processor with an NVIDIA Tesla K20-series GPU (in later configurations) and shared memory, within the liquid-cooled cabinet design shared with the XE6 line. The Gemini interconnect links nodes in a three-dimensional torus, supporting systems of tens of thousands of nodes.

Titan

The best-known XK7 installation was Titan at Oak Ridge National Laboratory in the United States, completed in 2012 for the Department of Energy. Titan comprised 18,688 compute nodes with a combined peak performance of more than 27 petaFLOPS, and on its debut it was ranked the fastest supercomputer in the world on the TOP500 list. It was used for open scientific research in fields including climate modelling, materials science, astrophysics, and combustion. Titan was decommissioned in 2019 and succeeded at Oak Ridge by the IBM system Summit.
