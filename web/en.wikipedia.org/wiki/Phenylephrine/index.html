Phenylephrine

ClassAlpha-1 adrenergic agonist
UsesNasal decongestion, hypotension, mydriasis
RoutesOral, nasal, intravenous, ophthalmic
ATC codeC01CA06

Phenylephrine, sold under the brand name Neo-Synephrine among others, is a medication primarily used as a decongestant to unblock the nose, to dilate the pupil, to increase blood pressure, and to relieve hemorrhoids. It is a selective α1-adrenergic receptor agonist of the phenethylamine class. It can be taken orally, as a nasal spray, given by injection into a vein or muscle, or applied to the skin.

Medical uses

As a decongestant, phenylephrine constricts blood vessels in the nasal mucosa, reducing swelling. It became the dominant oral decongestant in the United States after pseudoephedrine sales were restricted. Intravenously, it is widely used in anaesthesia and critical care to treat hypotension, since it raises blood pressure without significantly increasing heart rate. In ophthalmology, phenylephrine eye drops produce mydriasis (pupil dilation) for examinations and surgery.

Efficacy debate

Concerns about the effectiveness of oral phenylephrine at approved doses date to the 1970s, since the drug is extensively metabolized in the gut wall. In September 2023, an advisory committee of the U.S. Food and Drug Administration unanimously concluded that oral phenylephrine is ineffective as a nasal decongestant, and the FDA subsequently proposed removing it from the over-the-counter monograph. Nasal spray and injectable forms remain considered effective.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, anxiety, and nausea. Serious effects of intravenous use can include reflex bradycardia, dangerously high blood pressure, and reduced cardiac output. Phenylephrine should be used with caution in people with hypertension, cardiovascular disease, or hyperthyroidism, and it interacts with monoamine oxidase inhibitors.
