Leshy

A leshy (Russian: леший, literally " [he] from the forest") is a tutelary deity of the forests in pagan Slavic mythology. As the spirit rules over the forest and hunting, he may be related to the Slavic god Porewit.

There is also a deity, named Polevik, who is a field spirit and has similar characteristics, being connected to the forest when there is no grain. The leshy also has a close relation with the Slavic spirit of the swamps.

Appearance and behaviour

The leshy is usually described as a tall man, but he is able to change his size from that of a blade of grass to a very tall tree. He has hair and a beard made from living grass and vines, and is sometimes depicted with a tail, hooves, and horns. He has pale white skin that contrasts with his bright green eyes. He is said to have no shadow. He carries a club to indicate his status as master of the forest.

Leshies are mischievous and may lead travellers astray, tickle people to death, or steal children, though they can be placated with offerings. Hunters and herders made agreements with them to protect livestock and crops.

In popular culture

Leshies appear in numerous folk tales, literature, video games and fantasy works drawing on Slavic folklore.
