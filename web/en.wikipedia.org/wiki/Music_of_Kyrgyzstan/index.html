Music of Kyrgyzstan

The music of Kyrgyzstan is rooted in the nomadic culture of the Kyrgyz people and centres on storytelling, epic singing, and distinctive instruments such as the komuz, a three-stringed fretless lute that is a national symbol of Kyrgyzstan.

Epic tradition

The centrepiece of Kyrgyz culture is the Epic of Manas, one of the longest epics in the world, sung or recited by specialist bards called manaschi. The trilogy of Manas, Semetey, and Seytek runs to over half a million lines in its fullest recorded versions. Other epic singers, akyns, improvise songs and engage in competitive poetic duels (aitysh) accompanied by the komuz.

Instruments

Traditional instruments include the komuz; the kyl kiak, a two-stringed bowed fiddle; the temir komuz (Jew's harp); the choor, an end-blown flute; the surnay; and the kerney. Contemporary Kyrgyz ensembles combine these with Western instruments.

Soviet and modern periods

During the Soviet era, Kyrgyz melodies were arranged for orchestras and choirs, and composers such as Abdylas Maldybayev wrote the first Kyrgyz operas. Toktogul Satylganov (1864–1933), a celebrated akyn and komuz player, remains a towering figure whose songs form part of the national repertoire. Modern Kyrgyz popular music includes pop, rock, and hip-hop in Kyrgyz and Russian.
