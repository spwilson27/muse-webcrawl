The Bluebook

The Bluebook: A Uniform System of Citation is a style guide that prescribes the most widely used legal citation system in the United States. It is compiled by the editors of the Columbia Law Review, the Harvard Law Review, the University of Pennsylvania Law Review, and the Yale Law Journal.

History

The first edition of The Bluebook was published in 1926 as a 26-page pamphlet entitled A Uniform System of Citation, intended to standardise citations in the Harvard Law Review. Its title derives from the colour of its cover. Successive editions have grown considerably: the twenty-first edition, published in 2020, runs to more than 500 pages.

Contents

The guide is divided into three main parts: the Bluepages, which provide accessible guidance on citation in court documents and legal memoranda; the main body of rules governing citation form in law review scholarship, covering cases, statutes, books, periodicals, foreign and international materials; and extensive tables listing abbreviations for reporters, courts, jurisdictions, and periodicals.

Criticism

The Bluebook has been criticised for its complexity, its frequent revisions, and for rules seen as arbitrary or excessively detailed. Alternative systems, such as the ALWD Citation Manual and the "Maroonbook" used at the University of Chicago, compete with it, and some jurisdictions have adopted their own public-domain citation systems. Judge Richard Posner was a prominent critic, calling it "a monstrous growth".
