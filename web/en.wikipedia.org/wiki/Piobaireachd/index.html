Piobaireachd

Piobaireachd is the classical music of the Great Highland bagpipe.

Piobaireachd (pronounced roughly "pee-broch"; Scottish Gaelic pìobaireachd, "pipe music") is the classical repertoire of the Great Highland bagpipe, also known by the Gaelic term ceòl mòr, "the great music," in contrast to the lighter marches, strathspeys and reels of ceòl beag ("little music").

Form

A piobaireachd is built as a theme and variations. It begins with the ùrlar ("ground"), a slow theme stated in a flexible, expressive rhythm. A series of variations follows, each ornamenting the ground more elaborately — typically through movements known as taorluath and crunluath variations, with their flurries of grace notes — before the ùrlar returns, often in an abbreviated reprise, to close the piece. A full piobaireachd may last from five to twenty-five minutes.

History

The music was for centuries maintained by hereditary piping families, above all the MacCrimmons of Skye, pipers to the MacLeods of Dunvegan, who are credited with shaping its mature form. Tunes are salutes, laments, gatherings and commemorations of battles and chiefs. The tradition was transmitted orally through canntaireachd, a sung syllabic notation in which vocables encode melody and ornament, before staff notations and published collections appeared in the early nineteenth century. The Piobaireachd Society, founded in 1903, publishes the standard editions used in today's competitions, such as those at the Argyllshire Gathering and the Northern Meeting.
