Tetany

Tetany
SpecialtyEndocrinology, neurology
SymptomsMuscle cramps, spasms, paraesthesia, carpopedal spasm
CausesHypocalcaemia, hypomagnesaemia, alkalosis

Tetany, or tetanic seizure, is a medical sign consisting of the involuntary contraction of muscles, which may be caused by disorders that increase the action potential frequency of muscle cells or of the nerves that innervate them. Muscle cramps caused by the disease tetanus are not classed as tetany; rather, they are due to a lack of inhibition to the neurons that supply muscles.

Carpopedal spasm, a characteristic sign of tetany

Causes

The usual cause of tetany is a deficiency of calcium. An excess of phosphate (a high phosphate-to-calcium ratio) can also trigger the spasms. Hypomagnesaemia can also cause tetany, as can respiratory or metabolic alkalosis, which lowers the fraction of ionised calcium in the blood. Conditions that may produce tetany include hypoparathyroidism, vitamin D deficiency, acute pancreatitis, and chronic kidney disease.

Signs

Two classic eponymous signs are used at the bedside:

  • Chvostek sign — twitching of the facial muscles in response to tapping over the area of the facial nerve.
  • Trousseau sign — carpopedal spasm induced by inflating a blood-pressure cuff above systolic pressure for several minutes.

Laryngospasm and, in severe cases, seizures may occur, making tetany a medical emergency when it compromises the airway.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause; acute symptomatic hypocalcaemia is treated with intravenous calcium gluconate, with concurrent correction of magnesium deficiency where present.
