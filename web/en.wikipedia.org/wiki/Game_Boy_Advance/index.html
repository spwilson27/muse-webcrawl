Game Boy Advance

The Game Boy Advance (GBA) is a 32-bit handheld game console developed, manufactured and marketed by Nintendo as the successor to the Game Boy Color. It was released in Japan on March 21, 2001, in North America on June 11, 2001, and in Europe and Australia later that month. The system sold more than 81.5 million units worldwide, including its later revisions.

Hardware

The GBA is powered by a 16.8 MHz ARM7TDMI CPU with an embedded Z80 coprocessor that provides backward compatibility with Game Boy and Game Boy Color cartridges. It has a 2.9-inch reflective TFT colour screen with a resolution of 240 × 160 pixels, capable of displaying 512 colours in character mode or 32,768 colours in bitmap mode. The unit is powered by two AA batteries giving roughly 15 hours of play. Cartridges could hold up to 32 MB. A link cable allowed multiplayer play for up to four players, and accessories included the e-Reader and the Wireless Adapter.

Revisions

The Game Boy Advance SP (2003) introduced a clamshell design, a rechargeable battery, and a frontlit screen (later models, from 2005, used a brighter backlit screen). The Game Boy Micro (2005), the final system in the Game Boy line, shrank the hardware into a tiny unit with a 2-inch backlit display and removable faceplates, dropping compatibility with original Game Boy cartridges.

Software

The library includes Pokémon Ruby and Sapphire, Pokémon FireRed and LeafGreen, Mario Kart: Super Circuit, The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap, Metroid Fusion, Advance Wars and Golden Sun. The best-selling title is Pokémon Ruby and Sapphire, with over 16 million copies sold. The GBA's catalogue of ports and originals is regarded as one of the strongest in handheld gaming.
