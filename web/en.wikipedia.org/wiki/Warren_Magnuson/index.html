Warren Grant Magnuson (1905–1989) was an American lawyer and Democratic politician who represented the state of Washington in the United States House of Representatives (1937–1944) and the United States Senate (1944–1981).

Senate career

Appointed to the Senate in December 1944 to fill a vacancy, Magnuson was then elected to six full terms. Known affectionately as "Maggie", he became one of the Senate's most powerful members, chairing the Commerce Committee and later the Appropriations Committee, and serving as president pro tempore from 1979 to 1981.

Legislation

Magnuson was a leading consumer-protection legislator, associated with the Magnuson–Moss Warranty Act, flammable-fabrics standards, and the creation of the Consumer Product Safety Commission. He shepherded the Magnuson Fishery Conservation and Management Act (1976), extending US fisheries jurisdiction to 200 nautical miles, later renamed the Magnuson–Stevens Act. He also championed federal funding for medical research and for his home state's infrastructure.

He lost re-election in the Republican wave of 1980 and died in Seattle in 1989. The Warren G. Magnuson Health Sciences Center at the University of Washington bears his name.
