Airline

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A row of airliners in different airline liveries at terminal gates]
Airliners of several airlines at a hub.

An airline is a company that provides air transport services for passengers and freight using aircraft it owns or leases. Airlines range from flag carriers such as Lufthansa and Singapore Airlines to low-cost giants like Ryanair and Southwest, and cargo specialists such as FedEx Express. The industry is organised through alliances (Star Alliance, SkyTeam, Oneworld), codeshares and the global distribution systems overseen commercially by IATA.

History

DELAG in Germany (1909) carried passengers by Zeppelin; the first fixed-wing scheduled service flew in Florida in 1914. KLM (1919) is the oldest airline still operating under its original name. Deregulation — the US in 1978, Europe in the 1990s — unleashed competition and the low-cost model.

Business models

Full-service \'legacy\' carriers run hub-and-spoke networks with multiple cabin classes; low-cost carriers use single fleets, quick turnarounds and ancillary fees; ultra-low-cost and hybrid models blur the lines. Freight airlines and integrators run their own global networks, with Memphis and Louisville among the busiest cargo airports.

Economics and safety

Airlines are famously cyclical, hit by fuel prices, recessions, 9/11 and the COVID-19 pandemic, during which governments provided large bailouts. Safety has improved relentlessly: modern Western-built jets have fatal accident rates measured in one per several million flights.
