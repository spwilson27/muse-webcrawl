Taieri Gorge

Taieri Gorge is a deep canyon carved by the Taieri River in Otago, on the South Island of New Zealand. The gorge is best known for the Taieri Gorge Railway, a heritage railway running from Dunedin through the gorge to Pukerangi and Middlemarch.

Railway

The line through the gorge forms part of the former Otago Central Railway, opened in stages from 1879. The gorge section is an engineering feat featuring tunnels, viaducts (including the Wingatui Viaduct, among the largest wrought-iron structures in the Southern Hemisphere), and steep gradients, all built largely by hand. After KiwiRail withdrew services in 1990, the line was taken over by a charitable trust and the Dunedin City Council; tourist trains operated by Dunedin Railways traverse the gorge, offering views of the river, schist cliffs, and native bush.
