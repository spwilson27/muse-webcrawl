Twin

Twins are two offspring produced by the same pregnancy. Twins may be monozygotic ("identical"), arising from a single fertilized egg that splits into two embryos, or dizygotic ("fraternal"), arising from two separate eggs fertilized by two separate sperm. About one in 80 human pregnancies results in twins naturally, though rates vary by population and have risen with fertility treatments and older maternal age.

Types

Dizygotic twins share on average half their DNA, like ordinary siblings, and may be of different sexes. Monozygotic twins share essentially the same genome, though small genetic and epigenetic differences accumulate; they are almost always of the same sex. Depending on when the zygote splits, identical twins may share a placenta (monochorionic) or have separate ones, and very rarely are conjoined, when separation is incomplete.

Twin studies

Because monozygotic twins share nearly all their genes while dizygotic twins share only about half, comparing the two groups allows researchers to estimate the heritability of traits and diseases — the classic twin study design, used extensively in psychology, medicine, and behaviour genetics. Studies of twins reared apart, such as the Minnesota Study of Twins Reared Apart, have been particularly influential and debated.

Complications

Twin pregnancies carry higher risks than singletons, including preterm birth, low birth weight, and, in monochorionic pairs, twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome. Vanishing twin syndrome, in which one embryo is lost early, is also recognized.
