Lapiths

The Lapiths (Greek: Λαπίθαι, Lapíthai) were a legendary people in Greek mythology, said to inhabit the mountains and valleys of Thessaly, around Mount Pelion and the Peneus river. They were famed for their skill with horses and, above all, for their battle with the Centaurs, the Centauromachy.

Mythology

The Lapiths and Centaurs were kin, both descending from Ixion. At the wedding feast of the Lapith king Pirithous and Hippodamia, the drunken centaur Eurytion tried to carry off the bride, provoking a battle in which the Lapiths, helped by Theseus and the hero Caeneus, defeated the centaurs. The story became a favourite subject of Greek art, appearing on the Parthenon's south metopes and the west pediment at Olympia, where it symbolised Greek order triumphing over barbarism.

Figures

Lapith heroes named in the sources include Pirithous, Caeneus (born female as Caenis and transformed by Poseidon), Polypoetes, and the seer Mopsus, who sailed with the Argonauts.
