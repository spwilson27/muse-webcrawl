Binary form

Hero image: schematic of binary form — two complementary, usually repeated, sections A and B.

Binary form is a musical form in two related sections, both usually repeated. It was the dominant structure for short instrumental pieces and dance movements during the Baroque era and remained common in the Classical period. A typical binary movement divides into a first section (A) that moves from the tonic to a contrasting key — usually the dominant in major keys or the relative major in minor keys — and a second section (B) that works its way back to the tonic.

Types

Theorists distinguish several varieties. In simple binary form the second section presents new melodic material of roughly equal weight to the first. In rounded binary form the opening material returns, in whole or in part, at the end of the second section, producing a design that anticipates sonata form. Binary movements are also described as sectional if the first section ends with a clear perfect cadence in the new key, or continuous if it ends with a weaker cadence that drives onward. The terms "balanced binary" describe B sections that close with the same music that closed A, transposed into the tonic.

History and repertoire

Binary form arose from Renaissance dance music and became the standard plan for movements of the Baroque suite — allemandes, courantes, sarabandes, and gigues by composers such as Bach and Handel are almost invariably binary, each half repeated. The keyboard sonatas of Domenico Scarlatti stretched the form to its limits. In the Classical era, binary form persisted in minuets and other dances, and its "rounded" variant provided the germ from which sonata form developed.

Relation to other forms

Binary form contrasts with ternary form (ABA), in which a contrasting middle section is followed by a literal return of the first. When the B section of a binary movement ends with a restatement of A's opening, the result can blur into rounded binary or "incipient sonata" territory — a distinction that analysts draw by the extent and treatment of the returning material.
