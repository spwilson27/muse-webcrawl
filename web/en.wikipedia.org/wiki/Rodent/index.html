Rodent

Rodents (order Rodentia) are mammals characterised by a single pair of continuously growing incisors in each of the upper and lower jaws. About 40 percent of all mammal species are rodents; they are found in vast numbers on all continents except Antarctica, in habitats ranging from tundra to rainforest. Familiar rodents include mice, rats, squirrels, prairie dogs, porcupines, beavers, guinea pigs, hamsters and capybaras.

Characteristics

The incisors grow throughout life and are kept worn down by gnawing; behind them is a gap (the diastema) where canine teeth would be, allowing rodents to gnaw with the lips closed. Most rodents are small, robust quadrupeds, though the capybara can exceed 60 kg. Many species are social and communicate with complex vocalisations, including ultrasound.

Interactions with humans

Rodents are important in ecosystems as prey, seed dispersers and soil engineers. Some are major agricultural pests and vectors of disease, while others — the laboratory rat and mouse above all — are indispensable model organisms, and species such as the guinea pig are kept as pets and livestock.
