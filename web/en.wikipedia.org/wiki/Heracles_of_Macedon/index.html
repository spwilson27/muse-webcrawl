Heracles of Macedon (Ancient Greek: Ἡρακλῆς; 327–309 BC) was a putative illegitimate son of Alexander the Great and his mistress Barsine, daughter of the satrap Artabazus of Phrygia. Named after the mythical hero Heracles, the claimed ancestor of the Argead kings, he became a pawn in the Wars of the Diadochi and was murdered in 309 BC, extinguishing with his half-brother's death the royal line of Alexander.

Birth and early life

Alexander met Barsine at Damascus after the Battle of Issus in 333 BC, when she, the widow of Memnon of Rhodes, was captured in the Persian royal baggage. According to Plutarch, she was the only woman Alexander knew before his marriage to Roxane. Heracles was born in 327 BC. After Alexander's death in 323 BC the commander Nearchus proposed the boy as king, but the proposal found no support, and he was raised quietly in obscurity, largely at Pergamon.

Role in the Diadochi wars

In 310/309 BC the regent Polyperchon, seeking leverage against Cassander, brought the seventeen-year-old Heracles out of retirement, proclaimed him the true heir, and marched on Macedon with an army; Alexander's formidable grandmother-generation figure, Olympias, had earlier used similar claims. Cassander, however, bribed Polyperchon — offering him a command and lands in the Peloponnese — to abandon and murder the young man. Polyperchon had Heracles strangled, along with Barsine, after a banquet.

Historical assessment

Ancient sources disagree on Heracles' paternity: some doubted that Alexander acknowledged him at all, and modern historians remain divided between accepting Barsine's son as Alexander's or treating the claim as convenient Diadochi propaganda. His murder, together with the killing of Alexander IV, removed the last Argead claimants and cleared the way for the Successors to assume royal titles.
