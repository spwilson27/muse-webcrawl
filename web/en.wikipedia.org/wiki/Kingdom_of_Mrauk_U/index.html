The Kingdom of Mrauk U was an Arakanese kingdom that ruled the coast of what is now Rakhine State, Myanmar, and at its height parts of southeastern Bengal, from its founding in 1429/1430 until the Burmese conquest of 1785. Its capital, Mrauk U, was one of the great fortified port-cities of Asia, and the kingdom — the last independent Arakanese state — is remembered as Arakan's golden age, leaving a dense landscape of temples and fortifications around the modern town of Mrauk U.

History

Founding

The kingdom was founded by Narameikhla (Min Saw Mon), a prince of the earlier Arakanese kingdom of Launggyet, who after years of exile at the Bengal Sultanate's court in Gaur retook his throne with Bengali military help in 1429/1430 and founded Mrauk U as his new capital in 1430. In consequence, early Mrauk U kings acknowledged a loose Bengali suzerainty and some even used Muslim titles and struck coins with Bengali and Persian legends alongside Buddhist ones, governing a cosmopolitan trading state of Buddhists, Hindus, and Muslims.

Golden age

Under kings such as Min Bin (r. 1531–1554) Mrauk U reached its zenith. Min Bin built the great Shite-thaung Temple (1535–36) — a fortress-like shrine housing, by tradition, 80,000 Buddha images — ringed the capital with walls, lakes and moats, and built a powerful navy that dominated the Bay of Bengal coast. Arakanese fleets, often crewed with Portuguese mercenaries and freebooters based at Dianga and Chittagong, raided the Bengal delta and controlled the rice and slave trades. In 1599, allied with the Portuguese adventurer Filipe de Brito e Nicote at Syriam, Arakanese forces helped destroy the Toungoo power in Lower Burma, though de Brito's subsequent defiance ended with his defeat and execution by Anaukpetlun in 1613.

Decline and fall

From the mid-seventeenth century the kingdom weakened: the Mughals under Shaista Khan seized Chittagong in 1666, cutting Arakan off from its richest province, the slave-raiding economy collapsed, and succession disputes and court factions consumed the monarchy. In late 1784 the Konbaung king Bodawpaya of Burma invaded; Mrauk U fell on 2 January 1785, the last king, Maha Thammada, was deported to Amarapura, and the kingdom's palladium, the Mahamuni Buddha image, was carried off to Mandalay, ending more than three centuries of Arakanese independence.

Culture

Mrauk U's kings patronised Theravada Buddhism, crowding the capital's hills with stone temples (Shite-thaung, Htukkanthein, Koe-thaung, Andaw-thein) quite unlike the brick stupas of Bagan, while court literature flourished in Arakanese and Bengali; the Bengali poets Daulat Qazi and Alaol wrote at the Mrauk U court. The ruins of the city remain one of Myanmar's major archaeological sites.
