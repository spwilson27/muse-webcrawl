Isle of Dogs

Location: London Borough of Tower Hamlets, East London

Bounded by: River Thames on the east, south and west

Notable areas: Canary Wharf, Millwall, Cubitt Town, Island Gardens

Postcode district: E14

The Isle of Dogs is a large peninsula bounded on three sides by a meander of the River Thames in East London, England. It lies south of the commercial estates of Poplar and Limehouse and east of the City, and forms part of the London Borough of Tower Hamlets. Despite its name, it is not a true island: it became effectively insular after the construction of canals and docks. The origin of the name is uncertain — theories link it to royal kennels kept there, to "Isle of Ducks", or to corruption of an earlier personal name.

History

For centuries the area was marshy grazing land, known as Stepney Marsh, protected from flooding by river walls. In the 19th century it was transformed by the construction of the West India Docks (opened 1802) and Millwall Dock (1868), among the largest enclosed dock systems in the world; surrounding neighbourhoods such as Millwall and Cubitt Town grew up to house dock workers and industries including shipbuilding, engineering and food processing. The area was heavily bombed in the Blitz because of the docks.

Containerisation shifted trade downstream to Tilbury, and the docks closed in the 1970s and early 1980s, leaving dereliction and high unemployment. In 1981 the area became part of the London Docklands Development Corporation's enterprise zone.

Canary Wharf

The northern part of the peninsula was redeveloped from the late 1980s as Canary Wharf, one of London's two main financial districts alongside the City. Its first tower, One Canada Square (completed 1990), was for a time the tallest building in the United Kingdom. The district is served by the Jubilee line and the Docklands Light Railway. Redevelopment brought dramatic social change and tension with the established working-class community, a conflict symbolised by the 1993 election of a British National Party councillor in Millwall ward. Today the Isle of Dogs combines the office towers of Canary Wharf, extensive new housing, the surviving dock basins, and landmarks such as Island Gardens, with its celebrated view across the Thames to Greenwich.
