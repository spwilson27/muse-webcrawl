Floral emblem

A floral emblem is a flower or plant officially chosen to represent a country, state, province, or other region. Such emblems are adopted for their beauty, abundance in the region, cultural symbolism, or historical associations, and they appear in heraldry, ceremonies, and official imagery.

Examples

National floral emblems include the lotus of India and Egypt, the cherry blossom and chrysanthemum of Japan, the protea of South Africa, the golden wattle of Australia, and the maple leaf (a tree emblem) of Canada. In the United States, the rose is the national floral emblem, and every state has an official state flower, such as the California poppy and the Texas bluebonnet. English counties and regions are symbolised by flowers including the Tudor rose for England, the thistle for Scotland, and the daffodil and leek for Wales.

Selection

Emblems are chosen by legislation, proclamation, or public vote, and campaigns for adoption have sometimes involved schoolchildren and public referendums, as with several Australian state emblems chosen in the 20th century.
