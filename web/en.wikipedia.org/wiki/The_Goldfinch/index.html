The Goldfinch

Author: Donna Tartt
Published: September 23, 2013
Publisher: Little, Brown and Company
Awards: Pulitzer Prize for Fiction (2014)

The Goldfinch is a novel by the American author Donna Tartt. Published in 2013, it won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction in 2014. The novel is a coming-of-age story narrated by Theo Decker, a New York boy whose life is derailed when his mother is killed in a terrorist bombing at the Metropolitan Museum of Art and he impulsively steals Carel Fabritius's 1654 painting The Goldfinch from the wreckage.

Plot summary

Thirteen-year-old Theo survives the bombing that kills his mother and, amid the chaos, takes the small Dutch Golden Age painting of a chained goldfinch at the urging of a dying old man, Welty, whose niece Pippa Theo falls for. Theo is taken in by the wealthy Barbour family, then by his alcoholic father in Las Vegas, where he befriends the Ukrainian émigré Boris. Returning to New York, he works in the antiques shop of Welty's partner, Hobie, while secretly keeping the painting — a possession that haunts him for over a decade and drags him into the criminal art underworld, culminating in an Amsterdam shootout over the stolen picture.

Reception

The novel spent more than thirty weeks on the New York Times bestseller list and sold millions of copies. The Pulitzer board praised it as "a beautifully written coming-of-age novel with exquisitely drawn characters." Critical opinion was polarized: admirers compared its Dickensian sweep favourably to Tartt's earlier work, while some prominent critics dismissed it as bombastic or juvenile. The debate itself became a notable literary controversy.

Adaptation

A film adaptation directed by John Crowley, starring Ansel Elgort and Oakes Fegley, was released in 2019 to poor reviews and was a box-office failure.
