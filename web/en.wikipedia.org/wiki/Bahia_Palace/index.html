The Bahia Palace (Arabic: قصر الباهية; French: Palais de la Bahia) is a 19th-century palace and garden complex in Marrakesh, Morocco. Built for the grand vizier Si Musa and greatly enlarged by his son Ba Ahmed, it is considered a masterpiece of Moroccan architecture and is now a museum and major tourist attraction.

History

Si Musa, grand vizier to Sultan Muhammad IV, built the northern part of the palace in the 1860s. His son Ba Ahmed, chamberlain and later grand vizier to Sultan Abd al-Aziz, expanded it southwards in the 1890s, employing craftsmen from across Morocco — including architect Muhammad al-Mekki — and expropriating neighbouring houses. "Al-Bahia" means "the brilliant" or "the beautiful" and was also the name of Ba Ahmed's favourite wife. After his death in 1900 the palace was looted, and under the French protectorate it became the residence of the resident-general, Lyautey.

Description

The palace comprises some 150 rooms arranged around courtyards and gardens, including the great marble-paved Court of Honour, riad gardens planted with orange trees, and apartments decorated with carved and painted cedar wood, zellij tilework and stucco.
