Páramo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Páramo (Spanish: [ˈpaɾamo]) may refer to a variety of alpine tundra ecosystems located in the Andes Mountains of South America. Some ecologists describe the páramo broadly as "all high, tropical, montane vegetation above the continuous timberline". A narrower term classifies the páramo according to its regional placement in the northern Andes of Colombia and Ecuador.

Ecology

Páramos lie between about 3,100 and 5,000 metres elevation. Their vegetation is dominated by giant rosette plants such as Espeletia (frailejones), tussock grasses, cushion plants and shrubs, adapted to cold, wind, intense ultraviolet radiation and large daily temperature swings. The organic, water-retentive soils and abundant mosses make páramos crucial water sources: they supply much of the drinking water of Andean cities such as Bogotá and Quito.

Distribution

The largest páramo complex is in Colombia (including Sumapaz, considered the largest in the world), with extensive areas in Ecuador, Venezuela and northern Peru. Threats include grazing, burning, mining and climate change.
