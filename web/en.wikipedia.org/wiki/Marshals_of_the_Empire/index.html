Marshals of the Empire

The Marshals of the Empire (French: Maréchaux de l'Empire) were the highest-ranking military officers of the First French Empire, appointed by Napoleon Bonaparte after he proclaimed himself Emperor in 1804. The title revived the earlier dignity of Marshal of France, which had been abolished during the French Revolution.

Creation

On 18 May 1804, the day the Empire was proclaimed, Napoleon named eighteen generals as Marshals of the Empire. Fourteen were active commanders and four (Kellermann, Lefebvre, Pérignon, and Sérurier) were honorary appointments for distinguished senators. Over the course of the Empire a total of twenty-six men held the marshalate, each receiving the iconic baton decorated with golden eagles.

Legacy

The marshals formed a military aristocracy at the heart of the Napoleonic legend. Several became kings or princes, and their names are inscribed on boulevards around Paris and on the Arc de Triomphe.
