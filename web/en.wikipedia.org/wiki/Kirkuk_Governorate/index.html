Kirkuk Governorate

Kirkuk Governorate (Arabic: محافظة كركوك; Kurdish: Parêzgeha Kerkûkê; Turkish: Kerkük İli) is a governorate in northern Iraq. Its capital is the city of Kirkuk. The governorate is home to a mixed population of Kurds, Arabs, Turkmens and Assyrians, and its status is disputed between the federal government in Baghdad and the Kurdistan Regional Government.

Geography

The governorate lies at the foot of the Zagros foothills, between the Little Zab river to the north and the Diyala basin to the south. It contains some of Iraq's richest oilfields: the Kirkuk field, discovered in 1927 at Baba Gurgur, was one of the largest in the world and remains a mainstay of northern Iraqi production, piped to the Turkish port of Ceyhan.

History

Under Saddam Hussein's Ba'athist regime, Kirkuk was subjected to an "Arabisation" campaign in which Kurdish and Turkmen families were expelled and replaced with Arab settlers from the south. After 2003 many displaced families returned, and the 2005 Iraqi constitution's Article 140 promised a census and referendum on the governorate's status, which was never held. Kurdish Peshmerga forces took control of Kirkuk in June 2014 as the Iraqi army withdrew before the advance of the Islamic State; Iraqi federal forces retook the city in October 2017 following the Kurdish independence referendum.

Districts

The governorate is divided into four districts: Kirkuk, Daquq, Dibis and Hawija. Hawija district, largely Sunni Arab, was occupied by the Islamic State from 2014 until its liberation in 2017.
