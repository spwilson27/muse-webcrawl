Emirate of Jabal Shammar

The Emirate of Jabal Shammar (Arabic: إمارة جبل شمر), also known as the Emirate of Ha'il or the Rashidi Emirate, was a state in the northern part of the Arabian Peninsula, including Najd, existing from the mid-nineteenth century until 1921. Its capital was Ha'il, and it was ruled by the Al Rashid dynasty. At its height it controlled much of central Arabia, rivalling the Saudi states of Riyadh.

History

The emirate emerged as the power of the second Saudi state waned. Abdullah bin Rashid established Rashidi rule in Ha'il around 1836 and consolidated control over the Shammar tribal confederation. Under Muhammad bin Rashid (r. 1869–1897), the emirate reached its greatest extent, defeating the Saudis at the Battle of Mulayda in 1891 and occupying Riyadh.

During the First World War, Jabal Shammar aligned with the Ottoman Empire, while its Saudi rivals were backed by Britain. The last Rashidi emir, Muhammad bin Talal, surrendered Ha'il to Ibn Saud in November 1921, and the emirate's territory was absorbed into the Sultanate of Nejd, a step on the road to the unification of Saudi Arabia.
