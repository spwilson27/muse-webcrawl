Shear force

In solid mechanics, a shear force is the component of force acting parallel (tangential) to the cross-section of a structural member, as opposed to the normal force, which acts perpendicular to the section. In beam theory, the shear force at any point along a beam is the algebraic sum of the vertical forces acting on one side of that point; it is related to the bending moment by the differential relation dM/dx = V, meaning the shear is the rate of change of bending moment along the beam.

Shear stress and diagrams

Shear forces produce shear stresses distributed across the section; for a rectangular beam the horizontal shear stress follows a parabolic distribution, τ = VQ/(Ib), being greatest at the neutral axis and zero at the top and bottom faces. Engineers plot shear force and bending moment diagrams to find the locations of maximum internal loading when designing beams, shafts, and other structural elements. Concentrated loads cause abrupt jumps in the shear diagram, while uniformly distributed loads produce linearly varying shear.

Everyday meaning

More generally, shearing forces are unaligned forces pushing one part of a body in one direction and another part in the opposite direction — the action by which scissors cut, in which two blades apply opposed shear forces to the material between them. In fluids, shear is the gradient of velocity between adjacent layers, quantified through viscosity.
