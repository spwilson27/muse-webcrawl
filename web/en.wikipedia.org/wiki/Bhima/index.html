Bhima is a hero in the Hindu epic Mahabharata. The second of the five Pandava brothers, he is famed for his immense strength, his prodigious appetite, and his central role in the Kurukshetra War, where he fulfilled his vows against the Kauravas.

Birth and youth

Bhima was born to Queen Kunti through the wind god Vayu. Even as a child his strength surpassed that of his cousins the Kauravas, provoking the enmity of Duryodhana, who once tried to poison him. During the Pandavas' exile he slew the rakshasas Hidimba and Baka and the asura Jatasura; he married Hidimba's sister Hidimbi, by whom he had the son Ghatotkacha.

Exile and war

Disguised during the incognito year at King Virata's court, Bhima killed the commander Kichaka, who had molested Draupadi. After Draupadi's humiliation in the dice game, Bhima vowed to drink the blood of Duhshasana and to break Duryodhana's thigh — vows he fulfilled in the war. On the final day he fought Duryodhana in a mace duel, striking him below the waist on Krishna's hint, ending the war. He also killed all one hundred Kaurava brothers, including Duhshasana.

Later life

After the war Bhima served under his brother King Yudhishthira at Hastinapura and joined the Pandavas' final journey toward the Himalayas, falling second on the ascent. In Indonesian and Southeast Asian tradition Bhima (Bima) is one of the most popular wayang figures. See also Arjuna.
