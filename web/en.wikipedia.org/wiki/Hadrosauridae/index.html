Hadrosauridae is a family of ornithopod dinosaurs, commonly known as duck-billed dinosaurs for the flat, duck-bill appearance of the bones of their snouts. They were prevalent herbivores during the Late Cretaceous period and had a worldwide distribution in Asia, Europe, and North and South America. Hadrosaurs were among the last and most specialised ornithopods; they became extinct in the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago.

Description

Hadrosaurs ranged from about 3 metres to over 15 metres in length. Their flattened, elongated snouts were covered by a keratinous beak used for cropping vegetation, while the cheeks held extraordinary dental batteries — packed columns of hundreds of teeth with multiple replacement generations — capable of grinding tough plant material. They could walk bipedally or quadrupedally; the forelimbs ended in hoof-like fingers. Many hadrosaurs bore elaborate head crests: solid in saurolophines and hollow, resonating structures in lambeosaurines such as Parasaurolophus, which may have been used for visual display and vocalisation.

Palaeobiology

Fossil evidence — including "mummified" specimens such as the Edmontosaurus mummy AMNH 5060 and Dakota, skin impressions, and gut contents — has revealed details of their integument, diet (including conifers and crustaceans in some cases), and growth. Mass accumulations of hadrosaur bones, and nesting colonies of Maiasaura, indicate herding behaviour and parental care. Famously abundant, hadrosaurs were major prey for tyrannosaurids.

Classification

The family was named by Edward Drinker Cope in 1869. Major genera include Hadrosaurus (the type genus, from New Jersey), Edmontosaurus, Maiasaura, Corythosaurus, Lambeosaurus, Parasaurolophus, and the giant Asian form Shantungosaurus, one of the largest ornithischians known.
