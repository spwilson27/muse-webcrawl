1995 Quebec referendum

The referendum of October 30, 1995 was decided by less than one percentage point.

The 1995 Quebec referendum was the second referendum to ask voters in the Canadian province of Quebec whether Quebec should proclaim sovereignty and become an independent country, with the condition of offering a political and economic agreement to Canada. Held on October 30, 1995, it was defeated by the narrowest of margins: 50.58 percent voted "No" and 49.42 percent voted "Yes", on a turnout of 93.52 percent.

Background

The referendum followed the failure of the Meech Lake Accord (1990) and the Charlottetown Accord referendum (1992), which had sought constitutional recognition of Quebec as a "distinct society". The sovereigntist Parti Québécois, led by Premier Jacques Parizeau, won the 1994 provincial election promising a referendum within a year. Lucien Bouchard, leader of the federal Bloc Québécois, became the charismatic public face of the Yes campaign in its final weeks.

Campaign

The No side, led by Quebec Liberal leader Daniel Johnson Jr. and backed by Prime Minister Jean Chrétien, began far ahead but saw its lead collapse after Bouchard took over the Yes campaign and the question of a post-sovereignty "partnership" with Canada was emphasised. A massive Unity Rally of Canadians from across the country in Montreal on October 27 — later controversial for subsidised travel — was a dramatic late show of federalist support. On referendum night the lead changed hands repeatedly before the No victory by just 54,288 votes.

Aftermath

Parizeau resigned after making a controversial speech blaming "money and ethnic votes" for the defeat. The narrow result led Chrétien's government to refer the legality of unilateral secession to the Supreme Court of Canada, which ruled in 1998 that Quebec could not secede unilaterally but that a clear majority on a clear question would oblige negotiations; Parliament then passed the Clarity Act in 2000. Sovereignty support subsequently declined, and no third referendum has been held.
