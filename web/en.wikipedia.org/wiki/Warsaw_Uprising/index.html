The Warsaw Uprising (Polish: Powstanie Warszawskie) was a major World War II operation by the Polish underground resistance, led by the Polish Home Army (Armia Krajowa), to liberate Warsaw from German occupation. It occurred in the summer of 1944 as the Red Army approached the city, and was timed to coincide with the German retreat — part of the nationwide Operation Tempest. The uprising was the largest single military effort by any European resistance movement during the war.

Course of the uprising

The uprising began at "W-hour", 17:00 on 1 August 1944, under General Tadeusz Komorowski ("Bór"). The Home Army, some 40,000–50,000 strong but poorly armed, initially seized much of the city west of the Vistula. The Soviet Red Army, having reached the eastern bank by mid-September, halted its advance; Western Allied air supply drops from Italy proved costly and insufficient, and Soviet permission for shuttle bombing support was granted only late and in limited form.

German reinforcements — including SS units notorious for atrocities — retook the city district by district. In the Wola massacre of early August, tens of thousands of civilians were murdered. The Old Town fell in early September after its defenders evacuated through the sewers; the city centre and Żoliborz held out into late September. Home Army headquarters surrendered on 2 October 1944 after 63 days of fighting, having negotiated recognition of its soldiers as regular combatants entitled to prisoner-of-war status.

Aftermath

Between 150,000 and 200,000 Polish civilians were killed, mostly in mass executions, and some 700,000 were expelled from the city. In reprisal, German demolition detachments systematically burned and dynamited Warsaw: about 85 percent of the city was destroyed, more than in any other European capital during the war. The Red Army entered the ruins on 17 January 1945. The uprising's memory — suppressed under the post-war communist regime — became a central symbol of Polish national identity, commemorated annually in Warsaw at 17:00 on 1 August.
