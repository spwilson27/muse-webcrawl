Music of Spain

The music of Spain has a long history and has exerted wide influence on Western music. Spain's regional diversity produced distinct traditions: flamenco in Andalusia, the jota in Aragon, Galician gaita (bagpipe) music, Catalan sardana dances, and Basque folk music, among many others.

Early music

Medieval Spain was a crossroads of Christian, Muslim, and Jewish cultures. Notable repertoires include the Cantigas de Santa Maria compiled under Alfonso X of Castile and the music of the Sephardic Jews, later carried to the Mediterranean diaspora. Renaissance Spain produced masters of polyphony such as Tomás Luis de Victoria and vihuela composers like Luis de Milán.

Classical music

The zarzuela, a Spanish lyric-dramatic genre alternating speech and song, flourished from the 17th century and again in the 19th. Spanish composers Isaac Albéniz, Enrique Granados, Manuel de Falla, and Joaquín Rodrigo (composer of the Concierto de Aranjuez) drew on folk idioms. Guitarists Andrés Segovia and Narcís Yepes made the classical guitar a concert instrument; Pablo Casals, though Catalan-born, was a towering cellist. Opera stars include Plácido Domingo and Montserrat Caballé.

Flamenco

Flamenco, from Andalusia, combines cante (song), toque (guitar), baile (dance), and palmas (handclaps) and is Spain's most internationally recognized art form.

Popular music

Spain's modern scene includes copla stars of the Franco era, the rock scene of the movida madrileña in the 1980s, singer-songwriters such as Joan Manuel Serrat and Joaquín Sabina, pop act Mecano, and global successes such as Julio Iglesias, Enrique Iglesias, and Rosalía, whose work fuses flamenco with urban music.
