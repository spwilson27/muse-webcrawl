Life-cycle assessment

Life-cycle assessment
Also known asLife-cycle analysis, ecobalance, cradle-to-grave analysis
StandardISO 14040 / ISO 14044
GoalQuantify environmental impacts of a product, process or service across its whole life cycle

Life-cycle assessment (LCA), also known as life-cycle analysis, is a methodology for assessing the environmental impacts associated with all the stages of the life cycle of a commercial product, process, or service. For instance, in the case of a manufactured product, environmental impacts are assessed from raw material extraction and processing (the "cradle"), through the product's manufacture, distribution and use, to the recycling or final disposal of the materials composing it (the "grave").

An LCA study involves a thorough inventory of the energy and materials that are required across the industry value chain of the product, process or service, and calculates the corresponding emissions to the environment. LCA thus assesses cumulative potential environmental impacts. The aim is to document and improve the overall environmental profile of the product by serving as a holistic baseline upon which carbon footprints can be accurately compared.

Phases

The procedures of life-cycle assessment are part of the ISO 14000 environmental management standards: in ISO 14040:2006 and 14044:2006. An LCA consists of four main phases:

Goal and scope definition

The goal and scope define the product, process or service to be assessed; the functional unit, which is the basis for comparison; the system boundary; and the level of detail required.

Life-cycle inventory (LCI)

An inventory analysis compiles all the inputs (resources, energy) and outputs (emissions to air, water and soil, waste) of the product system. Data are collected for each unit process within the system boundary, often from databases such as ecoinvent.

Life-cycle impact assessment (LCIA)

The inventory results are assigned to impact categories — such as climate change, ozone depletion, eutrophication, acidification and human toxicity — and converted into category indicator results using characterisation factors, for example expressing greenhouse-gas emissions as kilograms of CO2 equivalents.

Interpretation

The results of the inventory and impact assessment are summarised and discussed as a basis for conclusions, recommendations and decision-making, with checks for completeness, sensitivity and consistency.

Types

Cradle-to-grave assessment covers the full life cycle from resource extraction to disposal. Cradle-to-gate studies end at the factory gate, before distribution to the consumer. Cradle-to-cradle is a variant in which the end-of-life disposal step is a recycling process that produces new, identical products. Well-to-wheel analysis is used for transport fuels and vehicles. Approaches also include economic input–output LCA (EIO-LCA) and hybrid LCA.

Uses

LCA is used by manufacturers for product development and improvement, by governments for strategic planning and public policy (for example eco-labelling schemes such as environmental product declarations), and in marketing to substantiate environmental claims. It underpins carbon footprinting and many corporate sustainability reports.

Criticism

LCA has been criticised for its dependence on assumptions, system-boundary choices and data quality, which can make results subjective and hard to compare between studies. Results can vary widely depending on the functional unit chosen and the weighting applied across impact categories.
