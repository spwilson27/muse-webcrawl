Appointments Clause

The Appointments Clause is a provision of Article Two of the United States Constitution, found in Section 2, Clause 2. It empowers the President of the United States to nominate, and with the advice and consent of the Senate, appoint public officials, including federal judges, ambassadors, Cabinet secretaries, and other officers of the United States.

Source: U.S. Constitution, Art. II, §2, cl. 2
Subject: Appointment of federal officers

Text and structure

The clause distinguishes principal officers, who must be appointed by the President with Senate confirmation, from inferior officers, whose appointment Congress may vest in the President alone, in the courts, or in the heads of departments. The President may also make recess appointments to fill vacancies that occur while the Senate is in recess, though the scope of that power has been limited by Supreme Court decisions.

Jurisprudence

Important cases interpreting the clause include Buckley v. Valeo, Morrison v. Olson, Edmond v. United States, NLRB v. Noel Canning, and Lucia v. SEC.
