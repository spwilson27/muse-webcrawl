Twelve-dish Christmas Eve supper

The twelve-dish Christmas Eve supper is a traditional festive meal in Central and Eastern European cultures, especially in Poland (Wigilia), Lithuania (Kūčios), Ukraine, Slovakia and Belarus. The meatless supper, served on Christmas Eve, consists of twelve dishes symbolising the twelve apostles or the twelve months of the year.

Traditions

The meal traditionally begins when the first star appears in the evening sky. An extra place is often set at the table for an unexpected guest, and hay may be placed under the tablecloth in memory of the manger. In many households a wafer (opłatek) is shared with wishes before the meal. Because the supper falls within the Advent fast, the dishes are traditionally meatless, though fish — especially carp and herring — is central.

Typical dishes

  • Barszcz (beetroot soup) with uszka dumplings
  • Mushroom soup
  • Carp, fried or in aspic
  • Herring in oil or cream
  • Pierogi with cabbage and mushrooms
  • Kutia, a sweet grain pudding
  • Kompot of dried fruit
  • Poppy seed cake (makowiec)
  • Sauerkraut with peas or beans
  • Stuffed cabbage or potato dishes, varying by region

Regional variations

Lithuanian Kūčios emphasises kūčiukai (small pastries) served with poppy seed milk, while Ukrainian Sviata Vecheria includes kutia as the ceremonial first dish. The exact composition of the twelve dishes varies widely by region and family custom.
