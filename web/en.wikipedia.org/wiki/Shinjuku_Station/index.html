Shinjuku Station (新宿駅, Shinjuku-eki) is a major railway station in Shinjuku, Tokyo, Japan. It is the main connecting hub for inter-city rail, commuter rail, and subway lines in western central Tokyo, and is recognised by Guinness World Records as the busiest transport hub in the world, with an average of about 3.6 million passengers passing through each day.

Layout

The station complex comprises the JR East station plus directly interconnected stations of the Odakyu Odawara Line, Keio Line, Tokyo Metro Marunouchi Line, Toei Shinjuku Line, and Toei Ōedo Line. There are well over 200 exits, and the underground passages extend so far that the station is sometimes called a "three-dimensional labyrinth" or "Shinjuku dungeon."

History

Shinjuku opened in 1885 on the Akabane–Shinagawa line, the precursor of today's Yamanote Line, when Shinjuku was still a quiet post town on the Kōshū Kaidō. Private railways arrived with the Keio (1915) and Odakyu (1927) lines, and after the Second World War the station grew with Tokyo's westward expansion into its present labyrinthine form. The Busta Shinjuku expressway bus terminal opened above the JR tracks in 2016.
