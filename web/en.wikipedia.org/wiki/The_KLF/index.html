The KLF (also known as the Justified Ancients of Mu Mu, the JAMs, the Timelords and by other names) were a British electronic band formed in London in 1987 by Bill Drummond (alias King Boy D) and Jimmy Cauty (alias Rockman Rock). Drawing on the mythos of the Illuminatus! trilogy and situationist ideas, they fused sample-heavy dance music with elaborate publicity stunts.

Career

Beginning with sample-collage records as the JAMs and the 1988 novelty hit "Doctorin' the Tardis" (as the Timelords, number one in the UK), the KLF pioneered "stadium house" with international hits including "What Time Is Love?", "3 a.m. Eternal", "Last Train to Trancentral" and "Justified & Ancient". Their album The White Room (1991) was among the best-selling of the year, and the ambient album Chill Out (1990) is considered a landmark of the genre.

Exit and aftermath

In May 1992, shortly after a chaotic BRIT Awards performance in which Drummond fired blanks from a machine gun over the audience, the KLF announced their immediate retirement from the music industry and deleted their back catalogue. As the K Foundation, Drummond and Cauty staged art actions culminating in 1994 in the burning of one million pounds sterling on the island of Jura — an act they never explained. A 23-year moratorium on the project ended in 2017 with the publication of the novel 2023: A Trilogy and related events.
