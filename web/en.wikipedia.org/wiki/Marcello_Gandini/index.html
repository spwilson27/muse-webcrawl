Marcello Gandini (1938–2024) was an Italian car designer, widely regarded as one of the greatest automobile designers of the 20th century. As chief designer at Bertone, he created iconic wedge-shaped sports cars that defined an era.

Career at Bertone

Gandini joined Bertone in 1965 and quickly designed the Lamborghini Miura (1966), whose transverse mid-engine layout and curvaceous body made it the archetypal supercar. He followed with the Marzal and Alfa Romeo Montreal concepts.

Signature designs

His most radical works include the Lancia Stratos Zero concept (1970), the Lamborghini Countach (1974) with its scissor doors, the Lancia Stratos HF rally car, the Lamborghini Diablo, Bugatti EB 110, Maserati Quattroporte IV and the first-generation BMW 5 Series (E12). He pioneered the sharp-edged "wedge" profile.

Later work

After leaving Bertone in 1979 he worked independently on vehicles ranging from the Renault 5 Turbo and Supercinq to trucks and helicopters, remaining active in design into his eighties.
