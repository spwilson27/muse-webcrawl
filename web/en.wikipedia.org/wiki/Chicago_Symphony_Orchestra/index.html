Overview

The Chicago Symphony Orchestra (CSO) is an American symphony orchestra based in Chicago, Illinois. Founded in 1891 by its first music director, Theodore Thomas, it is consistently ranked among the finest orchestras in the world and is one of the United States' 'Big Five' orchestras. It performs in Orchestra Hall at Symphony Center on Michigan Avenue and in summer at the Ravinia Festival.

History

Thomas led the orchestra until his death in 1905; successors included Frederick Stock, whose 37-year tenure was among the longest of any American conductor, and later Désiré Defauw, Rafael Kubelík, Jean Martinon, Fritz Reiner, Georg Solti, Daniel Barenboim and Riccardo Muti. Reiner's and Solti's recordings established the orchestra's international reputation, particularly for its powerful brass section.

Recordings and tours

The CSO has won numerous Grammy Awards — more than sixty — and in 2007 launched its own label, CSO Resound. It was the first American orchestra to establish a regular radio broadcast series, and its extensive touring has included landmark visits to Europe, Asia and the former Soviet Union.
