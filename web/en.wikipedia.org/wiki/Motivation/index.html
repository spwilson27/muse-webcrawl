Motivation

Motivation is an internal state that propels individuals to engage in goal-directed behaviour. It is often understood as a force that explains why people or other animals initiate, continue, or terminate a certain behaviour at a particular time. Motivational states are characterised by the desire to bring about a goal, and their strength varies between individuals and within an individual over time.

Motivation directs and energises behaviour toward goals, from basic needs to long-term ambitions.

Types

A central distinction is between intrinsic motivation, doing an activity for its inherent satisfaction, and extrinsic motivation, doing it for an external reward or to avoid punishment. Research in self-determination theory suggests that intrinsic motivation flourishes when the basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness are satisfied, and that expected tangible rewards can sometimes undermine intrinsic interest.

Major theories

  • Drive theory: behaviour is motivated by the need to reduce internal drives such as hunger and thirst and maintain homeostasis.
  • Maslow's hierarchy of needs: Abraham Maslow proposed that needs are arranged from physiological and safety needs up to belonging, esteem, and self-actualization.
  • Expectancy theory: Victor Vroom held that motivation depends on expectancy (effort will produce performance), instrumentality (performance will produce outcomes), and valence (the value of those outcomes).
  • Goal-setting theory: specific, challenging goals with feedback produce higher performance than vague goals.
  • Self-determination theory: Edward Deci and Richard Ryan emphasised autonomy, competence, and relatedness as the basis of sustained motivation.

Neuroscience

Motivation is closely linked to the brain's reward system, particularly dopaminergic pathways running from the ventral tegmental area to the nucleus accumbens. Dopamine is associated less with pleasure itself than with "wanting" — the incentive salience that makes rewards attractive and effort worthwhile.

Applications

Motivation research informs education (student engagement), the workplace (incentives, job design), health behaviour (exercise and smoking cessation), and sport psychology. Loss of motivation, or avolition, is a symptom of depression and other mental disorders.
