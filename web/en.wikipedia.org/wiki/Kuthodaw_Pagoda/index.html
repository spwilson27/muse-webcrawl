Kuthodaw Pagoda (Burmese: ကုသိုလ်တော် ဘုရား) is a Buddhist stupa located in Mandalay, Burma (Myanmar), at the foot of Mandalay Hill. Built during the reign of King Mindon Min, founder of Mandalay, the pagoda complex is famous for housing what is popularly known as "the world's largest book": the entire Theravada Buddhist canon inscribed on 729 marble slabs, each housed in its own small whitewashed shrine, called kyauksa gu.

History

King Mindon, who founded Mandalay as his new capital in 1857, ordered the construction of the pagoda beginning in 1860 as part of the dedication of the royal city. Mindon was deeply concerned about the preservation of the Buddha's teachings, fearing they might be lost in an age of decline. He therefore commissioned the carving of the complete Tipitaka (Pali Canon) — the Vinaya Pitaka, Sutta Pitaka and Abhidhamma Pitaka — onto marble tablets. The carving work was completed in 1868. In 1871, Mindon convened the Fifth Buddhist Council at Mandalay, attended by some 2,400 monks, at which the text was recited and verified.

The marble slabs

Each of the 729 slabs is about 1.5 metres tall and just over a metre wide, and is sheltered by an individual stupa-like shrine arranged in neat rows around the central golden pagoda. A 730th slab records how the project was carried out. The slabs were originally gilded, with the lettering filled in gold; during the British colonial period the gold and other adornments were looted, and the inscriptions were later refilled with black ink. The site is a popular place of pilgrimage and merit-making; visitors can hire guides and make offerings of flowers and candles.

Central pagoda

The central stupa, about 57 metres (187 ft) high and modelled on the Shwezigon Pagoda in Bagan, rises above the rows of shrines. The complex also includes rest houses, pavilions and a small museum. Kuthodaw lies near other major Mandalay religious sites, including the Sandamuni Pagoda, which houses a further 1,774 marble slabs bearing commentaries on the canon, added later by King Mindon's successors.
