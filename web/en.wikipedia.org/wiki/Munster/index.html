Munster (Irish: An Mhumhain [ənˠ ˈwuːnʲ] or Cúige Mumhan) is one of the four provinces of Ireland, in the south of the island. It comprises the six counties of Clare, Cork, Kerry, Limerick, Tipperary and Waterford. It is the largest of Ireland's provinces by area, and its flag — three gold crowns on a blue field — derives from the arms of the medieval Lordship of Ireland and the old kings of Munster.

History

In the early Middle Ages Munster was ruled by the Eóganachta dynasty from the Rock of Cashel, rivals to the Uí Néill high kings at Tara. Brian Boru, of the Dál gCais of Clare, broke their monopoly and became High King of Ireland, dying at the Battle of Clontarf in 1014. The province was later dominated by the MacCarthy and O'Brien dynasties and, after the Norman invasion, by the FitzGerald Desmond earls, whose rebellion led to the Desmond Rebellions and the Munster Plantation of the 1580s.

Geography and cities

Munster contains Ireland's highest mountain, Carrauntoohil (1,038 m) in County Kerry's MacGillycuddy's Reeks, and its longest river, the Shannon, which flows through Limerick. Its principal cities are Cork, Limerick and Waterford — Ireland's oldest city, founded by Vikings in 914. The province is famed for its rugged Atlantic peninsulas (Dingle, Iveragh, Beara) and tourist towns such as Killarney.

Sport

The province is represented in rugby by Munster Rugby, twice European champions, based at Thomond Park in Limerick and Musgrave Park in Cork. In Gaelic games, Cork, Kerry, Tipperary, Limerick, Waterford and Clare compete for the Munster championships in hurling and Gaelic football, among the most fiercely contested in Ireland.
