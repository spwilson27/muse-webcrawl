Edenbridge is a town and civil parish in the Sevenoaks district of Kent, England, near the borders with Surrey and East Sussex. It lies on the flood plain of the River Eden, a tributary of the Medway, between the Weald and the North Downs, about 40 kilometres south of London.

History

The town's name derives from the Old English "Eadhelmsbrigge" — the bridge of Eadhelm — over the Eden; in medieval times it was written as Eadinbridge. The old town grew along a Roman road and around its bridge and parish church of St Peter and St Paul, parts of which date to the thirteenth century. The area was part of the Wealden iron industry, and later of tanning and milling along the river. The Old High Street retains many timber-framed medieval buildings. In the nineteenth century Edenbridge became known for cricket-ball making; the Duke family business in the adjoining village of Penshurst and local makers supplied balls for first-class cricket.

The town today

Edenbridge has two railway stations: Edenbridge station on the Redhill–Tonbridge line, and Edenbridge Town on the line from East Croydon to Uckfield, making it a commuter town for London. The Eden Valley Museum, housed in a medieval farmhouse on the High Street, presents local history. The town holds a bonfire celebration each November associated with the Edenbridge Bonfire Society, noted for its large effigies. Nearby attractions include Hever Castle, childhood home of Anne Boleyn, and Chartwell, the home of Winston Churchill, a few kilometres to the north.
