Paramount Records

Founded1917
FounderWisconsin Chair Company
GenreJazz, blues, country
LocationGrafton, Wisconsin, United States

Paramount Records was an American record label known for its recordings of jazz and blues in the 1920s and early 1930s, including such artists as Ma Rainey, Blind Lemon Jefferson, and Charley Patton. It was founded in 1917 by the Wisconsin Chair Company, a furniture manufacturer based in Port Washington, Wisconsin.

History

Paramount began as a sideline of the chair company, which made phonograph cabinets. Its pressing plant in Grafton, Wisconsin, became famous for its "race records" series marketed to African American audiences. The label's advertisements, some illustrated by underground cartoonists decades later, became iconic.

Legacy

Paramount ceased operations in 1932 during the Great Depression. Its catalogue was later revived and reissued; in 2013 and 2014, Jack White's Third Man Records and Revenant Records released lavish box sets of Paramount recordings, The Rise and Fall of Paramount Records.
