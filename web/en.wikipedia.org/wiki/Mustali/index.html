Musta'li

Musta'li (Arabic: مستعلي) is a branch of Isma'ilism that emerged from a succession dispute following the death of the Fatimid caliph-imam al-Mustansir Billah in 1094. The Musta'lis upheld the imamate of his younger son, al-Musta'li Billah, against the claim of his elder son Nizar, whose followers formed the rival Nizari branch.

History

Al-Mustansir had designated Nizar as successor, but the powerful vizier al-Afdal Shahanshah installed al-Musta'li on the throne in Cairo. Nizar's revolt in Alexandria was defeated and he was executed in 1095. The Musta'li cause was upheld by the Fatimid state in Egypt and by the Sulayhid dynasty of Yemen, while the Assassins of Alamut in Persia sided with Nizar.

Tayyibi and Hafizi branches

After the assassination of the caliph al-Amir (al-Musta'li's son) in 1130, the Musta'lis split again. The Hafizi branch recognized al-Hafiz, while the Tayyibi branch—founded in Yemen around Queen Arwa al-Sulayhi—held that al-Amir's infant son al-Tayyib had gone into occultation. The Hafizi line ended with the fall of the Fatimids in 1171, and the Tayyibis are the sole surviving Musta'li community, led by a succession of da'i al-mutlaq ("absolute missionaries") acting on behalf of the hidden imam.

Communities today

Tayyibi Musta'li communities include the Dawoodi Bohras (the largest group), the Sulaymani Bohras, and the Alavi Bohras, centred mainly in Gujarat, India; Mumbai; Yemen; and a worldwide diaspora. Their religious life is led by the Da'i al-Mutlaq—the 53rd and current Dawoodi da'i is Mufaddal Saifuddin.
