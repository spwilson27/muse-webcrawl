Maggot Brain

Maggot Brain is the third studio album by the American funk rock band Funkadelic, released by Westbound Records in July 1971. It was the final album recorded by the original Funkadelic lineup. The album incorporates rock and psychedelic influences alongside funk, and its ten-minute title track — a largely improvised guitar solo by Eddie Hazel — is widely regarded as one of the greatest guitar performances in popular music.

Recording

According to legend, during the recording session bandleader George Clinton told guitarist Eddie Hazel to play the title track's solo as if he had been told his mother had died — then, midway, that she was still alive. The resulting single-take performance, drenched in fuzz and echo, anchors the album. Other tracks, including "Can You Get to That" (which samples an earlier Parliaments recording), "Hit It and Quit It" and the closing "Wars of Armageddon", mix social commentary with studio experimentation.

Reception and legacy

Though only a modest commercial success on release, Maggot Brain has grown steadily in stature and regularly appears on critics' lists of the greatest albums of all time, including Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums list. Pitchfork named the title track among the best songs of the 1970s. The album's cover — a woman's head emerging from the earth — is one of the most recognisable images of the P-Funk catalogue.
