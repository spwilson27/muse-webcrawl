Oloron-Sainte-Marie

Oloron-Sainte-Marie (Occitan: Auloron e Senta Maria) is a commune in the Pyrénées-Atlantiques department in south-western France, in the region of Nouvelle-Aquitaine. It is a subprefecture of the department and the capital of the arrondissement of Oloron-Sainte-Marie. The town lies at the confluence of the Gave d'Aspe and the Gave d'Ossau, which join to form the Gave d'Oloron, at the foot of the Pyrenees.

History

Oloron was founded by the Romans in the 1st century BC on the road between Dax and the Somport pass. It was an important stop on the Via Tolosana, one of the pilgrimage routes to Santiago de Compostela. In the Middle Ages it became a centre of trade between Béarn and Spain. The separate settlement of Sainte-Marie, across the river around its cathedral, was long a rival; the two towns were administratively united in 1858.

Landmarks

The Cathedral of Sainte-Marie, begun in the 12th century, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Routes of Santiago de Compostela in France, and is notable for its carved Romanesque portal. The church of Sainte-Croix in Oloron dates from the 11th–12th centuries. The town is also known as France's historic centre for the manufacture of berets and espadrilles, and hosts a factory of the Lindt chocolate company.

Geography

Oloron-Sainte-Marie is a gateway to the Aspe and Ossau valleys of the Pyrenees. Its population is roughly 11,000. The climate is oceanic, strongly influenced by the nearby mountains, with high rainfall.
