Contact centre

A contact centre (also spelled contact center in American English) is a centralised office or facility used for receiving and transmitting a large volume of customer interactions by telephone, email, web chat, text messaging, and social media. It is an evolution of the call centre, differing in that it handles multiple communication channels rather than voice calls alone. Contact centres may be operated in-house by an organisation or outsourced to third-party providers, including offshore locations.

ChannelsVoice, email, chat, SMS, social media
Key technologiesACD, IVR, CRM systems, workforce management
ModelsIn-house, outsourced, virtual/home-based

Technology

Core technologies include automatic call distributors (ACDs) that route calls to agents, interactive voice response (IVR) systems, computer telephony integration with customer relationship management (CRM) databases, call recording, and analytics. Modern "omnichannel" platforms unify interactions across channels so an agent can see a customer's history in one interface. Cloud-based contact centre as a service (CCaaS) offerings have enabled remote and distributed agent workforces.

Operations

Performance is measured with metrics such as average handling time, first contact resolution, service level (for example, the proportion of calls answered within a target time), and customer satisfaction scores. Workforce management software forecasts demand and schedules staff accordingly. The industry has seen rapid adoption of artificial intelligence tools, including chatbots and agent-assist systems, alongside continuing debate about job quality and automation.
