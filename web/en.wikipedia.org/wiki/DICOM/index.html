DICOM (Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine) is the international standard for the communication and management of medical imaging information and related data. It defines both a file format and network protocols, enabling images from modalities such as CT, MRI, ultrasound and X-ray to be exchanged between scanners, archives (PACS), workstations and printers from different manufacturers.

History

DICOM grew out of the ACR-NEMA standards published by the American College of Radiology and the National Electrical Manufacturers Association in 1985 and 1988, in response to the difficulty of interoperating with proprietary formats. Version 3.0, released in 1993 and renamed DICOM, added network support and a vastly expanded object model; the standard has been continuously extended since, and is maintained by the DICOM Standards Committee under NEMA/MITA, published as NEMA PS3 / ISO 12052.

Format and services

A DICOM file combines image data with a header of data elements (identified by group/element tags) describing the patient, study, series and equipment, organised in a hierarchy of Patient → Study → Series → Instance. Pixel data may be stored uncompressed or compressed with schemes including JPEG, JPEG 2000 and JPEG-LS. Beyond storage, DICOM defines services for network query/retrieve, print, modality worklists, structured reporting, radiotherapy objects, waveforms and — with DICOMweb — RESTful access using WADO, QIDO and STOW services for web-based imaging.

Use

DICOM is implemented in virtually every medical imaging device and hospital PACS worldwide, making it one of the most widely deployed healthcare messaging standards. Viewing software ranges from vendor workstations to open-source tools such as 3D Slicer, Horos and Weasis.
