Nueva canción

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Nueva canción musicians revived folk instruments such as the quena and charango.

Nueva canción (Spanish for "new song") is a movement of folk-inspired music and social song that emerged in Latin America and the Iberian Peninsula in the 1960s and 1970s. Rooted in traditional folk music, it combined Andean and other Indigenous musical elements with literate, poetic lyrics and an explicit commitment to social justice, anti-imperialism and the rights of the poor. Closely related movements include Chile's Nueva Canción Chilena, Argentina's Movimiento del Nuevo Cancionero, Cuba's nueva trova and Catalonia's Nova Cançó.

Origins

The movement drew heavily on the folk-revival work of figures such as Violeta Parra in Chile and Atahualpa Yupanqui in Argentina, who collected and reworked rural traditional music and wrote songs about poverty and injustice. In the 1960s a generation of singer-songwriters — Víctor Jara, Violeta Parra, Mercedes Sosa, Silvio Rodríguez, Pablo Milanés, Daniel Viglietti, Alfredo Zitarrosa, Joan Manuel Serrat and others — brought the style to mass audiences, often using folk instruments such as the charango, quena, zampoña and bombo alongside the guitar.

Political role and repression

Nueva canción became the soundtrack of the Latin American left and of movements for land reform, literacy and Indigenous rights. After the 1973 Chilean coup d'état, the Pinochet dictatorship banned Andean instruments from the airwaves and persecuted the movement's musicians; Víctor Jara was tortured and murdered in the Estadio Chile days after the coup. Many artists went into exile, where their concerts sustained international solidarity movements. Songs such as "El pueblo unido jamás será vencido" (Sergio Ortega / Quilapayún) and "Gracias a la vida" (Violeta Parra) became worldwide anthems of resistance and hope.

Legacy

The movement reshaped Latin American popular music, influencing rock en español, nueva trova's successors and contemporary folk and protest music across the Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking world. In 2019 the Chilean protests revived many of its songs in the streets, and "El baile de los que sobran" by the rock band Los Prisioneros — heirs of the tradition — became an anthem of that movement.
