Childhood apraxia of speech

Childhood apraxia of speech (CAS) is a motor speech disorder that makes it difficult for children to speak. Children with this diagnosis generally have a much smaller speech sound repertoire than other children of their age, and their speech is typically inconsistent and difficult to understand.

Characteristics

Core features identified by the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association (ASHA) include inconsistent errors on consonants and vowels across repeated productions of the same word, lengthened and disrupted co-articulatory transitions between sounds and syllables, and inappropriate prosody, particularly in the realization of word and phrase stress. The difficulty lies in planning and programming the movements for speech, rather than in muscle weakness.

Causes

CAS can occur as part of known neurological or genetic conditions — mutations in the FOXP2 gene have been associated with speech apraxia in some families — or as a neurobehavioral disorder without a clear cause (idiopathic). It may also co-occur with other developmental disorders.

Treatment

Treatment is intensive, frequent, one-on-one speech-language therapy focused on motor learning principles, with many repetitions of speech movements and multisensory cues. Early intervention improves outcomes, though progress is often gradual.
