Scottish Football League

Founded: 1890
Folded: 2013 (merged into the SPFL)
Country: Scotland
Divisions: First, Second, Third (at dissolution)
Most championships: Rangers (top division, shared record)

The Scottish Football League (SFL) was the national association football league competition in Scotland from its founding in 1890 until 2013, when it merged with the Scottish Premier League to form the Scottish Professional Football League (SPFL). For most of its existence it was the senior league structure for Scottish clubs, operating promotion and relegation across multiple divisions.

History

The SFL was formed in 1890 by 11 clubs, following the example of the English Football League (1888) and the legalisation of professionalism in Scotland in 1893. A second division was added in 1893. The league suspended competition during both World Wars and expanded repeatedly over the 20th century, reaching a three-division, then four-division format in 1994. In 1998 the top clubs broke away to form the Scottish Premier League (SPL), leaving the SFL to run the First, Second, and Third Divisions. In June 2013, after years of discussion, SFL clubs voted to merge with the SPL into a single body, the SPFL, reuniting the Scottish league system under one organisation with a restructured pyramid.

Competitions

Besides the league championship, the SFL administered the Scottish League Cup for much of its history (the competition later passed to the SPFL), and the Scottish Challenge Cup for lower-division clubs. Its member clubs included long-established sides such as Queen's Park — the only amateur club in the senior leagues for much of the modern era — and community clubs across Scotland.
