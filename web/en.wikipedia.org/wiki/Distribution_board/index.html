Distribution board

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A distribution board (also known as a panelboard, breaker panel, or consumer unit) is a component of an electricity supply system that divides an electrical power feed into subsidiary circuits while providing a protective fuse or circuit breaker for each circuit, all in a common enclosure. Normally, a main switch and residual-current devices (RCDs) or residual current breakers with overcurrent protection (RCBOs) are also incorporated.

Terminology

In the United Kingdom, a distribution board designed for domestic use is called a consumer unit. In North America, the equivalent is called a panelboard or load center, and colloquially a "breaker box" or "fuse box". Larger commercial and industrial installations use multiple distribution boards fed from a main switchboard.

Design

Power enters the board from the supply cable and passes through a main switch that can isolate the whole installation. Internal busbars distribute power to the individual protective devices. Each circuit – lighting, socket outlets, cooker, immersion heater, and so on – has its own circuit breaker or fuse rated for the cable it feeds. Modern boards mount devices on a standard DIN rail and use miniature circuit breakers (MCBs) rather than rewirable fuses.

RCDs protect groups of circuits or the whole board against earth leakage, providing protection against electric shock and fire; modern practice increasingly fits individual RCBOs per circuit so that a fault trips only the affected circuit. Surge protection devices are also now commonly included. Boards are typically metal (in the UK, domestic consumer units have been required to be of non-combustible construction since 2016) and are installed by qualified electricians under wiring regulations such as BS 7671 in the UK or the National Electrical Code in the US.
