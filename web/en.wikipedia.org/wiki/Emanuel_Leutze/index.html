Emanuel Gottlieb Leutze (May 24, 1816 – July 18, 1868) was a German-American history painter best known for his 1851 painting Washington Crossing the Delaware, one of the most famous images of the American Revolutionary War.

Early life and training

Leutze was born in Schwäbisch Gmünd and emigrated with his family to the United States as a child, settling in Philadelphia. He returned to Germany in 1841 to study at the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf, becoming a leading figure of the Düsseldorf school of painting and supporting the liberal artists' association Malkasten during the revolutions of 1848.

Washington Crossing the Delaware

Painted in Düsseldorf in 1850–1851, the monumental canvas (378.5 × 647.7 cm) depicts General George Washington's crossing of the Delaware River on the night of 25–26 December 1776 before the Battle of Trenton. Leutze hoped the painting would encourage German reformers in their own revolutionary moment. A first version was damaged by fire; the surviving second version was exhibited across the United States to acclaim and later hung in the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York.

Later work

Leutze returned permanently to the United States in 1859. In 1861 he was commissioned to paint the mural Westward the Course of Empire Takes Its Way for the United States Capitol. Other major works include Washington Rallying the Troops at Monmouth and Mrs. Schuyler Burning Her Husband's Wheat Fields on the Approach of the British. He died in Washington, D.C., in 1868.
