Isma'ilism

ClassificationShia Islam
Named forIsma'il ibn Ja'far
BranchesNizari, Musta'li (Tayyibi, Hafizi), Druze (historical)

Isma'ilism is a branch of Shia Islam named after Isma'il ibn Ja'far, the eldest son of the sixth Shia Imam, Ja'far al-Sadiq. When Ja'far died in 765, a group of his followers held that the imamate had passed to Isma'il (who had predeceased his father) and through him to his son Muhammad ibn Isma'il, rather than to Isma'il's younger brother Musa al-Kazim, ancestor of the Twelver line.

History

Isma'ili missionaries (da'is) spread the movement widely in the 9th and 10th centuries, leading to the establishment of the Fatimid Caliphate in North Africa and Egypt (909–1171), which founded Cairo and al-Azhar. After the death of the Fatimid caliph al-Mustansir in 1094, the movement split over the succession into Nizari and Musta'li branches. The Nizaris, led in Persia by Hasan-i Sabbah from the fortress of Alamut, became famous in Crusader-era legend as the "Assassins".

Branches today

The largest living branch is the Nizari Isma'ilis, led by the Aga Khan, with communities in Central and South Asia, East Africa and the West. The Musta'li branch survives mainly as the Tayyibi Dawoodi, Sulaymani and Alavi Bohras in India and Yemen.
