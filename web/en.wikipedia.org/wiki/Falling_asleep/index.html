Sleep onset

Sleep onset is the transition from wakefulness into sleep. Sleep onset usually involves the transition from wakefulness through the first stage of non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep. The period of time it takes to fall asleep is called sleep onset latency, which in healthy adults is typically between 10 and 20 minutes.

Physiology

During sleep onset, brain wave patterns slow from the beta and alpha rhythms of wakefulness into the theta activity of stage N1 sleep. Muscle tone decreases, eye movements slow and roll, and awareness of the external environment fades. Brief involuntary muscle jerks, known as hypnic jerks, may occur at this transition, sometimes accompanied by a sensation of falling.

The hypnagogic state — the transitional state between wakefulness and sleep — may feature vivid imagery, fleeting thoughts and, occasionally, hypnagogic hallucinations.

Factors affecting sleep onset

Sleep onset is governed by homeostatic sleep pressure, which builds with time awake and is associated with the accumulation of adenosine, and by circadian rhythms driven by the suprachiasmatic nucleus. Caffeine, bright light, stress, irregular schedules and electronic device use can prolong sleep latency, while darkness, a cool room and consistent routines shorten it.

Disorders

Difficulty falling asleep is a hallmark of sleep-onset insomnia. In narcolepsy, sleep onset is abnormally rapid and may proceed directly into REM sleep, producing sleep-onset REM periods used in diagnosis via the Multiple Sleep Latency Test.
