Robert Duane Ballard (born June 30, 1942) is an American retired United States Navy officer and oceanographer, best known for discovering the wreck of the RMS Titanic in 1985. He also located the wrecks of the German battleship Bismarck, the aircraft carrier USS Yorktown, and John F. Kennedy's patrol boat PT-109.

Career

Ballard worked at the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution from the 1970s and took part in the first manned exploration of mid-ocean ridge hydrothermal vents in the submersible Alvin, including the 1977 Galápagos Rift expedition that discovered black smokers and chemosynthetic ecosystems. His search for Titanic was partly funded by a secret Navy mission to survey the wrecks of the submarines Thresher and Scorpion; using the debris-field search strategy and the towed camera sled Argo, his joint French-American expedition found the liner's wreck on September 1, 1985.

Exploration and education

Ballard founded the JASON Project for science education by telepresence and later the Ocean Exploration Trust, whose vessel E/V Nautilus streams live exploration. He holds a professorship at the University of Rhode Island's Graduate School of Oceanography.
