TANSTAAFL

TANSTAAFL is an acronym for "There ain't no such thing as a free lunch", a phrase popularized by science fiction writer Robert A. Heinlein in his 1966 novel The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress. The saying expresses the economic idea that everything has a cost, even goods and services that appear to be free — the costs are merely hidden or borne by someone else.

Origins

The phrase "there ain't no such thing as a free lunch" refers to the once-common American saloon practice of offering a "free lunch" to patrons who bought drinks; the food was typically salty, encouraging further drinking, and its cost was built into the price of the beverages. Print uses of the sentiment date to the 1930s and 1940s. Economist Milton Friedman further popularized the expression by using it as the title of his 1975 book There's No Such Thing as a Free Lunch.

In economics

In economics, the phrase is a shorthand for the concept of opportunity cost: choosing one course of action always means forgoing alternatives, and supposedly free goods are paid for by taxes, prices, or other mechanisms. The idea is related to the scarcity of resources and to the notion that trade-offs are unavoidable in public policy.

Use in other fields

The acronym and its variant TINSTAAFL are used in science and engineering — for instance, in physics to summarize aspects of thermodynamics, and in discussions of evolutionary biology to note that adaptations involve trade-offs. Author Robert A. Heinlein's version in The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress is frequently quoted in libertarian discourse.
