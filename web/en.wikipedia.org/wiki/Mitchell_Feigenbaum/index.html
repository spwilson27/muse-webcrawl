Mitchell Feigenbaum

Mitchell Jay Feigenbaum (December 19, 1944 – June 30, 2019) was an American mathematical physicist whose pioneering studies in chaos theory led to the discovery of the Feigenbaum constants.

Work

Working at Los Alamos National Laboratory in the 1970s, Feigenbaum studied period-doubling in the logistic map and found that the ratio of successive bifurcation intervals approaches a universal constant, δ ≈ 4.6692, the same for a wide class of functions. A second constant, α ≈ 2.5029, describes the scaling of the bifurcation structure. This universality showed that very different systems share the same route to chaos.

Life and awards

Feigenbaum studied at City College of New York and MIT, where he earned his PhD in 1970. He later worked at Rockefeller University. He received a MacArthur Fellowship in 1984 and the Wolf Prize in Physics in 1986.
