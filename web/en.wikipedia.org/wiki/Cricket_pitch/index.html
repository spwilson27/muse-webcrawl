Cricket pitch

The pitch is the central strip of the cricket field

In the game of cricket, the pitch is the rectangular central strip of the field between the two wickets. It is the surface on which the bowler bounces the ball towards the batsman, and its condition — hard or soft, grassy or bare, fast or slow — profoundly affects the contest between bat and ball.

Dimensions and markings

Under the Laws of Cricket, the pitch is 22 yards (20.12 m) long and 10 feet (3.05 m) wide. At each end stand the three stumps of the wicket, with painted creases: the bowling crease, the popping crease (4 feet in front of it, marking the batsman's safe ground), and the return creases. A "protected area" in the middle of the pitch, beginning 5 feet from each popping crease, must not be run on by bowlers in their follow-through or damaged by players, to keep the surface fair.

Preparation and behaviour

Turf pitches are mown, watered, and rolled over days or weeks before a match. A green, moist pitch assists seam and swing bowling; a dry, cracked pitch increasingly favours spin bowlers as a multi-day match wears on; a hard, true pitch favours batsmen. Pitches deteriorate over a match, which is why winning the toss and choosing whether to bat or bowl first is strategically important. Artificial and matting pitches are used at lower levels, and "drop-in" pitches, grown away from the ground, are used in some stadiums.

Pitch condition and the Laws

Umpires inspect the pitch and can intervene if it is dangerous or unfit for play; deliberate pitch tampering or damage is penalised, and in professional cricket governing bodies rate pitches after matches, with demerit points for poor or unfit surfaces.
