Apologia Pro Vita Sua

AuthorJohn Henry Newman
LanguageEnglish
GenreAutobiography, apologetics
Publication date1864

Apologia Pro Vita Sua (Latin: "A Defence of One's Life") is the classic spiritual autobiography of John Henry Newman, published in 1864. Written in response to a public attack by Charles Kingsley, who had questioned Newman's truthfulness as a Roman Catholic priest, the work traces the history of Newman's religious opinions from his youth through his conversion from Anglicanism to Catholicism in 1845.

Background

In January 1864, Kingsley wrote in Macmillan's Magazine that "truth, for its own sake, had never been a virtue with the Roman clergy," citing Newman as an example. After an exchange of letters, Newman published his reply in weekly pamphlet installments between April and June 1864, appending the correspondence with Kingsley.

Content

Rather than arguing abstract doctrine, Newman narrated the development of his own mind: his early Calvinism, his leadership of the Oxford Movement, his growing doubts about the Anglican via media, and his eventual reception into the Catholic Church. The work is notable for its candour and its argument that religious conversion can be a gradual, intellectually honest process.

Reception

The Apologia was an immediate success, restoring Newman's reputation with the British public and becoming a landmark of Victorian prose. It is frequently ranked among the greatest autobiographies in English literature and has remained in print ever since.
