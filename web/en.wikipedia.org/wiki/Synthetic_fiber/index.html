Synthetic fibers or synthetic fibres (in American English, fibers) are fibers made by humans through chemical synthesis, as opposed to natural fibers directly derived from living organisms. They are the result of extensive research by scientists to replicate naturally occurring animal and plant fibers. In general, synthetic fibers are created by extruding fiber-forming materials through spinnerets, forming a fiber; these are called synthetic or artificial fibers, and most are made from raw materials derived from petroleum.

History

The first successful fully synthetic fiber was nylon, developed by Wallace Carothers's team at DuPont and announced in 1938; nylon stockings went on sale in 1940 and the material was diverted to parachutes and other military uses during the Second World War. Polyester (developed in Britain as Terylene, and sold by DuPont as Dacron) followed in the 1950s, along with acrylic fibers; spandex (elastane) arrived in 1958. Polyester surpassed cotton as the world's most-used fiber in the early 2000s.

Properties and uses

Synthetic fibers are generally durable, cheap, easy to dye, and resistant to staining and wrinkling, but are less breathable than natural fibers and melt rather than char when burned. They are used in clothing, carpets, upholstery, ropes and industrial fabrics; high-performance synthetics such as aramids (Kevlar) and carbon fiber serve in body armour, aerospace and sporting goods.

Environmental issues

Synthetic textiles shed microplastic fibers in washing, a recognised source of microplastic pollution, and are derived mainly from fossil fuels. Recycling rates remain low, though chemically recycled polyester and bio-based synthetic fibers are growing areas of research.
