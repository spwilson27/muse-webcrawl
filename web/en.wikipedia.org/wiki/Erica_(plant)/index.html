Erica (plant)

Erica is a genus of roughly 857 species of flowering plants in the family Ericaceae, commonly known as heaths or, together with the closely related Calluna, as heathers. Most species are small evergreen shrubs with tiny needle-like leaves and masses of urn-shaped or bell-shaped flowers.

Distribution

The great majority of species—over 600—are endemic to the fynbos of South Africa's Cape Floristic Region. Other species occur in the Mediterranean basin, Macaronesia, and Atlantic and northern Europe, where species such as Erica tetralix (cross-leaved heath) and Erica cinerea (bell heather) are characteristic plants of heathland and moorland.

Cultivation

Many species and cultivars are grown as garden plants for their long flowering seasons, including the winter- and spring-flowering Erica carnea and Erica × darleyensis. Most require acidic, well-drained soil, though E. carnea tolerates some lime. Tree heath (Erica arborea) root burl is the source of briarwood used for smoking pipes.
