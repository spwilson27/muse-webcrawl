Abuja (UK: /əˈbuːdʒə/, US: /ɑːˈbuːdʒə/) is the capital and eighth-most-populous city of Nigeria. Situated in the middle of the country within the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), it is a planned city built mainly in the 1980s based on a master plan by International Planning Associates. It replaced Lagos, the country's most populous city, as the capital on 12 December 1991.

History

In 1976, the military government of Murtala Mohammed decreed that Nigeria's capital should move from Lagos — congested, and associated in many minds with one region and ethnic group — to a neutral, centrally located site. A panel selected the Abuja area, homeland of the Gbagyi people, and the Federal Capital Territory was carved out of surrounding states. Construction boomed through the 1980s under a master plan whose first phase laid out the central districts around Aso Rock, a prominent 400-metre monolith. The formal transfer of the capital took place on 12 December 1991 under President Ibrahim Babangida. Meanwhile, the older settlement of Abuja town in Niger State renamed itself Suleja, ceding the name to the new capital.

Geography and cityscape

Abuja lies among the rolling hills and savanna of Nigeria's Middle Belt. Its Phase 1 districts include the Central District, Maitama, Asokoro, Wuse and Garki, organised around wide avenues and the Three Arms Zone containing the Presidential Villa (Aso Villa), the National Assembly complex and the Supreme Court. Landmarks include the Nigerian National Mosque, the National Christian Centre (national ecumenical church), the National Stadium (Moshood Abiola National Stadium) and Millennium Tower.

Demographics and economy

Abuja is one of the fastest-growing cities in Africa, its population swollen by government employment, construction and services; much of the growth occurs in satellite towns outside the planned core, such as Kubwa, Lugbe and Nyanya. As seat of the federal government, the city hosts Nigeria's federal institutions, foreign embassies, the headquarters of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) and offices of many national and international organisations. Nnamdi Azikiwe International Airport serves the city, and a light-rail system, the Abuja Light Rail, opened in 2018.
