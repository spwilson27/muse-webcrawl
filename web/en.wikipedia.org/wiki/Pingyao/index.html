Pingyao

Pingyao, officially Pingyao Ancient City, is a walled city in central Shanxi province, China, famed for its importance in Chinese economic history and for its well-preserved Ming and Qing urban planning and architecture. The Ancient City of Ping Yao was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997.

History

Pingyao's history stretches back some 2,700 years. During the Ming dynasty its massive city wall was rebuilt (1370), and it retains much of its Ming–Qing layout. In the 19th century Pingyao became the financial centre of China: its draft banks, known as piaohao, pioneered a nationwide remittance system. Rishengchang, founded in 1823 and often called the first bank in China, had branches across the country and abroad before the system declined with the fall of the Qing dynasty.

Architecture

The city wall, about 6 kilometres in circumference with six gates and dozens of watchtowers, encloses streets lined with nearly 4,000 Ming and Qing residences, temples, and shops. Notable sites include the Temple of the City God, the Qingxu Temple, and the former Rishengchang bank museum.

Tourism

Since its World Heritage inscription, Pingyao has become a major domestic tourist destination. Conservation challenges include managing visitor numbers and the impact of tourism on residents of the ancient city.
