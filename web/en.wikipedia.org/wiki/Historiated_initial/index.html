Historiated initial

A historiated initial is an enlarged letter at the beginning of a paragraph or section of a medieval manuscript that contains an identifiable figure or narrative scene within its strokes or the space it encloses. The term derives from the Latin historia, "story" — the images literally tell a story related to the accompanying text. Historiated initials are a hallmark of medieval illumination, appearing from the early Middle Ages through the age of print, and are distinguished from merely decorated initials, inhabited initials (containing figures or animals not tied to a specific narrative), and zoomorphic initials formed from animal shapes.

Development and use

Early examples appear in Insular and Carolingian manuscripts; the device flourished in Romanesque Bibles and reached a peak of refinement in Gothic psalters, books of hours, and university texts of the 13th–15th centuries. The initial marked major textual divisions, and its picture often previewed the episode that followed — a psalm's author, a saint's martyrdom, or a scene from a chronicle — serving as visual navigation for the reader. Workshops divided the labour: a scribe left a gap, a rubricator added guide letters, and an illuminator painted the initial, often on a burnished gold ground.

Famous examples

Notable historiated initials include the great initials of the Winchester Bible, the portrait-initials of the St Albans Psalter, and the densely populated letters of Parisian books of hours. With the coming of printing, woodcut and metalcut initials imitated the form, and printers sometimes left spaces for initials to be painted in by hand in the traditional manner.
