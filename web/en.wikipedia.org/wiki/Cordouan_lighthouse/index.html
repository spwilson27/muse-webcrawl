Cordouan lighthouse

Coordinates: 45.586°N 1.173°W

Status: Active; UNESCO World Heritage Site (2021)

Cordouan lighthouse (French: Phare de Cordouan) is an active lighthouse located 7 kilometres (4.3 mi) at sea, near the mouth of the Gironde estuary in France. At a height of 67.5 metres (221 ft), it is the tenth-tallest traditional lighthouse in the world and the oldest lighthouse in France still in operation.

History

Small towers had stood on the islet since 880, and an early structure was built under Edward, the Black Prince, in the 1360s when the region was English. The present Renaissance tower was designed by the architect Louis de Foix, commissioned by Henri III of France in 1584; construction took 27 years. The lavishly decorated interior includes a chapel (the second floor), an ornate entrance hall and a royal apartment, earning it the nickname "the Versailles of lighthouses". In 1789 the engineer Joseph Teulère raised the tower and installed improved optics; the first Fresnel lens ever used in a lighthouse was installed here in 1823.

In 2021 the lighthouse was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as "The Lighthouse of Cordouan", recognised as a masterpiece of maritime signalling and Renaissance and Enlightenment engineering.
