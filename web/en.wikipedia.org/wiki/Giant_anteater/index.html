The giant anteater (Myrmecophaga tridactyla) is an insectivorous mammal native to Central and South America. It is one of four living species of anteaters, of which it is the only member of the genus Myrmecophaga. It is classified in the order Pilosa along with the sloths.

Description

The giant anteater can be identified by its large size, elongated muzzle, and long bushy tail. It has a total body length of 182 to 217 cm (72 to 85 in) and weighs 33 to 50 kg (73 to 110 lb) for females and 41 to 65 kg (90 to 143 lb) for males. The coat is generally greyish, brown or black and banded with white. The forelimbs are white with black bands and end in large claws, and it walks on its knuckles to protect the claws from wearing down.

Distribution and habitat

The giant anteater is native to Central and South America, ranging from Honduras south to northern Argentina. It inhabits multiple habitats including grassland, savanna, shrubland and rainforest. It is largely active during the day on unpopulated areas and at night in populated areas.

Diet and behaviour

The giant anteater feeds primarily on ants and termites, using its long, sticky tongue — which can protrude more than 60 cm (24 in) — to lap up insects. An individual may visit many nests each day and consume up to 30,000 insects. It is a mostly solitary animal except during mother–offspring relationships, aggressive interactions between males, and mating.

Conservation

The species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN, having been extirpated from many parts of its former range. Threats include habitat destruction, fire, and poaching for fur and bushmeat, and roadkill.
