The Canadair CL-215 (nicknamed the "Scooper") is a twin-engine amphibious aircraft designed and built by Canadair in Montreal, Canada. It was the first aircraft designed specifically for aerial firefighting, able to scoop up 5,455 litres (about 1,200 imperial gallons) of water from a lake or sea in roughly twelve seconds while skimming the surface, and then drop it on a forest fire.

Design and development

The CL-215 is a high-wing monoplane powered by two Pratt & Whitney R-2800 Double Wasp radial piston engines. Its boat-like hull, amphibious landing gear and corrosion-resistant structure allow operation from water or land. First flown in 1967, it entered service with the government of Quebec and the French Sécurité Civile in 1969.

Operational history

A total of 125 CL-215s were built between 1969 and 1990. They have served with fire services in Canada, France, Spain, Greece, Italy, Croatia and elsewhere, becoming a familiar yellow-and-red sight over Mediterranean summer wildfires. Many were later converted with turboprop engines as the CL-215T, and the type was developed into the improved Canadair CL-415.
