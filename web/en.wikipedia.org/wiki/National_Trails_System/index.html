National Trails System

The National Trails System is a network of scenic, historic and recreation trails in the United States, created by the National Trails System Act of 1968. The system is administered jointly by the National Park Service, the Bureau of Land Management and the US Forest Service.

Trail classes

The system comprises National Scenic Trails (such as the Appalachian Trail and Pacific Crest Trail), National Historic Trails (such as the Oregon Trail and Trail of Tears), National Recreation Trails, and connecting or side trails. It includes more than 1,300 recreation trails in all 50 states.

Scale

Together the scenic and historic trails alone extend for tens of thousands of miles across the country, protecting corridors of natural beauty and routes of historic significance.
