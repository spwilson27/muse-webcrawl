Five-paragraph essay

The five-paragraph essay is a format of essay writing taught in many schools in the United States and other English-speaking countries. It consists of an introductory paragraph containing a thesis statement, three body paragraphs that develop supporting points, and a concluding paragraph that restates the thesis. The format gives beginning writers a clear scaffold for organising an argument.

Structure

  1. Introduction. Opens with a hook, provides background, and ends with a thesis statement that usually previews the three main points.
  2. First body paragraph. Begins with a topic sentence stating the first point, followed by evidence, examples and analysis, and a concluding or transitional sentence.
  3. Second body paragraph. Develops the second point in the same pattern.
  4. Third body paragraph. Develops the third point.
  5. Conclusion. Restates the thesis in new words, summarises the three points, and ends with a broader closing thought.

In education

The five-paragraph essay is a mainstay of middle-school and high-school English classes and of standardised-test preparation, because timed exams reward a predictable structure that graders can score quickly. Many textbooks present the form with graphic organisers in which each paragraph corresponds to a box in a chart.

Criticism

Composition scholars frequently criticise the format as formulaic: it can teach students that essays exist only to fill a template, that arguments always have exactly three parts, and that the conclusion merely repeats the introduction. Defenders respond that it is a training structure rather than a finished style — a set of training wheels that novice writers discard once they can organise longer and more flexible pieces. The debate is a standard topic in the field of rhetoric and composition.
