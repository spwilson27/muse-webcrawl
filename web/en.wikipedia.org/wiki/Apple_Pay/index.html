Apple Pay

Apple Pay allows contactless payments using an iPhone or Apple Watch.

Apple Pay is a mobile payment service developed by Apple Inc. that allows users to make payments in person, in iOS apps, and on the web. It is supported on the iPhone, Apple Watch, iPad, Mac, and Vision Pro. It digitizes and can replace a credit or debit card chip and PIN transaction at a contactless-capable point-of-sale terminal. Apple Pay does not require Apple Pay–specific contactless payment terminals; it can work with any merchant that accepts contactless payments.

History

The service was announced on September 9, 2014, at Apple's iPhone 6 event, and released in the United States on October 20, 2014, with the iOS 8.1 update. It launched with the support of the three major payment networks — American Express, Mastercard, and Visa — and hundreds of issuing banks. It later expanded to dozens of countries and to transit systems, person-to-person payments via Apple Cash, and identity documents in Apple Wallet.

Security and privacy

Apple Pay uses near-field communication (NFC) together with a dedicated chip called the Secure Element to store encrypted payment information. When a card is added, the actual card number is neither stored on the device nor on Apple servers; instead, a unique Device Account Number is created and encrypted. Each transaction is authorized with a one-time dynamic security code, and purchases on iPhone are confirmed with Face ID, Touch ID, or the device passcode. Apple states that it does not track transaction details that can be tied back to the user.

Compatibility

In-store payments require an iPhone 6 or later, or an Apple Watch paired with an iPhone 5 or later. Availability varies by country and by issuing bank.
