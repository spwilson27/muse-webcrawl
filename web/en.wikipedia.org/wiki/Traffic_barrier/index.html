Traffic barrier

A traffic barrier is a structure installed along a road to prevent vehicles from leaving the carriageway, crossing into oncoming traffic, or striking roadside hazards. Barriers are a key element of road safety engineering and are designed to redirect errant vehicles at shallow angles while minimising injury to occupants.

Types

Guardrails (in Britain, crash barriers) are typically W-beam or thrie-beam steel rails mounted on posts; on impact the rail flattens against the posts and guides the vehicle along the barrier. Concrete barriers, such as the Jersey barrier profile, are used in medians and at roadworks; their sloped face lifts and redirects the vehicle. Cable barriers use high-tension steel wire ropes on weak posts, which capture and decelerate vehicles over a longer distance and are cheaper to repair. Movable barriers use a transfer machine to shift concrete segments, creating reversible lanes.

Design standards

In Europe, barriers are tested under EN 1317, which classifies containment levels and working width. In the United States, the AASHTO Manual for Assessing Safety Hardware (MASH) sets crash-test criteria. Terminals at barrier ends are specially designed — flared, buried, or fitted with crash cushions — because an unprotected end can spear a vehicle.

Motorcyclist safety

Exposed posts and rail edges can be hazardous to sliding motorcyclists, leading some jurisdictions to fit underrun protection rails beneath the main beam.
