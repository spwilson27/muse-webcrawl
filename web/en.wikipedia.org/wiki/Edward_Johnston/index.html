Edward Johnston, CBE (11 February 1872 – 26 November 1944) was a British craftsman, calligrapher and type designer who, together with Eric Gill, is credited with reviving the art of penmanship in the modern era. Born in Uruguay and raised in England, he taught at the Central School of Arts and Crafts and the Royal College of Art.

Work

Johnston's 1906 manual Writing & Illuminating & Lettering — based on his study of historical manuscripts and his analysis of the broad-edged pen — became the foundational text for generations of calligraphers. His most public legacy came from the London Underground: in 1913 Frank Pick commissioned him to design a distinctive sans-serif typeface for the system. The result, completed in 1916 as "Underground" (later Johnston's Railway Type, now Johnston), was a landmark humanist sans-serif based on classical proportions, and in 1917 Johnston redrew the Underground's "bullseye" into the modern roundel logo. The typeface remains in use, in a revised form (New Johnston), across Transport for London today. From 1906 Johnston lived at Ditchling in Sussex, centre of a community of craftspeople; his pupil Eric Gill was later his neighbour there.
