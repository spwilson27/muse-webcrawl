Republic P-47 Thunderbolt

The Republic P-47 Thunderbolt is a World War II-era American fighter aircraft produced by Republic Aviation from 1941 through 1945. The P-47 was one of the main United States Army Air Forces (USAAF) fighters of World War II and also served with other Allied air forces, including those of France, Britain and the Soviet Union. Mexican and Brazilian squadrons fighting alongside the USAAF also flew the P-47.

Design and development

The P-47 was designed around the powerful Pratt & Whitney R-2800 Double Wasp 18-cylinder radial engine, which was also used by two other US fighters of the era, the F6F Hellcat and the F4U Corsair. A large turbosupercharger in the rear fuselage gave the aircraft excellent high-altitude performance. The P-47 was designed to have eight .50 in (12.7 mm) M2 Browning machine guns, four in each wing — heavier armament than most contemporary fighters.

The Thunderbolt was the largest and heaviest single-engine fighter aircraft of the war, and one of the heaviest single-seat fighters ever built. Nicknamed the "Jug" (short for "Juggernaut"), it was effective as a medium-range escort fighter and high-altitude air-to-air fighter, but proved especially adept at ground attack in the fighter-bomber role, carrying bombs and rockets and able to absorb heavy battle damage and still return home.

Operational history

The P-47 entered service in Europe in April 1943. Initially criticised for its sluggish climb and turn, improved variants with paddle-blade propellers, water injection and "bubble-top" canopies made it one of the war's finest all-round fighters. Top-scoring American aces in Europe, such as Francis "Gabby" Gabreski, flew the type. After long-range escort work was increasingly taken over by the P-51 Mustang, Thunderbolts excelled in ground-attack missions following the Normandy landings. The P-47 also served in the Pacific, Italy and with Lend-Lease users.

Post-war, the type (redesignated F-47 in 1948) served in the Air National Guard and with numerous foreign air forces, the last operator, Peru, retiring it in 1966.
