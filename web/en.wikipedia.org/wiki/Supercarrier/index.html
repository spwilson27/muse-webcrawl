Supercarrier is an unofficial term for the largest type of aircraft carrier, typically those displacing over about 65,000 tonnes and operated principally by the United States Navy. The ten Nimitz-class and the newer Gerald R. Ford-class nuclear-powered carriers — each displacing around 100,000 tonnes and embarking some 75–90 aircraft — are the definitive examples, and remain the largest warships ever built.

History

The term arose in the late 1940s in American debates over the proposed USS United States, a huge flush-decked carrier intended to launch nuclear-armed bombers; her cancellation in 1949, days after her keel was laid, triggered the "Revolt of the Admirals". The concept was realised instead in the Korean War era: USS Forrestal, commissioned in 1955, introduced the angled flight deck, steam catapults and a displacement that dwarfed Second World War carriers. The Kitty Hawk class followed, and USS Enterprise (1961) became the first nuclear-powered carrier. The ten Nimitz-class ships, built from the 1970s to the 2000s, standardised the type; the Ford class adds electromagnetic catapults (EMALS), advanced arresting gear and greater electrical generation capacity.

Characteristics

Supercarriers are powered by two nuclear reactors driving four shafts, giving effectively unlimited range at speeds above 30 knots and a service life of around fifty years with one mid-life refuelling. A crew of roughly 3,200 ship's company plus about 2,500 air wing personnel makes them floating cities. Four catapults (steam on Nimitz class, electromagnetic on Ford class) and an angled landing area allow simultaneous launch and recovery of a full carrier air wing of strike fighters, electronic warfare aircraft, airborne early warning aircraft and helicopters.

Role and debate

Supercarriers are the centrepiece of US carrier strike groups and the principal instrument of American naval power projection. Their cost — over US$13 billion for the lead Ford-class ship — and their vulnerability to modern anti-ship ballistic and hypersonic missiles, particularly those of China, are perennial subjects of strategic debate. China's growing fleet of large carriers, including the Fujian with electromagnetic catapults, has extended the term's usage beyond the US Navy.
