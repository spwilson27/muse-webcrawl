Mary Jean "Lily" Tomlin (born September 1, 1939) is an American actress, comedian, writer, singer, and producer. Tomlin started her career in stand-up comedy and sketch comedy before transitioning her career to acting across stage and screen. In a career spanning over fifty years, she has received numerous accolades, including seven Emmy Awards, a Grammy Award, and two Tony Awards, as well as nominations for an Academy Award, a British Academy Film Award, and two Golden Globe Awards.

Career

Tomlin achieved national fame on the sketch show Rowan & Martin's Laugh-In (1969–1973), where her characters included the snorting telephone operator Ernestine and the precocious five-and-a-half-year-old Edith Ann. Her comedy albums, including This Is a Recording (1971), won a Grammy Award. She made her film debut in Robert Altman's Nashville (1975), receiving an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actress, and went on to star in The Late Show (1977), 9 to 5 (1980), All of Me (1984), Big Business (1988), Flirting with Disaster (1996) and I Heart Huckabees (2004).

On Broadway, her one-woman show The Search for Signs of Intelligent Life in the Universe (1985), written by her partner Jane Wagner, won her a Tony Award. From 2015 to 2022 she starred opposite Jane Fonda in the Netflix series Grace and Frankie, earning Emmy nominations. She received the Screen Actors Guild Life Achievement Award in 2017 and a Kennedy Center Honors in 2014.

Personal life

Tomlin met writer Jane Wagner in 1971; they collaborated throughout her career and married in 2013 after 42 years together. Tomlin has been a prominent supporter of feminist and LGBTQ causes.
