Émile Bernard

Émile Henri Bernard (28 April 1868 – 16 April 1941) was a French Post-Impressionist painter and writer, who developed the style of Cloisonnism together with Paul Gauguin and was a friend and correspondent of Vincent van Gogh.

Career

Expelled from the École des Beaux-Arts for insubordination, Bernard walked through Brittany and Normandy, arriving at Pont-Aven, where in 1888 he and Gauguin forged Synthetism — bold flat areas of colour enclosed by dark outlines, inspired by medieval stained glass and Japanese prints. His Breton Women in the Meadow (1888) is a manifesto of the style.

Later life

Bernard quarrelled with Gauguin over credit for the new style and turned toward more classical and religious art. He travelled in Italy, Spain, and Egypt, where he lived for a decade, before returning to France. His voluminous letters and memoirs remain valuable sources on the Post-Impressionist circle, though his claims to priority in the Pont-Aven innovations have been debated.
