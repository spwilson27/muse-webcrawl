Toolebuc Formation

The Toolebuc Formation is a geological formation in the Eromanga Basin of Queensland and adjacent states, Australia, deposited during the late Albian stage of the Early Cretaceous, roughly 104 million years ago, when a shallow epicontinental sea — the Eromanga Sea — covered much of inland Australia.

Lithology

The formation consists mainly of finely laminated, organic-rich oil shale, calcareous shale, siltstone and coccolith limestone. It reaches about 60 metres in thickness in places and is laterally extensive, forming a distinctive marker horizon across the basin. The high organic content reflects anoxic bottom waters during a global oceanic anoxic event.

Palaeontology

The Toolebuc is one of Australia's most important marine fossil units, famous for articulated ichthyosaurs (such as Platypterygius material from its equivalents) and, especially, the plesiosaur Kronosaurus queenslandicus and the beautifully preserved pliosaur and fish assemblages from around Richmond and Hughenden. Ammonites, inoceramid bivalves, teleost fish and occasional dinosaurs washed in from land also occur. Many fossils are preserved in limestone concretions split open by collectors.

Economic geology

As a rich oil shale, the Toolebuc has been assessed as a potential unconventional petroleum resource and also hosts elevated vanadium and molybdenum, targets of exploration projects near Julia Creek.
