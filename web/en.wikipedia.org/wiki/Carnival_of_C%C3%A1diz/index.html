Carnival of Cádiz

The Carnival of Cádiz (Spanish: Carnaval de Cádiz) is one of the best-known carnivals in Spain, held in the city of Cádiz, Andalusia. It is famous throughout Spain for its wit, satire and musical groups, and has been declared a Festival of International Tourist Interest. The whole city participates, especially during the two weekends of celebration, in the run-up to Lent.

History

Cádiz's carnival tradition dates back to the 16th century, influenced by the Venetian carnival through the city's trade links with Italy. During the Franco dictatorship, carnival celebrations were officially banned across Spain, but Cádiz kept the tradition alive surreptitiously, moving the festivities and disguising them under the name of "Fiestas Típicas Gaditanas" from 1949; the carnival was fully restored under its original name in 1977.

Musical groups

The heart of the carnival is its singing groups, which compete in the Concurso Oficial de Agrupaciones Carnavalescas at the Gran Teatro Falla. The main categories are the chirigotas, satirical groups of around twelve singers known for their humorous, topical songs; the comparsas, more serious and poetic groups in elaborate costume; the coros, large choirs of up to forty-five members who perform from floats; and the cuartetos, quartets performing comic sketches. Their lyrics comment satirically on politics and current events, and the carnival is renowned for its defence of freedom of expression.

Celebration

Beyond the official contest, thousands of people in costume fill the streets of the old town, especially on the first Saturday and during the closing weekend, singing and celebrating in the open air. Popular events include the Pregón (opening proclamation), the burning of the God Momo, and the final fireworks and the burning of the Bruja Piti over the bay.
