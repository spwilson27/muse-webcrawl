Monotherapy

Monotherapy is therapy using a single drug or treatment modality, in contrast to combination therapy, in which two or more agents are used together. The term is applied most often to pharmacotherapy, but can also describe the use of a single non-drug intervention such as psychotherapy or radiotherapy alone.

Applications

Monotherapy is common in the initial treatment of conditions such as hypertension, epilepsy, depression and many infections, where guidelines often recommend starting with one agent and adding others only if the response is inadequate. In oncology and HIV medicine, by contrast, combination regimens are usually preferred because monotherapy can allow resistant disease to emerge — historically, zidovudine monotherapy for HIV was abandoned in favour of combination antiretroviral therapy.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages of monotherapy include simpler dosing, fewer drug interactions, lower cost and easier attribution of side effects. Disadvantages include the risk of inadequate efficacy in severe disease and, in antimicrobial and anticancer treatment, the selection of drug-resistant organisms or tumour cells. Clinical trials frequently compare monotherapy against combination approaches to determine optimal strategies.
