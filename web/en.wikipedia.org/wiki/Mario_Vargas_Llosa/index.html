Jorge Mario Pedro Vargas Llosa (28 March 1936 – 13 April 2025) was a Peruvian novelist, journalist, essayist and politician. One of Latin America's most significant novelists and essayists, he was a central figure of the Latin American Boom of the 1960s and 1970s. In 2010 he won the Nobel Prize in Literature, "for his cartography of structures of power and his trenchant images of the individual's resistance, revolt, and defeat."

Life and career

Vargas Llosa was born in Arequipa and educated in Bolivia, Peru and at the Royal Military Academy Leoncio Prado in Lima, an experience that formed the basis of his breakthrough novel The Time of the Hero (1963). His second novel, The Green House (1966), and the vast Conversation in The Cathedral (1969) established his reputation. Later works include Aunt Julia and the Scriptwriter, The War of the End of the World, The Feast of the Goat and The Dream of the Celt.

Originally a supporter of the Cuban Revolution, he broke with the left over the Padilla affair in 1971 and moved towards classical liberalism. In 1990 he ran for the presidency of Peru as the candidate of the Democratic Front coalition, losing the run-off to Alberto Fujimori. He later took Spanish citizenship, was created Marqués de Vargas Llosa in 2011, and in 2023 was inducted into the Académie française, the first member never to have written a book in French.

Style and themes

His fiction is noted for structural experimentation — shifting narrators, interleaved dialogues and nonlinear time — and for its exploration of authoritarianism, corruption and the collision of public and private life. He died in Lima in April 2025, aged 89.
