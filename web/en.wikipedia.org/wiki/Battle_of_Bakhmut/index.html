Battle of Bakhmut

The Battle of Bakhmut was a protracted series of military engagements near the city of Bakhmut in Donetsk Oblast, Ukraine, during the Russo-Ukrainian War. Fighting for the city intensified from August 2022 and continued until May 2023, making it one of the longest and bloodiest battles of the war. Russian forces — led on the ground largely by the Wagner Group paramilitary organization, using assault units heavily recruited from convicts — gradually captured the city at enormous cost, while Ukrainian forces conducted a fighting withdrawal before abandoning the ruined city in late May 2023.

Background

After Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 and the capture of Sievierodonetsk and Lysychansk in the summer, Russian forces turned toward Bakhmut, a city of about 70,000 pre-war residents and a regional road junction. Its strategic value was debated, but the battle assumed major symbolic and political importance for both sides.

Battle

Wagner assault groups, many composed of prisoners promised pardons, attacked in waves behind heavy artillery fire, advancing block by block through the autumn and winter of 2022–23. Ukrainian defenders held the western districts and the flanks, inflicting heavy losses; the fighting was frequently compared to the attritional battles of the First World War. In May 2023, after capturing the last Ukrainian-held districts, Wagner announced the city's capture; Ukraine acknowledged losing the city while continuing to contest the flanks. The ruined city was almost entirely destroyed and depopulated.

Aftermath and significance

Bakhmut became emblematic of the war's attritional character. Western officials estimated Russian casualties in the tens of thousands, including a large share of Wagner's convict recruits. Weeks after the battle, Wagner chief Yevgeny Prigozhin launched a short-lived mutiny in June 2023, citing grievances against the Russian military leadership; he died in a plane crash two months later.
