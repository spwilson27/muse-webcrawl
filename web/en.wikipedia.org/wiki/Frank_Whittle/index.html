Frank Whittle

Born1 June 1907, Coventry, England
Died9 August 1996, Columbia, Maryland, US

Air Commodore Sir Frank Whittle (1 June 1907 – 9 August 1996) was an English engineer and Royal Air Force officer who invented the turbojet engine. Working largely independently, he patented the concept in 1930 and ran the first bench test of a jet engine in 1937. Hans von Ohain in Germany developed a similar engine independently; his powered the first jet flight in 1939, while Whittle's engine flew the Gloster E.28/39 on 15 May 1941.

Early career

Whittle joined the RAF as an apprentice in 1923 and became a pilot officer in 1928. His thesis at the RAF Central Flying School proposed high-altitude, high-speed flight using a gas turbine driving a compressor — the turbojet. The Air Ministry initially showed little interest, and his patent lapsed in 1935 when he could not afford the renewal fee.

Power Jets

In 1936 Whittle and partners founded Power Jets Ltd. The company's first experimental engine, the Whittle Unit, ran on 12 April 1937. Development led to the W.1 engine fitted to the Gloster E.28/39. Production engines were built by Rolls-Royce (which took over Power Jets' work and named its engines after rivers, beginning with the Welland) and powered the Gloster Meteor, the only Allied jet fighter to see action in the Second World War.

Later life

Whittle retired from the RAF in 1948 with the rank of air commodore and was knighted the same year. He later worked in the United States, becoming a research professor at the U.S. Naval Academy. He died in Maryland in 1996.
