The River Bure is a river in the county of Norfolk, England, and a major waterway of the Norfolk Broads. It rises near Melton Constable and flows south-eastwards for about 87 kilometres (54 mi) through Aylsham, Wroxham, Horning and Acle before entering the North Sea at Gorleston via Breydon Water and the River Yare estuary at Great Yarmouth.

Course

The Bure's source is near the village of Melton Constable. It passes Blickling Hall, the market town of Aylsham and the villages of Coltishall and Horstead before reaching Wroxham, often described as the "capital of the Broads". From Wroxham the river becomes navigable and tidal in its lower reaches, flowing past Horning, Ranworth (where it connects to Ranworth Broad), and South Walsham Broad. Below Acle it is joined by the River Thurne and the River Ant, its principal tributaries, before passing through Breydon Water to Great Yarmouth.

Navigation

The river is navigable from the sea as far as Coltishall, a distance of about 51 kilometres (31.7 mi), and historically carried wherries laden with grain, coal and other goods. Today navigation is dominated by pleasure craft: the Bure is one of the busiest rivers of the Broads cruising network, administered by the Broads Authority. The river is crossed by notable bridges at Wroxham, Acle and Great Yarmouth.

Ecology

Along with the other Broads rivers, the Bure supports rich wetland habitats including reedbeds, grazing marshes and the shallow lakes (broads) that give the area its name, many of them Sites of Special Scientific Interest. The lower valley is important for wintering wildfowl and breeding waders.
