Chipmunk

Chipmunks are small, striped rodents of the family Sciuridae, found mainly in North America, with one species — the Siberian chipmunk — native to Asia. They are recognised by the pale and dark stripes running along their backs and cheeks.

Habitat and behaviour

Chipmunks live in burrows and wooded areas, feeding on nuts, seeds, fruit, buds and occasionally insects and eggs. They carry food in large cheek pouches to their burrows, where they store it for winter. Unlike true hibernators such as groundhogs, chipmunks enter periods of torpor and wake periodically to eat from their caches.

Classification

Most chipmunks belong to the genus Neotamias, with the eastern chipmunk in Tamias and the Siberian chipmunk in Eutamias.
