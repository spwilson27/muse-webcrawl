Jacky Cheung Hok-yau (born 10 July 1961) is a Hong Kong singer and actor. One of the best-selling Chinese recording artists of all time, with more than 60 million records sold, he is widely regarded as one of the "Four Heavenly Kings" of Cantopop alongside Andy Lau, Aaron Kwok, and Leon Lai. Known for his powerful and emotive voice — earning him the title "God of Songs" — Cheung has sustained a career spanning four decades in both Cantonese and Mandarin pop music.

Career

Cheung began his career after winning the Amateur 18-Hong Kong district singing contest in 1984 with the song "Father Earth," leading to a contract with PolyGram Records. His 1985 debut album Smile was a major success. After a commercial slump in the late 1980s, he returned to form with albums such as Just Want to Spend Life with You (1992); his Mandarin album The Goodbye Kiss (1993) sold over four million copies across Asia and made him a superstar in the Mandarin-speaking market. He was named one of the world's best-selling artists by the IFPI in the mid-1990s and performed the lead role in the first major Chinese-language musical, Snow.Wolf.Lake (1997).

Concerts and records

Cheung is famous for his marathon concert tours. His "1/2 Century World Tour" (2010–2012) ran 146 shows, setting records for a Chinese artist, and his "A Classic Tour" (2016–2019) comprised 233 concerts, among the largest tours in music history. In 2023–2025 his "60+" tour celebrated his fifth decade in music.

Acting

As an actor, Cheung has appeared in more than 60 films, winning the Hong Kong Film Award for Best Supporting Actor for As Tears Go By (1988), Wong Kar-wai's directorial feature debut, and acclaim for performances in The Swordsman (1990) and July Rhapsody (2002). He was inducted into the Avenue of Stars in Hong Kong and continues to record and tour.
