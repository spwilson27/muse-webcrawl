Rodeo (from Spanish rodear, "to surround") is a competitive equestrian sport arising from the working practices of cattle herders in Spain, Mexico, and the American West. Cowboys tested everyday skills against one another at roundups; formal contests emerged in the late 19th century (Pecos, Texas, is often credited with an early competition in 1883, and Buffalo Bill's Wild West shows popularised the spectacle). Modern professional rodeo is centred in the United States and Canada, with variants in Latin America (such as Chilean rodeo) and Australia.

Events

  • Roughstock events — bareback bronc riding, saddle bronc riding, and bull riding, scored for an eight-second ride, half the points from the rider and half from the animal.
  • Timed events — steer wrestling (bulldogging), team roping, tie-down roping, steer roping, and barrel racing (predominantly women's).
  • Other — breakaway roping, goat tying, mutton busting for children, and rodeo clown/bullfighter performances.

Organisations and championships

The PRCA sanctions hundreds of rodeos; season standings culminate in the National Finals Rodeo, held in Las Vegas each December, where world champions are crowned in each event. Bull riding also has its own circuit through Professional Bull Riders (founded 1992). Women's competition is organised chiefly by the Women's Professional Rodeo Association.

Animal welfare debate

Rodeo is criticised by animal-protection groups over flank straps, spurs, and injuries to livestock; the industry responds with veterinary rules and notes the low injury rates and the value placed on bucking stock. Some jurisdictions restrict specific practices such as calf roping.
