The Sinai and Palestine Campaign was a series of battles in the Middle Eastern theatre of the First World War, fought between the British Empire (later joined by forces of the Arab Revolt) and the Ottoman Empire, supported by Germany. Beginning with an Ottoman raid on the Suez Canal in early 1915, the campaign ended with the Armistice of Mudros in October 1918, by which time British-led forces had conquered Palestine, Transjordan, Lebanon and Syria.

Defence of Egypt and the Sinai offensive, 1915–1916

An Ottoman force crossed the Sinai in January–February 1915 and briefly attacked the Suez Canal before being repulsed. The British Egyptian Expeditionary Force (EEF), under General Archibald Murray from 1916, then advanced methodically eastward across the Sinai, building a railway and water pipeline as it went. Ottoman attacks were defeated at Romani (August 1916), and by December 1916 the EEF had taken El Arish and won at Magdhaba; Rafa fell in January 1917, completing the reconquest of the Sinai.

Gaza and the fall of Jerusalem, 1917

Two British attacks on Gaza in March and April 1917 failed with heavy losses, and Murray was replaced by General Edmund Allenby. Reorganised and reinforced, the EEF broke the Gaza–Beersheba line in October–November 1917: the capture of Beersheba on 31 October — famous for the charge of the Australian 4th Light Horse Brigade — unhinged the Ottoman position, and after victories at Mughar Ridge and the Junction Station, Allenby entered Jerusalem on foot on 11 December 1917. In the east, Arab forces under Emir Faisal and T. E. Lawrence, operating from Aqaba (captured July 1917), raided the Hejaz railway and covered the EEF's right flank.

Megiddo and the end, 1918

After spring raids across the Jordan toward Amman and Es Salt, Allenby launched his final offensive in September 1918. In the Battle of Megiddo (19–25 September 1918), infantry broke through the coastal front while cavalry swept through the passes into the Esdraelon plain and air power destroyed retreating Ottoman columns in the Wadi Fara defile. The Ottoman armies in Palestine effectively ceased to exist; Damascus fell on 1 October and Aleppo on 25 October. The Armistice of Mudros, signed on 30 October 1918, ended Ottoman participation in the war.

Consequences

The campaign cost the EEF roughly 60,000 battle casualties, with disease — especially malaria — causing many more admissions. Its political legacy was immense: Britain administered Palestine and Transjordan under League of Nations mandates, France took Syria and Lebanon, and the conflicting wartime promises made to Arabs, Zionists and allies (the McMahon–Hussein Correspondence, the Sykes–Picot Agreement and the Balfour Declaration) shaped the modern Middle East.
