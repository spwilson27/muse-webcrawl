Pedanius Dioscorides (Greek: Πεδάνιος Διοσκουρίδης; c. 40–90 AD) was a Greek physician, pharmacologist and botanist from Anazarbus in Cilicia, Asia Minor. Practising in Rome in the time of Nero, he is famous for writing De materia medica, a five-volume encyclopedia of herbal medicine and related medicinal substances that remained the standard pharmacological reference in Europe and the Islamic world for more than 1,500 years.

Dioscorides as depicted in a medieval manuscript of De materia medica.

Life

Little is known of Dioscorides' life beyond his own preface, which says he had led a "soldier's life" — he appears to have served as a surgeon with the Roman army, giving him the opportunity to study plants and medicines across the empire, from Greece and Italy to Gaul, Spain and North Africa.

De materia medica

Written in Greek around 50–70 AD, De materia medica describes roughly 600 plants, 35 animal products and 90 minerals, with some 1,000 simple medicines and around 4,740 medicinal uses. Unlike earlier herbals it organised substances by their effects on the body rather than alphabetically, and it gave synonyms in several languages so readers could identify plants locally. The work was copied, translated into Latin, Syriac and Arabic, and lavishly illustrated; the most famous surviving copy is the Vienna Dioscorides, a Byzantine manuscript of c. 512 AD made for the princess Juliana Anicia. Its authority lasted into the Renaissance — print editions appeared from the late 15th century — and many of its plant names survive in modern botanical nomenclature; Linnaeus named the yam genus Dioscorea in his honour.
