Peritonitis

Peritonitis is inflammation of the peritoneum, the tissue that lines the inner wall of the abdomen and covers and supports most of the abdominal organs. It is usually caused by bacterial infection, either spreading from a perforated or diseased abdominal organ or occurring spontaneously in people with ascites due to liver cirrhosis. Peritonitis is a medical emergency.

Signs and symptoms

Typical features include severe, constant abdominal pain and tenderness, abdominal rigidity ("board-like" abdomen), fever, nausea, vomiting and an inability to pass stool or gas (ileus). Patients often lie still, since movement worsens the pain. Rebound tenderness and guarding are classic examination findings. Untreated, peritonitis leads to sepsis and shock.

Causes

Primary (spontaneous bacterial) peritonitis develops in ascitic fluid without an obvious intra-abdominal source, most often in cirrhosis. Secondary peritonitis follows perforation of the gastrointestinal tract — for example a perforated peptic ulcer, appendix or diverticulum — or follows abdominal surgery, trauma or pancreatitis. Peritoneal dialysis can also be complicated by peritonitis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is based on the clinical picture, blood tests and imaging; sampling of ascitic fluid showing a high neutrophil count confirms spontaneous bacterial peritonitis. Treatment involves antibiotics, intravenous fluids and urgent source control, usually emergency surgery for secondary peritonitis.
