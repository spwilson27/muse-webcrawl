Greater Tokyo Area

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Greater Tokyo Area is the most populous metropolitan area in the world, consisting of the Kantō region of Japan (including Tokyo Metropolis and the prefectures of Chiba, Kanagawa, and Saitama) as well as surrounding areas. Its population is roughly 37–38 million people.

There are several definitions of the area. The narrowest, Tokyo Metropolis itself, has about 14 million inhabitants. The "One Metropolis, Three Prefectures" definition (Tokyo plus Kanagawa, Chiba, and Saitama) covers around 36 million. The National Capital Region (Shuto-ken) adds Ibaraki, Tochigi, Gunma, and Yamanashi prefectures.

Geography

The metropolis spreads across the Kantō Plain, the largest plain in Japan, centred on Tokyo Bay. Major satellite cities include Yokohama (Japan's second-largest city), Kawasaki, Saitama City, Chiba, and Sagamihara. The area is served by the world's busiest railway network, with Shinjuku Station the busiest station on Earth.

Economy

Greater Tokyo is one of the largest metropolitan economies in the world, hosting the Tokyo Stock Exchange and the headquarters of a large share of Japan's major corporations. The region was designated one of the world's three "command centres" of the global economy, alongside New York and London.
