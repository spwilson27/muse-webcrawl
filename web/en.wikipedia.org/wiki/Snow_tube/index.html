Snow tube

A snow tube is a large inflatable ring, usually made of durable rubber or vinyl with a fabric cover, used for sliding down snow-covered slopes — an activity known as snow tubing. Closely analogous to a sled or toboggan but nearly impossible to steer, the snow tube spins and accelerates freely as it descends, which is central to its appeal and its risks.

Activity

Snow tubing requires no skill or specialized technique, making it a popular winter recreation for families. Many ski resorts and dedicated tubing parks operate groomed lanes with banks separating runs, conveyor or handle-tow lifts to return riders to the top, and staff-controlled start and runout zones. Riders may descend seated in the centre of the tube, lying on top of it, or linked together in groups. Variants include summer tubing on artificial surfaces and water tubing behind boats, though the snow version descends on gravity alone.

Safety

Because tubes offer no brakes or steering and can reach high speeds, collisions and runout-area accidents are the principal hazards; resorts mitigate them with lane design, spacing rules, and stop barriers. Riders are advised to use designated venues rather than open hills, never to tie tubes to vehicles, and to clear the runout promptly. Purpose-made commercial tubes with handles and reinforced bottoms are safer than improvised inner tubes, which can catch on obstacles or burst.
