Nouakchott is the capital and largest city of Mauritania. Situated on the Atlantic coast at the edge of the Sahara Desert, it grew from a small fishing village into a city of more than a million people within a few decades of Mauritanian independence in 1960.

History

Nouakchott was chosen as the capital of the new nation and laid out in 1958 to house about 15,000 people. Droughts in the 1970s and 1980s drove large-scale migration from the desert interior, causing explosive growth and extensive informal settlements on the city's sandy outskirts.

Geography and economy

The city is built on a flat coastal plain threatened by encroaching sand dunes and by flooding, as parts lie near or below sea level. Its economy centres on government, trade, the fishing port (Port de l'Amitié, built with Chinese assistance) and fish processing. Landmarks include the Grand Mosque (donated by Saudi Arabia), the National Museum and busy artisanal and camel markets. The University of Nouakchott was founded in 1981.
