Antiarrhythmic agents
UseTreatment of cardiac arrhythmias
ClassificationVaughan Williams classes I–IV

Antiarrhythmic agents are medications used to suppress abnormal rhythms of the heart (cardiac arrhythmias), such as atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation.

Classification

The Vaughan Williams classification divides agents by their primary mechanism: class I drugs block sodium channels (e.g. flecainide, lidocaine); class II are beta blockers; class III block potassium channels and prolong repolarisation (e.g. amiodarone, sotalol); class IV are calcium channel blockers (verapamil, diltiazem). Adenosine and digoxin are sometimes grouped as class V.

Risks

Antiarrhythmics can themselves provoke arrhythmias (proarrhythmia). The CAST trial showed that suppressing ventricular ectopy after myocardial infarction with class Ic drugs increased mortality, reshaping their use.
