The Argentine Regional Workers' Federation (Spanish: Federación Obrera Regional Argentina, FORA) is an Argentine anarchist trade union federation founded in 1901. It became the principal organisation of the Argentine labour movement in the first decades of the twentieth century.

History

Founded as the Argentine Workers' Federation, it adopted the FORA name in 1904, and at its fifth congress in 1905 endorsed anarchist communism. The FORA led major strikes, including the general strikes surrounding the Semana Trágica of 1919 and the Patagonia rebelde strikes of 1920–1922, which were violently repressed. In 1915 it split into the FORA of the V Congress (anarchist) and a syndicalist FORA of the IX Congress.

Later years

Repression under the Uriburu dictatorship after 1930 sharply reduced its strength, but the FORA continues to exist as an anarcho-syndicalist federation affiliated with the International Workers' Association.
