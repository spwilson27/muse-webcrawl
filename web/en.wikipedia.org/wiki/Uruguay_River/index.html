The Uruguay River (Spanish: Río Uruguay; Portuguese: Rio Uruguai) is a major river in South America. It flows from north to south for about 1,838 kilometres, forming first the border between Brazil and Argentina and then between Argentina and Uruguay, before joining the Paraná River to form the Río de la Plata estuary. The country of Uruguay takes its name from the river, whose Guaraní name is often interpreted as "river of the painted birds" or "river of the uru bird".

Course

The river begins in the Serra do Mar in southern Brazil at the confluence of the Canoas and Pelotas rivers. Its upper course runs westward through rapids in Rio Grande do Sul and Santa Catarina; the middle course marks the Brazil–Argentina frontier; and the lower course, between Argentina's Mesopotamia (Misiones, Corrientes, Entre Ríos) and Uruguay, widens and becomes navigable. Tributaries include the Ibicuí, the Cuareim (the boundary's source with Brazil) and the Negro, Uruguay's largest internal river, which joins near the mouth.

Use and disputes

The binational Salto Grande Dam (1979), shared by Argentina and Uruguay, is the river's largest hydroelectric work; Brazil operates dams on the upper course. Navigation is significant on the lower river, with ports at Paysandú, Fray Bentos, Concordia and Salto. The river was the subject of the Pulp Mills dispute between Argentina and Uruguay (2005–2010), over Uruguayan cellulose plants at Fray Bentos, which went to the International Court of Justice; the Court found Uruguay had breached procedural obligations but allowed the operating mill to continue.
