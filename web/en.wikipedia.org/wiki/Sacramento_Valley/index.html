Sacramento Valley

Location: Northern California, United States

Length: About 200 miles (320 km)

Principal river: Sacramento River

Largest city: Sacramento

The Sacramento Valley is the area of the Central Valley of California that lies north of the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta and is drained by the Sacramento River. It encompasses all or part of ten counties: Butte, Colusa, Glenn, El Dorado, Placer, Sacramento, Shasta, Sutter, Yolo and Yuba.

Geography

The valley floor is a broad, flat alluvial plain framed by the Sierra Nevada to the east, the Coast Ranges to the west, and the Cascade Range and Klamath Mountains to the north. The Sacramento River, California's largest, flows south through the valley joined by tributaries including the Feather, American, Pit and McCloud rivers. Before flood-control works, the river seasonally inundated vast areas; levees, bypasses and Shasta Dam now regulate its flow.

Economy and settlement

The valley is one of the world's great agricultural regions, producing rice, almonds, walnuts, tomatoes, prunes and sunflower seed, supported by irrigation from federal and state water projects. Sacramento, the state capital, anchors the metropolitan south; other centres include Chico, Redding, Marysville and Woodland. The valley's history includes the homelands of Maidu, Miwok, Wintun and other Native peoples, the Mexican-era ranchos, Sutter's Fort, and the Gold Rush of 1849, which transformed settlement and the river itself through hydraulic mining.
