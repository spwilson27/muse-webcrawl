The Alamo

The Alamo is a historic Spanish mission and fortress compound in San Antonio, Texas, founded in 1718 as Mission San Antonio de Valero. It is famous as the site of the Battle of the Alamo (23 February – 6 March 1836) during the Texas Revolution, when a small Texian garrison was besieged and overwhelmed by Mexican troops under General Santa Anna.

The battle

Some 180–260 defenders — including William B. Travis, James Bowie and Davy Crockett — held the mission for thirteen days before a final assault killed nearly all of them. The cry "Remember the Alamo!" became a rallying slogan, and six weeks later Texian forces under Sam Houston defeated Santa Anna at San Jacinto, securing Texan independence.

Today

The chapel and the Long Barrack are preserved as a museum and shrine administered by the Texas General Land Office, drawing over a million visitors a year. The Alamo and the other San Antonio missions were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015.
