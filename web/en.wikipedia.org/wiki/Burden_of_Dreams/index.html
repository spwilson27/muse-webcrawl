Burden of Dreams is a 1982 American documentary film directed by Les Blank. It chronicles the chaotic production of Werner Herzog's film Fitzcarraldo (1982) in the Peruvian Amazon rainforest, and is widely regarded as one of the greatest documentaries about filmmaking ever made.

Content

Fitzcarraldo tells the story of an Irish rubber baron who dreams of building an opera house in the jungle and has a steamship hauled over a mountain between two rivers. Herzog insisted on recreating this feat practically, without special effects: a 320-ton steamship was dragged over a steep hill between the Camisea and Urubamba river systems by hundreds of laborers.

Blank's film documents the production's many calamities: casting changes (Jason Robards and Mick Jagger were replaced by Klaus Kinski), a border conflict between Peru and Ecuador that forced a relocation, plane crashes, a camp destroyed, crew members injured, and Herzog's increasingly fraught relationship with the local Indigenous peoples, some of whom were hired as laborers and extras. The film includes Herzog's famous monologues about the jungle, which he describes as a place of "overwhelming and collective murder," while birds "are in misery."

Reception

Burden of Dreams won the BAFTA Award for Best Documentary and the Grand Prize at the San Francisco International Film Festival. Critics have praised it as a portrait of artistic obsession that in some ways surpasses the film it documents. Excerpts of Herzog's speeches in the documentary have been widely quoted and sampled in later works.
