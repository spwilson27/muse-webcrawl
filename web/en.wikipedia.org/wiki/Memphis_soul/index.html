Memphis soul, also known as Memphis sound, is a style of soul music that emerged in Memphis, Tennessee in the 1960s and early 1970s. Characterised by a gritty, earthy feel, prominent horn sections, organ, and a tight rhythm section laying down languid, funky grooves, it centred on the Stax and Hi Records labels and their integrated house bands — a notable feature in the segregated American South.

Sound and style

Compared with the polished productions of Detroit's Motown, Memphis soul was rawer and more gospel- and blues-inflected: melodic bass lines, "chick" rhythm guitar, Hammond organ, and punchy horn arrangements answered impassioned vocals. Booker T. & the M.G.'s, with the Mar-Keys' horn players as the Memphis Horns, provided the backing for much of the Stax catalogue, while producer Willie Mitchell crafted a smoother, string-tinged variant at Hi Records.

Key artists and recordings

Stax/Volt artists included Otis Redding ("(Sittin' On) The Dock of the Bay"), Sam & Dave ("Soul Man", "Hold On, I'm Comin'"), Rufus and Carla Thomas, Eddie Floyd, Wilson Pickett (recording in Memphis), and Isaac Hayes, whose Hot Buttered Soul (1969) and the Shaft soundtrack (1971) expanded the style into lush orchestral funk. At Hi, Al Green, Ann Peebles, Syl Johnson and O. V. Wright produced a string of hits including "Let's Stay Together" and "Tired of Being Alone".

Decline and legacy

Stax collapsed in 1975 amid financial and legal troubles, and changing tastes toward disco and funk ended the classic era. Memphis soul's grooves were heavily sampled in hip hop and continue to influence contemporary R&B and retro-soul artists; the Stax Museum of American Soul Music now stands on the original studio site in Soulsville, USA.
