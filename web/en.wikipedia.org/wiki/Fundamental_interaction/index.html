Fundamental interaction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Table comparing the four fundamental interactions: their carriers, strengths, ranges and typical effects]
The four fundamental interactions compared.

In physics, the fundamental interactions are the interactions that do not appear to be reducible to more basic interactions. There are four known: gravity, electromagnetism, the strong interaction and the weak interaction. Their strengths, ranges and carriers differ enormously — from gravity, the weakest but longest-ranged, to the strong force, some 10³⁸ times stronger than gravity but confined to nuclear distances.

Physicists seek to unify them: electromagnetism and the weak interaction are already described by the unified Electroweak interaction; grand unified theories would merge the strong force too; and a theory of everything would include gravity — described by General relativity rather than a Quantum field theory — the outstanding obstacle. The search for unification, from Maxwell's merging of electricity and magnetism onward, has been the most successful research programme in the history of physics.

The four forces

Gravity (carrier: hypothetical graviton; described classically by general relativity) governs the large-scale universe. Electromagnetism (photon) underlies chemistry and light. The strong interaction (gluons) binds quarks and nuclei. The weak interaction (W and Z bosons) changes particle flavour and powers radioactive decay and stellar fusion. Whether a fifth force exists is an open experimental question, constrained tightly by precision tests.
