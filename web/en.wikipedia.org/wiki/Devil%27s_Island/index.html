Devil's Island

Devil's Island (French: Île du Diable) is the smallest and northernmost of the three islands of the Salvation Islands (Îles du Salut), off the coast of French Guiana. It is famous for having been used as a penal colony by France from 1852 to 1953.

Penal colony

The island housed political prisoners and hardened criminals in harsh conditions; strong currents and shark-infested waters made escape almost impossible. Its most famous prisoner was Captain Alfred Dreyfus, wrongly convicted of treason, who was held there from 1895 to 1899 during the Dreyfus affair. The writer Henri Charrière, author of Papillon, also claimed imprisonment in the Guiana penal system.

Geography

The rocky island rises about 40 metres above sea level and covers roughly 14 hectares. It lies about 14 kilometres off the coast near Kourou.

Today

The penal colony closed in 1953. The island is now managed in connection with the Guiana Space Centre at Kourou, and access is restricted, though the ruins of the prison can be seen from the neighbouring islands.
