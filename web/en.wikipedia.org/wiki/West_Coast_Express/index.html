West Coast Express

The West Coast Express is a commuter railway service in the Lower Mainland of British Columbia, Canada, operated by TransLink. It links Mission and communities in the Fraser Valley and the northeast sector of Metro Vancouver with downtown Vancouver's Waterfront Station.

Route and service

The line runs 69 kilometres along Canadian Pacific Railway tracks via Port Moody, Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Pitt Meadows, Maple Ridge, and Mission, ending at Mission City station. Service is peak-direction only: five trains inbound to Vancouver in the morning and five outbound in the afternoon/evening on weekdays, supplemented by the "TrainBus" coach service at other times. Trains use MPI MP36PH-3C locomotives and Bombardier BiLevel coaches.

History

The West Coast Express began operating on 1 November 1995 as part of the region's transport plan, becoming the first new commuter rail service in the Vancouver area in decades. Ridership is modest compared with the SkyTrain network, but the service is noted for high customer satisfaction and scenic views along Burrard Inlet and the Fraser River.
