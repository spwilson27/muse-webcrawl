An agricultural show is a public event exhibiting the equipment, animals, sports and recreation associated with agriculture and animal husbandry. The largest comprise a trade fair and a livestock show, in which farmers and breeders exhibit their animals and produce in competition for prizes. Agricultural shows are held in many countries, often annually, and are a fixture of rural life, combining serious competition in livestock breeding and crop production with entertainment, food stalls and displays of farm machinery.

History

Organised agricultural shows developed in Britain in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, as agricultural societies were founded to promote improved breeding and farming methods. The Royal Agricultural Society of England was founded in 1838 and held its first show in Oxford in 1839. County and local shows followed, and the model spread to the Commonwealth and the United States, where state and county fairs developed a similar character.

Competitions

The centrepiece of most shows is the judging of livestock — cattle, sheep, pigs, goats and poultry — in which animals are assessed against breed standards. Equestrian events, including show jumping, driving and heavy-horse classes, are also common. Produce competitions cover crops, vegetables, flowers, baking, preserves and handicrafts, often organised through local institutes. Young farmers' organisations run their own classes, and the showmanship of the handler may be judged alongside the animal.

Notable shows

Major events include the Royal Highland Show in Scotland, the Royal Welsh Show, the Sydney Royal Easter Show in Australia, and numerous state fairs in the United States such as the Iowa State Fair. Many shows also feature main-ring entertainment, vintage machinery parades and sheepdog demonstrations.
