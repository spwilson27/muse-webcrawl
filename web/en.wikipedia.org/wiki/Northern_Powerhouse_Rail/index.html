Northern Powerhouse Rail

Proposed Northern Powerhouse Rail network

Northern Powerhouse Rail (NPR), sometimes informally called HS3, is a proposed railway network intended to improve east–west connections between the major cities of northern England, including Liverpool, Manchester, Leeds, Sheffield, Hull and Newcastle. The project forms part of the "Northern Powerhouse" strategy to boost the economy of the North.

Development

The idea was championed by Chancellor George Osborne from 2014 and developed by Transport for the North, which published proposals for new lines — notably a new trans-Pennine route between Manchester and Leeds via Bradford — alongside upgrades to existing lines. Journey times between the northern cities remain slow compared with routes to London, which supporters cite as a constraint on the northern economy.

Integrated Rail Plan

In November 2021 the UK government's Integrated Rail Plan scaled back the original ambitions: instead of a wholly new Manchester–Leeds line, it proposed upgrading the existing trans-Pennine route, and it relied on parts of the eastern leg of HS2 that were themselves later cancelled in 2023. Northern leaders criticised the plan as inadequate. Subsequent governments have revived discussion of a new Liverpool–Manchester railway as a first phase of NPR, but firm funding and timescales have remained unsettled.

Proposed benefits

Supporters argue that faster, more frequent intercity services would create an integrated labour market of some 16 million people, raise productivity, and rebalance the UK economy away from London and the South East.
