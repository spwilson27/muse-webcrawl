Right to Buy

The Right to Buy is a policy in the United Kingdom, first introduced nationally by the Housing Act 1980 under Margaret Thatcher's Conservative government, giving secure tenants of council housing the legal right to purchase their homes at a substantial discount.

Policy

Tenants qualified after a minimum period of public-sector tenancy, receiving discounts based on length of occupation — originally 33% to 50%, later increased. Over two million homes were sold under the scheme, making it one of the largest privatisations in British history and dramatically expanding home ownership.

Effects and criticism

Supporters credit Right to Buy with giving working-class families an asset and a stake in society. Critics argue councils were prevented from using receipts to build replacements, depleting social housing stock, increasing waiting lists, and that many sold homes later entered the private rental sector at higher rents.

Devolution

Scotland abolished Right to Buy in 2016 and Wales in 2019. In England the scheme continues for council tenants, with discounts reduced in 2024, while a proposed extension to housing association tenants was piloted but not rolled out nationally.
