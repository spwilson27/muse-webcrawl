North Sea Jazz Festival

The North Sea Jazz Festival is an annual music festival held each July in the Netherlands. Founded in 1976, it is one of the largest indoor music festivals in the world, presenting jazz alongside blues, soul, funk, hip hop, and world music across many simultaneous stages.

A performance at the North Sea Jazz Festival

History

The festival was founded by Dutch businessman and jazz enthusiast Paul Acket. The first edition took place in 1976 at the Netherlands Congresgebouw in The Hague, featuring performers such as Sarah Vaughan, Count Basie, and Dizzy Gillespie. Around 9,000 visitors attended the inaugural event. Acket's ambition was to present the full breadth of American jazz history, from traditional styles to avant-garde, in a single event.

Move to Rotterdam

In 2006 the festival outgrew its The Hague venue and moved to the larger Rotterdam Ahoy complex in Rotterdam. The modern festival typically runs over three days with more than a dozen stages and over a thousand musicians, drawing on the order of 65,000–75,000 visitors. Sister events and spin-offs have included festivals in Curaçao and Hong Kong.

Awards

The festival has repeatedly been named among the world's best jazz festivals and has won tourism and event awards in the Netherlands. Each year the Paul Acket Award is presented to a talented younger artist deserving wider recognition. See also jazz, Rotterdam, and music festival.
