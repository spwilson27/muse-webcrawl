Six Degrees of Kevin Bacon


Kevin Bacon, the game's centre

Six Degrees of Kevin Bacon or Bacon's Law is a parlour game based on the "six degrees of separation" concept, which posits that any two people on Earth are six or fewer acquaintance links apart. Movie buffs challenge each other to find the shortest path between an arbitrary actor and prolific actor Kevin Bacon, counting films in which two actors have appeared together. An actor's Bacon number measures that distance: Bacon himself has number 0, actors who appeared with him in a film have number 1, and so on.

Origin

The game was created in 1994 by three students at Albright College in Pennsylvania — Craig Fass, Brian Turtle and Mike Ginelli — after watching a television interview in which Bacon remarked that he had worked with everybody in Hollywood or with somebody who had worked with everybody. The students promoted the game on talk shows, and it became a pop-culture phenomenon. Bacon initially disliked the premise but embraced it, founding the charitable organization SixDegrees.org in 2007, which connects users with charities.

Bacon numbers and the Erdős–Bacon number

Most working actors have a Bacon number of 2 or 3; very few reach 5 or more. Databases such as the Oracle of Bacon compute numbers from the Internet Movie Database. In playful analogy with the Erdős number of mathematics, a combined Erdős–Bacon number sums a person's distance to mathematician Paul Erdős (through co-authored papers) and to Bacon (through films); a small number of scientist-actors, such as Natalie Portman and Stephen Hawking, possess finite Erdős–Bacon numbers.
