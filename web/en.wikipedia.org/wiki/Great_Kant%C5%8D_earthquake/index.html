The Great Kantō earthquake (関東大震災) struck the Kantō Plain on the Japanese island of Honshū at 11:58:44 JST on Saturday, September 1, 1923. The earthquake had a magnitude of 7.9 on the moment magnitude scale, with its focus deep beneath Izu Ōshima Island in Sagami Bay. It devastated Tokyo, Yokohama and the surrounding prefectures, killing an estimated 105,000 to 142,000 people.

Damage

The quake struck at lunchtime, when many people were cooking over open fires; fires broke out across the cities and, fanned by strong winds from a nearby typhoon, developed into firestorms. The single greatest loss of life occurred when a fire whirl engulfed some 38,000 people who had taken refuge in an open space at Honjo, Tokyo. Tsunami waves up to 12 metres struck the coast of Sagami Bay.

Aftermath

In the chaos following the disaster, false rumours spread that Koreans were poisoning wells and committing arson, leading to the Kantō Massacre, in which vigilantes, police and soldiers killed several thousand ethnic Koreans and others. Reconstruction of Tokyo under the direction of Gotō Shinpei reshaped the capital with wider roads and parks. September 1 is now observed in Japan as Disaster Prevention Day.
