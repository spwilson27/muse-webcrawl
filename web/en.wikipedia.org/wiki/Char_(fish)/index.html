Char (fish)

Char, or charr, is the common name for fish of the genus Salvelinus in the salmon family Salmonidae. Chars are distributed throughout the cold fresh and coastal waters of the Northern Hemisphere and include some of the most northerly distributed freshwater fishes in the world. They are distinguished from true trout (genus Salmo) and Pacific salmons (Oncorhynchus) by their light spots on a darker body — the reverse of the dark-on-light pattern of trout.

Species

The taxonomy of Salvelinus is notoriously complex, with more than 50 species recognized by some authorities. Well-known members include the Arctic char (S. alpinus), a circumpolar species prized as a food fish; the brook trout (S. fontinalis) of eastern North America; the lake trout (S. namaycush); the bull trout (S. confluentus); and the Dolly Varden (S. malma). Many chars form both anadromous (sea-run) and landlocked freshwater populations.

Distribution and habitat

Chars inhabit Arctic and subarctic lakes, rivers and coastal waters of North America, Europe and Asia, as well as cold, deep alpine lakes farther south, where they survive as glacial relict populations. The Arctic char is the most northerly freshwater fish, found in lakes on Ellesmere Island at over 80°N. Most species prefer cold, well-oxygenated water and are sensitive to warming and pollution.

Human use

Chars are highly valued by anglers and as food fish. Arctic char is farmed in several countries and marketed as a more sustainable alternative to Atlantic salmon. Brook trout have been widely introduced beyond their native range, sometimes to the detriment of native species.
