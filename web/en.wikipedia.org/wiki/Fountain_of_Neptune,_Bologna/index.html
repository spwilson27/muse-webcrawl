Fountain of Neptune, Bologna

The Fountain of Neptune (Italian: Fontana di Nettuno) is a monumental civic fountain in the Piazza del Nettuno, next to Piazza Maggiore in Bologna, Italy. It was commissioned by Cardinal Legate Pier Donato Cesi and built between 1563 and 1566 to celebrate the election of Pope Pius IV.

Description

The fountain was designed by the Palermitan architect Tommaso Laureti, and its bronze statue of Neptune was sculpted by the Flemish artist Jean de Boulogne, known as Giambologna, one of his masterpieces of Mannerist bronze casting. The god stands about 3.2 metres tall atop a high pedestal, extending his right hand in a commanding gesture, surrounded at the base by four sirens representing the winds or the four known continents of the age, and four cherubs with dolphins for the great rivers — the Ganges, Nile, Amazon, and Danube.

Bolognese know the statue affectionately as il Gigante ("the Giant"). A popular student legend holds that walking around the fountain twice brings luck before examinations — or, in another version, bad luck to be avoided.
