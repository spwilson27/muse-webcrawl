Polymorphism

Polymorphism refers to the occurrence of something in several different forms. The term is used in several scientific and technical disciplines:

Biology

In biology, polymorphism is the occurrence of two or more clearly different morphs or forms, also referred to as alternative phenotypes, in the population of a species. For a polymorphism to persist, the different forms must occur in the same habitat at the same time and belong to the same panmictic population. Examples include the different colour morphs of the peppered moth and blood groups in humans. Genetic polymorphism refers to variation at the DNA level.

Computer science

In programming languages and type theory, polymorphism is the provision of a single interface to entities of different types, or the use of a single symbol to represent multiple different types. Major forms include ad hoc polymorphism (such as function overloading), parametric polymorphism (generics), and subtype polymorphism.

Materials science

In materials science and crystallography, polymorphism describes the ability of a solid material to exist in more than one form or crystal structure. Carbon, for example, exists as diamond, graphite, and fullerenes.
