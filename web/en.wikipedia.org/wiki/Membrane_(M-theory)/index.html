Membrane (M-theory)

Membrane (M2-brane)
FieldTheoretical physics, string theory
Dimensionality2 spatial dimensions (a 2-brane)
FrameworkEleven-dimensional supergravity / M-theory
Related objectsM5-brane, D-branes, fundamental string

In theoretical physics, a membrane or M2-brane is a spatially extended object with two spatial dimensions that arises as a fundamental (or solitonic) solution of eleven-dimensional supergravity and is a central object of M-theory. Just as a string sweeps out a two-dimensional worldsheet in spacetime, a membrane sweeps out a three-dimensional worldvolume. Membranes generalise the point particle (0-brane) and the string (1-brane) to higher dimensions.

History

Extended objects beyond strings were considered as early as Paul Dirac's 1962 model of the electron as a membrane. In 1987 Eric Bergshoeff, Ergin Sezgin and Paul Townsend showed that eleven-dimensional supergravity admits a supersymmetric membrane solution — the "supermembrane" — whose low-energy dynamics completed the picture of branes in string theory. When Edward Witten proposed M-theory in 1995 as the unifying eleven-dimensional framework behind the five string theories, the M2-brane and its magnetic dual, the M5-brane, became its principal extended objects.

Properties

The M2-brane couples electrically to the three-form gauge field of eleven-dimensional supergravity. Wrapping an M2-brane on the compact eleventh dimension of M-theory on a circle yields the fundamental string of type IIA string theory, a key piece of evidence for the duality between the two theories. Open membranes can end on M5-branes, in analogy with open strings ending on D-branes.

Quantising a single membrane is described by the dimensional reduction of super-Yang–Mills theory; the quantum mechanics of many coincident membranes led to the BFSS matrix model (1996), a proposed non-perturbative definition of M-theory. In 2008 the Bagger–Lambert–Gustavsson (BLG) theory gave a conformal field theory describing multiple M2-branes, deepening the AdS/CFT correspondence for membrane systems (AdS4/CFT3).
