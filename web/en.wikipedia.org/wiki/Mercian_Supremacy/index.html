The Mercian Supremacy was the period during the 7th, 8th and early 9th centuries when the Anglo-Saxon kingdom of Mercia was the dominant power in England south of the Humber, exercising overlordship over most of the other English kingdoms.

Rise and zenith

The supremacy began with the victories of Penda in the mid-7th century and continued under his sons Wulfhere and Æthelred. It reached its fullest extent under Æthelbald (716–757), who controlled all England south of the Humber, and Offa (757–796), whose power extended over Kent, Sussex and East Anglia, who negotiated with Charlemagne as an equal, and who built Offa's Dyke along the Welsh border.

Decline

Mercian dominance survived Offa's death under Cenwulf, but ended after the Mercian defeat by Egbert of Wessex at the Battle of Ellendun in 825, after which Kent, Sussex, Surrey and Essex passed to Wessex. Within a generation Mercia itself was divided by Viking conquest.
