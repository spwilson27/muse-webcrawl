Learning English (version of English)

Learning English is the simplified form of English used by the BBC's language-teaching service for learners of English as a foreign or second language. Produced by BBC Learning English (part of the BBC World Service), it presents news, features, and graded lessons in English written and spoken at an intermediate level, with vocabulary and structures chosen to be accessible to learners.

History

The BBC has broadcast English-teaching programmes since 1943, when the BBC English by Radio service began. The modern Learning English service grew with the World Service's educational output and moved online in the internet era, offering daily audio, video, and text features such as The English We Speak, 6 Minute English, News Review, and pronunciation workshops.

Characteristics

Learning English material uses a restricted core vocabulary (roughly 1,500–3,000 words), shorter sentences, and slower, clearly articulated delivery in audio and video. Idioms and new expressions are explicitly explained in each episode, and transcripts are provided so that learners can read along. The approach is related in spirit to other controlled versions of English, such as Voice of America's Learning English (formerly Special English), which uses a 1,500-word vocabulary and one-third normal speaking speed, and to simplified reference works such as Simple English Wikipedia.

Audience and use

The service is aimed at the hundreds of millions of people learning English worldwide, and its programmes are used by self-study learners, teachers in classrooms, and partner broadcasters. It is distinct from the BBC's general news output, which is written for fluent speakers.
