PCR

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

PCR most commonly refers to the polymerase chain reaction, a laboratory technique used to make millions of copies of a specific segment of DNA. The abbreviation is also used for other things (such as a test result in some medical contexts), but in biology PCR means the amplification method invented by Kary Mullis in 1983, which earned the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1993.

1. Overview

Overview

See the full article at Polymerase chain reaction. In brief: a sample is repeatedly heated and cooled in the presence of primers, nucleotides and a heat-stable DNA polymerase, doubling the target region each cycle. Variants include reverse-transcription PCR (starting from RNA, as in many COVID-19 tests), quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR), and digital PCR.
