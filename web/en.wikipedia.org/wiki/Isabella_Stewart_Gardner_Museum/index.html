Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum

Location25 Evans Way, Boston, Massachusetts, US
Opened1 January 1903
FounderIsabella Stewart Gardner

The Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum is an art museum in Boston, Massachusetts, housing the collection of Isabella Stewart Gardner (1840–1924). Its galleries surround a glass-covered courtyard garden in a palace built in the style of a 15th-century Venetian palazzo in the Fenway–Kenmore neighbourhood.

Collection

Gardner, advised by the young Bernard Berenson, assembled one of America's great private collections: works by Titian (The Rape of Europa), Rembrandt (including his only seascape, The Storm on the Sea of Galilee), Botticelli, Raphael, Vermeer (The Concert), Matisse and John Singer Sargent, whose portrait of Gardner and whose studio works are central to the museum. Gardner's will stipulated that the arrangement of the galleries must never be altered, on pain of the collection being sold.

The 1990 theft

In the early hours of 18 March 1990, two thieves disguised as police officers entered the museum and stole 13 works worth an estimated $500 million — the largest art theft in American history. The stolen works, including Vermeer's Concert and Rembrandt's Storm on the Sea of Galilee, have never been recovered; their empty frames still hang in the galleries, and a $10 million reward remains on offer.

Expansion

A new wing designed by Renzo Piano opened in 2012, providing space for exhibitions, performance and conservation while relieving pressure on the historic palace.
