Jean Chouan

Jean Chouan
Born: Jean Cottereau, 30 October 1757, Saint-Berthevin
Died: 28 July 1794, near Laval
Known for: Leader of the Chouannerie

Jean Chouan (born Jean Cottereau; 30 October 1757 – 28 July 1794) was a French counter-revolutionary leader during the French Revolution. His nickname — from imitating the cry of an owl (chat-huant) as a signal — gave its name to the Chouannerie, the royalist insurgency in Brittany, Maine and Normandy.

Life

Cottereau came from a family of clog-makers and was involved in salt smuggling against the gabelle tax. When the Revolution's conscription decree of 1793 provoked revolt in the west, he and his brothers became leaders of the insurgent bands in Mayenne. He was killed in July 1794, either in combat or by republican troops near Laval.

Legacy

The Chouan brothers became symbols of royalist resistance; Balzac's novel Les Chouans dramatised the insurgency.
