Prague astronomical clock

LocationOld Town Hall, Prague, Czech Republic
Installed1410
MakersMikuláš of Kadaň; Jan Šindel
TypeAstronomical clock

The Prague astronomical clock, or Prague Orloj (Czech: Pražský orloj), is a medieval astronomical clock attached to the Old Town Hall in Prague, the capital of the Czech Republic. First installed in 1410, it is the third-oldest astronomical clock in the world and the oldest still in operation.

History

The oldest part of the clock, the mechanical works and astronomical dial, dates to 1410 and was made by clockmaker Mikuláš of Kadaň in collaboration with Jan Šindel, an astronomer and mathematician at Charles University. The calendar dial below was added around 1490, as were Gothic sculptural decorations; tradition once credited the clock to a master "Hanuš", who legend claimed was blinded by the town councillors so he could never repeat his work — a story now considered apocryphal.

The clock fell into disrepair several times and was extensively repaired in the 18th and 19th centuries, when the wooden figures of the Apostles were added. It was heavily damaged in May 1945 during the Prague uprising, when German forces set fire to the Old Town Hall; the machinery was repaired and the Apostles restored by 1948. Major restorations took place again in 2005 and in 2017–2018.

Components

The Orloj has three main components:

  • The astronomical dial — a mechanical astrolabe showing the position of the Sun and Moon, the zodiac ring, Old Bohemian time (counted from sunset), Babylonian hours, and the phase of the Moon.
  • The Walk of the Apostles — an hourly show (between 9 a.m. and 11 p.m.) in which figures of the twelve Apostles appear in two windows above the dial, while allegorical statues move beside it: Death (a skeleton striking the time), the Turk, the Miser with his bag of gold, and Vanity admiring himself in a mirror. The show ends with a golden rooster crowing.
  • The calendar dial — below the astronomical dial, painted by Josef Mánes in 1866 (the original is now in the City of Prague Museum), showing medallions of the months and the days of the year.

Cultural significance

The clock is one of Prague's most famous landmarks; crowds gather in the Old Town Square each hour to watch the Walk of the Apostles. In 2010 the clock's 600th anniversary was marked with a projection-mapping show on the tower.
