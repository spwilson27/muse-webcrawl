Propaganda Due (Italian for "Propaganda Two"), usually abbreviated P2, was a Masonic lodge belonging to the Grand Orient of Italy that, under its master Licio Gelli, degenerated into a clandestine, quasi-criminal network — described by a parliamentary commission as a "state within the state" — implicated in corruption, fraud, political manipulation and connections to terrorism during Italy's Years of Lead.

History

The lodge originated in 1877 as Propaganda Massonica, a lodge whose members' identities were kept secret even by Masonic standards, and was reconstituted after the Second World War. When financier Licio Gelli took control in the late 1960s, he transformed it into a covert organisation recruiting the Italian establishment: eventually nearly a thousand members, including cabinet ministers, dozens of parliamentarians, generals and heads of the secret services, senior judges, bankers, police chiefs, newspaper editors and industrialists — and the future prime minister Silvio Berlusconi.

Discovery and scandal

P2 came to light on 17 March 1981, when police investigating Gelli's role in the Sindona banking affair raided his villa at Arezzo and found the membership list. The scandal brought down the government of Arnaldo Forlani in May 1981 — the first Italian government felled by a scandal of this kind. A parliamentary commission of inquiry chaired by Tina Anselmi concluded that P2 was a criminal conspiracy aimed at subverting Italian democracy. In 1982 parliament passed a law (the Anselmi law) banning secret associations, and the lodge was formally dissolved. Investigators found Gelli's "Plan for Democratic Rebirth" (Piano di rinascita democratica), a document outlining the capture of the media, judiciary, unions and parties by a small authoritarian elite.

Connections

P2 has been linked, to varying degrees of proof, to the collapse of Banco Ambrosiano and the death of its chairman Roberto Calvi ("God's banker", found hanged under Blackfriars Bridge in London in 1982), to the strategy of tension and neo-fascist terrorism, to Michele Sindona's financial crimes, and to networks in Latin America including Argentine military circles. Gelli himself, convicted on several charges including the Banco Ambrosiano fraud, died in 2015.
