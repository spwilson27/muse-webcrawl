Spotted dove
KingdomAnimalia
FamilyColumbidae
GenusSpilopelia
SpeciesS. chinensis

The spotted dove (Spilopelia chinensis) is a small, long-tailed pigeon native to tropical Asia, from India and Sri Lanka through Southeast Asia to southern China. It is easily recognised by the black patch with white spots on the back of its neck, pinkish-brown underparts, and brown, white-spotted wings. Its call is a soft, repeated cooing.

Distribution and habitat

Spotted doves inhabit woodland, scrub, farmland, parks, and gardens. The species has been widely introduced beyond its native range — to Australia, New Zealand, Hawaii, California, Mauritius, and elsewhere — and has become a familiar urban bird in many of these places.

Behaviour

These doves feed mainly on seeds and grain, foraging on the ground in pairs or small groups. They breed throughout much of the year in warm climates, building a flimsy platform of twigs in a tree or shrub and laying two white eggs. Flight is swift with regular wingbeats, and displaying males perform a steep climb followed by a glide with wings and tail spread.
