Honnō-ji Incident

Honnō-ji Incident
Date: 21 June 1582
Location: Honnō-ji, Kyoto, Japan
Result: Death of Oda Nobunaga; Akechi Mitsuhide's brief ascendancy

The Honnō-ji Incident (Honnō-ji no Hen) was the assassination of Japanese daimyō Oda Nobunaga at the Honnō-ji temple in Kyoto on 21 June 1582 by his retainer Akechi Mitsuhide, ending Nobunaga's campaign to unify Japan.

Events

Nobunaga, staying at Honnō-ji with a small guard while preparing to reinforce Toyotomi Hideyoshi against the Mōri, was surrounded at dawn by Mitsuhide's army of about 13,000. Nobunaga fought, then retreated into the burning temple and died — by suicide or in the fire; his body was never found. His son and heir Oda Nobutada also died at nearby Nijō Palace.

Motives

Mitsuhide's motives remain debated: personal grievances and humiliations, ambition, fear of demotion, or acting for another party. No single explanation is proven.

Aftermath

Hideyoshi rapidly made peace with the Mōri, force-marched to Kyoto and defeated Mitsuhide at the Battle of Yamazaki 13 days later; Mitsuhide was killed fleeing, earning him the nickname "the Thirteen-Day Shogun."
