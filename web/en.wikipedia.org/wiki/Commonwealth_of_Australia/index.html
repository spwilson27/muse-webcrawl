Commonwealth of Australia

Official name of: Australia
Formed: 1 January 1901 (federation)
Capital: Canberra
System: Federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy

The Commonwealth of Australia is the formal, official name of Australia, the federal state formed on 1 January 1901 when the six British self-governing colonies of New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia and Tasmania federated under the Constitution of Australia.

Federation

Following constitutional conventions in the 1890s and referendums in the colonies, the Commonwealth of Australia Constitution Act 1900 was passed by the British Parliament, and the Commonwealth was proclaimed at Centennial Park, Sydney, on 1 January 1901, with Edmund Barton as first prime minister. The federal capital was later established at Canberra in the Australian Capital Territory; the Northern Territory and ACT later gained self-government.

Government

Australia is a federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy: the monarch is head of state, represented by the Governor-General, while executive power is exercised by a prime minister and cabinet responsible to the Parliament of Australia, comprising the Senate and House of Representatives. Powers are divided between the Commonwealth and the states under the Constitution, with the High Court of Australia as final court of appeal. Legal independence from Britain was completed in stages, through the Statute of Westminster Adoption Act 1942 and the Australia Acts 1986.

Use of the name

"Commonwealth of Australia" appears in legal, governmental and formal contexts — in legislation, on currency issued in earlier eras, and in the styling of federal courts and agencies — while "Australia" is the ordinary short name.
