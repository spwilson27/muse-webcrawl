Cupola

[Image: A glazed cupola crowning the roof of a classical building]

In architecture, a cupola is a relatively small, dome-like structure on top of a building, used to provide light and ventilation to the space below and often serving as a lookout or decorative crown. The word derives from the Italian cupola, "small cup", itself from Latin cupa.

Characteristics

Cupolas are usually polygonal or circular in plan and sit on a roof or turret, frequently above a larger dome. They may be topped by a small lantern, spire or weather vane. Famous examples include the cupola-lantern of Florence Cathedral above Brunelleschi's dome, the cupola of St Peter's Basilica in Rome, and those of St Paul's Cathedral in London and the United States Capitol. In domestic and vernacular architecture, cupolas on barns and houses traditionally helped ventilate haylofts and attics.

Other uses of the term

The name is applied to several unrelated structures by analogy: the armoured observation cupola on tanks; the Cupola observation module of the International Space Station, a seven-windowed dome installed in 2010 that gives astronauts a panoramic view of Earth; and the cupola furnace, a melting furnace used in foundries to melt cast iron.
