Hebrew Christians

Hebrew Christians is a historical term for Jewish converts to Christianity, particularly in the 19th and early 20th centuries, and for Jewish Christians who maintained aspects of their Jewish identity and heritage while accepting Jesus as Messiah. The term predates and is related to, but distinct from, the modern Messianic Judaism movement.

History

In 19th-century Britain and elsewhere, societies were founded to evangelize Jews and to support converts, who often faced ostracism from Jewish communities and suspicion in Christian ones. Hebrew Christian congregations and alliances were formed, including the Hebrew Christian Alliance of America (1915) and the International Hebrew Christian Alliance.

Identity and practice

Hebrew Christians typically affirmed both their Jewish ethnic heritage and Christian faith. Practice varied widely, from full assimilation into mainstream churches to observance of some Jewish festivals and customs alongside Christian worship.

Relation to Messianic Judaism

From the 1960s and 1970s, many younger Hebrew Christians adopted the name "Messianic Jews" and developed congregational forms blending Jewish liturgy with evangelical theology, leading to the contemporary Messianic Jewish movement.
