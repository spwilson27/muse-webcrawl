Liquid crystal on silicon

Liquid crystal on silicon (LCoS) is a miniaturised reflective active-matrix liquid-crystal display or "microdisplay" using a layer of liquid crystal on top of a silicon backplane. It is also referred to as a spatial light modulator. LCoS was initially developed for projection televisions but is now used for wavelength selective switching (WSS), structured illumination, near-eye displays, and optical pulse shaping.

Technology

In an LCoS device, CMOS transistors and pixel electrodes are fabricated on a silicon chip, which is coated with a reflective aluminium layer and covered by a liquid crystal layer and a glass substrate bearing a transparent common electrode. Voltages applied to individual pixels alter the liquid crystal alignment and hence the polarisation or phase of reflected light, modulating an image with high fill factor and resolution.

Applications

Three-chip LCoS projectors, marketed in variants such as JVC's D-ILA and Sony's SXRD, deliver high contrast and resolution for home cinema. Single-panel LCoS microdisplays are used in head-mounted and near-eye displays, including viewfinders and augmented-reality devices. In telecommunications, LCoS-based wavelength selective switches steer individual wavelength channels in reconfigurable optical networks, and in research the technology serves as a programmable phase modulator for holography and beam shaping.
