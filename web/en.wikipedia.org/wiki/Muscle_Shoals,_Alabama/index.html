Muscle Shoals is a city in Colbert County, Alabama, United States, on the south bank of the Tennessee River. With Florence, Sheffield and Tuscumbia it forms the four-city "Shoals" metropolitan area. Despite its modest size, Muscle Shoals became world-famous in the 1960s and 1970s as a recording centre whose studios produced a distinctive soul, R&B and rock sound.

History

The city's name derives from the shoals in the Tennessee River — where, a traditional explanation holds, mussels (historically spelled "muscles") gathered, or where the river's current made boatmen use their muscles — which blocked navigation until canals and later Wilson Dam (completed 1924) tamed them. In the 1930s the Tennessee Valley Authority made the area a showcase of its electrification programme, and the dams and nitrate plants built for the First World War anchored local industry.

The Muscle Shoals sound

In 1959 Rick Hall founded what became FAME Studios in Florence and then Muscle Shoals; Arthur Alexander's "You Better Move On" (1961) launched the sound, and FAME sessions produced Aretha Franklin's breakthrough "I Never Loved a Man" (1967), Percy Sledge's "When a Man Loves a Woman" and hits by Wilson Pickett, Etta James and Clarence Carter. In 1969 FAME's house band — the Swampers, immortalised in Lynyrd Skynyrd's "Sweet Home Alabama" — left to found Muscle Shoals Sound Studio at 3614 Jackson Highway in Sheffield, where the Rolling Stones recorded "Brown Sugar" and "Wild Horses," and Paul Simon, Bob Seger, Cher and many others cut records. The 2013 documentary Muscle Shoals revived broad interest in the studios, both of which remain active.

Geography and demographics

Muscle Shoals covers about 16 square miles of mostly flat terrain above the Tennessee River. Its population was 16,275 at the 2020 census, within a metro area of roughly 150,000. It is the birthplace of W. C. Handy ("Father of the Blues," born in neighbouring Florence) celebrations aside, its neighbour Tuscumbia is the birthplace of Helen Keller.
