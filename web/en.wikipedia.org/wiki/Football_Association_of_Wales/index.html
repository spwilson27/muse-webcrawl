The Football Association of Wales (FAW; Welsh: Cymdeithas Bêl-droed Cymru, CBDC) is the governing body of association football and futsal in Wales. It is responsible for the Wales national football teams, the Welsh Cup, the Welsh League Cup, and the domestic league system including the Cymru Premier.

History

The FAW was founded in Wrexham in 1876, making it the third-oldest national football association in the world, after England's and Scotland's. Wales played its first international match against Scotland in 1876. Together with the other three home nations, the FAW is a permanent member of the International Football Association Board (IFAB), which maintains the Laws of the Game. The association joined FIFA in 1910 (with interruptions) and was a founding member of UEFA in 1954.

Competitions and role

The FAW organizes the Welsh Cup, first played in 1877–78, and oversees Welsh football from the professional game down to grassroots level, including refereeing, coaching, and discipline. The national team, nicknamed the Dragons, famously reached the semi-finals of UEFA Euro 2016 and qualified for the 2022 FIFA World Cup, its first World Cup since 1958.
