Syntagma Square

Syntagma Square (Greek: Πλατεία Συντάγματος, "Constitution Square") is the central square of Athens, Greece. It is named after the Constitution that King Otto was compelled to grant after a popular and military uprising on 3 September 1843. The square is fronted by the Hellenic Parliament, housed in the former Old Royal Palace, and by the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, guarded by the Evzones of the Presidential Guard.

Features

The square is a major transport hub, served by a station of the Athens Metro, and is surrounded by hotels, offices, and the shopping district of Ermou Street. The ceremonial changing of the guard takes place hourly before the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier.

History

Laid out in the early 19th century after Athens became the capital of the modern Greek state, the square has been the scene of major political events, including the 1843 uprising that gave it its name, mass demonstrations during the Greek government-debt crisis of 2010–2012, and numerous national celebrations.
