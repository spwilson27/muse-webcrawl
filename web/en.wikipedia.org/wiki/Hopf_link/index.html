Hopf link


The Hopf link, two circles linked once

In mathematical knot theory, the Hopf link is the simplest nontrivial link, consisting of two circles linked together exactly once. It is named after the mathematician Heinz Hopf. The Hopf link is the link analogue of the unknot: each component individually is unknotted, yet the two cannot be separated without cutting.

Properties

The Hopf link has crossing number two and linking number ±1, depending on orientation. It is a fibered link: its complement in the 3-sphere fibers over the circle, with the fibration given by the Hopf map, which sends each fiber to a pair of linked circles. The Hopf link is also the (2,2) torus link, since it can be drawn on the surface of a torus winding twice around each cycle. Its link group and Alexander polynomial are among the simplest nontrivial examples studied in knot theory.

Realizations and occurrences

The Hopf link occurs in many contexts. In biochemistry, two interlocked rings of DNA form a Hopf link after replication, and enzymes called topoisomerases resolve such links. Linked ring structures in chemistry (catenanes) realize the Hopf link at the molecular scale. The symbol also appears in art and heraldry and as the emblem of some academic institutions. In physics, the related Hopf fibration is important in the study of magnetic monopoles and in the geometry of the 3-sphere.
