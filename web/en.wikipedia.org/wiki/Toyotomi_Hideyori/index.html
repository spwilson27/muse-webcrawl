Toyotomi Hideyori

Toyotomi Hideyori (豊臣 秀頼, 1593–1615)
Born: 29 August 1593, Osaka Castle
Died: 5 June 1615 (aged 21), Osaka Castle
Father: Toyotomi Hideyoshi
Mother: Yodo-dono
Spouse: Senhime

Toyotomi Hideyori (豊臣 秀頼; 1593 – 5 June 1615) was the son and designated successor of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, the general who unified Japan in the late 16th century. He was head of the Toyotomi clan following his father's death in 1598, and his existence as a rival focus of loyalty ultimately led to the Siege of Osaka in 1614–1615, in which the Tokugawa shogunate destroyed the Toyotomi clan and consolidated its rule over Japan.

Early life

Hideyori was born at Osaka Castle in 1593 to Hideyoshi and his concubine Yodo-dono, a niece of Oda Nobunaga. He was Hideyoshi's second son; the first, Tsurumatsu, had died in infancy. When Hideyoshi died in 1598, the five-year-old Hideyori became head of the clan under the regency of five great lords (the Go-Tairō), including Tokugawa Ieyasu, who had sworn to protect the boy's inheritance.

Between two powers

Ieyasu steadily increased his own power, defeating his rivals at the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600 and taking the title of shōgun in 1603. Hideyori nonetheless remained at Osaka Castle as a great daimyō of some 650,000 koku, and in 1603 he was married to Senhime, Ieyasu's granddaughter, in accordance with Hideyoshi's earlier wishes. Relations between the Toyotomi household and the Tokugawa shogunate remained formally correct but tense; a meeting between Hideyori and Ieyasu at Nijō Castle in 1611 appeared to settle matters, but Toyotomi wealth and the presence of masterless samurai (rōnin) gathering at Osaka alarmed Edo.

Siege of Osaka

In 1614 a dispute over an inscription on a temple bell at Hōkō-ji in Kyoto, which the shogunate interpreted as a curse against Ieyasu, provided a pretext for war. In the winter campaign of 1614 the Tokugawa army besieged Osaka Castle; a negotiated settlement filled in the castle's outer moats, leaving it defenceless. In the summer campaign of 1615 the castle fell and was burned. Hideyori and his mother Yodo-dono committed suicide in a burning storehouse on 5 June 1615. His young son Kunimatsu was captured and executed, ending the Toyotomi line; his daughter was spared and became a nun.

Legacy

Hideyori's death removed the last serious rival to Tokugawa authority and is generally treated as the event that completed the unification settlement of Japan, ushering in more than two centuries of Tokugawa rule. In popular culture he is often portrayed as a tragic figure, a legitimate heir overshadowed and destroyed by Ieyasu's ambition.
