Laterality

Laterality is the preference most humans show for one side of the body over the other, exemplified by handedness. About 90% of people are right-handed for writing, and corresponding preferences exist for the foot, eye and ear. The term also covers the asymmetric organisation of the brain, in which functions such as language are lateralised predominantly to one hemisphere in most individuals.

Types

  • Handedness: right, left or mixed/ambidextrous.
  • Footedness: preferred foot for kicking or leading.
  • Ocular dominance: the eye preferred for sighting.
  • Cerebral lateralisation: e.g., Broca's and Wernicke's areas are in the left hemisphere in most people.

Development and causes

Laterality has genetic, prenatal and cultural influences; hand preference can be observed before birth in thumb-sucking asymmetry. Historically left-handed children were often forced to write with the right hand. In non-human animals, population-level paw preferences occur in some species, though many show individual preferences without a population bias.

Clinical aspects

Atypical lateralisation has been studied in relation to developmental disorders and schizophrenia, though links are modest. Unilateral neglect after stroke illustrates hemispheric specialisation for spatial attention.
