History of the Philippines

The history of the Philippines covers the archipelago from the arrival of modern humans tens of thousands of years ago, through Austronesian settlement, Indian and Chinese trade contacts, three centuries of Spanish colonization, American rule, Japanese occupation, and the independent republics since 1946.

Precolonial period

By the first millennium CE, maritime polities called barangays traded with China, India and Southeast Asia; the Laguna Copperplate Inscription (c. 900 CE) is the earliest dated Philippine document. Islam spread in the south from the 14th century, founding sultanates such as Sulu and Maguindanao.

Spanish era

Ferdinand Magellan's expedition reached the islands in 1521; colonization began with Miguel López de Legazpi in 1565, and Manila became the hub of the galleon trade with Acapulco. Spanish rule lasted until 1898, punctuated by revolts and by the reform and revolutionary movements of José Rizal, Andrés Bonifacio and Emilio Aguinaldo, whose forces proclaimed independence in 1898.

American period and war

The United States acquired the islands under the Treaty of Paris (1898) and fought the Philippine–American War; the Commonwealth was established in 1935. Japan occupied the country from 1942 to 1945. Independence was granted on 4 July 1946.

Republic

Postwar politics included the martial-law era of Ferdinand Marcos (1972–86), ended by the People Power Revolution that brought Corazon Aquino to office, and the continuing development of a democratic, service- and remittance-driven economy in the decades since.
