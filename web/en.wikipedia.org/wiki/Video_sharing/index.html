Video sharing

Video sharing is the distribution of video content over the Internet, typically through websites or apps that allow users to upload, view, comment on and redistribute videos. Online video-sharing services transformed media consumption in the mid-2000s as broadband access spread and streaming technology replaced clumsy file downloads.

History

Early attempts at web video in the late 1990s were constrained by bandwidth. The launch of YouTube in 2005 — whose first video, "Me at the zoo", was uploaded in April of that year — demonstrated a simple model of free uploads with browser-based Flash playback, and the service grew explosively before being acquired by Google in 2006. Competing and complementary platforms followed, including Vimeo (founded 2004, emphasising quality and creators), Dailymotion, and later short-form and livestreaming services such as Twitch, TikTok, and the video features of social networks.

Impact

Video sharing enabled new forms of user-generated content, citizen journalism, online education, music promotion and political campaigning, and created a class of professional independent creators supported by advertising revenue shares. It also raised persistent issues around copyright enforcement (addressed in part by systems such as YouTube's Content ID), misinformation, content moderation and the economics of traditional television and film distribution.
