King of the Mountains

The King of the Mountains (French: Roi de la Montagne) is the title given to the leader of the mountains classification in a cycling stage race — most famously the Tour de France. Points are awarded to riders who reach the summits of categorised climbs first, and the overall leader at each stage wears a distinctive jersey.

Tour de France

The mountains classification at the Tour was introduced in 1933, when Vicente Trueba was the first winner. Since 1975 the leader has worn the polka dot jersey (maillot à pois rouges) — white with red dots, colours taken from the sponsor Chocolat Poulain. Climbs are graded from fourth category to hors catégorie ("beyond categorisation"), with points increasing by difficulty and doubled at some summit finishes.

The record for victories is held by Richard Virenque, who won the classification seven times (1994–1997, 1999, 2003, 2004). Federico Bahamontes and Lucien Van Impe each won six. Riders such as Tadej Pogačar have won the mountains title in the same year as the general classification.

Other races

The Giro d'Italia awards a blue jersey (maglia azzurra, formerly green) to its mountains leader, whose highest summit each year is the Cima Coppi; the Vuelta a España uses a white jersey with blue polka dots. The classification exists in most multi-day stage races and rewards specialist climbers distinct from the overall contenders.
