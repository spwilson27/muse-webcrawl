Haskell Indian Nations University is a public tribal land-grant university in Lawrence, Kansas, United States. Founded in 1884 as the United States Indian Industrial Training School, it is operated by the Bureau of Indian Education and provides tuition-free higher education to members of federally recognized Native American tribes. Students represent more than 140 tribal nations from across the United States.

History

Haskell opened in 1884 as an off-reservation boarding school for Native American children, modelled on the Carlisle Indian School, with instruction in elementary subjects and trades such as farming, blacksmithing, and sewing. Like other boarding schools of the era, it pursued forced assimilation: children were separated from their families and forbidden to speak their languages. The school was named for Congressman Dudley C. Haskell, who secured the land for it. Over the twentieth century it evolved into a high school, then a junior college (1970, as Haskell Indian Junior College), and finally a university offering bachelor's degrees, adopting its current name in 1993.

Academics and student life

Haskell offers associate and bachelor's degrees in areas including business administration, elementary education, environmental science, and Indigenous and American Indian studies. Its cultural life centres on institutions such as the Haskell Cultural Center and Museum (opened 2002) and annual events including the Haskell Indian Art Market. The Haskell Cemetery contains the graves of more than 100 students who died at the school in its early decades.

Athletics

The Haskell football teams of the 1920s and 1930s were nationally famous, and Haskell Memorial Stadium, dedicated in 1926, honoured Native American veterans of World War I. Notable alumni-athletes include Olympian Jim Thorpe (who attended briefly) and John Levi. Today the Fightin' Indians compete in the NAIA.
