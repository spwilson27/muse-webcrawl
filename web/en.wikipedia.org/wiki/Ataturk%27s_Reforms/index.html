Atatürk's Reforms

Atatürk's Reforms were a series of political, legal, cultural, social and economic changes implemented in the Republic of Turkey under its first president, Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, during the 1920s and 1930s. Their aim was to transform the former Ottoman Empire into a modern, secular nation-state.

Major reforms

The reforms included the abolition of the Sultanate (1922) and the Caliphate (1924); the adoption of a Swiss-style civil code (1926) replacing sharia-based law; the introduction of the Latin-based Turkish alphabet (1928); the Surname Law (1934); the granting of women's suffrage (1930–1934); dress reforms discouraging the fez and veil; the adoption of the Gregorian calendar and Western time; and state-led industrialisation and educational expansion, including the unification of education.

Legacy

The reforms form the core of Kemalist ideology and remain central, and sometimes contested, elements of Turkish political life.
