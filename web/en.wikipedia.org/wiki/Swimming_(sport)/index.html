Swimming is an individual or team racing sport that requires the use of one's entire body to travel through water, taking place in pools or open water. Competitive swimming is one of the most popular Olympic sports, with events in the four strokes — freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke and butterfly — over distances from 50 metres to 1500 metres, plus individual medley and relay events. It is governed by World Aquatics (formerly FINA).

Swimming has featured at every modern Olympic Games since 1896. The sport's supreme figure is Michael Phelps, whose 23 gold medals define its history; other greats include Ian Thorpe, Katie Ledecky — the most decorated female swimmer, dominant in distance freestyle — and Mark Spitz. The 2008–2009 era of full-body polyurethane suits, which shattered dozens of world records, ended with their ban in 2010. Open-water marathon swimming (10 km) joined the Olympic programme in 2008.
