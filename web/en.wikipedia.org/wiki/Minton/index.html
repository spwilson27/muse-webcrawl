Minton

Minton was a major British ceramics company, founded by Thomas Minton in Stoke-on-Trent in 1793. Operating as Mintons Ltd from the nineteenth century, it became renowned for bone china, encaustic tiles, Parian ware and majolica.

History

Thomas Minton, trained as an engraver, established his pottery in Stoke in 1793, initially producing blue printed earthenware. Under his son Herbert Minton, the factory expanded greatly, supplying encaustic floor tiles for buildings including the Palace of Westminster and the United States Capitol.

Minton pioneered or popularised several techniques: acid-etched gilding, the pâte-sur-pâte method of decoration developed by Marc-Louis Solon, and richly coloured Victorian majolica shown at the Great Exhibition of 1851.

Later years

The company became part of the Royal Doulton group in 1968, and the Minton brand and factory declined in the following decades; the Stoke works closed in the early 2000s. Minton china remains highly collectible.
