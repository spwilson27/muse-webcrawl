Overview

Child sexual abuse is a form of child abuse in which an adult or older adolescent uses a child for sexual stimulation. Because children cannot consent to sexual activity, it is treated in law and clinical practice as abuse. Forms include sexual contact offences and non-contact offences such as exploitation and the production of abusive imagery. This article presents the subject in clinical and scholarly terms.

Prevalence and effects

Prevalence estimates vary by definition and method; international meta-analyses of self-report studies commonly find that roughly one in five women and about one in thirteen men report some experience of sexual abuse in childhood. Research links child sexual abuse to increased risk of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, anxiety and other adverse outcomes, though outcomes vary widely and many survivors recover, particularly with timely support and therapy.

Law and prevention

Virtually all jurisdictions criminalise sexual activity with children below an age of consent, and many operate mandatory reporting laws, sex offender registration and child-protection services. Prevention scholarship emphasises school-based education programmes for children, safeguarding and vetting in youth-serving organisations, bystander and parent education, and treatment services for people at risk of offending. International frameworks include the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child and the Council of Europe's Lanzarote Convention.
