Indie rock

Indie rock is a genre of rock music that originated in the United States and the United Kingdom in the 1970s. Originally used to describe independent record labels, the term became associated with the music they produced and was initially interchangeable with alternative rock or "guitar pop rock". Rooted in post-punk and the underground scenes of the 1980s, indie rock retained a commitment to independent labels, college radio and a do-it-yourself ethos even as some of its acts achieved mainstream success.

History

1980s: the underground

American bands such as R.E.M., the Replacements, Hüsker Dü, Black Flag and Sonic Youth built national touring and college-radio networks outside the major-label system, supported by independent labels like SST, Dischord and Twin/Tone. In Britain, the Smiths and the C86 scene defined an indie sound centred on jangly guitars.

1990s: alternative goes mainstream

After Nirvana's breakthrough in 1991, "alternative" became a major-label marketing category, and "indie" increasingly denoted a sound and aesthetic rather than literal independence. Pavement's lo-fi landmark Slanted and Enchanted (1992), Pixies, the Flaming Lips and labels such as Merge and Matador anchored the decade's indie rock.

2000s revival

The early 2000s brought a widely publicised indie resurgence led by the Strokes (Is This It, 2001), the White Stripes, Arcade Fire and LCD Soundsystem, aided by internet music blogs and file sharing. By the 2010s "indie" described a broad spectrum from folk (Bon Iver) to electronic-tinged acts, and the boundary with mainstream pop grew porous.

Characteristics and ethos

Indie rock is associated with artistic autonomy, small-label economics, lo-fi or unpolished production, and lyrics that favour irony, introspection or literary reference over arena-rock spectacle. Critics have debated the scene's demographics and its sometimes insular culture, but its infrastructure — independent labels, venues, festivals and media — remains a durable alternative pathway in the music industry.
