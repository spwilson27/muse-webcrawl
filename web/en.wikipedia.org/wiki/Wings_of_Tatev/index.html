The Wings of Tatev (Armenian: Տաթևի թևեր, Tatevi tever) is a 5.7 km (3.5 mi) cableway between the village of Halidzor and the Tatev Monastery in the Syunik Province of Armenia. It is the longest reversible aerial tramway built in only one section, and holds the record for the longest non-stop double track cable car. Construction was finished in October 2010, and the tramway was officially opened on 16 October 2010.

The tramway crosses the deep gorge of the Vorotan River at a maximum height of about 320 metres (1,050 ft) above the gorge floor. The journey, which takes about twelve minutes, replaced a gruelling drive of roughly an hour along the steep and winding serpentine road descending into and climbing out of the gorge.

History

The project was initiated as part of the Tatev Revival Program, an initiative of the Initiatives for Development of Armenia (IDeA) Foundation associated with Ruben Vardanyan and Veronika Zonabend, aimed at restoring the Tatev Monastery complex and developing tourism and infrastructure in the Syunik region. The tramway was engineered by the Austrian-Swiss Doppelmayr Garaventa Group, a leading manufacturer of ropeway systems. The total cost of construction was approximately US$18 million, funded largely through private donations.

Technical details

The line is supported by three towers and has two stations: one on a hill above the village of Halidzor and the other near the Tatev Monastery. Two cabins operate on the line, each able to carry up to 30 passengers and an attendant, moving at speeds of up to 37 km/h (23 mph). The system is a reversible aerial tramway: the cabins move back and forth along the track cables rather than circulating in a loop. It was entered into Guinness World Records as the world's longest non-stop double track aerial tramway, at 5,752 metres.

Tourism and impact

The opening of the Wings of Tatev sharply increased visitor numbers to the Tatev Monastery, a medieval Armenian Apostolic monastery founded in the 9th century that once housed a major university. Ticket revenues support the restoration program and local community development. The tramway has become one of Armenia's best-known tourist attractions and a symbol of the country's developing tourism industry, drawing over a million passengers in its first decade of operation.
