The Deming Prize is a global quality award that recognises organisations and individuals for excellence in total quality management. Established in 1951 by the Union of Japanese Scientists and Engineers (JUSE), it is named after the American statistician W. Edwards Deming, whose lectures in Japan in 1950 and royalties from the transcript of those lectures funded the prize. It is the longest-running and one of the most prestigious national quality awards in the world.

History

Deming was invited to Japan by the occupation authorities and JUSE in 1950 to lecture on statistical quality control. His eight-day seminar for engineers and his meeting with Japanese industrial leaders are credited with catalysing the Japanese quality movement. In gratitude, JUSE established the prize in December 1950, financing it from the royalties of the seminar transcript that Deming insisted be given to them. Early winners were Japanese manufacturers; in 1984 eligibility was opened to foreign companies, and in 1989 Florida Power & Light became the first non-Japanese company to win. Companies from India, Thailand and elsewhere have since won in significant numbers.

Categories and criteria

The prize has several categories, including the Deming Prize for individuals who have made outstanding contributions to the study or dissemination of statistical quality control and TQM; the Deming Application Prize (now simply the Deming Prize) for organisations that have achieved outstanding performance through company-wide quality control; and the Deming Grand Prize (formerly the Japan Quality Control Medal, established 1969) for past-winning organisations that have sustained and further improved their quality systems. Applicants undergo a rigorous examination against criteria covering management policies, organisation, education, quality information, standardisation, and activities such as quality circles, with examiners assessing whether TQM principles are genuinely embedded throughout the enterprise rather than applied superficially.

Influence

The Deming Prize inspired national quality awards elsewhere, including the Malcolm Baldrige National Quality Award in the United States (1987) and the EFQM Excellence Award in Europe. Its emphasis on company-wide participation, statistical thinking and continuous improvement helped codify the Japanese approach to quality that became internationally known as TQC and later TQM.
