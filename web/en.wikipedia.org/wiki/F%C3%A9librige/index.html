Félibrige

Formation21 May 1854
FoundersFrédéric Mistral, Joseph Roumanille and five others
PurposePromotion of Occitan language and culture

The Félibrige is a literary and cultural association founded in 1854 to restore and promote the Occitan language and the culture of Provence and the wider south of France. Its members, known as félibres, sought to revive Provençal as a literary language at a time when it was being displaced by standard French.

Founding

The movement was founded on 21 May 1854 at the Château de Font-Ségugne near Avignon by seven young poets: Frédéric Mistral, Joseph Roumanille, Théodore Aubanel, Jean Brunet, Paul Giéra, Anselme Mathieu and Alphonse Tavan. Mistral became its most famous figure, winning the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1904 and producing the epic poem Mirèio and a monumental Provençal dictionary.

Activities

The Félibrige fixed a literary orthography for Provençal, published almanacs and journals, and organised festivals such as the Fèsto Vierginenco in Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer. The association still exists today, promoting Occitan language and culture across the regions where it is spoken.
