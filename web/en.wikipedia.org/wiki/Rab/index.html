Rab (Italian: Arbe) is a Croatian island in the Kvarner Gulf of the northern Adriatic Sea, and also the name of its principal town. Together with the neighbouring islands it forms part of Primorje-Gorski Kotar County. The island is about 22 kilometres long, covers some 91 square kilometres, and has a population of around 8,000, concentrated in the town of Rab and villages such as Lopar and Banjol.

Geography and nature

Rab is notable for its contrasting landscapes: the Kalifront peninsula is covered by one of the last Mediterranean holm-oak forests, protected as the Dundo forest reserve, while the northeast coast, exposed to the bura wind, is barren karst. The island has a mild Mediterranean climate and many sandy beaches — unusual in Croatia — including Paradise Beach at Lopar, making tourism the dominant industry. Rab claims a pioneering role in naturism: in 1936 Edward VIII and Wallis Simpson were famously permitted to swim nude at Kandarola beach, an episode the town still celebrates.

History

Known in antiquity as Arba, the island was settled by Liburnians and Romans, passed through Byzantine, Croatian, Hungarian and Venetian hands, and belonged to Italy between the world wars. The town of Rab is famous for its skyline of four Romanesque church bell towers, its medieval old town, and the Rab Fiera, a medieval festival held each July. A darker chapter is the Rab concentration camp, run by Fascist Italy in 1942–43 at Kampor, where thousands of Slovenes, Croats and Jews were interned and many died.
