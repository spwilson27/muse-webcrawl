Battle of Als

The Battle of Als (Danish: Slaget om Als) was fought on 29 June 1864 during the Second Schleswig War between Denmark and Prussia. In a daring night operation, Prussian troops under General Herwarth von Bittenfeld crossed the Als Sound (Alssund) from Sundeved to the island of Als, defeating the Danish forces defending the island and effectively ending organised Danish resistance in the war.

Als Sound, between Sønderborg and the Sundeved peninsula.

Background

After the Prussian storming of the Dybbøl fortifications on 18 April 1864, the Danish army withdrew to the island of Als, where about 12,000 men under General Steinmann defended positions around Sønderborg. A London peace conference failed in late June, and the armistice expired on 26 June. Prussian command decided to seize Als as a decisive stroke.

The crossing

At about 2 a.m. on 29 June, some 2,500 Prussian troops in the first wave crossed the sound in boats at Sottrup Skov, north of the main Danish positions. Danish warships, including the ironclad Rolf Krake, intervened but could not prevent the landing. The Prussians rapidly overran the coastal defences, advanced on Sønderborg and forced the Danes into a hurried evacuation toward Kegnæs and then to Funen. Prussian losses were around 370 killed and wounded; Danish losses were heavier — roughly 600–700 killed and wounded and some 2,000–3,000 taken prisoner, along with most of the island's artillery.

Consequences

The loss of Als broke Denmark's ability to continue the war. An armistice followed on 20 July, and by the Treaty of Vienna (30 October 1864) Denmark ceded the duchies of Schleswig, Holstein and Lauenburg to Prussia and Austria — about 40 percent of the Danish monarchy's territory. Als remained German until the Schleswig plebiscites of 1920 returned it to Denmark.
