Equestrianism

Equestrianism (from Latin equester, "horseman", from equus, "horse"), commonly known as horse riding or horseback riding, refers to the skill of riding, driving, or vaulting with horses. This broad description includes the use of horses for practical working purposes, transportation, recreational activities, artistic or cultural exercises, and competitive sport.

History

Evidence suggests that horses began to be ridden around 3500 BC, in the steppes of Central Asia, where the Botai culture kept horses for milk, meat and possibly riding. Horsemanship developed through cavalry warfare, herding, and transport across Eurasia. Classical schools of riding were codified in the Renaissance, notably in works such as Federico Grisone's Gli ordini di cavalcare (1550), and in the baroque riding academies of Europe.

Disciplines

Equestrian sports governed internationally by the Fédération Équestre Internationale (FEI) include the three Olympic disciplines:

  • Dressage — the progressive training of the horse to carry a rider with ease and obedience, performed as a series of prescribed movements in an arena.
  • Show jumping — horse and rider jump a course of obstacles within a time limit.
  • Eventing — a combined test of dressage, cross-country jumping, and show jumping, originally derived from cavalry exercises.

Other FEI disciplines include driving, endurance riding, vaulting, reining, and para-equestrian events. Outside the FEI, popular forms include horse racing, rodeo, polo, hunting, trail riding, and Western riding disciplines such as barrel racing and cutting.

Equipment and riding styles

The two broad traditions are English riding and Western riding. Both use a saddle, bridle and bit, but differ in tack design and riding style: English tack is lighter and closer-fitting, adapted from European cavalry and hunting; the Western saddle, with its prominent horn, developed for cattle work on ranches in the Americas. Riders use the seat, legs, hands (through the reins), and voice as "aids" to communicate with the horse.

Horse care and welfare

Equestrianism depends on the management of the horse's health, feeding, grooming, shoeing and stabling. Modern debates in the sport include the use of whips and spurs, training methods such as rollkur, the welfare of horses in transport and competition, and the social licence of equestrian sport.
