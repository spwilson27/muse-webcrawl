Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab (1703–1792) was an Arab Islamic scholar and theologian from Najd in central Arabia, founder of the revivalist movement known to its followers as al-Muwahhidun ("the Unitarians") and to its critics as Wahhabism. Preaching an uncompromising monotheism (tawhid), he condemned practices he regarded as idolatry — the veneration of saints and tombs, Sufi rituals, and much of the popular religion of his day — and called for a return to Islam as practised by the earliest generations, drawing on the Qur'an, the Sunna and the Hanbali jurist-theologian Ibn Taymiyya.

The Diriyah pact

Born in the oasis town of 'Uyayna into a family of Hanbali scholars, Ibn Abd al-Wahhab studied in Medina and travelled in Basra and al-Ahsa before returning to preach in Najd. Expelled from 'Uyayna for his attacks on local customs, he was welcomed in 1744 by Muhammad bin Saud, the ruler of the small emirate of Diriyah. Their pact — religious authority for Ibn Abd al-Wahhab, political and military leadership for the House of Saud — founded the first Saudi state and fused a dynasty with a doctrine, a partnership that, in renewed form, underlies the modern Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

His principal work, Kitab al-Tawhid ("The Book of Monotheism"), together with short treatises such as The Three Fundamental Principles and The Four Rules, remains central to Salafi teaching. His movement's legacy is intensely debated: supporters credit it with purifying Arabian Islam; critics, historically including the Ottoman religious establishment, blamed it for iconoclasm and violence against other Muslims. His numerous descendants, the Al al-Shaykh family, have held the highest religious offices in Saudi Arabia ever since.
