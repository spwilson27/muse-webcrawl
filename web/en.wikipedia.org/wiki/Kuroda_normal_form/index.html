Kuroda normal form

In formal language theory, Kuroda normal form is a normal form for context-sensitive grammars, named after the linguist Sige-Yuki Kuroda, who also coined the term "linear bounded automaton".

Definition

A grammar is in Kuroda normal form if every production rule is of one of the forms:

  • AB → CD
  • A → BC
  • A → B
  • A → a
  • AB → A or AB → B (in some formulations)

where A, B, C, D are nonterminal symbols and a is a terminal symbol. Every context-sensitive grammar can be converted into a weakly equivalent grammar in Kuroda normal form, just as every context-free grammar can be converted into Chomsky normal form — indeed, Kuroda normal form generalises that construction to the context-sensitive case.
