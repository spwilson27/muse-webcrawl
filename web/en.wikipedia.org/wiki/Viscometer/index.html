Viscometer

A viscometer (also called a viscosimeter) is an instrument used to measure the viscosity of a fluid. For liquids with viscosities that vary with flow conditions, the related instrument is a rheometer. Viscometers are used throughout industry — in petroleum refining, paints and coatings, food processing, pharmaceuticals and polymer manufacture — because viscosity strongly affects pumping, spraying, lubrication and product quality.

Capillary viscometers

The classical design is the glass capillary viscometer, of which the Ostwald and Ubbelohde (suspended-level) types are the best known. A fixed volume of liquid is drawn through a fine capillary tube and the time it takes for the meniscus to pass between two marks is measured; with the density known, the kinematic viscosity follows from Poiseuille's law. Ubbelohde viscometers are widely used for determining the molecular weight of polymers from intrinsic viscosity.

Falling-sphere and falling-piston viscometers

In a falling-sphere viscometer, a sphere of known density and diameter falls through the liquid in a vertical tube, and its terminal velocity gives the viscosity through Stokes' law. Falling-piston instruments, used in oil analysis, instead time the fall of a piston through a sample chamber.

Rotational viscometers

Rotational instruments — cone-and-plate, parallel-plate, and coaxial-cylinder (Couette) geometries — measure the torque needed to rotate a spindle or cone in the fluid at a known speed. They can impose a controlled shear rate and therefore characterise non-Newtonian fluids such as paints, blood and polymer melts. The Brookfield viscometer is a widely used spindle-type instrument of this kind.

Other types

Vibrational viscometers measure the damping of an oscillating probe; bubble viscometers time the rise of an air bubble; Saybolt and Engler viscometers, historically important in the petroleum industry, measure the efflux time of a fixed volume through a standard orifice and report empirical scales such as Saybolt Universal Seconds.
