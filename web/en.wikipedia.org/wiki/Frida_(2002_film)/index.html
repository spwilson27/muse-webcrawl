Frida is a 2002 American biographical drama film directed by Julie Taymor and starring Salma Hayek as Mexican painter Frida Kahlo. Based largely on Hayden Herrera's biography Frida: A Biography of Frida Kahlo, the film follows Kahlo's life from the bus accident that left her in lifelong pain, through her tumultuous marriage to muralist Diego Rivera, to her artistic career and death.

Plot

In 1925, eighteen-year-old Frida Kahlo is gravely injured when a streetcar crashes into the bus she is riding, leaving her with fractures that cause chronic pain for the rest of her life. Confined to bed, she begins to paint. She later shows her work to the celebrated muralist Diego Rivera, who encourages her and becomes her mentor, lover and husband. The film depicts their stormy relationship, marked by infidelities on both sides, Kahlo's affairs, her encounters with figures such as Leon Trotsky, and her growing recognition as an artist in her own right.

Reception and awards

The film received generally positive reviews, with particular praise for Hayek's performance and the film's visual style, which incorporates Kahlo's paintings as animated tableaux. Frida was nominated for six Academy Awards, winning two: Best Makeup and Best Original Score by Elliot Goldenthal. Hayek, who had pursued the project for years as a producer, was nominated for Best Actress.
