Nynorsk

A Nynorsk-language sign in western Norway.

Nynorsk (literally "New Norwegian") is one of the two official written standards of the Norwegian language, the other being Bokmål. It was created by the linguist Ivar Aasen in the 19th century, based on rural western Norwegian dialects, as an alternative to the Danish-influenced written language of the cities.

History

Aasen published his grammar in 1848 and dictionary in 1850. Under the name Landsmål, the standard gained official equality with (Riks)mål in 1885, and was renamed Nynorsk in 1929. Its use peaked in the 1940s at roughly a third of school pupils; today about 12% of Norwegians use it as their primary written form, concentrated in western Norway.

Use

Nynorsk is used in public administration — 25% of government documents must be in Nynorsk by law — and by the broadcaster NRK, newspapers and a substantial body of literature.
