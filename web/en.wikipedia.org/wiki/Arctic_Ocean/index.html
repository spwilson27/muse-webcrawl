Arctic Ocean

Arctic Ocean
AreaAbout 14 million km² (smallest ocean)

The Arctic Ocean is the smallest and shallowest of the world's five major oceans, located mostly within the Arctic Circle around the North Pole. It is surrounded by North America, Greenland, Europe and Asia, and is connected to the Pacific by the narrow Bering Strait and to the Atlantic mainly through the Greenland and Labrador seas. Much of its surface is covered by sea ice, whose extent varies seasonally — historically from about 7 million km² in summer to 15 million in winter — though summer ice has declined dramatically in recent decades.

Geography and climate

Under the ice lies a basin divided by ridges — the Lomonosov Ridge splits it into Eurasian and Amerasian basins — and fringed by the wide continental shelves of Siberia, the broadest in the world. Great Siberian and Canadian rivers (Ob, Yenisei, Lena, Mackenzie) freshen its surface waters. The Arctic climate is moderated by the ocean itself, yet remains among Earth's coldest environments; in winter the polar night lasts for months at the Pole.

Ice, life and change

Arctic sea ice has lost roughly half its late-summer extent since satellite records began in 1979, and the Arctic is warming several times faster than the global average ("Arctic amplification"). The ecosystem is built on ice: algae growing under the ice feed krill, fish, seals, and at the top the polar bear, an icon of the region and of climate change. Melting ice is opening shipping routes — the Northern Sea Route along Russia and the Northwest Passage through Canada — and access to oil, gas and minerals, raising geopolitical competition among the Arctic states, coordinated loosely through the Arctic Council.
