President of Israel

The president of Israel (Hebrew: Nesi Medinat Yisra'el) is the head of state of Israel. The position is largely a ceremonial figurehead role, with executive power effectively exercised by the prime minister. The president is elected by the Knesset for a single seven-year term. The incumbent is Isaac Herzog, who took office in July 2021.

Functions

The president signs laws and treaties, accredits diplomats, appoints judges, the governor of the Bank of Israel and other officials, and grants pardons and commutes sentences. After a general election, the president consults with party representatives and formally tasks a Knesset member — usually the leader judged most likely to succeed — with forming a government. The president resides at Beit HaNassi in Jerusalem.

Election

Candidates are nominated by groups of at least ten Knesset members. Voting is by secret ballot, requiring an absolute majority; if no candidate achieves one in two rounds, the candidate with the fewest votes is eliminated until one prevails. The term was changed from five years (renewable) to a single seven-year term in 2000.

History

Chaim Weizmann became Israel's first president in 1949. Later presidents include Yitzhak Ben-Zvi, Zalman Shazar, Ephraim Katzir, Yitzhak Navon, Chaim Herzog, Ezer Weizman, Moshe Katsav, Shimon Peres and Reuven Rivlin. Katsav's resignation in 2007 amid criminal proceedings, and his subsequent conviction, marked the office's gravest scandal.
