Leo Beranek

Leo Leroy Beranek (September 15, 1914 – October 10, 2016) was an American acoustics engineer, inventor, and author who co-founded the technology company Bolt Beranek and Newman (BBN). He is widely regarded as a founder of modern architectural acoustics, and his comparative studies of the world's concert halls remain standard references.

Career

After earning his doctorate at Harvard, Beranek directed the university's Electro-Acoustic Laboratory during the Second World War, working on aircraft communication and noise reduction. In 1948 he founded the acoustical consulting firm Bolt, Beranek and Newman with Richard Bolt and Robert Newman. BBN grew into a pioneering technology company that built some of the first computer networks, including major contributions to the ARPANET.

Beranek served as acoustic consultant for concert halls and performance spaces around the world, and his books Music, Acoustics, and Architecture (1962) and Concert Halls and Opera Houses (revised 2004) systematically ranked venues using interviews with conductors and musicians combined with physical measurements. He also helped preserve open space in Massachusetts as president of the Boston television station WCVB's parent organizations, and received the National Medal of Science in 2003.
