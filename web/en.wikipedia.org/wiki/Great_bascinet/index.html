Great bascinet
TypeHelmet
Periodc. 1400 – mid-15th century

The great bascinet was a type of helmet developed in the early 15th century from the earlier bascinet. Unlike its predecessor, which was worn with a mail aventail, the great bascinet incorporated solid plate defences for the neck and throat — a deep bevor-like plate — and its rounded skull was often attached to the breastplate and backplate, transferring the weight of the helmet to the shoulders and restricting head movement.

Design

The great bascinet typically had a globular or rounded skull, a detachable visor — often of the "hounskull" or rounded form — and large side plates hinged to protect the cheeks and neck. The lower plate defences were sometimes riveted or strapped directly to the cuirass, so the wearer's head could move only slightly within the fixed helmet.

Use

The type was worn by knights and men-at-arms across western Europe from about 1400 until the mid-15th century, when it was largely superseded by the sallet and armet. Its strong all-round protection made it popular for tournament as well as battlefield use.
