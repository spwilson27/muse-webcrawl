Continuing medical education

Continuing medical education (CME) refers to educational activities that help physicians and other health professionals maintain, develop and increase their knowledge, skills and professional performance throughout their careers. In many jurisdictions a minimum amount of CME is required to maintain a medical license.

Requirements

Most US states require physicians to complete CME credits for licence renewal — for example the AMA PRA Category 1 Credit system, overseen by the ACCME, which accredits providers. The UK uses an annual appraisal and five-yearly GMC revalidation, supported by continuing professional development.

Formats

Activities include conferences, grand rounds, workshops, journal-based learning, point-of-care searching, simulation, and online modules. Effectiveness research suggests interactive, practice-linked formats change clinical behaviour more than didactic lectures.

Industry funding and regulation

Pharmaceutical and device companies historically funded a large share of CME, raising conflict-of-interest concerns; accreditation standards now require content independence from commercial supporters and disclosure of relevant financial relationships, reinforced in the US by the Sunshine Act.
