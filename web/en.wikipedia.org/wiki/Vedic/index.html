Vedic

Relates toThe Vedas; Vedic religion; Vedic period
LanguageVedic Sanskrit
Periodc. 1500 – 500 BCE

Vedic is an adjective relating to the Vedas, the oldest scriptures of Hinduism, and by extension to the religion, language, culture, and history of the people who composed them in ancient India. The term derives from the Sanskrit word veda, "knowledge".

Senses of the term

  • Vedic Sanskrit — the archaic Indo-Aryan language of the Vedas, the oldest attested stage of Sanskrit, differing in grammar and vocabulary from later Classical Sanskrit.
  • Vedic period — the Late Bronze Age and early Iron Age period of northern Indian history (c. 1500–500 BCE) during which the Vedas were composed, spanning the early pastoral society of the Rigveda and the later, more settled Kuru kingdom.
  • Vedic religion (Vedism) — the religion of the Vedic texts, centred on ritual sacrifice (yajna), fire offerings, and deities such as Indra, Agni, and Varuna; it is the historical precursor of classical Hinduism.
  • Vedic literature — the corpus of the four Vedas (Rigveda, Samaveda, Yajurveda, Atharvaveda) together with their Brahmanas, Aranyakas, and Upanishads.

"Vedic" is also used in modern contexts such as "Vedic astrology" (Jyotisha) and "Vedic mathematics".
