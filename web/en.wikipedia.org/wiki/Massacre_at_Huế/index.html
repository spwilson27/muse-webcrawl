The Massacre at Huế refers to the summary execution of civilians by the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) and the Viet Cong during their occupation of the city of Huế, South Vietnam, in February 1968, as part of the Tet Offensive. During the month-long Battle of Huế, communist forces seized the city and, according to captured documents and subsequent investigations, systematically rounded up and killed civil servants, teachers, police, religious figures, foreign civilians and others identified from prepared lists as enemies of the revolution. Mass graves containing thousands of bodies were discovered in and around the city in 1968 and 1969.

Events

PAVN and Viet Cong forces captured most of Huế on 31 January 1968. Cadres with lists moved through neighbourhoods arresting officials of the South Vietnamese government, teachers, students, Catholic and Buddhist clergy, and foreign residents — among them French and German doctors and a Filipino musician. Many victims were shot or bludgeoned and buried, some reportedly alive, in mass graves at sites including Da Mai Creek and the Gia Hoi high school.

Estimates and debate

The number of victims remains contested. A captured communist document acknowledged about 2,800 people "eliminated"; South Vietnamese and US official compilations, based on exhumed bodies (roughly 2,800 recovered) and missing persons, put total civilian deaths at Huế from all causes at up to about 6,000, of which a large share were attributed to the executions rather than to crossfire or Allied bombing. The government of Vietnam has historically characterised the episode differently, attributing many deaths to American bombing, and the topic remains politically sensitive.
