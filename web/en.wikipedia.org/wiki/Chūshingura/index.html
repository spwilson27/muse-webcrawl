Chūshingura

The night attack on Kira's mansion

Chūshingura (忠臣蔵, "The Treasury of Loyal Retainers") is the title given to a celebrated cycle of Japanese plays, puppet theatre works, stories, and films dramatizing the historical vendetta of the forty-seven rōnin. The most famous version is the 1748 puppet play Kanadehon Chūshingura, later adapted for kabuki, which remains one of the most popular plays in Japan.

The historical incident

In 1701, Asano Naganori, the young daimyō of Akō, was goaded by the court official Kira Yoshinaka and drew his sword within Edo Castle, for which he was ordered to commit seppuku and his domain was confiscated. After nearly two years of patient planning, forty-seven of his now-masterless retainers (rōnin), led by Ōishi Yoshio, raided Kira's Edo mansion on a snowy night in early 1703, killed Kira, and presented his head at Asano's grave at Sengaku-ji temple. The shogunate ordered the rōnin to commit seppuku, which all but one did.

The plays

Because censorship forbade staging recent events involving samurai, dramatists disguised the story by shifting its setting to the 14th century and renaming the characters (Asano became En'ya Hangan, Kira became Kō no Moronao, Ōishi became Ōboshi Yuranosuke). Kanadehon Chūshingura, written by Takeda Izumo II, Miyoshi Shōraku, and Namiki Senryū, premiered as a bunraku puppet play in Osaka in 1748 and was adapted for kabuki the same year. The story became central to the Japanese ideal of bushidō loyalty, and has been filmed and televised scores of times.
