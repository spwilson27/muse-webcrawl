Darter (fish)

Darters are small, colourful freshwater fishes of the subfamily Etheostomatinae, part of the perch family Percidae. Found primarily in eastern North America, they comprise more than 200 described species in genera such as Etheostoma, Nothonotus, Percina and Ammocrypta, making them one of the most diverse groups of fishes on the continent.

Description and habitat

Most darters are less than 10 cm (4 in) long and live on the bottoms of clear, fast-flowing streams and rivers, where they perch on rocks and dart between hiding places — behaviour that gives them their name. They lack a functional swim bladder, which helps them stay on the substrate in currents. Males of many species develop brilliant blue, green, orange or red breeding colours in spring. Darters feed mainly on aquatic insect larvae and other small invertebrates.

Diversity and conservation

Darter diversity is greatest in the Mississippi River basin and the southeastern United States. Because they require clean, well-oxygenated water and undisturbed streambeds, many species are sensitive indicators of water quality, and numerous darters are threatened or endangered by pollution, siltation and damming. The snail darter (Percina tanasi) became famous in the 1970s when its protection under the Endangered Species Act halted construction of the Tellico Dam, leading to the Supreme Court case TVA v. Hill (1978).
