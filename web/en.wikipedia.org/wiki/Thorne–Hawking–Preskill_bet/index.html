Thorne–Hawking–Preskill bet

The Thorne–Hawking–Preskill bet was a public wager between theoretical physicists Stephen Hawking on one side and Kip Thorne and John Preskill on the other, concerning the black hole information paradox — whether information that falls into a black hole is destroyed when the black hole evaporates, in violation of quantum mechanics.

The bet

The wager was made in 1997. Hawking, building on his earlier conclusion that black holes emit thermal radiation (Hawking radiation) containing no information about what fell in, bet that information is genuinely lost in black hole evaporation. Thorne and Preskill bet that quantum mechanics is correct and that information must escape, encoded somehow in the radiation. The stake was an encyclopedia of the winner's choice, "from which information can be recovered at will."

Resolution

In 2004, Hawking announced that he had changed his mind, persuaded in part by developments in string theory (the AdS/CFT correspondence) suggesting that black hole evaporation is unitary and information is preserved. He conceded the bet and paid up with a baseball encyclopedia, presenting it to Preskill. Thorne declined to concede, maintaining that the question was not yet definitively settled. The underlying paradox continues to be an active research area in quantum gravity, with proposals including black hole complementarity, firewalls, and holographic encoding of information.

Related bets

Hawking participated in other famous scientific wagers, including an earlier bet with Thorne and Preskill over whether Cygnus X-1 is a black hole (conceded by Hawking in 1990) and a bet over the cosmic censorship hypothesis.
