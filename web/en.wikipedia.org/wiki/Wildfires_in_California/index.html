Wildfires in California

Wildfires in California are a recurring and intensifying hazard driven by the state's Mediterranean climate, drought, heatwaves, Santa Ana and Diablo winds, accumulated fuels and human ignitions, including power-line failures. CAL FIRE and federal agencies record thousands of fires annually.

Notable fires

The largest recorded include the August Complex (2020, over one million acres), the Dixie Fire (2021) and the Mendocino Complex (2018). The deadliest and most destructive include the Camp Fire (2018), which destroyed Paradise and killed 85 people; the Tubbs Fire (2017) in wine country; and the January 2025 Palisades and Eaton fires in Los Angeles County.

Causes and trends

Climate change has lengthened fire season and increased extreme fire weather; a century of fire suppression built up fuels in forests adapted to frequent low-severity fire, including Indigenous burning. Responses include prescribed burning, defensible-space rules, utility grid hardening and public-safety power shutoffs.

Smoke and health

Wildfire smoke causes widespread PM2.5 exposure across the western US, with significant respiratory and cardiovascular impacts.
