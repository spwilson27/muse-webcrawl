Steelband

A steelband or steel orchestra is a musical group that plays music on steelpans (also called steel drums), instruments originating in Trinidad and Tobago. Steelbands perform a wide repertoire, from calypso and soca to arrangements of classical music, jazz, and popular songs.

Development

The steelband movement grew out of the tamboo bamboo bands of early 20th-century Port of Spain, which used lengths of bamboo as percussion instruments after African drumming had been repeatedly restricted by colonial authorities. When bamboo was itself curtailed, musicians turned to metal objects — biscuit tins, dustbins, brake drums and, by the late 1930s and 1940s, discarded 55-gallon oil drums, whose ends could be hammered into tuned concave surfaces. Pioneers such as Winston "Spree" Simon, Ellie Mannette, Neville Jules and Anthony Williams developed chromatic instruments and the modern layout of the pan.

Instrumentation and competition

A full steel orchestra contains sections of tenor pans (lead), double tenors, double seconds, guitar pans, cello pans, tenor basses and six-, nine- or twelve-bass instruments, together with a rhythm section ("engine room") of drum kit, congas, iron, and cowbell. The movement's centrepiece is Panorama, the annual competition held at the Queen's Park Savannah in Port of Spain during Carnival, in which large bands of a hundred or more players perform elaborate arrangements. Steelbands are now found throughout the Caribbean diaspora and worldwide, and the steelpan is the national instrument of Trinidad and Tobago.
