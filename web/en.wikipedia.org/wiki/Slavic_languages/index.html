The Slavic languages are a branch of the Indo-European language family spoken natively by the Slavic peoples across much of Central, Eastern and Southeastern Europe and into northern Asia. They descend from Proto-Slavic, itself a close relative of the Baltic languages, and began to diverge as the Slavs expanded from their early medieval homeland during the 6th and 7th centuries CE. With over 315 million native speakers, the group includes Russian, the most widely spoken Slavic language.

Branches

East Slavic

  • Russian (c. 150 million native speakers)
  • Ukrainian (c. 35–40 million)
  • Belarusian (c. 5 million)
  • Rusyn (recognised as a minority language in several states)

West Slavic

  • Lechitic: Polish (c. 40 million), Kashubian, Silesian
  • Czech–Slovak: Czech and Slovak
  • Sorbian: Upper and Lower Sorbian (eastern Germany)

South Slavic

  • Western: Serbian, Croatian, Bosnian, Montenegrin (closely related standards of pluricentric Serbo-Croatian), and Slovene
  • Eastern: Bulgarian and Macedonian
  • Liturgical: Old Church Slavonic and Church Slavonic

History

Proto-Slavic remained remarkably uniform until the Slavic migrations; the first written Slavic language, Old Church Slavonic, was created in the 9th century from the South Slavic dialect of Thessalonica for the mission of Saints Cyril and Methodius, who devised the Glagolitic alphabet, from which Cyrillic later developed. Dialectal differentiation accelerated after the 10th century, but substantial mutual intelligibility survives within each branch.

Features

Slavic languages are typically highly inflected, with up to seven grammatical cases, three genders, and a rich system of verbal aspect (perfective/imperfective pairs) rather than complex tenses. Most preserve numerous consonant clusters and palatalisation contrasts. Word order is relatively free and pragmatically driven. Bulgarian and Macedonian are notable exceptions, having largely lost nominal case inflection and developed a definite article and evidential verb forms under Balkan sprachbund influence.
