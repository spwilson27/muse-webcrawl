Generalized cohomology theory

In mathematics, specifically algebraic topology, a generalized cohomology theory (also called an extraordinary cohomology theory) is a cohomology theory that satisfies the Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms except for the dimension axiom. That is, the coefficient groups may be nonzero in many degrees rather than concentrated in degree zero. Ordinary cohomology is the special case in which the theory is determined by an abelian group of coefficients.

Generalized cohomology theories are represented by spectra in stable homotopy theory

Definition and examples

A (reduced) generalized cohomology theory is a sequence of functors from pointed spaces (or pairs) to graded abelian groups, equipped with suspension isomorphisms and satisfying homotopy invariance, excision, and exactness. Important examples include:

  • Complex K-theory, which classifies stable equivalence classes of complex vector bundles and is 2-periodic by Bott periodicity.
  • Cobordism theories, such as complex cobordism (MU), whose coefficient ring encodes formal group laws.
  • Morava K-theories and Morava E-theories, central to chromatic homotopy theory.
  • Elliptic cohomology and topological modular forms (tmf), connecting topology with modular forms.

Spectra

By the Brown representability theorem, every generalized cohomology theory is represented by a spectrum: a sequence of spaces with structure maps whose homotopy classes of maps give the cohomology groups. Conversely every spectrum determines such a theory. This equivalence places generalized cohomology at the heart of stable homotopy theory.

Spectral sequences

The Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence computes a generalized cohomology theory from ordinary cohomology, with differentials constrained by the structure of the coefficient ring. Characteristic classes, the Adams spectral sequence, and operations such as Steenrod and Adams operations organize computations in these theories.
