Osteomalacia

SpecialtyRheumatology, endocrinology
SymptomsBone pain, muscle weakness, fractures
CausesVitamin D deficiency, phosphate deficiency
Related conditionRickets (in children)

Osteomalacia is the softening of the bones caused by impaired bone metabolism, principally the defective mineralisation of newly formed bone matrix (osteoid). The equivalent disorder in children, where growth plates are affected, is rickets. The most common cause in adults is severe vitamin D deficiency, leading to reduced intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate; rarer causes include renal phosphate wasting, tumour-induced (oncogenic) osteomalacia mediated by FGF23, certain drugs, and inherited disorders.

Unmineralised osteoid accumulates in osteomalacia

Signs and symptoms

Patients typically present with diffuse bone pain and tenderness, proximal muscle weakness with a waddling gait, and fatigue. Bones fracture easily, and radiographs may show Looser zones (pseudofractures) — radiolucent bands perpendicular to the cortex — which are characteristic. Laboratory findings in vitamin-D-deficiency osteomalacia include low or normal calcium, low phosphate, raised alkaline phosphatase and secondary hyperparathyroidism.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on clinical features, biochemistry and imaging; the definitive test is a transiliac bone biopsy showing excess unmineralised osteoid, rarely performed today. Treatment addresses the cause: vitamin D and calcium supplementation for nutritional deficiency, phosphate and active vitamin D for phosphate-wasting forms, and removal of the tumour in tumour-induced cases.
