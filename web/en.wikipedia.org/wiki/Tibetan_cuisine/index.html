Tibetan cuisine

Tibetan cuisine comprises the culinary traditions of the Tibetan people, shaped by the high-altitude environment of the Tibetan Plateau. Staple foods include tsampa (roasted barley flour), yak and mutton, dairy products such as yak butter and cheese, and butter tea.

Staples

Tsampa, eaten by hand mixed with butter tea or formed into dough, is the traditional staple of farmers and nomads. Barley is the principal crop able to ripen at high altitude, and barley beer, called chang, is also widely consumed.

Dishes

Well-known dishes include momos, steamed dumplings filled with meat or vegetables; thukpa, a noodle soup; thenthuk, a hand-pulled noodle soup; and shabhaley, fried meat pies. Butter tea (po cha), made by churning tea with yak butter and salt, is drunk throughout the day for warmth and energy. Meat is often dried for storage by nomadic herders.

Influences

Tibetan cooking shows influences from neighbouring Nepalese and Indian cuisines, particularly in exile communities, where spicier dishes and a wider range of vegetables are common.
