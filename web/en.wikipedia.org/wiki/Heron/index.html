Heron

Scientific classification

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Aves
Order: Pelecaniformes
Family: Ardeidae

The herons are long-legged, long-necked wading birds in the family Ardeidae, which also includes the egrets and bitterns. There are more than sixty species, some referred to as egrets or bitterns rather than herons on the basis of traditional usage rather than biological relationship. Members of the genus Ardea are typically large, with stature ranging from 80 to 100 cm or more in length.

Description

Herons are medium- to large-sized birds with long legs and necks, dagger-like bills, and broad wings. The neck is able to retract into an S-shape because of the modified shape of the sixth cervical vertebra. Unlike cranes and storks, herons fly with their necks retracted rather than extended. They possess powder down, patches of modified feathers that disintegrate into a powder used in preening.

Distribution and habitat

The family has a cosmopolitan distribution, absent only from the high Arctic and some oceanic islands. Herons are strongly associated with wetlands, lakes, rivers, ponds and the sea coast, where they feed on fish, frogs, crustaceans, insects and other aquatic prey, usually caught by patient standing or slow stalking followed by a rapid strike.

Behaviour

Many species are colonial nesters, building bulky stick nests in trees in mixed colonies called heronries. The grey heron (Ardea cinerea) is a familiar and widespread species across Europe and Asia, while the great blue heron fills a similar role in North America.
