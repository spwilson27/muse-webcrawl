Entrepreneur

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An entrepreneur is an individual who creates a new business, bearing most of the risks and enjoying most of the rewards. The process of setting up a business is known as entrepreneurship. The entrepreneur is commonly seen as an innovator, a source of new ideas, goods, services, and business procedures.

The term, borrowed from French, was first defined by the economist Richard Cantillon in the 18th century. Jean-Baptiste Say later described entrepreneurs as those who shift economic resources from an area of lower productivity to an area of higher productivity and greater yield.

Economic theories

Joseph Schumpeter emphasized the entrepreneur's role as an innovator whose new combinations drive "creative destruction", the process by which new products and methods render old ones obsolete. Frank Knight distinguished the entrepreneur by the willingness to bear uncertainty, while Israel Kirzner stressed alertness to previously unnoticed profit opportunities.

Characteristics and financing

Entrepreneurs typically raise capital from personal savings, bank loans, angel investors, or venture capital firms. Many begin in small businesses or startups, and success depends on opportunity recognition, risk management, and the ability to build an organization. Entrepreneurship is widely regarded as a driver of economic growth, employment, and innovation.
