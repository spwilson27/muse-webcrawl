Pavel Nikolayevich Milyukov (27 January 1859 – 31 March 1943) was a Russian historian and liberal politician. He was the founder, leader and most prominent member of the Constitutional Democratic Party (the Kadets), and served as Minister of Foreign Affairs in the Russian Provisional Government in 1917 after the February Revolution.

Early life and scholarship

Milyukov was born in Moscow and studied history at Moscow University, where he became a lecturer. His multi-volume Outlines of Russian Culture established his reputation as a historian. His liberal politics led to clashes with the tsarist authorities: he was dismissed from his university post in 1895 and later imprisoned briefly. He travelled and lectured in the United States, including at the University of Chicago.

Political career

In 1905 Milyukov co-founded the Constitutional Democratic Party, which demanded a constitutional monarchy, civil liberties and land reform. He edited the party newspaper Rech and was elected to the Third and Fourth State Dumas, where he became the most prominent liberal critic of the government. In November 1916 he delivered his famous "stupidity or treason" speech attacking the government's wartime failures.

1917 and exile

After the February Revolution of 1917, Milyukov became Minister of Foreign Affairs in the Provisional Government. His insistence that Russia honour its commitments to the Allies and continue the war — expressed in the so-called Milyukov Note of April 1917 — provoked mass protests and his resignation in May. After the October Revolution he opposed the Bolsheviks, briefly hoped for German support against them, and in 1920 emigrated to Paris, where he edited the émigré newspaper Posledniye Novosti and remained a leading figure of the Russian emigration until his death at Aix-les-Bains in 1943.
