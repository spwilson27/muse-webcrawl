Cutting

Cutting separates a material into parts by applying force through a tool.

Cutting is the separation or opening of a physical object into two or more portions through the application of an acutely directed force. Implements commonly used for cutting are the knife and saw, or in medicine and science the scalpel and microtome. However, any sufficiently sharp object is capable of cutting if it has a hardness sufficiently larger than the object being cut, and if it is applied with sufficient force.

Cutting is a phenomenon of compression and shear: the edges of the tool wedge into the material, and the material fails in shear along the cut plane. Processes such as milling, drilling, turning, sawing, laser cutting, water-jet cutting, and plasma cutting are all industrial cutting methods, differing in the tool, the source of energy, and the materials to which they are suited.

Principles

For a clean cut, stress must be concentrated at the tool edge so that it exceeds the shear strength of the workpiece material. The geometry of the cutting edge, the speed of cutting, lubrication and cooling, and the properties of the material all affect the quality of the cut and the wear of the tool. In machining, the removed material forms chips, and the science of metal cutting studies chip formation, cutting forces, and tool life.

Applications

Cutting is fundamental to cookery (slicing and chopping food), crafts and tailoring, surgery, construction, forestry, and manufacturing. Domestic cutting tools include scissors, knives, and saws; industrial machinery ranges from shears and guillotines to computer-controlled cutting tables for textiles and composites.
