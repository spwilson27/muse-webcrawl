Netscape Navigator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Netscape Navigator
Released15 December 1994
DeveloperNetscape Communications
Final releaseVersion 9 (2007–2008)
SuccessorFirefox (via Mozilla)
[Image: The Netscape Navigator logo: a capital N on a teal hill under a starry sky]

Netscape Navigator was a proprietary web browser that was the dominant browser in the mid-1990s and the flagship product of Netscape Communications. It was based on the code of Mosaic, which several of its authors had created at the University of Illinois.

Navigator 1.0 shipped in December 1994 with innovations — on-the-fly page display as images loaded, then cookies, JavaScript (created by Brendan Eich in ten days in 1995), and eventually the browser as a platform for applications — that essentially defined the web experience. Netscape's August 1995 IPO, in which the stock doubled on its first day, is generally taken as the starting gun of the dot-com boom.

The 'browser wars' followed: Microsoft's Internet Explorer, bundled with Windows, eroded Netscape's share from around 90% to almost nothing by 2001. Netscape's lasting legacies were the release of its code as open source (Mozilla, ancestor of Firefox), JavaScript, the SSL protocol behind HTTPS, and the proof that the web browser was the most important software in the world. AOL acquired Netscape in 1999; the Navigator brand was finally discontinued in 2008.
