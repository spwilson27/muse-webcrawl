Tableware

Tableware is the dishes, cutlery, glassware, and related items used for setting a table, serving food, and dining. The term encompasses dinnerware (plates, bowls, and serving dishes), flatware or cutlery (knives, forks, and spoons), glassware (cups, glasses, and stemware), and serving pieces such as platters, tureens, and pitchers.

Categories

  • Dinnerware — plates, bowls, cups and saucers, typically of ceramic (earthenware, stoneware, porcelain, bone china), glass, metal, wood, or plastic.
  • Cutlery (flatware/silverware) — eating utensils, historically of silver or silver plate and today commonly of stainless steel.
  • Glassware and drinkware — tumblers, goblets, wine glasses, mugs, and cups.
  • Serveware and hollowware — platters, serving bowls, gravy boats, teapots, and similar vessels used to bring food and drink to the table.

History

Tableware has been made from clay, wood, horn, bronze, pewter, and precious metals since antiquity. Chinese porcelain, exported to Europe from the 16th century onward, transformed European dining; European factories at Meissen and elsewhere later developed their own porcelain and refined earthenware (creamware, pearlware). The Industrial Revolution made matched dinner services affordable to the middle classes, and etiquette manuals codified elaborate place settings. In East Asia, tableware traditionally centers on bowls, chopsticks, and ceramic or lacquered spoons.

Place settings

A formal Western place setting arranges the dinner plate centrally, forks to the left, knives and spoons to the right, with glassware above the knives and additional courses set from the outside in. Japanese, Chinese, Korean and other settings follow their own conventions, typically involving rice bowls, soup bowls, chopsticks, and small side dishes.
