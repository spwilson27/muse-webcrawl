Sumner Murray Redstone (born Rothstein; 27 May 1923 – 11 August 2020) was an American billionaire businessman and media magnate. Starting from his family's drive-in theatre business, National Amusements, he built one of the world's largest media empires, controlling Viacom and the CBS Corporation through supervoting shares held by National Amusements.

Early life

Redstone was born in Boston to Belle (née Ostrovsky) and Michael Rothstein, who founded the Northeast Theatre Corporation, the precursor of National Amusements; the family name was anglicised to Redstone (a literal translation of Rothstein). He attended Boston Latin School and Harvard College, graduating in three years, then served in the Second World War in a U.S. Army signals intelligence unit working on Japanese codes. After the war he earned a law degree at Harvard Law School and worked in government and private practice in Washington, D.C., before joining the family business in 1954. In 1979 he survived a fire at Boston's Copley Plaza Hotel, clinging to a window ledge; the burns he suffered required repeated surgery throughout his life.

Building the empire

As head of National Amusements, Redstone expanded the theatre chain and invested shrewdly in media stocks. In 1987 he won a hostile takeover battle for Viacom, then chiefly a cable and syndication company. He then engineered two of the defining media deals of the era: the 1994 acquisition of Paramount Pictures (after a bidding war with Barry Diller) and of Blockbuster Video, and the 2000 merger with CBS. His motto was "content is king". In 2006 Viacom and CBS were split into separate public companies, with Redstone controlling both.

Later years and succession

Redstone's final years were marked by public legal battles over his mental competency, the removal of Viacom CEO Philippe Dauman in 2016, and the rise of his daughter Shari Redstone, who succeeded him as chair of ViacomCBS (now Paramount Global) after the companies re-merged in 2019. He died at his Los Angeles home on 11 August 2020, aged 97. At his death his fortune was estimated at several billion dollars, and he had made major philanthropic donations to law schools, hospitals, and global health causes.
