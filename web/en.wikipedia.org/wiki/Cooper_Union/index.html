The Cooper Union for the Advancement of Science and Art, commonly known as Cooper Union, is a private college in New York City. Founded in 1859 by industrialist and philanthropist Peter Cooper, the school is located at Cooper Square in the East Village neighborhood of Manhattan. It offers undergraduate programs in architecture, fine arts, and engineering through the Irwin S. Chanin School of Architecture, the School of Art, and the Albert Nerken School of Engineering.

History

Peter Cooper, a self-taught inventor and businessman who amassed a fortune in glue manufacturing, iron, and real estate, chartered the institution in 1859 with the conviction that education should be "as free as air and water." The Foundation Building, completed the same year, was among the first structures in the United States to use rolled steel I-beams, supplied by Cooper's own iron works. Its Great Hall became a celebrated public forum: in February 1860, Abraham Lincoln delivered his famous "Right Makes Might" address there, a speech credited with helping secure his nomination for the presidency. Susan B. Anthony, Frederick Douglass, and Mark Twain also spoke in the Great Hall.

For most of its history Cooper Union charged no tuition, funding operations through its endowment, which historically included the land beneath the Chrysler Building. In 2014, facing sustained budget deficits, the college began charging tuition for the first time, a decision that prompted protests, a lawsuit by alumni and students, and a legal settlement with the New York Attorney General. In 2018 the board adopted a plan to restore full-tuition scholarships for all undergraduates over a ten-year period; half-tuition scholarships were provided in the interim, and the school announced progress toward returning to a tuition-free model for students in financial need and, ultimately, for all.

Academics

Cooper Union is highly selective and small, enrolling roughly 900 undergraduates. The Albert Nerken School of Engineering is the largest of the three schools, offering degrees in chemical, civil, computer, electrical, and mechanical engineering, as well as interdisciplinary programs. The School of Art offers a Bachelor of Fine Arts, and the Irwin S. Chanin School of Architecture offers a five-year Bachelor of Architecture. All students take a common core in the humanities and social sciences.

Campus

The campus centers on the historic Foundation Building at 7 East 7th Street and the newer academic building at 41 Cooper Square, completed in 2009 and designed by Thom Mayne of Morphosis. 41 Cooper Square was the first academic building in New York City to receive LEED Platinum certification. A residence hall at 29 Third Avenue houses students nearby.

Notable alumni

Alumni include architect Daniel Libeskind, sculptor Augustus Saint-Gaudens, inventor and Gillette founder King Camp Gillette, artist Lee Krasner, graphic designer Milton Glaser (a visiting figure in the school's orbit), and engineer Edith Clarke, the first woman to earn an electrical engineering degree from MIT and a Cooper Union instructor. Thomas Edison took chemistry classes at Cooper Union early in his career.
