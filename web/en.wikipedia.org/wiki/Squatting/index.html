Squatting

Squatting is the action of occupying an abandoned or unoccupied area of land or a building, usually residential, that the squatter does not own, rent or otherwise have lawful permission to use. The United Nations estimated in 2003 that roughly one billion people worldwide were squatters, most living in informal settlements in the developing world.

History

Squatting has a long history, from peasants occupying common land in early modern England — the Diggers of 1649 being a famous example — to settlers occupying frontier land in the United States. In the twentieth century, squatting movements grew in European cities after the Second World War, driven by housing shortages, and again from the 1960s–1980s, when movements in cities such as Amsterdam (the kraken movement), Berlin, Copenhagen and London combined housing protest with counter-cultural social centres.

Legal status

The law varies widely. Many jurisdictions treat unauthorised occupation as trespass and a civil matter between owner and occupier; some have criminalised squatting in residential buildings, as England and Wales did in 2012. In some legal systems, long occupation can ripen into ownership through adverse possession (sometimes called "squatters' rights"), provided the occupation is open, continuous and without permission for a statutory period.

Motivations

Squatting may be driven by poverty and housing need, by protest against speculation and empty property, or by the desire to create autonomous social and cultural spaces. Owners and authorities often point to property rights, safety and neighbourhood concerns. The topic remains politically contested wherever housing costs rise faster than incomes.
