Pitching moment

In aerodynamics, the pitching moment on an airfoil is the moment (torque) produced by the aerodynamic force on the airfoil if that force is considered to be applied at the aerodynamic center of the airfoil. The pitching moment about the quarter-chord point of a symmetric airfoil is zero and approximately constant for cambered airfoils, a property exploited in stability analysis.

Pitching moment coefficient

The pitching moment is usually expressed as a dimensionless coefficient, Cm, equal to the moment divided by the product of dynamic pressure, wing area, and mean aerodynamic chord. For longitudinal static stability of an aircraft, the pitching moment coefficient must decrease as angle of attack increases, i.e. its derivative with respect to angle of attack must be negative.

Contributions

The total pitching moment of an aircraft is the sum of contributions from the wing, fuselage, nacelles, and the horizontal tail. The tail, acting at a long moment arm, is usually the dominant stabilising contributor, generating a nose-down or nose-up moment that counteracts the destabilising moment of the wing-fuselage combination.
