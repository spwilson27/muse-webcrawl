Tilbury is a port town in the borough of Thurrock, Essex, England, on the north bank of the River Thames opposite Gravesend. It is home to the Port of Tilbury, one of Britain's major ports and the principal port for London's container, paper and bulk cargo trades. The town grew up around the docks, which opened in 1886 as part of the East and West India Docks company, taking over deep-water traffic from the older upriver docks.

History

The surrounding marshes were defended by Tilbury Fort, built under Henry VIII and rebuilt in the 17th century into one of the finest star forts in England; it guarded the Thames approach to London. In 1588 Elizabeth I reviewed her land forces at nearby West Tilbury and delivered her famous Speech to the Troops at Tilbury as the Spanish Armada threatened. In June 1948 the ship Empire Windrush docked at Tilbury carrying Caribbean migrants who became symbolic of post-war migration to Britain — the "Windrush generation". The modern town largely dates from housing built for dock workers in the late 19th and 20th centuries.

The port today

Tilbury handles containers, forest products, grain, aggregates, vehicles and cruise passengers, and was expanded with the Tilbury2 terminal on the site of the former Tilbury power stations. The town is served by Tilbury Town railway station on the London, Tilbury and Southend line, and a passenger ferry still crosses the Thames to Gravesend.
