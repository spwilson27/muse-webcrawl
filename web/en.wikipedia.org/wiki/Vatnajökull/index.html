Vatnajökull


The Vatnajökull ice cap
Geography
Location:Southeast Iceland
Area:~7,700 km² (shrinking)
Thickness:Up to ~1,000 m
National park:Vatnajökull National Park (est. 2008)

Vatnajökull ("the water glacier" in Icelandic) is the largest ice cap in Iceland and one of the largest in Europe by volume. It covers about 7,700 km²—roughly eight percent of the country—in southeastern Iceland, with ice up to about a kilometre thick. Beneath the ice lie several active volcanoes and Iceland's highest peak, Hvannadalshnúkur (2,110 m), on the rim of the Öræfajökull volcano.

Volcanoes beneath the ice

The ice cap overlies part of Iceland's most active volcanic zone. Subglacial eruptions can melt vast quantities of ice and unleash glacial outburst floods called jökulhlaups. Notable events include the 1996 eruption under the Grímsvötn caldera, whose flood, peaking at a discharge rivalling the Amazon River, destroyed bridges on the Skeiðarársandur plain, and the 2010 eruption of Eyjafjallajökull occurred on a smaller neighbouring ice cap. Grímsvötn itself erupted most recently in 2011 with a large ash plume. The Bárðarbunga system's 2014–15 Holuhraun eruption, at the ice cap's northwestern margin, was Iceland's largest since the 18th century.

Environment and tourism

Most of the ice cap and surrounding highlands form Vatnajökull National Park, established in 2008 and inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2019 as a "dynamic nature of fire and ice." Outlet glaciers such as Breiðamerkurjökull calve into the Jökulsárlón glacial lagoon, a celebrated tourist site. The ice cap is retreating rapidly under climate change, losing roughly a metre of thickness per year on average in recent decades.
