Dystrophin

Dystrophin
Gene: DMD (Xp21)
Protein size: 427 kDa
Associated disease: Duchenne muscular dystrophy

Dystrophin is a rod-shaped cytoplasmic protein, part of a protein complex that connects the cytoskeleton of a muscle fibre to the surrounding extracellular matrix through the cell membrane. It is encoded by the DMD gene, one of the largest known human genes, on the X chromosome.

Function

Dystrophin links F-actin to the dystrophin-associated glycoprotein complex in the sarcolemma, stabilising muscle fibres during contraction and preventing membrane damage.

Clinical significance

Mutations causing absence of functional dystrophin cause Duchenne muscular dystrophy, a severe X-linked recessive disorder; partially functional, truncated dystrophin causes the milder Becker muscular dystrophy. Therapies include exon skipping, gene therapy delivering micro-dystrophin, and corticosteroids. See also Muscular dystrophy.
