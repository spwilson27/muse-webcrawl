Merv (Turkmen: Merw; Persian: مرو, Marv), formerly known as the Antiochia in Margiana, was a major oasis city in Central Asia, on the historical Silk Road, located near today's Mary in Turkmenistan. Several cities have existed on this site, which is significant for the interchange of culture and politics at a site of major strategic value.

Ruins of ancient Merv in the Karakum Desert

History

The oasis of the Murghab River delta has been settled since the Bronze Age. The earliest urban centre, Erk Kala, dates to the Achaemenid period, when the region was the satrapy of Margiana. Alexander the Great visited the area, and the Seleucid king Antiochus I rebuilt the city as Antiochia Margiana, surrounded by a great circular wall at Gyaur Kala.

Under the Parthians and then the Sasanians, Merv was an important frontier city facing the steppe. It became a major centre of Nestorian Christianity and Buddhism as well as Zoroastrianism. In 651 CE the last Sasanian shah, Yazdegerd III, was killed near Merv, and the city passed to the Arab Caliphate. Under the Abbasids, Merv was briefly the capital of the caliphate: the revolutionary Abu Muslim organised the Abbasid takeover from Merv in 748, and the caliph al-Ma'mun ruled the empire from the city between 813 and 818.

Merv reached its zenith under the Seljuks in the 11th and 12th centuries, when Sultan Ahmad Sanjar made it his capital. The city was famed for its libraries — one reportedly held 150,000 volumes — and for scholars such as the astronomer and poet Omar Khayyam, who worked at its observatory. The mausoleum of Sultan Sanjar, with its great turquoise dome, still dominates the ruins.

Destruction and aftermath

In 1221 the city surrendered to Tolui, son of Genghis Khan, and was almost completely destroyed; medieval chroniclers reported enormous death tolls, and Merv never recovered its former size. Timur later restored its irrigation, and a smaller city, Abdullah Khan Kala, was built in the 15th century. The oasis passed between Persia, Bukhara, and Khiva before the Russian Empire annexed it in 1884 after the battle of Geok Tepe. In 1999 UNESCO inscribed the State Historical and Cultural Park "Ancient Merv" as a World Heritage Site.
