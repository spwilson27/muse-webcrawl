Free energy suppression conspiracy theory

Free energy suppression
TypeConspiracy theory
Alleged suppressorsGovernments, energy corporations, scientific establishments

Free energy suppression (or suppression of free energy) is a conspiracy theory which claims that technologically viable, pollution-free, no-cost energy sources — such as perpetual motion machines, cold fusion generators or zero-point energy devices — are being suppressed by governments, corporations or powerful interest groups in order to protect existing energy industries and social control structures.

Claims

Advocates claim that a succession of inventors have produced working "over-unity" devices — machines that output more energy than they consume — only to be silenced by patent seizures, raids, threats, bankruptcy or suspicious deaths. Figures commonly cited include Nikola Tesla (whose Wardenclyffe Tower project lost funding), Stanley Meyer (who claimed a "water fuel cell" could power a car with water), and various cold fusion researchers after 1989.

Criticism and status

The claimed devices contradict established physics, in particular the conservation of energy and the laws of thermodynamics, and none has ever been demonstrated under controlled conditions. Patent offices generally refuse applications for perpetual-motion machines without a working model. Skeptics note that the conspiracy explanation functions to explain away the persistent failure of the devices to reach the market: each failure is attributed to suppression rather than to the device not working. Historians of science also observe that genuinely disruptive energy technologies — from steam to solar photovoltaics — were commercialised despite incumbent opposition.

In popular culture

The idea appears frequently in fringe-science literature, documentaries and internet forums, and overlaps with wider conspiracy narratives about suppressed inventions, such as the mythical 100-mile-per-gallon carburetor said to have been bought and buried by oil companies.
