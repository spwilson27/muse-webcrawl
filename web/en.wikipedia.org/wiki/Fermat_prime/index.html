Fermat prime

A Fermat prime is a prime number of the form Fn = 22n + 1, where n is a non-negative integer. They are named after Pierre de Fermat, who conjectured that all numbers of this form are prime.

Known examples

Only five Fermat primes are known: F0 = 3, F1 = 5, F2 = 17, F3 = 257 and F4 = 65,537. Euler showed that F5 = 4,294,967,297 is divisible by 641, disproving Fermat's conjecture. Every Fermat number from F5 to F32 whose factorisation status is settled has been shown to be composite, and whether any more Fermat primes exist is an open problem.

Constructible polygons

Gauss proved that a regular n-gon can be constructed with compass and straightedge if the odd prime factors of n are distinct Fermat primes. This is why the regular pentagon and heptadecagon (17-gon) are constructible.
