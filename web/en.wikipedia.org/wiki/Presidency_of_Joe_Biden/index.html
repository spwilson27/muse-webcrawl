The presidency of Joe Biden began on January 20, 2021, when Joe Biden was inaugurated as the 46th president of the United States, and ended on January 20, 2025. Biden, a Democrat, took office after defeating incumbent Republican president Donald Trump in the 2020 presidential election. His vice president, Kamala Harris, was the first woman and first person of colour to hold the office. Biden was succeeded by Trump, who won the 2024 election.

Domestic policy

Biden took office amid the COVID-19 pandemic and an economic downturn. He signed the $1.9 trillion American Rescue Plan Act in March 2021, overseeing a mass vaccination campaign. Major legislation of his term included the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (2021), the CHIPS and Science Act (2022), and the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, a major investment in clean energy and healthcare cost reduction. His presidency saw high inflation peaking in 2022, followed by disinflation alongside low unemployment and strong job growth. He appointed Ketanji Brown Jackson, the first Black woman on the Supreme Court, in 2022.

Foreign policy

Biden completed the withdrawal of US troops from Afghanistan in August 2021, ending America's longest war amid a chaotic evacuation as the Taliban retook Kabul. After Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, his administration led the Western response, supplying extensive military aid to Ukraine and strengthening NATO, which expanded with the accession of Finland (2023) and Sweden (2024). He also responded to the Hamas attack on Israel of October 2023 and the ensuing Gaza war, supporting Israel while pressing for humanitarian aid.

End of term

Biden initially ran for re-election in 2024 but withdrew from the race in July 2024 following concerns about his age and a widely criticised debate performance; Harris became the Democratic nominee and lost to Trump in November 2024.
