Boeing Everett Factory

The Boeing Everett Factory, officially the Everett Site, is an airplane assembly facility owned by Boeing in Everett, Washington, United States. It sits on the north side of Paine Field and includes the largest building in the world by volume at 13,385,378 m³ (472,370,319 cu ft), covering 399,480 m² (98.7 acres; 4,300,000 sq ft) of floor area. The factory was originally built in 1967 to construct the Boeing 747, and has since been expanded several times to accommodate new programs.

History

Boeing selected the site at Paine Field in 1966 for the 747 program, building the plant in roughly a year; workers who constructed the first 747s while the factory itself was still being completed were dubbed "the Incredibles". The building was enlarged for the 767 (1980s), the 777 (1990s), and the 787 Dreamliner, and it later housed final assembly of the KC-46 tanker. Programs assembled at Everett have included the 747 (through the final 747 delivery in January 2023), 767, 777/777X, and KC-46; the 787 line was consolidated to Charleston, South Carolina, in 2021.

The building

The factory's main building has six huge doors, each about 25 m (82 ft) high, emblazoned with murals of Boeing aircraft. The interior is so large it once formed its own clouds near the ceiling before air-circulation systems were improved — an often-repeated local legend. The facility has no internal supporting columns in the assembly bays, contains its own fire department, security force, childcare center, medical clinic, and more than a mile of pedestrian tunnels. A rail spur delivers fuselage sections and parts; 777X composite wings are made in an adjacent dedicated building using large autoclaves.

Public tours

The Future of Flight Aviation Center & Boeing Tour, opened in 2005 at the southwest corner of the site, offers the only public tour of a commercial jet assembly plant in North America, taking visitors onto catwalks overlooking the production lines. Tours were for many years operated under strict rules (no cameras or electronics). The site also features the adjacent Paine Field, used for first flights and delivery of Everett-built aircraft.
