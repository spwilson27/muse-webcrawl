Winfield Scott Hancock

Winfield Scott Hancock (February 14, 1824 – February 9, 1886) was a United States Army officer and the Democratic nominee for President of the United States in 1880. He served with distinction in the Army for four decades, including the Mexican–American War and as a Union general in the American Civil War, where his leadership on the second and third days at the Battle of Gettysburg earned him the nickname "Hancock the Superb."

Civil War service

A graduate of West Point (1844), Hancock commanded a brigade in the Peninsula Campaign and at Antietam before taking command of a division at Fredericksburg and Chancellorsville. At Gettysburg he led the II Corps, organizing the Union defense on Cemetery Ridge and repulsing Pickett's Charge on July 3, 1863, during which he was severely wounded. He later fought in the Overland Campaign before his wound forced him from field command.

Postwar career and 1880 election

After the war Hancock commanded the Department of the East and was regarded as a fair-minded military governor of Louisiana and Texas during Reconstruction, earning him Democratic support. In 1880 he won the Democratic presidential nomination but lost the general election narrowly to James A. Garfield; the popular vote margin was among the smallest in American history, though Garfield won the Electoral College 214 to 155. Hancock remained on active duty until his death in 1886.
