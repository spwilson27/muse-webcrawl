Carmenère is a wine grape variety originally planted in the Médoc region of Bordeaux, France, where it was used to produce deep red wines and occasionally used for blending purposes in the same manner as Petit Verdot.

History

One of the six original red grapes of Bordeaux, Carmenère was nearly wiped out in Europe by the phylloxera epidemic of the 1860s–1870s and was long considered extinct. Cuttings had been taken to Chile in the 1850s, where the grape thrived but was mistaken for Merlot for more than a century. In 1994 ampelographer Jean Boursiquot identified the Chilean "Merlot" as Carmenère.

Characteristics

The name derives from carmin, French for crimson, for the brilliant red colour of its autumn foliage. Carmenère ripens late and needs a long season; underripe fruit shows strong green, herbal (bell pepper) notes, while fully ripe grapes give soft, plummy wines with red-fruit and spice flavours.

Chile

Chile is now the world's principal producer, with the Colchagua and Maipo valleys especially noted; Carmenère is marketed as Chile's signature grape.
