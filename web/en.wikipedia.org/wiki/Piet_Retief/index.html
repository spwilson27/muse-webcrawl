Piet Retief

Pieter Mauritz Retief (12 November 1780 – 6 February 1838) was a South African Voortrekker leader. Settling in 1814 in the frontier region of the eastern Cape Colony, he assumed command of punitive expeditions in the frontier wars. Following a period of case litigation and the abolition of slavery by the British colonial government, Retief led a large party of Boers north across the Orange River and into Natal in 1837.

The Great Trek

Retief published a manifesto in 1837 setting out the grievances of the emigrant farmers, including insecurity on the frontier, losses from slave emancipation, and dissatisfaction with British rule. Elected governor and commandant-general by the trekkers in Natal, he sought to secure land from the Zulu king Dingane by treaty.

Death

On 6 February 1838, after signing a deed of cession granting land to the trekkers, Retief and about 100 of his men were killed at Dingane's kraal, uMgungundlovu, in what became known as the Retief massacre. His death, and the subsequent Zulu attacks on trekker camps, led to the Battle of Blood River later that year. The city of Pietermaritzburg is commonly said to be named partly in his honour.
