Oil boom

Oil boom
DefinitionA period of rapid growth in petroleum extraction and associated economic activity in a region
Notable examplesPennsylvania oil rush (1859), Texas oil boom (1901–), North Dakota oil boom (2006–2015), Guyana oil boom (2015–)
Related phenomenaBoomtown, resource curse, Dutch disease

An oil boom is a period of rapid expansion in the production of petroleum in a particular area, typically following the discovery of a major oil field or the adoption of a new extraction technology. Oil booms bring sudden wealth, population growth and infrastructure development to producing regions, but are often followed by a bust when prices fall or reserves decline.

Causes

Oil booms are usually triggered by one or more of the following:

  • Discovery of a large new field, such as the Spindletop gusher near Beaumont, Texas, in 1901.
  • Technological change, such as the combination of hydraulic fracturing and horizontal drilling that unlocked shale oil in the Bakken Formation of North Dakota and the Permian Basin of Texas and New Mexico.
  • High prices, which make previously uneconomic reserves profitable to develop.
  • Policy changes, including new leasing regimes or the lifting of export restrictions.

Effects

Economic effects

Boom regions typically experience rapid job creation, rising wages and rents, strain on housing and public services, and heavy investment in roads, pipelines and processing facilities. Places such as Williston, North Dakota, saw their populations roughly double during the Bakken boom. Manufacturing and service businesses expand to supply the industry, but local economies can become dangerously dependent on a single commodity.

Social and environmental effects

The influx of a largely young, male, transient workforce can transform small communities, prompting the growth of temporary "man camps" and increased pressure on schools, hospitals and law enforcement. Environmental effects include flaring of associated gas, wastewater disposal, habitat fragmentation and increased greenhouse gas emissions.

Notable oil booms

  • Pennsylvania oil rush (1859–1870s) — began with Edwin Drake's well at Titusville, the first commercially successful oil well in the United States.
  • Texas oil boom (1901–1940s) — centred on Spindletop and later the giant East Texas Oil Field, discovered in 1930.
  • North Sea oil (1970s–) — transformed the economies of Norway and Scotland, particularly Aberdeen.
  • North Dakota oil boom (2006–2015) — shale-driven boom in the Bakken Formation that made North Dakota the second-largest oil-producing US state.
  • Guyana oil boom (2015–) — following ExxonMobil's Liza discovery offshore Guyana, making it one of the fastest-growing economies in the world.
