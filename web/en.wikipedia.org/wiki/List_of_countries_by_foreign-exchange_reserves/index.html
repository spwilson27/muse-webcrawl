List of countries by foreign-exchange reserves

This is a list of countries by foreign-exchange reserves, based mainly on data from the International Monetary Fund and national central banks. Foreign-exchange reserves are assets held by a central bank in foreign currencies, together with gold, special drawing rights (SDRs) and the country's reserve position in the IMF. Reserves are used to back liabilities, influence monetary policy, intervene in currency markets and cushion external shocks.

Overview

China has held the world's largest reserves since 2006, peaking above US$4 trillion in 2014 including the reserves of Hong Kong. Japan has long been second. Switzerland's reserves grew sharply after 2009 because of Swiss National Bank interventions against the appreciation of the franc. India, Taiwan, Saudi Arabia, South Korea, Russia, Hong Kong and Singapore also hold reserves in the hundreds of billions of dollars.
