Academic publishing

Academic publishing
FieldScholarly communication
Key formatsJournals, books, conference proceedings

[Image: A university press bookshop shelf of scholarly journals and monographs]

Academic publishing is the system through which scholarly research is distributed, principally in academic journals, scholarly books and conference proceedings. It is the main route by which researchers announce results, build reputations and establish priority for discoveries, and it underpins hiring, promotion and grant decisions in universities.

Process and economics

Manuscripts are usually assessed by peer review before acceptance. Traditional publishing charged readers and libraries through subscriptions, while open access models make work free to read and often charge authors or funders instead. Large commercial publishers, university presses and learned societies dominate the field, and debates continue over costs, the reproducibility of published results and the use of citation metrics such as the impact factor.
