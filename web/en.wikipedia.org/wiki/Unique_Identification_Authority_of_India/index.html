The Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI) is a statutory authority established under the Aadhaar (Targeted Delivery of Financial and Other Subsidies, Benefits and Services) Act, 2016, by the Government of India under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology. It is responsible for implementing Aadhaar, the world's largest biometric identification system, which assigns a 12-digit unique identity number to residents of India.

History

The UIDAI was first created on 28 January 2009 as an attached office of the Planning Commission of India. Nandan Nilekani, co-founder of Infosys, was appointed its first chairperson with the rank of a cabinet minister. Enrolment began in September 2010, with the first Aadhaar number issued to a resident of Tembhli village in Maharashtra.

Following legal and political debate over the programme's statutory basis and privacy implications, Parliament passed the Aadhaar Act in 2016, reconstituting the UIDAI as a statutory body. In 2017, in the landmark Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India judgment, the Supreme Court of India recognized privacy as a fundamental right; in 2018 a five-judge bench upheld Aadhaar's constitutionality for welfare delivery while striking down several provisions, including mandatory linking to bank accounts and mobile phones by private entities.

Functions

The UIDAI issues Aadhaar numbers, maintains the Central Identities Data Repository, provides authentication services, and sets standards for enrolment through registrars and enrolment agencies across the country. By the early 2020s more than 1.3 billion Aadhaar numbers had been issued, covering the overwhelming majority of India's adult population. The number is used for direct benefit transfers, financial inclusion through Aadhaar-enabled payment systems, and identity verification for public services.

Structure

The authority consists of a chairperson, two part-time members, and a chief executive officer. It operates regional offices across India and two data centres. Its technology and security policies, including biometric encryption and virtual ID tokens, are designed to limit the exposure of Aadhaar numbers in authentication transactions.
