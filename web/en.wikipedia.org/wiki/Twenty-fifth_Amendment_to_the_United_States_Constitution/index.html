Twenty-fifth Amendment to the United States Constitution

The Twenty-fifth Amendment (Amendment XXV) to the United States Constitution addresses presidential succession and disability. It was proposed by Congress on July 6, 1965, and ratified by the states on February 10, 1967.

Provisions

Section 1 provides that if the President is removed, dies, or resigns, the Vice President becomes President (not merely acting president), resolving the ambiguity left by earlier successions such as John Tyler's in 1841. Section 2 allows a vacancy in the vice presidency to be filled by presidential nomination confirmed by a majority of both Houses of Congress. Section 3 permits the President to voluntarily transfer power to the Vice President as Acting President by written declaration—used when presidents underwent medical procedures, for example by Ronald Reagan (1985, though he denied formally invoking it), George W. Bush (2002, 2007), and Joe Biden (2021). Section 4 allows the Vice President and a majority of the Cabinet (or another body Congress designates) to declare the President unable to serve, with Congress deciding disputes; it has never been invoked.

History and use

The amendment was spurred by the assassination of John F. Kennedy and earlier episodes of presidential illness, including Woodrow Wilson's stroke. Section 2 was first used in 1973 when Gerald Ford replaced the resigning Vice President Spiro Agnew, and again in 1974 when Nelson Rockefeller filled the vice presidency after Ford succeeded Richard Nixon—giving the United States, briefly, both a president and vice president who had not been elected to those offices.
