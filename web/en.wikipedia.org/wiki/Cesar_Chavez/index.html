Cesar Chavez (born Cesario Estrada Chavez; March 31, 1927 – April 23, 1993) was an American labour leader and civil rights activist. A Mexican American farm worker, he co-founded the National Farm Workers Association (NFWA) in 1962 with Dolores Huerta; the organisation later became the United Farm Workers (UFW).

Career

Chavez became a community organiser with the Community Service Organization in the 1950s before founding the NFWA. In 1965 the NFWA joined Filipino workers of the Agricultural Workers Organizing Committee in the Delano grape strike, a five-year struggle that, with a national consumer boycott of table grapes, won union contracts with major growers in 1970. Chavez drew on the nonviolent methods of Gandhi and the civil rights movement, undertaking widely publicised fasts — including a 25-day fast in 1968 and a 36-day "Fast for Life" in 1988 against pesticide use.

Legacy

Chavez's birthday, March 31, is a state holiday or commemorative day in several U.S. states, and he received the Presidential Medal of Freedom posthumously in 1994. His motto, "Sí, se puede" ("Yes, it can be done"), remains a rallying cry of labour and Latino movements. His life also drew later scrutiny and debate, but he remains the most prominent figure of the farm labour movement.
