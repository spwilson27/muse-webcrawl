Trinitron was a brand name used by Japan's Sony for its line of aperture-grille-based cathode-ray tube (CRT) televisions and computer monitors. Launched in 1968, the Trinitron was widely acclaimed for its bright, sharp picture and became one of Sony's most successful product lines, selling well over 100 million units worldwide.

Technology

Unlike the shadow-mask tubes used by competitors, the Trinitron used an aperture grille of vertical wires, allowing more electrons to reach the phosphor screen and producing a brighter image. Its phosphors were arranged in vertical stripes, and the tube face was cylindrical—curved vertically but flat horizontally—which reduced reflections. One or two faint horizontal damper wires, visible on close inspection, held the grille in place.

History

Sony founder Masaru Ibuka championed the design despite its manufacturing difficulty. The Trinitron won numerous awards, including an Emmy in 1973, and became synonymous with high-quality television. Production wound down in the 2000s as flat-panel LCD and plasma displays displaced CRTs; the last Trinitron sets were made in 2008.
