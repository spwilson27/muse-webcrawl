Bag Valve Mask

A bag valve mask (BVM), sometimes known by the proprietary name Ambu bag or generically as a manual resuscitator or self-inflating bag, is a hand-held device commonly used to provide positive-pressure ventilation to patients who are not breathing adequately. It is a normal part of a resuscitation kit for trained healthcare providers and is used extensively in hospitals and by ambulance crews.

Construction and use

The device consists of a flexible air chamber (the bag, about 1.5 litres in adult size) attached through a one-way valve to a face mask; supplemental oxygen can be connected, and an attached reservoir bag lets the delivered oxygen concentration approach 100%. Squeezing the bag forces air through the valve into the patient's lungs; releasing it allows the bag to refill and the patient to exhale through the valve. Effective use generally requires a good mask seal — ideally maintained with a two-handed "E-C clamp" technique by one rescuer while another squeezes the bag.

History

The self-inflating resuscitator was developed in 1953–1956 by the Danish anaesthetist Henning Ruben together with Holger Hesse, whose company Ambu gave the device its common name. The design freed resuscitation from dependence on heavy gas cylinders and electricity.

Risks and limitations

Improper BVM ventilation can cause gastric insufflation and aspiration, hyperventilation, or inadequate ventilation; used correctly with an advanced airway or by trained personnel it is a safe interim means of ventilation until definitive airway management or mechanical ventilation is available.
