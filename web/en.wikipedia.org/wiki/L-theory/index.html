L-theory

L-theory is a branch of algebraic topology, developed largely by C. T. C. Wall in the 1960s and 1970s, that provides the algebraic framework for surgery theory on manifolds. Its central objects are the L-groups Ln(R) of a ring R (with involution), which measure obstructions to performing surgery on manifolds and thereby to classifying manifolds up to h-cobordism or diffeomorphism.

Definition

The L-groups are defined as cobordism groups of quadratic forms over a ring R: in even dimensions L2k(R) is built from nonsingular quadratic forms modulo hyperbolic forms, and in odd dimensions from quadratic formations. Simplifications exist for common rings: over the integers, L4k(ℤ) ≅ ℤ (detected by signature, divided by 8) and L4k+2(ℤ) ≅ ℤ/2 (detected by the Arf invariant), while the odd L-groups of ℤ vanish. The groups are 4-periodic, a fundamental feature of the theory.

Role in surgery theory

In the surgery exact sequence of Browder, Novikov, Sullivan and Wall, the L-groups provide the obstruction to converting a normal map into a homotopy equivalence: a degree-one normal map of manifolds of dimension ≥ 5 can be surgered to a homotopy equivalence if and only if its surgery obstruction in Ln(ℤ[π1]) vanishes. L-theory is the quadratic counterpart of algebraic K-theory (built from symmetric forms), and the comparison between quadratic and symmetric L-theory is a key theme of the work of Ranicki, who recast the theory in terms of algebraic Poincaré complexes.
