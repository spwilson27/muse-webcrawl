PsycINFO is a bibliographic database of abstracts and citations for psychology and related behavioural and social sciences, produced by the American Psychological Association (APA). It is the principal research database for the psychological literature, indexing peer-reviewed journal articles, books and book chapters, dissertations and technical reports from more than 50 countries and in some 29 languages, with records reaching back to the 1880s (and selected older material). Together with its companion products—PsycARTICLES (full-text journals), PsycTESTS (psychological tests and measures), PsycTHERAPY (counselling videos) and the APA PsycNet platform—it forms the core discovery infrastructure for academic psychology.

History

PsycINFO descends from the APA's print abstracting journal Psychological Abstracts, founded in 1927, which for eighty years published summaries of the world psychology literature, famously produced for decades under editor Lloyd V. Jones and printed in thick monthly issues. The APA began computerising the operation in the 1960s—its Psychological Abstracts Information Service was among the earliest online bibliographic services—and launched the electronic database under the name PsycINFO in the 1980s, distributing it successively on tape, CD-ROM and the web. The print Psychological Abstracts ceased in 2006 as searching moved entirely online. The database passed one million records in the 1990s and five million in the 2020s.

Coverage and indexing

Unlike general citation indexes, PsycINFO selects and indexes sources for psychological relevance: core coverage comes from roughly 2,500 journals, supplemented by books from major publishers, doctoral dissertations (from Dissertation Abstracts International) and reports. Each record carries a structured abstract and rich metadata fields—including the age group, population and methodology of the study—and is indexed with terms from the APA Thesaurus of Psychological Index Terms, a controlled vocabulary of some 8,400 authorised descriptors (with entry terms) that is revised as the science evolves, enabling precise subject searching (for example distinguishing the descriptor "Emotional Trauma" from free-text mentions). A classification-code scheme assigns each record to one of 22 broad categories and finer subfields, from "History & Systems" to "Clinical Psychological Testing".

Use

PsycINFO is the standard starting point for literature reviews and meta-analyses in psychology, clinical science, psychiatry, neuroscience, education and social work, and search strategies in systematic reviews routinely specify it alongside PubMed and other databases. Access is by institutional subscription through APA PsycNet or licensed vendor platforms such as EBSCOhost, ProQuest and Ovid, each offering the same underlying records with different search interfaces. Because PsycINFO indexes only the abstract and metadata—not full text—it functions as a discovery layer, with link resolvers connecting users to articles their libraries hold.
