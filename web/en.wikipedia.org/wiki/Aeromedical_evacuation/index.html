Aeromedical evacuation (AE) is the system and process of moving patients, under the supervision of medical personnel, by fixed-wing or rotary-wing aircraft from one location to another for a higher level of care. It is a core function of military medicine and is also provided commercially by air ambulance operators for civilian patients.

History

Air evacuation began in the First World War with improvised carriage of casualties in aircraft, and was organised on a large scale in the Second World War, when the U.S. Army Air Forces moved over a million patients by air with very low in-flight mortality, aided by flight nurses. Helicopter evacuation matured in the Korean War (immortalised by M*A*S*H units) and Vietnam, where "Dustoff" crews dramatically cut the time from wounding to surgery. Modern strategic AE uses large transports such as the C-17 Globemaster III configured as flying hospitals.

Organisation

Patients are categorised by urgency and classification (litter or ambulatory), regulated through patient-movement control centres, and cared for in flight by aeromedical crews; the most critical patients travel with Critical Care Air Transport Teams (CCATT) — a physician, critical-care nurse and respiratory therapist with intensive-care equipment. Civilian aeromedical evacuation covers inter-hospital transfers, repatriation of injured travellers and disaster response.
