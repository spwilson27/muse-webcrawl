Beijing Central Axis

The Beijing Central Axis (Chinese: 北京中轴线) is a north–south axis line, about 7.8 kilometres long, that runs through the historic centre of Beijing, China. Established under the Yuan dynasty and developed through the Ming and Qing dynasties, the axis organised the imperial capital's palaces, ceremonial altars, gates and public buildings according to classical Chinese ideals of urban order. In 2024 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as "Beijing Central Axis: A Building Ensemble Exhibiting the Ideal Order of the Chinese Capital."

Layout

From north to south, the principal sites along the axis include the Bell and Drum Towers, Wanning Bridge, Jingshan Park, the Forbidden City, the Upright Gate (Duanmen), Tiananmen, the Outer City gates including Zhengyangmen, the Tianqiao area, and Yongdingmen at the southern end. The Temple of Heaven and the Altar of Agriculture (Xiannongtan) stand symmetrically to the east and west of the axis's southern section. In 2008 the axis was extended northward to the Olympic Green, with the Olympic Park, Bird's Nest stadium and Water Cube sited along its line.

Significance

The axis embodied the cosmological and political principles of the traditional Chinese capital described in the Kaogongji, with the emperor's palace at the centre facing south. Urban planners such as Liang Sicheng praised it as an outstanding achievement of city planning. Its World Heritage inscription recognises fifteen component sites as testimony to the dynastic planning tradition and to the continuing evolution of Beijing.
