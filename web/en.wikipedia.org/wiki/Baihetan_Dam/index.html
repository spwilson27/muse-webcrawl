Overview

The Baihetan Dam is a large hydroelectric dam on the Jinsha River, the upper course of the Yangtze, on the border between Sichuan and Yunnan provinces in China. Its power station, at 16,000 MW from sixteen 1,000-MW turbines — the largest single-unit turbines ever built — is the second-largest in the world after the Three Gorges Dam. The dam is a 289-metre double-curvature concrete arch dam, key project of China's west-to-east power transmission programme.

Construction

Built by the China Three Gorges Corporation, Baihetan was the first dam to use low-heat cement throughout its whole structure to control thermal cracking. First units entered service in June 2021 for the Communist Party centenary, and the station was fully commissioned in December 2022. It displaces tens of millions of people only in small numbers by Chinese mega-dam standards — resettlement was in the tens of thousands — and its output serves the industrial east.
