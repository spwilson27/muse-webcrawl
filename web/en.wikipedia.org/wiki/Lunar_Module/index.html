Apollo Lunar Module

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Lunar Module Eagle in lunar orbit, Apollo 11.

The Apollo Lunar Module (LM, pronounced 'lem') was the lunar lander spacecraft of the Apollo program, built by Grumman. It carried two astronauts from lunar orbit to the surface of the Moon and back — the only crewed vehicle ever to operate beyond low Earth orbit as a standalone spacecraft, and the first true spaceship, designed never to fly in an atmosphere.

The ungainly, foil-covered craft had two parts: a descent stage with the engine and landing gear that stayed behind on the surface, and an ascent stage with the cramped crew cabin from which the astronauts stood to fly. Fifteen were built; ten flew in space. The most famous, Apollo 11's Eagle, landed in the Sea of Tranquility on 20 July 1969. In April 1970 Apollo 13's LM Aquarius served as a lifeboat that kept its crew alive after the service module explosion.
