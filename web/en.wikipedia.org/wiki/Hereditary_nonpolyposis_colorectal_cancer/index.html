Hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer (HNPCC), also known as Lynch syndrome, is an inherited condition that greatly increases the risk of colorectal cancer and cancers of the endometrium, ovary, stomach, small intestine, urinary tract, and other organs. It is the most common cause of inherited colorectal cancer, accounting for roughly 2–4% of all colorectal cancer cases.

Cause

HNPCC is caused by inherited mutations in DNA mismatch repair genes — most commonly MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2 (and deletions in EPCAM affecting MSH2). Loss of mismatch repair produces microsatellite instability, accelerating mutation and cancer development. The condition is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. Unlike familial adenomatous polyposis, HNPCC is not characterised by hundreds of colonic polyps — hence "nonpolyposis".

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis combines family-history criteria (the Amsterdam criteria) with tumour testing for microsatellite instability and immunohistochemistry, followed by germline genetic testing. Management centres on intensive surveillance — frequent colonoscopy beginning in early adulthood — which substantially reduces mortality, and consideration of risk-reducing surgery for some patients. The condition is named after Henry T. Lynch, the physician who described the cancer-prone families in the 1960s.
