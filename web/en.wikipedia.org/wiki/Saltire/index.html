Saltire

A saltire, also called Saint Andrew's Cross or the crux decussata, is a heraldic symbol in the form of a diagonal cross, like the shape of the letter X in the Roman alphabet. It corresponds to the ordinary in heraldry known as a bend sinister and bend placed together. The saltire is the traditional emblem of Saint Andrew, who is said to have been crucified on an X-shaped cross, and it forms the basis of several national and regional flags.

Uses

Most famously, the flag of Scotland is a white saltire on an azure field, one of the oldest national flags in continuous use; tradition associates it with a vision of a white cross in the sky before the Battle of Athelstaneford in 832. The saltire appears in the Union Jack, where Saint Andrew's cross is combined with the crosses of Saint George and Saint Patrick (the latter itself a red saltire). Other examples include the flags of Jamaica (gold saltire), Alabama, Florida and Mississippi, the Cross of Burgundy used by Spain's empire, and the Russian naval ensign. In architecture and furniture the term describes X-shaped supports, and road signs use a saltire-like crossbuck at level crossings.
