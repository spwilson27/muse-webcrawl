Abebe Bikila (7 August 1932 – 25 October 1973) was an Ethiopian marathon runner who became the first athlete to win the Olympic marathon twice, and the first black African Olympic gold medallist. He is famous for winning the 1960 Rome marathon running barefoot, just weeks after being added to the Ethiopian team.

Olympic victories

In Rome on 10 September 1960, Bikila — a member of Emperor Haile Selassie's Imperial Guard — ran barefoot over the cobblestones and won in a world-record 2:15:16, passing the Arch of Constantine near the finish, a powerful symbol in the city of Ethiopia's former occupier Italy. In Tokyo in 1964, only 40 days after an appendectomy, he won again in a world record of 2:12:11, this time wearing shoes, and famously performed calisthenics after finishing, seemingly fresh.

Later life

Bikila's career ended after the 1968 Mexico City marathon, which he left with a leg injury. In 1969 a car accident left him paralysed from the waist down; he took up archery and other wheelchair sports. He died of a cerebral haemorrhage in 1973, aged 41, and is celebrated as a national hero in Ethiopia; the national stadium in Addis Ababa bears his name.
