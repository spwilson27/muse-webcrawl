Telephoto lens

A telephoto lens is a specific type of long-focus camera lens in which the physical length of the lens is shorter than its focal length. This is achieved by a special optical arrangement: a positive front lens group combined with a negative rear group (a telephoto group), which magnifies the effective focal length. In common usage, "telephoto" loosely refers to any long-focus lens.

Properties

Telephoto lenses have a narrow angle of view and make distant subjects appear magnified. They produce a characteristic compression of perspective, in which background elements appear closer and larger relative to the subject. Long focal lengths also yield a shallow depth of field, isolating subjects against blurred backgrounds.

Categories

For 35 mm format, medium telephotos (around 85–135 mm) are favoured for portraiture; telephotos of 200–600 mm are used for sports and wildlife; and extreme "super-telephoto" designs exceed 600 mm. Inverse-telephoto (retrofocus) designs apply the opposite principle for wide-angle lenses on SLR cameras.

Uses and drawbacks

Telephoto lenses are essential for wildlife, sports, surveillance and astrophotography. Their drawbacks include size, weight and cost, along with vulnerability to camera shake — mitigated by image stabilisation, fast apertures and supports such as monopods.
