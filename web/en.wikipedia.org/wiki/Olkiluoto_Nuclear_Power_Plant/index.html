The Olkiluoto Nuclear Power Plant (Finnish: Olkiluodon ydinvoimalaitos) is one of Finland's two nuclear power plants, the other being the Loviisa Nuclear Power Plant. It is located on Olkiluoto Island, on the shore of the Gulf of Bothnia in the municipality of Eurajoki, western Finland. The plant is owned and operated by Teollisuuden Voima Oyj (TVO).

Together, the plant's three units supply a substantial share of Finland's electricity — roughly 30 percent of national consumption following the commissioning of the third unit.

Units 1 and 2

Units 1 and 2 consist of two boiling water reactors (BWRs) of Swedish design, supplied by ASEA-Atom (later ABB Atom). Olkiluoto 1 began commercial operation in 1978 and Olkiluoto 2 in 1980. Through successive modernisation and power uprate programmes, each unit's output has been raised from the original 660 MW to approximately 890 MW.

Unit 3 (OL3 EPR)

Olkiluoto 3 is an EPR pressurised water reactor built by a consortium of Areva NP and Siemens. Construction began in August 2005 under a fixed-price turnkey contract of about €3 billion. The project was beset by delays and cost overruns, with the final cost estimated at around €11 billion, and regular electricity production did not begin until April 2023 — some fourteen years behind the original schedule. With a net output of about 1,600 MW, it was at completion Europe's largest nuclear reactor unit and the first EPR to enter service in Europe.

Spent fuel repository

The plant site is also home to Onkalo, the deep geological repository for spent nuclear fuel being constructed by Posiva adjacent to the power plant, intended to be the world's first permanent disposal facility for high-level radioactive waste.
