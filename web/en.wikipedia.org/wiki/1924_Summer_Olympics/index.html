The 1924 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the VIII Olympiad, were held in Paris, France, from May 4 to July 27, 1924. Paris became the first city to host the Olympics twice, having staged the 1900 Games; the award honoured Baron Pierre de Coubertin, founder of the modern Olympic movement, who retired after these Games. The Olympic motto Citius, Altius, Fortius ("Faster, Higher, Stronger") was used for the first time, and the first Olympic Village was built.

Highlights

The "Flying Finns" dominated distance running: Paavo Nurmi won five gold medals, including the 1,500 m and 5,000 m finals held less than an hour apart, and Ville Ritola won four golds. British runners Harold Abrahams and Eric Liddell won the 100 m and 400 m respectively — a story later dramatised in the film Chariots of Fire. American swimmer Johnny Weissmuller won three golds, and the marathon was run over the now-standard distance of 42.195 km. A separate Winter Sports Week held in Chamonix earlier that year was retrospectively recognised as the first Winter Olympic Games.

Participating nations and medal table

Forty-four nations competed; Germany was still excluded. The United States topped the medal table with 45 gold and 99 total medals, followed by Finland (14 golds) and host nation France. The closing ceremony tradition of three flags — the Greek, host, and next host flags — began at these Games.
