Heathrow Airport (IATA: LHR, ICAO: EGLL) is the main international airport serving London, England. It is the largest of the six international airports in the London airport system. The airport is owned and operated by Heathrow Airport Holdings. In 2024 Heathrow handled nearly 84 million passengers, a record for the airport, making it the busiest airport in Europe and the fourth-busiest in the world by passenger traffic.

History

The airport originated as a small grass airfield, Great West Aerodrome, on land near the village of Heathrow in the 1930s. It was requisitioned by the Air Ministry during the Second World War and developed as an RAF station, though it was never used operationally by the RAF. It transferred to civil control on 1 January 1946 and opened for passenger flights that year, with the first terminals housed in former military tents and then prefabricated buildings.

Growth through the jet age led to the construction of the central terminals, Terminal 4 (1986) and Terminal 5 (2008). Terminal 1 closed in 2015; its site was absorbed by an expanded Terminal 2, which reopened in 2014 as The Queen's Terminal.

Facilities

Heathrow has two parallel east–west runways and four operational passenger terminals. It is the main hub of British Airways and a base for Virgin Atlantic. The airport is connected to central London by the Piccadilly line of the London Underground, the Heathrow Express, and the Elizabeth line.

Expansion

Proposals for a third runway have been debated for decades; a northwest runway scheme received government support in 2016 and parliamentary approval in 2018, though legal challenges and the COVID-19 pandemic delayed the project, which the airport continues to pursue.
