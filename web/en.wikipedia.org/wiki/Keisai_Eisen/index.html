Keisai Eisen

Keisai Eisen (渓斎英泉)
Born: 1790, Edo, Japan
Died: 1848
Field: ukiyo-e woodblock prints and painting
Known for: bijin-ga, landscapes of the Kisokaidō

Keisai Eisen (1790–1848) was a Japanese ukiyo-e artist of the late Edo period, specialising in bijin-ga (pictures of beautiful women) and known for an elegant, sometimes sensuous depiction of courtesans and townswomen. Born Ikeda Eisen in Edo, he was the son of a calligrapher and studied painting before turning to print design.

Work

Eisen designed many bijin-ga series in the 1820s and 1830s, often printed with luxurious techniques and rich blue pigments. He also wrote popular literature and a continuation of the biographical compilation Ukiyo-e Ruikō. With Hiroshige he contributed to the famous landscape series Sixty-nine Stations of the Kisokaidō (c. 1835–38), for which Eisen designed the first 24 stations before Hiroshige completed the set.

Life

Contemporary gossip portrayed Eisen as hard-drinking and dissolute; he was said even to have kept a brothel, which burned down. Whatever the truth, his prints of the pleasure quarters he knew so well remain among the finest bijin-ga of the period.
