Les Paul

Les Paul (born Lester William Polsfuss; 9 June 1915 – 12 August 2009) was an American jazz and country guitarist, songwriter, and inventor. He pioneered the solid-body electric guitar and multitrack recording, and his name is borne by the Gibson Les Paul, one of the most famous guitar models in the world.

Career

Born in Waukesha, Wisconsin, Paul performed on radio from his teens and developed a fast, fluid guitar style influenced by Django Reinhardt. Seeking greater sustain and freedom from feedback, in 1940–41 he built "The Log" — a solid pine block with strings and pickups, fitted with hollow body halves for appearance — one of the first solid-body electric guitars. With his wife Mary Ford he scored a string of 1950s hits, including "How High the Moon" and "Bye Bye Blues", using overdubbing ("sound on sound"), echo, and tape-speed effects he had devised; he also persuaded Ampex to build the first eight-track recorder, the "Sel-Sync".

The Gibson Les Paul

After Fender's solid-body success, Gibson worked with Paul to launch the Les Paul Model in 1952. The single-cutaway, carved-top guitar, especially the 1958–60 "sunburst" Standard, became an icon of rock music, played by generations of guitarists. Paul continued performing weekly at New York's Iridium Jazz Club into his nineties. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1988 and died in White Plains, New York, aged 94.
