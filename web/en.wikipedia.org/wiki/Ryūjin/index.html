Ryūjin (龍神, "dragon god"), also known as Ryūō (龍王, "dragon king"), is a tutelary deity of the sea in Japanese mythology and Shinto belief. A great dragon with a vast undersea palace, Ryūjin rules the tides through magical tide jewels and is served by sea creatures, with sea turtles acting as his messengers. He is closely associated with the older sea god Watatsumi, and the two are often identified with one another.

Mythology

Ryūjin lives in Ryūgū-jō, a palace of red and white coral beneath the sea. From there he controls the tides with the kanju (干珠, "ebb jewel") and manju (満珠, "flow jewel"). In one legend, the Empress Jingū used the tide jewels to manipulate the tides during her campaign in Korea, first beaching the Korean fleet with the ebb jewel and then drowning its crews with the flow jewel.

Urashima Tarō

Ryūjin features in the famous folktale of Urashima Tarō, a fisherman who rescues a turtle — in some versions a messenger of the dragon king, in others his daughter in disguise — and is rewarded with a visit to Ryūgū-jō. He spends what seem to be a few days in the palace, but on returning home discovers that centuries have passed on land.

Family and descendants

Ryūjin's daughter Toyotama-hime married Hoori, the hunter grandson of the sun goddess Amaterasu; their son Ugayafukiaezu was in turn the father of Emperor Jimmu, Japan's legendary first emperor — making Ryūjin an ancestor of the imperial line. The dragon god is worshipped at shrines across Japan, including the Ryūgū shrines and, in some traditions, at Enoshima and Itsukushima.
