Rason Special Economic Zone

The Rason Special Economic Zone, earlier called the Rajin-Sonbong Economic Special Zone, is a special economic zone in Rason, in north-eastern North Korea, near the borders with China and Russia. It was established in 1991 as the country's first special economic zone, in an attempt to attract foreign investment while keeping it geographically contained.

Geography and transport

The zone encompasses the cities of Rajin and Sonbong (Chongjin's former neighbour port area) and includes the ice-free port of Rajin, which has been leased in part to Chinese and Russian interests. Russia uses the port and a rail connection from Khasan for coal exports; a joint Russian–North Korean venture rebuilt the port's Pier No. 3.

Development

Development has been slow and hampered by international sanctions, infrastructure shortages and North Korea's broader economic difficulties. Nevertheless, the zone hosts foreign-invested hotels, a casino aimed at Chinese tourists, markets, and small joint-venture factories. Rason has periodically been promoted by Pyongyang as a showcase for limited economic experimentation.
