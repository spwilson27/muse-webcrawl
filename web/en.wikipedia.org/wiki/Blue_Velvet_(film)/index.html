Blue Velvet is a 1986 American neo-noir mystery thriller film written and directed by David Lynch. Blending psychological horror with film noir, the film stars Kyle MacLachlan, Isabella Rossellini, Dennis Hopper and Laura Dern. It takes its title from Bobby Vinton's 1963 recording of the song "Blue Velvet".

Plot

College student Jeffrey Beaumont returns to his hometown of Lumberton, North Carolina, after his father suffers a stroke. In a field he discovers a severed human ear, which leads him to investigate nightclub singer Dorothy Vallens and the violent criminal Frank Booth, who has kidnapped Dorothy's husband and son. Jeffrey's journey contrasts the town's sunny suburban surface with a criminal underworld of violence and depravity.

Reception and legacy

Initially dividing critics and audiences for its disturbing content, the film earned Lynch an Academy Award nomination for Best Director and has since come to be regarded as one of his masterpieces and one of the greatest films of the 1980s. Dennis Hopper's portrayal of Frank Booth is frequently cited among the most memorable screen villains. The film was selected for preservation in the U.S. National Film Registry in 2008.
