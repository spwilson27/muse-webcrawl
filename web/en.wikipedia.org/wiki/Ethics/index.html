Ethics, or moral philosophy, is the branch of philosophy that systematises, defends and recommends concepts of right and wrong conduct. Major frameworks include consequentialism (judge by outcomes — utilitarianism), deontology (duty and rules — Kant), and virtue ethics (character — Aristotle).

Applied ethics

Ethical reasoning governs medicine (consent, triage), research, business, war and new technologies such as AI and genetic editing. Plato and his successors began Western ethics; religious traditions worldwide developed parallel systems. Disagreement — over abortion, euthanasia, animal rights — keeps ethics central to government and law.
