McLean (often spelled McLean, /məˈkleɪn/) is an unincorporated community and census-designated place in Fairfax County, Virginia, United States. Located in Northern Virginia just northwest of Washington, D.C., across the Potomac River, McLean is home to many diplomats, military officers, members of Congress, and high-ranking government officials, and is known as one of the wealthiest communities in the United States.

History

The community takes its name from John Roll McLean, the former publisher and owner of The Washington Post, who, with Stephen Benton Elkins and French aristocrat Jean-Pierre Guenard, built the Great Falls and Old Dominion Railroad. The railway connected McLean to downtown Washington in 1906, spurring suburban development. The community was formed in 1910 when the villages of Lewinsville and Langley merged.

Geography and notable sites

McLean is bordered by the Potomac River to the north, Arlington County to the east, and Vienna and Tysons to the west and south. Notable locations include:

  • Langley, home to the headquarters of the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) at the George Bush Center for Intelligence
  • Chain Bridge and Scott's Run Nature Preserve along the Potomac
  • Hickory Hill, the former home of Robert F. Kennedy
  • Salona, a historic estate now a park
  • Clemyjontri Park, an inclusive children's playground

Demographics and economy

McLean is one of the most affluent communities in the U.S., with a median household income well over $200,000. ZIP code 22101 is frequently listed among the most expensive in the country. The nearby Tysons area hosts the headquarters of firms such as Capital One and Hilton, and McLean itself hosts the Mars, Incorporated headquarters.

Education

Public schools are operated by Fairfax County Public Schools, including Langley High School and McLean High School. Private schools in the area include The Madeira School and The Potomac School.
