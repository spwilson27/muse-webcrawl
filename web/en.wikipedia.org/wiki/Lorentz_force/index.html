Lorentz force

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lorentz force
DefinitionForce on a charged particle in electric and magnetic fields
Named afterHendrik Antoon Lorentz
EquationF = q(E + v × B)

In physics, particularly in electromagnetism, the Lorentz force (or electromagnetic force) is the combination of electric and magnetic force on a point charge due to electromagnetic fields. A particle of charge q moving with a velocity v in an electric field E and a magnetic field B experiences a force:

F = q(E + v × B)

The equation was developed by Hendrik Antoon Lorentz in the 1890s, building on earlier formulations by Maxwell, Heaviside and others. Together with Maxwell's equations, the Lorentz force law completes the theory of classical electromagnetism.

Effects and applications

Because the magnetic part of the force is always perpendicular to the velocity, a charged particle in a uniform magnetic field moves in a helix; this principle underlies cyclotrons and other particle accelerators, mass spectrometers, and the confinement of plasma in magnetic fusion devices. The force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field — the sum of Lorentz forces on its moving charges — drives electric motors. The Hall effect, used in magnetic field sensors, is also a manifestation of the Lorentz force in conductors.
