Dadaab

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dadaab (Somali: Dhadaab) is a semi-arid town in Garissa County, Kenya. It is the site of a UNHCR base hosting a cluster of refugee camps, which for many years formed one of the largest refugee camp complexes in the world. The camps were established beginning in 1991 to receive people fleeing the civil war in Somalia, and later also hosted refugees from other countries in the region.

History

The first camps—Ifo, Dagahaley and Hagadera—were set up in 1991–1992 as Somalis fled the collapse of the Siad Barre regime. Designed for around 90,000 people, the complex at times held several hundred thousand residents, with numbers peaking during the 2011 East Africa drought and famine, when tens of thousands of new arrivals came in a matter of months. Additional camps, Ifo II and Kambioos, were opened in 2011 to relieve congestion.

Dadaab became a semi-permanent settlement: generations of residents have been born and raised there, with markets, schools and mosques, though refugees in Kenya have historically faced restrictions on movement and formal employment under the country's encampment policy.

Closure debates and repatriation

The Kenyan government repeatedly announced intentions to close Dadaab, notably in 2016 following attacks attributed to al-Shabaab, and again in later years, prompting litigation and international concern. A tripartite agreement between Kenya, Somalia and UNHCR provided for voluntary repatriation, and tens of thousands of residents returned to Somalia. In subsequent years the population fluctuated, rising again with drought-driven arrivals in the early 2020s.

In 2023–2024 Kenya moved toward a new refugee law framework (the Shirika Plan) intended to integrate refugees into host communities and transform camps such as Dadaab into integrated settlements.
