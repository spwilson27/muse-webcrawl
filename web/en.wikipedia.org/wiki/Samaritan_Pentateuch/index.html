The Samaritan Pentateuch, also called the Samaritan Torah, is the sacred scripture of the Samaritans, consisting of the first five books of the Hebrew Bible written in the Samaritan alphabet. It differs from the Jewish Masoretic Text in some 6,000 readings, many of them minor matters of spelling and grammar, but some theologically significant. The Samaritans regard it as the only true and uncorrupted Torah, and it constitutes the whole of their biblical canon.

Distinctive readings

The most famous doctrinal variant occurs in Deuteronomy 27:4, where the Samaritan text commands building an altar on Mount Gerizim — the Samaritan holy place — rather than on Mount Ebal as in the Masoretic Text; the Samaritan version of the Ten Commandments incorporates a commandment to worship on Gerizim. Other characteristic features include harmonising expansions borrowed from parallel passages (many of which agree with the Greek Septuagint against the Masoretic Text), the removal of anthropomorphisms, and small chronological differences in the genealogies of Genesis.

Manuscripts and study

The community's most treasured manuscript is the Abisha Scroll, kept at the Samaritan synagogue on Mount Gerizim and attributed by tradition to Abisha, great-grandson of Aaron; scholars date its oldest sections to roughly the 12th–13th centuries AD. The text became known to European scholarship in the 17th century, when manuscripts were brought from Damascus and Nablus; Pietro della Valle obtained a copy in 1616, and the text was printed in the Paris Polyglot Bible of 1645. Initially championed as superior to the Masoretic Text, it was later, following Wilhelm Gesenius (1815), judged largely secondary and harmonistic — though the discovery of similar "pre-Samaritan" texts among the Dead Sea Scrolls showed that its base text is an authentic ancient recension, expanded by specifically Samaritan edits.

The Samaritan Pentateuch remains a primary witness for the textual criticism of the Torah and is read liturgically in Samaritan Hebrew pronunciation.
