Gnaeus Naevius

Gnaeus Naevius (/ˈniːviəs/; c. 270 – c. 201 BC) was a Roman epic poet and dramatist of the Old Latin period. A Campanian by birth, he served in the First Punic War, about which he wrote the epic Bellum Punicum in Saturnian verse. He is one of the earliest known Latin authors after Livius Andronicus.

Bornc. 270 BC, Campania
Diedc. 201 BC, Utica
OccupationPoet, playwright
Notable workBellum Punicum

Works

Naevius wrote tragedies on Greek and Roman subjects (fabula praetexta), including Romulus and Clastidium, and some thirty comedies adapted from Greek models, of which fragments survive through quotations by later grammarians. His Bellum Punicum, divided by the grammarian Lampadio into seven books, wove contemporary history with the mythic wanderings of Aeneas, influencing Virgil.

Conflict and exile

His outspoken verses satirising the powerful Metelli family — 'by fate the Metelli become consuls at Rome' — reportedly led to imprisonment; he was freed through tribunician intervention but was later exiled to Utica in North Africa, where he died. His self-composed epitaph, preserved by Aulus Gellius, boasts that after his death Rome forgot how to speak Latin.
