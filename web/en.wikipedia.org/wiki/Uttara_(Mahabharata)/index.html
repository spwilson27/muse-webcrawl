Uttara (Sanskrit: उत्तर, "the northern one" / "the younger"), also called Uttara Kumara, is a prince of the Matsya kingdom in the Hindu epic Mahabharata. He is the son of King Virata and Queen Sudeshna, and the brother of Princess Uttarā. Uttara is best known for his role in the Virata Parva, the epic's fourth book, which recounts the Pandavas' year of incognito exile at his father's court.

Role in the epic

During the Pandavas' thirteenth year of exile, spent in disguise at Virata's court, the Kaurava army — suspecting the Pandavas' presence — raids Matsya's cattle in alliance with the Trigartas. Boastful Prince Uttara declares he will drive off the raiders, but insists on having the eunuch dance-teacher Brihannala as his charioteer. Brihannala is in fact Arjuna in disguise. When Uttara sees the vast Kaurava host and the great warriors commanding it, his courage collapses and he tries to flee; Arjuna reveals the hidden weapons in the shami tree, and Uttara serves as his charioteer while Arjuna single-handedly routs the Kaurava army and recovers the cattle.

Death in the Kurukshetra War

At the great Kurukshetra War Uttara fights on the Pandava side. On the first day of battle he attacks the veteran Shalya, king of Madra, and is killed by him, becoming one of the war's earliest notable casualties. His sister Uttarā marries Arjuna's son Abhimanyu; through her son Parikshit the Kuru line continues.
