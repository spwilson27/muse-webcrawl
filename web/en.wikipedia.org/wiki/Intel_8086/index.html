Intel 8086

The Intel 8086 is a 16-bit microprocessor developed by Intel and released in 1978. It gave rise to the x86 architecture that dominates personal computing. The chip contained about 29,000 transistors and ran initially at 5 MHz.

Architecture

The 8086 has a 20-bit address bus, allowing it to address 1 MB of memory through a segmented memory model in which addresses are computed from a segment register shifted left four bits plus an offset. Its internal architecture is divided into a bus interface unit and an execution unit, with a small instruction prefetch queue — an early form of pipelining.

Legacy

A cost-reduced variant, the 8088 with an 8-bit external data bus, was chosen for the original IBM Personal Computer in 1981, cementing the architecture's success. Successors including the 80186, 80286, and 80386 extended the design while retaining compatibility, and x86 processors remain in wide use today.
