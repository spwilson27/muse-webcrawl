Chūshingura

Chūshingura (忠臣蔵, "Treasury of Loyal Retainers") is the title given to fictionalised accounts in Japanese literature, theatre and film of the historical incident involving the forty-seven rōnin and their vendetta for their master, Asano Naganori.

The historical incident

In 1701 Asano, the young daimyō of Akō, attacked the court official Kira Yoshinaka in Edo Castle after repeated provocations, and was ordered to commit seppuku. His retainers, now masterless rōnin led by Ōishi Yoshio, waited nearly two years before storming Kira's mansion on 14 December 1702 and killing him. The forty-six surviving rōnin were themselves ordered to commit seppuku, and were buried beside their lord at Sengaku-ji temple in Tokyo.

Adaptations

The story became the most famous example of the samurai code of honour, bushidō. The 1748 puppet play and kabuki adaptation Kanadehon Chūshingura, which disguised the story in a fourteenth-century setting to evade censorship, remains a cornerstone of the Japanese stage, and the tale has been retold in countless films and television dramas.
