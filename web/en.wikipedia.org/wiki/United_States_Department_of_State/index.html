United States Department of State

The United States Department of State (DOS) is the federal executive department responsible for the foreign policy and international relations of the United States. It was created in 1789, making it the first cabinet department established.

Department of State
Formed: July 27, 1789
Headquarters: Harry S. Truman Building, Washington, D.C.
Head: Secretary of State
First Secretary: Thomas Jefferson

History

Created as the Department of Foreign Affairs in 1789 and soon renamed, the department early also handled some domestic duties, later transferred elsewhere. Under Thomas Jefferson, its first secretary, it established American diplomatic practice.

Duties

The department leads diplomacy, negotiates treaties and agreements in support of the President, represents the United States at the United Nations, issues passports, protects Americans abroad through embassies and consulates, and administers foreign assistance and visa services.

Organization

The Secretary of State is the President's principal foreign-policy adviser and fourth in the line of presidential succession. The department is organized into regional and functional bureaus, with the Foreign Service staffing missions worldwide.
