Remote control

A remote control is an electronic device used to operate another device from a distance, typically wirelessly. The term most commonly refers to the handset used to control a television or other consumer electronics, but remote controls are also used for industrial machinery, garage doors, drones and military systems.

History and technology

Early remote controls included Nikola Tesla's 1898 demonstration of a radio-controlled boat. Television remotes appeared in the 1950s: Zenith's wired "Lazy Bones" and the wireless Flash-Matic (1955) and Space Command, which used ultrasound. From the 1980s, infrared light became the dominant technology, with signals sent as coded pulses of invisible light to a receiver. Modern remotes increasingly use radio protocols such as Bluetooth and Wi-Fi, and smartphone apps can replace dedicated handsets. In industrial and military contexts, remote control enables the operation of vehicles and equipment in hazardous environments.
