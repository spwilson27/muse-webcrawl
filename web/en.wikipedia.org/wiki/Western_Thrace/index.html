Western Thrace

Western Thrace (Greek: Δυτική Θράκη) is a geographic and historical region of Greece, between the Nestos and Evros rivers in the northeast of the country. Together with the regional units of East Macedonia, it forms the administrative region of East Macedonia and Thrace. It comprises the regional units of Evros, Rhodope, and Xanthi.

Geography

The region covers about 8,578 km² and is bounded by Bulgaria to the north, Turkey to the east across the Evros River, and the Aegean Sea to the south. Its main cities are Alexandroupolis, Komotini, and Xanthi. The Rhodope Mountains cross its northern part.

History

Part of the Ottoman Empire for centuries, Western Thrace was contested during the Balkan Wars and the First World War. It was administered by Bulgaria, then by an Entente administration, before being ceded to Greece under the Treaty of Sèvres in 1920 and confirmed by the Treaty of Lausanne in 1923. It is home to Greece's officially recognised Muslim minority, exempted from the 1923 population exchange, including ethnic Turks, Pomaks, and Roma.

Economy

Agriculture — including tobacco, cotton, and cereals — forestry, and tourism around the Dadia forest and the Evros delta are important to the regional economy.
