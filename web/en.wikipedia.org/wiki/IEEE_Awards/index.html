IEEE Awards

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The IEEE Awards program honours outstanding contributions to electrical and electronics engineering, computing, and related fields. Administered by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers through its Awards Board, the program dates back to the awards of its predecessor organizations, the AIEE and IRE, and includes medals, technical field awards, corporate recognitions, and student awards presented at the annual IEEE Honors Ceremony.

Medals

The highest award is the IEEE Medal of Honor, established in 1917 as the IRE Medal of Honor and given annually for an exceptional contribution or an extraordinary career in the IEEE fields of interest; recipients have included major figures in radio, computing, information theory, and semiconductors. Other medals include the IEEE Edison Medal (for a career of meritorious achievement in electrical science or engineering), the IEEE Alexander Graham Bell Medal (telecommunications), the IEEE John von Neumann Medal (computer-related science and technology), the IEEE James H. Mulligan Jr. Education Medal, the IEEE Jack S. Kilby Signal Processing Medal, and the IEEE Jun-ichi Nishizawa Medal.

Technical field awards

IEEE also bestows dozens of technical field awards recognizing specific achievements, such as the IEEE Cledo Brunetti Award (nanotechnologies), the IEEE Emanuel R. Piore Award (information processing), and the IEEE Donald G. Fink Prize Paper Award for outstanding survey or tutorial papers. Many of IEEE's technical societies sponsor their own awards as well. Alongside awards, IEEE recognizes members through its grades, notably elevation to IEEE Fellow for extraordinary accomplishment.
