Polignac's conjecture

In number theory, Polignac's conjecture was first stated by Alphonse de Polignac in 1849: for any positive even integer n, there are infinitely many prime gaps of size n. In other words, there are infinitely many pairs of consecutive primes that differ by n.

Special cases

For n = 2, the conjecture is the twin prime conjecture. For n = 4 it asserts infinitely many cousin primes, and for n = 6 infinitely many sexy primes. The conjecture remains unproven for every value of n, although major progress on bounded prime gaps was made in 2013 by Yitang Zhang, who proved that some gap of at most 70 million occurs infinitely often; subsequent work by James Maynard and the Polymath project reduced the bound to 246 unconditionally.

Prime k-tuples

Polignac's conjecture is a special case of the prime k-tuple conjecture, which predicts the frequency of all admissible patterns of primes. Dickson's conjecture likewise generalises it to families of linear polynomials.
