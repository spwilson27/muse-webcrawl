Baguazhang (Chinese: 八卦掌; pinyin: bāguàzhǎng; lit. "eight-trigram palm") is one of the three major "internal" Chinese martial arts, alongside tai chi and xingyiquan. It is an internal (neijia) style whose signature practice is circle walking, with fluid, evasive footwork and palm changes modelled on the eight trigrams of the I Ching.

History

The art is credited to Dong Haichuan (c. 1813–1882), who taught in Beijing from the 1860s and adapted his teaching to students who often already had experience in other styles, giving rise to the major lineages of Yin Fu, Cheng Tinghua, and Liang Zhenpu. Bagua bodyguards were famed in the capital, and Dong's tomb in Beijing became a pilgrimage site for practitioners.

Practice

Training centres on walking a circle with the "mud-stepping" gait while holding postures and executing palm changes; the eight basic palm changes correspond to the eight trigrams. Weapons include the broadsword, spear, deer horn knives, and the oversized bagua broadsword.
