The Busan International Film Festival (BIFF, previously the Pusan International Film Festival, PIFF), held annually in Haeundae District, Busan, South Korea, is one of the most significant film festivals in Asia. First staged from 13 to 21 September 1996, it was the first international film festival in Korea and has grown to attract around 200,000 attendees and hundreds of films from dozens of countries each October.

Program

The festival is especially noted for its focus on new Asian cinema and first or second features by Asian directors. Its competitive section, New Currents, awards the New Currents Award to the best two films by emerging Asian directors. Other sections include Gala Presentation, A Window on Asian Cinema, Korean Cinema Today, World Cinema, Wide Angle (shorts and documentaries), Midnight Passion, and Open Cinema. Since the 27th edition in 2022, the festival has also presented the Jiseok section for established Asian auteurs, named in memory of its late senior programmer Kim Ji-seok.

Industry events

Alongside the festival run the Asian Film Market (now the Asian Contents & Film Market), the Asian Project Market (formerly Pusan Promotion Plan), and the Asian Film Academy, which train and connect filmmakers across the region. The festival's main venue, the Busan Cinema Center, opened in 2011 and is noted for its enormous cantilevered roof covered in LED lights.

Controversies

The 2014 screening of the documentary The Truth Shall Not Sink with Sewol (also known as Diving Bell), about the Sewol ferry disaster, led to disputes with the Busan city government, funding cuts, and an audit; the film industry responded with a boycott movement, and the affair became a prominent episode in debates over artistic freedom in South Korea.
