Green Island (Queensland)

Green Island (Aboriginal: Wunyami) is a coral cay located in the Coral Sea, about 27 kilometres (17 mi) north-east of Cairns, Queensland, Australia. The island, some 6,000 years old, formed as sediment accumulated on an elongated coral reef platform, and covers about 12 hectares (30 acres). It lies within the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park and is a popular day-trip and resort destination.

History

The island is part of the traditional lands and sea country of the Gunggandji Aboriginal people, who know it as Wunyami, a place of spirits. Lieutenant James Cook named it in 1770 after the astronomer Charles Green aboard HMS Endeavour. From the 19th century the island was used by fishermen and for beche-de-mer (sea cucumber) processing; a resort has operated there since the 1930s, and the island was declared a national park in 1937.

Environment

Unlike most coral cays, Green Island supports a mature rainforest of some 120 plant species — the result of seeds carried by birds, sea currents, and people — and is home to dozens of bird species. The surrounding fringing reef hosts soft and hard corals, giant clams, turtles, reef sharks, and abundant reef fish, accessible to visitors by snorkelling, semi-submersible vessels, and a long-established underwater observatory and seawalking.

Tourism

Ferries from Cairns reach the island in about 45 minutes. A single resort occupies part of the island alongside day-visitor facilities; historically the island also housed a crocodile farm attraction founded around Marineland Melanesia, home to the large crocodile Cassius.
