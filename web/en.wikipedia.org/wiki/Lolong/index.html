Lolong (died 10 February 2013) was the largest saltwater crocodile ever measured in captivity. He measured 6.17 metres (20 ft 3 in) from snout to tail tip and weighed approximately 1,075 kilograms (2,370 lb), and was certified by Guinness World Records as the world's largest crocodile in captivity.

Capture

Lolong was captured in a creek in Bunawan, Agusan del Sur, in the Philippine island of Mindanao, on 3 September 2011, after a three-week hunt involving dozens of men. He was suspected of having attacked and killed several people, including a fisherman and a 12-year-old girl. He was named after Ernesto "Lolong" Goloran Cañete, an expert crocodile hunter from the Palawan Crocodile and Wildlife Reservation Center who led the hunt and died shortly before the capture.

Captivity and death

The crocodile became the star attraction of an ecotourism park in Bunawan, drawing large numbers of visitors. Lolong died on 10 February 2013, reportedly of pneumonia complicated by a fungal infection and stress; his health had declined after he swallowed a nylon cord. His remains were preserved and are displayed at the Philippine National Museum of Natural History in Manila.
