Effective dose (radiation)

Effective dose is a dose quantity in the International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP) system of radiological protection. It is a measure of the health effect of low levels of ionizing radiation on the human body, intended to represent the stochastic health risk — the probability of cancer induction and genetic effects — of a non-uniform irradiation in terms of an equivalent whole-body irradiation.

Definition

Effective dose E is the sum of the tissue-weighted equivalent doses in all specified tissues and organs of the body. Each organ's equivalent dose is multiplied by a tissue weighting factor, wT, which represents the relative contribution of that tissue to the total detriment resulting from uniform irradiation of the whole body. The tissue weighting factors sum to 1.0.

Units

The SI unit of effective dose is the sievert (Sv), the same unit used for equivalent dose. Typical values include a few microsieverts for a dental X-ray, roughly 0.1 mSv for a chest X-ray, several mSv for a CT scan of the abdomen, and an average annual background dose of about 2–3 mSv from natural sources.

Uses and limitations

Effective dose is used in radiological protection for setting dose limits for workers and the public, for comparing procedures, and for optimising protection. The ICRP cautions that it is a protection quantity intended for prospective assessment of populations and reference persons; it should not be used for detailed retrospective assessment of individual exposures or for epidemiological evaluation of specific persons.
