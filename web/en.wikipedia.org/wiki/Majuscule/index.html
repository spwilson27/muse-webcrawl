Majuscule

Majuscule (from Latin maiuscula littera, "somewhat larger letter") refers to large letter forms, particularly the capital or uppercase letters of bicameral scripts such as Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic. In handwriting and typography, majuscules contrast with minuscules, the smaller lowercase letter forms.

History

Classical Roman writing used only majuscule forms: first the angular capitals of inscriptions (capitalis monumentalis), seen on monuments such as Trajan's Column, and later more rounded book hands such as uncial and half-uncial. Between the 4th and 8th centuries, scribes in late antiquity and the early Middle Ages developed minuscule hands that were faster to write, and by the Carolingian era a clear two-case system had emerged, with majuscules reserved for emphasis, initials, and headings.

Usage

In modern orthography, majuscules mark the beginnings of sentences, proper nouns, and titles, and are used in acronyms and certain stylistic conventions. In typographic terminology inherited from letterpress printing, majuscules are called "uppercase" because printers stored them in the upper of a pair of type cases, with minuscules in the lower case.

Some scripts — including Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, and Chinese — have no case distinction and thus no majuscules. The Georgian script historically used the asomtavruli letters as majuscules.
