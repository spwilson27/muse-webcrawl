Ski jumping

Ski jumping is a winter sport in which competitors ski down a specially constructed ramp, known as a ski jump, and attempt to fly as far as possible before landing. Points are awarded for both distance and style. Along with cross-country skiing, it constitutes the Nordic combined discipline in part, and ski jumping itself has been part of the Winter Olympic Games since the first Games in Chamonix in 1924.

History

Ski jumping originated in Norway in the early 19th century, where it grew out of military ski training and rural recreation in Telemark and Morgedal. Sondre Norheim is credited as a pioneer of modern skiing technique. The famous Holmenkollen festival in Oslo began holding jumping competitions in the 1890s, and the sport spread to North America and central Europe with Scandinavian emigrants. The first Winter Olympics in 1924 included a normal hill event.

Technique

A jump consists of the inrun, in which the jumper crouches to gain speed; the take-off, a precisely timed extension at the lip of the jump; the flight, in which the jumper leans forward over the skis to form a lifting surface; and the landing, ideally in the Telemark position with one foot ahead of the other and knees flexed. Since the 1980s and 1990s the V-style, with ski tips spread apart, has replaced the parallel style because it generates greater lift.

Hills and competition

Hills are classified by their K-point (critical point) and hill size: normal hills have a K-point around 90 m, large hills around 120 m, and ski flying hills exceed 185 m, where jumps of over 250 metres have been recorded. Five judges award style marks, and distance points are calculated relative to the K-point, with wind and gate compensation systems used in modern competitions.

Major competitions include the FIS Ski Jumping World Cup, the Four Hills Tournament in Germany and Austria over the New Year period, the World Ski Flying Championships, and the Winter Olympics. Women's ski jumping was added to the Olympics in 2014 after a long campaign for inclusion.

Notable jumpers

Finland's Matti Nykänen and Poland's Adam Małysz are among the most successful jumpers of the modern era, while Austria, Germany, Japan and Norway have produced numerous champions. Stefan Kraft of Austria set the ski flying world record of 253.5 metres at Vikersund in 2017.
