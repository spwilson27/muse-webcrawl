Fifth Avenue

Fifth Avenue
Location: Manhattan, New York City
South end: Washington Square Park
North end: Harlem River (142nd St)
Known for: Luxury shopping, Museum Mile, parades

Fifth Avenue is a major thoroughfare in the borough of Manhattan in New York City. It stretches north from Washington Square Park in Greenwich Village to West 142nd Street in Harlem, and serves as the dividing line between the east and west sides of Manhattan for street addresses. Fifth Avenue is among the most famous streets in the world: its midtown stretch is one of the most expensive and prestigious shopping districts anywhere, and its Upper East Side section fronting Central Park — "Museum Mile" — contains many of the city's great cultural institutions.

History

Laid out under the Commissioners' Plan of 1811, Fifth Avenue in the 19th century became the address of choice for New York's wealthiest families, whose mansions — including those of the Vanderbilts and Astors — lined the avenue above 34th Street. As commerce moved north in the early 20th century, department stores and luxury retailers (Bergdorf Goodman, Saks Fifth Avenue, Tiffany & Co.) replaced the mansions in Midtown, while the upper stretch retained its residential and cultural character. St. Patrick's Cathedral, the New York Public Library, the Empire State Building, and Rockefeller Center all stand on or just off the avenue.

Museum Mile and landmarks

Between 82nd and 105th Streets, the avenue passes the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Guggenheim Museum, the Cooper Hewitt, the Jewish Museum, the Frick Collection, and others — the densest concentration of museums in the city. Fifth Avenue also hosts New York's great parades, including the St. Patrick's Day Parade and the Thanksgiving and Easter traditions; the Easter Parade and the avenue's Christmas window displays are seasonal institutions.

Retail

The blocks between 49th and 60th Streets routinely rank as the most expensive retail real estate in the world, with flagship stores of global luxury brands. The intersection with 57th Street is sometimes called the "crossroads of luxury".
