Margot Fonteyn

Dame Margaret Evelyn de Arias DBE (18 May 1919 – 21 February 1991), known by her stage name Margot Fonteyn, was an English ballerina regarded as one of the greatest classical ballet dancers of all time. She spent her entire career with the Royal Ballet (formerly the Sadler's Wells Ballet) and was appointed prima ballerina assoluta by Queen Elizabeth II.

Born Margaret Hookham in Reigate, Surrey, she studied dance in England and Shanghai, where her family lived for a time, and joined the Vic-Wells Ballet in 1934, taking her stage name from her mother's family name. By the age of twenty she had taken over the leading classical roles of the repertoire, forming a celebrated partnership with choreographer Frederick Ashton, whose ballets—including Symphonic Variations and The Sleeping Beauty production that made the company famous in New York in 1949—defined her lyrical, musical style.

Partnership with Nureyev

In 1962, when Fonteyn was considering retirement, she began partnering the recently defected Soviet dancer Rudolf Nureyev, nearly twenty years her junior. Their partnership—beginning with Giselle and extending to works such as Ashton's Marguerite and Armand, created for them in 1963—became one of the most famous in ballet history and extended her career by more than a decade and a half.

Fonteyn married Panamanian diplomat Roberto Arias in 1955; after he was shot and paralyzed in an assassination attempt in 1964, she continued dancing in part to pay for his care. She retired in 1979, settled in Panama, and died of ovarian cancer in Panama City in 1991.
