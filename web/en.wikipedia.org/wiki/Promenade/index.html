Promenade

A promenade is a long, open, level area, usually next to a river or large body of water, where people may walk for pleasure. The term derives from the French promenade, an act of walking, and in English it also names the social ritual of strolling in one's best clothes to see and be seen.

History

Seaside promenades became a hallmark of British and European resorts in the Victorian era, when piers, bandstands and wide paved esplanades were built for health-giving sea air and genteel recreation. Famous examples include the Promenade des Anglais in Nice, the seafronts of Brighton and Blackpool, and the boardwalks of American resorts such as Atlantic City. Riverside and lakeside esplanades serve the same function in cities such as Budapest, Lucerne and Shanghai (the Bund).

Modern usage

Today promenades form part of urban waterfront regeneration, providing car-free routes for walking, cycling and events. The word also survives in "promenade concerts" — informal concerts, most famously the BBC Proms, where part of the audience stands and historically could walk about.
