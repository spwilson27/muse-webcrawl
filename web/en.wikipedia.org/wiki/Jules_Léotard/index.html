Jules Léotard (1 March 1838 – 17 August 1870) was a French acrobat who invented the flying trapeze act and popularized the one-piece, form-fitting garment that still bears his name — the leotard.

Life and act

Léotard was born in Toulouse, the son of a swimming-pool and gymnasium instructor. Destined for the law, he instead trained on the bars and ropes hung over his father's pool, developing the technique of flying between swinging trapezes. On 12 November 1859 he performed the first flying trapeze act at the Cirque Napoléon in Paris, leaping between three trapezes. The act made him a sensation across Europe; he performed in London and drew large crowds, commanding extraordinary salaries for the era.

The leotard and the song

For freedom of movement Léotard performed in a close-fitting knitted costume — he called it a maillot — which the English-speaking world soon named after him. In 1867 the music-hall song "The Daring Young Man on the Flying Trapeze", written by George Leybourne and Gaston Lyle, immortalized him: "He'd fly through the air with the greatest of ease, that daring young man on the flying trapeze."

Léotard's career was cut short when he died at 32, probably of an infectious disease such as smallpox or cholera, in his native Toulouse. His innovation remains the foundation of the flying trapeze acts still performed in circuses today.
