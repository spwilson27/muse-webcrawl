Regions of Japan

The regions of Japan are not official administrative units, but they are a traditional and widely used grouping of the country's 47 prefectures. Eight regions are conventionally recognised, running from north to south: Hokkaido, Tōhoku, Kantō, Chūbu, Kansai (Kinki), Chūgoku, Shikoku and Kyūshū (often with Okinawa listed separately or within Kyūshū). Regions are used by government agencies, businesses, weather forecasts, sports leagues and statistical publications, and many state bodies maintain regional bureaus on these lines.

Subdivisions and usage

Chūbu is often divided into the Hokuriku, Kōshin'etsu and Tōkai subregions, and Kantō may be subdivided into North and South Kantō. The division echoes older systems: the Edo period's goki-shichidō ("five provinces and seven circuits") grouped provinces into circuits such as Tōkaidō and Saikaidō, whose names survive in railways and highways. Proposals for a formal regional tier of government (the dōshūsei system), which would replace prefectures with larger states roughly matching these regions, have been debated since the 2000s without enactment. Regional identities remain strong — the historic rivalry between Kantō (Tokyo) and Kansai (Osaka–Kyoto) is a staple of Japanese cultural commentary.
