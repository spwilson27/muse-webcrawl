John Yudkin FRSC (8 August 1910 – 12 July 1995) was a British physiologist and nutritionist and the founding Professor of Nutrition at Queen Elizabeth College, London. He is best known for his argument, set out most fully in Pure, White and Deadly (1972), that excessive consumption of sugar — rather than dietary fat alone — is a major cause of coronary heart disease, obesity and diabetes. Marginalised during his lifetime in favour of the diet–heart hypothesis associated with Ancel Keys, Yudkin's work has been substantially re-evaluated since the 2000s.

Career

Yudkin studied natural sciences at Christ's College, Cambridge, took a PhD in biochemistry, and qualified in medicine. After wartime service in the Royal Army Medical Corps in West Africa, where he worked on soldiers' nutrition, he became professor of physiology and then, in 1954, Britain's first professor of nutrition at Queen Elizabeth College, building its Department of Nutrition into a leading research and teaching centre.

The sugar hypothesis

From the late 1950s Yudkin published epidemiological and experimental studies linking sugar intake to coronary thrombosis, showing that sugar consumption correlated with heart disease across countries and that high-sugar diets raised blood triglycerides and insulin in volunteers and rats. He regarded the sucrose-rich modern diet — historically very recent — as a more plausible culprit than fat. His rivalry with Ancel Keys, champion of the saturated-fat hypothesis, was bitter and has since been shown to have been influenced by sugar-industry funding that promoted the fat hypothesis and disparaged Yudkin's research.

Pure, White and Deadly

His popular book Pure, White and Deadly: The Problem of Sugar (1972; revised 1986) warned that sugar was implicated in heart disease, diabetes, obesity, dental caries and other conditions. Long out of print, it was reissued in 2012 with an introduction by Robert Lustig, amid renewed scientific and public concern about added sugars.

Other contributions

Yudkin wrote widely on food policy and popularised nutrition science in works such as This Nutrition Business (1976) and The Penguin Encyclopedia of Nutrition (1985). He died in London in 1995.
