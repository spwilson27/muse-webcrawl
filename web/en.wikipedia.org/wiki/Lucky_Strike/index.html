Lucky Strike is an American brand of cigarettes owned by the British American Tobacco group and, in the United States, by R. J. Reynolds. Introduced in 1871 by the R. A. Patterson company of Richmond, Virginia—as a chewing tobacco and later as cigarettes—the brand was acquired by the American Tobacco Company and became one of the best-selling cigarette brands in the United States during the first half of the 20th century.

History

The name is said to have been inspired by the California Gold Rush, during which only a few miners—those with a "lucky strike"—found gold. The brand's distinctive dark green pack was changed to white in 1942, in a campaign said to donate the green dye's copper to the war effort, accompanied by the slogan "Lucky Strike Green has gone to war." Its red circle logo, designed in 1917-era form and refined by industrial designer Raymond Loewy, became iconic. Famous advertising slogans include "It's toasted"—referring to the tobacco being toasted rather than sun-dried—and "Lucky Strike means fine tobacco" (LSMFT).

Marketing and culture

Lucky Strike was a heavy sponsor of radio and early television programs, including Jack Benny's show and Your Hit Parade. The brand is strongly associated with the Second World War, when cigarettes were included in American soldiers' rations, and has appeared extensively in period film and television, notably the series Mad Men.
