The Battle of Jerusalem was a series of operations fought from November to December 1917 during the Sinai and Palestine Campaign of the First World War, culminating in the surrender of Jerusalem to the British Egyptian Expeditionary Force under General Edmund Allenby. The city passed from Ottoman rule, which had lasted 400 years, into British hands.

Background

After the victories at Beersheba and Gaza in October–November 1917, Allenby's force advanced into the Judaean Hills. The Ottoman Yildirim Army Group under Erich von Falkenhayn attempted to hold a line covering Jerusalem, fighting rearguard actions around Nebi Samwil and Jaffa.

Battle

British and Empire troops — including the 60th (London) and 74th (Yeomanry) Divisions and Australian mounted troops — fought through the hills in wet winter weather. Ottoman counter-attacks in late November failed, and on the night of 8–9 December the Ottoman garrison withdrew. Jerusalem's mayor, Hussein al-Husseini, surrendered the city on 9 December; two sergeants of the 60th Division were the first to receive the surrender. Allenby entered formally through the Jaffa Gate on foot on 11 December, out of respect for the holy city. Ottoman counter-offensives later in December were defeated, consolidating the British line north of the city by 30 December.

Aftermath

The capture of Jerusalem was a major propaganda success for the Allies near the end of a grim year of war. Palestine came under British administration, leading eventually to the Mandate for Palestine. The campaign resumed in 1918 with the decisive victory at Megiddo.
