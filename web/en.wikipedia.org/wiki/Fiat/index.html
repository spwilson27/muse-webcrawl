Fiat (stylised as FIAT; originally an acronym of Fabbrica Italiana Automobili di Torino, "Italian Automobiles Factory of Turin") is an Italian automobile brand founded in 1899. It became Italy's largest car manufacturer and one of Europe's biggest, and since 2021 has been a brand of the multinational group Stellantis.

History

Fiat was founded in Turin in 1899 by a group of investors including Giovanni Agnelli, who became its dominant figure. The company grew rapidly, building the huge Lingotto factory in Turin (opened 1923, famous for its rooftop test track) and later the Mirafiori plant. After the Second World War, Fiat motorised Italy with small, affordable cars such as the 500 (1957), designed by Dante Giacosa — a symbol of the country's economic miracle. The 124, 126, 128, and later the Uno and Punto were major European successes, and Fiat cars won the European Car of the Year award more times than any other marque.

The wider Fiat Group came to control Ferrari, Maserati, Alfa Romeo, and Lancia, as well as trucks (Iveco) and agricultural machinery. In 2014 the group's automobile business merged with Chrysler to form Fiat Chrysler Automobiles, and in 2021 FCA merged with France's PSA Group to form Stellantis, under which Fiat remains the group's highest-volume brand worldwide, with strong positions in Italy, Brazil, and Turkey.

Models and markets

Fiat specialises in city cars and small family cars: the 500 (revived in retro form in 2007 and as an electric car in 2020), the Panda, the Tipo, and light commercial vehicles such as the Ducato van. Production is centred in Italy, Poland, Serbia, Turkey, and Brazil, where locally developed models such as the Strada pickup have led the market.
