Kruskal–Wallis test

The Kruskal–Wallis test by ranks, Kruskal–Wallis H test (named after William Kruskal and W. Allen Wallis), or one-way ANOVA on ranks is a non-parametric statistical test for testing whether samples originate from the same distribution. It is used for comparing two or more independent samples of equal or different sample sizes. It extends the Mann–Whitney U test, which is used for comparing only two groups.

Method

All observations from the groups are pooled and ranked together, with tied values receiving average ranks. The test statistic H is computed from the sums of ranks within each group:

H = (12 / (N(N+1))) Σ (Rᵢ² / nᵢ) − 3(N+1),

where N is the total number of observations, nᵢ the number in group i, and Rᵢ the sum of ranks in group i. Under the null hypothesis of identical distributions, H approximately follows a chi-squared distribution with k − 1 degrees of freedom, where k is the number of groups.

Assumptions and interpretation

The test assumes independent observations and an ordinal or continuous measurement scale. If the distributions have the same shape across groups, the test can be interpreted as a test of equal medians. A significant result indicates that at least one sample stochastically dominates another; post-hoc pairwise comparisons, such as Dunn's test, are used to identify which groups differ.

History

The test was introduced in 1952 by William H. Kruskal and W. Allen Wallis in their paper "Use of Ranks in One-Criterion Variance Analysis" in the Journal of the American Statistical Association.
