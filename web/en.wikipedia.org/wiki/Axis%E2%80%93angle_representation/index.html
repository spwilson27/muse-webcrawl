Axis–angle representation

In mathematics, the axis–angle representation of a rotation parameterises a rotation in three-dimensional Euclidean space by two quantities: a unit vector e indicating the direction of an axis of rotation, and an angle θ describing the magnitude of the rotation about that axis.

The representation follows from Euler's rotation theorem, which states that any rotation or sequence of rotations of a rigid body about a fixed point is equivalent to a single rotation by some angle about some axis through that point. The rotation vector, widely used in computer graphics, robotics and physics, is the axis scaled by the angle, so its direction gives the axis and its length the angle in radians.

Relation to other representations

Axis–angle

ParametersUnit axis (3) + angle (1), or rotation vector (3)

ConversionRodrigues' formula gives the rotation matrix
RelatedEuler angles, quaternions

Rodrigues' rotation formula converts an axis–angle pair into the equivalent 3×3 rotation matrix. The axis–angle form is also directly connected to unit quaternions: a rotation through angle θ about axis e corresponds to the quaternion (cos θ/2, e sin θ/2). The exponential map from the Lie algebra so(3) of skew-symmetric matrices to the rotation group SO(3) takes a rotation vector to its rotation, and the axis–angle representation is essentially its inverse, the logarithm map.
