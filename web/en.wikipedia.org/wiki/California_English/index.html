California English

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

California English is the collection of English dialects spoken in the U.S. state of California. California was once considered largely dialect-neutral — a perception reinforced by the speech of the Hollywood film industry — but research since the late 20th century has identified a distinctive California vowel shift and notable regional and ethnic variation within the state.

The California vowel shift

Linguists have documented a chain shift in which the vowels of words such as bit and bet are lowered and retracted, front vowels in words like boot and boat move forward, and the vowel in bat is raised before nasal consonants. Many Californians also show the cot–caught merger, pronouncing the vowels of cot and caught the same or nearly the same.

Popular stereotypes

Two widely recognised California speech styles are the "Valley girl" style associated with the San Fernando Valley — popularised in the 1980s with uptalk and discourse markers such as like and totally — and the "surfer dude" style of coastal Southern California. Neither stereotype reflects most Californians' speech, but features such as uptalk (high rising terminal) have spread nationwide.

Lexicon

Distinctive California vocabulary includes hella ("very", Northern California), saying "the" before freeway numbers (e.g., "the 405", mainly Southern California), and regional terms for weather phenomena such as the Santa Ana winds and "June gloom".
