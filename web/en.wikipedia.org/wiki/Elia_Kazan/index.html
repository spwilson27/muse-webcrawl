Elia Kazan (born Elias Kazantzoglou; 1909–2003) was a Greek-American film and theatre director, known for championing method acting and directing some of the most acclaimed American works of the mid-20th century.

Career

Born in Constantinople to Greek parents and raised in New York, Kazan acted with the Group Theatre, co-founded the Actors Studio (1947), and directed the original Broadway productions of A Streetcar Named Desire and Death of a Salesman. His films include Gentleman's Agreement (1947), A Streetcar Named Desire (1951), On the Waterfront (1954) — both winning him Academy Awards for Best Director — East of Eden (1955, introducing James Dean), A Face in the Crowd (1957), Splendor in the Grass (1961) and the semi-autobiographical America America (1963).

HUAC controversy

In 1952 Kazan testified before the House Un-American Activities Committee, naming former Communist Party colleagues — an act that shadowed his reputation for decades and was protested when he received an honorary Oscar in 1999.
