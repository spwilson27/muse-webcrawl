Giuseppe "Joe" Masseria (January 17, 1886 – April 15, 1931), known as Joe the Boss, was an Italian-American Mafia boss in New York City. He headed what became known as the Genovese crime family from the early 1920s until his assassination in 1931, which ended the Castellammarese War. Masseria was one of the old-guard Sicilian "Mustache Petes" whose dominance was challenged by a younger generation of Americanized gangsters.

Early life and rise

Masseria was born in Menfi, Sicily, and immigrated to the United States in 1902 or 1903. He became involved with the Morello crime family and survived several brushes with the law, including a burglary conviction. After Prohibition began in 1920, he rose rapidly through bootlegging and by the mid-1920s had seized control of much of the old Morello organization, building a family that included future bosses Charles "Lucky" Luciano, Vito Genovese and Frank Costello.

Castellammarese War

In 1930 Masseria's demand for tribute and loyalty provoked open war with Salvatore Maranzano, whose Castellammarese faction had arrived from Sicily. The conflict, fought largely by lieutenants rather than the bosses themselves, drained both sides. Luciano, secretly negotiating with Maranzano, concluded that the war served neither side. On April 15, 1931, Masseria was shot dead over lunch at the Nuova Villa Tammaro restaurant in Coney Island; legend holds that his killers found him after Luciano excused himself to the washroom. Maranzano's subsequent reorganization of the Five Families — and his own murder five months later — ushered in the modern Commission structure of the American Mafia.
