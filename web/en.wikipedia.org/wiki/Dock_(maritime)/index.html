Dock (maritime)

A dock is the area of water between or next to one or a group of human-made structures that are involved in the handling of boats or ships, usually on or close to a shore. In most contexts and languages, docks are harbours where vessels may load and unload cargo, embark and disembark passengers, or be repaired.

Types

Docks include dry docks, which can be emptied of water so that the hull of a vessel can be cleaned and repaired; wet docks, which maintain a constant water level regardless of the tide and allow ships to remain afloat; and floating docks. A basin or impounded area of water in a harbour is also called a dock in British usage, while in American usage the term more often denotes the structure (a pier or wharf) at which a vessel is tied.

Dockyards

A dockyard is a facility where ships are built and repaired. Historically, dockyards such as those at Portsmouth, Chatham and Devonport in England were centres of naval power, employing large workforces of shipwrights, engineers and dock labourers.
