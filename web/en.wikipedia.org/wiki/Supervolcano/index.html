Supervolcano

Supervolcano
VEI: 8
Ejecta: >1,000 km³
Examples: Yellowstone, Toba, La Garita

A supervolcano is a volcano capable of an eruption with ejecta volume greater than 1,000 cubic kilometres, rated VEI-8 on the Volcanic Explosivity Index — thousands of times larger than a normal eruption.

Formation

Supervolcanoes form where magma accumulates in a huge magma chamber under a continental hotspot or subduction zone and cannot easily erupt, building pressure until a caldera-forming eruption empties the chamber and the ground collapses.

Known examples

The Yellowstone Caldera in Wyoming erupted about 640,000 years ago (and 1.3 and 2.1 million years ago). Lake Toba in Sumatra erupted ~74,000 years ago, the largest eruption of the last 25 million years, sometimes linked to a human population bottleneck. Other sites include La Garita Caldera (Fish Canyon Tuff, ~28 million years ago, the largest known), Taupō in New Zealand, Campi Flegrei in Italy, and Long Valley Caldera in California.

Effects

A super-eruption would devastate regionally with pyroclastic flows and ashfall, and globally by injecting sulphur into the stratosphere, causing a volcanic winter, crop failures and climate cooling for years.
