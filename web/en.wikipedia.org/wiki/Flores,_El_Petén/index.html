Flores is the capital of the Petén Department in the north of Guatemala. The town occupies a small island in Lake Petén Itzá, connected by a causeway to its mainland twin town of Santa Elena; together with San Benito the three form the department's main urban area and the principal gateway for visitors to the Maya ruins of Tikal, some 65 km to the northeast. The island is built over the site of Tayasal — in Itza Maya, Noj Petén, "great island" — the last independent Maya capital, which held out against the Spanish Empire until 1697.

History

The Itza Maya, migrants from Yucatán centuries earlier, made the island their capital and religious center, dense with temples and palaces. Hernán Cortés passed the lake in 1525 on his march to Honduras and left behind a lame horse that the Itza reportedly worshipped after its death — the origin of the region's famous "horse idolatry" legend involving the deity Tzimin Chac. Franciscan missions in 1618–1619 and military expeditions thereafter all failed to subdue the Itza, and for another eighty years Tayasal remained the capital of an independent Maya kingdom deep inside nominally Spanish territory. It fell at last to an expedition from Yucatán under Martín de Ursúa y Arizmendi, who built a road to the lake, launched an armed galley, and stormed the island on 13 March 1697. The Spanish razed the temples, exiled the Itza king Kan Ek' and his people, and raised a fortified colonial town on the site — the grid of streets, central plaza, and church of the present island.

The colonial and 19th-century town remained a remote presidio and prison settlement, reached for most of its history only by water and mule trail. The modern name honors Cirilo Flores, a physician and liberal statesman of the independence era who briefly served as acting head of state of the Central American federation in 1826. Roads and the airport arrived in the mid-20th century, and since the 1970s the growth of Tikal tourism and of Petén's population has swollen Flores and especially its mainland suburbs; the historic island, with its pastel houses, ring road, and hilltop church and plaza (over the buried Maya acropolis), is now largely given over to hotels and restaurants.

Geography and sights

The island rises to a small summit crowned by the church of Nuestra Señora de los Remedios and the main plaza; on its northwest shore stand the scant excavated remains of Tayasal. Across the lake lies the village of San José with the ARCAS wildlife rescue center, and the ruins of Tayasal's mainland districts; the island is also the jumping-off point for boat trips to the Yaxha–Nakum–Naranjo ruins and the Petencito zoo. Flores's rainy-season lake levels periodically flood its shoreline streets — a problem worsened in recent decades, and a threat to the Maya archaeology buried beneath it.
