James Clark Jr. OBE (4 March 1936 – 7 April 1968) was a British Formula One racing driver from Scotland who twice won the World Championship, in 1963 and 1965. A versatile driver, he competed in sports cars, touring cars, and in the Indianapolis 500, which he won in 1965, and is regarded as one of the greatest racing drivers of all time.

Career

Clark, a sheep farmer from the Scottish Borders, began racing locally in the mid-1950s and joined Colin Chapman's Team Lotus for Formula Junior and Formula Two. He made his Formula One debut at the 1960 Dutch Grand Prix and scored his first championship victory at Spa in 1962 in the revolutionary monocoque Lotus 25.

In 1963 Clark won seven of the ten championship races to take his first title, a record season at the time. In 1965 he won six of the first seven rounds and the championship again, and in the same year won the Indianapolis 500 in the rear-engined Lotus 38, leading 190 of the 200 laps — the first victory for a mid-engined car at Indianapolis. He won a total of 25 Grands Prix from 72 championship starts and took 33 pole positions.

Death and legacy

Clark was killed on 7 April 1968 in a Formula Two race at the Hockenheimring in West Germany, when his Lotus left the road and struck trees, most likely after a slow tyre puncture. His death shocked the racing world. Clark's smooth, sympathetic driving style, supreme car control, and partnership with Chapman remain legendary; he is consistently ranked among the finest drivers in the history of the sport, and a museum in Duns, Scotland, commemorates his life.
