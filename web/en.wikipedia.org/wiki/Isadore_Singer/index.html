Isadore Singer

Born: May 3, 1924, Detroit, Michigan

Died: February 11, 2021

Known for: Atiyah–Singer index theorem

Awards: Abel Prize (2004), National Medal of Science (1983)

Isadore Manuel Singer (May 3, 1924 – February 11, 2021) was an American mathematician at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. He is famous for his work with Michael Atiyah, proving the Atiyah–Singer index theorem in 1962, which states that for an elliptic differential operator on a compact manifold, the analytical index equals the topological index.

The index theorem

The index theorem created a new bridge between analysis, geometry, and topology, and later found applications in theoretical physics, including gauge theory and string theory. Singer and Atiyah received the Abel Prize in 2004 for its discovery and proof, and for their role in building new connections between mathematics and physics.

Career

Singer earned his PhD from the University of Chicago in 1950 and spent most of his career at MIT, where he was an Institute Professor. He also helped shape science policy, serving on advisory bodies and advocating for the support of basic research.
