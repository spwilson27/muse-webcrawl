The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (HKEX's securities market) is one of the world's largest stock exchanges by market capitalisation, listing over 2,500 companies. It is a principal gateway for international investors into mainland Chinese companies and for Chinese firms raising capital offshore.

History

Share trading in Hong Kong dates to the 1860s–1890s; competing exchanges were unified in 1986 after the 1973 and 1987 crashes prompted reform. In 2000 the exchange demutualised and listed itself through Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing.

Trading

Securities trade in Hong Kong dollars; the Stock Connect programmes (Shanghai 2014, Shenzhen 2016) link it with mainland markets. Major listings include Tencent, Alibaba's secondary listing and HSBC.
