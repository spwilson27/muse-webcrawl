Illusion

An illusion is a distortion of the senses, which can reveal how the mind normally organises and interprets sensory stimulation. Although illusions distort the perception of reality, they are generally shared by most people.

Types

Optical illusions present visual stimuli that are perceived differently from their physical properties, such as the Müller-Lyer and Ponzo illusions. Auditory illusions include the Shepard tone; tactile illusions include the Aristotle illusion in which crossed fingers misjudge a single touched object.

Explanation

Illusions arise from the brain's strategies for making fast, useful inferences about the world — strategies that can misfire with ambiguous or unusual input. Studying them informs perception science, art, and stage magic.
