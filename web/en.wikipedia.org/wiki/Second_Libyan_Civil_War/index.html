Second Libyan Civil War

The Second Libyan Civil War (2014–2020) was a multilateral civil war fought in Libya between rival organisations seeking control of the country. The main belligerents were the internationally recognised Government of National Accord (GNA), based in Tripoli and led by Prime Minister Fayez al-Sarraj, and the Libyan National Army (LNA) under Field Marshal Khalifa Haftar, aligned with the rival government in Tobruk and later Bayda.

Background

After the fall of Muammar Gaddafi in the First Libyan Civil War of 2011, Libya fragmented among armed militias and rival political bodies. The crisis erupted in May 2014 when Haftar launched Operation Dignity against Islamist groups in Benghazi, and escalated after disputed elections in June 2014 produced two rival parliaments and governments.

Course of the war

Fighting spread across the country, including battles for Tripoli's airport in 2014 and an Islamic State enclave in Sirte, which was defeated by Misrata-led forces with US air support in 2016. The UN-brokered Libyan Political Agreement of December 2015 created the GNA, but it failed to unify the armed groups. In April 2019 Haftar launched an offensive to capture Tripoli; the assault stalled and, with Turkish military intervention on the GNA's side, was repelled in 2020, with GNA forces advancing to Sirte and Jufra.

Ceasefire

On 23 October 2020 the rival sides signed a permanent ceasefire agreement in Geneva under United Nations auspices. In March 2021 an interim Government of National Unity was sworn in, although planned national elections were postponed and Libya's political division persisted.
