The Gnawa (or Gnaoua) are an ethnic group inhabiting Morocco, descended in part from West African soldiers and enslaved people brought across the Sahara in past centuries. They are famous for Gnawa music: a body of Moroccan and Sub-Saharan African Islamic songs and rhythms, performed with the guembri (a three-stringed bass lute), metal castanets called qraqeb, and call-and-response chanting.

Lila ceremonies

At the centre of Gnawa spiritual life is the lila (or derdeba), an all-night healing and trance ceremony led by a master musician (maâlem) and a priestess (moqaddema). Through sequences of songs dedicated to a pantheon of spirits (mluk), each associated with colours, incense and dances, participants seek healing and reconciliation with the spirits. The rituals blend Islamic devotion with older African spirit practices.

Music and influence

Gnawa grooves, built on repeating guembri bass lines, have influenced Moroccan popular music and attracted international jazz and rock musicians. The annual Gnaoua World Music Festival in Essaouira, founded in 1998, brought the tradition to global audiences. In 2019, UNESCO inscribed "Gnawa" on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
