Carl Schurz

Carl Christian Schurz (German: [ʃʊʁts]; March 2, 1829 – May 14, 1906) was a German revolutionary and an American statesman, journalist, and reformer. He migrated to the United States after the German revolutions of 1848–49 and became a prominent member of the new Republican Party. He served as a Union general in the American Civil War, as U.S. Senator from Missouri, and as Secretary of the Interior under President Rutherford B. Hayes.

Early life

Schurz was born in Liblar, in the Rhine Province of Prussia. As a student at the University of Bonn he joined the democratic movement and took part in the revolutions of 1848, including the siege of Rastatt. He famously escaped through a sewer after the fortress fell and fled to Switzerland. He later rescued his mentor Gottfried Kinkel from Spandau Prison in a daring operation in 1850, an exploit that made him famous among German democrats.

Career in America

Schurz emigrated to the United States in 1852 and settled first in Philadelphia and then in Watertown, Wisconsin. He became a leading voice of German-American voters for the Republican Party and supported Abraham Lincoln, who appointed him ambassador to Spain. Returning in 1862, he was commissioned a brigadier general and fought at Second Bull Run, Chancellorsville and Gettysburg. Elected to the Senate in 1868, he championed civil service reform, sound money and amnesty for former Confederates. As Secretary of the Interior (1877–1881), he reformed the department, worked to conserve forests, and attempted to protect Native American rights through a policy of assimilation, a legacy that remains debated.

Later life

After leaving office Schurz edited the New York Evening Post and Harper's Weekly, wrote a celebrated autobiography, The Reminiscences of Carl Schurz, and remained a leading independent ("Mugwump") voice in American politics. His most famous maxim, responding to "My country, right or wrong!", was: "My country, right or wrong; if right, to be kept right; and if wrong, to be set right." He died in New York City in 1906.
