Guatemala City (Spanish: Ciudad de Guatemala) is the capital and largest city of Guatemala and the largest city in Central America. It stands in a high mountain valley (the Valle de la Ermita) at about 1,500 m, giving it a spring-like climate despite its tropical latitude. The city was founded in 1776 when the colonial capital was moved from Antigua Guatemala after the Santa Marta earthquakes of 1773; it was itself shattered by earthquakes in 1917–18 and again in 1976, and rebuilt each time in the sprawling, zone-divided grid of today.

The city

The historic centre (Zone 1) holds the Metropolitan Cathedral, the National Palace of Culture — the vast green-stone seat of government built under the dictator Jorge Ubico — and the central market, while business has drifted south to the towers and malls of Zones 9, 10 ("Zona Viva") and 14. Within the modern city lie the grassed-over pyramids of Kaminaljuyú, a major Maya highland city more than 2,000 years ago, echoed in the Museo Miraflores and the Popol Vuh Museum's Maya collections. Guatemala City is the country's economic and political engine — and the hinge of its turbulent modern history, from the 1954 coup against President Árbenz (plotted with CIA support, see the Cold War in Latin America) through the 36-year civil war to the peace accords of 1996, signed here. Inequality and violent crime remain the city's heavy counterweights; its metro population is now around three million.
