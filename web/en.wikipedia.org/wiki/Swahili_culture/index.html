Swahili culture is the culture of the Swahili people, an Afro-Arab coastal society of East Africa formed over more than a millennium of interaction between Bantu-speaking Africans and traders from Arabia, Persia and beyond. It is centred on the cities and islands of the Swahili Coast, expressed in the Swahili language, Islam, distinctive stone architecture, poetry, dress, cuisine and a mercantile, urban way of life.

Language and literature

Swahili is a Bantu language whose vocabulary draws perhaps a third of its words from Arabic, with later loans from Persian, Portuguese, German, English and Hindi. Written for centuries in Arabic script (ajami) as well as in Latin script today, it has a rich poetic tradition of epic and lyric verse (mashairi), including the 17th-century Utendi wa Tambuka of Mwengo of Pate. Swahili is now a lingua franca for well over a hundred million people in East and Central Africa, an official language of the African Union, and one of the world's most widely spoken languages.

Architecture, dress and food

Old Swahili towns are built of coral rag and lime mortar, with flat-roofed stone houses organised around courtyards, elaborately carved wooden doors, and mosques with distinctive pillar tombs. Traditional dress includes the men's kanzu robe and embroidered kofia cap and the women's printed kanga and buibui. Coastal cuisine blends African staples with Arab, Persian and Indian influences: coconut rice, pilau and biriyani, samosas, grilled seafood, and sweet spiced coffee and tea. Music — notably the sung poetry of taarab — and celebrations such as Maulidi (the Prophet's birthday, famously at Lamu) and Eid anchor the cultural calendar.

Society and identity

Classic Swahili society distinguished patrician stone-town families (waungwana) from commoners, clients and, historically, enslaved people, a hierarchy tied to the plantation and caravan trades of the 19th century. Modern Swahili identity — stretched between African roots and claimed Arab or Persian (Shirazi) ancestry — remains a live cultural and political theme in Kenya and Tanzania.
