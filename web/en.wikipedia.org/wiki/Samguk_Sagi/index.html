Samguk Sagi

Author: Kim Pusik and team of scholars

Published: 1145

Language: Classical Chinese

Subject: History of the Three Kingdoms of Korea

The Samguk Sagi (Korean: 삼국사기; Hanja: 三國史記, "History of the Three Kingdoms") is a historical record of the Three Kingdoms of Korea — Goguryeo, Baekje and Silla — as well as the Unified Silla period. Commissioned by King Injong of Goryeo and compiled by the court scholar-official Kim Pusik with a team of historians, it was completed in 1145. It is the oldest surviving Korean history text.

Contents

Written in Classical Chinese and modelled on Chinese dynastic histories such as Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian, the work comprises 50 volumes: annals (bongi) of the three kingdoms, chronological tables, treatises on subjects such as astronomy, geography and offices, and biographies of notable figures.

Sources and historiography

Kim Pusik drew on earlier, now mostly lost, Korean records and Chinese histories. Because he wrote from a Confucian and Silla-centred perspective, later historians have debated his treatment of Goguryeo and Baekje and his omission of some legendary material preserved in the later Samguk Yusa.

Significance

The Samguk Sagi remains a fundamental source for the history of early Korea, providing dates, royal genealogies and accounts of wars, diplomacy and court life for the period from the traditional founding of the kingdoms in the 1st century BCE to the fall of Silla in 935.
