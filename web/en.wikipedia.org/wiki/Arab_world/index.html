Arab world

The Arab world comprises the 22 member states of the Arab League.

The Arab world (Arabic: اَلْعَالَمُ ٱلْعَرَبِيُّ, al-ʿālam al-ʿarabī), formally the Arab homeland, is the collective term for the 22 countries of the Arab League, stretching from the Atlantic Ocean in the west to the Arabian Sea in the east, and from the Mediterranean Sea in the north to the Horn of Africa and the Indian Ocean in the southeast. The combined population is over 450 million people.

Definition

The Arab world is defined primarily by language and culture: its inhabitants are united by the Arabic language, which is the official language of all Arab League states, though many countries include large populations speaking other native languages such as Kurdish, Berber (Tamazight), Somali, and Nubian. The region broadly coincides with the area conquered by Arab armies during the early Muslim conquests of the 7th and 8th centuries CE and subsequently Arabized.

Geography and institutions

The Arab states can be grouped into the Maghreb of North Africa (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Mauritania), the Nile Valley (Egypt, Sudan), the Levant (Palestine, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria), Iraq, and the Arabian Peninsula (Saudi Arabia, Yemen, Oman, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait), plus Djibouti, Somalia, and the Comoros. The Arab League, founded in Cairo in 1945, is the region's principal political organization.
