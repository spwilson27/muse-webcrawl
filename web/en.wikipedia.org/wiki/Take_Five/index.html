AlbumTime Out (1959)
ComposerPaul Desmond
GenreCool jazz
Meter5/4 ("quintuple time")

"Take Five" is a jazz standard composed by saxophonist Paul Desmond and originally recorded by the Dave Brubeck Quartet for their 1959 album Time Out. It became the best-selling jazz single of all time and is famous for its distinctive two-chord piano vamp, its catchy blues-scale saxophone melody, and its unusual 5/4 time signature, from which the title derives — both a reference to the quintuple meter and to the slang phrase "take five" (take a break).

Composition and recording

Brubeck had asked Desmond to write a piece in 5/4 for an album experimenting with odd meters inspired by Eurasian folk music heard on tour. Desmond combined two melody ideas over a piano vamp devised with the group; drummer Joe Morello's light, inventive solo on the record became a celebrated feature. The quartet on the recording was Brubeck (piano), Desmond (alto saxophone), Eugene Wright (bass) and Morello (drums).

Reception and legacy

Released as a single in 1961, "Take Five" reached No. 25 on the Billboard Hot 100 and No. 5 on the adult contemporary chart — an extraordinary success for an instrumental jazz recording in an odd meter. Desmond bequeathed his performance royalties from the song to the American Red Cross, which has received well over six figures from them. The piece has been recorded by countless artists across genres and is widely used in film, television and advertising. In 2020 the Time Out recording was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame.
