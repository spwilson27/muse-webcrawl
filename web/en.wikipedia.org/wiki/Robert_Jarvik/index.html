Robert Jarvik

Robert Koffler Jarvik (born May 11, 1946) is an American scientist, researcher and entrepreneur best known for his role in developing the Jarvik-7, the first permanently implantable artificial heart. The device was implanted into retired dentist Barney Clark on December 2, 1982, at the University of Utah; Clark survived for 112 days, an event that drew worldwide attention to artificial organ research.

Early life and education

Jarvik was born in Midland, Michigan, and grew up in Stamford, Connecticut. He studied engineering at Syracuse University before earning a master's degree in medical engineering from New York University. He later entered the University of Utah College of Medicine, completing his MD in 1976, though he never completed a residency or became licensed to practise medicine.

The Jarvik-7

Working under Willem Kolff, the pioneer of artificial organs, Jarvik refined designs originating with Paul Winchell and others into the Jarvik-7, a pneumatically driven two-chamber heart made of polyurethane and aluminium. It was implanted in four further patients after Clark, the longest survivor living 620 days. Ethical and quality-of-life concerns eventually led the FDA to withdraw approval for permanent use in 1990, though the design continued as a bridge-to-transplant device.

Later work

Through his company Jarvik Heart, Inc., Jarvik developed the Jarvik 2000, a compact axial-flow ventricular assist device small enough to fit inside the left ventricle. He became a familiar public figure through advertisements for the cholesterol drug Lipitor in the 2000s, which drew scrutiny over his medical credentials and the campaign was withdrawn.
