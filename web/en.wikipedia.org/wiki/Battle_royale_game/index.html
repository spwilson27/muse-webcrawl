Battle royale game

A battle royale game is an online multiplayer video game genre that blends last-man-standing gameplay with the exploration, scavenging, and survival elements of a survival game. Battle royale games involve dozens to hundreds of players, who start with minimal equipment and must search for weapons and armor while eliminating all other opponents and avoiding being trapped outside of a shrinking "safe area", with the winner being the last player or team alive.

The name for the genre is taken from the 2000 Japanese film Battle Royale, which itself was based on the 1999 novel of the same name, and which presents a similar theme of a last-man-standing competition in a shrinking play zone. The genre's origins arose from mods for large-scale online survival games such as Minecraft and ARMA 2 in the early 2010s. By the end of the decade, the genre became a cultural phenomenon, with standalone games such as Fortnite Battle Royale, PUBG: Battlegrounds, Apex Legends, and Call of Duty: Warzone each amassing tens of millions of players within months of release.

Concept

Battle royale games are played between many individual players, or a number of small squads (typically two to five players). In each match, the objective is to be the last player or team standing by eliminating all other opponents. A match starts by placing the player-characters into a map, typically by having them skydiving from a large aircraft, with little to no equipment. The map may include a mix of urban and rural environments. Once landed, the players must search the map for equipment to help them survive, including weapons, ammunition, armor, and healing items.

As a match progresses, the playable safe area of the map shrinks, typically centered on a random location chosen during the match. Players caught outside the safe area take damage over time and will eventually be eliminated if they do not return. This mechanic forces encounters between surviving players. The last player or team alive wins the match.

History

Elements of the battle royale format appeared in mods for survival games in the early 2010s. Brendan "PlayerUnknown" Greene developed mods for ARMA 2 and ARMA 3 inspired by the Battle Royale film, and later consulted on H1Z1: King of the Kill before creating PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds (2017), the first standalone title to popularize the genre. Epic Games released Fortnite Battle Royale in September 2017, free-to-play, and it rapidly became a global phenomenon. Subsequent major titles include Respawn Entertainment's Apex Legends (2019) and Activision's Call of Duty: Warzone (2020), as well as mobile successes such as PUBG Mobile and Garena Free Fire.

Reception and cultural impact

The genre grew explosively: by 2018 battle royale games were estimated to generate billions of dollars in annual revenue, and Fortnite Battle Royale alone surpassed 125 million players in its first year. Battle royale design influenced other genres, spawned live-streaming and esports scenes, and introduced popular crossover events with film, music, and sports brands.
