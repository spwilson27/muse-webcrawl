European Arrest Warrant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The European Arrest Warrant (EAW) is an arrest warrant valid throughout the territory of the European Union, introduced by a Council Framework Decision of 13 June 2002 and in force from 1 January 2004. It replaced political extradition between member states with a judicial, deadline-driven surrender system based on mutual recognition: a warrant issued by a judicial authority in one member state must be executed by another, generally within sixty days (ten where the person consents), for offences punishable by at least twelve months' imprisonment; for a list of 32 serious offence categories, the executing state may not check double criminality.

The EAW drastically cut surrender times (from an average of about a year to a matter of weeks) and enabled high-profile returns (failed London bomber Hussain Osman from Italy in 2005 among the early cases), but generated friction over proportionality (warrants for trivial offences, later curbed by guidance and reforms), prison conditions (the Court of Justice in Aranyosi and Căldăraru, 2016, permitted refusal where detention conditions breach fundamental rights) and rule-of-law backsliding in issuing states. Since Brexit the United Kingdom participates in a similar surrender regime under the EU–UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement.
