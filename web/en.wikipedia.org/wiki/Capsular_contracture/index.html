Capsular contracture

Diagram of a breast implant surrounded by a fibrous capsule. (Image placeholder)

Capsular contracture is a complication of breast implant surgery in which the fibrous capsule that normally forms around an implant tightens and squeezes the implant, causing the breast to feel firm or hard, become distorted in shape, and sometimes become painful. It is one of the most common reasons for reoperation following breast augmentation and reconstruction.

Mechanism and causes

The body naturally forms a capsule of scar tissue around any implanted foreign object. In capsular contracture this capsule becomes abnormally thick and contracts, compressing the implant. The precise cause is not fully understood, but proposed contributing factors include bacterial biofilm and subclinical infection, haematoma or seroma, silicone gel bleed or implant rupture, radiotherapy after reconstruction, and individual variation in the inflammatory and healing response.

Classification

Severity is commonly graded using the Baker classification:

  • Grade I — the breast is soft and looks natural; the capsule is not palpable.
  • Grade II — the breast is slightly firm but looks normal.
  • Grade III — the breast is firm and visibly distorted.
  • Grade IV — the breast is hard, painful and severely distorted.

Treatment and prevention

Mild cases may be managed with observation or medication such as leukotriene inhibitors. Significant contracture usually requires surgery: capsulotomy (releasing the capsule) or capsulectomy (removing it), often with implant exchange, change of implant position, or use of an acellular dermal matrix. Preventive measures emphasised by surgeons include sterile technique, minimising contamination of the implant (the "no-touch" technique), appropriate pocket irrigation, and careful haemostasis. Reported rates vary widely, from a few percent to over 15% depending on implant type, indication and length of follow-up; contracture is more common after reconstruction and radiotherapy than after primary cosmetic augmentation.
