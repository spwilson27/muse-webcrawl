General Electric F404

The General Electric F404 and F412 are a family of afterburning turbofan engines in the 10,500–19,000 lbf (47–85 kN) class, developed by GE Aerospace (formerly General Electric Aircraft Engines). The F404, derived from the YJ101 engine of the Northrop YF-17, became one of the most widely used fighter engines in the world, best known for powering the Boeing F/A-18 Hornet. More than 4,000 F404 engines have been delivered.

The F404 powers the F/A-18 Hornet and several other military aircraft.

Development

GE developed the F404 from the YJ101 built for the losing YF-17 in the US Air Force Lightweight Fighter competition. When the YF-17 design evolved into the Navy's F/A-18 Hornet, the enlarged F404 was selected as its engine. Design priorities were reliability, rapid throttle response and resistance to compressor stalls during aggressive manoeuvring, rather than maximum raw performance; the engine entered service with the Hornet in the early 1980s and developed a strong reputation for dependability.

Variants and applications

The baseline F404-GE-400 powered early Hornets; the enhanced F404-GE-402 (Enhanced Performance Engine) equipped later C/D models and export aircraft. Non-afterburning versions power the F-117 Nighthawk stealth attack aircraft. Licensed and derivative installations include Sweden's Saab JAS 39 Gripen (Volvo RM12), South Korea's T-50 family, India's HAL Tejas, and Singapore's A-4SU Super Skyhawk. The uprated F414, with about 22,000 lbf of thrust, powers the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet and Gripen E/F. The F412 was a non-afterburning derivative developed for the cancelled A-12 Avenger II.

Specifications (F404-GE-402)

  • Length: about 154 in (3.9 m); diameter about 35 in (0.89 m).
  • Dry weight: about 2,282 lb (1,035 kg).
  • Compressor: 3 fan stages and 7 high-pressure compressor stages.
  • Overall pressure ratio: about 26:1; bypass ratio about 0.34:1.
