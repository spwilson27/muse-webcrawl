Mixed reality (MR) is a term used to describe the merging of real and virtual worlds to produce new environments and visualisations, where physical and digital objects co-exist and interact in real time. Mixed reality is a blend of physical and digital worlds, unlocking natural and intuitive three-dimensional human–computer interactions. The term was introduced in a 1994 paper by Paul Milgram and Fumio Kishino, "A Taxonomy of Mixed Reality Visual Displays", which defined the reality–virtuality continuum running from the completely real environment to a completely virtual one, with augmented reality (AR) and augmented virtuality (AV) lying in between.

Definitions

On Milgram's continuum, mixed reality covers all variations between the real environment and a fully virtual environment. Augmented reality, in which virtual content overlays the real world without deeply interacting with it, sits near the real end; augmented virtuality, in which real objects are inserted into a virtual world, sits near the virtual end. In industry usage, particularly following Microsoft's promotion of the term for its Windows Mixed Reality platform and HoloLens headset, "mixed reality" is often used more narrowly for experiences in which virtual objects are anchored to, occluded by, and interactive with the physical environment — sometimes treated as a subset or advanced form of augmented reality. The umbrella term extended reality (XR) is also used to cover AR, MR and VR together.

Technology

Mixed reality systems rely on inside-out tracking (cameras and sensors on the headset), simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM), depth sensing, and spatial mapping so that holograms remain fixed in space and can be hidden behind real objects. Input is typically provided by gaze, hand gestures, voice, or six-degree-of-freedom controllers. Applications are commonly built with game engines such as Unity and Unreal and standards such as OpenXR.

Applications

Applications include remote assistance and guided repair for field technicians, surgical planning and medical training, architecture and engineering design reviews, military and industrial training simulations, education, and entertainment. Devices include the Microsoft HoloLens (2016) and HoloLens 2 (2019), Magic Leap headsets, and passthrough-based headsets such as Meta Quest 3 and Apple Vision Pro (2024), which blend camera feeds of the real world with virtual content.
