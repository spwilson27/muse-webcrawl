Advertising agency

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


An agency creative department at work (placeholder image).

An advertising agency, often referred to as a creative agency, is a business dedicated to creating, planning and handling advertising and other forms of promotion and marketing for its clients. An agency is generally independent of the client and provides an external point of view to the effort of selling the client's products or services; it may also handle overall marketing, branding strategies and sales promotions.

History

The first recognised advertising agency was founded by William Taylor in London in 1786; in the United States, Volney B. Palmer opened an agency in Philadelphia in 1843, acting as a broker of newspaper space. In the late nineteenth century agencies such as N. W. Ayer & Son began offering full services including copywriting and art. The twentieth century saw the rise of large agency networks and, from the 1980s, consolidation into global holding companies such as WPP, Omnicom, Publicis Groupe and Interpublic Group.

Services and structure

A full-service agency typically combines account management, strategic planning, creative departments (copywriters and art directors), media planning and buying, and production. Historically agencies were compensated by a commission (traditionally 15%) on media billings; modern compensation more often takes the form of fees, retainers or performance-based arrangements.

Types of agency

Besides full-service agencies, the industry includes media agencies specialising in planning and buying, digital and social agencies, branding consultancies, and in-house agencies operated by advertisers themselves.
