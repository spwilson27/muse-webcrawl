The Mohammed bin Rashid Space Centre (MBRSC) is a Dubai government organisation working on the UAE space programme. Founded in 2006 as the Emirates Institution for Advanced Science and Technology (EIAST) and renamed in 2015, the centre builds and operates Earth observation satellites, develops interplanetary missions, and runs the UAE Astronaut Programme. It works in coordination with the UAE Space Agency.

Programmes

The centre's satellite projects include DubaiSat-1 (launched 2009) and DubaiSat-2 (2013), built in partnership with South Korean firms, and KhalifaSat (2018), the first satellite designed and built entirely in the UAE by Emirati engineers. In 2021 MBRSC launched the Emirates Mars Mission's Hope probe (Al Amal), which entered orbit around Mars in February 2021, making the UAE the first Arab nation to reach the planet.

Through the UAE Astronaut Programme, Hazza Al Mansouri became the first Emirati in space in 2019, flying to the International Space Station, and Sultan Al Neyadi completed a six-month mission aboard the station in 2023. The centre is also developing the Rashid lunar rover programme and the MBZ-SAT high-resolution imaging satellite.

Facilities

MBRSC is headquartered in Dubai, where it operates satellite clean rooms, ground stations and mission control facilities used to support its Earth observation and deep-space missions.
