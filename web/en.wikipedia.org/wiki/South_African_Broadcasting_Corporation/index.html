The South African Broadcasting Corporation (SABC) is the public broadcaster of South Africa, providing television and radio services in the country's official languages. It operates television channels including SABC 1, SABC 2, SABC 3, and SABC News, and a large network of radio stations such as Ukhozi FM, Metro FM, and Radio Sonder Grense.

History

The SABC was established in 1936, taking over from the earlier African Broadcasting Company. It introduced television only in 1976, having long resisted the medium under the apartheid government, and for decades served as a mouthpiece of the state. After the transition to democracy in 1994 it was restructured as a public broadcaster with a mandate of editorial independence, though it has repeatedly faced political interference controversies and severe financial difficulties.

Funding

The SABC is funded mainly through advertising and television licence fees, supplemented at times by government support. Licence-fee compliance is low, and the broadcaster has required repeated bailouts.
