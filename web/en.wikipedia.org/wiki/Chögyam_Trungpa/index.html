Chögyam Trungpa

Chögyam Trungpa Rinpoche
Born: 28 February 1939, Kham, Tibet
Died: 4 April 1987, Halifax, Canada
Tradition: Kagyu and Nyingma (Tibetan Buddhism)
Founded: Naropa University, Shambhala International

Chögyam Trungpa (1939–1987) was a Tibetan Buddhist meditation master who played a major role in bringing Tibetan Buddhism to the West. Recognised as the 11th Trungpa tulku, he fled Tibet in 1959 after the Chinese occupation, escaping over the Himalayas to India.

Life in the West

He studied at Oxford, co-founded Samye Ling monastery in Scotland, then moved to the United States in 1970, founding centers in Vermont and Boulder, Colorado, and Naropa University (1974), the first accredited Buddhist university in North America. His book Cutting Through Spiritual Materialism (1973) became a classic.

Shambhala and legacy

From the late 1970s he presented the secular Shambhala teachings on enlightened society. His unconventional 'crazy wisdom' style and his alcohol use and personal conduct drew both devotion and controversy, which continued around his successor. He died in Halifax, Nova Scotia, where the community had relocated.
