Enterprise bargaining agreement

An enterprise bargaining agreement (EBA), now generally simply an enterprise agreement, is a collective agreement made at an enterprise level between an employer (or employers) and employees about terms and conditions of employment, under Australian labour law. Enterprise agreements sit alongside modern awards and the National Employment Standards as sources of legally enforceable minimum terms in the Australian federal workplace relations system.

History

Enterprise bargaining was introduced into Australia in the early 1990s as wage-fixing moved away from centrally determined awards. The Industrial Relations Reform Act 1993 established certified agreements, the Workplace Relations Act 1996 continued the model (alongside individual Australian Workplace Agreements), and the Fair Work Act 2009 consolidated the current framework of enterprise agreements, overseen by the Fair Work Commission.

Making and approval

An agreement is negotiated between the employer and employees (usually represented by a trade union as bargaining representative), must be approved by a majority of employees who vote, and must then be approved by the Fair Work Commission. The Commission must be satisfied, among other things, that the agreement passes the better off overall test (BOOT): each employee must be better off overall under the agreement than under the relevant modern award. Agreements have a maximum nominal term of four years but continue to operate until replaced or terminated. Types include single-enterprise agreements, multi-enterprise agreements and greenfields agreements made for a new enterprise before employees are hired.

Industrial action

Employees may take protected industrial action — such as strikes — in support of a proposed agreement during a defined bargaining period, provided procedural requirements (including a protected action ballot) are met. Action outside these rules is unprotected and unlawful. Bargaining disputes can be referred to the Fair Work Commission for conciliation and, in limited circumstances, arbitration.
