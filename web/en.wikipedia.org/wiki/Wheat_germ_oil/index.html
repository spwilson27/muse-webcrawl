Wheat germ oil

Wheat germ oil is a vegetable oil extracted from the germ (embryo) of the wheat kernel, which makes up only about 2–3% of the grain by weight. Because the germ is rich in lipids, the oil is notable for its exceptionally high content of vitamin E and of octacosanol, and it is widely used in cosmetics, food supplements, and some cooking applications.

Composition

Wheat germ oil is among the richest natural sources of vitamin E (tocopherols, especially alpha-tocopherol), typically containing around 150 mg per 100 g. Its fatty acid profile is dominated by polyunsaturated linoleic acid (omega-6, roughly 55–60%), with oleic acid (omega-9) and palmitic acid making up most of the rest, plus a small share of alpha-linolenic acid (omega-3). It also contains octacosanol, a long-chain fatty alcohol studied in sports nutrition, and phytosterols.

Production and properties

The oil is obtained by cold pressing or solvent extraction of separated wheat germ, a by-product of flour milling. Unrefined wheat germ oil is dark amber, strongly flavoured, and highly prone to oxidation because of its unsaturated fat content; it should be refrigerated and has a relatively low smoke point, making it better suited to dressings and finishing than to frying.

Uses

In cosmetics the oil is used in skin and hair products for its emollient and antioxidant properties. As a supplement it is sold in capsules as a source of vitamin E and octacosanol. Claims of performance or fertility benefits are traditional and only weakly supported by clinical evidence.
