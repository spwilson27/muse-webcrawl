Kunrei-shiki romanization

Kunrei-shiki rōmaji (Japanese: 訓令式ローマ字, "Cabinet-ordered romanization") is a system for transcribing the Japanese language into the Latin alphabet. It is based on the older Nihon-shiki system and was promulgated by the Japanese government in 1937, again in 1954. It is taught in Japanese primary schools and used in government publications, though the modified Hepburn system remains more common internationally.

Characteristics

Kunrei-shiki follows the phonemic structure of Japanese more regularly than Hepburn. Syllables are written to match the structure of the kana syllabary, so that, for example, the syllable spelled shi in Hepburn is si in Kunrei-shiki, chi becomes ti, tsu becomes tu, fu becomes hu, and ji becomes zi.

Usage

The system is officially used by Japanese government ministries, in elementary school education, and forms the basis of the ISO 3602 standard. Its regularity makes it advantageous for morphophonemic analysis and for Japanese language processing, though its spellings can mislead non-Japanese readers about pronunciation.
