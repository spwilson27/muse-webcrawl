Ring of Brodgar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ring of Brodgar (or Brogar, or Ring o' Brodgar) is a Neolithic henge and stone circle about 6 miles northeast of Stromness on Mainland, the largest of the Orkney Islands in Scotland. It is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site known as the Heart of Neolithic Orkney. The ring stands on a narrow isthmus between the Lochs of Stenness and Harray.

Description

The stone circle is 104 metres (341 ft) in diameter, the third largest in the British Isles after Avebury and Stanton Drew. Originally composed of some 60 stones, 27 survive standing today, ranging from 2.1 m to 4.7 m in height. The stones are encircled by a rock-cut circular ditch up to 3 m deep and 9 m wide, with two entrance causeways. Unlike some henges, there is no evidence of stones inside the ditch.

Dating and purpose

The monument was built in the late Neolithic, probably between 2500 and 2000 BC, making it among the last of the great Neolithic monuments built in Orkney. Its exact purpose remains unknown; theories include ritual and ceremonial use and astronomical observation — alignments with the nearby Barnhouse Stone and other outliers have been proposed, though some claimed alignments are debated. Excavations around the Ness of Brodgar, between the Ring and the Stones of Stenness, have revealed a large complex of monumental buildings, reinforcing the area's ritual significance.

Folklore

Local tradition associated the circle with the "Temple of the Sun" (the Stones of Stenness being the "Temple of the Moon") and with betrothal customs centred on the Odin Stone, destroyed in 1814.
