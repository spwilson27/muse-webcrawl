Patan Durbar Square

Patan Durbar Square
Location: Lalitpur, Nepal
Period: Malla dynasty, mainly 17th century
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (Kathmandu Valley)

Patan Durbar Square is one of the three Durbar Squares in Nepal's Kathmandu Valley, situated in the city of Lalitpur (historically Patan). It is a showcase of Newar architecture and the artistry of the Malla dynasty, whose kings built palaces, courtyards, and temples there between the 14th and 18th centuries. The square is part of the Kathmandu Valley UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Description

The palace complex comprises three main courtyards — Mul Chowk, Sundari Chowk, and Mani Keshab Narayan Chowk — and houses the Patan Museum, noted for its collection of Hindu and Buddhist art. Facing the palace is a dense cluster of temples, including the stone Krishna Mandir (1637) built by King Siddhi Narasingh Malla in the shikhara style, the Bhimsen Temple, the Vishwanath Temple, and the Taleju Bhawani Temple. A tall column bearing a statue of King Yoganarendra Malla faces the palace.

Earthquake damage

The square was severely damaged by the April 2015 Nepal earthquake, which destroyed or badly damaged several temples and palace structures, including the Char Narayan and Hari Shankar temples. Extensive restoration, supported by international and local organizations, has since rebuilt many of the monuments using traditional materials and documentation.
