Chan Heung

Chan Heung (Chinese: 陳享; 23 August 1806 – 20 August 1875) was a Chinese martial artist and the founder of the Choy Li Fut (蔡李佛) system of kung fu, one of the most widely practised Southern Chinese martial arts. He combined the teachings of his three masters — the Choy family style, the Li family style, and the Fut (Buddhist) style he learned at the Shaolin Temple — into a single system, which he named in their honour.

Early life and training

Chan Heung was born in Jing Mei (King Mui) village in Xinhui district, Guangdong. At the age of seven he began training under his uncle Chan Yuen-Wu, a practitioner of the Choy family style who had studied at the Southern Shaolin Temple. After about ten years, his uncle sent him to Li Yau-San, a master of the Li family style, under whom he trained for several more years.

Seeking to complete his training, Chan Heung was directed to the monk Choy Fook, a former Shaolin martial artist who had become a hermit on Mount Luofu. After years of persistence, Chan Heung was accepted as a student and studied the Buddhist (Fut Gar) arts and Shaolin kung fu under Choy Fook for roughly a decade.

Founding of Choy Li Fut

In 1836 Chan Heung returned to Jing Mei and formally established his system, naming it Choy Li Fut (蔡李佛) by combining the surnames of his teachers Choy Fook and Li Yau-San with Fut (佛, "Buddha") to honour the Buddhist source of the art and his first teacher Chan Yuen-Wu's tradition. He opened his first school in his home village and soon was teaching throughout Guangdong.

The system is characterised by powerful long-arm swinging techniques, low stances, and a large curriculum of hand forms, weapons forms, and training apparatus routines. Chan Heung's students included many who spread the art through China and later overseas; several of them were involved in the revolutionary movements of the late Qing period, and Choy Li Fut schools became associated with anti-Qing secret societies.

Legacy

Chan Heung died in 1875. His descendants — notably his son Chan Koon-Pak — and his senior students carried the system forward, producing the major branches of Choy Li Fut, including the King Mui (Jing Mei), Jiangmen (Kong Moon), and Buk Sing lines. Today Choy Li Fut is practised worldwide and is among the most popular styles of Chinese martial arts outside China.
