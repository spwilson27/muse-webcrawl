Gjakmarrja

Gjakmarrja ("blood-taking") is the Albanian tradition of blood feuding, governed for centuries by the Kanun, the customary legal code attributed to Lekë Dukagjini. Under its rules, a killing obliges the victim's family to take revenge on the male members of the killer's family, who may be confined to their homes for years to avoid being targeted.

History

The practice, concentrated in northern Albania and parts of Kosovo and Montenegro, was suppressed under the communist regime but revived after 1991 amid weak state institutions, leaving many families — and especially children — housebound in "blood feud" isolation.

Reconciliation

In the 1990s a mass reconciliation movement led by Anton Çetta brought together thousands of feuding families in Kosovo. In Albania, NGOs and religious figures continue mediation work, and modern law treats feud killings as serious crimes, though cases persist.
