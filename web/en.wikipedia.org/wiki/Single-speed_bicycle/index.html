Single-speed bicycle

A single-speed bicycle is a type of bicycle with a single gear ratio. These bicycles are without derailleur gears, hub gearing or other methods for varying the gear ratio of the bicycle. Many modern single-speed bicycles have a flip-flop hub, which allows the rider to swap between a freewheel and a fixed gear.

Advantages and disadvantages

Compared with multi-geared bicycles, single-speed bicycles are mechanically simpler, lighter, more durable and cheaper, with fewer components to adjust or repair. The absence of shifters and derailleurs also makes them popular for urban commuting and winter riding. Their principal disadvantage is that the single gear must be a compromise: a ratio suitable for climbing hills is slow on the flat, and a ratio suitable for speed makes climbing difficult. Riders must also pedal at widely varying cadences.

Types

Freewheel single-speeds

Most single-speeds allow the rider to coast, using a freewheel mechanism at the rear hub. They are common as city bikes, cruisers, BMX bicycles and children's bikes.

Fixed-gear bicycles

A fixed-gear bicycle ("fixie") has the rear cog fixed to the hub, so the pedals turn whenever the bicycle moves and coasting is impossible. Track bicycles are built this way, and fixed-gear machines have a strong following among urban riders and couriers.

Conversions

Older multi-speed bicycles are frequently converted to single-speed by removing the derailleurs and fitting a single chainring and cog, a practice facilitated by horizontal dropouts on vintage frames.
