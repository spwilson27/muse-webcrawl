Treble (association football)

A treble in association football is achieved when a club team wins three trophies in a single season. A continental treble involves winning the club's top domestic league competition, its main domestic cup competition, and its continental trophy — in Europe, the UEFA Champions League. A domestic treble involves winning three domestic trophies.

Continental trebles

In European football, the treble of league, main cup and European Cup/Champions League has been achieved by a small number of clubs: Celtic (1967, the first), Ajax (1972), PSV Eindhoven (1988), Manchester United (1999), Barcelona (2009 and 2015), Inter Milan (2010), Bayern Munich (2013 and 2020), Manchester City (2023) and Paris Saint-Germain (2025). Barcelona and Bayern Munich are the only men's clubs to have achieved it twice. Pep Guardiola is the only manager to have won it with two different clubs (Barcelona 2009 and Manchester City 2023).

Domestic trebles

Domestic trebles — typically a league title plus two domestic cups — are more common, particularly in countries with both a principal cup and a league cup. Scottish clubs Celtic and Rangers have each won several domestic trebles, and Celtic completed an unprecedented "quadruple treble" of four consecutive domestic trebles between 2017 and 2020.

Other trebles

The term is also applied to combinations including continental super cups and intercontinental trophies, and in women's football, where clubs such as Lyon and Barcelona have won trebles including the UEFA Women's Champions League.
