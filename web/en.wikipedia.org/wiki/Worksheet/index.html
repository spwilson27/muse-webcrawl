Worksheet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A worksheet, in the original meaning, is a sheet of paper on which one performs work. They come in many forms, most commonly associated with children's school work assignments, tax forms, and accounting. In education, a worksheet may have questions for students and places to record answers. In accounting, a worksheet is (or was) a sheet of ruled paper with rows and columns on which an accountant could record information or perform calculations.

In education

Worksheets are widely used in schools for practice in mathematics, language and reading comprehension. They range from simple fill-in exercises to structured problem sets with worked examples. Educators and researchers debate their balance against open-ended activities: worksheets offer standardised, easily marked practice, but critics argue they can encourage rote learning if overused. Digital worksheets and interactive PDF or tablet-based versions have become common with classroom technology.

In spreadsheets

In spreadsheet software such as Microsoft Excel and LibreOffice Calc, a single document (a workbook) may contain multiple sheets, called worksheets, each a grid of cells identified by column letters and row numbers. This usage descends directly from the paper accounting worksheet.
