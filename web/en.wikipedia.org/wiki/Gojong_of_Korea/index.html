Gojong (8 September 1852 – 21 January 1919), personal name Yi Hui, was the last king of the Joseon dynasty and the first emperor of the Korean Empire. Ascending the throne as a child in 1864, he reigned during the turbulent final decades of Korean independence, as Joseon was forced open to foreign trade and became the object of rivalry between Qing China, the Empire of Japan and the Russian Empire.

Early reign and regency

Gojong came to the throne at the age of eleven; his father, the Heungseon Daewongun, ruled as regent for a decade, pursuing isolationist policies and reforms of the court. From the 1870s Gojong ruled personally alongside his wife Queen Min (posthumously Empress Myeongseong), whose faction favoured opening to foreign powers. Korea signed its first modern treaty with Japan in 1876 and further treaties with the United States and European powers in the 1880s.

Korean Empire

Following the First Sino-Japanese War, the Donghak Peasant Revolution and the assassination of Queen Min by Japanese agents in 1895, Gojong took refuge for a year in the Russian legation. In 1897 he proclaimed the Korean Empire (Daehan Jeguk) and took the imperial title, launching the Gwangmu Reform to modernise the military, economy and administration. After Japan's victory in the Russo-Japanese War, the Eulsa Treaty of 1905 made Korea a Japanese protectorate. Gojong's secret emissaries to the Hague Peace Conference of 1907 led Japan to force his abdication in favour of his son Sunjong. Korea was annexed by Japan in 1910.

Death and legacy

Gojong died in 1919 at Deoksugung Palace; rumours that he had been poisoned by the Japanese helped ignite the March First Movement for Korean independence. He is buried at the Hongneung tomb in Namyangju.
