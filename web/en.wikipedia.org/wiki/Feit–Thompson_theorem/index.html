Feit–Thompson theorem

In mathematics, the Feit–Thompson theorem, or odd order theorem, states that every finite group of odd order is solvable. It was proved by Walter Feit and John Griggs Thompson in 1963.

Significance

The theorem was a landmark in the classification of finite simple groups: it implies that a nonabelian finite simple group must have even order and therefore contain an involution, focusing subsequent work on involutions. It settled a conjecture originating with Burnside.

Proof

The original proof, published in the Pacific Journal of Mathematics, filled an entire issue and ran to about 255 pages, drawing on character theory and local analysis. Simplified and revised proofs followed, and the theorem was the first major result to be fully formalized in a proof assistant, completed in the Coq system in 2012 by a team led by Georges Gonthier.
