Chief Statistician of Canada

The Chief Statistician of Canada (French: Statisticien en chef du Canada) is the head of Statistics Canada, the federal government agency responsible for producing statistics to help Canadians better understand their country, its population, resources, economy, society and culture. The Chief Statistician is the deputy head of the agency and is appointed by the Governor in Council.

Statistics Canada operates under the Statistics Act, which gives the Chief Statistician considerable independence in technical and statistical matters. The agency's best-known products include the Census of Population, conducted every five years, the Labour Force Survey, and a wide range of economic indicators such as the consumer price index and gross domestic product.

History

The office traces its origins to the creation of the Dominion Bureau of Statistics in 1918, following the Statistics Act of that year. The Bureau's first chief was Robert H. Coats, who served from 1918 to 1942 and is credited with building the foundation of Canada's modern statistical system. The Dominion Bureau of Statistics became Statistics Canada in 1971.

Munir Sheikh resigned in 2010 in protest over the government's decision to replace the mandatory long-form census with a voluntary survey, a controversy that highlighted the office's tradition of professional independence. The mandatory long-form census was restored in 2015, and Wayne Smith subsequently resigned in 2016 over concerns about the agency's information technology independence.
