Zombie fiction

Zombie fiction is a genre of fiction in which stories concern zombies — reanimated corpses or creatures that feed on human flesh. The modern zombie originates in Haitian folklore, entered Western popular culture through works such as William Seabrook's The Magic Island (1929), and was fundamentally reshaped by George A. Romero's film Night of the Living Dead (1968), which established the flesh-eating ghoul archetype.

History and themes

Zombie fiction spans film, literature, comics, television and video games. Major works include Romero's Dead series, Max Brooks's novel World War Z (2006), the comic and television series The Walking Dead, and games such as Resident Evil. The genre frequently functions as social commentary and apocalyptic fiction: the zombie outbreak stands in for plague, consumerism, social collapse, or loss of individuality, while the human survivors' struggles carry the narrative.

Subgenres vary the formula — fast-moving infected as in 28 Days Later (2002), comic treatments such as Shaun of the Dead (2004), and "zombie apocalypse" survival scenarios. Interest in the genre surged in the 2000s and 2010s, making zombie fiction a major strand of horror.
