Abusir

Abusir (Arabic: أبو صير; Coptic: ⲃⲟⲩⲥⲓⲣⲓ, "house of Osiris") is an archaeological locality on the west bank of the Nile south of Cairo, Egypt, forming part of the vast necropolis of ancient Memphis. It is famed for the pyramids of several Fifth Dynasty pharaohs and for the Abusir Papyri, the oldest known papyri from the Old Kingdom.

Monuments

Four kings of the Fifth Dynasty (c. 2494–2345 BCE) built pyramid complexes at Abusir: Sahure, Neferirkare Kakai, Neferefre (unfinished, later converted to a mastaba-like monument) and Nyuserre Ini, who also completed a sun temple at nearby Abu Ghurab. The pyramids are smaller and less solidly built than those of Giza, with cores of loose local stone that have collapsed into mounds, but their mortuary temples were richly decorated with some of the finest relief art of the Old Kingdom; Sahure's causeway and temple reliefs depict expeditions to Punt and Syrian campaigns. Queens' pyramids and the mastaba of the vizier Ptahshepses complete the field, and later periods are represented by the Late Period shaft tombs, including that of the priest Iufaa, found intact in the 1990s.

Abusir Papyri and excavation

The Abusir Papyri, discovered by illicit diggers in the 1890s and largely acquired by museums in Berlin and London, are administrative archives from the mortuary temple of Neferirkare, recording inventories, duty rosters and accounts. Systematic excavation began with Ludwig Borchardt's German expeditions (1902–1908); since 1976 a Czech (formerly Czechoslovak) mission has worked at Abusir South, uncovering further tombs and the intact shaft tomb of Iufaa. The site lies within the UNESCO World Heritage listing of Memphis and its Necropolis.
