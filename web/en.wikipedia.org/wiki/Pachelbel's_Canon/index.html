Pachelbel's Canon

Pachelbel's Canon is the common name for the Canon and Gigue in D major by the German Baroque composer Johann Pachelbel (1653–1706). It was composed around 1694–1706 and is scored for three violins and basso continuo.

Structure

The canon is built over a two-bar ground bass (ostinato) of eight notes — D, A, B, F♯, G, D, G, A — repeated throughout, while the three violins play a strict canon at the unison with increasingly elaborate variations. The canon is followed by a lively gigue, which is far less frequently performed.

Popularity

Long obscure, the piece became famous in the 1970s through recordings such as that of the Jean-François Paillard chamber orchestra and its use in the 1980 film Ordinary People. It is now among the most recognisable pieces of classical music, a staple of weddings, and its chord progression has been echoed in numerous popular songs.
