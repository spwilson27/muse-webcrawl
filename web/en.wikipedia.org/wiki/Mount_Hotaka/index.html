Mount Hotaka

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hotaka massif in Chūbu-Sangaku National Park.

Mount Hotaka (Japanese: 穂高岳, Hotaka-dake) is a mountain in the Hida Mountains (the "Northern Alps") of Japan, straddling the border of Nagano and Gifu prefectures. Its highest summit, Okuhotaka, rises to 3,190 metres (10,466 ft), making it the third-highest peak in Japan after Mount Fuji and Mount Kita. The massif lies within Chūbu-Sangaku National Park.

Peaks and climbing

The Hotaka group includes Okuhotaka, Karasawa-dake, Kitahotaka, Maehotaka, and Myōjin-dake, connected by a jagged, heavily eroded ridgeline that is among the most popular — and most demanding — alpine traverses in Japan. The cirque of Karasawa, at the mountain's eastern foot, fills each autumn with coloured maple leaves and rows of tents. The resort valley of Kamikōchi below the west face is the usual gateway, and the missionary mountaineer Walter Weston, who popularised the Japanese Alps in the 1890s, praised the peak's "splendid precipices."

Geology

The massif consists of volcanic rocks uplifted with the Hida range; glaciers carved its cirques during the last ice age, and small remnant ice patches survive in shaded gullies. A shrine to the sea god Hotaka no Mikoto — also enshrined at Hotaka Shrine near Azumino — stands on the summit of Okuhotaka, reflecting the mountain's role as a guardian of sailors despite its inland position.
