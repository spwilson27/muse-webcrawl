Referral marketing

Referral marketing is a strategy that encourages existing customers to refer new customers to a business, often through structured referral programmes that reward the referrer, the new customer, or both. It builds on word-of-mouth marketing but differs in being deliberately initiated, tracked and incentivised by the company.

Mechanisms

Common mechanisms include personal referral links and codes, two-sided rewards (for example, credit to both the referrer and the friend), and tiered bonuses. Firms such as Dropbox, which grew its user base dramatically by offering extra storage for referrals, and PayPal, which paid cash bounties for new users, are frequently cited examples.

Effectiveness

Referred customers tend to be acquired at lower cost and to show higher retention and lifetime value than customers from advertising, because recommendations from trusted contacts carry credibility. Referral programmes must usually comply with advertising and disclosure rules governing incentivised endorsements.
