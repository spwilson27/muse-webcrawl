Spiral Jetty

Spiral Jetty is an earthwork sculpture constructed in April 1970 by American artist Robert Smithson. Built on the northeastern shore of the Great Salt Lake near Rozel Point in Utah, it is considered the central work of Smithson's career and a landmark of Land Art.

Spiral Jetty
Artist: Robert Smithson
Year: 1970
Medium: Basalt, earth, salt crystals
Dimensions: 460 m long, 4.6 m wide
Location: Great Salt Lake, Utah

Construction

Using over six thousand tons of black basalt rocks and earth from the site, a crew with dump trucks and tractors formed a coil 1,500 feet (460 m) long and 15 feet (4.6 m) wide that winds counterclockwise into the lake. Smithson chose the site for its red-coloured water, caused by bacteria and algae, and its industrial remnants.

Visibility

The jetty was submerged for decades as lake levels rose, re-emerging during drought after 1999, encrusted in white salt crystals. Its visibility continues to vary with the shrinking lake.

Legacy

Smithson made a 32-minute film of the same name and an essay. The work is owned by the Dia Art Foundation. It exemplifies Land art and Smithson's concept of entropy.
