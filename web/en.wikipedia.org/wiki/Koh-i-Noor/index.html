The Koh-i-Noor (Persian for "Mountain of Light"; also spelled Kohinoor and Koh-i-Nur) is one of the largest cut diamonds in the world, weighing 105.6 carats (21.12 g). It is part of the Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom. The diamond is currently set in the Crown of Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother.

Probably mined in the Kollur Mine in the Golconda region of present-day Andhra Pradesh, India, possibly in the 13th century, the diamond passed through the hands of successive rulers of South Asia, including the Kakatiya dynasty, the Delhi Sultanate, the Mughal Empire (in whose Peacock Throne it was set), the Persian ruler Nader Shah — who is credited with giving it its present name after seizing it in Delhi in 1739 — the Afghan Durrani Empire, and the Sikh Empire of Maharaja Ranjit Singh at Lahore.

Acquisition by the British Crown

Following the British annexation of the Punjab in 1849 after the Second Anglo-Sikh War, the diamond was ceded to Queen Victoria under the Treaty of Lahore. The ten-year-old Maharaja Duleep Singh signed the treaty, whose terms included surrender of the gem. It arrived in England in 1850 and was displayed at the Great Exhibition of 1851. Disappointed by its lack of brilliance, Prince Albert had it recut in 1852 by Coster Diamonds of Amsterdam under the supervision of Garrard's; the recut reduced it from 186 carats to its present 105.6 carats but greatly increased its fire.

Dispute and legend

India, Pakistan, Iran and Afghanistan have all at various times claimed ownership of the diamond and demanded its return. The British government has rejected these claims, maintaining it was obtained legally. A persistent legend holds that the Koh-i-Noor carries a curse: "He who owns this diamond will own the world, but will also know all its misfortunes. Only God, or a woman, can wear it with impunity." Since 1850 it has been worn only by female members of the British royal family. Queen Camilla did not wear it at the coronation of Charles III in 2023, when the Crown of Queen Mary — reset without the Koh-i-Noor — was used instead.
