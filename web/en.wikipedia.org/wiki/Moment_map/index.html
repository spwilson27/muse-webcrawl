Moment map

In mathematics, specifically in symplectic geometry, a moment map (also spelled momentum map) is a tool associated with a Hamiltonian action of a Lie group on a symplectic manifold, used to construct conserved quantities for the action. If a Lie group G acts on a symplectic manifold preserving the symplectic form, and the action is Hamiltonian, the moment map is an equivariant map from the manifold to the dual of the Lie algebra of G whose components are the Hamiltonian functions generating the infinitesimal group actions.

The moment map formalizes the classical observation, expressed by Noether's theorem, that symmetries of a mechanical system give rise to conserved quantities: for example, rotational symmetry gives conservation of angular momentum — the word "moment" in the name reflects this connection with momentum and angular momentum. Moment maps are fundamental in symplectic reduction (Marsden–Weinstein reduction), in the study of toric manifolds, and in geometric invariant theory. They were developed in the work of Jean-Marie Souriau and, independently, Bertram Kostant.
