Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons

Abbreviation: OPCW

Founded: 29 April 1997

Headquarters: The Hague, Netherlands

Members: 193 states parties

Director-General: Fernando Arias (since 2018)

Honour: Nobel Peace Prize 2013

The Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) is an intergovernmental organisation based in The Hague that implements the Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC), which entered into force in 1997. Its 193 member states cover about 98% of the world's population and chemical industry. Israel has signed but not ratified the Convention; Egypt, North Korea and South Sudan remain outside it.

Mandate

The OPCW verifies the destruction of declared chemical weapons stockpiles and production facilities, inspects chemical industry sites to ensure toxic chemicals are used only for permitted purposes, and maintains the capability to investigate allegations of use. In July 2023 the United States completed destruction of the last declared stockpile, so that all declared chemical weapons worldwide — over 72,000 metric tons — have been verifiably destroyed.

Structure

The Conference of the States Parties is the plenary organ; a 41-member Executive Council supervises the Technical Secretariat of inspectors and staff. The Director-General is appointed for a four-year term.

Investigations

OPCW fact-finding missions investigated chemical attacks in the Syrian civil war, and from 2018 an Investigation and Identification Team has attributed responsibility for specific attacks — work Syria and Russia disputed. Inspectors also verified the nerve agents used in the 2018 Salisbury poisoning and the 2020 poisoning of Alexei Navalny.

Nobel Peace Prize

The OPCW received the 2013 Nobel Peace Prize "for its extensive efforts to eliminate chemical weapons."
