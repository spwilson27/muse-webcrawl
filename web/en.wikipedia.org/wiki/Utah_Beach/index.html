Utah Beach

Utah Beach was the code name for one of the five sectors of the Allied invasion of German-occupied France in the Normandy landings on 6 June 1944 (D-Day), during the Second World War. The westernmost of the landing beaches, it lay on the Cotentin Peninsula in the Manche department of Normandy, between the villages of Pouppeville and La Madeleine.

The landing

The beach was assaulted by the US 4th Infantry Division, supported by airborne landings inland by the US 82nd and 101st Airborne Divisions, whose paratroopers were to seize the causeways across the flooded lowlands behind the beach and secure its exits. Strong currents pushed the first wave of landing craft some 1,800 metres south of the intended landing zone, which proved fortuitous: the new sector was less heavily defended. Brigadier General Theodore Roosevelt Jr., the assistant division commander, famously declared, "We'll start the war from right here!"

Outcome

Utah was the most successful of the five D-Day beaches. By the end of the day, some 23,000 troops and 1,700 vehicles had come ashore at a cost of roughly 200 casualties — the lightest of any landing beach. The division linked up with airborne forces and began advancing toward the port of Cherbourg, which fell later in June. Roosevelt, the only general to land with the first wave, received the Medal of Honor for his actions that day.
