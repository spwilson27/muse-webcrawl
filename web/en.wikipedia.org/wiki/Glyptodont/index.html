Glyptodont

Glyptodonts are an extinct clade of large, heavily armoured armadillos, belonging to the subfamily Glyptodontinae within the family Chlamyphoridae. They were native to the Americas and lived from the middle Eocene (around 40 million years ago) until the end of the Late Pleistocene and beginning of the Holocene, around 10,000 years ago.

Glyptodonts were distinguished by a rigid, dome-shaped carapace of fused bony scutes protecting the back, a bony head shield, and often a tail armoured with rings or a club. Some late genera, including Glyptodon and Doedicurus, reached the size of small cars; Doedicurus wielded a spiked tail club reminiscent of ankylosaur dinosaurs.

Originally placed in their own family, genetic and anatomical analyses have shown glyptodonts to be nested within living armadillos. They spread into North America during the Great American Interchange after the formation of the Isthmus of Panama, and died out in the Quaternary extinction event.
