Modular group

In mathematics, the modular group is the projective special linear group PSL(2, Z) of 2 × 2 matrices with integer entries and determinant 1, with matrices A and −A identified. It is a fundamental object in number theory, complex analysis, geometry and the theory of modular forms.

Definition

The modular group can be presented as the group generated by two transformations of the upper half-plane:

S: z ↦ −1/z    and    T: z ↦ z + 1,

with relations S² = (ST)³ = 1. It acts on the complex upper half-plane by fractional linear (Möbius) transformations: a matrix [[a, b], [c, d]] sends z to (az + b)/(cz + d).

Fundamental domain

A standard fundamental domain for the action consists of points z in the upper half-plane with |z| ≥ 1 and |Re(z)| ≤ 1/2. Every orbit of the modular group meets this region. The quotient of the half-plane by the group is the modular curve, closely tied to the j-invariant and to elliptic curves.

Subgroups and applications

The principal congruence subgroups Γ(N), consisting of matrices congruent to the identity modulo N, and the Hecke congruence subgroups Γ₀(N) are central to the theory of modular forms and appear in the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem through the modularity theorem. The modular group is also the symmetry group of the (2, 3, ∞) triangle tessellation of the hyperbolic plane, and it arises in string theory as part of the duality group of certain compactifications.

Related groups

The full special linear group SL(2, Z) is a double cover of the modular group. Analogues over other rings and the mapping class group of the torus — which is isomorphic to SL(2, Z) — connect the modular group to low-dimensional topology.
