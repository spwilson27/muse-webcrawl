Yacht

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A white superyacht anchored off a Mediterranean resort coast]
A superyacht at anchor.

A yacht is a recreational craft, originally a light, fast sailing vessel used by the Dutch navy and wealthy civilians (from the Dutch jacht, \'hunt\'). Today \'yacht\' most often means a sailing or motor vessel used for pleasure, cruising or racing; \'superyachts\' over 24 metres (and giants over 100 metres) come with professional crews and facilities rivalling small ships. Charles II popularised yachting in England after his exile in the Netherlands, and the first yacht club was founded at Cork in 1720.

Sailing yachts

Racing yachts are built to rating rules or as one-designs; cruising yachts prioritise comfort and seaworthiness. Prestigious events include the Fastnet Race, Sydney to Hobart, and the America\'s Cup, held by the Royal Yacht Squadron\'s 1851 race around the Isle of Wight that America won.

Motor yachts and superyachts

Motor yachts range from weekenders to expedition vessels with helipads, submarines and beach clubs. The world\'s largest, such as Azzam (180 m) and Dilbar (by volume), are owned by heads of state and billionaires and built mainly in Germany and the Netherlands.

Industry

Yacht building centres include Italy (production motor yachts), the Netherlands and Germany (custom superyachts) and France (sailing multihulls — catamarans from yards like Fountaine Pajot). Charter markets in the Mediterranean and Caribbean employ thousands of crew.
