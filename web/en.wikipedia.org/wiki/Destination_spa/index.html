Destination spa

A destination spa or health resort is a resort centered on a spa, such as a mineral spa. Historically many such spas were developed at the location of natural hot springs or mineral springs; in the era before modern biochemical knowledge, the healing powers of mineral water were often attributed to God or to supernatural forces. Such spas have been popular since at least Roman times, but have experienced a modern revival as part of the wellness tourism industry.

Characteristics

Unlike a day spa, which customers visit for individual treatments, a destination spa offers an immersive, multi-day programme. Typically it provides an all-inclusive package: accommodation, healthy meals, fitness classes, educational lectures, and services such as massage, facials and body treatments, all oriented towards improving guests' health and habits. Guests usually stay for several nights, follow structured daily schedules, and may undergo consultations with nutritionists, physicians or fitness instructors.

History

Taking the waters at spa towns such as Bath in England, Spa in Belgium, and Baden-Baden and Karlovy Vary in Central Europe was a central ritual of European elite society for centuries. In the United States, resorts at Saratoga Springs and Hot Springs, Arkansas, drew health seekers in the 19th century. The modern destination spa emerged in the mid-20th century with establishments such as Rancho La Puerta (founded 1940 in Mexico) and Canyon Ranch (founded 1979 in Tucson, Arizona), which combined fitness, nutrition and stress reduction in a resort setting.

Industry

Destination spas form a distinct segment of the global wellness economy, alongside day spas, hotel and resort spas, medical spas and thermal spring resorts. Industry bodies such as the International Spa Association (ISPA) categorise and track them, and leading properties compete on programmes covering weight management, detoxification, sleep, mindfulness and preventive health.
