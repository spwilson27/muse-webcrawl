Adjustable spanner

An adjustable spanner (in North America, adjustable wrench or Crescent wrench) is any of various styles of spanner (wrench) with a movable jaw, allowing it to be used with different sizes of fastener head (nut or bolt). The jaw width is set by a knurled screw or worm gear turned by the user's thumb.

History

Early adjustable wrenches appeared in the nineteenth century; the Swedish inventor Johan Petter Johansson patented an improved design in 1891, manufactured by Bahco. In the United States, the Crescent Tool Company popularized its version from 1907, making "Crescent wrench" a genericized trademark there.

Use

The adjustable spanner should be oriented so that force is applied against the fixed jaw, which is stronger than the movable jaw, and the jaws should fit snugly on the flats of the fastener to avoid rounding it. Common sizes range from about 100 mm (4 in) to 600 mm (24 in) in length.
