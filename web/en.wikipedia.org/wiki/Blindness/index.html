Blindness

Blindness is the state of being unable to see, ranging from severe visual impairment to the complete absence of light perception. Definitions vary: the World Health Organization classifies blindness by visual acuity worse than 3/60 in the better eye with best correction, while legal blindness in the United States is acuity of 20/200 or worse, or a visual field of 20 degrees or less. Globally, tens of millions of people are blind and hundreds of millions have moderate to severe vision impairment.

Causes

Worldwide, cataract — clouding of the lens — is the leading cause of blindness, and most of it is curable by surgery. Other major causes include uncorrected refractive errors, glaucoma, macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, corneal scarring, and infectious diseases such as trachoma and onchocerciasis (river blindness). In children, vitamin A deficiency and congenital conditions are significant causes in low-income countries. The WHO and the International Agency for the Prevention of Blindness estimate that the large majority of vision loss is preventable or treatable.

Living with blindness

Blind people navigate using white canes, guide dogs, tactile paving, and increasingly GPS and smartphone apps; text is accessed through Braille, audio, and screen readers. Orientation and mobility training, and laws such as disability discrimination legislation, support education and employment, though blind people still face high unemployment in many countries.

Prevention and treatment

Strategies include cataract surgery programmes, mass drug administration for trachoma and river blindness, vitamin A supplementation, diabetes management, and affordable spectacles — interventions counted among the most cost-effective in public health.
