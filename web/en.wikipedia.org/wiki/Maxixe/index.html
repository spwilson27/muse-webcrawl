Maxixe
Origin: Rio de Janeiro, Brazil (late 19th century)
Type: Partner dance and music genre

The maxixe (pronounced mah-SHEESH) is a Brazilian dance and musical genre that originated in Rio de Janeiro in the last decades of the 19th century. Danced in 2/4 time to lively music, it blended European polka with Afro-Brazilian rhythms and the Brazilian tango (not to be confused with the Argentine tango), and it is considered a precursor of samba.

History

Emerging from the city's theaters and dance halls, the maxixe was initially considered scandalous for its close embrace and syncopated movements, and was at times banned. Composers such as Chiquinha Gonzaga and Ernesto Nazareth wrote celebrated maxixes. In the 1910s the dance spread to the United States and Europe, where it influenced ballroom dancing and ragtime-era popular music.

Characteristics

The dance features quick steps, breaks and improvisational figures performed by couples, accompanied by music with syncopated rhythms played by ensembles of flute, guitar and cavaquinho (the choro ensemble).
