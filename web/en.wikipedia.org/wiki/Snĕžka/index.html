Sněžka

Sněžka (Czech and Polish: Śnieżka; German: Schneekoppe) is a mountain on the border between the Czech Republic and Poland in the Krkonoše (Giant Mountains) range. At 1,603 metres (5,259 ft) above sea level, it is the highest point of the Czech Republic and of the Silesian region, and one of the most prominent and frequently visited summits in Central Europe.

Geography and climate

Sněžka rises as a distinctive bare, rocky pyramid above the surrounding ridges, straddling the Czech–Polish frontier. Its slopes fall within Krkonoše National Park on the Czech side and Karkonosze National Park on the Polish side. The mountain is known for severe weather — frequent fog, high winds, and an alpine climate with low average temperatures — and for the temperature inversions that leave its summit above a sea of cloud.

Summit buildings

The summit is shared by both countries and carries several structures: the Polish meteorological observatory, a distinctive disc-shaped building completed in the 1970s; the Czech post office, one of the highest buildings in the Czech Republic; and the small Chapel of St. Lawrence on the Polish side, dating from the 17th century. A historic tradition involves carrying letters to be stamped at the summit post offices.

Access and tourism

Sněžka is a major tourist destination reached by marked trails from Pec pod Sněžkou in the Czech Republic and Karpacz in Poland, and by cable car from Pec pod Sněžkou to the summit (rebuilt and reopened in 2013). Hundreds of thousands of visitors ascend each year, making it one of the most climbed mountains in the region; conservation measures in the national parks regulate access along its paths.
