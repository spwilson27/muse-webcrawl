McKay correspondence

In mathematics, the McKay correspondence, named after John McKay, is a correspondence between finite subgroups of SU(2) and the extended (affine) Dynkin diagrams of type A, D, and E, which in turn are related to the simple Lie groups of those types. More precisely, given a finite subgroup Γ of SU(2), one constructs a graph whose vertices are the nontrivial irreducible representations of Γ, with edges recording how representations appear in the tensor product with the natural two-dimensional representation. The resulting graph is an extended Dynkin diagram of ADE type.

Statement

Let Γ be a finite subgroup of SU(2). Let R0, R1, …, Rn be its irreducible representations, with R0 the trivial representation, and let V be the natural two-dimensional representation coming from the inclusion Γ ⊂ SU(2). Write V ⊗ Rj = ⊕i aij Ri. Then the matrix (2I − A), where A = (aij), is an extended Cartan matrix of ADE type, and the graph with adjacency given by A is the corresponding extended Dynkin diagram. The dimensions of the representations Ri give the coefficients of the highest root in the extended diagram.

Background

The finite subgroups of SU(2) are classically classified: cyclic groups, binary dihedral groups, and the binary tetrahedral, octahedral, and icosahedral groups. These correspond respectively to the extended Dynkin diagrams of types Ã, D̃, Ẽ6, Ẽ7, and Ẽ8. The quotient of ℂ² by such a subgroup is a Kleinian singularity, and the correspondence has been developed further in algebraic geometry through the study of minimal resolutions of these singularities, quiver varieties, and derived categories, in work associated with mathematicians including Michael Atiyah, Igor Frenkel, and Miles Reid.
