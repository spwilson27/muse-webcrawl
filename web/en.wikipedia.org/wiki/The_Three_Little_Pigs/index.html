The Three Little Pigs

"The Three Little Pigs" is a fable about three pigs who build houses of different materials and a wolf who seeks to eat them. It first appeared in print in the 1840s and was popularised in Joseph Jacobs's English Fairy Tales (1890). The story, Aarne–Thompson type 124, has become one of the best-known fairy tales in the English-speaking world.

Story

Three little pigs set out to seek their fortunes. The first builds a house of straw and the second a house of sticks; both are quickly blown down by the Big Bad Wolf, who in the older versions eats the two careless pigs. The third pig builds a house of bricks, which the wolf cannot blow down ("I'll huff, and I'll puff, and I'll blow your house in" — "Not by the hair of my chinny chin chin"). Foiled, the wolf tries to enter by the chimney and falls into a pot of boiling water; in Jacobs's version the third pig cooks and eats him.

Cultural impact

The tale is traditionally read as a moral about the rewards of hard work and foresight. Walt Disney's 1933 Silly Symphony cartoon adaptation, with the hit song "Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Wolf?", was a cultural phenomenon during the Great Depression. The story has been retold, parodied and reversed many times, notably in Jon Scieszka's The True Story of the Three Little Pigs! (1989), told from the wolf's point of view. The phrase "three little pigs" and the wolf's huffing and puffing are fixtures of popular culture.
