Honopu Valley

Honopu Valley is a landmark valley in the Nā Pali Coast State Park on the island of Kauaʻi, Hawaii. It is known for its dramatic natural arch, which separates Honopu Beach from a neighbouring beach and is among the largest natural arches in Hawaii. The valley is accessible only by swimming from Kalalau Beach or a boat — landing is prohibited.

History and legend

Honopu means "conch shell" in Hawaiian. The valley was once inhabited and contains archaeological sites and burial caves in its cliffs; for this reason it is regarded as a sacred place in Hawaiian tradition, sometimes called a "valley of the lost tribe". The cliffs were said to be home of the legendary Menehune according to some traditions.

In popular culture

Honopu Beach has served as a filming location for several movies, including King Kong (1976), Six Days, Seven Nights and Pirates of the Caribbean: On Stranger Tides.

Access

There is no legal trail into the valley. Visitors who reach Kalalau Beach via the Kalalau Trail may swim around the point to Honopu when ocean conditions allow, a hazardous undertaking discouraged unless conditions are calm.
