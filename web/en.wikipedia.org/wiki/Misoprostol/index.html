Misoprostol

Misoprostol is a synthetic prostaglandin E1 analogue used to prevent and treat stomach and duodenal ulcers, to induce labour, to cause abortion, and to treat postpartum haemorrhage. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

For ulcer prevention, misoprostol reduces gastric acid and increases mucus production, particularly in people taking nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). Combined with mifepristone or methotrexate, it is used for medical abortion. Alone or with oxytocin it is used for labour induction and for postpartum bleeding, especially where oxytocin storage is difficult, as misoprostol is stable at room temperature.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include diarrhoea, abdominal pain, nausea, and cramping. It must not be used for ulcer prevention in pregnancy because it can cause uterine contractions.

History

Misoprostol was developed by G. D. Searle & Company and marketed as Cytotec.
