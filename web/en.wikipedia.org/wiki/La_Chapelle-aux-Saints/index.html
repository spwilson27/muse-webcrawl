La Chapelle-aux-Saints

La Chapelle-aux-Saints is a commune in the Corrèze department in central France, in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. It is internationally known for the discovery in 1908 of a Neanderthal burial site in a cave near the village, one of the most famous Neanderthal finds ever made.

The Neanderthal discovery

On 3 August 1908, the brothers Amédée and Jean Bouyssonie, local priests and amateur prehistorians, excavated an almost complete Neanderthal skeleton in a small cave (the Bouffia Bonneval). The individual, an elderly male around 40–50 years old, had been deliberately placed in a shallow pit — the first widely recognized evidence that Neanderthals buried their dead. The skeleton, known as the "Old Man of La Chapelle," suffered from severe arthritis and had lost many teeth, suggesting his group may have cared for him.

Paleontologist Marcellin Boule's influential reconstruction depicted the man as stooped and brutish, shaping the popular image of Neanderthals as primitive ape-men for decades. Later reanalysis corrected this posture, attributing much of it to the individual's osteoarthritis and Boule's biases.

Renewed excavations in the 2010s confirmed that the pit was deliberately dug, strengthening the case for intentional Neanderthal burial. The original skeleton is held by the Musée de l'Homme in Paris.
