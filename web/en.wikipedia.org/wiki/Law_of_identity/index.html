Law of identity

In logic, the law of identity states that each thing is identical with itself: for any object x, x = x. It is the first of the historical three laws of thought, alongside the law of noncontradiction and the law of excluded middle.

The law of identity: everything is identical to itself (A = A).

Formulations

The law can be expressed as a principle of identity of indiscernibles in reverse: whereas Leibniz's principle says things sharing all properties are identical, the law of identity asserts the uncontroversial converse, that a thing is identical with itself and has all its own properties. In predicate logic it appears as the reflexivity of equality, ∀x (x = x).

Philosophical discussion

Although often treated as trivial, the law has been examined by philosophers from Aristotle and the scholastics to Leibniz, and its various formulations raise questions about identity over time, identity across possible worlds, and the meaning of equality in formal systems.
