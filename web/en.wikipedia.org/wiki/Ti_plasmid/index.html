A Ti plasmid (tumour-inducing plasmid) is a large plasmid carried by the soil bacterium Agrobacterium tumefaciens, which causes crown gall disease in many dicotyledonous plants. The plasmid is the agent of a natural form of genetic engineering: a segment of its DNA is transferred into the plant cell and integrated into the plant's chromosomes.

Structure

Ti plasmids are roughly 200 kilobase pairs in size. Their key features are:

  • T-DNA (transferred DNA): the segment that is cut out and transferred to the plant nucleus. In wild-type plasmids it carries genes for the synthesis of plant growth hormones (auxin and cytokinin), which cause the uncontrolled growth of the crown gall tumour, and genes for the production of opines, unusual amino-acid derivatives that only the bacteria can use as food.
  • Virulence (vir) region: a set of genes (virA–virG and others) whose protein products sense wound chemicals from the plant (such as acetosyringone), excise the T-DNA at its 25-base-pair border sequences, and pilot it into the plant cell through a type IV secretion system.
  • Regions for plasmid conjugation, replication, and opine catabolism.

Mechanism of infection

When a plant is wounded, phenolic compounds released from the wound activate the VirA/VirG two-component system of the bacterium. The vir gene products nick the T-DNA borders, producing a single-stranded T-strand coated with VirE2 protein, which is transported into the plant cell and its nucleus, where the T-DNA integrates into the plant genome. Expression of the T-DNA genes then produces the tumour and opines.

Use in biotechnology

Because the transfer machinery will move any DNA placed between the T-DNA borders, "disarmed" Ti plasmids — with the tumour genes removed — became the foundational tool for creating transgenic plants. Modern practice usually uses a binary vector system, in which the T-DNA sits on a small plasmid maintained separately from a helper plasmid supplying the vir functions. Agrobacterium-mediated transformation remains the most widely used method of plant genetic engineering.
