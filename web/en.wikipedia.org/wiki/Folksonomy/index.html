Folksonomy

A folksonomy is a classification system in which end users apply public tags to online items, typically to make those items easier for themselves or others to find later. The practice is also known as collaborative tagging, social classification, social indexing, and social tagging. Folksonomy was originally "the result of personal free tagging of information and objects for one's own retrieval", but has since expanded to describe systems in which tags created by many users are aggregated and shared.

Origin of the term

The term folksonomy is credited to Thomas Vander Wal, who coined it in 2004 as a blend of folk and taxonomy. Vander Wal described folksonomy as the result of personal free tagging of information and objects (anything with a URL) for one's own retrieval, developed in social environments where people consuming the information create and manage the tags.

Tagging and platforms

Folksonomies became popular around 2004 on social software applications including social bookmarking and photograph annotation. Websites such as Delicious and Flickr allowed users to label their resources with freely chosen keywords, producing tag clouds that visualized the most frequently used terms. Unlike formal taxonomies and controlled vocabularies, folksonomies have no fixed hierarchy, allowing multiple, overlapping, and personally meaningful labels.

Advantages and criticism

Supporters argue that folksonomies reflect the vocabulary of their users, surface unexpected connections, and require no expert cataloguers. Critics note problems including synonyms, homonyms, spelling variations, lack of precision in searching, and vulnerability to spam or manipulation.
