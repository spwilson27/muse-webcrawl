The Bank of Korea (BOK; Korean: 한국은행) is the central bank of the Republic of Korea (South Korea) and issuer of the South Korean won. It was established on June 12, 1950, under the Bank of Korea Act, shortly before the outbreak of the Korean War, replacing the Bank of Chosen of the Japanese colonial period.

Functions

The Bank's primary purpose is price stability. Its Monetary Policy Board, chaired by the Governor, sets the Bank of Korea Base Rate, the policy rate used in open-market operations to steer inflation toward a target of 2 percent. The Bank also issues currency, manages the country's foreign-exchange reserves, operates and oversees payment and settlement systems, compiles economic statistics, and acts as the government's banker and fiscal agent. Revisions to the Bank of Korea Act, most significantly in 1997–98 following the Asian financial crisis and again in 2011, strengthened its independence and added an explicit financial-stability mandate.

Organisation

The Governor is appointed by the President for a four-year term and chairs the seven-member Monetary Policy Board. The Bank's head office is in Jung-gu, Seoul, and it maintains regional branches across South Korea. The Bank of Korea Museum, in the former head-office building (a designated historic site), displays Korean currency and central-banking history.
