Laird Hamilton

Laird John Hamilton (born March 2, 1964) is an American big-wave surfer, co-inventor of tow-in surfing, and a pioneer of stand-up paddleboarding and foil surfing. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential big-wave surfers of his generation.

Early life

Hamilton was born in San Francisco and raised in Hawaii, where his stepfather, surfer Billy Hamilton, introduced him to surfing on the North Shore of Oahu. By his teens he was an accomplished surfer and briefly pursued a modelling career.

Career

In the early 1990s, Hamilton and a group of friends including Buzzy Kerbox and Darrick Doerner developed tow-in surfing, using personal watercraft to tow surfers onto waves too large to catch by paddling, particularly at Peʻahi ("Jaws") off Maui. On August 17, 2000, Hamilton rode a famously large wave at Teahupoʻo in Tahiti, widely publicized as the "Millennium Wave".

Other ventures

Hamilton helped popularize stand-up paddleboarding and hydrofoil surfing. He appeared in the surfing film Riding Giants (2004) and has worked as a stunt performer and model. With his wife, volleyball player Gabrielle Reece, he has promoted fitness ventures including the XPT training system.
