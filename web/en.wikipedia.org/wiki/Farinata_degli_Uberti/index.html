Farinata degli Uberti

Manente degli Uberti (c. 1210 – 11 November 1264), nicknamed Farinata, was an Italian aristocrat and military leader of Florence, a leader of the Ghibelline faction in the city's struggles with the Guelphs. He is chiefly remembered today for his appearance in Canto X of Dante's Inferno.

Life

Farinata led the Ghibellines of Florence in their wars against the Guelphs, defeating them at the Battle of Montaperti in 1260 with the aid of Manfred of Sicily. After the victory, the Ghibelline allies proposed destroying Florence altogether; Farinata passionately opposed the plan and is credited with saving his city. He died in 1264. Two decades later, in 1283, he and his wife were posthumously tried for heresy, their bodies exhumed and burned.

In Dante

Dante places Farinata among the Epicurean heretics of the sixth circle of Hell, enclosed in fiery tombs. In Canto X, Farinata rises from his tomb "as if he had great scorn for Hell" and debates Dante about Florentine politics and the fortunes of their families, predicting Dante's exile. Dante's portrait is ambivalent: Farinata is damned, yet presented with heroic dignity and civic pride.
