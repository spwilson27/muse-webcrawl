Celilo Falls

Celilo Falls (Wyam, meaning "echo of falling water") was a series of waterfalls and rapids on the Columbia River, on the border of Oregon and Washington, United States. For thousands of years it was one of the most important salmon fishing and trading sites in North America, used by Indigenous peoples including the Wishram, Wasco, Tenino and other tribes of the Columbia Plateau.

Fishing and trade

Fishers stood on wooden platforms over the falls and caught Chinook and other salmon with dip nets as the fish leapt upstream. The nearby village of Celilo was among the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in North America and a hub of a trade network reaching from the Pacific coast to the Great Plains.

Submergence

On 10 March 1957 the falls were submerged when the gates of The Dalles Dam closed, raising the river behind it. The flooding ended the fishery at Wyam and displaced the village, an event still mourned by tribal communities; fishing rights and compensation remained subjects of dispute for decades.
