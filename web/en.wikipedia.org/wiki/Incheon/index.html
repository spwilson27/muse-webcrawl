Incheon (Korean: 인천, literally "kind river"), formerly romanised as Inchon, is a metropolitan city on the northwest coast of South Korea, bordering Seoul and Gyeonggi Province to the east. It is South Korea's third-largest city and its principal west-coast port, serving the capital region.

History

Known in earlier centuries as Jemulpo, the port was opened to foreign trade in 1883. In September 1950, during the Korean War, Incheon was the site of the daring amphibious Incheon Landing (Operation Chromite) by United Nations forces under General Douglas MacArthur, which reversed the course of the war and led to the recapture of Seoul. The city hosted matches of the 2002 FIFA World Cup and the 2014 Asian Games.

Economy and transport

Incheon International Airport, opened in 2001 on Yeongjong Island, is South Korea's main international gateway and consistently ranked among the world's best airports. The Port of Incheon handles extensive trade with China. The Incheon Free Economic Zone includes Songdo, a purpose-built smart city on reclaimed land; Cheongna; and Yeongjong. The 21.4 km Incheon Bridge, one of the world's longest cable-stayed bridges, links Songdo to the airport island.

Geography

The city administers numerous Yellow Sea islands, including Ganghwa Island and the Northwest Islands near the Northern Limit Line with North Korea. Incheon's tidal flats, among the world's largest tidal ranges, support important migratory bird habitats.
