Siege of Rhodes (305–304 BC)

Date305–304 BC
LocationRhodes
ResultRhodian victory

The Siege of Rhodes of 305–304 BC was mounted by Demetrius I of Macedon, son of Antigonus I, against the island city-state of Rhodes, which had refused to join his war against Ptolemaic Egypt.

The siege

Demetrius — nicknamed Poliorcetes, "the Besieger" — deployed enormous siege machinery, including the Helepolis, a multi-storey armoured tower some 40 metres high, and a massive battering ram crewed by a thousand men. The Rhodians flooded ground before the walls, mounted counterattacks, and received supplies by sea; after a year Demetrius accepted a negotiated settlement that left Rhodes independent.

Legacy

To celebrate, the Rhodians sold the abandoned siege equipment and used the proceeds to build the Colossus of Rhodes, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.
