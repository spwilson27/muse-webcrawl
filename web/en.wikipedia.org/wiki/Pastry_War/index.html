Pastry War

Pastry War
Date27 November 1838 – 9 March 1839
LocationVeracruz, Mexico
BelligerentsFrance vs Mexico
ResultMexico agrees to pay 600,000 pesos in damages

The Pastry War (Spanish: Guerra de los pasteles; French: Guerre des Pâtisseries), also known as the First French intervention in Mexico, was an invasion of Mexico by France in 1838. It began when a French pastry cook, Monsieur Remontel, claimed that Mexican officers had looted his shop in Tacubaya and demanded 60,000 pesos in damages—an exorbitant sum folded into France's broader demands of 600,000 pesos for injuries to French citizens during Mexico's civil unrest.

Course of the conflict

When Mexico refused to pay, a French fleet under Rear Admiral Charles Baudin blockaded Mexican ports from April 1838 and, on 27 November, bombarded the fortress of San Juan de Ulúa guarding Veracruz; French landing parties also captured a number of Mexican naval vessels in a celebrated cutting-out raid using early explosive shells. Mexico declared war, and General Antonio López de Santa Anna, emerging from retirement, led the defence of Veracruz—losing a leg to French fire, a wound that became central to his political legend.

Settlement

Through British mediation, peace was signed on 9 March 1839 and Mexico agreed to pay the 600,000 pesos. France withdrew, but the unpaid claims and grievances festered, forming part of the background to the much larger French intervention of 1861–1867.
