Several complex variables

The theory of several complex variables is the branch of mathematics studying holomorphic functions of more than one complex variable, defined on domains in Cn for n > 1.

Differences from one variable

The subject differs sharply from single-variable complex analysis. By Hartogs's phenomenon, a holomorphic function on a domain in Cn (n > 1) automatically extends across compact holes, so isolated singularities do not exist, and not every domain is the natural domain of a holomorphic function. The classification of domains of holomorphy, pseudoconvexity, and the Levi problem are central themes.

Key results

Important tools include the Cauchy integral formula in several variables, plurisubharmonic functions, and the results of Oka, Cartan, and Grauert on Stein manifolds and coherent sheaves.
