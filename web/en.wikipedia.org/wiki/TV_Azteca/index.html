TV Azteca

Type: Public

Industry: Mass media

Founded: 1993

Headquarters: Mexico City, Mexico

Owner: Grupo Salinas

TV Azteca, S.A.B. de C.V. is a Mexican multimedia conglomerate owned by Grupo Salinas. It is one of the two largest producers of Spanish-language television programming in the world, alongside Televisa. The company operates two national television networks in Mexico, Azteca Uno and Azteca 7, in addition to other channels such as adn40 and a+.

History

TV Azteca was created in 1993 when the Mexican government of President Carlos Salinas de Gortari privatized the state-owned Imevisión network. A consortium led by Ricardo Salinas Pliego purchased the network package, which included channels 7 and 13, for approximately $645 million. The new company quickly became a formidable competitor to Televisa, which had long dominated Mexican television.

During the 1990s TV Azteca gained attention for its news coverage and its telenovelas, which offered an alternative style to Televisa's productions. Programmes such as Nada personal and Mirada de mujer were notable successes. The company also expanded internationally, participating in ventures in other Latin American countries.

Television networks

The company's flagship network is Azteca Uno (formerly Azteca Trece), which carries telenovelas, news, sports and entertainment programming. Azteca 7 focuses on series, films, sports and children's programming, and has been the Mexican home of major sporting events and imported series. Other services include adn40, an all-news channel, and a+, a network of local stations.

Sports

TV Azteca has traditionally broadcast Mexican football, holding rights to several Liga MX clubs, and its sports department became famous for the commentary team of its football broadcasts, including the popular Los Protagonistas programme.
