Overview

Surveying is the technique and profession of determining the positions of points on Earth's surface and the distances and angles between them — the practical ground-level partner of geodesy. Surveyors fix boundaries, set out buildings and roads, and make the measurements from which maps are drawn.

Its classical method was triangulation: measure one baseline with extreme care, then extend a network of triangles across the country using only angles read with a theodolite — the method of the great national surveys, whose hilltop pillars still dot the British countryside. Satellite positioning (GNSS) and electronic total stations have replaced most of the chain-and-theodolite work, but the geometry is the same one Eratosthenes used to size the Earth: angles, a baseline, and patience.
