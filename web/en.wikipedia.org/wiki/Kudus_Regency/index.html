Kudus Regency

Kudus Regency (Indonesian: Kabupaten Kudus) is a regency (kabupaten) in Central Java province, Indonesia. Its capital is the town of Kudus. It is the smallest regency in Central Java by area — around 425 km² — but one of the most densely populated, with a population of roughly 850,000 at the 2020 census. It is bordered by Pati Regency to the east, Grobogan to the south, Demak to the west and Jepara to the north.


The brick minaret of the Menara Kudus Mosque (Al-Aqsa Mosque), built in the 16th century in a Majapahit style.

History

Kudus was founded in 1549 by Sunan Kudus (Ja'far Shadiq), one of the nine saints (Wali Songo) credited with spreading Islam in Java. The name derives from the Arabic al-Quds (Jerusalem). The Menara Kudus Mosque's temple-like brick minaret reflects the fusion of Islamic and earlier Hindu-Javanese architecture.

Economy

Kudus is nationally known as the centre of Indonesia's kretek (clove cigarette) industry: the major manufacturer Djarum was founded there in 1951, and the town's cigarette trade dates to the kretek pioneer Nitisemito in the early 20th century. The regency is also known for jenang Kudus (a sweet confection) and other food industries.

Administrative divisions

The regency is divided into nine districts (kecamatan): Kaliwungu, Kota Kudus, Jati, Undaan, Mejobo, Jekulo, Bae, Gebog and Dawe.
