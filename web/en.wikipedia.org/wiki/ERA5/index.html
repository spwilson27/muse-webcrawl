ERA5 is the fifth generation of global atmospheric reanalysis produced by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) on behalf of the Copernicus Climate Change Service of the European Union. A reanalysis combines a numerical weather prediction model with vast archives of observations — from satellites, weather stations, ships, aircraft and balloons — to produce a physically consistent, gridded reconstruction of the state of the atmosphere over past decades.

ERA5 provides hourly estimates of many atmospheric, land-surface and ocean-wave variables on a grid of about 31 km resolution, with 137 vertical levels reaching up to 80 km altitude. It replaced ERA-Interim in 2019 and was later extended back to 1940. Data are distributed freely through the Copernicus Climate Data Store.

Uses

ERA5 is widely used in climate research to study trends and extreme events, in the renewable-energy industry to assess wind and solar resources, in hydrology and agriculture, and as training and evaluation data for weather and climate models, including machine-learning forecasting systems. Its hourly resolution and long record make it especially valuable for studying heatwaves, storms and droughts.

Limitations

Because it relies on a model, ERA5 does not reproduce every local detail: very fine-scale phenomena such as individual thunderstorms are not resolved, precipitation and near-surface variables can be biased in mountains and coastal zones, and the observing system changes over time, so early decades are constrained by far fewer observations than the satellite era.
