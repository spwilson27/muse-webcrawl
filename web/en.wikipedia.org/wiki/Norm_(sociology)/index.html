Norm (sociology)

In sociology, a social norm is a shared standard of acceptable behaviour by a group. Social norms can be both informal understandings that govern the behaviour of members of a society, and formal rules codified into law. Behaviour that departs from a norm is called deviance.

Types

The sociologist William Graham Sumner distinguished folkways (everyday conventions, weakly enforced), mores (norms with strong moral significance), taboos (prohibitions whose violation provokes disgust), and laws (formally enacted rules). Later work distinguishes descriptive norms (what most people do) from injunctive norms (what most people approve of).

Enforcement and change

Norms are maintained through socialisation and sanctions — rewards for conformity and punishments, from raised eyebrows to imprisonment, for violation. Émile Durkheim treated shared norms (the collective conscience) as the glue of society, while later theorists emphasised conflict, power, and how norms change through social movements and norm entrepreneurs.
