Stored-value card

A stored-value card is a payment card on which a monetary value is stored, either on the card itself (on a chip or magnetic stripe) or in a central account linked to the card, and spent down as purchases are made.

Types

Examples include gift cards, prepaid debit cards, transit smart cards such as Hong Kong's Octopus and London's Oyster, campus cards, and phone cards. Closed-loop cards are accepted only by the issuer; open-loop cards (bearing Visa or Mastercard brands) work widely.

Regulation and issues

Stored-value products raise consumer-protection questions about fees, expiry dates, unused balances ("breakage"), and money-laundering risk for anonymous high-value cards; many jurisdictions now regulate them.
