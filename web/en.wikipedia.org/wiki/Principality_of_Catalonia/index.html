Principality of Catalonia

Placeholder image: map of the Principality of Catalonia

The Principality of Catalonia (Catalan: Principat de Catalunya) was a medieval and early modern polity in the northeastern Iberian Peninsula, formed from the Catalan counties and ruled by the Counts of Barcelona. Its rulers held the title of Prince (Princeps) rather than King, and from the 12th century it formed part of the Crown of Aragon.

History

The Catalan counties emerged from the Carolingian Marca Hispanica. In 1137 the dynastic union of the County of Barcelona with the Kingdom of Aragon created the Crown of Aragon, but Catalonia retained its own laws, the Usatges, its Courts, and from 1359 the Generalitat as standing institution. The first recorded use of the term "Principality" for Catalonia dates to the 14th century, under Peter IV of Aragon.

Institutions

The principality was governed under a pactist constitutional tradition: the prince (who was also King of Aragon and later King of Spain) swore to respect Catalan constitutions, and new legislation required approval by the Catalan Courts, composed of the three estates (braços). The Constitucions de Catalunya formed one of Europe's earliest bodies of constitutional law.

Dissolution

Following the Catalan revolt of 1640 and the War of the Spanish Succession, in which Catalonia backed the Habsburg claimant, Philip V abolished Catalan institutions through the Nueva Planta decree of 1716, after the fall of Barcelona on 11 September 1714.
