Raoult's law
Named afterFrançois-Marie Raoult
Proposed1887
FieldPhysical chemistry, thermodynamics

Raoult's law is a law of thermodynamics established by French chemist François-Marie Raoult in 1887. It states that the partial vapour pressure of each component of an ideal mixture of liquids equals the vapour pressure of the pure component multiplied by its mole fraction in the mixture.

Consequences

A direct consequence is that the vapour pressure of a solution of a non-volatile solute is lower than that of the pure solvent, in proportion to the solvent's mole fraction. This vapour-pressure lowering underlies the colligative properties of solutions, including boiling-point elevation and freezing-point depression.

Deviations

Real mixtures deviate from Raoult's law when intermolecular forces between unlike molecules differ from those between like molecules. Positive and negative deviations are accommodated in modified treatments, and Raoult's law serves as the reference behaviour for the solvent in the theory of ideal and dilute solutions, complementing Henry's law for the solute.
