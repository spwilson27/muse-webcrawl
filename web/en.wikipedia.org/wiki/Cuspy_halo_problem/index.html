Cuspy halo problem

The cuspy halo problem (also known as the core/cusp problem) is a discrepancy between the inferred dark matter density profiles of low-mass galaxies and the density profiles predicted by cosmological N-body simulations.

Background

Simulations of cold dark matter, such as those producing the Navarro–Frenk–White profile, predict that dark matter density rises steeply toward the centres of halos (a "cusp"). Observations of rotation curves of dwarf and low surface brightness galaxies instead favour profiles that flatten toward the centre (a "core").

Proposed solutions

Proposed resolutions include baryonic feedback processes—such as supernova-driven outflows—that push dark matter outward and flatten the cusp, self-interacting dark matter, and modifications to the dark matter model itself. The problem remains an active area of research in galaxy formation.
