Chancellor of Germany

Chancellor of the Federal Republic of Germany
German: Bundeskanzler(in)
Style: Mr./Madam Chancellor; His/Her Excellency (diplomatic)
Residence: Federal Chancellery, Berlin
Appointer: President of Germany, following election by the Bundestag
Term: 4 years, renewable
Formation: 15 September 1949 (Konrad Adenauer, first)

The Chancellor of Germany (German: Bundeskanzler, "Federal Chancellor") is the head of government of the Federal Republic of Germany. The chancellor is elected by the Bundestag (federal parliament) on the proposal of the Federal President and appoints the federal ministers who form the cabinet. Under Article 65 of the Basic Law, the chancellor determines and is responsible for the general guidelines of government policy (Richtlinienkompetenz).

The Federal Chancellery building in Berlin.

History

The office descends from the chancellors of the North German Confederation and the German Empire (from 1867/1871), a role dominated by Otto von Bismarck, and from the Reich Chancellor of the Weimar Republic. The modern office was created by the Basic Law of 1949, which deliberately strengthened the chancellor relative to the head of state and to parliament: the Bundestag can remove a chancellor only by simultaneously electing a successor (the constructive vote of no confidence), a provision used successfully in 1982 when Helmut Kohl replaced Helmut Schmidt.

Notable chancellors

  • Konrad Adenauer (1949–1963) — first chancellor of the Federal Republic
  • Willy Brandt (1969–1974) — Ostpolitik
  • Helmut Kohl (1982–1998) — German reunification
  • Angela Merkel (2005–2021) — first woman chancellor
  • Olaf Scholz (2021–2025)
  • Friedrich Merz (from 2025)
