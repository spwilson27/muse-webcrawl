Abramo Giulio Umberto Federigo Enriques (5 January 1871 – 14 June 1946) was an Italian mathematician, now known principally for his classification of algebraic surfaces, and as a major figure in the Italian school of algebraic geometry. He also made significant contributions to the history and philosophy of science and to mathematics education.

Life

Enriques was born in Livorno and studied at the University of Pisa and the Scuola Normale Superiore, where he was influenced by Eugenio Bertini. He held chairs at Bologna (from 1896) and at Rome (from 1923). Beginning in 1938, Italy's racial laws, enacted under the fascist regime, forced him, as a Jew, into retirement and clandestinity; he survived the war in hiding in Rome and died there in 1946.

Work

Working with Guido Castelnuovo, Enriques developed the birational classification of algebraic surfaces, distinguishing them by Kodaira dimension and introducing invariants such as the arithmetic genus and the plurigenera. This "Enriques–Kodaira classification" was later completed by Kunihiko Kodaira. Enriques surfaces, characterised by specific numerical invariants, are named after him. With Francesco Severi he shared the Bordin Prize of the Paris Academy of Sciences. Enriques also wrote widely on the history of mathematics and science and edited the periodical Scientia.
