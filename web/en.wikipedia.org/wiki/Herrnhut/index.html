Herrnhut

Herrnhut (Upper Sorbian: Ochranow) is a town in the district of Görlitz, in the Free State of Saxony, Germany. It has a population of around 6,000 and is famous as the founding place of the Moravian Church (Unity of the Brethren), established in 1722 by religious refugees from Moravia on the estate of Count Nikolaus Ludwig von Zinzendorf.

History

In 1722 a small group of persecuted Protestant families from Moravia, descendants of the Unity of the Brethren founded by followers of Jan Hus, were granted asylum on Zinzendorf's Berthelsdorf estate. They named their settlement Herrnhut — "the Lord's watch" or "under the Lord's protection". Under Zinzendorf's patronage the community revived the ancient Brethren's church and, from 1732, dispatched missionaries across the world: to the Caribbean, Greenland, South Africa, North America (founding Bethlehem, Pennsylvania) and beyond, making the tiny town the hub of one of the first worldwide Protestant mission movements.

Sights and heritage

The planned baroque town centre, the Moravian church hall, God's Acre cemetery on the Hutberg, Berthelsdorf manor and the Ethnographic Museum (Völkerkundemuseum) preserve the heritage of the settlement. Herrnhut is also known for the Herrnhuter Sterne — the three-dimensional Moravian stars produced since the 19th century and now a widespread Advent and Christmas decoration. In 2024 the Moravian Church settlements, including Herrnhut, were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as a transnational site.
