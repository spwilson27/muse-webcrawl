Bobby Dodd Stadium, known for most of its history as Grant Field, is a college football stadium in Atlanta, Georgia, on the campus of the Georgia Institute of Technology. Opened in 1913, it is the oldest continuously used on-campus stadium in NCAA Division I FBS football and the oldest in the South. It is the home of the Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets and is named for longtime Tech coach and athletic director Bobby Dodd.

History

The original Grant Field was built largely by students beginning in 1913 on land that coach John Heisman helped level; a $15,000 gift from John W. Grant in memory of his son Hugh funded early grandstands, giving the field its name. Concrete stands followed through the 1920s, including the south stands built partly by alumnus and coach William Alexander. The stadium was expanded repeatedly, peaking at roughly 71,500 seats in the 1980s before renovations reduced capacity.

Naming and renovations

In April 1988, the stadium was renamed Bobby Dodd Stadium at Historic Grant Field, honoring Dodd, who coached Georgia Tech from 1945 to 1966 and won the 1952 national championship. A major renovation completed in 2003 added a new north end zone structure and modernized facilities. The stadium is noted for its midtown setting, with the Atlanta skyline rising behind the north stands.

Other events

Beyond Georgia Tech football, the stadium hosted Atlanta's NFL Falcons briefly as an alternate venue in the late 1960s and early 1970s, and the Atlanta Apollos of the NASL in 1973. It has also been used for concerts and other large campus events.
