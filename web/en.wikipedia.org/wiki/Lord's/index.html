Lord's Cricket Ground, commonly known as Lord's, is a cricket venue in St John's Wood, London. Named after its founder, Thomas Lord, it is owned by the Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC) and is widely referred to as the "Home of Cricket". It is the home of Middlesex County Cricket Club and hosts Test matches, One Day Internationals and domestic finals.

History

The present ground is the third established by Thomas Lord. The first, Lord's Old Ground (1787), stood on the site of Dorset Square; the second, Lord's Middle Ground (1811), was abandoned when the Regent's Canal was cut through it; the current ground opened in 1814 on the site of a duck pond. The first match played there was between MCC and Hertfordshire in June 1814.

The Pavilion, built in 1889–90, is one of the most famous buildings in sport, with its Long Room through which players walk to the field. Father Time, the celebrated weather vane depicting a figure removing the bails, was presented in 1926. The ground's famous slope falls approximately 2.5 metres from the Grand Stand side to the Tavern side.

The ground today

Lord's has hosted more than 100 Test matches since its first in 1884, and staged the finals of the Cricket World Cup in 1975, 1979, 1983, 1999 and 2019 — the last ending in England's dramatic victory over New Zealand. The ground also hosted the archery competition at the 2012 Olympic Games.

Stands include the Grand Stand, Compton and Edrich Stands, the Allen Stand, and the futuristic J.P. Morgan Media Centre (1999), which won the Stirling Prize for architecture. The MCC Museum at the ground is among the oldest sporting museums in the world and holds the Ashes urn. The ground's capacity was increased to 31,100 by the redevelopment of the Compton and Edrich stands completed in 2021.
