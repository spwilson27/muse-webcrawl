Splatterpunk

Splatterpunk is a movement within horror fiction originating in the 1980s, distinguished by its graphic, often hyper-intensive depictions of violence, and a confrontational, countercultural stance modelled in part on punk rock.

Splatterpunk
Emergence: 1980s
Key figures: Clive Barker, David J. Schow, John Skipp, Craig Spector, Poppy Z. Brite
Parent genre: Horror fiction

History

The term was coined by David J. Schow at a World Fantasy Convention in 1986 as a joke that stuck. Anthologies such as Splatterpunks (1990) and John Skipp and Craig Spector's Book of the Dead (1989) defined the movement. Clive Barker's Books of Blood (1984–85) were a major influence, blending gore with literary ambition.

Characteristics

Splatterpunk rejects the restrained, suggestive horror of earlier traditions in favour of explicit body horror, urban settings, and amoral or alienated protagonists. Critics accused it of gore for its own sake; defenders saw social and philosophical seriousness.

Legacy

By the 1990s the label faded as its writers diverged, but it influenced extreme horror, bizarro fiction, and films. Related movements include cyberpunk, from which its name was adapted.
