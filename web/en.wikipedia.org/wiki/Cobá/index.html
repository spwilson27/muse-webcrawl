Cobá is an ancient Maya city on the Yucatán Peninsula, located in the Mexican state of Quintana Roo, about 44 km (27 mi) northwest of Tulum and 90 km east of Chichén Itzá. The name is often rendered without the accent as Coba in English-language texts.

History

Settled by about AD 100, Cobá grew into one of the largest and most powerful cities of the northern Maya lowlands, controlling a network of more than 50 sacbeob (white roads) radiating to satellite settlements; the longest, to Yaxuná, stretches roughly 100 km. The city maintained a long rivalry and exchange relationship with Tikal far to the south. After AD 900 its influence declined, but the site was never fully abandoned and remained a place of pilgrimage into the Spanish colonial era.

The site today

Only a fraction of the estimated 6,500 structures has been excavated. Visitor highlights include the Nohoch Mul pyramid group, the Xaibé structure, two ball courts, and carved stelae depicting rulers — unusually, including powerful queens of the Classic period. Much of the site remains shaded under dense jungle canopy, reached by foot and bicycle paths.
