Fort St. George is a fortress in Chennai (Madras), India, founded in 1644 by the British East India Company. It was the first English fortress in India and the nucleus around which the city of Madras grew, giving its name to the Madras Presidency and, through the St. George's flag tradition, occupying a foundational place in the history of British India.

History

Company agents Francis Day and Andrew Cogan obtained a grant of land from the local nayak rulers in 1639 and began building a fortified factory, completing the first fortifications on 23 April 1644 — St. George's Day, hence the name. The fort withstood sieges and was captured by the French under the Comte de Lally in 1758, before being returned to Britain by the Treaty of Paris in 1763. It later survived attacks by Haider Ali of Mysore. Within its walls stands St. Mary's Church (consecrated 1680), the oldest English church in India, where Robert Clive was married and Elihu Yale — benefactor of Yale University — worshipped as governor.

The fort today

The fort now houses the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly and the state Secretariat. It also contains the Fort Museum, which displays artefacts of the colonial period, and a 150-foot flagstaff, among the tallest in India, on which the national flag is flown. The complex remains a prominent landmark of Chennai.
