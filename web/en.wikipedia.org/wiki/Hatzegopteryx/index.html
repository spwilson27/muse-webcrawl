Hatzegopteryx ("Hațeg wing") is a genus of azhdarchid pterosaur found in the Maastrichtian deposits of the Hațeg Basin in Transylvania, Romania. Named in 2002, it is among the largest flying animals known, with a wingspan estimated at 10 to 12 metres (33 to 39 feet), rivalling Quetzalcoatlus.

Discovery and description

The first remains were found in the 1970s in the Densuș-Ciula Formation near Vălioara. The holotype consists of fragments of the rear skull and humerus; its species name thambema derives from the Greek for "terror" or "monster," reflecting its great size. Unusually for a giant pterosaur, the skull was massive and robust — about 2.5 metres long — with a broad, deep beak, and the neck vertebrae were thick-walled and strongly built, quite unlike the more lightly constructed bones of other azhdarchids.

Palaeobiology

Hatzegopteryx lived on Hațeg Island, a subtropical island in the Tethys Sea famous for its dwarf dinosaurs, such as the sauropod Magyarosaurus. With no large terrestrial predators on the island, researchers have proposed that Hatzegopteryx occupied the apex-predator niche: a terrestrial stalker that hunted the dwarf dinosaurs themselves, its reinforced skull and neck adapted to seizing and subduing large prey on the ground. Like other azhdarchids it was quadrupedal on land and fully capable of flight despite its size.
