Wide-angle lens

A wide-angle lens is a photographic lens whose focal length is substantially shorter than the diagonal of the film or sensor format, giving a wider angle of view than a normal lens. For full-frame 35 mm, lenses below about 35 mm are considered wide-angle, and those below 24 mm ultra-wide.

Characteristics

Wide-angle lenses produce strong perspective: near objects appear enlarged and spatial depth is exaggerated. They offer great depth of field at a given aperture, making them useful for landscapes, architecture, interiors and reportage. Rectilinear designs keep straight lines straight but stretch objects at frame edges; fisheye lenses instead embrace curvilinear distortion.

Design

For SLR cameras, retrofocus (inverted telephoto) designs move the rear element away from the film plane to clear the mirror. Aberrations such as barrel distortion, vignetting and lateral chromatic aberration are key design challenges.

Use

Wide lenses are favoured for environmental portraiture, real estate, astro-landscape photography and confined spaces, but require care with converging verticals and foreground placement.
