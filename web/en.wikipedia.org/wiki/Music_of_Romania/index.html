Music of Romania

The music of Romania draws on the country's position between Central Europe, the Balkans and the East, encompassing rich folk traditions, a distinguished classical school, and vibrant popular genres.

Folk music

Romanian folk music varies by region — Transylvania, Moldavia, Wallachia, Banat, Maramureș and Dobruja each have characteristic dances and song types, including the doina, a free-rhythm improvisatory lament, and fast circle dances such as the hora and sârba. Professional folk musicians known as lăutari, historically often Roma, play fiddle, cimbalom, pan flute (nai), accordion and double bass at weddings and festivals. Béla Bartók collected thousands of Romanian folk melodies in Transylvania in the early twentieth century.

Classical music

George Enescu, Romania's most celebrated composer, united Romanian folk idioms with Western forms in works such as the Romanian Rhapsodies and the opera Œdipe. Bucharest's Romanian Athenaeum and the biennial George Enescu Festival anchor the country's classical life.

Popular music

During the communist era, state-controlled pop (muzică ușoară) and rock bands such as Phoenix developed under censorship. After 1989, manele — a controversial urban genre blending Balkan, Turkish and pop influences, associated with Roma performers such as Adrian Minune and Florin Salam — became hugely popular, alongside Romanian dance-pop acts like O-Zone, whose "Dragostea din tei" was a global hit in 2004, and Inna.
