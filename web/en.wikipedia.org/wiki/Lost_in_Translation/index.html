Lost in Translation is a 2003 romantic comedy-drama film written and directed by Sofia Coppola. Bill Murray stars as Bob Harris, a fading American movie star who is having a midlife crisis when he travels to Tokyo to appear in a series of commercials for Suntory whisky. There, he befriends Scarlett Johansson's Charlotte, the neglected wife of a photographer.

Plot

Bob Harris, an aging American film star, arrives in Tokyo to film a whisky commercial for a fee of two million dollars. Suffering from insomnia and alienation, he spends his nights at the hotel bar, where he meets Charlotte, a young Yale philosophy graduate accompanying her celebrity-photographer husband. Both sleepless and adrift in an unfamiliar culture, the two form a quiet, deepening friendship over a week of karaoke bars, arcades, hospitals and late-night conversations, before a bittersweet farewell in which Bob whispers something inaudible into Charlotte's ear.

Reception

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing over $119 million against a $4 million budget. Coppola won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and Murray won the BAFTA and Golden Globe for his performance. It is frequently ranked among the greatest films of the 2000s.

Themes

Critics have discussed the film's treatment of loneliness, insomnia, culture shock and the quiet connection between two people at turning points in their lives. The title refers both to literal linguistic mistranslation and to the emotional dislocation experienced by the characters.
