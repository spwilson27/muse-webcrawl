Kit (association football)

The standard football kit is defined by Law 4 of the Laws of the Game.

In association football, a kit (also referred to as a strip or uniform) is the standard equipment and attire worn by players. Law 4 of the Laws of the Game ("The Players' Equipment") specifies the compulsory items: a jersey or shirt with sleeves, shorts, socks, shinguards covered by the socks, and footwear. Goalkeepers must wear colours that distinguish them from both teams and the officials.

History

Early footballers wore heavy cotton shirts, knickerbockers, and boots; distinctive team colours became necessary as the game organised, and by the 1890s most clubs had settled identities. Shirt numbers were introduced in England in 1928 and became standard after the Second World War; players' names appeared on shirts in the 1990s. Sponsors' logos, first seen in the 1970s, transformed kits into major commercial products, with replica shirts a significant source of club revenue. "Third kits" and special-edition shirts are now routine in case of colour clashes.

Rules and customs

Players may not wear anything dangerous, and slogans or images on undergarments are restricted. A long-standing custom prohibits outfield players from wearing the same colours as the opposing goalkeeper, and many competitions require an approved change kit to be carried to every match.
