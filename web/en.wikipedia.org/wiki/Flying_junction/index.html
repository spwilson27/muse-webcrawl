Flying junction

A flying junction, or flyover, is a railway junction at which one or more tracks cross over other tracks on a bridge or viaduct rather than at grade, eliminating conflicting movements between trains.

Purpose

By grade-separating diverging routes, flying junctions increase capacity and reduce delays compared with flat junctions, where trains must wait for conflicting paths.

They are common on busy main lines, metro systems, and approaches to major stations, though they require more land and construction cost.

Examples

Notable examples include flyovers built on British, German, and Japanese rail networks, and in the United States at major commuter rail junctions.
