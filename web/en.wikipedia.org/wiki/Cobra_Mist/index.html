Cobra Mist

The Cobra Mist antenna array at Orford Ness, Suffolk

Type: Over-the-horizon radar

Location: Orford Ness, Suffolk, England

Operator: United States Air Force / Royal Air Force

Active: 1971–1973

Cobra Mist was the codename for an Anglo-American experimental over-the-horizon radar station at Orford Ness, Suffolk, England. Officially designated AN/FPS-95, the system was intended to monitor aircraft and missile activity deep inside the Soviet Union by bouncing high-frequency radio signals off the ionosphere, giving warning far beyond the range of conventional line-of-sight radar. It operated, with persistent problems, from about 1971 until its abrupt closure in 1973.

Design

Cobra Mist formed part of a family of American over-the-horizon systems. Its most striking feature was a huge fan-shaped antenna array: eighteen radiating strings spreading across the shingle spit of Orford Ness, each roughly 600 metres long, supported on masts and covering an arc aimed north-east across the North Sea and Scandinavia toward the western USSR. The transmitter was capable of megawatt-class peak power in the HF band, and the receiving and processing equipment was housed in a purpose-built block protected against electromagnetic pulse.

Problems and closure

From its first tests, the radar was plagued by an unexplained noise problem that degraded its sensitivity, particularly at certain times of day. Despite extensive investigation, the source was never conclusively identified; theories ranged from interference by Soviet transmitters or jamming to ionospheric effects and interference from other radio services. With satellite surveillance maturing and the system's performance disappointing, the project was shut down in June 1973 after only a short operational life. The site, its buildings later reused for the BBC World Service transmitting station at Orfordness, remains in a landscape now managed largely as a nature reserve.

Legacy

Cobra Mist is frequently cited in popular accounts of Cold War secrecy at Orford Ness, a location also associated with earlier radar research and weapons testing. Conspiracy theories linking the site to UFO incidents — notably the nearby Rendlesham Forest incident of 1980 — regularly mention the radar, though no connection has been substantiated.
