East Coast hip hop

East Coast hip hop is a regional subgenre of hip hop music that originated in the South Bronx, New York City, during the 1970s. East Coast hip hop has been defined by complex lyrical wordplay, multi-syllabic rhymes, and dense, sample-based production, often drawing on jazz, soul, and funk. In contrast to the heavier bass and party orientation of other regional styles, the East Coast sound of its golden age emphasised lyricism and boom bap drums.

Origins and golden age

Hip hop began at Bronx block parties where DJ Kool Herc extended breakbeats, soon joined by Grandmaster Flash, Afrika Bambaataa, and MCs who developed rapping over the breaks. By the mid-1980s the "golden age" produced artists such as Run-DMC, LL Cool J, the Beastie Boys, Public Enemy, Eric B. & Rakim, Big Daddy Kane, KRS-One and Boogie Down Productions, and A Tribe Called Quest and De La Soul of the Native Tongues collective.

1990s and rivalry

The 1990s brought the mafioso rap of The Notorious B.I.G., Jay-Z, and Raekwon, the jazz-inflected sounds of A Tribe Called Quest, Nas's Illmatic (1994), and the Wu-Tang Clan's gritty Staten Island sound. A much-publicised East Coast–West Coast rivalry, centred on The Notorious B.I.G. and Tupac Shakur, ended tragically with both artists' murders in 1996 and 1997 and dominated hip hop media coverage of the era.

Later developments

New York's commercial dominance faded in the 2000s as Southern hip hop rose, but the East Coast remained influential through artists such as Jay-Z, 50 Cent, and later A$AP Rocky, Joey Bada$$, and the drill scenes of Brooklyn. The boom bap aesthetic continues to be revived by contemporary acts and producers.
