Homework

Homework, or a homework assignment, is a set of tasks assigned to students by their teachers to be completed outside the classroom. Common assignments may include required reading, writing or typing tasks, mathematical exercises, information to be reviewed before a test, or other skills practice.

Effects

Research on homework's academic benefit is mixed and age-dependent. Meta-analyses, notably those of Harris Cooper, find a moderate positive correlation between homework and achievement in high school, a smaller effect in middle school, and little measurable effect in elementary school. Proposed benefits include reinforcing learning, building study habits and self-discipline, and involving parents in education. Critics point to stress, sleep loss, reduced free time, and the widening of inequality between students with and without supportive home environments, quiet space and internet access.

History and policy

Compulsory homework grew with mass schooling in the 19th and 20th centuries. In the United States it was attacked as unhealthy in the early 1900s (California banned it for younger pupils in 1901), surged after Sputnik in 1957 amid Cold War anxiety, and has swung in and out of fashion since. A common guideline is the "10-minute rule": roughly ten minutes per grade level per night. Some countries, such as Finland, assign comparatively little; others, such as China and South Korea, assign much more, alongside extensive private tutoring. Several jurisdictions have recently restricted or banned homework for young children.
