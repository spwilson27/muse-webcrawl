In Roman mythology, Carmenta (also Carmentis) was a goddess of childbirth and prophecy, associated with technological innovation. She was one of the Camenae (water goddesses) and was honoured at the festival of the Carmentalia in January.

Mythology

Carmenta was said to be the mother of Evander of Pallantium, the Arcadian hero who founded a settlement on the future site of Rome. Her name derives from the Latin carmen, meaning a song, spell or prophetic verse. Two altars were dedicated to her by the Porta Carmentalis in Rome, in the forms Carmenta Prorsa (for head-first births) and Carmenta Postverta (for breech births), reflecting her role as protector of women in labour.

The alphabet tradition

Later Roman tradition credited Carmenta (under the name Nicostrata) with adapting the Greek alphabet into the Latin alphabet, modifying fifteen letters for Latin use. The story, recorded by authors such as Hyginus and echoed by Tacitus, reflects Rome's cultural link between Greek civilisation and Latin literacy.

Worship

Her festival, the Carmentalia, was celebrated chiefly by women on 11 and 15 January. Leather was banned from her temple precinct, as it was thought inauspicious to bring the skin of dead animals into the shrine of a goddess of birth.
