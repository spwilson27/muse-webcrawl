Loop space

In topology, a branch of mathematics, the loop space ΩX of a pointed topological space X is the space of based loops in X; that is, of continuous maps from the unit circle S1 to X such that the beginning and end point coincide, equipped with the compact-open topology. Loops can be multiplied by concatenation, making ΩX a topological group (up to homotopy, an A∞-space), fundamental to algebraic topology and homotopy theory.

Definitions

Let X be a topological space with basepoint x0. Then ΩX = { f : S1 → X continuous, f(1) = x0 }. More generally, one distinguishes the free loop space LX = Map(S1, X) of all unbased loops, on which the circle acts by rotation, from the based loop space.

Properties

The set of connected components of ΩX is the fundamental group π1(X), and more generally the homotopy groups shift: πn(ΩX) ≅ πn+1(X). The loop space is adjoint to the (reduced) suspension: maps ΣX → Y correspond to maps X → ΩY, the loop-suspension adjunction underlying spectra and stable homotopy theory. The based loop space of an H-space and iterated loop spaces characterise infinite loop spaces, which correspond to generalized cohomology theories (Boardman–Vogt, May).

Applications

Loop spaces appear in string topology, in the study of geodesics via the energy functional on the free loop space of a Riemannian manifold (Morse theory in infinite dimensions), and in physics, where loops (Wilson loops, string worldsheets) motivate their study.
