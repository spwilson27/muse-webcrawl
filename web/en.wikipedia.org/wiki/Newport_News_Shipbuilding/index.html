Newport News Shipbuilding

Newport News Shipbuilding (NNS), a division of Huntington Ingalls Industries, is the largest industrial employer in Virginia and the sole designer, builder and refueller of United States Navy aircraft carriers and one of two providers of U.S. Navy submarines. Its shipyard in Newport News, Virginia, on the James River, covers more than 550 acres.

History

The company was founded in 1886 by Collis P. Huntington as the Chesapeake Dry Dock and Construction Company, and built its first ships for the U.S. Navy in the 1890s. It built six of the Navy's first battleships and went on to construct famous vessels including the aircraft carrier USS Enterprise (CV-6), the nuclear-powered USS Enterprise (CVN-65), the ocean liner SS United States, and the Apollo command module recovery ship. Its giant gantry crane and Dry Dock 12, among the largest in the Western Hemisphere, dominate the yard.

Current work

NNS builds the Gerald R. Ford-class aircraft carriers and, jointly with General Dynamics Electric Boat, Virginia-class submarines, and performs refuelling and complex overhauls of Nimitz-class carriers. It employs more than 25,000 people.
