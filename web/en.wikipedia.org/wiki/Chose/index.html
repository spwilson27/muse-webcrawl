Chose (English law)

A chose (pronounced "shows", from the French for "thing") is a term used in English law to describe a bundle of rights, traditionally applied to intangible personal property. Choses are divided into two categories: choses in action and choses in possession.

Chose in action

A chose in action is a right to sue — a right to recover money or property through legal proceedings. Examples include a debt owed, shares in a company, intellectual property rights such as patents and copyrights, and rights under a contract or insurance policy. Historically, choses in action could not be assigned at common law, but equity permitted assignment, and statute (notably the Law of Property Act 1925, s.136) now allows legal assignment of a chose in action provided it is absolute, in writing, and notice is given to the debtor.

Chose in possession

A chose in possession is a tangible item of personal property that is capable of physical possession, such as a chattel — goods, furniture, or a vehicle. Unlike a chose in action, it is enjoyed by actual possession rather than by bringing a claim.

Significance

The distinction matters for assignment, enforcement, and insolvency law. Modern courts have treated newer forms of intangible property, such as cryptoassets, as property capable of being held on trust, extending the chose in action concept into the digital era.
