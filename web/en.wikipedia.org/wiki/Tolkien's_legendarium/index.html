Tolkien's legendarium

Tolkien's legendarium is the body of mythopoeic writing by J. R. R. Tolkien forming the background to The Hobbit (1937) and The Lord of the Rings (1954–55). It encompasses the creation of the world (Arda), its history through successive Ages, and the peoples, languages, and geographies of Middle-earth.

Contents

The core narrative, told in The Silmarillion (published posthumously in 1977, edited by Christopher Tolkien), begins with the Ainulindalë, a creation myth in which Eru Ilúvatar shapes the world through music, and proceeds through the wars of the Elves and Men against Morgoth in the First Age, the fall of Númenor in the Second Age, and the events leading to the War of the Ring in the Third Age. Companion texts include Unfinished Tales and the twelve-volume History of Middle-earth, which trace the legendarium's decades-long evolution.

Development and themes

Tolkien began the mythology during the First World War, intending to create "a mythology for England." He developed its invented languages — Quenya and Sindarin chief among them — with professional philological rigour, and wove in themes of mortality, stewardship, providence, and the corrupting lure of power. Scholars debate questions of canon among the many overlapping versions, since Tolkien never published a definitive Silmarillion in his lifetime.
