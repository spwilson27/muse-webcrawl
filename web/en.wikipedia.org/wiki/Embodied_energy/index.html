Embodied energy

Embodied energy
Units: megajoules per kg or per unit
Method: life-cycle assessment
Related: embodied carbon, EROI

Embodied energy is the total energy consumed by all the processes involved in producing a product or building — extracting raw materials, manufacturing, transport, assembly, and installation — as if that energy were "embodied" in the item itself. It is a key measure in life-cycle assessment.

Assessment

Analysts sum the energy inputs across a product's supply chain up to a chosen system boundary, such as "cradle to gate" (factory exit) or "cradle to grave" (including use and disposal). Materials like aluminium, steel, and cement have high embodied energy per kilogram, while timber and earth materials have low values; databases such as the Inventory of Carbon and Energy compile typical figures.

Buildings

For efficient modern buildings, embodied energy in materials can rival the energy used in decades of operation, so design choices — reusing structures, choosing low-energy materials, and designing for disassembly — are important to reducing total impacts.

Embodied carbon

The related concept of embodied carbon measures the greenhouse-gas emissions associated with production, and is increasingly regulated in construction codes.
