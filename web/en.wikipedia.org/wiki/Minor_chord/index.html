Minor chord

In music theory, a minor chord is a chord built from a root, a minor third and a perfect fifth. The minor triad is one of the basic building blocks of Western harmony and is often described as sounding darker or more sombre than the major chord.

Structure

A minor triad consists of the root note, the note a minor third (three semitones) above it, and the note a perfect fifth (seven semitones) above the root. For example, the A minor chord contains A, C and E. In figured notation it may be written Am, A min, or A−.

Use

Minor chords occur naturally on the second, third and sixth degrees of the major scale and form the tonic chord of the minor keys. Extended minor chords include the minor seventh chord and minor ninth chord, common in jazz and popular music.
