Kingdom of Asturias

The Kingdom of Asturias (Asturian: Reinu d'Asturies) was the first Christian political entity established in the Iberian Peninsula after the collapse of the Visigothic Kingdom and the Muslim conquest of Hispania. It existed from 718 until 924, when it was succeeded by the Kingdom of León.

Origins

The kingdom originated with the revolt of the Visigothic nobleman Pelagius (Pelayo), whose victory over a Muslim force at the Battle of Covadonga (c. 718 or 722) is traditionally regarded as the starting point of the Reconquista. From mountain strongholds in the Cantabrian Mountains, Pelagius and his successors built a kingdom with capitals at Cangas de Onís, Pravia and finally Oviedo.

Expansion

Under Alfonso I and his successors the kingdom expanded westward into Galicia and eastward into Álava, while Alfonso II (r. 791–842) established diplomatic contact with Charlemagne and promoted the cult of Saint James at Compostela. Alfonso III "the Great" pushed the frontier south to the Duero valley. Asturian pre-Romanesque architecture — churches such as Santa María del Naranco and San Miguel de Lillo — survives as a distinctive legacy of the kingdom.

Succession

After the death of Alfonso III in 910, the kingdom was divided among his sons; the principal heir, Fruela II, moved the court to León in 924, and the realm continued as the Kingdom of León.
