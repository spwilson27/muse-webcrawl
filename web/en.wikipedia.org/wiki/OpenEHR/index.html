openEHR

openEHR is an open standard specification for electronic health records (EHRs) that describes how health data should be structured, stored, and exchanged so that records can be maintained over a patient's lifetime, independent of any single software vendor. It is managed by the openEHR Foundation, a not-for-profit organization, with specifications published openly.

Approach

The central idea of openEHR is a two-level modelling approach. A stable, relatively small reference model defines generic structures for health information, while clinical content is defined separately in formal models called archetypes and templates, created and governed by clinicians. This separation is intended to let systems evolve as medicine changes without redesigning the underlying data model, and to make recorded data semantically interoperable between different systems and countries.

Components

The open specifications include the reference model, the archetype formalism (ADL/AOM), query language (AQL), service models and REST APIs, and clinical model repositories such as the Clinical Knowledge Manager, where archetypes for concepts like blood pressure, diagnosis, and medications are shared and reviewed internationally.

Adoption

openEHR-based systems have been deployed in national and regional health services and hospitals in a number of countries, and the approach has influenced standards work in health informatics alongside HL7 FHIR and ISO standards. Commercial and open-source platforms implement the specifications.
