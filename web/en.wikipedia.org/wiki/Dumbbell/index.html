Dumbbell

The dumbbell, a type of free weight, is a piece of equipment used in weight training. It is usually used individually or in pairs, with one in each hand. A dumbbell consists of a short handle bar with weights at either end, and it allows exercises that train one side of the body at a time.

History

The forerunner of the dumbbell, the halteres, was used in ancient Greece as lifting weights and also as weights in long jump events. In 17th- and 18th-century England the term "dumbbell" referred to apparatus used for ringing church bells for exercise but without the bell, hence producing no sound — "dumb". Adjustable plate-loading dumbbells appeared in the 19th century, and modern gym dumbbells are commonly made of cast iron, steel or rubber-coated metal, sometimes with fixed weights in small increments.

Types

Fixed-weight dumbbells are cast as a single piece. Adjustable dumbbells let the user add or remove plates secured by collars, or select a weight via a dial mechanism. Spinlock dumbbells use threaded ends. Dumbbells range from under 1 kg for aerobic workouts to over 100 kg for strongman training.

Use

Dumbbells are used for exercises including bicep curls, presses, rows, lunges and flyes. Compared with a barbell, dumbbells demand more stabilisation, allow a greater range of motion, and reveal strength imbalances between limbs.
