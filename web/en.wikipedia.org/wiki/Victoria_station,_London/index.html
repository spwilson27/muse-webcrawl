London Victoria station, generally known as Victoria, is a central London railway terminus and connected London Underground station in Victoria, in the City of Westminster, managed by Network Rail. It is one of the busiest stations in the United Kingdom, serving as the London terminus for services to the south coast, Kent, and Gatwick Airport.

The station was built in two halves by rival companies: the London, Brighton and South Coast Railway opened its station in 1860, and the London, Chatham and Dover Railway opened an adjacent station in 1862. The two remained physically separate (though sharing the name) until a rebuilding under the Southern Railway in the 1920s united them operationally. The adjoining Underground station opened in 1868 on what are now the Circle and District lines; the Victoria line station followed in 1971.

Services

  • Southern services to Brighton, the Sussex coast, and Gatwick Airport (including the Gatwick Express)
  • Southeastern services to Kent destinations including Dover, Ramsgate, and Maidstone
  • London Underground: Circle and District lines (subsurface station) and the Victoria line (deep-level station) — the Victoria line takes its name from the station

Adjacent to the rail station is Victoria Coach Station, London's principal coach terminus, opened in 1932. The district around the station, developed on land belonging to the Grosvenor estate, was named after nearby Victoria Street and the station itself; the station in turn was named in honour of Queen Victoria.
