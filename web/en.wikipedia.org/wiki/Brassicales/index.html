Brassicales

The Brassicales are an order of flowering plants, belonging to the malvids group of the rosids in the eudicots. The order contains around 4,700 species in some 18 families, including many economically important plants: the cabbage family Brassicaceae (mustards, cabbages, broccoli, rapeseed, and the model organism Arabidopsis thaliana), the caper family Capparaceae, papaya (Caricaceae), and the horseradish tree family Moringaceae.

Characteristics

A unifying chemical feature of most Brassicales is the production of glucosinolates — the mustard-oil compounds responsible for the pungent flavours of mustard, horseradish, wasabi, and capers — together with the enzyme myrosinase, which releases isothiocyanates when tissues are damaged. This "mustard oil bomb" defends the plants against herbivores, though some insect specialists have evolved to exploit it. Flowers typically have four petals arranged in a cross (hence the older name Cruciferae for Brassicaceae), and the gynoecium commonly consists of two fused carpels.

Classification

Under the APG IV system (2016), the Brassicales are placed in the malvid clade of the rosids and are closely related to the orders Malvales and Sapindales. The order includes the families Akaniaceae, Bataceae, Brassicaceae, Capparaceae, Caricaceae, Cleomaceae, Emblingiaceae, Gyrostemonaceae, Koeberliniaceae, Limnanthaceae, Moringaceae, Pentadiplandraceae, Resedaceae, Salvadoraceae, Setchellanthaceae, Stixaceae, Tiganophytaceae, and Tropaeolaceae. An older name for the order, Capparales, is no longer used in APG classifications.

Evolution

Molecular estimates place the origin of the Brassicales in the Late Cretaceous, roughly 90 million years ago. The order is notable for ancient whole-genome duplications within Brassicaceae, which contributed to its diversification. Fossil and biogeographic evidence suggests an early diversification in seasonally dry and saline habitats, consistent with the presence of salt-tolerant families such as Bataceae.

Economic importance

Brassicales include some of the world's most important vegetable and oilseed crops: cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli, kale, turnip, radish, rapeseed (canola), and mustards are all Brassicaceae. Papaya is a major tropical fruit; capers are flower buds of Capparis spinosa; moringa is grown as a nutritious leaf vegetable; and nasturtiums (Tropaeolaceae) are common garden ornamentals. The small weed Arabidopsis thaliana is the pre-eminent model organism of plant biology.
