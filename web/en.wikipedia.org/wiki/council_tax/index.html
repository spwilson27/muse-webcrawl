Council Tax

Council Tax is a local taxation system used in England, Scotland, and Wales. It is a tax on domestic property collected by local authorities to help fund local services such as rubbish collection, policing, fire services, and libraries. Council Tax was introduced in 1993 by the Local Government Finance Act 1992, replacing the unpopular Community Charge (the "poll tax").

Banding

Each dwelling is allocated to one of eight valuation bands (A to H in England and Scotland; A to I in Wales) based on its capital value at 1 April 1991 (2003 values in Wales). Band A properties pay the least and Band H the most, at three times the Band A amount. The tax is normally payable by the residents of a property rather than its owner, and a 25% discount applies for a single adult resident. Various exemptions and reductions exist, for example for students and people on low incomes through Council Tax Reduction schemes.

Administration

Billing authorities — district, unitary, and metropolitan councils, and London boroughs — collect the tax, part of which goes to precepting authorities such as police and fire authorities and parish councils. Councils set their rates annually, though central government has at times limited increases or required referendums above a threshold.

Criticism

Council Tax has been criticised because valuations in England and Scotland still rest on 1991 property values, because it is only loosely related to current ability to pay, and because bills vary considerably between areas. Repeated reviews have proposed reform or replacement, but no UK government has implemented wholesale change.
