Churchyard

Churchyard
DefinitionEnclosed ground surrounding a church, traditionally used for burials
FeaturesGravestones, lychgate, churchyard cross, yew trees
Distinguished fromCemetery (separate, non-church burial ground)

A churchyard is the area of land adjoining or surrounding a church, typically consecrated and historically used as the parish burial ground. Churchyards are characteristic of Britain, Ireland and older settlements in northern Europe and their former colonies, where the parish church stood at the centre of community life and death for centuries.

Features

Traditional churchyards are enclosed by a wall or hedge and entered through a lychgate, where coffins once rested awaiting the priest. They contain gravestones and table tombs — often centuries deep — a churchyard cross, and frequently ancient yew trees, symbols of death and resurrection that may predate the church itself. The south side, sunniest, was the favoured burial side; the north side was traditionally reserved for the unbaptised, suicides and strangers in some regions.

History

Burial in churchyards became standard in early medieval Europe as burial moved from extramural cemeteries to consecrated ground beside churches. By the 19th century, urban churchyards were catastrophically overcrowded — a public-health scandal addressed in Britain by the Burial Acts of the 1850s, which closed many inner-city churchyards and shifted burial to large garden cemeteries outside parishes. Many rural churchyards remain in use; closed ones are maintained by local authorities as green spaces, and are valued wildlife refuges, home to unimproved grassland, wildflowers and lichens rare elsewhere.
