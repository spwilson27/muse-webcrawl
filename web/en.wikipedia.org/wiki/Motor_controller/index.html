Motor controller

A motor controller is a device or group of devices that governs, in some predetermined manner, the performance of an electric motor. It may include a manual or automatic means for starting and stopping the motor, selecting forward or reverse rotation, regulating speed, limiting torque and current, and protecting the motor and driven machinery against overloads and faults. Motor controllers range from a simple toggle switch for a small appliance to multi-megawatt variable-frequency drives and servo amplifiers.

Types

  • Motor starters — contactors with overload protection; direct-on-line (DOL), star–delta, autotransformer, and soft starters progressively reduce starting current and mechanical shock.
  • Variable-frequency drives (VFDs) — power electronics that vary the frequency and voltage supplied to an AC motor, giving efficient continuous speed control.
  • DC drives — controlled rectifiers or chopper (PWM) circuits regulating armature voltage of DC motors.
  • Servo controllers and stepper drivers — closed-loop controllers using encoder or resolver feedback for precise position, velocity, and torque control in automation and robotics.

Functions

Beyond start/stop and speed control, modern controllers provide current and torque limiting, dynamic braking and regeneration, direction reversal, sequencing and interlocking with other equipment, fault diagnostics, and communications via industrial networks (for example Modbus, EtherCAT, PROFINET). Protective functions include overload relays, short-circuit and earth-fault protection, phase-loss detection, and stall prevention.

History

Early motor control used hand-operated faceplate rheostats and drum controllers; the magnetic contactor, introduced in the early twentieth century, allowed remote and automatic control and is still the backbone of industrial motor control centres. The advent of power semiconductors — thyristors in the 1960s, then IGBTs — replaced electromechanical speed control with efficient solid-state drives, transforming energy use in pumps, fans, compressors, and traction.
