Big wall climbing

TypeRock climbing discipline
Famous wallsEl Capitan, Yosemite

Big wall climbing is a style of rock climbing in which a route up a very large rock face takes multiple days to complete, with climbers sleeping on the wall in hanging bivouacs called portaledges.

Techniques

Big wall ascents combine free climbing and aid climbing, with heavy loads of food, water and equipment hauled up the wall in bags. Classic big walls include El Capitan and Half Dome in Yosemite, the walls of Zion, and the great faces of Patagonia and Baffin Island.

History

The multi-day first ascent of the Nose on El Capitan by Warren Harding's team in 1958 established the style. Modern speed climbers have since climbed the same route in under two hours, and free climbers have ascended big walls without aid.
