United Way

United Way is an international network of more than 1,100 local nonprofit fundraising affiliates, coordinated by United Way Worldwide, based in Alexandria, Virginia. It is one of the largest privately funded charities in the world, raising several billion dollars annually, primarily through workplace payroll-deduction campaigns.

History

The movement traces its origins to Denver, Colorado, in 1887, when Frances Wisebart Jacobs, the Rev. Myron W. Reed, and other religious leaders founded the Charity Organization Society to coordinate fundraising for local charities — the first "united" campaign. Similar Community Chest organisations spread across North America in the early 20th century. The umbrella organisation adopted the name United Way of America in 1970.

Activities

Local United Ways raise funds and allocate them to community programmes in education, income stability, and health. Since the 2000s the organisation has emphasised measurable community impact over pure fund distribution, and operates the 2-1-1 helpline service in many communities, connecting callers to local social services.

Criticism

The organisation faced a major scandal in 1992 when its national president, William Aramony, was convicted of fraud. Local affiliates have periodically debated the balance between donor-designated gifts and community-wide allocation.
