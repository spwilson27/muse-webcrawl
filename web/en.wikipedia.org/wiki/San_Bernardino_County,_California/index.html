San Bernardino County, officially the County of San Bernardino, is a county located in the southern portion of the U.S. state of California. As of the 2020 census, the population was about 2.18 million, making it the fifth-most populous county in California. Its county seat is San Bernardino. By land area — over 20,000 square miles — it is the largest county in the contiguous United States, larger than each of the nine smallest U.S. states.

San Bernardino County extends from the Los Angeles suburbs across the Mojave Desert to the Nevada and Arizona borders.

Geography

The county spans three broad zones: the urbanized southwest corner in the Inland Empire (San Bernardino Valley), continuous with metropolitan Los Angeles; the San Bernardino and San Gabriel mountain ranges, including the resort communities of Lake Arrowhead and Big Bear Lake; and the vast Mojave Desert covering most of the county's area, containing parts of Death Valley National Park, the Mojave National Preserve, and Joshua Tree National Park, as well as the military installations of Fort Irwin and Twentynine Palms. The Colorado River forms its eastern boundary with Arizona.

History

The county was formed in 1853 from parts of Los Angeles County; territory was later transferred to Kern and Riverside counties. Its early development followed mining booms in the desert and mountains, the Mormon settlement of San Bernardino, the railroads, and the citrus industry of the valley. In the twenty-first century, population growth has centred on valley cities such as Fontana, Ontario, Rancho Cucamonga, and Victorville in the high desert, and the county is a major national hub for freight logistics, with interstates 10, 15, 40, and 215 crossing it.

Communities

Incorporated cities include San Bernardino, Fontana, Ontario, Rancho Cucamonga, Victorville, Rialto, Hesperia, Apple Valley, Redlands, Chino, Chino Hills, Upland, Highland, Yucaipa, Colton, Loma Linda, Barstow, Needles, Twentynine Palms, and Big Bear Lake, among others. Unincorporated areas include Joshua Tree, Yucca Valley (a town), Crestline, and the communities along the Colorado River.
