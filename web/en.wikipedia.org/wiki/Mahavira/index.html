Mahavira ('Great Hero'), born Vardhamana, was an Indian ascetic teacher of the 6th–5th century BC, regarded by Jains as the 24th and last Tirthankara ('ford-maker') of the present age. A contemporary of the Buddha from the same region and milieu, he systematised the doctrines of Jainism — above all absolute non-violence (ahimsa) — and organised a fourfold community of monks, nuns, laymen and laywomen that survives today.

Life

Tradition holds that Vardhamana, son of a local chief, renounced wealth at thirty, wandered naked for twelve years of harsh asceticism, and attained omniscience (kevala jnana) seated under a sal tree, after which he preached for thirty years and died at Pavapuri — where Diwali, for Jains, marks his liberation. Some scholars date him slightly later than tradition and regard the 23rd Tirthankara, Parshvanatha, as an earlier historical forerunner whose teachings Mahavira reformed.
