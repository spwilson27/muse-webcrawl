Junkers Jumo 004

The Junkers Jumo 004 was the world's first production axial-flow turbojet engine, developed by Junkers in Germany during the Second World War. It powered the Messerschmitt Me 262 jet fighter and the Arado Ar 234 jet bomber.

Development

Designed under Anselm Franz at Junkers' engine division, the Jumo 004 used an eight-stage axial compressor, six combustion chambers, and a single-stage turbine. The first prototype ran in 1940 and a flight-rated version powered the Me 262 prototype in July 1942. Production began in 1944; roughly 8,000 units were built by the war's end.

Design and service

The engine produced about 8.8 kilonewtons (1,980 pounds) of thrust. Wartime shortages forced the use of inferior alloys in turbine blades, giving production engines a service life of sometimes only 10–25 hours. Pilots had to handle throttles gently to avoid flameouts or compressor stalls.

Legacy

After the war, the Jumo 004 was produced in Czechoslovakia as the M-04 and copied in the Soviet Union as the RD-10, which powered early Soviet jets such as the Yakovlev Yak-15. Its axial-flow architecture set the pattern for nearly all subsequent jet engines.
