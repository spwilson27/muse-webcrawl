Michael Peter Fay (born 30 May 1975) is an American citizen who was sentenced to caning in Singapore in 1994 for theft and vandalism. The case provoked widespread controversy in the United States and strained relations between the two countries, with U.S. President Bill Clinton appealing unsuccessfully for clemency.

The case

Fay, who was living in Singapore with his family, pleaded guilty to charges arising from a spree of vandalism in which cars were spray-painted and property stolen. He was sentenced to four months' imprisonment, a fine, and six strokes of the cane; Singapore's president reduced the caning to four strokes. The sentence was carried out on 5 May 1994.

Aftermath

The case sparked an international debate about corporal punishment and deterrence, and it was widely discussed in American media as a contrast between U.S. and Singaporean approaches to crime. Fay later returned to the United States.
