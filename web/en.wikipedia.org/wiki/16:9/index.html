16:9 is the international standard aspect ratio for high-definition television, widescreen displays and most online video. It describes a width of 16 units to a height of 9 units, equivalent to 1.78:1.

History

The ratio was proposed in the 1980s by Kerns H. Powers of the Society of Motion Picture and Television Engineers as a compromise able to frame, with minimal letterboxing or cropping, all common formats from 4:3 television to 2.39:1 Cinemascope films. It is the geometric mean of 4:3 (1.33:1) and 2.35:1.

Usage

16:9 became the native shape of HDTV standards (720p, 1080i, 1080p) and of 4K and 8K UHD, and from the late 2000s the dominant aspect ratio for computer monitors, laptops and smartphone video. Common resolutions include 1280×720, 1920×1080 and 3840×2160.
