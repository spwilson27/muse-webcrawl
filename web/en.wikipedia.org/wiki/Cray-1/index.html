Cray-1


A Cray-1 at a computing centre

The Cray-1 was a supercomputer designed by Seymour Cray and Leslie Technologies, succeeded by Cray Research, first delivered in 1976. It was one of the best-known and most successful supercomputers in history, famous for its distinctive cylindrical tower surrounded by a padded bench seat that concealed its power supplies and Freon cooling system.

Design

The Cray-1 was a vector processor machine running at an 80 MHz clock, achieving up to 160 MFLOPS, with up to 8 megabytes of main memory. Its compact, tightly packed chassis — folded into a C-shaped column to keep wire lengths short — used emitter-coupled logic and some 115 km of wiring. The first system was installed at Los Alamos National Laboratory in 1976; the machine, priced at roughly US$8 million, sold to weather, defence, and research laboratories worldwide. Its optional front-end and the Cray Operating System (later UNICOS) completed the system.

Legacy

The Cray-1 established the template for vector supercomputing and made "Cray" a byword for high-performance computing. It was followed by the Cray X-MP and Cray-2. Examples are preserved in museums including the Computer History Museum and the Deutsches Museum.
