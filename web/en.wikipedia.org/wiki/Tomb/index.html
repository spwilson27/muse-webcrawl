Tomb

A tomb is a repository for the remains of the dead. It is generally any structurally enclosed interment space or burial chamber, of varying sizes.

Tombs have been built since prehistoric times, from simple stone-lined pits and burial mounds to elaborate mausoleums. In many cultures the tomb was conceived as a house for the dead, furnished with grave goods, food, weapons, and objects the deceased might need in an afterlife.

Types

Common forms include the mastaba and pyramid of ancient Egypt, the rock-cut tombs of Petra and the Valley of the Kings, the tumulus or barrow of prehistoric Europe, the sarcophagus, the crypt beneath a church, and the modern mausoleum. Some tombs are communal, such as ossuaries and catacombs, while others are individual monuments.

Famous tombs

Among the best known are the tomb of Tutankhamun in Egypt, the Taj Mahal in India, the Mausoleum at Halicarnassus, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, and the tomb of the Unknown Soldier, a form of war memorial found in many countries.
