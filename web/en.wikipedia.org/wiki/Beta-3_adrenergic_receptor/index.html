Beta-3 adrenergic receptor

ADRB3
GeneADRB3 (chromosome 8p11.23)
TypeG protein-coupled receptor
Main locationsAdipose tissue, bladder detrusor, gallbladder

Beta-3 receptors are prominent in adipose tissue and the bladder

The beta-3 adrenergic receptor (β3 adrenoceptor), also known as ADRB3, is a beta-adrenergic receptor, and also denotes the human gene encoding it. It was the last of the three beta receptor subtypes to be cloned, in 1989, and is found mainly in adipose tissue and in the detrusor muscle of the urinary bladder.

Function

In adipocytes, beta-3 activation stimulates lipolysis and, in brown adipose tissue, thermogenesis through uncoupling protein 1. In the bladder, activation relaxes the detrusor muscle during the storage phase of micturition, increasing bladder capacity. The receptor is relatively resistant to desensitisation compared with the beta-1 and beta-2 subtypes, lacking certain phosphorylation sites in its cytoplasmic tail.

Clinical significance

The selective beta-3 agonist mirabegron is approved for the treatment of overactive bladder, and a related agent, vibegron, is also in clinical use. Beta-3 agonists were long investigated as anti-obesity and anti-diabetic drugs because of their metabolic effects in rodents, but translation to humans proved disappointing, in part because of species differences in receptor expression. A Trp64Arg polymorphism of ADRB3 has been associated in some studies with obesity and insulin resistance, though findings are inconsistent.
