GDDR6 SDRAM

Graphics Double Data Rate 6 Synchronous Dynamic Random-Access Memory (GDDR6 SDRAM) is a type of synchronous graphics random-access memory designed for high-bandwidth applications such as graphics cards, game consoles, and high-performance computing. It succeeded GDDR5 and GDDR5X, with the JEDEC standard published in 2018.

GDDR6 operates at up to 16–20 Gbit/s per pin, uses dual 16-bit channels per chip, and offers improved power efficiency over GDDR5. It is used in Nvidia's GeForce RTX 20 series and later cards, AMD's Radeon RX 5000 series onward, and the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S consoles. The faster GDDR6X variant, developed with Micron, uses PAM4 signalling.
