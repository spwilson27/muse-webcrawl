Fuxing (train)

The Fuxing (Chinese: 复兴号, "Rejuvenation") is a series of high-speed electric multiple unit trains developed and operated in China by China Railway. Introduced in 2017, the Fuxing trains were designed to Chinese technical standards to reduce dependence on the foreign technology incorporated in the earlier Hexie (CRH) series, which had been adapted from Japanese, German, French, and Swedish designs.

Development

Development of a "China Standard" EMU began in 2012 under the China Railway Corporation with CRRC's Qingdao Sifang and Changchun subsidiaries building the first trainsets, initially classified CR400AF and CR400BF. The trains were officially named Fuxing in June 2017 and entered revenue service on the Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway, initially operating at up to 350 km/h — restoring the top speed that had been reduced after 2011 — from September 2017.

Variants

The family has expanded to include the CR400 series rated for 350 km/h operation, the CR300 series for 250 km/h, and the CR200J, a 160 km/h power-concentrated trainset used to upgrade conventional services. Longer 16- and 17-car variants serve busy corridors, and intelligent versions with automated driving functions were introduced for the Beijing–Zhangjiakou line built for the 2022 Winter Olympics.

Service

Fuxing trains operate throughout China's high-speed network, the largest in the world, and by the early 2020s formed the backbone of China Railway's high-speed fleet.
