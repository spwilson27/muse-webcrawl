The Scottish Football Association Challenge Cup, commonly known as the Scottish Cup, is the world's oldest national football trophy and Scotland's main annual knockout cup competition for men's association football clubs. First contested in the 1873–74 season, it is organised by the Scottish Football Association (SFA) and is open to all SFA member clubs, with entry now extending to clubs from the lower leagues and the Highland and Lowland leagues.

History

The trophy was first presented in 1874, when Queen's Park beat Clydesdale 2–0 in the inaugural final. Queen's Park dominated the early years. The competition grew into one of the most prestigious in Scottish football; its trophy, manufactured in 1873, is the oldest national football trophy in the world (the FA Cup itself is older as a competition, but its original trophy was stolen and replaced). The final has traditionally been held at Hampden Park in Glasgow.

Format

The tournament is a single-elimination knockout. Clubs from the lower tiers enter in the preliminary and early rounds, with Scottish Premiership clubs entering in the fourth round. Ties are decided over one match; drawn matches after extra time are settled by a penalty shoot-out (replays were abolished for later rounds in recent years). The winner traditionally qualifies for the UEFA Europa League.

Records

Celtic are the most successful club in the competition's history, with more than 40 wins, followed by Rangers. Notable upsets include East Fife's win in 1938 (as a second-tier club), Motherwell's 1991 victory, and Inverness Caledonian Thistle's 2015 triumph, as well as famous "giant-killings" such as Berwick Rangers' defeat of Rangers in 1967 and Celtic's loss to Inverness in 2000, which produced the famous headline "Super Caley Go Ballistic Celtic Are Atrocious".

Sponsorship

The cup has carried various sponsors' names, notably as the Tennents Scottish Cup, the Scottish Gas Scottish Cup in recent sponsorship arrangements, and the William Hill Scottish Cup during the 2010s.
