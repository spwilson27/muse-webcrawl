Arkham Asylum: A Serious House on Serious Earth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dave McKean's expressionistic painted art defines the book's nightmarish tone.

Arkham Asylum: A Serious House on Serious Earth is a Batman graphic novel written by Grant Morrison and illustrated by Dave McKean, published by DC Comics in 1989. It tells the story of Batman being called upon to quell a riot at Arkham Asylum, the psychiatric hospital for the criminally insane in Gotham City, while interweaving the history of the asylum's founder, Amadeus Arkham.

Plot

On April Fools' Day, the inmates of Arkham Asylum, led by the Joker, seize control of the facility and threaten to kill the staff unless Batman comes to them alone. Batman enters the asylum and is forced to run a gauntlet of his enemies—among them Two-Face, the Mad Hatter, Killer Croc, Clayface, and Scarecrow—while confronting the fear that he may belong among them. Intercut with this is the diary of Amadeus Arkham, describing the asylum's founding, his mother's madness, and his own descent into insanity.

Style and themes

The book is notable for McKean's mixed-media painted artwork—combining painting, photography, sculpture, and collage—and for Morrison's dense symbolism drawn from Jungian psychology, tarot, mythology, and the works of Lewis Carroll and Aleister Crowley. The Joker's dialogue is lettered in a scratchy red script, and Two-Face's coin is replaced by tarot cards and a multi-sided die as his therapy progresses. Themes include madness and sanity, duality, and the fragile boundary between Batman and his foes.

Reception and legacy

Arkham Asylum was a major commercial success, selling hundreds of thousands of copies and becoming one of the best-selling graphic novels of its era. It is frequently cited among the greatest Batman stories ever published. Its title quotes a Philip Larkin poem ("Church Going"), and its portrayal of the asylum influenced later works, including the 2009 video game Batman: Arkham Asylum, which Morrison-era concepts inform. A 15th-anniversary edition reprinted Morrison's annotated script.
