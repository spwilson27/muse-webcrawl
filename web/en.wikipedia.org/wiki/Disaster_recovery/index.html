Disaster recovery (DR) is the set of policies, tools and procedures that enables an organisation to restore and continue vital technology infrastructure and systems following a natural or human-induced disaster — such as fire, flood, cyberattack or major equipment failure. It is a subset of business continuity planning, focused specifically on the IT and data systems that support critical business functions.

Objectives and metrics

Two parameters dominate disaster recovery planning. The recovery time objective (RTO) is the maximum acceptable time within which systems must be restored. The recovery point objective (RPO) is the maximum acceptable amount of data loss, expressed as the time between the last usable backup and the disaster. Plans are engineered so that both targets can demonstrably be met, and are verified through regular testing and exercises.

Strategies

Common strategies include periodic backups (on tape, disk or cloud storage), synchronous or asynchronous replication to a secondary site, and tiered recovery sites: cold sites (space only), warm sites (equipped but not live) and hot sites (fully mirrored and ready for immediate failover). Cloud computing has popularised Disaster Recovery as a Service (DRaaS), in which a provider replicates an organisation's systems and orchestrates failover.

Planning

A disaster recovery plan typically follows a business impact analysis and risk assessment, documents responsibilities and communication trees, and specifies step-by-step runbooks for restoring each service in priority order. Because untested plans often fail, regular drills — from tabletop exercises to full failover tests — are considered essential.
