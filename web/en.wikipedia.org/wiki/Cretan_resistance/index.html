Cretan resistance

Cretan partisans and SOE agents during the occupation.

The Cretan resistance was a resistance movement by the local population of Crete and by Allied forces against the Axis occupation of the island during the Second World War, lasting from the Battle of Crete in May–June 1941 until the German surrender in 1945. It is remembered as one of the most determined resistance movements in occupied Europe, aided by the island's mountainous terrain and strong traditions of defiance dating from Ottoman rule.

Occupation and resistance

After German paratroopers captured Crete in the first major airborne invasion in history, Cretan civilians who had fought the invaders faced harsh reprisals. Resistance groups — including the National Organisation of Crete (EOK) and the communist-led National Liberation Front (EAM/ELAS) — formed armed bands (andartes) in the White Mountains and Psiloritis ranges. Britain's Special Operations Executive (SOE) maintained agents on the island, among them Patrick Leigh Fermor, W. Stanley Moss, George Psychoundakis ("the Cretan Runner") and John Pendlebury, coordinating sabotage, intelligence gathering and the evacuation of stranded Allied soldiers by caique and submarine.

Major episodes

Notable operations included the sabotage of Heraklion airfield (1942), the destruction of the Kastelli airfield, the Viannos and Kedros massacres carried out in reprisal, and the celebrated abduction of General Heinrich Kreipe, the German divisional commander, by Leigh Fermor and Moss in April 1944. German reprisals destroyed whole villages such as Kandanos, Anogeia and the Amari villages. The occupation ended with the German surrender of the Chania garrison in May 1945.
