Joel Spolsky (born 1965) is an American software engineer and writer. He is the author of Joel on Software, a blog on software development, and the creator of the "Joel Test", a twelve-point measure of the quality of a software team. He co-founded Fog Creek Software, Stack Overflow, and the project-management application Trello.

Early life and career

Spolsky was born in Albuquerque, New Mexico, and grew up in Israel from age fifteen until he returned to the United States for college. He graduated from Yale University in 1991 with a degree in computer science. He then worked at Microsoft as a program manager on the Excel team, where he was the designer behind Excel Basic, which became Visual Basic for Applications. He later worked at Viacom and Juno Online Services before founding Fog Creek Software in New York City in 2000.

Joel on Software

Spolsky began the Joel on Software weblog in 2000, writing widely read essays on software project management, hiring, design, and business strategy. His essays—including "The Joel Test: 12 Steps to Better Code" and pieces on architecture astronauts and the perils of rewriting software from scratch—were collected in several books and translated into many languages.

Companies

Fog Creek Software produced FogBugz, an issue-tracking system, and incubated the project-management tool Trello, which was spun out as its own company in 2014 and sold to Atlassian in 2017. In 2008 Spolsky co-founded Stack Overflow with Jeff Atwood, a question-and-answer site for programmers that grew into the Stack Exchange network; he served as its chief executive from 2010 to 2019.
