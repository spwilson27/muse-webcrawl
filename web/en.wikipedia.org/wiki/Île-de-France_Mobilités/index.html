Île-de-France Mobilités (IDFM) is the public transport authority that controls and coordinates the different transit companies operating in the Île-de-France region of France, including Paris. Formerly the Syndicat des transports d'Île-de-France (STIF), it was renamed in 2017. The authority organises, finances, and develops public transport across the region's network of Métro lines, RER and Transilien suburban trains, trams, and thousands of bus routes, used for some 9–10 million journeys a day before the COVID-19 pandemic.

Role

IDFM plans services and frequencies, sets fares — including the flat-rate Navigo pass, which it made zone-free in 2015 — awards operating contracts, and funds infrastructure and rolling stock renewal. Services are provided principally by the RATP, the SNCF (Transilien), and private bus groups under the Optile umbrella, progressively opened to competition under French law.

Governance and funding

The authority is governed by a board dominated by the Île-de-France Regional Council, whose president chairs it, alongside representatives of Paris and the region's departments. Funding comes from fares, the versement mobilité payroll tax on employers, regional taxes, and state contributions. IDFM has ordered large fleets of new trains (such as the RER NG and MI 20) and is the contracting authority for much of the Grand Paris Express's operations, while construction of that network rests with the Société du Grand Paris.
