Culture of Latvia

The Latvian Song and Dance Festival gathers tens of thousands of performers.

The culture of Latvia combines an ancient Baltic heritage — preserved in folklore, song and craft — with centuries of German, Polish, Swedish and Russian influence. Latvia's capital, Riga, is noted for one of the world's finest collections of Art Nouveau architecture.

Folklore and song

The dainas, short four-line folk songs numbering over a million texts, form the heart of Latvian identity; Krišjānis Barons collected them into the famed Cabinet of Dainas. The Latvian Song and Dance Festival, held since 1873 and listed by UNESCO, culminates in massed choirs of thousands, and the singing tradition helped power the "Singing Revolution" that restored independence in 1991.

Traditions and festivals

Jāņi, the midsummer festival, is the most beloved holiday, marked with bonfires, wreaths of oak and wildflowers, cheese and beer. Nature looms large in Latvian life — mushrooming, berry-picking and the sauna (pirts) are enduring pastimes, and roughly half the country is forested.

Arts and cuisine

Latvia has produced painters such as Vilhelms Purvītis, the composer Pēteris Vasks and conductor Mariss Jansons. The cuisine features rye bread, grey peas with bacon, smoked fish, piragi (bacon buns) and Riga Black Balsam liqueur. Ice hockey is the most popular spectator sport.
