Raymond Collishaw

Raymond Collishaw CB, DSO & Bar, OBE, DSC, DFC (22 November 1893 – 28 September 1976) was a Canadian fighter pilot and air commander. He was the highest-scoring Royal Naval Air Service ace of the First World War and one of the top Allied aces overall, credited with 60 aerial victories, and later rose to air vice-marshal in the Royal Air Force.

First World War

Collishaw joined the RNAS in 1916 and became famous commanding the "Black Flight" of No. 10 (Naval) Squadron, whose black-nosed Sopwith Triplanes were among the most successful units on the Western Front in 1917. After the war he served in South Russia against the Bolsheviks and in the interwar RAF in Iraq and elsewhere.

Second World War and later life

In 1939–41 Collishaw commanded the RAF's Desert Air Force in North Africa (initially No. 202 Group), directing air operations during Operation Compass against Italian forces in Libya. Sidelined after disputes with superiors, he retired in 1943 and returned to Canada, where he died in 1976.
