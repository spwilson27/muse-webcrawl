Norman L. Bowen

Norman Levi Bowen FRS (21 June 1887 – 11 September 1956) was a Canadian geologist. He is regarded as the founding father of experimental petrology and is best known for Bowen's reaction series, the sequence in which minerals crystallise from cooling magma, which he set out from his experimental work at the Geophysical Laboratory of the Carnegie Institution in Washington, D.C.

Career and work

Bowen studied at Queen's University in Kingston and earned his PhD at MIT in 1912. At the Geophysical Laboratory he conducted pioneering high-temperature, high-pressure experiments melting and cooling silicate systems, showing how fractional crystallisation drives magmatic differentiation: early-formed ferromagnesian minerals react with the melt (the discontinuous series: olivine → pyroxene → amphibole → biotite) while plagioclase feldspar changes composition continuously from calcium- to sodium-rich, leaving residual melts enriched in silica, potassium feldspar and quartz. He summarised decades of work in The Evolution of the Igneous Rocks (1928), one of the most influential geology books of the century. Bowen spent part of his later career as research professor at the University of Chicago (1937–1947) before returning to the Geophysical Laboratory.

Legacy

The Geological Society of America's Bowen Award, given for outstanding contributions to geochemistry, and the mineral bowenite are named for him, as is the lunar crater Bowen.
