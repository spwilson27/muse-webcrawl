Andy Lau

Andy Lau Tak-wah (born September 27, 1961) is a Hong Kong actor, singer-songwriter and film producer. One of the most commercially successful and enduring figures in Chinese-language entertainment since the 1980s, he has starred in more than 160 films and is one of the "Four Heavenly Kings" of Cantopop, alongside Jacky Cheung, Aaron Kwok and Leon Lai.

Career

Lau joined the TVB artist training programme in 1981 and gained fame through television dramas such as The Return of the Condor Heroes (1983). As one of TVB's "Five Tigers," he transitioned to film, achieving acclaim in Wong Kar-wai's As Tears Go By (1988) and Days of Being Wild (1990), and popular success with God of Gamblers (1989) and A Moment of Romance (1990).

Film achievements

Lau has won the Hong Kong Film Award for Best Actor three times — for Running Out of Time (1999), Infernal Affairs III (2003) and A Simple Life (2011) — and the Golden Horse Award for Best Actor for Infernal Affairs III and A Simple Life. His role as the mole Lau Kin-ming in Infernal Affairs (2002) is among his most internationally recognized performances. He also founded the Focus Group production company and has supported new Hong Kong directors through the First Feature Film Initiative.

Music

Lau released his first Cantonese album in 1985 and became one of the best-selling Cantopop and Mandopop artists of the 1990s, famous for songs such as "Forget Love Potion" (忘情水). He holds records for the most awards won by a Cantopop male artist and has performed hundreds of solo concerts across Asia.

Personal life and recognition

Known for his work ethic and philanthropy, Lau established the Andy Lau Charity Foundation in 1994. He was a torchbearer for the 2008 Beijing Olympics and has received numerous honors from the Hong Kong government. In 2024 he continued touring and acting well into his sixties, including the film The Goldfinger opposite Tony Leung.
