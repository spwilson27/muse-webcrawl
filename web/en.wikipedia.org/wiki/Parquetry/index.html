Parquetry

Parquetry is a geometric mosaic of wood pieces used for decorative effect in flooring and furniture. Contrasting woods, sometimes in different colours, are arranged in regular geometric patterns — squares, triangles, lozenges and the well-known herringbone, chevron, basketweave and Versailles patterns.

History

Large-scale parquet floors were introduced in 17th-century France, where the parquet de Versailles, first installed at the Palace of Versailles in 1684, replaced marble floors that required constant washing which rotted the joists beneath. Parquetry became a hallmark of grand European interiors and spread to Britain and America. In furniture, parquetry — geometric veneer — is distinguished from marquetry, which forms pictorial designs.

Materials and construction

Traditional parquet is solid hardwood — oak, walnut, cherry, mahogany and maple are common — laid as blocks or panels over a subfloor. Modern parquet is often engineered, with a hardwood wear layer over plywood, supplied as tiles or click-together panels. Patterns exploit the grain direction of adjoining pieces, which reflect light differently and give the floor a shifting, three-dimensional appearance.
