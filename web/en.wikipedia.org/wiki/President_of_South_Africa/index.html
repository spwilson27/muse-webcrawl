President of South Africa

President of the Republic of South Africa
Residences: Mahlamba Ndlopfu (Pretoria); Genadendal (Cape Town)
Seat: Union Buildings, Pretoria
Term: 5 years, renewable once
Formation: 31 May 1961 (State President); 1994 (current form)

The president of South Africa is the head of state and head of government of the Republic of South Africa. The president directs the executive branch of government and is the commander-in-chief of the South African National Defence Force.

History

From 1961 to 1984 the head of state was the largely ceremonial State President, while executive power rested with the Prime Minister. Constitutional reforms in 1984 created an executive State Presidency. After the end of apartheid and the first universal elections in 1994, Nelson Mandela became the first president of the democratic republic. His successors have been Thabo Mbeki (1999–2008), Kgalema Motlanthe (2008–2009), Jacob Zuma (2009–2018), and Cyril Ramaphosa (2018–present).

Election and term

The president is elected by the National Assembly from among its members after each general election, and serves a term of five years, renewable once. Once elected, the president ceases to be a member of the National Assembly and appoints the Cabinet, including a deputy president. The president may be removed by a vote of no confidence or by impeachment by a two-thirds majority of the Assembly.

Powers

Presidential powers include assenting to bills, appointing ministers, ambassadors, and senior judges (on advice of the Judicial Service Commission), conferring honours, granting pardons, and accrediting foreign envoys. Official residences include Mahlamba Ndlopfu in Pretoria and Genadendal in Cape Town; the presidential office is at the Union Buildings.
