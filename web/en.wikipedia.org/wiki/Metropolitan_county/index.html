Metropolitan county

A metropolitan county is a type of county-level administrative division used in England. There are six metropolitan counties, which each cover large urban areas, with populations of between 1.2 and 2.8 million. They were created in 1974 and are each divided into several metropolitan districts or boroughs.

History

The metropolitan counties were created by the Local Government Act 1972, taking effect on 1 April 1974. They were formed around the major conurbations outside London: the SELNEC area became Greater Manchester, the Liverpool area became Merseyside, Sheffield and South Yorkshire, Tyne and Wear around Newcastle and Sunderland, the West Midlands around Birmingham, and West Yorkshire around Leeds and Bradford.

Initially each metropolitan county had its own county council, which shared local government functions with the district councils below it. However, the metropolitan county councils were abolished in 1986 under the Local Government Act 1985 by the government of Margaret Thatcher, which accused them of excessive spending. Most of their functions passed to the metropolitan district councils, with some services (such as fire, police and public transport) run by joint boards.

Current status

Although their councils have gone, the metropolitan counties still exist legally as ceremonial counties, each retaining a lord-lieutenant and a high sheriff. They are also used for statistical purposes. Since the 2010s, most metropolitan counties have been covered by combined authorities, several with directly elected mayors, which have taken on strategic functions in transport, housing and economic development; Greater Manchester was the first to agree such a "devolution deal".
