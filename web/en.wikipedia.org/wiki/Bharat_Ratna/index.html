The Bharat Ratna (Hindi pronunciation: [bʰaːɾət̪ rət̪nə]; "Jewel of India") is the highest civilian award of the Republic of India. Instituted on 2 January 1954, the award is conferred in recognition of "exceptional service/performance of the highest order", without distinction of race, occupation, position or sex.

History

The award was established by Rajendra Prasad, the first President of India. Originally limited to achievements in the arts, literature, science, and public services, the criteria were expanded in 2011 to include "any field of human endeavour". Recommendations are made by the Prime Minister to the President; a maximum of three awards may be made in one year, a rule that has occasionally been exceeded. Recipients receive a certificate signed by the President and a peepal-leaf-shaped medallion; there is no monetary grant.

Regulations and controversies

The award has periodically been controversial, with litigation and public debate over selections and over posthumous awards. The conferment on Sachin Tendulkar in 2014 made him the youngest recipient and the first sportsperson so honoured. Former Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee and educationist Madan Mohan Malaviya were awarded in 2015. In 2019 the award went to Pranab Mukherjee, Bhupen Hazarika, and Nanaji Deshmukh. In 2024, five awards were announced, including for Karpoori Thakur, L. K. Advani, P. V. Narasimha Rao, Charan Singh, and M. S. Swaminathan.

Notable recipients

Recipients include Jawaharlal Nehru, Indira Gandhi, Mother Teresa, Nelson Mandela (one of only two non-citizens, along with Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan), A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, Lata Mangeshkar, and Satyajit Ray.
