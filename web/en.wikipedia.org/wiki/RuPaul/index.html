RuPaul

RuPaul Andre Charles (born November 17, 1960), known mononymously as RuPaul, is an American drag queen, television host, singer, producer, writer and actor. He produces, hosts and judges the reality competition series RuPaul's Drag Race and its many international spin-offs, and is widely credited with bringing drag performance into mainstream popular culture. He has won multiple Primetime Emmy Awards and received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2018.

Early life and career

RuPaul was born and raised in San Diego, California. He studied performing arts in Atlanta, Georgia, and became part of the city's underground film and music scene before moving to New York City, where he became a fixture of the downtown club scene and appeared with the B-52's in the video for "Love Shack". His breakthrough came with the 1992 dance single "Supermodel (You Better Work)" from the album Supermodel of the World (1993). He went on to host a VH1 talk show, co-host a morning radio show, and record duets including "Don't Go Breaking My Heart" with Elton John.

Drag Race and later work

In 2009 he began hosting RuPaul's Drag Race, a competition to find "America's next drag superstar". The show became a global franchise with versions in numerous countries and won RuPaul numerous Emmy Awards as host and producer, including a Tony Award as a producer of the Broadway musical A Strange Loop. He has also acted in films and series including But I'm a Cheerleader, Broad City and Grace and Frankie, hosted the game show Lingo, and written memoirs including Lettin' It All Hang Out (1995), Workin' It! (2010), GuRu (2018) and The House of Hidden Meanings (2024).

Influence

RuPaul is frequently described as the most commercially successful drag queen in history. His catchphrases — "If you can't love yourself, how in the hell you gonna love somebody else?" and "Shantay, you stay" — have entered popular usage, and his show is credited with creating careers for hundreds of drag performers and changing mainstream attitudes toward drag.
