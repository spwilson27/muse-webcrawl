House of La Trémoille

The House of La Trémoille (also spelled La Trémouille) is a French noble family of Poitou whose members played prominent roles in French politics and warfare from the Middle Ages through the ancien régime. The family name derives from the village of La Trimouille in present-day Vienne.

Rise to prominence

The family's fortunes rose with Georges de La Trémoille (c. 1382–1446), Grand Chamberlain of France and a powerful, controversial favourite of Charles VII during the Hundred Years' War, whose rivalry with Arthur de Richemont and Yolande of Aragon shaped court politics during the era of Joan of Arc. Louis II de la Trémoille (1460–1503), the "chevalier sans reproche", commanded French armies in the Italian Wars and was killed at the Battle of Pavia's predecessor campaigns at Novara — he died at Pavia in 1525 fighting for Francis I.

Dukes of Thouars

Through marriage the family acquired the viscounty of Thouars, raised to a duchy in 1563. The dukes of Thouars became leading Huguenot nobles during the French Wars of Religion; Henri de La Trémoille converted to Catholicism in 1628. Through the marriage of Charlotte de La Trémoille, the family also acquired claims connected to the earldom of Derby in England, and the dukes long asserted a claim to the crown of Naples through descent from Anne de Laval. The senior line became extinct in the male line in the 20th century, the last duke, Louis Charles Marie de La Trémoille, dying in 1921.
