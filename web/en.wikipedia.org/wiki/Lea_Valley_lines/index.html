Lea Valley lines

OverviewSuburban rail routes in London and Hertfordshire
TerminiLondon Liverpool Street / Stratford — Cheshunt, Enfield Town, Chingford, Hertford East
OperatorGreater Anglia; London Overground (Enfield & Cheshunt and Chingford branches)

The Lea Valley lines are three commuter railway routes running north from London along the valley of the River Lea, forming part of the West Anglia Main Line network. They run from London Liverpool Street and Stratford to Cheshunt, Enfield Town and Chingford, with the main line continuing via Broxbourne to Hertford East, Bishop's Stortford and Cambridge.

Routes

  • Cheshunt branch — via Tottenham Hale and the Lea Valley to Cheshunt, with services continuing to Hertford East and beyond.
  • Enfield Town branch — via Seven Sisters and Edmonton Green to Enfield Town.
  • Chingford branch — via Hackney Downs and St James Street to Chingford, on the edge of Epping Forest.

History and operation

The lines were built by the Northern and Eastern Railway and the Eastern Counties Railway in the 1840s, with the Chingford branch completed in 1873. In May 2015 the Enfield & Cheshunt (via Seven Sisters) and Chingford services transferred from Abellio Greater Anglia to London Overground, with station upgrades and new rolling stock following. The Lea Valley corridor has been the subject of long-running proposals for four-tracking to increase capacity, associated with the proposed Crossrail 2 scheme.
