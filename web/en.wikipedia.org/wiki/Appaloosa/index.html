Appaloosa

The Appaloosa is an American horse breed best known for its distinctive leopard complex spotted coat patterns. The breed was developed in the Pacific Northwest by the Nez Perce people, who selectively bred horses descended from Spanish stock for colour, hardiness and disposition. The name derives from the Palouse River region where the horses were raised.

Characteristics

Appaloosas display a range of coat patterns produced by the leopard complex, including blanket, leopard, snowflake and frost patterns, alongside characteristics such as mottled skin, vertically striped hooves and white sclera around the eye. After the Nez Perce War of 1877 the tribe's herds were dispersed and the breed declined, until the Appaloosa Horse Club was founded in 1938 to preserve and register it. The breed is now used for western riding, endurance, showing and, in some cases, racing. Appaloosas are predisposed to certain conditions, including congenital stationary night blindness in horses homozygous for the leopard complex.
