Umpire (baseball)

In baseball, the umpire is the person charged with officiating the game, including beginning and ending the game, enforcing the rules of the game and the grounds, making judgment calls on plays, and handling disciplinary actions. The term is often shortened to ump.

Positions and duties

In Major League Baseball, a crew of four umpires works each game: the home plate umpire, who calls balls and strikes and fair or foul balls near home; and base umpires at first, second and third base, who judge safe/out calls and other plays. In the postseason, six umpires are used, adding left- and right-field umpires.

Judgment calls and replay

Balls and strikes are not reviewable, but many other calls can be challenged under MLB's instant replay system, introduced in limited form in 2008 and expanded in 2014. Umpires signal calls with distinctive gestures, such as the raised right fist for a strike or arms spread wide for "safe".

Training

Professional umpires typically attend an umpire school and work their way up through the minor leagues. Notable umpires include Bill Klem, the first umpire inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame.
