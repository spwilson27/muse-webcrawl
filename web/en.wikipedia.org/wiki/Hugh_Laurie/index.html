Hugh Laurie

James Hugh Calum Laurie (born 11 June 1959) is an English actor, comedian, writer, musician and director. He first gained recognition as one half of the comedy double act Fry and Laurie with Stephen Fry, and became known to British audiences through Blackadder and Jeeves and Wooster. International fame followed with his portrayal of Dr Gregory House in the American medical drama House (2004–2012), which won him two Golden Globe Awards and made him one of the highest-paid actors on television.

Early life and comedy career

Laurie was born in Oxford and educated at Eton and Selwyn College, Cambridge, where he read archaeology and anthropology and was president of the Footlights. With Fry he developed the sketch show A Bit of Fry & Laurie (1989–1995), while his roles as the dim-witted George in Blackadder and Bertie Wooster opposite Fry's Jeeves established him as a leading comic actor.

House and later work

As the misanthropic diagnostician Gregory House, Laurie mastered an American accent that famously deceived the show's producers. After House he won acclaim for roles in The Night Manager (2016), Veep, Catch-22 and the sitcom Avenue 5. His film work includes Sense and Sensibility, Stuart Little, 101 Dalmatians and The Personal History of David Copperfield.

Music and writing

A talented blues musician, Laurie released two albums, Let Them Talk (2011) and Didn't It Rain (2013), touring with his Copper Bottom Band. His comic spy novel The Gun Seller was published in 1996. He was appointed CBE in 2018 for services to drama.
