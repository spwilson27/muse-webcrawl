VO2 max

VO2 max
SynonymsMaximal oxygen uptake, maximal oxygen consumption, aerobic capacity
UnitsmL/(kg·min) or L/min
Measured byIncremental exercise test with gas analysis

VO2 max (also maximal oxygen consumption, maximal oxygen uptake or maximal aerobic capacity) is the maximum rate of oxygen consumption attainable during physical exertion. It reflects the cardiorespiratory fitness of an individual and is an important determinant of endurance capacity during prolonged exercise.

Measurement

VO2 max is most accurately measured during an incremental exercise test, in which exercise intensity is progressively increased while ventilation and the oxygen and carbon dioxide concentration of the inhaled and exhaled air are measured, typically using a treadmill or cycle ergometer. The test continues until exhaustion. A plateau in oxygen uptake despite increasing workload is a traditional criterion for having reached VO2 max, though secondary criteria such as a high respiratory exchange ratio and near-maximal heart rate are also used. Submaximal protocols and field tests, such as the Cooper test and the multistage fitness (beep) test, can estimate VO2 max where laboratory testing is impractical.

Typical values

Typical values for healthy untrained adults are roughly 35–45 mL/(kg·min) for men and 27–35 mL/(kg·min) for women. Trained endurance athletes commonly reach 55–70, and elite endurance athletes — notably cross-country skiers and cyclists — have recorded values above 80, with the highest reliably reported figures around 90 mL/(kg·min). Values decline with age and are influenced by genetics, sex, body composition and altitude.

Factors and trainability

VO2 max is limited by the ability of the cardiovascular system to deliver oxygen to the working muscles and by the muscles' ability to extract and use it. Endurance training can improve VO2 max by roughly 15–20% in previously sedentary individuals, through increases in stroke volume, capillarisation and mitochondrial density; high-intensity interval training is particularly effective. Genetic variation accounts for a substantial proportion of both baseline values and the response to training.
