Posidonia Shale

TypeGeological formation
Unit ofSchwarzjura (Black Jurassic)
LithologyBlack shale, bituminous limestone
LocationSouthwestern Germany (Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria)
AgeEarly Jurassic, Toarcian (c. 183–179 Ma)

The Posidonia Shale (German: Posidonienschiefer) is an Early Jurassic geological formation of southwestern Germany, famous as a Konservat-Lagerstätte preserving exceptionally complete marine fossils. Named for the small bivalve Posidonia bronni (now Bositra buchi) that carpets many bedding planes, the formation consists of finely laminated black shales deposited in a stagnant, oxygen-poor epicontinental sea during the Toarcian stage, around 183 to 179 million years ago.

Deposition and extent

The shales crop out along the Swabian and Franconian Jura, most famously around Holzmaden, Ohmden, Zell unter Aichelberg and Dotternhausen in Baden-Württemberg, where quarries have worked the stone — used for paving, tabletops and oil shale — for centuries. Equivalent strata occur across much of Europe, including the Whitby Mudstone Formation in England and formations in France and Luxembourg, recording the widespread Toarcian Oceanic Anoxic Event (T-OAE), a global episode of marine anoxia and mass extinction associated with Karoo-Ferrar volcanism and a major carbon-cycle perturbation.

Paleontology

Because the sea floor was poisonous with hydrogen sulfide and almost devoid of oxygen, carcasses sank undisturbed and were preserved in extraordinary detail, often with soft tissues, skin outlines and stomach contents. The formation is renowned for its marine reptiles: ichthyosaurs such as Stenopterygius (including females preserved with embryos), plesiosaurs, and marine crocodyliforms such as Steneosaurus. Fishes, crinoids — notably great "rafts" of the pseudoplanktonic Seirocrinus attached to driftwood — ammonites, belemnites and rare flying reptiles and even dinosaurs washed in from land complete the fauna. Many of the finest specimens are displayed at the Museum Hauff in Holzmaden and in museums worldwide.
