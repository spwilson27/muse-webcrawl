Federally Administered Tribal Areas

The Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) were a semi-autonomous tribal region in northwestern Pakistan, bordering Afghanistan, that existed from 1947 until 2018. The territory comprised seven tribal agencies — Bajaur, Mohmand, Khyber, Orakzai, Kurram, North Waziristan and South Waziristan — and six frontier regions, covering some 27,220 km² with a population of about five million, overwhelmingly Pashtun.

Governance

FATA was administered by the federal government through the governor of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and political agents in each agency, under the Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR) of 1901, a colonial-era law permitting collective punishment. Local governance rested with tribal elders (maliks), and disputes were settled by jirgas under Pashtunwali customs. Mainstream Pakistani courts and legislatures had no jurisdiction; FATA's residents elected representatives to the National Assembly and Senate but those bodies' laws did not extend to FATA unless the President directed.

History

The region formed part of British India's frontier buffer against Afghanistan, demarcated by the Durand Line (1893), which Afghanistan has never recognised. After 2001, FATA became a major theatre of the war in North-West Pakistan, as Taliban and al-Qaeda militants displaced from Afghanistan took refuge there; Pakistani military operations (notably Zarb-e-Azb in North Waziristan, 2014) and US drone strikes caused mass displacement and controversy.

Merger with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

On 31 May 2018, the Twenty-fifth Amendment to the Constitution of Pakistan merged FATA into the province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, extending the jurisdiction of the courts, police and provincial assembly to the former tribal areas and abolishing the FCR. The merged districts have since been integrated, though development and administrative challenges persist.
