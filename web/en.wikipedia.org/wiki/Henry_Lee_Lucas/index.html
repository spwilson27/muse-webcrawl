Henry Lee Lucas (23 August 1936 – 13 March 2001) was an American convicted serial killer. Lucas was convicted of murdering his mother in 1960 and ten subsequent homicides, but he is notorious chiefly for falsely confessing to hundreds of unsolved murders, which Texas law enforcement agencies "cleared" in a scandal that made him briefly famous as America's most prolific serial killer.

Early life and crimes

Lucas grew up in poverty and abuse in Virginia, losing an eye in childhood. In 1960 he stabbed his mother, Viola, to death and served ten years in Michigan. Released in 1970, he drifted around the southern United States. In the late 1970s he travelled with Ottis Toole, and the two are believed to have committed murders together; how many remains uncertain. In 1983 he was arrested in Texas and convicted of killing 82-year-old Kate Rich and Frieda "Becky" Powell, Toole's young niece.

The confessions scandal

Seeking better treatment in custody, Lucas began confessing to ever more murders, eventually claiming some 600. A special Texas Rangers task force closed over 200 cold cases on the strength of his confessions. A 1986 investigation by the Dallas Times Herald, and later the Texas Attorney General, showed that Lucas's work records and travel made most of the confessed crimes impossible — officers had fed him case details and he had obliged. The episode became a landmark case study in false confessions and police malpractice, dramatised in the 2019 Netflix documentary series The Confession Killer.

Death sentence and death

Lucas was sentenced to death for the 1979 murder of an unidentified woman known as "Orange Socks" (identified in 2019 as Debra Jackson). In 1998 Governor George W. Bush commuted the sentence to life imprisonment — the only death sentence Bush commuted as governor — because of serious doubts about the evidence. Lucas died of heart failure in prison in 2001.
