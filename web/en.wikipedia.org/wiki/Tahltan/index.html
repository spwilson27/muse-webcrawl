Tahltan

RegionsNorthwestern British Columbia, Canada (Stikine River country)
LanguagesTahltan (Tāłtān), English
CommunitiesTelegraph Creek, Dease Lake, Iskut
GovernmentTahltan Central Government

The Tahltan (or Tāłtān) are an Indigenous people of Northern Dene (Athabaskan) ancestry whose traditional territory covers a vast area of northwestern British Columbia centred on the Stikine River and its tributaries, sometimes called Tahltan country or the "Sacred Headwaters". Their modern communities are Telegraph Creek, Dease Lake, and Iskut.

Language and culture

The Tahltan language is a Northern Athabaskan language closely related to Kaska and Tagish; it is endangered, with a limited number of fluent speakers, and revitalisation programs are under way. Traditional society was organised into matrilineal clans — including the Crow (Tsesk'iye) and Wolf (CheYonne) groupings — with potlatch governance, seasonal rounds of hunting, fishing, and trade in obsidian from Mount Edziza, which the Tahltan traded widely across the northwest.

Modern history

The Tahltan asserted sovereignty in the 1910 Declaration of the Tahltan Tribe. In recent decades the Tahltan Central Government has been a major voice in land-use decisions in their territory, notably in opposition to coalbed methane development in the Klappan/Sacred Headwaters (which led Shell to relinquish its tenure in 2012) and in negotiating impact-benefit agreements and shared decision-making with British Columbia over mining projects such as Red Chris and Eskay Creek.
