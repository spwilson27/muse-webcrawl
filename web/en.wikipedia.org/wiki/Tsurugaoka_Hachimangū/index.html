Japanese:鶴岡八幡宮
Location:Kamakura, Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan
Deity:Hachiman (Emperor Ōjin), Himegami, Empress Jingū
Founded:1063; moved to present site 1180
Founder:Minamoto no Yoriyoshi; Minamoto no Yoritomo

Tsurugaoka Hachimangū (鶴岡八幡宮) is the most important Shinto shrine in the city of Kamakura, Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan. It is dedicated to Hachiman, the tutelary god of the Minamoto clan and of samurai. The shrine was founded by Minamoto no Yoriyoshi in 1063 near Yuigahama beach and was moved to its present site in 1180 by Minamoto no Yoritomo, founder of the Kamakura shogunate, who made it the spiritual and ceremonial centre of his warrior government.

Layout and history

The shrine is approached along the Wakamiya Ōji avenue, whose raised central path (dankazura) is lined with cherry trees. The precinct includes two lotus ponds — the Genpei ponds, said to have been laid out under Yoritomo — the Maiden dance pavilion, and the main hall (hongū) reached by a grand stone stairway, rebuilt in its present form in 1828. At the foot of those stairs Minamoto no Sanetomo, the third shōgun, was assassinated in 1219 by his nephew, the priest Kugyō, who had hidden behind the shrine's great ginkgo tree. The famous ginkgo, believed to be a thousand years old, was uprooted by a storm in March 2010; a sprout from its stump continues to grow. Until the Meiji government's separation of Shinto and Buddhism in 1868, the shrine functioned as a shrine-temple complex (jingū-ji) with Buddhist halls and clergy.

Festivals

The shrine's annual festival (Reitaisai, 14–16 September) features yabusame — horseback archery by riders in Kamakura-period hunting dress — along the approach avenue. The shrine draws enormous crowds at New Year (Hatsumōde), when it is among the most visited shrines in Japan.
