Westminster Cathedral is the mother church of the Roman Catholic Church in England and Wales, located in the City of Westminster, London. Dedicated to the Most Precious Blood of Jesus Christ, it is the seat of the Archbishop of Westminster. It should not be confused with the nearby Anglican Westminster Abbey.

Architecture

Designed by John Francis Bentley in a neo-Byzantine style of red brick banded with white stone, the cathedral was built between 1895 and 1903. Its campanile rises about 87 metres (284 feet). The vast interior is noted for its marble revetment and mosaics, which were still being added a century after opening; the unfinished upper walls show Bentley's intended effect in progress.

Music

The cathedral is renowned for its choir, which maintains a daily sung liturgy and has an extensive recording legacy, continuing the tradition of Westminster Cathedral Choir School.
