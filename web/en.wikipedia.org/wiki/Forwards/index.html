In the sport of rugby union, there are 15 players on each team, comprising eight forwards (wearing jersey numbers 1–8) and seven backs (numbered 9–15). The forwards are generally the larger, stronger players whose primary role is to contest possession in set pieces — the scrum and the line-out — and in the loose phases of play at rucks and mauls.

Positions

The forwards are divided into the front row (two props and the hooker), the second row (two locks), and the back row (two flankers and the number eight). Props support the hooker in the scrum; locks provide pushing power and are the principal jumpers in the line-out; flankers and the number eight link defence and attack, tackle heavily, and contest the breakdown.

In other sports

The term is used more loosely elsewhere: in association football a forward is an attacking player positioned nearest the opponent's goal; in ice hockey the forwards are the centre and two wingers; in rugby league the pack consists of six forwards numbered 8–13. This article focuses on the rugby union usage.
