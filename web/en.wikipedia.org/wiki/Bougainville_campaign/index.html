Bougainville campaign

Date1 November 1943 – 21 August 1945
LocationBougainville Island, Solomon Islands
CombatantsUnited States, Australia, New Zealand vs. Imperial Japan
ResultAllied victory

The Bougainville campaign was a series of land and naval battles of the Pacific campaign of the Second World War, fought between Allied and Imperial Japanese forces for control of Bougainville Island. It began with the Allied landings at Cape Torokina on 1 November 1943 and continued, after Australian forces took over from the Americans in late 1944, until the end of the war.

Background

Bougainville, the northernmost of the Solomon Islands, was occupied by Japan in 1942 and used as a base supporting operations against Guadalcanal and for airfields threatening Allied supply lines. Allied planners sought a foothold from which land-based aircraft could strike the great Japanese base at Rabaul.

The Torokina perimeter

United States Marines landed at Cape Torokina in Empress Augusta Bay against light opposition, deliberately bypassing heavily defended areas. Japanese counterattacks, including a major offensive in March 1944, failed to dislodge the perimeter, within which Allied engineers built airfields used to neutralize Rabaul. Naval actions in the surrounding waters, including the Battle of Empress Augusta Bay, protected the beachhead.

Australian phase

The Australian II Corps under Lieutenant General Stanley Savige took over in late 1944 and adopted a more aggressive posture, advancing toward Japanese strongholds at Buka, Numa Numa, and Buin. Fighting in jungle terrain continued until Japan's surrender; Japanese forces on the island numbered some 40,000, many of whom died from combat, disease, and starvation.
