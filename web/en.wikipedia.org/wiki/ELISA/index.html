ELISA

The enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) is a commonly used analytical biochemistry assay, first described by Eva Engvall and Peter Perlmann in 1971. The assay is a plate-based technique designed for detecting and quantifying soluble substances such as peptides, proteins, antibodies, and hormones.

ELISA
Type: Biochemical assay
Uses: HIV testing, pregnancy tests, food allergens

Principle

Antigens or antibodies are immobilised on a solid surface, typically a 96-well polystyrene plate. An enzyme-linked antibody binds the target, and addition of a substrate produces a measurable colour change proportional to the amount present.

Types

Direct, indirect, sandwich and competitive ELISAs are the main formats. Sandwich ELISA, using capture and detection antibodies, is highly sensitive and specific.

Applications

ELISA is used in medicine for HIV, Lyme disease and COVID-19 antibody testing, in pregnancy tests, and in food industry allergen detection.
