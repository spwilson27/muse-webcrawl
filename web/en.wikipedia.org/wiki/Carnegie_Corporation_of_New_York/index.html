Carnegie Corporation of New York

The Carnegie Corporation of New York is a philanthropic foundation established on June 9, 1911, by the industrialist Andrew Carnegie to promote "the advancement and diffusion of knowledge and understanding". Endowed initially with $125 million, it was the last of Carnegie's great philanthropic trusts and for many years the largest. Its work concentrates on education, democracy and civic participation in the United States, international peace and security, and higher education and research in Africa.

History

Andrew Carnegie created the Corporation as a flexible instrument, charging his trustees to adapt its purposes to changing conditions. Early grants supported public libraries, adult education, and the landmark studies of Gunnar Myrdal published as An American Dilemma (1944). The Corporation helped shape American education research and policy, supported the development of the Educational Testing Service and public television (including early funding for what became Sesame Street), and backed legal challenges that contributed to Brown v. Board of Education.

Programmes

In the 21st century the foundation awards roughly $150–200 million in grants annually. Its stated priorities include K–12 and higher education reform, strengthening democratic institutions and voting access, reducing nuclear risk and other threats to international security, and supporting African universities and scholarship.
