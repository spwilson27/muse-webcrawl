First Philippine Republic

The First Philippine Republic (Spanish: Primera República Filipina), officially the Philippine Republic (Spanish: República Filipina), also referred to by historians as the Malolos Republic, was a state established in Malolos, Bulacan, during the Philippine Revolution against the Spanish Empire (1896–1898) and the Spanish–American War between Spain and the United States (1898).

The republic was proclaimed following the Philippine Declaration of Independence at Kawit, Cavite, on 12 June 1898, and inaugurated on 23 January 1899 after the Malolos Congress ratified the Malolos Constitution — the first republican constitution in Asia. Emilio Aguinaldo served as its president. The republic was never recognized by any foreign government; the United States claimed the Philippines under the 1898 Treaty of Paris, and the Philippine–American War began in February 1899. Aguinaldo was captured by American forces on 23 March 1901, effectively ending the republic, although Macario Sakay continued a self-styled Tagalog Republic until 1907.

Government

The Malolos Constitution provided for a parliamentary system with an Assembly of Representatives, a president serving a four-year term, and guarantees of civil rights. Apolinario Mabini, the republic's first prime minister, was its leading intellectual and the author of much of its program.
