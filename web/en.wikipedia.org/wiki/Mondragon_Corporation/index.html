Mondragon Corporation (Basque: Mondragon Korporazioa) is a corporation and federation of worker cooperatives based in the Basque region of Spain. It is the world's largest worker cooperative federation and one of Spain's largest business groups, with operations in industry, retail, finance, and knowledge, including appliance manufacturer Fagor (re-founded after the 2013 bankruptcy of the original flagship), the supermarket chain Eroski, the bank Laboral Kutxa, and Mondragon University. Its member cooperatives are owned and governed by their worker-members on the principle of one member, one vote.

History

The corporation grew out of the work of the Catholic priest José María Arizmendiarrieta, who arrived in the town of Mondragón in 1941 and founded a technical school to educate local youth. In 1956 five graduates of the school founded the first cooperative, ULGOR (later Fagor Electrodomésticos), making paraffin stoves. A cooperative bank, the Caja Laboral (now Laboral Kutxa), followed in 1959, along with a social security cooperative and a consumer cooperative. In 1991 the cooperatives reorganised as the Mondragon Cooperative Corporation (MCC), later Mondragon Corporation, with a congress and standing committee coordinating the federation while each cooperative retains its own sovereign general assembly.

Principles and structure

Mondragon's ten basic cooperative principles include open admission, democratic organisation, the sovereignty of labour, wage solidarity, inter-cooperation between cooperatives, and education. Pay ratios between the lowest- and highest-paid members are capped — historically at 3:1 and now generally between about 6:1 and 9:1 — far narrower than in conventional corporations. Profits are partly pooled for collective funds and reinvestment, and workers who lose their jobs in a struggling cooperative are typically redeployed to other cooperatives in the group.

Challenges

Globalisation strained the model: many foreign subsidiary employees are wage workers rather than cooperative members, a longstanding criticism, and the 2013 bankruptcy of Fagor Electrodomésticos — amid the Spanish property crash and appliance-market competition — was the federation's largest failure, though most of its worker-members were relocated within the group.
