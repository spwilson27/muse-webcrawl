Regenerator

A regenerator is a type of heat exchanger in which heat from a hot fluid stream is intermittently stored in a thermal storage medium and then transferred to a cold fluid stream. Unlike a recuperator, in which the two streams flow simultaneously through separate channels, a regenerator alternately passes the hot and cold streams through the same matrix.

Operation

During the hot period, the matrix absorbs heat from the exhaust gas; during the cold period, the incoming fluid (for example combustion air) picks up the stored heat. Flow may be switched by valves, or the matrix itself may rotate slowly between the streams, as in a rotary regenerator or Ljungström air preheater.

Applications

Regenerators are used in blast-furnace hot blast stoves, glass-melting furnaces, open-hearth furnaces, gas turbines (in regenerative cycles), Stirling engines, and cryogenic refrigeration. Regenerative heat exchangers can achieve high effectiveness but involve some carry-over between streams and cyclic temperature variation.
