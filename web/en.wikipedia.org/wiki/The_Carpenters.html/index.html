Carpenters

Origin: Downey, California, U.S.
Years active: 1969–1983
Members: Karen Carpenter, Richard Carpenter
Genre: Pop, soft rock, easy listening

The Carpenters were an American vocal and instrumental duo consisting of siblings Karen (1950–1983) and Richard Carpenter (born 1946). With Karen's distinctive contralto voice and Richard's arrangements and piano playing, they became one of the best-selling music acts of the 1970s, selling more than 90 million records worldwide.

History

The Carpenters signed to A&M Records in 1969. Their breakthrough came in 1970 with the singles "(They Long to Be) Close to You" and "We've Only Just Begun", both major hits written respectively by Burt Bacharach and Hal David, and by Roger Nichols and Paul Williams. Their sound — lush orchestration, overdubbed vocal harmonies and mellow melodies — defined the soft side of 1970s pop and earned them three Grammy Awards, including Best New Artist.

Subsequent hits included "For All We Know", "Rainy Days and Mondays", "Superstar", "Hurting Each Other", "Top of the World", "Yesterday Once More", "Please Mr. Postman" and their cover of Klaatu's "Calling Occupants of Interplanetary Craft". Their albums Close to You (1970), Carpenters (1971) and A Song for You (1972) were commercial and critical successes.

Karen's death and legacy

By the late 1970s, Karen was suffering from anorexia nervosa and Richard from addiction to Quaaludes; the duo's career stalled. Karen Carpenter died on 4 February 1983, aged 32, from heart failure brought on by complications of anorexia — an event that brought widespread public attention to eating disorders. Karen's voice is widely regarded as one of the finest in popular music, praised for its warmth, control and melancholy tone. The duo was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame for recordings including "Close to You".
