HBO

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The HBO logo appearing on screen before a programme, static dissolving into the letters]
The HBO logo appearing on screen before a programme, static dissolving into the letters

Home Box Office (HBO) is an American pay television network owned by Warner Bros. Discovery, launched on 8 November 1972 as the first cable-originated television service. Renowned for prestige original programming — The Sopranos, The Wire, Game of Thrones, Succession — it pioneered the subscription, advertiser-free model whose creative freedom reshaped television; its streaming successor service is branded Max (launched as HBO Max, 2020).

History

HBO began transmitting sports and films by microwave to Pennsylvania cable systems in 1972; the 1975 satellite feed of the 'Thrilla in Manila' made it national. Under slogans like 'It's Not TV. It's HBO.', the network moved from movies and boxing into original series in the 1990s: Oz (1997), Sex and the City (1998) and The Sopranos (1999), whose acclaim established the 'prestige TV' era and the antihero drama.

Peak TV era

The 2000s–2010s brought The Wire, Deadwood, Curb Your Enthusiasm, True Detective and Game of Thrones (2011–19) — the last its biggest hit, with 59 Emmy Awards. HBO's awards dominance and auteur-driven model were imitated by every network and streamer. Time Warner's sale to AT&T (2018) and the WarnerMedia–Discovery merger (2022) brought repeated rebranding of its streaming arm — HBO Max (2020), Max (2023), and an announced return to the HBO Max name in 2025 — widely mocked as corporate indecision.

Business model

HBO has historically sold subscriptions wholesale through cable operators (and direct streaming), spending heavily per hour of programming on a small slate. Its brand equity — quality over quantity — survives as the industry's prestige benchmark even as streaming economics press it toward franchise volume.
