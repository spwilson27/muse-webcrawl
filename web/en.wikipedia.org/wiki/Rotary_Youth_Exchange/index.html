Rotary Youth Exchange

Rotary Youth Exchange (RYE) is a student exchange programme run by Rotary International. It offers secondary-school students, generally aged 15 to 19, the opportunity to study abroad while living with host families and attending a local school. Around 8,000–9,000 students participate each year across more than 80 countries, making it one of the largest school-based exchange programmes in the world.

Programme structure

Exchanges are arranged between Rotary districts in pairs of countries. In a long-term exchange, a student spends a full academic year abroad, typically living with two or three successive host families and attending school full-time. Short-term exchanges last from several weeks to about three months and may take the form of reciprocal family-to-family stays, international camps, or tours.

Students are sponsored by a local Rotary club at home and hosted by a club in the destination country. Host clubs provide a volunteer counsellor for each student. Students pay for travel, insurance, and personal expenses, while hosts provide room, board, and schooling; many students receive a small monthly allowance from the host club.

History

The first Rotary-sponsored exchange took place in 1927, when the Rotary Club of Copenhagen hosted students. The programme expanded after the Second World War as a means of building international understanding, and it was formally adopted and standardised by Rotary International, which in the 2000s introduced mandatory certification and youth-protection training for participating districts.

Culture

Exchange students traditionally wear a blazer — in many countries a distinctive colour, such as dark blue — decorated with pins and patches traded with other students, which becomes a souvenir of the year. Alumni often join Rotex associations of former exchange students, who help orient and support new generations of participants.
