Volga Bulgaria

Volga Bulgaria (or Volga–Kama Bulgaria) was a state formed by the Turkic Bulgars in the Middle Volga region of present-day Russia between the 7th and 13th centuries. It was one of the earliest states to adopt Islam voluntarily (in 922) and a major trading power linking Northern Europe, the Rus', and the Islamic world, until its destruction in the Mongol invasion of 1236.

History

After the collapse of Old Great Bulgaria in the Pontic steppe, one Bulgar group under Kotrag migrated north to the confluence of the Volga and Kama rivers, subduing or allying with local Finno-Ugric tribes. The state grew rich on the fur trade and transit commerce along the Volga route, exporting furs, slaves, wax and honey in exchange for silver from the Islamic world.

In 921 the ruler Almish sent an embassy to the Abbasid Caliph in Baghdad; the famous traveller Ahmad ibn Fadlan accompanied the returning mission and left a celebrated account of the Bulgars and of the Rus' traders he met on the Volga. In 922 Bulgaria officially adopted Islam. Bulgar cities — Bolghar, Bilär, Suvar — had mosques, baths, and stone buildings; Bolghar's ruins are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Fall and legacy

The Bulgars repelled an initial Mongol probe in 1223 but were overwhelmed in 1236; Bilär and Bolghar were destroyed and the territory absorbed into the Golden Horde. The Chuvash people are generally regarded as descendants of the Volga Bulgars, and their language, Chuvash, is the sole living member of the Oghur branch of Turkic.
