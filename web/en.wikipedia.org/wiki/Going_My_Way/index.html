Going My Way


Bing Crosby in Going My Way (1944).

Going My Way is a 1944 American musical comedy-drama film directed by Leo McCarey and starring Bing Crosby and Barry Fitzgerald. It tells the story of a young, unconventional priest assigned to a struggling New York parish, and was the highest-grossing picture of 1944 and the most successful film of Crosby's career.

Plot

Father Charles "Chuck" O'Malley (Crosby), a genial young priest with modern methods and a love of golf and music, is sent to the debt-ridden parish of St. Dominic's in New York, where the elderly Irish pastor, Father Fitzgibbon (Fitzgerald), has served for forty-five years. The two initially clash — O'Malley has in fact been appointed to take charge, a fact he hides out of respect — but the young priest gradually wins over the parish: he reforms a gang of street boys into a choir, helps a runaway girl, and organises a benefit performance to save the church from foreclosure. When the church burns down, O'Malley's choir tour raises the money to rebuild, and Fitzgibbon, his health failing, is reunited with his aged mother brought from Ireland for Christmas — one of the most famously sentimental scenes in American cinema — before O'Malley departs for a new parish.

Reception and legacy

The film was nominated for ten Academy Awards and won seven, including Best Picture, Best Director for McCarey, Best Actor for Crosby, and Best Supporting Actor for Fitzgerald — who was unusually nominated for both Best Actor and Best Supporting Actor for the same role, prompting a rule change. Songs written by Jimmy Van Heusen and Johnny Burke included "Swinging on a Star", which won the Oscar for Best Original Song, and Crosby's renditions of "Ave Maria" and "Too Ra Loo Ra Loo Ral". A sequel, The Bells of St. Mary's (1945), with Crosby reprising O'Malley opposite Ingrid Bergman, was nearly as successful, and the character inspired an American television series in 1962–63.
