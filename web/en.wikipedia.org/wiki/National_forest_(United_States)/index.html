National forest (United States)

A national forest in the United States is a classification of federal lands managed by the United States Forest Service, an agency of the U.S. Department of Agriculture. National forests are large areas of forest and woodland — together with related national grasslands, they comprise about 193 million acres (780,000 km²), roughly 8.5 percent of the total land area of the United States. Unlike national parks, national forests are managed for multiple uses, including timber production, livestock grazing, water, wildlife habitat, and recreation.

History

The system originated with the Forest Reserve Act of 1891, which authorized the President to set aside forest reserves from the public domain. The first reserve was the Yellowstone Park Timber and Land Reserve, established in 1891. The Organic Act of 1897 defined the purposes of the reserves: securing favorable conditions of water flow and furnishing a continuous supply of timber. In 1905, management was transferred to the newly created Forest Service under Gifford Pinchot, and in 1907 the reserves were renamed national forests. The Weeks Act of 1911 allowed the federal government to purchase private land in the East for national forests, greatly expanding the system there.

Management

National forests are administered by forest supervisors and district rangers under the Forest Service's regional structure. Management is guided by the Multiple-Use Sustained-Yield Act of 1960, which recognizes outdoor recreation, range, timber, watershed, and wildlife and fish as coequal uses, and by the National Forest Management Act of 1976, which requires land and resource management plans and public participation. Some areas within national forests are designated as wilderness under the Wilderness Act of 1964 and are subject to stricter protections.

Extent

There are 154 national forests in the United States, found in 40 states. The largest is the Tongass National Forest in Alaska, at about 16.7 million acres, followed by the Chugach National Forest, also in Alaska. Alaska contains the largest share of national forest land, while states such as California, Idaho, and Montana also hold extensive forests. The system also administers 20 national grasslands, mostly on the Great Plains.
