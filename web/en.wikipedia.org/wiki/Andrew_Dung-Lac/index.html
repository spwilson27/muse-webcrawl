Andrew Dung-Lac

Andrew Dung-Lac (Vietnamese: Anrê Dũng-Lạc), born Tran An Dung (c. 1795 – December 21, 1839), was a Vietnamese Catholic priest who was martyred during the persecution of Christians under Emperor Minh Mang. He is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic Church and is the best known of the Vietnamese Martyrs, a group of 117 martyrs canonized by Pope John Paul II in 1988, whose collective feast day is celebrated on November 24.

Life

Tran An Dung was born around 1795 in Bac Ninh in northern Vietnam to a poor non-Christian family. As a boy he was taken in by a Catholic catechist, baptized with the name Andrew, and educated in the faith. He worked as a catechist and teacher before studying theology and being ordained a priest on March 15, 1823. He served in several parishes, adopting the name Dung-Lac, and became known as an exemplary and tireless pastor who lived in poverty and austerity.

Martyrdom

During the persecution ordered by Emperor Minh Mang, Father Dung-Lac ministered in secret. He was first arrested in 1835 but was ransomed by contributions from his parishioners. To avoid further attention he changed his name; he was arrested again on November 10, 1839, together with another Vietnamese priest, Peter Truong Van Thi. This time the ransom arranged for them was refused by the priests themselves, and after imprisonment and torture in Hanoi, both were beheaded on December 21, 1839.

Veneration

Andrew Dung-Lac was beatified by Pope Leo XIII in 1900 and canonized by Pope John Paul II on June 19, 1988, together with 116 other martyrs of Vietnam who died between 1745 and 1862. The group is known as the Vietnamese Martyrs or Martyrs of Vietnam, and their memorial is celebrated on November 24.
