The County of Burgundy (French: Comté de Bourgogne) was a medieval feudal county in what is now the French region of Franche-Comté. It should not be confused with the neighbouring Duchy of Burgundy to the west: the duchy was a fief of the Kingdom of France, whereas the county belonged to the Holy Roman Empire. Because of this imperial allegiance the county was also known as the "Free County" (Franche-Comté), a name that survives in the modern region.

History

The county was formed in 982 by Otto-William, son of Adalbert of Ivrea, who had acquired lands around Dole, Besançon and Salins. In 1032–1034, on the death of Rudolf III of Burgundy, the county passed with the Kingdom of Burgundy to Emperor Conrad II, binding it to the Empire. The counts nevertheless enjoyed wide autonomy, and the county's salt works at Salins-les-Bains made it prosperous.

Through marriage the county passed successively to the houses of Anscarids, Hohenstaufen, Chalon and finally, in 1384, to the Valois dukes of Burgundy, uniting it politically with the duchy. After the death of Charles the Bold in 1477, the county went to the Habsburgs as part of the inheritance of Mary of Burgundy, while the duchy was seized by France. It remained a Habsburg (later Spanish Habsburg) possession until Louis XIV conquered it in 1668 and again in 1674; it was formally ceded to France by the Treaty of Nijmegen in 1678.

Geography

The county occupied the land between the Saône river and the Jura Mountains, corresponding roughly to the modern departments of Doubs, Jura and Haute-Saône. Its capital was Dole, though Besançon, an imperial free city, was its largest urban centre.
