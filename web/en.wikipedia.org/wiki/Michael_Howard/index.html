Michael Howard

Born: 7 July 1941, Swansea, Wales
Offices: Home Secretary (1993–1997); Leader of the Conservative Party (2003–2005)
Later title: Baron Howard of Lympne

Michael Howard, Baron Howard of Lympne (born 7 July 1941) is a British politician who served as Leader of the Conservative Party and Leader of the Opposition from November 2003 to December 2005, and as Home Secretary from 1993 to 1997.

Early life and career

Howard was born in Swansea to Romanian Jewish immigrant parents and read law at Cambridge, where he was president of the Cambridge Union. Called to the Bar in 1964, he became a successful QC. He was elected MP for Folkestone and Hythe in 1983 and rose quickly through the Thatcher governments, holding ministerial posts in employment, the environment and local government — where he was responsible for introducing the community charge (poll tax) legislation — before John Major appointed him Home Secretary in 1993.

Home Secretary

As Home Secretary he took a hard line on crime under the slogan "prison works", overseeing rising prison sentences; in a famous 1997 television interview, Jeremy Paxman asked him twelve times whether he had threatened to overrule the head of the Prison Service. He stood for the party leadership in 1997 but was eliminated in the first ballot.

Party leader

After Iain Duncan Smith was removed in 2003, Howard was elected leader unopposed. He steadied the party, and at the 2005 general election the Conservatives gained 33 seats, though Labour under Tony Blair retained power. Howard announced his resignation the day after the election and was succeeded by David Cameron in December 2005. He left the Commons in 2010 and entered the House of Lords as Baron Howard of Lympne, later moving into business roles, including chairman of an oil company — a position that drew attention during debates over fracking.
