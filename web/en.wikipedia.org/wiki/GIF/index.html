The Graphics Interchange Format (GIF) is a bitmap image format developed by a team at the online services provider CompuServe led by American computer scientist Steve Wilhite; it was released on 15 June 1987. It has since come into widespread usage on the World Wide Web due to its wide support and portability between applications and operating systems.

Characteristics

GIF supports up to 8 bits per pixel for each image, allowing a single image to reference its own palette of up to 256 different colours chosen from the 24-bit RGB colour space. It uses Lempel–Ziv–Welch (LZW) lossless data compression. The GIF89a specification (1989) added support for animation delays, transparent background colours, and text labels; the ability to store multiple images in one file, displayed in sequence, made GIF the standard vehicle for short looping animations and reaction clips on the internet.

Patent history

The LZW algorithm used by GIF was covered by a Unisys patent, and licensing demands in the 1990s spurred development of the patent-free PNG format as an alternative. The relevant patents expired in 2003–2004, after which GIF became free to use everywhere.

Usage and culture

Although technically limited, GIF animations became a staple of web culture, used for memes, reaction images, cinemagraphs, and short demonstrations; many platforms now convert uploaded "GIFs" to silent looping video for efficiency while retaining the name. The pronunciation of the acronym — hard "g" as in "gift" or soft "g" as in "giraffe" — has been a perennial point of debate; Wilhite stated his preference for the soft pronunciation ("JIF").
