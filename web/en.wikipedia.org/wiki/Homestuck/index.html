Homestuck is a webcomic created by Andrew Hussie, serialised on the MS Paint Adventures website from 2009 to 2016. Told through a mixture of static and animated panels, chat logs, Flash animations and playable games, it follows four teenagers who play a reality-altering computer game, Sburb, that triggers the end of the world and a sprawling quest across universes and timelines. It became one of the most famous works of internet fiction, noted for its enormous length, complexity and devoted fandom.

John Egbert, Rose Lalonde, Dave Strider and Jade Harley — the four original protagonists.

Plot

On his thirteenth birthday, John Egbert installs the beta of Sburb, a game that lets players manipulate each other's real environments. Playing it unleashes a meteor apocalypse on Earth, transporting the four friends — John, Rose Lalonde, Dave Strider and Jade Harley — into a game universe, the Medium, where they must build towards a new universe. The story expands enormously: Act 5 introduces twelve alien trolls from the planet Alternia, whose own session and chat-handle conversations (in coloured, quirky-typed text) become central, and later acts add new sets of characters, time travel, alternate timelines and metafictional elements. The villainous Lord English and the long-foreshadowed confrontation drive the endgame, resolved in the animated finale "[S] ACT 7" on 13 April 2016, seven years after launch.

Style and production

Homestuck mimics a text adventure game: pages are captioned with commands (early on, drawn from reader suggestions), dialogue appears as "pesterlogs", and major moments are delivered as "[S]" sound pages — Flash animations set to music by a team of fan-turned-official composers. Hussie produced the comic at extraordinary speed, sometimes updating daily. A 2012 Kickstarter for the adventure game Hiveswap raised about $2.4 million; the game was eventually released in episodes from 2017. Print editions were published by VIZ Media.

Reception and legacy

At its peak Homestuck attracted millions of readers and a huge creative fandom, influencing webcomic form, internet humour and fandom culture; its readership was compared in scale to major media franchises. Critics praised its ambition and multimedia storytelling while noting its formidable complexity. Sequels and continuations — The Homestuck Epilogues (2019) and Homestuck^2: Beyond Canon — followed. Composer Toby Fox, a major contributor to Homestuck's music, later created Undertale.
