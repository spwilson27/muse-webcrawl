Pearl Zane Grey (January 31, 1872 – October 23, 1939), better known by his pen name Zane Grey, was an American author best known for his popular adventure novels and stories associated with the Western genre in literature and the arts. He idealised the American frontier and helped define the myth of the Old West for generations of readers and filmgoers.

Life and career

Grey was born in Zanesville, Ohio, and trained as a dentist, practising in New York City while writing in his spare time. After trips to the American Southwest — including a 1907 expedition to Arizona with the hunter Charles "Buffalo" Jones — he devoted himself to Westerns. His breakthrough novel, Riders of the Purple Sage (1912), became one of the best-selling Westerns of all time. In total he wrote more than 90 books, including 56 Western novels, many published posthumously from his manuscripts.

Angling and legacy

Grey was also one of the world's most celebrated big-game anglers, holding numerous world records for saltwater fish and popularising sport fishing in New Zealand, Australia, and Tahiti. His novels were adapted into over a hundred films, and his works have sold tens of millions of copies. Zane Grey Creek in Florida and numerous landmarks bear his name.
