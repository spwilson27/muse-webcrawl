Peeps

Yellow chick Peeps, the original shape and colour.

Peeps are marshmallow candies sold in the United States and Canada, shaped into chicks, bunnies, and other animals and seasonal forms. They are made from sugar, corn syrup, and gelatin, coated in coloured sugar, and produced by Just Born, a family-owned confectioner based in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania.

History

Just Born acquired the Rodda Candy Company in 1953, gaining its marshmallow chick line; company patriarch Sam Born and his team then mechanised production — the process of forming a chick, originally done by hand with a pastry tube (taking over 27 hours including drying), was cut to about six minutes. The candies became an Easter staple: yellow chicks and pink bunnies remain the classic forms, but the range now includes Halloween, Christmas, and Valentine's shapes, plus flavoured and chocolate-dipped varieties sold year-round.

Cultural presence

Peeps have acquired a cult following beyond the candy aisle. Annual diorama contests — most famously the one long run by The Washington Post — invite elaborate scenes built from the marshmallows, and "Peep jousting" (microwaving two toothpick-armed Peeps until one swells into the other) is a well-known piece of internet folklore. Roughly two billion Peeps are produced each year, the great majority for the Easter season.
