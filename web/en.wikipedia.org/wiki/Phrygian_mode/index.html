Phrygian mode

The Phrygian mode is one of the seven diatonic modes of Western music. On a piano, it corresponds to the scale from E to E using only white keys. It is characterised by its flattened second degree, which gives it a dark, exotic, and often "Spanish" colour.

Structure

The Phrygian mode consists of the interval pattern semitone–tone–tone–tone–semitone–tone–tone. In E Phrygian the notes are E, F, G, A, B, C, D. The semitone between the tonic and the flattened supertonic (E–F) is its most distinctive feature and produces characteristic cadences in flamenco and metal music.

History

The name derives from Phrygia, a kingdom in ancient Anatolia, and was applied by Greek theorists to one of the harmoniai. In medieval church music the third and fourth modes (the authentic and plagal Phrygian modes) were built on E. The mode's association with lament and intensity persisted into later eras.

Modern use

Phrygian harmony is prominent in flamenco (the so-called "Spanish Phrygian" and the Andalusian cadence), in heavy metal, and in film scores evoking the Middle East or ancient settings.
