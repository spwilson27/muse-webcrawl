Jewish Defense League

The Jewish Defense League (JDL) is a Jewish political organisation founded in New York City in 1968 by Rabbi Meir Kahane. Its stated purpose was to protect Jews from antisemitism through militant self-defence, summed up in its slogan "Never Again". The FBI has characterised the JDL as a violent extremist organisation, and it has been linked to bombings, shootings, and plots in the United States.

Founding and early activities

Kahane, a Brooklyn rabbi and editor, founded the JDL amid concern over crime against elderly Jews in poor New York neighbourhoods. Members, often working-class Jewish youths, ran street patrols, escorted worshippers, and confronted Black and Puerto Rican gangs and militants — activities that drew both support and accusations of vigilantism and racism from mainstream Jewish organisations. In the early 1970s the JDL campaigned for the emigration of Soviet Jews, harassing Soviet diplomats and offices; a 1972 bombing of impresario Sol Hurok's office, linked to the JDL milieu, killed an employee, and Kahane was convicted in 1975 of conspiracy to manufacture explosives.

Kahane in Israel

Kahane emigrated to Israel in 1971 and founded the Kach party, which won a Knesset seat in 1984 before being barred in 1988 under anti-racism legislation. He was assassinated in New York in 1990 by El Sayyid Nosair. The JDL in America fractured into rival factions, notably under Irv Rubin, who was charged in 2001 with plotting to bomb a mosque and a congressman's office and died in custody in 2002.

Later activity

Small JDL chapters persisted in Canada, France, and elsewhere; the French Ligue de défense juive has clashed with pro-Palestinian demonstrators and faced dissolution proceedings. In Israel, successor movements of Kahanism have remained politically active under other names. Mainstream Jewish bodies such as the Anti-Defamation League have long condemned the JDL's tactics as counterproductive and extremist.
