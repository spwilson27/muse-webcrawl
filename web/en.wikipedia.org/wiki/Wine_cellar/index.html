Wine cellar

A wine cellar is a room or space, traditionally underground, used for the storage and aging of wine in bottles, barrels, or other vessels. The key properties of a good cellar are darkness, stable cool temperature, moderate humidity, and freedom from vibration.

Conditions

Ideal storage temperatures are generally considered to be around 13 °C (55 °F), with minimal fluctuation. Humidity of roughly 50–70% keeps natural corks from drying out and shrinking, which would admit oxygen and spoil the wine. Ultraviolet light can degrade wine ("light strike"), so cellars are kept dark. Underground cellars in wine regions such as the chalk caves of Champagne and the tuffeau cellars of the Loire Valley naturally provide these conditions.

Active and passive cellars

A passively cooled cellar relies on its underground location and insulation; an actively cooled cellar uses refrigeration and humidification equipment, common in modern homes, restaurants, and wine cabinets. "Cellaring" also describes the practice of holding young wines — especially structured reds such as Bordeaux and Barolo — for years so that tannins soften and complex tertiary aromas develop.
