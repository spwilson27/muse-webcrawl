Job scheduler

A job scheduler manages the unattended execution of background jobs.

A job scheduler is a computer application for controlling unattended background program execution of jobs. This is commonly called job scheduling, to distinguish it from other forms of scheduling. Synonyms include batch system, distributed resource management system (DRMS), distributed resource manager (DRM), and, commonly today, workload automation.

Overview

Job schedulers allow jobs to be queued and executed according to schedules, priorities, resource availability, and dependencies between jobs. They provide features such as job monitoring, error recovery, backfill scheduling, and fair-share policies across users and groups. Basic job scheduling has long been built into operating systems: examples include cron and the Windows Task Scheduler for time-based execution, and batch systems on mainframes dating back to the earliest days of commercial computing.

In high-performance computing

On computer clusters and supercomputers, job schedulers such as Slurm, PBS/Torque, and IBM Spectrum LSF allocate compute nodes to large parallel jobs, enforcing queue limits and priorities. In enterprise settings, workload automation platforms orchestrate complex chains of jobs across many systems, often triggered by events or completion of upstream processing rather than the clock alone.
