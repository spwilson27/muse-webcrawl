Distribution network operator

A distribution network operator (DNO), also known as a distribution system operator (DSO), is the operator of a distribution system: the part of an electricity supply system that carries electricity from the high-voltage transmission system to end customers. DNOs own and operate the towers, cables, and substations that deliver power locally.

Role

In restructured electricity markets, distribution is separated from generation, transmission, and retail supply. The DNO is typically a regulated monopoly for its geographic area: it maintains the network, connects new customers and generators, restores supply after faults, and charges suppliers for use of its wires — charges that appear within customers' electricity bills.

Great Britain

In Great Britain there are a number of DNOs, each responsible for a distribution licence area inherited from the former regional electricity boards. They are regulated by Ofgem under price-control frameworks (such as RIIO) that set allowed revenue and performance incentives. Independent distribution network operators (IDNOs) build and run smaller networks, often for new housing developments.

Changing functions

The growth of rooftop solar, batteries, electric vehicles, and other distributed energy resources is pushing DNOs toward an active "distribution system operator" role, managing local flexibility and congestion rather than merely fitting demand to a passive network.
