Bridgestone

Bridgestone Corporation is a Japanese multinational manufacturing corporation founded in 1931 by Shojiro Ishibashi in Kurume, Japan. It is the world's largest manufacturer of tyres by revenue, and also produces diversified products including industrial rubber, bicycles, and sporting goods. The name Bridgestone is a loose English rendering of the founder's surname Ishibashi, meaning "stone bridge."

History

Shojiro Ishibashi began in the traditional footwear business, producing tabi socks and rubber-soled work shoes, before founding Bridgestone Tire Co. in 1931. The company expanded internationally after World War II, opening overseas plants from the 1960s. In 1988 Bridgestone acquired the American Firestone Tire and Rubber Company for about $2.6 billion, transforming itself into a genuinely global manufacturer with a major North American footprint. Its American subsidiary is headquartered in Nashville, Tennessee.

Motorsport and products

Bridgestone supplied tyres to Formula One from 1997 to 2010, serving as sole supplier in its final seasons, and long supplied the IndyCar Series under the Firestone brand. Its tyre brands include Bridgestone, Firestone, Dayton, and Fuzion, and its bicycle division sells under the Bridgestone and Anchor names. The company's Turanza, Potenza, Dueler, and Blizzak lines are among its best-known tyre families.
