Beatus of Liébana (c. 730 – c. 800) was a monk and theologian from the Kingdom of Asturias, associated with the monastery of Santo Toribio de Liébana in present-day Cantabria, northern Spain. He is famous for his Commentary on the Apocalypse, one of the most influential works of early medieval Spain.

Commentary on the Apocalypse

Compiled around 776 and revised in later editions, the commentary gathers extracts from earlier Church Fathers to interpret the Book of Revelation. Surviving illuminated copies, made in Mozarabic monasteries from the tenth century onward, are known collectively as the "Beatus manuscripts" and are celebrated for their vivid, distinctive miniatures in bold colour. Around two dozen complete or fragmentary copies survive.

Adoptionism controversy

Beatus was a leading opponent of Adoptionism, the doctrine taught by Elipandus, Archbishop of Toledo, that Christ as man was the adoptive son of God. With Etherius, bishop of Osma, Beatus wrote the Adversus Elipandum attacking the doctrine, which was condemned by church councils. Beatus may also have promoted the apostolic tradition of Saint James in Spain and is sometimes linked to the early cult of Santiago de Compostela.
