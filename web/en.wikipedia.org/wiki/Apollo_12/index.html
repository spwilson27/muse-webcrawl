Apollo 12

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Apollo 12
CrewCharles Conrad, Richard Gordon, Alan Bean
Launch14 November 1969
Landing siteOcean of Storms
Duration10 days
Intrepid and Surveyor 3.

Apollo 12 was the sixth crewed mission of the Apollo program and the second to land on the Moon, launched on 14 November 1969 in a rainstorm. Thirty-six seconds after liftoff, lightning struck the Saturn V — then struck again — knocking the command module's electrical systems offline and filling the cabin with warning lights. Flight controller John Aaron recognised the telemetry pattern from a test a year earlier and called "SCE to AUX", a switch flip that saved the launch and became mission-control legend.

Commander Charles 'Pete' Conrad and Alan Bean then landed the LM Intrepid with pinpoint precision in the Ocean of Storms, within walking distance of the Surveyor 3 probe that had landed in 1967 — proving Apollo could land exactly where planners wanted, the capability the science missions required. They retrieved pieces of Surveyor, including its camera, for return to Earth.

Character

Where Apollo 11 was tense and terse, Apollo 12 was exuberant: Conrad, stepping onto the surface, joked about his short stature ('Whoopee! Man, that may have been a small one for Neil, but that's a long one for me'), and the crew's laughter runs through the transcripts. The mission's precision landing opened the era of targeted lunar science that Apollo 14 and the J-missions exploited.
