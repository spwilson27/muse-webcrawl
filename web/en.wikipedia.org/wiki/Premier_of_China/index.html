The premier of China, officially the Premier of the State Council of the People's Republic of China (中华人民共和国国务院总理), is the head of government of China and leader of the State Council, the country's chief administrative authority. The premier is formally nominated by the president and approved by the National People's Congress (NPC). In practice, the premier is chosen through deliberations within the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), and typically also serves as a member of the Politburo Standing Committee, ranking second or third in the party hierarchy.

Functions and powers

The premier directs the work of the State Council, convenes and presides over its executive and plenary meetings, and oversees the ministries, commissions, the People's Bank of China and the National Audit Office. The premier signs State Council administrative regulations and orders, proposes the nominees for vice-premiers, state councillors and ministerial posts for NPC approval, and bears primary responsibility for economic administration. Since the reform era the premier has usually been the leadership's foremost figure on macroeconomic policy.

Term and selection

Under the 1982 Constitution, the premier serves a five-year term and may serve no more than two consecutive terms. The incumbent premier, Li Qiang, assumed office on 11 March 2023, succeeding Li Keqiang. The premier must be a citizen of at least 45 years of age.

List of premiers

  • Zhou Enlai (1949–1976) — first premier of the People's Republic, served until his death in office.
  • Hua Guofeng (1976–1980)
  • Zhao Ziyang (1980–1987)
  • Li Peng (acting 1987–1988; premier 1988–1998)
  • Zhu Rongji (1998–2003)
  • Wen Jiabao (2003–2013)
  • Li Keqiang (2013–2023)
  • Li Qiang (2023–present)

Republic of China

A separate premiership existed under the Republic of China before 1949 (held among others by Tang Shaoyi, the first premier in 1912, and by Chiang Kai-shek); since 1949 the premier of the Republic of China government on Taiwan is styled President of the Executive Yuan and is a distinct office from the premier of the People's Republic described here.
