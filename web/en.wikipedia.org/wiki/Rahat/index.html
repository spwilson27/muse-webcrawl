Rahat (Arabic: رهط, Hebrew: רַהַט) is an Arab Bedouin city in the Southern District of Israel. It is the largest Bedouin settlement in Israel and the only Bedouin locality with the status of a city. Located in the northern Negev desert near Beersheba, Rahat was founded in 1972 as part of a government program to settle the formerly nomadic Negev Bedouin in permanent townships.

History

The area around Rahat has archaeological remains dating to earlier periods, including a Byzantine-era site and an early Islamic farm whose mosque, discovered in the 2010s, is among the earliest known rural mosques in the region. The modern city was established in 1972 and initially named Al-Hazil before being renamed Rahat, from the Arabic word for "relief" or "tranquillity". It received city status in 1994.

Demographics and society

Rahat's residents are overwhelmingly Negev Bedouin, descended from tribes that moved into permanent housing from the surrounding desert. The population is young and has grown rapidly. The city struggles with high rates of poverty and unemployment relative to the Israeli average, though it has developed commercial areas, industrial zones, and public institutions including schools, clinics, and a cultural center.

Economy

Many residents work in construction and services in nearby Beersheba and other towns. The Idan HaNegev industrial park, shared with neighboring Jewish localities, was developed to increase local employment.
