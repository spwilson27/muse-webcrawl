Suomenlinna

Suomenlinna (Finnish: [ˈsuo̯menˌlinːɑ]; until 1918 Viapori, in Swedish Sveaborg) is a sea fortress built on six islands off Helsinki, Finland. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the country's most popular visitor attractions, as well as a district of Helsinki home to around 800 residents.

History

Construction began in 1748, when Finland was part of the Kingdom of Sweden. After Sweden's defeats in wars against Russia, the fortress of Sveaborg ("Fortress of Sweden") was built under the direction of Augustin Ehrensvärd to defend Helsinki and the Finnish coast against Russian expansion. It was one of the largest dry docks of its time and a major naval base, at one point rivalling the city it protected in population.

During the Finnish War of 1808, the fortress controversially surrendered to Russian forces, and Finland passed to the Russian Empire. Suomenlinna then served as a Russian naval base for over a century. It was bombarded by Anglo-French fleets during the Crimean War in 1855. After Finnish independence, the fortress was renamed Suomenlinna ("Finland's Castle") in 1918. During the Finnish Civil War it housed a prison camp. In the Second World War it served coastal defence and anti-aircraft roles, and Finland's submarine Vesikko, now a museum ship, was based there.

Geography and sights

The fortress spans the islands of Kustaanmiekka, Susisaari, Iso Mustasaari, Pikku Mustasaari and Länsi-Mustasaari, connected by bridges. Notable sites include the King's Gate, the great courtyard with Ehrensvärd's tomb, the Suomenlinna church (originally built for a Russian garrison), museums, and the dry dock, among the oldest still in use. The islands are reached by ferry from Helsinki's Market Square and are car-free beyond service traffic.
