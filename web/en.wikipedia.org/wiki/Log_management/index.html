Log management

Log management is the process of collecting, storing, processing, analysing and disposing of the log data generated by operating systems, applications, servers, network devices and other components of an information system. Logs record events such as user logins, system errors, transactions and security-relevant activity, and they are a primary source of evidence for troubleshooting, auditing, compliance and security monitoring.

Process

A typical log management pipeline consists of several stages:

  • Collection — agents or protocols such as syslog gather log messages from distributed sources and forward them to a central repository.
  • Transport and ingestion — messages are transmitted, often over encrypted channels, and normalised into a common format.
  • Storage and retention — logs are indexed and archived according to retention policies driven by operational needs and regulatory requirements.
  • Analysis — search, correlation, dashboards and alerting turn raw events into actionable information; security information and event management (SIEM) systems specialise in security analysis.
  • Disposal — logs are securely deleted when their retention period expires.

Challenges

Modern infrastructures can produce terabytes of log data per day, so log management must deal with volume, inconsistent message formats, clock synchronisation between sources, and protecting logs from tampering, since attackers often attempt to erase records of their activity. Standards such as syslog (RFC 5424) and structured formats like JSON logging help to make machine-generated logs consistent and searchable.
