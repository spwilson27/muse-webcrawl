Crantor

Crantor (Greek: Κράντωρ; c. 335 – c. 275 BC) was a Greek philosopher of the Old Academy, a pupil of Xenocrates and a fellow-student of Polemon. A native of Soli in Cilicia, he moved to Athens to study at Plato's Academy and was the first commentator on Plato's works, writing an influential commentary on the Timaeus.

Works

Crantor's most celebrated work was the treatise On Grief, addressed to his friend Hippocles on the death of his children. It became a model for the ancient genre of consolation literature, was praised by Cicero — who drew on it in his own Consolatio — and was later adapted by the Middle Platonist Plutarch in his Consolation to Apollonius. Cicero called Crantor's book a "little golden work" worth learning by heart. None of Crantor's writings survive intact; they are known through quotations in later authors.

Crantor is also associated with transmitting the story of Atlantis as a historical account, citing Egyptian records, in his Timaeus commentary as reported by Proclus.
