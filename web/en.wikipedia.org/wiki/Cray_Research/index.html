Cray Research

Cray Research, Inc., often shortened to Cray, was an American manufacturer of supercomputers founded in 1972 by computer designer Seymour Cray. The company, headquartered in Chippewa Falls, Wisconsin, defined the supercomputer industry for two decades with its vector-processing machines.

History

Seymour Cray left Control Data Corporation in 1972 to found Cray Research, aiming to build the fastest computers in the world. The company's first product, the Cray-1 (1976), was a landmark design: a cylindrical, elegantly packaged vector processor capable of around 160 MFLOPS — unprecedented at the time — and it became the most famous supercomputer ever built. It was followed by the Cray X-MP (1982), the first Cray multiprocessor, and the Cray-2 (1985), a compact, liquid-immersion-cooled machine with enormous memory.

In 1989 Seymour Cray left to form Cray Computer Corporation, while Cray Research developed the massively parallel Cray T3D and T3E systems and the Cray J90. After financial difficulties, Cray Research was acquired by Silicon Graphics in 1996; its remains were sold to Tera Computer in 2000, which renamed itself Cray Inc. That successor company built systems such as the Cray XT and XK series and the Shasta architecture, and was acquired by Hewlett Packard Enterprise in 2019 for $1.3 billion, with the Cray brand continuing within HPE.

Legacy

Cray machines were used for weather forecasting, nuclear weapons simulation, aerodynamics and scientific research at national laboratories and universities. The name "Cray" became nearly synonymous with supercomputing, and Seymour Cray is remembered as the "father of supercomputing".
