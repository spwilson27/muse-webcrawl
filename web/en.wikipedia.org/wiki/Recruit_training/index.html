Recruit training

Image placeholder: recruits undergoing basic training.

Recruit training, commonly known as basic training or boot camp, is the initial instruction given to new military personnel. It is a physically and psychologically intensive process designed to transform civilians into soldiers, sailors, airmen or marines who can function effectively within a military organisation.

Overview

Recruit training typically lasts from several weeks to several months depending on the service and country. It combines physical fitness training, weapons handling, drill, fieldcraft, first aid and instruction in military law, customs and values. A central aim is resocialisation: recruits learn to subordinate individual preferences to unit cohesion and to respond promptly to orders under stress.

By country

In the United States, each service runs its own programme: the Army's Basic Combat Training, the Marine Corps' recruit training at Parris Island and San Diego, the Navy's Recruit Training Command at Great Lakes, and the Air Force's Basic Military Training at Lackland. The United Kingdom conducts initial training at establishments such as Catterick and Pirbright for the Army, HMS Raleigh for the Royal Navy and RAF Halton for the Royal Air Force. Many other states operate comparable systems, often linked to conscription.

Training methods

Instruction is delivered by drill instructors or drill sergeants in a highly structured environment with strict schedules, inspections and progressive standards. Training culminates in exercises that test the skills learned, after which graduates proceed to specialised or advanced individual training in their chosen trade or branch.
