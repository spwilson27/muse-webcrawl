The Crown is a historical drama television series about the reign of Queen Elizabeth II, created and principally written by Peter Morgan and produced by Left Bank Pictures and Sony Pictures Television for Netflix. It ran for six seasons and 60 episodes from 2016 to 2023.

The series dramatizes the Queen's life from her marriage to Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, in 1947 through to events of the early twenty-first century. The principal cast was replaced every two seasons: Claire Foy (seasons 1–2), Olivia Colman (seasons 3–4), and Imelda Staunton (seasons 5–6) portrayed the Queen at successive stages of her life, with Matt Smith, Tobias Menzies, and Jonathan Pryce as Prince Philip.

Seasons overview

Season 1 covers the death of George VI, Elizabeth's accession and coronation, and the early years of her reign, including the resignation of Winston Churchill. Season 2 treats the Suez Crisis, the Profumo affair, and the births of Princes Andrew and Edward. Seasons 3 and 4 introduce Margaret Thatcher (Gillian Anderson) and Diana, Princess of Wales (Emma Corrin), covering the Falklands War and the breakdown of Charles and Diana's marriage. Seasons 5 and 6 depict the "annus horribilis" of 1992, the death of Diana (Elizabeth Debicki), the Golden Jubilee, and the early relationship of Prince William and Catherine Middleton.

Reception

The Crown has been praised for its acting, direction, writing, cinematography, and production values, though its historical liberties have drawn criticism, including calls for a fictionalization disclaimer. The series won numerous awards, including 21 Primetime Emmy Awards from its first five seasons, sweeping all seven drama categories at the 73rd Primetime Emmy Awards in 2021, and Golden Globe Awards for Best Television Series – Drama in 2016 and 2020.
