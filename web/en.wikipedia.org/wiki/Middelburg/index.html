Middelburg is a city and municipality in the southwestern Netherlands, capital of the province of Zeeland. It is situated on the Walcheren peninsula, near the coast of the North Sea. Founded around a 9th-century ring castle (burg) — the "middle burg" between settlements to the north and south — it received city rights in 1217 and grew into one of the most important trading cities of the Dutch Republic.

History

During the Dutch Golden Age, Middelburg was the second most important city of the Dutch East India Company (VOC), which had one of its six chambers there, and it was also a centre of the Dutch West India Company and the transatlantic slave trade — a history the city has increasingly acknowledged in public memorials. The city was heavily damaged in May 1940 during the Battle of Zeeland, and its centre was rebuilt in traditional style after the war, including the late-Gothic town hall on the market square. The Lange Jan ("Long John") abbey tower, 90.5 metres tall, dominates the skyline.

Present day

Today Middelburg is a regional administrative and educational centre, home to University College Roosevelt, a liberal arts college affiliated with Utrecht University. Its canals, merchant houses and the Zeeuws Museum attract visitors, and the city lies within the protected urban landscape of Walcheren.
