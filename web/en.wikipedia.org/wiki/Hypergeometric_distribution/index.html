In probability theory and statistics, the hypergeometric distribution is a discrete probability distribution that describes the probability of k successes in n draws without replacement from a finite population of size N containing exactly K objects with a given feature. It differs from the binomial distribution in that the draws are not independent — each draw changes the composition of the remaining population.

Definition

The probability mass function is

P(X = k) = [ C(K, k) · C(N − K, n − k) ] / C(N, n),

where C(a, b) denotes a binomial coefficient. The mean is nK/N and the variance includes the finite population correction factor (N − n)/(N − 1).

Applications

The distribution underlies Fisher's exact test for contingency tables, capture–recapture estimates of wildlife populations, quality-control sampling (such as acceptance sampling of lots), and calculations of lottery and card-game odds — for example, the probability of being dealt a certain number of aces from a shuffled deck. When the population is large relative to the sample, the hypergeometric distribution is well approximated by the binomial distribution.
