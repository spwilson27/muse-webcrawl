De Klassieker

A packed stadium during De Klassieker, the fiercest rivalry in Dutch football.

De Klassieker (Dutch for "The Classic") is the main football rivalry in the Netherlands, contested between AFC Ajax of Amsterdam and Feyenoord of Rotterdam. It is regarded as one of the most intense derbies in European football, reflecting the long-standing cultural and civic rivalry between the country's two largest cities.

The fixture dates back to the early twentieth century; the first official meeting between the clubs took place on 9 October 1921 in Rotterdam and ended in a draw. Since the founding of the Eredivisie in 1956, the two clubs — together with PSV Eindhoven — have dominated Dutch football, and meetings between Ajax and Feyenoord traditionally draw the largest television audiences of the Dutch season.

History

Amsterdam and Rotterdam have been rivals for centuries, competing as centres of trade, culture and industry. Ajax, founded in 1900, came to be associated with an elegant, attacking style of play and the working of the "Total Football" philosophy of the 1970s, while Feyenoord, founded in 1908, built its identity around hard work, physical commitment and its famously loyal supporters, known as Het Legioen ("The Legion").

The rivalry produced some of the most memorable matches in Dutch football history, including Ajax's 8–2 victory in 1983, a match notable for Johan Cruyff — having controversially transferred from Ajax to Feyenoord — playing against his former club. Matches have occasionally been marred by crowd trouble, leading to restrictions on away supporters and heightened security measures.

Both clubs have won the European Cup, Feyenoord in 1970 and Ajax in 1971, 1972, 1973 and 1995, and both have lifted the UEFA Cup. The clubs' stadiums, the Johan Cruyff Arena in Amsterdam and De Kuip in Rotterdam, are among the largest in the Netherlands.
