Tetris

Developer: Alexey Pajitnov (original)

First release: 6 June 1984

Genre: Puzzle, tile-matching

Platforms: Electronika 60, IBM PC, Game Boy, and numerous others

Tetris (Russian: Тетрис) is a puzzle video game created in 1984 by Soviet software engineer Alexey Pajitnov. It has been published by several companies on more than 65 platforms, setting a Guinness World Record for the most ported game. Tetris is a tile-matching game in which the player must place falling geometric shapes, called tetrominoes, into complete horizontal lines; when a line is completed it disappears and any blocks above it fall.

Gameplay

The primary objective is to place the tetrominoes — geometric shapes composed of four square blocks each — by moving them sideways and rotating them in quarter turns, creating a horizontal line of ten units without gaps. When such a line is completed, it disappears and any blocks above it fall, uncovering more space. As the game progresses the tetrominoes fall faster, and the game ends when the stack of tetrominoes reaches the top of the playing field.

There are seven distinct tetrominoes, each named after a letter of the alphabet that it resembles: I, O, T, S, Z, J, and L. Clearing multiple lines simultaneously awards more points, with clearing four lines at once known as a "Tetris".

History

Pajitnov created Tetris while working at the Dorodnitsyn Computing Centre of the Soviet Academy of Sciences in Moscow. The first playable version was completed on 6 June 1984 for the Electronika 60 terminal computer. The name derives from the Greek numerical prefix tetra- (all pieces contain four segments) and tennis, Pajitnov's favorite sport.

The game was ported to the IBM PC by Vadim Gerasimov, a 16-year-old school student, and spread through Moscow and then the wider world. Rights to the game became the subject of a complex legal dispute involving the Soviet state agency ELORG, Nintendo, Atari Games, and Mirrorsoft. The 1989 Game Boy version, bundled with the handheld console, became one of the best-selling video games of all time and is widely credited with popularizing Tetris globally.

Legacy

Tetris has sold hundreds of millions of copies across all platforms and is consistently ranked among the greatest video games ever made. The "Tetris effect" — in which people who play the game for extended periods begin to see falling shapes in their mind's eye and in dreams — is named after it. Competitive Tetris, particularly the Classic Tetris World Championship played on the NES version, has grown into a substantial esports scene, and modern releases such as Tetris Effect (2018) and Tetris 99 (2019) have kept the formula current.
