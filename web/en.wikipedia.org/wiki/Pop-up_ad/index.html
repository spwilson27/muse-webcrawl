Pop-up ad

An example of a pop-up advertisement window.

Pop-up ads or pop-ups are forms of online advertising on the World Wide Web. A pop-up is a graphical user interface display area, usually a small window, that suddenly appears ("pops up") in the foreground of the visual interface in a web browser.

History

Pop-up ads were invented by Ethan Zuckerman at Tripod.com in the mid-1990s, as a way to display advertisements in a separate window so that an ad on a page would not imply the page owner's endorsement of the advertised product. Zuckerman later publicly apologised for the format. Pop-ups proliferated on the late-1990s web, often generated by JavaScript when a page loaded or was closed.

Variants

  • Pop-under ads open behind the active window and are seen when the window is closed.
  • Hover ads and interstitials are rendered within the page itself using dynamic HTML.
  • Exit-intent pop-ups appear when the user's cursor moves toward leaving the page, often used for newsletter sign-ups.

Blocking

Because of their intrusiveness and association with scams and malware, pop-ups provoked a strong backlash. Pop-up blockers became a standard feature of web browsers in the early 2000s — Internet Explorer added one in 2004 — and most browsers now block unsolicited pop-up windows by default. Search engines also penalise pages with intrusive interstitials.
