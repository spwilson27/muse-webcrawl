West Dunbartonshire (Scots: Wast Dunbairtonshire; Scottish Gaelic: Siorrachd Dhùn Breatann an Iar) is one of the 32 local government council areas of Scotland. It lies to the north-west of Glasgow, bordering Argyll and Bute, East Dunbartonshire, Renfrewshire and Stirling. The administrative centre is Dumbarton, and the largest settlements include Clydebank, Alexandria, Balloch and Bonhill.

Geography

The area stretches from the north bank of the River Clyde at Clydebank through Dumbarton to the southern shore of Loch Lomond. Dumbarton Rock, a volcanic plug crowned by Dumbarton Castle, is the area's most recognisable landmark. The Vale of Leven runs north from Dumbarton to Loch Lomond at Balloch.

History

The council area was created in 1996 from the former Dumbarton district (excluding the Helensburgh area, which went to Argyll and Bute) together with Clydebank district. The region has a strong industrial heritage: Clydebank was a major shipbuilding centre, home to the John Brown & Company yard that built liners including the Queen Mary, and Singer's sewing machine factory.

Governance

West Dunbartonshire Council is the local authority. The area is represented in the Scottish Parliament by the Dumbarton and Clydebank and Strathkelvin constituencies, and in the UK Parliament by the West Dunbartonshire constituency.
