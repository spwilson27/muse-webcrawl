Pharmaceutical care

Pharmaceutical care is a patient-centred philosophy of pharmacy practice in which the pharmacist takes responsibility for a patient's medication-related needs and works with the patient and other healthcare professionals to achieve definite outcomes that improve the patient's quality of life. The concept was formalised by Charles Hepler and Linda Strand in 1990, arguing that pharmacists should go beyond dispensing to prevent and resolve drug therapy problems.

Process

In the pharmaceutical care process, the pharmacist assesses the patient's medication therapy, identifies actual or potential drug therapy problems — such as unnecessary therapy, ineffective drugs, dosage too low or too high, adverse drug reactions, and non-adherence — develops a care plan, implements interventions, and follows up to evaluate outcomes. It is closely related to medicines optimisation and to structured services such as medication therapy management and medicines use reviews.

Practice

Pharmaceutical care underpins modern clinical pharmacy in hospitals and community settings, supporting roles for pharmacists in chronic disease management, anticoagulation and vaccination clinics, and prescribing support. Studies have associated pharmacist-led medication review with improved medication appropriateness and reduced adverse drug events, though the model requires documentation, communication with prescribers and time protected from dispensing duties.
