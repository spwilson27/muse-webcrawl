Rhodamine

Rhodamines are a family of synthetic dyes based on the xanthene core structure, closely related to fluorescein. They are characteristically bright pink to red in colour and strongly fluorescent, which makes them valuable in scientific and industrial applications.

Members and properties

Well-known members include rhodamine B, rhodamine 6G, rhodamine 123, and the sulforhodamines. Rhodamine 6G is widely used as a laser dye and as a tracer in fluorescence microscopy. Texas Red, a sulforhodamine derivative, is a common fluorescent label for proteins and nucleic acids.

Uses

Rhodamines are used as water tracers to determine flow rates and directions, as biological stains, in fluorescent probes and sensors, and historically in inks and colourants. Rhodamine B was once used to colour paper and textiles but its use has been restricted in many places because of toxicity concerns; it is not permitted as a food colouring.
