Fake news

[Image: A phone screen of fabricated headlines being shared on social media]

Fake news is false or misleading information presented as news, spread for political, financial or malicious ends. The term surged during the 2016 US presidential election to describe wholly fabricated stories (often produced for advertising revenue or influence operations), and was subsequently applied — contentiously — to mainstream reporting politicians dislike, diluting its meaning.

History and types

Fabricated news long predates the internet — from the Great Moon Hoax of 1835 to wartime propaganda. Scholars distinguish misinformation (false, unintentional), disinformation (false, deliberate) and malinformation, and study formats from invented stories to manipulated images and deepfakes.

Responses

Fact-checking organisations, platform policies, media-literacy education and regulation (such as the EU's Digital Services Act) are the main institutional responses; researchers debate their effectiveness and free-speech trade-offs.
