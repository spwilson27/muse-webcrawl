Piedmont wine

RegionPiedmont, north-west Italy
Key grapesNebbiolo, Barbera, Dolcetto, Moscato, Arneis, Cortese
Famous winesBarolo, Barbaresco, Asti, Gavi, Barbera d'Asti

Piedmont wine is the wine produced in Piedmont, a region in north-western Italy at the foot of the Alps (the name means "foot of the mountain"). Piedmont is one of Italy's most prestigious wine regions, producing a disproportionate share of the country's DOCG wines, most famously the great reds Barolo and Barbaresco made from the Nebbiolo grape in the Langhe hills.

Geography and grapes

The region is sheltered by the Alps and the Apennines, with a continental climate, foggy autumns (the fog, nebbia, gives Nebbiolo its name) and calcareous marl soils. Red grape varieties dominate: besides Nebbiolo, the widely planted Barbera produces Barbera d'Asti and Barbera d'Alba; Dolcetto makes soft, early-drinking reds. Important whites include Moscato Bianco, used for the sparkling Asti and Moscato d'Asti, Cortese (Gavi), Arneis (Roero Arneis) and Erbaluce.

Major wines

Barolo, produced in and around the town of Barolo from 100% Nebbiolo, must be aged at least 38 months and is known as "the king of wines"; Barbaresco, from the neighbouring communes, is somewhat lighter and matures earlier. In 2014 the vineyard landscapes of Langhe-Roero and Monferrato were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The region is also the home of vermouth, created in Turin in 1786 by Antonio Benedetto Carpano.
