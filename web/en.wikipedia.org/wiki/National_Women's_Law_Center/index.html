National Women's Law Center

The National Women's Law Center (NWLC) is a United States non-profit organization founded in 1972 to protect and advance the progress of women and girls, with an emphasis on low-income women and their families. Based in Washington, D.C., the Center works through litigation, policy advocacy, research, and public education on issues including education, employment, health care, reproductive rights, and income security.

History

The NWLC began in 1972 as the women's rights project of the Center for Law and Social Policy, founded by Marcia Greenberger, who later became its co-president and president. It became an independent organization in 1981. Greenberger led the Center for decades; Fatima Goss Graves became president and CEO in 2017.

Work

The Center played a significant role in enforcing and expanding Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972, particularly in athletics and sexual harassment in schools. It has litigated and advocated on pay equity, child care funding, abortion rights, and the rights of women in the military. In 2017, the NWLC established the Time's Up Legal Defense Fund, administered with the National Women's Law Center Fund, to connect workers facing workplace sexual harassment with attorneys and public relations assistance.
