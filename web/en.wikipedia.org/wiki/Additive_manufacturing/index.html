Additive manufacturing

Additive manufacturing, commonly known as 3D printing, is the construction of a three-dimensional object from a CAD model or digital 3D model by successively adding material layer by layer.

Processes

Processes include fused deposition modelling (material extrusion), stereolithography and vat photopolymerisation, powder bed fusion such as selective laser melting, material jetting, binder jetting, and directed energy deposition. ISO/ASTM standards group them into seven categories.

Applications

Uses range from rapid prototyping and tooling to production parts in aerospace, medicine (implants, prosthetics, dental work), automotive and construction, including printed buildings.

History

Early equipment emerged in the 1980s with stereolithography patented by Chuck Hull (1984). Expiring patents in the 2000s spurred low-cost desktop printers and the RepRap movement.
