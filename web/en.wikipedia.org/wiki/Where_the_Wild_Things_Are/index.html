Where the Wild Things Are

Author/illustrator: Maurice Sendak
Published: 1963
Publisher: Harper & Row
Awards: Caldecott Medal (1964)

Where the Wild Things Are is a 1963 children's picture book by American writer and illustrator Maurice Sendak, published by Harper & Row. It won the 1964 Caldecott Medal and is regarded as one of the most influential picture books ever published.

Plot

One night, after making mischief in his wolf suit, young Max is sent to bed without supper. In his imagination his bedroom transforms into a jungle, and he sails away "in and out of weeks and almost over a year" to the land of the Wild Things — huge, roaring monsters who make him their king and join him in a "wild rumpus". Eventually homesick and lonely, Max gives up the kingdom and sails home, where he finds his supper waiting for him, "and it was still hot". The book contains only 338 words and is celebrated for its crosshatched illustrations and its frank depiction of childhood anger and fantasy.

Adaptations

The book has been adapted as an animated short (1973), an opera by Oliver Knussen (premiered 1980), and a feature film directed by Spike Jonze in 2009.
