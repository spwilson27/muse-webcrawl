Wind power in Brazil is one of the fastest-growing sources of electricity in the country. Brazil has among the best wind resources in the world, particularly along the north-eastern coast, where steady trade winds blow with exceptional consistency and capacity factors frequently exceed 40–50%, roughly double the world average. By the mid-2020s installed capacity had passed 29 gigawatts, making wind the second-largest source of electricity in Brazil after hydroelectricity and placing Brazil among the top ten wind-power nations worldwide.

History

The first Brazilian wind turbines were installed in the 1990s on the island of Fernando de Noronha and in Ceará. Large-scale development began after the federal PROINFA programme of 2004, which guaranteed purchase of renewable energy, and accelerated with competitive energy auctions from 2009 onwards. Annual installations grew rapidly through the 2010s as turbine prices fell and a domestic manufacturing industry developed, with factories in Bahia, Ceará, and Pernambuco.

Geography

Most capacity is concentrated in the Northeast Region, especially Rio Grande do Norte, Bahia, Ceará, and Piauí, where the wind regime is strongly complementary to hydroelectric generation: winds are strongest in the dry season, when river flows and reservoir levels are lowest. The southern state of Rio Grande do Sul also hosts major wind farms, including the Osório complex. Offshore wind, with a technical potential estimated in the hundreds of gigawatts, began to be studied in earnest in the 2020s.

Outlook

Wind energy is central to Brazilian plans for a low-carbon power sector and for supplying green hydrogen production in the north-east. Grid integration and transmission bottlenecks from the interior north-east to the south-east demand centres remain the main challenges.
