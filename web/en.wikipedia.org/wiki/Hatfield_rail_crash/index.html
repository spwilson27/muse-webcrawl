Hatfield rail crash

Date: 17 October 2000
Location: Hatfield, Hertfordshire, England
Deaths: 4; Injuries: ~70
Cause: Broken rail (gauge corner cracking / rolling contact fatigue)

The Hatfield rail crash occurred on 17 October 2000 when a London King's Cross to Leeds express operated by Great North Eastern Railway derailed south of Hatfield station at about 115 mph (185 km/h) after passing over a rail that shattered due to rolling contact fatigue. Four people were killed and about 70 injured.

Cause and aftermath

The broken rail had been identified months earlier but a replacement rail was not installed and no speed restriction imposed. The crash undermined confidence in privatised infrastructure manager Railtrack, led to nationwide emergency speed restrictions and chaos, and contributed to Railtrack entering administration in 2001 and its replacement by Network Rail.

Prosecutions

Network Rail (as successor) and contractor Balfour Beatty were fined for health and safety breaches; manslaughter charges against individuals were dropped or resulted in acquittals.
