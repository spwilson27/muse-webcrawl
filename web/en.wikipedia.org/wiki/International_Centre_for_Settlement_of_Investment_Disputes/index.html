International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes

The International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID) is an international arbitration institution, part of the World Bank Group, established in 1966 by the ICSID Convention to settle disputes between states and foreign investors.

ICSID
Established1966
HeadquartersWashington, D.C., US
ParentWorld Bank Group

Function

ICSID provides facilities for conciliation and arbitration of investor–state disputes (ISDS) arising under treaties and contracts; awards are enforceable as final judgments in member states. It does not decide cases itself — tribunals of appointed arbitrators do.

Criticism

ISDS has drawn criticism over sovereignty, regulatory chill and inconsistency, prompting reform efforts at UNCITRAL and ICSID rule updates in 2022. Bolivia, Ecuador and Venezuela withdrew from the Convention in 2007–2012.
