Contributory copyright infringement

Contributory copyright infringement is a form of secondary liability for copyright infringement in United States law. A party is liable for contributory infringement when it, with knowledge of the infringing activity, induces, causes or materially contributes to the infringing conduct of another. The doctrine derives from general tort principles and was articulated by Judge Learned Hand, who observed that one who supplies the means for infringement while knowing of it may be held responsible as a contributory infringer.

Elements and case law

A contributory infringement claim generally requires an underlying act of direct infringement, knowledge (actual or constructive) of that infringement, and a material contribution to it. In Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios (1984), the Supreme Court held that selling an article of commerce does not constitute contributory infringement if the product is widely used for legitimate, unobjectionable purposes or is merely capable of substantial non-infringing uses — the "staple article of commerce" doctrine, applied there to the Betamax video recorder.

In MGM Studios v. Grokster (2005), the Court recognised a separate theory of inducement liability: one who distributes a device with the object of promoting its use to infringe copyright, as shown by clear expression or affirmative steps, is liable for the resulting acts of infringement by third parties. File-sharing services such as Napster were found liable for contributory (and vicarious) infringement because they maintained centralised indexes and ongoing control over their networks.

Related doctrines

Contributory infringement is distinguished from vicarious liability, which requires the right and ability to control the infringing conduct plus a direct financial interest in it, and does not require knowledge. Statutory safe harbours, notably in the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, limit the liability of online service providers that comply with notice-and-takedown procedures.
