Scale (zoology)

In zoology, a scale is a small rigid plate that grows out of an animal's skin to provide protection. Scales occur in fish, reptiles, pangolins, and in modified forms on the wings of butterflies and moths (order Lepidoptera, from the Greek for "scale wing").

Fish scales

Fish scales are classified into several types: placoid scales (the tooth-like denticles of sharks and rays, made of dentine and enamel), ganoid scales (thick, rhomboid, enamel-covered scales of gars and bichirs), and the thin flexible cycloid and ctenoid scales of most bony fish, which grow by adding concentric rings that can be used to estimate a fish's age.

Reptile scales

Reptilian scales are formed in the epidermis and are chiefly keratinous. In snakes and lizards they are periodically shed in a process called ecdysis; in crocodilians and turtles they are reinforced in places by bony scutes (osteoderms).

Other scales

Pangolins are covered in large overlapping keratin scales, the only mammals so protected. Birds have scales on their feet, derived from their reptilian ancestry, and butterfly wing scales produce structural and pigmentary colour.
