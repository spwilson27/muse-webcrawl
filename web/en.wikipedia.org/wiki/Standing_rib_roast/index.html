The standing rib roast, also known as prime rib, is a cut of beef from the primal rib, one of the nine primal cuts of beef. While the entire rib section comprises ribs six through twelve, a standing rib roast may contain anywhere from two to seven ribs.

It is most often roasted "standing" on the rib bones so that the meat does not touch the pan. An alternative cut removes the top end of the ribs for easier carving, creating a rib eye roast. The name "prime rib" is used loosely; in the United States the cut need not be from USDA Prime grade beef, though many restaurants use the term for their highest-quality rib roast.

Preparation

The roast is typically brought to room temperature, seasoned with salt and pepper (sometimes with garlic and herbs), and roasted slowly at a low temperature until the centre reaches the desired doneness, then rested before carving between the ribs into thick slices. A common method sears the exterior at a high temperature at the beginning or end of cooking. The roast is often served with au jus, horseradish sauce, and, in Britain, Yorkshire pudding.

Traditions

Standing rib roast is a traditional centrepiece of Christmas and holiday dinners in the United States and of Sunday roast dinners in Britain and its former dominions. A full seven-rib roast can weigh 16 lb (7.3 kg) or more and feed twelve or more people.
