Athens Polytechnic uprising

Date14–17 November 1973
LocationAthens, Greece
Caused byThe Greek military junta of 1967–1974
ResultUprising crushed by the military; junta fell eight months later
Deaths24 civilians confirmed

The Athens Polytechnic uprising occurred in November 1973 as a massive student demonstration of popular rejection of the Greek military junta of 1967–1974. The uprising began on 14 November 1973, escalated to an open anti-junta revolt, and ended in bloodshed in the early morning of 17 November after a series of events starting with a tank crashing through the gates of the Athens Polytechnic.

Background

Since the coup of 21 April 1967, the military regime had abolished political parties, censored the press and suppressed student unions. In 1973, as discontent grew, students occupied the Polytechnic (the National Technical University of Athens) on 14 November, barricading themselves inside and setting up a pirate radio station broadcasting the famous appeal "Here is the Polytechnic! People of Greece, the Polytechnic bears the flag of our struggle…"

17 November

In the early hours of 17 November 1973, an AMX 30 tank crashed through the main gate of the Polytechnic. Soldiers cleared the campus; snipers and clashes around the city killed at least 24 civilians. Nobody was ever convicted of a shooting-related offence during the events, and no deaths occurred within the campus itself, but the uprising shook the junta. Eight months later, in July 1974, the regime collapsed after the Cyprus crisis, and democracy was restored.

Legacy

17 November is a public school holiday in Greece and is commemorated annually with a march from the Polytechnic to the United States Embassy in Athens. A political party, the far-left-then-revolutionary organisation 17N ("Revolutionary Organization 17 November"), later took its name from the date.
