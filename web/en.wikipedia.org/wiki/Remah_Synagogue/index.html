The Remah Synagogue (Polish: Synagoga Remuh) is a 16th-century Jewish temple and the smallest of the historic synagogues in the Kazimierz district of Kraków, Poland. It is named after Rabbi Moses Isserles (c. 1525–1572), known by the Hebrew acronym ReMA (רמ״א), who is buried in the adjacent Remah Cemetery. The synagogue was founded by Isserles's father, Israel ben Josef, in memory of his wife Malka, and the original wooden building of 1553 was replaced by a stone structure after a fire, built in 1557 in the late Renaissance style.

History

Unlike many Kraków synagogues, the Remah Synagogue survived the Second World War largely intact, though it was used as a warehouse and its furnishings looted. After the war it was restored and returned to religious use. It remains one of only two active synagogues in Kraków, and is a major site of pilgrimage for Jews visiting the graves of Rabbi Isserles and other notable figures in the adjoining Old Jewish Cemetery.

Interior

The prayer hall features a reconstructed central bimah enclosed by a wrought-iron grille, a Renaissance Torah ark, and inscriptions in Hebrew. The women's gallery is reached from an annexe.
