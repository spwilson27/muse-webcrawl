List of galaxies

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

There are an estimated 200 billion to 2 trillion galaxies in the observable universe. This article lists notable named galaxies — members of the Local Group, bright Messier and NGC objects, and record-holding galaxies — with their approximate distances and types.

  • Sagittarius Dwarf Spheroidal Galaxy — being absorbed by the Milky Way.
  • Leo I, Leo II, Ursa Minor Dwarf, Draco Dwarf, Sculptor Dwarf, Fornax Dwarf, Carina Dwarf — classic Milky Way satellite dwarf spheroidals.

Nearest and most distant

The nearest known galaxy outside the Milky Way is the Canis Major Dwarf (disputed) at about 25,000 light-years, followed by the Sagittarius Dwarf at about 70,000 light-years. The nearest large galaxy is Andromeda. At the other extreme, JWST has confirmed galaxies whose light left them less than 300 million years after the Big Bang, at redshifts above 14.
