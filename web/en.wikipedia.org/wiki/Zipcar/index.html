Zipcar

Zipcar is an American car-sharing company and a subsidiary of Avis Budget Group. Founded in 2000 in Cambridge, Massachusetts, by Antje Danielson and Robin Chase, it provides automobile reservations to its members, billable by the minute, hour or day; members receive a "Zipcard" or use a mobile app to unlock vehicles parked in designated spaces.

History

Zipcar was modelled on European car-sharing cooperatives such as Berlin's Stattauto. It grew across US college towns and cities, merged with its West Coast rival Flexcar in 2007, acquired the British company Streetcar in 2010, and went public on NASDAQ in April 2011. In January 2013 Avis Budget Group announced it would acquire Zipcar for about US$500 million, completing the purchase that March.

Service model

Members reserve a specific car at a specific location ("home location") and return it there. Rates typically include fuel, insurance and maintenance. Cars are accessed via RFID Zipcard or smartphone; the model is designed so trips are short and self-service, making car access cheaper than ownership for urban residents who drive infrequently. Zipcar operates in hundreds of cities and on many university campuses, and has reported around a million members at its peak, though it has since withdrawn from several markets, including the United Kingdom (announced 2025).

Impact

Studies of car sharing, with Zipcar as the leading example, associate membership with reduced private car ownership and vehicle miles travelled, as members drive less and use transit, walking and cycling more for routine trips.
