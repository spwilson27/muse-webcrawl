Space form

FieldRiemannian geometry
DefinitionComplete Riemannian manifold of constant sectional curvature
Model spacesEuclidean space, sphere, hyperbolic space

In Riemannian geometry, a space form is a complete Riemannian manifold of constant sectional curvature. By Killing–Hopf theorem, any space form is a quotient of one of three model spaces: Euclidean space (curvature 0), the sphere (positive curvature), or hyperbolic space (negative curvature), by a discrete group of isometries acting freely and properly discontinuously.

Examples

Flat space forms include tori and the Klein bottle in dimension two, and Bieberbach's theorems classify compact flat manifolds in higher dimensions. Spherical space forms include real projective spaces and lens spaces; in dimension three, the classification connects with the Poincaré homology sphere. Hyperbolic manifolds are the most abundant class in higher dimensions.

The space form problem

The classical space form problem asks which groups can act freely on spheres and which manifolds admit metrics of constant curvature. It drew on work by Killing, Hopf, Wolf, and Milnor, and relates to deep questions in topology about free group actions on spheres.

Applications

Space forms are the local models of cosmology: the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metrics assume spatial sections of constant curvature, so the global shape of the universe is modelled by a space form.
