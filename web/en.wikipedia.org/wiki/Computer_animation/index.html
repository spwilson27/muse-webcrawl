Computer animation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Computer animation is the process used for digitally generating moving images, using computer graphics to create animated sequences rather than drawing or photographing each frame by hand. It encompasses 2D animation created or assisted by computer, and 3D animation in which characters and environments are modelled in virtual space, posed with digital rigs, and rendered. Computer animation is the dominant technique in modern feature animation, visual effects, and video games.

Techniques

Animators set keyframes and use interpolation ("tweening") to generate intermediate poses, guided by the traditional principles of animation such as squash and stretch, anticipation, and timing. In 3D, characters are built as polygon meshes controlled by a skeleton (rigging and skinning). Motion capture records real performers' movements and applies them to digital characters. Physics simulation produces cloth, hair, fluids, crowds, and destruction; procedural animation generates motion from rules and algorithms.

History

Early experiments date to the 1960s (e.g. at Bell Labs). John Whitney pioneered motion graphics with analogue and digital computers. The 1980s brought CGI into feature films (Tron, 1982; the stained-glass knight in Young Sherlock Holmes, 1985), and the liquid-metal T-1000 of Terminator 2 (1991) and the dinosaurs of Jurassic Park (1993) established photorealistic CGI. Pixar's Toy Story (1995) was the first fully computer-animated feature film; computer animation subsequently displaced hand-drawn animation at most major studios.

Real-time animation

Video games and interactive media render animation in real time, using skeletal animation, blend trees, and inverse kinematics so characters respond to player input at 30–120 frames per second. Machinima and virtual production use game engines such as Unreal Engine to create film content in real time.

Software

Industry tools include Autodesk Maya and 3ds Max, Blender (open source), Houdini, Cinema 4D, and Toon Boom Harmony for 2D production. Rendering is performed by engines such as RenderMan, Arnold, and Cycles.
