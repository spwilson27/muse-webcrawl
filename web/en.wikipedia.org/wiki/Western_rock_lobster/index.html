Western rock lobster

The western rock lobster (Panulirus cygnus) is a species of spiny lobster found in shallow waters off the west coast of Australia, from Shark Bay to the Leeuwin Current region near Albany. It is the basis of Australia's most valuable single-species fishery.

Description

Adults are reddish-brown to purplish above, with pale yellow legs banded with darker colour. Like other spiny lobsters they lack the large claws of true lobsters and instead have long, spiny antennae. They can grow to about 5 kg, though animals above 2 kg are uncommon in the fished population.

Life cycle

Larvae (phyllosoma) spend 9–11 months drifting in the Indian Ocean before settling on inshore reefs as puerulus, an event whose strength is used to forecast catches several years ahead. Juveniles, called "whites", migrate offshore into deeper water in early summer.

Fishery

The Western Rock Lobster Managed Fishery, centred on Geraldton, Kalbarri, and Fremantle, is worth hundreds of millions of dollars annually, most of the catch being exported live, chiefly to China. It was one of the first fisheries in the world certified as sustainable by the Marine Stewardship Council, in 2000.
