The Wivenhoe Dam is a rock and earth-fill embankment dam across the Brisbane River in South East Queensland, Australia. Completed in 1984, it was built for the dual purposes of water supply for Brisbane and flood mitigation, following the devastating 1974 Brisbane floods. Its reservoir, Lake Wivenhoe, is the largest water supply storage in the region.

The Wivenhoe Dam wall and gated spillway

Design and operation

The dam wall is about 2.3 kilometres long and 59 metres high, with a gated central spillway of five radial gates. In addition to its drinking-water compartment of roughly 1.165 million megalitres, the dam reserves a large flood compartment that can temporarily hold a further 2.28 million megalitres. Operators release water according to a flood operations manual intended to reduce downstream flooding in Brisbane and Ipswich. The associated Wivenhoe pumped-storage power station, with two 285 MW units, was the first major pumped hydroelectric scheme in Australia.

2011 floods

During the January 2011 Queensland floods, inflows were so large that Lake Wivenhoe briefly held about 190% of its supply capacity, and controlled releases contributed to flooding downstream. A subsequent commission of inquiry examined the dam engineers' compliance with the operating manual and the balance between water-supply and flood-mitigation objectives, leading to litigation and revisions of the flood manual.
