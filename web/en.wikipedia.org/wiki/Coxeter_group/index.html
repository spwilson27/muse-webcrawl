Coxeter group

Named afterH. S. M. Coxeter
GeneratorsInvolutions (reflections)
Key invariantCoxeter matrix / diagram

In mathematics, a Coxeter group, named after H. S. M. Coxeter, is an abstract group that admits a formal description in terms of reflections. Indeed, the finite Coxeter groups are precisely the finite reflection groups: the symmetry groups of regular polytopes and of kaleidoscopic tilings can all be described in this way.

Definition

A Coxeter group is a group W with a presentation of the form W = ⟨s1, …, sn | (sisj)mij = 1⟩, where each mii = 1 (so every generator is an involution), mij = mji ≥ 2 for i ≠ j, and mij may be infinite, in which case the corresponding relation is omitted. The symmetric matrix (mij) is called the Coxeter matrix.

The data of the matrix is conveniently drawn as a Coxeter diagram: a graph whose vertices are the generators, with an edge between si and sj labelled by mij (unlabelled edges mean m = 3, and no edge means m = 2).

Examples

The symmetric group Sn+1 is a Coxeter group of type An, generated by adjacent transpositions. The dihedral group of order 2m is the Coxeter group I2(m). The symmetry groups of the Platonic solids and of the regular polygons are finite Coxeter groups, as are the Weyl groups of semisimple Lie algebras.

Classification

Finite Coxeter groups were classified by Coxeter in 1935. The connected diagrams giving finite groups are An, Bn, Dn, E6, E7, E8, F4, G2 (= I2(6)), H3, H4, and I2(m). Affine Coxeter groups, which act on Euclidean space by reflections and translations, admit a parallel classification and appear in the theory of affine Lie algebras.

Properties

Every Coxeter group has a canonical faithful representation as a group generated by reflections on a vector space equipped with an invariant bilinear form (the Tits representation). Coxeter groups are linear, residually finite, and have solvable word problem; their combinatorics is governed by the Bruhat order and the length function counting the minimal number of generators in an expression for an element.
