Union of Uzhhorod

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Union of Uzhhorod was an agreement concluded on 24 April 1646 at Uzhhorod Castle (then Ungvár, in the Kingdom of Hungary) by which a group of Orthodox priests of the eparchy of Mukachevo entered into communion with the Roman Catholic Church while retaining their Byzantine rite, Church Slavonic liturgy, married clergy, and local customs. Modelled on the earlier Union of Brest (1596), the act is regarded as the founding event of what became the Ruthenian Greek Catholic Church in Subcarpathian Rus'.

Background and terms

The initiative came from the Basilian monk and bishop-elect Petro Parfenii (Peter Parthenius) and the local clergy, encouraged by the Catholic magnate György Drugeth and the Bishop of Eger. Sixty-three priests subscribed to the union. As at Brest, the clergy stipulated that they would keep the Eastern liturgical tradition, that their priests might marry, that bishops would be chosen from among their own, and that they would enjoy the same rights and social standing as Latin clergy — conditions only partly honoured in the following decades, which produced recurring tension with the Hungarian Latin hierarchy. The union was confirmed by Rome in 1655, though the Holy See had earlier withheld recognition over procedural questions.

Legacy

The communities formed by the Union of Uzhhorod survived Habsburg rule, the Czechoslovak and Hungarian interwar periods, and Soviet suppression: after 1949 the Greek Catholic eparchy of Mukachevo was forcibly liquidated in the USSR and its faithful driven underground, until legalisation in 1989–1991. Its diaspora descendants include the Ruthenian Greek Catholic Church in the United States. The original signed document of 1646 is lost; the union is known from contemporary copies and reports.
