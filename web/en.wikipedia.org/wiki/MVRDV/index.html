MVRDV is a Dutch architecture and urban design practice based in Rotterdam, founded in 1993 by Winy Maas, Jacob van Rijs and Nathalie de Vries — the initials of whose surnames form the firm's name. It is known for research-driven, often exuberant designs that experiment with density, stacking and the programming of buildings.

History

The three founders met while working at the Office for Metropolitan Architecture. MVRDV gained prominence with the Villa VPRO headquarters in Hilversum (1997), which folds the office floor plates into a continuous landscape-like interior, and with the Netherlands pavilion at Expo 2000 in Hanover, a celebrated stack of artificial Dutch landscapes. Publications such as FARMAX (1998) set out the firm's data-driven approach to density and urbanism.

Major works

Notable projects include the WoZoCo housing block in Amsterdam with its cantilevered coloured flats; the Silodam housing complex; the Markthal (2014), a vast arched market hall with housing in Rotterdam whose interior carries a large mural; the Tianjin Binhai Library in China with its spherical central atrium; Depot Boijmans Van Beuningen (2021), a mirrored, bowl-shaped public art storage building in Rotterdam; and the Valley mixed-use towers in Amsterdam. The firm also works extensively in urban planning and landscape, and has established the research think tank The Why Factory at TU Delft.
