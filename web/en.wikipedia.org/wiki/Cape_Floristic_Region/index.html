The Cape Floristic Region is a floristic region located near the southern tip of South Africa. It is the only floristic region of the Cape Floristic Kingdom, and includes only one floristic province, the Cape Floristic Province. Despite occupying less than 0.5% of Africa's land area, it is home to nearly 20% of the continent's flora.

Biodiversity

The region contains more than 9,000 vascular plant species, of which around 69% are endemic — found nowhere else on Earth. Much of this diversity is associated with the fynbos biome, a fine-leaved shrubland adapted to nutrient-poor soils, Mediterranean climate, and regular fires. Iconic plant families include the Proteaceae (proteas), Ericaceae (heaths), and Restionaceae (Cape reeds), as well as an extraordinary richness of geophytes such as irises and amaryllids.

Conservation

The region is recognised as one of the world's biodiversity hotspots and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Major threats include habitat loss to agriculture and urban expansion, invasive alien plants, and altered fire regimes, compounded by climate change.
