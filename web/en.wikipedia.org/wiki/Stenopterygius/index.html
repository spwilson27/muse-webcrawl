Stenopterygius

Stenopterygius is an extinct genus of ichthyosaur, a marine reptile superficially resembling a dolphin, that lived during the Early Jurassic period, roughly 183–179 million years ago. It is known from abundant, exquisitely preserved fossils found in the Posidonia Shale of Holzmaden and surrounding localities in south-western Germany.

Description

Stenopterygius was a relatively small ichthyosaur, typically 2–3 metres (about 7–10 feet) long. It had a streamlined, fish-shaped body, a long pointed snout armed with numerous sharp teeth suited to catching fish and squid, large eyes supported by bony sclerotic rings, and a crescent-shaped tail fluke in which the vertebral column bent sharply downward. Some specimens preserve the outline of the soft tissues, showing the dorsal fin and the smooth skin of the living animal.

Reproduction

The genus is famous for fossils of pregnant females and of young in the process of being born. Like other ichthyosaurs, Stenopterygius gave birth to live young at sea, the babies typically emerging tail-first so that they would not drown before reaching the surface. Hundreds of specimens in museums worldwide include mothers with embryos, making it one of the best documented of all prehistoric marine vertebrates.

Classification

Stenopterygius belongs to the family Stenopterygiidae within the ichthyosaur order. Several species have been recognised, including S. quadriscissus and S. triscissus. Its fossils were among the first ichthyosaurs to be studied scientifically in the nineteenth century.
