San Clemente, California


Location: Orange County, California, United States
Incorporated: February 27, 1928
Population: 64,293 (2020 census)
Nickname: Spanish Village by the Sea

San Clemente is a coastal city in Orange County, California, United States. The population was 64,293 at the 2020 census. Located in the Orange Coast region of the South Coast, San Clemente is known for its ocean, hill, and mountain views, mild Mediterranean climate, and Spanish Colonial Revival-style architecture. Its city slogan is "Spanish Village by the Sea."

History

The area was long inhabited by the Acjachemen people before Spanish colonization. The city itself was founded in 1925 by former Seattle mayor Ole Hanson, who envisioned a Spanish-style resort community and required buildings to follow Spanish Colonial Revival design. San Clemente was incorporated on February 27, 1928. Hanson also built the San Clemente Pier and the Casa Romantica estate, now a cultural center.

In 1969, President Richard Nixon purchased part of the H. H. Cotton estate, known as La Casa Pacifica, which became the "Western White House" during his presidency. The area is also home to the celebrated surf breaks of Trestles, on the city's northern edge, and San Onofre State Beach. Interstate 5 runs through the city, and Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton lies to the south.
