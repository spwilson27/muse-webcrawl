Savant syndrome

Savant syndrome is a condition in which a person with a developmental disorder, brain injury, or other disability demonstrates a striking ability or brilliance in a specific domain that contrasts sharply with their overall limitations. Skills typically fall into areas such as music, visual art, calendar calculation, mathematics, and extraordinary memory.

Characteristics

Roughly half of known savants are autistic; savant skills are estimated to occur in about 10% of autistic people. Well-documented cases include Kim Peek, the inspiration for the film Rain Man, who memorized thousands of books; the artist Stephen Wiltshire, who can draw detailed cityscapes from memory; and Leslie Lemke, a blind musical savant. Rare "prodigious" savants, perhaps fewer than one hundred living, have abilities that would be remarkable even in a neurotypical person.

Mechanisms

Proposed explanations include left-hemisphere dysfunction with right-hemisphere compensation, enhanced low-level perceptual processing, and literal, systemizing cognitive styles. A rare "acquired savant syndrome" can follow head injury or dementia, suggesting that savant-like capacities may be latent in the brain and released by damage or inhibition of other regions.
