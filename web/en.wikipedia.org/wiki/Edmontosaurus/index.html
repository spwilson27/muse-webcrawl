Edmontosaurus

Edmontosaurus (meaning "lizard from Edmonton") is a genus of hadrosaurid (duck-billed) dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous period, about 73 to 66 million years ago, in western North America. It is one of the best-known dinosaurs, represented by numerous specimens including remarkably preserved "mummies" with skin impressions.

Description

Edmontosaurus grew up to about 12–13 metres long and weighed several tonnes. It had a long, flattened skull lacking a bony crest, a broad beak for cropping vegetation, and dental batteries of hundreds of teeth for grinding plant material. It probably walked on all fours but could rear up on its hind legs.

Species and history

The type species, E. regalis, was named by Lawrence Lambe in 1917 from fossils found in the Horseshoe Canyon Formation of Alberta, near Edmonton. A second species, E. annectens, is known from Montana, South Dakota, and Wyoming. Edmontosaurus was prey for Tyrannosaurus rex, with which it coexisted at the end of the Cretaceous.
