The Carson, Pirie, Scott and Company Building, now officially the Sullivan Center, is a 12-story department store building at 1 South State Street in the Loop district of Chicago, designed by architect Louis Sullivan and built between 1899 and 1903 (with a southern addition completed in 1906 by D. H. Burnham & Co.). It is regarded as one of the masterpieces of the Chicago School of architecture and a pioneering example of the steel-frame skyscraper used for retail.

Architecture

The building's steel skeleton is expressed in a grid of wide "Chicago windows" separated by slender piers and spandrels sheathed in white glazed terracotta, maximising daylight to the selling floors. The first two storeys, by contrast, are wrapped in an exuberant screen of cast-iron ornament — intricate, plant-like geometric tracery designed by Sullivan and executed under his chief draughtsman George Grant Elmslie — which frames the shop windows and the celebrated rounded corner entrance pavilion at State and Madison. The contrast between the lush base and the disciplined upper grid, capped by a simple cornice band, embodies Sullivan's maxim that "form follows function".

History

The store was commissioned by the Schlesinger & Mayer department store and sold on completion to Carson Pirie Scott, which occupied it for over a century, until the flagship closed in 2007. The building was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1975 and a Chicago Landmark in 1970. A major restoration in 2001 returned the State Street façade and the corner entrance to their historic appearance, and the complex was renamed the Sullivan Center; it now houses offices, shops and educational tenants. The building's ornament and structure continue to be studied as a summit of American commercial design.
