Alabama Claims
Parties: United States and United Kingdom
Issue: Neutrality during the American Civil War
Settlement: Treaty of Washington (1871)
Award: US$15.5 million to the United States (1872)

The Alabama Claims were a series of demands for damages sought by the government of the United States from the United Kingdom in 1869, for the attacks upon Union merchant ships by Confederate Navy commerce raiders built in British shipyards during the American Civil War. The claims focused principally on the most famous of these raiders, CSS Alabama, which sank or captured dozens of Union vessels.

Arbitration

The dispute embittered Anglo-American relations for years. It was finally settled by the Treaty of Washington (1871), under which an international tribunal at Geneva arbitrated the claims — a landmark early use of international arbitration. In 1872 the tribunal awarded the United States $15.5 million in gold, Britain expressing regret for the escape of the raiders. The tribunal's rules on neutral "due diligence" became influential in the development of international law.

Legacy

The Alabama Claims arbitration is widely regarded as a milestone in peaceful dispute resolution between states, helping to inspire later institutions of international arbitration and adjudication, including the Hague conferences and the Permanent Court of Arbitration.
