Motorized bicycle

A motorized bicycle is a bicycle with an attached motor or engine and transmission used either to power the vehicle unassisted, or to assist pedalling. Since it sometimes retains both pedals and a discrete connected drive for rider-powered propulsion, the motorized bicycle is in technical terms a true bicycle, albeit a power-assisted one. Many jurisdictions classify them separately from mopeds and motorcycles because of their low power output.

History

Auxiliary engines for bicycles date to the earliest days of motor transport: the steam-driven Michaux-Perreaux velocipede of the late 1860s is arguably the first motorcycle, while petrol clip-on engines proliferated in the early 20th century. The French VéloSoleX, introduced in 1946, became the most famous mass-produced example, with millions sold. Modern kits — both petrol two-strokes and electric hub-motor conversions — enjoy a hobbyist following; the electric versions overlap conceptually with e-bikes, though e-bikes are usually purpose-built and pedal-assist limited.

Design

Drive arrangements include friction drive against the tyre, chain or belt drive to the rear wheel (frame-mounted "mid-drive" kits), and hub motors. Petrol kits typically use 48–80 cc two-stroke single-cylinder engines producing 1–3 horsepower. Top speeds commonly range from 25 to 50 km/h. Legal treatment varies widely: many places limit engine size, power, and speed for licence-free use, while others regulate motorized bicycles as mopeds requiring registration.
