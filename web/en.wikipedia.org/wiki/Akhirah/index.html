ʾĀkhirah (Arabic: الآخرة) is the Islamic term for the afterlife — the life that follows death, resurrection and the final judgement of humankind. Belief in the akhirah is one of the six articles of faith in Islam. It is frequently contrasted with al-dunya, the fleeting present world, and the Quran repeatedly exhorts believers to prepare for the eternal life to come.

Stages of the afterlife

According to Islamic eschatology, after death the soul enters Barzakh, an intermediate state lasting until the Day of Resurrection (Yawm al-Qiyamah). Signs precede the end of the world, after which the dead are raised, gathered for judgement, and their deeds weighed. Those judged righteous enter Jannah (paradise), described as gardens beneath which rivers flow, while the condemned enter Jahannam (hell).

In the Quran

The term akhirah occurs over a hundred times in the Quran, often paired with descriptions of reward and punishment and with exhortations that the hereafter is "better and more lasting" than worldly life. Major surahs devoted to eschatological themes include Al-Waqi'a, Al-Qari'ah and Al-Zalzalah.

Significance

Belief in the akhirah shapes Muslim ethics, charity, and attitudes toward death, and is commemorated in practices such as funerary prayer (janazah) and remembrances of the dead.
