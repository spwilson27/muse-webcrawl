Charles Martin Jones (September 21, 1912 – February 22, 2002) was an American animator, director, and painter best known for his work with Warner Bros. Cartoons on the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies series of short films. Over a career spanning more than 60 years, he directed over 300 animated films, won three Academy Awards as a director, and received an Honorary Academy Award in 1996.

Jones helped define the personalities of Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, and Elmer Fudd.

Career

After working at Ub Iwerks' studio and for Walter Lantz, Jones joined Leon Schlesinger Productions (later Warner Bros. Cartoons) in 1933 as an assistant animator. He became a director in 1938. Though his early shorts were considered slow and cute, he matured into one of the studio's defining directors alongside Tex Avery, Bob Clampett, and Friz Freleng.

Jones created or co-created many characters, including Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner (1949), Pepé Le Pew, Marvin the Martian, the Michigan J. Frog, Ralph Wolf and Sam Sheepdog, and Marc Antony and Pussyfoot. His celebrated films include What's Opera, Doc? (1957), often voted the greatest cartoon of all time, Duck Amuck (1953), One Froggy Evening (1955), Rabbit of Seville (1950), and the "Hunting Trilogy" of Rabbit Fire, Rabbit Seasoning, and Duck! Rabbit, Duck!

Later work

After Warner Bros. Cartoons closed in 1963, Jones founded Sib Tower 12 Productions with Les Goldman. For MGM he directed new Tom and Jerry shorts and produced the television special How the Grinch Stole Christmas! (1966) and the feature film The Phantom Tollbooth (1970). He later formed Chuck Jones Enterprises and continued producing specials, including adaptations of Rudyard Kipling's Jungle Book stories and Rikki-Tikki-Tavi.

Legacy

Jones wrote the memoir Chuck Amuck (1989) and Chuck Reducks (1996). His famous nine rules for the Road Runner cartoons codified his discipline of character and gag construction. The Chuck Jones Center for Creativity, founded in 1999, preserves his work and promotes arts education.
