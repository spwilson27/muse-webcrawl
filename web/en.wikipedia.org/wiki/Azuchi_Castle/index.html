Azuchi Castle

Azuchi Castle (Azuchi-jō) was one of the primary castles of Oda Nobunaga, built from 1576 to 1579 on the shores of Lake Biwa in Ōmi Province, in present-day Azuchi, Shiga Prefecture, Japan. Nobunaga intentionally built Azuchi Castle close enough to Kyoto so that he could watch over and guard the approaches to the capital, but outside the capital so that fire or fighting would not reach it.

History

Unlike earlier castles and fortresses, Azuchi was not intended to be solely a military structure, cold and forbidding. Nobunaga intended it as a mansion that would impress and intimidate his rivals by its size and magnificence, as well as serving as a political and commercial centre. The castle town that grew around it housed thousands of residents.

The castle's most striking feature was its seven-storey main keep (tenshu), lavishly decorated with gold leaf and paintings by the artist Kanō Eitoku. Azuchi is often cited as Japan's first "modern" castle and the prototype for the grand castles of the Azuchi–Momoyama period, including Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Osaka Castle and Fushimi Castle.

Destruction

After Nobunaga was assassinated in the Honnō-ji Incident in June 1582, Azuchi Castle was burned and looted only days later. The keep was destroyed and never rebuilt, ending the castle's brief existence after only three years of use. Today, only the stone base of the main keep remains, and a partial reconstruction of the upper floors of the tenshu was created for Expo '92 in Seville and is now displayed in Japan.
