The Rockefeller family is an American industrial, political, and banking family that owns one of the world's largest fortunes. The fortune was made in the late 19th and early 20th centuries by brothers John D. Rockefeller and William A. Rockefeller Jr., founders of Standard Oil, whose refining monopoly made John D. Rockefeller the first widely recognised billionaire in the United States and, by common assessment, one of the richest Americans of all time.

Generations

Second generation: John D. Rockefeller Jr. (1874–1960) devoted himself to philanthropy and stewardship, building Rockefeller Center and funding the restoration of Colonial Williamsburg. Third generation: his five sons — John D. Rockefeller III (philanthropy, Population Council), Nelson Rockefeller (Governor of New York and Vice President under Gerald Ford), Laurance Rockefeller (conservation and venture capital), Winthrop Rockefeller (Governor of Arkansas), and David Rockefeller (Chase Manhattan Bank chairman) — along with their sister Abby, defined the family's public role. Later generations include Senator John D. "Jay" Rockefeller IV of West Virginia and, in the sixth generation, figures in philanthropy and environmental activism; family members were prominent among the heirs who pressed the Rockefeller funds to divest from fossil fuels in 2014–2016.

Philanthropy

The family created or seeded many enduring institutions: the University of Chicago (1890), Rockefeller University (1901), the Rockefeller Foundation (1913), the Museum of Modern Art (1929), the Rockefeller Brothers Fund (1940), and large contributions to public health (hookworm eradication, the "Green Revolution" in agriculture).
