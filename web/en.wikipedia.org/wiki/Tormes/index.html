The Tormes is a river in western Spain, a left-bank tributary of the Duero (Douro). It rises in the Sierra de Gredos in the province of Ávila and flows generally westward for about 284 kilometres through the provinces of Ávila, Salamanca, and Zamora before joining the Duero near Villarino de los Aires, close to the Portuguese border, in the Arribes del Duero gorge country.

Course and cities

The Tormes passes the towns of El Barco de Ávila and Alba de Tormes before reaching the city of Salamanca, where it is spanned by a celebrated Roman bridge, the Puente Romano, part of the old Via de la Plata. The river's monuments in Salamanca — the bridge, the bull sculpture known as the Verraco del Puente, and the Lazarillo monument — are city landmarks. Downstream the river is impounded by the great Almendra Dam, one of the largest reservoirs in Spain, and by the Santa Teresa reservoir upstream of Salamanca, before cutting through deep granite canyons to meet the Duero.

Cultural references

The river gives its name to the protagonist of the anonymous 1554 novella Lazarillo de Tormes, a founding work of the picaresque genre: Lazarillo was born in a mill on the Tormes near Salamanca, and the novel's opening episode has him serving a blind beggar by the river. Salamanca's Tormes is also associated with the university city scenes of the novel La Celestina, traditionally said to unfold on its banks.
