Moscato d'Asti

TypeLightly sparkling white wine (frizzante)
RegionAsti, Piedmont, Italy
GrapeMoscato Bianco (Muscat Blanc à Petits Grains)
StatusDOCG since 1993
Alcoholtypically 5–5.5%

Moscato d'Asti is a DOCG sparkling white wine produced mainly in the province of Asti, in north-west Italy, and in smaller nearby areas of the provinces of Alessandria and Cuneo in Piedmont. Since 1993 it has held DOCG status, the highest classification for Italian wines.

Style and production

The wine is made from the Moscato Bianco grape (Muscat Blanc à Petits Grains) and is sweet, lightly sparkling (frizzante), and low in alcohol, at around 5–5.5%. Fermentation is stopped early by chilling and filtration to preserve the grape's natural sugars and aromatic character, producing flavours of peach, apricot, orange blossom, and sage. It should generally be drunk young and well chilled.

Relation to Asti wine

Moscato d'Asti is closely related to Asti (formerly Asti Spumante), which is fully sparkling (spumante), slightly higher in alcohol, and produced from the same grape in the same region. Moscato d'Asti is prized as a dessert wine and an accompaniment to fruit tarts and hazelnut cakes, and its popularity surged internationally in the 2000s and 2010s.
