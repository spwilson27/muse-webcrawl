Ambrose Everett Burnside (23 May 1824 – 13 September 1881) was an American soldier, railroad executive, inventor, industrialist, and politician from Rhode Island. He served as a general in the Union Army in the American Civil War, conducting successful campaigns in North Carolina and East Tennessee but suffering costly defeats at the Battle of Fredericksburg and the Battle of the Crater. His distinctive facial hair gave rise to the word "sideburns".

Early life and military career

Burnside was born in Liberty, Indiana, and was appointed to the United States Military Academy at West Point, graduating in 1847. He served in the Mexican–American War and on the western frontier, where he was wounded fighting the Apache. Resigning from the army in 1853, he manufactured a breech-loading carbine of his own design, the Burnside carbine, and worked as a railroad executive in Rhode Island.

Civil War

At the outbreak of the Civil War Burnside organised the 1st Rhode Island Infantry and was promoted to brigadier general. His expedition to the North Carolina coast in early 1862 won victories at Roanoke Island and New Bern. His IX Corps fought at South Mountain and Antietam in September 1862.

After Antietam, President Abraham Lincoln gave Burnside command of the Army of the Potomac, despite Burnside's own doubts about his fitness for the post. His frontal assaults against entrenched Confederates at Fredericksburg on 13 December 1862 resulted in a disastrous defeat, and after the failed "Mud March" he was replaced by Joseph Hooker in January 1863. Later commanding the Department of the Ohio, he captured Knoxville, Tennessee, and defended it against James Longstreet. Returning east, his troops led the assault at the Crater during the Siege of Petersburg in July 1864, another costly failure, after which he was relieved.

Postwar life

Burnside served three one-year terms as Governor of Rhode Island (1866–1869) and was elected to the United States Senate in 1874, serving until his death in 1881. He was the first president of the National Rifle Association in 1871.
