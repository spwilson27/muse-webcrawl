Primary school

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
A primary school classroom.

A primary school, also known as an elementary school in certain countries, is a school for primary education of children who are 4 to 10 years of age (and in certain cases, 11 or 12 years of age). Primary schooling follows pre-school and precedes secondary schooling.

The International Standard Classification of Education considers primary education as a single phase where programmes are typically designed to provide fundamental reading, writing, and mathematics skills and establish a solid foundation for learning. This is ISCED Level 1: Primary education or the first stage of basic education.

Curriculum

The primary curriculum typically centres on literacy and numeracy, together with introductory science, social studies or humanities, the arts, physical education, and—increasingly—computing and a foreign language. Instruction is usually given by a single class teacher who remains with the same group of children for most subjects during the school day, in contrast to the subject-specialist teaching of secondary schools.

Organisation by country

Starting ages and structures vary. In England and Wales children begin primary school at age 4 or 5 (Reception and Years 1–6) and move to secondary school at 11. In the United States, elementary school usually covers kindergarten through grade 5 or 6, ages 5 to 11, often followed by middle school. In many European and Asian systems, primary school runs from age 6 to about 12. Most countries make primary education compulsory and provide it free in state schools; private and faith-based primary schools also operate in many systems.

History

Compulsory mass primary schooling developed in the 19th century—Prussia, Massachusetts, and England were among the early adopters—driven by industrialisation, democratisation, and the growing demand for a literate workforce. The kindergarten movement founded by Friedrich Fröbel extended formal early education downward in age, while reformers such as John Dewey influenced the child-centred methods characteristic of primary classrooms today.
