EB-5 visa

EB-5 Immigrant Investor Program
Created: Immigration Act of 1990
Requirement: Investment creating 10 U.S. jobs

The EB-5 visa, employment-based, fifth preference category, is a United States immigrant visa created by the Immigration Act of 1990. It provides a method for eligible investors, their spouses and unmarried children under 21 to obtain lawful permanent residence (a green card) by investing in a U.S. commercial enterprise and creating jobs.

Requirements

Investors must invest the statutory minimum amount of capital — raised by the EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022 to $1,050,000, or $800,000 in a targeted employment area or infrastructure project — in a new commercial enterprise that creates or preserves at least 10 full-time jobs for qualifying U.S. workers. Investment may be made directly or through USCIS-designated regional centers, which pool funds for larger projects.

Process and criticism

Investors first receive conditional permanent residence for two years after approval of the I-526 petition, and must petition to remove conditions by showing the investment and jobs were sustained. The program has drawn criticism over fraud cases, gerrymandering of targeted employment areas, and long backlogs for applicants from China and India, as well as praise for financing major developments.
