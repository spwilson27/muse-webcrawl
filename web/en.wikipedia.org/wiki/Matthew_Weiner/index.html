Matthew Hoffman Weiner (born 29 June 1965) is an American television writer, producer, and director, best known as the creator of the AMC drama series Mad Men (2007–2015) and as a writer and executive producer on HBO's The Sopranos. He has won nine Primetime Emmy Awards.

Career

Weiner began in television comedy, writing for series such as The Naked Truth and Becker. In 1999 he wrote the pilot script for Mad Men, which was passed over for years; David Chase hired him onto The Sopranos after reading the script, and Weiner wrote or co-wrote a dozen episodes of that series' final seasons, winning two Emmys. AMC picked up Mad Men in 2006, and it premiered in July 2007 to wide acclaim, winning the Emmy for Outstanding Drama Series for each of its first four seasons. Weiner was known for tight control of the show's writing, secrecy about plotlines, and meticulous period detail in its depiction of 1960s advertising and American society.

Later work

Weiner wrote and directed the feature film Are You Here (2013), published the novel Heather, the Totality in 2017, and created the Amazon anthology series The Romanoffs (2018). In 2017, former Mad Men writer Kater Gordon accused Weiner of sexual harassment, which he denied.
