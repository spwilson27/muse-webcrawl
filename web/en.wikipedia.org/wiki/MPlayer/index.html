MPlayer is a free and open-source media player distributed under the GNU General Public License. It runs on Linux and other Unix-like systems, macOS and Windows, and is notable for its extremely broad support for audio and video formats, largely through the codec libraries of the FFmpeg project and, on x86 systems, binary codec packages.

Features

MPlayer is a command-line program with an optional graphical interface (gmplayer) and numerous third-party front ends. It can play files, discs, network streams and capture devices; apply video and audio filters; display subtitles; and be controlled in "slave mode" by other programs. The package also includes MEncoder, a companion tool for transcoding audio and video between formats.

History

The project was started in 2000 by Árpád Gereöffy in Hungary, frustrated that no existing Linux player handled the formats he wanted. It grew rapidly with the help of a large developer community, becoming the back end of choice for many other players and the subject of disputes over code authorship and licence compliance during its early years. Development slowed in later years, and many users migrated to the active fork mpv, but MPlayer continues to be maintained and packaged by major Linux distributions.
