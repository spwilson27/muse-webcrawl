Bab-el-Mandeb

The Bab-el-Mandeb (Arabic: باب المندب, "Gate of Tears" or "Gate of Grief") is a strait between Yemen on the Arabian Peninsula and Djibouti and Eritrea in the Horn of Africa. It connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and is one of the world's most important maritime chokepoints for international trade and oil shipments.

The Bab-el-Mandeb links the Red Sea with the Gulf of Aden and the Indian Ocean.

Geography

The strait is about 29 km wide at its narrowest. The island of Perim divides it into two channels: the eastern channel, Bab Iskender (Alexander's Strait), about 3 km wide, and the western channel, Dact-el-Mayun, about 25 km wide, through which the main traffic separation scheme runs with lanes each roughly 3 km wide. Near the African coast lie the Sawabi Islands (the "Seven Brothers").

Strategic importance

Ships transiting the Suez Canal between Europe and Asia must pass through the Bab-el-Mandeb; millions of barrels of oil and a large share of global trade move through it daily. Its chokepoint status has drawn foreign military presence — including bases in Djibouti — and made it a focus during the Red Sea shipping crisis beginning in 2023, when Houthi attacks on shipping in and near the strait forced widespread rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope.

History and name

Arabic tradition derives the name "Gate of Tears" from the dangers of navigation or from a legend of an earthquake that severed Arabia from Africa, drowning many. In antiquity the strait region was tied to the incense trade and the kingdom of Aksum; some researchers also propose it as a route by which early humans crossed from Africa into Arabia during periods of low sea level.
