Basis (tax law)

Basis, in tax law, is the measure of a taxpayer's investment in an asset or entity interest, used throughout income tax systems to determine gain, loss, depreciation, and the tax consequences of distributions. This article surveys the concept across jurisdictions and contexts, complementing the detailed treatment of the rules in United States federal income taxation.

Basis (tax law)
FieldTax law
ConceptMeasure of investment in property
ApplicationsGain/loss computation, depreciation, entity taxation, distributions

General concept

Most income tax systems tax gains on the disposition of property rather than gross proceeds, and basis is the mechanism that recovers the taxpayer's capital tax-free. Until the taxpayer has recovered their basis, sale proceeds are treated as a return of capital; only the excess is taxable gain. Conversely, loss is recognised only to the extent proceeds fall short of basis.

Basis in entities

In partnership and S corporation taxation, owners maintain outside basis in their interests, increased by contributions and shares of income and decreased by distributions and shares of loss; deductible losses are generally limited to basis. In corporate taxation, shareholders take basis in stock, and corporations take basis in contributed assets, with carryover rules preserving built-in gain or loss.

Comparative note

Other jurisdictions use analogous concepts: the United Kingdom taxes chargeable gains by reference to allowable acquisition cost, and many civil-law systems similarly track acquisition cost adjusted for improvements and depreciation. Details of gifts, inheritances, and reorganisations differ widely.
