Landing at Anzac Cove

Battle details
Date:25 April 1915
Location:Gallipoli Peninsula, Ottoman Empire
Part of:Gallipoli campaign, First World War

Result:ANZAC beachhead established; advance halted short of objectives

The landing at Anzac Cove on Sunday, 25 April 1915, was the first major military action of the Australian and New Zealand Army Corps (ANZAC) in the First World War. Beginning before dawn, troops of the Australian 1st Division and the New Zealand and Australian Division landed on the Aegean coast of the Gallipoli Peninsula in the Ottoman Empire, as the Allied arm of the campaign to seize the peninsula and open the Dardanelles. The bay where they came ashore — a small cove north of Gaba Tepe — became universally known as Anzac Cove.

The landing

The covering force came ashore around 4:30 a.m., landing about 1.6 km north of the intended beach on ground far more rugged than expected. Small parties pushed inland toward the Third Ridge, but Ottoman defenders, reinforced under the direction of officers including Mustafa Kemal, counter-attacked and confined the invaders to a shallow perimeter. By the end of the first day some 16,000 men had landed, at the cost of roughly 2,000 casualties, and the Anzacs dug in on the ridges above the cove.

Legacy

The Anzac beachhead was held, under constant shellfire and through the failed August Offensive, until the force was evacuated in December 1915 — the most successful phase of the campaign. The landing became central to the national identities of Australia and New Zealand: 25 April is commemorated as Anzac Day, with dawn services held at Anzac Cove itself and around both countries, and the cove and its cemeteries are protected as a historic site in Turkey.
