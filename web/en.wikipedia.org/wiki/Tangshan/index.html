Tangshan

Tangshan, an industrial prefecture-level city in northeastern Hebei, China

Tangshan (Chinese: 唐山; pinyin: Tángshān) is a coastal, industrial prefecture-level city in the northeast of Hebei province, China. It lies on the North China Plain, east of Beijing and near the Bohai Sea. The city is known as a centre of coal mining, steel production and ceramics, and as the site of the devastating 1976 Tangshan earthquake.

History

Tangshan's industrial development began in the late Qing dynasty, when the Kaiping coal mines were opened in 1878 and China's first standard-gauge railway and domestically built steam locomotive were associated with the mining enterprises there. The city grew around coal, steel and cement production, earning a reputation as one of China's early industrial bases.

1976 earthquake

On 28 July 1976, an earthquake measuring about magnitude 7.6 (with some estimates higher) struck the city in the early morning, devastating Tangshan and killing at least 242,000 people by official Chinese figures — among the deadliest earthquakes in recorded history. The city was rebuilt in the following decade, and a large earthquake memorial and museum commemorate the disaster.

Economy

Tangshan is one of China's largest steel-producing centres; its crude steel output has at times rivalled that of entire nations. Coal mining, machinery, building materials and ceramics are also significant. The Caofeidian district hosts a major deep-water port development and heavy industry zone, while Tangshan Port ranks among the world's busiest by cargo tonnage.

Geography and administration

The prefecture-level city administers districts including Lubei and Lunan, several county-level cities such as Zunhua, Qian'an and Luanzhou, and counties including Fengnan and Fengrun. The terrain rises from coastal plains on the Bohai Sea to hills in the north, near sections of the Great Wall at places such as Qinglong and the Eastern Qing Tombs in Zunhua.

Culture

Tangshan is associated with pingju opera, which developed in the region, as well as shadow puppetry and Laoting drums. Local specialities include ceramics dating to the Ming dynasty and various coal- and iron-related industrial heritage sites.
