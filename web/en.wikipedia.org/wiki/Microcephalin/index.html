Microcephalin

Microcephalin (MCPH1) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the MCPH1 gene, located on chromosome 8. Mutations in the gene cause primary microcephaly, a condition in which the brain develops to a much smaller size than normal while its basic architecture remains largely intact. Microcephalin is expressed during fetal brain development and plays roles in regulating the cell cycle, chromosome condensation, and the DNA damage response in neural progenitor cells.

Function

Microcephalin contains three BRCT domains, motifs commonly found in proteins involved in DNA repair and checkpoint control. It helps regulate the timing of chromosome condensation by interacting with condensin II, recruits repair proteins to sites of DNA damage, and influences whether neural progenitor cells divide symmetrically (producing two progenitors) or asymmetrically (producing neurons). Loss of function shifts progenitor division in ways that deplete the progenitor pool, producing a smaller brain, particularly a reduced cerebral cortex.

Evolutionary studies

The gene attracted attention in 2005 when studies reported that a particular derived haplotype (haplogroup D) of MCPH1 had risen to high frequency in humans within roughly the last 37,000 years, which the authors interpreted as evidence of recent positive selection, and suggested the variant might have entered modern human populations through interbreeding with an archaic lineage. These claims were widely reported but remain disputed: subsequent studies did not find the proposed associations with brain size or cognition in normal populations, and the selection and introgression hypotheses are not established. Similar claims were made for the gene ASPM, another microcephaly gene.
