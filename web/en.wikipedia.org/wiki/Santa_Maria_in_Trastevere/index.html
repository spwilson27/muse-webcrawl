Santa Maria in Trastevere

The Basilica of Santa Maria in Trastevere (Italian: Basilica di Santa Maria in Trastevere) is a titular minor basilica in the Trastevere district of Rome, Italy, and one of the oldest churches in Rome. The basic floor plan and wall structure date to the 340s, making it one of the first places where Mass was openly celebrated.

History

According to tradition, the site was associated with an early Christian community and the taberna meritoria, a refuge for retired soldiers. Pope Callixtus I is said to have founded a church here in the early 3rd century; the present basilica was built under Pope Innocent II between 1138 and 1143, reusing ancient Roman columns from the Baths of Caracalla.

Art and architecture

The church is celebrated for its medieval mosaics. The apse mosaic of the Coronation of the Virgin, and below it scenes from the Life of the Virgin by Pietro Cavallini (1291), are masterpieces of medieval Roman art. The façade mosaic (12th–13th century) depicts the Virgin enthroned between ten women with lamps. The nave's coffered wooden ceiling was designed by Domenichino, who also painted the Assumption at its centre. The 22 granite columns of the nave are spolia from Roman buildings.

The piazza

The church fronts the Piazza di Santa Maria in Trastevere, the heart of the district, with an octagonal fountain that is one of the oldest in Rome and was restored by architects including Bramante and Carlo Fontana.
