British Rail Class 43 (HST)

British Rail Class 43
Type: Diesel-electric power car
Built: 1975–1982, Crewe
Number built: 197
Top speed: 125 mph (201 km/h); record 148 mph

The British Rail Class 43 is the power car of the InterCity 125 High Speed Train (HST), introduced in 1976, which transformed long-distance rail travel in Britain and remains an icon of British engineering.

Design

Two Class 43 power cars, each with a Paxman Valenta (later MTU) diesel engine of about 2,250 hp, sandwich seven to nine Mark 3 coaches. In 1987 an HST set the world diesel speed record at 238 km/h (148 mph).

Service

Originally on Western, Eastern and Midland routes, HSTs served for over four decades; many were withdrawn after replacement by Class 800/802 IET trains, with shortened "Castle" sets in the West and ScotRail "Inter7City" sets continuing, and exports to Mexico and Nigeria.
