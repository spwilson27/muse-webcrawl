Godaan

AuthorMunshi Premchand
LanguageHindi
Published1936
Title meaning"The Gift of a Cow"

Godaan (Hindi: गोदान, "The Gift of a Cow") is a Hindi novel by Munshi Premchand, published in 1936, shortly before the author's death. It is regarded as one of the greatest novels of modern Hindi literature and as Premchand's masterpiece, a sweeping portrait of peasant life under colonial rule, structured around the contrast between village and city.

Plot

The story centres on Hori, a poor peasant in the village of Belari, whose deepest wish is to own a cow, a symbol of status and piety. He acquires one on credit from a cowherd, but the animal is poisoned amid family quarrels, and the debt begins a spiral of obligations to moneylenders, landlords and the village panchayat. Hori and his steadfast wife Dhania struggle against fines, rent demands and social disgrace, sheltering their son Gobar and the pregnant Jhunia despite the village's censure. Interwoven is the urban story of the zamindar Rai Sahib, the banker Khanna, the philosopher Professor Mehta and the doctor Malti. In the celebrated closing scene, the dying Hori has no cow to offer as the ritual gift of godaan; Dhania places the few coins earned from selling rope in his hand as his gift instead.

Themes and legacy

The novel anatomises the exploitation of the Indian peasantry by landlords, moneylenders, police and priesthood, and the crushing weight of debt and caste obligation, while giving its peasant characters, especially Dhania, dignity and moral force. It has been translated into English and many other languages, adapted for film in 1963, and staged and televised repeatedly; it remains a staple of Indian school and university syllabuses.
