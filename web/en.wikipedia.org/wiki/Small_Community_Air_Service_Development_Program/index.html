Small Community Air Service Development Program

The Small Community Air Service Development Program (SCASDP) is a program of the United States Department of Transportation designed to help small communities in the United States enhance their air service. The program provides grants to communities to address air service deficiencies, such as excessively high fares and insufficient service.

History and purpose

The program was authorized by the Wendell H. Ford Aviation Investment and Reform Act for the 21st Century (AIR-21), signed into law in April 2000. It is operated by the Office of Aviation Analysis within the Department of Transportation. Unlike the Essential Air Service program, which subsidizes ongoing flights, SCASDP offers one-time federal grants that communities can use for revenue guarantees to airlines, marketing campaigns, studies, and other initiatives intended to attract or retain commercial air service.

Eligibility and funding

Congress has typically appropriated between 6 and 20 million dollars a year for the program. Small-hub and non-hub airports, and communities without any service, may apply; preference is given to communities where air fares are higher than the national average or where service is inadequate. In each grant round, the Department selects a limited number of communities — historically, no more than 40 communities per year, with limits on how many may be in any one state.

Usage

Common uses of grant funds include minimum revenue guarantees offered to carriers to launch a new route, joint marketing with airlines, fare studies, and passenger leakage analyses. The effectiveness of grants varies, and the Department reports periodically on which funded projects succeeded in establishing self-sustaining service.
