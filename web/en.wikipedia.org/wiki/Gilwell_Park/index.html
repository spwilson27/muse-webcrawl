Gilwell Park is a camp site and activity centre in Waltham Abbey, Essex, on the edge of Epping Forest, and a place of international significance to the Scout movement. Purchased in 1919 for the Boy Scouts Association, it became the home of leader training.

Wood Badge

The first Wood Badge training course was held at Gilwell in September 1919 under Francis "Skipper" Gidney. Holders of the Wood Badge wear beads copied from a necklace associated with Baden-Powell, and leaders trained at Gilwell are members of the 1st Gilwell Park Scout Group wherever they live.

Facilities

The estate includes the White House (built 1754), camp fields, woodland, and modern activity and conference facilities. It hosts national Scout events and is also hired for weddings and corporate use.
