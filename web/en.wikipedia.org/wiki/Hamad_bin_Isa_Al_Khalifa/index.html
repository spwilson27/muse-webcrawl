Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa

Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa (Arabic: حمد بن عيسى آل خليفة, born 28 January 1950) is the King of Bahrain, reigning since 1999. He became Emir upon the death of his father, Isa bin Salman Al Khalifa, on 6 March 1999, and proclaimed himself King in 2002 following constitutional changes that converted the emirate into a kingdom. He is a member of the House of Khalifa, which has ruled Bahrain since 1783.

Early life and reign

Hamad was educated in Bahrain, at The Leys School in Cambridge, and at the Mons Officer Cadet School and the US Army Command and General Staff College. He founded the Bahrain Defence Force and served as its commander before succeeding his father. His early reign brought political reforms including the release of political prisoners, the National Action Charter of 2001, and the 2002 constitution, which re-established an elected parliament and gave women the right to vote and stand for election. His government faced sustained unrest during the 2011 Bahraini uprising, part of the Arab Spring, which was suppressed with the help of Saudi-led Peninsula Shield forces; an independent commission of inquiry (the BICI) documented abuses and recommended reforms.

Personal life

King Hamad has four wives and twelve children; his eldest son, Salman bin Hamad Al Khalifa, is Crown Prince and, since 2020, Prime Minister. The King is known for interests in falconry, golf, and the preservation of Bahraini heritage, including the restoration of forts and traditional houses.
