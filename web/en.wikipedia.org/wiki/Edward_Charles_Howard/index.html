Edward Charles Howard FRS (28 May 1774 – 27 September 1816) was a British chemist, the youngest brother of the Catholic Duke of Norfolk. He has been called the first chemical engineer of any eminence.

Work on fulminates

In 1800 Howard published his discovery of mercury fulminate, a powerful primary explosive prepared from mercury, nitric acid and alcohol. His paper won the Royal Society's Copley Medal. Mercury fulminate later became the standard detonating compound in percussion caps and blasting caps throughout the nineteenth century, making possible reliable ignition of firearms and explosives.

Meteorites and other work

Howard performed the first systematic chemical analyses of meteoric iron and stony meteorites, showing (with the mineralogist Jacques Louis, comte de Bournon) that they contained nickel-iron alloys unlike terrestrial rocks — evidence supporting their extraterrestrial origin. Howardite, a class of achondrite meteorites, is named after him. He also did important work for the sugar industry, developing vacuum-pan evaporation and refining methods; his vacuum pan, patented in 1813, transformed sugar refining.
