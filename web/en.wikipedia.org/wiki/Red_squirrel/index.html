The red squirrel (Sciurus vulgaris), also called the Eurasian red squirrel, is a species of tree squirrel in the genus Sciurus common throughout Europe and Asia. It is an arboreal, primarily herbivorous rodent.

Description

The red squirrel has a typical head-and-body length of 19 to 23 cm, a tail length of 15 to 20 cm, and a mass of 250 to 340 g. Its coat ranges from bright red to almost black, always with a white underside, and it grows distinctive ear tufts in winter. It sheds and regrows its coat twice a year.

Ecology and decline

Red squirrels feed on tree seeds, fungi, nuts, berries, and young shoots, and store food in caches. In Britain and Ireland their numbers have declined sharply due to competition and disease (squirrelpox) spread by the introduced North American grey squirrel, and the species is the subject of active conservation programs.
