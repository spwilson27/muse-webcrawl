Little Skellig (Irish: Sceilig Bheag) is the smaller of the two Skellig Islands off the coast of County Kerry, Ireland, the larger being Skellig Michael. Rising to about 134 metres above the Atlantic, the island is never visited by the public because of its importance as a seabird sanctuary.

Wildlife

Little Skellig hosts one of the largest colonies of northern gannets in the world, with on the order of 70,000 pairs — giving the rock its distinctive white appearance from the sea. It also supports a large population of Manx shearwaters and smaller numbers of European storm petrels and other seabirds. Together with Skellig Michael, it is protected as a Special Protection Area for birds and is managed as a nature reserve.

Geography

The island lies roughly 11 kilometres off the Iveragh Peninsula and under a kilometre from Skellig Michael. Its steep, craggy slopes made it unsuitable for the monastic settlement found on its neighbour; there are no buildings of significance and no landing for visitors, though boat tours circling the island are popular.
