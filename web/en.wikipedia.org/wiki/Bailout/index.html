Bailout

Bailout (image placeholder)

A bailout is the provision of financial help to a corporation, financial institution, or country that is on the brink of failure or bankruptcy, typically by a government seeking to prevent wider economic damage.

Rationale

Governments bail out firms considered "too big to fail", whose collapse could cascade through the financial system. Support can take the form of loans, cash injections, guarantees, or purchase of distressed assets. Critics argue bailouts create moral hazard by insulating institutions from the consequences of risky behaviour, while supporters argue they avert economic catastrophe.

Notable examples

Famous bailouts include the 1979 rescue of Chrysler, the 2008 Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) in the United States during the financial crisis, which supported banks and automakers including General Motors, and the sovereign debt bailouts of Greece, Ireland, and Portugal during the eurozone crisis, funded by the EU and IMF.

Bail-in

A related mechanism, the bail-in, forces a failing institution's creditors and shareholders to bear losses by writing down debt or converting it to equity, rather than using public funds.
