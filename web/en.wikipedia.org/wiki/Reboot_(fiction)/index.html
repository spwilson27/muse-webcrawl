Reboot (fiction)

In serial fiction, a reboot is a new start of an established fictional universe, work, or series that restarts its continuity, resetting it to the beginning. A reboot usually discards all previous continuity in the work's history, re-inventing its characters, plot lines and backstory for a new audience. The term derives from the computing term "reboot", meaning to restart a computer system.

Definition and usage

A reboot differs from a remake, which retells essentially the same story, and from a sequel or prequel, which continue an existing continuity. It also differs from a retcon (retroactive continuity), which alters selected established facts while keeping most of the continuity intact. A "soft reboot" keeps some elements of prior continuity while largely starting fresh, often functioning simultaneously as a sequel, as in Star Wars: The Force Awakens (2015) or Halloween (2018).

Examples

Prominent film reboots include Batman Begins (2005), which restarted the Batman film series in Christopher Nolan's distinct style; Casino Royale (2006), which reset the James Bond timeline; Star Trek (2009), which used a time-travel device to create an alternate timeline; and The Amazing Spider-Man (2012). In comics, DC Comics' Crisis on Infinite Earths (1985–86) and The New 52 (2011) rebooted decades of accumulated continuity. Video game examples include Tomb Raider (2013), God of War (2018, a soft reboot) and Doom (2016).

Rationale

Studios reboot franchises to attract new audiences unburdened by complex backstory, to update tone and technology, to recast ageing stars, and to revive valuable intellectual property whose prior continuity is considered limiting or commercially exhausted. Critics sometimes view the prevalence of reboots as evidence of creative risk-aversion in franchise filmmaking.
