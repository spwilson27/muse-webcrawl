Wards of Japan

A ward (区, ku) is a subdivision of one of the large cities of Japan that is large enough to have been designated by government ordinance. Wards are local entities directly controlled by the municipal government and handle functions such as resident registration, health insurance and local taxation.

Types of wards

There are two main kinds. Special wards (特別区, tokubetsu-ku) exist only in Tokyo: the 23 special wards together form the core of the metropolis, each functioning almost as an independent city with its own elected mayor and assembly. Administrative wards (行政区, gyōsei-ku) are found in the designated cities (政令指定都市) such as Osaka, Yokohama, Nagoya, Sapporo and Fukuoka; these wards do not elect their own mayors and serve as branch offices of the city government, though they have elected representation through the city assembly.

Background

The ward system grew out of the municipal organisation of the Meiji period. Tokyo's 23 special wards were created in 1947 under the Local Autonomy Law, inheriting the boundaries of the wards of the former City of Tokyo, which had been abolished as a municipality in 1943.
