Qadisha Valley

The Qadisha Valley (Arabic: وادي قاديشا), also romanized as Kadisha, is a gorge carved by the Qadisha River in the Bsharri and Zgharta districts of northern Lebanon. "Qadisha" derives from an Aramaic root meaning "holy", and the valley is often called the Holy Valley. Together with the Forest of the Cedars of God, it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1998.

Christian heritage

For centuries the steep cliffs of the valley sheltered Christian — especially Maronite — hermitages and monasteries, some cut into the rock. Notable sites include the monasteries of Qozhaya (Saint Anthony), Qannubin (Our Lady of Qannubin), Saint Elisha, and Our Lady of Hawqa. Qannubin served as a seat of the Maronite patriarchate for centuries.

Geography

The valley runs below the village of Bsharri, birthplace of poet Kahlil Gibran, whose tomb and museum stand nearby. The Qadisha River rises in a grotto beneath the cedars and flows to the Mediterranean near Tripoli.
