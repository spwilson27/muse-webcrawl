Designer

A designer is a person who plans the form, look, or workings of something before it is made, by preparing drawings, models, specifications, or other detailed plans. Designers work across many fields in which design is applied, balancing functional requirements with aesthetics, ergonomics, cost, materials, and the needs of users. In common usage the word is often associated with fashion and the applied arts, but the role spans engineering, architecture, digital media, and industry.

Types of designers

  • Graphic designer — visual communication: typography, layout, branding, print and digital media.
  • Fashion designer — clothing and accessories.
  • Industrial / product designer — manufactured goods, from furniture to electronics.
  • Interior designer — interior spaces.
  • UX / UI designer — user experience and interfaces of software and websites.
  • Web designer — websites and web applications.
  • Game designer — rules, systems, and content of games.
  • Architectural designer — buildings and built environments (working alongside architects).
  • Automotive designer — vehicles.
  • Set, costume, lighting, and sound designers — theatre, film, and television production.

Role and process

Design typically proceeds through research, ideation, sketching and prototyping, testing with users, and refinement. Unlike the artist, whose work is often an end in itself, the designer usually works to a brief and to constraints set by a client, manufacturer, or context. Modern design education ranges from art-school studio traditions — influenced by the Bauhaus — to engineering-led and human-centred design methodologies.
