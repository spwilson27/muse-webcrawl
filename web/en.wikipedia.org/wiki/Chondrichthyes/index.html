Chondrichthyes

Chondrichthyes
Kingdom: Animalia · Phylum: Chordata
Class: Chondrichthyes
Subclasses: Elasmobranchii (sharks, rays), Holocephali (chimaeras)
Fossil range: Silurian – present

Chondrichthyes is a class of jawed fish whose skeletons are made primarily of cartilage rather than bone. It includes sharks, rays, skates, sawfish and chimaeras — more than 1,200 living species.

Anatomy

Cartilaginous fish have paired fins, paired nostrils, placoid scales (dermal denticles) in elasmobranchs, and five to seven pairs of gill slits. They lack a swim bladder and maintain buoyancy with large oil-rich livers. Many possess electroreception through the ampullae of Lorenzini and a keen sense of smell.

Reproduction

Fertilisation is internal. Species may be oviparous (egg-laying, e.g. some sharks and skates with 'mermaid's purse' egg cases), viviparous, or ovoviviparous. Reproduction is generally slow, making many species vulnerable to overfishing.

Evolution and conservation

The group diverged in the Silurian–Devonian. Modern sharks appear by the Jurassic. Today a large fraction of species are threatened, chiefly by fisheries and the shark-fin trade. See Osteichthyes (bony fish) for the sister group.
