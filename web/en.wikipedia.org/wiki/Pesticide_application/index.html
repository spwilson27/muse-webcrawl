Pesticide application

Pesticide application refers to the practical way in which pesticides (including herbicides, fungicides, insecticides and bactericides) are delivered to their biological targets, such as a pest organism, a crop or other plants. The effectiveness of a pesticide depends heavily on its formulation and the way it is applied: too little may fail to control the pest, while too much wastes product, increases cost and can cause environmental harm.

Methods

The most common method is hydraulic spraying, in which the pesticide is mixed with water and applied through nozzles that produce droplets of a controlled size. Equipment ranges from hand-held and knapsack sprayers used by gardeners and smallholders to tractor-mounted or self-propelled boom sprayers, and aircraft used for aerial application over large areas. Other methods include dusting, granular application to soil, seed treatment, fumigation, and ultra-low-volume spraying.

Drift and safety

Spray drift — the movement of droplets away from the target by wind — can damage neighbouring crops, contaminate water and expose people and wildlife. Applicators are trained and, in many jurisdictions, licensed; they must follow label instructions, observe buffer zones and wear personal protective equipment. Weather conditions, nozzle choice and droplet size are managed to reduce drift and off-target effects, and application practices are a key element of integrated pest management.
