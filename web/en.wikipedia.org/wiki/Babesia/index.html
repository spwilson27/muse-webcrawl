Babesia is a genus of apicomplexan parasites that infect red blood cells of mammals and birds, causing the tick-borne disease babesiosis. The genus is named after Romanian bacteriologist Victor Babeș, who described the organisms in cattle in 1888.

Life cycle and transmission

Babesia species are transmitted by ixodid ticks, especially Ixodes scapularis in North America — the same tick that transmits Lyme disease. Inside the host, merozoites invade erythrocytes and divide, sometimes forming characteristic tetrads ("Maltese cross" forms). Babesia microti is the main cause of human babesiosis in the United States; B. divergens causes severe disease in Europe, particularly in splenectomised patients.

Disease

Infection ranges from asymptomatic to malaria-like illness with fever, haemolytic anaemia and jaundice, and can be fatal in immunocompromised people. Treatment typically uses atovaquone plus azithromycin, or clindamycin plus quinine for severe cases. Babesia is also an important pathogen of cattle (Texas cattle fever) and dogs.
