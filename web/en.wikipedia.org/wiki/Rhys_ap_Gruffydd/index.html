Rhys ap Gruffydd
Born: c. 1132
Died: 28 April 1197
Title: Prince of Deheubarth (the Lord Rhys)
Reign: 1155–1197

Rhys ap Gruffydd (c. 1132 – 28 April 1197) was the ruler of the kingdom of Deheubarth in south Wales from 1155 to 1197. He is commonly known as The Lord Rhys (Welsh: Yr Arglwydd Rhys) and was the most powerful Welsh ruler of his generation, successfully resisting and then negotiating with Henry II of England.

Rise to power

Rhys was a son of Gruffydd ap Rhys and grandson of Rhys ap Tewdwr. After the deaths of his father and elder brothers, he rebuilt Deheubarth's fortunes by campaigning against the Norman lords of the south during the Anarchy in England, capturing castles including Llansteffan and Aberystwyth.

Settlement with Henry II

Although Henry II invaded in 1163 and forced Rhys's submission, by 1171 the two reached a lasting settlement: Rhys paid homage yet was recognised as ruler of a large part of south Wales and later made the king's justiciar in south Wales. The peace held for the rest of Henry's reign, and Rhys even attended the royal court at Gloucester and Woodstock.

Culture and later years

At Christmas 1176, Rhys held a great festival of music and poetry at Cardigan Castle — regarded as the first recorded eisteddfod. He patronised the Cistercian abbeys of Strata Florida and Whitland and Welsh poets. After Henry II's death in 1189 relations with the English Crown deteriorated, and Rhys spent his last years campaigning against Marcher lords. He died in 1197 and was buried in St Davids Cathedral; disputes among his many sons soon fragmented his kingdom.
