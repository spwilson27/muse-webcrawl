Economy of India

[Image: the Bandra-Worli Sea Link and Mumbai skyline, symbol of India's growing economy]

The economy of India is the world's fifth-largest by nominal GDP (around US$3.5–4 trillion in the mid-2020s) and third-largest at purchasing power parity; it has been among the fastest-growing major economies, at roughly 6–8% a year in recent years. Services — especially information technology and business services centred on Bengaluru, Hyderabad and other hubs — supply over half of output, while agriculture still employs over 40% of workers.

Reform and structure

Independent India (1947) began with planning and the 'Licence Raj' of permits and state control, yielding the modest 'Hindu rate of growth' (~3.5%). The 1991 balance-of-payments crisis triggered liberalisation under Finance Minister Manmohan Singh — delicensing, trade opening and privatisation — after which growth roughly doubled. Modern features include a huge informal sector, world-scale digital public infrastructure (Aadhaar identity, UPI payments), leading generics-pharmaceutical and IT exports, large remittance inflows (the world's largest, over $100 billion a year recently), and persistent challenges: job creation for a young population, infrastructure, education quality and female labour-force participation. India overtook China as the most populous country in 2023.
