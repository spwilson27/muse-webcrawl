Anthocyanin

Anthocyanins (from Greek anthos, "flower", and kyaneos, "dark blue") are water-soluble vacuolar pigments that, depending on their pH, may appear red, purple, blue, or black. They belong to the flavonoid class of molecules and are glycosides of anthocyanidins. Food plants rich in anthocyanins include blueberries, raspberries, black rice, red cabbage, grapes and aubergines.

Colour and chemistry

Anthocyanins act as pH indicators: in acidic conditions they are red, at neutral pH purple, and in alkaline conditions blue to green. In plants they colour flowers (attracting pollinators), fruits (attracting seed dispersers) and autumn leaves. Several hundred distinct anthocyanins have been identified, built from anthocyanidin cores such as cyanidin, delphinidin and pelargonidin attached to sugars and sometimes organic acids.

Uses and health research

Anthocyanins are used as natural food colourings (E163). Diets rich in anthocyanin-containing foods are associated epidemiologically with cardiovascular benefits, and the compounds show antioxidant activity in vitro, though the clinical significance in humans remains under study. Red cabbage juice is a popular home and classroom pH indicator.
