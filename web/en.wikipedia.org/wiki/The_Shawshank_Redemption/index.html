The Shawshank Redemption

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Andy Dufresne standing in the rain outside the prison, arms raised to the sky, in the film's emblem of hard-won freedom]

The Shawshank Redemption is a 1994 American drama film written and directed by Frank Darabont, adapted from Stephen King's 1982 novella Rita Hayworth and Shawshank Redemption. Tim Robbins plays Andy Dufresne, a banker sentenced to life at Shawshank State Penitentiary for murders he says he did not commit, whose quiet intelligence, friendship with the lifer 'Red' Redding (Morgan Freeman, also the narrator) and nineteen years of patience build to one of the most beloved endings in cinema.

A box-office failure on release (it grossed modestly against its budget and was re-released after seven Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture), the film became a phenomenon on video and cable, and has topped IMDb's user-voted Top 250 since 2008.

Themes and craft

Hope against institutional time is the subject: Andy's library campaign (six years of letters for funding), his broadcast of Mozart over the prison speakers (the Marriage of Figaro duet, in the film's most famous sequence), the roof-tarring beer, and the poster hiding a tunnel dug a spoonful at a time. Darabont's classical direction and Roger Deakins's photography give King's story the patience it preaches.

Reception

King sold the rights for a token sum and has called it among the best adaptations of his work; the film's rehabilitation from flop to fixture is the modern parable of word-of-mouth. The old Ohio State Reformatory at Mansfield, its location, is now a museum drawing pilgrims to the cell, the tunnel's end and the oak tree of the final scenes.
