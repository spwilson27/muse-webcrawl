National Grange

Founded: December 4, 1867
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Type: Fraternal and advocacy organisation
Full name: National Grange of the Order of Patrons of Husbandry

The National Grange, formally the National Grange of the Order of Patrons of Husbandry, is an American social and advocacy organisation for farmers and rural communities, founded in 1867.

History

The Grange was founded by Oliver Hudson Kelley and six associates after a tour of the war-torn South convinced Kelley that a national farmers' fraternity could rebuild sectional ties. It grew explosively in the 1870s, organising cooperatives, lobbying against railroad monopolies, and securing state "Granger laws" regulating freight rates — upheld in landmark cases such as Munn v. Illinois (1877).

Structure and legacy

The Grange is organised in local (subordinate), county (Pomona), state and national levels, with a seven-degree ritual modelled partly on Freemasonry; unusually for its era, women held full membership and office from the start. The Grange campaigned for rural free mail delivery, the extension service, direct election of senators and women's suffrage. Local Grange halls remain community centres in many rural towns.
