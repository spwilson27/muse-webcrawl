Single-context recording

FieldArchaeology
Developed1970s, Museum of London
RelatedHarris matrix

Single-context recording is a practice in archaeology in which each discrete archaeological context (a layer, fill, cut or feature) is recorded individually on its own standardised record sheet, plan and set of photographs, rather than describing a whole site in narrative form. It was developed in the 1970s by the Museum of London and is now standard in British commercial archaeology.

Method

Each context is given a unique number and described in terms of its physical characteristics, relationships to other contexts, and interpretation. The stratigraphic relationships between contexts are summarised in a Harris matrix, allowing the sequence of deposition and cutting events to be reconstructed.

History

The system emerged from large urban excavations in London, where deep, complex stratigraphy made traditional methods unmanageable. Edward Harris's work on stratigraphic sequencing, published in 1979, provided the complementary matrix method.

Use

Single-context planning draws each context separately at scale, so that plans can be combined in GIS to reconstruct phases of a site. The records form the primary site archive used in post-excavation analysis and publication.
