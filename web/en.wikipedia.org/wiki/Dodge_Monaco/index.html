Dodge Monaco

The Dodge Monaco is an automobile marketed by Dodge in three separate generations. It was introduced in 1965 as a full-size hardtop coupe, a flagship personal luxury model. The name returned for mid-size and full-size sedans through the 1970s. A final generation (1990–1992), derived from the Eagle Premier and built at Bramalea, Ontario, served as Dodge's full-size front-wheel-drive sedan before being replaced by the Intrepid. The Monaco is well known in popular culture as the "Bluesmobile" model used by police fleets, as in the 1980 film The Blues Brothers (a 1974 Monaco).
