Huang Tingjian

Huang Tingjian (Chinese: 黃庭堅; 1045–1105), courtesy name Luzhi and art name Shangu Daoren ("the Taoist of the Mountain Valley"), was a Chinese calligrapher, painter and poet of the Northern Song dynasty. He is counted among the Four Masters of the Song dynasty in calligraphy, together with Su Shi, Mi Fu and Cai Xiang, and was the leading disciple of Su Shi in literature.

Life

Huang was born in Fenning (present-day Xiushui County, Jiangxi). He passed the jinshi examination in 1067 and served in a series of provincial posts and as an editor of the Veritable Records of Emperor Shenzong. His association with Su Shi's faction brought repeated political exile under the reformist administrations; in 1103 he was banished to Yizhou in Guangxi, where he died in 1105.

Poetry and calligraphy

Huang founded the Jiangxi school of poetry, emphasising dense allusion, rigorous craft and the transformation of earlier poets' lines — his maxims "change the bone" and "seize the embryo and change it" became lasting (and contested) principles of Chinese poetic composition. In calligraphy his running and cursive scripts are famous for their long, radiating strokes — likened by critics to a swordsman leaning into a stroke or a boatman pushing with a long pole — while his monumental work The Scroll of Poems and the Fu Bo Shen Tang Ji are treasured masterpieces. His slender-gold-influenced regular script and his large-character inscriptions influenced calligraphy throughout East Asia, and he was revered in Japan as a model master.
