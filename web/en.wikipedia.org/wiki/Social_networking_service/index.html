A social networking service (SNS), or social networking site, is an online platform that people use to build social networks or social relationships with other people who share similar personal or career content, interests, activities, backgrounds, or real-life connections. Following danah boyd and Nicole Ellison's widely used definition, social network sites allow individuals to construct a public or semi-public profile, articulate a list of other users with whom they share a connection, and view and traverse those connections.

History

Early examples included Six Degrees (1997), LiveJournal and Friendster (2002), and Myspace and LinkedIn (2003). Facebook, founded in 2004 at Harvard, opened to the general public in 2006 and became the world's largest social network; YouTube (2005), Twitter (2006), Instagram (2010), Snapchat (2011), and TikTok (internationally from 2017) shifted the emphasis toward media sharing and algorithmic feeds. By the 2020s, billions of people — a majority of the world's online population — used at least one social networking service.

Features

Typical features include user profiles, friend or follower lists, news feeds, groups, events, photo and video sharing, reactions ("likes"), and private messaging. Business models rest mainly on targeted advertising informed by user data, which has made privacy practices a persistent controversy.

Impact

Social networking services have transformed communication, marketing, journalism, and politics, enabling movements and rapid mobilisation as well as the spread of misinformation, harassment, and addictive usage patterns. Research and public debate continue over effects on mental health — particularly among adolescents — attention, polarisation, and democratic discourse, and regulators in the EU, US, and elsewhere have moved toward greater platform accountability.
