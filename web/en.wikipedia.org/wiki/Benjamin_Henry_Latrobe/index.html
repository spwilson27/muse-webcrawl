Benjamin Henry Latrobe

Benjamin Henry Boneval Latrobe (May 1, 1764 – September 3, 1820) was a British-American neoclassical architect who emigrated to the United States and is regarded as the first professional architect in the country. He is best known for his design of the United States Capitol and the Baltimore Basilica, the first Roman Catholic cathedral built in the United States.

Early life and training

Born in Yorkshire to Moravian parents, Latrobe was educated in England and Germany and trained in London under engineer John Smeaton and architect Samuel Pepys Cockerell. Financial difficulties and the death of his first wife prompted his emigration to Virginia in 1796.

Career in America

Latrobe quickly won major commissions, including the Virginia State Penitentiary and the Bank of Pennsylvania in Philadelphia, a landmark of the Greek Revival. President Thomas Jefferson appointed him Surveyor of Public Buildings in 1803, and he took charge of completing the U.S. Capitol, designing its House and Senate wings; after the building was burned in the War of 1812 he returned to supervise its restoration. He also engineered Philadelphia's pioneering municipal waterworks.

Death and legacy

Latrobe died of yellow fever in New Orleans in 1820 while supervising a waterworks project there. His influence, through works such as the Baltimore Basilica and his training of students including Robert Mills and William Strickland, shaped the early architecture of the American republic.
