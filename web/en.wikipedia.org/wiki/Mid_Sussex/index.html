Mid Sussex is a local government district in West Sussex, England. Its administrative centre is Haywards Heath. The district was formed on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, by merging urban and rural districts around the towns of East Grinstead, Haywards Heath and Burgess Hill, together with the village of Ardingly and surrounding parishes. It lies inland in the north-east of the county, bordering Surrey and East Sussex.

Geography

The district spans the High Weald Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty in the north and part of the South Downs National Park in the south. The three largest towns — Burgess Hill, East Grinstead and Haywards Heath — are commuter towns served by the Brighton Main Line to London. Notable places include Wakehurst (the country estate of the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew), the Bluebell Railway heritage line, Ardingly Reservoir and the Ouse Valley Viaduct (Balcombe Viaduct).

Governance

Mid Sussex District Council provides district-level services, with West Sussex County Council providing county services. The area is covered by parliamentary constituencies centred on Mid Sussex and East Grinstead and Uckfield. Elections to the district council are held every four years.
