Sphingomyelin

Sphingomyelin (SPH) is a type of sphingolipid found in animal cell membranes, especially in the membranous myelin sheath that surrounds some nerve cell axons. It usually consists of phosphocholine and ceramide, or a phosphoethanolamine head group. In humans, sphingomyelin represents roughly 85% of all sphingolipids.

Structure and function

Unlike most membrane phospholipids, which are built on a glycerol backbone, sphingomyelin is based on the amino alcohol sphingosine. Together with cholesterol it forms ordered membrane domains ("lipid rafts") involved in signalling. In the myelin sheath it provides electrical insulation that speeds nerve conduction.

Metabolism and disease

Sphingomyelin is broken down by the enzyme sphingomyelinase to ceramide and phosphocholine. Deficiency of acid sphingomyelinase causes Niemann–Pick disease types A and B, in which sphingomyelin accumulates in lysosomes. Ceramide generated from sphingomyelin is also an important second messenger in apoptosis and stress responses.
