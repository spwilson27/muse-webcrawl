Wanda Mound

Location: Nowa Huta, Kraków, Poland
Height: about 14 m
Coordinates: 50°04′N 20°04′E

Wanda Mound (Polish: Kopiec Wandy) is an artificial burial mound in Kraków, Poland, in the district of Nowa Huta, traditionally said to be the tomb of Princess Wanda, the legendary daughter of King Krakus, founder of Kraków. It is one of four historic memorial mounds in the city, alongside Krakus Mound, Kościuszko Mound, and Piłsudski Mound.

Legend

According to legend, Wanda ruled Kraków after her father's death and refused marriage to the German prince Rytygier (Rüdiger), who invaded her lands. Wanda led her army to victory but, to prevent further wars and protect her people — or, in some versions, because she had vowed herself to the gods — she threw herself into the Vistula and drowned. Her body was recovered downstream near the present site of the mound, where her people raised the tumulus in her honour. The name of the Vistula itself was folk-etymologically linked to Wanda's story.

History and description

The mound, roughly 14 metres high and some 50 metres in diameter at the base, is generally dated to the early medieval period, around the 7th–8th centuries, like the neighbouring Krakus Mound. Archaeological investigations have not confirmed Wanda's burial; the mound may originally have served as a watch point or cult site aligned with solar events. A marble statue of Wanda, designed by Jan Matejko, once crowned the summit; today the mound is a grass-covered landmark within industrial Nowa Huta, near the ArcelorMittal steelworks, and offers views over the Vistula valley. In the 19th century the Austrians incorporated the mound into the ring of fortifications of the Kraków Fortress.
