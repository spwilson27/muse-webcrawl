Xeroderma pigmentosum

SpecialtyMedical genetics, dermatology
CauseDefective nucleotide excision repair (NER) of UV-induced DNA damage
InheritanceAutosomal recessive
FrequencyAbout 1 in 1,000,000 in the US and Europe; higher in Japan and North Africa

Xeroderma pigmentosum (XP) is a rare inherited disorder characterised by extreme sensitivity to ultraviolet (UV) radiation from sunlight. People with XP develop sunburn, freckling and skin cancers at a very young age because their cells cannot repair UV-induced DNA damage by the nucleotide excision repair pathway.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms usually appear in infancy or early childhood. Affected children may sunburn severely after only brief sun exposure, develop dense freckling on sun-exposed skin before the age of two, and suffer eye involvement including photophobia, conjunctivitis and corneal damage. Without rigorous sun protection, skin cancers — basal cell and squamous cell carcinomas and melanoma — commonly arise in childhood; the risk of skin cancer before age 20 is increased roughly a thousand-fold. Around a quarter to a third of patients develop progressive neurological degeneration, including hearing loss, cognitive decline and loss of reflexes.

Genetics

XP is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. Classic XP results from mutations in any of seven genes (XPA through XPG) encoding components of the nucleotide excision repair machinery; a variant form (XP-V) is caused by mutation in the POLH gene, encoding DNA polymerase eta, which normally replicates past UV lesions.

Management

There is no cure. Management centres on complete avoidance of UV exposure — protective clothing, UV-blocking films on windows, and lifestyle adjustments (patients are sometimes called "children of the night") — combined with vigilant dermatological surveillance and early excision of tumours. Vitamin D supplementation and psychological support are also important.
