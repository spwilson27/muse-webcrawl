Executive producers oversee productions at the business and financing level.

An executive producer (EP) is a senior producer who, depending on the medium, supervises the creation and financing of a film, television series, album, or video game, without necessarily being involved in day-to-day production. The title is one of the most variable in the entertainment industries: an executive producer may be the person who raised the money, the owner of the underlying rights, a studio executive, or a star whose name helps secure funding.

Film

In film, executive producers are typically financiers, distributors, or representatives of a production company who make major business decisions and secure the budget, while producers and line producers handle daily production. Because the credit is often given in exchange for financing or rights, large studio films may list many executive producers.

Television

In television the term has the opposite connotation: the executive producer is usually the most senior creative authority on a series. The head writer who runs a show — the showrunner — is almost always credited as executive producer, and senior writers are commonly promoted to executive producer as they take on managerial duties; credits such as "co-executive producer" and "supervising producer" form a hierarchy beneath it. The Writers Guild of America regulates these credits on scripted series.

Music and games

In music, an executive producer oversees the album's budget and overall direction, sometimes selecting songs and producers. In video games, executive producers are senior managers at a publisher or studio responsible for a project's schedule, budget, and delivery across development teams.
