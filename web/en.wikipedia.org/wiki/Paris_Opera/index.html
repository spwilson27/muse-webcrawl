Paris Opera

The Paris Opera (French: Opéra de Paris, officially the Académie nationale de musique) is the primary opera and ballet company of France. Founded in 1669 by Louis XIV as the Académie d'Opéra, it is among the oldest opera institutions in the world. The company presents productions at two Paris venues: the Palais Garnier (opened 1875), used mainly for ballet, and the Opéra Bastille (opened 1989), its principal opera stage. Its ballet company, the Paris Opera Ballet, is the oldest national ballet company in the world.

History

Louis XIV granted Pierre Perrin a monopoly for sung theatre in French, and in 1672 the enterprise passed to Jean-Baptiste Lully, who as director of the renamed Académie Royale de Musique effectively created French opera with his tragédies en musique. The company occupied a succession of theatres, including the Salle Le Peletier (1821–1873), where romantic ballet reached its height and the white tutu tradition began.

Palais Garnier and Opéra Bastille

Napoleon III commissioned a new house; Charles Garnier's opulent Beaux-Arts Palais Garnier was inaugurated in 1875 and gave its name — and its famous underground "lake" — to Gaston Leroux's novel The Phantom of the Opera (1910). The modern Opéra Bastille, designed by Carlos Ott on the Place de la Bastille, opened on 13 July 1989 for the bicentenary of the French Revolution.

Ballet

The Paris Opera Ballet traces its origins to the court ballet of Louis XIV and to the Académie Royale de Danse (1661). Its school, founded in 1713, is the oldest ballet school in the world. Directors of dance have included Rudolf Nureyev (1983–1989) and Benjamin Millepied (2014–2016); José Martínez became director of dance in 2022.
