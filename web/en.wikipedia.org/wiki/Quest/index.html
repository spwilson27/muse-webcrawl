Quest

Placeholder: the quest — a journey undertaken to achieve a distant, difficult goal — is among the oldest narrative patterns.

A quest is a journey towards a specific mission or a goal. It serves as a plot device in mythology and fiction: a difficult journey towards a goal, often symbolic or allegorical. Tales of quests figure prominently in the folklore of every nation and ethnic culture. In literature, the object of a quest requires great exertion on the part of the hero, who must overcome many obstacles, typically including much travel. The aspect of travel allows the storyteller to show exotic locations and cultures (an objective of the narrative, not of the character).

In mythology and literature

Archetypal quests include Jason's search for the Golden Fleece, the Greek hero Perseus's hunt for the head of Medusa, and the journeys of Odysseus in the Odyssey. The supreme example in medieval romance is the quest for the Holy Grail, undertaken by the knights of King Arthur's Round Table — achieved only by the pure knight Galahad. Religious literature is rich in quests: the Israelites' journey to the Promised Land in Exodus, the Buddha's search for enlightenment, and allegories such as John Bunyan's The Pilgrim's Progress (1678), in which the protagonist journeys to the Celestial City. In modern literature, Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings is structured as an inverted quest — a journey not to obtain but to destroy the One Ring.

Analysis

Joseph Campbell placed the quest at the heart of the "hero's journey" or monomyth: departure, initiation through trials, and return bearing a boon for the community. Vladimir Propp's morphology of the folktale similarly treats the quest as a chain of functions — lack, dispatch, trials, acquisition of a magical agent, victory, and return. Anthropologists note that quest narratives parallel real rites of passage and pilgrimage.

Video games

In role-playing and adventure video games, a quest is a task a player character undertakes in return for a reward — experience, items, or progress in the story — divided conventionally into main quests and optional side quests. The term structures much of game design in franchises from World of Warcraft to The Elder Scrolls.
