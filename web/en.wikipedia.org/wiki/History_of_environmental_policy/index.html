History of environmental policy

The history of environmental policy traces government action to protect the natural environment, from early smoke-control ordinances and public-health laws to modern international climate agreements.

Early measures

Industrial cities adopted alkali and smoke abatement laws in the 19th century, and conservation movements led to national parks, beginning with Yellowstone in 1872, and forest reserves. Britain's Alkali Act of 1863 created one of the first environmental inspectorates.

Modern environmentalism

Rachel Carson's Silent Spring (1962) galvanised public concern. In 1970 the United States created the Environmental Protection Agency and passed the Clean Air Act; many countries followed with environment ministries and pollution laws. The 1972 Stockholm Conference launched international environmental cooperation and UNEP.

Global challenges

The 1987 Montreal Protocol addressed ozone depletion, while climate change produced the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (1992), the Kyoto Protocol (1997), and the Paris Agreement (2015). Policy debates continue over biodiversity, plastics, and environmental justice. See also Environmental policy.
