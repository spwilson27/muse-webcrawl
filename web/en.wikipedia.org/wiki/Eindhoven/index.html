Eindhoven (Dutch: [ˈɛintˌɦoːvə(n)]) is a city and municipality in the province of North Brabant in the south of the Netherlands. It is the fifth-largest city in the country and the centre of a major technology and design region, originating as a small town that grew explosively after the founding of the Philips electronics company there in 1891.

The Lichttoren, a landmark of the Philips era in Eindhoven.

History

Eindhoven received city rights in 1232 from Henry I, Duke of Brabant. The town suffered repeated destruction in wars over the centuries. Its modern history begins with the industrial revolution and especially with Philips, founded by Gerard Philips and his father Frederik as a light-bulb factory. Philips grew into a global electronics concern, drawing related industries — notably the truck maker DAF — and research institutions to the city. Heavy bombing in the Second World War, including during Operation Market Garden, destroyed much of the centre, which was rebuilt in modernist style.

Technology and design

Today Eindhoven brands itself the "City of Light" and is the heart of the Brainport region, one of Europe's leading high-tech clusters, centred on the High Tech Campus and companies such as ASML (in nearby Veldhoven), NXP and Philips' research successors. The Design Academy Eindhoven and the annual Dutch Design Week give the city a parallel reputation for design.

Sport

The city is home to PSV Eindhoven, one of the Netherlands' most successful football clubs, playing at the Philips Stadion.
