Christ in Majesty

Christ in Majesty (Latin: Maiestas Domini, "Majesty of the Lord") is a depiction of Jesus as the enthroned ruler of the universe, seated with one hand raised in blessing and the other holding a book (often inscribed EGO SUM LUX MUNDI, "I am the light of the world"). It was the central image of medieval Christian art from the early Middle Ages through the Romanesque period, dominating apses, tympana and illuminated manuscripts.

Development

The image descends from late-antique imperial and divine portraiture — Christ as the heavenly counterpart of the emperor — and from the Byzantine Pantocrator. Carolingian and Ottonian art elaborated it; Romanesque sculpture placed Christ in Majesty over church portals (for example at Vézelay and Autun), surrounded by the four Evangelist symbols (the Tetramorph), seraphim, and scenes of the Last Judgement, expressing Christ as creator, judge and ruler.

Later forms

With the Gothic period the image evolved toward the more approachable Last Judgement and devotional images of Christ, but Christ in Majesty persisted in Byzantine apses and was revived in various forms, including monumental 20th-century works such as the statue of Cristo Rei in Lisbon and the Christ the Redeemer statue in Rio de Janeiro.
