Sexism

Sexism is prejudice or discrimination based on a person's sex or gender, especially against women and girls. It encompasses stereotypes, attitudes, and institutional practices that devalue or disadvantage one sex. Sexism is linked to beliefs in the intrinsic superiority of one sex over another and to misogyny (hatred of women) and misandry (hatred of men), though it is most commonly analysed as a system of male dominance historically embedded in law, the economy, and culture.

Forms

Social psychologists Peter Glick and Susan Fiske distinguish hostile sexism — overt antipathy toward women who challenge male power — from benevolent sexism, superficially positive but patronising attitudes that idealise women in traditional roles; the two together form "ambivalent sexism" and correlate across cultures with gender inequality. Sexism may also be institutional (embedded in laws, pay structures, and organisational practices) or internalised by its targets.

Manifestations

Examples historically include legal disenfranchisement, exclusion from education and professions, and coverture laws. Contemporary research documents persistent gender pay gaps, under-representation of women in political and corporate leadership, occupational segregation, gender stereotypes in media, and everyday behaviours such as street harassment. Sex-based discrimination is prohibited in many jurisdictions, and the 1979 UN Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW) commits signatories to ending it.

History of the term

The word "sexism" was coined in the 1960s, modelled on "racism," and spread through the second-wave feminist movement. Feminist theorists analysed sexism as one expression of patriarchy, while later scholarship emphasised intersectionality — how sexism interacts with race, class, and other axes of inequality.

Effects

Research associates sexist attitudes and experiences with wage penalties, reduced political representation, adverse mental-health outcomes, and exposure to gender-based violence. Men can also be harmed by rigid gender norms, though scholars debate terminology for anti-male prejudice.
