Mercedes-Benz in Formula One

Mercedes returned as a constructor in 2010

Mercedes-Benz, a German luxury automotive brand, has competed in Formula One in several eras. The works team, currently racing as the Mercedes-AMG Petronas Formula One Team and based in Brackley, England, is one of the most successful in the sport's history, winning eight consecutive Constructors' Championships from 2014 to 2021. Mercedes also supplies engines to customer teams through Mercedes AMG High Performance Powertrains.

1950s

Mercedes entered Formula One in 1954 with the advanced W196, driven by Juan Manuel Fangio, who won the World Championship in both 1954 and 1955 with the team. After the 1955 Le Mans disaster, in which a Mercedes car crashed into the crowd killing more than 80 spectators, Mercedes withdrew from motor racing entirely at the end of the 1955 season and did not return for decades.

Engine supplier era

Mercedes returned to Formula One in 1994 as an engine supplier, first with Sauber and then in a long partnership with McLaren that brought World Championships for Mika Häkkinen (1998, 1999) and Lewis Hamilton (2008). Mercedes engines also powered Brawn GP to both titles in 2009.

Works team return

Mercedes bought the Brawn team and returned as a full constructor in 2010, initially signing Michael Schumacher out of retirement. From the start of the hybrid era in 2014, the team dominated: Lewis Hamilton and Nico Rosberg, then Hamilton and Valtteri Bottas, delivered eight straight Constructors' titles (2014–2021), with Hamilton winning six drivers' championships in that span to reach seven overall. The team's streak ended as Red Bull Racing became dominant in 2022–2023. George Russell joined as Hamilton's teammate in 2022, and Hamilton departed for Ferrari after the 2024 season.
