The Westland Lynx is a British multi-purpose twin-engined military helicopter designed and built by Westland Helicopters at its factory in Yeovil, Somerset. Originally intended as a utility craft for both civil and naval usage, military interest led to the development of battlefield and naval variants. The Lynx went into operational usage in 1977 and was later adopted by the armed forces of over a dozen nations, primarily for battlefield utility, anti-armour, anti-submarine and anti-surface warfare, and search and rescue roles.

Design and development

The Lynx was developed under the Anglo-French helicopter agreement of 1967, alongside the Aérospatiale Gazelle and Puma. It is a fully aerobatic helicopter, capable of loops and rolls, thanks to its rigid, hingeless titanium rotor head. In 1986 a specially modified Lynx, registration G-LYNX, set the world absolute speed record for helicopters at 400.87 km/h (249.09 mph) — a record for conventional helicopters that still stands. Naval versions have a wheeled undercarriage and folding rotors for shipboard operation from small flight decks.

Operational history

Lynx helicopters served with the Army Air Corps as battlefield utility and anti-tank aircraft (armed with TOW missiles) and with the Fleet Air Arm as the Royal Navy's principal small-ship helicopter, armed with Sea Skua anti-ship missiles — used to devastating effect against Iraqi patrol boats in the 1991 Gulf War — as well as torpedoes and depth charges for anti-submarine work. Lynx helicopters saw action in the Falklands War, the Gulf Wars, the Balkans and Afghanistan. The type was retired from British service in stages, with the Army's last Lynx leaving in 2018; its successor is the AW159 Wildcat. Export operators have included the navies of Germany, France, the Netherlands, Brazil, South Korea and others.

Variants

Major variants include the Army's AH.1, AH.7 and AH.9/AH.9A, and the Navy's HAS.2, HAS.3 and HMA.8. The much-upgraded Super Lynx 300 was marketed for export. The Lynx programme was the foundation of Westland's later work and of the record-breaking BERP rotor blade technology used on the EH101/Merlin.
