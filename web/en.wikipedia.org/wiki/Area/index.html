Area is the measure of a region's size on a surface. The area of a plane region or plane area refers to the area of a shape or planar lamina, while surface area refers to the area of an open surface or the boundary of a three-dimensional object. Area can be understood as the amount of material with a given thickness that would be necessary to fashion a model of the shape, or the amount of paint necessary to cover the surface in a single coat.

Units

The SI unit of area is the square metre (m²). Other common units include the square kilometre, hectare (10,000 m²), are, acre (4,046.86 m²), square yard, square foot, and square mile. Every unit of length has a corresponding unit of area, namely the area of a square with the given side length.

Basic formulas

For a rectangle of length l and width w, the area is A = lw. A triangle has half the area of the rectangle on the same base and height, A = ½bh. The area of a circle of radius r is A = πr², and the area of an ellipse with semi-axes a and b is πab. The areas of other polygons can be found by dividing them into triangles, or by the shoelace formula for polygons whose vertices are known. For curved surfaces, integration is used: the area under a curve is computed with definite integrals, and surface areas of solids of revolution with surface integrals.

History

The problem of measuring area is ancient: Egyptian and Babylonian mathematicians had rules for the areas of fields, and land measurement for taxation in the Nile valley drove early geometry. Greek mathematicians made area rigorous — Eudoxus's method of exhaustion underlies Euclid's treatment of circles, and Archimedes computed areas bounded by parabolas and spirals. The isoperimetric problem, asking which shape encloses the greatest area for a given perimeter, was known in antiquity; its answer is the circle. Modern measure theory, culminating in Lebesgue measure, places the intuitive notion of area on a rigorous foundation and shows, through results such as the Banach–Tarski paradox, that "area" cannot be assigned consistently to every subset of space.
