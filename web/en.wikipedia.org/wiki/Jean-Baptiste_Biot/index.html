Jean-Baptiste Biot

Jean-Baptiste Biot
Born: 21 April 1774, Paris, France
Died: 3 February 1862, Paris
Fields: Physics, astronomy, mathematics

Jean-Baptiste Biot (21 April 1774 – 3 February 1862) was a French physicist, astronomer and mathematician. With Félix Savart he established the Biot–Savart law, describing the magnetic field generated by a steady electric current.

Work

Biot made early balloon ascents with Gay-Lussac in 1804 to study the atmosphere and Earth's magnetic field. He conducted pioneering research on the polarisation of light, including the rotation of the plane of polarisation by optically active substances such as sugar solutions — work that founded polarimetry and saccharimetry. He also studied meteorites, helping establish their extraterrestrial origin after the 1803 L'Aigle fall, and took part in geodetic surveys of the meridian arc.

Honours

The mineral biotite and a lunar crater are named after him, as is the Biot number in heat transfer.
