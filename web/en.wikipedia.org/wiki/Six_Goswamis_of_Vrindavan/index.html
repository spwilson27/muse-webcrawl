Six Goswamis of Vrindavan

The Six Goswamis of Vrindavan were a group of six devotees and theologians of the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition in 16th-century India. Sent by Chaitanya Mahaprabhu to Vrindavan, they systematised the theology of devotion (bhakti) to Krishna and rediscovered and developed the sacred sites of the Braj region.

The six

The six are Rupa Goswami, Sanatana Goswami, Jiva Goswami, Raghunatha Bhatta Goswami, Raghunatha Dasa Goswami and Gopala Bhatta Goswami. Rupa and Sanatana, brothers who had served in the Bengal government, authored foundational works on devotional practice and aesthetics; Jiva, their nephew, produced comprehensive philosophical treatises including the Sat-sandarbhas.

Legacy

The Goswamis built major temples in Vrindavan, such as Govindaji, Madan Mohan and Gopinath, established the literary and theological canon of Gaudiya Vaishnavism, and shaped the tradition later carried worldwide by movements including ISKCON.
