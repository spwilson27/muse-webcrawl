Driver's Privacy Protection Act

Driver's Privacy Protection Act
Enacted1994
Codification18 U.S.C. §§ 2721–2725
PurposeLimit disclosure of personal information from state motor vehicle records

The Driver's Privacy Protection Act of 1994 (DPPA) is a United States federal statute that governs the privacy and disclosure of personal information gathered by state departments of motor vehicles. It restricts states and private parties from disclosing a driver's name, address, photograph, Social Security number and similar data held in motor vehicle records.

Background

The Act was prompted in part by the 1989 murder of actress Rebecca Schaeffer, whose killer obtained her home address from California motor vehicle records through a private investigator. Before the DPPA, many states routinely sold driver data to marketers and investigators.

Provisions

The DPPA prohibits disclosure of personal information except for fourteen permissible purposes, including use by government agencies, insurers, employers verifying commercial licences, and in connection with litigation and law enforcement. Highly sensitive information such as photographs, Social Security numbers and medical information receives additional protection. The Supreme Court upheld the Act as a valid exercise of Congress's commerce power in Reno v. Condon (2000).
