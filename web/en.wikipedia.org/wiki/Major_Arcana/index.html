Major Arcana

The Major Arcana are the named or numbered cards in a cartomantic tarot pack. There are usually 22 such cards in a standard 78-card pack, numbered from 0 to 21 (or I to XXI, with the Fool unnumbered or zero). They contrast with the 56 Minor Arcana suit cards.


Cards of the Major Arcana.

History

The Major Arcana originated as the trump cards (trionfi) of 15th-century Italian tarot (tarocchi) packs used for card games. From the late 18th century, occultists such as Court de Gébelin and Etteilla attributed mystical and divinatory meanings to them, a tradition developed by the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn and popularised by the Rider–Waite tarot deck of 1909, illustrated by Pamela Colman Smith.

Interpretation

In cartomancy, the Major Arcana are held to represent significant life lessons, karmic influences and the great archetypal themes of human experience, in contrast to the day-to-day concerns of the Minor Arcana. In the influential account of A. E. Waite, the sequence is sometimes read as the "Fool's Journey", a spiritual progression from innocence to integration.
