Preamplifier

A preamplifier, also known as a preamp, is an electronic amplifier that converts a weak electrical signal into an output signal strong enough to be noise-tolerant and sufficient for further processing, or for sending to a power amplifier and a loudspeaker. Without this, the final signal would be noisy or distorted.

Uses

Preamplifiers are used with microphones, phonograph pickups, musical-instrument pickups and various sensors. A phono preamplifier applies RIAA equalisation and boosts the millivolt-level output of a moving-magnet cartridge to line level. Microphone preamplifiers raise signals of a few millivolts to nominal line level (around +4 dBu in professional audio) while adding as little noise as possible.

Design

In a home audio system the preamplifier often serves as the control centre: it provides source selection, volume and tone controls, and buffers the source from the power amplifier. An ideal preamplifier is linear (accurate amplification), has high dynamic range, reproduces a wide range of frequencies and adds minimal noise and distortion; in practice it may be built with vacuum tubes, discrete transistors or operational amplifiers. Guitar preamplifiers deliberately add distortion and overdrive as a musical effect.
