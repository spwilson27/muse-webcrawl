Stadium of Domitian

The Stadium of Domitian (Italian: Stadio di Domiziano), also known as the Circus Agonalis, was an ancient Roman stadium in the Campus Martius of Rome. Built by the emperor Domitian and completed in AD 86, its footprint is preserved today by Piazza Navona, one of Rome's most famous squares.

History

The stadium was dedicated for the Capitoline Games of AD 86, a festival of Greek-style athletic competitions, musical contests and oratory established by Domitian. Measuring roughly 275 by 106 metres, it held Greek foot races, wrestling, boxing and throwing events, rather than the chariot racing of the Circus Maximus. The Greek word for contest, agōn, gave the stadium its later name; through forms such as "in agone" the name evolved into "Navona".

The site today

When the stadium fell out of use after late antiquity, its stands were built over, but the arena remained open ground, preserving the long, curved-ended shape. The site became a market place and eventually the Baroque Piazza Navona, famous for Bernini's Fountain of the Four Rivers and the church of Sant'Agnese in Agone, which commemorates the tradition that Saint Agnes was martyred in the stadium's arcades. Remains of the stadium's substructures, accessible from an entrance on the north side of the piazza, lie about 5 metres below modern street level and are open to visitors.
