Kalki (Sanskrit: कल्कि), also called Kalkin, is the prophesied tenth and final incarnation (avatar) of the Hindu god Vishnu. According to Hindu eschatology, Kalki is described as the avatar who appears at the end of the Kali Yuga, the current age of darkness and decline, to restore dharma (righteousness) and usher in a new Satya Yuga.

Description

Puranic texts describe Kalki as a warrior riding a white horse named Devadatta, wielding a blazing sword, who will destroy the wicked and corrupt rulers of the age. In the Kalki Purana he is born to Vishnuyashas and Sumati in the village of Shambhala, marries Princess Padmavati of Sinhala, battles and defeats the demon Kali and his armies, and then inaugurates the renewed golden age before departing.

Development of the concept

Kalki is mentioned briefly in the Mahabharata and developed in the Vishnu Purana and Bhagavata Purana; the dedicated Kalki Purana is a later text (likely composed between the 16th and 18th centuries). The concept of an eschatological saviour riding a white horse has parallels with Maitreya in Buddhism, the Mahdi in Islam, and figures in Zoroastrian and other traditions, and Kalki is also incorporated into Buddhist Kalachakra-related texts as a king of Shambhala.

Timing

Traditional calculations place Kalki's advent at the very end of the Kali Yuga, conventionally said to have begun in 3102 BCE and to last 432,000 years—meaning, on the traditional reckoning, his arrival lies far in the future. Various claimants and movements have nonetheless identified Kalki with historical or contemporary figures at different times.
