Total conversion

A total conversion (TC) is a modification ("mod") of an existing video game that replaces virtually all of its artistic assets, levels, characters, and often gameplay mechanics, producing what is effectively a new game built on the original's engine. The term arose in the 1990s around first-person shooters such as Doom and Quake.

Characteristics

Unlike partial mods, which add maps or weapons, a total conversion changes the setting, story, models, textures, sounds and user interface; the underlying engine, file formats and networking code are all that remain. Early celebrated examples include Aliens TC for Doom (1994). Some total conversions have become commercial products or standalone successes: Counter-Strike began as a Half-Life mod, and Team Fortress as a Quake mod; elaborate TCs such as Enderal (for Skyrim) are full-length games in their own right.

Legal and practical notes

Total conversions usually require ownership of the original game and are distributed free, since they incorporate the developer's engine and sometimes residual assets; supportive studios release official modding tools (e.g., id Software, Bethesda) that enable them.
