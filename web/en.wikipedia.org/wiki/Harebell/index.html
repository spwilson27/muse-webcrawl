The harebell (Campanula rotundifolia) is a species of flowering plant in the bellflower family Campanulaceae. It is a slender, rhizomatous perennial herb known in Scotland as the bluebell, and elsewhere as the Scottish bluebell to distinguish it from the common bluebell.

Description

The harebell grows to about 15–60 cm tall. The basal leaves are rounded, giving the species its Latin name rotundifolia ("round-leaved"), but these often wither before flowering; the stem leaves are long and narrow. The flowers are delicate, bell-shaped, violet-blue bells about 1–2 cm long, borne in loose racemes on wiry stems from midsummer into autumn.

Distribution and habitat

Campanula rotundifolia is native to temperate regions of Europe, Siberia, and North America. It grows in dry grasslands, heathland, dunes, cliffs, and rocky places, often on poor, well-drained soils, and is tolerant of considerable cold.

In culture

The harebell is associated with folklore and fairies in Britain, and it appears in Scottish song and poetry. It is the county flower of several British counties, including Yorkshire and Dumfriesshire.
