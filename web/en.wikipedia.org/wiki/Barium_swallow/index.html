Barium swallow

SynonymsBarium esophagram, esophagram
TypeMedical imaging (fluoroscopy)
Contrast agentBarium sulfate suspension
ExaminesPharynx and esophagus

A barium swallow, also called a barium esophagram, is a medical imaging procedure used to examine the upper gastrointestinal tract, in particular the pharynx and esophagus. The patient swallows a suspension of barium sulfate, a radiopaque contrast medium, while a series of X-ray images or real-time fluoroscopy records the passage of the contrast through the swallowing mechanism.

Procedure

The patient drinks barium sulfate preparations of varying thickness, sometimes together with gas-producing crystals to distend the esophagus (a double-contrast study), and may also swallow barium-coated marshmallows or tablets to test the passage of solids. The radiologist observes the swallow under fluoroscopy and takes spot images in several positions.

Indications

The study is used to investigate dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), odynophagia (painful swallowing), globus sensation, unexplained weight loss, and suspected structural abnormalities such as strictures, webs, rings, diverticula (including Zenker's diverticulum), hiatal hernia, achalasia, and esophageal tumors. A modified barium swallow study, performed with a speech-language pathologist, evaluates the safety of swallowing and the risk of aspiration.

Risks

Barium is inert and normally passes through the digestive tract, but aspiration into the lungs can cause inflammation, and leakage of barium through a perforation into the mediastinum can be serious; where perforation is suspected, a water-soluble contrast agent such as Gastrografin may be used first. The procedure also involves a small dose of ionizing radiation.
