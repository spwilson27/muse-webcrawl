List of Jewish Nobel laureates

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Nobel Prize is an international prize awarded annually since 1901 for achievements in physics, chemistry, physiology or medicine, literature, and peace, with an additional prize in economic sciences awarded since 1969. Of the roughly 970 individuals who have received Nobel Prizes, at least about 210 — more than one in five — have been identified as Jewish or of Jewish descent, although Jews constitute only about 0.2 percent of the world's population.

Overview

Jewish laureates have received prizes in every category: roughly 27 percent of physics laureates, 27 percent of medicine laureates, about 30 percent of chemistry laureates, and significant shares in economics (around 38 percent), literature, and peace. Notable examples include Albert Einstein (Physics, 1921), Niels Bohr (Physics, 1922, of partly Jewish descent), Boris Pasternak (Literature, 1958), Jonas Salk-era medicine laureates such as Rita Levi-Montalcini (1986), Henry Kissinger (Peace, 1973), Elie Wiesel (Peace, 1986), Milton Friedman (Economics, 1976), and Bob Dylan (Literature, 2016).

Context

Commentators frequently cite the phenomenon in discussions of Jewish cultural emphasis on education and literacy, urbanisation, and historical occupational patterns. The count depends on definitions: the list generally includes laureates who were Jewish by religion or ethnicity, including many who were secular or of mixed ancestry. The first Jewish laureate was Adolf von Baeyer, who received the Chemistry prize in 1905.
