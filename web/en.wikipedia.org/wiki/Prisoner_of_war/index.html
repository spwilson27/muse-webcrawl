Prisoner of war
Type: Wikipedia article (recreated from memory)

A prisoner of war (POW) is a person held captive by a belligerent power during or immediately after an armed conflict — typically a member of the armed forces, or certain accompanying categories, who has fallen into enemy hands. The Third Geneva Convention (1949) governs their treatment: humane treatment, adequate food and medical care, protection from violence, insult and public curiosity, correspondence and ICRC visits, and release and repatriation after the end of active hostilities.

History is full of grim counter-examples: the mass deaths of Soviet POWs in German captivity and of Allied prisoners in Japanese camps in the Second World War; the Bataan Death March; Korean War camps. POW status does not apply to spies or (in contested modern categories) many non-state fighters, whose rights rest on Common Article 3 and human-rights law.
