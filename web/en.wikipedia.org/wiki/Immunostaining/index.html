Immunostaining

Immunostaining refers to any antibody-based method used to detect a specific protein or other antigen in a sample. The term encompasses immunohistochemistry (IHC), performed on tissue sections; immunocytochemistry (ICC), performed on cultured cells; and immunofluorescence, in which the label is a fluorophore rather than an enzyme-generated coloured product. Immunostaining is a routine tool of diagnostic pathology and biomedical research.

Method

A typical protocol involves fixing and sectioning the sample, retrieving antigens masked by fixation (commonly by heating in buffer), blocking non-specific binding sites, incubating with a primary antibody against the target antigen, and then with a detection system — usually a secondary antibody conjugated to an enzyme such as horseradish peroxidase, which produces a coloured precipitate (for example brown DAB), or to a fluorescent dye as in immunofluorescence.

Applications

In surgical pathology, panels of immunostains help classify tumours of uncertain origin, determine hormone receptor and HER2 status in breast cancer (guiding therapy), identify infectious organisms, and distinguish reactive from malignant proliferations. In research, immunostaining maps protein expression and localisation in cells and tissues.

Limitations

Results depend heavily on antibody specificity and quality, fixation and processing conditions, and appropriate positive and negative controls; cross-reactivity and non-specific staining can mislead interpretation, prompting validation standards for antibodies used in diagnostics.
