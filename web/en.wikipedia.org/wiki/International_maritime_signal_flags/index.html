International maritime signal flags

International maritime signal flags are various flags used to communicate with ships. The principal system of flags associated with the International Code of Signals (INTERCO) is the set of 40 flags and pennants: 26 alphabetic flags, 10 numeral pennants, 3 substitutes or repeaters, and the code/answering pennant. Each flag has an associated letter or number and, when flown singly, an assigned meaning under the International Code of Signals.

Letter flags and meanings

Examples of single-flag meanings include:

  • Alfa (A): Diver down; keep clear at slow speed.
  • Bravo (B): I am taking in, discharging or carrying dangerous goods.
  • Charlie (C): Yes / affirmative.
  • Delta (D): Keep clear; I am manoeuvring with difficulty.
  • Hotel (H): I have a pilot on board.
  • Oscar (O): Man overboard.
  • Quebec (Q): My vessel is healthy; request free pratique (clearance).
  • Sierra (S): My engines are going astern.
  • Uniform (U): You are running into danger.
  • Victor (V): I require assistance.

Use

Combinations of flags spell out coded messages defined in the International Code of Signals, published by the International Maritime Organization. The flags are also used decoratively, strung together in sequence ("dressing ship") on festive occasions, and the letter flags serve as substitutes for letters in other signalling contexts such as yacht racing, where they have additional procedural meanings.

History

Signal flag systems were developed by navies and merchant services in the 19th century; the first International Code of Signals was published in 1857 and revised repeatedly, with the modern coloured designs standardized in the 20th century.
