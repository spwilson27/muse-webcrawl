Aladdin is a 2019 American musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Pictures and directed by Guy Ritchie, who co-wrote the screenplay with John August. A live-action remake of Disney's 1992 animated film of the same name — itself based on the tale of Aladdin from One Thousand and One Nights — it stars Mena Massoud as Aladdin, Naomi Scott as Princess Jasmine, Will Smith as the Genie, and Marwan Kenzari as the sorcerer Jafar.

Plot

In the fictional Arabian city of Agrabah, the kind-hearted street thief Aladdin falls for Princess Jasmine, who chafes against a law forbidding her to rule and requiring her to marry a prince. The Sultan's grand vizier Jafar sends Aladdin into the Cave of Wonders to retrieve a magic lamp; trapped inside, Aladdin rubs the lamp and releases a wish-granting Genie. Wishing to become a prince to court Jasmine, Aladdin arrives as "Prince Ali", but Jafar steals the lamp, seizes the throne and banishes him. With the Genie's freedom granted as Aladdin's final wish, Jasmine is recognised as Sultan in her own right and the pair marry.

Production

The remake was announced in October 2016 as part of Disney's series of live-action re-imaginings. Casting drew attention for its open call for Middle Eastern and South Asian leads; Massoud and Scott were cast in July 2017, and Smith signed on as the Genie, a role indelibly associated with Robin Williams in the original. Filming took place in England and Jordan's Wadi Rum in 2017. Songs from the 1992 film by Alan Menken, Howard Ashman and Tim Rice were reprised, with Menken composing the score and the songwriting duo Pasek and Paul contributing the new ballad "Speechless" for Jasmine.

Reception

The film received mixed reviews — praise for the performances of Massoud, Scott and Smith and for the music, criticism for Ritchie's direction, the effects and Kenzari's Jafar — but was a major commercial success, grossing over $1.05 billion worldwide and becoming one of the highest-grossing films of 2019.
