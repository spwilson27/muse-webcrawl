Nobades

The Nobades (also Nobatae, Greek: Νοβάδες) were a people of Nubia in late antiquity. After the withdrawal of Roman forces, they established a kingdom in the northern Nile valley between the First and Third Cataracts, with their capital at Pachoras (Faras), and competed with the neighbouring Blemmyes for control of Lower Nubia.

History

By about AD 350 the Nobades had taken over the territory of the former kingdom of Meroë's northern reaches. The Roman emperor Diocletian had earlier withdrawn the frontier to Aswan and paid subsidies to the Nobades and Blemmyes to guard the border. In the 5th century the two peoples fought over access to the temple of Isis at Philae. A famous victory inscription of the Nobadian king Silko, written in Greek at the temple of Kalabsha, records his triumphs over the Blemmyes.

Conversion and kingdom

In the 6th century the Nobades were converted to Christianity — traditionally in 543 by missionaries sent from Constantinople under the patronage of the empress Theodora — and their kingdom became known as Nobadia, one of the three Christian Nubian kingdoms alongside Makuria and Alodia. Nobadia was eventually absorbed into Makuria.
