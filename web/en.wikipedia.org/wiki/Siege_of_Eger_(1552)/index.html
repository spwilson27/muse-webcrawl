Siege of Eger (1552)

Date9 September – 17 October 1552
LocationEger, Kingdom of Hungary
ResultHungarian victory; Ottoman army withdraws
CommandersIstván Dobó (Hungarian defenders); Kara Ahmed Pasha and Sokollu Mehmed Pasha (Ottoman)
Strengthc. 2,000–2,300 defenders; Ottoman army estimated at 35,000–40,000 or more

The Siege of Eger took place in 1552, during the Ottoman wars in Hungary, when a large Ottoman army under Kara Ahmed Pasha besieged the castle of Eger in northern Hungary. Against overwhelming odds, the small garrison commanded by Captain István Dobó held the fortress for 39 days, and the siege ended in a celebrated Hungarian victory that became a lasting symbol of patriotic resistance.

Background

After the fall of Buda in 1541, Hungary was divided between the Ottoman-occupied centre, the Habsburg-ruled west and north, and Transylvania in the east. In 1552 Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent launched a major campaign in which Temesvár and Szolnok fell to the invaders. Eger, guarding the route toward the mining towns of Upper Hungary and toward Vienna, was the next strategic objective.

The siege

The Ottoman army arrived before Eger on 9 September 1552. Dobó had some 2,000 to 2,300 defenders, including soldiers, townsfolk and, famously, women who assisted in the defence. The Ottomans bombarded the castle with artillery, breached the outer walls, mined beneath the bastions and launched repeated mass assaults. The defenders resisted with artillery, boiling water and oil, and sorties. A large gunpowder magazine explosion failed to break their resolve. Facing the onset of winter, dwindling supplies and heavy losses, the Ottoman commanders raised the siege on 17 October 1552 and withdrew.

Aftermath and legacy

The successful defence halted the Ottoman advance in northern Hungary for that campaign, though Eger eventually fell to the Ottomans in 1596. The siege was immortalised in Géza Gárdonyi's 1901 novel Eclipse of the Crescent Moon (Egri csillagok, "Stars of Eger"), a Hungarian national classic, and in the 1968 film adaptation. The episode of the women of Eger fighting on the walls, depicted in Bertalan Székely's painting, remains an iconic image in Hungarian culture. Eger Castle is now a museum.
