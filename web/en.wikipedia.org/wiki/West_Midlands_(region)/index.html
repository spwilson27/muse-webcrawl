The West Midlands is one of nine official regions of England at the first level of statistical geography. It covers the western half of the area traditionally known as the Midlands. The region consists of the counties of Herefordshire, Shropshire, Staffordshire, Warwickshire, Worcestershire, and the metropolitan county of the West Midlands.

Geography

The region is landlocked, bordered by the East Midlands, the North West, the South East, the South West, and Wales. Its landscape ranges from the urban conurbation centred on Birmingham — England's second city — through the Shropshire Hills and Malvern Hills to the Peak District fringe in the north and the Cotswolds in the south. The River Severn, Britain's longest river, flows through the region.

Economy and transport

The West Midlands was a cradle of the Industrial Revolution, built on coal, iron, metalworking, and later the motor industry in Birmingham, Coventry, and the Black Country. Manufacturing remains important, and the region is crossed by major motorways (M5, M6, M40, M42) and by the West Coast Main Line, with Birmingham New Street one of Britain's busiest stations. Since 2017 the metropolitan county has been served by the West Midlands Combined Authority under an elected mayor.

Settlements

Major settlements besides Birmingham include Coventry, Wolverhampton, Stoke-on-Trent, Worcester, Hereford, Shrewsbury, Telford, Warwick, and Stratford-upon-Avon, the birthplace of William Shakespeare.
