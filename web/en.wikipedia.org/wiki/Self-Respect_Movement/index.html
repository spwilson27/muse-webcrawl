Self-Respect Movement

The Self-Respect Movement was a social reform movement founded in 1925 in Tamil Nadu, India, by E. V. Ramasamy, widely known as Periyar. It sought to eradicate the caste system, challenge Brahminical dominance and religious orthodoxy, and promote rationalism, self-respect, and equality — including the equality of women. The movement became a foundational influence on modern Tamil politics and the Dravidian movement.

Periyar campaigned for decades against caste hierarchy and superstition.

Origins and aims

Ramasamy, who had left the Indian National Congress in 1925 after disputes over caste discrimination, launched the movement and its Tamil journal Kudi Arasu to argue that people degraded by the caste order should reject religious sanction for their subordination and claim dignity — "self-respect" — as equals. The movement criticized Hindu scriptures and priestcraft, promoted atheism and rationalist thinking, and demanded the abolition of caste, untouchability, and child marriage.

Practices

Among its best-known innovations were self-respect marriages: weddings conducted without Brahmin priests and without religious rituals, based on mutual consent and equality, which were later given legal recognition in Tamil Nadu in 1967. The movement organized conferences (the first at Chengalpattu in 1929), propagated inter-caste dining and marriage, encouraged women's education and property rights, and championed widow remarriage and birth control — positions advanced for their time.

Political legacy

The movement fed into the Justice Party tradition and, in 1944, Ramasamy transformed the organization into the Dravidar Kazhagam. Its offshoots — the DMK (founded 1949 by C. N. Annadurai) and later the AIADMK — have dominated Tamil Nadu politics ever since, and the state's distinctive welfare-oriented, anti-caste political culture is widely traced to the movement's ideas.
