Zbigniew Cybulski

Zbigniew Herbert Cybulski (3 November 1927 – 8 January 1967) was a Polish actor, widely considered one of the greatest Polish film actors of the twentieth century. His intense, modern style of performance led critics to call him the "Polish James Dean."

Career

Cybulski studied at the State Academy of Drama in Kraków, graduating in 1953, and co-founded the student theatre Bim-Bom in Gdańsk. His breakthrough came with Andrzej Wajda's Ashes and Diamonds (1958), in which he played Maciek Chełmicki, a young Home Army soldier ordered to assassinate a communist official on the last day of the Second World War. The role — leather jacket, dark glasses, restless energy — made him the emblem of a generation and an icon of the Polish Film School.

Death and legacy

Cybulski died on 8 January 1967 at Wrocław railway station, when he attempted to board a moving train and fell under its wheels. He was 39. A plaque marks the platform where he died, and the Polish film critics' awards for young actors bear his name. Wajda's later film Everything for Sale (1969) was a tribute to his memory.
