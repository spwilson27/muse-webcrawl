Gothic metal (or goth metal) is a fusion genre combining the heaviness of heavy metal with the dark atmospheres of gothic rock. The music of gothic metal is diverse, with bands having adopted the gothic approach to different styles of metal music. The genre originated during the early 1990s in the United Kingdom originally as an outgrowth of death-doom, a fusion of death metal and doom metal.

Characteristics

Gothic metal songs are typically characterised by dark, melancholic atmospheres, romantic and tragic lyrical themes drawn from gothic literature, and contrasts between heavy guitar passages and ethereal keyboard or orchestral textures. A common feature is the "beauty and the beast" vocal pairing of a harsh male death growl with a clean, operatic female soprano.

History

The genre's foundations were laid by the so-called "Peaceville Three" — Paradise Lost, My Dying Bride, and Anathema — along with Type O Negative in the United States. In the mid-to-late 1990s, bands such as Theatre of Tragedy, Tristania, The Gathering, and Within Temptation broadened the style, and some acts achieved mainstream success in Europe in the 2000s.
