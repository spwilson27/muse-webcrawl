The University of North Carolina is the multi-campus public university system for the state of North Carolina. Oversight is provided by a Board of Governors, which has to govern the 16 public universities in the state, as well as the North Carolina School of Science and Mathematics, the nation's first public residential high school for gifted students.

History

The system traces its origin to the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, chartered in 1789 and opened in 1795, the first public university in the United States to admit students. In 1931, the North Carolina General Assembly consolidated UNC–Chapel Hill, North Carolina State University, and the Women's College (now UNC Greensboro) into a single university under one board. In 1972, all 16 of the state's public four-year institutions were brought into the system under the current Board of Governors structure.

Constituent institutions

The constituent universities include the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, North Carolina State University, East Carolina University, North Carolina A&T State University, UNC Charlotte, UNC Greensboro, UNC Wilmington, Appalachian State University, North Carolina Central University, Western Carolina University, UNC Asheville, UNC Pembroke, Winston-Salem State University, Fayetteville State University, Elizabeth City State University, and the University of North Carolina School of the Arts. Together they enroll more than 240,000 students and grant the majority of bachelor's and graduate degrees in the state.
