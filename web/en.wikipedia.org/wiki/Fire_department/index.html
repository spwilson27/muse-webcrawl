Fire department

A fire department (North American English) or fire brigade (Commonwealth English), also known as a fire company, fire authority, fire district, fire and rescue, or fire service in some areas, is an organization that provides firefighting services. Fire departments are most commonly a public sector organization operated as a department of a local government; some are independent, and some are privately operated, depending on jurisdiction and location.

Fire departments also provide other emergency and non-emergency services, including fire prevention, arson investigation, technical rescue, hazardous materials response, and emergency medical services.

Organization

Fire departments are organized into companies with different types of apparatus, such as engine companies, ladder companies, rescue companies, and squad companies. The head of a fire department is typically called the fire chief. Departments are usually divided geographically into battalions or districts, each containing several fire stations.

History

Organized firefighting dates back to ancient Rome, where the Corps of Vigiles patrolled the streets and fought fires. In the United States, the first volunteer fire company was founded by Benjamin Franklin in Philadelphia in 1736 (the Union Fire Company). The first professional, paid fire department in the United States was established in Cincinnati, Ohio, in 1853, which also introduced the first successful steam fire engine.

Emergency medical services

In many jurisdictions, fire departments are the primary providers of emergency medical services, with firefighters cross-trained as emergency medical technicians (EMTs) or paramedics, and a large share of fire department calls are medical rather than fire-related.
