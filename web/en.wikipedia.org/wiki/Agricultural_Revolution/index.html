Agricultural Revolution
May refer to: Neolithic Revolution; British Agricultural Revolution; Green Revolution

The term Agricultural Revolution most commonly refers to several distinct transformations in farming history. This article summarises the main usages.

Neolithic Revolution

The Neolithic Revolution (c. 10,000 BC onward) was the first agricultural transformation: domestication of plants and animals in the Fertile Crescent and other independent centres, leading to settled villages, surplus, and population growth.

British Agricultural Revolution

Between about 1700 and 1900, British agriculture saw unprecedented productivity increases through enclosure, four-course crop rotation (turnips and clover), selective livestock breeding, improved ploughs, and Jethro Tull's seed drill. This released labour and food supplies supporting the Industrial Revolution.

Arab and Green Revolutions

The medieval Arab agricultural revolution diffused new crops and irrigation across the Islamic world. The 20th-century Green Revolution spread high-yield crop varieties, synthetic fertiliser, and irrigation, dramatically raising yields — later critiqued and complemented by approaches such as agroecology.
