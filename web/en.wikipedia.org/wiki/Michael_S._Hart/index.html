Michael S. Hart

Born: Michael Stern Hart, 8 March 1947, Tacoma, Washington, U.S.

Died: 6 September 2011 (aged 64), Urbana, Illinois, U.S.

Occupation: Author, entrepreneur

Known for: Founding Project Gutenberg

Michael Stern Hart (March 8, 1947 – September 6, 2011) was an American author, best known as the inventor of the ebook and the founder of Project Gutenberg, the first project to make ebooks freely available via the Internet and one of the oldest digital libraries.

Project Gutenberg

While a student at the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign, Hart was granted generous access to the Xerox Sigma V mainframe computer at the university's Materials Research Laboratory, courtesy of friends working there. On July 4, 1971, he typed the United States Declaration of Independence into the computer — widely credited as the first ebook — and made it available for download on ARPANET. Hart later said he reasoned that the greatest value created by computers would not be computing itself but the storage, retrieval, and searching of information in libraries.

He followed the Declaration with other foundational American documents and classic works of literature, typing texts by hand in early years and later recruiting volunteers. Project Gutenberg grew into a volunteer-run digital library offering tens of thousands of public-domain ebooks, and its model inspired later mass digitization efforts including Google Books and the Internet Archive. Hart championed plain-text formats, arguing they would remain readable on any future system, and he resisted what he saw as restrictive copyright extensions, criticizing the "Mickey Mouse Protection Act" (the Copyright Term Extension Act of 1998).

Later life and legacy

Hart lived frugally in Urbana, Illinois, funding the project himself and through donations, and promoted the goal of distributing a trillion ebook files. He died at home in Urbana on September 6, 2011, at the age of 64. He is remembered as a pioneer of open access to literature and information, having anticipated the ebook era by decades — mainstream commercial ebooks did not take off until the launch of the Amazon Kindle in 2007.
