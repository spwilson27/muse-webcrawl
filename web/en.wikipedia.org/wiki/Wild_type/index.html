Wild type

Wild type (WT) is the phenotype of the typical form of a species as it occurs in nature, and by extension the allele(s) or genotype regarded as standard or "normal" against which mutants are compared. Originally the term meant the most common allele at a locus; in modern genetics it usually denotes the reference strain used in a laboratory.

Notation

In model-organism nomenclature, wild-type alleles are often written with a "+" superscript (e.g. w+ in Drosophila) and gene names frequently derive from the mutant phenotype, so the wild type is the unmutated version. In microbiology, a prototroph able to grow on minimal medium is wild type for nutrition, versus auxotrophic mutants.

Use

Wild-type controls anchor experiments in genetics, developmental biology and pharmacology, including comparisons of transgenic and knockout organisms against the wild-type background.
