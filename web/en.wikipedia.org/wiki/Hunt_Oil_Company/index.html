Hunt Oil Company is a privately held American oil and gas exploration and production company headquartered in Dallas, Texas. Founded in 1934 by wildcatter H. L. Hunt, it is part of the Hunt family's Hunt Consolidated group and remains one of the largest privately owned independent oil companies in the United States.

History

H. L. Hunt built his fortune on the East Texas Oil Field, acquiring the Daisy Bradford No. 3 discovery lease from Columbus Marion "Dad" Joiner in 1930 and organizing Hunt Oil in 1934 (initially in Tyler, later Dallas). The company expanded internationally after the Second World War and in 1968 was a partner in the discovery of the Sarir field in Libya, then one of the largest onshore fields in the world; its Libyan assets were nationalized under Muammar Gaddafi in the 1970s.

Operations

Hunt Oil has operated in the United States (including the Williston Basin/Bakken, Eagle Ford, and Permian regions), Canada, Yemen, Peru, Romania, and elsewhere. It operated the Yemen LNG project and the Block 18 (Marib) concession—the backbone of Yemen's oil industry—until operations were handed over amid the civil war. Its Peru LNG project, in which Hunt holds a leading stake, was South America's first LNG export plant (2010). The company's refining arm, Hunt Refining Company, operates a refinery in Tuscaloosa, Alabama. The family, long led by Ray L. Hunt, also owns Rosewood Hotels & Resorts and extensive real-estate holdings.
