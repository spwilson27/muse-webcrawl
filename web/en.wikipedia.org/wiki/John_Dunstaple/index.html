John Dunstaple

John Dunstaple (or Dunstable; c. 1390 – 24 December 1453) was an English composer of polyphonic music of the late medieval era and early Renaissance. He was one of the most famous composers active in the early 15th century, and his style had a profound influence on European music, notably through the contenance angloise, the "English manner."

Life

Little is known with certainty about Dunstaple's life. He may have been in the service of John, Duke of Bedford, brother of Henry V, and may have travelled to France during the English occupation. He held properties in Cambridgeshire and Essex, and his epitaph in St Stephen Walbrook, London, described him as skilled in mathematics, astronomy, and music.

Music

Dunstaple's surviving works include Mass movements, motets, and secular songs, many preserved in continental manuscripts such as the Old Hall Manuscript and sources in Trent and Aosta. His use of thirds and sixths, and of pan-consonant harmony, transformed the sound of European polyphony. Composers such as Guillaume Du Fay credited the English style with inspiring a new era of music.
