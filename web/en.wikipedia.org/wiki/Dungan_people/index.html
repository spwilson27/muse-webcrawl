Dungan people

Dungan people (东干族)
Regions: Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan, Russia
Population: approx. 110,000–160,000
Languages: Dungan (a Mandarin variety), Russian
Religion: Islam (Sunni)

The Dungan people are a Muslim ethnic group of Hui Chinese origin living in Central Asia, principally in Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan, with smaller communities in Russia. They are descendants of Hui refugees who fled Qing China after the defeat of the Dungan Revolt (1862–1877), crossing the Tien Shan mountains into Russian territory in the winter of 1877–78.

Language

The Dungan language is a variety of Mandarin Chinese (based on the dialects of Gansu and Shaanxi) written in the Cyrillic alphabet since Soviet times — making it the only widely used form of Chinese written in Cyrillic. It retains many 19th-century Chinese expressions alongside Russian and Turkic loanwords, and Dungan-language newspapers, schools and literature continue in use.

Culture

Dungans remain predominantly Sunni Muslim and are known for their agriculture (including rice and vegetable farming), distinctive cuisine blending Chinese and Central Asian traditions, and villages such as Yrdyk and Milyanfan in Kyrgyzstan. Relations with neighbouring groups have generally been peaceful, though Dungans were among the communities caught up in Kyrgyzstan's 2010 ethnic violence.
