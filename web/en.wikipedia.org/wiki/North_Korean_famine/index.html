North Korean famine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The North Korean famine, officially referred to in North Korea as the Arduous March (고난의 행군), was a famine that struck North Korea from 1994 to 1998, during the rule of Kim Jong Il. It is estimated to have caused between 240,000 and 3.5 million deaths out of a population of about 22 million; demographic studies commonly estimate between 600,000 and 1 million excess deaths.

Causes

The famine had multiple causes. North Korea's economy depended heavily on subsidized trade with the Soviet Union and China; after the dissolution of the USSR, fuel, fertilizer, and machinery imports collapsed. The state-run Public Distribution System for food broke down as agricultural output fell, handicapped by collectivized farming, fuel shortages, land degradation from terracing and deforestation, and the diversion of resources to the military. Severe floods in 1995 and 1996, followed by drought in 1997, destroyed crops and infrastructure, deepening the crisis.

Course

Rations dwindled from 1994; by 1997 the distribution system had effectively stopped for much of the population. North Korea appealed for international food aid in 1995, and the World Food Programme mounted one of its largest operations. The black market (jangmadang) that grew as ordinary people foraged and traded for survival became a lasting feature of the economy. Many North Koreans fled across the border into China.

Aftermath

Harvests stabilized somewhat after 1998, but chronic malnutrition persisted, with high rates of stunting among children. The famine profoundly changed North Korean society, weakening the state's monopoly on distribution and encouraging de facto market activity that the regime later alternately tolerated and suppressed.
