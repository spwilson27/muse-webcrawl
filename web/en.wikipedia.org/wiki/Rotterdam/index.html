Rotterdam

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Rotterdam
Country: Netherlands
Population: about 660,000 (city)
Port: Europe's largest; world's busiest until 2002

Rotterdam is the second-largest city in the Netherlands and home to the largest seaport in Europe, set where the Rhine–Meuse delta meets the North Sea. Founded around a dam on the Rotte river (the name means "Rotte dam", c. 1270), it grew into the gateway of the German industrial heartland.

Destruction and rebirth

On 14 May 1940 the German Luftwaffe bombed Rotterdam's centre to force the Dutch surrender, destroying some 25,000 homes and the medieval heart of the city in a day. Rotterdam rebuilt as a deliberate experiment in modernism — the Lijnbaan pedestrian shopping precinct (1953), the Cube Houses, the Erasmus Bridge (1996) and the Markthal — becoming a byword for architectural daring, the counter-image of Amsterdam's preserved canals and the Golden Age city.

The port

The port stretches 40 km from city to sea through Europoort and the Maasvlakte reclamations; it was the world's busiest port by cargo tonnage from 1962 until 2002, when Asian ports (Singapore, then Shanghai — see Singapore) surpassed it. It remains Europe's indispensable harbour — and, as a low-lying delta city, a laboratory of climate adaptation, with water plazas and floating neighbourhoods designed for the flood its history predicts.
