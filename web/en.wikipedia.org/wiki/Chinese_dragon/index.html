Chinese dragon

The Chinese dragon (simplified Chinese: 龙; traditional Chinese: 龍; pinyin: lóng) is a legendary creature in Chinese mythology, Chinese folklore, and Chinese culture at large. Chinese dragons have many animal-like forms, such as turtles and fish, but are most commonly depicted as snake-like with four legs. Academicians have identified four reliable theories on the origin of the Chinese dragon: snakes, Chinese alligators, thunder, and nature worship. They traditionally symbolize potent and auspicious powers, particularly control over water, rainfall, typhoons, and floods.

The dragon is also a symbol of power, strength, and good luck for people who are worthy of it in East Asian culture. During the days of Imperial China, the Emperor of China usually used the dragon as a symbol of his imperial strength and power.

Symbolism

Historically, the dragon was the symbol of the Emperor of China. In the Zhou dynasty, the five-clawed dragon was assigned to the Son of Heaven, the four-clawed dragon to nobles, and the three-clawed dragon to ministers. In the Qing dynasty, the Azure Dragon was featured on the first Chinese national flag.

The dragon is one of the 12 Chinese zodiac signs, and people born in the Year of the Dragon are traditionally considered lucky, charismatic, and natural leaders.

Description

Chinese dragons are typically described as a composite of many animals: the head of a camel, the horns of a deer, the eyes of a demon, the ears of a bull, the neck of a snake, the belly of a clam, the scales of a carp, the claws of an eagle, the soles of a tiger, and whiskers like a catfish. Most dragons possess 117 scales.

Mythological origins

One of the earliest dragon images is a pig dragon carved from jade in the Hongshan culture (4700–2900 BC). The dragon was linked to rain and water early on; the Yinglong was a winged dragon said to have helped Yu the Great control the floods. Four Dragon Kings (Longwang) rule the seas: Ao Guang of the East Sea, Ao Qin of the South Sea, Ao Run of the West Sea, and Ao Shun of the North Sea.

Dragon dance and culture

The dragon dance is performed during Chinese New Year and other festivals to frighten away evil spirits and bring prosperity. Dragon boat racing, held during the Duanwu Festival, features boats carved and painted like dragons.
