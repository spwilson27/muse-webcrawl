Life in a Jar

Life in a Jar
Origin: Uniontown, Kansas, United States
Founded: 1999
Subject: Irena Sendler and the rescue of Jewish children in Warsaw

Life in a Jar is a project that began in 1999 as a National History Day entry by students at a high school in Uniontown, Kansas, and grew into an international educational movement about Irena Sendler, the Polish social worker who helped smuggle approximately 2,500 Jewish children out of the Warsaw Ghetto during the Holocaust.

Origin

Students Elizabeth Cambers, Megan Stewart, Sabrina Coons and Jessica Shelton, guided by teacher Norman Conard, chose Sendler after reading a brief mention of her in a magazine. Almost unknown at the time, Sendler had worked with the Polish resistance organisation Żegota, smuggling children out of the ghetto in ambulances, sacks and coffins, and recording their real names on slips of paper buried in jars so they could later be reunited with their families — the "life in a jar" of the title.

Growth

The students' play about Sendler was performed hundreds of times around the world. They travelled to Poland, met Sendler (who died in 2008), and helped bring her story to global attention; she was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize in 2007. The project evolved into the Lowell Milken Center for Unsung Heroes in Fort Scott, Kansas, which promotes stories of moral courage in education.

Legacy

Life in a Jar is credited with transforming Sendler from an obscure figure into one of the best-known rescuers of the Holocaust era, and with popularising the "unsung hero" model of history education.
