Congonhas

Congonhas is a historical Brazilian city located in the state of Minas Gerais. It is situated about 90 kilometres south of Belo Horizonte, the state capital. The city is notable for its basilica and the soapstone sculptures of the Twelve Prophets, the masterworks of the Brazilian sculptor Antônio Francisco Lisboa, known as Aleijadinho.

History

Congonhas grew during the eighteenth-century gold cycle in Minas Gerais. Its sanctuary, the Basílica do Bom Jesus de Matosinhos, was built in the second half of the 18th century in Brazilian rococo style.

The Twelve Prophets

Between 1800 and 1805, Aleijadinho carved the Twelve Prophets in soapstone for the terrace of the basilica. The figures — Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Hosea, Joel, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, and Daniel — are regarded as among the finest works of Baroque art in the Americas. In 1985 the sanctuary was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Economy

The modern economy of Congonhas is heavily influenced by iron-ore mining in the surrounding region, alongside tourism centered on its historic sanctuary.
