Black helicopter

Black helicopters are a symbol in American conspiracy theory culture of an alleged covert military takeover of the United States by the federal government or the United Nations. The term became widespread in the 1990s, particularly within the militia movement, whose members claimed that unmarked black helicopters were evidence of secret operations against American citizens.

Origins

Reports of mysterious helicopters circulated in the 1970s in connection with cattle mutilation claims in the American West. The phrase gained its modern meaning in the early 1990s through books and broadcasts by figures in the patriot and militia movements, including claims that black helicopters had been sighted near militia compounds and that they presaged the imposition of a "New World Order". Radio host Glenn Beck has been credited by some with early use of the phrase in print media.

Explanations

Skeptics note that military helicopters are frequently painted in dark, low-visibility colour schemes, that units such as the U.S. Army's 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment operate dark-painted aircraft at night, and that training exercises, law-enforcement flights and utility work account for most sightings. Under certain lighting, ordinary helicopters also appear black in silhouette.

In popular culture

"Black helicopter" entered general usage as shorthand for paranoid suspicion of government surveillance; the phrase "black helicopter crowd" is used pejoratively in political journalism for conspiracy-minded groups. Unmarked helicopters also appear in popular fiction about UFOs and secret government programmes.
