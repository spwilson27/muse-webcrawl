Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University, commonly known as Virginia Tech, is a public land-grant research university with its main campus in Blacksburg, Virginia. Founded in 1872 as an agricultural and mechanical college, it has grown into a comprehensive university especially noted for engineering, agriculture, and its Corps of Cadets. Its motto, Ut Prosim ("That I May Serve"), reflects its land-grant mission. The university's sports teams are the Hokies, and its colours — Chicago maroon and burnt orange — were adopted in 1896.

History

The university was established under the Morrill Land-Grant Act as Virginia Agricultural and Mechanical College. It developed a strong military tradition through its Corps of Cadets, and alumni served prominently in both world wars. The institution became a university in 1944 and adopted the name Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University in 1970; women were fully admitted from 1964. On 16 April 2007 the campus was the site of a mass shooting in which 32 people were killed — then the deadliest school shooting in US history — an event commemorated by a memorial on the Drillfield and the annual remembrance events.

Academics

Virginia Tech comprises nine colleges, including the College of Engineering (its largest and highest-ranked), the College of Agriculture and Life Sciences, the Pamplin College of Business, and the Virginia–Maryland College of Veterinary Medicine. It also operates the Fralin Biomedical Research Institute, the Virginia Tech Transportation Institute, and the Carilion School of Medicine in Roanoke. Research expenditure ranks among the top 50 US universities, and the university is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity".

Campus and student life

The Blacksburg campus of about 2,600 acres is famous for its Collegiate Gothic buildings constructed from the local limestone known as Hokie Stone. Landmarks include Burruss Hall, War Memorial Chapel, Lane Stadium (home of the football team, coached to prominence by Frank Beamer), and Cassell Coliseum. Virginia Tech consistently ranks highly for quality of life and campus food in student surveys, and its Marching Virginians and alumni corps traditions remain distinctive features.
