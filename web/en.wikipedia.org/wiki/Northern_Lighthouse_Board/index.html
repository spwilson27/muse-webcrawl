Northern Lighthouse Board

A Northern Lighthouse Board lighthouse

The Northern Lighthouse Board (NLB) is the general lighthouse authority for Scotland and the Isle of Man. Based at 84 George Street in Edinburgh, it is responsible for some 208 lighthouses, 161 buoys and other navigational aids in Scottish and Manx waters, and is one of three general lighthouse authorities for the United Kingdom and Ireland, alongside Trinity House and the Commissioners of Irish Lights.

History

The board was established by Act of Parliament in 1786 as the Commissioners of Northern Lighthouses, initially to build four lights, including Kinnaird Head and the Bell Rock. The engineering of its lights became the work of the Stevenson family dynasty, beginning with Thomas Smith and his stepson Robert Stevenson, builder of the Bell Rock Lighthouse (1811). Over the 19th century the Stevensons constructed most of Scotland's great coastal lights, including Skerryvore and Muckle Flugga.

Modern operations

All NLB lighthouses were automated by 1998, when Fair Isle North became the last to lose its keepers; they are now monitored from Edinburgh. The board operates two vessels, NLV Pharos and NLV Pole Star, for buoy work and lighthouse servicing. Its costs are met from light dues levied on commercial shipping into the General Lighthouse Fund. The board's lighthouses, many of them Category A listed buildings, remain iconic features of the Scottish coast, though the NLB has consulted on discontinuing some lights in the satellite navigation era.
