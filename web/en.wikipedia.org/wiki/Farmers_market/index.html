Farmers' market

A farmers' market (or farmers market) is a physical retail marketplace intended to sell foods directly from farmers to consumers. Farmers' markets may be indoors or outdoors and typically consist of booths, tables, or stands where farmers sell fruits, vegetables, meats, cheeses, and sometimes prepared foods and beverages.

History

The concept dates to pre-industrial times, when farmers gathered in towns to sell surplus produce. Modern farmers' markets experienced a revival in the later twentieth century as part of the local food movement, driven by consumer interest in fresh, locally grown, and organic food, and by farmers seeking higher margins than wholesale channels provide.

Benefits and criticism

Supporters argue that farmers' markets support small farms, shorten supply chains, preserve farmland, and provide fresh seasonal produce and community gathering places. Critics note that prices may be higher than supermarkets, that not all vendors sell their own produce, and that markets can vary widely in rules and certification — some require producers to sell only goods they grow themselves, while others permit reselling.

Around the world

Farmers' markets are common throughout North America and Europe; the United States Department of Agriculture counted more than 8,700 farmers' markets in the US in 2019. Similar institutions include wet markets in Asia and street markets worldwide, though these typically include resellers as well as producers.
