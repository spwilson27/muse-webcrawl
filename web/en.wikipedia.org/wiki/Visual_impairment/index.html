Visual impairment

Visual or vision impairment (VI) is the partial or total inability of visual perception. In the absence of treatment (such as corrective eyewear), visual impairment may cause the individual difficulties with normal daily tasks including reading and walking. Low vision is a subset of visual impairment, and blindness is complete (or near-complete) vision loss.

Classification

The World Health Organization classifies vision impairment by visual acuity in the better-seeing eye: mild (worse than 6/12), moderate (worse than 6/18), severe (worse than 6/60), and blindness (worse than 3/60). Legal definitions vary by country; in the United States, legal blindness is acuity of 20/200 or worse in the better eye with correction, or a visual field of 20 degrees or less.

Causes

Globally, the leading causes are uncorrected refractive errors and cataracts, followed by age-related macular degeneration, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy and corneal opacities. In children, causes include congenital cataract, retinopathy of prematurity and vitamin A deficiency. The WHO estimates that at least 2.2 billion people have a near or distance vision impairment, of which at least 1 billion could have been prevented or treated.

Management and adaptation

Treatment targets the underlying cause — spectacles, cataract surgery, or medication. People with permanent impairment use assistive technology and techniques: Braille, screen readers, magnifiers, orientation and mobility training, white canes and guide dogs. Many countries provide disability benefits, accessible transport and educational support.
