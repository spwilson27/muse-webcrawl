Ben Affleck

Benjamin Géza Affleck (born 15 August 1972) is an American actor and filmmaker. He is the recipient of two Academy Awards and three Golden Globe Awards. Affleck began his career as a child actor and rose to prominence with Good Will Hunting (1997), whose screenplay he co-wrote with Matt Damon. He later gained acclaim as a director with Gone Baby Gone (2007), The Town (2010) and Argo (2012), the last of which won the Academy Award for Best Picture.

Career

Affleck appeared as a child in the PBS series The Voyage of the Mimi and in Dazed and Confused (1993) before his breakthrough with Good Will Hunting, which won him and Damon the Oscar for Best Original Screenplay. He became a leading man in blockbusters including Armageddon (1998), Pearl Harbor (2001) and Daredevil (2003), though a string of poorly received films in the mid-2000s stalled his acting career. Critical rehabilitation followed his directing debut and roles in Gone Girl (2014) and The Way Back (2020). From 2016 to 2023 he played Bruce Wayne / Batman in the DC Extended Universe. In 2023 he directed and starred in Air, about Nike's signing of Michael Jordan.

Other work

Affleck co-founded the production companies LivePlanet and Artists Equity, and the Eastern Congo Initiative, a grant-making and advocacy organisation. He has been active in Democratic Party politics and humanitarian causes.
