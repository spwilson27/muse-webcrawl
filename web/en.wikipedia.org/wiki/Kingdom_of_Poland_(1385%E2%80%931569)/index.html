Kingdom of Poland (1385–1569)

The Kingdom of Poland in the period 1385–1569 covers the Polish state from the Union of Krewo, which joined Poland in a personal union with the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, to the Union of Lublin of 1569, which created the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.

Jagiellonian era

Under the Jagiellonian dynasty, Poland became one of the largest states in Europe. Jogaila (Władysław II Jagiełło) married Queen Jadwiga and converted Lithuania to Christianity. The Polish-Lithuanian victory over the Teutonic Order at Grunwald in 1410, and the Thirteen Years' War (1454–1466), won Royal Prussia for the Crown. Kraków flourished as a centre of learning, home to Nicolaus Copernicus.

Union of Lublin

In 1569 the personal union was transformed into a real union: the Commonwealth of Two Nations, with a common elected monarch and parliament, while Poland and Lithuania retained separate administrations, armies and treasuries.
