Church of the Intercession on the Nerl

The church reflected in the spring flood waters of the Nerl.

The Church of the Intercession on the Nerl (Russian: Церковь Покрова на Нерли, Tserkov Pokrova na Nerli) is an Orthodox church and a symbol of medieval Russia, situated near the village of Bogolyubovo in Vladimir Oblast. Dedicated to the feast of the Intercession of the Theotokos, it was built in 1165 by Prince Andrei Bogolyubsky, reportedly in memory of his son Izyaslav, who died in a campaign against the Volga Bulgars. The church stands on a low artificial hill in the meadows at the confluence of the Nerl and Klyazma rivers, and in spring floods it appears to float on the water.

Architecture

Built of white limestone, the small single-domed church is celebrated for its harmonious proportions and the refinement of its carved ornament. The facades are divided by pilasters and decorated with blind arcading; carved reliefs include King David with animals, lions, doves, griffins, and female masks. Inside, almost nothing of the original twelfth-century frescoes survives. The church is considered the masterpiece of the Vladimir-Suzdal school of architecture and a pinnacle of medieval Russian stone building.

History and status

Originally part of a riverside monastic ensemble with stair turrets and galleries, the church lost its annexes in the eighteenth century and narrowly escaped demolition in 1784, when the abbot who wanted its stone could not agree on a price with contractors. In the Soviet period it was protected as a museum. Since 1992 it has been inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the White Monuments of Vladimir and Suzdal, and it is shared between the museum and the Russian Orthodox Church for services.
