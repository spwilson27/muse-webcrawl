Racecourse

A racecourse, showing the oval track and grandstand.

A racecourse (also known as a race track in some contexts) is a facility built for racing of animals — mainly horses (in horse racing) or greyhounds. A racecourse also can be for athletics, cycling, or motor racing, though the term is most closely associated with horse racing venues, which are also sometimes called hippodromes in some languages.

Horse racecourses typically consist of an oval or irregular loop track of turf, dirt, or synthetic surface, a parade ring (paddock) where horses are shown before races, stabling, betting facilities (bookmakers and totalisator), and spectator accommodation ranging from open grounds to multi-storey grandstands. Famous racecourses include Ascot and Epsom Downs in England, Churchill Downs in the United States, Longchamp in France, and Flemington in Australia, home of the Melbourne Cup.

Track configurations

Tracks vary in circumference (commonly 1–2.5 km), direction (left-handed in North America and much of Europe; right-handed at many British courses), and surface. Steeplechase and hurdle courses add obstacles, while harness racing uses its own tracks and sulkies. Modern racecourses employ sophisticated drainage, irrigation, and surface-testing to keep going (track condition) safe and consistent.
