University of California, Riverside

The University of California, Riverside (UCR or UC Riverside) is a public land-grant research university in Riverside, California. It is one of the ten campuses of the University of California system, enrolling more than 26,000 students. The campus is noted for its research in entomology and plant science, its high proportion of first-generation and low-income students, and its social mobility rankings.

History

UCR began in 1907 as the UC Citrus Experiment Station, a research facility supporting Southern California's citrus industry; the parent navel orange tree, source of California's Washington navel oranges, still grows in Riverside. The undergraduate College of Letters and Science opened in 1954, and UCR became a general university campus in 1959. A school of medicine, the first new public medical school in California in more than four decades, admitted its first students in 2013.

Campus

The 1,200-acre campus sits near Box Springs Mountain, marked by a large "C" on its slopes. The 161-foot Carillon Bell Tower, designed by A. Quincy Jones, is the campus landmark. The UCR Botanic Gardens occupy 40 acres on the campus's eastern side.

Athletics

The UC Riverside Highlanders compete in the Big West Conference.
