Waimea Bay

Waimea Bay is a bay on the North Shore of Oahu, Hawaii, United States, at the mouth of the Waimea River in the town of Haleiwa. It is famous worldwide as a big-wave surfing location and for its significance in Hawaiian history.

Surfing

In winter, powerful Pacific storms generate swells that produce waves at Waimea reaching 30 feet (9 metres) or more on the face. The bay was one of the original big-wave spots ridden in the 1950s by surfers such as Greg Noll. Waimea hosts The Eddie — the Eddie Aikau Big Wave Invitational — held in honour of lifeguard and surfer Eddie Aikau, and run only when open-ocean swells consistently reach the required size. In summer the bay is typically calm and popular for swimming and snorkelling.

History and culture

The Waimea Valley above the bay was a centre of Hawaiian religious life, overseen by high priests (kahuna nui) for centuries. In 1779, after the death of Captain James Cook at Kealakekua Bay, ships of his expedition visited Waimea. A well-known feature of the beach is a large rock offshore used for jumping into the water. The bay is part of Waimea Bay Beach Park, maintained by the City and County of Honolulu.
