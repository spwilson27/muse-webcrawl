Ratio test

In mathematics, the ratio test is a test (or "criterion") for the convergence of a series whose terms are real or complex numbers. The test was first published by Jean le Rond d'Alembert and is sometimes known as d'Alembert's ratio test or as the Cauchy ratio test.

The test

Consider the series Σan and the limit L = lim |an+1 / an| as n → ∞. The test states:

  • If L < 1, the series converges absolutely.
  • If L > 1, the series does not converge (its terms do not tend to zero).
  • If L = 1, the test is inconclusive, and another test must be used.

Examples

Applied to Σ n / 2n, the ratio of successive terms tends to 1/2, so the series converges. Applied to the harmonic series Σ 1/n, the ratio tends to 1 and the test gives no information — the harmonic series in fact diverges. When the ratio test is inconclusive, the root test or Raabe's test may succeed.
