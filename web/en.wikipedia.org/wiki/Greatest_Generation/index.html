Greatest Generation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Greatest Generation, also known as the G.I. Generation and the World War II generation, is the Western demographic cohort following the Lost Generation and preceding the Silent Generation. The generation is generally defined as people born from 1901 to 1927. They were shaped by the Great Depression and were the primary generation that served in World War II.

The term was popularised by the title of a 1998 book by American journalist Tom Brokaw, who profiled men and women who grew up during the Depression and went on to fight in the war or support the effort on the home front.

Characteristics

Members of the generation experienced economic privation in childhood, followed by wartime service and the postwar boom: expanding home ownership (aided by the G.I. Bill), suburbanisation, and the growth of the American middle class. Some 16 million Americans served in uniform during the Second World War; by the 2020s only a small fraction of those veterans remained alive.

Terminology

"Greatest Generation" is a term largely used in the United States. Comparable wartime cohorts exist in other Allied nations, though without the same label. The cohort is preceded by the Lost Generation (born roughly 1883–1900) and followed by the Silent Generation (born roughly 1928–1945).
