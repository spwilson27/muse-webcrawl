Kapil Dev Nikhanj (born 6 January 1959) is a former Indian cricketer and captain, widely regarded as one of the greatest all-rounders in the history of the game. A fast-medium bowler with a graceful outswinger and an aggressive middle-order batsman, he led India to its first Cricket World Cup victory in 1983, defeating the West Indies in the final at Lord's.

Career

Kapil made his Test debut against Pakistan in 1978. In the 1983 World Cup his unbeaten 175 against Zimbabwe at Tunbridge Wells, scored with India at 17 for 5, became one of the tournament's legendary innings. He was the first player to take 400 Test wickets and for a time held the world record with 434, and he remains the only player to score 5,000 Test runs and take 400 Test wickets. He retired in 1994.

Honours

Kapil Dev was named by Wisden as the Indian Cricketer of the Century in 2002 and was inducted into the ICC Cricket Hall of Fame. He received the Arjuna Award, the Padma Shri (1982) and the Padma Bhushan (1991). He later coached the Indian national team (1999–2000) and became a prominent golfer and sports administrator.
