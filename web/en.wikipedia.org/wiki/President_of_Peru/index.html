The President of Peru (Spanish: Presidente del Perú), officially called the President of the Republic of Peru (Presidente de la República del Perú), is the head of state and head of government of Peru. The president directs the executive branch of the Government of Peru and is the commander-in-chief of the Armed Forces of the Republic of Peru.

Role and powers

Under the 1993 Constitution, the president is elected by direct popular vote for a five-year term and may not be re-elected for an immediately following term, though a former president may run again after one term out of office. The president appoints the President of the Council of Ministers (prime minister) and, on their advice, the other members of the cabinet. Powers include promulgating laws, issuing decrees, conducting foreign relations, commanding the armed forces and police, and granting pardons. Congress may remove the president through impeachment or by declaring the office vacant on grounds including "permanent moral incapacity", a provision that has been invoked frequently in Peru's recent political history.

Election

Presidential elections are held every five years alongside congressional elections. If no candidate obtains more than half of the valid votes in the first round, a runoff is held between the two leading candidates. Candidates for president run with two vice-presidential candidates on the same ticket.

History

The office dates to the first constitutions of the Peruvian Republic in the 1820s, following independence from Spain proclaimed by José de San Martín in 1821 and secured after the Battle of Ayacucho in 1824. Peru's presidential history has been marked by frequent instability, military coups, and—especially since 2016—a rapid succession of presidents amid conflicts between the executive and Congress.

Residence

The president's official residence and workplace is the Government Palace (Palacio de Gobierno), located on the Plaza Mayor in the historic centre of Lima.
