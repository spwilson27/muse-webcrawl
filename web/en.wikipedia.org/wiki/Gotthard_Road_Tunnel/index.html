Gotthard Road Tunnel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The southern portal of the Gotthard Road Tunnel at Airolo.

The Gotthard Road Tunnel in Switzerland runs from Göschenen in the canton of Uri at its northern portal, to Airolo in the canton of Ticino to the south, passing below the Saint-Gotthard Massif. At the time of its construction, in 1980, it was the longest road tunnel in the world; at 16.9 kilometres (10.5 mi) in length, it is currently the fifth-longest. It forms part of the A2 motorway, one of the principal north–south transit routes through the Swiss Alps.

Construction

Construction began in 1970 and the tunnel opened on 5 September 1980. It is a single bidirectional tube with one lane in each direction, accompanied by a parallel service and safety tunnel. The tunnel replaced the seasonal Gotthard Pass road as the year-round route across the main Alpine ridge.

Traffic and safety

The tunnel carries heavy transalpine freight and tourist traffic, and queues at its portals are common during holiday periods. Because it is a single tube with two-way traffic, heavy goods vehicles are metered through a "drip-feed" system. After a serious fire in October 2001, caused by a head-on collision between two lorries, killed 11 people, safety measures and traffic management were tightened.

Second tube

To allow renovation of the original tunnel without closing the route, Swiss voters approved construction of a second road tube in a 2016 referendum. The second bore is scheduled to open around 2030, after which the first tube will be refurbished; in the long term both tubes are intended to operate with one lane each, keeping total capacity unchanged.

Related tunnels

The Gotthard corridor also includes the historic Gotthard Rail Tunnel (opened 1882) and the Gotthard Base Tunnel (opened 2016), at 57 kilometres the world's longest railway tunnel.
