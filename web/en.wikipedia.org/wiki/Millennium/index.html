Millennium

Millennium celebrations were held worldwide on 31 December 1999, though the third millennium formally began on 1 January 2001.

A millennium (plural millennia or millenniums) is a period of one thousand years. The term derives from the Latin mille (thousand) and annus (year). In the Gregorian calendar, which has no year zero, the first millennium AD ran from year 1 to 1000, the second from 1001 to 2000, and the third from 2001 to 3000.

The year 2000 debate

Because centuries and millennia in the Anno Domini system begin with the year ending in 01, popular celebrations of the new millennium on 1 January 2000 technically marked the start of the final year of the second millennium. Nevertheless, public festivities — and the "Y2K" computer problem, in which two-digit year fields threatened to misread 2000 as 1900 — centered on the turn of 1999 to 2000. Structures built for the occasion include London's Millennium Dome and the London Eye (Millennium Wheel).

Religious meaning

In Christian eschatology, the Millennium (or chiliasm) refers to the thousand-year reign of Christ described in the Book of Revelation, interpreted variously by premillennialist, postmillennialist, and amillennialist schools of thought. Millenarian expectations have inspired numerous religious movements throughout history.
