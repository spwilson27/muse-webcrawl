Gary Works

LocationGary, Indiana, United States
OwnerU.S. Steel
Established1906
ProductsSteel sheet, plate, tin products

Gary Works is a steel mill in Gary, Indiana, operated by U.S. Steel. Built on the southern shore of Lake Michigan beginning in 1906, it grew to become the largest steel mill in the United States and remains one of the largest integrated steel works in North America, producing steel sheet and tin products.

History

The city of Gary itself was founded by U.S. Steel in 1906 as a planned company town around the new mill. Construction involved reclaiming a large stretch of sand dunes along the lake; the plant combined blast furnaces, open-hearth furnaces and rolling mills in a single integrated operation, supplied with iron ore by lake freighter and coal by rail. For much of the twentieth century Gary Works employed tens of thousands of workers and anchored the economy of northwestern Indiana.

Operations

The works includes blast furnaces, basic oxygen steelmaking, continuous casters, a hot strip mill and finishing facilities producing hot-rolled, cold-rolled and coated sheet, tin mill products and plate. Employment declined sharply from its mid-century peak with automation and the contraction of the American steel industry, though the plant remains the flagship mill of U.S. Steel and a major employer in Gary.
