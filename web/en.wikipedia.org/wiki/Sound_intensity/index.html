Sound intensity

Sound intensity, also known as acoustic intensity, is defined as the power carried by sound waves per unit area in a direction perpendicular to that area. The SI unit of intensity, which includes sound intensity, is the watt per square metre (W/m2). One application is the noise measurement of sound intensity in the air at a listener's location as a sound energy quantity.

Mathematical definition

Sound intensity, denoted I, is defined by

I = p v

where p is the sound pressure and v is the particle velocity. Both I and v are vectors, which means that both have a direction as well as a magnitude. The direction of sound intensity is the average direction in which energy is flowing.

The average sound intensity during a time period T is given by the time integral of the product of pressure and particle velocity divided by T. For a spherical sound source, the intensity as a function of distance r follows the inverse-square law: I(r) = P / (4πr2), where P is the sound power emitted by the source.

Sound intensity level

A logarithmic measure, the sound intensity level, is commonly used:

LI = 10 log10(I / I0) dB

where I0 = 10−12 W/m2 is the reference intensity, approximately the threshold of human hearing at 1,000 Hz.

Measurement

Sound intensity is measured with a sound intensity probe, typically a pair of phase-matched microphones separated by a small spacer (the p-p method) or a microphone combined with a particle-velocity sensor (the p-u method). Intensity measurement allows the sound power of sources to be determined in situ and the direction of energy flow to be identified, which a single pressure microphone cannot do.
