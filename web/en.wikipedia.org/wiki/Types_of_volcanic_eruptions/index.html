Types of volcanic eruptions

Volcanic eruptions vary enormously in style, from gentle lava flows to catastrophic explosions. Volcanologists classify the main types of volcanic eruptions by their explosivity and the behaviour of the magma, a spectrum governed largely by magma viscosity, gas content and the involvement of external water. The Volcanic Explosivity Index (VEI) ranks eruptions by the volume of material ejected.

Magmatic eruptions

  • Hawaiian — fluid basaltic lava fountains and flows, minimal ash (e.g. Kīlauea).
  • Strombolian — rhythmic mild explosions throwing incandescent cinders a few hundred metres (named for Stromboli, Italy).
  • Vulcanian — short, violent explosions of viscous magma producing ash columns up to several kilometres.
  • Peléan — collapse of lava domes generating pyroclastic flows (named for Mount Pelée, 1902).
  • Plinian — sustained, enormous eruption columns tens of kilometres high with widespread ash fall (named for Pliny the Younger's account of Vesuvius in AD 79); the largest are termed ultra-Plinian.

Phreatomagmatic and other eruptions

Where magma meets water, explosive steam-driven fragmentation produces Surtseyan eruptions in shallow seas (named for the Icelandic island born in 1963), phreatic (steam-only) explosions, and submarine eruptions. Effusive eruptions dominated by lava emission include the vast Icelandic fissure eruptions, while subglacial eruptions beneath ice caps can trigger glacial outburst floods (jökulhlaups).
