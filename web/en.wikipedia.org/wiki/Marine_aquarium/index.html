Marine aquarium

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A marine aquarium is an aquarium that keeps marine plants and animals in a contained environment. Marine aquaria are further divided by hobbyists into fish-only (FO), fish-only-with-live-rock (FOWLR) and reef aquariums, the latter keeping stony and soft corals and other invertebrates.

Water and equipment

Marine aquarists use synthetic sea salt mixes to achieve a specific gravity of about 1.020–1.026. Filtration typically combines a protein skimmer, which removes organic waste as foam, with biological filtration in porous live rock. Reef tanks require intense lighting — LED, metal halide or T5 fluorescent — and careful supplementation of calcium, alkalinity and magnesium, often via kalkwasser or a calcium reactor.

History

Keeping marine animals was long considered far harder than freshwater fishkeeping; the modern hobby grew from the 1960s and 1970s with better salts and filtration, and expanded greatly with the reef systems developed in the 1980s and 1990s.
