Lawspeaker

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A lawspeaker (Old Norse: lǫgsǫgumaðr; Swedish: lagman; Finnish: laamanni) was an office in medieval Scandinavia whose holder was responsible for knowing, reciting, and interpreting the law at the assembly known as the thing. In societies whose law was unwritten and transmitted orally, the lawspeaker functioned as a living repository of legal tradition.

Iceland

In the Icelandic Commonwealth, the lawspeaker (lögsögumaður) was the only formal national official and presided over the Althing at Þingvellir. Elected for a three-year term, he was required to recite the entire body of law from the Law Rock (Lögberg) over the course of his term, one third each year, as well as to proclaim procedural law annually and advise on points of law. Notable Icelandic lawspeakers include Úlfljótr, credited with bringing the first laws from Norway around 930, and the historian Snorri Sturluson, who held the office twice.

Sweden and elsewhere

In Sweden, the lagman presided over a provincial legal district (lagsaga) and its assembly, and led the delegation that participated in the election of kings. Analogous offices existed in Norway, Denmark, and the Faroe Islands. As written law codes spread from the 13th century onward, the lawspeaker's role shifted from living memory of the law to that of a senior judge, and the title survived in some Nordic legal systems as a judicial rank.
