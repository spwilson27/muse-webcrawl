John David Marks (10 November 1909 – 3 September 1985), known as Johnny Marks, was an American songwriter. He specialised in Christmas songs and wrote many standards associated with the season, most famously "Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer".

Life and career

Marks was born in Mount Vernon, New York, and educated at Colgate University and Columbia University. He served in the United States Army during the Second World War, earning a Bronze Star and four battle stars. In 1949 he founded the music publishing company St. Nicholas Music.

Christmas songs

Marks's brother-in-law, Robert L. May, had created the character Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer in a 1939 booklet for the Montgomery Ward department store. Marks adapted the story into the song "Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer", recorded by Gene Autry in 1949; it became one of the best-selling singles of all time. Marks went on to write a string of enduring Christmas songs, including "Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree" (a hit for Brenda Lee), "A Holly Jolly Christmas" (recorded by Burl Ives), "Silver and Gold", "I Heard the Bells on Christmas Day" (adapting Longfellow's poem), and the music for the 1964 Rankin/Bass television special Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer.

Legacy

Despite being Jewish, Marks became the defining songwriter of American popular Christmas music. He was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 1981. His catalogue continues to generate new hit recordings every holiday season.
