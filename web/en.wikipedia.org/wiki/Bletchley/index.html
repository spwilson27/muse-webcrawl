Bletchley is a town in the City of Milton Keynes, Buckinghamshire, England. It grew as a railway town at the junction of the London and North Western Railway with the Oxford–Cambridge "Varsity line," and became part of the new city of Milton Keynes designated in 1967. It is best known worldwide as the home of Bletchley Park, Britain's wartime codebreaking centre.

History

Recorded in Domesday Book, Bletchley remained a village until the railway arrived in 1846; the junction made it a modest Victorian railway town of brickworks and printing. In 1938 the government bought the Bletchley Park estate as a wartime evacuation site for the Government Code and Cypher School. During the Second World War some 9,000 people, including Alan Turing and other leading mathematicians, worked there breaking German Enigma and Lorenz ciphers — work kept secret until the 1970s. The site is now a museum, and the adjacent National Museum of Computing houses a rebuilt Colossus computer.

Modern town

Bletchley is one of the older constituent towns of Milton Keynes, with its own high street along Queensway, a market, and the stadium of the former Wimbledon F.C. (now MK Dons) nearby at Denbigh. The East West Rail project is restoring direct rail services between Oxford, Bletchley, and Bedford, with Bletchley station rebuilt to serve them.
