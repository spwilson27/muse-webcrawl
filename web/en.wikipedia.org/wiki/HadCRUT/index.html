HadCRUT is the dataset of worldwide monthly surface temperature anomalies relative to a 1961–1990 reference period. It is produced jointly by the Met Office Hadley Centre and the Climatic Research Unit of the University of East Anglia in the United Kingdom, by combining the CRUTEM land-surface air temperature record with the HadSST sea-surface temperature record. The dataset extends back to 1850 and is one of the principal instrumental temperature records used in climate science and by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC).

Versions

Successive versions have incorporated more stations, improved bias adjustments, and better treatment of uncertainty. HadCRUT3 (2006) was succeeded by HadCRUT4 (2012), which extended coverage (particularly in the Arctic and Africa) and introduced a measurement-and-sampling model ensemble. HadCRUT5, released in 2020–2021, uses statistical infilling to interpolate across data-sparse regions such as the Arctic and an updated sea surface temperature analysis (HadSST4), showing slightly more warming than HadCRUT4.

Findings

The record shows global mean surface warming of about 1.2 °C between 1850–1900 and the early 21st century, with the warmest years of the record concentrated in the 2010s and 2020s. Like other surface records (NASA GISTEMP, NOAA GlobalTemp), HadCRUT shows a clear warming trend punctuated by interannual variability associated with El Niño and La Niña events and volcanic eruptions.
