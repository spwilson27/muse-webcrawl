Motion compensation

Motion compensation predicts a frame from a reference frame using motion vectors

Motion compensation is an algorithmic technique used to predict a frame in a video, given the previous and/or future frames, by accounting for motion of the camera and/or objects in the video. It is employed in the encoding of video data for video compression, for example in the generation of MPEG files, and is the counterpart of motion estimation.

Function

Motion compensation describes a picture in terms of the transformation of a reference picture to the current picture. The reference picture may be a previous frame or one that has yet to occur. When images can be accurately synthesised from previously transmitted ones, compression efficiency improves, because only the motion vectors and a small residual error need to be stored or transmitted rather than the full image.

Block motion compensation

In block motion compensation, the current frame is partitioned into blocks of pixels (macroblocks in older standards, coding tree units in newer ones). Each block is predicted from a block of equal size in the reference frame, offset by a motion vector. The transformation is often limited to pure translation, though modern codecs such as H.264 and HEVC support sub-pixel accuracy, multiple reference frames, and variable block sizes.

Global motion compensation

Global motion compensation models camera movement — panning, tilting, rotation and zoom — with a small set of parameters applied to the whole frame, allowing complex camera motion to be described with very few bits. It was included in MPEG-4 Part 2 but saw limited adoption in practice.
