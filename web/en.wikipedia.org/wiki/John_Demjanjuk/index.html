John Demjanjuk

John Demjanjuk (born Ivan Mykolaiovych Demjanjuk; 3 April 1920 – 17 March 2012) was a Ukrainian-American autoworker who was prosecuted over several decades for alleged service as a guard at Nazi extermination camps during the Second World War.

Background

Demjanjuk, a Red Army soldier captured by the Germans in 1942, emigrated to the United States in 1952 and settled in Ohio. In 1977 US investigators identified him as "Ivan the Terrible," a notoriously brutal guard at Treblinka. He was stripped of US citizenship and extradited to Israel in 1986.

Trials

An Israeli court convicted Demjanjuk in 1988 and sentenced him to death, but in 1993 the Israeli Supreme Court overturned the verdict after Soviet archives produced evidence suggesting Ivan the Terrible was another man, Ivan Marchenko. Demjanjuk returned to the US, but new evidence of his service as a guard at Sobibor and other camps led to a second denaturalisation and deportation to Germany. In 2011 a Munich court convicted him as an accessory to the murder of some 28,000 Jews at Sobibor in 1943 — the first conviction of a camp guard based solely on service at a death camp, without proof of a specific act. He died in Germany in 2012 while his appeal was pending, so under German law he was not legally guilty at death.
