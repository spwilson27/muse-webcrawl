Quadrature (astronomy)

Positions of a superior planet at quadrature, opposition, and conjunction.

In spherical astronomy, quadrature is the configuration of a celestial object in which its elongation is perpendicular, i.e. at right angles, to the direction of the Sun as seen from Earth. It can apply to the position of a superior planet (a planet farther from the Sun than Earth) or of the Moon at its first and last quarter phases. As shown in the diagram, a planet (or other object) can be at the western quadrature (when it is to the west of the Sun as seen from Earth) or at the eastern quadrature (when it is to the east of the Sun).

Quadrature of the Moon

The Moon is at (east or west) quadrature when the difference in ecliptic longitude between the Moon and the Sun is 90°. At these moments the Moon appears exactly half illuminated — at first quarter, the Moon is at east quadrature and visible in the evening sky; at last quarter, it is at west quadrature and visible in the morning sky. Neap tides occur around quadrature, when the Sun's tidal influence partly cancels the Moon's.

Quadrature of planets

A superior planet such as Mars, Jupiter, or Saturn passes through quadrature twice in each synodic period — once on each side of opposition. Near quadrature a superior planet shows a slight phase defect (gibbous form), since the Sun illuminates it from the side as seen from Earth. For Mars the effect is marked and historically important in confirming the planet's phases. Inferior planets (Mercury and Venus) never reach quadrature, since their maximum elongation is well under 90°.

Related configurations

  • Conjunction — elongation of 0°
  • Opposition — elongation of 180°
  • Elongation — the angular separation from the Sun
