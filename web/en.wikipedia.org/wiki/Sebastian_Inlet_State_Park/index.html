Sebastian Inlet State Park

Sebastian Inlet State Park is a Florida State Park located on a barrier island at the Sebastian Inlet, where the Indian River Lagoon meets the Atlantic Ocean, straddling the line between Brevard and Indian River counties. The park covers more than 970 acres of the barrier island plus submerged lands, and fronts about three miles of Atlantic beach. It is among the most visited state parks in Florida.

History

Local settlers attempted to cut an inlet at this site as early as the nineteenth century, but shifting sands repeatedly closed their hand-dug channels. A permanent inlet was finally opened in 1948 after several failed efforts, and the jetties were progressively extended and rebuilt to stabilise it. The state park was established in 1970. Hurricanes have repeatedly damaged the park's bridges, fishing piers, and campgrounds, most severely in the 2004 season.

Recreation

The inlet is famous on Florida's east coast for surfing — the break at "First Peak" offshore of the north jetty was long considered one of the state's best — and for saltwater fishing from the jetties, catwalks, and T-dock. Snook fishing at the inlet is especially celebrated. On the lagoon side, a calm tidal pool area is popular for swimming and snorkelling, and the park offers boating, kayaking, camping, and wildlife viewing; it is a well-known site for observing manatees, dolphins, and migrating birds, and loggerhead sea turtles nest on its beaches.

Museums

The park contains two museums. The McLarty Treasure Museum, opened in 1971 on the site of a survivors' camp of the 1715 Spanish treasure fleet, displays artefacts and explains the fleet's wreck along this coast, which gives the region its "Treasure Coast" name. The Sebastian Fishing Museum, housed in a former fish house, interprets the history of the local commercial fishing families.
