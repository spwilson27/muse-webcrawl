Open-channel flow

Flow in an open channel is driven by gravity and has a free surface.

In fluid mechanics, open-channel flow is flow in a conduit or channel with a free surface, such as flow in rivers, canals, spillways, drainage channels and partially full sewers. Because the upper surface is exposed to the atmosphere, the flow is driven by gravity rather than by pressure differences, and the pressure at the surface is atmospheric.

Classification

Open-channel flow may be classified by how depth varies with time and distance:

  • Steady vs unsteady — depth constant or changing with time
  • Uniform vs varied — depth constant or changing along the channel; varied flow is further divided into gradually varied and rapidly varied flow
  • Subcritical, critical and supercritical — according to the Froude number, Fr = V/√(gD). Below 1 the flow is subcritical (tranquil), above 1 supercritical (shooting); a hydraulic jump connects the two regimes.

Governing relationships

Uniform flow is widely estimated with the Manning formula, V = (1/n) R2/3 S1/2, where n is a roughness coefficient, R the hydraulic radius and S the slope; the older Chézy formula serves a similar purpose. The Saint-Venant equations — conservation of mass and momentum for a shallow-water flow — underpin one-dimensional modelling of gradually varied and unsteady flow, including flood routing and dam-break analysis.

Specific energy

The concept of specific energy, introduced by Bakhmeteff, relates depth and velocity head for a given discharge and explains phenomena such as choking at constrictions and the alternate depths on either side of a hydraulic jump.
