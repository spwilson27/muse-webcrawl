Pundit

A pundit is a person who offers opinion, commentary or analysis on a subject — most often politics, sport or current affairs — typically through the mass media. The word comes from the Hindi paṇḍit, derived from the Sanskrit paṇḍita, meaning a learned man or scholar; in India a pandit was traditionally a Brahmin scholar versed in Sanskrit, Hindu law, religion and philosophy. In modern English usage the term has shifted from "learned scholar" to "media commentator", and it is sometimes used ironically or pejoratively to suggest a self-appointed expert.

Original meaning

In British India, pandit was an honorific for learned Hindus; colonial courts employed "court pandits" to advise on Hindu law before codification. In the 19th century the word acquired a specific secondary meaning: the "Pundits" were native Indian surveyors trained by the British Survey of India who, from the 1860s, covertly mapped Tibet and Central Asia — regions closed to Europeans — travelling disguised as pilgrims and counting paces with modified rosaries. The most famous of these explorer-spies was Nain Singh.

Media commentators

In contemporary journalism, pundits — also billed as analysts, commentators or columnists — provide interpretation of events such as elections, court decisions and foreign crises. Political punditry grew with 24-hour cable news and the opinion pages of newspapers, and expanded again with blogs, podcasts and social media. Critics of the genre argue that pundits are rewarded for confidence and entertainment value rather than accuracy: studies of pundit predictions (notably Philip Tetlock's Expert Political Judgment) found many media experts' forecasts little better than chance. The term "punditocracy" has been used to describe the relatively small, self-reinforcing circle of prominent commentators in capitals such as Washington, D.C.

Sports pundits

In British and international broadcasting, a sports pundit is usually a former player or manager who analyses matches, tactics and transfers before, during and after broadcasts. The role became a staple of football coverage in particular, and former-player pundits are major television personalities in the United Kingdom and elsewhere.
