Severn Estuary

The Severn Estuary is the estuary of the River Severn in the United Kingdom, the longest river in Great Britain. It is the confluence of the Severn, the Wye, the Usk and the Avon, and flows into the Bristol Channel between South West England and South Wales. The estuary is famous for having one of the largest tidal ranges in the world — up to about 15 metres (50 ft), second only to the Bay of Fundy — which produces the natural phenomenon known as the Severn bore.

Geography and ecology

The funnel shape of the estuary amplifies the incoming tide, exposing vast mudflats, sandbanks and saltmarshes at low water. These habitats support internationally important populations of wading birds and wildfowl — around 65,000 waterbirds winter there — and the estuary is protected under national and European designations. It is also a major fishery and a migration route for Atlantic salmon and the endangered European eel.

Energy proposals

The exceptional tidal range has repeatedly prompted proposals for a Severn barrage to generate electricity — a scheme first studied in the 1920s and most recently in the 2000s–2010s — but successive governments have declined to fund one because of its cost and environmental impact on the protected estuary. Smaller tidal lagoon schemes have also been proposed.
