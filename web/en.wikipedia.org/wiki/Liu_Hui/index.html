Liu Hui
Bornc. 225 CE, Cao Wei

Known forCommentary on the Nine Chapters; approximation of pi; Liu Hui's pi algorithm

Liu Hui (fl. 3rd century CE) was a Chinese mathematician of the state of Cao Wei during the Three Kingdoms period. He is best known for his commentary, written in 263 CE, on the Jiuzhang suanshu (The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art), in which he supplied rigorous proofs and refinements of the procedures given in that classic text, as well as an additional chapter, the Haidao suanjing (Sea Island Mathematical Manual), on surveying by similar right triangles.

Approximation of pi

Liu Hui computed the value of pi (π) by inscribing regular polygons in a circle and repeatedly doubling the number of sides. Starting from a hexagon, he used an iterative algorithm—now known as Liu Hui's pi algorithm—equivalent to applying the half-angle recurrence for the side length of the inscribed polygon. With a 96-sided polygon he obtained the bounds 3.141024 < π < 3.142708, and with a 3072-sided polygon he reached π ≈ 3.1416, an accuracy not surpassed for centuries. He also gave the practical approximation 157/50 = 3.14.

Other work

Among Liu Hui's contributions are early uses of ideas resembling Cavalieri's principle for volumes, a method for solving systems of linear equations equivalent to Gaussian elimination, the computation of volumes of pyramids, wedges and other solids, and precise statements about fractions and square and cube roots. His commentary corrected errors in the Nine Chapters and provided theoretical justification for its algorithms, making him one of the most important figures in the history of Chinese mathematics. The astronomer and mathematician Zu Chongzhi later refined Liu Hui's method to obtain an even more accurate value of pi.
