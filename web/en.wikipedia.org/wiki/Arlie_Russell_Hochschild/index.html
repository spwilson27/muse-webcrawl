Arlie Russell Hochschild (born 15 January 1940) is an American sociologist, professor emerita at the University of California, Berkeley, noted for founding the sociology of emotion and for influential studies of work, family, and American politics.

Key works

In The Managed Heart (1983), based on studies of flight attendants and bill collectors, Hochschild introduced the concept of emotional labor — the management of feeling to create a publicly observable display as part of one's job. The Second Shift (1989) documented the unpaid household labor women performed after paid work. The Time Bind (1997) examined workplaces becoming more home-like than homes, and The Outsourced Self (2012) explored market services entering intimate life.

Later work

Her book Strangers in Their Own Land (2016), based on fieldwork among Tea Party supporters in Louisiana, was a National Book Award finalist and widely credited as an empathetic study of the American right. Stolen Pride (2024) examined pride, loss, and politics in Appalachian Kentucky.
