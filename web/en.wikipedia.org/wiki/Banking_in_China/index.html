Banking in China

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Banking in China refers to the banking system of the People's Republic of China, the largest in the world by total assets. It is dominated by state-owned commercial banks — including the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC), China Construction Bank, the Agricultural Bank of China and the Bank of China, the "Big Four", which rank among the largest banks on earth — and is overseen by the central bank, the People's Bank of China. The system has also produced enormous growth in shadow banking, local government financing and mobile payment platforms such as Alipay and WeChat Pay.

History

China has a long financial history: paper money originated under the Song dynasty, and Shanxi draft banks (piaohao) remitted funds across the empire in the nineteenth century. Modern banking arrived with foreign banks such as HSBC in the 1860s and Chinese institutions including the Bank of China (1912). After 1949 the People's Bank of China operated as a mono-bank, combining central and commercial functions in a planned economy. Reforms beginning in 1978 re-created specialised banks, established specialised commercial banking in the 1990s, and led to stock-market listings of the big state banks in the 2000s.

Structure

The sector comprises the central bank; policy banks (China Development Bank, Export–Import Bank of China, Agricultural Development Bank of China); large state-owned commercial banks; joint-stock commercial banks; city and rural commercial banks; and foreign bank branches. Regulation is handled by the National Financial Regulatory Administration (formed in 2023 from the earlier banking and insurance regulator) and the China Securities Regulatory Commission. Interest rates were long administered by the state and have been gradually liberalised, with the loan prime rate serving as the main benchmark since 2019.

Issues

Analysts point to high corporate and local-government debt, a large shadow banking sector of trust and wealth-management products, and periodic stress in smaller regional banks, most visibly during the property downturn of the 2020s involving developers such as Evergrande. At the same time, Chinese banks lead the world in digital payments and financial technology adoption, and the renminbi has slowly internationalised.
