Alphabet Inc. is an American multinational technology conglomerate holding company headquartered in Mountain View, California. It was created in 2015 through a restructuring of Google, which became Alphabet's largest subsidiary; Alphabet is the parent company of Google and of several former Google divisions, and is one of the world's most valuable companies.

The restructuring, announced by co-founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin in August 2015, was intended to make Google's core internet businesses 'cleaner and more accountable' while allowing its more speculative ventures — its 'Other Bets' — to be reported separately.

Structure

Alphabet's businesses include Google (search, advertising, Android, YouTube, Google Cloud and consumer hardware), and subsidiaries grouped as 'Other Bets' such as Waymo (self-driving technology), Verily (life sciences), DeepMind (Artificial intelligence research), Wing (drone delivery) and CapitalG and GV (investment arms).

Sundar Pichai, who became CEO of Google in 2015, became CEO of Alphabet in December 2019 when Page and Brin stepped back from management roles, though the founders retain voting control through special share classes.

Business

Alphabet earns the great majority of its revenue from advertising on Google's services. It is routinely ranked among the largest companies in the world by market capitalisation, and, with Apple, Amazon, Microsoft and Meta, is counted among the 'Big Tech' companies.

The company has faced major antitrust actions, including a 2024 United States court ruling that Google illegally maintained a monopoly in general search — a case whose remedies phase drew wide attention.
