Technology lifecycle

The technology lifecycle (or technology life cycle) describes the commercial development of a technology over time, from its initial research and development through market introduction, growth, and maturity, to its eventual decline and replacement by a successor technology. The model is related to, but distinct from, the product life cycle: several products may be based on one technology, and a technology typically outlives any single product.

Stages

The lifecycle is usually depicted as an S-curve of performance or adoption against time, and is commonly divided into four phases:

  • Research and development (emergence): the technology is invented and developed; performance is low, costs are high, and the technology is often subsidised or confined to niche applications — sometimes called the "bleeding edge".
  • Ascent (growth): the technology is commercialised and adopted rapidly; performance improves quickly and a dominant design may emerge. Technologies at the start of this phase are sometimes described as "leading edge".
  • Maturity: the technology is widely accepted, improvements are incremental, and it becomes a standard part of industry practice. Revenues and know-how are maximised.
  • Decline (obsolescence): a newer technology with superior performance or economics supersedes the old one, which retreats to niches or disappears.

Related concepts

The technology lifecycle underlies the Gartner hype cycle, which maps technologies through phases of inflated expectations and disillusionment, and Geoffrey Moore's technology adoption lifecycle, which classifies adopters from innovators and early adopters through the early and late majority to laggards. Innovation scholars use the model to explain phenomena such as disruptive innovation, in which an initially inferior technology improves along its S-curve until it displaces an incumbent whose own curve has plateaued. Businesses use lifecycle analysis in technology management, roadmap planning, and decisions about when to invest in, harvest, or abandon a technology.
