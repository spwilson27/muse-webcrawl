Kalalau Valley

The Kalalau Valley is one of the largest and most spectacular valleys on the Nā Pali Coast of the Hawaiian island of Kauai. Located within Nā Pali Coast State Wilderness Park, the valley opens onto Kalalau Beach, a broad crescent of sand backed by fluted cliffs rising some 2,000 feet.

Access

No road reaches the valley. It is accessible by the Kalalau Trail, an 11-mile (18 km) footpath from Haʻena State Park along the coast, by kayak in summer conditions, or by boat. Camping at Kalalau Beach requires a permit from Hawaii's Division of State Parks, and stays are limited.

History

The valley was inhabited by Native Hawaiians, who farmed taro in irrigated terraces, until the early twentieth century. Stone walls, heiau (temples) and house platforms remain among the guava and Java plum forest that now covers the valley floor. In the modern era the remote valley also attracted a number of unauthorised long-term campers, periodically removed by authorities.

The valley and beach have appeared in films including the 1976 King Kong remake and The Amazing Race television series.
