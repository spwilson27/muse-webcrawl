Flare (countermeasure)

TypeInfrared countermeasure
Used byMilitary aircraft, some civilian aircraft
CountersInfrared homing (heat-seeking) missiles

A flare or decoy flare is an aerial infrared countermeasure used by aircraft to counter an infrared homing ("heat-seeking") surface-to-air missile or air-to-air missile. Flares are commonly ejected in salvos from dispensers mounted on the aircraft and burn at temperatures hotter than the aircraft's engines, presenting the missile's seeker with a brighter infrared target than the aircraft itself.

Operation

When a missile approach warning system detects an incoming infrared-guided missile — or when a pilot elects to dispense pre-emptively in a threat area — pyrotechnic cartridges are fired from the dispenser. A typical flare pellet is composed of magnesium or another hot-burning metal combined with a fluoropolymer such as Teflon (commonly called MTV, for magnesium/Teflon/Viton), and ignites on ejection, burning for a few seconds while decelerating rapidly away from the aircraft's flight path.

Effectiveness and counter-countermeasures

Early seekers homed on the brightest infrared source in their field of view, so a flare could seduce them reliably. Modern seekers use two-colour sensing, spectral discrimination, rise-rate logic, and spatial imaging to distinguish the flare's fast-decaying, fast-separating signature from an aircraft, prompting counter-tactics such as dispensing flares in programmed patterns and combining them with directional infrared countermeasures (DIRCM) and towed decoys.

Safety and civilian use

Because spent flares can start fires on the ground, training ranges restrict their use in dry conditions. Some civilian aircraft operating into high-risk regions have been fitted with flare dispensers or missile-jamming systems; an attempt to shoot down an Israeli airliner at Mombasa in 2002 accelerated interest in civil protection systems.
