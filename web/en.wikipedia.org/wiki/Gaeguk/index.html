Gaeguk

Gaeguk (Korean: 개국; Hanja: 開國), literally "the founding of a state", is a Korean historical concept and term referring to the establishment or foundation of a new kingdom or dynasty, most famously the founding of the kingdom of Goguryeo in 37 BC by Jumong, and of the Joseon dynasty in 1392 by Yi Seong-gye (King Taejo). In Korean historiography, founding narratives — often framed by mythic elements such as the descent of a divine ancestor — legitimised the new state and its ruling house.

Usage

The term is used in the titles of anniversaries and national foundation myths. Korea's most famous foundation legend is that of Dangun, the legendary founder of Gojoseon in 2333 BC, whose "opening of the country" (gaecheon) is commemorated by the national holiday Gaecheonjeol on 3 October. Royal era names and temple names of dynastic founders also invoke the act of state-founding, and later Korean states traced their legitimacy to earlier founding acts.

Founding myths

Recorded in texts such as the Samguk Sagi and Samguk Yusa, Korean gaeguk narratives typically combine a heaven-sent ancestor, an egg or miraculous birth, and the gathering of followers at a chosen capital. Jumong's flight from Buyeo and founding of Goguryeo, Kim Suro's founding of Geumgwan Gaya, and Park Hyeokgeose's founding of Silla all follow related patterns of sacred kingship.
