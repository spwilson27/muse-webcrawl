Tendering

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tendering is the process of inviting bids (tenders) for a project, contract, or supply of goods and services, and of evaluating and selecting among the bids received. It is widely used in public procurement and in the construction industry, where a client issues an invitation to tender (ITT) or request for tender (RFT) describing the work required, the conditions of contract, and the criteria by which bids will be assessed.

Process

A typical tendering process involves: preparing tender documents (specifications, drawings, bills of quantities and draft contract); advertising or selectively inviting bidders; a period during which bidders may ask clarification questions; submission of bids, traditionally sealed to prevent tampering; opening and evaluation of bids against price and non-price criteria; and award of the contract to the successful tenderer. Unsuccessful bidders are usually notified and may be offered a debriefing.

Types of tendering

  • Open tendering — any supplier may submit a bid; common for government contracts to ensure competition and transparency.
  • Selective (restricted) tendering — only pre-qualified suppliers are invited, often after a pre-qualification questionnaire stage.
  • Negotiated tendering — the client negotiates terms with one or a small number of suppliers, used for specialist work or extensions of existing contracts.
  • Single-stage and two-stage tendering — in two-stage tendering, an initial bid on partial information is followed by negotiation and a final price once the design is developed.
  • E-tendering — the process conducted through an electronic procurement portal.

Public procurement

Government tendering is regulated in many jurisdictions to promote fairness, value for money and the prevention of corruption. In the European Union, procurement directives historically set threshold values above which contracts must be advertised across member states; in the United Kingdom, the Procurement Act 2023 reformed the regime. The World Trade Organization's Agreement on Government Procurement sets similar rules internationally.

Criticism

Tendering can be costly for bidders, and lowest-price selection may disadvantage quality. Collusive tendering (bid rigging), in which competitors secretly agree on who will win, is illegal in most jurisdictions.
