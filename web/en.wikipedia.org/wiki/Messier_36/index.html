Messier 36

Messier 36 (also known as M36 or NGC 1960) is an open star cluster in the constellation Auriga, lying roughly 4,100 light-years from Earth. It was discovered by Giovanni Battista Hodierna before 1654 and later catalogued by Charles Messier in 1764.

Characteristics

The cluster contains at least 60 stars and spans about 14 light-years. Its brightest members are hot B-type stars, and the cluster is relatively young, with an estimated age of about 25 million years. It has no red giants, a sign of its youth compared with clusters like the Pleiades, which it otherwise resembles in miniature.

Observation

With an apparent magnitude of about 6.3, M36 is just visible to the naked eye under dark skies and is an easy target for binoculars. It forms a famous trio of open clusters in Auriga with the nearby Messier 37 and Messier 38.
