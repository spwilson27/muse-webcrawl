Salvation Mountain


Location: Near Niland, Imperial County, California, United States
Coordinates: 33°15′15″N 115°28′21″W
Creator: Leonard Knight (1931–2014)
Medium: Adobe, straw, latex paint

Salvation Mountain is a hillside environmental artwork covering much of a small hill near Niland, California, in the Lower Desert of Southern California, north of Calipatria at the southern end of the Salton Sea area. It was created by local resident Leonard Knight (1931–2014) out of adobe bricks, discarded tires and windows, automobile parts, and thousands of gallons of latex paint. It is a monumental work of folk art dedicated to the message that "God Is Love."

History

Knight, a Vermont native, arrived in the area in the 1980s. After his first smaller mound collapsed in 1989, he rebuilt the mountain using adobe made from clay and straw, applying countless coats of paint to protect the structure from the desert elements. Over roughly three decades the artwork grew to stand about 50 feet (15 m) high and cover a large hillside, decorated with painted flowers, trees, waterfalls, suns, and birds, alongside extensive Bible verses and religious messages, including the Sinner's Prayer and the words "God Is Love" emblazoned across the top.

Preservation

Salvation Mountain has been recognised as an important work of American folk art. In 2002, U.S. Senator Barbara Boxer described it in the Congressional Record as "a unique and visionary sculpture... a national treasure." After Knight's health declined and he died in 2014, the site has been maintained by Salvation Mountain Inc., a non-profit organisation, which continues preservation efforts against the harsh desert climate. The site draws tens of thousands of visitors annually and sits near the Slab City community.
