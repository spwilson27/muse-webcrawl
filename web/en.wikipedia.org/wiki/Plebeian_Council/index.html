Plebeian Council

The Plebeian Council (Latin: Concilium Plebis) was the principal assembly of the common people of the ancient Roman Republic. It functioned as a legislative assembly, through which the plebeians (commoners) could pass legislation (called plebiscites), elect plebeian tribunes and plebeian aediles, and try judicial cases.

History

The council was created as a result of the Conflict of the Orders, beginning with the first secession of the plebs in 494 BC. Initially its resolutions applied only to plebeians, but after the Lex Hortensia of 287 BC, plebiscites became binding on all Roman citizens, making the Plebeian Council the principal legislative body of the Republic.

Procedure

The council was convened and presided over by a plebeian tribune, and voted by Roman tribe. Only plebeians could vote in it; patricians were excluded.
