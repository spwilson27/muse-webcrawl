Eilat

Eilat (Hebrew: אֵילַת) is Israel's southernmost city, a busy port and resort on the northern shore of the Red Sea, at the head of the Gulf of Aqaba. With a population of around 53,000, it is known for its warm, dry climate, coral reefs, and tax-free shopping, and it is a popular destination for domestic and international tourists.

History

The area has been inhabited since antiquity and is associated in biblical tradition with the port of Ezion-Geber. The modern city was founded in 1949, after Israeli forces reached the site — then known as Umm al-Rashrash — during the final operation of the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, an event commemorated by the famous "Ink Flag." Eilat developed as Israel's only Red Sea port, giving access to shipping routes to Asia and East Africa; the Port of Eilat was opened in 1955.

Geography and climate

Eilat lies in the Arava valley between the Negev desert mountains and the sea, bordering Egypt at Taba to the south and Jordan, whose city of Aqaba lies just across the gulf, to the east. It has a hot desert climate with very high summer temperatures and abundant sunshine year-round, while the sea remains warm enough for swimming in all seasons.

Tourism and nature

Attractions include the Coral Beach Nature Reserve, the Underwater Observatory Marine Park, and nearby Timna Valley with its ancient copper mines. The surrounding Eilat Mountains and the coral reefs of the gulf are important for bird migration and marine conservation; the city hosts an international birdwatching centre on the migration route between Africa and Europe.
