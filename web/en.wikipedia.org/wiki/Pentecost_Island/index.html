Pentecost Island

Pentecost Island

Location: Coral Sea, Vanuatu

Coordinates: 15°45′S 168°11′E

Area: 490 km²

Highest point: Mount Vulmat (946 m)

Population: about 17,000

Pentecost Island is one of the 83 islands that make up the South Pacific nation of Vanuatu. It is located in Penama Province, between the islands of Ambae and Maewo to the north and Ambrym to the south.

Geography

The island is mountainous and heavily forested, stretching about 60 km north to south. A rugged mountain range runs down its spine, rising to Mount Vulmat at 946 metres. The east coast receives heavy rainfall and is cut by waterfalls, while the west coast is somewhat drier. There are no towns; the population lives in villages, mostly along the coasts.

Land diving

Pentecost is famous as the home of land diving (in local languages, Gol), a ritual in which men jump from tall wooden towers with vines tied to their ankles. Practised in the island's south, the ceremony — related to the harvest of yams and ideas of fertility and manhood — is widely regarded as a precursor of modern bungee jumping. The jumper's hair is meant to brush the ground, fertilising the soil.

Culture and language

Pentecost is linguistically diverse, with several indigenous languages including Raga, Apma, Ske and Sa. Chiefs and customary systems remain strong, and the island is known for finely woven mats used as traditional currency and in ceremony.

History

The island was sighted by the French explorer Louis Antoine de Bougainville in 1768, who named it after the Christian feast of Pentecost, the day of his arrival. During the colonial era it lay within the Anglo-French Condominium of the New Hebrides until Vanuatu's independence in 1980.
