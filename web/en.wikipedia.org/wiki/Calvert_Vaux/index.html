Calvert Vaux

BornDecember 20, 1824, London, England
DiedNovember 19, 1895 (aged 70), Brooklyn, New York, U.S.
OccupationArchitect, landscape architect
Known forCo-design of Central Park

Calvert Vaux (December 20, 1824 – November 19, 1895) was an English-American architect and landscape designer, best known as the co-designer, with Frederick Law Olmsted, of New York City's Central Park. Vaux's picturesque approach to landscape design, learned in the English romantic tradition, shaped many of the great urban parks of nineteenth-century America.

Early life and career

Vaux was born in London and trained as an architect. In 1850 he travelled to the United States to work with the influential landscape designer Andrew Jackson Downing in Newburgh, New York. After Downing's death in 1852, Vaux continued the practice with Frederick Clarke Withers.

Central Park

In 1857 Vaux persuaded Olmsted, then superintendent of Central Park, to join him in entering the design competition for the park. Their "Greensward Plan" won, and the two men formed a partnership that lasted, with interruptions, until 1872. Vaux designed many of the park's original structures, including Bethesda Terrace's architectural elements, Bow Bridge, and the Belvedere Castle.

Later work

With Olmsted and Jacob Wrey Mould, Vaux laid out Prospect Park in Brooklyn and other parks. He also designed buildings for the Metropolitan Museum of Art and the American Museum of Natural History in their early forms. Vaux drowned in Gravesend Bay, Brooklyn, in November 1895.

Legacy

Although long overshadowed by Olmsted, Vaux is now recognised as an equal creative partner in the design of Central Park and a pioneer of the American landscape architecture profession.
