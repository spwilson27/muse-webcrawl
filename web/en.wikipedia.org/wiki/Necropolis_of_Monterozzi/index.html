The Necropolis of Monterozzi (Italian: Necropoli dei Monterozzi) is an Etruscan necropolis on a hill east of Tarquinia in Lazio, Italy. It contains about 6,000 graves cut into the rock, of which around 200 are decorated with wall paintings — the largest surviving corpus of Etruscan painting and a principal source of knowledge about Etruscan life and beliefs about the afterlife.

The painted tombs

The tombs, mostly single-chamber underground rooms with pitched ceilings imitating houses, date mainly from the seventh to the third century BC. Famous examples include the Tomb of the Leopards, with its banquet scene; the Tomb of Hunting and Fishing; the Tomb of the Lionesses; and the Tomb of the Augurs. The paintings depict banquets, dances, games, hunting, and mythological scenes in vivid colour.

Heritage status

Together with the Banditaccia necropolis at Cerveteri, Monterozzi was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in 2004 as the "Etruscan Necropolises of Cerveteri and Tarquinia". Original detached frescoes and grave goods are displayed in the National Museum of Tarquinia in the Palazzo Vitelleschi, while the tombs themselves are visited via protective glass screens on site.
