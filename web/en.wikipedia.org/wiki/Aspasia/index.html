Aspasia (c. 470 – c. 400 BC) was a metic woman of Miletus who became the partner of the Athenian statesman Pericles and one of the most famous women of classical antiquity. Celebrated for her intellect and skill in rhetoric, she was said to have hosted gatherings of leading Athenians and to have taught rhetoric to Socrates, though the surviving sources — comedy, philosophy and later biography — mix fact with hostile caricature.

Life

As a foreign-born woman, Aspasia could not legally marry an Athenian citizen under Pericles' own citizenship law of 451 BC, but she lived with him as his partner from about 445 BC and bore him a son, Pericles the Younger, who was later legitimised by special decree. After Pericles died of the plague in 429 BC, she is said to have lived with the sheep-dealer and politician Lysicles.

Reputation and sources

Comic poets such as Cratinus and Aristophanes mocked Aspasia, blaming her for the Peloponnesian War and portraying her as a brothel-keeper. Plato, by contrast, in the Menexenus, has Socrates credit her as his teacher of rhetoric and composer of Pericles' famous funeral oration — though the dialogue may be ironic. Xenophon mentions her respectfully, and Plutarch's Life of Pericles preserves much of the later tradition.

Legacy

Aspasia has been a recurring figure in literature and feminist scholarship, often read as evidence for the intellectual life of women in classical Athens despite the restrictions placed upon them.
