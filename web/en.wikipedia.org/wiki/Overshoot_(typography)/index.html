Overshoot (typography)

Round and pointed letters overshoot the baseline and cap line

In typeface design, overshoot is the degree to which a letter extends slightly beyond the baseline, cap height, or x-height lines in order to appear the same size as flat-topped letters. Glyphs with curved or pointed parts — such as the letters C, O and Q, the apex of A, and the bottom of round lowercase letters — must be drawn a little taller, typically by about 1–3% of the letter height, because the human eye perceives a curve that only touches a boundary at one point as smaller than a flat edge that runs along the boundary.

Without overshoot, round letters placed exactly between the same guidelines as flat letters would look visibly smaller and appear to float above the baseline. Overshoot is applied both above and below the nominal limits: for example, a capital O extends slightly above the cap height and slightly below the baseline.

The required amount of overshoot varies with the size at which the type will be used; in text faces the effect is subtle, while at very small sizes relatively more overshoot and other optical corrections are needed.
