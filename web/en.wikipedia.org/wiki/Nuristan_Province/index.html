Nuristan Province (Pashto/Dari: نورستان) is one of the 34 provinces of Afghanistan, located in the eastern part of the country on the southern slopes of the Hindu Kush mountains, bordering Pakistan. Its capital is Parun. The province is one of the most remote and least developed in Afghanistan, characterised by rugged, densely forested mountain terrain, deep valleys, and a distinct population speaking Nuristani languages.

History

Until the 1890s the region was known as Kafiristan, "land of the unbelievers", because its inhabitants — the Nuristani people — practised an ancient polytheistic religion related to Vedic traditions and resisted conversion to Islam for centuries. Alexander the Great is said to have campaigned in the region, and local tradition sometimes claims descent from his soldiers. In 1895–1896 the Emir Abdur Rahman Khan conquered the region and forcibly converted its population to Islam; it was renamed Nuristan, "land of light". The conquest and conversion are commemorated in the region's name change and remain a defining episode in Nuristani identity.

Geography and people

Nuristan is divided into districts including Parun, Kamdesh, Barg-i Matal, Du Ab, Nurgaram, Wama, Waygal and Mandol. The province is known for its carved wooden architecture, terraced fields, and distinctive dress and customs. The Nuristani languages — including Kati, Prasun, Waigali, Tregami and Ashkun — form a separate branch of the Indo-Iranian language family, distinct from both Iranian and Indo-Aryan languages.

Recent history

Nuristan was an early centre of resistance to the Soviet-backed Afghan government in the late 1970s, and during the war in Afghanistan after 2001 it saw heavy fighting between coalition and Afghan forces and Taliban and other insurgent groups, with several remote American outposts attacked, most famously at Wanat in 2008 and Combat Outpost Keating in 2009. The province returned to Taliban control following the August 2021 offensive as international forces withdrew.
