Pier

A pier is a raised structure that rises above a body of water and usually juts out from its shore, typically supported by piles or pillars, and provides above-water access to offshore areas. Frequent pier uses include access for seaplanes, boat landing, fishing, and leisure, such as promenading and sightseeing. Although similar, jetties and wharves serve different purposes: a wharf lies parallel to the shore, while a pier extends perpendicular to it.

A pleasure pier extending over the sea.

Types

  • Working piers handle passengers and cargo, and may include warehouses, cranes and ferry terminals.
  • Pleasure piers, built in large numbers at British and American seaside resorts in the Victorian era, offer amusements, theatres, pavilions and rides; examples include Brighton Palace Pier (opened 1899) and the Santa Monica Pier in California.
  • Fishing piers provide anglers access to deeper water without a boat.

Construction

Piers are supported on piles of timber, steel or concrete driven into the seabed, or on masonry or concrete pillars; the deck may be timber, concrete or steel. Designers must account for waves, tides, storms, ship impact and marine borers. The oldest surviving cast-iron pier is often considered to be Ryde Pier on the Isle of Wight (1814), while the first purely recreational iron pleasure pier is usually credited to Southport Pier (1860) or West Pier, Brighton (1866).
