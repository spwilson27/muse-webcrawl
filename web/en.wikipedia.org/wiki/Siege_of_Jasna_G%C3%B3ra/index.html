Siege of Jasna Góra

Date18 November – 27 December 1655
LocationJasna Góra Monastery, Częstochowa
ResultPolish victory

The Siege of Jasna Góra took place in the winter of 1655 during the Swedish invasion of Poland known as the Deluge. A Swedish force of roughly 2,000–3,000 men under Burchard Müller von der Lühnen besieged the fortified Pauline monastery of Jasna Góra at Częstochowa, home of the Black Madonna icon.

The defence

The garrison — about 160 soldiers, 70 monks, and volunteers under Prior Augustyn Kordecki — repelled assaults for forty days until the Swedes withdrew on 27 December. Kordecki's account of the siege made it a national legend.

Legacy

The successful defence became a turning point in morale for the Polish resistance, inspired King John II Casimir's Lwów Oath proclaiming Mary Queen of Poland, and remains a central episode of Polish national and Catholic identity, dramatised in Henryk Sienkiewicz's novel The Deluge.
