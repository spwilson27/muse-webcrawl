Seminole Tribe of Florida

The Seminole Tribe of Florida is a federally recognised Seminole tribe based in the U.S. state of Florida. It received federal recognition in 1957 and governs reservations at Hollywood, Big Cypress, Brighton, Immokalee, Fort Pierce and Tampa, with its headquarters in Hollywood, Florida. Its members descend from the Seminoles who resisted removal during the Seminole Wars and remained in the Florida Everglades.

History

After the Third Seminole War (1855–1858), only a few hundred Seminoles remained in Florida, living in relative isolation in the Everglades and Big Cypress Swamp. Road construction and tourism in the 20th century brought greater contact; many Seminoles worked in tourist villages or made crafts for sale. During the federal termination era, the tribe organised under a constitution in 1957 to preserve its status, adopting the name Seminole Tribe of Florida.

Government and economy

The tribe is governed by an elected Tribal Council representing the reservations. In 1979 it opened one of the first high-stakes bingo halls on tribal land, a venture upheld in litigation that helped establish modern tribal gaming; the Supreme Court's later decision in California v. Cabazon Band (1987) and the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act (1988) provided the legal framework. In 2006 the tribe purchased Hard Rock International for $965 million, becoming owners of the global hospitality and casino brand. Gaming revenue funds per-capita distributions, health, education and housing programmes for tribal citizens.

Culture

Members speak Mikasuki and Muscogee (Creek). Cultural traditions include patchwork clothing, chickee architecture, alligator wrestling, and the annual Green Corn Dance ceremony.
