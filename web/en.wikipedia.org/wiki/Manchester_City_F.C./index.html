Manchester City Football Club is a professional football club based in Manchester, England, that competes in the Premier League, the top flight of English football. Founded in 1880 as St. Mark's (West Gorton), it became Ardwick Association Football Club in 1887 and adopted its current name in 1894.

History

City won its first major honour, the FA Cup, in 1904, and enjoyed a successful period in the late 1960s and early 1970s under Joe Mercer and Malcolm Allison, winning the league title, FA Cup, League Cup and the European Cup Winners' Cup. After years of decline that saw the club drop to the third tier in 1998, City recovered and returned to the top flight.

The club's modern era began with its takeover in 2008 by the Abu Dhabi United Group. Heavy investment brought a new wave of success, beginning with the FA Cup in 2011 and a dramatic first Premier League title in 2012, won on the final day with Sergio Agüero's stoppage-time goal. Under manager Pep Guardiola, appointed in 2016, City became the dominant force in English football, winning multiple Premier League titles — including the 2017–18 "Centurions" season with a record 100 points — and in 2023 won the UEFA Champions League to complete a continental treble.

Stadium and rivalry

City played at Maine Road from 1923 until 2003, when it moved to the City of Manchester Stadium, known as the Etihad Stadium for sponsorship reasons. The club's fiercest rivalry is with Manchester United, contested as the Manchester derby.
