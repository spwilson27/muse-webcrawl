Gravimetry

Gravimetry is the measurement of the strength and variation of a gravitational field. It is a branch of geophysics concerned with determining Earth's gravity field, its spatial variations (gravity anomalies), and the shape of the Earth through combined terrestrial, airborne, marine and satellite measurements.

Methods and instruments

Instruments used to measure gravity are called gravimeters. Most gravimeters are sophisticated spring balances measuring relative changes in gravity; the classic LaCoste-Romberg instrument uses a "zero-length spring" to support a proof mass, and modern absolute gravimeters time the free fall of a reflective mass in a vacuum using laser interferometry. Gravity is measured in gals, with the microgal (µGal) used for high-precision work; field surveys must correct for tides, instrument drift, latitude, elevation and terrain.

Applications

Gravity anomalies reveal subsurface density contrasts, so gravimetry is used in mineral and petroleum exploration, volcanology, geodesy, seismology, archaeology and groundwater monitoring. Satellites such as GRACE and GOCE have mapped the global gravity field and its time variations, tracking ice-sheet loss and ocean mass changes.
