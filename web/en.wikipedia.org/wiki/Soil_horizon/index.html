Soil horizon

A soil horizon is a layer parallel to the soil surface whose physical, chemical and biological characteristics differ from the layers above and beneath. Horizons are defined in many soil classification systems, and the sequence of horizons from the surface down is called a soil profile. Most soils have three major horizons (A, B, C) plus an organic layer (O).

Master horizons

HorizonDescription
OSurface layer dominated by organic material — litter and partly decomposed matter.
AMineral topsoil mixed with humus; usually dark; most biological activity occurs here.
EPale, leached layer from which clay, iron and aluminium have been removed (eluviation); characteristic of podzols.
BSubsoil where materials leached from above accumulate (illuviation) — clays, iron oxides, carbonates.
CWeathered parent material with little biological influence.
RHard bedrock.

Formation

Horizons develop through pedogenesis: additions, losses, translocations and transformations of material over time, driven by climate, organisms, relief, parent material and time (the CLORPT factors of Hans Jenny). Suffixes denote specific features, e.g. Bt for clay accumulation, Bk for carbonates, Ap for a ploughed A horizon.

Importance

Horizon depth, texture and chemistry determine drainage, rooting, nutrient supply and the storage of soil carbon; describing them is the basis of soil survey and land evaluation.
