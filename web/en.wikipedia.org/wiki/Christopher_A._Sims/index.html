Christopher Albert Sims (October 21, 1942 – March 14, 2025) was an American econometrician and macroeconomist. He shared the 2011 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Thomas J. Sargent "for their empirical research on cause and effect in the macroeconomy." His development of vector autoregression (VAR) transformed empirical macroeconomics and the analysis of monetary policy.

Career

Sims earned his PhD in economics from Harvard University in 1968 and taught at Harvard, the University of Minnesota, Yale University and, from 1999, Princeton University. He served as president of the Econometric Society and the American Economic Association.

Research

In his influential 1980 paper "Macroeconomics and Reality," Sims attacked the "incredible identification restrictions" of large structural Keynesian models and proposed VARs — systems in which each variable is regressed on lags of itself and all other variables — letting the data speak with minimal theoretical assumptions. Impulse-response analysis from VARs became the standard language for studying how economies react to policy shocks. Sims later championed rational-inattention theory and, with colleagues, the fiscal theory of the price level, arguing that price-level determination depends jointly on monetary and fiscal policy.
