Hot Wheels is an American brand of scale model cars invented by Elliot Handler and introduced by his company Mattel on May 18, 1968. It was the primary competitor of Matchbox until Mattel bought Matchbox owner Tyco Toys in 1997. Many automobile manufacturers have since licensed Hot Wheels to make scale models of their cars, allowing the use of original design blueprints and detailing. Although Hot Wheels were originally intended to be for children and young adults, they have become popular with adult collectors, for whom limited edition models are now made available.

History

Hot Wheels were conceived in 1968 by Mattel co-founder Elliot Handler, who saw his son playing with Matchbox cars and decided to create a competitor line made in California. The first line of Hot Wheels cars, known collectively as "The Original Sweet 16", was made in 1968. These were the first of the Red Lines, so named because of the red-painted line on the sidewalls of their tyres. The cars featured Spectraflame paint jobs, chrome-plated exposed engines, and low-friction wheels that made them roll much faster than contemporary Matchbox cars.

The early design team included former General Motors designer Harry Bradley, who designed the Custom Fleetside, based on his own 1968 Chevrolet El Camino. The brand grew rapidly, and by 1970 Mattel was producing themed lines, playsets, and track sets. Over the decades Hot Wheels expanded into video games, animated series, and a vast collector market, with billions of cars produced.

Collecting

Hot Wheels are among the most widely collected toys in the world. Collectors hunt for variations in paint, wheels, and packaging, and rare models — such as the 1969 pink rear-loading Volkswagen "Beach Bomb" — have sold for six-figure prices. Mattel issues regular limited series including Treasure Hunts and premium collector lines.
