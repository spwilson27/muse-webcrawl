Handicraft

ExamplesPottery, weaving, woodworking, metalwork, embroidery, glassblowing
Related movementsArts and Crafts movement, folk art, DIY culture

A handicraft is a type of craft in which people make things primarily by hand or using only simple hand tools, rather than by fully automated industrial processes. Handicrafts are usually decorative or functional objects — pottery, textiles, jewellery, furniture, toys, and clothing — and the term also describes the traditional, often rural industries that produce them.

History

Before industrialisation, nearly all manufactured goods were handicrafts, produced by artisans organised in guilds or working in households under the putting-out system. The Industrial Revolution displaced hand production from most mass markets during the 18th and 19th centuries, provoking a reaction in the Arts and Crafts movement led by figures such as William Morris, who argued that hand work preserved both quality and the dignity of the worker. In the developing world, handicraft production remained a major source of rural employment.

Types

Major handicraft families include fibre arts (weaving, knitting, embroidery, lace, quilting), ceramics and pottery, woodwork and basketry, metalwork and jewellery, glasswork, leatherwork, and paper crafts. Many are strongly regional: particular patterns, glazes, and techniques function as markers of local and ethnic identity, and some, such as traditional craftsmanship itself, have been inscribed on UNESCO's lists of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Economic and cultural role

Handicrafts support millions of livelihoods, particularly for women and rural communities, and supply tourism and export markets with distinctive goods. Organisations promote fair-trade and geographical-indication labelling to protect artisans from machine-made imitations. In wealthy countries, handicrafts also flourish as hobbies and through online marketplaces, part of a wider revival of interest in handmade goods.
