Mist net

A mist net is a fine mesh net used by ornithologists and bat researchers to capture wild birds and bats for banding (ringing) and other research. The netting is nearly invisible when set against vegetation, and birds flying into it become entangled in loose pockets of net.

Use

Nets are typically strung between poles across flight paths, near water, roosts, or feeding sites, and checked every few minutes so captured animals can be extracted promptly, measured, weighed, banded, and released. Use requires permits in most countries because of the risk of injury if nets are left unattended.

History

Similar nets were used in Japan for centuries to trap birds for food; their scientific use spread after the Second World War.
