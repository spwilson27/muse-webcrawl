Leduc 0.21

The Leduc 0.21 was a research aircraft built in France in the early 1950s to explore ramjet propulsion, following the earlier Leduc 0.10. Like its predecessor, it was designed by René Leduc and was essentially a flying ramjet engine, with the pilot seated inside the intake's central body.

Design

The aircraft consisted of a large cylindrical fuselage that formed the ramjet duct, with a conical centre-body housing the cockpit. Because a ramjet produces no static thrust, the 0.21 could not take off unaided: it was carried aloft on the back of a SNCASE Languedoc mother ship and released at altitude and speed, after which its ramjet could sustain flight.

Flight testing

The 0.21 first flew, as a glider after release, in August 1953, and made its first powered flight later that year; a second example followed. The type reached speeds around Mach 0.85–0.9 in testing, gathering data that fed into the larger, supersonic Leduc 0.22 interceptor project, which was cancelled in 1958 when funding was withdrawn in favour of the Dassault Mirage III.

Specifications

The 0.21 was roughly 13 m long with a wingspan of about 11.6 m. Its single ramjet was supplemented by small auxiliary turbojets in some configurations to assist low-speed handling. One surviving example is preserved in France.
