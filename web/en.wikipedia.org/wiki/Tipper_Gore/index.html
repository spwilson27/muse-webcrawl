Mary Elizabeth Gore (née Aitcheson; born 19 August 1948), known as Tipper Gore, is an American author, photographer, and social activist. She was the Second Lady of the United States from 1993 to 2001 as the wife of Vice President Al Gore. She is best known as a co-founder of the Parents Music Resource Center (PMRC), whose 1985 Senate hearings led the recording industry to adopt "Parental Advisory" labels for music with explicit lyrics.

Early life and marriage

Gore was born in Washington, D.C., and raised in Arlington, Virginia. She met Al Gore at a high-school prom in 1965; they married in 1970. She earned a degree in psychology from Boston University and a master's degree in psychology from Vanderbilt University (1975), and worked as a newspaper photographer and in a home-improvement business while her husband entered politics.

Parents Music Resource Center

In 1985, after hearing Prince's song "Darling Nikki" played by her young daughter, Gore co-founded the PMRC with other Washington political spouses. The group publicized a list of songs it considered objectionable (the "Filthy Fifteen") and testified before a Senate committee. Although no law resulted, the Recording Industry Association of America agreed to voluntary warning labels — the black-and-white "Parental Advisory" stickers that became standard. Gore's 1987 book Raising PG Kids in an X-Rated Society defended the campaign. The effort drew lasting criticism from musicians and free-speech advocates — Frank Zappa, Dee Snider, and John Denver testified against it — and became a touchstone in debates over art and censorship.

Second Lady and later advocacy

As Second Lady (1993–2001), Gore worked on mental-health policy, serving as the President's adviser on mental health and advocating for parity in insurance coverage; she spoke publicly about her own treatment for depression. She also worked on homelessness and disaster relief. The Gores announced their separation in 2010 after forty years of marriage. Gore has continued advocacy on mental health and homelessness and is a published photographer whose work has been exhibited and included in her books.
