Marketing

[Image: a brightly lit billboard advertising campaign in a city square at night]

Marketing is the set of activities by which organisations identify, anticipate and satisfy customer needs profitably: researching markets, designing products, setting prices, promoting them and delivering them through distribution channels. The American Marketing Association defines it as the activity and processes for creating, communicating, delivering and exchanging offerings of value to customers and society.

The marketing mix and development

The classical framework is the four Ps — product, price, place and promotion (McCarthy, 1960), later extended for services — supported by market segmentation, targeting and positioning (STP) and by the management of brands. Advertising is its most visible promotional tool. Twentieth-century mass marketing gave way to relationship and digital marketing: search and social media, content, influencer campaigns and data-driven personalisation now dominate budgets, raising questions of privacy and manipulation studied by regulators and by behavioural economics. Marketing is a major business function and employer, and an academic discipline combining economics, psychology and statistics.
