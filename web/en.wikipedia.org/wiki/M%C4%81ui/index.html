Māui

Māui is a culture hero and trickster in Polynesian mythology. Very little distinction is made between his deity and mortal forms: in Māori tradition he is a demigod (atua), while in Hawaiian mythology he is an akua and descendant of the gods. Versions of his exploits are found across Polynesia, including in the traditions of Hawaiʻi, Tahiti, Tonga, Samoa, and Aotearoa (New Zealand).

Feats

The exploits attributed to Māui include:

  • Fishing up islands — using a magical fishhook made from the jawbone of his grandmother (or ancestress), Māui pulled great fish from the sea which became islands; in Māori tradition, New Zealand's North Island is Te Ika-a-Māui, "the fish of Māui", and the South Island his canoe (waka), Te Waipounamu.
  • Snaring the sun — Māui caught the sun in a noose of ropes (in Māori lore, made from the hair of his sister Hina) and beat it until it agreed to travel more slowly, lengthening the days.
  • Bringing fire to humans — Māui tricked or compelled the ancestress of fire, Mahuika, into giving him her burning fingernails, the source of fire in the world.
  • Lifting the sky — in some versions Māui pushed the sky upward so that people could stand upright.
  • Quest for immortality — Māui attempted to win immortality for mankind by entering the body of the goddess of death, Hine-nui-te-pō, but was crushed when a bird laughed and woke her; with his death, humans remained mortal.

In popular culture

Māui appears as a principal character in the 2016 Disney animated film Moana, voiced by Dwayne Johnson, whose shape-shifting feats and song "You're Welcome" draw on the traditional stories.
