Edward Angle

Edward Hartley Angle (June 1, 1855 – August 11, 1930) was an American dentist, widely regarded as "the father of modern orthodontics". He founded the first school and college of orthodontics, organised the first orthodontic society, and founded the first dental specialty journal.

Contributions

Angle's classification of malocclusion, published in the late nineteenth century, remains in use worldwide: it describes the relationship of the upper and lower first molars as Class I (normal), Class II (the lower teeth sit too far back) or Class III (the lower teeth sit too far forward). He designed a series of orthodontic appliances, culminating in the edgewise appliance, whose rectangular bracket and archwire became the foundation of modern fixed-appliance treatment. In 1900 he founded the Angle School of Orthodontia, the first formal training programme in the specialty, first in St. Louis and later in other cities including Pasadena, California.

Legacy

Angle insisted that orthodontics be a distinct specialty of dentistry and set demanding standards for its training. His journal, founded in 1915 as the International Journal of Orthodontia, survives as a specialty publication, and the Angle Society of Orthodontists continues his name.
