The NASA X-43 was an experimental unmanned hypersonic aircraft with multiple planned scale variations, forming part of NASA's Hyper-X programme. The X-43A, a waverider-configured lifting body about 3.7 metres long, was designed to test an air-breathing supersonic combustion ramjet (scramjet) engine at speeds above Mach 7 — the first aircraft to fly with a scramjet producing net thrust.

Development

The Hyper-X programme, begun in 1996, sought to demonstrate that a scramjet — a ramjet in which air passes through the combustion chamber at supersonic speed — could power flight. Because the X-43 could not take off under its own power, it was launched from a B-52 carrier aircraft stacked atop a modified Pegasus rocket booster, which accelerated it to test conditions where it separated and fired its hydrogen-fuelled scramjet for about ten seconds.

Flights

The first flight, on 2 June 2001, ended in failure when the booster lost control and was destroyed. The second flight, on 27 March 2004, reached approximately Mach 6.8, making the X-43A the fastest air-breathing aircraft in history at that time. The third and final flight, on 16 November 2004, reached Mach 9.6 (about 10,600 km/h) at roughly 34,000 metres altitude — still the fastest speed ever achieved by an air-breathing, winged aircraft. The test vehicles were expendable and splashed into the Pacific Ocean.

Legacy

Data from the X-43 programme informed later scramjet research, including the Boeing X-51 Waverider, which flew longer sustained scramjet missions in 2010–2013.
