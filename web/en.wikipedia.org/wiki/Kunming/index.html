Kunming is the capital and largest city of Yunnan province, China. A prefecture-level city, it is the political, economic, communications and cultural centre of the province as well as its industrial seat. Located at the northern edge of Lake Dian and surrounded by temples, limestone hills and lake scenery, Kunming is famous for its mild climate and is nicknamed the "City of Eternal Spring".

Geography and climate

Kunming lies on the Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau at an elevation of about 1,900 metres, which tempers its subtropical highland climate (Köppen Cwb): winters are short, dry and mild, and summers warm but rarely hot, with temperatures averaging roughly 15 °C annually. Flowers bloom year-round, and the city is a major centre of China's flower and horticultural trade; the Dounan flower market is the largest in Asia.

History

The Dian culture of the Bronze Age flourished around Lake Dian in the first millennium BCE. A Chinese settlement at the site dates to 765 CE as Tuodong, a frontier town of the Nanzhao kingdom. Marco Polo described the city, then called Yachi. In the 19th century Kunming (then Yunnanfu) was a centre of the Panthay Rebellion. During the Second Sino-Japanese War it became the terminus of the Burma Road and hosted the American Volunteer Group, the "Flying Tigers", and the wartime National Southwestern Associated University (Lianda), which preserved Chinese higher education in exile.

Economy and transport

Modern Kunming is a hub for trade with Southeast Asia, with industries including tobacco, machinery, chemicals, phosphorus and copper processing. Kunming Changshui International Airport (opened 2012) is among China's busiest. Rail links include the line to Hanoi via Hekou (the historic Yunnan–Vietnam railway of 1910), high-speed lines to Shanghai and Guangzhou, and the China–Laos railway, which opened in 2021 from Kunming to Vientiane.

Sights

Nearby attractions include the Stone Forest (Shilin) karst formations, the Western Hills (Xishan) and Dianchi Lake, the Yuantong and Qiongzhu temples, and Green Lake Park, where Siberian black-headed gulls winter. Kunming hosted the 1999 World Horticultural Exposition and the first phase of the UN Biodiversity Conference (COP15) in 2021.
