Xin Qiji (28 May 1140 – 3 October 1207), courtesy name You'an and art name Jiaxuan, was a Chinese poet, military leader and statesman of the Southern Song dynasty. He is regarded as one of the greatest writers of ci poetry, and his works, filled with patriotic fervour and martial imagery, occupy a central place in Chinese literature.

Life

Xin was born in Jinan, Shandong, then under the rule of the Jurchen Jin dynasty. Inspired by his grandfather to restore the lost northern territories to the Song, he joined a rebel uprising at the age of 21 and famously led a daring raid of fifty horsemen into a Jin camp of tens of thousands to seize a captured rebel traitor. Crossing south, he entered Song service, where he submitted detailed strategic memorials urging recovery of the north — proposals the pacifist court repeatedly declined to act upon.

Career

Xin served in various posts in Jiangxi, Hunan and Fujian, where he founded an effective local militia known as the "Flying Tiger Army." His hawkish advocacy and independent manner repeatedly cost him office, and he spent long periods in frustrated retirement at Shangrao. He was briefly recalled to high office late in life before the failed northern expedition of 1206–1207; he died in 1207 reportedly calling out "Kill the enemy!" on his deathbed.

Poetry

About 620 of Xin's ci poems survive. Alongside Su Shi, he is the leading figure of the "heroic abandon" (haofang) style, writing of war, landscape, drink and political frustration with unmatched vigour and range. Poems such as "Written on a Wall in Zaokou" and his lyrics to the tunes "Partridge Sky" and "Eternal Joy" remain widely anthologised.
