Los Tigres del Norte (English: The Tigers of the North) are a Mexican norteño band from Sinaloa, Mexico, founded in 1968. Based in San Jose, California, they are one of the best-known groups in the genre and among the most successful Latin acts in the world, having sold tens of millions of records and won multiple Grammy and Latin Grammy Awards.

The group consists of the Hernández brothers — Jorge (accordion, lead vocals), Hernán (bass, vocals), Eduardo (saxophone, bajo sexto, vocals) and Luis (guitar, vocals) — along with their cousin Óscar Lara on drums. They took their name from an immigration official who, as the young musicians crossed into the United States, referred to them as "little tigers" (tigres), adding "del Norte" to distinguish them as emigrants heading north.

Music and themes

Los Tigres are renowned for their corridos — narrative ballads telling true-to-life stories of immigration, border life, labour, love, betrayal and social injustice. Songs such as "Contrabando y Traición" (1974), their breakthrough hit about a drug-smuggling couple, and "La Jaula de Oro" ("The Golden Cage"), about an undocumented immigrant who prospers materially in the United States yet feels imprisoned, have made them widely regarded as the voice of the Mexican immigrant experience. Jorge Hernández has often said that the band's songs are simply news stories set to music, drawn from the lives of their audience.

Career highlights

The band has recorded more than 60 albums and some 700 songs, appeared in films, and performed for audiences across the Americas and Europe, including shows for U.S. troops abroad. In 1988 they won the Grammy Award for Best Mexican-American Album for Gracias!... América... Sin Fronteras. They received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2014, and in 2015 became the first norteño act to perform at the Walt Disney Concert Hall. Their documentary Los Tigres del Norte at Folsom Prison (2019) marked fifty years since Johnny Cash's famed concert at the same prison.

Legacy

Frequently called "los ídolos del pueblo" (the idols of the people), Los Tigres del Norte transformed the corrido into a vehicle for social commentary and remain the defining norteño group for generations of listeners on both sides of the U.S.–Mexico border.
