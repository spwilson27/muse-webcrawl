Multiple (mathematics)

In mathematics, a multiple is the product of any quantity and an integer. In other words, for quantities a and b, b is a multiple of a if b = na for some integer n, in which case n is also called a cofactor or multiplier. If a is not zero, this is equivalent to saying that b/a is an integer with no remainder. If a and b are both integers, and b is a multiple of a, then a is called a divisor of b.

Examples

14, 49, −21 and 0 are multiples of 7, whereas 3 and −6 are not, because there are integers that 7 may be multiplied by to reach the values of 14, 49, 0 and −21 (2, 7, −3, and 0 respectively), but there are no integers that 7 may be multiplied by to reach the values of 3 or −6.

  • 14 = 7 × 2
  • 49 = 7 × 7
  • −21 = 7 × (−3)
  • 0 = 7 × 0

Properties

  • Every integer is a multiple of itself and of 1 and −1.
  • 0 is a multiple of every integer.
  • The product of any integer n and any integer is a multiple of n; in particular n itself, which equals the product of n and 1.
  • If a and b are multiples of x, then a + b and a − b are also multiples of x.
  • The set of multiples of an integer forms an infinite sequence obtained by repeated addition (skip counting).

Related notions

A submultiple is the dual concept: a is a submultiple of b if b is a multiple of a. The least common multiple of two integers is the smallest positive integer that is a multiple of both, a central notion in adding fractions and solving problems about periodic processes. If a number is a multiple of two smaller numbers it is coprime to neither unless further conditions hold; a multiple of a prime may be composite. In abstract algebra the multiples of an element of a module or ring generalize the integer notion.
