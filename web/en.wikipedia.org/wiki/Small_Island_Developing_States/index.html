Small Island Developing States

Small Island Developing States (SIDS) are a group of low-lying island countries and territories recognized by the United Nations as facing unique social, economic, and environmental vulnerabilities. The UN's Office of the High Representative for the Least Developed Countries, Landlocked Developing Countries and Small Island Developing States (UN-OHRLLS) classifies 39 states and 18 associate members as SIDS, grouped in three regions: the Caribbean; the Pacific; and the Atlantic, Indian Ocean and South China Sea (AIS).

Characteristics

SIDS share common traits: small populations and land areas, remoteness from major markets, narrow resource and export bases, dependence on imports (especially fuel and food), and high exposure to natural disasters and external economic shocks. Many Pacific SIDS have average elevations of only a few metres above sea level, making them acutely threatened by sea-level rise and storm surge driven by climate change. Their exclusive economic zones, by contrast, are often vast relative to their land area, making ocean resources central to their economies.

International recognition

SIDS were first recognized as a distinct category at the 1992 UN Conference on Environment and Development (Earth Summit) in Rio de Janeiro. Subsequent UN programmes include the Barbados Programme of Action (1994), the Mauritius Strategy (2005), the SAMOA Pathway (2014), and the Antigua and Barbuda Agenda for SIDS (2024), adopted at the fourth International Conference on SIDS. The Alliance of Small Island States (AOSIS) acts as their negotiating bloc in international climate negotiations and has been influential in pressing for the 1.5 °C global warming limit and for loss-and-damage finance.

Climate change

Sea-level rise threatens the very habitability of the lowest-lying atoll nations — Tuvalu, Kiribati, the Marshall Islands, and the Maldives. Tuvalu's 2023 constitutional declaration that its statehood persists regardless of loss of physical territory, and its "digital nation" initiative, exemplify the legal and existential questions SIDS raise for international law. Intensifying tropical cyclones, ocean acidification, and drought also strain freshwater supplies, fisheries, and infrastructure.

List

Caribbean SIDS include Barbados, Jamaica, the Bahamas, Trinidad and Tobago, Haiti, Cuba, and the Dominican Republic; Pacific SIDS include Fiji, Papua New Guinea, Samoa, Vanuatu, Tuvalu, Kiribati, and Palau; AIS SIDS include the Maldives, Mauritius, Seychelles, Singapore, and Cabo Verde.
