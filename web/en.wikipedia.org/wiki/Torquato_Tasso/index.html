Torquato Tasso (11 March 1544 – 25 April 1595) was an Italian poet of the 16th century, best known for his poem La Gerusalemme liberata (Jerusalem Delivered, 1581), in which he depicts a largely mythified version of the First Crusade.

Life

Son of the poet Bernardo Tasso, Torquato was educated at the courts of Urbino and Padua and entered the service of the Este family at Ferrara. There he wrote the pastoral drama Aminta (1573) and completed his epic. In later years he suffered severe mental illness — paranoia and delusions of persecution — and was confined for seven years in the hospital of Sant'Anna by Duke Alfonso II.

Jerusalem Delivered

The poem, in ottava rima, follows Godfrey of Bouillon's crusaders alongside invented romances — the warrior maiden Clorinda, the enchantress Armida, and the lovers Rinaldo and Sofronia. It became one of the most widely read and translated works of early modern Europe, influencing Spenser and Milton.

Later reputation

Tasso's suffering made him a Romantic archetype of the tortured genius, inspiring works by Goethe, Byron, and Liszt. He died in Rome in 1595, on the eve of a planned coronation as poet laureate on the Capitoline Hill.
