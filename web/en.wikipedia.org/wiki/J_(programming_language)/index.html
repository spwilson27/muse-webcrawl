J (programming language)

ParadigmArray, functional, tacit
Designed byKenneth E. Iverson, Roger Hui
First appeared1990
Typing disciplineDynamic

J is a high-level, general-purpose programming language in the APL family, developed in 1990 by Kenneth E. Iverson and Roger Hui. It is notable for its terse, ASCII-only notation, its array-oriented computation model, and its support for tacit (point-free) programming, in which functions are composed without explicit reference to their arguments.

History

J grew out of Iverson's work on APL and the functional language FP. Whereas APL required a special character set, J was designed so that its primitives could be typed with ordinary ASCII punctuation combined into digraphs. The language was implemented by Jsoftware, the company Iverson and Hui founded, and an open-source implementation has since been maintained by the community.

Language features

Programs in J are built from verbs (functions), nouns (data) and adverbs and conjunctions that modify verbs. Data are arrays of arbitrary rank, and most verbs apply element-wise or over whole arrays, so loops are rarely written explicitly. Trains of verbs can be combined into forks and hooks, giving tacit definitions their characteristic compactness. Famous one-liners include expressions that compute averages, prime numbers or the Game of Life in a few characters.

Reception

J is used in mathematical, statistical and financial applications and is admired for the elegance of its notation, though its density is also criticised as difficult for newcomers to read. It remains influential in the array-language community alongside APL and K.
