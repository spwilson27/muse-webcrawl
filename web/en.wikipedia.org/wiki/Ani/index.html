Ani (Armenian: Անի) is a ruined medieval Armenian city, now in Turkey's Kars Province beside the closed border with Armenia, on a triangular plateau above the Akhurian (Arpaçay) river gorge. Capital of the Bagratid Armenian kingdom from 961 to 1045, it was famed as the "city of 1,001 churches" and at its height may have held 100,000 inhabitants, rivalling Constantinople and Baghdad as a trading hub on routes between the Black Sea, Persia and Central Asia.

History

King Ashot III moved the Armenian capital to Ani in 961. Under the Bagratids the city flourished, ringed by massive double walls built by Smbat II, adorned with churches by the architect Trdat — who also repaired the dome of Hagia Sophia in Constantinople — including the great cathedral (completed 1001). Ani surrendered to the Byzantine Empire in 1045, fell to the Seljuks in 1064, passed through Shaddadid, Georgian and Mongol hands, and was devastated by an earthquake in 1319. Trade routes shifted, and the city dwindled until it was wholly deserted by the 1700s.

The site

Surviving monuments include the cathedral, the Church of St Gregory of Tigran Honents with its frescoes, the Church of the Holy Apostles, the mosque of Manuchihr (among the earliest in Anatolia), the citadel, and stretches of the city walls. Across the gorge in Armenia stands the ruined monastery of Horomos. Ani was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2016.
