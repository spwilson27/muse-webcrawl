Adobe After Effects

Image placeholder: the After Effects workspace

Adobe After Effects is a digital visual effects, motion graphics and compositing application developed by Adobe Inc. It is used in the post-production process of film making, video games and television production for creating motion graphics and visual effects, including keying, tracking, rotoscoping, compositing and animation. It is available as part of the Adobe Creative Cloud subscription.

Developer: Adobe Inc.
Initial release: January 1993
Platform: macOS, Windows
Type: Visual effects, motion graphics

History

After Effects was originally created by the Company of Science and Art (CoSA) in Providence, Rhode Island; version 1.0 was released in January 1993. CoSA was acquired by Aldus Corporation later that year, and Adobe acquired the software when it bought Aldus in 1994. Early versions ran only on Macintosh computers.

Features

The program organises work into compositions containing stacked layers of video, images, text and shapes, each animated over time with keyframes and expressions written in a JavaScript-based scripting language. Built-in tools include masks and mattes, chroma keying (Keylight), 2D and 3D camera tracking, the Roto Brush for isolating moving subjects, puppet tools for character animation, and a large library of effects. A large third-party ecosystem supplies plug-ins for particles, 3D elements, transitions and colour work.

Use

After Effects is a standard tool for title sequences, broadcast graphics, advertising, music videos and film visual effects, and it integrates closely with Adobe Premiere Pro, Photoshop, Illustrator and Cinema 4D.
