The Passover massacre was a suicide bombing carried out by Hamas at the Park Hotel in Netanya, Israel, on 27 March 2002, during a Passover seder attended by some 250 guests. Thirty civilians were killed and 140 were injured. It was the deadliest attack against Israelis during the Second Intifada up to that point.

The Park Hotel, Netanya.

The attack

The bomber, Abdel-Basset Odeh, a 25-year-old Palestinian from the nearby West Bank city of Tulkarm, entered the hotel's dining room disguised as a woman and detonated an explosive belt containing about ten kilograms of explosives among the guests gathered for the seder meal. Many of the victims were elderly people, including several Holocaust survivors.

Aftermath

The attack came amid a wave of suicide bombings during March 2002, sometimes called "Black March". Two days later, on 29 March, the Israeli government launched Operation Defensive Shield, the largest military operation in the West Bank since 1967, reoccupying Palestinian cities in an effort to dismantle militant infrastructure. Members of the Hamas cell behind the bombing were subsequently captured or killed; Odeh's dispatchers, including Abbas al-Sayed, were tried and sentenced in Israel to multiple life terms.
