Martyrs of Otranto

The Martyrs of Otranto, also known as Saints Antonio Primaldo and his Companions, were 813 inhabitants of the Salentine city of Otranto in southern Italy who were executed by Ottoman forces on 14 August 1480 for refusing to convert to Islam after the city fell.

The siege

On 28 July 1480 an Ottoman fleet under Gedik Ahmed Pasha landed near Otranto, part of a planned invasion of Italy by Sultan Mehmed II. The city, defended by a small garrison and its citizens, refused to surrender and was stormed on 11 August after a siege. The male population over fifteen was largely massacred; women and children were enslaved. According to tradition, the survivors who refused conversion — some 813 men led by the tailor Antonio Primaldo — were taken to the Hill of Minerva and beheaded.

Veneration

The martyrs' remains were collected by Alfonso II of Naples after Otranto was retaken in 1481 and are preserved in Otranto Cathedral, where their skulls are displayed in glass cases along the altar walls. Antonio Primaldo and his companions were beatified in 1771 by Pope Clement XIV and canonised by Pope Francis on 12 May 2013 — the first canonisation of Francis's pontificate — making them collectively the largest group of martyrs canonised in a single ceremony in that era.

Historiography

Modern historians note that details of the account — including the precise number 813 and the refusal-of-conversion narrative — rest on later tradition and have been debated, though the massacre itself is well attested.
