Antidote

An antidote is a substance that can counteract a form of poisoning. The term derives from the Greek antidoton, "given against". Antidotes act by various mechanisms: binding or chelating the poison, blocking its receptor, inhibiting its metabolic activation, or supplying a competing substrate.

Examples

  • Naloxone — opioid antagonist reversing opioid overdose
  • Atropine and pralidoxime — for organophosphate poisoning
  • N-acetylcysteine — for paracetamol (acetaminophen) poisoning
  • Flumazenil — benzodiazepine antagonist
  • Activated charcoal — adsorbs many ingested poisons in the gut
  • Antivenom — antibody preparations against snake and other venoms
  • Hydroxocobalamin — for cyanide poisoning

History

Universal antidotes such as theriac — a compound of dozens of ingredients associated with Mithridates VI of Pontus — were prized from antiquity into the 18th century, though their efficacy was largely mythical. Modern toxicology relies on specific antidotes, supportive care and poison-control centres.
