Dinefwr Castle (also spelled Dynevor) is a ruined medieval castle overlooking the River Towy near the town of Llandeilo in Carmarthenshire, Wales. It was traditionally the chief seat of the rulers of Deheubarth and is associated above all with the Lord Rhys (Rhys ap Gruffydd, reigned 1155–1197), who rebuilt it in stone as the centre of his kingdom in south-west Wales.

History

Tradition ascribes the first fortification on the site to Rhodri the Great in the 9th century. Rhys ap Gruffydd made Dinefwr the capital of Deheubarth and held a celebrated eisteddfod at nearby Cardigan in 1176. After his death the castle was fought over by his sons and grandsons, and by Welsh princes and Anglo-Norman marcher lords. Edward I captured it in 1277, and it remained in English hands, declining after a new castle and town were founded at Newton (now within Dinefwr Park) in the 1660s; the old castle was partially remodelled as a summer house by the Rice (Rhys) family, owners of the surrounding estate, before being abandoned as a ruin. The castle stands within Dinefwr Park, famous for its herd of white park cattle and its landscape, later laid out around Newton House.

Architecture

The castle occupies a steep-sided ridge defended by a rock-cut ditch. Its dominant feature is a great round keep of about 1170s date, three storeys high, with a later turret added when the ruin was converted into a summer house. The curtain wall encloses a small inner ward; a second, earlier castle site within the park — Castell Dinefwr's companion fortification — reflects the disputed history of the ridge.
