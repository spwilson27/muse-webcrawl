The Goetheanum, located in Dornach in the canton of Solothurn, Switzerland, is the world centre for the anthroposophical movement founded by Rudolf Steiner. It is named after Johann Wolfgang von Goethe. The present building — the second Goetheanum — is a monumental sculptural structure in cast concrete and one of the pioneering works of organic, expressionist architecture.

First Goetheanum

The first Goetheanum (1913–1920) was built largely of carved wood by artists from many nations, with interpenetrating domes and elaborate interior carving and painting. It was destroyed by arson on New Year's Eve 1922/23, a devastating loss for the movement.

Second Goetheanum

Steiner designed the replacement in reinforced concrete, with massive, angular, sculptural forms developed from his model before his death in 1925; the building was completed in 1928. Its great hall seats around 1,000 and is used for eurythmy performances, theatre, conferences and the School of Spiritual Science. The Goetheanum houses the General Anthroposophical Society, archives, a library, and exhibition spaces including Steiner's unfinished sculptural group "The Representative of Humanity".

Architecture and influence

Steiner's Dornach buildings — including auxiliary structures such as the boiler house and Haus Duldeck — formed a small colony of "organic functionalism" that influenced later expressionist and organic architecture. The second Goetheanum is protected as a Swiss cultural property of national significance.
