Gaucher's disease

SpecialtyMedical genetics, hematology
SymptomsEnlarged spleen and liver, bone pain, fatigue, easy bruising
CauseDeficiency of the enzyme glucocerebrosidase (GBA gene mutation)
InheritanceAutosomal recessive

Gaucher's disease (also Gaucher disease) is a genetic disorder in which the fatty substance glucocerebroside accumulates in cells and certain organs. It is the most common of the lysosomal storage diseases. The disorder is caused by a deficiency of the enzyme glucocerebrosidase (also called acid β-glucosidase), which normally breaks down glucocerebroside; the undegraded lipid collects in macrophages, producing characteristic "Gaucher cells" with a wrinkled tissue-paper appearance.

Signs and symptoms

Common features include painless enlargement of the spleen and liver (hepatosplenomegaly), anemia, low platelet counts with easy bruising, fatigue, and skeletal abnormalities such as bone pain, fractures, and the Erlenmeyer flask deformity of the femur. Severity varies widely, and some affected people remain almost symptom-free.

Types

Type 1, the non-neuronopathic form, is by far the most common and does not primarily affect the brain; it is especially prevalent among Ashkenazi Jewish populations. Types 2 and 3 are neuronopathic forms involving the central nervous system; type 2 begins in infancy and is rapidly fatal, while type 3 progresses more slowly.

Cause and diagnosis

The disease is inherited in an autosomal recessive manner and results from mutations in the GBA gene on chromosome 1. Diagnosis is confirmed by measuring glucocerebrosidase activity in white blood cells and by genetic testing.

Treatment

Enzyme replacement therapy with recombinant glucocerebrosidase (such as imiglucerase) and substrate reduction therapy (such as eliglustat or miglustat) are effective for type 1 disease. The condition is named after the French physician Philippe Gaucher, who first described it in 1882.
