Gloss

A gloss is a brief notation, especially a marginal one, of the meaning of a word or wording in a text. It may be in the language of the text or in the reader's language if that is different. A collection of glosses is a glossary; a collection of medieval legal glosses, made by glossators, is called an apparatus. The word comes from the Greek glōssa, meaning "tongue" and, by extension, an obsolete or foreign word requiring explanation.

In manuscripts

Glosses were a central scholarly tool of the Middle Ages. Scribes and commentators wrote explanations between the lines of a manuscript (interlinear glosses) or in its margins (marginal glosses), clarifying difficult words, grammar and doctrine. Extensive traditions of glossing surrounded the Bible, Roman law and classical authors; the Glossa Ordinaria on the Bible and Accursius's Great Gloss on the Corpus Juris Civilis became standard reference works. In linguistics, glosses are still used to annotate examples from languages the reader may not know, most systematically in interlinear morpheme-by-morpheme glossing.

Other senses

The word also denotes a superficial shine or lustre on a surface — as in gloss paint or a glossy photograph — and, by metaphorical extension, an attractive appearance that conceals something less pleasant, as in the phrase "to gloss over" a fault. Lip gloss is a cosmetic product giving the lips a shiny finish. These senses derive from a separate Scandinavian root related to words for shining and glowing.
