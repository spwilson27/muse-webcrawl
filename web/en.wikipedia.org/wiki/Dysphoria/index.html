Dysphoria

Dysphoria
SpecialtyPsychiatry; psychology
SymptomsProfound unease, dissatisfaction, restlessness, low mood
EtymologyGreek dysphoros: "hard to bear"
OppositeEuphoria

Dysphoria (from Ancient Greek dysphoros, "hard to bear") is a profound state of unease or generalized dissatisfaction with life. It is the semantic opposite of euphoria. In psychological contexts, dysphoria is a mood state characterised by discomfort, restlessness, sadness and indifference, and it appears as a symptom in many psychiatric and medical conditions rather than constituting a disorder by itself.

Occurrence

Dysphoria is a feature of major depressive disorder, bipolar disorder (including mixed states, where it can accompany agitation), premenstrual dysphoric disorder, and withdrawal states from various substances. A dysphoric, restless quality is also seen in akathisia, in certain personality disorders, and in response to some medications. Brief dysphoric reactions can occur in anyone following disappointment or loss.

Related terms

The term is used in more specific compounds. Gender dysphoria refers to the distress a person may feel due to a mismatch between their gender identity and their sex assigned at birth; it is a formal diagnosis in the DSM-5, replacing the earlier label "gender identity disorder". Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) is a severe form of premenstrual syndrome dominated by mood symptoms. "Rejection sensitive dysphoria" is a popular (not formally diagnostic) term for intense emotional reactions to perceived rejection, often discussed in connection with ADHD.

Management

Because dysphoria is a symptom, treatment targets the underlying condition — for example antidepressant or mood-stabilising medication, psychotherapy, or hormone and social interventions in the case of gender dysphoria. Transient dysphoria in healthy people typically resolves without treatment.
