Surface tension

Surface tension
Symbol: γ
Unit: N/m or J/m²
Water at 20°C: ~0.0728 N/m

Surface tension is the tendency of liquid surfaces at rest to shrink into the minimum surface area possible, caused by cohesion between molecules. It allows insects such as water striders to walk on water and makes droplets form beads.

Causes

Molecules at a surface lack neighbours above them, so they cohere more strongly to neighbours at and below the surface, creating an inward net force and an effective elastic film.

Effects and measurement

Surface tension drives capillary action, meniscus formation, and the Young–Laplace pressure difference across curved surfaces. It is measured with tensiometers, the Du Noüy ring, Wilhelmy plate, pendant drop and capillary rise methods. Surfactants such as soap lower surface tension.
