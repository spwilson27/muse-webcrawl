Majuli (Assamese: মাজুলী) is a large river island in the Brahmaputra River in the Indian state of Assam. It was once recognised as the largest river island in the world, and in 2016 it became the first island in India to be made a district. The island is formed by the Brahmaputra in the south and the Kherkutia Xuti, an anabranch, joined by the Subansiri River in the north.

Culture

Majuli is the heartland of Assamese neo-Vaishnavite culture founded by the 15th–16th-century saint Srimanta Sankardev. Its monasteries, called sattras, preserve classical Sattriya dance (recognised as one of India's eight classical dances), devotional music, mask-making and manuscript traditions. Villages of the Mising community, whose stilt houses line the waterways, are known for handloom weaving.

Erosion

The island is severely threatened by erosion: floods of the Brahmaputra have washed away large areas over the past century, reducing it from roughly 1,250 km² in the early 20th century to a fraction of that today. Anti-erosion works, including the traditional bamboo-and-earth methods promoted by environmentalist Jadav Payeng — who single-handedly planted the Molai forest nearby — have drawn wide attention. Majuli is on UNESCO's tentative list for World Heritage status.

Access

Ferries connect Majuli with Jorhat on the southern bank; a bridge linking the island to the mainland has long been under discussion and construction.
