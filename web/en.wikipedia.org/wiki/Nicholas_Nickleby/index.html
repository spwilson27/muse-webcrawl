Nicholas Nickleby

The Life and Adventures of Nicholas Nickleby is a novel by Charles Dickens, serialised in monthly parts in 1838–1839. It was Dickens's third novel and centres on the young Nicholas, who must support his widowed mother and sister after his father's death leaves the family destitute.

Plot

At the mercy of his cold, money-lending uncle Ralph Nickleby, Nicholas is sent as an assistant to Wackford Squeers, the brutal proprietor of Dotheboys Hall, a Yorkshire boarding school where unwanted children are starved and beaten. Nicholas befriends the simple, abused boy Smike, eventually flees with him, and joins a travelling theatre company under Vincent Crummles. Returning to London, Nicholas thwarts Ralph's schemes against his sister Kate and the innocent Madeline Bray; Ralph, ruined and revealed to be Smike's father, hangs himself, and Nicholas marries Madeline.

Significance

The novel's exposure of the Yorkshire schools contributed to their reform and decline. It remains one of Dickens's most theatrical books, adapted many times for stage and screen, notably in the Royal Shakespeare Company's celebrated eight-hour production of 1980 and a 2002 film.
