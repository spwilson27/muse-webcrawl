s-cobordism theorem

In mathematics, the s-cobordism theorem is a fundamental result in the topology of manifolds, proved in the 1960s by Stephen Smale, John Stallings, and Erik Christopher Zeeman, and developed further by Barry Mazur. It refines the h-cobordism theorem by identifying exactly which cobordisms between manifolds are trivial (i.e. diffeomorphic to a product), in terms of an algebraic invariant called Whitehead torsion.

Statement

Let W be a compact cobordism between two closed manifolds M and M′, of dimension at least 6 (so W has dimension n + 1 ≥ 6... more precisely the boundary manifolds have dimension at least 5), such that both inclusions M ↪ W and M′ ↪ W are homotopy equivalences — an h-cobordism. The Whitehead torsion τ(W, M) of the cobordism is an element of the Whitehead group Wh(π₁M) of the fundamental group. The s-cobordism theorem states that W is diffeomorphic to the product M × [0, 1] (relative to M) if and only if its Whitehead torsion vanishes. Moreover, every element of the Whitehead group can be realised as the torsion of some h-cobordism, so the theorem gives a complete classification: h-cobordisms from a fixed manifold M are in bijection with Wh(π₁M).

Consequences

When the fundamental group is trivial, the Whitehead group vanishes, and the s-cobordism theorem reduces to Smale's h-cobordism theorem, from which the high-dimensional Poincaré conjecture follows. The theorem is also the starting point of surgery theory, since it underlies the classification of manifolds within a homotopy type. Its proof uses handle decompositions and the Whitney trick, which is why it requires dimension at least 5; the theorem fails in dimension 4 in the smooth category.
