Insurgency in Northeast India

Insurgency in Northeast India
Date: 1954 – present
Location: Northeast India (Assam, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh)
Status: Ongoing; much reduced since the 2010s

The insurgency in Northeast India is an ongoing series of armed conflicts between the Government of India and various separatist and ethnic militant groups in the northeastern states connected to the rest of India by the narrow Siliguri Corridor. Grievances include demands for independence or greater autonomy, ethnic identity, and opposition to migration from other parts of India and from Bangladesh.

Principal conflicts

  • Nagaland – the Naga movement, among India's oldest insurgencies, began in the 1950s under the Naga National Council; the NSCN and its factions later negotiated ceasefires from 1997.
  • Assam – the United Liberation Front of Asom (ULFA), founded in 1979, sought a sovereign Assam.
  • Manipur – numerous Meitei, Naga, and Kuki groups have operated since the 1960s.
  • Mizoram – the Mizo National Front insurgency (1966–1986) ended with the Mizoram Peace Accord, widely cited as a model settlement.
  • Tripura and Meghalaya – smaller insurgencies that largely faded after accords and surrenders in the 2000s.

Government response

The Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act (AFSPA) of 1958 grants security forces special powers in "disturbed areas"; it has been progressively withdrawn from many areas as violence declined. Peace accords—including the Bodo accords (1993, 2003, 2020)—and development initiatives accompany military operations.

Decline

Incidents and fatalities fell sharply from the 2000s onward, aided by cooperation from Bangladesh and Myanmar against cross-border sanctuaries and by mass surrenders of cadres.
