Randolph Severn "Trey" Parker III (born October 19, 1969) is an American animator, writer, producer, director, actor and composer. He is best known for co-creating the animated television series South Park (1997–present) with his creative partner Matt Stone, and co-writing the satirical stage musical The Book of Mormon (2011) with Stone and Robert Lopez.

Career

Parker studied at the University of Colorado Boulder, where he met Stone. Their short films The Spirit of Christmas (Jesus vs. Frosty, 1992, and Jesus vs. Santa, 1995) led Comedy Central to commission South Park, which premiered in 1997 and became one of the longest-running and most controversial American animated series. Parker voices many of the show's characters, including Stan Marsh, Eric Cartman and Randy Marsh, and writes and directs most episodes, which are famously produced in just six days before broadcast.

With Stone he also made the films Cannibal! The Musical (1993), Orgazmo (1997), BASEketball (1998, as an actor), South Park: Bigger, Longer & Uncut (1999) — whose song "Blame Canada" was nominated for an Academy Award — and the puppet satire Team America: World Police (2004).

The Book of Mormon and awards

The stage musical The Book of Mormon, created by Parker, Stone and Robert Lopez, opened on Broadway in 2011 to great acclaim, winning nine Tony Awards including Best Musical, and a Grammy Award for its cast album. Parker has won multiple Primetime Emmy Awards for South Park. His work is known for rapid-fire satire of current events, religion, and politics across the spectrum.
