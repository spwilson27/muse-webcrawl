Colegio de San Ildefonso

The Antiguo Colegio de San Ildefonso in the historic centre of Mexico City.

The Colegio de San Ildefonso is a historic building and museum in the historic centre of Mexico City, considered the birthplace of the Mexican muralism movement. Founded by the Jesuits in 1588 as a seminary school, it became one of the most prestigious educational institutions of colonial New Spain.

History

The present baroque complex was built between 1712 and 1749. After the expulsion of the Jesuits in 1767 the college passed through several uses; in 1867, under President Benito Juárez, it became the National Preparatory School (Escuela Nacional Preparatoria), directed by Gabino Barreda and later associated with figures such as Justo Sierra. The school moved out in 1978, and after restoration the building reopened in 1992 as a museum and cultural centre administered jointly by UNAM, the federal culture ministry and the Mexico City government.

Murals

In the 1920s Secretary of Education José Vasconcelos commissioned young artists to paint the walls, launching Mexican muralism. The building holds major early frescoes by José Clemente Orozco (including the Cortés and La Malinche and the satirical The Aristocrats), Diego Rivera's first mural Creation in the Simón Bolívar amphitheatre, and works by Jean Charlot, Fernando Leal, David Alfaro Siqueiros and others.

Museum

Today the Antiguo Colegio de San Ildefonso hosts temporary exhibitions of Mexican and international art alongside its permanent murals.
