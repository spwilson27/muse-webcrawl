Engine configuration

The engine configuration describes the fundamental operating principles by which internal combustion engines are categorised. Piston engines are often categorised by their cylinder layout and arrangement, while rotary engines such as the Wankel engine are classified by rotor number, and gas turbines by shaft arrangement.

Common piston-engine cylinder layouts.

Cylinder arrangements

The main arrangements for reciprocating engines are: the straight (inline) engine, with all cylinders in a single bank; the V engine, with two banks inclined at an angle; the flat engine, with two banks lying horizontally opposite each other (as in the "boxer" engine); the W engine, combining two V banks; and the radial engine, with cylinders arranged in a circle around the crankshaft, once dominant in aircraft.

Other classifications

Engines are also classified by ignition type (spark-ignition or compression-ignition), cycle (two-stroke or four-stroke), fuel, cooling method and valve arrangement. The configuration chosen balances compactness, balance and smoothness, manufacturing cost and the space available in the vehicle.
