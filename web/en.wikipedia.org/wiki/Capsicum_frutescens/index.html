Capsicum frutescens

Capsicum frutescens is a wild and cultivated chili pepper species in the nightshade family Solanaceae, native to Central and South America. Its small, upright, pungent fruits include well-known cultivars such as Tabasco, bird's eye (piri piri) and Malagueta peppers.

Description

Plants are compact, bushy perennials in frost-free climates, grown as annuals elsewhere. Flowers are white to greenish with a distinctive constriction at the calyx; fruits are small, conical or elongated, ripening to red, and often held upright.

Cultivation and uses

Tabasco peppers are the basis of Tabasco sauce, fermented and aged in oak barrels on Avery Island, Louisiana. Bird's eye chilies are central to Thai, Vietnamese and African cuisines. Like other chilies, the species spread worldwide after the Columbian exchange. See Chili pepper and Tabasco sauce.

Taxonomy

The boundary between C. frutescens, C. annuum and C. chinense is complex, and some cultivars are reassigned as research continues.
