Parental testing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Parental testing is the use of genetic fingerprinting to determine whether two individuals have a biological parent–child relationship. A paternity test determines whether a man is the biological father of a child, while a maternity test determines whether a woman is the biological mother. Tests can also be performed for siblingship and grandparentage, and on samples obtained before birth (prenatal testing).

Methods

Modern parental testing uses DNA profiling, typically polymerase chain reaction (PCR) analysis of short tandem repeat (STR) loci. Samples are usually collected by buccal (cheek) swab. A child inherits one allele at each locus from each parent; if the child's alleles cannot be accounted for by the tested individual across many loci, that individual is excluded as the biological parent. Non-exclusion results are reported with a statistical probability of parentage, commonly above 99.9% when parentage is supported.

Older methods used blood group typing and human leukocyte antigen (HLA) testing, which could exclude but not conclusively identify a parent. Before DNA testing, these methods were used in legal disputes over parentage.

Legal and social issues

Parental testing is used in child support, custody, inheritance and immigration cases. Court-ordered tests follow strict chain-of-custody procedures; "peace of mind" home tests use self-collected samples and may not be admissible in court. Laws vary by jurisdiction: some countries restrict private testing, and questions have been raised about consent, the rights of the child, and the discovery of misattributed paternity, which studies estimate occurs in a small percentage of cases.

Prenatal paternity testing can be performed using fetal DNA in the mother's blood (non-invasive prenatal testing) or samples obtained by chorionic villus sampling or amniocentesis, which carry a small procedure-related risk.
