The First War of Scottish Independence was the first of a series of wars fought between the Kingdom of Scotland and the Kingdom of England. It lasted from the English invasion of Scotland in 1296 until the de facto restoration of Scottish independence under the Treaty of Edinburgh–Northampton in 1328. De jure independence was established in 1320 by the Declaration of Arbroath and confirmed by the papacy in 1323.

Background

The crisis began with the death of Alexander III in 1286 and of his heir, Margaret, Maid of Norway, in 1290, leaving the Scottish throne vacant. Edward I of England was invited to arbitrate between rival claimants — the "Great Cause" — and awarded the crown to John Balliol in 1292, while demanding recognition of English overlordship. When Balliol resisted and allied with France in 1295 (the beginning of the Auld Alliance), Edward invaded, sacked Berwick and defeated the Scots at Dunbar in 1296.

Wallace and the Guardians

Resistance continued under William Wallace and Andrew Moray, who defeated an English army at the Battle of Stirling Bridge in 1297. Wallace was in turn defeated at Falkirk in 1298 and, after years as a fugitive, was captured and executed in London in 1305. Robert the Bruce and John Comyn served as Guardians of Scotland in a period of uneasy negotiation and intermittent warfare.

Robert the Bruce

In 1306 Robert the Bruce killed his rival John Comyn and was crowned King of Scots at Scone. After early defeats he waged a successful guerrilla campaign, retaking castles one by one. His victory over Edward II at the Battle of Bannockburn in 1314 secured his kingship, and Scottish raids into northern England, together with the Bruce invasion of Ireland, increased pressure on England to settle.

Settlement

Scottish nobles issued the Declaration of Arbroath in 1320, asserting Scotland's independence to the Pope. After further warfare and the accession of Edward III, England finally recognised Robert I as king of an independent Scotland by the Treaty of Edinburgh–Northampton in 1328. Conflict resumed in 1332 with the Second War of Scottish Independence.
