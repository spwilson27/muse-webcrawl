Extended stay hotel

A typical extended stay suite, with in-room kitchen facilities

An extended stay hotel (also known as an aparthotel, residential hotel or apart-hotel depending on the market) is a type of lodging establishment that combines the services of a hotel with apartment-style amenities for guests staying five nights or longer. Rates are typically structured so that the nightly price decreases with the length of stay.

Features

Extended stay properties differ from conventional hotels in offering larger, suite-style rooms with full or partial kitchens, separate living and sleeping areas, on-site laundry facilities and reduced housekeeping frequency. Services such as reception and fitness rooms are usually retained, while restaurants and meeting facilities — costly features aimed at transient guests — are typically absent or limited. The model keeps operating costs low because rooms turn over less often and require less servicing.

Market and brands

The segment grew rapidly in the United States from the 1970s onwards, serving business travellers on long assignments, relocating families, construction and medical crews, and people between homes. Major brands include Residence Inn by Marriott, Extended Stay America, Homewood Suites by Hilton, Staybridge Suites and Candlewood Suites, spanning economy to upscale tiers. Economists have noted that the segment traditionally weathers downturns better than other hotel categories, because demand is driven by necessity-based, longer stays rather than discretionary short trips.
