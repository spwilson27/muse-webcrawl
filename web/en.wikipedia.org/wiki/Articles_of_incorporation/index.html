Articles of incorporation

Articles of incorporation
Also called: Certificate of incorporation; charter
Filed with: State government (US)
Governs: Formation of a corporation

Articles of incorporation are a set of formal documents filed with a government body to legally document the creation of a corporation. In the United States they are generally filed with the secretary of state of the state of incorporation; in some states, including Delaware, the equivalent document is called a certificate of incorporation.

Contents

The articles typically state the corporation's name, its purpose, the address of its registered office and the name of its registered agent, the duration of the corporation (usually perpetual), and the number and classes of shares it is authorised to issue. Some jurisdictions require or permit additional provisions, such as the names of the initial directors or limitations on director liability.

Legal effect

The corporation comes into legal existence when the articles are accepted and filed by the state. Together with the bylaws, which regulate internal management, the articles form the corporation's basic governing documents. Filing also gives public notice of the corporation's existence and of facts such as its authorised capital stock.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom the equivalent constitutional documents are the articles of association, adopted on incorporation with Companies House; historically British companies also filed a memorandum of association, which since the Companies Act 2006 serves only as a statement of the subscribers' intent to form the company.
