Formula Nippon was the premier single-seater racing championship of Japan, contested from 1996 until 2012, when it was renamed the Japanese Championship Super Formula. It descended from the earlier All-Japan Formula 2000, Formula Two and Formula 3000 championships, and served as the top domestic open-wheel series and a proving ground for Japanese and international drivers.

History

The series began in 1996 after the Japanese round of the international Formula 3000 era ended, using F3000-type chassis with 3.0-litre engines, notably the Mugen-Honda MF308. Promoted by the Japan Racing Promotion organisation, it raced at circuits such as Suzuka, Fuji, Motegi and Sugo. Chassis suppliers included Lola and Reynard before the series moved to a spec Swift chassis in 2009 with Toyota and Honda 3.4-litre V8 engines.

Drivers

Formula Nippon champions and graduates include Satoshi Motoyama, a four-time champion, Tsugio Matsuda, André Lotterer, Loïc Duval and Kazuki Nakajima. Several drivers used the series as a springboard to Formula One, and F1 drivers such as Pedro de la Rosa, Ralf Schumacher and Narain Karthikeyan also raced in it. In 2013 the championship was rebranded Super Formula, continuing the same role in Japanese motorsport.
