Web decentralization

Web decentralization refers to efforts to distribute control of web infrastructure, data and services away from a small number of large centralised platforms toward networks of independent servers and users. It draws on the early architecture of the internet and World Wide Web, which were designed as decentralised systems, and reacts against the later concentration of hosting, search, social media and identity in a few companies.

Approaches

Federated protocols allow independently operated servers to interoperate, as with email, the Fediverse's ActivityPub protocol (used by Mastodon and others), and Matrix for messaging. Tim Berners-Lee's Solid project proposes personal online data stores ("pods") that separate user data from applications. Peer-to-peer technologies such as IPFS aim to distribute content storage and addressing, while blockchain-based naming and identity systems underpin much of what has been promoted as "Web3".

Motivations and criticism

Advocates argue decentralization improves resilience, privacy, competition and freedom from platform gatekeeping. Critics note usability hurdles, moderation difficulties, the tendency of nominally decentralised systems to recentralise around popular providers, and scalability limits.
