Taylor's theorem

Taylor's theorem is a fundamental result in calculus giving an approximation of a k-times differentiable function around a point by a polynomial of degree k, the Taylor polynomial, together with a precise estimate of the approximation error, called the remainder.

Statement

If f is k+1 times differentiable near a, then f(x) = f(a) + f′(a)(x−a) + … + f⁽ᵏ⁾(a)(x−a)ᵏ/k! + Rₖ(x). The remainder can be expressed in several forms: Lagrange's form Rₖ = f⁽ᵏ⁺¹⁾(ξ)(x−a)ᵏ⁺¹/(k+1)! for some ξ between a and x, the integral form, and Peano's form Rₖ = o((x−a)ᵏ).

History and applications

The theorem is named after Brook Taylor, who stated a version in 1715; the importance of the remainder was recognised by Lagrange. Taylor approximations underlie numerical computation, error analysis and series solutions throughout physics and engineering.
