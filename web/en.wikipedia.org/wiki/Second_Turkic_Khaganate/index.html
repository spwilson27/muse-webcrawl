The Second Turkic Khaganate (Old Turkic: Türük Kaganlïk) was a nomadic empire of the Göktürks on the Mongolian steppe, restored in 682 by Ilterish Qaghan after roughly five decades of subjugation to China's Tang dynasty. It succeeded the First Turkic Khaganate (552–603) and its eastern successor state, and endured until 744, when it was overthrown by the Uyghurs.

Restoration

Kutlug, a descendant of the Ashina royal clan, raised rebellion against Tang rule in 682 and took the title Ilterish ("unifier of the people") Qaghan. With the able commander Tonyukuk as his adviser and his brother Qapaghan, he rebuilt Turkic power around the sacred Ötüken forest in the Orkhon valley, subduing steppe peoples including the Oghuz and the Türgesh in the west.

Height and decline

Under Qapaghan Qaghan (691–716) the khaganate campaigned from Manchuria to Central Asia and raided northern China. After his death in battle, power passed to Bilge Khagan, whose reign, together with his brother Kül Tigin and the counsellor Tonyukuk, is commemorated in the Orkhon inscriptions — the oldest surviving texts in a Turkic language, written in the Old Turkic runiform script. Bilge's death in 734, possibly by poisoning, was followed by a rapid succession of weak rulers.

Fall

In 744 a coalition of Uyghurs, Karluks, and Basmyls destroyed the khaganate; the last qaghan was killed and the Uyghur Khaganate took control of the steppe. The Second Turkic Khaganate is central to early Turkic history for its inscriptions and its revival of steppe imperial tradition.
