Balliol College is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England. Founded around 1263 by John I de Balliol, it is among the university's oldest colleges and traditionally claims the largest number of graduate students. Its alumni include four British prime ministers, five Nobel laureates, and numerous heads of state and government worldwide.

History

John de Balliol, a landowner from Barnard Castle, established a house of scholars in Oxford around 1263 as penance imposed by the Bishop of Durham; after his death his widow Dervorguilla endowed the college with statutes in 1282, among the earliest surviving for an Oxford college. For centuries small and poor, Balliol was transformed in the Victorian era under Master Benjamin Jowett (1870–1893), who made it a leading force in the university through open scholarships awarded on merit. The front quadrangle and chapel, by Alfred Waterhouse, date from the 1860s–70s.

Academics and life

Balliol admits undergraduates in most subjects and a large graduate community centred on the Holywell Manor graduate centre. It is consistently among Oxford's strongest colleges in examinations and is known for an intellectual and politically engaged culture, an active debating tradition, and the Balliol–Trinity rivalry with Trinity College. The college library holds important medieval manuscripts, including works associated with its early benefactors.

Notable alumni

Alumni include prime ministers H. H. Asquith, Harold Macmillan, Edward Heath, and Boris Johnson; economists Adam Smith (a graduate scholar) is associated with the college; writers Aldous Huxley, Graham Greene, and Evelyn Waugh; and Nobel laureates including Oliver Smithies and John Hicks.
