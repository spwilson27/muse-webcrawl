Thioredoxin


Structure of human thioredoxin

Thioredoxins (Trx) are a class of small redox proteins present in all organisms. They act as antioxidants by facilitating the reduction of other proteins by cysteine thiol–disulfide exchange. Thioredoxin is found in nearly all known organisms and is essential for life in mammals. It is a 12 kDa oxidoreductase enzyme containing a dithiol–disulfide active site, characterized by the amino acid sequence motif -Trp-Cys-Gly-Pro-Cys-.

Function

The function of thioredoxins is carried out by their characteristic redox active site, in which two cysteine residues can cycle between the reduced (dithiol) and oxidized (disulfide) states. Reduced thioredoxin acts as an efficient reducing agent, donating electrons to peroxiredoxins and ribonucleotide reductase, among other targets. Oxidized thioredoxin is in turn reduced by the flavoenzyme thioredoxin reductase, using NADPH as the electron donor.

Together, thioredoxin, thioredoxin reductase, and NADPH constitute the thioredoxin system, a major cellular redox-regulating and antioxidant system alongside the glutathione/glutaredoxin system.

Roles

  • Antioxidant defence — reducing oxidized proteins and supporting peroxiredoxin-mediated removal of hydrogen peroxide.
  • DNA synthesis — supplying reducing equivalents to ribonucleotide reductase, essential for producing deoxyribonucleotides.
  • Signalling — regulating transcription factors such as NF-κB and AP-1, and modulating apoptosis via ASK1.
  • Photosynthesis — in plants, thioredoxins reduced by ferredoxin regulate Calvin cycle enzymes in response to light.

Clinical significance

Thioredoxin is overexpressed in many human tumours and is associated with resistance to chemotherapy, making the thioredoxin system a target for anticancer drug development. Elevated serum thioredoxin levels have also been reported in inflammatory conditions.
