Umaswati

Era: c. 2nd–5th century CE (dates debated)
Religion: Jainism
Known for: Tattvartha Sutra

Umaswati, also known as Umasvati and sometimes identified with Umaswami, was an Indian Jain monk and scholar, traditionally regarded as the author of the Tattvartha Sutra (also called the Tattvarthadhigama Sutra), the first surviving Sanskrit compendium of Jain doctrine.

Life

Little is known for certain of Umaswati's life. Scholars place him variously between the 2nd and 5th centuries CE. Uniquely, he is revered by both of the major Jain traditions, Digambara and Shvetambara, although the two sects preserve somewhat different recensions of his work and differ over details of his biography. Some traditions identify him with the monk Kundakunda, an attribution other scholars reject.

Works

The Tattvartha Sutra ("Manual for Understanding the True Nature of Things") summarises Jain philosophy in 344 aphorisms across ten chapters, covering the soul, karma, cosmology, ethics and the path to liberation (moksha). It opens with the famous statement that right faith, right knowledge and right conduct together constitute the path to liberation. Umaswati is credited with many other works, including a commentary on his own sutra, though their attribution is debated.
