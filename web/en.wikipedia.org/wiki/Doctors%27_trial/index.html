Doctors' trial

The Doctors' trial (United States of America v. Karl Brandt et al.) was the first of the twelve "Subsequent Nuremberg trials" for war crimes and crimes against humanity after the end of World War II, held before a United States military tribunal at Nuremberg, Germany, from 9 December 1946 to 20 August 1947.

Defendants and charges

Twenty-three defendants, mostly physicians and administrators in the medical services of Nazi Germany, were charged with conspiring in and performing medical experiments on concentration camp inmates and other living human subjects, without consent, in the course of which the victims were murdered, tortured, and subjected to atrocities. The charges also included membership in a criminal organisation and involvement in the "euthanasia" programme, Aktion T4.

Verdict

Seven defendants, including Karl Brandt, Hitler's personal physician, were found guilty and sentenced to death by hanging; nine received prison sentences ranging from ten years to life; and seven were acquitted. The trial produced the Nuremberg Code, a set of principles governing permissible medical experiments on human subjects, centred on informed consent.
