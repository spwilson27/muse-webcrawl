The Ballard Maturational Assessment, or Ballard score, is a technique used to estimate the gestational age of a newborn infant by assessing neuromuscular and physical characteristics. Developed by Dr. Jeanne L. Ballard in 1979 and expanded in the New Ballard Score of 1991, it allows assessment of infants from about 20 to 44 weeks of gestation and is widely used when the date of the last menstrual period is unknown or obstetric ultrasound is unavailable.

Physical and neuromuscular signs are scored to date gestation

Components

The score combines two sets of signs, each graded on a scale (from −1 to 5):

  • Neuromuscular maturity — posture, square window (wrist flexion), arm recoil, popliteal angle, scarf sign, and heel-to-ear manoeuvre. Muscle tone increases predictably with gestation.
  • Physical maturity — skin texture and opacity, lanugo, plantar surface creases, breast tissue, eye and ear development, and genitals.

Scoring and interpretation

The individual scores are summed and converted by a maturity rating table into an estimated gestational age in weeks, accurate to within about two weeks. The assessment should be performed within the first 48 hours of life for greatest reliability. Compared with the earlier Dubowitz score, the Ballard score is quicker to perform and its expanded version covers extremely premature infants.
