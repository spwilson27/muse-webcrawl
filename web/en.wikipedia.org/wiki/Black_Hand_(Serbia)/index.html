The Black Hand, formally Unification or Death (Ujedinjenje ili smrt), was a secret military society formed in 1911 by officers of the Royal Serbian Army, dedicated to uniting all Serb-populated territories — including those inside Austria-Hungary — into a single Greater Serbia.

Origins

Many of its founders, led by Colonel Dragutin Dimitrijević, known by the codename "Apis", had taken part in the 1903 May Coup that murdered King Alexander Obrenović. The organisation recruited through cells, used a seal showing a skull and crossbones beside a knife, a bomb and a vial of poison, and bound members by oath. It built links with Serbian frontier guards and with Young Bosnia, the revolutionary youth movement in Austrian-ruled Bosnia.

Sarajevo assassination

Black Hand connections supplied the weapons and border crossing used by Gavrilo Princip and his fellow conspirators, who assassinated Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 — the spark for the July Crisis and the outbreak of the First World War. The extent of the Serbian government's foreknowledge remains debated by historians.

Downfall

During the war, the Serbian government-in-exile and Crown Prince Alexander moved against the organisation as a rival power centre. At the Salonika Trial of 1917, Dimitrijević and other leaders were convicted on charges widely regarded as trumped up and executed by firing squad; the Black Hand was dissolved.
