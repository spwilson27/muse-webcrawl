Sparring

Boxers sparring with protective equipment

Sparring is a form of training common to many combat sports and martial arts. Although the precise form varies, it is essentially "free-form" fighting, with enough rules, customs, or agreements to minimise injuries. By extension, argumentative debate is sometimes called "verbal sparring".

Training use

Sparring bridges the gap between drills — such as hitting pads, shadowboxing, or practising forms — and actual competition. It allows practitioners to apply technique against a resisting, unpredictable partner, developing timing, distance management, composure under pressure, and tactical judgement. In boxing and kickboxing, sparring is typically done in timed rounds with larger gloves and headguards; intensity ranges from light technical sparring ("flow sparring") to near-competitive "hard sparring".

In grappling arts such as Brazilian jiu-jitsu and judo, the equivalent free practice is called randori in Japanese traditions — free sparring against resisting opponents, usually beginning from specified positions. Fencing, taekwondo, karate (as kumite), and mixed martial arts each have their own sparring conventions and protective requirements.

Safety and debate

Because sparring involves repeated head impacts in striking arts, its volume and intensity are subjects of ongoing medical debate. Research on chronic traumatic encephalopathy and gym culture has led many boxing and MMA coaches to limit hard sparring in favour of technical work; governing bodies increasingly mandate headguards in amateur contexts and supervise sessions. Sparring partners are also a professional occupation: elite boxers and fighters employ partners chosen to imitate the style of an upcoming opponent.
