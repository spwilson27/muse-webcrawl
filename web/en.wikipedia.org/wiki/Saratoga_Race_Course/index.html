Saratoga Race Course is a Thoroughbred horse racing track located in Saratoga Springs, New York, United States. Opened in 1863, it is the oldest organised sporting venue of any kind in the United States and is regarded as one of the premier race meetings in American horse racing. Its summer meet traditionally runs for 40 days from mid-July to Labor Day.

The historic grandstand at Saratoga, the centrepiece of the summer racing season.

History

The first races at Saratoga were organised in 1863 by John "Old Smoke" Morrissey, a former boxer and casino operator, initially on a course across from the present track. The success of the meeting led to the formation of the Saratoga Association and the opening of the current course in 1864. The centrepiece race, the Travers Stakes, was first run that year and is the oldest major Thoroughbred race in the United States still contested.

Saratoga acquired the nickname "Graveyard of Champions" after a series of famous upsets, including the defeat of the great Man o' War by Upset in 1919 — his only career loss — and the loss of Triple Crown winner American Pharoah in the 2015 Travers Stakes.

The meet

The summer meeting is a social and sporting fixture of the American Northeast, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors to Saratoga Springs. Major races include the Travers Stakes (the "Midsummer Derby"), the Whitney Stakes, the Alabama Stakes and the Woodward Stakes.
