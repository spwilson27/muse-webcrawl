Songwriter

A songwriter is a person who creates musical compositions, including lyrics, for other artists or for themselves. Songwriters work in many genres, from pop and rock to country, hip hop, and musical theatre. Some write only lyrics, others only music, and many do both. Professional songwriters may be staff writers for music publishers, freelance writers, or recording artists who write their own material.

Roles and responsibilities

Songwriters create melodies, chord progressions, and lyrics that form a song. They may collaborate with other writers, producers, and recording artists, and they typically demonstrate finished songs through demos. In the music industry, songwriting is a separate copyright from the sound recording, and songwriters earn royalties whenever a song is sold, streamed, performed publicly, or licensed for film and advertising.

History

Before the recording era, songwriting for publication was centred on sheet music, with professional writers working in districts such as New York's Tin Pan Alley. The rise of radio and records created the modern staff-writer system at firms such as the Brill Building in New York and in Nashville's country music industry. From the 1960s onward, singer-songwriters such as Bob Dylan, Joni Mitchell, and Carole King popularised the idea of the artist as author.

Career and earnings

Songwriters are usually signed to music publishing deals in which the publisher administers the copyright and collects mechanical and performance royalties, split with the writer. Performing rights organisations such as ASCAP and BMI collect public-performance royalties on their behalf. Hit songwriters can earn substantial income from a small catalogue of successful songs.
