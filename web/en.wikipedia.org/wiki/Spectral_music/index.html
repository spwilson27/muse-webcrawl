Spectral music

Spectral music is a compositional approach that emerged principally in France in the 1970s, in which musical material is derived from the acoustic properties of sound itself — above all the harmonic spectrum and its evolution in time. Rather than beginning from abstract pitch systems, spectral composers take the internal structure of a tone, as revealed by Fourier analysis and sonograms, as a model for harmony, timbre, and form.

History

The movement coalesced around composers Gérard Grisey, Tristan Murail, and Hugues Dufourt, associated with the ensemble L'Itinéraire founded in 1973, with important contributions from Horațiu Rădulescu and, in parallel, the English and American minimal and electroacoustic communities. Grisey's cycle Les espaces acoustiques (1976–1985), opening with Partiels — whose harmonies are drawn directly from the spectrum of a low trombone E — is a canonical example.

Techniques

Spectral works characteristically orchestrate a single sound's partials across an ensemble ("instrumental additive synthesis"), stretch brief spectra into long formal spans, and interpolate smoothly between harmony and timbre, consonance and noise. Computer analysis of recorded sounds, developed at IRCAM and elsewhere, later supplied composers with precise spectral data. Grisey described the approach as a music of transitions between states of sound, with time treated as continuous rather than as a grid of discrete events.

Influence

Spectral thinking influenced later generations including Kaija Saariaho, Magnus Lindberg, and Georg Friedrich Haas, and helped blur the boundaries between composition, acoustics, and electronic music.
