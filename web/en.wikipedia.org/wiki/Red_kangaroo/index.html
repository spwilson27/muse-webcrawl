Red kangaroo

The red kangaroo (Osphranter rufus) is the largest of all kangaroos, the largest terrestrial mammal native to Australia, and the largest extant marsupial. It is found across mainland Australia, except for the more fertile areas in the south, the east coast, and the northern rainforests.

Description

Males have short, red-brown fur, markedly paler below and on the limbs, and can stand over 1.8 m tall in large individuals, with a long muscular tail used as a counterbalance when hopping and as a prop when standing. Females are smaller and often blue-grey with a brown tinge, and are called "blue flyers". Red kangaroos hop at speeds of around 20–25 km/h, and can reach 70 km/h over short distances, clearing 8–9 m in a single bound.

Ecology and behaviour

The species inhabits arid and semi-arid scrubland and grassland, grazing mainly at night on grasses and forbs. It can survive long periods without drinking, and conserves water by panting and licking its forearms. Females exhibit embryonic diapause: development of a fertilised egg can pause while a joey occupies the pouch, allowing near-continuous breeding when conditions allow. Groups, called mobs, usually contain 2–10 individuals, though many more may gather at rich food sources.

Relationship with humans

Red kangaroos are commercially harvested under quota in several states for meat and leather, and are a national symbol of Australia, appearing with the emu on the Australian coat of arms.
