Buckingham Palace

Buckingham Palace is a royal residence in London, and the administrative headquarters of the monarch of the United Kingdom. Located in the City of Westminster, it is the focal point for national celebrations and mourning, and the site of the daily Changing of the Guard ceremony. Originally Buckingham House, built for the Duke of Buckingham in 1703, it became the monarch's London residence on Queen Victoria's accession in 1837.

History

George III bought Buckingham House in 1761 as a home for Queen Charlotte. George IV commissioned John Nash to transform it into a palace; Edward Blore added the east front, whose famous facade was refaced in Portland stone by Aston Webb in 1913. The palace was bombed during the Blitz but survived.

Interior and gardens

The State Rooms — including the Throne Room, Ballroom, and Picture Gallery — host investitures and state banquets. The 39-acre garden is the largest private garden in London. The Royal Mews houses state coaches. The palace opens its State Rooms to visitors each summer. See also British royal family.
