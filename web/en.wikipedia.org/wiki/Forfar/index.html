Forfar

Location: Angus, Scotland
Population: ~14,000
Status: County town of Angus
Known for: Forfar bridie

Forfar (Scottish Gaelic: Baile Fharfair) is a town and former royal burgh in Angus, Scotland, and the historic county town of the county. It lies in Strathmore, about 14 miles northeast of Dundee.

History

Forfar's origins are ancient; a royal residence stood near the town, and tradition associates the area with Malcolm III (Canmore), who is said to have held courts at Forfar in the 11th century. The town received a royal burgh charter in the 13th century. In the 17th century Forfar was the scene of witch trials in 1661–62, in which several local women were accused and executed; a "Witches Stone" in the town marks the site traditionally linked to the burnings. Forfar developed as a market town and a centre for linen and jute weaving and shoemaking during the Industrial Revolution.

Food and football

Forfar is famous for the Forfar bridie, a horseshoe-shaped meat pasty said to have been created by a local baker in the 19th century. The town's football club, Forfar Athletic, plays at Station Park; the club became part of football folklore through the near-half-time scoreline "Forfar 4, East Fife 5", celebrated as a tongue-twister for results readers.

Landmarks

Local landmarks include the Meffan Museum and Art Gallery, Forfar Loch and its country park, and the Reid Hall. Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother was born and raised at nearby Glamis Castle.
