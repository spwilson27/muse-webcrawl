The Hugo Award for Best Novel is one of the Hugo Awards given each year for the best science fiction or fantasy novel of 40,000 words or more published in the previous year. It is voted on by members of the World Science Fiction Convention ("Worldcon") and is widely regarded, together with the Nebula Award, as the most prestigious honour in science fiction.

History

The Hugos were first awarded in 1953, and the Best Novel category has been presented nearly every year since (there was no award in 1954). The very first winner was Alfred Bester's The Demolished Man. Voting uses an instant-runoff system after an open nomination round. In 2015–16 the voting process was disrupted by organised slate campaigns (the "Sad" and "Rabid Puppies"), prompting rule changes such as E Pluribus Hugo.

N. K. Jemisin became the first author to win Best Novel three years running (2016–2018), for each volume of her Broken Earth trilogy. Repeat winners overall include Robert A. Heinlein, Isaac Asimov, Ursula K. Le Guin, Lois McMaster Bujold, Connie Willis, and Vernor Vinge.
