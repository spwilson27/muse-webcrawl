Tom Bombadil

Tom Bombadil is a character in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings (1954–55). A merry, enigmatic figure living in the Old Forest with his wife Goldberry, he rescues the hobbits twice early in the novel and is famously unaffected by the One Ring.

Appearances

Bombadil first appeared in Tolkien's 1934 poem "The Adventures of Tom Bombadil," inspired by a Dutch doll owned by Tolkien's children. In The Fellowship of the Ring he frees Merry and Pippin from Old Man Willow, shelters the hobbits, and later saves them from a barrow-wight, giving them the barrow-blades that prove significant later. At the Council of Elrond, Gandalf explains that the Ring has no power over Bombadil because he is entirely his own master and takes no interest in dominion — but for that reason he would be an unsafe guardian.

Interpretation

Tolkien called Bombadil an intentional enigma, representing among other things the spirit of the vanishing English countryside and a stance of pure contemplative knowledge outside the struggle for power. He is omitted from most adaptations, including Peter Jackson's films, but appears in the 1962 poetry collection The Adventures of Tom Bombadil and in the second season of The Rings of Power.
