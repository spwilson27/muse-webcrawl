United Aircraft Corporation

The United Aircraft Corporation was an American aircraft manufacturer and holding company formed in 1929 by William E. Boeing and Frederick Rentschler of Pratt & Whitney. Headquartered initially in Hartford, Connecticut, it combined Boeing's aircraft and airline interests with Pratt & Whitney engines, Hamilton propellers, Chance Vought, Sikorsky and several airlines, creating a vertically integrated aviation conglomerate.

Formation and breakup

United Aircraft and Transport Corporation, as it was first known, brought together Boeing Airplane Company, Boeing Air Transport, Pratt & Whitney Aircraft, Hamilton Aero Manufacturing, Chance Vought, Sikorsky Aviation, Northrop Aircraft and others. Its airline subsidiaries were merged into United Air Lines in 1931.

Following the Air Mail scandal of 1934, the Air Mail Act prohibited manufacturers from also operating airlines. The corporation was split into three parts: United Aircraft Corporation (the eastern manufacturing businesses — Pratt & Whitney, Chance Vought, Sikorsky and Hamilton Standard), Boeing Airplane Company in the west, and United Air Lines as an independent carrier.

The manufacturing company

Under Rentschler's leadership, United Aircraft became one of the largest American defence contractors of the Second World War. Its divisions produced the Pratt & Whitney R-2800 Double Wasp engine, the Chance Vought F4U Corsair fighter, Sikorsky helicopters including the R-4, and Hamilton Standard propellers fitted to a huge share of Allied aircraft. After the war the company expanded into jet engines, and Vought and Sikorsky continued as major suppliers of naval aircraft and helicopters.

United Technologies

In 1975 United Aircraft changed its name to United Technologies Corporation (UTC), reflecting diversification into elevators (Otis), air conditioning (Carrier) and other industrial products. Pratt & Whitney, Sikorsky (until its sale to Lockheed Martin in 2015) and Hamilton Sundstrand remained its aerospace core. In 2020 UTC merged with Raytheon Company to form Raytheon Technologies, later RTX Corporation.
