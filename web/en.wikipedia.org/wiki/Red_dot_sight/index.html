Red dot sight

Image placeholder: view through a red dot sight.

A red dot sight is a type of non-magnifying reflector (reflex) sight for firearms and other devices that require aiming, in which an illuminated red dot is projected onto a lens as the point of aim. The dot appears superimposed on the target regardless of eye position, allowing fast, both-eyes-open aiming at close to medium range.

Principle

A light-emitting diode at the focus of collimating optics projects an image of the reticle onto a partially reflective lens. Because the reticle image is collimated, it appears at optical infinity, so the shooter does not need to align front and rear sights and the dot stays on target as the eye moves within the sight window.

History and use

Reflector sights date to the early twentieth century and were used on aircraft and anti-aircraft guns before shrinking to small-arms size. Modern miniaturised red dot sights, produced by companies such as Aimpoint and Trijicon, became standard equipment on military carbines from the 1990s and are ubiquitous in law enforcement, sport shooting, hunting and airsoft. Designs range from open "reflex" models to rugged enclosed tube sights, with battery lives of tens of thousands of hours in the best models.
