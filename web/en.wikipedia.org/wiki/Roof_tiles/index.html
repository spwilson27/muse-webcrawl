MaterialsFired clay (terracotta), concrete, slate, metal, glass
PurposeWeatherproof covering for pitched roofs

Roof tiles are overlapping coverings designed mainly to shed rainwater and protect pitched roofs. Traditionally made of fired clay — terracotta — they are also produced in concrete, slate, metal and other materials. Clay tile roofing dates to the ancient world: Greek and Roman roofs used the paired system of flat tegulae covered by curved imbreces, the ancestor of today's Roman and mission tiles.

Types

Flat tiles are small rectangles laid in double or triple overlap. Pantiles are S-shaped in cross-section, characteristic of the Netherlands and eastern England. Barrel or mission tiles — pairs of half-round tiles — typify Mediterranean and Spanish Colonial architecture. Modern interlocking tiles, often concrete, mimic traditional profiles while fixing mechanically. Colours come from the clay body and firing, or from glazes and engobes.

Properties

Tile roofs are durable, fire-resistant and heavy — requiring strong roof structures — and are valued for longevity and appearance. Regional traditions vary worldwide, from Chinese glazed tiles on temple roofs to the dark clay tiles of Japan and the bright orange pantiles of the Mediterranean.
