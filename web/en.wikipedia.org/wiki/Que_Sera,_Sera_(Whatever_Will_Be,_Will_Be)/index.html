"Que Sera, Sera (Whatever Will Be, Will Be)" is a song written by the team of Jay Livingston and Ray Evans, first published in 1956. It was introduced by Doris Day in Alfred Hitchcock's film The Man Who Knew Too Much (1956), in which Day played a character who was a popular singer. The song won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Content

The song progresses through three verses — childhood, young adulthood, and parenthood — in which the narrator asks what the future holds and is answered with the refrain:

Que será, será,
Whatever will be, will be;
The future's not ours to see.
Que será, será,
What will be, will be.

The title phrase is a Spanish-like rendering of an Italian saying, "che sarà, sarà" ("what will be, will be"), expressing cheerful fatalism.

Legacy

Day's recording reached No. 2 on the Billboard charts and became her signature song. It has been widely covered and used as a football chant by English supporters ("Que sera, sera, whatever will be, will be, we're going to Wembley…"). The song also served as the theme to the television sitcom The Doris Day Show.
