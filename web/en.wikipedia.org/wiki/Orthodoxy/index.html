Orthodoxy

Interior of an Eastern Orthodox church.

Orthodoxy (from Greek ὀρθοδοξία, orthodoxía, "correct opinion" or "right belief") is adherence to correct or accepted creeds, especially in religion. In the Christian context, the term is used for the Eastern Orthodox Church and Oriental Orthodox churches, and more broadly for conformity with the doctrines defined by the early church and ecumenical councils, as opposed to heresy or heterodoxy.

Uses of the term

In Judaism, "Orthodox Judaism" designates movements that hold traditional observance of Torah and halakha to be binding. In Islam, orthodoxy is sometimes applied to Sunni Islam as the historically dominant interpretation, though the concept maps imperfectly onto Islamic tradition. The word is also used metaphorically outside religion — for example, "economic orthodoxy" — for any set of dominant, conventionally accepted beliefs within a field.

Orthodoxy and heresy

The concept developed in the early Christian centuries as creeds and councils defined authoritative teaching. Historians note that what counts as orthodox has often been settled retrospectively by the communities and institutions that prevailed, and the term can carry pejorative connotations of rigidity when used by critics, or positive connotations of fidelity when used by adherents.
