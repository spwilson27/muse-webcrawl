Unlockable (video gaming)

Locked characters on a select screen are a classic form of unlockable content.

An unlockable in video gaming is a piece of content — such as a character, level, weapon, vehicle, costume, or game mode — that is present in a game but inaccessible to the player until specific conditions are met. Unlockables reward progress, skill, or exploration, and extend a game's replay value by giving players goals beyond finishing the main content.

Types and conditions

Common unlocking conditions include completing the game or a campaign mode, defeating a boss, achieving a high score or fast time, finding hidden collectibles, winning a set number of matches, or fulfilling achievements. Fighting games traditionally hide secret characters unlocked by arcade-mode completions; racing games lock cars and tracks behind career progress; and hidden levels or "New Game Plus" modes reward finishing the story.

History

Unlockables became prominent in the 1990s with titles such as GoldenEye 007, whose cheat modes were unlocked by speedrunning levels, and the Tekken and Super Smash Bros. series, which hid much of their rosters. The famous Konami Code and other cheat codes sometimes offered shortcuts to the same content. Strategy guides and early internet forums built communities around discovering unlock conditions.

Modern practice

With the rise of downloadable content and microtransactions, content once earned through play is often sold instead, prompting debate among players about "unlockables" being converted into paid DLC, season passes, and battle passes. Many games now combine both models: cosmetic items may be earned in-game or purchased, while achievement systems on modern platforms institutionalized goal-based rewards.
