Osama bin Laden (10 March 1957 – 2 May 2011) was a Saudi Arabian-born militant leader who founded the Islamist organisation al-Qaeda. He masterminded the September 11 attacks on the United States in 2001, which killed nearly 3,000 people and prompted the U.S.-led "war on terror". For a decade he was the world's most wanted fugitive before being killed by United States Navy SEALs in Pakistan in 2011.

Early life

Bin Laden was born in Riyadh, one of more than 50 children of Mohammed bin Awad bin Laden, a Yemeni-born construction magnate whose company had close ties to the Saudi royal family. He studied in Jeddah and was drawn to pan-Islamist thought. After the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in 1979 he went to Peshawar and helped fund, organise, and recruit the Arab foreign fighters — the mujahideen — who fought the Soviets, co-founding the Maktab al-Khidamat services bureau with Abdullah Azzam.

Al-Qaeda

Al-Qaeda ("the Base") was founded around 1988 as the war in Afghanistan wound down, redirecting the network of Arab volunteers toward a global jihad. Bin Laden's 1996 and 1998 fatwas declared war on the United States, citing American troops in Saudi Arabia and support for Israel. Al-Qaeda carried out the 1998 U.S. embassy bombings in Kenya and Tanzania, the 2000 attack on USS Cole, and the September 11, 2001 attacks, in which 19 hijackers crashed airliners into the World Trade Center, the Pentagon, and a field in Pennsylvania.

Manhunt and death

Bin Laden evaded capture after the U.S. invasion of Afghanistan in late 2001 and eventually settled in a compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan. U.S. intelligence traced him through a courier, and on 2 May 2011 (Pakistan time) President Barack Obama authorised Operation Neptune Spear; SEALs raided the compound and killed bin Laden, whose body was buried at sea. Documents seized in the raid were later declassified in part.
