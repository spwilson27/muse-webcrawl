Practical joke

A practical joke, or prank, is a mischievous trick played on someone, generally causing the victim to experience embarrassment, perplexity, confusion, or discomfort. A person who performs a practical joke is called a practical joker or prankster. Unlike verbal jokes, practical jokes involve a physical element, such as a whoopee cushion, a rigged chair, or short-sheeting a bed.

History

Practical jokes have a long history. In the 19th century, the English writer and humorist tradition celebrated elaborate hoaxes, while the American humorist Edgar Allan Poe published essays on hoaxing. April Fools' Day, observed on 1 April, is the traditional occasion for practical jokes in many Western countries, and media outlets often participate with fabricated stories.

Examples

Common practical jokes include placing a whoopee cushion on a chair, setting clocks forward or backward, wrapping a colleague's office supplies in wrapping paper or gelatin, and the "kick me" sign placed on a victim's back. Elaborate media pranks include the BBC's 1957 spaghetti-tree hoax, in which a Panorama segment showed Swiss farmers harvesting spaghetti from trees.

In popular culture

Practical jokes are a staple of hidden-camera television programmes such as Candid Camera, and of comedy films. Critics note a fine line between harmless pranks and bullying or harassment; jokes that cause injury, property damage, or humiliation may have legal consequences in some jurisdictions.
