VY Canis Majoris (abbreviated to VY CMa) is an extreme red hypergiant star which is among the largest known stars by radius. It is located in the constellation Canis Major, roughly 4,900 light-years from Earth. If placed at the centre of the Solar System, its surface would extend beyond the orbit of Jupiter.

History of observation

VY Canis Majoris was first catalogued in 1801 by Jérôme Lalande. It is a semiregular variable star with a period of about 2,000 days, and it varies in apparent magnitude between about 6.5 and 9.6. The star is surrounded by a complex, asymmetric nebula of ejected material, including knots and arcs visible in Hubble Space Telescope images.

Properties

Estimates of its radius have varied historically; many early estimates made it one of the largest stars known, with values near or above 2,000 solar radii, while later interferometric measurements suggest around 1,420 solar radii. Its luminosity is roughly 270,000 times that of the Sun, and its mass is estimated at 17–25 solar masses. The star is losing mass at an enormous rate and is expected to explode as a supernova within the next 100,000 years, possibly leaving behind a black hole.
