British United Airways (BUA) was the largest independent British airline of the 1960s. It was formed in 1960 by the merger of several independent operators, including Hunting-Clan Air Transport and Airwork Services, under the holding company Air Holdings. Based principally at London Gatwick Airport, BUA operated scheduled services, charters, trooping flights and car-ferry services across Europe, Africa, South America and the Middle East.

History

BUA's associated companies ran the cross-Channel vehicle ferry services formerly operated by Silver City Airways under the British United Air Ferries name. The airline became a significant operator of the Vickers VC10 on routes to South America and Africa, and was an early customer for the BAC One-Eleven short-haul jet. Under managing director Freddie Laker, who left in 1965 to found Laker Airways, the group had pioneered many features of the independent sector.

Despite its scale, BUA struggled against the state corporations BEA and BOAC, and the 1969 Edwards Committee recommended the creation of a "second force" airline. In November 1970 BUA was taken over by the Scottish charter airline Caledonian Airways, and the two merged to form British Caledonian, which operated until its takeover by British Airways in 1987–88.
