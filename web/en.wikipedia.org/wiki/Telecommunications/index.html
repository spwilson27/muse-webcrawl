Telecommunications

Telecommunications, or telecom, is the transmission of information by various types of technologies over wire, radio, optical, or other electromagnetic systems, over significant distances. It has its origin in the human desire for communication over a distance greater than that feasible with the human voice, and its modern forms began with the electrical telegraph in the 1830s and 1840s.

History

Early distance communication used visual signals such as semaphore lines and signal fires. The electrical telegraph, commercialized by Samuel Morse and others, revolutionized long-distance messaging. Alexander Graham Bell's telephone (1876), Guglielmo Marconi's wireless telegraphy, and John Logie Baird's and others' television followed. The transistor, satellites, fiber-optic cables, and the Internet transformed telecom in the twentieth century.

Modern networks

Modern telecommunications networks are built from transmission links, switches and routers, and end-user equipment, organized by protocols such as the Internet protocol suite. Services include telephony, mobile communications, broadband Internet access, broadcasting, and data services. The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) coordinates global standards and spectrum allocation.
