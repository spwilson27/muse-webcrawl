Major-General Sir Alexander Cunningham (23 January 1814 – 28 November 1893) was a British army engineer with the Bengal Engineer Group who founded the Archaeological Survey of India in 1861 and became its first director-general. He is regarded as the father of Indian archaeology.

Career

Cunningham obtained a cadetship with the East India Company and served as an engineer in India, including as executive engineer to the king of Oudh and in the Second Anglo-Burmese War. A protégé of James Prinsep, who deciphered the Brahmi script, Cunningham developed a deep interest in Indian antiquities, coins and inscriptions.

Archaeological work

Following the itineraries of the Chinese pilgrims Faxian and Xuanzang, Cunningham identified and surveyed many lost sites of early Indian history, including Taxila, Sarnath, Bharhut (whose stupa sculptures he arranged to be moved to museums), Sanchi and the Mahabodhi Temple at Bodh Gaya. He published extensively, notably The Ancient Geography of India (1871) and numerous volumes of survey reports, and assembled a major collection of Indian coins.

Legacy

Cunningham's systematic surveys laid the institutional foundations for archaeology in South Asia, and many of his site identifications remain accepted today.
