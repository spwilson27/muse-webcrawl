Moro people

The Moro people are the predominantly Muslim ethnolinguistic groups of the southern Philippines, native mainly to Mindanao, the Sulu Archipelago, and Palawan. The name derives from "Moor", the Spanish term for Muslims, applied by Spanish colonisers by analogy with the Muslims of Iberia and North Africa. The Moros number roughly six million, about five per cent of the Philippine population, and regard their homeland as Bangsamoro ("Moro nation").

Groups

The Moro peoples comprise some thirteen ethnolinguistic groups, the largest being the Maguindanao, the Maranao of the Lanao provinces, and the Tausug of Sulu. Others include the Iranun, Yakan, Sama-Bajau, Sangil, Kalagan, Palawano, Molbog, Jama Mapun, Kalibugan, and Kamayo-related groups. Their languages belong to the Philippine branch of the Austronesian family, while their cultures combine indigenous Southeast Asian traditions with Islam, adopted from the fourteenth century onward through trade with Malay and Arab merchants and missionaries.

History

From the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, Muslim sultanates — above all the Sultanate of Sulu and the Sultanate of Maguindanao — dominated the region's trade and resisted Spanish colonisation for over three hundred years of intermittent warfare known as the Spanish–Moro conflict. Spain never fully subdued the Moro sultanates, and the United States fought its own Moro campaigns after 1899, including the battles of Bud Dajo (1906) and Bud Bagsak (1913). Under the Commonwealth and Republic, large-scale Christian settlement in Mindanao reduced Moros to a minority in much of their ancestral territory.

Armed struggle and autonomy

The grievances produced by land loss and political marginalisation erupted after the 1968 Jabidah massacre into an armed separatist movement led first by the Moro National Liberation Front (MNLF) and later by the Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF). Peace agreements produced the Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (ARMM) in 1989, and, after the 2014 Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro, the plebiscite of 2019 created the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM), intended as a fuller self-governing homeland with its own parliament.

Culture

Moro culture features distinctive arts such as the Maranao okir carving tradition, brassware, the malong tubular garment, kulintang gong music, and epics such as the Maranao Darangen. Islam shapes law, dress, diet (excluding pork), and observance, though pre-Islamic customs persist in many communities.
