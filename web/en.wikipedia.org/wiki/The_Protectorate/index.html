The Protectorate was the period during the Commonwealth of England, Scotland and Ireland when the British Isles were governed by a Lord Protector. It began on 16 December 1653 under the Instrument of Government, Britain's first written constitution, with Oliver Cromwell as Lord Protector, and ended in 1659 when his son Richard Cromwell resigned.

History

The Protectorate followed the execution of Charles I and the rule of the Rump Parliament and the short-lived Barebone's Parliament. Under the Instrument of Government, legislative power rested with a single-chamber Parliament and executive power with the Lord Protector and a Council of State. In 1655 Cromwell instituted the Rule of the Major-Generals, dividing England into military districts. The Humble Petition and Advice of 1657 offered Cromwell the crown, which he declined, though he accepted expanded powers and a second chamber.

End

After Oliver Cromwell's death in September 1658, Richard Cromwell succeeded him but lacked support in the army and Parliament; he resigned in May 1659. The ensuing chaos led to the Restoration of Charles II in 1660.
