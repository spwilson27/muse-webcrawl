Climate change in Australia

Climate change in Australia is a critical issue. Australia is experiencing the effects of the rapid warming of the planet due to climate change, and it is understood to be a major contributor, with its per capita emissions being among the highest in the world. Since the beginning of the 20th century, Australia has warmed by an average of over 1.4 °C, with the frequency of extreme heat events increasing and rainfall patterns shifting.

Impacts

Observed and projected impacts include more intense and longer heatwaves, changes in rainfall (declining in the south-west and parts of the south-east, with more intense downpours), sea level rise along the coasts, increased bushfire weather, and severe stress on ecosystems such as the Great Barrier Reef, which has suffered repeated mass coral bleaching events in warming ocean waters. The Black Summer bushfires of 2019–20 burned an estimated 18 million hectares and destroyed thousands of buildings.

Emissions

Australia's emissions derive principally from electricity generation (historically dominated by coal), transport, agriculture, and a large export-oriented fossil fuel industry: Australia is one of the world's largest exporters of coal and liquefied natural gas. Land use and land-use change also contribute significantly.

Policy and politics

Climate policy has been one of the most contested areas of Australian politics since the 2000s. Australia ratified the Kyoto Protocol in 2007 and the Paris Agreement in 2016. A national carbon pricing mechanism operated from 2012 until its repeal in 2014. In 2022 the Climate Change Act 2022 legislated targets of a 43% reduction in emissions by 2030 (against 2005 levels) and net zero by 2050.
