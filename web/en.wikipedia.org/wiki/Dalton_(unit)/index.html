Dalton (unit)

Symbol: Da (also u)
Unit of: Mass
Named after: John Dalton
In SI units: 1 Da ≈ 1.66053906660×10−27 kg

The dalton (symbol Da), or unified atomic mass unit (symbol u), is a unit of mass defined as one twelfth of the mass of an unbound neutral atom of carbon-12 in its nuclear and electronic ground state at rest. It is used to express atomic and molecular masses, especially in chemistry, biochemistry and molecular biology, where ordinary units such as the kilogram are inconveniently large.

Use

Molecular masses of proteins and other macromolecules are conventionally given in kilodaltons (kDa); a typical protein might be described as having a mass of 64 kDa. One dalton is approximately the mass of one hydrogen atom. Following the 2019 redefinition of the SI base units, the dalton remains defined relative to carbon-12 and is accepted for use with the SI, its value in kilograms being determined experimentally via the Avogadro constant.

History

The unit is named for John Dalton, whose atomic theory first proposed relative atomic weights. Earlier "atomic mass units" were based on oxygen before the carbon-12 standard was adopted in 1961.
