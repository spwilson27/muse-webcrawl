History of Poland (1918–1939)

The history of Poland from 1918 to 1939 covers the Second Polish Republic, reborn after 123 years of partition. The new state fought a series of border wars, most famously the Polish–Soviet War of 1919–1921, experimented with parliamentary democracy, and after Józef Piłsudski's May Coup of 1926 moved towards authoritarian rule, before being destroyed by the German and Soviet invasions of September 1939.

Interwar Poland stretched from the Baltic "corridor" to the Carpathians, bordering Germany, Czechoslovakia and the USSR.

Rebirth and border wars

As the partitioning empires collapsed in 1918, Piłsudski took power in Warsaw and Poland regained independence on 11 November 1918. The borders were settled by fighting: the Greater Poland uprising against Germany, the Silesian uprisings, the Polish–Ukrainian war over Lwów, and above all the Polish–Soviet War. The Red Army's drive on Warsaw was defeated at the Battle of Warsaw in August 1920 — the "Miracle on the Vistula" — and the Treaty of Riga (1921) fixed the eastern border.

Democracy and the May Coup

The March Constitution of 1921 created a parliamentary republic. Politics was fragmented and governments short-lived; the first president, Gabriel Narutowicz, was assassinated in 1922. The new state faced the task of integrating three former partitions with different currencies, laws and rail systems, and built the new Baltic port of Gdynia to bypass the Free City of Danzig. In May 1926 Piłsudski seized power in a coup, inaugurating the Sanacja ("sanitation") regime.

The Sanacja era

After Piłsudski's death in 1935, power passed to a group of colonels around President Ignacy Mościcki and Edward Śmigły-Rydz. The April Constitution of 1935 strengthened the presidency. Poland pursued a policy of balance between Germany and the USSR, signing non-aggression pacts with both (1932 and 1934). About a third of the population belonged to ethnic minorities — Ukrainians, Jews, Belarusians and Germans — and minority relations were often tense.

Road to war

In 1938 Poland annexed the Zaolzie region from Czechoslovakia. In 1939 Germany demanded Danzig and an extraterritorial corridor; Poland, backed by British and French guarantees, refused. The secret Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of 23 August 1939 agreed to partition Poland. Germany invaded on 1 September 1939 and the Soviet Union on 17 September, ending the Second Republic.
