Isabella of Angoulême

Isabella of Angoulême
Queen consort of England
Tenure: 1200 – 1216
Born: c. 1188
Died: 4 June 1246, Fontevraud Abbey, France
Spouses: John, King of England; Hugh X of Lusignan
Issue: Henry III of England; Richard, Earl of Cornwall; Joan, Queen of Scotland; Isabella, Holy Roman Empress; Eleanor, Countess of Leicester

Isabella of Angoulême (c. 1188 – 4 June 1246) was Queen of England as the second wife of King John from 1200 until his death in 1216. She was also Countess of Angoulême in her own right from 1202, and later Countess of La Marche by her second marriage to Hugh X of Lusignan.

Marriage to John

Isabella, the only child and heiress of Aymer, Count of Angoulême, was betrothed to Hugh IX of Lusignan, but King John married her himself at Bordeaux on 24 August 1200, when she was about twelve years old. The marriage provoked the Lusignans to appeal to Philip II of France, whose judgment against John in 1202 became the pretext for the French conquest of Normandy and most of the Angevin lands in France. Isabella was crowned queen at Westminster Abbey in October 1200.

Children and second marriage

Isabella gave John five children, including Henry III and Richard of Cornwall. After John's death she returned to France and in 1220 controversially married Hugh X of Lusignan, son of her former fiancé, with whom she had nine more children. Their daughter Alice married John de Warenne, Earl of Surrey.

Later life

In 1244 Isabella was accused of conspiring to poison Louis IX of France and fled to Fontevraud Abbey, where she died two years later. Her effigy lies at Fontevraud beside those of Henry II and Richard I.
