The Progressive Conservative Party of Canada (PC; French: Parti progressiste-conservateur du Canada) was a centre to centre-right federal political party in Canada that existed from 1942 to 2003. It was one of the two major parties that governed Canada for most of the country's history, alternating in power with the Liberal Party.

History

The party traced its origins to the Conservative parties of John A. Macdonald, Canada's first prime minister. It adopted the "Progressive Conservative" name in 1942 when Manitoba premier John Bracken, formerly of the Progressive Party, agreed to lead the Conservatives on condition the name be changed. Under John Diefenbaker the party won a landslide in 1958, then the largest majority in Canadian history to that point. Joe Clark briefly led a minority government in 1979–80.

Under Brian Mulroney, the PCs won record majorities in 1984 and 1988, pursuing free trade with the United States and the Meech Lake and Charlottetown constitutional accords. After Mulroney's resignation and the brief premiership of Kim Campbell, the party was reduced to two seats in the 1993 election, damaged by the rise of the Reform Party in the west and the Bloc Québécois in Quebec.

Merger

In 2003, under leader Peter MacKay, the party merged with the Canadian Alliance to form the modern Conservative Party of Canada, ending the federal Progressive Conservatives after 61 years. Provincial Progressive Conservative parties continue to exist independently in several provinces. See also Conservative Party of Canada and Red Tory.
