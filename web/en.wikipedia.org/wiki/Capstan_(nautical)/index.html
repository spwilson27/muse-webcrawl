Capstan (nautical)

A capstan is a vertical-axled rotating machine developed for use on sailing ships to multiply the pulling force of seamen when hauling in ropes, cables, and hawsers. The principle is similar to that of the windlass, which has a horizontal axle. Steam and later electric capstans were also used on railways and docks.

Design and use

The capstan consists of a drum or gypsy mounted on a vertical spindle, with sockets around its head for removable bars. Sailors pushed on the bars to rotate the drum, winding in a messenger cable connected to the anchor cable or other heavy line. Pawls prevented the drum from slipping backwards. A related mechanical advantage arises from the capstan equation, in which a small holding force can restrain a large load because friction increases exponentially with the number of turns of rope around the drum.

History

Capstans were essential for weighing anchor on large sailing warships, a task that might occupy over a hundred men working in shifts and accompanied by music. Modern ships use powered windlasses and capstans for mooring lines.

In culture

Sea shanties were timed to hauling and capstan work; the capstan bar and capstan songs feature widely in nautical literature.
