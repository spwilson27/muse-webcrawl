Cantar de mio Cid

The Cantar de mio Cid ("The Song of My Cid"), or Poema de mio Cid, is the oldest preserved Castilian epic poem. It narrates the deeds of the Castilian hero Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar, known as El Cid, during the Reconquista. Composed around 1200, it survives in a single manuscript, a 14th-century copy now held by the Biblioteca Nacional de España.

Content

The poem, nearly 3,800 lines long, is divided into three cantars (songs). The first tells of the Cid's exile by King Alfonso VI and his campaigns against the Moors; the second celebrates his conquest of Valencia and the marriages of his daughters to the Infantes of Carrión; the third recounts the Infantes' dishonourable beating of their wives (the outrage of Corpes) and the Cid's legal vindication at the court of Toledo, ending with more honourable marriages for his daughters into the royal houses of Navarre and Aragon.

Authorship and style

The poem is anonymous. Its style is sober and realistic compared with the French chansons de geste, with a restrained heroism, attention to legal and economic detail, and a poetic formula — "¡Dios, qué buen vassallo, si oviesse buen señore!" ("God, what a good vassal, if only he had a good lord!") — recurring as a refrain. The scholar Ramón Menéndez Pidal championed its study and helped establish it as Spain's national epic.
