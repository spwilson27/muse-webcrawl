Toys "R" Us

Toys "R" Us is an American toy and juvenile-products retail brand, founded in 1957 by Charles Lazarus, who had run a baby-furniture store in Washington, D.C., from 1948. Its backwards "R" logo and giraffe mascot Geoffrey made it one of the most recognisable children's retailers in the world, at its peak operating hundreds of stores across the United States and internationally, plus the Babies "R" Us chain.

Rise and leveraged buyout

The company popularised the big-box "category killer" toy superstore format and went public in 1978. In 2005 it was taken private in a leveraged buyout of about $6.6 billion by Bain Capital, KKR, and Vornado Realty Trust; the resulting debt burden, combined with competition from Walmart, Target, and Amazon, squeezed investment in stores and e-commerce.

Bankruptcy and afterlife

Toys "R" Us filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in September 2017 and, failing to restructure, liquidated its US stores in 2018; many international operations were sold separately. The brand's intellectual property passed to WHP Global, and the name returned via shop-in-shops inside Macy's department stores in the United States from 2021–2022, along with a flagship store at the American Dream mall in New Jersey.
