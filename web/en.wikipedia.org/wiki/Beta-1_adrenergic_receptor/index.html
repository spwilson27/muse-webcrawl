Beta-1 adrenergic receptor

ADRB1
GeneADRB1 (chromosome 10q25.3)
TypeG protein-coupled receptor
Endogenous agonistsNorepinephrine, epinephrine
Main locationsHeart, kidneys, adipose tissue
The beta-1 receptor is a seven-transmembrane G protein-coupled receptor

The beta-1 adrenergic receptor (β1 adrenoceptor), also known as ADRB1, is a beta-adrenergic receptor, and also denotes the human gene encoding it. It is a G protein-coupled receptor associated with the Gs heterotrimeric G-protein and is expressed predominantly in cardiac tissue.

Function

Activation of the beta-1 receptor by catecholamines such as norepinephrine and epinephrine stimulates adenylyl cyclase, raising intracellular cyclic AMP. In the heart this produces positive chronotropic (heart rate), dromotropic (conduction velocity), inotropic (contractility) and lusitropic (relaxation) effects. In the kidneys, beta-1 receptors on juxtaglomerular cells stimulate the release of renin, activating the renin–angiotensin system. The receptor also plays a role in lipolysis in adipose tissue.

Clinical significance

Beta-1 receptors are the principal target of cardioselective beta blockers such as metoprolol, atenolol and bisoprolol, used in hypertension, angina, heart failure and after myocardial infarction. Conversely, the selective beta-1 agonist dobutamine is used to increase cardiac contractility in acute heart failure and cardiogenic shock. Common polymorphisms of ADRB1, such as Ser49Gly and Arg389Gly, have been studied for their influence on responses to beta-blocker therapy.
