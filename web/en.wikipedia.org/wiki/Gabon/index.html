Gabon (French: Gabon), officially the Gabonese Republic (French: République gabonaise), is a country on the Atlantic coast of West Central Africa. Located on the equator, it borders Equatorial Guinea to the northwest, Cameroon to the north, the Republic of the Congo on the east and south, and the Gulf of Guinea to the west. It has an area of nearly 270,000 square kilometres (100,000 sq mi) and a population of 2.4 million people. There are four major language groups: Fang, Eshira, Punu, and Teke.

Gabon achieved independence from France in 1960. It is a member of the United Nations, the African Union, ECOWAS, the Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa (CEMAC), the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, and OPEC.

History

The earliest inhabitants of the area were Pygmy peoples. They were largely replaced and absorbed by Bantu tribes arriving in waves during migrations. Portuguese explorers and traders arrived in the area in the late 15th century. Gabon became a French colony and later part of French Equatorial Africa. Léon M'ba was elected Prime Minister in 1957 and became the first President upon independence in 1960. Omar Bongo succeeded him in 1967 and ruled until his death in 2009, making him one of Africa's longest-serving leaders. His son Ali Bongo Ondimba succeeded him; in August 2023, a military coup removed Ali Bongo from power shortly after a disputed election.

Geography

Gabon is located on the Atlantic coast of West Central Africa, on the equator, between latitudes 3°N and 4°S. Its terrain is a narrow coastal plain with a hilly interior (the Cristal Mountains and the Chaillu Massif in the centre) and savanna in the east and south. About 85% of the country is covered by dense tropical rainforest. The largest river is the Ogooué, about 1,200 kilometres (750 mi) long.

Economy

Gabon is rich in natural resources, notably petroleum, manganese, uranium, and timber. Oil production has historically accounted for roughly half of government revenue, though output has declined from its 1990s peak. Timber — especially okoumé — and manganese mining are major export sectors. Gabon's per-capita GDP is among the highest in sub-Saharan Africa, but income inequality remains high.

Biodiversity

Gabon is a significant refuge for wildlife, home to forest elephants, western lowland gorillas, chimpanzees, and the Loango National Park's famous surfing hippos. A system of 13 national parks was established in 2002, and in 2022 Gabon became the first African country to receive payments for reducing deforestation emissions.
