The River Cuckmere is a river in East Sussex, England, rising near Heathfield on the southern slopes of the Weald and flowing south through the South Downs to the English Channel at Cuckmere Haven, between Seaford and Eastbourne. It is about 32 kilometres long. The lower valley — with its meanders, oxbow lakes and backdrop of the Seven Sisters chalk cliffs — is one of the most photographed landscapes in southern England, and the estuary is unusual in having remained largely undeveloped.

Course

The Cuckmere passes Hellingly and Arlington Reservoir (a major local water supply), then flows past Alfriston and through the Cuckmere Valley within the South Downs National Park. Its lower course was partly straightened by a canalised cut dug in the 1840s to improve drainage and reduce flooding, bypassing the natural meanders; the old loops survive as oxbow lakes beside the river. The shoreline at Cuckmere Haven has a history of smuggling and was defended during the Second World War with pillboxes and anti-tank obstacles, some still visible.

Management

The estuary and valley are protected for their chalk grassland, saltmarsh, shingle beach and birdlife, and are crossed by the South Downs Way and Vanguard Way. Long-running debate concerns the future of the valley's flood defences: the Environment Agency has proposed allowing the estuary to return to a more natural, self-maintaining state rather than perpetually rebuilding shingle defences and embankments — a plan that would gradually reshape the famous meanders, making the Cuckmere a prominent English example of managed coastal realignment policy.
