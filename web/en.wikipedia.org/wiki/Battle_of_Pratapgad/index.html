The Battle of Pratapgad was fought on 10 November 1659 between the Maratha leader Shivaji and Afzal Khan, a general of the Adil Shahi dynasty of Bijapur, at the fort of Pratapgad near Satara in present-day Maharashtra. It was the first major victory of the young Maratha polity and made Shivaji a formidable power in the Deccan.

Background

The Bijapur court, alarmed by Shivaji's growing territory in the Sahyadri hills, dispatched Afzal Khan with a large army to destroy him. Afzal Khan ravaged the countryside and desecrated temples to provoke Shivaji into open battle, but Shivaji withdrew to the hill fort of Pratapgad and pretended submission, agreeing to a personal meeting at the foot of the fort.

The meeting and the battle

Fearing treachery, Shivaji came to the meeting wearing armour beneath his clothes and carrying concealed weapons — the bagh nakh ("tiger claws") and a dagger. When Afzal Khan embraced him and attempted to stab or strangle him, Shivaji disembowelled the Khan with the tiger claws. In the confusion, Shivaji's bodyguard Jiva Mahala killed the Khan's attendant Sayyid Banda. Maratha troops hidden in the surrounding forest then ambushed the leaderless Bijapuri army in the narrow valley, routing it completely and capturing its artillery, treasure and supplies.

Aftermath

The victory at Pratapgad broke Bijapur's offensive power and netted Shivaji twenty-two forts and vast resources in the following campaign. Afzal Khan's tomb was built at the site, and the encounter became one of the most celebrated episodes in Maratha history, retold in ballads (powadas) and later literature.
