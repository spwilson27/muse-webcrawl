Violet Constance Jessop (2 October 1887 – 5 May 1971) was an Irish-Argentine ocean liner stewardess and nurse who became famous for surviving the disasters of all three Olympic-class liners: she was aboard RMS Olympic when it collided with a warship in 1911, survived the sinking of RMS Titanic in 1912, and survived the sinking of HMHS Britannic in 1916. She was nicknamed "Miss Unsinkable".

Violet Jessop in her Voluntary Aid Detachment nurse's uniform.

Life and career

Jessop was born in Argentina to Irish immigrant parents. After her father's death the family returned to Britain, and she went to sea as a stewardess to support her mother, first with the Royal Mail Line and then the White Star Line. She was aboard Olympic on 20 September 1911 when it collided with HMS Hawke; the ship survived.

On Titanic's maiden voyage in April 1912 Jessop was ordered into lifeboat 16; as it was lowered, an officer handed her a baby to care for, later reclaimed by a woman aboard the rescue ship Carpathia. During the First World War she served as a nurse aboard the hospital ship Britannic. On 21 November 1916 Britannic struck a mine in the Aegean Sea; Jessop's lifeboat was drawn into the still-turning propellers, and she jumped clear, suffering a fractured skull. She nonetheless returned to work at sea for decades, retiring in 1950.

Legacy

Jessop's memoir, written in 1934, was published posthumously and remains a vivid primary source on life aboard Edwardian liners. Her story is a staple of Titanic literature and exhibitions.
