The British Council is the United Kingdom's international organisation for cultural relations and educational opportunities. Founded in 1934, it works in more than 100 countries, promoting the English language, British education, arts and culture, and building connections between people in the UK and other countries. It is a charity and an executive non-departmental public body sponsored by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office.

History

The Council was established in 1934 as the British Committee for Relations with Other Countries, at a time when the British government sought to counter the cultural propaganda of other European powers. It received a Royal Charter in 1940. During and after the Second World War it expanded rapidly, opening institutes, libraries and teaching centres across Europe, the Middle East and the Commonwealth.

Activities

The British Council is one of the world's largest providers of English language teaching and assessment; it co-owns the IELTS English language test with partners in Australia and Cambridge. It administers education exchanges and scholarships, supports study in the UK, runs arts festivals and exhibitions, and delivers development programmes in areas such as education, society and the creative economy. Its work is funded by a mixture of government grant, teaching and exam fees, and project income.

Controversies

The Council's close relationship with the British state has periodically drawn criticism, and its offices have occasionally been caught up in diplomatic disputes — most notably in Russia, where its regional offices were ordered closed in 2007–08 and its operations were shut down entirely in 2018.
