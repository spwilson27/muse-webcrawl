Columba is a genus of birds in the pigeon family Columbidae. Its members are typically medium to large pigeons, mostly of the Old World, with stocky bodies, short necks and slender bills. The name derives from the Latin columba, "dove" or "pigeon". The best-known species is the rock dove (Columba livia), ancestor of the domestic and feral pigeons found in cities worldwide.

Species

The genus includes around 35 species, among them the common wood pigeon (C. palumbus), the stock dove (C. oenas), the hill pigeon (C. rupestris), the snow pigeon (C. leuconota), the speckled pigeon (C. guinea) and the white-crowned pigeon (C. leucocephala) of the Caribbean. Several island species are threatened or extinct; the extinct Socorro dove's relative the pink pigeon belongs to a different genus, Nesoenas.

Distribution and behaviour

Columba species occur across Europe, Asia, Africa and the Americas, inhabiting woodland, cliffs and farmland. They feed mainly on seeds and fruit, drink by suction (unusual among birds), and produce "crop milk" to feed their nestlings. Like other pigeons, they are strong fliers with remarkable homing ability.
