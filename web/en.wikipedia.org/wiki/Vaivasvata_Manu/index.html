Vaivasvata Manu

Vaivasvata Manu (Sanskrit: वैवस्वत मनु), also called Sraddhadeva, is the seventh and current Manu — the progenitor of humanity — in Hindu tradition, out of the fourteen Manus who preside over successive manvantaras. He is the son of Vivasvan (the sun god Surya) and the father of Ikshvaku, founder of the Solar dynasty.

The flood legend

Vaivasvata Manu is the hero of the Hindu flood myth, first told in the Shatapatha Brahmana and expanded in the Mahabharata and Puranas. Warned by a fish — the Matsya avatar of Vishnu — of a coming deluge, Manu built a boat and, when the waters rose, was towed by the fish to safety on a Himalayan peak, after which he repopulated the earth.

Legislation

Tradition attributes to Manu the Manusmriti (Laws of Manu), the most authoritative of the Dharmashastra texts, said to have been taught by Manu to the sages. Hindu timekeeping holds that the present age lies within Vaivasvata's manvantara.
