Mixed model

A mixed model (or mixed-effects model) is a statistical model containing both fixed effects and random effects. These models are widely used in research where measurements are made repeatedly on the same statistical units, or where observations are clustered or grouped.

Definition

In a linear mixed model, the response is modeled as a combination of population-level fixed effects, unit-level random effects that are assumed to be drawn from a probability distribution (usually normal), and residual error. In matrix notation the model is often written as y = Xβ + Zu + ε, where β represents fixed effects and u random effects.

Estimation

Parameters are commonly estimated by maximum likelihood or by restricted maximum likelihood (REML), which accounts for the degrees of freedom used by the fixed effects when estimating variance components. Generalized linear mixed models extend the framework to non-normal responses such as counts and binary outcomes.

Applications

Mixed models are standard tools for longitudinal studies, clinical trials with repeated measures, educational research (students nested in schools), animal breeding, and multi-center studies, because they handle unbalanced data and within-unit correlation naturally.
