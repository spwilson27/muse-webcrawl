Shaanxi

Shaanxi (Chinese: 陕西; pinyin: Shǎnxī) is a landlocked province of the People's Republic of China. Its capital and largest city is Xi'an, one of the oldest cities in China and the eastern terminus of the Silk Road. The province covers an area of about 205,800 square kilometres (79,500 sq mi) and had a population of roughly 37.5 million in 2020, making it the 16th most populous Chinese province.

Shaanxi is the historical home of many Chinese dynasties, including parts of the Zhou, Qin, Han and Tang dynasties, whose capitals stood in or near modern Xi'an. The spelling "Shaanxi" (with a doubled "a") is used in the postal romanization to distinguish the province from neighbouring Shanxi, which is pronounced with a different tone.

Geography

The province stretches from the Loess Plateau in the north, through the Guanzhong Plain along the Wei River in the centre, to the Qinling Mountains in the south, which form a major climatic divide between northern and southern China. Beyond the Qinling lies the subtropical Hanzhong basin. The Yellow River forms much of the province's eastern border with Shanxi, while the great bend of the river at Hancheng and the Hukou Waterfall are among its natural landmarks.

History

Shaanxi is regarded as one of the cradles of Chinese civilization. The semi-legendary Zhou dynasty had its early capitals in the Wei valley, and in 221 BC Qin Shi Huang unified China from his capital at Xianyang, just northwest of present-day Xi'an. The Han dynasty capital Chang'an and the Tang dynasty capital of the same name were among the largest cities in the world of their eras. The Terracotta Army, buried with the First Emperor near Xi'an, was discovered in 1974 and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The province suffered severely in the 1556 Shaanxi earthquake, centred near Huaxian, which remains the deadliest earthquake in recorded history with an estimated 830,000 deaths. In 1936 Xi'an was the site of the Xi'an Incident. From 1935 to 1948 Yan'an in northern Shaanxi served as the headquarters of the Chinese Communist Party, and the region retains great symbolic importance in party history.

Economy

Shaanxi's economy is built on energy and heavy industry, agriculture, and increasingly high technology. The province is a major producer of coal, natural gas and oil from the Ordos Basin fringe in the north. Xi'an is a centre for aerospace, software and higher education, sometimes called China's "Silicon Valley of the West." Agriculture includes wheat and corn on the loess plateau and fruit, especially apples — Shaanxi is China's largest apple-producing province.
