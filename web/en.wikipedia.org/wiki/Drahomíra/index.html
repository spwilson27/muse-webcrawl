Drahomíra

Died after 935
Spouse Vratislaus I, Duke of Bohemia
Issue Wenceslaus I; Boleslaus I

Drahomíra (died after 935) was a Bohemian duchess, wife of Duke Vratislaus I and mother of Wenceslaus I (Saint Wenceslaus) and Boleslaus I. Born a princess of the pagan Hevelli (Stodorané) Slavs, she married into the Přemyslid dynasty around 906.

Regency

When Vratislaus died in 921, Drahomíra became regent for the young Wenceslaus. Her regency was marked by conflict with her mother-in-law, Ludmila, who had influence over Wenceslaus's upbringing and favoured Christianity and accommodation with East Francia. In 921 Ludmila was murdered — strangled, according to legend, with her own veil — by retainers acting on Drahomíra's orders, an act for which later Christian chroniclers, writing in praise of the martyred Ludmila, portrayed Drahomíra as a cruel pagan persecutor.

Legacy

Modern historians treat the hostile portrait cautiously, noting the power politics behind the feud. Drahomíra appears as a character in Dvořák's opera Švanda dudák-era tradition and in Bedřich Smetana's and Antonín Dvořák's works about early Bohemia; Dvořák's opera Dimitrij and Libuše tradition are distinct, but she features prominently in Czech national-historical drama.
