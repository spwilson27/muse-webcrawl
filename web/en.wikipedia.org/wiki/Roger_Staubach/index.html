Roger Thomas Staubach (born February 5, 1942) is an American former professional football quarterback who played in the National Football League (NFL) for 11 seasons, all with the Dallas Cowboys. Nicknamed "Roger the Dodger" and "Captain Comeback", he won the Heisman Trophy at the United States Naval Academy, served four years in the Navy including a tour in Vietnam, and then led the Cowboys to two Super Bowl victories, becoming one of the most popular players of the 1970s.

College and military service

Staubach starred at the Naval Academy, winning the Heisman Trophy in 1963 as a junior and leading Navy to a 9–1 season and the Cotton Bowl. After graduating in 1964 he fulfilled his four-year military commitment, serving as a supply corps officer with a tour in Vietnam, and so did not begin his NFL career until 1969, as a 27-year-old rookie.

NFL career

The Cowboys had drafted Staubach in the tenth round of the 1964 NFL draft as a futures pick. He became the team's starter in 1971 and that season led Dallas to victory in Super Bowl VI over Miami, earning the game's MVP award. Famous for late comebacks — he engineered 23 fourth-quarter game-winning drives — he coined a lasting piece of football vocabulary with his desperate long touchdown pass to Drew Pearson in the 1975 playoffs against Minnesota, which he called a "Hail Mary". Staubach quarterbacked Dallas to a second title in Super Bowl XII and retired after the 1979 season because of repeated concussions, finishing with a then-record passer rating and a 85–29 record as a starter.

After football

Staubach built a major commercial real-estate company, the Staubach Company, which merged with Jones Lang LaSalle in 2008. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1985 and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2018.
