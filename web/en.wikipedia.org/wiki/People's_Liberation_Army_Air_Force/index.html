People's Liberation Army Air Force


Founded: 11 November 1949
Country: China
Branch: People's Liberation Army
Role: Aerial warfare

The People's Liberation Army Air Force (PLAAF), also called the Chinese Air Force, is the aerial warfare service branch of China's People's Liberation Army. Founded on 11 November 1949, it has grown into one of the largest air forces in the world, with roughly 400,000 personnel and several thousand aircraft, and the largest air force in Asia.

History

The PLAAF was built with Soviet assistance and first saw major combat in the Korean War, where its MiG-15s fought United Nations forces over "MiG Alley". Cut off from Soviet support after the Sino-Soviet split, it relied for decades on copies and derivatives of Soviet designs such as the J-6 and J-7. From the 1990s, economic reform funded a sweeping modernisation: imports of Russian Su-27, Su-30, and Su-35 fighters, and rapid development of indigenous types.

Modern force

Today the PLAAF fields the Chengdu J-10 and J-16 multirole fighters, the J-20 stealth fighter (in service from 2017), Xi'an Y-20 strategic transports, KJ-500 airborne early warning aircraft, and H-6 bombers capable of launching cruise missiles, alongside large fleets of unmanned aerial vehicles. Since the 2016 PLA reforms it is organised under five theatre commands, shifting from a territorial air-defence force toward joint, offensive-capable operations.

Role and doctrine

Chinese doctrine describes the PLAAF as a "strategic air force" able to conduct integrated offensive and defensive operations, with emphasis on informationised warfare, long-range strike, and operations around Taiwan and the South China Sea.
