Crystallographic point group

In crystallography, a crystallographic point group is a set of symmetry operations, like rotations or reflections, that leave a point fixed while the crystal structure remains indistinguishable from its starting state. These operations may carry the crystal from one orientation to another that is indistinguishable, but not necessarily identical. There are exactly 32 crystallographic point groups in three dimensions, a consequence of the crystallographic restriction theorem, which limits rotational symmetries of a lattice to orders 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6.

Notation

Point groups are denoted in two main systems. In Hermann–Mauguin (international) notation, symbols describe the symmetry along principal directions (e.g. mmm, 4/m). In Schoenflies notation, widely used in spectroscopy, groups are labelled Cn, Dn, T, O, and variants with subscripts for mirror planes and inversion (e.g. C2v, D4h).

Relation to space groups and crystal systems

Combining the 32 point groups with the 14 Bravais lattices and translational symmetry elements (screw axes and glide planes) gives the 230 three-dimensional space groups. The point groups fall into the seven crystal systems: triclinic, monoclinic, orthorhombic, tetragonal, trigonal, hexagonal and cubic. The symmetry of a crystal's point group constrains its physical properties, for example whether it can be piezoelectric or optically active (Neumann's principle).
