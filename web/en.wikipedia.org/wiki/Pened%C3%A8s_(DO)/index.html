Penedès (DO)


Type: Denominación de origen (DO)
Country: Spain
Region: Penedès, Catalonia
Sub-regions: Alt Penedès, Baix Penedès, Garraf (Medio Penedès)
Key grapes: Xarel·lo, Macabeo, Parellada; Tempranillo, Garnacha, Cabernet Sauvignon
Known for: Still wines; heartland of Cava production

Penedès is a Spanish denominación de origen (DO) for wines produced in the Penedès comarca of Catalonia, southwest of Barcelona. It is one of Spain's oldest and most prestigious wine regions, with viticulture dating to the Phoenicians and Romans, and today one of its most innovative, noted especially for white wines and as the production centre of the sparkling wine Cava.

Geography and climate

The region stretches from the Mediterranean coast up into pre-coastal hills, divided into the low, warm Baix Penedès, the mid-altitude Medio (Garraf) zone, and the cooler, higher Alt Penedès around Vilafranca del Penedès and Sant Sadurní d'Anoia. Soils vary from coastal sands and clays to limestone-rich uplands; this diversity supports a wide palette of styles and grape varieties.

Wines

Traditional white varieties — Xarel·lo, Macabeo (Viura), and Parellada — dominate, the same trio used for Cava, most of which is produced around Sant Sadurní d'Anoia (under the separate Cava DO). Still wines include fresh whites increasingly led by Xarel·lo, rosés, and reds from Tempranillo (locally Ull de Llebre), Garnacha, Cariñena, Merlot, and Cabernet Sauvignon. Producers such as Torres, based in Pacs del Penedès, helped pioneer modern Spanish winemaking and international varieties in the region.

Regulation

The DO was formally established in the twentieth century and is administered by its own regulatory council, overseeing authorised varieties, yields, and winemaking practices. In recent years many leading estates have emphasised organic and biodynamic viticulture, and some producers have left the Cava DO to market premium sparkling wines under designations such as Corpinnat, reshaping the region's sparkling-wine landscape.
