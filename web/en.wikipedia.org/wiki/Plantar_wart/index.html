Plantar wart

SpecialtyDermatology
CauseHuman papillomavirus (HPV)
LocationSoles of the feet
TreatmentSalicylic acid, cryotherapy, watchful waiting

A plantar wart is a wart occurring on the sole or toes of the foot. It is caused by the human papillomavirus (HPV). The virus enters the body through tiny cuts or breaks in the skin on the bottom of the feet, and infection is commonly acquired in damp communal environments such as swimming pools and locker rooms.

Signs and symptoms

Plantar warts appear as small, fleshy, rough growths on pressure points of the sole, often flattened by walking and surrounded by thickened callus. Tiny black dots (thrombosed capillaries, sometimes called wart seeds) may be visible within the lesion. They can be painful when walking or standing, and multiple warts may merge into a "mosaic" cluster.

Treatment

Many plantar warts resolve spontaneously. Treatments include topical salicylic acid, cryotherapy with liquid nitrogen, curettage, laser therapy, and immunotherapy. No treatment is universally effective, and recurrence is common.

Prevention

Keeping feet clean and dry, wearing footwear in communal wet areas, and avoiding direct contact with warts reduce the risk of infection.
