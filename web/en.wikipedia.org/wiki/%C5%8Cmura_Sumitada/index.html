Ōmura Sumitada (1533 – 23 June 1587) was a Japanese daimyō lord of the Sengoku period. He is best known as the first daimyō to convert to Christianity, taking the baptismal name Dom Bartolomeu, and for opening the port of Nagasaki to Portuguese trade, a decision that shaped the city's history.

Early life

Sumitada was the son of Arima Haruzumi, lord of Shimabara, and was adopted into the neighbouring Ōmura clan. As a minor lord hemmed in by powerful rivals such as the Ryūzōji, he sought Portuguese firearms and trade as a means of survival.

Conversion and trade

In 1561 Sumitada invited Portuguese ships to his port of Yokoseura, and in 1563 he was baptised by Jesuit missionaries, becoming the first Christian daimyō. His conversion was accompanied by campaigns against Buddhist temples and Shinto shrines in his domain, which provoked rebellion and attacks in which Yokoseura was burned.

Nagasaki

In 1570 Sumitada opened the fishing village of Nagasaki to Portuguese trade, and in 1580 he ceded Nagasaki and Mogi to the Society of Jesus in perpetuity. Under Jesuit administration and Portuguese commerce, Nagasaki grew into Japan's principal gateway for the Nanban trade with Europe and China. Sumitada also joined the Tenshō embassy initiative, sending representatives with other Christian lords toward Europe.

Later life

Pressed by the expanding Ryūzōji clan, Sumitada became their vassal in his later years. He died in 1587, the same year Toyotomi Hideyoshi seized Nagasaki back under direct control and issued his edict expelling missionaries. His son Ōmura Yoshiaki succeeded him.
