Gospel

Gospel originally meant the Christian message of "good news" (Greek: εὐαγγέλιον, euangelion). It came to refer to the four canonical accounts of Jesus's life — Matthew, Mark, Luke and John — in the New Testament.

Gospel
Canonical gospels: Matthew, Mark, Luke, John
Synoptic gospels: Matthew, Mark, Luke
Written: c. 70–110 AD

Canonical gospels

Mark, the earliest (c. 70 AD), was a source for Matthew and Luke; these three are the Synoptic Gospels, sharing much material, including the hypothetical Q source. John (c. 90–110) is theologically distinct, opening with the Logos hymn.

Non-canonical gospels

Many other gospels circulated — Thomas, Peter, Judas, Mary — reflecting diverse early Christian communities; most were excluded from the canon formalised by the 4th century. "Gospel" also denotes a genre of African-American religious music originating in the late 19th–20th centuries.
