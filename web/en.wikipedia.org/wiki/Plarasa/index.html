Plarasa

Plarasa is a genus of moths belonging to the subfamily Tortricinae of the family Tortricidae — the tortrix moths, a large family of small moths whose larvae are commonly known as leafrollers. As with many small genera of microlepidoptera, Plarasa consists of a modest number of described species known from a limited region, and its members are small, inconspicuously patterned moths identified largely by the structure of their genitalia.

Taxonomy

The genus belongs to the tribe-level assemblage of tortricine moths characterised by a well-developed gnathos and particular wing-venation patterns. Genera of Tortricinae are delimited principally on male and female genitalia, because external colour patterns in the family are notoriously homoplastic — many unrelated species share mottled brown, grey and ochreous forewings that provide camouflage on bark and dead leaves. Species-level catalogues such as the Online World Catalogue of Tortricidae record the described species of Plarasa, their type localities and any recorded host plants.

Biology

By analogy with related Tortricinae, the larvae of Plarasa are leafrollers: a caterpillar spins silk to fold or roll a leaf of its host plant into a shelter, feeding inside it and pupating either within the roll or in leaf litter. Adults are nocturnal and come to light. Tortricids generally have one or more generations per year depending on climate, overwintering as larvae or pupae. Host-plant records for small tropical and subtropical tortricine genera are often sparse, since documenting them requires rearing larvae through to adult emergence.

Distribution

Tortricidae is a worldwide family of more than 11,000 described species, particularly diverse in temperate and montane tropical regions. Individual small genera such as Plarasa typically have narrower ranges documented from type material and subsequent collecting, and their full distributions are usually incompletely known.
