Yellow ribbon

A yellow ribbon is a symbol with various meanings, most prominently as a sign of support for military troops and of remembrance for absent loved ones, particularly prisoners of war, hostages, and service members deployed overseas. It is displayed as a literal ribbon tied around trees, poles, and railings, or as a printed ribbon emblem.

Origins

The association of a yellow ribbon with a waiting sweetheart or wife draws on folk tradition popularized in song: the 1917 song "Round Her Neck She Wears a Yeller Ribbon," the 1949 John Wayne film She Wore a Yellow Ribbon, and the 1973 hit "Tie a Yellow Ribbon Round the Ole Oak Tree" by Tony Orlando and Dawn, in which a released convict asks for a ribbon as a sign he is welcome home.

The hostage crisis and the Gulf War

The symbol entered American public life during the 1979–1981 Iran hostage crisis, when Penelope Laingen, wife of hostage Bruce Laingen, tied a yellow ribbon around a tree at her home and urged others to do the same. Yellow ribbons proliferated across the United States again during the 1991 Gulf War, displayed on homes, cars, and lapels in support of deployed troops — a practice repeated during the Iraq and Afghanistan wars, often in the form of magnetic car ribbons.

Other uses

Yellow ribbons also symbolize suicide prevention awareness, sarcoma and bone cancer awareness, endometriosis awareness, and, in Hong Kong's 2014 Umbrella Movement and subsequent pro-democracy protests, support for universal suffrage. In Catalonia, yellow ribbons became a symbol of support for imprisoned pro-independence leaders after 2017, prompting political controversy in Spain.
