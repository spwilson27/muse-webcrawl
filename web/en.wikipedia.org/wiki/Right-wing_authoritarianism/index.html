Right-wing authoritarianism

Right-wing authoritarianism (RWA) is a personality and ideological construct in political psychology, developed principally by Bob Altemeyer beginning in the 1980s. It describes a cluster of three attitudinal tendencies: authoritarian submission (deference to established authorities), authoritarian aggression (hostility toward groups sanctioned as targets by those authorities), and conventionalism (rigid adherence to social conventions and norms).

Measurement

RWA is measured with the RWA Scale, a balanced self-report instrument. Scores correlate with prejudice toward out-groups, support for punitive policies, and political conservatism in many Western samples. The construct is often studied alongside social dominance orientation (SDO); together they form the dual process model of ideology, in which RWA reflects a view of the world as dangerous and threatening, motivating a preference for social control and security.

Criticism

Critics argue the scale may conflate authoritarianism with ordinary conservatism and religiosity, and researchers have debated whether a symmetric "left-wing authoritarianism" exists and how it should be measured.
