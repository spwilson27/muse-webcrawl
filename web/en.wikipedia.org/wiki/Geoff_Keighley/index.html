Geoff Keighley (born 1978) is a Canadian video game journalist, television presenter and producer. He is the creator and executive producer of The Game Awards, the annual awards ceremony celebrating the video game industry, and of the Summer Game Fest showcase event series.

Career

Keighley began writing about games as a teenager in the early 1990s and became known for his long-form "Final Hours" behind-the-scenes features on major game developments, published on GameSpot and later as interactive apps. On television he hosted GameTrailers TV and later GTTV on Spike TV, where he was executive in charge of game publisher relations for the network's VGX (formerly Spike Video Game Awards) shows from 2003 to 2013.

The Game Awards

When Spike discontinued its awards show, Keighley invested his own money to launch The Game Awards in 2014, retaining ownership and hosting duties. The show grew into the games industry's most-watched event, combining awards with world-premiere announcements and musical performances, reaching over 100 million livestreams in the early 2020s. In 2020 he launched Summer Game Fest as a season of digital showcases, which grew to fill the void left by the cancellation of E3, and he also became a co-host of the gamescom Opening Night Live event.

Public image

Keighley is one of the most visible intermediaries between the game industry and its audience; a 2012 photograph of him interviewing during a Doritos-and-Mountain Dew-branded segment spawned the "Doritos Pope" meme, which he has since addressed when discussing journalistic ethics and sponsorship in games media. He has been recognized with a New York Game Awards honour and has published books on the making of games such as Half-Life: Alyx and Death Stranding (his likeness appears in the latter as a character model, reflecting his friendship with Hideo Kojima).
