125cc

125cc is a motorcycle engine displacement class, referring to engines with a swept volume of approximately 125 cubic centimetres. The class was for decades one of the principal categories of Grand Prix motorcycle racing and remains widespread in entry-level road racing, commuter motorcycles and learner-legal machines in many licensing regimes.

Grand Prix racing

The 125cc class was part of the FIM Road Racing World Championship from its inception in 1949 until the end of the 2011 season. The machines were single-cylinder two-strokes for most of the class's history, producing over 50 horsepower in their final years despite their small capacity. Manufacturers such as Honda, Aprilia, Derbi and KTM dominated different eras, and the class served as the traditional entry point for future world champions including Valentino Rossi, Dani Pedrosa and Marc Márquez. In 2012 the class was replaced by Moto3, which uses 250cc four-stroke single-cylinder engines.

Road and learner machines

In many European licensing systems, the A1 licence permits riders from age 16 or 17 to ride motorcycles of up to 125cc with a maximum power output of 11 kW (about 15 hp), making 125cc machines the standard learner and commuter class. Popular models have included the Honda CB125, Yamaha YBR125 and numerous scooters.
