Baiju Noyan (Mongolian: Байжу ноён; died c. 1259) was a Mongol commander (noyan) who governed Persia and campaigned in Anatolia, the Caucasus and the Middle East in the 1230s–1250s. He succeeded Chormaqan as commander of the Mongol forces in Persia in 1241–42.

Campaigns

Operating from the Mughan plain in Azerbaijan, Baiju pressured the Sultanate of Rum; in 1243 he crushed the Seljuk army at the Battle of Köse Dağ, making the sultanate a Mongol vassal. He raided into Syria, took Erzurum, and intervened in Georgia and Armenia, extracting tribute and hostages. Suspected by the regent Töregene and later out of favour at Möngke's court, he was repeatedly summoned and investigated but retained command until Hülegü's arrival in the 1250s, after which he disappears from the record, reportedly executed around 1259.

Legacy

Baiju's campaigns fixed Mongol dominance over Anatolia and the Caucasus for a generation and set the stage for the Ilkhanate founded by Hülegü.
