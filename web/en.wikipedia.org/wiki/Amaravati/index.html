Amaravati

Known forAmaravati Stupa; Amareswara Temple; planned state capital

Amaravati is a city on the banks of the Krishna River in Guntur district of the Indian state of Andhra Pradesh. It is a historic pilgrimage and archaeological site, famed for the great Buddhist stupa whose sculpted limestone reliefs — the "Amaravati school" of art — flourished between roughly the 2nd century BCE and the 3rd century CE, and for the Amareswara Temple, one of the five Pancharama Kshetra shrines of Shiva. The name Amaravati, "abode of the immortals", is also that of the mythical capital of Indra.

In 2015 the name was adopted for the planned new capital city of Andhra Pradesh, being developed in the surrounding capital region after the state's bifurcation and the loss of Hyderabad to Telangana; the foundation stone was laid that year, though the project's scope and status have changed repeatedly with successive state governments, with construction of the capital revived again after 2024.

History

The site, ancient Dhanyakataka, was a major centre under the Satavahanas and later dynasties, and a crossroads of trade and both Buddhist and Hindu worship. Buddhist tradition associates Amaravati with the Buddha's preaching of the Kalachakra tantra. Excavations by Colin Mackenzie and others in the late 18th and 19th centuries recovered the stupa's celebrated sculptures, many of which were removed to museums in Chennai and London.

The great stupa and Amaravati art

The Amaravati Mahachaitya was among the largest stupas in India, about 50 metres in diameter, encased in carved limestone slabs depicting scenes from the Buddha's life and the Jataka tales. The Amaravati style — slender, fluid figures in swirling compositions — is one of the three great schools of early Indian art, alongside Mathura and Gandhara.

Religious significance

The Amareswara Temple, with its tall gopuram and 15-foot white marble lingam, draws Shaivite pilgrims, while the site also contains a modern giant statue of the Buddha in dhyana pose overlooking the river.

The modern capital project

The Andhra Pradesh Capital Region Development Authority planned a greenfield capital of over 200 square kilometres incorporating the historic town. Development stalled after a change of government in 2019 proposed three regional capitals instead; after 2024 the Amaravati project was relaunched with renewed central support.
