Bustle

Placeholder image: a dress with a bustle, c. 1885.

A bustle is a padded undergarment or framework used to add fullness, or create a particular profile, to the back of a woman's dress. Bustles were worn under the skirt in the mid-to-late 19th century to keep the skirt from dragging and to emphasise the rear, replacing the earlier all-round fullness of the crinoline.

History

The bustle first came into fashion in the early 1870s, when crinoline fullness migrated to the back of the skirt. This first bustle era (roughly 1870–1878) featured soft draped fullness supported by pads and half-crinoline structures. After a period of slimmer silhouettes, the bustle returned in the 1880s in a more extreme form: a rigid, shelf-like projection at the back, supported by frameworks of steel bands or cane, sometimes collapsible. By about 1890 the bustle faded as skirts moved toward the hourglass silhouette of the 1890s and the S-bend corset of the 1900s.

Construction

Bustles were made from a variety of materials including horsehair, down-filled pads, wire and steel springs. Some patented designs could be folded or adjusted, allowing the wearer to sit down. The bustle was worn over the corset and under petticoats, with the dress's skirt draped over it.
