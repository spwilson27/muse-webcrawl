Widow (typesetting)

In typesetting, a widow is the final line of a paragraph that appears alone at the top of a new page or column, separated from the rest of its paragraph. The converse defect, the first line of a paragraph stranded at the bottom of a page, is an orphan; a single short word alone on a paragraph's last line is sometimes called a runt.

Avoidance

Typographers avoid widows and orphans by adjusting page breaks, tracking, spacing or lightly editing text. Word processors offer "widow/orphan control," and CSS provides the widows and orphans properties for paged media. Style guides such as the Chicago Manual of Style treat widows as faults to be corrected in page proofs.
