Aosta Valley

Country: Italy
Capital: Aosta
Area: 3,263 km²
Population: about 124,000
Official languages: Italian, French

The Aosta Valley (Italian: Valle d'Aosta; French: Vallée d'Aoste) is a mountainous autonomous region in northwestern Italy. It is bordered by Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, France, to the west; by Valais, Switzerland, to the north; and by Piedmont, Italy, to the south and east. The region's capital is Aosta. It is the smallest, least populous, and least densely populated region of Italy.

Geography

The valley is the basin of the Dora Baltea river, surrounded by some of the highest peaks in Europe, including Mont Blanc (shared with France), Monte Rosa, the Matterhorn, and Gran Paradiso, whose massif is protected by Italy's oldest national park, established in 1922. Lateral valleys radiate from the central valley, each historically a distinct community.

History and culture

Inhabited in antiquity by the Celtic Salassi, the valley was conquered by Rome, whose legacy survives in Aosta's Roman walls, theatre, and the Arch of Augustus. In the Middle Ages it passed to the House of Savoy, and it formed part of the Kingdom of Sardinia and then of Italy. Granted special autonomous status in 1948, the region is officially bilingual: French enjoys equal status with Italian, and the local Franco-Provençal dialect, Valdôtain, persists in everyday speech. The Walser communities of the Lys valley preserve a Germanic heritage.

Economy

Tourism, centred on ski resorts such as Courmayeur, Cervinia, and Pila and on summer hiking, is the mainstay of the economy, together with hydroelectric power, dairy farming (noted for Fontina cheese), and wine production from some of Europe's highest vineyards.
