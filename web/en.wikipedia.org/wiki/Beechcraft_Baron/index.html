Beechcraft Baron

The Beechcraft Baron is a family of twin-engine piston light aircraft developed by Beechcraft of Wichita, Kansas. Introduced in 1961 and derived from the earlier Travel Air, the Baron became one of the best-known and longest-produced light twin designs, widely used for personal and business travel, flight training, and charter operations.

Development and variants

The original Model 55 Baron, first flown in 1960, combined the fuselage of the single-engine Debonair with a new twin-engine wing. Successive variants — the A55 through E55 — added more powerful engines, improved avionics, and seating for up to six. The Model 56TC introduced turbocharged engines, while the Model 58, introduced in 1969 with a stretched fuselage, club seating and double rear doors, became the definitive variant. The pressurised 58P and turbocharged 58TC followed in the 1970s. The Baron G58, with a modern glass cockpit, remained in production into the 2020s, more than six decades after the type's introduction.

Characteristics

The Baron 58 series cruises at around 200 knots with a range of roughly 1,000 nautical miles, powered by two Continental piston engines of 300 horsepower each. The type is noted for its solid handling, sturdy construction, and strong single-engine performance relative to its contemporaries, but also for higher operating costs typical of piston twins. Barons have served with air taxi operators, corporate flight departments, and some military users as trainers and liaison aircraft.
