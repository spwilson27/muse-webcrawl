La movida madrileña

La movida madrileña (also known as La Movida or the Madrid scene) was a countercultural movement that took place mainly in Madrid during the Spanish transition to democracy after the death of Francisco Franco in 1975, flourishing in the late 1970s and 1980s.

Characteristics

The movement was hedonistic and creative, embracing freedom of expression in music, film, photography, fashion and nightlife after decades of Francoist conservatism. It was centred on neighbourhoods such as Malasaña and venues like Rock-Ola. Iconic figures include the filmmaker Pedro Almodóvar, the photographer Ouka Leele, the designer Ágatha Ruiz de la Prada, and bands such as Alaska y los Pegamoides, Radio Futura, Nacha Pop and Kaka de Luxe.

Legacy

The mayor of Madrid, Enrique Tierno Galván, famously encouraged the city's youth to enjoy themselves ("¡El que no esté colocado, que se coloque!"). La Movida is remembered as a defining episode in the modernization of Spanish culture, though it was also associated with the era's heroin epidemic.
