Distal convoluted tubule

Distal convoluted tubule
PrecursorMetanephric blastema
SystemUrinary system
LocationKidney (cortex)

The distal convoluted tubule (DCT) is a portion of the kidney nephron between the loop of Henle and the collecting duct system. It is partly responsible for the regulation of potassium, sodium, calcium and pH.

Function

It has the following functions:

  • pH regulation — by secreting protons and absorbing bicarbonate;
  • Sodium and potassium balance — the DCT reabsorbs sodium via the sodium-chloride cotransporter (NCC) and regulates potassium handling downstream;
  • Calcium absorption — under the influence of parathyroid hormone, the DCT increases calcium reabsorption through the apical calcium channel TRPV5 and the basolateral sodium-calcium exchanger;
  • Magnesium absorption — the DCT is the principal site of active magnesium reabsorption in the nephron, via the TRPM6 channel.

Although the DCT is the shortest segment of the nephron, spanning only about 5 mm in length, it plays a critical role in a number of homeostatic processes, including sodium chloride reabsorption, potassium secretion, and calcium and magnesium handling. The early DCT is also the site of the macula densa, a plaque of specialised cells that senses sodium chloride concentration in the tubular fluid and participates in tubuloglomerular feedback and renin release as part of the juxtaglomerular apparatus.

Clinical significance

Thiazide diuretics act by inhibiting the sodium-chloride cotransporter of the DCT, increasing sodium and water excretion; they are widely used to treat hypertension and edema. Inherited defects of DCT transporters cause Gitelman syndrome (loss of function of NCC) and, in related segments, contribute to disorders of salt and potassium balance. Reduced DCT calcium handling is implicated in idiopathic hypercalciuria and kidney stone formation.
