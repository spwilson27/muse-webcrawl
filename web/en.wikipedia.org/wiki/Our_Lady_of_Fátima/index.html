Our Lady of Fátima (Portuguese: Nossa Senhora de Fátima) is a Catholic title of the Virgin Mary based on the Marian apparitions reported in 1917 by three shepherd children at the Cova da Iria in Fátima, Portugal. The children—Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto—said a lady "brighter than the sun" appeared to them on the 13th day of six consecutive months, beginning on 13 May 1917.

The apparitions

The visionaries said Mary called for prayer, penance and devotion to her Immaculate Heart, and entrusted them with three prophecies known as the Secrets of Fátima. The final apparition, on 13 October 1917, was witnessed by a crowd estimated at 30,000–70,000 people and accompanied by the reported "Miracle of the Sun", in which the sun appeared to whirl and plunge in the sky. The Catholic Church declared the apparitions "worthy of belief" in 1930.

Shrine and devotion

The Sanctuary of Fátima, with its basilica (consecrated 1953) and vast esplanade, is one of the world's largest Marian pilgrimage sites, drawing millions of pilgrims annually, especially on 13 May and 13 October. Popes Pius XII, Paul VI, John Paul II, Benedict XVI and Francis have all promoted or visited the shrine; John Paul II credited Our Lady of Fátima with saving his life in the 1981 assassination attempt, which occurred on the feast day. Francisco and Jacinta Marto were canonised by Pope Francis in 2017, the centenary of the apparitions.
