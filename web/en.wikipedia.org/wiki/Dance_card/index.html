Dance card

A dance card or ballspenden is a card, often decorative, used by a woman at a formal ball to record the names of the gentlemen with whom she intends to dance each successive dance of the evening. Dance cards were most common in the 19th and early 20th centuries, particularly in Vienna and in the United States.

Description

A typical dance card consisted of a small booklet or folded card listing the evening's dances — waltzes, quadrilles, polkas, galops and, later, two-steps and fox-trots — with a blank line beside each. A tiny pencil, attached by a silk cord, was used to write in partners' names; the card was often worn on the wrist or attached to the gown. Many were elaborate souvenir objects made of celluloid, ivory, silver or mother-of-pearl, produced by the hostess or sponsoring organisation as keepsakes.

Social function

Under ballroom etiquette, a lady was expected to reserve dances in advance and could not properly refuse an invitation unless the dance was already promised. A filled card signalled popularity; an empty one, a "wallflower" evening. The idiom "my dance card is full" survives as a general expression meaning that one is fully occupied. Usage declined after the First World War as ballroom customs relaxed, though dance cards persisted at American college proms and military balls into the mid-20th century, and remain popular collectible ephemera.
