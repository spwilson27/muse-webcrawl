Mordor

Mordor is a fictional realm in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium, the land of the Dark Lord Sauron. In the Black Speech its name means "Black Land"; in Sindarin likewise Morndor, "dark land." It lies in the south-east of Middle-earth, east of Gondor and the great river Anduin.

Geography

Mordor is ringed on three sides by mountain ranges — the Ephel Dúath (Mountains of Shadow) to the west and south and the Ered Lithui (Ash Mountains) to the north — leaving it open mainly to the east toward Rhûn. Within lie the volcanic plateau of Gorgoroth, the ash-choked vale of Udûn, and Mount Doom (Orodruin), the volcano in whose fires the One Ring was forged and where alone it could be destroyed. Sauron's fortress of Barad-dûr stands on a spur of the Ered Lithui. The main entrance from the west is the Black Gate (Morannon); a guarded pass at Cirith Ungol, watched by Shelob, leads past Minas Morgul. The south of Mordor, around the inland Sea of Núrnen, was farmed by enslaved peoples to feed Sauron's armies.

Role in the War of the Ring

In The Lord of the Rings, Frodo Baggins and Samwise Gamgee cross Mordor in disguise to reach Mount Doom, while Aragorn's feint at the Black Gate draws Sauron's eye away from them. With the Ring's destruction, Barad-dûr collapses, Sauron is undone, and Mordor's dominion ends.
