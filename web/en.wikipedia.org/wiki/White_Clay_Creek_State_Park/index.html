White Clay Creek State Park

White Clay Creek State Park is a Delaware state park along the White Clay Creek valley near Newark, Delaware, in New Castle County. Covering roughly 3,600 acres, it preserves rolling Piedmont terrain, forests, meadows and historic mill sites along the creek, which is designated a National Wild and Scenic River.

History

The park was established in 1979 as the Walter S. Carpenter Jr. State Park, incorporating land from the du Pont family's Granogue estate and other acquisitions; it was renamed White Clay Creek State Park in 1995. The park protects the Judge Morris Estate and the historic Chambers House Nature Center, and includes segments of the Mason–Dixon Trail.

Recreation

Visitors use more than 37 miles of trails for hiking, mountain biking and horseback riding, fish for trout in the creek, and visit the Possum Hill and Bryan's Field areas. The park connects with Pennsylvania's White Clay Creek Preserve across the state line.
