1920 United States presidential election

The 1920 United States presidential election was the 34th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 2, 1920. Republican Senator Warren G. Harding of Ohio resoundingly defeated Democratic Governor James M. Cox of Ohio, in the first election held after the end of the First World War and the first in which women voted nationwide under the Nineteenth Amendment. Harding campaigned for a "return to normalcy" after the tumult of the Wilson years.

Background

President Woodrow Wilson was incapacitated and deeply unpopular amid post-war recession, labour unrest, the Red Scare, race riots and the Senate's rejection of the League of Nations. At the Republican convention in Chicago, delegates deadlocked among favourites before settling on the amiable Harding, who ran a front-porch campaign from Marion, Ohio. The Democrats nominated Cox in San Francisco, with the young Franklin D. Roosevelt as his running mate.

Campaign and results

Harding's promise of a "return to normalcy" captured the public mood. He won the largest popular-vote percentage margin recorded to that time — 60.3% to Cox's 34.1% — and 404 electoral votes to 127. Socialist candidate Eugene V. Debs, campaigning from federal prison in Atlanta, won 3.4% of the vote, the last notable Socialist showing. The election was also the first whose results were broadcast by radio, over KDKA in Pittsburgh.

Aftermath

Harding's victory began a decade of Republican dominance in presidential politics. He died in office in 1923 and was succeeded by Calvin Coolidge. His administration is remembered both for pro-business policies and for the Teapot Dome scandal.
