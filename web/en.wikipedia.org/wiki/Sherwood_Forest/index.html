Sherwood Forest is a royal forest in Nottinghamshire, England, famous throughout the world because of its historic association with the legend of Robin Hood. The area has been wooded since the end of the Last Glacial Period, and today Sherwood Forest National Nature Reserve encompasses 423.2 hectares (1,046 acres) surrounding the village of Edwinstowe, site of the Sherwood Forest visitor centre.

History

Sherwood was a royal hunting forest by the Norman period, governed by strict forest law that protected the "vert and venison" for the Crown. Medieval Sherwood covered roughly a quarter of Nottinghamshire. Over the centuries much of it was cleared for agriculture, industry, and coal mining, though large estates such as Thoresby, Welbeck, and Clumber preserved fragments of ancient woodland.

The Major Oak, possibly 800–1,000 years old

The Major Oak and Robin Hood

The forest's most famous tree is the Major Oak, a huge Quercus robur with a trunk circumference of about 11 metres, beneath which — legend holds — Robin Hood and his Merry Men sheltered. The forest's outlaw associations are celebrated each year at the Robin Hood Festival in Edwinstowe.

Ecology

Sherwood is one of Britain's most important sites for ancient oak trees and the rare invertebrates that depend on decaying wood. Some 1,000 veteran oaks survive, a legacy of its days as a royal hunting forest.
