Deferred sentence

A deferred sentence is a form of criminal sentencing in which a defendant pleads guilty or is found guilty, but the court postpones imposing or executing the sentence for a specified period. During the deferral period the defendant is typically placed under conditions similar to probation. If the defendant successfully completes the period without violations, the court may dismiss the case, reduce the sentence, or allow the plea to be withdrawn; if the defendant violates the conditions, the court may impose the original sentence.

Variations

Related arrangements include deferred adjudication (common in Texas), in which the court defers a finding of guilt so that no conviction is formally entered if the term is completed; suspended sentences, in which a sentence is imposed but its execution is suspended; and diversion programs, which route defendants — often first-time or drug offenders — into treatment or community service in place of prosecution.

Purpose

Deferred sentences are intended to give offenders, particularly first-time and low-level offenders, an opportunity to avoid the lasting consequences of a conviction, such as a criminal record affecting employment, while still holding them accountable through court supervision.
