Put-in-Bay, Ohio

Put-in-Bay is a village on South Bass Island in Lake Erie, located in Ottawa County, Ohio, United States. The population was around 150 at recent censuses, though the village hosts tens of thousands of visitors each summer. The village is a popular summer resort and recreational destination, accessible by ferry, private boat, and aircraft.

Put-in-Bay was historically significant during the War of 1812 as the location from which Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry sailed to engage the British fleet in the Battle of Lake Erie in September 1813. Perry's flagship, the brig Niagara, and the other vessels of his squadron departed from the bay, and following the American victory, the bay served as the base for the fleet. The battle gave the United States control of Lake Erie for the remainder of the war.

Geography

Put-in-Bay is located on South Bass Island, one of the Lake Erie Islands, roughly three miles from the Ohio mainland near Port Clinton. The village occupies the northern shore of the island, centered on a sheltered natural harbor. According to the United States Census Bureau, the village has a total area of about 0.63 square miles, of which most is land and a small portion is water.

Attractions

The village's best-known landmark is Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial, a 352-foot (107 m) Doric column completed in 1915 that commemorates the Battle of Lake Erie and the long peace between the United States, Canada, and Great Britain. It is among the tallest monuments in the United States and is administered by the National Park Service.

Other attractions include:

  • Perry's Cave, a limestone cave discovered beneath the island
  • Crystal Cave, home to one of the world's largest known geodes
  • The Lake Erie Islands Historical Society museum
  • Numerous marinas, restaurants, bars, and golf-cart rental businesses serving summer tourists

Transportation

The village is served by ferry services from Port Clinton, Sandusky, and Catawba Island, including the Miller Ferry and the Jet Express. Put-in-Bay Airport (3W2) provides general aviation access. Golf carts and bicycles are the most common means of getting around the island during the tourist season.
