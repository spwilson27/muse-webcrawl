Manele

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Manele (from Romanian manéa, plural manele) is a genre of pop folk music from Romania, drawing on Turkish, Arabic, Balkan and Romani influences combined with electronic production. The genre originated with lăutari (traditional Romani musicians) and grew to mass popularity after 1989.

Characteristics

Manele songs typically deal with love, money, rivalry and the pleasures and sorrows of life, and are sung in Romanian, sometimes with Romani or Turkish phrases. Instrumentation mixes synthesizers and drum machines with clarinet, accordion and violin. The genre is closely associated with Romani performers and audiences, and with weddings and parties.

Reception

Despite huge commercial success and chart dominance in Romania, manele has been controversial: critics and some broadcasters have stigmatised it as vulgar or low-quality, and attempts have been made to restrict its public performance. Scholars have studied it as an expression of post-communist social change and minority identity.
