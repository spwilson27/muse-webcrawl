Tres Zapotes

LocationVeracruz, Mexico
CultureOlmec, Epi-Olmec
Periodc. 1500 BCE – 900 CE

Tres Zapotes is a Mesoamerican archaeological site located in the southern Mexican state of Veracruz, in the Papaloapan River basin, near the foothills of the Tuxtla Mountains. It was one of the major centres of the Olmec civilization and is unusual in that its occupation extended over some two millennia, long outlasting the other Olmec capitals.

History

Founded in the Early Formative period, Tres Zapotes rose to prominence after the decline of San Lorenzo (c. 900 BCE) and shared regional influence with La Venta. Unlike those sites, it continued to flourish through the Late Formative and into the Classic period under the successor Epi-Olmec culture, before finally declining around 900 CE.

Important finds

  • Colossal heads — two Olmec colossal heads (Monuments A and Q) were found here; they are smaller than those of San Lorenzo and La Venta, carved from basalt quarried in the Tuxtla Mountains.
  • Stela C — discovered in 1939, one side carries an Olmec-style mask; the reverse bears one of the earliest Long Count dates found anywhere, corresponding to 3 September 32 BCE, which helped demonstrate the antiquity of the Mesoamerican calendar.
  • The "Magliabechiano" and other stelae illustrate Epi-Olmec art and early writing.

Significance

Tres Zapotes provides a bridge between the Olmec and later cultures of the Gulf Coast, and its monuments have been central to debates about the origins of Mesoamerican writing systems, including the Isthmian (Epi-Olmec) script.
