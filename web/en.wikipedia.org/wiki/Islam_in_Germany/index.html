Islam in Germany

Islam in Germany is the country's second-largest religion after Christianity. Muslims make up roughly 6–7% of the population, numbering around 5.3 to 5.6 million people. The largest group are people of Turkish origin, followed by communities from Arab countries, the Balkans, Iran, Afghanistan and Pakistan.

History

Contacts between the German lands and the Islamic world date back centuries, including diplomatic relations between the Holy Roman Empire and the Ottoman Empire. Muslim prisoners of war were settled in Prussia in the 18th century, and the first mosque on German soil, the Wünsdorf mosque, was built for Muslim prisoners during the First World War. Large-scale Muslim immigration began with the recruitment of Turkish Gastarbeiter (guest workers) after the 1961 labour agreement between West Germany and Turkey. Later waves included asylum seekers and refugees, notably during the 2015 European migrant crisis.

Organisations

Major umbrella organisations include the Turkish-Islamic Union for Religious Affairs (DİTİB), the Islamic Council, the Central Council of Muslims in Germany, and the Ahmadiyya Muslim Jamaat. The German Islam Conference (Deutsche Islamkonferenz), launched in 2006, serves as a forum for dialogue between the state and Muslim representatives. Unlike the major Christian churches, Islam does not have the status of a public-law corporation at the federal level, which affects issues such as religious education and mosque financing.

Demographics

Most German Muslims are Sunni; there are also significant Alevi, Shia and Ahmadi communities. Large concentrations live in North Rhine-Westphalia, Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria, Hesse and Berlin.
