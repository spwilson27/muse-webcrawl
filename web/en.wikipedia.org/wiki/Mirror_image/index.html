Mirror image

Text appearing reversed in a mirror image

A mirror image (in a plane mirror) is a reflected duplication of an object that appears almost identical, but is reversed in the direction perpendicular to the mirror surface. As an optical effect it results from reflection off substances such as a mirror or water.

Geometry

In geometry, the mirror image of an object or two-dimensional figure is the virtual image formed by reflection in a plane mirror; it is of the same size as the original object, yet different, unless the object or figure has reflection symmetry. Mirror images swap front and back while leaving up/down and left/right coordinates otherwise unchanged — the apparent "left–right reversal" arises because the image's front faces the opposite way.

Mirror writing and chirality

Text in a mirror appears reversed, the basis of mirror writing, famously used by Leonardo da Vinci in his notebooks. In chemistry, molecules that are non-superimposable mirror images of one another are called enantiomers and exhibit chirality.
