Off-road racing

Off-road racing takes specially prepared vehicles over deserts, beaches, mud, snow and forests.

Off-road racing is a form of motorsport in which specially prepared vehicles — including trophy trucks, buggies, motorcycles, and all-terrain vehicles — race over rough natural terrain such as deserts, mountain trails, beaches, and mud rather than on paved circuits.

Major events

The most famous off-road race is the Dakar Rally, a multi-day rally raid held in South America from 2009 to 2019 and in Saudi Arabia since 2020, after originating as the Paris–Dakar Rally. In North America, desert racing is centred on events such as the Baja 1000 and Baja 500 on Mexico's Baja California peninsula, the Mint 400 in Nevada, and the King of the Hammers in California, which combines desert running with extreme rock crawling. Short-course off-road racing is held on closed dirt circuits with jumps and banked turns.

Vehicles

Desert racing vehicles feature long-travel suspension, reinforced roll cages, large off-road tyres, and powerful engines. Classes range from unlimited Trophy Trucks producing over 900 horsepower to limited classes based on production vehicles such as the Volkswagen Beetle-derived Baja Bug. Motorcycles and quads compete in their own classes, with riders navigating by roadbook.

Organisation

In the United States, sanctioning bodies such as SCORE International organise the Baja races, while the FIA governs cross-country rallying internationally, including the World Rally-Raid Championship, of which the Dakar is the flagship round.
