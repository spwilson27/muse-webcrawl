Volyn Oblast (Ukrainian: Волинська область, Volynska oblast) is an oblast (province) in north-western Ukraine. Its administrative centre is the historic city of Lutsk. The oblast borders Rivne Oblast to the east, Lviv Oblast to the south, Poland to the west, and Belarus to the north. It takes its name from the historic region of Volhynia.

Geography

Volyn Oblast covers about 20,144 square kilometres of the Polesian lowland and the Volhynian upland. The terrain is largely flat and forested, with extensive wetlands in the north, including parts of the Pripyat marshes. The principal rivers are the Pripyat, the Styr, and the Western Bug, which forms part of the Polish border. The Shatsk Lakes, a group of more than thirty lakes including Lake Svityaz — Ukraine's deepest lake — lie in the north-west and are protected within Shatsk National Natural Park.

History

The region was part of the medieval principality of Volhynia, later of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, and after the partitions of Poland largely of the Russian Empire. Between the world wars it formed Poland's Wołyń Voivodeship. Annexed by the Soviet Union in 1939 under the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, the oblast was formally created on 4 December 1939. During the Second World War it was the scene of the Volhynian massacres of Poles by the Ukrainian Insurgent Army in 1943, an event that remains central to Polish–Ukrainian historical memory. Since 1991 it has been part of independent Ukraine.

Administration and economy

Following Ukraine's 2020 decentralisation reform, the oblast is divided into four raions: Lutsk, Kovel, Kamin-Kashyrskyi, and Volodymyr. The economy is based on agriculture, food processing, forestry, and coal mining around Novovolynsk. Lutsk's medieval upper castle (Lubart's Castle) is the region's best-known landmark.
