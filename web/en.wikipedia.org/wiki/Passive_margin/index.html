Passive margin

Placeholder image

A passive margin is the transition between oceanic and continental lithosphere that is not an active plate margin. A passive margin forms during continental rifting and the opening of a new ocean basin, and is characterized by thick accumulations of sediment, little volcanic activity, and few earthquakes.

Formation

When a continent splits apart, the stretched and thinned continental crust of the rift subsides as it cools. Sediment shed from the continent builds outward over the margin, forming a broad continental shelf, slope, and rise. Classic examples include the eastern coasts of North and South America facing the Atlantic Ocean, and the margins of Africa, Australia, and India.

Contrast with active margins

Active margins, by contrast, coincide with plate boundaries and feature subduction zones, mountain building, and frequent seismicity, as along much of the Pacific "Ring of Fire". Passive margins may become active margins if subduction later initiates along them. See also plate tectonics and continental shelf.
