Ponte Fabricio

Ponte Fabricio
Also known as: Pons Fabricius; Ponte dei Quattro Capi
Location: Rome, Italy
Built: 62 BC
Crosses: River Tiber to Tiber Island

The Ponte Fabricio (Latin: Pons Fabricius) is the oldest Roman bridge in Rome, Italy, still existing in its original state. Built in 62 BC, it spans half of the Tiber River, from the Campus Martius on the east bank to Tiber Island in the middle (the other half is crossed by the Ponte Cestio). According to Dio Cassius, it replaced an earlier wooden bridge destroyed by fire.

Description

The bridge is named for Lucius Fabricius, the curator viarum (commissioner of roads) who oversaw its construction; his name is recorded four times in inscriptions on the structure ("L. Fabricius C.f. cur. viar. faciendum coeravit" — "Lucius Fabricius, son of Gaius, superintendent of the roads, took care of its construction"), with an additional inscription noting approval by the consul Quintus Lepidus and Marcus Lollius in 21 BC after flood damage repairs.

It is about 62 metres long and 5.5 metres wide, built of tufa and peperino stone faced with travertine, with two wide arches supported by a central pier. A smaller arch in the pier helps relieve flood pressure. The bridge is also called the Ponte dei Quattro Capi ("Bridge of the Four Heads") for the four-faced herms of Janus set on its parapet, said to come from the church of St Gregory. In the Middle Ages it was known as the Pons Iudaeorum because of its proximity to the Jewish Ghetto.
