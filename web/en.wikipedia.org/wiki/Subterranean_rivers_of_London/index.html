Subterranean rivers of London

The subterranean rivers of London are the tributaries of the River Thames and the River Lea that were built over during the growth of the metropolis. Some twenty or more former surface streams — including the Fleet, Tyburn, Westbourne, Effra, Walbrook, and Neckinger — now flow underground through culverts and sewers before discharging into the Thames.

History

As London expanded from the 18th century onward, its smaller rivers were progressively covered over and incorporated into the sewer system, a process accelerated after the Great Stink of 1858 and the construction of Joseph Bazalgette's intercepting sewers. The River Fleet, the largest of the lost rivers, once joined the Thames at Blackfriars and is now carried in a great brick sewer beneath Farringdon Street and New Bridge Street.

Legacy

The buried rivers survive in street names and street patterns (such as Fleet Street, Tyburn, and Westbourne Grove), in the topography of the city, and in the names of districts like Holborn (the "hollow bourne"). There are periodic proposals to restore some of the rivers to the surface, and walks tracing their courses are popular among London historians.
