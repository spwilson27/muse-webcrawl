Annwn

Annwn (also Annwfn or Annwfyn) is the Otherworld in Welsh mythology. Ruled by the king Arawn and later also by Gwyn ap Nudd, Annwn is depicted as a realm of eternal youth, abundance, and delight, without sickness or death — very different from a land of the dead in the Christian sense, though later tradition sometimes identified it with the underworld.

In the Mabinogi

In the First Branch of the Mabinogi, Pwyll, prince of Dyfed, offends Arawn, king of Annwn, by driving his hounds from a stag; to make amends Pwyll exchanges shapes and kingdoms with Arawn for a year and defeats Arawn's enemy Hafgan. Pwyll's son Pryderi later receives a herd of otherworldly swine from Arawn, whose theft by Gwydion precipitates Pryderi's death. In the Second Branch, Brân's expedition returns with a cauldron originally from Annwn that restores slain warriors to life.

Preiddeu Annwfn

The poem Preiddeu Annwfn ("The Spoils of Annwn"), preserved in the Book of Taliesin, describes King Arthur's sea expedition to Annwn to seize a magical cauldron; of seven shiploads of men only seven returned. The poem catalogues Annwn's fortresses — Caer Sidi, Caer Pedryfan, Caer Vedwit and others — and is one of the earliest Arthurian texts in Welsh.

Folklore

In later Welsh folklore Annwn is associated with the Cŵn Annwn, spectral white hounds with red ears whose baying foretells death, led by Gwyn ap Nudd on the Wild Hunt. Fairy folk, the Tylwyth Teg, were also said to dwell in Annwn or in a closely related otherworld.
