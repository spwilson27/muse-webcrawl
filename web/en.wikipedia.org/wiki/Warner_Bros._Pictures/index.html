Warner Bros. Pictures

Warner Bros. Pictures is an American film production and distribution company, and the flagship film studio of Warner Bros. Entertainment, a division of Warner Bros. Discovery. Founded in 1923 as part of Warner Bros. by Harry, Albert, Sam and Jack Warner, it is one of the "Big Five" major American film studios.

History

Warner Bros. rose to prominence with The Jazz Singer (1927), the first feature-length film with synchronised dialogue, and built a vast library through the studio era with stars such as Humphrey Bogart, Bette Davis and James Cagney, and franchises from Casablanca to the Looney Tunes cartoons. The studio later produced the Harry Potter series, The Lord of the Rings and The Dark Knight trilogies, and the DC Extended Universe films.

Divisions and labels

Warner Bros. Pictures releases films under its own banner and through New Line Cinema, and works closely with Warner Bros. Pictures Animation. Its distribution arm handles theatrical releases worldwide, while its Burbank lot remains one of Hollywood's major production centres.
