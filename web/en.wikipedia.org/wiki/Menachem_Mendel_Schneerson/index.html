Menachem Mendel Schneerson (18 April 1902 – 12 June 1994), known to adherents of the Chabad-Lubavitch movement as simply the Rebbe, was a Russian-born American Orthodox rabbi and the seventh and last dynastic leader (rebbe) of the Chabad Hasidic movement. He is widely considered one of the most influential Jewish leaders of the 20th century, transforming Chabad from a movement devastated by the Holocaust into a global network of emissaries and institutions.

Early life

Born in Nikolaev to Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneerson, a noted kabbalist, Schneerson studied in Yekaterinoslav and later in Berlin and Paris, where he took courses in engineering and philosophy. In 1928 he married Chaya Mushka Schneersohn, daughter of the sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe, Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn. The couple escaped Nazi-occupied France in 1941 and settled in Crown Heights, Brooklyn.

Leadership

Schneerson formally accepted leadership of Chabad in 1951, a year after his father-in-law's death. He built a worldwide network of shluchim (emissaries) who established Chabad houses on university campuses and in communities across the globe, conducted mass outreach campaigns (the Mitzvah campaigns), and addressed public talks lasting hours at farbrengens at the movement's headquarters, 770 Eastern Parkway. He corresponded with thousands of individuals and met public figures including several US presidents' emissaries; in 1978 Congress designated his birthday as Education Day, U.S.A.

Later years and legacy

In his final years, many followers proclaimed him the Messiah, a belief that intensified after his death in 1994 and remains divisive within and beyond the movement; Chabad has not appointed a successor. His resting place, the Ohel in Queens, draws hundreds of thousands of visitors annually. In 1994 he was posthumously awarded the Congressional Gold Medal.
