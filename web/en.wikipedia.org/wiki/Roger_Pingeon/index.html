Roger Pingeon (28 August 1940 – 19 March 2017) was a French professional road bicycle racer who won the 1967 Tour de France and the 1969 Vuelta a España. A tall, lean rider nicknamed "l'Échassier" ("the Stilt"), he was a Peugeot team leader in an era dominated by Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx and Felice Gimondi.

Career

Pingeon was born in Hauteville-Lompnes in the Ain department and came late to professional cycling, turning professional with Peugeot in 1965 after working as a plumber. He placed twelfth in his first Tour de France in 1965 and fifth in 1966, announcing himself as a stage-race contender.

In the 1967 Tour, raced in national teams, Pingeon took the yellow jersey after a long solo escape to Jambes and defended it through the mountains to Paris, winning by 3 minutes 40 seconds over Julio Jiménez in a Tour overshadowed by the death of Tom Simpson on Mont Ventoux. He finished second to Eddy Merckx in the 1969 Tour. At the 1969 Vuelta a España he won two stages and the overall classification, becoming one of the few riders to win both the Tour and the Vuelta. Injuries and illness blighted his later seasons; he retired in 1974.

After cycling

Pingeon later worked as a commentator on Swiss television for the Tour de France, a role he filled for many years, and lived quietly in Beaupont, where he died on 19 March 2017. His younger brother René Pingeon was also a professional cyclist and rode the Tour de France.
