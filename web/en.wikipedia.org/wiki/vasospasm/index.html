Vasospasm

Vasospasm narrows a blood vessel and reduces blood flow through it.

Vasospasm is a condition in which an arterial spasm leads to vasoconstriction, the narrowing of a blood vessel. This can lead to tissue ischemia and, if prolonged, tissue death (necrosis). Cerebral vasospasm may arise in the context of subarachnoid hemorrhage. Symptomatic vasospasm or delayed cerebral ischemia is a major contributor to post-operative stroke and death following subarachnoid haemorrhage.

Types

Cerebral vasospasm occurs after rupture of a cerebral aneurysm, typically developing between 4 and 14 days after the haemorrhage. Blood breakdown products around the arteries at the base of the brain irritate the vessel walls and provoke sustained contraction.

Coronary vasospasm (Prinzmetal's or variant angina) is a spasm of a coronary artery that can cause chest pain at rest and, in severe cases, myocardial infarction. Peripheral vasospasm underlies Raynaud phenomenon, in which cold or stress triggers reversible spasm of small arteries in the fingers and toes.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the site and cause. Cerebral vasospasm after subarachnoid haemorrhage is managed with the calcium-channel blocker nimodipine, maintenance of adequate cerebral perfusion, and in refractory cases endovascular therapy such as intra-arterial vasodilators or balloon angioplasty. Coronary spasm is treated with calcium-channel blockers and nitrates; smoking cessation is important, as tobacco is a common trigger.
