Malèna

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Malèna is a 2000 Italian erotic comedy-drama film directed by Giuseppe Tornatore, from a story by Luciano Vincenzoni. It stars Monica Bellucci as the title character and Giuseppe Sulfaro as the young narrator, with a score by Ennio Morricone.

Plot

In 1940, in the fictional Sicilian town of Castelcutò, 13-year-old Renato Amoroso becomes infatuated with Maddalena "Malèna" Scordia, a beautiful young war wife whose husband is reported killed in Africa. The town's women, envious of her beauty, spread rumours about her, while the men lust after her. Widowed and impoverished, Malèna is ostracised, driven to desperation and collaboration to survive, and finally publicly humiliated by the townspeople after the war. When her husband — alive, but maimed — returns, Renato performs his one act of courage by helping reunite them, and the couple leave the town. Renato's closing reflection is that he has loved many women since, but could never forget Malèna.

Reception

The film was nominated for Academy Awards for Best Cinematography and Best Original Score, and for the Golden Bear at Berlin. The international version released by Miramax was substantially cut relative to the Italian original. Critics were divided, praising the cinematography and Bellucci's performance while some found the portrayal of its protagonist problematic.
