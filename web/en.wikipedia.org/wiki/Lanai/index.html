Lanai

Lanai (Hawaiian: Lānaʻi) is the sixth-largest of the Hawaiian Islands and the smallest publicly accessible inhabited island in the chain. Often called the Pineapple Island, it covers about 364 square kilometres (140 square miles) and has a population of roughly 3,000, most of whom live in Lanai City. It lies within Maui County, Hawaii.

History

Lanai was first settled by Hawaiians by about AD 900. In 1922, James Dole purchased the island for his Hawaiian Pineapple Company, developing it into the world's largest pineapple plantation, which at its peak produced most of the world's pineapple supply. As production declined, the island was developed for tourism under later owners. In 2012, Larry Ellison purchased about 98% of the island.

Geography

The island's highest point is Lānaʻihale, at 1,030 metres. Notable sites include the Garden of the Gods (Keahiakawelo), a landscape of eroded rock formations; Shipwreck Beach on the north shore; and Manele Bay and Hulopoʻe Beach, a marine preserve on the south coast. There are no traffic lights on the island.

Tourism

Tourism centres on two resort hotels near Manele Bay and Lanai City, along with golf courses, off-road touring, and diving. The island is reached by ferry from Maui and by air to Lanai Airport.
