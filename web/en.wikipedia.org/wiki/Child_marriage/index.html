Child marriage

Child marriage prevalence worldwide

Child marriage is a marriage or similar union, formal or informal, between a child and an adult or another child under a certain age, typically 18. The vast majority of child marriages are between a girl and a man, and are rooted in gender inequality, poverty, and social norms. UNICEF estimates that around 12 million girls are married in childhood every year, and that some 650 million women alive today were married as children.

Causes

Drivers of child marriage include poverty (in which a daughter is seen as an economic burden, or bride price is a source of income), traditions surrounding honour and virginity, insecurity in conflict and humanitarian settings, and laws that permit marriage below 18 with parental or judicial consent.

Consequences

Child marriage is associated with early pregnancy and its health risks — complications in pregnancy and childbirth are a leading cause of death for girls aged 15–19 in low- and middle-income countries — as well as interrupted education, reduced lifetime earnings, and higher exposure to domestic violence.

Legal and policy responses

The Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women and the Convention on the Rights of the Child set 18 as the minimum marriage age, and ending child marriage by 2030 is a target of the UN Sustainable Development Goals (Goal 5). Many countries have raised the legal age, though exceptions and unregistered customary marriages remain common.
