Reductive Lie algebra

In mathematics, a reductive Lie algebra is a Lie algebra whose radical — the maximal soluble ideal — coincides with its centre. Equivalently, over a field of characteristic zero, a Lie algebra is reductive if it is the direct sum of a semisimple Lie algebra and an abelian Lie algebra: 𝔤 ≅ 𝔤ss ⊕ Z(𝔤).

Definition

A finite-dimensional Lie algebra 𝔤 over a field is called reductive if its adjoint representation is completely reducible, or equivalently if the radical of 𝔤 equals the centre of 𝔤. Reductive Lie algebras are exactly the Lie algebras of reductive algebraic groups.

Examples

  • The general linear Lie algebra 𝔤𝔩n is reductive: it is the direct sum of the semisimple 𝔰𝔩n and the one-dimensional centre of scalar matrices.
  • Any semisimple Lie algebra is reductive (with trivial centre).
  • Any abelian Lie algebra is reductive.
  • The unitary Lie algebra 𝔲n is reductive.

Properties

Every finite-dimensional representation of a reductive Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero is completely reducible, generalising Weyl's theorem for semisimple Lie algebras. Reductive Lie algebras are classified by root data: their derived subalgebras are semisimple, hence determined by Dynkin diagrams, and the centre contributes a torus of additional data.
