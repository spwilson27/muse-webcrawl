Gundobad

Gundobad (Latin: Gundobadus; c. 452 – 516) was king of the Burgundians from 473 to 516, succeeding his father Gundioc of Burgundy. He is remembered for issuing the Lex Burgundionum, a law code for his subjects, and for his role in the politics of the late Western Roman Empire and the Ostrogothic kingdom of Italy.

Life

Gundobad was the nephew of the powerful Roman general Ricimer. In 472, after Ricimer's death, Gundobad briefly held the office of magister militum in Italy and elevated the emperor Glycerius to the throne in 473, before leaving Italy to succeed his father in Burgundy. He initially shared the Burgundian kingdom with his brothers Chilperic II, Godomar, and Godegisel.

Through a series of conflicts Gundobad eliminated his brothers: Chilperic II was killed (traditionally said to have been murdered on Gundobad's orders), and Godegisel was defeated and killed at Vienne in 501 after allying with the Frankish king Clovis I. Gundobad thereby became sole king of the Burgundians. He later fought alongside and against Clovis, and maintained an alliance with the Ostrogothic king Theodoric the Great.

Legislation

Gundobad is best known for promulgating the Lex Burgundionum (also called the Loi Gombette after him), a code of laws governing relations between Burgundians and between Burgundians and Gallo-Romans, covering matters such as inheritance, wergild payments, marriage, and criminal penalties. He also issued the Lex Romana Burgundionum for his Roman subjects.

Religion and succession

Like most Burgundians of his generation, Gundobad was an adherent of Arian Christianity, though he maintained cordial relations with the Catholic bishop Avitus of Vienne and tolerated his son Sigismund's conversion to Catholicism. He died in 516 and was succeeded by Sigismund; the Burgundian kingdom was absorbed into the Frankish realm in 534, shortly after his death.
