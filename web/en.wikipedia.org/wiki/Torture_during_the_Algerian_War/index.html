Torture during the Algerian War
Period: 1954–1962
Context: Algerian War of Independence
Key figures: Jacques Massu, Paul Aussaresses, Henri Alleg, Djamila Boupacha

During the Algerian War (1954–1962), the French military made systematic use of torture against FLN fighters and Algerian civilians — a practice that became a major political scandal in France and remains a sensitive issue in Franco-Algerian memory.

Practice

During the Battle of Algiers (1956–57), General Jacques Massu's paratroopers, granted police powers, used electric shock (the gégène), water torture, beatings and rape in interrogation centres. Summary executions and disappearances — euphemised as attempted escapes — were common; Maurice Audin, a young mathematician and FLN supporter, disappeared in custody in 1957.

Exposure and debate

Henri Alleg's La Question (1958), describing his own torture, was banned in France but widely read. The case of Djamila Boupacha, defended by Gisèle Halimi with Simone de Beauvoir's support, further galvanised opinion. In 2000–2001 General Paul Aussaresses publicly admitted and defended torture and executions, prompting renewed controversy; France formally recognised Audin's death under torture in 2018. The FLN also used torture and terror against rivals and civilians. See Algerian War and Battle of Algiers.
