The Long Lost Friend

The Long Lost Friend (German: Der lang verborgene Freund) is a book of folk magic, charms, and home remedies written by Johann Georg Hohman, a German immigrant healer, and first published in Reading, Pennsylvania, in 1820. It became the most influential grimoire of the Pennsylvania German folk-healing tradition known as powwow or Braucherei.

Contents

The book collects charms for healing humans and livestock, stopping bleeding, removing warts, protecting against fire and thieves, and compelling good fortune. Many charms invoke the Trinity and include a ritual known as the "Himmelsbrief" (heaven's letter) tradition of protective texts said to shield the bearer from harm.

Influence

The book spread throughout German-American communities and later influenced Appalachian and wider American folk magic. It was famously connected to the 1928 "hex murder" case of John Blymire in York County, Pennsylvania, which led to public scrutiny of powwow practices; controversy also surrounded the book's inclusion in occult catalogues throughout the twentieth century.
