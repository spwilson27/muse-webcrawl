Refinement (computing)

Image placeholder: refinement from abstract specification to implementation.

Refinement in computing is the process of transforming an abstract description of a system — such as a formal specification or a high-level design — into a more concrete one, ultimately executable code, while preserving correctness. Each refinement step adds detail or resolves nondeterminism, and is justified by a proof or argument that the result still satisfies the original specification.

Formal refinement

In formal methods, refinement calculi were developed for notations such as Z, VDM and B, and are associated with work by Edsger Dijkstra, Ralph Back, Carroll Morgan and others on stepwise derivation of programs from specifications using weakest-precondition semantics. Data refinement replaces abstract data structures with concrete representations, linked by a retrieve relation; operation refinement replaces abstract operations with implementable algorithms.

Related senses

The term is also used for refinement typing in programming languages, where types are decorated with logical predicates, and for mesh or partition refinement in numerical computation. The general idea — successive approximation toward a concrete artefact under a correctness relation — connects all of these uses.
