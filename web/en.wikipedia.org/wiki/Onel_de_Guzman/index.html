Onel de Guzman

Onel A. de Guzman is a Filipino computer programmer associated with the creation and release of the ILOVEYOU computer worm, which spread rapidly around the world in May 2000 and caused billions of dollars in estimated damage.

The ILOVEYOU worm

On 4 May 2000, the ILOVEYOU worm began spreading from the Philippines via email. Messages carried the subject line "ILOVEYOU" and an attachment named "LOVE-LETTER-FOR-YOU.txt.vbs". When opened, the Visual Basic script emailed copies of itself to contacts in the victim's address book and overwrote files on infected machines. Within days it had infected millions of computers worldwide, disrupting government and corporate networks, including those of the Pentagon and the British Parliament.

Investigation

Philippine and international investigators traced the outbreak to Manila. De Guzman, a student at AMA Computer College, acknowledged to investigators that he might have released the program accidentally; he said he had written it based on concepts in his thesis work concerning password-stealing software, which the college had rejected. He became the focus of the investigation along with another man, Reonel Ramones, who was arrested and later released.

Legal outcome

At the time, the Philippines had no law specifically criminalizing the creation of computer malware, and charges against de Guzman under existing statutes were dropped in August 2000. The case spurred the Philippines to enact the E-Commerce Act of 2000, which introduced penalties for hacking and piracy offenses.

In a 2020 interview with a journalist, de Guzman, by then working at a mobile phone repair shop in Manila, discussed the episode and said he had not intended the worldwide scale of the damage.
