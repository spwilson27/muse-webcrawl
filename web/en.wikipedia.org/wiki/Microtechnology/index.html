Microtechnology

Microtechnology is technology whose features have dimensions of the order of one micrometre (one millionth of a metre). It deals with structures and devices at the scale of 1 to 100 micrometres, lying between everyday macro-scale engineering and nanotechnology, whose structures are below about 100 nanometres.

Microfabrication

Microtechnology grew out of the semiconductor industry's microfabrication processes: photolithography, thin-film deposition, etching, doping, and wafer bonding carried out in cleanrooms. Originally developed to make integrated circuits, these techniques were extended to build mechanical, optical, and fluidic structures in silicon and other materials.

Microelectromechanical systems (MEMS)

The best-known products of microtechnology are microelectromechanical systems (MEMS): accelerometers and gyroscopes in smartphones and cars (for airbag deployment and stability control), inkjet printer heads, digital micromirror devices (DMD) in projectors, pressure sensors, microphones, and microfluidic "lab-on-a-chip" devices for medical diagnostics and chemistry.

Applications and outlook

Microtechnology underpins modern electronics, biomedical implants and drug-delivery devices, environmental sensors, and precision optics. It merges conceptually with nanotechnology in the field sometimes called micro-nano technology (MNT), and is a foundation of the Internet of Things, where tiny low-power sensor nodes are deployed at scale.
