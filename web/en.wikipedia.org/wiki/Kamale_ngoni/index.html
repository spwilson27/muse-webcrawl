Kamale ngoni

The kamale ngoni (also spelled kamalengoni or kamalen ngoni) is a plucked lute-harp of Mali and Burkina Faso, a smaller, more recent relative of the traditional hunters' harp, the donso ngoni. Its name means "young people's ngoni".

Development

The instrument was developed in the Wassoulou region of southwestern Mali in the 1960s, generally credited to Alata Brulaye. Where the donso ngoni — the instrument of the hunters' society, used in ritual and praise music — typically has six strings, the kamale ngoni was built lighter, with more strings (commonly 8 to 14 in modern versions) tuned pentatonically, suited to dance music and popular song rather than ritual.

Construction and playing

Like its ancestor it has a body made from a large calabash covered with cowhide, a hardwood neck, and strings of nylon fishing line fastened to the soundboard. It is played with the fingers, producing fast, rippling pentatonic patterns that underpin the Wassoulou sound associated with singers such as Oumou Sangaré, and it anchors modern bands led by players like Cheick Hamala Diabaté and the late Bako Dagnon's accompanists.
