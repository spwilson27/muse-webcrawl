Energy independence

Energy independence is the state in which a nation does not need to import energy resources such as crude oil, natural gas or coal to meet its needs, or more loosely, a condition of reduced vulnerability to supply disruptions and price shocks from foreign suppliers. It is closely related to the broader concept of energy security.

Concepts

Strict self-sufficiency is rare, because modern economies trade fuels and electricity across borders and because a country may export crude oil while importing refined products. Analysts therefore often distinguish between physical independence (net-zero imports) and resilience — diversified suppliers, strategic reserves, flexible demand and domestic production capacity.

United States

In the United States the goal was popularised after the 1973 oil crisis, when President Richard Nixon announced "Project Independence". For decades imports rose, but the tight oil and shale gas revolutions of the 2000s–2010s transformed the picture: the United States became the world's largest oil and gas producer and, in 2019, a net exporter of petroleum products on an annual basis for the first time in decades.

European Union

The European Union imports the majority of its fossil fuels, and its energy policy has balanced market integration, climate goals and supply security. Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022 sharply accelerated EU efforts — under plans such as REPowerEU — to end dependence on Russian gas through diversification, renewables and efficiency.

Criticisms

Critics note that oil is priced on a global market, so even a net-exporting country remains exposed to world price swings, and that autarky can be costlier than diversified trade. Others argue that the low-carbon transition — domestic wind, solar, hydro and nuclear generation — offers a more durable route to independence from imported fuels.
