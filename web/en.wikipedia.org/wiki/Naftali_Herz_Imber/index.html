Naftali Herz Imber


Naftali Herz Imber
Born: 27 December 1856, Zolochiv, Galicia
Died: 8 October 1909, New York City, US
Occupation: Poet
Known for: Writing "Hatikvah", Israel's national anthem

Naftali Herz Imber (1856–1909) was a Jewish Hebrew-language poet and Zionist who wrote the poem "Tikvatenu" ("Our Hope"), which became the basis of "Hatikvah", the national anthem of Israel.

Life

Imber was born in Zolochiv (Złoczów) in Galicia, then part of the Austrian Empire, into a Hasidic family. He began writing Hebrew poetry as a youth and received a prize from Emperor Franz Joseph for a poem written for the centenary of Bukovina's annexation. He travelled widely, and in 1882 moved to Ottoman Palestine as secretary to Sir Laurence Oliphant, a British supporter of Jewish settlement. There he wrote for Hebrew periodicals and published his first book of poems, Barkai ("Morning Star", 1886), which included "Tikvatenu", first drafted in 1878 in Iași, Romania.

Hatikvah

"Tikvatenu" expressed the longing of the Jewish people to return to their land. Set to a melody based on a Moldavian folk tune (arranged by Samuel Cohen), its first stanza and refrain were adopted as "Hatikvah" by the Zionist movement, sung at the First Zionist Congress era gatherings, and eventually — officially in 2004 — as Israel's national anthem.

Later years

Imber later lived in England and the United States, struggling with poverty and alcoholism. He died in New York in 1909; in 1953 his remains were reinterred in Jerusalem.
