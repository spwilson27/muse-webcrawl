Data quantities span dozens of orders of magnitude, from a single bit to the zettabytes of global storage.

An order of magnitude is a factor of roughly ten. This article compares quantities of digital information, expressed in bits and bytes, across many orders of magnitude, using both decimal (SI) prefixes, where 1 kilobyte = 1,000 bytes, and binary (IEC) prefixes, where 1 kibibyte = 1,024 bytes.

Reference points

The text of a typical book is on the order of a megabyte; the complete works of Shakespeare around 5 MB uncompressed. The human genome encodes roughly 750 megabytes of information. Estimates of the information capacity of the human brain vary widely, from terabytes to petabytes. The total data created worldwide per year — the "global datasphere" — reached tens of zettabytes in the early 2020s.

Entropy and information content

Quantities are often stated in terms of Shannon entropy: an English text carries roughly 1 bit of entropy per character, so the effective information content of a file may be far smaller than its byte count.
