Bible Belt

The Bible Belt, roughly corresponding to the southeastern United States.

The Bible Belt is an informal region of the Southern and Midwestern United States in which evangelical Protestantism plays an especially strong role in society and politics. The region is characterized by high rates of church attendance, a predominance of Baptist and other evangelical denominations, and socially conservative attitudes.

Extent

Definitions vary, but the Bible Belt broadly includes states such as Texas, Oklahoma, Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia, Tennessee, Kentucky, Missouri, the Carolinas, Virginia, and West Virginia, with Nashville, Tennessee — a centre of Christian publishing and music — sometimes called its "buckle". The term was popularized by the journalist H. L. Mencken in the 1920s, who used it (perhaps pejoratively) in coverage of the Scopes "Monkey Trial" of 1925 in Dayton, Tennessee.

Characteristics

Churches, particularly Southern Baptist congregations, function as central community institutions, and the region has historically supported "blue laws" restricting Sunday commerce and alcohol sales, though these have eroded. Evangelical voters in the Bible Belt have been a key constituency in American politics, strongly influencing the Republican Party since the late twentieth century. Similar expressions exist elsewhere — the Netherlands, for example, has its own Bijbelgordel.
