Supplementary motor area syndrome

Supplementary motor area (SMA) syndrome is a neurological condition that typically occurs following surgery involving the supplementary motor area of the frontal lobe, on the medial surface of the cerebral hemisphere. It was characterised in detail by Laplane and colleagues in 1977 based on patients who underwent resection of the SMA.

Symptoms

The syndrome classically presents with a contralateral hemiparesis or even complete paralysis of the limbs on the opposite side of the body, together with mutism or speech arrest when the dominant hemisphere is affected. Deficits are often most severe immediately after surgery, with a characteristic pattern of recovery.

Prognosis

Remarkably, SMA syndrome is usually transient. Most patients recover motor function and speech over days to weeks, and near-complete recovery within a few months is typical, a fact attributed to plasticity and to the bilateral organisation of supplementary motor networks. Persistent deficits are uncommon, which is relevant when planning resections of tumours located in the region.
