PROSUB

PROSUB (Portuguese: Programa de Desenvolvimento de Submarinos, "Submarine Development Program") is a Brazilian Navy programme, launched in 2008 in partnership with France, to rebuild Brazil's submarine-building capability and to construct a fleet of modern submarines, culminating in Brazil's first nuclear-powered attack submarine. The programme is one of the largest defence-industrial undertakings in Brazilian history and includes the construction of a dedicated naval base and shipyard complex at Itaguaí, in the state of Rio de Janeiro.

Background and agreement

Brazil had built German-designed Type 209 submarines under licence in the 1980s and 1990s but had lost momentum in its submarine arm. Under the National Defence Strategy of 2008, Brazil sought not merely to buy submarines but to acquire the technology to design and build them, and to master the naval nuclear fuel cycle for propulsion — a long-standing ambition dating to the navy's parallel nuclear programme of the late 1970s. A strategic partnership signed with France in December 2008 provided for technology transfer from the French shipbuilder DCNS (now Naval Group), explicitly excluding the nuclear reactor itself, which Brazil insisted on developing indigenously.

Components

PROSUB comprises:

  • Four conventional submarines (S-BR) of the Riachuelo class, enlarged derivatives of the French Scorpène design: Riachuelo (S40), Humaitá (S41), Tonelero (S42) and Angostura (S43). The lead boat, Riachuelo, was launched in December 2018 and commissioned in September 2022; her sisters followed progressively.
  • One nuclear-powered submarine (SN-BR), named Álvaro Alberto in honour of the Brazilian nuclear pioneer admiral and scientist. The boat will use a Brazilian-designed pressurised-water reactor (RENAP) fuelled with low-enriched uranium produced domestically; a land-based prototype reactor (LABGENE) is being built at the Aramar Experimental Center in Iperó, São Paulo.
  • Infrastructure: the Itaguaí naval complex, including the UFEM metal-structure factory, two shipyards, a submarine base (Base de Submarinos da Ilha da Madeira) and a radiological complex, together employing thousands of workers at peak construction.

Strategic rationale

Brazil justifies the programme as necessary to patrol its 4.5 million km² maritime jurisdiction — the so-called "Blue Amazon" (Amazônia Azul) — including offshore oil and gas fields of the pre-salt layer, and to give the country an independent deterrent capability. A nuclear submarine offers effectively unlimited submerged endurance, transforming Brazil's ability to operate across the South Atlantic. Brazil emphasises that its programme is for propulsion only: it is a non-nuclear-weapon state, party to the Non-Proliferation Treaty and to the Treaty of Tlatelolco, and its safeguards arrangements with the IAEA over naval nuclear fuel have been the subject of long-running international discussion.

Programme issues

PROSUB has suffered from delays and cost growth, compounded by Brazil's fiscal crises of the 2010s, and the nuclear boat's completion has repeatedly slipped, with delivery expected in the 2030s. The programme was also touched by the Odebrecht (Novonor) corruption scandal, as the conglomerate was involved in the construction consortium for the Itaguaí complex. Nevertheless, it has created a domestic submarine design and construction capability unique in Latin America.
