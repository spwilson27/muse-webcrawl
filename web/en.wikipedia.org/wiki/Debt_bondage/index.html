Debt bondage

Debt bondage, also known as debt slavery or bonded labour, is a form of modern slavery in which a person pledges their labour as security for a debt, but the value of their work is never applied to repay it, trapping them indefinitely.

The United Nations defines debt bondage in the 1956 Supplementary Convention on the Abolition of Slavery. It remains prevalent in parts of South Asia, in industries such as brick kilns, agriculture and carpet weaving, often passing from parents to children.

Response

Debt bondage is prohibited under international law and by national legislation such as India's Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act 1976, though enforcement remains a challenge.
