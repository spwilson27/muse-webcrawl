Datasheet

A datasheet, data sheet, or spec sheet is a document that summarises the performance and other characteristics of a product, machine, component (e.g., an electronic component), material, subsystem (e.g., a power supply), or software in sufficient detail that it allows a design engineer to integrate the component into a system.

Contents

A typical electronics datasheet includes:

  • Manufacturer's name and part number, with ordering information.
  • Pinout diagrams and package dimensions (mechanical drawings).
  • Absolute maximum ratings — conditions beyond which the device may be damaged.
  • Recommended operating conditions and electrical characteristics, given as tables of minimum, typical, and maximum values measured under stated test conditions.
  • Timing diagrams, characteristic curves (for example, transistor output curves or sensor response), and typical application circuits.
  • Application notes, safety and handling information, and revision history.

Use

Engineers rely on datasheets as the authoritative contract between a manufacturer and its customers: designs must stay within guaranteed limits, since typical values are not guaranteed. Safety data sheets (SDS) are a related but distinct class of document describing the hazards of chemical substances. Historically printed in databooks, datasheets are now distributed almost exclusively as PDF documents on manufacturers' websites.
