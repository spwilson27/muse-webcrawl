Archie Stark (21 December 1897 – 27 May 1985) was a Scottish-American soccer player who became the dominant goalscorer of the first American Soccer League during the 1920s. Playing most famously for Bethlehem Steel, Stark holds the world record for goals scored in a single league season: 67 goals in the 1924–25 American Soccer League season, a mark surpassed only decades later and still the North American professional record.

Early life

Born in Glasgow, Stark emigrated as a boy with his family to Hudson County, New Jersey, the heart of early American soccer. He began playing with local clubs including West Hudson and the Scottish-Americans of Kearny, and served in the United States Army during the First World War, which interrupted his early career.

Bethlehem Steel

Stark joined Bethlehem Steel in 1924 and formed the spearhead of one of the era's great teams. In his first season he scored the record 67 league goals (70 in all competitions), and he remained among the league's scoring leaders throughout his tenure, winning league championships and National Challenge Cups with the club. His combination of power, positional sense, and finishing made him the most feared centre-forward in American soccer of the period.

Later career and legacy

After Bethlehem Steel folded in 1930 amid the collapse of the ASL, Stark played for the Newark Americans and finished his career with the Kearny Irish. Despite his record, he earned only two caps for the United States national team, scoring four goals in a 1925 series against Canada — club commitments and the era's economics kept him from a longer international career. He was inducted into the National Soccer Hall of Fame in 1954.
