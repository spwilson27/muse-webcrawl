Davros is a fictional character from the long-running British science fiction television programme Doctor Who. He was created by screenwriter Terry Nation, originally as an homage of the classic "mad scientist" character, and first appeared in the 1975 serial "Genesis of the Daleks". Davros is the creator of the Doctor's deadliest enemies, the Daleks, and ranks among the programme's most iconic villains.

Character

Davros is a megalomaniac scientist of the Kaled people of Skaro, scarred and crippled by (in most accounts) a Thal missile attack, who survives only within a mobile life-support chair that formed the design basis for the Dalek casings. Blind, with a withered hand and a single electronic eye set in his forehead, he speaks in an electronically distorted whisper that rises to a rant at moments of triumph. Believing the Kaleds must evolve to survive their endless war with the Thals, he deliberately bred out the mutants' pity, conscience, and remorse — and refused to accept that his creations had turned on him.

Appearances

After his debut opposite Tom Baker's Fourth Doctor — in which the Doctor, sent by the Time Lords to avert the Daleks' creation, agonises over whether he has the right to commit genocide — Davros returned in "Destiny of the Daleks", "Resurrection of the Daleks", "Revelation of the Daleks", and "Remembrance of the Daleks", increasingly as a rival power to the Dalek Emperor. In the revived series he reappeared in "The Stolen Earth"/"Journey's End" (2008), plotting to detonate a "reality bomb", and in "The Magician's Apprentice"/"The Witch's Familiar" (2015), which recalled his childhood rescue by the Doctor. In 2023 a redesigned Davros, no longer portrayed with facial scarring, appeared in a Children in Need special, reflecting a production decision to move away from conflating disability with villainy.

Reception

Davros is routinely listed among the greatest villains in television science fiction, with Michael Wisher's and Julian Bleach's performances singled out for praise, and the character's scenes debating morality with the Doctor considered high points of the series.
