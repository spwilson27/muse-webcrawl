Energy in Greece

Energy in Greece covers the production, consumption and import of energy in Greece. Historically the Greek electricity system depended on domestic lignite — a low-grade brown coal mined in Western Macedonia and at Megalopolis — together with imported oil and natural gas. Since the 2010s the country has undergone a rapid transition toward renewable energy.

Electricity

The Public Power Corporation (PPC, or DEI) was for decades the dominant generator and supplier, though the market has been progressively liberalised. In 2019 Greece announced a full phase-out of lignite generation, originally targeted for 2028, and lignite's share of electricity fell sharply as wind and solar capacity expanded.

Renewables

Greece enjoys high solar irradiation and strong Aegean winds. Photovoltaics and wind farms have grown quickly, and on some days renewable sources have covered the entirety of national electricity demand. Island grids, many formerly dependent on expensive oil-fired plants, are being interconnected to the mainland system by submarine cables.

Natural gas and oil

Greece imports essentially all of its oil and natural gas. Gas arrives by pipeline from Russia via Bulgaria (historically), from Azerbaijan through the Trans Adriatic Pipeline, and as liquefied natural gas through the Revithoussa terminal near Athens and a floating terminal at Alexandroupoli. Refining is centred on plants operated by Helleniq Energy and Motor Oil Hellas.

Policy

Energy policy is framed by European Union climate targets and Greece's National Energy and Climate Plan, which emphasise renewables, storage, interconnections and the electrification of transport and heating.
