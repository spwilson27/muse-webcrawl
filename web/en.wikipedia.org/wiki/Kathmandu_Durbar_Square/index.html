Kathmandu Durbar Square

Kathmandu Durbar Square is the plaza in front of the old royal palace (Hanuman Dhoka) of the Kathmandu Kingdom, in the heart of the Nepalese capital. One of three Durbar Squares in the Kathmandu Valley, it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and contains a dense concentration of palaces, courtyards and temples built between the 12th and 18th centuries by the Malla kings and the later Shah dynasty.

Monuments

The complex includes the Hanuman Dhoka palace complex itself; the towering pagoda-roofed Taleju Temple (1564), open to Hindus only; the Kasthamandap, a great wooden pavilion said in legend to have been built from a single tree — and which gave Kathmandu its name; the Jagannath and Kotilingeshwara temples; the Kal Bhairav shrine with its fierce stone image; and the Kumari Ghar (built 1757), residence of the Kumari, the living goddess, whose carved windows overlook the square. Shiv-Parvati temple, the "hippie temple" era Freak Street (Jhochhen) nearby, and the nine-storey Basantapur tower also form part of the ensemble.

Earthquakes

The square was badly damaged by the 1934 Bihar–Nepal earthquake and again by the April 2015 Gorkha earthquake, which toppled the Kasthamandap, Maju Dega and other temples. Extensive reconstruction with international and local support has progressively restored the fallen monuments, with the Kasthamandap rebuilt using traditional materials and techniques and reopened in the early 2020s.
