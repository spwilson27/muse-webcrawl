The Faisal–Weizmann Agreement was an agreement signed on 3 January 1919, between Emir Faisal, the third son of Hussein ibn Ali al-Hashimi, King of the short-lived Kingdom of Hejaz, and Chaim Weizmann, President of the World Zionist Organization, at the Paris Peace Conference following the First World War.

The agreement provided for Arab–Jewish cooperation in the development of a Jewish national home in Palestine and an Arab nation in the rest of the Middle East, contingent on Arab independence being achieved as Faisal understood it to have been promised in his father's correspondence with the British (the McMahon–Hussein Correspondence).

Background

Faisal and Weizmann had first met in June 1918 at Faisal's camp in Aqaba, in meetings arranged in the context of the Arab Revolt against the Ottoman Empire. T. E. Lawrence ("Lawrence of Arabia") served as interpreter and liaison. The Zionists sought Arab acquiescence to the Balfour Declaration of 1917; Faisal sought Jewish financial and diplomatic support for Arab independence.

Terms

The nine-article agreement declared that Palestine, within borders to be determined by the Peace Conference, would admit large-scale Jewish immigration and settlement, while protecting the rights of Arab tenant farmers and peasants. The two parties pledged mutual goodwill, and Faisal accepted the Balfour Declaration conditionally. In a handwritten Arabic reservation appended to the document, Faisal stipulated that his agreement depended on the Arabs receiving the independence he had demanded; if that condition were not met, he would not be bound by it.

Aftermath

The condition Faisal attached was, in his view, never fulfilled: France expelled his government from Syria in July 1920, and he later maintained the agreement was void. The document was presented at the Peace Conference by the Zionist delegation and remains a frequently cited — and disputed — episode in the history of Arab–Zionist relations.
