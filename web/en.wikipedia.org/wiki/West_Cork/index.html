West Cork


The rugged Atlantic coastline of West Cork.

West Cork (Irish: Iarthar Chorcaí) is a region in County Cork, Ireland, comprising the southwestern part of the county. It is known for its rugged peninsulas — including the Beara, Sheep's Head, and Mizen Head peninsulas — island communities such as Cape Clear and Sherkin Island, and harbour towns including Bantry, Skibbereen, Clonakilty, Kinsale, Baltimore, and Castletownbere. Mizen Head is one of the most southwesterly points of mainland Ireland.

Economy and culture

The area's economy rests on tourism, fishing, and agriculture, and it has a strong reputation for artisan food production; the region is promoted as part of Ireland's Wild Atlantic Way touring route. West Cork was severely affected by the Great Famine of the 1840s — Skibbereen became a byword for famine suffering — and the region saw significant activity during the Irish War of Independence, including the Kilmichael Ambush of 1920. In modern times it has attracted artists and in-migrants from elsewhere in Ireland and abroad, giving it a distinctive cultural character.
