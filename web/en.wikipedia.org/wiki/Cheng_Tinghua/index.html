Cheng Tinghua

Cheng Tinghua (Chinese: 程廷華; 1848–1900) was a Chinese martial artist, a senior student of Dong Haichuan and the founder of the Cheng style of baguazhang, one of the major branches of the art. He is regarded as one of the most influential figures in the early transmission of baguazhang in Beijing.

Life

Cheng was born in Shen County, Hebei, and moved to Beijing as a young man, where he was apprenticed in a spectacles shop — the source of his nickname "Spectacles Cheng" (Yanjing Cheng). Before turning to baguazhang he had become a highly accomplished practitioner of Chinese wrestling (shuai jiao), undefeated in the capital's wrestling circles by many accounts. On meeting Dong Haichuan, the founder of baguazhang, Cheng became his student and studied with him for several years, blending his wrestling experience with Dong's circle-walking palm methods.

Cheng-style baguazhang

The style Cheng developed and taught emphasises smooth, continuous "swimming" body movement, flowing palm changes and wrestling-influenced throws and grappling entries. Its characteristic palms include the "dragon claw" hand shapes, and its practice centres on the eight mother palms derived from Dong's teaching. Cheng taught publicly in Beijing, and his students — among them his sons Cheng Yousheng and Cheng Youlong, and masters such as Sun Zhijun's forebears in the lineage — spread Cheng style widely; it remains, together with Yin style, one of the two principal surviving branches of Dong Haichuan's art.

Death

During the Boxer Rebellion and the occupation of Beijing by the Eight-Nation Alliance in 1900, Cheng Tinghua resisted foreign troops and was shot and killed by German soldiers, according to the standard accounts of his lineage. His death at the hands of occupying forces made him a patriotic figure in Chinese martial-arts tradition, and his tomb in Beijing has been maintained by the baguazhang community.
