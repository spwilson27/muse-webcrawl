Motorola Solutions, Inc. is an American video equipment, telecommunications equipment, software, systems and services provider that succeeded Motorola, Inc. following the spinoff of the mobile phone division into Motorola Mobility in 2011. It is headquartered in Chicago, Illinois. Motorola Solutions is the legal successor of the original Motorola, founded in 1928 as the Galvin Manufacturing Corporation, and retains its stock-market listing and much of its public-safety heritage.

Business

The company focuses on mission-critical communications and safety technology for public safety agencies and enterprises. Its two reportable product areas cover:

  • Products and systems integration — land mobile radio (LMR) infrastructure and devices (APX and MOTOTRBO lines) to standards including P25, TETRA, and DMR; video security and access control, expanded by the acquisitions of Avigilon (2018), Pelco, Openpath and Ava; and body-worn cameras and in-car video following the acquisition of WatchGuard.
  • Software and services — command-centre and 911 software (including the acquired companies Spillman, VaaS and Rave Mobile Safety), managed and support services, and command-and-control platforms that integrate radio, video, and data for police, fire, and emergency medical services.

History after the split

After 2011 the company sold its enterprise business (including Symbol Technologies and Psion) to Zebra Technologies in 2014 for $3.45 billion, and its networks infrastructure business had earlier passed to Nokia Siemens Networks. A series of acquisitions — Airwave in the UK (2016), Avigilon (2018) — repositioned Motorola Solutions around public-safety communications and video. Long led by chairman and CEO Greg Brown, the company has been a strong performer on the S&P 500, with revenues around $10 billion and a market capitalisation among the largest of any dedicated public-safety technology firm.

Heritage

The predecessor Motorola pioneered car radios, the first walkie-talkies used by the US Army, the radio equipment that carried the first words from the surface of the Moon in 1969 ("one small step"), and the first commercial cellular phone system and handset — businesses and legacies divided between the two successor companies in 2011.
