String band

A typical old-time string band lineup.

A string band is an old-time and/or bluegrass music band made up in whole or in part of string instruments. Typically a string band has a fiddle playing the melody, a five-string banjo playing rhythmic arpeggios and melody, and a guitar (or sometimes a second fiddle, mandolin, or upright bass) supplying chordal accompaniment and bass runs. String bands were the principal ensembles of rural American dance music in the 19th and early 20th centuries, playing reels, breakdowns, waltzes, and song accompaniments at dances and social gatherings.

History

The string band grew out of the encounter between European fiddle traditions (brought by Scots-Irish and English settlers) and the banjo, an instrument of West African origin developed by enslaved Africans in the Americas. Minstrel shows of the 1840s popularized the fiddle-and-banjo combination nationally. By the late 19th century, string bands were ubiquitous in the rural South and Midwest, with regional styles in places such as Round Peak, North Carolina, and Galax, Virginia.

In the 1920s, string bands were among the first rural acts recorded commercially: Eck Robertson made the first country fiddle recordings in 1922, and groups such as Gid Tanner and the Skillet Lickers, Charlie Poole and the North Carolina Ramblers, the Carter Family (with Maybelle Carter's guitar), and Uncle Dave Macon became best-selling "hillbilly" artists. The Great Depression and the rise of radio, honky-tonk, and western swing reduced the old-time string band's commercial role, while Bill Monroe's Blue Grass Boys in the 1940s transformed the format into modern bluegrass — adding mandolin leads, Scruggs-style banjo, and high harmony singing.

Modern era

The folk revival of the 1950s–60s (e.g., the New Lost City Ramblers) revived old-time string band music, and fiddlers' conventions at Galax and Clifftop sustain a large participatory scene today. Related traditions include jug bands (which add improvised instruments), western swing string sections, and the rondalla string ensembles of the Philippines.
