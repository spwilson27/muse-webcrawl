Support and resistance

In technical analysis of financial markets, support and resistance are price levels at which a trend is expected to pause or reverse. A support level is a price level where a downtrend tends to pause as buying interest overcomes selling pressure; a resistance level is the opposite — a price level where an uptrend tends to stall as selling interest overcomes buying pressure.

A price chart oscillating between a support level below and a resistance level above

Identification

Traders identify support and resistance from previous turning points on a price chart, round numbers, trendlines, and moving averages. A level is generally considered stronger the more times the price has tested it without breaking through. When the price breaks through a level decisively, the breakout is often interpreted as signalling a continuation of the move, and a former resistance level, once broken, may act as support on a subsequent pullback — a principle known as the change of polarity.

Psychological basis

The concept is usually explained in terms of market participants' behaviour: traders who missed an opportunity at a previous low resolve to buy if the price returns there (creating support), while holders who bought at a previous high look to sell at break-even when the price returns (creating resistance). Anchoring to round numbers reinforces some levels.

Criticism

Empirical evidence for the predictive value of support and resistance is mixed, and academic finance often regards such patterns as vulnerable to data-mining and hindsight bias, although some studies find that the levels help explain the clustering of stop-loss and take-profit orders.
