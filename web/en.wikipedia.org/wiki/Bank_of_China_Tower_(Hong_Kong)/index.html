Bank of China Tower (Hong Kong)

The Bank of China Tower (often abbreviated BOC Tower) is a skyscraper in Central, Hong Kong, housing the headquarters of the Bank of China (Hong Kong). Designed by the Chinese-American architect I. M. Pei and completed in 1990, it rises 315 metres (1,033 ft) with 72 storeys, or 367 metres including its two masts. On completion it was the tallest building in Hong Kong and in Asia, and the first building outside the United States to break the 1,000-foot mark.

Design

The tower's form is a growing triangular prism: its plan begins as a square at the base and is successively reduced to a single triangle at the top, a geometry said to evoke a rising bamboo shoot, symbolising growth and prosperity. Its structural steel frame uses diagonal cross-bracing expressed on the facade, transferring loads to four massive corner columns; the design required no interior columns and used far less steel than a conventional frame. A 15th-floor sky lobby and a small observation deck on the 43rd floor are notable features.

Reception

The building quickly became one of Hong Kong's most recognisable landmarks and appears on Hong Kong banknotes issued by the Bank of China. Its sharp triangular angles drew criticism from feng shui practitioners, and the neighbouring Cheung Kong Center was reportedly designed with a boxy, shield-like form in response. The tower stands at 1 Garden Road, on part of the former Murray Barracks site.
