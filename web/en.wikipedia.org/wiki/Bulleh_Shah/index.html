Bulleh Shah

Bulleh Shah
Born: c. 1680, Uch or Pandoke, Punjab
Died: 1757, Kasur, Punjab
Occupation: Sufi poet and philosopher
Language: Punjabi
Genre: Kafi poetry

Bulleh Shah (c. 1680–1757), also known by the pen name Bulla, was a Punjabi philosopher and Sufi poet, universally regarded as "The father of Punjabi Enlightenment". Born Abdullah Shah, he belonged to a Syed family and received a traditional religious education in Kasur. He became a disciple of the Sufi saint Shah Inayat Qadiri of Lahore, whose influence shaped his poetry and his life — including a celebrated period of estrangement and reconciliation with his master, the subject of his famous kafi Tere Ishq nachaiyaan.

Poetry

Bulleh Shah wrote in the kafi form: short, intensely musical lyric poems traditionally sung by qawwali and folk singers. His verse attacks religious formalism, caste and clerical hypocrisy of all religions, and expresses a humanist, pantheistic devotion centred on the search for God within oneself. His most famous poem, Bulla Ki Jaana ("Bulla, who knows who I am?"), rejects all fixed identities of birth, religion and learning. Other widely known kafis include Mera Piya Ghar Aaya, Ni Main Jana Jogi De Naal and Ze Haal-e-Miskin.

Legacy

Bulleh Shah died in Kasur in 1757, where his shrine remains a major centre of devotion and an annual urs festival. His poetry remains immensely popular in Punjab on both sides of the India–Pakistan border: it has been sung by qawwali masters such as Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, folk singers such as Pathaney Khan and Abida Parveen, and adapted by modern rock and pop acts including Rabbi Shergill, whose song "Bulla Ki Jaana" (2005) introduced Bulleh Shah to a new generation.
