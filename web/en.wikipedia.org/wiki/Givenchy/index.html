Givenchy

Givenchy (French pronunciation: [ʒivɑ̃ʃi]) is a French luxury fashion and perfume house founded in 1952 by designer Hubert de Givenchy. The house is known for its haute couture clothing, accessories, perfumes and cosmetics, most famously the Parfums Givenchy line. Givenchy is owned by the luxury conglomerate LVMH, which acquired the house in 1988.

History

Hubert de Givenchy founded his namesake house in 1952, opening a boutique on the Rue Alfred de Vigny in Paris. His debut collection was noted for its elegant separates, including the Bettina blouse, named after the model Bettina Graziani. Givenchy quickly became a defining name in post-war Parisian couture.

The house's identity was shaped by its long association with the actress Audrey Hepburn, who wore Givenchy designs in films such as Sabrina (1954), Funny Face (1957) and Breakfast at Tiffany's (1961), for which Edith Head won the Academy Award for Best Costume Design, though Givenchy designed much of Hepburn's wardrobe uncredited. Hepburn also became the face of the house's first perfume, L'Interdit, launched in 1957.

In 1988 Givenchy was sold to LVMH. Hubert de Givenchy retired in 1995 and was succeeded by a series of creative directors, including John Galliano, Alexander McQueen, Julien Macdonald, Riccardo Tisci (2005–2017), Clare Waight Keller (2017–2020) and Matthew M. Williams (2020–2023). Tisci's tenure reoriented the house toward streetwear-inflected luxury and a darker aesthetic, while Waight Keller designed the wedding dress worn by Meghan Markle at her 2018 marriage to Prince Harry.

Parfums Givenchy

The perfume division, founded in 1957, produces fragrances including L'Interdit, Amarige, Organza, Very Irrésistible and Gentleman Givenchy, as well as a cosmetics and skincare line. Parfums Givenchy operates as a separate division within LVMH's Perfumes & Cosmetics group.
