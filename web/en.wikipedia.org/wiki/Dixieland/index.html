Dixieland

Dixieland music, sometimes called traditional jazz, hot jazz, or simply Dixie, is a style of jazz based on the music that developed in New Orleans at the start of the twentieth century. It combines brass band marches, French quadrilles, ragtime, and blues with collective polyphonic improvisation, and was one of the earliest recorded forms of jazz.

Characteristics

The classic Dixieland band has a "front line" of trumpet (or cornet), clarinet, and trombone improvising simultaneously around a melody — the trumpet stating the tune, the clarinet weaving faster lines above, and the trombone supplying rhythmic slides and counter-melodies — supported by a rhythm section of piano, banjo or guitar, tuba or bass, and drums playing a two-beat feel. Solo breaks and ensemble choruses alternate, and the repertoire draws heavily on standards such as "When the Saints Go Marching In" and "Tiger Rag".

History

The Original Dixieland Jass Band made the first jazz recordings in 1917, popularising the style nationally. New Orleans musicians such as King Oliver, Jelly Roll Morton, and the young Louis Armstrong carried the music to Chicago and New York in the 1920s. Dixieland enjoyed a major revival in the 1940s and 1950s (the "trad" boom in Britain) as a reaction against bebop, and it remains a staple of festivals and riverboat bands.
