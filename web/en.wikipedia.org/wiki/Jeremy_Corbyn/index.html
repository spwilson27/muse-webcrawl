Jeremy Corbyn

Jeremy Bernard Corbyn (born 26 May 1949) is a British politician who was Leader of the Labour Party and Leader of the Opposition from 2015 to 2020. He has been Member of Parliament for Islington North since 1983. A longtime democratic socialist and backbench campaigner, his leadership moved Labour leftward before defeat in the 2019 general election.

Early life and backbench career

Born in Chippenham and raised in Shropshire, Corbyn worked as a trade union organiser for NUPE and as a Haringey councillor before his election to Parliament. For three decades he was a prominent left-wing backbencher — campaigning against apartheid, nuclear weapons and the Iraq War, chairing the Stop the War Coalition, and rebelling frequently against Labour governments; he was among the most rebellious Labour MPs under Tony Blair and Gordon Brown.

Labour leadership

Corbyn won the 2015 leadership election as an outsider with 59.5% of first-preference votes, after £3 registered supporters swelled party membership. He survived a 2016 leadership challenge by Owen Smith. In the 2017 election Labour under Corbyn gained seats and 40% of the vote, denying Theresa May a majority, on a manifesto promising nationalisation, abolition of tuition fees and an end to austerity.

2019 defeat and after

Amid divisions over Brexit and a long-running controversy over antisemitism in the party — an EHRC report in 2020 found Labour responsible for unlawful acts — Labour fell to its worst result since 1935 in December 2019. Corbyn resigned; Keir Starmer succeeded him in April 2020 and later suspended and expelled him from the parliamentary party. Corbyn was re-elected in Islington North as an independent in 2024.
