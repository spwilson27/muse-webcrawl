Empress Jingū

Empress Jingū (Japanese: 神功皇后, Jingū-kōgō) was a legendary Japanese empress who ruled as regent following the death of her husband, Emperor Chūai, around AD 201. She was the mother of Emperor Ōjin. According to the early chronicles Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, she led an invasion of the Korean peninsula — traditionally described as a conquest of Silla — after which she returned to give birth to Ōjin.

Legend and history

The chronicles relate that Jingū, possessed by the gods, received an oracle promising dominion over a land across the sea. Chūai doubted the oracle and died; Jingū concealed his death, led the expedition herself, and delayed the birth of her son by placing a stone in her clothing until her return. Modern historians regard Jingū as a legendary figure, though possibly based on a real 4th- or 5th-century ruler or shamaness; her traditional dates were long included in the official imperial list, but she is no longer counted among the numbered emperors.

Cultural legacy

Jingū became a symbol of female martial prowess and was worshipped at shrines including Sumiyoshi Taisha. Her image appeared on early Japanese banknotes in the Meiji era, making her the first woman depicted on Japanese paper currency, and she was a prominent motif in Japanese nationalist art of the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
