Covered passages of Paris

The covered passages of Paris (French: passages couverts de Paris) are a set of elegant shopping arcades built mainly in the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries in the central districts of Paris. Roofed with glass canopies and lined with small shops, cafés and theatres, they allowed pedestrians to stroll and shop sheltered from the mud, traffic and weather of the pre-Haussmann streets. More than 150 passages were built at their height; about twenty survive today, concentrated in the 1st, 2nd, 8th and 9th arrondissements, and they are regarded as forerunners of the modern shopping mall. The philosopher Walter Benjamin made them the emblem of nineteenth-century consumer capitalism in his unfinished Arcades Project.

History

The earliest arcades grew up around the Palais-Royal in the 1780s. The Passage du Caire (1798) is the oldest surviving passage; the Passage des Panoramas (1800) was among the first places in Paris lit by gas, in 1817. A building boom followed in the 1820s–1840s, producing the Galerie Vivienne (1823, later home to the wine merchant Legrand Filles et Fils and the former premises of the revolutionary Jean-Paul Bouchardy's contemporaries), the Galerie Colbert (1826, now part of the Institut national d'histoire de l'art), the Passage Choiseul (1827, later the haunt of the writer Louis-Ferdinand Céline), and the Passage Jouffroy (1846) and Passage Verdeau (1847), today filled with antiquarian book and print dealers.

Decline and revival

From the 1850s the passages declined: Haussmann's broad boulevards offered brighter, grander shopping, and the new department stores — Le Bon Marché, Printemps, La Samaritaine — drew away fashionable trade. Many arcades were demolished or fell into shabby disuse. A revival began in the 1970s and 1980s, when collectors' shops, tea rooms and bistros (such as the celebrated Le Grand Véfour at the Palais-Royal) restored their appeal; the Galerie Vivienne was renovated and several passages were listed as historic monuments.

Notable passages

  • Passage des Panoramas (1799–1800) — philately and old postcards, historic restaurants.
  • Galerie Vivienne (1823) — mosaic floors by Giandomenico Facchina, wine bar and boutiques.
  • Passage Jouffroy (1846) — home to the Musée Grévin waxworks entrance and the Librairie du Passage.
  • Passage du Grand-Cerf (1825) — tallest glass roof of the passages, artisan and design shops.
  • Passage Brady (1828) — centre of Paris's Indian and Pakistani restaurants.
