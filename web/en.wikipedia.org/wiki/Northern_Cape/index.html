The Northern Cape is the largest and most sparsely populated province of South Africa. Created in 1994 from the northern part of the former Cape Province, together with parts of the former Bophuthatswana homeland, it covers nearly a third of the country's land area yet holds only about two percent of its population. Its capital and largest city is Kimberley, famous for its diamond mines.

Geography

Much of the province is arid or semi-arid. The Kalahari Desert occupies the north, the Namaqualand coast lies along the Atlantic in the west, and the Orange River forms the northern border with Namibia. The Great Escarpment crosses the south. The Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park, shared with Botswana, protects red sand dunes and large predators, while Augrabies Falls on the Orange River is a major natural attraction.

Economy

Mining — diamonds, iron ore, manganese and copper — dominates the economy. Kimberley's "Big Hole", an immense hand-dug diamond excavation of the 1870s, symbolises the province's mining heritage. Agriculture along the Orange River produces grapes, wine and dates, and the Namaqualand spring wildflower displays draw tourists.

Science

The province's clear, dry skies host major astronomy projects: the Southern African Large Telescope (SALT) at Sutherland and the South African core of the Square Kilometre Array radio telescope near Carnarvon.
