Central Greece (geographic region)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Central Greece (Greek: Κεντρική Ελλάδα, Kentriki Ellada) is one of the traditional geographic regions of Greece. It occupies the central part of the Greek mainland, north of the Peloponnese and south of Thessaly and Epirus. In colloquial usage the region is also called Roúmeli (Ρούμελη), a name dating from Ottoman times.

Extent

As a traditional region, Central Greece comprises the historical territories of Attica, Boeotia, Phocis, Locris, Doris, Aetolia, Acarnania, Eurytania and Phthiotis, together — in some definitions — with the island of Euboea and the Sporades. It should not be confused with the modern administrative region of Central Greece (Στερεά Ελλάδα, "Continental Greece"), which covers only part of the traditional region and excludes Attica, which forms its own administrative region, and Aetolia-Acarnania, which belongs to the region of Western Greece.

Geography

The region is dominated by the Pindus range in the west and by mountains such as Parnassus, Helicon and Parnes in the east. Between them lie the plains of Boeotia — once partly occupied by Lake Copais, drained in the 19th century — and the Attic basin, in which Athens lies. Major cities of the region include Athens, Piraeus, Thebes, Chalcis, Livadeia, Lamia and Agrinio.

History

Central Greece contained many of the most famous sites of ancient Greece: Athens, Thebes, Delphi, Thermopylae and Plataea. In the Ottoman period the name Roúmeli was applied broadly to Ottoman lands in the Balkans; after Greek independence it narrowed to the mainland part of the kingdom north of the Gulf of Corinth.
