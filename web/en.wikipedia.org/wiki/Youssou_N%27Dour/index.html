Youssou N'Dour (born 1 October 1959) is a Senegalese singer, songwriter, musician, composer, occasional actor, businessman and politician. He is widely regarded as one of the most celebrated African musicians in history and helped develop mbalax, the popular Senegalese music style that blends traditional Wolof drumming and dance with Afro-Cuban and Western popular music influences.

Career

Born in Dakar into a family with griot heritage on his mother's side, N'Dour began performing as a child and joined the Star Band, Dakar's most famous orchestra, while still a teenager. In 1979 he formed his own group, Étoile de Dakar, and later Super Étoile de Dakar, with which he refined the mbalax sound driven by the tama talking drum and sabar percussion.

N'Dour achieved international fame through collaborations with Western artists, notably Peter Gabriel, with whom he toured and recorded, and through his 1994 duet with Neneh Cherry, "7 Seconds", a worldwide hit. His albums include The Lion (1989), Set (1990), and Egypt (2004), the latter winning the Grammy Award for Best Contemporary World Music Album. He also composed music for the 1988 film soundtrack work with Ryuichi Sakamoto and others, and performed the anthem for the 1998 FIFA World Cup.

Politics and activism

N'Dour has been an outspoken advocate on social and political issues, supporting campaigns against malaria and for debt relief and African development. He attempted to run for president of Senegal in 2012 but was disqualified; he subsequently supported Macky Sall and served as Minister of Tourism and Culture (2012–2013) and later as a minister-adviser. He also founded media ventures in Senegal, including the newspaper L'Observateur and radio and television outlets.
