Nynetjer

Nynetjer (also Ninetjer) was the third pharaoh of the Second Dynasty of ancient Egypt, reigning in the twenty-eighth century BC — often dated to around 2785–2742 BC, with later king lists crediting him with a very long reign of some 40 years or more. His name means "he who is divine" (or "likeness of the gods"). His predecessor was Raneb (Nebra) and his successor Weneg, though the sequence of kings late in the dynasty is debated.

Attestations

Nynetjer is among the better-attested kings of the Second Dynasty. The Palermo Stone preserves year-by-year annals for much of his reign, recording festivals, royal progresses, the census of cattle, and the height of the Nile inundation, and it mentions events such as the "smiting" of a place called Shem-Re and the celebration of feasts of the god Sokar. His name also appears on stone vessels — some found beneath the Step Pyramid at Saqqara — seal impressions, and a fragmentary statuette base.

End of reign

The later years of Nynetjer's reign are obscure. Some Egyptologists argue that after his death (or even during his lifetime) Egypt was divided between rival rulers, a suggested background to the shadowy kings who follow — such as Weneg, Senedj, and the Horus–Seth king Peribsen — before reunification under Khasekhemwy. A large tomb at Saqqara (Tomb B) is often attributed to Nynetjer, though the attribution is not certain.
