Bear Mountain State Park is a 5,205-acre (21.06 km²) state park in Rockland and Orange Counties, New York, on the west bank of the Hudson River. Centred on Bear Mountain, one of the best-known peaks in the Hudson Highlands, the park is a popular recreation destination for the New York metropolitan area.

History

The park was assembled beginning in 1910 after the state abandoned plans to build a prison on the site. Land donations, including a major gift from Mary Averell Harriman in memory of her husband, railroad magnate E. H. Harriman, helped secure the mountain and surrounding woodland. The park officially opened in 1913.

Attractions

The park's rustic stone Bear Mountain Inn opened in 1915. Facilities include the Trailside Museums and Zoo, a large outdoor swimming pool, an ice skating rink, picnic grounds, a merry-go-round with hand-carved bears, and boat rentals on Hessian Lake. The Bear Mountain Bridge, opened in 1924, crosses the Hudson at the park. The Appalachian Trail runs through the park; its section here, built in the early 1920s, was among the first completed portions of the trail, and the park's Trailside Zoo sits at the lowest elevation point on the entire trail.
