The Embraer E-Jet family is a series of four-abreast, narrow-body, short- to medium-range, twin-engined jet airliners designed and produced by the Brazilian aerospace manufacturer Embraer. The family was formally launched at the Paris Air Show in 1999 and comprises four variants differing principally in fuselage length and seating capacity: the E170 (66–78 seats), E175 (76–88), E190 (96–114), and E195 (100–124).

Development

Unlike Embraer's earlier ERJ family of regional jets, the E-Jets were a clean-sheet design with a "double-bubble" fuselage cross-section, fly-by-wire controls, and underwing-mounted General Electric CF34 engines. The E170 first flew on 19 February 2002, and the type entered service with LOT Polish Airlines in March 2004. The stretched E190 followed in 2005 with launch customer JetBlue.

E-Jet E2

At the 2013 Paris Air Show, Embraer launched the re-engined E-Jet E2 family with new wings, Pratt & Whitney geared turbofan engines, and updated avionics, promising double-digit reductions in fuel burn. The E190-E2 entered service with Widerøe in April 2018, and the larger E195-E2 with Azul in 2019. The smallest member, the E175-E2, was put on hold because its maximum takeoff weight conflicts with U.S. regional-airline scope clauses.

Operations

E-Jets are flown by airlines on every continent, including Air Canada, KLM Cityhopper, Azul, JetBlue (historically), Republic Airways, and SkyWest. More than 1,600 E-Jets had been delivered by the mid-2020s, making the family one of the best-selling regional jet programmes in history. Military derivatives include airborne early warning conversions of the smaller ERJ airframe, and the Lineage 1000 business jet based on the E190.
