Sanctuary of Fátima

The Sanctuary of Fátima (Portuguese: Santuário de Fátima) is a group of Catholic religious buildings and structures in Cova da Iria, in the parish of Fátima, in the municipality of Ourém, Portugal. It is one of the largest and most important Marian shrines in the world, built at the site where, according to Catholic tradition, the Virgin Mary appeared to three shepherd children in 1917.

History

Between May and October 1917, Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto reported a series of apparitions of the Virgin Mary at Cova da Iria. The events drew enormous crowds, culminating in the so-called Miracle of the Sun on 13 October 1917. The Bishop of Leiria authorised the cult of Our Lady of Fátima in 1930, and construction of the sanctuary followed.

Structures

The sanctuary includes the Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary, begun in 1928 and consecrated in 1953, with a 65-metre tower; the Chapel of the Apparitions, built on the exact spot of the apparitions; and the much larger Basilica of the Holy Trinity, inaugurated in 2007 for the 90th anniversary of the apparitions. The vast prayer area between the basilicas can hold hundreds of thousands of pilgrims.

Pilgrimage

Millions of pilgrims visit annually, especially on the 12th and 13th of May and October, the anniversaries of the first and last apparitions. Popes including Paul VI, John Paul II, Benedict XVI and Francis have visited the shrine.
