Nerve block

A nerve block or regional nerve blockade is the deliberate interruption of signals travelling along a nerve, usually achieved by injecting local anaesthetic near the nerve. It is used to provide anaesthesia for surgery, to relieve acute postoperative pain, and in some cases to treat chronic pain.

Techniques

Common peripheral nerve blocks include the interscalene, supraclavicular, infraclavicular and axillary blocks of the brachial plexus; the femoral and sciatic blocks of the lower limb; and paravertebral and transversus abdominis plane (TAP) blocks of the trunk. Blocks may be performed as a single injection or via a catheter for continuous infusion. Ultrasound guidance and nerve stimulation are used to localize nerves accurately.

Risks

Complications are uncommon but include local anaesthetic systemic toxicity, nerve injury, bleeding, infection and block failure. Permanent neurolytic blocks use agents such as alcohol or phenol, or radiofrequency ablation, for long-term pain relief in selected patients.
