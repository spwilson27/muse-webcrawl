Coq is an interactive theorem prover first released in 1984. It allows the expression of mathematical assertions, mechanically checks proofs of those assertions, helps find formal proofs, and extracts a certified program from a constructive proof of its specification. Coq is developed by the French national research institute Inria. In 2025 the system was renamed Rocq, with "Coq" retained as a nickname.

Overview

Coq is based on the calculus of inductive constructions, a dependent type theory extending the calculus of constructions. Under the Curry–Howard correspondence, propositions are types and proofs are programs, so checking a proof reduces to type checking. Users interact with Coq through a tactic language (Ltac), building proofs step by step while a small trusted kernel verifies each completed proof term.

Notable formalisations

Coq has been used to formalise major results and systems, including:

  • The four color theorem, fully formalised by Georges Gonthier and Benjamin Werner (completed 2005).
  • The Feit–Thompson odd order theorem, formalised by Gonthier's team in 2012.
  • CompCert, a formally verified optimising C compiler developed by Xavier Leroy and collaborators.
  • Contributions to the homotopy type theory programme and the UniMath library.

Coq received the ACM Software System Award in 2013. Its name plays on the French word coq (rooster) and the calculus of constructions (CoC), and honours Thierry Coquand.
