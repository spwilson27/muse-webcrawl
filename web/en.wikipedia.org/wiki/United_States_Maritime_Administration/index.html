United States Maritime Administration

The United States Maritime Administration (MARAD) is an agency of the US Department of Transportation responsible for the nation's merchant marine and maritime transport system. Its mission is to foster, promote and develop the maritime industry of the United States to meet the nation's economic and security needs. MARAD is led by the Maritime Administrator and is headquartered in Washington, D.C.

History

MARAD was established on 24 May 1950, when the US Maritime Commission was abolished and its functions divided between the Federal Maritime Board and the new Maritime Administration within the Department of Commerce. In 1981 it was transferred to the Department of Transportation. The agency inherited programmes dating to the Merchant Marine Act of 1936, which established the policy of maintaining a US-flag merchant fleet manned by American crews.

Programmes

MARAD maintains the National Defense Reserve Fleet (NDRF), anchored at sites including Suisun Bay (California), Beaumont (Texas) and the James River (Virginia), and its Ready Reserve Force of fast-activation cargo ships that support military sealift. It administers the Maritime Security Program and the Jones Act's domestic trade regime alongside other agencies, provides war-risk insurance and ship-disposal programmes, and supports port infrastructure through grants such as the Port Infrastructure Development Program.

Maritime education

The agency operates the United States Merchant Marine Academy at Kings Point, New York — one of the five federal service academies — and provides training ships and support to six state maritime academies. It also administers the iconic training ship programme and the disposal of obsolete NDRF vessels, many of which are recycled at facilities in Texas.
