The Volkswagen Golf is a compact car/small family car (C-segment) produced by the German automotive manufacturer Volkswagen since 1974. Marketed worldwide across eight generations, it has been sold in various body configurations and under various nameplates, including the Volkswagen Rabbit in the United States and Canada (Mk1 and Mk5) and the Volkswagen Caribe in Mexico (Mk1).

History

The Golf was designed as the modern front-wheel-drive successor to the Beetle; the original Mk1 was styled by Giorgetto Giugiaro at Italdesign. The performance GTI variant, introduced in 1976, effectively created the "hot hatch" segment.

Generations

Successive generations (Mk1–Mk8) grew in size and sophistication. The Mk7, launched in 2012 on the MQB platform, won European Car of the Year 2013; the Mk8 followed in 2019 with digitalised controls and mild-hybrid engines. Electric variants included the e-Golf before Volkswagen's dedicated ID. series arrived.

Significance

With more than 37 million built, the Golf is Volkswagen's best-selling model and one of the best-selling cars of all time; the C-segment it defines is still often called the "Golf class" in Europe.
