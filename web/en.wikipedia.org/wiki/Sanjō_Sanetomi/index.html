Sanjō Sanetomi

Prince Sanjō Sanetomi (三条 実美, 13 March 1837 – 18 February 1891) was a Japanese Imperial court noble and statesman at the time of the Meiji Restoration. He served as the last Daijō-daijin (Chancellor of the Realm) in Japanese history and held high office in the early Meiji government.

Early life

Sanjō was born in Kyoto, the son of Naidaijin Sanjō Sanetsumu. He held various positions at the Imperial Court, including Chamberlain and Provisional Middle Counselor (gon-chūnagon). At the time of the arrival of the Perry Expedition, Sanjō opposed the Tokugawa shogunate's handling of foreign affairs and associated with the sonnō jōi ("revere the Emperor, expel the barbarians") movement, becoming a leading figure among radical court nobles.

Meiji Restoration

After the coup d'état of September 30, 1863, Sanjō was forced to flee to Chōshū Domain, returning after the Meiji Restoration. In the new Meiji government he served successively as Gijō (senior administrator), Administrative Director (gyōsei kan), Minister of the Right (udaijin, 1869), and Chancellor of the Realm (daijō-daijin, from 1871 to 1885), the last man to hold that ancient title.

Later career

After the cabinet system was established in 1885, Sanjō became Lord Keeper of the Privy Seal of Japan (naidaijin). In 1889, during Prime Minister Kuroda Kiyotaka's illness and resignation, Sanjō briefly served as acting Prime Minister and concurrently as Minister of Home Affairs. He was raised to the rank of prince (kōshaku) under the kazoku peerage system in 1884.
