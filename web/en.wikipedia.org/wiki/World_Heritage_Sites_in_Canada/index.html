World Heritage Sites in Canada

Total sites22 (as of 2024)
First inscribed1978 — L'Anse aux Meadows; Nahanni National Park
TypesCultural, natural, and one mixed site
Convention ratifiedJuly 23, 1976

Canada has 22 UNESCO World Heritage Sites, inscribed under the 1972 World Heritage Convention, which Canada accepted in 1976. The sites include ancient Indigenous settlements, fossil beds, national parks, historic towns and an early industrial landscape, spread across nine provinces and two territories.

List of sites

  • L'Anse aux Meadows (1978) — Norse settlement in Newfoundland, the earliest known European presence in the Americas.
  • Nahanni National Park (1978) — the South Nahanni River canyons of the Northwest Territories.
  • Dinosaur Provincial Park (1979), Head-Smashed-In Buffalo Jump (1981) and Wood Buffalo National Park (1983) in Alberta.
  • Kluane / Wrangell–St. Elias / Glacier Bay / Tatlatamu (1979–1994) — transboundary with the United States.
  • Anthony Island (SGang Gwaay) (1981) — Haida village site in British Columbia.
  • Historic District of Old Québec (1985), Old Town Lunenburg (1995) and Landscape of Grand Pré (2012) in Atlantic Canada.
  • Canadian Rocky Mountain Parks (1984), Waterton Glacier International Peace Park (1995), Gros Morne National Park (1987), Joggins Fossil Cliffs (2008) and Miguasha National Park (1999).
  • Rideau Canal (2007) in Ontario; Red Bay Basque Whaling Station (2013), Mistaken Point (2016) and Pimachiowin Aki (2018) — the latter a mixed cultural-natural site.
  • Writing-on-Stone / Áísínai'pi (2019) in Alberta and Anticosti Island (2023) in Quebec; Tr'ondëk-Klondike (2023) in Yukon.

Tentative list

Canada maintains a tentative list of candidate sites, revised periodically; entries have included the Klondike gold fields (inscribed in 2023), Hecate Strait glass sponge reefs, and sites associated with the Inuit Nunangat.
