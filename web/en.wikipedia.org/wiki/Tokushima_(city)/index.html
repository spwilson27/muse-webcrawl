Tokushima (city)

Tokushima (徳島市)
Region: Shikoku
Prefecture: Tokushima
Population: approx. 250,000

Tokushima (Japanese: 徳島市, Tokushima-shi) is the capital city of Tokushima Prefecture on the island of Shikoku in Japan. The city lies at the mouth of the Yoshino River, which flows into the Kii Channel.

History

Tokushima developed as a castle town of the Hachisuka clan, who ruled the Tokushima Domain during the Edo period. The local indigo (ai) trade made the region wealthy, and indigo merchants helped patronize the arts. The modern city was founded in 1889.

Culture

Tokushima is best known for the Awa Odori, a dance festival held each August during the Obon season, which draws more than a million visitors. Dancers in yukata and straw hats parade through the streets to the refrain that "the dancing fool and the watching fool are both fools, so why not dance?" The city also hosts the Awa Jurobe Yashiki puppet theatre, preserving the region's ningyō jōruri puppet tradition, part of the Bunraku heritage.

Geography

Mount Bizan rises near the city centre and offers views over the plain and the Naruto Strait. The city has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers and mild winters.
