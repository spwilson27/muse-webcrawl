Queens of the Stone Age

Queens of the Stone Age (commonly abbreviated as QOTSA) is an American rock band formed in Seattle, Washington, in 1996. The band was founded by vocalist and guitarist Josh Homme, its only continuous member, following the breakup of his previous band Kyuss. The band's music blends hard rock, alternative rock, and stoner rock, characterized by repetitive, hypnotic riffs that Homme has termed "robot rock".

History

Homme formed the band initially under the name Gamma Ray before adopting Queens of the Stone Age. Their self-titled debut album appeared in 1998. Rated R (2000) brought wider attention, and Songs for the Deaf (2002), featuring Dave Grohl on drums and Mark Lanegan on vocals, became their breakthrough, producing the singles "No One Knows" and "Go with the Flow". Subsequent albums include Lullabies to Paralyze (2005), Era Vulgaris (2007), ...Like Clockwork (2013), Villains (2017), and In Times New Roman... (2023).

Musical style

The band's sound is rooted in the Palm Desert Scene of Southern California's desert rock community. Their songs frequently use unusual time signatures, trance-like repetition, falsetto backing vocals, and blues-derived scales played through heavily amplified guitars. The band's rotating lineup has included Nick Oliveri, Troy Van Leeuwen, Joey Castillo, Dean Fertita, and Michael Shuman, among many collaborators from across the rock world.
