Theophanes the Greek

Russian: Феофан Грек (Feofan Grek)

Born: c. 1340, Constantinople

Died: c. 1410

Field: Icon and fresco painting

Notable works: Frescoes in Novgorod and Moscow; Deesis of the Annunciation Cathedral, Moscow Kremlin

Theophanes the Greek (Russian: Феофан Грек, Feofan Grek; c. 1340 – c. 1410) was a Byzantine Greek artist and one of the greatest icon painters of medieval Russia. Trained in Constantinople, he worked in Novgorod and Moscow, where his expressive, sketch-like fresco style — executed with bold white highlights on deep ochre and brown tones — transformed Russian painting and profoundly influenced his younger contemporary Andrei Rublev.

Career

Theophanes is first documented in Novgorod in 1378, painting the frescoes of the Church of the Transfiguration on Ilyina Street, the only surviving work that can be attributed to him with certainty; the fervent, ascetic figures he painted there include the celebrated image of the Holy Trinity. According to the letter of Epiphanius the Wise (c. 1415), our principal biographical source, Theophanes painted more than forty churches in Constantinople, Galata, Chalcedon, Kaffa, Novgorod, Nizhny Novgorod and Moscow.

In Moscow in 1405 he worked with Andrei Rublev and Prokhor of Gorodets on the iconostasis and frescoes of the Cathedral of the Annunciation in the Kremlin; the majestic Deesis tier of that iconostasis, with its monumental figures of Christ, the Virgin and the saints, is attributed to his hand or workshop.

Style and legacy

Epiphanius described Theophanes as a learned philosopher who painted from inner vision without constantly consulting models. His fluid, almost monochromatic manner, charged with spiritual intensity, is considered the summit of late Byzantine monumental painting on Russian soil.
