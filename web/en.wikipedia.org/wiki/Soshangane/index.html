Soshangane

Soshangane kaZikode (c. 1780 – 1858), born Soshangane Nxumalo, was the founder and first king of the Gaza Empire in what is now southern Mozambique and southeastern Zimbabwe. A general of the Ndwandwe under Zwide, he broke away during the upheavals of the Mfecane and marched north with his followers, conquering the Tsonga-speaking peoples between the Limpopo and Zambezi rivers and founding one of the largest Nguni states of the 19th century. He is also remembered as the founder of the Shangaan (Tsonga-Shangaan) people.

Origins and exodus

Soshangane served as a senior commander of Zwide kaLanga, king of the Ndwandwe. After Zwide's defeats by Shaka Zulu's forces (notably the Battle of Gqokli Hill in 1818 and the Mhlatuze River in 1819/20), Soshangane led his followers northward rather than submit to Shaka. Crossing the Limpopo, his army subdued or allied with local chiefdoms.

The Gaza Empire

Settling in the fertile country near the middle Limpopo and later around the Busi River, Soshangane built a centralised kingdom named Gaza after his grandfather. He organised conquered youth into age-regiments (mangqocho) on the Zulu model, imposed the Nguni language and customs on subject peoples, and exacted tribute in ivory, cattle and slaves traded through Lourenço Marques (Maputo). At its height his empire stretched from the Limpopo north towards the Zambezi and from the Indian Ocean to the eastern Transvaal.

Death and legacy

Soshangane died in 1858. His death triggered a civil war between his sons Mawewe and Mzila; Mzila eventually triumphed with Portuguese support. The empire survived under Mzila and Gungunyana until its final defeat by Portugal in 1895. The cultural fusion Soshangane imposed endures in the identity of the Shangaan people of Mozambique, South Africa and Zimbabwe.
