Oberon

Oberon is a name appearing in several contexts. In literature, Oberon is the king of the fairies in medieval and Renaissance works, most famously William Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream, where he quarrels with his queen, Titania, and engineers the play's love intrigues with a magic flower. The character derives from Alberich, a sorcerer figure in Germanic legend, via medieval French romance.

Other uses

Oberon is also the outermost major moon of Uranus, discovered by William Herschel in 1787 and named after Shakespeare's fairy king; it is heavily cratered and about 1,522 kilometres in diameter. In computing, Oberon is a programming language and operating system designed by Niklaus Wirth, descended from Modula-2 and used in the Oberon System at ETH Zurich. Oberon is also the name of towns in Australia and North Dakota, a beer brewed by Bell's Brewery, and Carl Maria von Weber's 1826 opera Oberon, or The Elf King's Oath.
