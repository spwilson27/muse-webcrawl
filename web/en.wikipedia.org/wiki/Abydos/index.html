Abydos

Abydos (Arabic: أبيدوس; Egyptian: Abdju; Coptic: Ⲉⲃⲱⲧ) is one of the oldest and most important archaeological sites of ancient Egypt, located in the Sohag Governorate about 11 kilometres west of the Nile and some 170 kilometres south of ancient Thebes. Sacred to the god Osiris, it was a royal necropolis of the earliest dynasties and a pilgrimage centre for over two thousand years.

History

Burials at Abydos begin in the Predynastic period. At Umm el-Qa'ab, at the desert edge, stand the tombs of the kings of the First and Second Dynasties (c. 3100–2686 BCE) — including the tomb of Narmer's predecessors and of Djer, which later Egyptians re-identified as the tomb of Osiris himself. The much-debated "Abydos boats," a fleet of full-sized wooden vessels buried around 3000 BCE beside a funerary enclosure, are among the oldest planked boats known anywhere. The site also preserves the enclosure of Khasekhemwy (the Shunet el-Zebib).

The Osiris cult

From the Middle Kingdom onward, Abydos became the pre-eminent cult centre of Osiris, god of the afterlife. Egyptians made pilgrimages to take part in the annual festival and "mysteries" of Osiris, erected commemorative stelae at the site by the thousand (the so-called Terrace of the Great God), and sought burial near the god. Kings built cenotaphs here, most famously the subterranean Osireion behind the temple of Seti I.

Temple of Seti I

The mortuary temple of Seti I (c. 1279 BCE) is celebrated for the finest relief sculpture of the New Kingdom and for the Abydos King List carved in its gallery, naming 76 kings from Menes to Seti I — a crucial source for Egyptian chronology, though it pointedly omits rulers deemed illegitimate, including the Amarna pharaohs and Hatshepsut. Adjacent stands the smaller temple of Ramesses II. The site's modern fame includes the "Abydos helicopter" hieroglyphs, a palimpsest carving that pareidolia makes resemble modern machines.
