Solaris is a proprietary Unix operating system originally developed by Sun Microsystems. It superseded SunOS in 1992, combining the System V Release 4-based operating system with the OpenWindows environment. After Oracle's acquisition of Sun in 2010, it became Oracle Solaris. Solaris was long noted for its scalability and for pioneering features on high-end servers.

History

SunOS 1.0 (1982) was BSD-based; in the late 1980s Sun joined AT&T in developing UNIX System V Release 4, and the resulting system was marketed as Solaris 2.x from 1992, with the older BSD-based SunOS 4.1.x retroactively called Solaris 1. Solaris ran first on Sun's SPARC processors and later also on x86. Solaris 10 (2005) was a major release introducing the ZFS filesystem, Solaris Zones (containers), DTrace observability, and the Service Management Facility. In 2005 Sun released most of the code as open source under the CDDL as OpenSolaris, a project Oracle discontinued in 2010; the community fork continues as illumos and distributions such as OpenIndiana.

Features

Solaris innovations and strengths include ZFS (widely regarded as one of the most advanced filesystems), DTrace dynamic tracing, lightweight container virtualisation (Zones), predictive self-healing, and support for very large multiprocessor systems. It historically powered telecommunications, financial, and database workloads, though Linux has largely displaced it since the 2010s; Oracle has extended Solaris 11.4 support into the 2030s while laying off much of the development team in 2017.
