The Empire of Thessalonica was a short-lived Byzantine successor state that existed from 1224 to 1246, centred on the city of Thessalonica (Thessaloniki) in northern Greece. It arose from the Despotate of Epirus, whose ruler Theodore Komnenos Doukas captured Thessalonica — the Byzantine Empire's second city — from the Latin Kingdom of Thessalonica in 1224 and had himself crowned emperor, directly challenging the rival imperial claim of the Empire of Nicaea.

Expansion under Theodore

Theodore rapidly expanded his realm across Macedonia and Thrace, taking Adrianople in 1225 and advancing to the walls of Constantinople itself. For a few years Thessalonica seemed the most likely of the Greek states to expel the Latins. The bid collapsed in 1230 at the Battle of Klokotnitsa, where the Bulgarian tsar Ivan Asen II crushed Theodore's army and captured Theodore himself, who was later blinded for plotting against his captor.

Decline

Theodore's brother Manuel ruled the shrunken empire as a Bulgarian client until 1237, when the released Theodore returned and installed his son John as emperor (with Theodore as the power behind the throne), reducing the state to Thessalonica and its environs. John III Vatatzes of Nicaea besieged the city in 1242 and forced John to renounce the imperial title, accepting the rank of despot instead. When John's brother Demetrios succeeded in 1244 and misgoverned, the leading citizens handed Thessalonica over to Vatatzes in 1246, ending the empire; the city and its territory were annexed to Nicaea.
