Hayam Wuruk

Hayam Wuruk (1334–1389), also called Rajasanagara, Pa-ta-na-pa-na-wu or Bhatara Prabhu, was a Javanese Hindu emperor from the Rajasa dynasty and the fourth monarch of the Majapahit Empire. Together with his prime minister Gajah Mada, he reigned over the empire at the time of its greatest power.

Early life

He was the son of Tribhuwana Wijayatunggadewi, third monarch of Majapahit, and Sri Kertawardhana. The Nagarakretagama, written during his reign by Mpu Prapanca, praises him as a handsome, bright and talented ruler accomplished in arts and warfare.

Reign

He ascended the throne in 1350 at age 16. Under Gajah Mada, Majapahit expanded across much of present-day Indonesia and Malaysia. The Bubat incident (1357), in which a Sundanese royal party was killed, marred relations with Sunda. After Gajah Mada's death in 1364, Hayam Wuruk struggled to find a comparable minister and the empire gradually began to decline after his death.

Legacy

He is remembered in Indonesia as a national figure; Gadjah Mada University and streets across the country bear names associated with his era.
