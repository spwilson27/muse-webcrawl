Energy transformation

Governed byLaws of thermodynamics

Energy transformation, also known as energy conversion, is the process of changing energy from one form to another. Energy exists in many forms — kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, electrical, electromagnetic, and nuclear — and nearly all technologies and natural processes involve transforming energy between them.

Examples

A steam power plant converts chemical energy in fuel to thermal energy, then to kinetic energy in a turbine, and finally to electrical energy in a generator. An electric motor performs the reverse final step. In photosynthesis, plants transform radiant energy from sunlight into chemical energy stored in sugars.

Efficiency and thermodynamics

The first law of thermodynamics states that energy is conserved in any transformation. The second law limits conversion efficiency: some energy is always dispersed as waste heat, increasing entropy. Conversion efficiencies range from under 40% for typical thermal power stations to over 90% for electric motors and generators.
