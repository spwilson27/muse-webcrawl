Summer pasture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A summer pasture is an area of grazing land used by livestock only during the summer season, forming part of the practice of transhumance, in which herds are moved between lowland winter quarters and higher or more distant summer grazing. Summer pastures are typically located where grass growth is available only in the warm months — above all in mountain regions, where alpine meadows lie under snow in winter.

Alpine summer pastures

In the Alps, the summer pasture is known as the Alp or Alm in German, alpage in French. Herds of cattle are driven up in late spring in the ceremonial Alpaufzug, milked on the mountain through the summer, and the milk is processed on site into cheese and butter; herdsmen and dairymaids live in huts on the alp for the season. The Alpabzug (descent) in autumn, often decorated and festive, returns the animals to the valley farms. Analogous institutions include the Norwegian and Swedish seter/fäbod, mountain summer farms, the yaylak of Anatolia and Central Asia, and high pastures in the Pyrenees and Carpathians.

Ecology and landscape

Centuries of seasonal grazing have shaped species-rich grassland ecosystems and open cultural landscapes that depend on continued management; where summer pasturing is abandoned, scrub and forest typically encroach. Alpine summer pastures also provide habitats for wildflowers, insects, and birds, and today often combine agriculture with tourism, with hikers visiting serviced huts and farms.

Other systems

Beyond the mountains, summer pastures occur wherever seasonal movement solves forage scarcity — in Scotland, the shielings (àirighean) were summer grazings with temporary dwellings; in Iceland, common highland pastures (afréttur) still receive sheep for the summer, gathered in the autumn réttir.
