Education in Germany

Education in Germany is primarily the responsibility of the individual federal states (Länder), with the federal government playing a minor role; standards are coordinated through the Standing Conference of the Ministers of Education (KMK). Schooling is compulsory from age six for nine or ten years. The system is noted for its early tracking of pupils into different secondary school types and for its "dual system" of vocational training, which combines classroom instruction with apprenticeships in companies.

Structure

Early childhood and primary school

Children may attend kindergarten (ages 3–6), which is not part of the state school system and is not compulsory. Primary school (Grundschule) covers grades 1–4 (grades 1–6 in Berlin and Brandenburg).

Secondary education

After primary school, pupils are separated — traditionally at about age ten — into different school types according to achievement and teacher recommendation:

  • Gymnasium — the academic track, leading to the Abitur after grade 12 or 13, which qualifies students for university.
  • Realschule — an intermediate track (to grade 10) leading to a middle school-leaving certificate (mittlere Reife).
  • Hauptschule — a general track (to grade 9 or 10) oriented toward vocational training.
  • Gesamtschule — comprehensive schools combining the tracks, common in some states; many states have merged the Haupt- and Realschule into a single secondary school type.

Vocational education

The dual system (duale Ausbildung) trains apprentices in roughly 300 recognised occupations: trainees work part-time in a firm and attend a vocational school (Berufsschule) for the remainder, usually for two to three and a half years. It is widely credited with Germany's low youth unemployment.

Higher education

Public universities generally charge no or low tuition fees, including for many international students. Institutions include research universities and universities of applied sciences (Fachhochschulen). Admission to university generally requires the Abitur or an equivalent qualification.

History and issues

The German education tradition descends from the Prussian reforms of the early 19th century and Wilhelm von Humboldt's ideal of Bildung. The mediocre performance of German students in the first PISA study of 2000 (the "PISA shock") prompted reforms including national educational standards, all-day schooling and earlier language support. Ongoing debates concern the fairness of early tracking, the integration of immigrant children, teacher shortages and digitalisation.
