Wim Wenders

Ernst Wilhelm "Wim" Wenders (born 14 August 1945) is a German filmmaker, playwright, author, and photographer. He is one of the most prominent figures of the New German Cinema, a movement of the 1970s that also included Rainer Werner Fassbinder and Werner Herzog. His films frequently explore themes of movement, landscape, memory, and the influence of American culture on postwar Germany.

Career

Wenders studied at the University of Television and Film Munich. His early road movies include Alice in the Cities (1974), Kings of the Road (1976), and The American Friend (1977), an adaptation of Patricia Highsmith's novel Ripley's Game. In 1984 his film Paris, Texas, written by Sam Shepard, won the Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival. His 1987 film Wings of Desire (Der Himmel über Berlin), set in divided Berlin and partly narrated from the perspective of angels, won Best Director at Cannes and became one of his most celebrated works.

Documentaries

Wenders has also directed acclaimed documentaries, including Buena Vista Social Club (1999), about Cuban musicians; Pina (2011), a 3D tribute to choreographer Pina Bausch; and The Salt of the Earth (2014), about photographer Sebastião Salgado. His 2023 feature Perfect Days, set in Tokyo, earned Koji Yakusho the Best Actor award at Cannes.

Photography

A serious photographer, Wenders is known for large-format images of landscapes and empty places, exhibited internationally and collected in books such as Written in the West.
