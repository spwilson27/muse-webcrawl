Wonder Woman is a superheroine created by American psychologist and writer William Moulton Marston and artist Harry G. Peter for DC Comics. She first appeared in All Star Comics #8 in December 1941 and is one of DC's "Trinity" alongside Superman and Batman.

Character

Diana is princess of the Amazons of Themyscira (Paradise Island), in many versions sculpted from clay by Queen Hippolyta and given life and powers by the Greek gods. Travelling to "Man's World" as ambassador, she wields the Lasso of Truth (inspired by Marston's work on the polygraph), indestructible bracelets, a tiara and sometimes an invisible jet, fighting for justice, love and peace. Marston intended her as a feminist role model combining strength with compassion.

In other media

Lynda Carter starred in the 1970s television series; Gal Gadot portrayed Diana in DC films beginning with Batman v Superman (2016) and the acclaimed Wonder Woman (2017), directed by Patty Jenkins. Wonder Woman is a United Nations honorary ambassador (briefly, 2016) and an enduring feminist icon. Her medium owes much to pioneers such as Will Eisner, and she has been parodied on Saturday Night Live.
