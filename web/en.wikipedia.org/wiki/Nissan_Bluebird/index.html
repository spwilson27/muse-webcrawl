Nissan Bluebird

The Nissan Bluebird is a compact-to-mid-size car produced by Nissan from 1957 to 2001, one of the company's longest-running nameplates and its key export model in establishing the Datsun brand abroad. Generations ran from the original 210-series through the rear-drive 910 to the front-drive U-series.

History

Early Bluebirds were small sedans and wagons popular as Japanese taxis. The 510 Bluebird (1967–72) — styled after European sport sedans with independent rear suspension — became a cult car in the US, where it was sold as the Datsun 510. Later generations included the turbocharged Bluebird SSS and the UK-built U11/T12 models; in Britain the Bluebird was the first Japanese car built there, at Nissan's Sunderland plant from 1986.

Successors

The Japanese line ended with the U14 in 2001, its role taken by the Primera and later the Sylphy, which reused the Bluebird name in some markets as the Bluebird Sylphy.
