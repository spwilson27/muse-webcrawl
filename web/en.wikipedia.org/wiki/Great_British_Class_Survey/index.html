Great British Class Survey

The Great British Class Survey (GBCS) was a survey of social class in the United Kingdom conducted in 2011 by academics including Mike Savage of the London School of Economics, in collaboration with the BBC. With around 325,000 respondents, it was promoted as the largest survey of social class ever conducted in Britain.

Method and findings

Drawing on the theories of Pierre Bourdieu, the study measured three forms of capital: economic capital (income and savings), social capital (the number and status of people's social contacts), and cultural capital (cultural interests and activities). Using latent class analysis of a smaller representative survey, the researchers proposed a new model of seven social classes, replacing the traditional upper/middle/working-class model:

  • Elite — the wealthiest and most privileged group (about 6% of the population)
  • Established middle class (about 25%)
  • Technical middle class (about 6%)
  • New affluent workers (about 15%)
  • Traditional working class (about 14%)
  • Emergent service sector (about 19%)
  • Precariat — the poorest group, with low levels of all three capitals (about 15%)

The results, published in 2013 in the journal Sociology, received wide media coverage. Critics noted that the large web survey was self-selecting and skewed towards middle-class respondents, though the class model itself was derived from a nationally representative sample.
