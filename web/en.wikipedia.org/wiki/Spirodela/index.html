Spirodela

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Araceae
Subfamily: Lemnoideae
Genus: Spirodela Schleid.

Spirodela is a genus of free-floating aquatic plants commonly called duckweeds, in the family Araceae. Species of Spirodela are among the largest of the duckweeds, with fronds that bear multiple roots hanging into the water — a feature distinguishing the genus from the rootless Wolffia and the single-rooted Lemna.

Description

The plant body is a flattened, oval frond between roughly 5 and 13 millimetres long. The upper surface is green, sometimes with red pigmentation caused by anthocyanins, and the underside may be reddish. Each frond produces several roots (up to about twenty in Spirodela polyrhiza) that dangle into the water. Reproduction is mainly vegetative, by budding of daughter fronds from pockets at the base of the parent frond.

Species

Recognised species include Spirodela polyrhiza (greater duckweed), which is distributed almost worldwide in quiet freshwater, and Spirodela punctata. The genus has been the subject of taxonomic revision, with some former members reassigned to the genus Landoltia.

Uses

Because duckweeds grow rapidly and accumulate protein and minerals, Spirodela has been studied for use in wastewater treatment, phytoremediation, animal feed and as a model organism for plant physiology and genomics. The genome of S. polyrhiza has been sequenced.
