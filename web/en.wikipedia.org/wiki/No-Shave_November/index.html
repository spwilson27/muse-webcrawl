No-Shave November

No-Shave November is an annual event and non-profit organisation in which participants refrain from shaving and grooming for the month of November in order to raise awareness of cancer and to donate the money they would otherwise spend on hair maintenance to cancer charities. Participants are encouraged to "embrace their hair, which many cancer patients lose", and to forgo grooming habits such as shaving, waxing and haircuts for the month.

History

The idea of setting aside a month without shaving grew out of informal traditions and social-media challenges. No-Shave November was formalised in 2009 by the family of Matthew Hill of Chicago, who had died of colon cancer in 2007; what began as a family tribute and small Facebook campaign grew into a registered non-profit that has raised millions of dollars for organisations such as the American Cancer Society, the Prevent Cancer Foundation and St. Jude Children's Research Hospital.

Participation

Participants traditionally start clean-shaven on 1 November and do not shave for the month, though trimming for professional requirements is generally accepted. The campaign is open to all genders — anyone can forgo some grooming ritual — and donations of the savings are voluntary. Police departments, companies and sports teams in the United States commonly take part, sometimes under formal grooming-policy waivers.

Relation to Movember

No-Shave November is often confused with Movember, the older Australian-born movement in which men grow moustaches during November to raise awareness of men's health issues such as prostate cancer, testicular cancer and suicide. The two are separate organisations with different rules — Movember requires a moustache grown from clean-shaven, while No-Shave November encompasses all grooming — but share the wider goal of using facial hair to start health conversations.
