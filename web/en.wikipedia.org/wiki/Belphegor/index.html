Belphegor

Belphegor is a demon of the Christian and occult tradition, derived ultimately from Baal-Peor, a Moabite god mentioned in the Hebrew Bible (Numbers 25), whose worship by the Israelites at Shittim provoked divine wrath. In later demonology Belphegor became one of the seven princes of Hell, associated with the deadly sin of sloth, and was said to tempt people with ingenious inventions and discoveries that would enrich them and make them idle.

In demonology and literature

Grimoires and occult catalogues describe Belphegor as a tempter who helps people make discoveries and who takes the form of a beautiful young woman or a monstrous bearded demon. He appears in Collin de Plancy's Dictionnaire Infernal, whose famous illustration shows him seated — in legend, he is often depicted sitting on a toilet, a throne he is said never to leave. Machiavelli's novella Belfagor arcidiavolo retells a folk tale in which the demon (as Belfagor) is sent to Earth to test whether marriage is worse than Hell, and returns preferring Hell. The character also figures in Milton's Paradise Lost among the fallen angels, and gives his name to the atmospheric ghost figure Belphégor of French serial fiction and the 1960s television series Belphégor, ou le fantôme du Louvre.

Modern uses

Belphegor's name has been borrowed for a prime number (Belphegor's prime, 1000000000000066600000000000001, whose digits and symbolism invite occult associations), for works of music and metal bands, and for various fictional demons. The association with sloth and seductive cleverness remains his defining trait in popular culture.
