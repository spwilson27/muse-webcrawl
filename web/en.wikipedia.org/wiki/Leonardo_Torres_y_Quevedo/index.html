Leonardo Torres y Quevedo

Leonardo Torres y Quevedo
Born: 28 December 1852, Santa Cruz de Iguña, Spain
Died: 18 December 1936, Madrid
Known for: Telekino, El Ajedrecista, cable cars

Leonardo Torres y Quevedo (28 December 1852 – 18 December 1936) was a Spanish civil engineer, inventor and mathematician. A pioneer of automation and remote control, he built analogue calculating machines, an early chess-playing automaton, radio-controlled vehicles, and successful cable car systems.

Inventions

Torres Quevedo designed multi-span aerial cable cars, including the Spanish Aerocar over the Whirlpool Rapids at Niagara Falls (1916), which still operates. In 1903 he demonstrated the Telekino, a radio-controlled boat guided from shore — among the earliest practical remote-control systems. His Essays on Automatics (1914) set out a theory of automata strikingly ahead of its time.

El Ajedrecista

In 1912 he built El Ajedrecista ("The Chess Player"), an electromechanical automaton that played a king-and-rook versus king endgame against a human opponent, detecting illegal moves and always winning from any position. It is regarded as the first computer game in history. A second, improved version used magnets beneath the board.

Computing machines

From the 1890s Torres Quevedo built analogue machines for solving algebraic equations, including machines with logarithmic scales and a device demonstrating that a fully electromechanical arithmometer with a stored programme was feasible — anticipating elements of later digital computing. He also experimented with airships, producing the semi-rigid Astra-Torres design used by Britain and France in the First World War.
