Simeon I of Bulgaria

Simeon I the Great (c. 864/865 – 27 May 927) ruled the First Bulgarian Empire as knyaz (893–913) and then as tsar (emperor, 913–927). His reign, marked by successful wars against Byzantium, the Magyars and Serbia, brought Bulgaria to its greatest territorial extent and a cultural flowering known as the Bulgarian Golden Age.

Early life and accession

Third son of Boris I, Simeon was educated in Constantinople – hence "half-Greek" to contemporaries – and destined for the church, but after his brother Vladimir-Rasate attempted a pagan restoration, Boris deposed him and placed Simeon on the throne in 893. The capital moved to Preslav and Slavonic replaced Greek in liturgy and administration.

Wars with Byzantium

War began over trade rights moved from Constantinople. Simeon defeated the Byzantines and their Magyar allies, won a crushing victory at Achelous in 917 – one of medieval Europe's largest battles – and was recognised as emperor of the Bulgarians. He twice threatened Constantinople itself but never took it, and his plans for a Romano-Bulgarian empire ended with his death in 927.

Culture

Preslav and the literary schools of Preslav and Ohrid produced translations and original works in Old Church Slavonic; Bulgaria's territory stretched from the Black Sea to the Adriatic approaches.
