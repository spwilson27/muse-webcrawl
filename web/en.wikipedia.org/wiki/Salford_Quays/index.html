Salford Quays

Salford Quays is an area of Salford, Greater Manchester, England, near the end of the Manchester Ship Canal. Previously the site of Manchester Docks, it became one of the first and largest urban regeneration projects in the United Kingdom following the closure of the docks in 1982. Today the Quays is home to MediaCityUK, The Lowry arts centre and Imperial War Museum North (across the canal in Trafford), and is one of Greater Manchester's principal business, leisure and residential districts.

History

The docks were built as part of the Manchester Ship Canal, opened in 1894, which made Manchester a major inland port; at their peak the Salford docks employed thousands. Trade declined from the 1960s as ships outgrew the canal, and the docks closed in 1982 with the loss of some 3,000 jobs. In 1983 Salford City Council bought the site, adopted a regeneration plan in 1985 and began transforming the polluted dock basins — a pioneering water-quality clean-up turned the basins into a clean-water asset used for watersports.

Development

Office and housing development through the 1990s was anchored by the opening of The Lowry theatre and gallery complex in 2000 and Imperial War Museum North in 2002, linked by the Lowry footbridge. From 2011 the BBC relocated major departments — including BBC Sport, Children's and Radio 5 Live — to MediaCityUK, joined by ITV Granada and the University of Salford, creating one of Europe's largest media hubs. Thousands of apartments, hotels and the Quayside shopping centre have made the Quays a substantial residential quarter, and events such as triathlons are regularly held in the basins.
