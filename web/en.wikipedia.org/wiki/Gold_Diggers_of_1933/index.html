Gold Diggers of 1933 is an American pre-Code musical film directed by Mervyn LeRoy, with musical numbers staged by Busby Berkeley. A backstage story set against the Great Depression, it follows chorus girls and a struggling producer trying to mount a Broadway show while outsmarting a disapproving Boston banker family.

Musical numbers

Songs by Harry Warren and Al Dubin include the opening "We're in the Money" (sung in part in Pig Latin by Ginger Rogers, amid dancers costumed as giant coins), "Pettin' in the Park", the "Shadow Waltz" — with dancers playing neon-lit violins filmed from above in Berkeley's kaleidoscopic style — and the finale, "Remember My Forgotten Man", a sombre tribute to unemployed World War I veterans that is widely considered one of the most striking sequences in American film.

Reception and legacy

The film was one of Warner Bros.' biggest hits of 1933 and helped define the backstage musical genre, spawning sequels (Gold Diggers of 1935, 1937, and in Paris). In 2003 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically, and aesthetically significant.
