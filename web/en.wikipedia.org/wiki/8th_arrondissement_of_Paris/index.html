The 8th arrondissement of Paris (VIIIe arrondissement) is one of the 20 arrondissements of the capital city of France. Known as the arrondissement de l'Élysée after the presidential palace it contains, it lies on the right bank of the River Seine and is one of the city's most affluent and prestigious districts, home to the Champs-Élysées, the Élysée Palace, and much of Paris's luxury shopping and corporate office space.

Landmarks

The arrondissement contains many of Paris's most famous sites: the Arc de Triomphe at its western edge on the Place Charles de Gaulle, the Élysée Palace (residence of the President of France), the Place de la Concorde with its Luxor obelisk, the church of La Madeleine, the Grand Palais and Petit Palais, and the Parc Monceau. The Avenue des Champs-Élysées runs through the district from Concorde to the Arc de Triomphe, and the "Golden Triangle" around Avenue Montaigne concentrates the flagship stores of French luxury houses.

Character

Although its resident population is relatively small — under 40,000 — the 8th hosts a large share of Parisian jobs in finance, law, luxury retail, and tourism. Its grand Haussmann-era boulevards, embassies, and headquarters buildings make it one of the symbolic centres of French political and economic power.
