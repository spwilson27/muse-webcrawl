Otto Frank

Otto Heinrich Frank (12 May 1889 – 19 August 1980) was a German businessman and the father of Anne Frank and Margot Frank. He was the only member of the eight people who hid in the Amsterdam "Secret Annex" to survive the Holocaust.

Born in Frankfurt, Otto Frank served as an officer in the German army in the First World War. After the Nazis came to power, he moved his family to Amsterdam in 1933 and ran a company trading in pectin and spices. When deportations of Dutch Jews began in 1942, the family went into hiding in rooms behind his office at Prinsengracht 263, aided by his employees. The group was betrayed and arrested in August 1944; Otto survived Auschwitz and was liberated in January 1945, learning on his return that his wife Edith had died at Auschwitz and later that his daughters had died at Bergen-Belsen.

Miep Gies had preserved Anne's diary, which Otto edited and published in 1947 as Het Achterhuis (The Diary of a Young Girl). He dedicated the rest of his life to promoting its message, helped establish the Anne Frank House museum in Amsterdam (opened 1960), and founded the Anne Frank Foundation in Basel. He died in Birsfelden, Switzerland, in 1980.
