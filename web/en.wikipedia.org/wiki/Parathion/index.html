Parathion

IUPAC nameO,O-diethyl O-(4-nitrophenyl) phosphorothioate
Chemical formulaC10H14NO5PS
Molar mass291.26 g/mol
CAS number56-38-2

Parathion, also called parathion-ethyl or diethyl parathion and locally known as "Folidol", is an organophosphate insecticide and acaricide. It was originally developed by the German chemical company IG Farben in the 1940s under the direction of Gerhard Schrader, who had earlier developed the nerve gases tabun and sarin.

Parathion is a potent acetylcholinesterase inhibitor. In the body it is converted by liver enzymes to paraoxon, an even more potent inhibitor, which blocks the breakdown of the neurotransmitter acetylcholine. The resulting accumulation of acetylcholine causes continuous stimulation of muscles and glands, leading to convulsions, respiratory failure and death.

Uses

Parathion was widely used as a non-systemic insecticide on fruit, cotton, vegetables and nuts, and as an alternative to DDT. It is highly toxic to birds, bees, aquatic organisms and mammals, including humans, and is absorbed through the skin, by inhalation and by ingestion.

Regulation

Because of its extreme acute toxicity and the many fatal poisonings of agricultural workers, parathion has been banned or severely restricted in most countries. It is classified by the World Health Organization as Class Ia, "extremely hazardous", and is listed under the Rotterdam Convention. Its less toxic relative parathion-methyl was similarly restricted. Treatment of poisoning involves atropine and an oxime such as pralidoxime.
