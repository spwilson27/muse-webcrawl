Steinway & Sons, also known as Steinway, is a German-American piano company founded in Manhattan in 1853 by German piano builder Heinrich Engelhard Steinweg (later known as Henry E. Steinway). The company is renowned for its high-quality grand pianos, which are widely considered the standard for concert performance.

History

Heinrich Steinweg emigrated from Germany to the United States in 1850 and founded Steinway & Sons with his sons in 1853. The company's innovations — including the overstrung scale, the cast-iron frame and the accelerated action — earned numerous patents and prizes at international exhibitions. A second factory was opened in Hamburg, Germany, in 1880.

Reputation

More than 1,800 performing artists and ensembles are official "Steinway Artists", and Steinway pianos are present in the vast majority of major concert halls. Each piano is largely handcrafted; the company produces a few thousand instruments per year.
