Ducted fan

A ducted fan is a propulsion arrangement in which a mechanical fan or propeller is mounted inside a cylindrical duct or shroud. The duct reduces tip losses and can increase static thrust compared with an open propeller of the same diameter, while also reducing noise and improving safety by enclosing the blades.

TypePropulsion system
ApplicationsAircraft, hovercraft, VTOL drones, ventilation
VariantsDucted propeller, fenestron, electric ducted fan (EDF)

Advantages and disadvantages

By preventing the formation of wingtip-like vortices at the blade tips, a well-designed duct allows a smaller-diameter fan to produce thrust comparable to a larger open propeller — valuable where ground clearance or overall size is limited. The shroud also shields bystanders from the blades. However, the duct adds weight and drag, and at high forward speeds or with poorly designed inlet lips it can reduce efficiency.

Applications

Ducted fans are used in VTOL aircraft designs, hovercraft lift and propulsion, airships, and the fenestron tail-rotor arrangement on many helicopters. Small electric ducted fans (EDFs) power many radio-controlled model jets, and ducted fans appear in personal drones, where the duct improves safety indoors. Large examples include the fans of fan-in-wing experimental aircraft such as the Ryan XV-5 Vertifan.
