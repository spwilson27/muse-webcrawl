Lunenburg is a port town on the South Shore of Nova Scotia, Canada, about 90 kilometres southwest of Halifax. Founded in 1753, it was one of the first British attempts to settle Protestants in Nova Scotia, established by "Foreign Protestant" settlers, mostly Germans, Swiss and French Protestants from Montbéliard.

History and heritage

The town was laid out on a steep hillside in a rigid British gridiron plan, with defensive blockhouses on the surrounding heights. The Old Town retains much of its original layout and a remarkable stock of wooden architecture from the 18th and 19th centuries, leading UNESCO to inscribe it as a World Heritage Site in 1995 as the best surviving example of a planned British colonial settlement in North America.

Fishing and shipbuilding

Lunenburg became a centre of the Atlantic fishery and shipbuilding. Its most famous vessel is the schooner Bluenose, launched in 1921, an undefeated racing champion and fishing icon that appears on the Canadian dime; its replica, Bluenose II, is Nova Scotia's sailing ambassador. The town is home to the Fisheries Museum of the Atlantic and remains a working fishing and tourism port, with firms such as High Liner Foods founded there.
