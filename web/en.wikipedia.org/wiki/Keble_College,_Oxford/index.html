Keble College, Oxford

Keble College
Established: 1870
Location: Parks Road, Oxford
Named for: John Keble
Undergraduates: ~400
Graduates: ~250

Keble College is one of the constituent colleges of the University of Oxford in England. Founded in 1870 in memory of John Keble, a leading figure in the Oxford Movement, it was established to widen access to Oxford education, particularly for men intending to become Anglican clergy.

History

The college was funded by public subscription following Keble's death in 1866. Architect William Butterfield designed its striking red-brick buildings in Victorian Gothic style. The chapel, completed in 1876, houses William Holman Hunt's painting The Light of the World.

Buildings and grounds

Keble occupies a large site between Parks Road and Banbury Road. Its quads — Pusey Quad and Liddon Quad — are among the largest in Oxford. The 20th-century additions include the Sloane-Robinson Building and facilities on the former Acland Hospital site.

Student life

Keble admits both undergraduates and postgraduates across most subjects. It is known for a strong rowing tradition and for its large dining hall, among the largest in Oxford.
