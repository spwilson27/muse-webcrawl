Eliezer "Elie" Wiesel (September 30, 1928 – July 2, 2016) was a Romanian-born American writer, professor, political activist, Nobel laureate, and Holocaust survivor. He authored 57 books, written mostly in French and English, including Night, a work based on his experiences as a Jewish prisoner in the Auschwitz and Buchenwald concentration camps.

Early life and the Holocaust

Wiesel was born in Sighet, in the Carpathian Mountains of Romania, to a Hasidic Jewish family. In 1944, when he was fifteen, his family was deported with the town's Jewish community to Auschwitz, where his mother and younger sister were murdered. Wiesel and his father were later transferred to Buchenwald, where his father died weeks before the camp's liberation by the U.S. Army in April 1945.

Writer and witness

After the war Wiesel studied in Paris and worked as a journalist. Encouraged by the novelist François Mauriac, he broke a self-imposed ten-year silence and published his memoir in Yiddish in 1956; the condensed French version, La Nuit, appeared in 1958 and in English as Night in 1960. It became one of the most widely read accounts of the Holocaust. He went on to write novels, plays, and memoirs, and taught at Boston University.

Activism and honours

Wiesel chaired the President's Commission on the Holocaust, helping found the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum. Awarding him the Nobel Peace Prize in 1986, the Norwegian Nobel Committee called him a "messenger to mankind". He campaigned against indifference and for victims of oppression in places including South Africa, Nicaragua, Kosovo, and Darfur. He died in New York City on July 2, 2016, aged 87.
