Jordan curve theorem

In topology, the Jordan curve theorem asserts that every Jordan curve — a plane simple closed curve, a non-self-intersecting continuous loop — divides the plane into an "interior" region bounded by the curve and an "exterior" region containing all points far away, so that any continuous path connecting a point of one region to a point of the other must intersect the curve somewhere.

Stated by Camille Jordan in 1887, the theorem's seemingly obvious conclusion proved surprisingly difficult to prove rigorously; Jordan's own proof was long debated and the first fully accepted proof is credited to Oswald Veblen (1905). The result generalises to the Jordan–Brouwer separation theorem in higher dimensions.
