Captain Nemo (Latin for "no one") is a fictional character created by the novelist Jules Verne (1828–1905). Nemo appears in two of Verne's science-fiction classics, Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (1870) and The Mysterious Island (1875). He also makes a cameo appearance in a play written by Verne with Adolphe d'Ennery, Les Enfants du capitaine Grant. One of literature's most famous antiheroes, Nemo is a scientific visionary and a brooding, vengeful exile who has renounced life on land and roams the oceans in his submarine, the Nautilus.

Character

In Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas, Nemo's origins are deliberately mysterious. He is a man of formidable intellect and wealth, a master of marine engineering, oceanography, music and several languages, who designed and built the electrically powered Nautilus in secret. He lives off the sea's resources — its food, its minerals, even tobacco made from seaweed and cigars — and declares that he has broken with a land-based society he despises, above all its imperialism and oppression. Yet his hatred has a violent edge: he sinks a warship of an unnamed nation that obsesses him, watching the crew drown with cold satisfaction, an episode that horrifies the narrator, Professor Pierre Aronnax, his guest-prisoner.

Nemo is nonetheless capable of deep feeling: he weeps over a dead crewman, risks his life to save a Ceylonese pearl diver, funds revolutions and oppressed peoples with salvaged treasure (he sends gold to the Cretan rebels against Ottoman rule), and grieves before a portrait of his lost wife and children.

Identity revealed

In The Mysterious Island, where an aged, dying Nemo befriends and secretly aids a group of castaways, Verne reveals his past: Nemo is Prince Dakkar, son of a raja of Bundelkhand in India, educated in Europe. He fought the British in the Indian Rebellion of 1857 (the "Sepoy Mutiny"); after its failure, his wife and children were killed and his country subjugated, and he devoted his fortune and genius to building the Nautilus and waging his solitary war on tyranny from beneath the seas.

Verne had originally intended Nemo to be a Polish nobleman avenging his family's deaths at Russian hands during the January Uprising of 1863, but his publisher, Pierre-Jules Hetzel, fearing offence to France's Russian ally, persuaded him to obscure Nemo's origins in the first novel; the Indian identity was a later solution compatible with the 1857 setting.

Name

"Nemo" is Latin for "no one" or "nobody" — a name Odysseus gives himself ("Outis") when confronting the Cyclops Polyphemus in Homer's Odyssey, an allusion to the captain's self-erasure from the human world. Verne may also have borrowed the name from the Parisian carriage company or from contemporary references; the allusion to Homer is generally accepted as primary.

Portrayals and legacy

Captain Nemo has become a stock figure of popular culture — the archetype of the brilliant, tortured, misanthropic captain — and has been portrayed many times, most famously by James Mason in Disney's 1954 film 20,000 Leagues Under the Sea. Other portrayals include Michael Caine (1997 television film), Patrick Stewart (2005), and Naseeruddin Shah in The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen (2003), in which Nemo appears as a member of a Victorian superhero team, following Alan Moore's comics. The character is the title figure of Disney+'s 2024 prequel series Nautilus, which dramatises Prince Dakkar's early life and revenge on the East India Mercantile Company. The United States Navy named its first nuclear submarine USS Nautilus (SSN-571) after Verne's vessel, and the character's name has been given to a remote point in the Pacific (Point Nemo, the oceanic pole of inaccessibility).
