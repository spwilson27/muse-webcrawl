Dal Khalsa (International)

Dal Khalsa (International) is a Sikh political organisation based in Punjab, India, whose stated aim is the establishment of an independent Sikh homeland, Khalistan. It takes its name from the historic Dal Khalsa, the 18th-century army of the Sikh Confederacy, but is a distinct modern organisation and should not be confused with it. The group presents itself as a political party advocating Sikh self-determination through peaceful and democratic means, though the Indian government has at times banned it and treated it as a separatist organisation.

History

Dal Khalsa was founded in August 1978 in Chandigarh, with the support of figures sympathetic to the Sikh nationalist current that grew during and after the Anandpur Sahib Resolution movement. Its early years coincided with the escalation of tension between Sikh activists and the Indian state that culminated in Operation Blue Star in 1984. A founding figure, Gajinder Singh, was among those who hijacked an Indian Airlines flight to Lahore in 1981 to demand the release of Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale; he was later associated with the organisation’s leadership in exile in Pakistan. The organisation was banned under Indian anti-terrorism legislation in the 1980s and remained outlawed for a period before resurfacing as a political group in the 1990s.

Activities

Since the 2000s Dal Khalsa (International) has functioned mainly as a small political and advocacy party in Punjab, holding conferences, observing anniversaries such as Operation Blue Star, and campaigning on Sikh religious and political issues, while continuing to endorse the goal of Khalistan. It has often allied with other Panthic organisations such as the Shiromani Akali Dal (Amritsar). Its activities and positions are controversial in India, and its events are monitored by security agencies.
