Double entendre

A double entendre (from French double entente, "double understanding") is a figure of speech or a particular way of wording that is devised to have a double meaning, one of which is typically obvious while the other often conveys a message that would be too socially awkward, sexually suggestive, or offensive to state directly. A double entendre may exploit puns or wordplay to redirect the listener's attention, or else rely on ambiguity in the listener's perception.

Usage

Double entendres are a staple of comedy, from Shakespeare's plays through British music-hall and seaside postcard humour to modern sitcoms. They allow a speaker to convey a risqué meaning with plausible deniability: the innocent interpretation remains available. In broadcasting, double entendres have historically been used to slip suggestive material past censors.

Examples

Groucho Marx's line "I once shot an elephant in my pajamas — how he got into my pajamas I'll never know" works by shifting the sense of a phrase mid-sentence. Mae West was famed for lines such as "Why don't you come up sometime and see me?", whose suggestiveness depends on delivery and context.

Related forms

Related devices include the innuendo, the malapropism, deliberate ambiguity, and the "garden path" sentence. A triple entendre extends the joke to three simultaneous meanings.
