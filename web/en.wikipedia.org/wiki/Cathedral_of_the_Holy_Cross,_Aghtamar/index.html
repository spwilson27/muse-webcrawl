The Cathedral of the Holy Cross (Armenian: Սուրբ Խաչ տաճար; Turkish: Akdamar Kilisesi) is a medieval Armenian Apostolic cathedral on Akdamar Island in Lake Van, eastern Turkey. Built between 915 and 921 for King Gagik I Artsruni of Vaspurakan by the architect-monk Manuel, it is one of the masterpieces of Armenian architecture and the best-preserved monument of its era, famous for the extensive cycle of relief carvings covering its exterior walls.

Architecture and sculpture

The church has a centralised, cruciform plan crowned by a conical dome. Its red tuff walls are carved with an extraordinary programme of reliefs: biblical scenes from Adam and Eve, Jonah and the whale, David and Goliath, and Daniel in the lions' den; rows of saints and kings; and bands of animals and hunting scenes, woven together with vine scrolls of remarkable liveliness. King Gagik himself is shown presenting a model of the church to Christ. Interior frescoes, more fragmentary, once covered the walls.

History

The cathedral served as the seat of the Catholicosate of Aghtamar, a regional Armenian church jurisdiction, from 1113 until the late 19th century, with a monastic complex around it. Abandoned in 1915 amid the Armenian genocide, the church suffered vandalism and stone-robbing. Turkish authorities carried out a major restoration from 2005 to 2006, reopening the building as a secular museum in 2007; since 2010 an annual Armenian liturgy has been permitted in the church.
