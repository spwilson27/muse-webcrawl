Extinct birds
More than 180 bird species have become extinct in the last 500 years, largely through human activity.

The list of extinct birds includes bird species that have become extinct since the year 1500, the conventional threshold used by BirdLife International and the IUCN Red List. The great majority of these extinctions occurred on oceanic islands, where endemic species were vulnerable to introduced predators, hunting and habitat destruction.

The rate of bird extinction has accelerated dramatically since human colonisation of the Pacific and other island groups. Famous examples include the dodo of Mauritius, the great auk of the North Atlantic, and the passenger pigeon of North America, once among the most numerous birds on Earth.

Causes

Island species account for roughly 90 per cent of recorded bird extinctions. The main drivers have been hunting, introduced predators such as rats, cats and pigs, habitat clearance, and, increasingly, climate change. Conservation programmes have since saved some species from the brink, but dozens remain critically endangered.
