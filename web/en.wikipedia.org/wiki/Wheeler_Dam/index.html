Wheeler Dam

Wheeler Dam is a hydroelectric dam on the Tennessee River in northern Alabama, United States, operated by the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA). Completed in 1936, it impounds Wheeler Lake and is named for Joseph Wheeler, a Confederate general and later U.S. congressman.

History

Construction began in 1933 as part of the TVA's programme of river development during the Great Depression. The dam was built primarily for navigation and flood control, with power generation as a secondary purpose. It was the second major TVA dam completed, after Norris Dam, and its construction provided thousands of jobs in the Tennessee Valley.

Structure and operation

The dam is a concrete gravity structure about 1,106 feet (337 m) long, with a navigation lock 110 by 600 feet. Its powerhouse has eleven generating units with a combined capacity of roughly 400 megawatts. Wheeler Lake extends about 60 miles upstream to Guntersville Dam, and the reservoir is a major centre for bass fishing and recreation. The dam also provides flood storage protecting downstream communities including Decatur, Alabama.
