Slider-crank linkage

The slider-crank linkage is a four-bar linkage with three revolute joints and one prismatic, or sliding, joint. The rotation of the crank drives the linear movement of the slider, or the linear movement of the slider drives the rotation of the crank.

Because the stroke of the slider is not exactly sinusoidal (unless the connecting rod is infinitely long), the linkage is used in many engines and pumps where this motion profile is acceptable, and its kinematics are a standard topic in mechanical engineering.

Components

The mechanism consists of a crank (a rotating link), a connecting rod (coupler), and a slider (such as a piston in a cylinder), together with the fixed frame that guides the slider. The ratio of the crank radius to the connecting-rod length determines the asymmetry of the piston's motion between the two halves of the cycle.

Applications

The slider-crank is the mechanism of the reciprocating engine: in steam engines and internal combustion engines, pressure on the piston drives the slider and rotates the crankshaft; in reciprocating compressors and pumps the crank is driven and the slider does the pumping. Variations include the inverted slider-crank used in some quick-return mechanisms and the scotch yoke, which produces exact simple harmonic motion instead.

Kinematics

The displacement of the slider can be expressed exactly in terms of the crank angle, crank radius, and rod length, using the law of cosines; the position involves a square-root term whose series expansion yields the familiar approximation of simple harmonic motion plus a secondary term at twice the crank frequency, which is a source of vibration in high-speed engines.
