Médoc AOC

Médoc is an appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) for wine in the Bordeaux wine region of southwestern France, on the left bank of the Gironde estuary that divides the Bordeaux wine region into areas commonly called the Left Bank and the Right Bank. The appellation takes its name from the Médoc peninsula, the triangular tract of land between the Gironde and the Atlantic Ocean to the west of Bordeaux.

Geographically, the appellation area is confined to the tip of the Médoc peninsula, about 15–20 km north-west of the city of Bordeaux. It is the northernmost of the Médoc appellations and covers an area of about 5,700 hectares planted to vine. The region is prized for its gravelly soils, which suit Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, the dominant grape varieties of the blend.

Wine style

Médoc AOC wines are almost exclusively red. They tend to be somewhat lighter and less concentrated than the wines of the communal appellations further south (Pauillac, Saint-Estèphe, Margaux, Saint-Julien), and are often described as elegant, structured, and somewhat austere when young, opening with age to show cedar, tobacco, cassis, and earthy notes. The term Bas-Médoc ("Lower Médoc") is sometimes used informally for the area, though it has no official standing.

History

Viticulture on the Médoc peninsula expanded greatly in the 17th century when Dutch engineers drained the marshlands, revealing gravel ridges well suited to vines. The modern AOC was established in 1936, at the creation of the French appellation system.
