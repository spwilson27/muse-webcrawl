Nautiloid

Nautiloids are a group of marine cephalopods (subclass Nautiloidea) originating in the Late Cambrian and peaking in diversity in the Ordovician and Silurian. They have external, chambered shells — straight (orthoconic) or coiled — with simple suture patterns, unlike the complex sutures of their relatives the ammonoids.

Survivors

Most nautiloid lineages are extinct, known from rich fossil records. The only living representatives are the nautiluses of the genera Nautilus and Allonautilus in the Indo-Pacific, often called living fossils.
