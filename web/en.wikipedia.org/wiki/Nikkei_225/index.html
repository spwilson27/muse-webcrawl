The Nikkei 225, commonly called the Nikkei Index, is Japan's leading stock market index, tracking 225 large, publicly traded companies listed on the Prime Market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange. It is a price-weighted index, calculated since 1950 by the newspaper The Nikkei, and is analogous to the Dow Jones Industrial Average.

History

The index peaked at 38,915.87 on 29 December 1989, at the height of the Japanese asset price bubble, then fell through two "lost decades". It finally surpassed its 1989 record in February 2024 amid corporate governance reforms and renewed foreign investment.

Composition

Constituents are reviewed annually and include major exporters and technology companies such as Fast Retailing, SoftBank Group, Toyota and Tokyo Electron; because the index is price-weighted, high-priced shares like Fast Retailing exert an outsized influence.
