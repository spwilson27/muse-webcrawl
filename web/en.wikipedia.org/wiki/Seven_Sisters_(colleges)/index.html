Seven Sisters (colleges)

The Seven Sisters are a group of seven private liberal arts colleges in the Northeastern United States that are historically women's colleges. Founded between 1837 and 1889, they were created to provide women with an education equivalent to that provided at the time by the (then all-male) Ivy League colleges, with which they were informally paired in the public imagination. The name derives from the Pleiades, the seven sisters of Greek mythology.

Members

The seven colleges are Barnard College (New York City, affiliated with Columbia University), Bryn Mawr College (Pennsylvania), Mount Holyoke College (Massachusetts, the oldest, founded 1837), Radcliffe College (Massachusetts, merged into Harvard University, its functions continuing in the Radcliffe Institute), Smith College (Massachusetts, the largest women's college in the US), Vassar College (New York, coeducational since 1969), and Wellesley College (Massachusetts). Of the seven, five remain women's colleges at undergraduate level.

History

The association took shape after a 1927 conference convened to coordinate fundraising among the colleges. The Sisters produced an outsized share of American women leaders in academia, politics and the arts, counting among their alumnae Hillary Clinton (Wellesley), Madeleine Albright (Wellesley), Katharine Hepburn (Bryn Mawr), Sylvia Plath and Betty Friedan (Smith), and Meryl Streep (Vassar). As the Ivy League went coeducational from 1969, the Sisters debated but mostly retained single-sex undergraduate education.
