Dr. No (film)

Dr. No is a 1962 spy film directed by Terence Young. It is the first film in the James Bond series, starring Sean Connery as the fictional MI6 agent James Bond. Based on the 1958 novel of the same name by Ian Fleming, the film was produced by Harry Saltzman and Albert R. Broccoli of Eon Productions. In the plot, Bond is sent to Jamaica to investigate the disappearance of a fellow British agent, and the trail leads to Dr. Julius No, a member of the criminal organisation SPECTRE, who is plotting to disrupt an American space launch from Cape Canaveral with a radio beam weapon.

Production and reception

Made on a modest budget of about $1 million, Dr. No was a major box-office success and established many of the series' signatures: the gun-barrel opening sequence, Maurice Binder's title designs, Monty Norman's "James Bond Theme", and Ursula Andress's celebrated emergence from the sea as Honey Ryder. The film launched one of the longest-running and most successful franchises in cinema history.
