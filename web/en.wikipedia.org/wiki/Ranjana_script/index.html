Ranjana script

TypeAbugida
Period11th century – mid 20th century; modern revival
RegionKathmandu Valley, Nepal
LanguagesNewar (Nepal Bhasa), Sanskrit

The Ranjana script (also called Lantsa in some contexts) is an abugida writing system developed in the 11th century in the Kathmandu Valley of Nepal. It was used to write Sanskrit and Newar (Nepal Bhasa) and is regarded as one of the most decorative of the Brahmic scripts, characterised by an elaborate horizontal headline from which the letters hang.

Use

Ranjana was the script of Buddhist manuscripts, temple inscriptions, ritual objects and sacred diagrams across Nepal and was carried into Tibet, China, Mongolia and Japan for writing Sanskrit mantras and dharanis; seed syllables (bija) in Ranjana appear on temples and prayer wheels throughout East Asia. The sacred syllable "Om" in Ranjana is a widely recognised emblem of Nepal.

Decline and revival

Suppression of the Newar language and its scripts under the Rana regime and later policies pushed Ranjana nearly out of everyday use by the mid-20th century. Since the late 20th century it has been revived for signage, book covers, religious prints and cultural identity in the Kathmandu Valley.
