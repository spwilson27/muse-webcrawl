Varicap

A varicap diode, also called a varactor diode, variable reactance diode or tuning diode, is a type of diode designed to exploit the voltage-dependent capacitance of a reverse-biased p–n junction. When a reverse voltage is applied, the depletion region of the junction widens and the capacitance falls; the device therefore behaves as a voltage-controlled capacitor, typically varying over a range of a few picofarads to a few hundred picofarads.

Applications

Varicaps are used as tuning elements in voltage-controlled oscillators (VCOs), where changing the control voltage adjusts the resonant frequency of an LC tank circuit. They replaced mechanically ganged variable capacitors in radio and television tuners, enabling electronic tuning and remote control, and remain common in phase-locked loops, frequency synthesizers, parametric amplifiers, adjustable filters and automatic frequency control circuits in FM receivers.

Characteristics

Key parameters include the capacitance range and tuning ratio (capacitance at minimum versus maximum reverse voltage), the quality factor Q, which falls with frequency and series resistance, and the junction law — graded, abrupt or hyperabrupt doping profiles giving different capacitance–voltage curves suited to linear tuning or wide-range applications.
