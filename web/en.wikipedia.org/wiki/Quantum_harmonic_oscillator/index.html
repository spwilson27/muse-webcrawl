Quantum harmonic oscillator

[Image: Energy levels of the quantum harmonic oscillator, equally spaced by ħω]

Overview

The quantum harmonic oscillator is the quantum-mechanical analogue of the classical harmonic oscillator — a system (such as a mass on a spring or a vibrating molecule) whose restoring force is proportional to displacement. Because an arbitrary smooth potential can be approximated as harmonic near a stable equilibrium, it is one of the most important model systems in quantum mechanics, and one of the few with an exact analytical solution.

Solution

Its energy levels are equally spaced: E_n = ħω(n + ½), including a zero-point energy of ½ħω. The problem is elegantly solved with ladder (creation and annihilation) operators, introduced by Dirac — algebra that generalises to quantum field theory, where each field mode is an oscillator and its excitations are particles. Applications include molecular vibrations, phonons in solids, photons in cavities and the quantisation of the electromagnetic field.
