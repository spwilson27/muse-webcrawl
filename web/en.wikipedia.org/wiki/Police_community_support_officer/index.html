Police community support officer

A police community support officer (PCSO) is a member of police staff in England and Wales who provides a visible police presence in neighbourhoods, dealing with anti-social behaviour and lower-level crime, and supporting sworn police officers. PCSOs were created by the Police Reform Act 2002 and first deployed by the Metropolitan Police in London.

Powers

PCSOs are not constables and do not have the full power of arrest of a police officer, but chief constables designate them with a range of powers, which may include issuing fixed penalty notices, confiscating alcohol and tobacco from young people, directing traffic, and detaining a suspect for up to 30 minutes until a constable arrives. Their uniform is similar to that of police officers but is distinguished by blue markings and "Police Community Support Officer" wording.

Role and debate

PCSOs were introduced to increase visible foot patrols and free constables for response and investigative work. Critics have sometimes derided them as "plastic police", while supporters credit them with building local knowledge and public confidence. Numbers rose to over 16,000 by 2009 before falling during austerity-era cuts, and have fluctuated since.
