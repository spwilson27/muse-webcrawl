Jai Singh II

Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II (3 November 1688 – 21 September 1743) was the ruler of the Kingdom of Amber, later Jaipur, in Rajasthan. A Kachhwaha Rajput, he founded the planned city of Jaipur in 1727 and is celebrated for his patronage of astronomy.

Reign

Succeeding as a child under Mughal overlordship, Jai Singh served emperors including Aurangzeb and Bahadur Shah, expanded his kingdom, and received the title Sawai. He shifted his capital from Amber to the new grid-planned city of Jaipur, designed with Vidyadhar Bhattacharya according to Vastu Shastra.

Astronomy

An accomplished astronomer, he built five masonry observatories — the Jantar Mantars — at Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Varanasi and Mathura, with giant instruments for naked-eye observation, compiled the Zij-i Muhammad Shahi tables, and sent missions to Europe for astronomical knowledge.

Legacy

He also performed Vedic rituals including the Ashvamedha, reformed social customs, and patronised scholars. The Jaipur Jantar Mantar is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
