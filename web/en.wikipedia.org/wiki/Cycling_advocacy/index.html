Cycling advocacy

Cycling advocacy consists of activities that call for, promote, or enable increased adoption and support for cycling and improved safety and convenience for cyclists, usually within urban areas or semi-urban regions.

Goals and methods

Advocacy organisations campaign for infrastructure such as protected cycle lanes, secure parking, and traffic calming; for legal reforms including safe-passing laws; and for programmes like bike-share schemes and cycle training. Groups range from local volunteer associations to national bodies such as Cycling UK and international federations.

History

Organised cycling advocacy dates to the late 19th century, when cyclists' organisations such as the League of American Wheelmen campaigned for paved roads. The modern movement grew in the 1970s in response to oil crises and urban traffic, with the Netherlands and Denmark often cited as models of high cycling mode share achieved through sustained policy and infrastructure.
