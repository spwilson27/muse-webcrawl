Bus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A red double-decker bus in London street traffic]
A London double-decker bus.

A bus is a road vehicle designed to carry many passengers, forming the most widely used form of public transport worldwide. Buses range from minibuses and city transit buses through articulated and double-decker types to long-distance coaches. The word derives from Latin omnibus (\'for all\'); horse-drawn omnibuses began in Nantes and Paris in the 1820s, and motor buses spread from the 1890s–1900s.

Types and services

City buses run fixed routes with frequent stops; Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) systems such as Curitiba\'s and Bogotá\'s TransMilenio add dedicated lanes and stations. Coaches serve intercity routes (Greyhound, FlixBus), schools (the American yellow school bus), airports and tours. London\'s red double-deckers and Hong Kong\'s franchised fleets are civic icons.

Technology

Diesel long dominated; fleets now shift to hybrid, battery-electric (led by China — Shenzhen electrified its entire ~16,000-bus fleet by 2017) and hydrogen fuel-cell buses. Low-floor designs, kneeling suspension and ramps made buses accessible; ticketing uses smartcards and phone payment.

Role

Buses are the cheapest transit mode to deploy and the only one serving most small towns. During the 20th century they replaced trams in many countries; modern policy often prioritises bus lanes to cut congestion and emissions.
