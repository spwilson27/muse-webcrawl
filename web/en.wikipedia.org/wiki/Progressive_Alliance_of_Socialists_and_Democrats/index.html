Progressive Alliance of Socialists and Democrats

The Progressive Alliance of Socialists and Democrats (S&D) is the political group of the Party of European Socialists (PES) in the European Parliament, and is the Parliament's second-largest group. It was founded on 23 June 2009 under its present name, succeeding the Socialist Group (established in the 1950s in the Common Assembly of the European Coal and Steel Community). The S&D brings together MEPs from social democratic, socialist, and labour parties across the European Union, including the German SPD, Spanish PSOE, Italian Democratic Party, and French Socialist Party.

History

The Socialist Group was for much of the Parliament's history its largest or second-largest group, supplying Parliament presidents and, through the PES, Commission presidents such as Jacques Delors (from a member party). After the 2009 European elections, the group adopted its current name to accommodate the newly joined Italian Democratic Party, whose members included non-socialists. The group has generally supported deeper European integration, social and labour protections, and has frequently governed the Parliament in an informal "grand coalition" with the centre-right EPP group.

Leadership

Presidents of the group have included Martin Schulz (2004–2012, later President of the European Parliament), Hannes Swoboda, Gianni Pittella, Udo Bullmann, and Iratxe García Pérez, who has led the group since 2019.

Member parties

Member parties include, among others, the Social Democratic Party of Germany (SPD), the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE), the Democratic Party (Italy), the Socialist Party (France), the Labour parties of Malta and the Netherlands (PvdA), the Social Democrats of Denmark, Sweden (SAP), Austria (SPÖ), and the Portuguese Socialist Party (PS).
