The Great Sioux War of 1876, also known as the Black Hills War, was a series of battles and negotiations that occurred in 1876 and 1877 in an alliance of Lakota Sioux and Northern Cheyenne against the United States. The cause of the war was the US government's desire to obtain ownership of the Black Hills. Gold had been rumoured in the hills since the early 19th century, and Custer's 1874 expedition confirmed it; miners poured into land guaranteed to the Sioux by the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868), and the government failed either to buy the hills or to keep the miners out.

Course

In December 1875 the government ordered all Sioux and Cheyenne bands to report to agencies by 31 January 1876 — an impossible deadline in winter — and turned those who did not comply over to the Army as "hostiles". Campaigns in 1876 converged on the free bands of Sitting Bull and Crazy Horse. After inconclusive fighting at the Powder River and the Rosebud, Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer's 7th Cavalry attacked the great encampment on the Little Bighorn River on 25–26 June 1876 and was annihilated: Custer and some 268 of his men were killed in the US Army's worst defeat of the Indian Wars. The shock — landing during the nation's Centennial celebrations — brought reinforcements and relentless winter campaigning. Mackenzie destroyed Dull Knife's Cheyenne village in November 1876; Crazy Horse surrendered in May 1877 (and was killed that September), while Sitting Bull fled to Canada, returning to surrender in 1881.

Outcome

By the Agreement of 1877 the Sioux ceded the Black Hills and other lands and accepted reduced reservations; the United States took the territory without the consent of three-quarters of adult Sioux males required by the 1868 treaty. In United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians (1980) the Supreme Court held the taking unconstitutional and awarded compensation, which the Sioux have refused, maintaining their claim to the land itself.
