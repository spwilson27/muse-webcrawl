Oligonucleotide

Oligonucleotide
TypeShort nucleic acid polymer
Typical length13–25 nucleotides (up to ~50)
ApplicationsPCR primers, probes, antisense therapy, aptamers, DNA synthesis

Oligonucleotides are short DNA or RNA molecules, or analogues of them, consisting of a small number of nucleotides — commonly 13 to 25, and generally no more than about 50. The name derives from the Greek oligos, "few". They are indispensable tools in molecular biology and genetics, and a growing class of therapeutic drugs.

Synthesis

Oligonucleotides are routinely produced by automated solid-phase chemical synthesis using phosphoramidite chemistry, in which protected nucleotide building blocks are added one at a time to a growing chain attached to a solid support. After synthesis the product is deprotected, purified (e.g. by HPLC or gel electrophoresis) and often verified by mass spectrometry. The technology makes it possible to obtain virtually any desired sequence cheaply and quickly, and underpins commercial "oligos" ordered by laboratories worldwide.

Uses in research

  • Primers for the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and DNA sequencing.
  • Probes for detecting specific sequences, e.g. in Southern and northern blotting, FISH and microarrays.
  • Site-directed mutagenesis and gene synthesis, assembling whole genes from overlapping oligonucleotides.
  • Antisense and RNA interference reagents for knocking down gene expression in experimental systems.

Therapeutic oligonucleotides

Chemically modified oligonucleotides — for example with phosphorothioate backbones or 2′-modified sugars — resist degradation by nucleases and can be used as drugs. Antisense oligonucleotides bind target mRNA and modulate its expression; approved examples include nusinersen (Spinraza) for spinal muscular atrophy and inotersen for hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis. Aptamers are oligonucleotides that fold into shapes binding specific targets, such as pegaptanib for macular degeneration. Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) such as patisiran act through the RNAi pathway.
