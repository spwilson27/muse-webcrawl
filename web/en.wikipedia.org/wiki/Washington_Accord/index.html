Washington Accord

The Washington Accord is an international agreement among engineering accreditation bodies for the mutual recognition of accredited undergraduate engineering degree programmes. Signed in 1989, its original signatories were the accreditation organizations of Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and the United States.

Purpose and operation

Under the Accord, each signatory recognizes the substantial equivalence of engineering degrees accredited by the others, so that a graduate of an accredited programme in one signatory country is treated as meeting the academic requirements for professional engineering practice in the others, subject to local licensing rules. The Accord is administered through the International Engineering Alliance and uses shared graduate-attribute exemplars to benchmark programmes. Signatories are admitted provisionally and then to full status after periodic review.

Growth

The Accord has expanded to include more than twenty signatories, among them bodies in South Africa, Japan, Korea, India, China, Singapore, Malaysia, Turkey, Russia and Sri Lanka. It is the senior of a family of educational accords in the alliance, alongside the Sydney Accord for engineering technologists and the Dublin Accord for engineering technicians.
