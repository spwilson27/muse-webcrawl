School prayer

School prayer, in the context of United States public schools, concerns the constitutional status of state-sponsored prayer and religious observance in schools. Under the Establishment Clause of the First Amendment, the U.S. Supreme Court has repeatedly held that public school officials may not sponsor or endorse prayer.

Key cases

In Engel v. Vitale (1962), the Court struck down a New York state-composed school prayer. In Abington School District v. Schempp (1963), it invalidated mandatory Bible reading. Later decisions barred clergy-led prayer at graduations (Lee v. Weisman, 1992) and student-led prayer at football games (Santa Fe ISD v. Doe, 2000). In Kennedy v. Bremerton (2022), the Court upheld a coach's personal prayer, emphasizing the distinction between private religious expression and state endorsement.

Private prayer

Students retain the right to pray privately and voluntarily, to form religious clubs under the Equal Access Act, and to discuss religion in assignments where appropriate.

Elsewhere

Practices differ internationally: England's state schools historically required a daily act of collective worship, while France's laïcité strictly excludes religious observance from public schools.
