Social theory

Field: Sociology, philosophy, anthropology

Social theory refers to ideas, arguments and frameworks used to analyse and explain social phenomena — the structures, institutions, interactions and changes of human societies. It spans classical thought from Marx, Weber and Émile Durkheim to contemporary critical, feminist, postcolonial and poststructuralist theories.

Classical foundations

Nineteenth-century thinkers responded to industrialisation, capitalism and secularisation: Marx analysed class and alienation, Durkheim solidarity and anomie, Weber rationalisation and authority, and Georg Simmel the forms of interaction. Edward Tylor and anthropologists extended theory to culture and evolution.

Twentieth century

Structural functionalism under Talcott Parsons, symbolic interactionism, exchange theory, and the interpretive turn of Clifford Geertz offered rival accounts of order and meaning; Marcel Mauss and Norbert Elias linked theory to gifts, bodies and long-term civilising processes.

Contemporary debates

Current social theory debates globalisation, power and identity, agency versus structure, and the decolonising of knowledge itself.
