Teri Ann Garr (December 11, 1944 – October 29, 2024) was an American actress and dancer known for her roles in some of the most popular films of the 1970s and 1980s. Her breakthrough came as Inga in Mel Brooks's Young Frankenstein (1974), followed by acclaimed performances in Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977), Tootsie (1982), for which she was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress, and Mr. Mom (1983).

Garr in the 1970s

Early life and career

Garr was born in Lakewood, Ohio, to vaudeville performers Eddie Garr and Phyllis Lind. Trained as a dancer, she appeared uncredited as a dancer in several Elvis Presley films in the 1960s before turning to acting, with appearances on television series such as Star Trek and The Sonny & Cher Comedy Hour.

Later work and illness

Garr continued to work in film and television, including a recurring role as Phoebe's mother on Friends. In 2002 she publicly revealed that she had been diagnosed with multiple sclerosis, and she became an ambassador for the National Multiple Sclerosis Society. She suffered a brain aneurysm in 2006 and largely retired from acting in 2011. Her memoir, Speedbumps: Flooring It Through Hollywood, was published in 2005. She died in Los Angeles in 2024 from complications of multiple sclerosis, aged 79.
