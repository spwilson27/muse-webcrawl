Léonin (also Magister Leoninus, Leonin) was a French composer of sacred vocal polyphony of the 12th century, associated with the Cathedral of Notre Dame in Paris. He is credited, in the treatise of the later theorist known as Anonymous IV, with compiling the Magnus liber organi ("Great Book of Organum"), a collection of two-voice organa for the liturgical year. Léonin is generally regarded as the first known composer of polyphonic organum in the modern sense and a leading figure of the Notre Dame school.

Life

Little is known of Léonin's life. Anonymous IV, an English student writing around 1280, describes him as an excellent organista (composer/singer of organum) and possibly a canon or chaplain at Notre Dame. The dates of his activity are placed roughly in the second half of the 12th century, coinciding with the construction of the new cathedral. His name suggests a connection to the name Leo, possibly as a diminutive used by colleagues.

Music

The Magnus liber contains organum duplum settings — two voices, a chant-bearing tenor and a florid upper voice — for the solo portions of responsorial chants used at Mass and the Divine Office throughout the church year. Léonin alternated passages of sustained-note (organum purum) writing with rhythmically patterned discant sections, and his settings introduced a new sense of large-scale musical organisation. According to Anonymous IV, the younger composer Pérotin later revised and expanded Léonin's book, adding substitute clausulae and works in three and four voices.
