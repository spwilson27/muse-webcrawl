Chinese Academy of Sciences

The Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS; 中国科学院) is the national academy of the People's Republic of China for the natural sciences. It is the world's largest research organisation, comprising around 100 research institutes, three universities, and a staff of roughly 70,000 people, including about 60,000 researchers. Founded in Beijing on 1 November 1949, CAS functions both as a national scientific think tank advising the Chinese government and as an operator of research institutes, and it is affiliated with the State Council of China.

History

CAS was established shortly after the founding of the People's Republic, absorbing institutes of the earlier Academia Sinica (which moved to Taiwan) and the Beiping Academy. It played a central role in China's "Two Bombs, One Satellite" programme and in major scientific achievements in mathematics, physics, chemistry and computing. The academy suffered disruption during the Cultural Revolution and was rebuilt during the reform era, launching the Knowledge Innovation Program in 1998 to modernise its institutes.

Organisation

The academy is organised into academic divisions — mathematics and physics, chemistry, life sciences and medicine, earth sciences, information sciences, and technological sciences — whose elected members (academicians) constitute one of China's highest academic honours. Its institutes operate major facilities including the Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical Telescope (FAST), synchrotron light sources and the Chinese Academy of Sciences' supercomputing network. CAS oversees the University of Science and Technology of China and the University of the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Research output

CAS regularly ranks among the world's top institutions by publication volume and in the Nature Index for high-quality natural-science output. It publishes dozens of academic journals and is a major holder of patents among Chinese research institutions.
