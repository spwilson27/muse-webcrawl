Christmas traditions

Christmas traditions worldwide include a mix of Christian and secular customs celebrating the birth of Jesus on December 25: decorated trees, gift-giving, carols, festive meals, Santa Claus (or Saint Nicholas, Father Christmas, and other gift-bringers), Advent observances, Nativity scenes, and lights.

Common customs

The Christmas tree originated in early modern Germany. Carol singing, Christmas cards (from 1843 in England), stockings, wreaths, mistletoe, and special foods — roast turkey or ham, mince pies, panettone, stollen — vary by country. Many traditions draw on older winter festivals such as Yule.

Around the world

In much of Europe gifts come on Christmas Eve; in Spain children traditionally receive gifts on Epiphany (January 6) from the Three Kings. Japan marks Christmas Eve with fried chicken and strawberry cake; the Philippines has one of the longest Christmas seasons.
