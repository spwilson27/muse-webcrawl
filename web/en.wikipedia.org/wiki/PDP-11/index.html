PDP-11

PDP-11
Manufacturer: Digital Equipment Corporation
Type: 16-bit minicomputer
Introduced: 1970
Predecessor: PDP-8

The PDP-11 is a series of 16-bit minicomputers sold by Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC) from 1970 into the late 1990s, one of the PDP series of computers. It is widely considered one of the most influential minicomputer designs ever made.

Architecture

The PDP-11 introduced a clean, orthogonal instruction set with eight general registers and a variety of addressing modes, together with the Unibus, which treated peripheral device registers as memory addresses. Its design strongly influenced later processors, including the Motorola 68000 and, indirectly, many others.

Software

The machine ran many operating systems, including DEC's RT-11, RSX-11 and RSTS/E. It was the first computer to run Unix, which was rewritten in the C programming language on a PDP-11; the popularity of Unix and C is inseparable from the success of the PDP-11.

Legacy

PDP-11s were used in laboratories, industrial control, telecommunications and education, and clones were produced in the Soviet Union and elsewhere. Millions of lines of code and generations of programmers were shaped by its elegant architecture.
