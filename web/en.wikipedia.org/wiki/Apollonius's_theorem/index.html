Apollonius's theorem

Apollonius's theorem is a theorem in geometry relating the length of a median of a triangle to the lengths of its sides. It is named after Apollonius of Perga.

Statement

In any triangle with sides a, b and c, if m is the median drawn to side a, then b² + c² = 2m² + a²/2. Equivalently, the sum of the squares of any two sides equals twice the square of half the third side plus twice the square of the median to that side.

Proof and relations

The theorem can be proved using coordinates, vectors, Stewart's theorem or the law of cosines applied to the two supplementary angles at the midpoint. It is closely related to the parallelogram law: applying it to the diagonals of a parallelogram yields the identity that the sum of the squares of the diagonals equals the sum of the squares of all four sides.

Stewart's theorem generalises Apollonius's theorem to a cevian that divides the opposite side in an arbitrary ratio rather than at its midpoint.
