Information visualization

Information visualization (or visualisation) is the study of visual representations of abstract data to reinforce human cognition. The abstract data include both numerical and non-numerical data, such as text and geographic information. It draws on human–computer interaction, computer science, graphics, visual design, psychology and business methods, and is closely related to data visualization, though the latter often focuses on statistical graphics while information visualization emphasises interactive exploration of abstract data sets.

Techniques

Common techniques include charts and graphs, tables, maps, treemaps, node-link diagrams, parallel coordinates and interactive dashboards. Interaction — filtering, zooming, brushing and linking multiple views — is central: visualisations are used not only to present conclusions but to explore data and form hypotheses.

History

The field builds on centuries of statistical graphics, from William Playfair's charts and Charles Joseph Minard's flow maps to Jacques Bertin's Sémiologie graphique (1967), and it emerged as a distinct research area with the growth of personal computing and graphical user interfaces in the 1980s and 1990s.
