Budō (Japanese: 武道, "martial way") is a Japanese term describing modern Japanese martial arts — disciplines such as judo, karate-dō, aikidō, kendō, kyūdō and iaidō — that evolved from the classical battlefield arts (bujutsu) of the samurai into paths of physical, mental and spiritual self-development.

Budō and bujutsu

The suffix -dō ("way" or "path") signals that the purpose of practice extends beyond combat effectiveness to character formation. During the Meiji period and after, educators such as Kanō Jigorō, founder of judo (1882), reframed martial training as physical education and moral cultivation suitable for schools and the public, in contrast to the secretive, combat-oriented traditions of the feudal era.

Philosophy and organisations

Budō emphasises etiquette (reigi), discipline, and concepts drawn from Zen Buddhism and Confucianism, such as mushin (no-mind) and continuous self-improvement. The Nippon Budokan in Tokyo, built for the 1964 Olympics, is the symbolic home of the modern budō disciplines, overseen by the Japanese Budo Association.
