The Plough and the Stars

Written by: Seán O'Casey
Premiered: 8 February 1926, Abbey Theatre, Dublin

The Plough and the Stars is a four-act play by the Irish writer Seán O'Casey, first performed at the Abbey Theatre in Dublin in 1926. It is the third of O'Casey's celebrated "Dublin Trilogy," following The Shadow of a Gunman (1923) and Juno and the Paycock (1924). Its title refers to the Plough and the Stars flag of the Irish Citizen Army.

Plot

Set in a Dublin tenement before and during the Easter Rising of 1916, the play follows young Nora Clitheroe, whose husband Jack is drawn into the Irish Citizen Army despite her desperate efforts to keep him home. Around them, a community of neighbours — the loquacious Fluther Good, the Covey, Bessie Burgess, and the dying Mollser — squabble and survive as the fighting engulfs the city. In the final act Nora loses her baby and her sanity after Jack is killed, while Bessie is shot sheltering her.

Controversy

The fourth performance was interrupted by riots; audience members objected to the play's unsparing portrayal of the tenement poor, the appearance of a prostitute character, and the display of the flag in a pub. W. B. Yeats defended the play from the stage, echoing his response to the earlier Playboy riots.
