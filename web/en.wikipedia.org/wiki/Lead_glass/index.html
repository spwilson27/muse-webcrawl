Lead glass

Other namesLead crystal, flint glass
Key componentLead(II) oxide (PbO), typically 18–40% by weight
Refractive index~1.5–1.7+, adjustable with PbO content

Lead glass, commonly called lead crystal or flint glass, is a variety of glass in which lead(II) oxide replaces the calcium oxide of typical potash glass. The addition of lead oxide — conventionally at least 24% for "full lead crystal" — raises the refractive index and dispersion, giving the glass its characteristic brilliance and sparkle when cut, and makes it softer and easier to work and engrave.

History

The technique was developed in England by George Ravenscroft, who patented lead glass in 1674 and produced it successfully at his Savoy glasshouse in London, solving the problem of crizzling that plagued earlier English glass. Lead crystal production became centred in centres such as Stourbridge, Waterford in Ireland, and Baccarat in France.

Uses and safety

Lead glass is used for fine tableware and decorative objects, for optical components, and — because lead strongly attenuates X-rays and gamma rays — for radiation shielding windows and the funnels of cathode-ray tubes. Because lead can leach into acidic liquids stored in crystal decanters, health authorities advise against long-term storage of food and drink in lead crystal; serving for short periods is generally considered low-risk.
