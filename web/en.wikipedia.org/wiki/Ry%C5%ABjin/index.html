Ryūjin (Japanese: 龍神, "Dragon God"), also known as Watatsumi and as the dragon king Ryūō, is the tutelary deity of the sea in Japanese mythology. Depicted as a great dragon, he rules the oceans from his coral palace, Ryūgū-jō, on the sea floor, and controls the tides with magical jewels: the ebb jewel (kanju) and the flood jewel (manju).

Mythology

In the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, Watatsumi's daughters Toyotama-hime and Tamayori-hime marry into the line of Japan's first emperor, making the sea god an ancestor of the imperial house. Legends tell how the Empress Jingū used Ryūjin's tide jewels to defeat a Korean fleet, and the tale of Urashima Tarō centres on a visit to his palace hosted by his daughter Otohime. In another famous episode, the monk Mongaku or the hero Tawara Tōda receives gifts from the dragon king, and the jellyfish is said to have lost its bones after failing Ryūjin as a messenger — a story explaining the creature's form.

Worship

Ryūjin is venerated at Shinto shrines associated with water and safe sea travel, such as Watatsumi Shrine in Tarumi, Kobe, and shrines on islands including Enoshima. In folk religion he is also a water deity invoked for rain and good fishing.
