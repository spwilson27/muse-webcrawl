Isabel, Princess Imperial of Brazil

Isabel, Princess Imperial
Born: 29 July 1846, Rio de Janeiro, Empire of Brazil
Died: 14 November 1921 (aged 75), Eu, France
Spouse: Prince Gaston, Count of Eu
Father: Pedro II of Brazil
Mother: Teresa Cristina of the Two Sicilies
House: Braganza

Dona Isabel (29 July 1846 – 14 November 1921), nicknamed "the Redemptress", was Princess Imperial of Brazil and heiress presumptive to the Brazilian throne. The eldest daughter of Emperor Pedro II, she served as regent of the empire three times while her father travelled abroad, and in that capacity signed the Lei Áurea (Golden Law) of 13 May 1888, which abolished slavery in Brazil.

Early life and marriage

Isabel was born in Rio de Janeiro and became heiress after the deaths of her two younger brothers in infancy. She received an education unusual for a woman of her time. In 1864 she married Prince Gaston of Orléans, Count of Eu, a grandson of King Louis-Philippe of France; they had three sons.

Regencies and abolition

During her regencies Isabel signed progressive legislation, including the Law of the Free Womb (1871), which freed children born to enslaved women thereafter. On 13 May 1888, as regent, she signed the Golden Law abolishing slavery outright, earning the devotion of former slaves and the hostility of the slave-owning planter class, whose defection weakened the monarchy.

Exile and death

Eighteen months after abolition, the empire was overthrown by a military coup that proclaimed the Republic (15 November 1889), and the imperial family went into exile in Europe. Isabel died in 1921 at the Château d'Eu in France. In 1953 her remains were repatriated to Brazil.
