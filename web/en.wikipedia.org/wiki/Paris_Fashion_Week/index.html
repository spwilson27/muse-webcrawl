Paris Fashion Week (French: Semaine de la mode de Paris) is a series of designer presentations held semi-annually in Paris, France, with spring/summer and autumn/winter events. It is the final and most prestigious of the "Big Four" global fashion weeks, following New York, London and Milan. The event is organised by the Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode and encompasses three principal calendars: Haute Couture (January and July), Menswear, and Womenswear ready-to-wear (prêt-à-porter), with dates determined for each season.

History

Paris has been a centre of fashion since the court of Louis XIV; couture salons presented collections to buyers and press from the time of Charles Frederick Worth in the 19th century. Coordinated shows were consolidated under the Chambre Syndicale from the early 20th century, and the modern unified Fashion Week dates from 1973, including the legendary "Battle of Versailles" benefit show that year, which pitted French couturiers against American designers and launched the American sportswear era. The legally protected haute couture designation — governed by rules on made-to-measure production, Paris ateliers and presentation schedules — is unique to Paris.

Format and significance

Major houses showing in Paris include Chanel, Dior, Louis Vuitton, Hermès, Saint Laurent, Balenciaga, Givenchy and Valentino, alongside international designers. Shows occupy monuments and purpose-built spaces across the city; invitations are limited to buyers, press, clients and celebrities, and presentations are now streamed worldwide. Paris Fashion Week sets the season's dominant trends and anchors the industry's commercial calendar of buying appointments and editorial coverage.

Criticism and change

The event has faced criticism over the environmental cost of fashion weeks, model health and body standards (France has legislated medical certificates and disclosure of retouched photographs), and disruption. In recent years the Fédération has promoted sustainability measures and digital/hybrid presentations, first adopted widely during the COVID-19 pandemic.
