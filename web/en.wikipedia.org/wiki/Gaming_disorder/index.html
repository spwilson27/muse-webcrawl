Gaming disorder

Gaming disorder is a pattern of persistent or recurrent gaming behaviour ("digital gaming" or "video-gaming") characterized by impaired control over gaming, increasing priority given to gaming over other activities, and continuation or escalation of gaming despite negative consequences, causing significant impairment in personal, family, social, educational or occupational functioning. It was included as a diagnosable condition in the World Health Organization's ICD-11, adopted in 2019.

Diagnosis

Under ICD-11, the behaviour pattern must be of sufficient severity to result in significant impairment and normally evident over at least 12 months, though the duration may be shortened if all diagnostic requirements are met and symptoms are severe. The American Psychiatric Association's DSM-5-TR lists "Internet Gaming Disorder" in its section for conditions requiring further study rather than as a formal diagnosis.

Debate

The classification has been debated. Supporters argue it legitimizes help-seeking and clinical services for a small minority of players whose gaming seriously harms their lives. Critics — including many games researchers — caution against pathologizing enthusiastic play, note weak and inconsistent evidence bases, and warn of moral panic and poor-quality studies. Prevalence estimates vary widely, generally around 1–3% of gamers depending on criteria.

Treatment

Treatment typically involves psychotherapy, especially cognitive behavioural therapy adapted from other behavioural addictions, treatment of comorbid conditions such as depression, ADHD and anxiety, and in some countries specialized inpatient programmes.
