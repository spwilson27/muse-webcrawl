The Salon d'Automne (Autumn Salon) is an annual art exhibition held in Paris, France, first organised in 1903. It was established as a reaction against the conservative policies of the official Paris Salon, and offered a platform for younger and more experimental artists outside the official Salon des Indépendants as well.

History

The first Salon d'Automne was held at the Petit Palais from 31 October to 8 December 1903, organised by a group including the architect Frantz Jourdain, with figures such as Georges Desvallières and Eugène Carrière involved. The founders aimed to provide opportunities for artists in all media — painting, sculpture, photography, engraving, applied arts and architecture — and to schedule the exhibition in the autumn so as not to compete with the spring salons.

The 1905 salon and Fauvism

The Salon is best remembered for its 1905 exhibition, where a room of paintings by Henri Matisse, André Derain, Maurice de Vlaminck and others, hung around a conventional Renaissance-style sculpture, provoked the critic Louis Vauxcelles to describe the sculptural work as "Donatello among the wild beasts" (Donatello parmi les fauves). The name Fauvism stuck to the movement.

Later years

The Salon d'Automne also gave important early exposure to Cubists, and in 1912 a dispute over foreign Cubist exhibitors led to questions in the French Chamber of Deputies. The exhibition continues to be held annually, in recent years on the Champs-Élysées.
