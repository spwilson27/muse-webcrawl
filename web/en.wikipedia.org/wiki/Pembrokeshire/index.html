Pembrokeshire

Country: Wales, United Kingdom
County town: Haverfordwest
Largest town: Milford Haven
Population: about 124,000

Pembrokeshire (Welsh: Sir Benfro) is a county in south-west Wales, bordered by Carmarthenshire to the east and Ceredigion to the north-east, and otherwise surrounded by sea. Its coastline, much of it protected as the Pembrokeshire Coast National Park, is famous for cliffs, beaches, and offshore islands such as Skomer and Ramsey.

Geography and economy

The county town is Haverfordwest; other towns include Tenby, Pembroke, Milford Haven, and Fishguard, from which ferries sail to Ireland. The Milford Haven Waterway hosts one of the largest ports in the UK and major energy terminals. Tourism, agriculture, and energy dominate the economy. The Preseli Hills in the north are the source of the bluestones used at Stonehenge.

History

The Norman lordship of Pembroke and the strong Flemish settlement of southern Pembrokeshire created a lasting cultural divide: the south, "Little England beyond Wales," became largely English-speaking, while the north remained Welsh-speaking — a boundary known as the Landsker Line. St Davids, Britain's smallest city, is a historic pilgrimage site around the shrine of Wales's patron saint.
