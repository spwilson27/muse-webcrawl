Ramón de Cardona

Ramón Folch de Cardona-Anglesola (1467 – 10 March 1522), Baron of Bellpuig, was a Catalan nobleman and military commander in the service of Ferdinand II of Aragon. He served as Viceroy of Sicily (1507–1509) and Viceroy of Naples (1509–1522), and commanded Spanish forces in Italy during the War of the League of Cambrai.

Career in Italy

A member of the powerful Catalan House of Cardona, Ramón de Cardona rose through service in the wars of Ferdinand the Catholic. After administering Sicily and Sardinia, he was appointed Viceroy of Naples in 1509, succeeding Gonzalo Fernández de Córdoba, the "Great Captain". As commander of the Spanish army in Italy during the War of the League of Cambrai, he fought the French in Lombardy and the Veneto. His army was defeated at the Battle of Ravenna in April 1512 — one of the bloodiest battles of the Italian Wars — although the French victory proved strategically fruitless. Cardona recovered, sacked Prato (an atrocity notorious in Italian memory), restored the Medici in Florence, and defeated the Venetians at La Motta in 1513.

Viceroy of Naples

As viceroy, Cardona presided over the consolidation of Spanish rule in the Kingdom of Naples. He kept his post through the transition from Ferdinand II to Charles I (the Emperor Charles V) and remained viceroy until his death in Naples in 1522. His body was transferred to Bellpuig in Catalonia, where his magnificent alabaster Renaissance tomb, attributed to the sculptor Giovanni Merliano da Nola, is considered one of the finest funerary monuments of the Catalan Renaissance.
