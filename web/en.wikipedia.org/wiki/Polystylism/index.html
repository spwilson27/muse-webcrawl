Polystylism

Type: Musical composition technique

Coined by: Alfred Schnittke

Polystylism is the use of multiple styles or techniques in a single work of music or literature. The term is most closely associated with the Soviet composer Alfred Schnittke (1934–1998), who described his method as combining elements from different historical periods and genres — Baroque concerto grosso, Viennese classicism, Romantic symphony, serialism, jazz, tango and film music — often through direct quotation and abrupt juxtaposition.

Schnittke's practice

Schnittke's First Symphony (1972) and his series of concerti grossi exemplify the technique: fragments of Beethoven, Haydn, Tchaikovsky and others collide within a single movement, creating an ironic, sometimes anguished commentary on musical history and on the cultural condition of the late Soviet Union. For Schnittke polystylism was not mere eclecticism but a way of holding the whole of musical tradition in view at once, with quotation functioning like memory.

Wider use

The word has been applied to earlier and later figures whose work deliberately mixes styles — Charles Ives, Gustav Mahler, Bernd Alois Zimmermann and Luciano Berio in music, and in literature to postmodern writers who blend genres and registers. It is related to, but more specific than, general notions of eclecticism and postmodern collage.
