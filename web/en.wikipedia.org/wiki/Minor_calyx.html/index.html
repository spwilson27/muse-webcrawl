Renal calyx

The renal calyces are cup-shaped chambers of the kidney through which urine passes. The minor calyces surround the apex of each renal papilla and collect urine draining from the papillary ducts; several minor calyces merge to form a major calyx, and the major calyces unite to form the renal pelvis, which leads into the ureter.

Function

Peristaltic contractions of smooth muscle in the walls of the calyces propel urine toward the renal pelvis and ureter.

Clinical significance

Calyces may become dilated in hydronephrosis, and kidney stones (calculi) may lodge within a calyx.
