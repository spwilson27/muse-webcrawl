John Grinder

John Thomas Grinder (born 10 January 1940) is an American linguist and author who, with Richard Bandler, co-created neuro-linguistic programming (NLP), a set of models and techniques for communication and personal development.

Early life and linguistics

Grinder served as a captain in the U.S. Army Special Forces in Europe during the Cold War, and later worked with the CIA. He earned a PhD in linguistics from the University of California, San Diego, in 1971, with a dissertation on deletion phenomena in English, and became a professor at the University of California, Santa Cruz.

Neuro-linguistic programming

At Santa Cruz, Grinder collaborated with undergraduate Richard Bandler to model the therapeutic communication patterns of Fritz Perls, Virginia Satir, and Milton H. Erickson. Their work produced The Structure of Magic (1975–76) and launched NLP. Grinder later developed "New Code NLP" with Judith DeLozier, emphasizing unconscious processes and state.
