Pemulwuy

Pemulwuy
Born: c. 1750, Botany Bay area, New South Wales
Died: 2 June 1802 (aged about 52), Sydney area
Known for: Aboriginal resistance leader of the Bidjigal people

The country around Botany Bay and the Georges River, Pemulwuy's homeland.

Pemulwuy (c. 1750 – 2 June 1802) was an Aboriginal Australian resistance leader of the Bidjigal clan of the Eora people, in the area around what is now Sydney. From 1790 until his death he led raids against the British colonists of New South Wales, in what is often described as a guerrilla war over land, food resources, and reprisals for violence and kidnappings. His name is sometimes rendered as meaning "earth" or "clay".

Resistance campaign

Pemulwuy first came to colonial attention in December 1790 when he speared Governor Arthur Phillip's gamekeeper, John McIntyre, who had a reputation for violence against Aboriginal people; McIntyre died of his wounds. Over the following decade Pemulwuy led attacks on farms and travellers around Botany Bay, the Georges River, Parramatta, and the Hawkesbury, burning crops and huts and taking food. In March 1797 he led a large body of warriors in the Battle of Parramatta, confronting soldiers and armed settlers; he was shot several times and captured, but escaped from hospital despite wearing a leg iron. In 1797 he was also shot and wounded in an earlier clash and briefly held.

Outlawry and death

Governor Philip Gidley King formally outlawed Pemulwuy in November 1801, offering a reward for his death or capture. On 2 June 1802 he was shot dead, reportedly by the settler Henry Hacking. His head was preserved and sent to England to Sir Joseph Banks; its whereabouts are now unknown, and repatriation has been sought. His son Tedbury continued resistance for several years afterward.

Legacy

Pemulwuy is remembered as one of the most prominent figures of Aboriginal resistance to colonisation. The Sydney suburb of Pemulwuy, in the city's west, is named after him, and his story is told in works such as Eric Willmot's novel Pemulwuy: The Rainbow Warrior (1987).
