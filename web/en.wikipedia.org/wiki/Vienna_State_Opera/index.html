Vienna State Opera

The Vienna State Opera (German: Wiener Staatsoper) is a historic opera house and opera company based in Vienna, Austria. The 1,709-seat Renaissance Revival venue was the first major building on the Vienna Ringstrasse, and it was built from 1861 to 1869 following plans drawn up by architects August Sicard von Sicardsburg and Eduard van der Nüll. It was opened as the Vienna Court Opera (Wiener Hofoper) in 1869 with a performance of Mozart's Don Giovanni.

History

The opera house was initially received unfavourably by the Viennese public and Emperor Franz Joseph I, criticism that reportedly contributed to van der Nüll's suicide and Sicardsburg's early death. The company flourished under directors including Gustav Mahler (1897–1907), whose reforms modernised staging and performance practice.

In March 1945 the building was badly damaged by Allied bombing and a subsequent fire gutted the auditorium and stage. Reconstruction followed, and the restored house reopened on 5 November 1955 with Beethoven's Fidelio under Karl Böhm.

The company today

The State Opera presents around 50 different operas and 15 ballets per season in a repertory system, with performances nearly every day from September to June. The Vienna Philharmonic is recruited from the opera's orchestra. Since 2020 the music director has been Philippe Jordan, and the house director Bogdan Roščić. The company is famous for its standing-room tickets and for the annual Vienna Opera Ball held in the auditorium each winter.
