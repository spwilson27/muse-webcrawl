Domus Municipalis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Domus Municipalis is a Romanesque civic building in Bragança, in northeastern Portugal, within the walls of the town's castle. Dating to the 12th or 13th century, it is considered unique in the Iberian Peninsula as a surviving example of Romanesque civil — rather than religious or military — architecture.

Description

The building has an irregular pentagonal plan built of granite and schist masonry. It consists of two storeys: a semi-subterranean cistern, designed to collect and store rainwater for the castle community, covered by a barrel vault, and an upper hall lit by twin-arched windows. The upper chamber served as a meeting place for the town's council and magistrates — hence the name Domus Municipalis, "municipal house".

History

The cistern is generally dated to the 12th century and the council hall added in the 13th. The building was restored in the 20th century and is classified as a National Monument of Portugal. It stands beside the Castle of Bragança and the pillory, forming part of the old citadel.
