Atherton is a town in the Metropolitan Borough of Wigan in Greater Manchester, England. Historically part of Lancashire, Atherton lies 5 miles (8.0 km) east of Wigan, 2 miles (3.2 km) north of Leigh, and 10.7 miles (17.2 km) northwest of Manchester. Along with neighbouring towns, it was part of the Lancashire coalfield and a centre of the cotton industry.

History

The name Atherton derives from Athelhere's farmstead or enclosure. For centuries the township was dominated by the Atherton family of Atherton Hall. Coal mining began in the area in the 17th century, drawing on shallow pits, and expanded greatly in the 19th century, when deep collieries were sunk and cotton mills — including the long-lived Howe Bridge Mills — were built. Nails and bolts were also manufactured in the town. The last deep pit closed in the 1960s, and the cotton industry also declined through the 20th century.

Governance

Atherton was an urban district in Lancashire until 1974, when it became part of the Metropolitan Borough of Wigan in the new county of Greater Manchester. It is represented in Parliament by the Leigh constituency, and locally by councillors for the Atherton ward and the Atherton South and Lilford ward.

Landmarks and culture

The parish church of St John the Baptist, built in 1878–79, stands in the town centre. Atherton is noted for its traditional celebrations, including the Atherton Carnival and the Bent and Bongs festival (a folk and beer festival whose name derives from local dialect humour comparing Atherton "bent" folk and Tyldesley "bongs" folk). The town has a railway station on the Manchester–Southport line.
