Oswald Avery

Died20 February 1955 (aged 77), Nashville, Tennessee, US
Known forAvery–MacLeod–McCarty experiment (1944): DNA carries heredity

Avery in 1937.

Oswald Theodore Avery Jr. (21 October 1877 – 20 February 1955) was a Canadian-American physician and medical researcher. The major part of his career was spent at the Rockefeller University Hospital in New York City. Avery was one of the first molecular biologists and a pioneer in immunochemistry.

He is best known for the experiment (published in 1944 with his co-workers Colin MacLeod and Maclyn McCarty) that showed that DNA alone could transform bacteria from one strain into another — the transforming principle first seen by Frederick Griffith in 1928 — and thus that DNA, not protein, is the material of heredity. The paper is one of the most consequential in twentieth-century biology, although its initial reception was cautious and Avery never received the Nobel Prize.

The transforming principle

Working on pneumococcal pneumonia for decades, Avery's laboratory painstakingly purified the transforming substance from virulent (smooth) bacteria and showed by chemical tests, enzymatic digestions and physical measurements that it was DNA. Published in the Journal of Experimental Medicine in February 1944, the result redirected biology toward nucleic acids and set the stage for the Hershey–Chase experiment (1952) and the double helix (1953). MacLeod died in 1944; Avery retired to Nashville, where he died in 1955.
