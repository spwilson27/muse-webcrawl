Nancy Kanwisher

Nancy G. Kanwisher
Born: 1958
Alma mater: MIT (BS, PhD)
Known for: Fusiform face area

Nancy G. Kanwisher (born 1958) is an American cognitive neuroscientist who is the Walter A. Rosenblith Professor of Cognitive Neuroscience in the Department of Brain and Cognitive Sciences at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and an investigator at the McGovern Institute for Brain Research. She is best known for identifying the fusiform face area (FFA), a region of the human fusiform gyrus that responds selectively to faces.

Career

Kanwisher received her bachelor's degree in biology and her PhD in cognitive psychology from MIT, the latter in 1986 under the supervision of Mary Potter, studying how people rapidly comprehend briefly presented pictures. After postdoctoral and faculty positions including at Harvard University, she joined the MIT faculty in 1997.

Research

Using functional magnetic resonance imaging, Kanwisher and her colleagues delineated a set of functionally specific cortical regions, including the fusiform face area, the parahippocampal place area (with Russell Epstein) for scenes, and the extrastriate body area for bodies. Her work is widely cited in debates about modularity of mind and the degree to which cortical function is innately specified versus learned. She is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, and received the NIH Director's Pioneer Award and other honours.
