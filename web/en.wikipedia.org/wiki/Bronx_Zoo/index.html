Bronx Zoo

The Bronx Zoo is a zoo in the Bronx, New York City. Operated by the Wildlife Conservation Society (WCS), it is among the largest zoos in the United States by area, covering 265 acres (107 ha) of parkland and naturalistic habitats. It opened on November 8, 1899, and is home to several thousand animals representing hundreds of species.

History

The zoo was founded by the New York Zoological Society (now WCS) on land in Bronx Park. Its first director, William T. Hornaday, designed it as a scientific institution devoted to the preservation of North American wildlife, and famously led the campaign that saved the American bison from extinction; a herd bred at the zoo was sent west to establish wild populations. The zoo's early history also includes the notorious 1906 exhibition of the Congolese man Ota Benga, an episode now widely condemned.

Exhibits

Signature exhibits include the Congo Gorilla Forest, a 6.5-acre rainforest habitat; JungleWorld, an indoor Asian jungle; Tiger Mountain; the Himalayan Highlands; and the Wild Asia Monorail. The zoo pioneered naturalistic, open habitat design, beginning with the African Plains in 1941.

Conservation

Through WCS, the zoo supports field conservation in dozens of countries and runs captive breeding programmes for endangered species, including snow leopards and Chinese alligators.
