Lee County is a county located in the U.S. state of North Carolina. As of the 2020 census, the population was 63,285. Its county seat is Sanford, which is also the county's largest city. The county lies roughly midway between the Raleigh–Durham metropolitan area and the Sandhills, and forms the core of the Sanford micropolitan statistical area.

History

Lee County was formed in 1907 from parts of Chatham and Moore counties, making it one of the last counties created in North Carolina. It was named in honor of Robert E. Lee, commanding general of the Confederate Army of Northern Virginia during the American Civil War. The new county developed around Sanford, a railroad town that grew as a junction of rail lines and a center for the region's brick-making, coal, and brownstone industries.

The Deep River coal field within the county was among the few sources of coal mined in North Carolina, and the surrounding clay deposits made the Sanford area a leading producer of brick. In the later twentieth century the economy diversified into manufacturing, agriculture, and services, and the county has grown as part of the broader expansion radiating from the Research Triangle.

Geography

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county has a total area of about 259 square miles, of which roughly 257 square miles is land and the remainder water. The terrain is rolling Piedmont country drained by the Deep River and its tributaries, including the Rocky River. Lee County is bordered by Chatham County to the north, Harnett County to the east, and Moore County to the south and west.

Communities

  • Sanford (county seat and largest city)
  • Broadway (town, shared with Harnett County)
  • Cumnock, Lemon Springs, Tramway, and other unincorporated communities

Demographics

The county's population grew from about 11,000 at its first census in 1910 to 63,285 in 2020. The community is home to a significant and long-established Hispanic population, and Sanford serves as a retail and employment center for the surrounding rural counties.

Economy and transport

Major industries include brick and tile manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, machinery, and agriculture. U.S. Route 1 and the freeway corridor of US 1/15/501 connect the county with Raleigh to the northeast and Pinehurst to the southwest; North Carolina Highway 87 links Sanford with Fayetteville. Raleigh Exec Jetport at Sanford-Lee County serves general aviation.
