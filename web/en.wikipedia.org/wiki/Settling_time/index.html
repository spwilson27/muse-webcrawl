Settling time

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Step response of a second-order system showing the settling time ts.

In control theory, the settling time of a dynamical system such as an amplifier or other device is the time elapsed from the application of an ideal instantaneous step input to the time at which the amplifier output has entered and remained within a specified error band.

Settling time includes a propagation delay plus the time required for the output to slew to the vicinity of the final value, recover from the overload condition associated with slewing, and finally settle to within the specified error.

Definition

Tay, Mareels and Moore (1998) defined settling time as "the time required for the response curve to reach and stay within a range of certain percentage (usually 5% or 2%) of the final value."

Systems with energy storage cannot respond instantaneously and will exhibit transient responses when they are subjected to inputs or disturbances.

Details

The settling time for a second-order, underdamped system responding to a step response can be approximated if the damping ratio ζ << 1 by

Ts ≈ −ln(tolerance fraction) / (ζωn)

A general expression for the settling time of a second-order system underdamped responding to a step input is

Ts = −ln(√(1−ζ2) × tolerance) / (ζωn)

Thus, settling time to within 2% (0.02) is:

Ts ≈ −ln(0.02) / (ζωn) ≈ 3.9 / (ζωn) ≈ 4Tc

where Tc is the time constant of the system.
