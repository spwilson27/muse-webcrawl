Bet Dwarka (also spelt Beyt Dwarka) is an island in the Gulf of Kutch, off the coast of Gujarat, India, situated about 3 km from the port of Okha and some 30 km north of the sacred city of Dwarka. In Hindu tradition it is identified as the site of the residence of Lord Krishna during his years as ruler of Dwarka, while the mainland city housed his courts and administration.

Religious significance

The island's principal temple is dedicated to Krishna and is traditionally said to stand where his palace stood. According to legend, Krishna's childhood friend Sudama visited him here bearing a humble gift of beaten rice, and devotees still offer rice at the temple in memory of the episode. Bet Dwarka forms part of the Char Dham and Dwarka pilgrimage circuits, and most visitors reach it by ferry from Okha; a bridge, the Sudarshan Setu, completed in 2024, now links Okha to the island.

Archaeology

Marine archaeological explorations off Bet Dwarka have recovered pottery, anchors and other artefacts indicating an ancient port settlement, and the island has yielded remains attributed to the late Harappan period and to early historic times. Finds from the surrounding waters have been discussed in connection with traditions about the submerged city of Dwarka, though interpretations remain debated among archaeologists.
