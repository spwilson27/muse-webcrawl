My Ladye Nevells Booke

My Ladye Nevells Booke is a music manuscript containing keyboard pieces by the English composer William Byrd, compiled in 1591. It is one of the most important collections of early keyboard music and, together with the Fitzwilliam Virginal Book and Parthenia, a principal source for Elizabethan virginal music. The manuscript was copied by the scrivener John Baldwin and dedicated to Lady Elizabeth Nevell; Byrd himself is thought to have supervised its preparation, making it a near-autograph source.

Contents

The book contains 42 pieces, all by Byrd, including pavans and galliards, variations on popular tunes such as "Walsingham" and "The Carman's Whistle", fantasias, grounds, and character pieces such as "The Battell" — a programmatic depiction of a battle — and "The Bells". The collection opens with the ten pavan-galliard pairs for which Byrd was renowned and shows the full range of his keyboard style. The dedicatee was Elizabeth, Lady Nevell, wife of Sir Henry Nevell of Billingbear House in Berkshire, to whom Byrd also dedicated the "Nevell" ground and the pavan and galliard named for her.

History

The manuscript, dated 11 September 1591, remained with the Nevell family and later passed through several private hands; it was acquired by the British Museum in 1952 and is now held by the British Library. A complete facsimile and modern edition was published in 1969, edited by Hilda Andrews with a foreword by Sir Adrian Boult.
