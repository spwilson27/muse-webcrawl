Wasp

A wasp is any insect of the narrow-waisted suborder Apocrita of the order Hymenoptera that is neither a bee nor an ant. The term is paraphyletic and covers an enormous diversity: tens of thousands of described species, from the familiar social yellowjackets and hornets of the family Vespidae to hornets, paper wasps, and vast numbers of solitary hunting wasps and parasitoid wasps.

Biology

Wasps are characterized by the narrow petiole or "wasp waist" joining the thorax and abdomen. Most species are predators or parasitoids: social wasps hunt other insects to feed their larvae, while parasitoid species lay eggs in or on hosts that the larvae consume — a mode of life that makes wasps important natural controllers of pest insects, a fact that moved Darwin to remark on the theological problem posed by the ichneumon wasps. Social species such as the German wasp and hornets build papery nests from chewed wood fibre and can sting repeatedly in defence of the colony.

Ecology and importance

Beyond pest control, many wasps are significant pollinators — fig wasps are the obligate pollinators of figs — and they serve as prey for birds and other wildlife. Some social species become nuisances or invasive pests outside their native ranges, notably in New Zealand and Australia.
