Trench coat

TypeRaincoat / overcoat
MaterialsGabardine, cotton drill, leather, poplin
OriginUnited Kingdom, 19th–early 20th century
Claimed byBurberry; Aquascutum

A trench coat is a waterproof heavy-duty fabric raincoat made of gabardine or similar material, generally featuring a removable insulated lining, raglan sleeves, and military-derived details such as epaulettes, a storm flap, throat latch, D-rings, and a belted waist. The classic trench coat is khaki or beige, double-breasted, and falls below the knee.

Origins

Two British firms claim to have invented the trench coat. Aquascutum, founded in 1851, supplied waterproof coats to officers in the Crimean War. Burberry, founded by Thomas Burberry in 1856, invented the breathable waterproof twill gabardine in 1879, and in 1901 submitted a design for an officer's raincoat to the War Office; Burberry's "Tielocken" coat of 1912 was adapted for the trenches of the First World War, giving the garment its name. Officers purchased the coats privately as kit, and the practical details — shoulder straps for rank insignia, D-rings for equipment, deep pockets for maps — were retained in civilian versions.

In popular culture

Returning officers wore their trench coats into civilian life, and the garment became a symbol of rugged sophistication. Humphrey Bogart wore one in Casablanca (1942), and trench coats became the uniform of film noir detectives, spies, and private eyes; Peter Falk's rumpled version in Columbo and Inspector Gadget's cartoon coat are later variations. The trench coat remains a staple of fashion houses, with Burberry's heritage designs — the Kensington and Chelsea fits — anchoring the brand's identity.
