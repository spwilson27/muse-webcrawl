Central Applications Office

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Central Applications Office (CAO) (Irish: An Lár-Oifig Iontrála) processes applications for undergraduate courses at Irish higher education institutions. It is a non-profit company controlled by those institutions, and is based at Tower House, Eglinton Street, Galway. Applications are submitted online through the CAO website, and places are allocated on the basis of Leaving Certificate examination points under the CAO points system.

Operation

Applicants list up to ten Level 8 (honours degree) and ten Level 6/7 course choices in order of preference. When results are published in August, offers are issued in rounds: an applicant receives an offer of the highest-preference course for which they meet the points requirement and for which a place is available, and may accept it or wait in hope of a higher-preference offer in a later round. Points cut-offs for each course are determined by supply and demand rather than set in advance, and are widely reported in Irish media each year.

The CAO also handles applications for many further-education and mature-student routes, and administers the "Change of Mind" facility, which allows applicants to revise their course choices up to a deadline in early July without charge after the initial closing date (normally 1 February).

Points system

Leaving Certificate grades are converted to points — up to 625 points are available, with bonus points awarded for higher-level mathematics — and offers are made in order of points until a course is full. The system is frequently debated in Ireland, particularly regarding the pressure of the points race and the influence of grind schools.

History

The CAO was established in 1976 to streamline admissions to the Irish universities, and its remit later expanded to cover the institutes of technology and other higher education institutions as the sector grew.
