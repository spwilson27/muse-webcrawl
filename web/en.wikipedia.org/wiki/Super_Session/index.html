Super Session is a 1968 blues-rock album by Mike Bloomfield, Al Kooper, and Stephen Stills, conceived by Kooper as a recorded jam session modelled on jazz practice. It became a major commercial success, reaching the top twenty of the Billboard album chart, and is credited with popularising the "supergroup" concept in rock music.

Background and recording

Al Kooper, formerly of Blood, Sweat & Tears, persuaded Columbia Records to fund two days of studio jamming with guitarist Mike Bloomfield, with whom he had played on Bob Dylan's Highway 61 Revisited sessions and in the Blues Project. Bloomfield, suffering from insomnia, left after the first day and could not be roused for the second. Kooper filled the gap by calling in Stephen Stills of Buffalo Springfield, who was between bands. The Bloomfield tracks form one side of the album and the Stills tracks the other, with a rhythm section of Harvey Brooks (bass) and Eddie Hoh (drums) and Kooper on keyboards and vocals.

Music

The Bloomfield side features extended electric blues jams on songs such as "Albert's Shuffle" and "Stop". The Stills side includes a celebrated eleven-minute version of Donovan's "Season of the Witch" and a cover of "You Don't Love Me". The different characters of the two guitarists — Bloomfield's searing blues phrasing against Stills's more textured approach — give the album its enduring appeal. Reissues add bonus tracks, including material from the sessions with horn overdubs removed.

Legacy

Super Session peaked at No. 12 on the Billboard 200 and was certified gold in the United States. Its success helped establish the market for all-star collaborative albums; Kooper followed it with The Live Adventures of Mike Bloomfield and Al Kooper (1969), recorded at the Fillmore West. The album remains a landmark of late-1960s blues rock and a showcase for Bloomfield, whose career and health were already in decline before his death in 1981.
