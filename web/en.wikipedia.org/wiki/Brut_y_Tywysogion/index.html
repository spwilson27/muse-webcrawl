Brut y Tywysogion ("Chronicle of the Princes") is a Welsh chronicle that is one of the most important primary sources for the history of medieval Wales. It is a Middle Welsh translation of a lost Latin chronicle, probably compiled at the Cistercian abbey of Strata Florida, and continues the annals begun in the Annales Cambriae tradition. The chronicle covers the years from 682 — the death of Cadwaladr — to the death of Llywelyn ap Gruffudd in 1282 and the end of independent Welsh princely rule; a continuation, the Brut y Saeson tradition and later additions, carries the record to 1332.

Versions

The chronicle survives in two main versions: the earlier text preserved in Peniarth Manuscript 20 (c. 1330) and the version in the Red Book of Hergest (c. 1400). Its entries are brief annals recording the deaths of princes and churchmen, battles, plagues, storms and portents, but they are fuller and more reliable for the 12th and 13th centuries, when the chronicle becomes a narrative of the struggles of the princes of Gwynedd, Deheubarth and Powys against each other and against the kings of England.
