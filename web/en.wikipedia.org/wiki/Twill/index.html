Twill

Twill is one of the three fundamental types of textile weave, along with plain weave and satin. It is characterized by a pattern of diagonal parallel ribs, created by passing the weft yarn over one or more warp yarns and then under two or more warp yarns — most commonly a 2/2 twill — with an offset ("step") on each successive row that forms the diagonal line.

Properties

Twill fabrics drape well, resist wrinkling, and hide soil better than plain weaves; the diagonal structure allows tighter packing of yarns, producing firm, durable cloth. The face and back differ: the side with the clearer diagonal (the "technical face") is usually the outside. Twills may be warp-faced or balanced, and the diagonal may run from bottom-left to top-right (a Z-twill) or the reverse (an S-twill).

Familiar twills

Denim is a warp-faced cotton twill with indigo-dyed warp; other classic twills include gabardine, chino, drill, serge, houndstooth, and herringbone (a broken twill). Tweed is commonly woven as a twill.
