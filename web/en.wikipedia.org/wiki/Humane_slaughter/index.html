Humane slaughter

Humane slaughter is the killing of animals for food in a manner intended to spare them suffering, fear and pain. In most jurisdictions this legally requires that animals be rendered insensible — typically by stunning with a captive bolt, electrical current or exposure to carbon dioxide — before being bled, so that death occurs while unconscious.

Law and practice

The United States Humane Methods of Slaughter Act (1958, amended 1978) and European Union Regulation (EC) No 1099/2009 set standards for handling, restraint and stunning at slaughterhouses. Animal scientist Temple Grandin has been influential in designing curved chutes and auditing systems that reduce stress in livestock handling.

Religious slaughter debate

Methods such as kosher shechita and some forms of halal slaughter traditionally require the animal to be conscious at the cut, creating tension with stunning requirements; many jurisdictions allow exemptions, while others require pre- or post-cut stunning. The debate engages questions of animal welfare science, religious freedom and food labelling.
