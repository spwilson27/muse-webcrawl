Dragon Quest, previously published in North America as Dragon Warrior, is a Japanese console role-playing game franchise created by Yuji Horii and published by Square Enix (formerly Enix). The games feature character and monster designs by Dragon Ball artist Akira Toriyama and music by Koichi Sugiyama. The first game appeared for the Famicom/NES in 1986, and the series has sold over 85 million copies worldwide, becoming a cultural institution in Japan.

Gameplay

Dragon Quest games feature turn-based combat viewed in first person, parties of player characters exploring an overworld, towns, and dungeons, and random encounters with monsters such as the iconic blue Slime, the series mascot. Core conventions — menu-driven battles, experience points and levels, saving at churches, and orchestral soundtracks — were largely established by the first entry and influenced the entire Japanese RPG genre, including Final Fantasy.

Main series

Mainline entries run from Dragon Quest (1986) through Dragon Quest XI: Echoes of an Elusive Age (2017), with Dragon Quest XII announced in 2021 for the franchise's 35th anniversary. The "Zenithian trilogy" (IV–VI) and the early Erdrick trilogy (I–III) form connected arcs.

Cultural impact

In Japan, launch days for new mainline titles drew such crowds that an urban legend claims a law restricted releases to weekends; the series' music has been performed by major orchestras, and Dragon Quest imagery appears on merchandise and even utility covers. Spin-offs include Dragon Quest Monsters, Dragon Quest Builders, and the MMO Dragon Quest X.
