Easter egg

Easter eggs, also called Paschal eggs, are eggs that are decorated for the Christian festival of Easter, which celebrates the resurrection of Jesus Christ. Traditionally chicken eggs are hard-boiled and dyed or painted, though chocolate eggs and plastic eggs filled with treats are now common in many countries.

History

Decorating eggshells is an ancient practice: engraved ostrich eggs have been found in Africa dating back some 60,000 years, and decorated eggs featured in spring festivals in pre-Christian Europe. The egg became a symbol of rebirth and new life. Early Christians in Mesopotamia stained eggs red in memory of the blood of Christ, and the practice spread through the Orthodox and later Western churches. During Lent, when eggs were traditionally forbidden, surplus eggs were boiled to preserve them and eaten at Easter.

Traditions

In many cultures eggs are dyed with natural colourings such as onion skins. The intricate wax-resist decorated eggs of Ukraine and other Slavic countries are known as pysanky. Common Easter customs include the egg hunt, in which decorated or chocolate eggs are hidden for children to find; egg rolling, famously held on the lawn of the White House in Washington, D.C.; and egg tapping, a game in which players knock eggs together.

The most luxurious Easter eggs were the jewelled Fabergé eggs made for the Russian imperial family between 1885 and 1917.

Other meanings

In modern media, an "Easter egg" is also a hidden message, feature or joke deliberately placed in a film, video game, book or piece of software for users to discover.
