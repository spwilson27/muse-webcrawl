Gobo (lighting)

A gobo is an object placed inside or in front of a light source to control the shape of the emitted light and its shadow. Derived from the onset of "go-between" or "goes before optics", gobos have a wide variety of applications in theatre, film and architectural lighting.

In theatrical profile spotlights, gobos are thin metal or glass discs etched or cut with a pattern — leaves, windows, stars or abstract "breakup" textures — which is projected onto the stage or scenery. Glass gobos can reproduce full-colour photographic images. In film and television, larger flags and cutters placed in front of lights are sometimes also called gobos.

The term is also used in photography for light modifiers, and in sound for acoustic isolation panels placed between instruments ("go-betweens").
