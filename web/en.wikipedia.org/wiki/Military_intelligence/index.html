Military intelligence

Military intelligence is a military discipline that uses information collection and analysis approaches to provide guidance and direction to assist commanders in their decisions. This aim is achieved by providing an assessment of data from a range of sources, directed towards the commanders' mission requirements or responding to questions as part of operational or campaign planning.

Process

The intelligence cycle traditionally comprises direction, collection, processing, analysis and dissemination. Collection disciplines include signals intelligence (SIGINT), human intelligence (HUMINT), imagery intelligence (IMINT), open-source intelligence (OSINT) and measurement and signature intelligence (MASINT).

Levels

Intelligence work is conducted at strategic, operational and tactical levels. Strategic intelligence informs national policy and long-term planning; operational intelligence supports campaigns; tactical intelligence supports units in contact with the enemy.

History

Organised military intelligence dates to antiquity — Sun Tzu's The Art of War devotes a chapter to espionage — but professional intelligence staffs emerged in the 19th and 20th centuries, with major expansion during the two World Wars and the Cold War.
