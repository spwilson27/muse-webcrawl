Portland Building

The Portland Building, alternatively referenced as the Portland Municipal Services Building, is a 15-story municipal office building in downtown Portland, Oregon. Designed by architect Michael Graves and completed in 1982, it is considered the first major example of postmodern architecture in the United States and one of the most influential — and controversial — buildings of its era.

Design

Graves won the commission through a design competition in 1980. Rejecting the glass-and-steel International Style, he produced a blocky, cube-like tower wrapped in cream-coloured tile with contrasting decorative elements: stylised blue tile "pilasters", small square windows, and enormous ornamental garlands (swags) on the side facades. The design deliberately echoed classical architecture in flat, graphic form — a hallmark of architectural postmodernism. In 1985 the copper hammered-statue Portlandia by Raymond Kaskey — the second-largest copper repoussé statue in the United States after the Statue of Liberty — was installed over the third-floor entrance.

Reception and problems

The building won an AIA honor award and was hailed internationally as a landmark of postmodernism. Users, however, complained about dark interiors caused by the tiny windows, and the building suffered from water leaks and structural shortcomings almost from the start. In 2011 it was added to the National Register of Historic Places despite being less than 30 years old.

Renovation

Faced with the cost of demolition versus repair, the City of Portland chose a full reconstruction: between 2017 and 2020 the building was stripped to its frame and rebuilt with a new weatherproof skin, larger windows and an aluminium rainscreen that preserved Graves's design motifs while fixing the leaks and energy performance. City offices reopened in the renovated building in 2020.
