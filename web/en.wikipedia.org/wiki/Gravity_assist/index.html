Gravity assist

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A gravity assist: the spacecraft trades momentum with the planet.

A gravity assist (or slingshot) is a manoeuvre in which a spacecraft flies close by a planet and uses its gravity to change speed and direction without burning fuel. Passing behind the planet in its orbit adds some of the planet's orbital momentum to the spacecraft; passing in front removes it. The planet's own velocity changes by an immeasurably small amount in exchange.

Gravity assists made the outer Solar System reachable: Voyager 2 used a rare alignment to tour all four giant planets, Galileo looped past Venus and Earth twice to reach Jupiter, and Cassini-Huygens used four assists on its way to Saturn. The technique also works in reverse — to slow down — as when MESSENGER shed energy to fall into orbit around Mercury, and it can change a trajectory's plane, as when Ulysses swung over Jupiter's poles to study the Sun's.
