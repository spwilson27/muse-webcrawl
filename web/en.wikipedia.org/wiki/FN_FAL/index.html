FN FAL

The FN FAL (French: Fusil Automatique Léger, "Light Automatic Rifle") is a battle rifle designed in Belgium by Dieudonné Saive and manufactured by FN Herstal. Chambered for the 7.62×51mm NATO cartridge, it was one of the most widely used rifles of the 20th century, adopted by more than 90 countries and earning the nickname "the right arm of the Free World" during the Cold War.

The FN FAL in its standard configuration.

History

Developed in the late 1940s and early 1950s, the FAL was initially designed around the German 7.92×33mm Kurz intermediate cartridge and later the British .280 round, before NATO standardisation led to its adaptation to 7.62×51mm. It entered service in 1953 and was produced under licence in many countries, including the United Kingdom (as the L1A1 Self-Loading Rifle), Canada, Australia, Austria, Brazil, and Argentina.

Design

The FAL is a gas-operated, tilting-breechblock rifle usually fed from a 20-round detachable box magazine. Variants include fixed-stock and folding-stock models, carbine versions, and heavy-barrelled light machine gun versions. It has seen service in numerous conflicts, including the Falklands War, in which both sides used versions of the rifle.
