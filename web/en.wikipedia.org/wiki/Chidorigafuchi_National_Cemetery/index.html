Overview

Chidorigafuchi National Cemetery (Chidorigafuchi Senbotsusha Boen) is a Japanese national cemetery and memorial in Chiyoda, Tokyo, for the unidentified remains of Japanese who died during the Second World War. Established in 1959, it lies near Chidorigafuchi, the moat northwest of the Imperial Palace, an area famous for cherry blossoms.

Description

The cemetery centres on a hexagonal memorial hall, the Rokkokudo, housing a large coffin-shaped ossuary (a ceramic casket from Saga prefecture) containing the first remains repatriated to the site; further remains of unidentified servicemen and civilians recovered from overseas battlefields, the former Soviet Union and Iwo Jima are interred in vaults on the grounds. More than 350,000 sets of remains are enshrined there.

Ceremonies

An annual government memorial ceremony is held each late May, traditionally attended by the Emperor of Japan, members of the Imperial Family and the Prime Minister. Unlike the controversial Yasukuni Shrine, Chidorigafuchi is a secular state cemetery, and it has sometimes been proposed as the basis of an expanded national memorial.
