Plant breeding

Plant breeding
FieldAgronomy, genetics
GoalImproved crop varieties (yield, resistance, quality)
MethodsSelection, hybridisation, mutation breeding, marker-assisted selection, genetic engineering
Key figuresGregor Mendel, Luther Burbank, Norman Borlaug

Plant breeding is the science of changing the traits of plants in order to produce desired characteristics — higher yield, disease and pest resistance, drought tolerance, better nutrition or flavour. It has been practised, at first unconsciously, since the domestication of crops some ten thousand years ago, and scientifically since the rediscovery of Mendel's laws of heredity around 1900.

History

Early farmers selected seed from the best plants each season, gradually transforming wild grasses into wheat, maize and rice. Nineteenth-century figures such as Luther Burbank produced hundreds of varieties by selection and crossing. After 1900, genetics-based breeding produced hybrid maize, whose "hybrid vigour" transformed yields. Norman Borlaug's dwarf wheat varieties powered the Green Revolution of the 1960s and won him the Nobel Peace Prize in 1970; parallel work at IRRI produced IR8 rice.

Methods

Conventional breeding uses selection within populations, hybridisation between varieties or related species (sometimes aided by embryo rescue), backcrossing to move a single trait into an elite line, and mutation breeding with radiation or chemicals — which has produced thousands of released varieties. Polyploidy is induced to cross species barriers, as in triticale. Modern breeding adds marker-assisted selection and genomic selection, doubled haploids, speed breeding, and biotechnology: transgenic (GM) crops and gene editing with CRISPR. Breeding programmes maintain genetic diversity through seed banks and crop wild relatives.

Issues

Debates surround intellectual property in seeds (plant breeders' rights and patents), the dominance of seed companies, the genetic erosion of landraces, and the regulation of genetically modified and gene-edited crops. Plant breeding remains central to food security under climate change.
