Coromandel screen

A black lacquer Coromandel screen with carved and coloured decoration.

A Coromandel screen is a Chinese folding screen coated in dark brown or black lacquer, carved and inlaid or painted with coloured designs. The technique, known in Chinese as kuancai ("carved colours"), flourished in the late Ming and Qing dynasties, especially from the late 17th century. The name "Coromandel" comes from India's Coromandel Coast, through whose ports such screens were shipped to European markets by the Dutch and English East India Companies.

Technique

The wooden panels were built up with many layers of lacquer, which was then cut away in designs — landscapes, figures, flowers, birds and pavilion scenes — and the recesses filled with coloured pigments, lacquer and sometimes mother-of-pearl or other materials, producing a relief surface that was then polished. Screens commonly had six, eight or twelve panels.

Use in Europe

Coromandel screens became fashionable furnishings in European aristocratic interiors in the late 17th and 18th centuries. Because the panels were large and durable, they were frequently cut up and repurposed: lacquer panels were applied as veneer to cabinets and commodes (a practice associated with Parisian ébénistes), and whole screens were used as wall panelling. The designer Coco Chanel was famously fond of Coromandel screens, collecting dozens of them and using them throughout her Paris apartment at 31 Rue Cambon.
