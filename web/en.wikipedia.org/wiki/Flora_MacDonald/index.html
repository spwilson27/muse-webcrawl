Flora MacDonald

Flora MacDonald (Gaelic: Fionnghal NicDhòmhnaill; 1722 – 5 March 1790) was a member of the Clan Macdonald of Sleat, best remembered for helping Charles Edward Stuart, the Jacobite pretender known as Bonnie Prince Charlie, evade government troops after his defeat at the Battle of Culloden in April 1746.

The escape

After Culloden, Charles fled to the Outer Hebrides. In June 1746 MacDonald agreed to help him escape from Benbecula to the Isle of Skye. The prince was disguised as her Irish maid, "Betty Burke", and they crossed by boat to Skye, from where he continued his flight. MacDonald was arrested and imprisoned for a time in the Tower of London, but was released under a general indemnity in 1747.

Later life

She married Allan MacDonald of Kingsburgh and emigrated with him to North Carolina in 1774, where her husband fought as a Loyalist officer in the American War of Independence. She returned to Scotland in 1779 and died at Kingsburgh, Skye, in 1790. The Skye Boat Song commemorates the escape.
