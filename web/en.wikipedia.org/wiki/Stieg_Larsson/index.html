Karl Stig-Erland "Stieg" Larsson (Swedish: [stiːɡ ˈlɑ̌ːʂɔn]; August 15, 1954 – November 9, 2004) was a Swedish writer, journalist, and anti-fascist activist. He is best known for writing the Millennium trilogy of crime novels, which were published posthumously and became international bestsellers, selling over 100 million copies worldwide.

Life and journalism

Larsson was born in Skelleftehamn, Västerbotten, and raised largely by his grandparents in the Swedish north. He worked for the TT news agency and became Sweden's leading expert on far-right and neo-Nazi movements. In 1995 he co-founded the anti-racist magazine Expo, which he edited; the work brought him repeated death threats.

The Millennium trilogy

In his spare time Larsson wrote crime novels featuring the disgraced journalist Mikael Blomkvist — whose magazine Millennium was modeled on Expo — and Lisbeth Salander, a brilliant, antisocial hacker and survivor of abuse. The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo (Swedish title Män som hatar kvinnor, "Men Who Hate Women") was published in 2005, months after Larsson died of a heart attack at his office in Stockholm, aged 50. Two sequels followed, and all three were adapted into acclaimed Swedish films in 2009 and a Hollywood version in 2011. Larsson's manuscripts later became the subject of a legal dispute between his estate and his long-term partner, Eva Gabrielsson; David Lagercrantz continued the series from 2015.
