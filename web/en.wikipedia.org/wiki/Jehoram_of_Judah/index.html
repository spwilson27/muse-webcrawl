Jehoram of Judah

Jehoram
King of Judah
Reign: c. 849–842 BC (approx.)
Predecessor: Jehoshaphat
Successor: Ahaziah
Father: Jehoshaphat
Spouse: Athaliah

Jehoram was the fifth king of the southern Kingdom of Judah.

Jehoram (Hebrew: יְהוֹרָם, Yəhōrām, "Yahweh is exalted"; also called Joram) was the fifth king of the southern Kingdom of Judah and the son of King Jehoshaphat. According to the biblical account, he was 32 years old when he began to reign and reigned eight years in Jerusalem, in the mid-ninth century BC.

Reign

Jehoram's marriage to Athaliah, a daughter of the Israelite king Ahab and Jezebel, sealed an alliance between Judah and the northern Kingdom of Israel but also, in the biblical writers' judgement, brought the worship practices of the house of Ahab into Jerusalem. The Book of Kings reports that Jehoram "walked in the way of the kings of Israel" and built high places in the hill country of Judah.

Upon ascending the throne, Jehoram is said to have killed all six of his brothers, along with some of the princes of Israel, to secure his rule. During his reign Edom revolted against Judah's dominance and set up its own king, and the city of Libnah likewise rebelled. Second Chronicles further reports an invasion by Philistines and Arabs who plundered the royal palace, and records a letter from the prophet Elijah rebuking Jehoram and predicting his death from a severe disease of the bowels, from which he duly died after two years of illness.

Chronology and sources

The principal sources for Jehoram's reign are 2 Kings 8 and 2 Chronicles 21, whose notices differ in emphasis. Chronological reconstruction is complicated by a probable co-regency with his father Jehoshaphat; scholars such as William F. Albright and Edwin R. Thiele proposed slightly different dates for his reign. He was succeeded by his son Ahaziah, and after Ahaziah's death his widow Athaliah seized the throne of Judah.
