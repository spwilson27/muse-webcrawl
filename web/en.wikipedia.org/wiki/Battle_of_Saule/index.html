Battle of Saule

The Battle of Saule was fought on 22 September 1236 between the Livonian Brothers of the Sword and a coalition of Samogitian and Semigallian peoples, near Šiauliai in present-day Lithuania (its exact location is debated). The crusading order was annihilated; its master, Volkwin von Naumburg, was killed along with most of his knights. The defeat destroyed the Sword Brothers as an independent order and forced the survivors into the Teutonic Order the following year.

The battle

Returning from a raid into Samogitia laden with plunder, the crusader army — joined reluctantly by Christian converts and a contingent of crusaders from Holstein — was forced to fight in boggy terrain unsuitable for heavy cavalry. The Lithuanian and Semigallian light troops attacked from the marshes and woods; the knights, dismounted or trapped in the mire, were cut down. Volkwin and some 48–60 brother-knights perished, along with large numbers of their men.

Aftermath

News of the disaster triggered uprisings among the Curonians and Semigallians and imperilled the entire Livonian crusade. In 1237 the remnant of the order was absorbed into the Teutonic Order as its Livonian branch. The victory is commemorated in Lithuania and Latvia; 22 September is marked as the Day of Baltic Unity, and the battle also inspired the name of the Lithuanian city of Šiauliai ("Saulė" meaning "sun").

Note: this battle is entirely unrelated to the Battle of Salsu in Korea (612 AD), despite the similar name.
