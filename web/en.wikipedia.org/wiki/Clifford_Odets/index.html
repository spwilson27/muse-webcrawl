Clifford Odets (July 18, 1906 – August 14, 1963) was an American playwright, screenwriter, and director, the foremost exponent of socially conscious drama of the 1930s. His plays for New York's Group Theatre, written in a distinctive poetic vernacular, dramatized the struggles of working-class and immigrant families during the Great Depression.

Career

Born in Philadelphia to Russian and Romanian Jewish immigrant parents and raised in the Bronx, Odets dropped out of high school to act, joining the Group Theatre in 1931. His one-act agitprop play Waiting for Lefty (1935), ending with the call to "Strike!", electrified audiences and made him famous overnight. Full-length plays followed: Awake and Sing! (1935) and Till the Day I Die (1935), Golden Boy (1937) — his greatest commercial success, about a boxer-violinist — Rocket to the Moon (1938), and Clash by Night (1941).

From 1936 Odets also worked in Hollywood, writing and sometimes directing films including The General Died at Dawn, None but the Lonely Heart (1944), and the screenplays Sweet Smell of Success (1957) and The Country Girl (1954, adapted from his 1950 play). In 1952 he testified as a friendly witness before the House Un-American Activities Committee, naming colleagues — a decision that shadowed his later reputation.

Legacy

Odets's dialogue, praised for its street poetry and "odetsian" rhythms, influenced playwrights including Arthur Miller. He died of stomach cancer in Los Angeles in 1963, while working on the musical adaptation of Golden Boy.
