Maestro di cappella

Maestro di cappella is the Italian term for the person in charge of music in a chapel or court, equivalent to the German Kapellmeister and the French maître de chapelle. Literally "master of the chapel", the maestro di cappella directed the singers and instrumentalists of a church, cathedral or princely court, and was responsible for composing or procuring music for services and ceremonies.

Role

From the Renaissance through the Baroque era, the post was one of the most prestigious musical positions in Europe. Its holder supervised the choir school, auditioned and trained singers, maintained the music library and instruments, and produced a steady stream of new masses, motets and other works for the liturgical calendar. Competition for posts at institutions such as St Mark's Basilica in Venice or the papal chapel in Rome was intense.

Notable holders

Famous maestri di cappella include Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina at the Cappella Giulia in Rome, Claudio Monteverdi at St Mark's, Venice (1613–1643), and Antonio Vivaldi, who was maestro di violino and later maestro de' concerti at the Ospedale della Pietà. In the German-speaking lands the corresponding Kapellmeister post was held by figures such as Heinrich Schütz at Dresden and Johann Sebastian Bach, Kapellmeister at Köthen before becoming cantor in Leipzig.
