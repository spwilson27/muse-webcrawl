B. R. Ambedkar

Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar (14 April 1891 – 6 December 1956) was an Indian jurist, economist, social reformer and political leader who headed the committee drafting the Constitution of India and served as Law and Justice minister in the first cabinet of Jawaharlal Nehru. A lifelong campaigner against the caste system, he inspired the Dalit Buddhist movement after converting to Buddhism in 1956.

Early life and education

Born into a Mahar family treated as untouchable, Ambedkar faced segregation at school yet became one of the first Dalits to obtain a college education in India. With scholarships from Sayajirao Gaekwad III of Baroda he studied at Columbia University, earning a PhD in economics, and at the London School of Economics, while being called to the bar at Gray's Inn.

Opposition to caste

Ambedkar led movements for Dalit access to public water and temple entry, notably the Mahad Satyagraha of 1927, where the Manusmriti was publicly burned. He clashed with Mahatma Gandhi over separate electorates, a dispute settled by the Poona Pact of 1932. In Annihilation of Caste (1936) he argued caste could not be reformed but must be destroyed. He founded political organizations culminating in the Scheduled Castes Federation and planned the Republican Party of India.

Architect of the Constitution

As chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly, Ambedkar steered the Constitution adopted on 26 November 1949, which abolished untouchability and guaranteed fundamental rights and affirmative-action reservations. As law minister he introduced the Hindu Code Bill to reform women's rights in marriage and inheritance; its stalling prompted his resignation in 1951.

Conversion and legacy

On 14 October 1956 at Nagpur, Ambedkar publicly converted to Buddhism with hundreds of thousands of followers, launching Navayana Buddhism. He died weeks later. Posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna in 1990, he is revered as Babasaheb and commemorated by Ambedkar Jayanti on 14 April. See also Dalit and caste system in India.
