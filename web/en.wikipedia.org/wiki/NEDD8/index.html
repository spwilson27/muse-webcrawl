NEDD8 (neural precursor cell expressed, developmentally down-regulated 8) is a small ubiquitin-like protein found in all eukaryotic cells. It shares about 60% sequence identity with ubiquitin, the highest of any ubiquitin-like modifier, and its covalent attachment to target proteins — a process called neddylation — regulates protein function rather than usually marking proteins for destruction.

Neddylation pathway

NEDD8 is activated by a dedicated E1 enzyme (the NAE1–UBA3 heterodimer), transferred to E2 conjugating enzymes (UBE2M or UBE2F), and ligated to substrates by E3 ligases. Its best-characterised substrates are the cullin scaffold proteins of cullin–RING ubiquitin ligases (CRLs): neddylation of a cullin activates the ligase, which then ubiquitinates proteins controlling the cell cycle, signalling and transcription. The COP9 signalosome removes NEDD8 (deneddylation).

Clinical relevance

Blocking neddylation blocks CRL activity. The NEDD8-activating enzyme inhibitor pevonedistat (MLN4924) has been investigated as an anticancer agent, notably in myelodysplastic syndromes and acute myeloid leukaemia. The gene was originally identified in mouse brain as developmentally down-regulated; its name reflects that origin.
