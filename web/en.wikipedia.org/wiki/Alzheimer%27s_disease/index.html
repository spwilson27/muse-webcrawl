Alzheimer's disease

SpecialtyNeurology
SymptomsMemory loss, problems with language, mood swings, loss of motivation
Usual onsetOver 65 years old
DurationLong term
PrognosisLife expectancy 3–9 years after diagnosis

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disease that usually starts slowly and progressively worsens. It is the cause of 60–70% of cases of dementia. The most common early symptom is difficulty in remembering recent events.

The cause of Alzheimer's disease is poorly understood. Many risk factors are believed to be involved, including genetics and a history of head injuries. The disease process is largely associated with amyloid plaques, neurofibrillary tangles, and the loss of neuronal connections in the brain.

Signs and symptoms

As the disease advances, symptoms can include problems with language, disorientation, mood swings, loss of motivation, self-neglect, and behavioural issues. Body functions are eventually lost, ultimately leading to death.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by examination of the brain tissue, which can only be performed after death, though cognitive testing and medical imaging support clinical diagnosis during life. No treatments can stop or reverse the progression, though some may temporarily improve symptoms.
