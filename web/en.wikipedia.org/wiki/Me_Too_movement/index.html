Me Too movement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Me Too movement (stylised #MeToo) is a social movement and awareness campaign against sexual abuse, sexual harassment and rape culture, in which women publicise their experiences of sexual abuse or sexual harassment. The phrase "Me Too" was initially used in this context on social media in 2006, on Myspace, by sexual assault survivor and activist Tarana Burke.

History

Burke founded the movement to promote "empowerment through empathy" among women of colour who had experienced sexual abuse. The phrase went viral in October 2017 after actress Alyssa Milano encouraged women to tweet "me too" to show the magnitude of the problem, in the wake of sexual abuse allegations against film producer Harvey Weinstein reported by The New York Times and The New Yorker. Within days, the hashtag had been used millions of times in dozens of countries, with local variants such as France's #BalanceTonPorc and Spain's #YoTambién.

Impact

The movement led to the exposure and fall of numerous powerful figures in entertainment, media, politics and business, and to Weinstein's criminal convictions in New York (2020) and Los Angeles (2022). It spurred legislative and institutional reforms, including restrictions on non-disclosure agreements, extended statutes of limitations, and workplace harassment policies. Time magazine named the "Silence Breakers" its 2017 Person of the Year. The movement has also attracted criticism and debate over due process, the conflation of different degrees of misconduct, and the risks of trial by media.

International spread

Adaptations appeared around the world: #MoiAussi in Canada, #YoTambién in Latin America and Spain, #MeToo in India (where a 2018 wave prompted resignations in media and government), and movements in China that evaded censorship through homophones such as "rice bunny" (米兔). In South Korea the movement influenced prosecutions of public figures and legal reforms.
