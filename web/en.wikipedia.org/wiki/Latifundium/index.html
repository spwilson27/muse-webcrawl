Latifundium

A latifundium (Latin: latus, broad + fundus, estate; plural latifundia) is a very extensive parcel of privately owned land, originally the great slave-worked estates of ancient Rome and later applied to large estates in Spain, Italy and Latin America.

OriginRoman Republic / Empire
LabourSlaves, later tenants and labourers
Modern regionsAndalusia, Sicily, Latin America

History

Rome's latifundia grew as smallholders, ruined by long military service and competition from cheap provincial grain, sold out to wealthy owners who farmed with gangs of slaves — a concentration Pliny the Elder lamented as having 'ruined Italy'. The system persisted in the Roman provinces and, through Spanish haciendas and Portuguese sesmarias, in colonial Latin America.

Modern usage

In modern Spain and Latin America, latifundios contrasted with smallholdings (minifundios) became a central target of land reform movements, blamed for inequality, underuse of land and rural poverty.
