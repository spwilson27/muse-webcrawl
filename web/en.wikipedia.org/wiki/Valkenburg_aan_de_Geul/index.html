Valkenburg aan de Geul

Valkenburg aan de Geul is a municipality and a city in the southeastern Dutch province of Limburg, in the valley of the river Geul. The municipality, formed in 1982 from the former municipalities of Valkenburg-Houthem and Berg en Terblijt, had a population of roughly 16,000. The town of Valkenburg is one of the oldest tourist resorts in the Netherlands, famous for its castle ruins, marl caves, and cycling races.

History

Valkenburg's history reaches back to Roman times; a Roman military settlement and watchtower stood near the present town. The name is associated with Valkenburg Castle, founded in the 11th century by the lords of Valkenburg; the castle was destroyed in 1672 on the orders of William III during the Franco-Dutch War, and its ruins still crown the hill above the town. The surrounding marlstone (mergel) quarries, dug since Roman times, produced an extensive labyrinth of caves, used as refuges in wartime and later as tourist attractions, including the Velvet Cave (Fluweelengrot) with its wall drawings and escape passages from the castle.

Tourism and sport

Valkenburg was among the first Dutch towns to build a tourism industry in the 19th century, aided by its hilly landscape, unusual in the Netherlands. Attractions include the Valkenburg Holland Casino, the thermae, a cable car to the Wilhelmina Tower, and several cave systems with guided tours. The town is renowned in cycling: the Cauberg climb in Valkenburg has repeatedly hosted the finish of the Amstel Gold Race and World Road Cycling Championships, and the town has hosted UCI Cyclo-cross World Championships.
