Net international investment position

The net international investment position (NIIP) is the difference between a country's external financial assets and its liabilities. It is a key component of the balance of payments accounts and measures the net creditor or debtor position of a country vis-à-vis the rest of the world at a point in time. A positive NIIP means the country is a net creditor to the rest of the world; a negative NIIP indicates a net debtor position.

Net international investment positions as a share of GDP vary widely between countries.

Definition

NIIP = (value of foreign assets owned by residents) − (value of domestic assets owned by non-residents). The assets and liabilities include foreign direct investment, portfolio investment (equity and debt securities), other investment (loans, deposits, trade credit), financial derivatives, and reserve assets. Changes in the NIIP over time reflect both current account transactions and valuation changes arising from exchange rate and asset price movements.

Relation to the current account

Cumulatively, a country's NIIP changes by its current account balances, plus valuation effects. Persistent current account deficits, such as those run by the United States for decades, tend to produce a deeply negative NIIP, whereas persistent surpluses in countries such as Japan, Germany, and Norway have built up large positive positions. The NIIP is commonly expressed as a percentage of gross domestic product to allow cross-country comparison.
