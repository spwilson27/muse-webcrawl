Hail Flutie

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Doug Flutie's 63-yard Hail Mary won the 1984 game against Miami.

The Hail Flutie is a famous play in American college football: on November 23, 1984, Boston College quarterback Doug Flutie threw a 63-yard Hail Mary touchdown pass to wide receiver Gerard Phelan as time expired, giving the Eagles a 47–45 victory over the defending national champion Miami Hurricanes at the Orange Bowl. Widely replayed ever since, the play is one of the most iconic moments in college football history and propelled Flutie to the Heisman Trophy two weeks later.

The game

The nationally televised day-after-Thanksgiving game matched #10 Boston College against #12 Miami, led by quarterback Bernie Kosar. In heavy rain and wind, the teams traded leads in a wild shootout; Kosar passed for 447 yards and Melvin Bratton scored four touchdowns. Miami took a 45–41 lead with 28 seconds left. Flutie drove the Eagles to the Miami 48-yard line with six seconds remaining.

The play

On the final play — a route Flutie's receivers had run since youth football, "Flood Tip" — all three receivers ran to the end zone. Flutie scrambled away from pressure and launched the ball from his own 37-yard line; it carried over 60 yards in the air, slipped past the clustered Miami defenders, and fell into Phelan's arms in the end zone. Broadcaster Brent Musburger's call ("Flutie flushed... throws it down... caught by Boston College! I don't believe it!") became part of the legend.

Legacy

Flutie, generously listed at 5 ft 10 in and repeatedly doubted for his size, won the 1984 Heisman Trophy in a landslide; he long credited the play with sealing the award. The game boosted Boston College's national profile ("the Flutie effect" is now a term for application surges after athletic success) and helped popularise the phrase "Hail Mary pass" in American sports vocabulary. A statue outside Boston College's Alumni Stadium depicts Flutie mid-throw.
