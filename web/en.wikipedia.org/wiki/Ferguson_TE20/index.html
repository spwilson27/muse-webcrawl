The Ferguson TE20 is an agricultural tractor designed by Harry Ferguson and built in the United Kingdom between 1946 and 1956. It is among the most popular and best-known tractors of all time, with about 517,000 built in England (with additional production elsewhere). Its name derives from "Tractor, England, 20 horsepower", and it is affectionately known as the Little Grey Fergie because of its grey paint scheme.

Design

The TE20 showcased the Ferguson System — a three-point linkage with hydraulic draft control that Ferguson had developed and earlier licensed to Ford in the United States under the famous 1938 "handshake agreement". The linkage allowed implements to be mounted on the rear of the tractor and partially supported by it, greatly improving traction and making a light tractor perform like a much heavier one. A wide range of matched implements was offered.

Production and variants

Early TE20s were built with a Continental petrol engine; later models used a Standard Motor Company petrol engine, with variants for TVO (tractor vaporising oil) and diesel (the TEF-20). Narrow and industrial versions were also made. After Ferguson's company merged with Massey-Harris in 1953 to form Massey Ferguson, TE20 production continued until 1956, when it was succeeded by the Ferguson FE35.

Legacy

The TE20's three-point linkage became the world industry standard and remains in use on tractors today. Restored TE20s are common sights at vintage rallies, and the model is a symbol of post-war British agricultural mechanisation.
