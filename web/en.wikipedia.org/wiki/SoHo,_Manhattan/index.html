SoHo, Manhattan

SoHo
Borough: Manhattan, New York City
Known for: cast-iron architecture, art galleries, shopping

SoHo, short for "South of Houston Street", is a neighbourhood in Lower Manhattan, New York City. It is renowned for its many buildings incorporating cast-iron architectural elements, its history as an artists' enclave, and its present-day upscale boutiques and galleries.

History

In the mid-19th century the area was a manufacturing district filled with cast-iron-fronted warehouses and factories. After manufacturing declined, artists moved into the large, cheap loft spaces in the 1960s and 1970s, and the SoHo–Cast Iron Historic District was designated in 1973 to protect the architecture. From the 1980s onward the neighbourhood gentrified into a fashionable shopping district.

Architecture

SoHo contains the world's largest concentration of cast-iron architecture, with facades produced by foundries such as Daniel D. Badger's Architectural Iron Works. Notable streets include Broadway, Prince Street, and Greene Street, famous for its cast-iron buildings.
