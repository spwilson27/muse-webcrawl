Shine–Dalgarno sequence

The Shine–Dalgarno (SD) sequence is a ribosomal binding site in bacterial and archaeal messenger RNA, located a few nucleotides upstream of the start codon. It is a purine-rich sequence, typically AGGAGG, that helps recruit the ribosome to the mRNA by aligning the ribosome with the start codon.

Mechanism

The SD sequence base-pairs with a complementary pyrimidine-rich "anti-Shine–Dalgarno" region at the 3' end of the 16S rRNA in the small ribosomal subunit. This interaction positions the start codon (usually AUG) in the ribosomal P site so translation can begin. The sequence is named after Australian scientists John Shine and Lynn Dalgarno, who proposed its role in 1974.

Occurrence and exceptions

Strong SD sequences characterise many bacterial genes, but their strength and spacing vary widely between species and genes, modulating translation efficiency. Some mRNAs — so-called leaderless mRNAs — lack an SD sequence altogether, and translation in eukaryotes instead typically depends on the 5' cap and the Kozak consensus sequence.
