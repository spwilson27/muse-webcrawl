Mink

Minks are dark-colored, semiaquatic, carnivorous mammals of the genera Neogale and Mustela in the family Mustelidae, which also includes otters, ferrets, and weasels. Two living species are commonly called mink: the American mink (Neogale vison) and the European mink (Mustela lutreola). The extinct sea mink was a larger relative from the Atlantic coast of North America.

Description and behavior

Minks have long, slender bodies, short legs, and rich, glossy fur highly valued in the fur trade. They live along streams, lakes, and marshes, hunting fish, frogs, crayfish, small mammals, and birds, and are strong swimmers aided by partially webbed toes. The American mink is the larger and more aggressive species; introduced to Europe through fur-farm escapes, it has displaced the now critically endangered European mink across much of its range.

Fur farming

Most mink pelts come from farmed animals, bred in many color mutations from the natural dark brown. Farming has been controversial on animal-welfare grounds and was sharply curtailed in several countries after farmed mink were found susceptible to SARS-CoV-2, prompting mass culls in Denmark and the Netherlands in 2020 and accelerating bans on fur farming. Minks are also raised historically as research animals. They are close relatives of the domestic ferret.
