Swindon is a large town in Wiltshire, England, lying between Bristol and Reading on the M4 corridor, about 70 miles (110 km) west of London. Until the 19th century it was a small market town; the arrival of the Great Western Railway transformed it into one of Britain's most important railway towns, and it remains a major centre for engineering, finance and logistics, governed since 1997 by the unitary Borough of Swindon.

History

Swindon's name probably derives from Old English swīn and dūn — "pig hill". The settlement appears in Domesday Book (1086) as Suindune. In 1840 Isambard Kingdom Brunel and Daniel Gooch sited the Great Western Railway's locomotive works here, mid-way between London and Bristol; a planned "New Swindon" of terraced cottages, a Mechanics' Institute and one of Britain's earliest railway medical services grew beside Old Swindon. The works employed some 14,000 people at their peak and built celebrated locomotives before closure in 1986.

Designated an Expanded Town under the Town Development Act 1952, Swindon absorbed London overspill and doubled in size, gaining employers in insurance, pharmaceuticals and technology — including, until 2021, Honda's car plant, whose closure marked the end of vehicle manufacture in the town. The town centre's 1970s development and the famous Magic Roundabout (five mini-roundabouts in a ring, opened 1972) are landmarks of its modern expansion.

Economy and culture

Major employers include Nationwide Building Society (headquartered in Swindon), the UK Space Agency's former neighbour research councils at the Polaris House campus (UK Research and Innovation), Intel, and distribution centres serving the M4 corridor. Cultural institutions include STEAM — Museum of the Great Western Railway, the Museum of Computing, Lydiard Park, and the Wyvern Theatre. Swindon Town F.C. won the League Cup in 1969 and played one season in the Premier League (1993–94).
