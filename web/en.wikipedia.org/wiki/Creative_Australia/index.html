Creative Australia

Formed: 1973 (as Australia Council for the Arts)
Jurisdiction: Australia
Type: Government arts funding and advisory body

Creative Australia, formerly the Australia Council for the Arts and the Australia Council, is the chief arts funding and advisory body of the Government of Australia. It was established in 1973 and is responsible for distributing government funding to artists and arts organisations across art forms including theatre, dance, music, literature, visual arts and community arts.

History

The Australia Council was created in 1973 under the Whitlam government as an interim body and was given statutory status by the Australia Council Act 1975. For decades it operated at arm's length from government, allocating funding through peer assessment by panels of artists and arts professionals.

In 2023, under the national cultural policy Revive, the body was restructured and rebranded as Creative Australia. The reforms established new internal entities, including Music Australia, to support the contemporary music sector, and Creative Workplaces, to promote fair, safe and respectful workplaces in the arts. The reforms also expanded the organisation's remit to include investment attraction and industry development alongside grant funding.

Functions

Creative Australia administers grants and strategic initiatives, undertakes research on the arts sector, and advises government on arts and cultural policy. Its programs support individual artists, small-to-medium organisations and major performing arts companies funded in partnership with state and territory governments.
