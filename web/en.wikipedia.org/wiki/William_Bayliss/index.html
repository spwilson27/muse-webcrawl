William Bayliss

Born: 2 May 1860, Wolverhampton, England
Died: 27 August 1924, London, England
Known for: Discovery of secretin; Bayliss effect
Field: Physiology

Sir William Maddock Bayliss (2 May 1860 – 27 August 1924) was an English physiologist. Together with Ernest Starling he discovered secretin in 1902, the first substance identified as a hormone, a finding that laid the foundations of endocrinology.

Life and career

Bayliss was born in Wolverhampton and studied at Wadham College, Oxford, before taking up a post at University College London (UCL), where he spent most of his career working alongside Ernest Starling. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1903 and was knighted in 1922.

Discovery of secretin

In 1902 Bayliss and Starling showed that acid entering the duodenum caused the release of a chemical messenger into the blood that stimulated the pancreas to secrete digestive juices, even when all nervous connections had been cut. They named the substance secretin, and Starling later coined the term "hormone" for such blood-borne messengers.

Other work

Bayliss also described the myogenic autoregulation of blood vessel diameter, now known as the Bayliss effect, and wrote the influential textbook Principles of General Physiology (1915). During the First World War he worked on the treatment of wound shock, advocating intravenous gum-saline infusions.
