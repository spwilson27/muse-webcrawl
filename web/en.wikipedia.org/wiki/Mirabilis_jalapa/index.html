Mirabilis jalapa, the marvel of Peru or four o'clock flower, is a widely cultivated ornamental flowering plant in the four o'clock family, Nyctaginaceae. Native to the dry tropical regions of South America, it has naturalised throughout the tropics and warm temperate zones worldwide.

Description

It is a bushy herbaceous perennial, grown as an annual in cold climates, reaching about a metre in height, with tuberous roots and trumpet-shaped, fragrant flowers in shades of pink, red, yellow, white, and orange. The flowers open in the late afternoon — hence "four o'clock" — and close by morning. A single plant, and even a single flower, may bear several colours or striped and speckled patterns. Carl Correns used M. jalapa in classic experiments demonstrating non-Mendelian, maternal (cytoplasmic) inheritance of leaf variegation.

Cultivation and uses

The plant is easily grown from its large black seeds, tolerates poor soil and heat, and self-seeds freely. It has been used in traditional medicine in parts of its range, though the roots and seeds are considered toxic if eaten. Flower pigments of Mirabilis are betalains, the same class of pigments found in bougainvillea and beetroot.
