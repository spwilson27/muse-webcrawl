Electric power system

An electric power system is a network of electrical components used to supply, transfer and use electric power. It comprises generation (power stations), transmission networks carrying power over long distances at high voltage, and distribution networks delivering power to end users. Large systems, such as national grids, are among the most complex machines ever built.

Structure

Power is generated at voltages of a few kilovolts to tens of kilovolts, stepped up to 110–765 kV for transmission to minimise resistive losses, and stepped down through substations to distribution voltages and finally to utilisation voltages such as 230 V or 120 V. Three-phase AC is standard; high-voltage DC links are used for very long distances, undersea cables and connecting asynchronous grids.

Operation

Because electricity is not stored in bulk on the grid, generation must match demand instant by instant. System operators forecast load, schedule generators by merit order, and hold spinning reserve. Protective relays and circuit breakers isolate faults to prevent cascading failures and blackouts.

Reliability and modernisation

Power systems are designed to N-1 security standards, surviving the loss of any single component. Modern grids increasingly integrate variable renewable generation, battery storage, demand response and digital monitoring under the "smart grid" concept, while distributed generation such as rooftop solar changes traditional one-way power flows.
