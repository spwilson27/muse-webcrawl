Haj Amin al-Husseini

Mohammed Amin al-Husseini
Born: c. 1897, Jerusalem, Ottoman Empire
Died: 4 July 1974, Beirut, Lebanon
Office: Grand Mufti of Jerusalem (1921–1937); President of the Supreme Muslim Council
Known for: Palestinian Arab nationalist leader

Mohammed Amin al-Husseini (Arabic: محمد أمين الحسيني; c. 1897 – 4 July 1974) was a Palestinian Arab nationalist and Muslim leader in Mandatory Palestine. A member of the prominent al-Husseini family of Jerusalem, he was appointed Grand Mufti of Jerusalem by the British in 1921 and became president of the Supreme Muslim Council, making him the most influential Palestinian leader of the Mandate period.

Early life

Al-Husseini was born in Jerusalem into a family that had supplied the city with muftis and mayors for generations. He studied at al-Azhar University in Cairo and, according to his memoirs, performed the Hajj as a young man, acquiring the honorific "Haj" or "Hajji". During the First World War he served as an officer in the Ottoman army before returning to Jerusalem, where he became active in Arab nationalist politics in the aftermath of the Balfour Declaration and the establishment of the British Mandate.

Grand Mufti

The British High Commissioner, Herbert Samuel, pardoned al-Husseini for his role in the 1920 Nebi Musa riots and in May 1921 appointed him Grand Mufti of Jerusalem, although he had placed fourth in the election for the post. As president of the Supreme Muslim Council from 1922, he controlled Muslim religious endowments (awqaf), sharia courts and appointments, and used the restoration of the Haram al-Sharif (Temple Mount) mosques to raise his standing across the Muslim world. He was a leading opponent of Zionist immigration and land purchase, and tensions around the Western Wall erupted in the 1929 Palestine riots.

Revolt and exile

Al-Husseini became chairman of the Arab Higher Committee in 1936 and was accused by the British of directing the 1936–39 Arab revolt in Palestine. Stripped of his offices in 1937, he fled to Lebanon, then to Iraq, where he backed the pro-Axis Rashid Ali government in 1941. After its fall he escaped to Nazi Germany, spending the war years in Berlin. He broadcast Arabic-language propaganda for the Axis, met Adolf Hitler in November 1941, and helped recruit Bosnian Muslims into the Waffen-SS Handschar division. His wartime record has made him one of the most controversial figures in the history of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.

Postwar years

After the war al-Husseini avoided prosecution and settled in Egypt. In 1948 the Arab League installed him as head of the short-lived All-Palestine Government in Gaza, but his authority waned as the Arab states pursued their own interests; Jordan's King Abdullah, his great rival, was assassinated in 1951 by a gunman linked to his supporters. Al-Husseini spent his later years in Beirut, Lebanon, in political obscurity, and died there in 1974. He was buried in Beirut after Israel declined to permit his burial in Jerusalem.
