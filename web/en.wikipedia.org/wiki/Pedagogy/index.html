Pedagogy

Pedagogy is the method and practice of teaching, especially as an academic subject or theoretical concept. Derived from the Greek paidagōgia — from paidagōgos, the slave who escorted children to school — the word now covers the art, science and profession of education: how teachers structure learning, choose materials, manage classrooms and assess progress.

Approaches

Pedagogical approaches vary along a spectrum from teacher-centred instruction to learner-centred inquiry. Major traditions include behaviourist approaches based on reinforcement; constructivist approaches associated with Piaget and Vygotsky, holding that learners build understanding through activity and social interaction; critical pedagogy, developed by Paulo Freire in Pedagogy of the Oppressed (1968), which treats education as a practice of liberation and critiques the "banking model" of teaching; and humanistic and Montessori approaches emphasising the child's own development and environment.

Elements

Pedagogy encompasses instructional strategies, curriculum design, classroom management, assessment and feedback, and the relationships between teachers and learners. Contemporary pedagogy is informed by educational psychology and learning sciences, and increasingly addresses digital technology, inclusion and differentiation for diverse learners. The related term andragogy refers specifically to adult learning, and didactics to the theory of instruction in particular subjects.

History

The systematic study of teaching developed through figures such as Johann Amos Comenius, who argued for universal education in the 17th century; Johann Heinrich Pestalozzi and Friedrich Fröbel, pioneers of child-centred education and the kindergarten; Johann Friedrich Herbart, who formalised lesson structure; and John Dewey, whose progressive education movement emphasised learning by doing and democratic schooling.
