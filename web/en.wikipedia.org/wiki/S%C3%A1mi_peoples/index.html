Sámi peoples

RegionsSápmi: northern Norway, Sweden, Finland, Russia's Kola Peninsula
LanguagesSámi languages, Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish, Russian
PopulationEstimated 80,000–100,000

The Sámi are an Indigenous people inhabiting Sápmi, a region spanning northern Norway, Sweden and Finland and Russia's Kola Peninsula. They are the only Indigenous people officially recognised in the European Union and have traditionally pursued reindeer herding, fishing, hunting and handicrafts.

Languages and culture

The Sámi languages belong to the Uralic family; Northern Sámi is the most widely spoken. Distinctive cultural markers include the colourful gákti garment, the joik vocal tradition, and duodji handicrafts. The Sámi flag, adopted in 1986, features a circle symbolising the sun and moon.

History

Sámi societies were pressured for centuries by taxation, missionary activity and assimilation policies such as Norwegianization. Reindeer herding cooperatives (siida/sameby) remain central to livelihoods, especially in the interior.

Politics

Sámi parliaments in Norway, Sweden and Finland represent Sámi interests, though their powers are largely consultative. Land and resource rights — including disputes over wind farms and mining — remain major contemporary issues.
