The Lorenz cipher was a rotor stream-cipher machine used by the German Army during the Second World War to encrypt high-level teleprinter communications between the Army High Command in Berlin and army group headquarters in the field. Developed by C. Lorenz AG, the machines were designated SZ40 and SZ42 (from Schlüssel-Zusatz, "cipher attachment"). British codebreakers at Bletchley Park codenamed the machine and its traffic Tunny, and the whole German teleprinter cipher network Fish.

Operation

The Lorenz machine generated a pseudorandom key stream of five-bit characters, which were combined with the plaintext (encoded in the ITA2 teleprinter code) using the XOR operation to produce ciphertext. It contained twelve wheels: five chi wheels and five psi wheels generating the key, whose movement was controlled by two mu (motor) wheels. The irregular stepping of the psi wheels made the cipher far stronger than a simple repeating-key system.

Codebreaking

The break into Tunny began with a catastrophic German operating error: on 30 August 1941 an operator retransmitted a nearly 4,000-character message using the same wheel settings, with small abbreviations in the second sending. The intercept allowed John Tiltman to recover the key stream, and mathematician Bill Tutte then deduced the entire logical structure of a machine the British had never seen — one of the greatest feats of cryptanalysis.

To automate the attack, engineers Harold Keen, Tommy Flowers and others built first the Heath Robinson machines and then Colossus, the world's first programmable electronic digital computer, which came into operation in early 1944. Ten Colossi were ultimately used at Bletchley Park to recover daily wheel settings, and Tunny decrypts ("Fish" intelligence) gave the Allies insight into German strategic intentions at the highest level.

Significance

Unlike the better-known Enigma, the Lorenz system protected communications between Hitler's headquarters and his field commanders. The secrecy surrounding Colossus meant the achievement remained classified for decades after the war.
