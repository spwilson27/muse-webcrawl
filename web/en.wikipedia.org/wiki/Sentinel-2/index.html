Sentinel-2 is an Earth observation mission developed by the European Space Agency as part of the European Union's Copernicus Programme. It consists of a constellation of satellites carrying a wide-swath, high-resolution, multispectral imager, providing free and open imagery used mainly for land monitoring — agriculture, forestry, land-cover change, natural disasters, and humanitarian relief.

Mission

Two identical satellites, Sentinel-2A (launched 2015) and Sentinel-2B (launched 2017), fly in the same sun-synchronous orbit phased 180° apart, imaging Earth's land and coastal areas every five days at the equator. Sentinel-2C, launched in September 2024, will replace Sentinel-2A. The MultiSpectral Instrument samples 13 spectral bands in the visible, near-infrared, and shortwave infrared at spatial resolutions of 10, 20, and 60 metres across a 290 km swath.

Applications

Sentinel-2 data support crop monitoring and yield forecasting, deforestation alerts, mapping of floods, wildfires and volcanic eruptions, urban planning, and water quality assessment. Because the data are free, full, and open, they have become a standard input for environmental science and for commercial geospatial services worldwide.
