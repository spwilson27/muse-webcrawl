National Curriculum for England

The National Curriculum for England is a set of subjects and standards used by primary and secondary schools in England to ensure that children learn the same things. It was introduced under the Education Reform Act 1988 and covers what is taught in state-funded schools, although academies and free schools are not legally required to follow it.

Structure

The curriculum is organized into Key Stages by age: Key Stage 1 (ages 5–7), Key Stage 2 (7–11), Key Stage 3 (11–14), and Key Stage 4 (14–16). In the first three key stages, pupils study a broad range of subjects including English, mathematics, science, history, geography, art and design, music, design and technology, computing, modern foreign languages, and physical education. At Key Stage 4, a smaller set of core and foundation subjects is compulsory, and most pupils work toward GCSE qualifications.

Assessment

Progress is checked by national assessments, including the phonics screening check in Year 1, multiplication tables check in Year 4, and statutory tests (SATs) at the end of Key Stages 1 and 2. At 16, pupils sit GCSE examinations; at 18 many proceed to A levels or other post-16 qualifications.

History and revision

The 1988 Act introduced a common curriculum for the first time in England. The curriculum was slimmed down following the Dearing Review of 1993–94 after complaints that it was overloaded. A substantially revised curriculum, developed after a review launched in 2011, was introduced from September 2014, with greater emphasis on knowledge, arithmetic fluency, grammar, and computer programming — which replaced ICT as a subject.

Devolution

The National Curriculum applies only to England. Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland each maintain their own curricula, such as Scotland's Curriculum for Excellence and the Curriculum for Wales introduced in 2022.
