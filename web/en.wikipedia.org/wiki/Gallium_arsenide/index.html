Gallium arsenide

Chemical formulaGaAs
Molar mass144.64 g/mol
AppearanceDark grey crystals
Density5.32 g/cm³
Melting point1,238 °C
Band gap1.424 eV (direct, at 300 K)
Crystal structureZinc blende

Gallium arsenide (GaAs) is a III–V direct band gap semiconductor with a zinc blende crystal structure. It is an important material in electronics and optoelectronics, used in integrated circuits operating at microwave and millimetre-wave frequencies, in light-emitting diodes, laser diodes, photodetectors and high-efficiency solar cells.

GaAs has several advantages over silicon: its electron mobility is much higher (up to about 8,500 cm²/V·s), allowing devices to operate at frequencies above 250 GHz; its direct band gap of 1.424 eV makes it efficient at emitting and absorbing light; and its semi-insulating substrates reduce parasitic capacitance. GaAs devices are also relatively insensitive to heat and radiation.

Preparation and chemistry

Single crystals are grown by methods such as the liquid encapsulated Czochralski and vertical gradient freeze processes, and epitaxial layers are deposited by metalorganic vapour phase epitaxy (MOVPE) or molecular beam epitaxy (MBE). Elemental gallium and arsenic react at high temperature to form the compound.

Applications

GaAs is the basis of high-electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs) and heterojunction bipolar transistors used in mobile phone power amplifiers, satellite communications and radar. Multi-junction GaAs-based solar cells hold efficiency records for photovoltaic conversion and power many spacecraft. Arsenic content makes GaAs toxic and a carcinogen, requiring careful handling in manufacture and disposal.
