Okazaki fragments

Okazaki fragments are short sequences of DNA nucleotides (approximately 150 to 200 base pairs long in eukaryotes, roughly 1000 to 2000 base pairs long in prokaryotes) which are synthesized discontinuously and later linked together by the enzyme DNA ligase to create the lagging strand during DNA replication. They were discovered in the 1960s by the Japanese molecular biologists Reiji and Tsuneko Okazaki, along with the help of some of their colleagues.

During DNA replication, the double helix is unwound and the complementary strands are separated by the enzyme DNA helicase, creating what is known as the DNA replication fork. Following this fork, DNA primase and DNA polymerase begin to act in order to create a new complementary strand. Because these enzymes can only work in the 5' to 3' direction, the two unwound template strands are replicated in different ways. One strand, the leading strand, undergoes a continuous replication process since its template strand has 3' to 5' directionality, allowing the polymerase assembling the leading strand to follow the replication fork without interruption. On the other hand, the lagging strand, whose template strand has 5' to 3' directionality, is replicated discontinuously into short Okazaki fragments, each beginning with an RNA primer and extending in the 5' to 3' direction away from the fork.

Discovery

In 1968, Reiji and Tsuneko Okazaki discovered the way the lagging strand of DNA is replicated via experiments involving the bacterium Escherichia coli and its virus, bacteriophage T4. The Okazakis grew phage-infected E. coli in the presence of radioactive thymidine and found that newly synthesized DNA appeared first as short fragments before being joined into longer molecules, supporting a discontinuous model of lagging-strand synthesis.

Pathways

Processing in prokaryotes

In bacteria, the RNA primers at the 5' end of each Okazaki fragment are removed by the 5' to 3' exonuclease activity of DNA polymerase I, which simultaneously fills the gap with DNA. DNA ligase then seals the remaining nick.

Processing in eukaryotes

In eukaryotes, primer removal is carried out mainly by flap endonuclease 1 (FEN1) and RNase H2, after which DNA ligase I joins the fragments into a continuous strand.

Clinical significance

Defects in the processing of Okazaki fragments, such as mutations in FEN1 or DNA ligase I, are associated with genomic instability and have been linked to certain cancers and immunodeficiency disorders.
