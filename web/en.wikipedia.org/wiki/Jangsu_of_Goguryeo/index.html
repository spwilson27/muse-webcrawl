Jangsu (Korean: 장수; Hanja: 長壽; 394–491, r. 413–491) was the 20th monarch of Goguryeo, the northernmost of the Three Kingdoms of Korea. His personal name was Geo-ryeon (거련). His posthumous name, Jangsu, means "long life," a reference to his remarkable 79-year reign — the longest of any Korean monarch and one of the longest reigns in East Asian history.

Succession and consolidation

Jangsu was the eldest son of Gwanggaeto the Great, whose conquests had expanded Goguryeo into Manchuria and down the Korean peninsula. Crowned in 413, Jangsu continued his father's work while shifting emphasis to diplomacy and consolidation. He balanced relations between China's Northern Wei and the southern dynasties, sending embassies to both, and pursued a policy of playing northern and southern Chinese courts against each other.

Moving the capital

In 427 Jangsu transferred the capital from Gungnae (modern Ji'an) to Pyongyang, strengthening Goguryeo's position on the Korean peninsula and signalling its ambitions southward. The move also tightened his control over the southern territories conquered by Gwanggaeto.

Southern campaigns

Jangsu intervened repeatedly in the affairs of Baekje and Silla. In 475 his armies captured the Baekje capital of Hanseong (modern Seoul), killed King Gaero, and drove Baekje south to Ungjin. Goguryeo thereby reached its greatest territorial extent, controlling much of Manchuria and the northern and central Korean peninsula, including the Han River valley. His era is commemorated by the Chungju Goguryeo stele, the only Goguryeo stone monument found on the Korean peninsula south of the Taedong region.

Legacy

Jangsu died in 491 at the age of 97 and was succeeded by his grandson Munjamyeong. His reign is generally seen as the apex of Goguryeo power together with that of his father, whose achievements are recorded on the Gwanggaeto Stele.
