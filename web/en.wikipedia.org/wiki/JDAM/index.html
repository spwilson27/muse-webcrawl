Joint Direct Attack Munition

Joint Direct Attack Munition (JDAM)
Type: Precision-guided bomb conversion kit
Manufacturer: Boeing
In service: 1999–present

The Joint Direct Attack Munition (JDAM) is a guidance kit that converts unguided bombs, or "dumb bombs", into all-weather precision-guided munitions (PGMs). JDAM-equipped bombs are guided by an integrated inertial guidance system coupled to a Global Positioning System (GPS) receiver, giving them a published range of up to 15 nautical miles (28 km).

Development

Developed after experience in the Gulf War highlighted the limitations of unguided and laser-guided bombs in poor weather, JDAM was produced by Boeing and first used in combat during Operation Allied Force in 1999, dropped by B-2 Spirit bombers.

Variants

Kits are produced for the 500 lb, 1,000 lb, and 2,000 lb bomb classes (the GBU-38, GBU-32, and GBU-31 respectively). The Laser JDAM (GBU-54) adds a laser seeker for engaging moving targets, and the JDAM-ER extended-range variant adds a wing kit.

Users

JDAM is used by the United States armed forces and numerous allied air forces worldwide, and can be carried by a wide range of strike aircraft.
