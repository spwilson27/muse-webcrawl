Salpingo-oophorectomy

Salpingo-oophorectomy is the surgical removal of an ovary together with its fallopian tube (salpinx). The procedure may be unilateral (one side) or bilateral (both sides, abbreviated BSO). When combined with removal of the uterus it is often described as a total abdominal hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (TAH-BSO).

Indications

The operation is performed for conditions including ovarian and fallopian tube cancers, some cases of endometriosis, ovarian torsion, ectopic pregnancy in the tube, pelvic inflammatory disease with tubo-ovarian abscess, and benign ovarian masses. Bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy is also offered as risk-reducing surgery to people at high hereditary risk of ovarian cancer, notably carriers of BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutations, in whom the tubes are now understood to be the site of origin of many "ovarian" serous cancers.

Procedure

The surgery is most often done laparoscopically (including robot-assisted approaches), though open laparotomy is used for large masses or advanced cancer. The surgeon identifies the ureter, ligates the infundibulopelvic ligament containing the ovarian vessels and the utero-ovarian ligament and tube, and removes the ovary and tube, usually in a containment bag when malignancy is a concern.

Consequences

Removal of both ovaries before natural menopause causes immediate surgical menopause, with increased long-term risks of cardiovascular disease, bone loss and cognitive effects; hormone replacement therapy is commonly discussed. Opportunistic salpingectomy — removing the tubes alone while conserving the ovaries — is increasingly recommended at the time of other pelvic surgery as a cancer-prevention measure.
