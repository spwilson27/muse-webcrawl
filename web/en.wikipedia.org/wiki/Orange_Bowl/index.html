Orange Bowl

Stadium: Hard Rock Stadium, Miami Gardens, Florida

First played: 1935

Conference tie-ins: ACC (primary)

Sponsor: Capital One

The Orange Bowl is an annual American college football bowl game played in the Miami metropolitan area. First contested in 1935, it is one of the oldest bowl games and, with the Sugar Bowl and Sun Bowl, the second-oldest after the Rose Bowl. Since 2007 it has been played at the stadium now known as Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens, having spent most of its history at the Miami Orange Bowl.

History

The game grew out of the 1932 Palm Festival Game and was first played as the Orange Bowl on 1 January 1935, when Bucknell defeated Miami (FL) 26–0. It became part of the Bowl Championship Series in 1998 and has served as the national championship game and later as a College Football Playoff semifinal on a rotating basis within the New Year's Six.

Historically the game hosted the champion of the Big Eight Conference; since 2014 its primary tie-in has been the Atlantic Coast Conference champion (or its highest-ranked available team), facing an opponent from the SEC, Big Ten or Notre Dame.

Memorable games

The 1965 game saw Alabama's Joe Namath stopped short of the goal line by Texas in a famous finish. The 1984 game, in which Miami upset Nebraska 31–30 after the Cornhuskers failed on a two-point conversion in the final minute, won Miami its first national title and is often ranked among the greatest college football games ever played. The 2006 game between Penn State and Florida State went to three overtimes and pitted the two winningest coaches in major college football, Joe Paterno and Bobby Bowden.

Festival

The game anchors the Orange Bowl Festival, which long included the King Orange Jamboree Parade on Biscayne Boulevard, first held in 1936.
