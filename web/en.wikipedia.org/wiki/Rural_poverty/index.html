Rural poverty
FieldDevelopment economics, rural sociology
Share of world povertyRoughly 80% of the extreme poor live in rural areas

Rural poverty refers to poverty found in rural areas, including factors of rural society, rural economy, and political systems that give rise to poverty found there. Most of the world's poor people live in the countryside: the World Bank estimates that around 80 percent of the extreme poor are rural, and most depend directly or indirectly on agriculture for their livelihoods.

Causes

Rural poverty is associated with small or marginal landholdings, landlessness, low agricultural productivity, dependence on rain-fed farming, distance from markets, poor roads and infrastructure, limited access to credit, education, and health care, and vulnerability to weather shocks, crop failure, and price swings. In many regions it is compounded by insecure land tenure, gender inequality in access to land and inputs, and the out-migration of working-age adults, leaving women, children, and the elderly behind.

Regional patterns

The greatest concentrations of rural poverty are in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. Rural poverty also persists in high-income countries: in the United States, non-metropolitan poverty rates have long exceeded urban rates, with particularly entrenched "persistent poverty" counties in Appalachia, the Mississippi Delta, the rural South, and Native American reservations.

Responses

Strategies against rural poverty include agricultural research and extension, irrigation, rural roads, land reform and secure tenure, microfinance and mobile banking, conditional cash transfers and social protection floors, diversification into rural non-farm employment, and investment in rural schooling and health. Growth in agricultural productivity is generally found to reduce poverty more effectively than equivalent growth in other sectors, because the poor are concentrated in farming.
