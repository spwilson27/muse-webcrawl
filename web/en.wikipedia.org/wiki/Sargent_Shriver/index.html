Robert Sargent Shriver Jr. (November 9, 1915 – January 18, 2011) was an American diplomat, politician, and activist. As the husband of Eunice Kennedy, he was a member of the Kennedy family, brother-in-law of President John F. Kennedy. Shriver was the driving force behind the creation of the Peace Corps, its first director, and later directed President Lyndon B. Johnson's War on Poverty through the Office of Economic Opportunity, creating programs such as Head Start, VISTA, Job Corps, and Legal Services. He was the Democratic Party's nominee for vice president in the 1972 presidential election.

Early life and education

Shriver was born in Westminster, Maryland, to a family of German Catholic descent that had been prominent in Maryland since the colonial era. He attended Yale University, was an editor of the Yale Daily News, and graduated from Yale Law School in 1941. He served in the United States Navy during World War II, participating in combat in the South Pacific, and was awarded the Purple Heart.

Career

After the war Shriver worked for Joseph P. Kennedy Sr., managing his business and political interests in Chicago, including the Merchandise Mart and the Chicago Board of Education, of which he became president. In 1953 he married Eunice Kennedy. When John F. Kennedy became president in 1961, Shriver was asked to build the Peace Corps, a program of American volunteers serving in developing countries; he served as its director until 1966 and built it into one of the most celebrated agencies of the New Frontier, with volunteers in dozens of countries.

Under President Johnson, Shriver directed the Office of Economic Opportunity and designed much of the War on Poverty legislation. He served as Ambassador to France from 1968 to 1970, and in 1972 he replaced Thomas Eagleton as George McGovern's running mate; the ticket lost in a landslide to Richard Nixon. Shriver later ran unsuccessfully for the Democratic presidential nomination in 1976.

Later life and legacy

With his wife Eunice, who founded the Special Olympics, Shriver supported the movement for people with intellectual disabilities; their daughter Maria Shriver became a journalist and First Lady of California. Diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease in 2003, he died in Bethesda, Maryland, in 2011. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1994.
