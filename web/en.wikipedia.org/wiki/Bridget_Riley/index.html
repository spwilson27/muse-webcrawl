Bridget Riley

Born24 April 1931, London, England
MovementOp art
Known forPainting, printmaking

Bridget Louise Riley (born 24 April 1931) is an English painter known for her op art paintings, in which geometric patterns create striking optical effects of movement, vibration and flicker. She is one of the foremost proponents of the op art movement that emerged in the 1960s.

Career

Riley studied at Goldsmiths College and the Royal College of Art. After early work in a semi-impressionist and then pointillist manner, she turned in about 1960 to the black-and-white paintings — such as Movement in Squares (1961) and Fall (1963) — that made her reputation. Their repeated units of squares, circles and waves seem to move and shimmer before the viewer's eyes.

She was included in the Museum of Modern Art's landmark exhibition The Responsive Eye (1965) in New York, and in 1968 she won the International Prize for Painting at the Venice Biennale, the first British painter and first woman to do so. From 1967 she introduced colour, developing stripe and curve paintings that explore subtle interactions of hue and rhythm.

Legacy

Riley's rigorously planned works, executed with the help of assistants from her precise studies, have influenced art, design and fashion. Major retrospectives have been held at Tate and the Hayward Gallery, and she remains one of Britain's most celebrated living artists.
