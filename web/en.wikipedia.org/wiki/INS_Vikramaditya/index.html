INS Vikramaditya (Sanskrit for "Brave as the Sun") is a modified Kiev-class aircraft carrier and the former flagship of the Indian Navy. Originally built for the Soviet Navy as Baku (later renamed Admiral Gorshkov), she was bought by India in 2004 and underwent an extensive refit at the Sevmash shipyard in Severodvinsk, Russia, converting her from a hybrid cruiser-carrier into a full STOBAR carrier with a ski-jump, enlarged flight deck, and arrestor gear.

Acquisition and refit

India signed the deal in January 2004, receiving the hull nominally free while paying for the refit and an air group of MiG-29K fighters. The project suffered repeated delays and cost growth — the final price was around $2.35 billion — amid disputes over scope and, in 2012, boiler failures during sea trials. The ship was formally handed over and commissioned into the Indian Navy on 16 November 2013 at Severodvinsk, and arrived at her homeport of Karwar in early 2014.

Service

Displacing about 45,400 tonnes and 284 metres long, Vikramaditya embarked up to roughly 30 aircraft, principally MiG-29K/KUB fighters and Kamov helicopters, and has a crew of around 1,600. She served as India's sole operational carrier until the indigenous INS Vikrant was commissioned in 2022. The ship has taken part in exercises and operational deployments in the Arabian Sea, and underwent refits including integration of the Barak-8 surface-to-air missile system. She is named after the legendary 1st-century BCE emperor Vikramaditya of Ujjain.
