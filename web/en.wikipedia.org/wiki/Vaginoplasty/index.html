Vaginoplasty

SpecialtyGynaecology, urology, plastic surgery
TypeReconstructive / gender-affirming surgery

Vaginoplasty is a surgical procedure that results in the construction or reconstruction of the vagina. It is performed for several reasons: as a gender-affirming surgery for transgender women and some non-binary people, to create a vagina in people born with congenital conditions such as Mayer–Rokitansky–Küster–Hauser (MRKH) syndrome or vaginal atresia, and to repair the vagina after injury, disease, or childbirth.

Techniques

The most common technique for transfeminine vaginoplasty is penile inversion, in which penile and scrotal skin are used to line a newly created vaginal canal. Alternatives include intestinal vaginoplasty, which uses a segment of colon, and peritoneal pull-through techniques. For patients with congenital absence of the vagina, skin grafts, tissue expanders, or non-surgical dilation may be used.

Aftercare and outcomes

Recovery generally involves regular dilation of the neovagina to maintain depth and width, especially in the first year. Possible complications include bleeding, infection, stenosis (narrowing), fistula, and loss of graft tissue. Studies generally report high patient satisfaction and good functional and aesthetic outcomes when surgery is performed by experienced teams.

History

Early vaginoplasty procedures were developed in the first half of the twentieth century to treat congenital conditions. Gender-affirming vaginoplasty became more widely known after the 1950s, and techniques have been progressively refined.
