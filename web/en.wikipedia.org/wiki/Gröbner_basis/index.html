Gröbner basis

In mathematics, a Gröbner basis is a particular kind of generating set of an ideal in a polynomial ring over a field. It generalizes, to multivariate polynomials, the concepts of the greatest common divisor of univariate polynomials and Gaussian elimination for linear systems. Gröbner bases make many problems about polynomial ideals computationally tractable: ideal membership, solving systems of polynomial equations, and elimination.

History and computation

The theory was introduced by Bruno Buchberger in his 1965 PhD thesis under Wolfgang Gröbner, for whom the bases are named. Buchberger's algorithm computes a Gröbner basis from any generating set by repeatedly forming and reducing "S-polynomials". Later algorithms such as F4 and F5 (Faugère) greatly improved performance.

Applications

Gröbner bases are implemented in computer algebra systems such as Singular, Macaulay2, CoCoA, and Mathematica, and are used in algebraic geometry, robotics, coding theory, cryptography, and automated theorem proving in geometry.
