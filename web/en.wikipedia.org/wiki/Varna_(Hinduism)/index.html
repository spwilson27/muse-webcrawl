Varna (Hinduism)

Varna (Sanskrit: वर्ण, romanized: varṇa) is a term in Hinduism referring to the social stratification into four broad classes: the Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (rulers and warriors), Vaishyas (farmers, traders and merchants) and Shudras (labourers and service providers). The system is described in ancient texts such as the Rigveda's Purusha Sukta and the Manusmriti.

Origins and texts

The Purusha Sukta hymn describes the varnas emerging from the body of the cosmic being Purusha — Brahmins from the mouth, Kshatriyas from the arms, Vaishyas from the thighs and Shudras from the feet. Dharmashastra texts systematised duties, occupations and rules for each varna.

Varna and jati

Varna is distinct from jati — the thousands of hereditary birth groups (often translated as castes) that govern everyday social life, marriage and occupation in South Asia. Communities outside the four varnas were historically classified as avarna, including Dalits, who faced severe discrimination and untouchability.

Interpretations and critique

Views differ: some texts and modern reformers interpret varna as based on qualities (guna) and actions (karma) rather than birth; others note its historical functioning as a hereditary hierarchy. Figures such as B. R. Ambedkar sharply criticised the system, and the Constitution of India prohibits caste discrimination while retaining affirmative-action categories.
