Haloform reaction

The haloform reaction is a chemical reaction in which a haloform (chloroform, bromoform or iodoform) is produced by the exhaustive halogenation of a methyl ketone in the presence of a base. It was among the earliest organic reactions described, in the 1820s.

Mechanism

Base repeatedly deprotonates the methyl group and halogen replaces each hydrogen, forming a trihalomethyl ketone. Hydroxide then attacks the carbonyl, cleaving the carbon–carbon bond to give a carboxylate and the haloform, whose anion is stabilised by three halogens.

Uses

The iodoform test — formation of yellow iodoform precipitate with iodine and sodium hydroxide — is a classic qualitative test for methyl ketones and for secondary alcohols (such as ethanol) that oxidise to them. The reaction was historically used commercially to prepare chloroform and iodoform antiseptic.
