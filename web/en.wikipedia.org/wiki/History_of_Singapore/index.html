History of Singapore

The history of Singapore may date back to the 3rd century, when the island was referred to in Chinese records as "Po Lo Chung", possibly a transcription of the Malay Pulau Ujong, "island at the end of a peninsula". In the 14th century the island, known as Temasek and later Singapura, was an important trading post of the Srivijaya and Majapahit spheres.

Early history

The Greek geographer Ptolemy's 2nd-century Geography may refer to a place called Sabara at the southern tip of the Malay Peninsula. By the 14th century, Singapura had grown into a trading settlement; according to the Malay Annals, it was founded by Sang Nila Utama, a Srivijayan prince from Palembang. The island later came under the influence of the Sultanate of Malacca and then the Sultanate of Johor.

Founding of modern Singapore

In 1819 Sir Stamford Raffles, an official of the British East India Company, signed a treaty with Sultan Hussein Shah of Johor and the Temenggong allowing the Company to establish a trading post on the island. Singapore grew rapidly as a free port, attracting migrants from China, India, the Malay Archipelago and beyond. It became a British crown colony in 1867.

Colonial growth

Trade expanded further after the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869. Singapore became a major port for the export of Malayan rubber and tin, and its population passed one million by the mid-20th century. The colony developed a diverse society of Chinese, Malay, Indian and European communities.

Second World War

Despite its reputation as an impregnable fortress, Singapore fell to Japanese forces in February 1942 after the Battle of Singapore, described by Winston Churchill as the worst disaster in British military history. The Japanese occupation lasted until 1945 and included the Sook Ching massacre of Chinese civilians.

Towards independence

After the war, Singapore returned to British rule but with growing anti-colonial sentiment. It gained internal self-government in 1959 under the People's Action Party led by Lee Kuan Yew, merged with Malaysia in 1963, and became a fully independent republic on 9 August 1965 after separation from Malaysia.

Modern Singapore

From 1965 Singapore pursued rapid industrialisation, export-oriented growth and investment in housing, education and infrastructure through agencies such as the Housing and Development Board and the Economic Development Board. It became one of the world's busiest ports and a global financial centre. Goh Chok Tong succeeded Lee Kuan Yew as prime minister in 1990, followed by Lee Hsien Loong in 2004 and Lawrence Wong in 2024.
