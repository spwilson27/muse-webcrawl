Henoch–Schönlein purpura
SpecialtyRheumatology, nephrology
Also known asIgA vasculitis
Usual onsetChildhood (ages 3–15)

Henoch–Schönlein purpura (HSP), also known as IgA vasculitis, is a disease of the skin, mucous membranes and sometimes other organs that most commonly affects children. It is a small-vessel vasculitis caused by deposits of immunoglobulin A (IgA) immune complexes.

Signs and symptoms

The classic tetrad consists of palpable purpura (a non-blanching rash, especially on the legs and buttocks), arthritis or arthralgia, abdominal pain, and kidney involvement (IgA nephropathy). It often follows an upper respiratory tract infection. Most cases resolve spontaneously within a few weeks.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is clinical, supported by skin or kidney biopsy showing IgA deposition. Treatment is usually supportive, with hydration and analgesia; corticosteroids may be used for severe abdominal or joint symptoms. Renal involvement requires monitoring, as a minority of patients develop significant kidney disease.

History

The condition is named after the German physicians Johann Lukas Schönlein and Eduard Heinrich Henoch, who described it in the 19th century.
