Johnny Unitas

John Constantine Unitas (May 7, 1933 – September 11, 2002), nicknamed "Johnny U" and "the Golden Arm", was an American professional football quarterback who played in the National Football League for 18 seasons, primarily with the Baltimore Colts. Widely regarded as one of the greatest quarterbacks of all time, he was a pioneer of the modern passing game and the two-minute drill.

Career

Unitas was drafted by the Pittsburgh Steelers in 1955 but cut before playing a game; signed by Baltimore in 1956, he became the Colts' starter and led them to victory in the 1958 NFL Championship Game against the New York Giants — the first sudden-death overtime game in NFL history, often called "the Greatest Game Ever Played" — and again in 1959. He won a third championship and Super Bowl V with the Colts, and set a record of 47 consecutive games with a touchdown pass that stood for 52 years.

Honours

Unitas was a ten-time Pro Bowl selection, three-time NFL Most Valuable Player, and was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1979. The Johnny Unitas Golden Arm Award is presented annually to the nation's top senior college quarterback.
