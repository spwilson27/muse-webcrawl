Bond number

In fluid mechanics, the Bond number (Bo), also called the Eötvös number (Eo), is a dimensionless quantity measuring the importance of gravitational forces compared to surface tension forces along an interface between two phases. It is named after the English physicist Wilfrid Noel Bond, and in European literature after the Hungarian physicist Loránd Eötvös. The Bond number is used to characterise the shape of bubbles and drops, and in the study of capillary flows in porous media.

Definition

The Bond number is defined as Bo = Δρ·g·L² / σ, where Δρ is the difference in density between the two phases, g is gravitational acceleration, L is a characteristic length (such as a drop radius or a pore size), and σ is the interfacial (surface) tension. A large Bond number indicates that gravity dominates and the interface is strongly deformed by hydrostatic forces — a large puddle flattens under its own weight. A small Bond number (much less than one) indicates that surface tension dominates, so small droplets and bubbles remain nearly spherical.

Related quantities

The Bond number is related to the capillary length λc = √(σ/(Δρ g)): it is the square of the ratio of the characteristic length to the capillary length. It is also connected to the Morton number and appears alongside the Reynolds and Weber numbers in regime maps for bubble columns, microfluidics, and enhanced oil recovery, where the balance of gravity and capillarity in porous rock governs how fluids are trapped and mobilised.
