Siege of Antwerp (1584–1585)

DateJuly 1584 – 17 August 1585
LocationAntwerp, Spanish Netherlands
ResultSpanish victory

The Siege of Antwerp during the Eighty Years' War lasted from July 1584 to August 1585 and ended with the fall of the great commercial city to Spanish forces under Alexander Farnese, Duke of Parma.

The siege

Farnese's engineering masterpiece was a fortified bridge of boats across the Scheldt, nearly a kilometre long, which cut Antwerp off from the sea. Dutch attempts to break it included explosive fireships — the "hellburners" of Antwerp, designed by Federico Giambelli — which destroyed part of the bridge in April 1585 but were not followed up decisively. Starving, the city capitulated on generous terms.

Consequences

Protestant inhabitants were given time to leave; tens of thousands emigrated north to Amsterdam and other Dutch cities, transferring Antwerp's commercial supremacy to Amsterdam and beginning the Dutch Golden Age.
