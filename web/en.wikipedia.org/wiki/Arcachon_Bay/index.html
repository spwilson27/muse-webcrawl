Arcachon Bay

Arcachon Bay (French: Bassin d'Arcachon) is a bay of the Atlantic Ocean on the southwest coast of France, in the Gironde department of Nouvelle-Aquitaine, between the town of La Teste-de-Buch and Cap Ferret. Covering roughly 150 km² at high tide (and far less at low tide), it is a major centre for oyster farming and seaside tourism.

Features

The bay is bordered on its western side by the great Dune of Pilat, the tallest sand dune in Europe, and contains the Île aux Oiseaux ("Bird Island") with its picturesque stilted huts, the cabanes tchanquées. The resort town of Arcachon, founded in the 19th century on the southern shore, is divided into quarters named for the seasons. The Leyre river flows into the bay from the Landes forest. Oysters from Arcachon Bay are prized across France, and the bay's tidal channels and sandbanks support rich bird life.
