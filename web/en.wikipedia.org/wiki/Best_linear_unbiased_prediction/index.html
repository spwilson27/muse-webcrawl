In statistics, best linear unbiased prediction (BLUP) is a method for predicting unobserved random effects in a linear mixed model. It is the counterpart of best linear unbiased estimation (BLUE), which concerns fixed effects: a BLUP is the predictor of a random effect that is linear in the data, unbiased, and has minimum mean squared error among such predictors.

Development and use

The method was developed by the American statistician Charles Roy Henderson in the 1940s and 1950s in the context of animal breeding, where the goal is to estimate the genetic merit ("breeding value") of sires from the performance records of their offspring. BLUP became the standard tool for genetic evaluation of livestock worldwide, because it can simultaneously account for fixed effects such as herd, year and season, relationships among animals through a pedigree-based relationship matrix, and data from relatives recorded in different environments.

Computation

BLUPs are obtained by solving Henderson's mixed model equations, a joint system of equations for the fixed effects and random effects derived from the model's normal equations. In the Bayesian interpretation, BLUP equals the posterior mean of the random effects under a normal prior. The same mathematics underlies kriging in geostatistics and prediction in modern genomic selection, where marker-based relationship matrices replace pedigrees. Extensions to non-normal data give generalised linear mixed models.
