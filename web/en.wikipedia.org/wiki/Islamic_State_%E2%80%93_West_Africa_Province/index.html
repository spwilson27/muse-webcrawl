Islamic State – West Africa Province

Active: 2015 – present

Area: Lake Chad Basin (Nigeria, Niger, Chad, Cameroon)

Predecessor: Boko Haram faction

Affiliation: Islamic State

The Islamic State – West Africa Province (ISWAP) is a militant Islamist group and a regional province of the Islamic State, active primarily in the Lake Chad Basin of northeastern Nigeria and neighboring Niger, Chad, and Cameroon. It emerged in 2015 when Boko Haram leader Abubakar Shekau pledged allegiance to the Islamic State, and it became a separate faction after a 2016 leadership split.

History

Following the Islamic State's appointment of Abu Musab al-Barnawi as governor of its West Africa province in 2016, the movement split: Shekau's followers retained the Boko Haram name while al-Barnawi's faction became ISWAP. ISWAP gained strength around Lake Chad, absorbed many fighters after Shekau's death in 2021, and has frequently clashed with rival jihadist factions.

Activities

ISWAP has carried out attacks on military positions, aid workers, and civilians; it also imposes taxation and provides some governance and services in areas under its influence, a strategy distinguishing it from Shekau's more indiscriminately violent approach. Regional forces, including the Multinational Joint Task Force, continue operations against the group.
