Fall of the Fascist regime in Italy

The fall of the Fascist regime in Italy, also known as the 25 July events (25 Luglio), occurred on 24–25 July 1943 when the Grand Council of Fascism passed a motion of no confidence in Benito Mussolini, leading King Victor Emmanuel III to dismiss and arrest him, ending 21 years of Fascist rule.

Background

By mid-1943 Italy faced military disaster: defeat in North Africa, the Allied invasion of Sicily on 10 July, and the first bombing of Rome eroded support for Mussolini among Fascist leaders, the military and the Crown.

The Grand Council vote

Dino Grandi tabled a motion restoring the King's constitutional powers. In the night session of 24–25 July the Grand Council approved it 19 votes to 8. The next afternoon the King dismissed Mussolini, who was arrested by carabinieri on leaving Villa Savoia, and appointed Marshal Pietro Badoglio prime minister.

Aftermath

Crowds celebrated and Fascist symbols were torn down during the "45 days" of the Badoglio government, which secretly negotiated an armistice with the Allies announced on 8 September 1943. Germany then occupied Italy, rescued Mussolini, and installed the Italian Social Republic in the north, sparking civil war alongside the Allied campaign.
