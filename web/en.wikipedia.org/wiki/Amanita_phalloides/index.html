Amanita phalloides

Amanita phalloides
Common name: death cap
Family: Amanitaceae
Toxicity: deadly poisonous (amatoxins)

Amanita phalloides, commonly known as the death cap, is a deadly poisonous basidiomycete fungus, one of many in the genus Amanita. Widely distributed across Europe, it has been introduced to other regions including North America, Australia, and New Zealand. It is believed to be responsible for the majority of fatal mushroom poisonings worldwide.

Description

The death cap has a greenish-yellow cap 5–15 cm across, white gills, a white stipe with a skirt-like ring, and a volva at the base. It grows in mycorrhizal association with broadleaved trees, especially oak and beech. Young fruit bodies can resemble edible mushrooms such as paddy straw mushrooms and Caesar's mushroom, which contributes to accidental poisonings.

Toxicity

The principal toxins are amatoxins, chiefly alpha-amanitin, which inhibit RNA polymerase II and cause liver and kidney failure. Symptoms are delayed 6–12 hours after ingestion, beginning with severe gastrointestinal distress, followed by an apparent recovery, then potentially fatal hepatic failure. Treatment is supportive, sometimes including silibinin and liver transplantation in severe cases.
