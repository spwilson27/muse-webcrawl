Transversal (geometry)

In geometry, a transversal is a line that intersects two or more other lines (or, in three dimensions, planes or other geometric objects) at distinct points. The study of transversals of parallel lines is a classical topic of Euclidean geometry, giving rise to the relations among corresponding angles, alternate interior angles, and co-interior (same-side interior) angles.

Angles formed by a transversal

When a transversal cuts two lines, eight angles are formed. Pairs of angles in matching corners are corresponding angles; angles on opposite sides of the transversal and inside the two lines are alternate interior angles; those on the same side are co-interior angles. If the two lines are parallel, corresponding and alternate interior angles are equal and co-interior angles are supplementary (sum to 180°); conversely, equality of corresponding angles implies that the lines are parallel — a statement closely related to Euclid's parallel postulate. These facts are used constantly in proofs and in practical surveying and construction.

Generalizations

The notion extends to transversals of families of curves and to higher dimensions: for example, a line transversal of a set of disjoint convex objects in the plane is a line intersecting all of them, a theme developed in transversal theory and Helly-type theorems.
