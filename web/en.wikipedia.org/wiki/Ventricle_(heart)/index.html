Ventricle (heart)

A ventricle is one of two large chambers toward the bottom of the heart that collect blood from an atrium and expel it into peripheral beds within the body and lungs. In a four-chambered heart, such as that in humans, there are two ventricles operating in a double circulatory system: the right ventricle pumps blood into the pulmonary circulation to the lungs, and the left ventricle pumps blood into the systemic circulation through the aorta.

Structure

The ventricles have thicker walls than the atria; the left ventricular wall is about three times thicker than the right, reflecting the higher pressures of the systemic circulation. Inside, trabeculae carneae line the walls, and papillary muscles anchor the atrioventricular valve leaflets via the chordae tendineae to prevent prolapse during contraction (systole).

Function

Ventricular contraction ejects a stroke volume of roughly 70 mL in a resting adult; the proportion ejected, the ejection fraction, is normally 55–70% for the left ventricle and is a key measure of cardiac function.

Clinical significance

Ventricular hypertrophy, dilation and failure are central features of many heart diseases. Life-threatening rhythms such as ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation arise in the ventricles and require urgent treatment, including defibrillation.
