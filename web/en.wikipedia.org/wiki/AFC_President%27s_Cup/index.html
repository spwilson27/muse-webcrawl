AFC President's Cup

AFC President's Cup
Founded: 2005
Abolished: 2014
Region: Asia (AFC)
Most successful club: Regar TadAZ (Tajikistan)

The AFC President's Cup was an annual international association football competition between domestic champion clubs sides run by the Asian Football Confederation (AFC), held between clubs from nations classified as "emerging" in Asian football.

History

The competition was created in 2005 as the third tier of Asian club football, below the AFC Champions League and the AFC Cup. The first tournament was held in Nepal in 2005 and won by Regar TadAZ of Tajikistan, who went on to win the cup three times, the most of any club.

Abolition

The last edition was held in 2014 and won by HTTU Aşgabat of Turkmenistan. From 2015, clubs from the participating nations were absorbed into the AFC Cup qualifying rounds, and the President's Cup was discontinued.
