Shrewsbury School

Shrewsbury School is a public school (an English fee-charging boarding school for pupils aged 13–18) in Shrewsbury, Shropshire, England. Founded in 1552 by Edward VI, it is one of the nine leading schools in England listed in the Public Schools Act 1868, and it is a member of the Headmasters' and Headmistresses' Conference.

History

Shrewsbury School was founded by a Royal Charter granted by King Edward VI in 1552. The school originally occupied buildings near St Mary's Church in the centre of Shrewsbury. Under its second headmaster, Thomas Ashton, appointed in 1561, the school rose to become one of the great schools of Elizabethan England, attracting pupils from across the country. Sir Philip Sidney and Fulke Greville were among its pupils in this period, and the school's drama productions were famous; Ashton is credited with founding English school drama.

In 1882, under headmaster Henry Whitehead Moss, the school moved to its present site at Kingsland, across the River Severn from the town centre, occupying the former Shrewsbury workhouse buildings and extensive grounds overlooking the river loop. The Main School Building, designed in an expansive red-brick style, dates from this move.

The school's most famous old boy is the naturalist Charles Darwin, who attended from 1818 to 1825 and famously disliked the classical curriculum, though the school now celebrates his connection strongly. Other notable Old Salopians include the poet and scholar A. E. Housman taught classics in the area, Nevil Shute's contemporary, the broadcaster and former Chancellor of the Exchequer? Notable alumni include Willie Rushton, Michael Palin and John Peel.

The school today

Shrewsbury became fully co-educational in September 2015, having admitted girls to the sixth form from 2008. There are around 800 pupils, most of them boarders, distributed among twelve houses (eight boys' houses and four girls' houses, several of them mixed by day). The school is known for its strong sporting tradition, particularly rowing on the River Severn (it is one of the founding schools of the sport and competes at Henley Royal Regatta), cross-country running (the school's Hunt is the oldest cross-country running club in the world, founded in 1831), and fives. Shrewsbury also has a distinguished music tradition.
