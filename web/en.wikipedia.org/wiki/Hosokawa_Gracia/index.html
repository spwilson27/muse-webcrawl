Hosokawa Gracia

Hosokawa Gracia
Born: 1563, Akechi Mitsuhide's daughter (Akechi Tama)
Died: 25 August 1600, Osaka
Spouse: Hosokawa Tadaoki

Hosokawa Gracia (1563 – 25 August 1600), born Akechi Tama (Tamako), was a Japanese noblewoman of the Sengoku period, daughter of Akechi Mitsuhide and wife of Hosokawa Tadaoki. She is remembered for her conversion to Christianity and her dramatic death.

Life

After her father assassinated Oda Nobunaga in the Honnō-ji Incident and was defeated at Yamazaki, Tama was hidden in a remote village, then restored as Tadaoki's wife. In Osaka she was exposed to Jesuit preaching, studied the faith, and was baptised in 1587 taking the name Gracia, defying Toyotomi Hideyoshi's anti-Christian edict.

Death

In 1600, as Ishida Mitsunari moved against Tokugawa allies, he attempted to take Gracia hostage. Following her instructions and Christian prohibition of suicide, she had a servant kill her, and the residence was set ablaze. Her death reportedly dissuaded other daimyō families from surrendering hostages and was widely publicised by Jesuit accounts, shaping European views of Japan.

Legacy

Gracia appears in novels, plays and James Clavell's Shōgun as the model for Lady Mariko.
