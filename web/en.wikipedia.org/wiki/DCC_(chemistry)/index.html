N,N'-Dicyclohexylcarbodiimide


Image placeholder: N,N'-Dicyclohexylcarbodiimide

N,N′-Dicyclohexylcarbodiimide (DCC or DCCD) is an organic compound with the formula (C6H11N)2C. It is a waxy white solid with a sweet smell, widely used as a coupling reagent to form amide and ester bonds, especially in peptide synthesis.

Reactions

DCC activates carboxylic acids toward nucleophilic attack by alcohols and amines, being converted in the process to the insoluble urea by-product dicyclohexylurea (DCU). It is the dehydrating agent in the Steglich esterification and was used in the first chemical syntheses of peptides by the Sheehan method in the 1950s. DCC also dehydrates alcohols in the Pfitzner–Moffatt oxidation.

Safety

DCC is a potent allergen and sensitizer; skin contact can cause severe dermatitis, and it should be handled with gloves in a fume hood. The insolubility of the DCU by-product can complicate purification, which has led to the use of alternative carbodiimides such as EDC in solution-phase work.
