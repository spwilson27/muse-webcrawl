Overclocking

Overclocking is the practice of increasing the clock rate of a computer component — such as a CPU, GPU or memory — beyond the rate specified by its manufacturer, in order to gain additional performance.

Overclocking increases power consumption and heat output, so enthusiasts use improved cooling, from larger heatsinks and fans to liquid cooling and, in extreme record attempts, liquid nitrogen. Raising voltages can improve stability at higher clocks but risks shortening component life or causing damage.

Manufacturers often sell unlocked processors and motherboards aimed at overclockers, and competitive overclocking is benchmarked with tools measuring stability and speed. The opposite practice, underclocking, reduces speed to save power or reduce noise.
