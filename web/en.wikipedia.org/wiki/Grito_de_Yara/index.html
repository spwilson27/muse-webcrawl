Grito de Yara

Commemoration of the Cry of Yara, 10 October 1868

The Grito de Yara (Spanish: "Cry of Yara") was the call to arms that began Cuba's Ten Years' War against Spanish colonial rule. It was issued on 10 October 1868 by the landowner and revolutionary Carlos Manuel de Céspedes at his sugar estate, La Demajagua, near the town of Yara in eastern Cuba.

Background

By the 1860s, discontent among Cuban creole landowners with Spanish taxation, trade restrictions and political exclusion had converged with the movement to abolish slavery. Conspiracies for independence spread through the island's east, and the revolutionary committees resolved to rise before Spanish authorities could arrest their leaders.

The uprising

On the morning of 10 October 1868, Céspedes freed his enslaved workers, invited them to join the struggle as free citizens, and raised the flag of an independent Cuba, proclaiming a manifesto that called for independence, gradual abolition of slavery and equality among all Cubans. The rebels soon captured Yara and then the city of Bayamo, where the Cuban national anthem, La Bayamesa, was first performed. Although Spain ultimately won the Ten Years' War, which ended with the Pact of Zanjón in 1878, the Grito de Yara is regarded as the founding act of the Cuban independence movement.

Legacy

The 10th of October is a national holiday in Cuba, celebrated as the beginning of the wars of independence that eventually ended Spanish rule in 1898. Céspedes is honoured as the "Father of the Homeland" (Padre de la Patria), and the bell of La Demajagua is a treasured national relic.
