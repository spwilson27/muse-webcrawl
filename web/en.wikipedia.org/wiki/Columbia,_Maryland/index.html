Columbia is a census-designated place in Howard County, Maryland, United States, and is one of the principal communities of the Baltimore–Washington metropolitan area. It is a planned community consisting of ten self-contained villages, founded by developer James W. Rouse and built by the Howard Hughes Corporation. Construction began in 1966 and the community was officially opened on June 21, 1967.

History

The Rouse Company began secretly acquiring land in Howard County in 1962–63, assembling some 14,000 acres between Baltimore and Washington, D.C. Rouse envisioned a community that would avoid the leapfrog, segregated sprawl typical of postwar suburbs, instead fostering economic, racial, and religious integration. Columbia was among the most prominent of the American "new towns" of the era, alongside Reston, Virginia, and Irvine, California.

Villages

Columbia is divided into ten villages — Wilde Lake, Harper's Choice, Oakland Mills, Long Reach, Owen Brown, Hickory Ridge, Town Center, King's Contrivance, Dorsey's Search, and River Hill — each with its own village center, schools, and community association. The Columbia Association maintains extensive open space, some 3,600 acres, plus lakes Kittamaqundi, Elkhorn, and Wilde Lake, and roughly 95 miles of pathways.

Demographics and economy

At the 2020 census the population was 104,681. Columbia regularly ranks among the wealthiest and most diverse communities in the United States and has appeared at or near the top of national "best places to live" lists. Major employers in the area include health care providers, defense and technology firms, and the nearby Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory.

Culture

The outdoor Merriweather Post Pavilion, designed by Frank Gehry, has hosted major concerts since 1967. The planned community also pioneered interfaith worship centers shared by multiple congregations.
