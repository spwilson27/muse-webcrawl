The Vatican Library, formally the Vatican Apostolic Library (Latin: Bibliotheca Apostolica Vaticana), is the library of the Holy See, located in Vatican City. It is one of the oldest libraries in the world and contains one of the most significant collections of historical texts anywhere, though access is restricted to qualified researchers.

History

Papal collections of books existed from late antiquity, but the modern library was founded by Pope Nicholas V in the mid-15th century as a humanist library for the Roman court, and formally established by Pope Sixtus IV with the bull Ad decorem militantis Ecclesiae in 1475, when it held some 3,500 items — then the largest collection in the West. The library grew through gifts and acquisitions, including the library of Queen Christina of Sweden (1690), the Barberini and Borgia collections, and manuscripts transferred from the Vatican's secret archives. Pope Leo XIII opened the library more fully to scholars in the 19th century.

Collections

The library holds about 75,000 manuscript codices in Latin, Greek, Hebrew, Syriac, Coptic, Arabic, Persian, Ethiopic and other languages, some 1.1 million printed books, around 8,500 incunabula, and large collections of coins, medals, prints and photographs. Treasures include the Codex Vaticanus (c. 325–350), one of the oldest near-complete Bibles; the Vergilius Vaticanus and Vergilius Romanus, late-antique illustrated Virgil manuscripts; and original letters of Martin Luther, Michelangelo and Henry VIII's love letters to Anne Boleyn. The frescoed Sistine Hall (1587–89) served as its grand reading room under Sixtus V.

Digitisation

Since 2010 the library has pursued a large-scale digitisation programme, working with partners including NTT Data and the Polonsky Foundation, gradually publishing high-resolution images of its manuscripts freely online through its DigiVatLib portal.
