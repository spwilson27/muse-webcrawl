Social construction of illness

The social construction of illness is a concept in medical sociology holding that the meanings, experiences, and even definitions of illness are shaped by cultural and social processes rather than biology alone. It draws on the broader theory of social constructionism.

Key ideas

Scholars distinguish the biological condition (disease) from the lived experience (illness) and its social role (sickness). Which conditions are recognised as legitimate illnesses, how sufferers are treated, and what treatments are offered vary across societies and historical periods. Historical examples include conditions once moralised or criminalised that are now medicalised, and vice versa.

Critique

Critics caution that emphasising social construction can understate the biological reality of disease; most medical sociologists treat the approach as complementary to biomedical explanation rather than a replacement.
