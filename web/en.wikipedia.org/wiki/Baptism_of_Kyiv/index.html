Baptism of Kyiv
Date988 CE
LocationKyiv, Kievan Rus'
Ordered byPrince Vladimir the Great
ResultAdoption of Orthodox Christianity as the religion of Kievan Rus'

The Baptism of Kyiv was the mass baptism of the inhabitants of Kyiv in 988, part of the Christianization of Kievan Rus' under Prince Vladimir the Great. According to the Primary Chronicle, Vladimir, having himself been baptized — traditionally at Chersonesus in Crimea, in connection with his marriage to the Byzantine princess Anna — ordered the people of Kyiv to assemble at the Dnieper River, where priests baptized them en masse. Pagan idols, including the great statue of Perun, were thrown into the river.

Background

Vladimir's grandmother Olga had converted to Christianity decades earlier, but the Rus' remained largely pagan. The Chronicle's famous account of emissaries comparing religions — Islam, Judaism, Latin Christianity and Byzantine Orthodoxy — is generally read as a literary framing of a political and diplomatic decision that bound Rus' to Byzantium.

Legacy

The baptism of 988 is treated as the founding moment of the Russian and Ukrainian Orthodox churches and is commemorated annually on 28 July. A monumental statue of St Vladimir overlooks the Dnieper in Kyiv, and the event remains central — and contested — in the historical memory of both Ukraine and Russia.
