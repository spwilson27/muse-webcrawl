Karamata's inequality

Karamata's inequality relates majorization of sequences to sums of convex functions applied to them.

Karamata's inequality, named after the Yugoslav (Serbian) mathematician Jovan Karamata (1902–1967), is an elementary algebraic inequality in the theory of majorization. It is a generalization of Jensen's inequality and is widely used in mathematical olympiads and in the theory of inequalities. Karamata published the inequality in 1932.

Statement

Let I be an interval of the real line and let f denote a real-valued, convex function defined on I. If x1, …, xn and y1, …, yn are numbers in I such that the sequence (x1, …, xn) majorizes (y1, …, yn) — that is, when both sequences are written in non-increasing order, the partial sums of the x's are at least those of the y's, and the total sums are equal — then

f(x1) + … + f(xn) ≥ f(y1) + … + f(yn).

If f is strictly convex, equality holds only if the two sequences are equal up to permutation, or if the sequences differ only where f is affine.

Proof idea

The standard proof uses Abel summation (summation by parts): the difference of the two sums can be rewritten as a sum of products of partial-sum differences and tangent-line slopes of f, each term being nonnegative because the partial sums of the majorizing sequence dominate and the slopes of a convex function are nondecreasing.

Applications

Karamata's inequality implies several classical inequalities. With f(x) = xr (r ≥ 1) or f(x) = ex it yields comparison results for moments and exponential means. It also follows from Popoviciu's inequality and is closely related to the concept of Schur-convex functions: a symmetric function is Schur-convex precisely when it preserves the majorization order in the sense of Karamata.
