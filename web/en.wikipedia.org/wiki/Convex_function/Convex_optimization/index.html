Convex optimization

Convex optimization is a subfield of mathematical optimization that studies the problem of minimizing a convex function over a convex set. The property of convexity means that any local minimum is also a global minimum, which makes convex problems far more tractable than general optimization problems.

Standard form

A convex optimization problem in standard form is to minimize f0(x) subject to inequality constraints fi(x) ≤ 0 and equality constraints hi(x) = 0, where the objective and inequality constraint functions are convex and the equality constraints are affine.

Important subclasses

  • Linear programming — linear objective and constraints.
  • Quadratic programming — quadratic objective, linear constraints.
  • Second-order cone programming and semidefinite programming — conic generalizations.
  • Least squares and entropy maximization problems.

Algorithms

Convex problems can be solved efficiently by interior point methods, which achieve polynomial-time complexity, and by first-order methods such as gradient descent and its variants for large-scale problems. Duality theory, in particular the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions, provides optimality certificates.

Applications

Convex optimization is used in control systems, signal processing, statistics and machine learning (for example support vector machines and LASSO regression), finance, circuit design and structural engineering.
