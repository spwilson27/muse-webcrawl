Jensen's inequality

In mathematics, Jensen's inequality, named after the Danish mathematician Johan Jensen, relates the value of a convex function of an integral to the integral of the convex function. It was proved by Jensen in 1906, building on an earlier proof with weaker conditions by Otto Hölder from 1889.

Jensen's inequality
FieldReal analysis, probability theory
Named afterJohan Jensen
Statement (finite form)φ(Σλixi) ≤ Σλiφ(xi) for convex φ

Forms

For a convex function φ, numbers x1, …, xn in its domain, and positive weights ai, the inequality states that φ of the weighted mean is at most the weighted mean of φ. For a concave function the inequality reverses. In probabilistic form, for a random variable X, φ(E[X]) ≤ E[φ(X)] whenever the expectations exist.

Proof idea

The finite form follows by induction from the defining property of convexity: a convex function lies below the chord between any two of its points. The general integral form follows by considering a supporting line of the convex function at the mean point and integrating.

Applications

Jensen's inequality underlies many classical results: the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means, Hölder's and Minkowski's inequalities, properties of entropy in information theory, and risk-aversion results in economics, where concavity of utility implies that a certain outcome is preferred to a gamble with the same mean.
