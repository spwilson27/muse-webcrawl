British National Party
Abbreviation: BNP
Founded: 1982
Ideology: far-right nationalism

The British National Party (BNP) is a far-right political party in the United Kingdom. It was formed in 1982 by John Tyndall from factions of the National Front. Under Nick Griffin, who led the party from 1999 to 2014, the BNP attempted to present a more moderate public image while retaining policies centred on opposition to immigration and multiculturalism.

Electoral history

The BNP won local council seats in the 2000s, a seat on the London Assembly in 2008, and two seats in the European Parliament in 2009, its electoral peak. It subsequently collapsed amid internal disputes, financial problems and competition from UKIP, losing most of its elected representatives.

Policies and criticism

The party historically restricted membership to "indigenous" white Britons; this policy was ruled unlawful in 2010 and the constitution was amended. The BNP has been widely characterised by opponents and researchers as fascist and racist.
