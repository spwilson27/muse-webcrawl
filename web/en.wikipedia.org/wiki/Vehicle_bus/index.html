Vehicle bus

A vehicle bus is a specialised internal communications network that interconnects components inside a vehicle, such as electronic control units (ECUs), sensors, and actuators. Vehicle buses allow multiple devices to share wiring, reducing the weight, cost, and complexity of point-to-point harnesses.

The most widely used vehicle bus is the CAN bus (Controller Area Network), developed by Bosch in the 1980s. Other standards include LIN for low-cost body electronics, FlexRay for high-speed safety-critical systems, MOST for multimedia, and Automotive Ethernet for high-bandwidth applications such as cameras and infotainment.

Design considerations

Vehicle networks must meet real-time constraints, work reliably in electrically noisy environments, and increasingly address cybersecurity, as external connectivity expands the attack surface of modern vehicles.
