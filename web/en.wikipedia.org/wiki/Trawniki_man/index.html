Trawniki man

A Trawniki man (German: Trawnikimänner) was a recruit trained at the SS training camp at Trawniki in German-occupied Poland during World War II, who served as an auxiliary policeman or guard in the Operation Reinhard extermination camps and other sites of the Holocaust.

The Trawniki men were recruited mainly from Soviet prisoners of war, many of them Ukrainians, who volunteered or were coerced into service to escape the lethal conditions of German POW camps. They were also drawn from ethnic German and other Eastern European populations. After training at Trawniki, they were deployed to Belzec, Sobibor, and Treblinka, as well as to ghettos, labour camps, and anti-partisan operations.

Role in the Holocaust

Trawniki guards performed tasks such as manning camp watchtowers, escorting transports, and participating in mass shootings and deportations. Their service freed German personnel for other duties and was integral to the functioning of the killing centres, where roughly 1.5 million people were murdered under Operation Reinhard.

Postwar

After the war, some Trawniki men were tried in the Soviet Union and West Germany. The most famous case in the United States was that of John Demjanjuk, who was accused of being a guard at Treblinka and Sobibor and was convicted in Germany in 2011 as an accessory to murder at Sobibor.
