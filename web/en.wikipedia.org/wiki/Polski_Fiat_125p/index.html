Polski Fiat 125p

A Polski Fiat 125p sedan.

The Polski Fiat 125p was a motor vehicle manufactured between 1967 and 1991 in Poland by Fabryka Samochodów Osobowych (FSO) under a licence from the Italian manufacturer Fiat. It was a simplified version of the Italian Fiat 125, combining the body of the 125 with the engines and mechanicals of the older Fiat 1300/1500.

History

Production began in 1967 in Warsaw. After the Fiat licence expired, the car was rebadged from 1983 as the FSO 125p, and later versions were known as the FSO 1500. Body styles included a four-door saloon, a five-door estate (kombi), and a pick-up. Over 1.4 million examples were built, making it one of the most common cars in Poland during the communist era.

Exports and assembly abroad

The 125p was exported to numerous countries, including the United Kingdom (where it was sold in right-hand-drive form), and assembled from kits in countries such as Yugoslavia and Egypt, where it was produced by Nasr as the Nasr 125.

Specifications

Early cars used 1.3-litre and 1.5-litre overhead-valve engines derived from the Fiat 1300/1500. Later versions received the 1.5-litre engine as standard. The car was noted for its simple, robust construction and ease of repair, though its design was dated by the end of production in 1991.

Legacy

Alongside the smaller Polski Fiat 126p, the 125p motorised Poland in the 1970s and 1980s and remains a common sight at classic car events in the country.
