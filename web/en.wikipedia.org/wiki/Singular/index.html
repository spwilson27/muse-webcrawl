Singular

FieldLinguistics
CategoryGrammatical number
ContrastPlural

In grammar, the singular is a form of a noun, pronoun, adjective or verb that indicates one person or thing, in contrast to the plural, which indicates more than one. Many languages also preserve a dual number for exactly two, and some have trial or paucal forms.

Marking

Languages mark singularity in different ways: in English the singular is usually the unmarked base form of a noun (cat) while the plural adds -s, but verbs show the opposite pattern, with the third-person singular taking -s (she runs). Pronouns distinguish singular I, you, he/she/it from plural we, you, they.

Agreement

Singular subjects take singular verb forms, and determiners and adjectives may agree in number with the noun they modify. Collective nouns such as 'team' or 'government' can behave as singular or plural depending on dialect and whether the group is viewed as a unit or as individuals.

Other languages

Some languages, including Welsh and Arabic, use singulative forms derived from collective nouns to denote one item. In such systems the basic form may denote a group, with the singular explicitly derived.
