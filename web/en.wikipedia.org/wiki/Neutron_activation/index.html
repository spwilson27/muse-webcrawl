Neutron activation

Neutron activation is the process in which neutron radiation induces radioactivity in materials. It occurs when atomic nuclei capture free neutrons, becoming heavier and entering excited states; the excited nucleus decays, often emitting gamma rays, to a more stable form.

Process

When a nucleus absorbs a neutron in an (n,γ) reaction, it forms a compound nucleus in an excited state that de-excites by gamma emission, leaving a heavier isotope of the same element. That product may itself be radioactive and decay by beta emission or other modes. The amount of activation depends on the neutron flux, the neutron capture cross-section, and the duration of irradiation.

Applications

Neutron activation analysis is a highly sensitive analytical technique for determining the elemental composition of samples, used in geochemistry, archaeology and forensics — famously in investigations of trace elements in hair. Activation is also used to produce medical and industrial radioisotopes. Conversely, activation of structural materials is a concern in nuclear reactors and fusion devices, influencing material choice, maintenance and waste classification; it is also a factor in the radiological aftermath of neutron weapons.
