Damn the Torpedoes

"Damn the torpedoes, full speed ahead!" is a famous order given by Rear Admiral David Farragut during the Battle of Mobile Bay, a decisive naval battle of the American Civil War, on 5 August 1864. The exact wording of Farragut's order is a matter of some dispute, but the phrase has become a byword for resolute action in the face of danger.

Background

Mobile Bay, on the coast of Alabama, was one of the last Confederate ports open to blockade runners. It was defended by forts, a small Confederate flotilla commanded by Admiral Franklin Buchanan, and a field of naval mines, which at the time were called "torpedoes". Farragut's fleet consisted of ironclad monitors and wooden steam sloops lashed together in pairs.

The battle

As the Union fleet entered the bay, the monitor Tecumseh veered toward the Confederate ram Tennessee, struck a torpedo, and sank almost immediately with most of her crew. The leading wooden ship, Brooklyn, stopped in confusion directly in the path of the minefield. Seeing the line falter, Farragut — who had lashed himself in the rigging of his flagship Hartford to see over the smoke — ordered his ship ahead.

The quotation

According to one account, Farragut shouted: "Damn the torpedoes! Four bells! Captain Drayton, go ahead! Jouett, full speed!" Other witnesses recalled variations such as "Damn the torpedoes, go ahead!" The popular form, "Damn the torpedoes, full speed ahead!", is a later condensation. Whatever the precise words, Hartford led the fleet safely through the minefield; several torpedoes were heard scraping along the hulls, but most had been under water too long and failed to explode.

Outcome and legacy

The Union fleet defeated the Confederate squadron and captured the Tennessee; the forts surrendered within weeks, closing Mobile Bay to blockade running, though the city of Mobile itself held out until April 1865. The phrase entered American idiom as an expression of determination. It is also the title of a 1979 album by Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers.
