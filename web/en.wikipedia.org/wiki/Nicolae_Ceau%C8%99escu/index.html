Nicolae Ceaușescu

BornJanuary 26, 1918, Scornicești, Romania
DiedDecember 25, 1989, Târgoviște, Romania

Nicolae Ceaușescu (January 26, 1918 – December 25, 1989) was a Romanian communist dictator who led the Socialist Republic of Romania from 1965 until his overthrow and execution in the Romanian Revolution of 1989. He succeeded Gheorghe Gheorghiu-Dej as general secretary of the Romanian Communist Party and initially pursued a relatively independent foreign policy within the Eastern Bloc, before consolidating an increasingly personalist and repressive regime at home.

Rise and early rule

Born to a peasant family in Scornicești, Ceaușescu joined the communist movement as a teenager and spent periods in prison before the party came to power. After 1965 he won Western favour by condemning the 1968 Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia and maintaining relations with China, Israel and the West, attracting foreign loans for ambitious industrialisation programmes.

In 1974 he created the office of President of Romania for himself. Inspired partly by visits to North Korea and China, he issued the July Theses of 1971, tightening ideological control and beginning a personality cult that glorified him as Conducător ('Leader').

Later rule

During the 1980s Ceaușescu imposed severe austerity to repay Romania's foreign debt, exporting food and fuel while the population endured rationing, cold apartments and shortages. His systematisation programme demolished villages and historic districts of Bucharest, where the colossal Palace of the Parliament was begun in 1984. His wife Elena wielded enormous power as deputy prime minister and head of scientific policy.

The Securitate secret police maintained pervasive surveillance, and pronatalist policies begun with Decree 770 of 1966 banned abortion and contraception, producing overcrowded orphanages that shocked the world after 1989.

Fall and execution

Protests that began in Timișoara in December 1989 spread to Bucharest; on December 21 a staged rally turned against Ceaușescu, and the next day he and Elena fled the capital by helicopter. They were captured, tried by an improvised military tribunal on charges including genocide, and executed by firing squad in Târgoviște on December 25, 1989 — the only violent overthrow of a communist government in the revolutions of 1989.
