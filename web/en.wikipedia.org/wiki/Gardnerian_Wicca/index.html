Gardnerian Wicca

Gardnerian Wicca, or Gardnerian witchcraft, is a tradition in the neopagan religion of Wicca, named after Gerald Gardner (1884–1964), a British civil servant and occultist who publicised the tradition in the 1950s and is widely regarded as the founder of modern Wicca.


Founder: Gerald Gardner
Emerged: 1950s, England
Practices: Initiatory, coven-based, oathbound

Beliefs and practice

Gardnerian Wicca is duotheistic, honouring a Goddess and a God, initiatory and mystery-based: adherents are initiated by a high priestess or priest into a coven, progressing through three degrees. Rituals, recorded in a Book of Shadows copied by hand, are traditionally performed skyclad and include the eight Sabbats of the Wheel of the Year.

History

Gardner claimed initiation in 1939 into a surviving coven in the New Forest; historians treat his account as drawing heavily on ceremonial magic, folklore and Margaret Murray's witch-cult hypothesis. Early initiates such as Doreen Valiente reshaped the liturgy. Gardnerian lineages spread to North America in the 1960s; see also Gaelic League for another cultural revival movement of similar vintage — in Ireland.
