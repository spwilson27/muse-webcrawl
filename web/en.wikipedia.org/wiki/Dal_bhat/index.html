Dal bhat

Dal bhat is a traditional meal from the Indian subcontinent, consisting of steamed rice (bhat) and a lentil soup (dal), accompanied by vegetable curries, pickles and other side dishes. It is the staple national dish of Nepal, widely eaten twice daily, and is also common across India and Bangladesh.

Composition

A typical dal bhat set includes white rice and dal made from lentils, black gram, chickpeas or other pulses cooked with turmeric, cumin, garlic and other spices. Accompaniments (tarkari) often include seasonal vegetable curry, saag (greens), tomato chutney, achaar (pickle), papad and sometimes meat curry or yoghurt. In Nepal it is commonly served on a brass or steel platter (thali) with unlimited refills.

Cultural significance

Dal bhat provides the energy base of the Nepali diet; the saying "Dal bhat power, 24 hour" is popular among trekkers in the Himalayas, where dal bhat is the standard lodge meal. Variations exist across regions, such as dhindo (a thick millet or buckwheat porridge) served in place of rice in hill areas.
