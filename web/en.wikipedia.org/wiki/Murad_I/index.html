Murad I (Ottoman Turkish: مراد اول; Turkish: I. Murad, 1326 – 15 June 1389), nicknamed Hüdavendigâr, was the sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1362 to 1389. Under his rule the small Anatolian beylik became a major Balkan power.

Reign

Murad succeeded his father Orhan in 1362 and immediately expanded into Thrace, taking Edirne (Adrianople), which became the Ottomans' European capital. His forces crushed a Serbian-Bosnian coalition at the Battle of Maritsa (1371), opening Macedonia and Serbia to Ottoman vassalage, and captured Sofia and Niš in the 1380s. In Anatolia he annexed territory from rival Turkish beyliks, sometimes by marriage and purchase as well as war.

Murad is credited with major institutional innovations: the office of grand vizier, the Janissary corps recruited through the devşirme levy, the timar system of military land grants, and the first Ottoman military judge (kazasker). He was the first Ottoman ruler to take and regularly use the title of sultan.

Death

Murad was killed at the Battle of Kosovo on 15 June 1389, according to tradition assassinated by the Serbian knight Miloš Obilić, who gained access to the sultan's tent by feigning desertion. His tomb (türbe) at the Kosovo field remains a place of veneration. He was succeeded by his son Bayezid I, "the Thunderbolt", who had his own brother executed on the battlefield — the first recorded Ottoman royal fratricide.
