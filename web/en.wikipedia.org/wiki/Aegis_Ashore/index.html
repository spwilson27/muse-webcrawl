Aegis Ashore

Image placeholder: Aegis Ashore site in Deveselu, Romania

Aegis Ashore is the land-based component of the Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense System, developed by the United States Missile Defense Agency. It installs the AN/SPY-1 radar, command-and-control equipment and vertical launching systems of a US Navy Aegis destroyer's superstructure in a fixed shore facility, allowing Standard Missile 3 (SM-3) interceptors to engage ballistic missiles outside the atmosphere.

Type: Land-based missile defence system
Operator: United States Navy / MDA
Sites: Deveselu (Romania), Redzikowo (Poland), Kauai test site (Hawaii)

Deployments

Aegis Ashore was developed under the European Phased Adaptive Approach to missile defence announced in 2009. The first operational site, at Deveselu in Romania, became operational in 2016. A second site at Redzikowo in Poland experienced construction delays and entered service in the mid-2020s. A test facility at the Pacific Missile Range Facility in Hawaii supports development and was briefly activated for the defence of Guam studies.

Japan's cancelled programme

Japan selected Aegis Ashore in 2017 to strengthen its defences against North Korean missiles, planning two sites in Akita and Yamaguchi prefectures. In June 2020 the government suspended and then cancelled the deployment, citing cost growth, schedule delays and concerns that interceptor booster stages could fall on populated areas; Japan instead chose to build two large Aegis-equipped vessels.

Controversy

Russia has repeatedly objected that the Mk 41 launchers used by Aegis Ashore could also fire Tomahawk cruise missiles, alleging a violation of the Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty while it remained in force; the United States denied the claim, arguing the ashore configuration lacks the software and equipment for offensive launches.
