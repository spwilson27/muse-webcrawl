Umbrella Movement

Date: 26 September – 15 December 2014
Location: Hong Kong (Admiralty, Causeway Bay, Mong Kok)
Cause: Beijing's restrictive framework for the 2017 Chief Executive election
Outcome: Protest sites cleared; no concessions on electoral reform

The Umbrella Movement (Chinese: 雨傘運動) was a pro-democracy protest movement that emerged during the 2014 Hong Kong protests. For 79 days, demonstrators occupied major roadways in Admiralty, Causeway Bay and Mong Kok, demanding genuine universal suffrage for the election of Hong Kong's Chief Executive. The movement took its name and symbol from the umbrellas protesters used to shield themselves from police pepper spray and tear gas.

Background

On 31 August 2014, China's National People's Congress Standing Committee ruled that candidates for the 2017 election would first have to be approved by a nominating committee, which critics said would screen out pro-democracy candidates. Students boycotted classes, and students and Occupy Central activists converged on government headquarters in late September.

Course of the protests

On 28 September police fired tear gas at the crowds, prompting tens of thousands more to join the occupation. Protest camps with tents, supply stations and study corners occupied the districts for over two months. Talks between the government and the Hong Kong Federation of Students in October produced no breakthrough. Court injunctions were used to clear Mong Kok in late November, and the main Admiralty site was cleared peacefully on 11 December, followed by Causeway Bay four days later.

Legacy

The movement politicised a generation of young Hong Kongers, gave rise to new political parties and "localist" currents, and foreshadowed the much larger protests of 2019. Several of its leaders, including Joshua Wong, Nathan Law and Agnes Chow, became internationally known democracy activists.
