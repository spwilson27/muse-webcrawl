Overview

The Merina people are the largest ethnic group of Madagascar, inhabiting the central highlands around the capital Antananarivo (the historic kingdom of Imerina). Their language, Merina, is the basis of standard Malagasy and of the country's official language. Merina society descends in part from Austronesian settlers who crossed the Indian Ocean over a thousand years ago, layered with African and later influences.

History

From the late 18th century, kings Andrianampoinimerina and his son Radama I unified Imerina and most of Madagascar, making the Merina monarchy the island's ruling power through the 19th century — advised at times by European missionaries and soldiers (the LMS brought schools, printing and Christianity under Radama and, after persecution under Ranavalona I, restored under her successors). The monarchy fell to French colonisation in 1896–97 (the Menalamba revolt followed). Merina elites have remained prominent in Madagascar's politics since independence in 1960, in tension with coastal peoples (côtiers) that recurs in the country's crises.
