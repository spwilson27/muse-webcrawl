Cor Caroli is a binary star designated Alpha Canum Venaticorum (α Canum Venaticorum), the brightest star in the northern constellation of Canes Venatici. Its name is Latin for "Charles's Heart", and it was named in honour of a King Charles of England — traditionally Charles I, though sometimes said to commemorate Charles II; the name was suggested by court physician Sir Charles Scarborough and appears on Edmond Halley's star chart of 1725 — usually credited in accounts of the naming.

Components

The system consists of two stars separated by about 19.6 arcseconds, easily split in small telescopes. The brighter component, α2 Canum Venaticorum, is the prototype of the Alpha2 Canum Venaticorum variables: a chemically peculiar A-type star with an extremely strong magnetic field (thousands of times the Sun's) and overabundances of silicon, mercury and europium. Its brightness varies slightly with a period of about 5.47 days as starspots rotate in and out of view. The fainter companion, α1, is an F-type main-sequence star of magnitude 5.6.

Properties

The pair lies roughly 110 light-years from Earth. The α2 component has about three times the mass of the Sun and is several hundred times more luminous. Cor Caroli marks the northern vertex of the spring asterism known as the Great Diamond, together with Arcturus, Spica and Denebola.
