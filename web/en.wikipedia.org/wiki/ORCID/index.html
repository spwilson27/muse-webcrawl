ORCID

Full nameOpen Researcher and Contributor ID
LaunchedOctober 2012
Identifier format16 digits in four groups of four, e.g. 0000-0002-1825-0097
Operated byORCID, Inc. (non-profit)

ORCID (Open Researcher and Contributor ID) is a non-proprietary alphanumeric code and associated registry that uniquely identifies authors and contributors to scholarly and research communication. It was created to solve the problem of name ambiguity in scholarship — many researchers share the same name, and one researcher's name may appear in many forms — by giving each researcher a persistent digital identifier, the ORCID iD, linked to a profile recording their affiliations, works, funding and other activities.

The identifier

An ORCID iD consists of 16 digits arranged in four groups separated by hyphens (the last character may be the letter X, serving as a checksum value), and is expressed as a URI such as https://orcid.org/0000-0002-1825-0097. The numbering is based on the ISNI standard. Registration is free for individuals, and millions of researchers have registered; many publishers, funders and universities require or encourage ORCID iDs in manuscript submissions, grant applications and institutional systems.

Organisation

ORCID, Inc., a non-profit organisation, launched the registry in October 2012. Publishers and other member organisations can, with the researcher's permission, add verified information such as publications (often via Crossref and DataCite) and employment affiliations to profiles, reducing the reporting burden on researchers. ORCID is part of a broader ecosystem of persistent identifiers alongside DOIs for outputs and ROR IDs for research organisations.
