Pilot certification in the United States

Pilot certification in the United States is required by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) for anyone wishing to act as pilot in command of an aircraft. Certificates are issued under Part 61 of the Federal Aviation Regulations and cover a graded series of privileges from student pilot through airline transport pilot.

Certificate levels

Student pilot

A student pilot certificate allows a person to fly solo under the supervision of a flight instructor after receiving the required endorsements. Applicants must be at least 16 years old (14 for gliders and balloons) and hold an appropriate medical certificate.

Recreational pilot

The recreational pilot certificate permits flying light aircraft within 50 nautical miles of the home airport, in daytime visual conditions, with limited passengers.

Private pilot

The private pilot certificate is the most common certificate. It requires at least 40 hours of flight time (with a minimum of 20 hours of instruction and 10 hours of solo flight), a written knowledge test, and a practical test (checkride) with an FAA examiner.

Commercial pilot

A commercial pilot certificate allows the holder to be compensated for flying. It generally requires 250 hours of total flight time and advanced maneuvers training.

Airline transport pilot

The airline transport pilot (ATP) certificate is required to act as pilot in command of scheduled air carrier operations. It generally requires 1,500 hours of total flight time, subject to restricted-ATP provisions for certain graduates and military pilots.

Ratings

Certificates carry category, class, and type ratings, such as airplane single-engine land, multiengine land, and instrument rating, which authorizes flight under instrument flight rules.

Medical certification

Most pilots must periodically pass an aviation medical examination. Since 2017, the BasicMed program has allowed eligible private pilots to fly under a driver's-license medical standard in lieu of a third-class medical certificate.
