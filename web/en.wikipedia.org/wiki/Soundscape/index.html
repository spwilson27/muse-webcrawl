Soundscape

A soundscape is the acoustic environment as perceived by humans, in context. The term was popularised by Canadian composer and researcher R. Murray Schafer, whose World Soundscape Project at Simon Fraser University in the 1970s pioneered the study of acoustic ecology.

Concepts

Schafer distinguished "hi-fi" soundscapes, where individual sounds are clearly audible (typical of rural settings), from "lo-fi" soundscapes in which signals are masked by dense noise, as in modern cities. He introduced terms such as keynote sounds, soundmarks (sounds unique to a place, analogous to landmarks), and schizophonia — the splitting of a sound from its source through recording and broadcast.

Soundscape ecology

In ecology, soundscape research (associated with Bernie Krause and others) divides the acoustic environment into biophony (animal sounds), geophony (wind, water, weather), and anthropophony (human-made noise), using sound recordings to assess ecosystem health and biodiversity.
