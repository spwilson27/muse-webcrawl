Cryolite

Names
Formula: Na3AlF6
Sodium hexafluoroaluminate
Crystal system: Monoclinic
Mohs hardness: 2.5–3

Cryolite (Na3AlF6, sodium hexafluoroaluminate) is an uncommon mineral identified with the once-large deposit at Ivittuut on the west coast of Greenland, which was mined commercially until 1987. It occurs as colourless to white glassy monoclinic crystals and was named in 1799 from the Greek words for "frost" and "stone", in allusion to its icy appearance.

Uses

Cryolite's principal importance is in the Hall–Héroult process for smelting aluminium: alumina dissolves in molten cryolite, lowering the working temperature of the electrolytic bath from over 2000 °C to around 940–980 °C. Because natural deposits are nearly exhausted, industrial cryolite is now synthesised from fluorite. Cryolite has also been used as a flux in glass and enamel manufacture, as a filler, and historically as an insecticide and pesticide.

Occurrence

Besides Ivittuut, cryolite has been reported from a small number of localities including Pikes Peak in Colorado, Mont Saint-Hilaire in Canada, and Miass in the Ural Mountains. See also Hall–Héroult process and Aluminium.
