Grose River

The Grose River, a perennial river of the Hawkesbury–Nepean catchment, is located in the Blue Mountains region of New South Wales, Australia. It rises in the Grose Valley near Mount Victoria and flows generally eastward through a deep sandstone gorge before joining the Nepean River at Agnes Banks, near Richmond, where the combined stream becomes the Hawkesbury River.

Course and features

The river descends several hundred metres over its course of roughly 55 kilometres. Much of its length lies within the Blue Mountains National Park, and its valley — the Grose Valley, sometimes called the "Blue Labyrinth" — is noted for its rugged cliffs, eucalypt forest, and the groves of blue gum trees around Blue Gum Forest, an iconic site in Australian conservation history. In 1932, bushwalkers led by conservationist Myles Dunphy campaigned successfully to save Blue Gum Forest from logging and clearing, an event regarded as a founding moment of the Australian conservation movement.

History

The river was named in 1794 by Governor Arthur Phillip in honour of Lieutenant Francis Grose, the lieutenant-governor of New South Wales. The valley country is the traditional land of the Gundungurra and Darug peoples. Explorers seeking a route over the Blue Mountains examined the Grose Valley but found its gorge impassable as a road route.

Recreation

The Grose Valley is popular with bushwalkers; tracks descend from Blackheath, Mount Victoria, and Bell into the valley, passing waterfalls such as Govetts Leap and Victoria Falls. The river and its tributaries are prone to dramatic flooding after heavy rain on the escarpment.
