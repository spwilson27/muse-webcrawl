The Lucasian Chair of Mathematics is a professorship in mathematics at the University of Cambridge, England, established in 1663 by Henry Lucas, Member of Parliament for the university. It is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious academic posts in the world, having been held by Isaac Newton, Paul Dirac, and Stephen Hawking.

History

Henry Lucas bequeathed land and his library of some 4,000 volumes to endow a lectureship in mathematics. The first Lucasian Professor, appointed in 1664, was Isaac Barrow, who resigned in 1669 in favour of his pupil Isaac Newton. Newton held the chair for more than three decades, during which he published the Principia Mathematica.

Notable holders

Later holders include Charles Babbage (1828–1839), the pioneer of computing; George Gabriel Stokes (1849–1903); Joseph Larmor (1903–1932); Paul Dirac (1932–1969), a founder of quantum mechanics; Stephen Hawking (1979–2009), who held the chair until the customary retirement age of 67; and Michael Green (2009–2015), a string theorist. Michael Cates, a soft-matter physicist, became the 19th Lucasian Professor in 2015.

The chair today

The Lucasian Professor is based in the Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics at Cambridge. Hawking's tenure — and his popular fame — cemented the chair's reputation beyond academia.
