Submersion (mathematics)

In differential topology, a submersion is a smooth map between differentiable manifolds whose differential is everywhere surjective. Intuitively, a submersion locally behaves like the projection of a higher-dimensional space onto a lower-dimensional one, collapsing dimensions without folding or pinching.

Definition

Let M and N be smooth manifolds of dimensions m and n with m ≥ n. A smooth map f : M → N is a submersion at a point p ∈ M if the differential dfp : TpM → Tf(p)N is a surjective linear map. The map is a submersion if it is a submersion at every point. Equivalently, in local coordinates the Jacobian matrix of f has full rank n everywhere; points where the differential is surjective are called regular points.

Consequences

By the implicit function theorem, near any point a submersion can be put into the standard form of a projection. It follows that the preimage (fiber) of any value of a submersion is a smooth submanifold of M of dimension m − n. Submersions are open maps. Important examples include projections of product manifolds, the quotient maps of fiber bundles, and covering maps.

A submersion that is also surjective and has a suitable properness condition gives rise to a fiber bundle by Ehresmann's theorem: a proper submersion between manifolds is a locally trivial fibration. Submersions are the dual notion to immersions, whose differentials are injective.

Examples

  • The projection ℝn+k → ℝn onto the first n coordinates.
  • The determinant map from invertible matrices, viewed as a map to the nonzero reals restricted appropriately.
  • Any smooth map from a manifold to a 0-dimensional manifold (a point) is vacuously a submersion.
