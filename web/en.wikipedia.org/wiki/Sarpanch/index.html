Sarpanch

A sarpanch is the elected head of a gram panchayat, the village-level institution of local self-government in India, and holds an equivalent role in parts of Pakistan and Nepal. The office is the lowest elected executive position in India's three-tier Panchayati Raj system, formalised by the 73rd Amendment to the Constitution of India in 1992.

Role and powers

The sarpanch presides over meetings of the gram sabha (the assembly of all registered voters in the village) and the gram panchayat, supervises local development works, oversees village-level staff, and is responsible for implementing government schemes relating to water, sanitation, roads, street lighting and welfare programmes. The sarpanch signs off on expenditure, maintains records with the panchayat secretary, and acts as the village's representative before block and district authorities.

Election and reservations

Sarpanches are chosen by direct election by the village electorate, typically for a five-year term. Seats are reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in proportion to population, and at least one-third of sarpanch positions — in many states now one-half — are reserved for women, a provision that has brought large numbers of women into elected office, though critics note the phenomenon of the "sarpanch pati," in which the husband of a woman sarpanch exercises power informally.
