The Royal Academy of Music (RAM) in London is the oldest conservatoire in the United Kingdom, founded in 1822 and granted a Royal Charter by King George IV in 1830. It is a member institution of the University of London and trains musicians to the highest professional standards in performance, composition, conducting, jazz, musical theatre, and opera.

History

The Academy was founded by Lord Burghersh (John Fane, 11th Earl of Westmorland) with the harpist Nicolas-Charles Bochsa among its early organisers, opening in premises in Tenterden Street, Hanover Square, in 1823. In 1911 it moved to its present purpose-built home on Marylebone Road, beside Regent's Park. Distinguished principals have included the composers William Sterndale Bennett and Sir Alexander Mackenzie, and in modern times Jonathan Freeman-Attwood.

The Academy's performance spaces include the Duke's Hall, the Angela Burgess Recital Hall, and the Susie Sainsbury Theatre (opened 2018), which hosts opera and musical theatre productions.

Collections and alumni

The Academy's museum, housed in the York Gate building, displays one of the world's finest collections of stringed instruments, including Stradivari violins such as the "Viotti ex-Bruce" of 1709, and the manuscripts and archives of many British composers. Notable alumni span every genre: conductors Sir Simon Rattle and Sir John Barbirolli, composers Michael Nyman and Sir Harrison Birtwistle, singers Dame Felicity Lott and Lesley Garrett, and in popular music Sir Elton John (a junior scholar there), Annie Lennox, and Dame Evelyn Glennie, the percussionist.

Professors have included many leading international performers, and the Academy regularly collaborates with orchestras and festivals in the United Kingdom and abroad.
