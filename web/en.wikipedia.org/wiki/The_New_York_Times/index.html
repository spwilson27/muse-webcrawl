The New York Times (NYT) is an American daily newspaper based in New York City, founded in 1851, long regarded as a national 'newspaper of record' and winner of more Pulitzer Prizes than any other news organisation. Its motto, 'All the News That's Fit to Print', has appeared on the front page since 1897.

History

Founded as the New-York Daily Times by Henry Jarvis Raymond and George Jones, the paper was bought in 1896 by Adolph Ochs, whose family — through the Sulzberger line — still controls it. It won renown for sober, comprehensive reporting, published the Pentagon Papers in 1971, and built a large international operation.

Digital era

The Times introduced a metered paywall in 2011 that became the model for the industry; its digital subscriptions grew into the tens of millions of readers and reshaped the company around them, alongside products such as The Daily podcast, Wirecutter, The Athletic and its games and cooking apps.

Organisation

The paper is published by The New York Times Company. Its newsroom is among the largest in the United States, with bureaus around the world; its opinion section is organisationally separate from the news pages.
