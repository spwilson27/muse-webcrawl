Lanzón

The Lanzón is a large granite sculpture, roughly 4.5 metres high and shaped like a lance or dagger thrust into the floor, created by the Chavín culture of ancient Peru around 500 BCE. It remains in situ in the cruciform chamber of the Old Temple at the ceremonial centre of Chavín de Huántar.

Iconography

The Lanzón portrays a deity combining human and animal traits, with fangs, clawed hands and feet, and hair that turns into snakes. Its right hand is raised in blessing while the left is lowered, a gesture interpreted as mediating between the heavens and the earth, or between life and death. Pilgrims visiting the temple could hear the god's voice through a duct connected to the chamber.
