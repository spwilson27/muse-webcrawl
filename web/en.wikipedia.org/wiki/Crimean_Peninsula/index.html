Crimean Peninsula

The Crimean Peninsula, or simply Crimea, is a major peninsula on the northern coast of the Black Sea, connected to the mainland by the narrow Isthmus of Perekop and separated from Russia's Taman Peninsula by the Kerch Strait. Long a crossroads of Greek, Roman, Gothic, Byzantine, Mongol, Genoese, Ottoman and Russian power, it was the heartland of the Crimean Khanate until annexed by the Russian Empire under Catherine the Great in 1783, and the theatre of the Crimean War (1853–56). Since 2014 Crimea has been administered by Russia but is internationally recognised by most states as part of Ukraine.

History in brief

Greek colonies such as Chersonesus (near modern Sevastopol) ringed the coast from the seventh century BC. The interior became the Crimean Khanate, a successor of the Golden Horde and an Ottoman vassal from 1475, whose slave raids into the steppe shaped Muscovite and Polish frontier history. Grigory Potemkin organised Russia's annexation in 1783, and the peninsula — "Taurida" — was developed with new cities (Simferopol, Sevastopol, Yalta) and the Black Sea Fleet. The Crimean War was fought largely here: the landings at Eupatoria, Alma, Balaclava, Inkerman and the siege of Sevastopol. In 1944 Stalin deported the entire Crimean Tatar population to Central Asia for alleged collaboration; they were allowed to return only at the end of the Soviet period. Transferred from the Russian SFSR to the Ukrainian SSR in 1954, Crimea voted with Ukraine in 1991 and hosted Russia's Black Sea Fleet by lease until Russia's annexation of 2014, condemned by the UN General Assembly.

Geography

Three zones run south to north in reverse: the mountainous, Mediterranean-climate south coast — the "Côte d'Azur of Russia", with Yalta (site of the 1945 conference) and the Livadia and Alupka palaces — the foothills, and the flat steppe, watered since the 1960s by the North Crimean Canal from the Dnieper (whose blocking after 2014, and the destruction of the Kakhovka dam in 2023, repeatedly affected the peninsula's water). The Kerch Bridge (2018), at 19 km Europe's longest, links Crimea to Russia.
