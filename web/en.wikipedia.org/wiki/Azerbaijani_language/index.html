Azerbaijani language

Azerbaijani (/ˌæzərbaɪˈdʒɑːni, -ˈdʒæni/; Azərbaycan dili) is a Turkic language spoken primarily by the Azerbaijani people. It is the official language of the Republic of Azerbaijan and one of the official languages of Dagestan (Russia). It is also spoken widely in Iranian Azerbaijan and by diaspora communities.

Varieties

Azerbaijani has two major varieties: North Azerbaijani, the standard language of the Republic of Azerbaijan, and South Azerbaijani, spoken in northwestern Iran. The varieties differ in phonology, lexicon, and writing system, but remain substantially mutually intelligible. Related dialects include Qashqai and the Aynallu varieties of Iran.

History

Azerbaijani developed from the Oghuz branch of Turkic brought to the region by Turkic migrations beginning in the 11th century. A rich literary tradition developed from the medieval period, with poets such as Imadaddin Nasimi and Fuzuli writing in Azerbaijani Turkic. In the Soviet era the language was written in Cyrillic; after independence in 1991, Azerbaijan adopted a Latin-based alphabet.

Phonology and grammar

Azerbaijani has nine vowels and exhibits vowel harmony. It is an agglutinative language with subject–object–verb word order, no grammatical gender, and extensive use of suffixes to mark case, possession, tense, and mood.
