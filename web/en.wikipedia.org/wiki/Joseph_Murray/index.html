Joseph Murray

Joseph Edward Murray (April 1, 1919 – November 26, 2012) was an American plastic surgeon who performed the first successful human kidney transplant, between identical twins Ronald and Richard Herrick, on December 23, 1954, at the Peter Bent Brigham Hospital in Boston. He shared the 1990 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with E. Donnall Thomas for discoveries concerning organ and cell transplantation.

Early life and training

Murray was born in Milford, Massachusetts, studied at the College of the Holy Cross and Harvard Medical School, and developed an interest in transplantation while treating burn victims during the Second World War, when he observed that skin grafts were rejected more slowly between related patients.

The first kidney transplant

Working with nephrologist John Merrill and others at the Brigham, Murray transplanted a kidney from Ronald Herrick into his identical twin Richard, who was dying of chronic nephritis. Because the brothers were genetically identical, no immunosuppression was needed; Richard lived eight more years. Murray went on to perform the first successful transplant from an unrelated donor in 1962, using azathioprine for immunosuppression.

Later career

Murray served as chief of plastic surgery at Brigham and Women's Hospital and at Children's Hospital Boston, and was a professor at Harvard Medical School. His work established organ transplantation as a practical treatment for end-stage renal disease.
