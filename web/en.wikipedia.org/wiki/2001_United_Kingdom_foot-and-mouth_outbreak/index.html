2001 United Kingdom foot-and-mouth outbreak

DatesFebruary – September 2001
LocationUnited Kingdom
Animals slaughteredOver 6 million
Cost~£8 billion

The 2001 United Kingdom foot-and-mouth outbreak was an epizootic of foot-and-mouth disease that struck British livestock in 2001. It caused a crisis in agriculture and tourism and led to the slaughter of more than six million sheep, cattle, and pigs.

Course of the outbreak

The disease was confirmed in February 2001 at an abattoir in Essex and traced to pigs at Burnside Farm in Northumberland, possibly fed on infected untreated swill. The highly contagious virus spread widely before movement bans took effect. The government pursued a cull strategy, with mass pyres of carcasses becoming an enduring image of the crisis.

Impact

Farming and tourism lost billions of pounds; the countryside was largely closed to visitors, and the general election was postponed by a month. The last case was recorded in September 2001 and the UK was declared disease-free in January 2002. The response was widely criticized, and later inquiries recommended reforms including contingency plans and consideration of vaccination.
