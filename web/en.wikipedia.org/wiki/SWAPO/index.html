SWAPO

The South West Africa People's Organisation (SWAPO; officially the SWAPO Party of Namibia) is a political party and former independence movement in Namibia. Founded in 1960, it has been the governing party of Namibia since the country achieved independence in 1990.

During the South African Border War, SWAPO's armed wing, the People's Liberation Army of Namibia (PLAN), fought South African forces, which administered South West Africa under a League of Nations mandate that the United Nations had revoked. The United Nations recognised SWAPO as the "sole and authentic representative of the people of Namibia". Following UN-supervised elections in 1989, SWAPO won a majority and its leader Sam Nujoma became Namibia's first president on 21 March 1990.

History

SWAPO grew out of the Ovamboland People's Organisation founded in 1957 by Andimba Toivo ya Toivo and others among Namibian contract workers in Cape Town. Renamed SWAPO in 1960, it began armed struggle in 1966 after the International Court of Justice failed to rule against South Africa's occupation. Decades of guerrilla war and diplomacy ended with the 1988 New York Accords, which linked Namibian independence to Cuban troop withdrawal from Angola.

In government

SWAPO has won every national election since independence, led successively by presidents Sam Nujoma (1990–2005), Hifikepunye Pohamba (2005–2015), and Hage Geingob (2015 until his death in 2024), after which Nangolo Mbumba served as interim president. Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah won the 2024 presidential election, becoming Namibia's first woman president.
