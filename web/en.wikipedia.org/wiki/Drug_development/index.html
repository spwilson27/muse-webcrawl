Drug development

Drug development is the process of bringing a new pharmaceutical drug to market once a lead compound has been identified through drug discovery.

Stages

Preclinical research in cells and animals is followed by clinical trials in humans: Phase I (safety, small group), Phase II (efficacy and side effects), Phase III (large confirmatory trials), then regulatory review — by the FDA in the US or EMA in Europe — approval, manufacturing and Phase IV post-market surveillance.

Cost and risk

Development typically takes over a decade and can cost over a billion dollars, with most candidates failing. Companies such as Pfizer pursue it at scale. Incentives include patents and orphan-drug provisions.
