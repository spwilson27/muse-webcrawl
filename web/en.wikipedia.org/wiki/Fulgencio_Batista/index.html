Fulgencio Batista

Fulgencio Batista y Zaldívar (16 January 1901 – 6 August 1973) was a Cuban military officer and politician who dominated Cuba for much of the period from 1933 to 1959. He served as elected president from 1940 to 1944, then seized power in a 1952 coup and ruled as a dictator until he was overthrown by Fidel Castro's 26th of July Movement in the Cuban Revolution.

Rise to power

Born poor in Banes, Batista joined the army and rose to sergeant. In the 1933 "Sergeants' Revolt" he emerged as strongman behind a succession of puppet presidents. As president from 1940 he enacted a progressive constitution but also built a powerful patronage network; after losing influence he staged a coup on 10 March 1952, weeks before elections he was expected to lose.

Dictatorship and fall

Batista's second regime suspended the constitution, cancelled elections and allied closely with the United States and American organized crime interests in Havana's casinos. Repression of the growing insurgency after Castro's 1956 landing failed to contain it; on 1 January 1959, as rebel forces advanced, Batista fled Cuba. He spent his exile in Portugal and Spain, dying at Marbella in 1973.
