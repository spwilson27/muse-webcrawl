Paternity (law)

Paternity law refers to the body of law concerning the legal relationship between a father and his child, including the establishment of paternity, the rights and obligations it creates, and the procedures for challenging it. Determining paternity is the legal process of deciding the biological or legal father of a child, and it governs matters such as child support, custody, inheritance, and citizenship.

Establishing paternity

In most legal systems, a man married to the mother at the time of a child's birth is presumed to be the father (the marital presumption). For children born outside marriage, paternity may be established by voluntary acknowledgment — a signed declaration by both parents, often at the hospital — or by a court order following a paternity suit, in which DNA testing is now the decisive evidence.

DNA testing

Modern genetic testing can establish paternity with probabilities exceeding 99.9 percent. Courts may order testing in disputed cases, and results can be used to impose support obligations or to rebut an existing presumption, although some jurisdictions limit challenges after a certain time in the interests of the child's stability.

Consequences and disputes

Once established, paternity generally obliges the father to support the child and entitles him to seek custody or visitation. Disputes also arise in cases of assisted reproduction, sperm donation, and surrogacy, where legal parenthood may diverge from genetics, and in cases of paternity fraud, where a man is misled about his biological relationship to a child.
