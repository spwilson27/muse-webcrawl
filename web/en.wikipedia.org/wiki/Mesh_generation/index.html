Mesh generation

Mesh generation is the practice of creating a mesh, a subdivision of a continuous geometric space into discrete geometric and topological cells. Meshes are used to discretise domains for numerical simulation — for example in the finite element method and computational fluid dynamics — and for rendering in computer graphics. A good mesh must approximate the geometry accurately, contain well-shaped elements, and have an element size appropriate to the local behaviour of the solution.


An unstructured triangular mesh generated for finite element analysis.

Types of mesh

Structured meshes have a regular, grid-like connectivity, in which each interior vertex has a fixed number of neighbours; they are efficient and accurate but can be difficult to fit to complex geometry. Unstructured meshes have irregular connectivity and can conform to arbitrary shapes; in two dimensions they are typically made of triangles or quadrilaterals, and in three dimensions of tetrahedra, hexahedra, prisms, or pyramids. Hybrid meshes combine structured regions, such as boundary layers of prismatic elements near walls, with unstructured elements elsewhere. Cells may also be polyhedral.

Techniques

Common generation methods include:

  • Delaunay methods, which insert points and connect them by Delaunay triangulation or tetrahedralisation, often with refinement to enforce size and quality criteria (for example Ruppert's and Chew's algorithms).
  • Advancing front methods, which grow elements inward from the boundary of the domain.
  • Quadtree and octree methods, which recursively subdivide space and then conform the result to the boundary.
  • Grid-based and sweeping methods, used for mapped and extruded structured or semi-structured meshes.

After generation, meshes are commonly improved by smoothing (repositioning vertices), local topological operations such as edge flipping and swapping, and refinement or coarsening, and are graded so that elements are small where the solution varies rapidly and larger elsewhere.

Applications

Mesh generation is a critical pre-processing step in engineering simulation, including aerodynamics, structural analysis, electromagnetics, and reservoir modelling, and mesh generation software ranges from open-source tools such as Gmsh and TetGen to commercial pre-processors bundled with major simulation packages.
