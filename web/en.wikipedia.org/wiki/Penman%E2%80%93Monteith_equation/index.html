Penman–Monteith equation

Penman–Monteith equation
Developed byHoward Penman (1948); John Monteith (1965)
PurposeEstimating evapotranspiration from vegetated land surfaces
InputsNet radiation, air temperature, humidity, wind speed, surface and aerodynamic resistances
Standard formFAO-56 Penman–Monteith (reference crop evapotranspiration, ET₀)

The Penman–Monteith equation is the most widely used method for estimating evapotranspiration — the combined loss of water from soil evaporation and plant transpiration. It combines an energy-balance term (driven by net radiation) with an aerodynamic term (driven by vapor pressure deficit and wind), and adds a surface resistance representing the control of water loss through plant stomata.

History

Howard Penman published his combination equation for evaporation from open water and land in 1948. In 1965 John Monteith extended the approach by incorporating the resistance of a vegetated canopy, producing a physically based formula linking evaporation to weather data and crop characteristics. The UN Food and Agriculture Organization standardized the method as the FAO-56 Penman–Monteith equation in 1998, defining a hypothetical reference crop so results are comparable worldwide.

Applications

The equation is central to irrigation scheduling, agricultural water management, drought monitoring and hydrological and climate modeling. Its reference-evapotranspiration output is multiplied by crop coefficients to estimate the water needs of specific crops.
