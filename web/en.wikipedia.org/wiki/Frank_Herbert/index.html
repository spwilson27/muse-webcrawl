Frank Herbert

Frank Patrick Herbert (1920–1986) was an American science-fiction author best known for the Dune saga — six novels beginning with Dune (1965), the best-selling science fiction novel of all time. His work explored ecology, politics, religion, power and human survival.

Frank Herbert
Born: 8 October 1920, Tacoma, Washington
Died: 11 February 1986, Madison, Wisconsin
Notable works: Dune and sequels
Genre: Science fiction

Life

Herbert worked as a journalist, photographer and researcher. Research on Oregon sand dunes and desert ecology inspired Dune, serialised in Analog and published in book form by Chilton. Early jobs included oyster diving research and writing for newspapers; only after Dune’s success could he write full-time.

Dune saga

Set millennia hence on desert planet Arrakis, source of the spice melange, the sequence follows Paul Atreides and his descendants. Sequels include Dune Messiah, Children of Dune, God Emperor of Dune, Heretics of Dune and Chapterhouse: Dune. Adaptations include David Lynch’s 1984 film and Denis Villeneuve’s films (2021, 2024). His son Brian Herbert with Kevin J. Anderson continued the series.
