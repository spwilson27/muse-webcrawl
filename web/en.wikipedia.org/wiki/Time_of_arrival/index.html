Time of arrival

Time of arrival (TOA or ToA) is the absolute time instant when a radio signal emanating from a transmitter reaches a receiver. Knowing the speed of propagation, the TOA gives the distance (range) between transmitter and receiver.

Positioning

Combining TOA measurements from several transmitters at known locations allows a receiver's position to be determined by trilateration. Because TOA requires the transmitter and receiver clocks to be synchronised, systems often use time difference of arrival (TDOA) instead, or two-way ranging to avoid clock errors. TOA methods underlie satellite navigation (in the form of pseudoranges), ultra-wideband indoor positioning, and mobile-phone geolocation.

Challenges

Multipath propagation and non-line-of-sight conditions bias TOA estimates, and precise timing — to nanoseconds for metre-level accuracy — is a central engineering challenge.
