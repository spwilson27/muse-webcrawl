History of the Republic of Turkey

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The history of the Republic of Turkey begins with the founding of the republic on 29 October 1923, following the Turkish War of Independence and the dissolution of the Ottoman Empire. Under its first president, Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, Turkey underwent sweeping secularizing and Westernizing reforms.

Single-party era and Atatürk's reforms

The Grand National Assembly abolished the sultanate in 1922 and the caliphate in 1924. Atatürk's reforms included the adoption of the Latin alphabet (1928), a Swiss-based civil code granting women equal rights in many areas, women's suffrage (1934), secular education, and the promotion of Turkish nationalism and state-led industrialization under the ideology of Kemalism. Turkey remained neutral for most of World War II and joined the United Nations in 1945.

Multi-party period and military interventions

The Democrat Party's victory in 1950 began multi-party democracy, but the armed forces intervened repeatedly: coups in 1960, 1971, and 1980, and a "postmodern coup" in 1997 forced out an Islamist-led government. Turkey joined NATO in 1952, intervened in Cyprus in 1974, and from 1984 fought an insurgency by the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK). Turgut Özal's governments in the 1980s liberalized the economy.

AKP era

The Justice and Development Party (AKP) of Recep Tayyip Erdoğan came to power in 2002, overseeing rapid economic growth, a once-promising EU accession process, and later democratic backsliding, a failed coup attempt in July 2016, and a 2017 referendum that created an executive presidency. Earthquakes in February 2023 devastated the southeast. Turkey remains a regional power and NATO member balancing ties between the West, Russia, and the Middle East.
