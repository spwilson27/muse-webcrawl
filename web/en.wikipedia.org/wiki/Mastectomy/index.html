Mastectomy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mastectomy is the medical term for the surgical removal of one or both breasts, partially or completely. It is usually carried out to treat breast cancer; in some cases it is performed prophylactically by people at high genetic risk of developing the disease. Mastectomy may be unilateral (one breast) or bilateral (both).

Types

  • Total (simple) mastectomy — removal of the entire breast including the nipple, areola and skin, but not the lymph nodes or chest muscle.
  • Modified radical mastectomy — removal of the breast and most of the axillary (underarm) lymph nodes, sparing the chest muscles.
  • Radical mastectomy — removal of the breast, lymph nodes and the pectoral muscles beneath; pioneered by William Stewart Halsted in the 1890s, it is now rarely performed because equally effective, less disfiguring options exist.
  • Skin-sparing and nipple-sparing mastectomy — preserve much of the breast skin (and, in the latter, the nipple-areola complex) to allow better cosmetic reconstruction.
  • Partial mastectomy — removal of the cancerous part of the breast, overlapping with breast-conserving surgery such as lumpectomy.

Indications and decisions

Mastectomy is indicated for large or multiple tumours, inflammatory breast cancer, recurrence after prior radiotherapy, or patient preference. Trials have shown that breast-conserving surgery plus radiotherapy gives survival equivalent to mastectomy for most early-stage cancers, so the choice often turns on recurrence risk, anatomy and personal values. Prophylactic (risk-reducing) mastectomy may be chosen by carriers of BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutations, reducing breast cancer risk by roughly 90–95%.

Reconstruction and recovery

Breast reconstruction may be immediate (during the same operation) or delayed, using implants or the patient's own tissue (autologous flaps such as the DIEP flap). Alternatives include wearing an external prosthesis or "going flat". Recovery typically takes several weeks; possible complications include infection, seroma, lymphoedema of the arm after node removal, and phantom breast sensations. The procedure also has psychological and body-image consequences, for which support services are commonly offered.

Other uses

Mastectomy is also performed as gender-affirming surgery ("top surgery") for transgender men and non-binary people, and occasionally for severe benign breast disease or male breast cancer.
