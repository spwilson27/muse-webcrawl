Renaissance of the 12th century

Periodc. 1070–1250
RegionMedieval Europe
Key featuresTranslation movement, universities, scholasticism, Gothic architecture

The Renaissance of the 12th century was a period of many changes at the outset of the High Middle Ages, including social, political, and economic transformations, and an intellectual revitalization of Western Europe with strong philosophical and scientific roots. The term was popularized by the American historian Charles Homer Haskins, whose 1927 book of that title argued that the period was a genuine renaissance preceding the better-known Italian Renaissance.

Translation movement

Latin scholars in Spain and Sicily translated Greek and Arabic works of philosophy, medicine, mathematics, and astronomy — including Aristotle, Euclid, Ptolemy, Galen, and Avicenna — mainly from Arabic into Latin. Figures such as Gerard of Cremona in Toledo rendered dozens of works that transformed the European curriculum.

Institutions and culture

The period saw the rise of the first universities (Bologna, Paris, Oxford), the development of scholastic method, a revival of Roman law, the growth of Gothic architecture, and flourishing vernacular literatures, alongside technological improvements in agriculture, mills, and navigation.
