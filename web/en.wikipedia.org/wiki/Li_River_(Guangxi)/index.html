Li River (Guangxi)

The Li River or Li Jiang (Chinese: 漓江; pinyin: Lí Jiāng) is a river in Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, China. It flows 83 kilometres (52 mi) from Guilin to Yangshuo, where the karst mountains and river sights are famous. The river originates in the Mao'er Mountains in Xing'an County and flows south through Guilin, Yangshuo and Pingle, joining the Gui River and ultimately the Pearl River system.

Scenery

The section between Guilin and Yangshuo is renowned for its dramatic limestone karst peaks, clear water, bamboo groves and traditional cormorant fishermen on bamboo rafts. Landmarks along the cruise route include Reed Flute Cave, Elephant Trunk Hill, Nine Horses Fresco Hill and the town of Xingping. The landscape near Yangshuo is depicted on the reverse of the 20 yuan banknote.

Tourism

River cruises from Guilin to Yangshuo are among the most popular tourist activities in China, and the Li River scenery is often considered the highlight of a visit to Guangxi. The river is also navigable in part by bamboo raft, particularly the quieter stretch from Yangdi to Xingping.
