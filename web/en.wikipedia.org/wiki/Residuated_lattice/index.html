Residuated lattice

In abstract algebra, a residuated lattice is an algebraic structure consisting of a lattice together with a monoid operation and two "residual" operations (division operations) that are adjoint to the monoid multiplication. Formally, it is a structure (L, ∧, ∨, ·, \, /, 1) such that (L, ∧, ∨) is a lattice, (L, ·, 1) is a monoid, and for all x, y, z in L, the equivalence x·y ≤ z if and only if y ≤ x\z if and only if x ≤ z/y holds.

History and applications

Residuated lattices were introduced by Ward and Dilworth in the 1930s in connection with ideals in rings. They provide the algebraic semantics for substructural logics, including many-valued and fuzzy logics: important varieties include MV-algebras (semantics of Łukasiewicz logic), BL-algebras, Heyting algebras, Boolean algebras, and Girard monoids used in linear logic.

Examples

The lattice of ideals of a commutative ring with ideal multiplication forms a residuated lattice. Every Boolean algebra is a residuated lattice with meet as multiplication, and every Heyting algebra is one as well. The unit interval [0,1] equipped with a left-continuous t-norm and its residuum is a standard example in fuzzy logic.
