Highpointing

Highpointing is the sport of ascending to the point with the highest elevation within a given area — for example, the highest point of each U.S. state, county, country or continent. Participants who visit the high points of all fifty U.S. states are among the best-known practitioners; the summits range from Denali in Alaska (6,190 m, a serious mountaineering expedition) to Britton Hill in Florida (105 m, a drive-up viewpoint).

Community

The Highpointers Club, founded in 1986 by Jack Longacre, promotes the activity, holds an annual convention, publishes the quarterly Apex to Zenith, and maintains county highpoint records through its associated county-highpointing community. Hundreds of club members have recorded completions of all fifty state highpoints.

Variations

Related pursuits include reaching the highpoints of the U.S. national parks, the counties of a state (a far larger undertaking — there are over 3,000 U.S. counties), the countries of Europe or the world, and the "Seven Summits" of the continents. Comparable hobbies include peak bagging of defined lists such as Scotland's Munros or the Adirondack Forty-Sixers, and "extreme points" tourism.
