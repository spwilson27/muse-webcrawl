Sihon

Sihon (Hebrew: סִיחוֹן, Sīḥōn) was, according to the Hebrew Bible, an Amorite king who ruled from Heshbon over territory east of the Jordan River, between the Arnon and the Jabbok, in the period of the Exodus and the Israelite conquest.

Biblical account

In Numbers 21, the Israelites under Moses sent messengers asking Sihon for peaceful passage through his land on the King's Highway, promising not to turn aside into fields or vineyards. Sihon refused, mustered his army, and fought Israel at Jahaz; he was defeated, and his cities, including Heshbon, were captured. The territory was afterwards allotted to the tribes of Reuben and Gad. Deuteronomy retells the episode, and Sihon's defeat — together with that of Og of Bashan — became a standard example of divine help, recalled in the Psalms (135 and 136) and in the speeches of Jephthah and Nehemiah.

The ballad of Heshbon

Numbers 21:27–30 quotes a taunt-song ("the ballad singers") celebrating that Heshbon had earlier been taken by Sihon from the king of Moab, before itself falling to Israel — a passage much discussed by biblical scholars. Sihon is not to be confused with Sigurd, the unrelated Norse dragon-slayer.
