Dominance (topography)

In topography, the dominance of a summit or peak is a measure of how isolated the peak is: it is the radius of the area over which the peak is the highest point. More precisely, the dominance of a peak is the distance from the peak to the nearest point of equal elevation on a higher peak — the distance one would have to travel from the summit before encountering any terrain of greater height. Along with prominence and isolation, dominance is one of the standard measures used to characterise and rank mountain summits.

The dominance of a peak describes the extent of the surrounding terrain for which it is the highest point.

Definition

Dominance is defined as the minimum distance from a summit to any point of the same elevation that belongs to, or lies on the way to, a higher summit. The region enclosed by the circle with this radius around the peak is the area "dominated" by the peak. For the highest point of a continent or island, dominance is unbounded in the sense that there is no higher terrain; in such cases dominance is sometimes taken to be the distance to the nearest point of equal elevation on the surrounding coastline, or left undefined.

Relationship to prominence and isolation

Topographic prominence measures the vertical drop from a summit to its key col (the highest saddle connecting it to higher ground), while isolation measures the distance to the nearest higher summit itself. Dominance differs from isolation because the reference point is not the higher summit but the nearest point of equal height on the route toward higher terrain, so dominance is always less than or equal to isolation.

Examples

The dominance of Mont Blanc, the highest peak of the Alps, is determined by the nearest equally high terrain in the Caucasus region, giving it one of the largest dominance values in Europe. Peaks on isolated islands typically have dominance limited by the size of the island itself.
