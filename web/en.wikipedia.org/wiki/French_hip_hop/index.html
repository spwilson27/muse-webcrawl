French hip hop is the hip hop music style developed in French-speaking countries. France is the world's second largest market for hip hop music, after the United States. The genre emerged in the early 1980s, strongly influenced by American hip hop, and developed a distinctive identity rooted in the suburbs (banlieues) of Paris and other large cities, where many performers were the children of immigrants from France's former colonies in Africa and the Caribbean.

History

Hip hop culture arrived in France in the early 1980s through films, records and tours by American artists. In 1982 the New York City Rap Tour brought acts such as Afrika Bambaataa to France. Dee Nasty, one of the scene's pioneers, released the early French rap album Paname City Rappin' in 1984 and organised block parties that helped spread the culture. Radio stations such as Radio Nova championed the new sound.

The genre broke through commercially around 1990–1991 with hits by Benny B in Belgium and MC Solaar in France, whose literate, playful wordplay on albums such as Qui sème le vent récolte le tempo (1991) won critical acclaim. The group IAM, from Marseille, brought a Mediterranean and Egyptian-inspired dimension to the music, while Suprême NTM, from the Paris suburb of Saint-Denis, delivered a harder, more confrontational style reflecting tensions in the banlieues.

Characteristics

French rap is noted for its emphasis on lyricism, verbal dexterity and social commentary. Common themes include life in the housing estates (cités), racism, police violence, unemployment, immigration and identity. Artists such as Kery James, Diam's, Booba, PNL, Nekfeu and Orelsan have carried the genre into the streaming era, with French-language rap dominating domestic charts.
