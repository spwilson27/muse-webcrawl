USS Missouri (BB-63)

USS Missouri (BB-63) is an Iowa-class battleship built for the United States Navy in the 1940s. She is the last battleship completed by the United States and is best known as the site of the surrender of the Empire of Japan, which ended World War II. She has also been called the "Mighty Mo".

Launched: 29 January 1944
Commissioned: 11 June 1944; 10 May 1986
Decommissioned: 31 March 1992
Main armament: 9 × 16-inch (406 mm) guns
Status: Museum ship, Pearl Harbor

World War II

Commissioned in June 1944, Missouri joined the Pacific Fleet in early 1945, screening carriers and bombarding shore targets at Iwo Jima and Okinawa. On 2 September 1945, in Tokyo Bay, the formal Japanese surrender was signed on her deck, with General Douglas MacArthur presiding. The "surrender deck" remains her most famous feature.

Korean War and Cold War

Missouri was the only US battleship in full commission when the Korean War began and made two combat tours providing naval gunfire support. Decommissioned in 1955, she was brought back under President Ronald Reagan's 600-ship Navy plan, modernized with Tomahawk and Harpoon missiles, and recommissioned on 10 May 1986.

Gulf War and museum career

In the 1991 Gulf War, Missouri fired Tomahawk missiles and more than 700 16-inch shells against Iraqi targets, and famously helped clear naval mines. She was decommissioned for the final time on 31 March 1992. Donated to the USS Missouri Memorial Association, she opened as a museum ship at Pearl Harbor, Hawaii, in January 1999, moored near the USS Arizona Memorial.
