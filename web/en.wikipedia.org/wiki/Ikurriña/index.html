Ikurriña

The ikurriña (Basque for "the flag"; also spelled ikurrina) is the official flag of the Basque Autonomous Community in northern Spain and a widely recognised symbol of the Basque people and of Basque nationalism.

Design and symbolism

The flag consists of a red field with a green saltire (diagonal cross) and a white cross superimposed. It was designed in 1894 by the brothers Luis and Sabino Arana, founders of the Basque Nationalist Party: the red field represents the Basque people (in early interpretations, Biscay), the green saltire the oak of Gernika and the old laws (fueros), and the white cross God and Catholic faith — reflecting the party motto "God and the Old Laws".

History

Originally the flag of Biscay and then of the nationalist party, the ikurriña became the flag of the autonomous Basque government during the Spanish Civil War in 1936. Under the Franco regime its display was prohibited and treated as a separatist offence; it was legalised during the Spanish transition to democracy and was adopted as the official flag in the 1979 Statute of Autonomy of the Basque Country.
