John van Hengel (21 February 1923 – 5 October 2005) was an American social activist regarded as the father of the modern food bank movement.

Founding food banking

Van Hengel's varied early career took in advertising, public relations and the garment business before a broken back and a move to Phoenix, Arizona changed his course. Volunteering at a soup kitchen, he saw the amount of edible food discarded by grocery stores and restaurants while people went hungry. In 1967, with the support of St Mary's Basilica parish, he established St Mary's Food Bank in Phoenix — the world's first food bank — collecting surplus food in a borrowed truck and distributing it to charities feeding the poor.

In 1976 he founded Second Harvest, a national network linking food banks across the United States, which grew into the country's largest hunger-relief organisation and was later renamed Feeding America. Van Hengel went on to help establish food banking internationally through what became the Global FoodBanking Network. He was named a Point of Light by President George H. W. Bush and is remembered for insisting that the model survive on donated food and volunteer labour.
