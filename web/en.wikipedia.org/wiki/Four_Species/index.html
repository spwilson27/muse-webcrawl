Four Species

The Four Species (Hebrew: ארבעת המינים, arba'at ha-minim) are four plants mentioned in the Torah as being relevant to the Jewish holiday of Sukkot. Observant Jews tie together three types of branches and one type of fruit and wave them in a special ceremony each day of the Sukkot holiday, excluding Shabbat. The waving of the four plants is a mitzvah prescribed by the Torah.

The species

Per Leviticus 23:40, the four species are: the etrog (citron, the "fruit of a goodly tree"), the lulav (a closed frond of the date palm), the hadass (three twigs of myrtle), and the aravah (two branches of the willow). The lulav, hadass and aravah are bound together and held with the etrog while blessings are recited and the bundle is waved in six directions.

Symbolism

Rabbinic midrash offers several interpretations: the species represent different parts of the body (spine, heart, eyes, lips), or four types of Jews — those with taste (learning) and scent (good deeds) in varying combinations — whom God does not cast aside but binds together in unity.
