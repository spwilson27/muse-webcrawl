Kim Jong-il (Korean: 김정일; 16 February 1941 or 1942 – 17 December 2011) was a North Korean politician who served as the second supreme leader of North Korea from 1994 until his death in 2011. He led North Korea as Chairman of the National Defence Commission and General Secretary of the Workers' Party of Korea, presiding over the country during the North Korean famine of the 1990s and the escalation of its nuclear weapons programme.

Early life and rise

Soviet records place Kim's birth in 1941 at the village of Vyatskoye near Khabarovsk, where his father Kim Il-sung commanded a battalion of the Soviet Red Army; North Korean official mythology instead claims he was born in a secret guerrilla camp on Mount Paektu in 1942, heralded by a double rainbow and a new star. After the Korean War he studied at Kim Il-sung University in Pyongyang and rose through the Workers' Party apparatus, dominating the country's cultural and propaganda work in the 1960s and 1970s. By 1980 he had been anointed as his father's successor, the first hereditary succession in a communist state.

Rule

On Kim Il-sung's death in July 1994, Kim Jong-il assumed power, though he ruled largely through the military under the doctrine of Songun ("military-first politics"). His rule coincided with a devastating famine (the "Arduous March") that killed hundreds of thousands, and with partial marketisation from below. Abroad, the 1994 Agreed Framework froze the Yongbyon nuclear programme for a time, but relations deteriorated in the 2000s; North Korea withdrew from the Non-Proliferation Treaty and conducted nuclear tests in 2006 and 2009, alongside long-range missile launches. Kim held summit meetings with South Korean presidents Kim Dae-jung (2000) and Roh Moo-hyun (2007).

Personality cult and death

North Korea's state cult presented Kim as the "Dear Leader" (later the "Great Leader"), a brilliant military and artistic genius, and invented lavish legends around his life, including claims about his golfing feats. He was reputed to be a film enthusiast with a collection of thousands of movies, and in 1978 he arranged the abduction of the South Korean director Shin Sang-ok and actress Choi Eun-hee to improve North Korean cinema.

Kim suffered a stroke in 2008 and thereafter groomed his youngest son, Kim Jong-un, as successor. He died of a heart attack on 17 December 2011 while travelling by train; his body was laid in state and later embalmed and displayed in the Kumsusan Palace of the Sun in Pyongyang.
