Hyperparathyroidism

Specialty: Endocrinology
Symptoms: Often none; kidney stones, bone pain, abdominal pain, depression
Causes: Primary: parathyroid adenoma; Secondary: vitamin D deficiency, chronic kidney disease

Hyperparathyroidism is an increase in parathyroid hormone (PTH) levels in the blood. This occurs from a disorder either within the parathyroid glands (primary hyperparathyroidism) or as a response to external stimuli (secondary hyperparathyroidism). In primary hyperparathyroidism, one or more of the parathyroid glands produces excess PTH, most commonly because of a benign single adenoma; the result is hypercalcemia. Symptoms are classically summarised as "stones, bones, groans, and psychiatric overtones" — kidney stones, bone pain and demineralisation, abdominal complaints, and mood or cognitive symptoms — although many patients are asymptomatic and detected by routine blood calcium measurement.

Signs and symptoms

Excess PTH raises blood calcium by increasing bone resorption, renal calcium reabsorption, and intestinal absorption via calcitriol. Consequences include nephrolithiasis, osteoporosis and fractures, constipation, peptic ulceration, pancreatitis, fatigue, depression, and confusion. Secondary hyperparathyroidism, by contrast, is a compensatory response — usually to vitamin D deficiency or chronic kidney disease — in which calcium is normal or low and the glands enlarge from chronic stimulation.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on blood tests: elevated (or inappropriately normal) PTH together with raised serum calcium in primary disease. Urinary calcium excretion, vitamin D levels, bone densitometry (DEXA), and imaging such as sestamibi scanning or ultrasound assist in classification and surgical planning. Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia is an important mimic.

Treatment

The definitive treatment for primary hyperparathyroidism is parathyroidectomy, recommended for symptomatic patients and many asymptomatic ones meeting guideline criteria. Patients managed without surgery require hydration, avoidance of thiazides and immobilisation, and monitoring; calcimimetics such as cinacalcet can lower calcium. Secondary hyperparathyroidism is treated by correcting the underlying cause, with vitamin D analogues, phosphate binders, and calcimimetics in kidney disease.
