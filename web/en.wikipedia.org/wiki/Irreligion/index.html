Irreligion

Irreligion is the absence of, indifference towards, or hostility towards religion. It encompasses atheism, agnosticism, deism, religious scepticism, freethought, secular humanism and various forms of non-affiliation; people without a religion are often described in surveys as "religious nones".

Demographics

According to Pew Research Center estimates, the religiously unaffiliated made up about 16% of the world's population in 2015 — the third-largest grouping after Christians and Muslims — and were projected to grow in absolute numbers while declining slightly as a share of world population by mid-century. Unaffiliation is especially high in China, Japan, Vietnam and much of Europe (notably Czechia, Estonia, the Netherlands and Scandinavia), and has risen rapidly in the United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand. Many unaffiliated people nonetheless retain some spiritual beliefs or practices, a pattern sometimes called "believing without belonging".

Forms

Irreligion ranges from explicit rejection of religion (atheism, antitheism) through uncertainty (agnosticism) and principled separation of religion from public life (secularism) to simple apathy (apatheism). State policy has at times mandated irreligion, as in the officially atheist states of the former Soviet bloc and Albania under Enver Hoxha, while in other countries non-believers face legal discrimination or penalties for apostasy and blasphemy.
