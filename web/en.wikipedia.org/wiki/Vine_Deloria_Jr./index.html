Vine Victor Deloria Jr. (26 March 1933 – 13 November 2005) was a Native American author, theologian, historian, and activist of the Standing Rock Sioux Tribe. One of the most important indigenous intellectuals of the twentieth century, he wrote more than twenty books, most famously Custer Died for Your Sins: An Indian Manifesto (1969), which brought national attention to Native American grievances and helped fuel the Red Power movement.

Vine Deloria Jr., author and leading voice of Native American self-determination.

Life and career

Deloria came from a prominent Dakota family—his father was an Episcopal archdeacon and his great-grandfather a Sioux leader. He served in the U.S. Marine Corps, earned degrees in theology and law (University of Colorado, 1970), and from 1964 to 1967 directed the National Congress of American Indians, transforming it into an effective advocacy organization. He later taught at Western Washington University, the University of Arizona—where he founded the first master's program in American Indian Studies—and the University of Colorado.

Work and ideas

With humor and polemic, Custer Died for Your Sins attacked federal Indian policy, anthropologists, and broken treaties. Subsequent books, including God Is Red (1973), contrasting tribal spatial religions with Christianity's temporal orientation, and Red Earth, White Lies (1995), challenging scientific accounts of indigenous origins, made him a central figure in debates over sovereignty, religion, and science. His scholarship underpinned the legal and intellectual case for tribal self-determination that reshaped U.S. federal Indian policy from the 1970s onward.
