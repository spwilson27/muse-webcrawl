Amorphophallus

Amorphophallus is a large genus of some 170–200 tropical and subtropical tuberous herbaceous plants in the arum family (Araceae), distributed from West Africa through Asia to northern Australia. The genus name derives from the Greek amorphos ("misshapen") and phallos, referring to the shape of the spadix. Many species produce enormous, foul-smelling inflorescences adapted to pollination by carrion beetles and flesh flies.

Description

Plants grow from an underground tuber (corm) that in large species can weigh over 50 kg. A single, highly divided, umbrella-like leaf emerges on a mottled stalk after the flowering phase. The inflorescence consists of a spathe wrapped around a central spadix bearing zones of female flowers at the base, male flowers above, and a sterile appendix that generates heat and releases carrion-like odours — compounds such as dimethyl trisulfide — to attract pollinators.

Notable species

The most famous member is the titan arum, Amorphophallus titanum of Sumatra, which produces the largest unbranched inflorescence of any plant, sometimes over 3 m tall, and draws crowds when it blooms in botanical gardens. Amorphophallus konjac is cultivated widely in East Asia for its starchy corms, processed into konnyaku and shirataki noodles and the soluble fibre glucomannan. The elephant foot yam, A. paeoniifolius, is a food crop in India and Southeast Asia.

Cultivation

Most species require a warm wet growing season and a dry dormancy, and are grown by specialists and botanical gardens; a few, notably A. konjac, are hardy enough for temperate gardens.
