Vacuum permeability

The vacuum magnetic permeability (variously vacuum permeability, permeability of free space, permeability of vacuum), also called simply the magnetic constant, is the magnetic permeability in a classical vacuum. It is a physical constant, conventionally written as μ0 (pronounced "mu nought" or "mu zero").

Definition and value

Vacuum permeability quantifies the strength of the magnetic field induced by an electric current in a vacuum. In SI units, its measured value (since the 2019 redefinition of the SI base units) is approximately μ0 = 4π × 10−7 H/m, with a small experimental uncertainty arising because it is now derived from the measured fine-structure constant rather than defined exactly.

Before 2019, μ0 was defined to be exactly 4π×10−7 H/m, a consequence of the former definition of the ampere in terms of the force between two parallel current-carrying wires.

Terminology

Historical terms for μ0 include "magnetic constant" and "absolute permeability of free space". Standards organizations prefer "magnetic constant" as the name of the quantity, regarding "vacuum permeability" as misleading because the constant is a property of the unit system and field equations rather than of a material medium.

Relation to other constants

μ0 is related to the electric constant ε0 and the speed of light c by the relation c² = 1/(ε0μ0), which emerges from Maxwell's equations for electromagnetic waves in vacuum.
