Pierre Bonnard (French: [bɔnaʁ]; 3 October 1867 – 23 January 1947) was a French painter, illustrator and printmaker, known especially for the stylised decorative qualities of his paintings and his bold use of colour. He was a founding member of the Post-Impressionist group Les Nabis, alongside Édouard Vuillard, with whom he is associated with the intimate domestic style called Intimism.

Early life and the Nabis

Bonnard studied law briefly before turning to art at the Académie Julian and the École des Beaux-Arts, where he met Vuillard and Maurice Denis. Influenced by Gauguin and Japanese prints, the Nabis ("prophets") pursued flat areas of colour and symbolic content. Bonnard's early work included celebrated posters, such as his 1891 design for France-Champagne, and illustrations for La Revue Blanche.

Mature work

From the 1900s Bonnard developed his signature subjects: sunlit interiors, dining tables seen from above, gardens, and his wife Marthe bathing or dressing — painted from memory rather than from life, with high, unsettled viewpoints, cropped figures and radiant, non-naturalistic colour. Works such as The Dining Room in the Country (1913) and the late The Bathroom series exemplify this vision.

Later years

In 1925 Bonnard bought a house, Le Bosquet, at Le Cannet on the Côte d'Azur, where the Mediterranean light intensified his palette. He continued to rework canvases for years, famously retouching paintings even as they hung in museums. He died at Le Cannet in 1947, shortly after completing his final painting, The Almond Tree in Blossom.

Legacy

Long overshadowed by Matisse and Picasso, Bonnard's reputation rose steadily in the later 20th century; major retrospectives (notably at the Tate and the Museum of Modern Art) established him as one of the great colourists of modern art.
