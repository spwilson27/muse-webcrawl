Zangezur Copper and Molybdenum Combine

Industry: Mining and metallurgy

Headquarters: Kajaran, Syunik Province, Armenia

Products: Copper concentrate, molybdenum concentrate, ferromolybdenum

Deposit: Kajaran (Qajaran) copper-molybdenum deposit

The Zangezur Copper and Molybdenum Combine (Armenian: Զանգեզուրի պղնձամոլիբդենային կոմբինատ), abbreviated ZCMC, is one of the largest mining and industrial enterprises in Armenia. Based in the town of Kajaran in the southern Syunik Province, the combine exploits the Kajaran copper-molybdenum deposit, among the largest molybdenum deposits in the world.

Operations

The combine mines ore by the open-pit method at Kajaran, one of the largest copper-molybdenum deposits of the former Soviet Union. After crushing and flotation, the plant produces copper concentrate and molybdenum concentrate, part of which is further processed into ferromolybdenum. Molybdenum makes the operation strategically significant: Armenia, through ZCMC, accounts for a notable share of world molybdenum output, and the concentrate is exported, largely for processing and sale abroad.

History

Industrial development of the Kajaran deposit began in the Soviet period, with the combine established in the 1950s to serve the USSR's demand for copper and molybdenum. After Armenia's independence the plant was privatized; in 2004 a majority stake was acquired by the German company Cronimet Mining, and later restructuring brought in other investors. ZCMC has consistently been one of Armenia's largest taxpayers and employers, and the welfare of Kajaran and the surrounding Syunik region is closely tied to its operations.

Environmental issues

The combine's tailings facilities, notably the Artsvanik tailings dam, have been the subject of environmental monitoring and public debate in Armenia regarding water quality and land use in the Voghji river basin.
