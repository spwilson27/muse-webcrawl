Trollstigen

The eleven hairpin bends of Trollstigen

Trollstigen (the Troll's Ladder, or Troll's Path) is a serpentine mountain road in Rauma Municipality in Møre og Romsdal county, Norway. It is part of Norwegian County Road 63 and is one of the country's most famous tourist attractions, a dramatic stretch of road climbing the mountainside through eleven hairpin bends.

Description

The road climbs from the Isterdalen valley to a summit about 850 metres above sea level, with a gradient of about 1:12. It is flanked by towering peaks such as Kongen ("the King"), Dronninga ("the Queen") and Bispen ("the Bishop"), and the Stigfossen waterfall plunges some 320 metres beside the road, passing under a stone bridge near the top.

Trollstigen opened on 31 July 1936, after eight years of construction, and was built largely by manual labour. Because of snow and the risk of rockfall, the road is normally open only from mid-May or June until the first snows in autumn.

Tourism

A visitor centre and viewing platforms, designed by Reiulf Ramstad Architects and opened in 2012, provide viewpoints over the bends and the valley below. Trollstigen forms part of the Geiranger–Trollstigen National Tourist Route.
