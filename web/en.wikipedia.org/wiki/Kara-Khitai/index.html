Kara-Khitai

The Kara-Khitai (also Qara Khitai, "Black Khitans"), known in Chinese historiography as the Western Liao (西遼), was a Sinicised Khitan empire in Central Asia ruled by the Yelü clan between 1124 and 1218. Founded by Yelü Dashi after the fall of the Liao dynasty to the Jurchen Jin, it controlled Transoxiana, the Tarim Basin margins and the steppe west to the Aral Sea from its capital at Balasagun in the Chu valley.

History

Yelü Dashi, a Liao prince, fled west after the Jin conquest and gathered the Khitan remnants in Mongolia before conquering the eastern Karakhanids. In 1134 he took Balasagun and made it his capital; in 1141, at the Battle of Qatwan near Samarkand, his forces decisively defeated the Seljuk sultan Ahmad Sanjar, making the Kara-Khitai suzerain over the Karakhanids and Khwarazm. News of a great Christian-adjacent eastern king defeating a Muslim sultan helped feed the European legend of Prester John.

Government

The rulers, who took the title Gurkhan ("universal khan"), governed a multi-religious empire with a light touch, retaining local dynasties as vassals and using Chinese-style administration, coinage and era names alongside Khitan customs. The empire was noted for religious tolerance; Buddhism flourished under the ruling house while the population was largely Muslim.

Fall

In 1211 the Naiman prince Kuchlug usurped the throne; his persecution of Islam alienated subjects, and in 1218 the Mongols under Jebe destroyed the state, absorbing its territory into the growing Mongol Empire.
