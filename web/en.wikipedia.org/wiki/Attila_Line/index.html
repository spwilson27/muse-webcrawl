The Attila Line is the de facto border dividing the island of Cyprus into the area controlled by the Republic of Cyprus in the south and the self-declared Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus in the north. It runs roughly 180 km across the island from Morphou Bay in the west to Famagusta Bay in the east, passing through the divided capital, Nicosia, where the corresponding boundary is commonly called the Green Line. The strip between the opposing ceasefire lines forms the United Nations Buffer Zone, patrolled since 1974 by the UN Peacekeeping Force in Cyprus (UNFICYP).

History

The name "Attila" derives from the Turkish military codename for its 1974 intervention in Cyprus (Operation Atilla), launched after a Greek junta-backed coup aimed at union with Greece. In two offensives in July and August 1974, Turkish forces secured the northern portion of the island, and the population movements that followed left Cyprus effectively partitioned along the ceasefire lines — the Attila Line — with Turkish Cypriots in the north and Greek Cypriots in the south.

Status

No country other than Turkey recognizes the northern administration, and the line is not an internationally recognized border. Crossing points, closed for decades, began opening in 2003, and in 2008 the barricade at Ledra Street in central Nicosia was removed, allowing pedestrians to cross in the capital's shopping district. Negotiations to reunify the island, including the 2004 Annan Plan referendums, have so far failed to produce a settlement, and the line remains one of the last divided-frontier zones in Europe.
