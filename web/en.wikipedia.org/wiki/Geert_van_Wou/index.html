Geert van Wou

Geert van Wou
Bornc. 1450, Nijmegen, Netherlands
Died1527, Kampen, Netherlands
OccupationBell founder

Geert van Wou (c. 1450–1527) was a Dutch bell founder, regarded as the greatest bell-founder of the late Middle Ages in northern Europe. Based in Kampen in the present-day Netherlands, he cast bells for churches across the Netherlands and northern Germany.

Work

Van Wou, whose full name was Gerardus de Wou, developed bell profiles of unusual tonal quality, producing bells tuned so that their main partials form a harmonious whole — a standard that anticipated later carillon tuning. His most famous bells are the thirteen bells he cast in 1505 for Utrecht Cathedral's Dom Tower; several, including the great Salvator bell, still ring. Other notable works survive in Kampen, Deventer, Zutphen, Lübeck, Hamburg, Osnabrück and Erfurt.

Legacy

His bells are prized for their purity of tone, and he is considered a forerunner of the seventeenth-century Hemony brothers who perfected the tuned carillon bell. His foundry in Kampen was continued by his son-in-law Johannes Tolhuys.
