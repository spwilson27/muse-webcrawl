Twist ending

A twist ending is a narrative device in which a work of fiction — a novel, film, play, short story, or television episode — concludes with an unexpected turn of events that radically reinterprets what has come before. Also called a surprise ending or plot twist when it occurs at the climax, the device depends on the audience's assumptions being overturned: information withheld or presented misleadingly is revealed, forcing a reassessment of characters, motives, or the nature of the story's world.

A twist ending recontextualises the entire preceding narrative.

Mechanics

Effective twist endings are usually held to require both surprise and retrospective coherence: the revelation should be unexpected on first encounter yet appear inevitable in hindsight, supported by clues planted earlier in the work. Techniques used to conceal the truth include the unreliable narrator, the red herring, misleading editing or framing in film, and the deliberate exploitation of genre conventions. A twist that contradicts established facts without preparation is often criticised as a deus ex machina or as "cheating" the audience.

History and notable examples

The device is ancient: O. Henry became famous for twist-ending short stories in the early twentieth century, as did, in a darker register, Roald Dahl in his Tales of the Unexpected. Agatha Christie's novels, notably The Murder of Roger Ackroyd (1926), scandalised and delighted readers with narrator-based twists. In cinema, celebrated examples include Psycho (1960), Planet of the Apes (1968), The Usual Suspects (1995), The Sixth Sense (1999) — which made director M. Night Shyamalan closely associated with the device — and Fight Club (1999). Television anthologies such as The Twilight Zone made the twist ending a structural signature.

Related devices

A twist ending differs from a cliffhanger, which withholds resolution rather than reinterpreting events, and from an ironic ending, in which an outcome inverts a character's expectations but not necessarily the audience's understanding of the plot. The term "plot twist" is broader and may occur anywhere in a narrative; a twist ending is specifically the final, recontextualising revelation.
