John Hanke (born 1967) is an American entrepreneur and business executive. He co-founded Keyhole, Inc., the geospatial company whose EarthViewer software became Google Earth after Google's 2004 acquisition, and later founded Niantic, Inc., developer of the augmented-reality games Ingress and the global hit Pokémon Go (2016).

Early life and education

Hanke grew up in Cross Plains, Texas, and graduated from the University of Texas at Austin. He worked in foreign affairs-related roles in Washington, D.C. and abroad, co-founded the game company Archetype Interactive (maker of the early MMO Meridian 59), and earned an MBA from the Haas School of Business at UC Berkeley in 1996.

Keyhole and Google

In 2001 Hanke co-founded Keyhole, Inc., whose EarthViewer 3D virtual-globe software gained fame during the 2003 Iraq War. Google acquired Keyhole in 2004 and relaunched the product as Google Earth in 2005. Hanke spent years as a Google vice president leading the Geo division — Earth, Maps, Street View, Local and related products — during the explosive growth of consumer mapping.

Niantic

In 2010 Hanke founded Niantic Labs as an internal Google startup, named after a Gold Rush-era whaling ship that became a buried San Francisco landmark. The lab released the location-based augmented reality game Ingress (2012), building a planetary database of public artworks and landmarks ("portals") from player submissions. Niantic spun out of Google as an independent company in 2015. In July 2016 it launched Pokémon Go with Nintendo and The Pokémon Company; the game became a worldwide phenomenon, breaking app-store records and grossing billions of dollars, and permanently established location-based AR gaming. Niantic later released Harry Potter: Wizards Unite (2019), Pikmin Bloom (2021) and Monster Hunter Now (2023), and developed the Lightship AR platform. In 2025 Niantic's games business, including Pokémon Go, was sold to Scopely, while its geospatial platform continued as Niantic Spatial, with Hanke as CEO.
