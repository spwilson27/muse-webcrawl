J-rock

J-rock (Japanese: 日本のロック) is an umbrella term for rock music produced in Japan, encompassing a wide range of styles from 1960s Group Sounds to punk, alternative, metal, and the flamboyant visual kei movement. Alongside J-pop, it forms a major pillar of the Japanese music industry, one of the largest in the world.

History

Japanese rock began in the 1960s with the eleki guitar boom and the Group Sounds bands (The Tigers, The Spiders), followed in the 1970s by pioneering acts such as Happy End, who sang rock in Japanese, and the hard rock of Flower Travellin' Band. The 1980s brought the band boom and visual kei pioneers X Japan, whose fusion of speed metal and ballads sold tens of millions of records. In the 1990s, B'z, Mr. Children, Glay, and L'Arc-en-Ciel dominated charts, while indie bands like Number Girl and Supercar shaped an alternative scene.

Notable artists

  • X Japan, Luna Sea, L'Arc-en-Ciel (visual kei / mainstream)
  • B'z — Japan's best-selling music act overall
  • Asian Kung-Fu Generation, Bump of Chicken, Radwimps
  • Boris, Mono, toe (internationally influential experimental/math rock)
  • Babymetal — kawaii metal crossover phenomenon of the 2010s

Anime tie-in singles ("anison") are a major commercial route for J-rock bands, and summer festivals such as Fuji Rock and Summer Sonic anchor the live calendar.
