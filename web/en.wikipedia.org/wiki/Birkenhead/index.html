Birkenhead is a town in the Metropolitan Borough of Wirral, Merseyside, England, on the Wirral Peninsula at the western bank of the River Mersey, opposite Liverpool. Historically part of Cheshire, it grew rapidly in the 19th century as a shipbuilding and port town centred on the Cammell Laird shipyard and the Great Float docks.

History

The name derives from Old English for "headland overgrown with birch". A priory was founded in the 12th century, and a ferry across the Mersey — famously celebrated in the song "Ferry Cross the Mersey" region-wide — operated for centuries. Birkenhead Park, opened in 1847 and designed by Joseph Paxton, was the world's first publicly funded park and influenced the design of Central Park in New York. The town pioneered street tramways in Britain (1860). The Mersey Railway tunnel (1886) and the Queensway road tunnel (1934) linked Birkenhead to Liverpool.

Economy and culture

Shipbuilding at Cammell Laird produced famous vessels including CSS Alabama and HMS Ark Royal; the yard remains a centre for ship repair. Birkenhead is the home of Tranmere Rovers F.C. and of the Williamson Art Gallery. Regeneration schemes have focused on the docks, including the Wirral Waters development.
