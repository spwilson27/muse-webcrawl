Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos

Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos refers to the period in Philippine history when President Ferdinand Marcos placed the country under martial law via Proclamation No. 1081, signed 21 September 1972 and announced 23 September 1972.

Declaration and rule

Marcos cited communist and Muslim insurgencies and an alleged ambush on Defence Minister Juan Ponce Enrile. Congress was shut, media closed, and thousands of opposition figures — including Benigno Aquino Jr. — were arrested. Martial law formally lasted until 1981, though Marcos retained authoritarian powers until 1986.

Human rights abuses

Amnesty International and later Philippine commissions documented roughly 70,000 arrests, 34,000 cases of torture, and over 3,200 extrajudicial killings during the era, alongside plunder of billions of dollars by the Marcos family, including Imelda Marcos.

End

The assassination of Aquino in 1983 galvanised opposition, leading to the People Power Revolution in February 1986 that ousted Marcos. The 2013 Human Rights Victims Reparation Act recognised victims.
