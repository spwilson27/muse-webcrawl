Drifting (motorsport)

Drifting is a driving technique and motorsport where the driver intentionally oversteers, causing a loss of traction in the rear wheels (or all the wheels), while maintaining control and driving the car through the entirety of a corner, or a set of corners. Competitive drifting is judged on speed, angle, line, and style rather than on who finishes first.

History

Modern drifting originated in Japanese street racing on mountain roads (touge) and the All Japan Touring Car Championship, popularised in the 1970s by Kunimitsu Takahashi and in the 1980s by Keiichi Tsuchiya, the "Drift King". The D1 Grand Prix series in Japan, founded in 2001, professionalised the sport, followed by Formula Drift in the United States from 2004.

Technique and cars

Drift cars are usually rear-wheel-drive, lightweight coupés with modified suspension, steering lock, and engines tuned for throttle control. Techniques to initiate a drift include the handbrake turn, clutch kick, weight transfer, and power-over.
