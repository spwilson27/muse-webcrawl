HM Customs and Excise

HM Customs and Excise (properly the Commissioners of Her Majesty's Customs and Excise) was a department of the British Government responsible for the collection of VAT and customs duties, excise duties, and other indirect taxes, and for the control of imported and exported goods. On 18 April 2005 it merged with the Inland Revenue to form HM Revenue and Customs.

History

Customs duties had been collected in England since the 13th century, and a dedicated Board of Customs was established in the 17th century; a separate Excise department grew from duties introduced during the Commonwealth in 1643. The two boards were united as the Board of Customs and Excise in 1909. The department administered VAT from its introduction in 1973, replacing purchase tax.

Enforcement

Beyond tax collection, HM Customs and Excise was the front-line agency against drug smuggling and the illegal importation of alcohol, tobacco, firearms, and endangered species. Its officers had powers of arrest and search, and it operated a fleet of cutters through HM Customs Cutter Service. Its investigative work on drugs and commercial fraud was later transferred in part to the Serious Organised Crime Agency and the UK Border Agency.

Merger

Following the O'Donnell review of 2004, HM Customs and Excise merged with the Inland Revenue on 18 April 2005 to create HM Revenue and Customs, unifying direct and indirect tax administration in a single department.
