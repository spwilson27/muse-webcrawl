Rupa

Rūpa (Sanskrit and Pāli: रूप, "form") is a central concept in Indian religions, especially Buddhism, where it denotes material form — matter and the body — as opposed to mind. In Buddhist analysis of experience, rūpa is the first of the five aggregates (skandhas) that make up a sentient being, the others being feeling, perception, mental formations and consciousness.

In Buddhism

In the early Buddhist texts, rūpa comprises the four great elements (mahābhūta) — earth (solidity), water (cohesion), fire (temperature) and air (motion) — together with the forms derived from them, including the sense organs and their visible objects, sounds, smells, tastes and tangibles. Because rūpa, like all conditioned things, is impermanent (anicca), unsatisfactory (dukkha) and without self (anattā), attachment to form is a source of suffering to be overcome through insight.

The term also appears in contexts such as the rūpa-loka, the "form realm" of meditative rebirth above the sense-desire realm, contrasted with the arūpa-loka or formless realm; and in nāma-rūpa ("name and form"), a link in the chain of dependent origination designating the psychophysical organism.

In Hindu thought

In Hindu philosophy, rūpa is one of the qualities perceived by the senses — colour and form, the object of sight — and in aesthetics it denotes the visible beauty or shape of a deity's image (murti), central to devotional practice.
