Stars in My Pocket Like Grains of Sand is a 1984 science fiction novel by Samuel R. Delany. It is the first volume of a projected diptych, the second volume of which, The Splendor and Misery of Bodies, of Cities, remains unpublished. The novel is widely regarded as one of the most significant works of literary science fiction of the 1980s and a landmark text in the genre's treatment of sexuality, language and cultural difference.

Plot summary

The novel is narrated in the first person by Marq Dyeth, an industrial diplomat from an aristocratic family on the planet Velm. It recounts his relationship with Rat Korga, sole survivor of the destroyed planet Rhyonon, whose brain was altered by "Radical Anxiety Termination" treatment that left him contented in a state of enforced servitude. Set within the conflict between two great cultural systems—the Sygn, which values diversity, and the Family, which promotes a single model of social organisation—the book alternates narrative episodes with dense ethnographic description of alien customs.

Themes

Delany explores desire, gender (his imagined languages have elaborate pronoun systems), utopia and the limits of cross-cultural understanding. The prose is noted for its density and its use of science-fictional estrangement to examine real-world issues of race, sexuality and power.

Reception

The novel was named among the best science fiction novels of its decade by critics and scholars and helped cement Delany's reputation; it is frequently taught in university courses on science fiction and queer literature.
