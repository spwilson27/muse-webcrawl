Vertex form

Formy = a(x − h)² + k
Vertex(h, k)
Related formsStandard form, factored form

In algebra, the vertex form of a quadratic function is the expression y = a(x − h)² + k, in which the point (h, k) is the vertex of the parabola — its maximum point if a < 0 or its minimum point if a > 0 — and a controls the width and direction of the opening.

Converting from standard form

Any quadratic in standard form y = ax² + bx + c can be converted to vertex form by completing the square. The vertex coordinates are h = −b / (2a) and k = c − b² / (4a), equivalently k = f(h). The vertical line x = h is the axis of symmetry of the parabola.

Uses

Vertex form makes the graph's turning point and transformations from the basic parabola y = x² immediately visible: h shifts the graph horizontally, k shifts it vertically, and the sign and size of a reflect and stretch it. It is therefore convenient for sketching parabolas and for solving optimisation problems in which the maximum or minimum value of a quadratic model is sought.
