Patient safety

Patient safety is a discipline within healthcare that applies safety science methods toward the goal of achieving a trustworthy system of health care delivery. It is also a health care discipline that emerged with the evolving complexity of health care systems and the resulting rise of patient harm in health care facilities. Patient safety aims to prevent and reduce risks, errors, and harm that occur to patients during the provision of health care.

Safety checklists, adapted from aviation, are a core patient safety intervention.

Scale of the problem

The modern patient safety movement is often dated to the 1999 U.S. Institute of Medicine report To Err Is Human, which estimated that between 44,000 and 98,000 Americans died each year as a result of preventable medical errors. The World Health Organization estimates that 1 in 10 patients is harmed in health care settings worldwide, and that medication errors alone cost tens of billions of dollars annually. Common categories of harm include healthcare-associated infections, surgical complications, diagnostic errors, medication errors, falls, and pressure ulcers.

Causes and systems thinking

Patient safety science, drawing on the work of James Reason, emphasizes that most errors arise from flawed systems rather than individual incompetence. Reason's "Swiss cheese model" depicts organizational defences as slices with holes; harm occurs when the holes align. Safety programs therefore focus on redesigning processes, standardizing work, building in redundancies and forcing functions, and fostering a "just culture" in which staff report errors and near misses without fear of punishment.

Interventions

Well-studied interventions include the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist, which in a landmark 2009 study was associated with substantial reductions in deaths and complications; central-line bundles that sharply reduced bloodstream infections in intensive care units; computerized provider order entry and barcode medication administration; structured handoff tools such as SBAR and I-PASS; and rapid response teams for deteriorating patients.

Organizations

Key bodies promoting patient safety include the World Health Organization's Patient Safety programme, the U.S. Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ), the Institute for Healthcare Improvement (IHI), and national reporting systems such as the United Kingdom's National Reporting and Learning System. The WHO launched World Patient Safety Day, observed annually on 17 September, in 2019.
