Sunrise (company)

Sunrise Inc. (Japanese: 株式会社サンライズ), operating since 2022 under the name Bandai Namco Filmworks with "Sunrise" retained as a brand, is a Japanese animation studio founded in 1972 by former members of Mushi Production. It is best known as the creator of the Gundam franchise, beginning with Mobile Suit Gundam (1979), and for acclaimed series including Cowboy Bebop, Code Geass, Gintama, Love Live!, and Tiger & Bunny.

History

After Osamu Tezuka's Mushi Production collapsed, its former staff founded several studios; Sunrise emerged in 1972, initially producing work-for-hire and co-productions under names such as Soeisha and Nippon Sunrise because sponsors, including toy maker Clover, demanded anonymity. Working with director Yoshiyuki Tomino, Sunrise shifted mecha anime toward realism with Zambot 3 (1977) and Mobile Suit Gundam (1979). Bandai acquired the studio in 1994, making it central to the group's character-merchandising strategy alongside Toei Animation as its chief rival.

Works and reputation

Beyond Gundam's many timelines (Universal Century, Gundam Wing, Gundam SEED, Gundam 00, The Witch from Mercury), Sunrise produced the stylish space-noir Cowboy Bebop (1998), the hit alternate-history mecha series Code Geass (2006), the long-running comedy Gintama, and the idol franchise Love Live!. In April 2022 the studio was merged into Bandai Namco Filmworks, though the Sunrise brand continues on its productions. Its internal studios (numbered 1–11 at various times) and affiliate Bones — founded by ex-Sunrise staff — have shaped modern anime production.
