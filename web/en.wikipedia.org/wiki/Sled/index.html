A sled, sledge, or sleigh is a land vehicle that slides across a surface, usually on snow or ice, supported by a smooth underside or by narrow runners. Sleds are propelled by gravity on slopes, pushed or pulled by people or draft animals, or fitted with sails or motors. The distinction commonly drawn is that a sledge is a heavier working vehicle, a sleigh is an animal-drawn passenger vehicle, and a sled is a smaller recreational device, though usage varies by region and the terms overlap.

History

Sleds are among the oldest forms of transport. Sledges were used in ancient Egypt to haul stone blocks for monuments, with workers pouring water on the sand ahead of the runners to reduce friction, as confirmed by wall paintings and modern experiments. In northern latitudes, dog sleds and reindeer sleds were essential to indigenous peoples of the Arctic and sub-Arctic, and horse-drawn sleighs were standard winter transport in Europe and North America before the ploughing of roads.

Types

  • Toboggan — a flat-bottomed sled of bent wood or plastic, without runners.
  • Bobsled (bobsleigh) — a steerable racing sled on runners, an Olympic sport since 1924.
  • Luge and skeleton — small racing sleds ridden feet-first or head-first respectively.
  • Dog sled — pulled by a team of sled dogs, used in racing events such as the Iditarod.
  • Kicksled — a Scandinavian chair-mounted sled pushed by one foot.
  • Snowmobile — a motorised sled with a track and skis.
  • Flexible Flyer — the classic steerable wooden recreational sled patented by Samuel Leeds Allen in 1889.
  • Sled (rescue and cargo) — including the pulk, a short Scandinavian cargo sled towed by a skier or dog.

Physics

A sled moves efficiently because the kinetic friction between smooth runners and snow or ice is low; pressure and frictional heating melt a thin film of water that lubricates the interface. Runner materials range from wood and steel to modern polymers chosen for low friction at speed.

Recreation and sport

Sledding is a widespread winter recreation. Competitive sled sports governed internationally include bobsleigh, luge, and skeleton under the IBSF and FIL, contested on refrigerated tracks with banked curves. Collisions and high speeds at sledding hills are nevertheless a notable cause of winter injuries, prompting widespread helmet recommendations for children.
