Non-commissioned officer


Labelled placeholder: NCOs

A non-commissioned officer (NCO) is a military officer who does not hold a commission. Non-commissioned officers usually earn their position of authority by promotion through the enlisted ranks; they are not commissioned by a head of state or government. In contrast, commissioned officers usually enter directly from a military academy or officer training, or hold a university degree. NCOs typically lead smaller groups of personnel and are responsible for their training, discipline and welfare.

The NCO corps often includes grades such as corporal, sergeant, and petty officer (in naval forces). Senior NCOs — such as sergeant majors, first sergeants and chief petty officers — serve as advisers to commanding officers and as the link between officers and enlisted personnel. NCOs are often described as the "backbone of the armed forces."

Function

The non-commissioned officer corps is often categorised as junior NCOs (corporals and sergeants) and senior NCOs (staff sergeants and above, or their equivalents). Junior NCOs supervise small teams and squads; senior NCOs exercise responsibility at platoon, company and battalion levels and beyond, manage training and logistics, and mentor junior officers.

By country

United States

In the United States Army and Marine Corps, NCOs range from corporal (E-4) to the senior-most grades (E-9). The US Navy and Coast Guard use the term petty officer for their NCO equivalents. The services maintain formal NCO professional development systems, including the Army's Noncommissioned Officer Professional Development System (NCOPDS).

United Kingdom

British NCO ranks include lance corporal, corporal, sergeant, staff/colour sergeant, and warrant officers class 2 and 1 above them. In the Royal Navy, petty officers and chief petty officers fill the role.
