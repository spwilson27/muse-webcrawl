Mount Royal

Mount Royal (French: Mont Royal) is a small mountain in the city of Montreal, Quebec, Canada, immediately west of downtown. The city takes its name from the mountain, which Jacques Cartier named Mont Royal in 1535. At 233 metres, its highest point is marked by the Mount Royal Cross, first erected in 1643 and replaced by the illuminated steel cross of 1924.

The park

Mount Royal Park, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and inaugurated in 1876, covers much of the mountain. It includes the Kondiaronk Belvedere lookout, Beaver Lake (Lac aux Castors), wooded trails used for hiking, cycling, cross-country skiing, and the popular Sunday "Tam-Tams" gatherings on its eastern slope. Olmsted intended the park to emphasize the mountain's natural topography rather than formal gardens.

Surroundings

The mountain has three peaks: Mount Royal proper, Outremont, and Westmount Summit. It is flanked by Mount Royal Cemetery and Notre Dame des Neiges Cemetery — among Canada's largest burial grounds — and by institutions including McGill University, the Université de Montréal, and Saint Joseph's Oratory, whose dome is a landmark on the northwestern slope. The mountain is one of the Monteregian Hills, ancient intrusive igneous formations of the St. Lawrence lowlands.
