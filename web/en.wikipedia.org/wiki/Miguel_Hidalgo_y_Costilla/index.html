Miguel Gregorio Antonio Ignacio Hidalgo y Costilla Gallaga Mandarte y Villaseñor (8 May 1753 – 30 July 1811), more commonly known as Miguel Hidalgo or El Padre de la Patria ("The Father of the Nation"), was a Mexican Roman Catholic priest and a leader of the Mexican War of Independence.

As a parish priest in the town of Dolores, Hidalgo was a learned and unconventional cleric who encouraged his parishioners to cultivate grapes and silkworms in defiance of colonial monopolies. In the early morning of 16 September 1810 he issued the Grito de Dolores (Cry of Dolores), a call to arms that launched the revolt against Spanish colonial rule in New Spain.

Early life and career

Hidalgo was born on a hacienda near Pénjamo in present-day Guanajuato to a creole family. He studied at the Colegio de San Nicolás in Valladolid (now Morelia), where he later became rector, earning a reputation as an unorthodox thinker influenced by Enlightenment ideas and French literature. Ordained a priest in 1778, he eventually became parish priest of Dolores, where he promoted education, industry, and agriculture among his largely Indigenous and mestizo parishioners, and questioned orthodox doctrines and papal authority — activities that brought him to the attention of the Inquisition.

The Grito de Dolores and the insurgency

Hidalgo joined a conspiracy of creole officers and intellectuals in Querétaro plotting independence. When the plot was discovered in September 1810, Hidalgo rang the church bell at Dolores and called his parishioners to rise against the Spanish government. His improvised army — tens of thousands of Indigenous people, mestizos, and creoles carrying an image of the Virgin of Guadalupe as its banner — swept through the Bajío, capturing Celaya, Guanajuato, and Guadalajara. The bloody storming of the Alhóndiga de Granaditas in Guanajuato, where royalist defenders and civilians were massacred, shocked creole opinion and cost the movement support.

Hidalgo's forces advanced to the outskirts of Mexico City but did not take the capital. Defeated by royalist forces at the Battle of Calderón Bridge in January 1811, the insurgents retreated north. Hidalgo was captured at Acatita de Baján in March 1811, defrocked, and executed by firing squad in Chihuahua on 30 July 1811. His head was displayed in a cage at the Alhóndiga in Guanajuato for ten years.

Legacy

Hidalgo is honoured in Mexico as the Father of the Nation. The anniversary of the Grito, 16 September, is celebrated as Mexico's Independence Day; the president re-enacts the cry from the balcony of the National Palace in Mexico City each year, ringing the same bell Hidalgo used. The state of Hidalgo, the city of Dolores Hidalgo, and innumerable towns, streets, and monuments bear his name.
