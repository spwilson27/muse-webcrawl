Country of production: United Kingdom
Date of production: 1854–1879
Face value: 1 penny

The Penny Red, Britain's standard letter stamp for over two decades

The Penny Red was a British postage stamp issued in 1854 as the successor to the famous Penny Black. Printed in red-brown ink — a change made so that black cancellation marks would show clearly and to prevent fraudulent reuse of the earlier black stamp — it carried the same portrait of the young Queen Victoria and served as the standard one-penny letter rate stamp for more than twenty years.

Issues and varieties

Early Penny Reds were imperforate and had to be cut from the sheet with scissors, as with the Penny Black. From 1854–55 perforations (initially gauge 16, then 14) were introduced, and stars appeared in the upper corners. From 1864 the stamps carried plate numbers engraved in the design and letters in all four corners, making the Penny Red a rich field for philatelists: hundreds of plates were used, and individual plate varieties are catalogued and collected avidly. Major printings include the issues of 1858–1869 with letters in all four corners and plate numbers from 71 to 225.

Replacement

In 1880 the Penny Red was replaced by the Penny Venetian Red, and in 1881 by the lilac-and-green issues required under Universal Postal Union colour rules. Because tens of billions were printed, ordinary Penny Reds remain inexpensive, though rare plates and printing errors command high prices.
