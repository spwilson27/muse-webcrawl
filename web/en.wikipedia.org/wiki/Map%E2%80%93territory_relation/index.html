Map–territory relation

The map–territory relation is the relationship between an object and a representation of that object, as in the relation between a geographical territory and a map of it. The Polish-American scholar Alfred Korzybski coined the dictum "the map is not the territory" in 1931.

Origins

Korzybski presented the idea in a 1931 paper to the American Association for the Advancement of Science and developed it in Science and Sanity (1933). The expression also reflects the broader point that models, abstractions and descriptions must not be confused with the things they describe.

Related ideas

The concept is related to Gregory Bateson's work on abstraction, Alan Watts's popularisation in the 1950s, and Jorge Luis Borges's one-paragraph story "On Exactitude in Science", which imagines a map drawn at a scale of 1:1. René Magritte's painting The Treachery of Images ("Ceci n'est pas une pipe") explores a similar point.

Use

The dictum is widely invoked in philosophy, semiotics, cognitive science and modelling to warn against confusing a model with reality, and underlies the idea that all models are wrong but some are useful.
