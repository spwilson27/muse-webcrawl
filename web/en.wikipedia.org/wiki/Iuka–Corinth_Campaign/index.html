Iuka–Corinth campaign

DateSeptember – October 1862
LocationNorthern Mississippi, United States
ResultUnion victory
Union commandersUlysses S. Grant; William Rosecrans; Edward Ord
Confederate commandersEarl Van Dorn; Sterling Price

The Iuka–Corinth campaign was a series of manoeuvres and battles in northern Mississippi during September and October 1862, early in the American Civil War, fought for control of the strategically vital rail junction at Corinth, Mississippi. Union forces under Major General Ulysses S. Grant defeated Confederate attempts to recapture Corinth, first at the Battle of Iuka (September 19, 1862) and then decisively at the Second Battle of Corinth (October 3–4, 1862).

Background

Corinth's railroads — the Memphis and Charleston and the Mobile and Ohio — made it one of the most important logistical points in the western Confederacy. Union forces under Henry Halleck had occupied the town in May 1862 after the Siege of Corinth. In the autumn, Confederate generals Earl Van Dorn and Sterling Price manoeuvred separately to retake it while Grant's forces were dispersed across northern Mississippi and western Tennessee.

Battle of Iuka

On September 14, Price occupied Iuka, Mississippi. Grant ordered a converging attack: William Rosecrans approaching from the southwest and Edward Ord from the northwest. Owing to an "acoustic shadow", Ord's force never heard the fighting and did not engage on September 19; Rosecrans fought Price alone and was nearly overwhelmed. Price, learning of Ord's proximity and of Van Dorn's movements, withdrew during the night, slipping past Rosecrans's unguarded escape route. Union casualties were roughly 780 and Confederate about 1,500.

Second Battle of Corinth

Van Dorn, now joined by Price, attacked Corinth on October 3 with about 22,000 men against Rosecrans's roughly 23,000 entrenched defenders. The Confederates drove the Union line back on the first day; on October 4 their assaults briefly penetrated the town and carried Battery Robinett in bloody fighting before being repulsed. Van Dorn retreated toward Holly Springs, harassed but not destroyed — Rosecrans's delayed pursuit let the Confederate army escape. Union losses were about 2,500; Confederate losses about 4,800.

Aftermath

The campaign ended Confederate efforts to regain northern Mississippi and kept Corinth in Union hands, securing Grant's position as he prepared the operations that would lead to the Vicksburg campaign. Rosecrans was soon transferred to command the Army of the Cumberland in Tennessee.
