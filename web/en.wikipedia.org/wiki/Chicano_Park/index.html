Chicano Park is a 32,000 square metre (about 8 acre) park located beneath the San Diego–Coronado Bridge in Barrio Logan, a predominantly Chicano and Mexican American community in central San Diego, California. It is home to the largest concentration of Chicano murals in the world, painted on the bridge's support pylons.

History

The community of Barrio Logan was bisected by Interstate 5 in 1963 and the Coronado Bridge in 1969, displacing many residents. When construction began in April 1970 on what residents had been promised would be a park — but which was to become a California Highway Patrol station — the community occupied the land in a twelve-day protest. On April 22, 1970, the takeover began; the city ultimately agreed to the park, and artist Salvador Torres envisioned the bridge pylons as canvases for monumental murals.

The murals

From 1973 onwards, artists including members of the Royal Chicano Air Force and local muralists decorated the pylons with images of Aztec and Maya heritage, Mexican history, the farmworker movement, and figures such as Emiliano Zapata, Frida Kahlo, and César Chávez. Chicano Park Day is celebrated every April. The park was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2013 and designated a National Historic Landmark in 2017.
