HMS Erebus was a Hecla-class bomb vessel of the Royal Navy, launched in 1826 and named after Erebus, the dark region of the Greek underworld. Strengthened for service in ice, she became one of the most famous exploration ships of the 19th century, serving in both the Antarctic and the Arctic before being lost with Sir John Franklin's expedition.

Antarctic service

From 1839 to 1843, Erebus and her sister ship HMS Terror undertook James Clark Ross's Antarctic expedition, the first to chart much of the continent's coastline. The expedition discovered the Ross Sea, Ross Island and the great ice barrier later named the Ross Ice Shelf, and located the south magnetic pole's approximate position. Mount Erebus on Ross Island — Antarctica's most active volcano — was named after the ship.

Franklin expedition

In 1845, Erebus and Terror, both refitted with auxiliary steam engines and reinforced bows, sailed for the Canadian Arctic under Sir John Franklin in search of the Northwest Passage. The ships were last seen by whalers in Baffin Bay in July 1845. They became trapped in ice off King William Island and were abandoned in April 1848; all 129 officers and men died. Decades of searches recovered graves, relics and Inuit testimony but not the ships.

Discovery of the wreck

On 2 September 2014, a Canadian search led by Parks Canada found the wreck of Erebus in the Queen Maud Gulf, off King William Island, in about 11 metres of water. Her ship's bell was recovered and the site is now jointly managed with Inuit organisations as part of the Wrecks of HMS Erebus and HMS Terror National Historic Site.
