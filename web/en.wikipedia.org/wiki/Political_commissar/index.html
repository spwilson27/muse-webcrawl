Political commissar

A political commissar or political officer is a supervisory officer responsible for the political education and loyalty of a military unit. The institution is most associated with communist states, where the commissar represented the ruling party within the armed forces and shared authority with the unit's military commander.

History

The office originated with the commissaires of the French Revolutionary armies and was revived by the Bolsheviks in 1918, when Leon Trotsky appointed commissars to watch over former Tsarist officers serving in the Red Army; orders required the commissar's countersignature. In the Soviet Union the role was repeatedly reorganised: commissars were subordinated in 1925, restored in 1937 during the Great Purge, and replaced in 1942 by deputy commanders for political affairs (zampolit) under unitary command. Nazi Germany introduced National Socialist Leadership Officers late in the Second World War, partly in imitation.

Modern usage

The People's Liberation Army of China retains a dual-command system in which a political commissar of equal rank serves alongside the commander of each unit, responsible for party organisation, personnel and political education. Similar roles exist in the armed forces of Vietnam, Cuba and North Korea.
