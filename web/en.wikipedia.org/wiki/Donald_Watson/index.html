Donald Watson (2 September 1910 – 16 November 2005) was an English animal rights advocate who coined the word vegan and co-founded The Vegan Society in 1944. A schoolteacher and woodworker by profession, he is regarded as the founding figure of the modern vegan movement.

Early life

Watson was born in Mexborough, South Yorkshire, into a meat-eating family. Visits to his uncle's farm as a child — where he witnessed a pig being slaughtered — left him horrified, and he later recalled being troubled that the animals he had befriended on farms were all eventually killed. He became a vegetarian at the age of 14, around the New Year of 1924, initially as a New Year resolution he never abandoned. In his thirties his concerns deepened to the exploitation involved in dairy and egg production, and he gave up all animal products.

Founding veganism

In November 1944 Watson and five others — including his future wife Dorothy — broke from the Vegetarian Society to form a new group for "non-dairy vegetarians." Seeking a name, Watson coined vegan from the first three and last two letters of vegetarian, remarking that it marked "the beginning and end of vegetarian." The Vegan Society was founded on 1 November 1944 — a date now marked as World Vegan Day — and Watson edited its first newsletter, The Vegan News. Under his definition, veganism was "a philosophy and way of living which seeks to exclude — as far as is possible and practicable — all forms of exploitation of, and cruelty to, animals for food, clothing or any other purpose," a definition formalised by the Society in later decades.

Life and legacy

A conscientious objector during the Second World War, Watson worked as a woodwork teacher in Leicestershire and later lived in Keswick in the Lake District, where he was an enthusiastic fell walker; he continued hill walking into his nineties. He remained a vegan for over 60 years, attributing his longevity and health to the lifestyle, and gave interviews about the movement's origins until shortly before his death in 2005 at the age of 95. From the handful of founders in 1944, veganism grew into a global movement, and Watson's choice of name — initially criticised as obscure — became its standard term in scores of languages.
