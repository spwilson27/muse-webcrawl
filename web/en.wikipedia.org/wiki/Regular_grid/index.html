Regular grid

A regular grid is a tessellation of n-dimensional Euclidean space by congruent parallelotopes (such as squares in two dimensions or cubes in three). Grids of this type appear on graph paper and underlie many computational methods, in which each cell of the grid can be addressed by integer coordinates.

Regular grids are used in numerical simulation (finite difference methods), image processing, where pixels form a square grid, cartography, and games. Related structures include rectilinear and curvilinear grids, which relax the requirement of uniform spacing, and unstructured grids such as triangulations.
