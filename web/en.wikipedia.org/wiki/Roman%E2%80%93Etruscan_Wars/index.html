Roman–Etruscan Wars

The Roman–Etruscan Wars were a series of conflicts between the Roman Republic (and before it, the Roman Kingdom) and the city-states of the Etruscan civilisation of central Italy, fought intermittently from legendary times until the last Etruscan resistance ended in the 3rd century BC. The wars culminated in the absorption of Etruria into the Roman state.

Early conflicts

Rome's own tradition held that the city had itself been ruled by Etruscan kings — the Tarquins — until the founding of the Republic in 509 BC, after which Lars Porsenna of Clusium briefly besieged or occupied Rome. The nearest and most persistent enemy was Veii, only a short distance across the Tiber; after a ten-year siege Rome captured and destroyed Veii in 396 BC under Camillus, roughly doubling Roman territory.

Conquest of Etruria

Further wars in the 4th and 3rd centuries BC subdued the remaining Etruscan cities one by one. Tarquinii, Caere, Volsinii and others were defeated, allied or annexed; Etruscan troops fought Rome at Sentinum (295 BC) as part of a coalition with Samnites and Gauls and lost. The sack of Volsinii in 264 BC is conventionally taken as the completion of the conquest. Etruscan culture was gradually Romanised, though the language survived in ritual use into the early Empire.
