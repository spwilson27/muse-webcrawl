Yorgos Lanthimos

Born23 September 1973, Athens, Greece
OccupationFilm director, screenwriter, producer
Known forDogtooth, The Lobster, The Favourite, Poor Things

Yorgos Lanthimos (born 1973) is a Greek filmmaker regarded as a leading figure of the "Greek Weird Wave" and one of the most distinctive auteurs in contemporary cinema. His films are known for deadpan performances, stilted dialogue, fisheye photography, and unsettling explorations of power, social ritual, and human cruelty, frequently written with Efthymis Filippou or Deborah Davis and Tony McNamara.

Career

Lanthimos studied directing in Athens and began with music videos, commercials, and experimental theatre works, including for the 2004 Athens Olympics opening ceremony. After early features My Best Friend (2001) and Kinetta (2005), he broke through internationally with Dogtooth (2009), about three adult siblings raised in total isolation by their parents; it won the Prix Un Certain Regard at Cannes and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. Alps (2011) won the Best Screenplay prize at Venice.

English-language films

His English-language debut, The Lobster (2015), a dystopian satire in which single people must find a partner or be turned into animals, won the Jury Prize at Cannes and received an Oscar nomination for its screenplay. The Killing of a Sacred Deer (2017) shared the Cannes Best Screenplay award. The Favourite (2018), a blackly comic period drama starring Olivia Colman, Rachel Weisz, and Emma Stone, won the Grand Jury Prize at Venice and ten Academy Award nominations, with Colman winning Best Actress. Poor Things (2023), adapted from Alasdair Gray's novel and starring Stone as Bella Baxter, won the Golden Lion at Venice and four Academy Awards, including Best Actress. He followed it with the anthology film Kinds of Kindness (2024).

Style

Lanthimos's work is characterized by deliberately flattened acting, absurd social systems treated with total seriousness, classical music used ironically, and cinematography (often by Robbie Ryan) employing wide-angle and fisheye lenses, tracking shots, and abrupt violence.
