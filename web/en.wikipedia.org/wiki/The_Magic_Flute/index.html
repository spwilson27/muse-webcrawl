The Magic Flute

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Papageno in his feather suit meeting Papagena, the Queen of the Night blazing in a starry vault behind them]

The Magic Flute (German: Die Zauberflöte), K. 620, is an opera in two acts by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart to a German libretto by Emanuel Schikaneder. The work is in the form of a Singspiel, a popular form during the time it was written that included both singing and spoken dialogue. It premiered at Schikaneder's Theater auf der Wieden in Vienna on 30 September 1791, two months before Mozart's death.

Prince Tamino, armed with a magic flute, and the bird-catcher Papageno set out to rescue Pamina from Sarastro — who proves not a villain but a high priest of light, opposed by Pamina's mother, the Queen of the Night, whose coloratura aria 'Der Hölle Rache' reaches a stratospheric F6. Freemasonic symbolism of trials by fire and water pervades the final act.
