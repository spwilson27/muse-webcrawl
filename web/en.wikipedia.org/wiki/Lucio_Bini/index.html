Lucio Bini (18 September 1908 – 24 August 1964) was an Italian psychiatrist and researcher at the University of Rome who, together with Ugo Cerletti, developed electroconvulsive therapy (ECT), the first successful electrical convulsive treatment for severe mental illness. Bini designed and built the apparatus used in the first human treatment in April 1938 and is credited with much of the technical development of the method.

Work with Cerletti

Working in Cerletti's Clinic for Nervous and Mental Diseases in Rome, Bini experimented with electrically induced seizures in animals and, crucially, worked out the safe parameters for applying an electric current across a human head—using a brief alternating current through electrodes placed on the temples. He constructed the first ECT device, a machine with dials for voltage and timing, and recorded detailed observations of its use. The first patient, a man found wandering incoherently at Rome's railway station who was diagnosed with schizophrenia, received a series of treatments and was discharged recovered.

Later career

Bini continued his academic career in neuropsychiatry in Rome. Although ECT became one of the most widely used—and later most debated—somatic treatments in psychiatry, Bini's contribution was long overshadowed by Cerletti's name; medical historians have since emphasised Bini's central technical and clinical role in the invention.
