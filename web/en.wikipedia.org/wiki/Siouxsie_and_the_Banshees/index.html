Siouxsie and the Banshees

Siouxsie and the Banshees were an English rock band formed in London in 1976 by vocalist Siouxsie Sioux and bassist Steven Severin. Pioneers of post-punk, they developed a distinctive sound blending guitar textures, tribal rhythms and Sioux's commanding vocals, influencing gothic rock, alternative and later artists.

History

The band debuted famously at the 100 Club Punk Festival in 1976 with an improvised set. After signing to Polydor, debut single "Hong Kong Garden" (1978) reached the UK top 10, followed by albums The Scream (1978), Join Hands (1979), Kaleidoscope (1980) with "Happy House", and Juju (1981) with "Spellbound". Guitarist John McGeoch and drummer Budgie defined their classic lineup; later hits included a cover of "Dear Prudence" (1983), "Cities in Dust" (1985), "Peek-a-Boo" (1988) and US breakthrough "Kiss Them for Me" (1991).

Legacy

The Banshees influenced Joy Division, The Cure, Radiohead, PJ Harvey, Massive Attack and many others. Side project The Creatures (Sioux and Budgie) ran in parallel. The band split in 1996 and briefly reunited in 2002.
