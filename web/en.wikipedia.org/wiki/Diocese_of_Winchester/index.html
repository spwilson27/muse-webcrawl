The Diocese of Winchester is a Church of England diocese in the Province of Canterbury, covering most of Hampshire and a small part of eastern Dorset. Founded in the seventh century, it is one of the oldest and historically most important sees in England. Its cathedral is Winchester Cathedral and its bishop, the Bishop of Winchester, is one of the five senior bishops who sit in the House of Lords and ranks in precedence behind only the archbishops of Canterbury and York and the bishop of London.

History

The see was established at Dorchester-on-Thames for the West Saxons in the 630s and was transferred to Winchester about 676, as Winchester became the capital of the kingdom of Wessex. Medieval bishops of Winchester were among the richest and most powerful figures in England; William of Wykeham founded Winchester College and New College, Oxford, while Wolvesey Palace served as the bishop's residence.

The diocese formerly covered a much larger area, including Surrey; the Diocese of Guildford was separated from it in 1927, and the Channel Islands, long attached to Winchester, were transferred to the Diocese of Salisbury in 2022.

Structure

The diocese is organised into two archdeaconries, Winchester and Bournemouth, and is assisted by the suffragan Bishops of Southampton and Basingstoke.
