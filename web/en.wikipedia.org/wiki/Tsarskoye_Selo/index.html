Tsarskoye Selo (Russian: Ца́рское Село́, "Tsar's Village") was the town containing a former residence of the Russian imperial family and visiting nobility, located 24 kilometres (15 mi) south of central Saint Petersburg. The residence now forms part of the town of Pushkin. Tsarskoye Selo forms one of the World Heritage Sites of Saint Petersburg and Related Groups of Monuments.

History

The estate was given by Peter the Great to his wife, the future Empress Catherine I, in 1710. Under Empress Elizabeth, the architect Bartolomeo Rastrelli rebuilt the Catherine Palace in a lavish Rococo style; Catherine the Great later added Neoclassical interiors by Charles Cameron. The neighbouring Alexander Palace, built for the future Alexander I, became the principal home of the last tsar, Nicholas II, and his family, who lived there throughout his reign and were held under house arrest there after his abdication in 1917.

The town was renamed Pushkin in 1937 to commemorate the centenary of the death of Alexander Pushkin, who had studied at the Imperial Lyceum there. The palaces were looted and badly damaged during the German occupation of 1941–1944; restoration has continued for decades, including the recreation of the famous Amber Room, reopened in 2003.

Sights

The ensemble includes the Catherine Palace with the Amber Room, the Cameron Gallery, the Alexander Palace, extensive landscape parks with pavilions and follies, and the Pushkin Lyceum museum.
