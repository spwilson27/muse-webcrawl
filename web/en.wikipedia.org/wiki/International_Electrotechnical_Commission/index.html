The International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC; French: Commission électrotechnique internationale) is an international standards organization that prepares and publishes international standards for all electrical, electronic and related technologies – collectively known as "electrotechnology". IEC standards cover a vast range of technologies from power generation, transmission and distribution to home appliances and office equipment, semiconductors, fibre optics, batteries, solar energy, nanotechnology and marine energy, as well as many others.

The IEC Central Office is located in Geneva, Switzerland.

History

The IEC was founded on 26 June 1906 in London, following discussions at the International Electrical Congress in St. Louis in 1904. Lord Kelvin was its first president. The IEC was instrumental in developing and distributing standards for units of measurement, particularly the gauss, hertz, and weber, and it first proposed the Giorgi system of units, which ultimately became the SI system. In 1938 it published the International Electrotechnical Vocabulary (IEC 60050), and it moved to Geneva in 1948.

Standards and work

IEC standards are numbered in the 60000 series and beyond; well-known examples include IEC 60320 appliance couplers (the C13/C14 "kettle lead"), IEC 60309 industrial plugs, IEC 61131 for programmable controllers, IEC 61850 for substation automation, and IEC 62368 for AV/ICT equipment safety. Joint work with ISO includes the ISO/IEC 27001 information security standard and the MPEG media coding standards of ISO/IEC JTC 1. The IEC also operates conformity assessment systems—IECEE, IECEx, IECQ and IECRE—and publishes standards in the form of International Standards, Technical Specifications, Technical Reports, and Publicly Available Specifications.

Membership

Members are National Committees, one per country, representing national electrotechnical interests. Full and associate membership spans more than 80 countries, with an affiliate programme for developing nations. Thousands of experts from industry, government, and academia participate in roughly 200 technical committees and subcommittees.
