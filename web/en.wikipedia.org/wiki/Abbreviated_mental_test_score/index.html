Abbreviated mental test score

The Abbreviated Mental Test Score (AMTS) is a 10-point screening test used to rapidly assess elderly patients for possible dementia and other cognitive impairment. It was introduced in 1972 by H. M. Hodkinson and is widely used in hospital and primary care settings in the United Kingdom.

The test

Each correct answer scores one point, for a maximum of 10. The questions are:

ItemQuestion
1Age
2Time (to nearest hour)
3An address to recall at the end of the test (e.g. 42 West Street)
4Year
5Name of the hospital (or current location)
6Recognition of two persons (e.g. doctor, nurse)
7Date of birth
8Year the First World War began
9Name of the present monarch
10Count backwards from 20 to 1

Interpretation

A score of 6 or fewer out of 10 suggests possible delirium or dementia and warrants further investigation with more detailed instruments such as the Mini–Mental State Examination (MMSE) or the Montreal Cognitive Assessment. The AMTS is a screening tool only and is not by itself diagnostic.
