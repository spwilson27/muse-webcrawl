Nellie Bly

Nellie Bly
Born5 May 1864, Pennsylvania
Died27 January 1922
OccupationJournalist (New York World)
[Image: Bly photographed at the start of her round-the-world journey]

Nellie Bly (1864–1922), pen name of Elizabeth Cochran Seaman, was an American journalist who pioneered stunt and undercover reporting: her 1887 exposé Ten Days in a Mad-House, feigning insanity to report from inside a New York asylum, made her famous and prompted reforms; in 1889–90 she travelled around the world in 72 days for Pulitzer's World, beating Jules Verne's fictional record.

Later life

Bly married industrialist Robert Seaman, ran his manufacturing company after his death, and returned to journalism as a First World War reporter and columnist. She is a foundational figure in the history of women in journalism.
