Pay-per-click
Also calledPPC; cost per click (CPC)
TypeInternet advertising model
Major platformsGoogle Ads, Microsoft Advertising

Pay-per-click (PPC) is an internet advertising model used to drive traffic to websites, in which an advertiser pays a publisher (typically a search engine, website owner, or network of websites) when the ad is clicked. It is commonly associated with first-tier search engines, whose sponsored results appear above or alongside organic search results.

How it works

Advertisers bid on keywords relevant to their target market. When a user searches for a keyword, the search engine runs an auction that ranks ads by bid amount and quality measures (such as expected click-through rate and landing-page relevance). The advertiser pays the agreed cost per click (CPC) only when a user actually clicks the ad and visits the advertiser's site.

History

The model emerged in the mid-1990s with early web directories and search engines. GoTo.com (later Overture, and acquired by Yahoo!) popularized the paid-placement auction model from 1998; Google launched AdWords in 2000 and introduced quality-based ad ranking in 2002, making PPC the dominant model of search advertising.

Advantages and issues

PPC gives advertisers measurable, targetable traffic and immediate visibility, but it also creates incentives for click fraud — automated or malicious clicking intended to deplete a competitor's budget or generate revenue for publishers. Search engines employ filtering systems to detect invalid clicks. PPC campaigns are commonly evaluated against metrics such as click-through rate (CTR), conversion rate, and return on ad spend (ROAS).
