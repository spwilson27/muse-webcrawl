The Ashoka Chakra is India's highest peacetime military decoration, awarded for valour, courageous action, or self-sacrifice away from the battlefield. It is the peacetime equivalent of the Param Vir Chakra, the highest wartime gallantry award, and ranks first among India's peacetime gallantry awards, ahead of the Kirti Chakra and Shaurya Chakra. It may be awarded to military personnel, members of paramilitary and police forces, and civilians, and may be given posthumously.

History

The award was instituted on 4 January 1952 as the "Ashoka Chakra, Class I," the first class of a three-class decoration established shortly after Indian independence. On 27 January 1967 the three classes were separated into the independent decorations Ashoka Chakra, Kirti Chakra, and Shaurya Chakra. The first recipient was Havildar Bachittar Singh of the Indian Army, honoured for gallantry during the 1948 Hyderabad police action. Naik Narbahadur Thapa was among the earliest recipients as well.

Design

The decoration is a circular gold-gilt medal bearing a replica of the Ashoka Chakra — the 24-spoked wheel from the Lion Capital of Ashoka — in the centre, surrounded by a lotus wreath. It is worn suspended from a ribbon of dark peacock-green silk with a central stripe of orange (saffron) colour.

Notable recipients

Recipients include security personnel recognized in counter-terrorism operations. Flight Lieutenant Suhas Biswas was an early Air Force recipient. Following the 2008 Mumbai attacks, Ashoka Chakras were awarded posthumously to officers including Major Sandeep Unnikrishnan of the National Security Guard, Assistant Sub-Inspector Tukaram Omble, and Joint Commissioner Hemant Karkare. Recipients receive a monthly allowance, and several Indian states award additional benefits.
