The salt flats of the Makgadikgadi.

The Makgadikgadi Pan is a vast complex of salt pans in the middle of the dry savanna of north-eastern Botswana, within the Kalahari Desert. Covering roughly 16,000 square kilometres, it is one of the largest salt flats in the world and is the remnant of the ancient Lake Makgadikgadi, which once covered much of what is now Botswana.

Geography

The complex includes the Ntwetwe Pan, Sua Pan and Nxai Pan. In the dry season the pans are blinding white, cracked salt flats; after good rains they flood shallowly, turning into nutrient-rich lakes that attract large numbers of waterbirds — notably flamingos — and herds of zebra and wildebeest in one of southern Africa's major migrations.

Flora and fauna

Surrounding grasslands support springbok, gemsbok, kudu and predators such as lion, cheetah and brown hyena. Isolated "islands" such as Kubu Island, a rocky granite outcrop with ancient baobab trees, are sacred sites and archaeological locations; stone tools and other evidence show the pans have been visited by humans for thousands of years.

Conservation

Parts of the complex fall within Makgadikgadi Pans National Park and Nxai Pan National Park. The Sua Pan is also used for soda ash extraction at Sua Pan town.
