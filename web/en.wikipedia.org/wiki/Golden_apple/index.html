Golden apple

The golden apple is an element that appears in various national and ethnic folk legends and fairy tales. Recurring themes depict a hero (such as Hercules or Făt-Frumos) retrieving a golden apple, often from a treacherous garden guarded by immortals or monsters, or a villain (such as the witch in Snow White) offering a golden apple as an enchanted or cursed gift.

Greek mythology

The Apple of Discord

The most famous golden apple is the Apple of Discord, thrown by Eris, goddess of discord, at the wedding of Peleus and Thetis after she was excluded from the feast. Inscribed "for the fairest", it was claimed by Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite. Zeus ordered the Trojan prince Paris to judge; he awarded the apple to Aphrodite, who had promised him Helen of Sparta — setting in motion the Trojan War.

The Hesperides

Hercules, as his eleventh Labour, was sent to steal the golden apples from the garden of the Hesperides, guarded by the dragon Ladon. The apples had been a wedding gift from Gaia to Hera.

Atalanta

The huntress Atalanta was defeated in a footrace when Hippomenes (or Melanion) dropped three golden apples given by Aphrodite; she stopped to pick them up and lost the race, and her hand in marriage.

Norse mythology

In Norse myth, the goddess Iðunn keeps the golden apples of youth in a box; the gods eat them to remain young and immortal until Ragnarök.
