Espresso martini

An espresso martini served in a cocktail glass, traditionally garnished with three coffee beans.

The espresso martini, also known as a vodka espresso, is a cold caffeinated alcoholic drink made with espresso, coffee liqueur, and vodka. It is not a true martini as it contains neither gin nor vermouth, but is one of many drinks that incorporate the term martini into their names. It is an official cocktail of the International Bartenders Association (IBA), classified in the "new era drinks" category.

History

The drink is widely credited to the British bartender Dick Bradsell, who is said to have created it in 1983 at the Soho Brasserie in London. According to the well-known anecdote, Bradsell invented the drink when a young model—later a celebrity—asked for a cocktail that would "wake me up, and then fuck me up". Bradsell, whose coffee machine stood next to his bar station, mixed vodka with fresh espresso, coffee liqueur, and sugar. He originally called it the vodka espresso; it was later also known as the "pharmaceutical stimulant" when he worked at the Pharmacy Bar in Notting Hill.

Preparation

The espresso martini is made with vodka, coffee liqueur (such as Kahlúa), a shot of freshly brewed espresso, and optionally simple syrup. The ingredients are shaken vigorously with ice—hard shaking creates the drink's characteristic creamy froth—and strained into a chilled cocktail glass. It is traditionally garnished with three coffee beans, said to represent health, wealth, and happiness, a convention borrowed from the serving of sambuca.

Popularity

After a period of decline in the 2000s, the espresso martini experienced a major revival in the late 2010s and 2020s, becoming one of the most-ordered cocktails in bars in the United States, United Kingdom, and Australia. Variants substitute other base spirits, such as tequila or rum, or add flavourings such as salted caramel.
