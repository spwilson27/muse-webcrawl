Carotid endarterectomy

Carotid endarterectomy (CEA) is a surgical procedure used to reduce the risk of stroke from carotid artery stenosis, in which fatty deposits (atherosclerotic plaque) narrow the carotid arteries in the neck. The surgeon opens the artery and removes the plaque from its inner lining.

Indications

Large trials such as NASCET and ECST showed that endarterectomy substantially reduces stroke risk in patients with severe symptomatic stenosis (70–99%), with more modest benefit for moderate stenosis and for asymptomatic patients. Decisions balance the degree of narrowing and symptoms against the operative risk of stroke or death, which should be below about 6% for symptomatic patients.

Procedure

The operation is performed under general or local anaesthesia through an incision along the sternocleidomastoid muscle. The artery is clamped — sometimes with a temporary shunt to maintain brain perfusion — the plaque is dissected out, and the artery is closed, often with a patch of vein or synthetic material. The alternative intervention is carotid artery stenting, which trials have shown carries a slightly higher periprocedural stroke risk but a lower risk of myocardial infarction in some groups.
