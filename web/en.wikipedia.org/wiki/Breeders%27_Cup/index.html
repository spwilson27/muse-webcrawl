The Breeders' Cup World Championships is an annual series of Grade I Thoroughbred horse races, operated by Breeders' Cup Limited, formed in 1982. First run in 1984 at Hollywood Park, the event was created by Kentucky horseman John R. Gaines as a year-end championship to showcase North American racing and reward breeders.

Races

The programme has expanded from seven races on a single day to fourteen races over two days. The richest and most prestigious is the Breeders' Cup Classic, run over 1¼ miles on dirt for three-year-olds and up, whose past winners include American Pharoah, Zenyatta and Flightline. Other championship races include the Turf, Distaff, Mile, Sprint and Juvenile.

Qualifying

Horses qualify through the "Win and You're In" Breeders' Cup Challenge series, held internationally, or by points and panel selection. Fields are limited to 14 starters (12 in some races), with preference given to Challenge winners and graded-stakes performers.
