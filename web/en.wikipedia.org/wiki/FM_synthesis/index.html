FM synthesis

Developed byJohn Chowning (Stanford University)
Commercialised byYamaha
Known forYamaha DX7 (1983)

Frequency modulation synthesis (or FM synthesis) is a form of sound synthesis in which the frequency of a waveform, the carrier, is varied by another waveform, the modulator, at audio rates. Developed by John Chowning at Stanford University in the late 1960s and licensed to Yamaha, it became one of the defining technologies of 1980s electronic music.

Principle

Modulating a carrier oscillator's frequency with a second oscillator generates rich sidebands around the carrier frequency, producing complex, evolving spectra — bright bells, metallic tones, electric pianos and basses — from very simple building blocks called operators. The harmonic content depends on the frequency ratio and modulation index of the operator pair.

Instruments

Yamaha's DX7 (1983) was the first commercially successful digital synthesiser and one of the best-selling synthesisers of all time; its sounds saturated 1980s popular music. FM also powered the GS-1, the Synclavier's FM voices and the sound chips of Sega game consoles and early PC sound cards.
