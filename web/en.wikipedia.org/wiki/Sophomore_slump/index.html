Sophomore slump

A sophomore slump (also called a sophomore jinx or sophomore syndrome) is a decline in performance or quality that occurs in the second year of an endeavour, or in a second effort following a successful debut. The term is used across education, sports, music, film, literature and business.

In education

In North American schools and universities, the sophomore slump describes the dip in motivation, grades and engagement commonly experienced by students in their second year. The novelty of the first year has worn off, academic demands typically increase, and students face pressure to declare a major or choose a career path, while support structures aimed at first-year students are withdrawn. Institutions have responded with sophomore-year experience programmes, advising initiatives and residential communities aimed at second-year retention.

In sports

In professional sports, a player who excels as a rookie may struggle in their second season. Explanations offered include opponents studying and adjusting to the player's tendencies, increased expectations and media attention, and simple regression to the mean after an unusually strong first year. Awards such as Rookie of the Year are often said to be followed by the sophomore jinx.

In entertainment

The phenomenon is frequently invoked for musicians whose second album is judged weaker than a celebrated debut, for film sequels, and for second novels by breakout authors. Causes suggested include years of accumulated material behind a debut versus months to produce a follow-up, changed personnel or producers, and the burden of heightened expectations.

Related concepts

Related ideas include the "second season syndrome" in sport, regression to the mean in statistics, and the difficulty of sustaining an initial level of success sometimes described as "second album syndrome" in the music press.
