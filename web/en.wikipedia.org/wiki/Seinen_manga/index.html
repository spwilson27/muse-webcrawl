Seinen manga (青年漫画) are manga marketed toward young adult men. In Japanese, the word seinen literally means "youth", but the term "seinen manga" is also used to describe the target audience of manga magazines aimed at male readers aged roughly 18 to 40.

Characteristics

Because the category is defined by marketing demographic rather than genre, seinen manga spans an enormous range of subjects — action, science fiction, historical drama, business, sports, horror, erotica, slice-of-life and literary fiction — often with more mature themes, psychological depth and graphic content than shōnen titles aimed at boys. Chapters commonly use kanji with furigana sparingly, assuming an adult reading level.

Notable magazines and works

Major seinen magazines include Shueisha's Weekly Young Jump and Ultra Jump, Shogakukan's Big Comic Spirits and Big Comic Original, and Kodansha's Morning and Afternoon. Landmark seinen series include Berserk by Kentaro Miura, Ghost in the Shell by Masamune Shirow, Akira by Katsuhiro Otomo, Monster by Naoki Urasawa, Vagabond by Takehiko Inoue, Vinland Saga by Makoto Yukimura and One Punch Man (as a webcomic/Young Jump remake).

History

Seinen grew out of the gekiga movement of the late 1950s and 1960s, when artists such as Yoshihiro Tatsumi sought a more dramatic, adult-oriented graphic storytelling; dedicated seinen magazines proliferated from the late 1960s (Big Comic, 1968) and the 1970s–80s boom created the modern market divisions of Japanese comics publishing.
