Nullity

Nullity is a term with technical meanings in law and mathematics.

In mathematics

In linear algebra, the nullity of a matrix or linear map is the dimension of its null space (kernel) — the set of vectors that the map sends to the zero vector. For a matrix representing a linear transformation, the rank–nullity theorem states that the rank of the matrix plus its nullity equals the number of columns (the dimension of the domain). Thus a large nullity indicates a transformation that collapses many directions to zero; a square matrix is invertible exactly when its nullity is zero.

In law

In law, nullity refers to the state of being legally void or invalid from the outset. A marriage may be annulled by a decree of nullity, which declares that no valid marriage ever existed — as distinct from a divorce, which ends a valid marriage. Grounds include prohibited degrees of relationship, bigamy, or lack of valid consent. Similarly, a contract, statute, or administrative act affected by nullity is treated as though it never had legal effect. In Catholic canon law, marriage tribunals issue declarations of nullity (annulments) where an essential element of marriage was absent at the time of the wedding.

Other uses

The term is occasionally used more loosely in other fields to mean nothingness or the absence of any legally or practically significant effect.
