Paris Foreign Missions Society

Paris Foreign Missions Society
French: Société des Missions étrangères de Paris (MEP)
Founded: 1658–1663, Paris, France
Founders: François Pallu, Pierre Lambert de la Motte
Type: Society of apostolic life (Catholic)
Headquarters: 128 Rue du Bac, Paris
Field: Missionary work in Asia

The Paris Foreign Missions Society (French: Société des Missions étrangères de Paris, abbreviated MEP) is a Catholic society of apostolic life of diocesan priests founded in Paris in the 17th century for missionary work in Asia. Unlike a religious order, its members do not take religious vows but commit themselves to lifelong missionary service, traditionally departing for their assigned mission territory for life.

History

The society grew out of the efforts of the Jesuit Alexandre de Rhodes, whose reports from Vietnam persuaded Pope Alexander VII to appoint the first French vicars apostolic for Asia: François Pallu (for Tonkin) and Pierre Lambert de la Motte (for Cochinchina), both consecrated in 1658–1660. A seminary for the foreign missions was established in Paris in 1663 on the Rue du Bac, which remains the society's headquarters. The foundation represented a deliberate Roman policy of building indigenous clergy in Asia under bishops responsible directly to the Holy See, rather than under the Portuguese padroado system.

MEP missionaries went on to work across Siam (Thailand), Vietnam, China, Korea, Japan, Cambodia, India, and elsewhere in Asia. Many died as martyrs, particularly during persecutions in Vietnam and Korea in the 19th century; a number have been canonised, including some of the Korean Martyrs and Vietnamese Martyrs. In 1805 the seminary was restored after suppression during the French Revolution, and the 19th century saw a great expansion of French missions in Asia.

The society today

The society today numbers a few hundred members, increasingly joined by Asian priests and lay volunteers. Its Paris headquarters houses a museum, archives, and the Martyrs' Chapel (Salle des Martyrs) displaying relics and artefacts of missionaries killed in Asia. The society also runs a volunteer programme sending young people to serve in Asian missions.
