Common nameRaptors

Birds of prey or predatory birds, also known as raptors, are hypercarnivorous bird species that actively hunt and feed on other vertebrates (mainly mammals, reptiles and smaller birds). In addition to speed and strength, these predators have keen eyesight for detecting prey from a distance or during flight, strong feet with sharp talons for grasping or killing prey, and powerful, curved beaks for tearing off flesh.

Classification

The term "raptor" is derived from the Latin word rapio, meaning "to seize or take by force." Birds of prey include species from several orders: the Accipitriformes (eagles, hawks, buzzards, kites, harriers, Old World vultures and the osprey), the Falconiformes (falcons and caracaras), the Cariamiformes (seriemas), and the owls of the order Strigiformes, which are usually treated separately as nocturnal birds of prey. The New World vultures are placed in the order Cathartiformes by some classifications. Although the term is not a natural (monophyletic) grouping in modern taxonomy, the raptors were defined by their shared hunting specialisations.

Characteristics

Raptors possess adaptations for a predatory lifestyle: forward-facing eyes giving binocular vision and exceptional visual acuity (an eagle's eyesight may be several times sharper than a human's), hooked beaks adapted for tearing flesh, and feet equipped with sharp talons. Many species show reversed sexual dimorphism, with females considerably larger than males. Most hunt by sight, although owls rely heavily on hearing, aided by facial discs and asymmetrical ears.

Migration and conservation

Many birds of prey are migratory, concentrating spectacularly at bottlenecks such as Panama, Gibraltar and the Bosphorus, where "raptor counts" are conducted. Raptors are apex predators and have been widely used as indicator species; several suffered severe declines in the 20th century from DDT-induced eggshell thinning, most famously the peregrine falcon and bald eagle, both of which have since recovered following pesticide bans and captive-breeding programmes. Raptors have long been trained for falconry.
