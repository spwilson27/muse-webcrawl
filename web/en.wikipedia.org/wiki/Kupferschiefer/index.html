Kupferschiefer
TypePermian marine black shale / marl
AgeLopingian, c. 258 Ma
DistributionGermany, Poland, Netherlands, England

The Kupferschiefer ("copper shale") is a thin but remarkably widespread Late Permian sedimentary unit of northern Europe, famous both as a copper-silver ore and as a fossil Lagerstätte.

Geology and mining

Typically under a metre thick, the bituminous shale was deposited in the Zechstein Sea. Metals scavenged from underlying Rotliegend sediments enriched it in copper, lead, zinc and silver. Mined since the Middle Ages around Mansfeld and Sangerhausen in Germany and at Lubin in Poland — where it underlies one of the world's largest copper-silver districts — it demands difficult underground mining because of its thinness.

Fossils

Anoxia preserved exquisite fish (such as Palaeoniscum), early reptiles including Protorosaurus, and plant remains, making the Kupferschiefer a classic window on Permian life just before the end-Permian extinction.
