Artashat is a town and urban community in the Ararat Province of Armenia, serving as the provincial capital. It lies on the Aras River plain, about 30 km southeast of Yerevan, near the site of the ancient Armenian capital of the same name.

Ancient capital

The ancient city of Artashat (Artaxata) was founded around 176 BC by King Artaxias I, reportedly on the advice of the exiled Carthaginian general Hannibal. It became the capital of the Kingdom of Armenia and a major Hellenistic commercial and cultural centre, sometimes called the "Armenian Carthage". Excavations on the hills near the modern town have revealed fortifications, public buildings and inscriptions.

Modern town

The modern town was established in 1945 by merging nearby villages. Today it is an agricultural and food-processing centre in the fertile Ararat plain, known for fruit, vegetables and wine production. The Khor Virap monastery, a major pilgrimage site with views of Mount Ararat, lies nearby.
