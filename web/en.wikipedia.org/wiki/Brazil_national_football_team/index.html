The Brazil national football team (Portuguese: Seleção Brasileira de Futebol), nicknamed Seleção Canarinho, represents Brazil in men's international football and is administered by the Brazilian Football Confederation (CBF). Brazil is the most successful national team in the history of the FIFA World Cup, having won the tournament five times, and is the only team to have played in every edition. Its yellow shirt with green trim and blue shorts is one of the most recognisable kits in world sport.

History

Brazil played its first international in 1914 and won its first major honours at the South American Championship (now Copa América), which it has won nine times. After the trauma of the Maracanazo — the deciding match of the 1950 World Cup lost at home to Uruguay — a golden era began under players such as Pelé, Garrincha and Didi: Brazil won the World Cup in Sweden in 1958, retained it in Chile in 1962, and won again in Mexico in 1970, a side widely regarded as the greatest of all time. The team was then allowed to keep the Jules Rimet Trophy permanently.

A 24-year wait ended in the United States in 1994, when captain Dunga's team beat Italy on penalties in the final; Brazil lost the 1998 final to France but won a fifth title in 2002, led by Ronaldo, Rivaldo and Ronaldinho. Hosting the tournament again in 2014 ended in a 7–1 semi-final defeat by Germany — the Mineirazo — and Brazil have not reached a World Cup final since 2002, though they won the Copa América in 2019 and Olympic gold in 2016 and 2020.

Style and culture

Brazilian football is culturally associated with flair, improvisation and attacking play — the jogo bonito ("beautiful game") — rooted in street and beach football and celebrated worldwide. Legendary players include Pelé, Garrincha, Zico, Romário, Ronaldo, Ronaldinho and Neymar, who surpassed Pelé as the team's record scorer in 2023. Brazil has spent more time ranked first in the FIFA World Rankings than any other team.
