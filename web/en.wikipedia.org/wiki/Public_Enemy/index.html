Public Enemy is an American hip hop group formed in Roosevelt, Long Island, New York, in 1985 by Chuck D and Flavor Flav. The group is known for politically charged lyrics addressing racism, media bias and the condition of African Americans, delivered over the dense, dissonant sample collages of production team the Bomb Squad. Their albums It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back (1988) and Fear of a Black Planet (1990) are widely ranked among the greatest recordings in hip hop and popular music generally, and the group was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2013.

History

Chuck D (Carlton Ridenhour), a graphic-design student at Adelphi University and host on college station WBAU, developed his booming MC style and social commentary in the mid-1980s; Flavor Flav (William Drayton) served as his comic foil, hype man and the group's mascot — famous for wearing a large clock around his neck, symbolising the value of time. Recruited by producer Rick Rubin to Def Jam Recordings, the group debuted with Yo! Bum Rush the Show (1987). It Takes a Nation of Millions (1988), built from frantic layers of James Brown, funk and noise samples, redefined rap production and included "Don't Believe the Hype" and "Bring the Noise". "Fight the Power" (1989), commissioned for Spike Lee's film Do the Right Thing, became their anthem.

Controversy and later career

In 1989 the group was engulfed by controversy over antisemitic remarks by its "Minister of Information", Professor Griff, whom Chuck D eventually dismissed; Griff's departure and the media storm marked the end of the group's commercial peak, though Fear of a Black Planet (1990) and Apocalypse 91… The Enemy Strikes Black (1991) were major successes. A crossover thrash-metal remake of "Bring the Noise" with Anthrax (1991) influenced nu metal. Public Enemy later pioneered online music distribution, releasing albums by MP3 in the late 1990s, and continued recording and touring for decades; Flavor Flav unexpectedly became a reality-TV star in the 2000s, and he and Chuck D publicly disputed his status in the group in 2020 before reconciling. In 2020 the group released What You Gonna Do When the Grid Goes Down? on a returned Def Jam.

Legacy

Public Enemy fused Black nationalist politics, media critique and avant-garde production into a template for conscious hip hop, and their crosshair logo became an enduring icon. It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back was selected for the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry in 2005.
