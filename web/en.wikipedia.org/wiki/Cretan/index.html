Cretan

Cretan most commonly refers to something of, from, or related to the island of Crete, the largest and most populous of the Greek islands, or to a person native to the island. The term may also refer to several more specific topics:

People and culture

  • The Cretans, the inhabitants of Crete, known in antiquity and mentioned in the New Testament (Titus 1:12, the "Cretan paradox" associated with Epimenides).
  • The Cretan dialect of Greek, still spoken on the island and in the Cretan diaspora.
  • Cretan music, centred on the lyra and laouto, and the island's mantinada verse tradition.
  • Cretan cuisine, noted for olive oil, wild greens, dakos, and cheeses such as graviera and mizithra.

History

Crete was the centre of the Minoan civilisation, Europe's earliest advanced civilisation, whose palaces at Knossos, Phaistos and Malia flourished in the Bronze Age. The island later passed through Mycenaean, Classical Greek, Roman, Byzantine, Arab, Venetian and Ottoman periods before uniting with Greece in 1913. Cretan resistance during the Battle of Crete in 1941 is a celebrated episode of the Second World War.

Other uses

  • Cretan hieroglyphs, an undeciphered Bronze Age script of the Minoans.
  • Cretan wild goat (kri-kri), an endemic subspecies of wild goat found in the White Mountains and on offshore islets.
  • The Cretan Sea, the part of the Aegean Sea south of the Cyclades and north of Crete.
  • "Cretan" in philosophy, in the liar paradox as phrased by Epimenides of Knossos.
