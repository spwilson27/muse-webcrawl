In thermodynamics, heat is the thermal energy transferred between systems because of a temperature difference — energy in transit, distinct from a body's internal energy or temperature. It flows by conduction, convection and radiation.

Theory

The caloric theory of heat as a fluid yielded in the 1840s–50s to Joule's mechanical equivalent of heat and the kinetic picture: heat is molecular motion. Heat is the input of every heat engine, the quantity bounded by Carnot's limit, and its irreversibility is expressed by entropy — the thread connecting this crawl's physics pages from Galileo onward.
