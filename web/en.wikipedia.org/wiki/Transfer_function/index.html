Transfer function

A transfer function (also known as a system function or network function) in engineering is a mathematical function that, in theory, models the output of a system for each possible input. For a linear time-invariant system it is the ratio of the Laplace transform of the output to the Laplace transform of the input, assuming zero initial conditions: H(s) = Y(s)/X(s).

Properties

The transfer function is usually a rational polynomial in s. Its denominator roots are the system's poles, which determine stability and natural modes; its numerator roots are zeros. Setting s = jω gives the frequency response, plotted as Bode magnitude and phase diagrams. The inverse Laplace transform of the transfer function is the impulse response.

Applications

Transfer functions are the central tool of classical control theory — used for PID design, root-locus and Nyquist stability analysis — and of filter design in signal processing and electronics. In practice they can be measured directly by exciting a system with known signals, an approach used in acoustics, structural dynamics and audio engineering (where head-related transfer functions describe how ears filter sound).
