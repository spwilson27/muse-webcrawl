The cuisine of Nice (cuisine niçoise) is the traditional cooking of the city of Nice and its surrounding county on the French Riviera. Shaped by the Mediterranean Sea, the olive groves of the Provençal hinterland, and centuries of Italian (Ligurian and Piedmontese) influence from the period when Nice belonged to the House of Savoy, it relies on olive oil rather than butter, abundant vegetables, herbs, anchovies, and local olives.

Signature dishes

  • Salade niçoise — tomatoes, hard-boiled eggs, olives, anchovies (or tuna in modern versions), and olive oil; purists reject cooked vegetables such as potatoes and green beans.
  • Socca — a thin chickpea-flour pancake baked in a wood-fired oven and eaten hot with black pepper, sold in the old town and markets.
  • Pissaladière — a flatbread topped with slowly caramelised onions, anchovies, and olives.
  • Pan bagnat — a round bread roll filled with salade niçoise ingredients and pressed, the classic beach sandwich.
  • Ratatouille — a stew of aubergines, courgettes, peppers, tomatoes, and onions, of Niçoise origin.
  • Daube niçoise, farcis (stuffed vegetables), tourte de blettes (sweet Swiss-chard tart), and beignets of courgette flowers.

Protection and promotion

Since the 1990s an official Cuisine Nissarde label, promoted by the city's tourist office with chefs and historians, certifies restaurants serving authentic recipes; former mayor Jacques Médecin codified the tradition in his influential cookbook La Cuisine du Comté de Nice (1972). Local products include the tiny black cailletier olives and olive oil of Nice (AOC), and wines of the Bellet appellation on the hills above the city.
