Édouard Manet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe (1863): a naked woman seated on grass in a glade beside two fashionably dressed men, another woman bathing beyond]

Édouard Manet (23 January 1832 – 30 April 1883) was a French modernist painter. He was one of the first 19th-century artists to paint modern life, as well as a pivotal figure in the transition from Realism to Impressionism. His early masterworks, Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe and Olympia (both 1863), provoked enormous controversy at the Salon des Refusés and on their exhibition, and remain foundational to modern art.

Manet's flat lighting, black-heavy palette, direct gazes and borrowings from Titian and Goya scandalised academic taste while electrifying younger painters — Monet, Renoir and Cézanne all passed through his orbit. Late works such as A Bar at the Folies-Bergère (1882) combine worldly modernity with deep pictorial ambiguity.
