A razor is a bladed tool primarily used in the removal of body hair through the act of shaving. Kinds of razors include straight razors, safety razors, disposable razors, and electric razors. Shaving implements date back to prehistoric times; razor-like instruments of flint, bronze, and gold have been found in ancient Egyptian and Bronze Age contexts.

Razors, from the traditional straight razor to modern cartridge designs.

History

Razors were used in ancient Egypt and India, and shaving became widespread in the ancient world; Alexander the Great reportedly ordered his soldiers to shave so enemies could not grab their beards in battle. The straight razor, with a blade that folds into its handle, became the standard shaving instrument in the 18th and 19th centuries.

In 1901, King Camp Gillette patented the safety razor with disposable double-edged blades, massively popularised during the First World War when the U.S. military issued them to soldiers. Cartridge razors followed in the 1960s and 1970s, with manufacturers adding multiple blades, pivoting heads, and lubricating strips. Disposable razors and electric shavers round out the modern market.

Types

  • Straight razor — a blade that folds into the handle; requires stropping and skill, long used by barbers.
  • Safety razor — a protective guard reduces cuts; uses replaceable double-edged or single-edged blades.
  • Cartridge razor — detachable heads holding one to several blades, often with a pivoting action.
  • Disposable razor — inexpensive cartridge-style razor discarded whole after use.
  • Electric razor — motor-driven foil or rotary cutters that shear hair without exposed blades.
