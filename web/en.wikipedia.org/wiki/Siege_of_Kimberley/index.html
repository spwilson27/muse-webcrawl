Siege of Kimberley

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Kimberley (14 October 1899 – 15 February 1900) took place during the Second Boer War, when Boer forces from the Orange Free State and the Transvaal invested the diamond-mining town of Kimberley in the Cape Colony for 124 days.

The siege

The town's defences were organised by Lieutenant-Colonel Robert Kekewich, while Cecil Rhodes — founder of De Beers, whose mines dominated the town — pressed for relief and clashed repeatedly with the military command. Some 40,000 people, including thousands of black mine workers confined in compounds, endured shelling, food rationing and disease; the workshops of De Beers built a large home-made gun, "Long Cecil," to answer the Boer artillery. Boer shelling from the surrounding kopjes killed and wounded civilians, and scurvy and enteric fever afflicted the population.

Relief

Kimberley was relieved on 15 February 1900 by a cavalry dash under Major-General John French, part of Lord Roberts's advance toward Bloemfontein, launched after the costly frontal attacks of the "Black Week" period and the fighting along the Modder River. The relief of Kimberley, followed weeks later by that of Ladysmith and (in May) Mafeking, was celebrated across the British Empire.
