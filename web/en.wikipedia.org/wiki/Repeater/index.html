Repeater

TypeElectronic communications device
FunctionReceives and retransmits signals to extend range
OSI layerPhysical layer (Layer 1)

In telecommunications, a repeater is an electronic device that receives a signal and retransmits it. Repeaters are used to extend transmissions so that the signal can cover longer distances or be received on the other side of an obstruction. Some types of repeaters broadcast an identical signal, but alter its method of transmission, for example on another frequency or baud rate.

Types

Digipeaters and bent-pipe transponders serve radio links; optical repeaters amplify light signals in fibre-optic cables, historically by converting light to electricity and back, and in modern systems with erbium-doped fibre amplifiers. In computer networking, an Ethernet repeater (hub) regenerates signals between segments but does not separate collision domains.

History

The concept descends from relay stations in optical and electrical telegraphy, where signals were re-transmitted along a line. Repeaters made transcontinental telephony and undersea cables practical, and cellular and Wi-Fi range extenders apply the same principle today.
