Ludmila of Bohemia (c. 860 – 15 September 921) was a Czech noblewoman and Christian martyr, the wife of Bořivoj I, the first Christian duke of Bohemia, and the grandmother and regent-educator of Saint Wenceslaus. She is venerated as the patron saint of Bohemia and regarded as a founding figure of Czech Christianity.

Life

Ludmila married Bořivoj, founder of the Přemyslid dynasty, and was baptised with him, according to tradition by Saint Methodius himself. Widowed young, she saw her sons Spytihněv and Vratislaus succeed in turn. On Vratislaus's death in 921, the nobles entrusted the regency and the upbringing of the young prince Wenceslaus largely to Ludmila rather than to his mother Drahomíra, provoking a bitter rivalry.

Martyrdom and cult

Drahomíra, resentful of Ludmila's influence and of her Christian policies, sent assassins to Ludmila's castle at Tetín, where the elderly duchess was strangled — legend says with her own veil — on 15 September 921. Miracles were reported at her tomb; Wenceslaus translated her remains to St. George's Basilica at Prague Castle in 925, and her cult spread rapidly. She became, with Wenceslaus, a national patron of the Czech lands. Antonín Dvořák's oratorio Saint Ludmila (1886) was composed for the Leeds Festival.
