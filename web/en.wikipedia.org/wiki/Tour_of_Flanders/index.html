The Tour of Flanders (Dutch: Ronde van Vlaanderen), also known as De Ronde ("The Tour"), is an annual road cycling race held in Belgium every spring. It is one of the five Monuments of professional cycling and one of the two great cobbled classics, together with Paris–Roubaix.

History

The race was first held in 1913, organised by Karel Van Wijnendaele for the sports newspaper Sportwereld. It was interrupted by the First World War and again held annually from 1919. The race became a symbol of Flemish identity and one of the most popular sporting events in Belgium.

Course

The modern route runs through the Flemish Ardennes and is famous for its short, steep, often cobbled hills known as hellingen or bergs, including the Koppenberg, the Oude Kwaremont and the Paterberg. Since 2017 the race has started in Antwerp and finished in Oudenaarde.

Winners

No rider has won the race more than three times. Record holders with three victories include Achiel Buysse, Fiorenzo Magni, Eric Leman, Johan Museeuw, Tom Boonen, Fabian Cancellara and Mathieu van der Poel. A women's race has been held since 2004.
