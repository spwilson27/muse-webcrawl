Sexagenary cycle

Sexagenary cycle
Chinese干支 (gānzhī)
Length60 units (years, days, etc.)

The sexagenary cycle, also known as the Stems-and-Branches or ganzhi, is a cycle of sixty terms used for recording days and years in traditional Chinese and other East Asian calendars, and in Chinese astrology. Each term combines one of the ten Heavenly Stems with one of the twelve Earthly Branches; because 10 and 12 share a factor of two, only sixty of the 120 conceivable pairings occur.

Structure

The cycle begins with jiǎzǐ (甲子), the yang Wood Rat year, and proceeds through combinations such as yǐchǒu and bǐngyín until it closes with guǐhài. The branches correspond to the twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac, and each stem-branch pair also carries a five-element (Wu Xing) attribution from its stem, so each full cycle contains each animal-element pairing once.

History and use

Stem-branch characters are attested on Shang dynasty oracle bones, where they recorded days. The cycle was applied to years by the Han dynasty and became standard in the Chinese calendar; it spread to Japan (as eto), Korea, and Vietnam. Years are still commonly named by the cycle in almanacs, and the day-cycle — unbroken for millennia — remains a cornerstone of Chinese astrology and feng shui.
