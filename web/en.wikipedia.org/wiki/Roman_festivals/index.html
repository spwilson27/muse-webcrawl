Roman festivals

Roman festivals were the religious celebrations of ancient Rome, filling much of the Roman calendar. They were of two broad kinds: feriae publicae, fixed or movable public holidays on which business and lawsuits ceased, and private or clan festivals. The earliest calendar, attributed to Numa Pompilius, already contained dozens of festivals tied to the agricultural year and the gods of the Roman state religion.

Major festivals

Among the best known are the Saturnalia (17–23 December), a feast of gift-giving and role reversal in honour of Saturn; the Lupercalia (15 February), a fertility rite in which priests ran through the city striking bystanders with goatskin thongs; the Lemuria, for appeasing ghosts; the Parentalia, honouring ancestors; the Floralia, with games and licentious celebration; the Cerealia for Ceres; and the great games (ludi) such as the Ludi Romani for Jupiter.

Development

Under the empire the calendar swelled with imperial birthdays, accession days and new games until holidays occupied well over 150 days of the year. With Christianization, many festivals were suppressed, absorbed or transformed, though their dates and customs left traces in later European folk practice.
