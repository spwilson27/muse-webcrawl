Sermon on the Mount

Jesus delivering the Sermon on the Mount, as depicted by Carl Bloch.

The Sermon on the Mount (anglicised from the Matthean Vulgate Latin section title: Sermo in monte) is a collection of sayings spoken by Jesus of Nazareth found in the Gospel of Matthew, chapters 5, 6, and 7. The sermon is the longest continuous discourse of Jesus found in the New Testament and has been one of the most widely quoted elements of the Canonical Gospels.

Contents

The sermon opens with the Beatitudes ("Blessed are the poor in spirit..."), continues with the metaphors of salt and light, and a series of teachings contrasting Jesus's interpretation of the Law with that of the scribes and Pharisees, including commandments on anger, adultery, oaths, retaliation ("turn the other cheek") and love of enemies. It includes the Lord's Prayer, teachings on prayer, fasting and almsgiving, the Golden Rule, the narrow gate, and the parable of the wise and foolish builders.

Interpretation and influence

The sermon's demanding ethics — non-resistance, unconditional forgiveness, and love of enemies — have shaped Christian moral teaching and inspired figures such as Leo Tolstoy, Mahatma Gandhi, and Martin Luther King Jr. Scholars debate whether its precepts are an achievable ethic, an "interim ethic" for an expected end-time, or an ideal that exposes human sinfulness.
