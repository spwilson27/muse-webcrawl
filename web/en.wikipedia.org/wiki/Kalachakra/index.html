Kālacakra

Kālacakra (Sanskrit: कालचक्र, "Wheel of Time") is a complex and esoteric tradition of Tibetan Buddhism, and the name of the tantric deity and body of literature associated with it. It is considered one of the highest classes of tantra (Anuttarayoga) and is distinguished by its integration of cosmology, astrology, medicine and subtle-body yoga into a single system.

Structure

The Kālacakra system is organised around three "wheels": the outer wheel (the cosmos, with elaborate calendrical and astronomical theory adapted in part from Indian and Central Asian sources), the inner wheel (the human body with its channels, winds and drops), and the "other" or alternative wheel (the path of initiation, generation-stage and completion-stage practice that leads to awakening). The root text, the Kālacakra Tantra, is traditionally said to have been taught by the Buddha in Andhra, India, to King Sucandra of Shambhala.

Prophecy and Shambhala

The tradition carries a famous prophecy of a future war in which the forces of Shambhala defeat barbarian invaders, followed by a golden age — a narrative that has inspired both devotion and speculation for centuries.

Public initiations

Unusually for an esoteric practice, the Kālacakra initiation has been given publicly to very large gatherings by the 14th Dalai Lama, who has conferred it dozens of times since 1954 in India, the West and elsewhere, presenting it as a blessing for world peace.
