Bacteroides fragilis

Bacteroides fragilis
DomainBacteria
PhylumBacteroidota
GenusBacteroides
TypeGram-negative obligate anaerobe

Bacteroides fragilis is a Gram-negative, rod-shaped, obligate anaerobic bacterium that is a normal and important member of the human gut microbiota. Although it makes up only about 1% of the faecal flora, it is the anaerobe most frequently isolated from clinical infections outside the gut.

Role in health

In the colon, B. fragilis helps digest complex polysaccharides and produces short-chain fatty acids. Its capsular polysaccharide A has been shown in animal studies to modulate the immune system and promote regulatory T-cell development, protecting against experimental colitis.

Pathogenicity

When the intestinal wall is breached — by surgery, trauma or perforation — B. fragilis can cause intra-abdominal infections, abscesses and bacteraemia. Its polysaccharide capsule promotes abscess formation. Enterotoxigenic strains (ETBF), which secrete the fragilysin metalloprotease toxin, are associated with inflammatory diarrhoea and have been linked epidemiologically to colorectal cancer. Treatment relies on agents such as metronidazole, though resistance is increasing.
