King of Germany

King of Germany (Latin: Rex Teutonicorum, German: König der Deutschen) was a title held by the elected monarch of the German kingdom within the Holy Roman Empire, from the 10th century until the Empire's dissolution in 1806.

Background

The German kingship developed from the East Frankish kingdom. Kings were chosen by the leading nobles and later by a defined college of prince-electors formalised by the Golden Bull of 1356. A king who was crowned by the Pope in Rome received the additional title of Holy Roman Emperor; between election and coronation he was styled King of the Romans. The title fell into disuse as the Empire fragmented, and the last emperor, Francis II, dissolved the Empire in 1806. A German national kingship was not revived in the unified German Empire of 1871, whose monarch was styled German Emperor.
