Rioplatense Spanish

Rioplatense Spanish (Spanish: español rioplatense) is a variety of Spanish spoken mainly in and around the Río de la Plata basin in Argentina and Uruguay. It is the most prominent dialect to employ voseo — the use of the pronoun vos instead of tú for the informal second person singular — in both speech and writing. Rioplatense is also distinguished by its intonation patterns, which were influenced by Italian immigration to the region in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and by the slang known as lunfardo.

Geographic distribution

The dialect is centered on Buenos Aires and Montevideo and extends across the Argentine provinces of Buenos Aires, Santa Fe, Entre Ríos, Córdoba (partially) and Patagonia, and throughout Uruguay. Its prestige form, based on the speech of Buenos Aires, dominates Argentine and Uruguayan media, education and broadcasting.

Phonology

Characteristic features include the pronunciation of the letters ll and y as a voiced postalveolar fricative or affricate [ʒ] or [dʒ] (a phenomenon called žeísmo), increasingly devoiced to [ʃ] among younger speakers (šeísmo). Like most American Spanish varieties, Rioplatense has seseo: the phoneme /θ/ does not exist, and c before e/i and z are pronounced [s]. Aspiration or elision of syllable-final /s/ is common, especially in coastal areas.

Voseo

In Rioplatense Spanish, vos is the standard informal second-person singular pronoun, with its own verb forms: vos hablás (you speak), vos tenés (you have), vení (come). The pronoun tú and its forms are essentially absent from everyday speech, though usted is retained for formal address. Uruguay shows some mixture, with tú combined with voseo verb forms in some regions.

Lunfardo

Lunfardo is a slang vocabulary that originated among the working classes and immigrant communities of Buenos Aires, drawing heavily on Italian dialects (especially Genoese, Neapolitan and Piedmontese), as well as French, Portuguese, Galician, English and indigenous languages. Words such as laburo (work), morfar (to eat), mina (woman) and pibe (kid) have entered general Rioplatense usage, and lunfardo is strongly associated with tango lyrics.
