Helepolis

Helepolis (Greek: ἑλέπολις, "Taker of Cities") was an ancient siege engine, a huge armoured assault tower, built by the engineer Epimachus of Athens for Demetrius I of Macedon during his unsuccessful siege of Rhodes in 305 BC.

Description

According to ancient sources including Diodorus Siculus, the Helepolis was a nine-storey tower about 41 metres (135 ft) high and 20.6 metres (68 ft) wide at the base, tapering towards the top. It rested on eight great iron-clad wheels and was moved by more than 3,000 men working inside it. Its front face was armoured with iron plates to resist fire and missiles, and each floor carried artillery — catapults and ballistae of various sizes firing through shuttered openings — while the lower levels included rams and drills. The structure is estimated to have weighed about 160 tonnes.

The siege of Rhodes

Demetrius deployed the Helepolis as the centrepiece of his assault on Rhodes in 305–304 BC. The Rhodians countered by flooding the ground before the tower's path, causing it to bog down, and by damaging its armour with concentrated fire. After a negotiated peace, Demetrius abandoned the tower and his other siege equipment at Rhodes. The Rhodians sold the abandoned material and used the proceeds to build the Colossus of Rhodes, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.

Legacy

The name "Helepolis" became a byword for enormous siege towers, and later engineers applied it to similar, smaller machines used in subsequent sieges in the Hellenistic world.
