Der Rosenkavalier (The Knight of the Rose), Op. 59, is a comic opera in three acts by Richard Strauss to an original German libretto by Hugo von Hofmannsthal. Set in Vienna in the 1740s, in the early reign of Maria Theresa, it combines comedy of manners with a bittersweet meditation on love, aging and the passage of time. It was first performed in Dresden on 26 January 1911 and quickly became one of the most popular operas of the 20th century.

Roles

  • The Marschallin, Princess Marie Thérèse von Werdenberg (soprano)
  • Octavian, Count Rofrano, her young lover (mezzo-soprano, trouser role)
  • Baron Ochs auf Lerchenau, her boorish cousin (bass)
  • Sophie von Faninal, a rich parvenu's daughter (soprano)
  • Herr von Faninal, her father (baritone)

Synopsis

The Marschallin, aware that her youth is passing, renounces her lover Octavian. Octavian is chosen as the "Knight of the Rose" to present a silver rose to Sophie, the fiancée of the lecherous Baron Ochs, as part of an old Viennese betrothal custom — and the two young people fall in love. After a farcical intrigue in Act 3 exposes Ochs's misconduct, the Marschallin graciously releases Octavian to Sophie, in the opera's celebrated final trio.

Music

Strauss's score is famous for its lush orchestration, its deliberately anachronistic Viennese waltzes, and set pieces including the Italian singer's aria, the Presentation of the Rose, and the final trio "Hab' mir's gelobt". The opera balances comedy with nostalgia for a vanished aristocratic world, and remains a staple of the international repertoire.
