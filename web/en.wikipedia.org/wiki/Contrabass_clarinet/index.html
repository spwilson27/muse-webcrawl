Contrabass clarinet

The contrabass clarinet is the largest member of the clarinet family in regular production. Pitched in B♭, it sounds two octaves below the soprano clarinet and one octave below the bass clarinet. Because of its great length — roughly two metres of tubing — the instrument is usually built in a folded, paperclip-like shape of metal, standing on a peg with the bell pointing upward.

History and use

Early contrabass clarinets were built in the nineteenth century, including Adolphe Sax's instruments, but the modern contrabass was perfected in the twentieth century by makers such as Leblanc, whose "paperclip" model became the best known. The instrument is rare and expensive; it appears in large wind ensembles, clarinet choirs and occasional orchestral and film scores, where it reinforces the lowest woodwind lines. An even lower octocontrabass clarinet exists only as a prototype.
