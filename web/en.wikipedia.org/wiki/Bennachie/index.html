Bennachie is a range of hills in Aberdeenshire, Scotland. Its highest point, Oxen Craig, reaches 529 metres (1,736 ft), and the distinctive peak of Mither Tap (518 m) is topped by an Iron Age hillfort.

Geography and history

Rising from the Garioch plain north-west of Aberdeen, Bennachie's granite tors are visible across much of Aberdeenshire and are a beloved local landmark. Mither Tap's fort, with massive stone ramparts, was an important Pictish centre. The hill is also associated — speculatively — with the Battle of Mons Graupius.

Recreation and culture

Waymarked trails from centres including the Bennachie Visitor Centre at Pitcaple make it one of Scotland's most walked hills. The Bailies of Bennachie, a volunteer group founded in 1973, help maintain its paths and heritage. The hill features strongly in north-east Scots (Doric) folklore and song.
