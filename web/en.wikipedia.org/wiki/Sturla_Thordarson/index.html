Sturla Thordarson

Sturla Þórðarson (29 July 1214 – 30 July 1284), commonly known as Sturla Thordarson, was an Icelandic chieftain and writer of sagas and contemporary history during the period of the Icelandic Commonwealth. He is the author of Íslendinga saga, the largest part of the Sturlunga saga compilation, as well as Hákonar saga Hákonarsonar, a saga of King Haakon IV of Norway.

Life

Sturla was a member of the powerful Sturlungar family clan, the son of Þórðr Sturluson and nephew of the historian Snorri Sturluson. He played a political role in the civil war of the Age of the Sturlungs and served as lawspeaker of Iceland. After the submission of Iceland to the Norwegian crown in 1262–64, Sturla became a retainer of King Magnus VI and was commissioned to write the sagas of Magnus's father, Haakon IV, and of Magnus himself.

Works

His Íslendinga saga is a contemporary history of Iceland in the thirteenth century and a principal source for the period. He also wrote Landnámabók material in the version known as Sturlubók, the saga of King Magnus the Lawmender (of which only a fragment survives), and a version of the saga of Bishop Gudmundur Arason. Sturla died in 1284, a day after his seventieth birthday.
