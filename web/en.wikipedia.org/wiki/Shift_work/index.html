Shift work

Shift work is an employment practice designed to make use of, or provide service across, all 24 hours of the clock each day of the week. The practice typically sees the day divided into shifts — set periods of time during which different groups of workers perform their duties. Terms such as "graveyard shift" and "swing shift" describe common scheduling patterns.

Types of schedules

Common arrangements include fixed shifts, in which employees always work the same hours; rotating shifts, in which workers cycle through day, evening, and night duties; split shifts, with two separate work periods in a day; and on-call arrangements. Continuous operations such as hospitals, power plants, emergency services, and many factories require coverage around the clock, while retail and hospitality often use shifts to cover extended opening hours.

Health effects

Shift work, especially night work, disrupts the circadian rhythm and is associated with sleep disorders, fatigue, gastrointestinal problems, and elevated risks of cardiovascular disease and metabolic conditions. The International Agency for Research on Cancer has classified night shift work as a probable carcinogen (Group 2A). "Shift work sleep disorder" is a recognised condition characterised by insomnia or excessive sleepiness tied to the work schedule.

Regulation and management

Many jurisdictions regulate maximum shift lengths, rest periods, and night-work premiums. Good scheduling practice favours forward rotation (day → evening → night), adequate recovery days, and limiting consecutive night shifts. Employers may also provide education on sleep hygiene and light exposure to help workers adapt.
