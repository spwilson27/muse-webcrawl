The Saab 96 is a small family car produced by the Swedish manufacturer Saab from 1960 to 1980. It evolved from the Saab 93, retaining that car's aerodynamic body shape and two-stroke engine in its early years, and achieved considerable success in international rallying.

A Saab 96, recognisable by its rounded aerodynamic body.

History

Launched in 1960, the 96 was initially powered by an 841 cc three-cylinder two-stroke engine producing 38 PS. It was Saab's first model to be widely exported, and an estate version, the Saab 95, was sold alongside it. In 1967, in response to tightening emissions requirements in the United States, Saab adopted the Ford Taunus 1.5-litre V4 four-stroke engine; two-stroke versions continued for some markets until 1968. Over its twenty-year production run the car received numerous safety and styling updates, including a longer nose in 1965 and larger windows.

Rallying

The 96 is closely associated with the Swedish driver Erik Carlsson, whose giant-killing victories in the car — including wins at the RAC Rally in 1960, 1961 and 1962 and the Monte Carlo Rally in 1962 and 1963 — established Saab's reputation. Its front-wheel drive and light weight made it exceptionally competitive on snow and loose surfaces.
