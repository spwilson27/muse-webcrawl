Vestigial sideband

Vestigial sideband (VSB) is a type of amplitude modulation in which one sideband and a vestige (a small part) of the other sideband are transmitted. It is a compromise between double-sideband (DSB) and single-sideband (SSB) modulation, saving bandwidth while remaining relatively simple to demodulate.

Principle

Ordinary amplitude modulation produces two mirror-image sidebands around the carrier. A single-sideband system removes one entirely but requires sharp filters that are impractical near the carrier, where low-frequency components lie close together. In VSB, one sideband is passed in full and the other is partially suppressed, leaving a "vestige" near the carrier. Provided the filter roll-off is complementary about the carrier frequency, the demodulated signal recovers the original baseband with little distortion.

Applications

VSB was the modulation used for the video signal of analogue television broadcasting systems (NTSC, PAL and SECAM), where a 6 MHz channel carried the full upper sideband and about 1.25 MHz of the lower sideband. A digital form, 8-VSB, was adopted as the modulation standard for ATSC digital television broadcasts in North America.
