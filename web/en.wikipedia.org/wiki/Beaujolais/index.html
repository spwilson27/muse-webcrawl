Beaujolais is a French Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée (AOC) wine produced in the region of the same name, located north of Lyon and forming part of the wider Burgundy wine region. The wines are made almost entirely from the Gamay grape and are generally light-bodied red wines known for their fruity character, often produced using carbonic maceration.

Appellations

The region has three levels of appellation: basic Beaujolais AOC, Beaujolais-Villages AOC from 39 communes, and ten crus — Saint-Amour, Juliénas, Chénas, Moulin-à-Vent, Fleurie, Chiroubles, Morgon, Régnié, Brouilly, and Côte de Brouilly — which are considered capable of producing more structured wines that can age.

Beaujolais nouveau

The region is internationally famous for Beaujolais nouveau, a young wine released each year on the third Thursday of November, just weeks after harvest, an event marked by the cry "Le Beaujolais nouveau est arrivé!" ("The new Beaujolais has arrived!"). See also French wine.
