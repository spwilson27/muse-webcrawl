Breastplate

A breastplate, the principal component of a cuirass.

A breastplate or chestplate is a device worn over the torso to protect it from injury, as an item of protective clothing, or as religious vestments. In modern times it is often made of metal or ballistic materials, but historically breastplates were a principal element of plate armour, where the breastplate combined with a backplate to form the cuirass.

History

Bronze breastplates were worn by Greek hoplites and other ancient soldiers. The muscle cuirass of classical antiquity was sculpted to imitate an idealised human torso. In medieval and early modern Europe, steel breastplates became the central defence of men-at-arms and pikemen; globose (rounded) forms were developed to deflect missiles, and in the 17th century breastplates were proofed against firearms, sometimes bearing a dent from a test shot as a guarantee of quality. Cuirassiers retained steel breastplates into the era of rifled weapons, and breastplates saw limited use in trench warfare in the First World War.

Religious use

The term also refers to the jewelled breastplate (hoshen) worn by the High Priest of Israel described in the Book of Exodus, set with twelve stones representing the tribes of Israel.
