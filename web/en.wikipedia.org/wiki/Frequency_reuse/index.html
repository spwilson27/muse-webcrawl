Frequency reuse

Frequency reuse is the process of using the same radio frequencies on radio transmitter sites separated by a distance sufficient to keep interference from becoming a problem. It is a central principle of the cellular network concept: a service area is divided into cells, each served by a low-power base station using a subset of the available channels, and the same subset is reused in cells far enough away that co-channel interference is acceptably low.

Reuse patterns

Cells using the same frequencies are arranged in clusters (commonly of 4, 7 or 12 cells); the reuse distance depends on the cluster size and propagation. Smaller clusters give more capacity per area but more interference. Frequency reuse is also used for satellite spot beams and for planning broadcast transmitters.
