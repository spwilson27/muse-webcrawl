Final good

A final good, or consumer good, is a commodity that is used by the consumer to satisfy current wants or needs, rather than being used to produce another good. In contrast, an intermediate good is used as an input in the production of other goods — for example, steel used in making cars, or flour bought by a bakery. Whether a good is final or intermediate depends on its use, not its nature: a tomato bought by a household is a final good, while the same tomato bought by a sauce manufacturer is an intermediate good.

In national accounts

The distinction is fundamental to measuring gross domestic product (GDP). To avoid double counting, GDP includes the value of final goods and services only: the value of intermediate goods is already embodied in the price of the final product. The value-added approach to GDP works by summing each firm's contribution — its sales minus the cost of its intermediate inputs — which aggregates exactly to the value of final output.

Types of final goods

  • Consumer durables — goods used over a long period, such as cars, appliances, and furniture.
  • Consumer non-durables — goods consumed quickly, such as food, fuel, and toiletries.
  • Services — intangible final outputs such as haircuts, healthcare, and entertainment.

Capital goods such as machinery and buildings present a special case. They are not consumed in a single production period but are used to produce other goods over many years; in national accounting, purchases of new capital goods are treated as investment, a component of final expenditure, rather than as intermediate purchases. Government purchases of goods and services likewise count as final demand.

Related concepts

Economists also distinguish final goods by rivalry and excludability into private goods, public goods, club goods, and common-pool resources. The concept relates to the classical distinction between goods for consumption and goods for production running through the work of Adam Smith and Karl Marx, the latter of whom divided production into Department I (means of production) and Department II (articles of consumption).
