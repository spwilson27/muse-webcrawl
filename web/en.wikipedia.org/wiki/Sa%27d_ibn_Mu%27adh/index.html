Sa'd ibn Mu'adh (Arabic: سعد بن معاذ; c. 590–627) was the chief of the Aws tribe in Medina and a prominent companion of the Islamic prophet Muhammad. He converted to Islam before the Hijra and played a leading role in the early Muslim community.

Life

Sa'd embraced Islam at the hands of Mus'ab ibn Umayr, the envoy sent by Muhammad to Medina, and his conversion led many of the Aws to follow. He fought at the battles of Badr (624) and Uhud (625), and at the Battle of the Trench (627) he was wounded by an arrow.

Judgment on Banu Qurayza

After the siege of the Banu Qurayza, Sa'd was chosen by that tribe to pronounce judgment on them; he ruled according to the law of the Torah, decreeing the execution of the fighting men and the enslavement of the women and children, a verdict Muhammad approved.

Death

Sa'd died of his wounds shortly after delivering the judgment in 627 and was buried in the Al-Baqi cemetery in Medina.
