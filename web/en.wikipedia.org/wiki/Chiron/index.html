Chiron

Species: Centaur

Parents: Cronus and Philyra

Known for: Wisdom, medicine, teaching of heroes

In Greek mythology, Chiron (Greek: Χείρων, "hand") was a centaur celebrated for his wisdom, gentleness and knowledge of medicine — in marked contrast to the wild and violent centaurs of myth generally. He was the son of the Titan Cronus and the Oceanid Philyra, and lived on Mount Pelion in Thessaly.

Tutor of heroes

Chiron was famed as the teacher of many Greek heroes, including Achilles, Jason, Asclepius, Actaeon, Aristaeus and, in some traditions, Heracles and the Dioscuri. He instructed them in music, medicine, hunting, archery and moral conduct. His cave on Pelion served as a school; Achilles was brought to him as a child by Peleus, and Chiron's daughter Endeïs married Aeacus, making Chiron an ancestor of Achilles.

Wounding and death

According to the best-known account, Chiron was accidentally wounded by an arrow of Heracles dipped in the venom of the Hydra during the fight with the other centaurs. Although immortal, he suffered incurable pain and eventually surrendered his immortality — in exchange, in some versions, for the release of Prometheus — and was placed among the stars, variously identified with the constellations Centaurus or Sagittarius.

Legacy

Chiron embodies the "wounded healer" archetype later taken up in literature and psychology. The minor planet 2060 Chiron, the first recognised centaur in astronomy (discovered in 1977), is named after him, as is the centaur class of small Solar System bodies.
