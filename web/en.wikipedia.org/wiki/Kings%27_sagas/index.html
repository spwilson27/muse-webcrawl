Kings' sagas

The kings' sagas (Old Norse: konungasögur) are a genre of Old Norse sagas, written mainly in Iceland in the 12th to 14th centuries, that narrate the lives of Scandinavian kings, above all the kings of Norway from the legendary Yngling dynasty to the 13th century.

Major works

The most famous collection is Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla (c. 1230), sixteen sagas from mythical origins to 1177. Other major compilations include Fagrskinna and Morkinskinna, and individual sagas such as Óláfs saga helga and the Sverris saga, the latter partly dictated by King Sverre himself.

Historical value

Though written long after many of the events and intermixed with legend, the kings' sagas incorporate skaldic verse that may preserve older traditions, and they remain the principal narrative sources for early Norwegian history.
