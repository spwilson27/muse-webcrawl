Khat

Khat (Catha edulis), also known as qat, is a flowering plant native to the Horn of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula. Its fresh leaves are chewed as a stimulant in Ethiopia, Somalia, Yemen, Kenya and neighbouring regions, where the practice has deep social and cultural roots.

Effects and use

Khat leaves contain the alkaloids cathinone and cathine, amphetamine-like stimulants. Chewing sessions, often lasting hours, produce euphoria, alertness and talkativeness, followed by a comedown that can include insomnia and low mood. The leaves lose potency quickly after harvest, so they are consumed fresh, traditionally in social gatherings.

Legal status

Khat is legal and widely used in Yemen and much of East Africa, where it is also an important cash crop, though its heavy water use and the time spent chewing are debated. It is a controlled substance in many Western countries, including the United States and (since 2014) the United Kingdom. Health authorities associate heavy chronic use with cardiovascular strain, dental problems and, in some users, psychiatric symptoms.
