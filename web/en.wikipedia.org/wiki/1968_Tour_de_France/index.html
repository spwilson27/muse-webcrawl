The 1968 Tour de France was the 55th edition of the Tour de France, one of cycling's Grand Tours. It ran from 27 June to 21 July 1968 over 22 stages and roughly 4,492 kilometres. The race was won by Dutch rider Jan Janssen, whose victory was only sealed in the final-day time trial in Paris — at the time the closest finish in Tour history, with Janssen beating Herman Van Springel by just 38 seconds.

Background

The 1968 Tour was run under the shadow of Tom Simpson's death on Mont Ventoux during the 1967 race, and it introduced drug testing for the first time: riders were tested after stages, and several were penalised. The race was contested by national teams, as in the preceding years. Favourites included five-time winner Jacques Anquetil — who did not start — defending champion Roger Pingeon, Raymond Poulidor and Felice Gimondi.

Race summary

No single rider dominated. The lead changed hands repeatedly in the Pyrenees and Alps, with stage wins spread among riders including Franco Bitossi, who also won the points classification's green jersey, and climber Aurelio González, winner of the mountains classification. Heading into the final stage, a time trial from Melun to Paris, Van Springel led Janssen by 16 seconds. Janssen produced the ride of his life to win the stage and overhaul the deficit, taking the yellow jersey on the very last day — the first Dutch victory in the Tour de France.

Legacy

Janssen's triumph remains famous as the archetypal "won on the last day" Tour, a feat matched only by Greg LeMond's eight-second victory in 1989. The 1968 edition was also the last Tour contested by national teams until trade teams returned permanently the following year, and its anti-doping controls marked a turning point in the sport's approach to performance-enhancing drugs. Janssen never won another Grand Tour but remains a national sporting hero in the Netherlands.
