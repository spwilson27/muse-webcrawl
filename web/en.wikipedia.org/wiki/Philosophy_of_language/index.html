Philosophy of language studies the nature of language, meaning and reference, and language's relation to mind and world. Central questions include how words refer (Frege's distinction between sense and reference — 'morning star' and 'evening star'), how sentences get truth-values, what speakers do with words (J. L. Austin's speech acts), and how conversation works beyond literal meaning (H. P. Grice's implicature).

Turns and figures

The 'linguistic turn' made language philosophy's central medium: Ludwig Wittgenstein's later work dissolved traditional problems into language-games, while Saul Kripke's Naming and Necessity (1972 lectures) revived metaphysics within the analytic mainstream by distinguishing rigid designators and necessary a posteriori truths.
