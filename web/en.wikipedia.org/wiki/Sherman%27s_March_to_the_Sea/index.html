Sherman's March to the Sea

Date15 November – 21 December 1864
LocationGeorgia, Confederate States
ResultUnion victory; capture of Savannah

Sherman's March to the Sea is the popular name for the Savannah Campaign conducted in late 1864 by Union Major General William Tecumseh Sherman during the American Civil War. After capturing Atlanta, Sherman marched some 60,000 troops about 285 miles (459 km) across Georgia to the port of Savannah on the Atlantic coast.

The campaign

Cutting loose from his supply lines, Sherman's army lived off the land while destroying railroads, mills, warehouses and other infrastructure of military value along a broad corridor. The campaign exemplified the Union's emerging "total war" strategy of breaking the Confederacy's economic capacity and civilian morale. Savannah fell on 21 December 1864, and Sherman famously telegraphed President Abraham Lincoln offering the city as a "Christmas gift."

Aftermath and legacy

From Savannah, Sherman turned north into the Carolinas in early 1865. The March remains one of the most debated operations of the war: celebrated in the song "Marching Through Georgia" and remembered in the South as a byword for devastation, it is credited by many historians with helping to shorten the conflict.
