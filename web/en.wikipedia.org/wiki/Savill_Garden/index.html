The Savill Garden is a 35-acre (14 ha) ornamental garden in Windsor Great Park, Berkshire, England. Created in 1932 by Sir Eric Savill, it is regarded as one of Britain's finest woodland gardens, noted for its collections of rhododendrons, azaleas, camellias, magnolias, and herbaceous borders arranged around informal glades and a large pond.

History

Eric Savill, Deputy Surveyor of Windsor Great Park, began work on the garden in 1932 with the support of King George V and Queen Mary. Starting from an area of boggy oak woodland, he dammed a stream to create a pond and planted drifts of shrubs and trees. The garden grew by gift and exchange of plants from many sources. After the Second World War, Savill continued to develop the garden, which was opened to the public and named in his honour following his retirement.

Features

  • The New Zealand Garden — plants from New Zealand in a sheltered setting
  • The Queen Elizabeth Temperate House — opened in 1995, housing tender plants
  • The Rose Garden — opened by Queen Elizabeth II in 2010, with a raised walkway designed by Andrew Wilson
  • The Savill Building — opened in 2006, a visitor centre with an award-winning undulating timber gridshell roof designed by Glenn Howells Architects
  • Spring Wood, Summer Wood and the Glades — seasonal woodland planting
  • The Bog Garden and Herbaceous Borders — moisture-loving and perennial plantings

Collections

The garden holds notable collections of Rhododendron species, magnolias, camellias, hydrangeas, and dwarf conifers. Many plants are of wild-collected origin and the garden participates in plant conservation and exchange through its links with other botanic and estate gardens.

Management

The Savill Garden is managed by the Crown Estate as part of Windsor Great Park, alongside the Valley Gardens and Virginia Water. It is open to the public throughout the year.
