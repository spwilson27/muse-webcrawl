Pimlico Race Course

Pimlico Race Course is a thoroughbred horse racetrack in Baltimore, Maryland, United States, best known as the home of the Preakness Stakes, the second leg of the American Triple Crown. Opened in 1870, it is nicknamed "Old Hilltop" after a small rise in the infield that was a favourite vantage point for trainers and racegoers.

History

The Maryland Jockey Club, founded in 1743, built Pimlico on land whose name recalled the London district of Pimlico, from whose taverns early settlers remembered the area. The course opened on 25 October 1870; the first stakes race was won by a colt named Preakness, after whom the Preakness Stakes (first run in 1873) was named. In 1877 the course hosted the famous "Great Race" in which Parole defeated Ten Broeck and Tom Ochiltree before a crowd that included members of Congress, who adjourned to attend.

Pimlico was the site of the celebrated 1938 match race between Seabiscuit and the Triple Crown winner War Admiral, won by Seabiscuit. The winner's weather vane atop the infield cupola is repainted in the colours of each Preakness winner.

The Preakness

The Preakness Stakes, run at 1 3/16 miles on the third Saturday in May, follows the Kentucky Derby by two weeks. Its winner is draped with a blanket of yellow flowers painted to resemble black-eyed Susans, the Maryland state flower. The Black-Eyed Susan Stakes for fillies is run on the Friday before.

Redevelopment

The ageing facility's future was debated for years. Under agreements reached in 2020 and revised in 2024, Pimlico is to be comprehensively rebuilt as Maryland's year-round thoroughbred racing hub, with racing operations transferred to a nonprofit authority and the Preakness remaining at Pimlico.
