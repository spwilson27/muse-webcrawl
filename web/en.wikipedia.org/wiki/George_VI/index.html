George VI (Albert Frederick Arthur George; 14 December 1895 – 6 February 1952) was King of the United Kingdom and the Dominions of the British Commonwealth from 11 December 1936 until his death. He acceded unexpectedly when his elder brother, Edward VIII, abdicated in order to marry Wallis Simpson, and his reign spanned the Second World War and the beginning of the transformation of the British Empire into the Commonwealth of Nations.

Early life

Known to his family as "Bertie", the second son of George V served in the Royal Navy and Royal Air Force during the First World War, fighting at the Battle of Jutland. He suffered from a stammer, which he worked to overcome with the speech therapist Lionel Logue — a story popularised by the film The King's Speech. In 1923 he married Lady Elizabeth Bowes-Lyon; their daughters were Elizabeth, the future queen, and Margaret.

Reign and war

As king, George VI became a symbol of national resolve during the Second World War: he and Queen Elizabeth remained at Buckingham Palace through the Blitz, toured bombed areas, and the king instituted the George Cross for civilian gallantry. After the war his reign saw the Labour government's welfare-state reforms and the independence of India and Pakistan in 1947, after which he ceased to be Emperor of India and became Head of the Commonwealth. His health declined from smoking-related illness, and he died in his sleep at Sandringham in February 1952, succeeded by his elder daughter, Elizabeth II.
