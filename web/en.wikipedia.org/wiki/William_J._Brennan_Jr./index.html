William J. Brennan Jr.


Born April 25, 1906, Newark, New Jersey, U.S.
Died July 24, 1997 (aged 91), Arlington, Virginia
Office Associate Justice of the U.S. Supreme Court (1956–1990)

William Joseph Brennan Jr. (April 25, 1906 – July 24, 1997) was an American lawyer and jurist who served as an Associate Justice of the Supreme Court of the United States from 1956 to 1990. A leading liberal voice on the Warren Court, he is regarded as one of the most influential justices of the 20th century.

Early life and career

Brennan was born in Newark, New Jersey, to Irish immigrant parents. He graduated from the University of Pennsylvania's Wharton School and Harvard Law School, practised labour law in New Jersey, served in the Army during the Second World War, and was appointed to the New Jersey Superior Court in 1949 and the New Jersey Supreme Court in 1952.

Supreme Court

President Dwight D. Eisenhower appointed Brennan by recess appointment in October 1956; he was confirmed by the Senate in 1957. Brennan authored landmark opinions including Baker v. Carr (1962) on legislative apportionment, New York Times Co. v. Sullivan (1964) on freedom of the press, and Frontiero v. Richardson (1973), and he helped forge majorities in cases expanding civil rights, free speech, and the rights of criminal defendants. He was a principal defender of the right to abortion recognised in Roe v. Wade.

Retirement and legacy

Brennan retired in 1990 for health reasons and died in 1997. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1993. His jurisprudence centred on human dignity and the protection of minorities and the politically powerless.
