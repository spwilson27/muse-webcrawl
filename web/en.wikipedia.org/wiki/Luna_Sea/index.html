Luna Sea is a Japanese rock band formed in Kanagawa in 1986 by school friends Ryuichi Kawamura, Shinobu "Inoran" Inoue, Jun "J" Onose, and drummer Shinya Yamada, joined soon after by guitarist Yasuhiro "Sugizo" Sugihara. One of the most successful and influential bands associated with the visual kei movement, they have sold over 10 million records and are credited with helping bring visual kei from the underground to stadiums.

History

Originally named Lunacy, the band was discovered by hide of X Japan and signed to his Extasy Records label, releasing their self-titled debut album in 1991. Their major-label debut Image (1992) began a run of best-selling albums — Eden (1993), Mother (1994), and Style (1996) — and hits such as "Rosier", "True Blue", "Desire", and "End of Sorrow". The double single "I for You" (1998), theme to a popular television drama, became one of their signature songs.

After the album Lunacy (2000), the band announced a "curtain closing" and ceased activity at the end of 2000. In 2007 they reunited for a one-night concert at the Tokyo Dome, and in 2010 they formally resumed activities with a world tour, later releasing new albums including A Will (2013), Luv (2017), and Cross (2019), produced with Steve Lillywhite.

Legacy

Luna Sea's musicianship and melodic songwriting influenced a generation of Japanese rock bands. Their official fan club is called Slave, and their concerts — including the recurring "Lunatic Fest" festival founded in 2015 — remain major events in Japanese rock.
