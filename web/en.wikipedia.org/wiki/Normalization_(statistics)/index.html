Normalization (statistics)

In statistics and applications of statistics, normalization can have a range of related meanings. In the simplest cases, normalization means adjusting values measured on different scales to a notionally common scale, often prior to averaging or further analysis. The term is used inconsistently across disciplines, and several distinct procedures travel under the name.

Common methods

  • Standardisation (z-scores): subtract the mean and divide by the standard deviation, giving values in units of standard deviations.
  • Min–max scaling: linearly rescale a variable to a fixed range, usually [0, 1].
  • Unit length: divide a vector by its norm so that it has length one.
  • Percentile/rank-based transforms and studentisation are also used.

Uses

Normalization is ubiquitous in data preprocessing for machine learning (feature scaling), in constructing indices from disparate indicators, in educational score equating, and in adjusting financial ratios. Failure to scale features can cause distance- and gradient-based methods to be dominated by variables with large raw magnitudes.
