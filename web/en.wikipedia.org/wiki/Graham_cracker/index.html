Graham cracker

A graham cracker (pronounced GRAHAM or GRAM in American English) is a sweet, honey- or cinnamon-flavoured cracker made with graham flour, a coarsely ground whole-wheat flour. It is a staple of American desserts, notably s'mores and cheesecake crusts.

TypeCracker / biscuit
Place of originUnited States
Created byInspired by Sylvester Graham
Main ingredientsGraham flour, wheat flour, sugar, honey

History

The cracker was inspired by Presbyterian minister Sylvester Graham (1794–1851), who advocated a bland vegetarian diet to suppress lust and ill health. His followers, Grahamites, ate unsweetened whole-wheat crackers. Commercial bakers later sweetened them; the National Biscuit Company introduced Honey Maid grahams in 1925.

Uses

Graham crackers are eaten as a snack, crumbled into pie and cheesecake crusts with butter and sugar, and toasted with marshmallow and chocolate as s'mores around campfires. They are also a common first finger food and a base for icebox cakes. See Cracker.
