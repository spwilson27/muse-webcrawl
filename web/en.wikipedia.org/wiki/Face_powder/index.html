Face powder

Face powder is a cosmetic product applied to the face to set foundation, reduce shine from sebum, even out skin tone, and give the skin a smoother, matte appearance. It is sold as loose powder or pressed into compacts.

Composition

Modern face powders are based on talc, mica, silica, kaolin, or rice starch, with pigments such as iron oxides and titanium dioxide providing colour and coverage, and binders (in pressed powders) holding the cake together. Translucent setting powders contain little pigment and are used over makeup of any shade.

History

Powders have been used since antiquity — rice powder in China and Japan, and white lead-based powders in Europe. Lead cosmetics caused poisoning, and in the 19th and 20th centuries safer talc- and starch-based products from makers such as Coty and Max Factor made powder a mass-market staple. Rice powder and blotting papers remain popular in East Asian beauty routines.

Use

Powder is applied with a puff, brush, or sponge, often concentrated on the oily T-zone. In theatre and photography it reduces glare under strong lights. Variants include mineral powder, finishing powder, and colour-correcting powders.
