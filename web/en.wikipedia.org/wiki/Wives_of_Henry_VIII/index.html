The wives of Henry VIII were the six queen consorts of King Henry VIII of England between 1509 and his death in 1547. Henry is best known for his six marriages and his efforts to have his first marriage, to Catherine of Aragon, annulled. His disagreement with Pope Clement VII about such an annulment led Henry to initiate the English Reformation, separating the Church of England from papal authority.

The six wives

The six women were:

  1. Catherine of Aragon (1485–1536), daughter of Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile. The marriage was annulled in 1533.
  2. Anne Boleyn (c. 1501–1536), beheaded in 1536 on charges of adultery, treason and incest.
  3. Jane Seymour (c. 1508–1537), who died after giving birth to Edward VI.
  4. Anne of Cleves (1515–1557), whose unconsummated marriage was annulled after six months.
  5. Catherine Howard (c. 1523–1542), beheaded.
  6. Catherine Parr (1512–1548), who survived Henry.

A popular mnemonic for their fates runs "Divorced, Beheaded, Died — Divorced, Beheaded, Survived."

Religious consequences

Henry's pursuit of an heir and his break with Rome made the successions of his marriages a central driver of the English Reformation, the dissolution of the monasteries, and English religious politics for the rest of the sixteenth century.
