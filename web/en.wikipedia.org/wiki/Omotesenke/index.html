Omotesenke (表千家) is one of the three main schools (san-Senke) of the Japanese tea ceremony descended from Sen no Rikyū. Together with Urasenke and Mushakōjisenke, it descends from Rikyū's grandson Sen no Sōtan, whose heirs divided into the three houses — the "front" (omote) Sen house, the "back" (ura) Sen house, and the Mushakōji Sen house.

History

Sōtan's third son, Kōshin Sōsa, inherited the main Sen estate facing the street (omote, front), hence the school's name. The Omotesenke head house maintains the tea houses Fushin-an and others at its Kyoto estate. The present head (iemoto) represents the fourteenth-plus generation of the line.

Style

Omotesenke style is often described as more restrained and classically rustic than Urasenke's. Distinguishing features include the use of the leather whisk rest and differences in whisking: Omotesenke traditionally prepares tea with a less frothy surface for koicha and uses the susudake (smoked bamboo) whisk, whereas Urasenke favours a full froth of matcha foam. Its utensils and procedures differ in small details from the other Sen schools.

The iemoto system

Like the other schools, Omotesenke is organised under the iemoto (grand master) system, with licensed teachers conferring certificates through graded curricula. Its foundation (the Omotesenke Domonkai) supports instruction across Japan and abroad.
