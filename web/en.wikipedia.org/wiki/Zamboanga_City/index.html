Zamboanga City, officially the City of Zamboanga (Chavacano: Ciudad de Zamboanga; Tagalog: Lungsod ng Zamboanga), is a highly urbanized city in the Zamboanga Peninsula region of the Philippines. It is the commercial and industrial center of the region and the sixth-most populous city in the Philippines.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Subanon, Yakan, Tausug, and Sama-Bajau peoples. Spanish colonization began in earnest in 1635, when Spanish forces built a fort, Real Fuerza de San José, in what is now the city center. The fort was later reconstructed in stone and renamed Real Fuerza de Nuestra Señora del Pilar de Zaragoza, commonly known today as Fort Pilar, a shrine dedicated to Our Lady of the Pillar, the city's patroness. In the 19th century Zamboanga was the capital of Spanish-held Mindanao and an important port.

The city is famous for its Chavacano language, a Spanish-based creole — often described as one of the oldest Spanish-based creole languages in the world — which earned Zamboanga the nickname "Asia's Latin City." During the American period the city was the capital of the Moro Province. It received its city charter in 1937.

Geography and economy

Zamboanga City sits at the southern tip of the Zamboanga Peninsula, facing the Basilan Strait and the Sulu Sea. The city has one of the largest land areas of any Philippine city and includes several islands. It is the Philippines' principal sardine-canning center — the majority of the country's canned sardines are produced here — and a hub for the seaweed, rubber, and fishing industries. The colorful vinta, a traditional outrigger boat with a rectangular sail, is an icon of the city and the region's maritime cultures.

Culture

The annual Hermosa Festival, held each October in honor of Our Lady of the Pillar, features street parades, a regatta of vintas, and cultural shows. Notable landmarks include Fort Pilar, Pasonanca Park, and the Great Santa Cruz Island with its pink-sand beach.
