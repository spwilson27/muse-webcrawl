Masculism

Masculism (or masculinism) refers to a set of movements and ideologies concerned with men's rights, roles, and experiences, advocating for the recognition and redress of issues affecting men and boys. Usage of the term varies: it can describe advocacy for men's interests analogous to feminism's advocacy for women, or, in other scholarly uses, an ideology upholding traditional masculinity and patriarchal values.

Meanings and movements

In its advocacy sense, masculism overlaps with the men's rights movement, which emerged in the 1970s alongside men's liberation. Issues raised include conscription and military service, higher rates of workplace deaths and suicide among men, family court and custody outcomes, educational underachievement of boys, male circumcision, and the availability of services for male victims of domestic violence and sexual assault. Authors such as Warren Farrell, originally a feminist who served on the board of the National Organization for Women, argued in works like The Myth of Male Power (1993) that traditional male roles also impose serious burdens.

Criticism

Critics argue that some masculist currents are reactive to feminism and minimise structural inequalities affecting women, while defenders respond that addressing men's issues need not be zero-sum. Academic gender studies often distinguishes egalitarian men's advocacy from anti-feminist or traditionalist uses of the term, and scholars caution that "masculinism" in sociological literature frequently denotes the ideology of male dominance rather than a rights movement.
