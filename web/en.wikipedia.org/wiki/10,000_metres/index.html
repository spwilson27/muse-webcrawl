10,000 metres

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 10,000 metres or the 10,000-metre run is a common long-distance track running event, consisting of 25 laps around a standard 400-metre outdoor track. It is the longest standard track event and has been part of the Olympic athletics programme for men since 1912 and for women since 1988.

History

The distance arose from the conversion of the six-mile run long contested in Britain. Finns dominated the event in the early Olympic era — Paavo Nurmi among them — while later decades saw East African ascendancy through runners such as Haile Gebrselassie, Kenenisa Bekele, and Tirunesh Dibaba. The event demands both aerobic endurance and tactical speed, and races often hinge on the final lap.

Records

The men's world record is held by Uganda's Joshua Cheptegei, who ran 26:11.00 in Valencia in 2020, surpassing Bekele's long-standing mark. The women's world record was set by Kenya's Beatrice Chebet in 2024 (28:54.14), the first women's time under 29 minutes, breaking Letesenbet Gidey's previous record.

Because elite 10,000 m races are staged infrequently outside championships, many top distance runners focus on the 5,000 metres, cross country, or road racing, and 10-kilometre road races — a separate event — are far more common worldwide.
