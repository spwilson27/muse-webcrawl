Omar Yaghi

Born: 9 February 1965, Amman, Jordan

Known for: Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs); reticular chemistry

Awards: Wolf Prize in Chemistry (2018); Nobel Prize in Chemistry (2025)

Institutions: University of California, Berkeley

Omar Mwannes Yaghi (born 9 February 1965) is a Jordanian-American chemist. He is a professor at the University of California, Berkeley, and the founding director of the Berkeley Global Science Institute. He is best known as the founder of reticular chemistry and for developing metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) and covalent organic frameworks (COFs).

Early life and education

Yaghi was born in Amman, Jordan, to a family of Palestinian refugees. Inspired by pictures of molecules in a library book, he emigrated to the United States at age 15 and studied at Hudson Valley Community College before completing his BS at the State University of New York at Albany in 1985. He earned his PhD at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign in 1990 under Walter G. Klemperer, and carried out postdoctoral work at Harvard University with Richard H. Holm.

Career

Yaghi held faculty positions at Arizona State University, the University of Michigan, and UCLA before moving to Berkeley in 2012. In the mid-1990s he showed that metal ions and organic linkers could be stitched into robust crystalline, permanently porous frameworks by design — the field he named reticular chemistry. His group reported the landmark frameworks MOF-5 and MOF-177, and later zeolitic imidazolate frameworks (ZIFs) and COFs built entirely from light elements linked by strong covalent bonds.

Applications

Frameworks from his laboratory have been applied to hydrogen and methane storage, carbon dioxide capture, and harvesting water from desert air; a MOF-based device developed with collaborators extracts drinking water from low-humidity air using only sunlight. Yaghi co-founded companies to commercialise these materials.

Honours

He received the Wolf Prize in Chemistry in 2018 and shared the 2025 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Susumu Kitagawa and Richard Robson "for the development of metal–organic frameworks."
