The Justice League, originally the Justice League of America, is a team of superheroes appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. Writer Gardner Fox conceived the team as a revival of the Golden Age Justice Society of America, and they first appeared in The Brave and the Bold #28 (March 1960).

History

The founding line-up — Superman, Batman, Wonder Woman, the Flash (Barry Allen), Green Lantern (Hal Jordan), Aquaman, and Martian Manhunter — headlined one of the most popular titles of the Silver Age. Rosters and concepts evolved over decades: the satellite-era League, the humorous international era of the late 1980s, Grant Morrison's JLA (1997), and the New 52 reboot (2011), which replaced Martian Manhunter with Cyborg among the founders.

In other media

The League stars in the animated series Justice League (2001–2004) and Justice League Unlimited, numerous direct-to-video films, and the live-action film Justice League (2017) and its director's cut Zack Snyder's Justice League (2021).

Concept

The team's premise — the world's greatest heroes uniting against threats no single hero can face — inspired rival Marvel's Avengers and became a template for superhero teams across popular culture, with headquarters including the Hall of Justice and the orbital Watchtower.
