Lo mai gai

CourseDim sum
Place of originGuangzhou, China
Main ingredientsGlutinous rice, chicken, Chinese sausage, mushrooms, dried shrimp

Lo mai gai (Chinese: 糯米雞; Jyutping: no6 mai5 gai1; literally "glutinous rice chicken") is a classic Cantonese dim sum dish originating in Guangzhou. It consists of glutinous (sticky) rice filled with chicken, Chinese sausage (lap cheong), mushrooms, dried shrimp, and sometimes salted egg yolk or scallions, wrapped in a dried lotus leaf and steamed.

Lo mai gai unwrapped from its lotus leaf

Preparation and serving

The rice is seasoned with soy sauce, oyster sauce, and sesame oil, and the lotus leaf imparts a subtle fragrance during steaming. Lo mai gai is served piping hot, the leaf folded back at the table; the parcels are relatively large compared with most dim sum items and are often shared or counted as a substantial item in a dim sum meal.

Variations

A smaller variation wrapped in lotus leaf is called jan zu gai ("pearl chicken"). Related sticky-rice parcels include zongzi, wrapped in bamboo leaves, and Malaysian and Nyonya adaptations; in Malaysia and Singapore, versions with curry or different fillings exist, and the dish is also sold as a takeaway breakfast item. Factory-made frozen lo mai gai is widely sold in Asian supermarkets.
