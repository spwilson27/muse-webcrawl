Carlo Petrini (born 22 June 1948) is an Italian journalist, activist, and writer best known as the founder of the international Slow Food movement, which promotes local food traditions, sustainable agriculture, and the protection of biodiversity against the standardization of industrial fast food.

Biography

Petrini was born in Bra, in the Piedmont region of northern Italy, the son of a railway worker and a teacher. He studied sociology at the University of Trento and became involved in left-wing politics and food and wine journalism, writing for newspapers such as La Stampa and magazines including Gambero Rosso.

Slow Food

In 1986 Petrini helped lead protests against the opening of a McDonald's restaurant near the Spanish Steps in Rome. Out of that campaign grew Slow Food, formally founded in Paris in 1989 with the signing of the Slow Food Manifesto. The movement, whose symbol is the snail, defends food that is "good, clean, and fair": pleasurable and rooted in local culture, produced without harming the environment, and priced fairly for both producers and consumers.

Under Petrini's leadership, Slow Food grew into a global organization active in more than 150 countries. Its projects include the Ark of Taste, a catalogue of endangered traditional foods; the Presidia, which support small-scale producers; the Terra Madre network of food communities, launched in Turin in 2004; and the University of Gastronomic Sciences, founded the same year in Pollenzo, near Bra.

Recognition

Petrini has written numerous books, including Slow Food: The Case for Taste and Slow Food Nation. In 2016 he was appointed a Special Ambassador of the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations for Zero Hunger in Europe.
