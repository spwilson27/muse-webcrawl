Governing Senate

Established: 22 February 1711 (O.S.)
Founded by: Peter I of Russia
Abolished: 1917
Headquarters: St Petersburg

The Governing Senate (Russian: Правительствующий сенат, Pravitel'stvuyushchiy Senat) was the highest state institution of law-making, executive and judicial power in the Russian Empire, subordinate only to the emperor. It was established by Peter the Great on 22 February 1711 (5 March, New Style) to replace the Boyar Duma, and existed until the fall of the monarchy in 1917.

History

Peter created the Senate to govern the empire during his frequent absences on military campaigns. Its original nine (later ten) members were appointed by the emperor, and it supervised the administration, issued decrees in the sovereign's name, and acted as the highest court of appeal. The office of Procurator General, created in 1722 and famously described by Peter as "the eye of the sovereign," supervised the Senate's work; holders of the post, beginning with Pavel Yaguzhinsky, became among the most powerful officials in the empire.

Under Catherine II the Senate was reorganized into departments and its administrative role was curtailed. In the 19th century, after the creation of the ministries (1802), the State Council (1810) and the Committee of Ministers, the Senate became primarily the empire's supreme court and guardian of legality — the "repository of the laws" — retaining the right to remonstrate with the emperor about illegal decrees, a right emperors could and did override. Alexander II's judicial reforms of 1864 strengthened its role as the highest court of cassation.

End

The Senate continued to function under the Provisional Government in 1917 and was formally abolished by the Bolshevik decree on the courts in November–December 1917. Its archive, one of the largest of imperial Russia, is preserved in the Russian State Historical Archive in St Petersburg.
