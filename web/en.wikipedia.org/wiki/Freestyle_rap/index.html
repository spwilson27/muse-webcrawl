Freestyle rap

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Freestyle is a style of hip hop performance in which a rapper improvises lyrics spontaneously, without prior writing or memorisation — "off the top of the head". In its original usage the term described any non-topic-based verse, but in contemporary usage freestyling specifically means improvised rapping, usually performed over a beat or in a cipher (a circle of rappers taking turns), and often in competitive battle rap.

A cipher: rappers improvise in turn, feeding off the crowd and each other's lines.

Technique

Freestylers draw on stock rhymes, internal rhyme schemes, word association and material from their immediate surroundings to keep an unbroken flow of coherent, rhyming lines in rhythm. Skilled improvisers use "callbacks" (returning to earlier lines), multi-syllabic rhyme patterns and topical references to the opponent, the venue or objects held up by the audience. Brain-imaging studies of freestyle rappers have found distinctive patterns of activity in regions associated with improvisation and reduced self-monitoring.

History

Improvised rhyming grew out of the party MCing of 1970s New York hip hop, the dozens, and African-American oral traditions. In the 1980s and 1990s, radio appearances and battles — such as those on the Wake Up Show — made reputations for artists like Supernatural, Juice and Eminem. New York group Freestyle Fellowship pushed improvisation toward jazz-like virtuosity in the early 1990s. Leagues such as King of the Dot and URL later formalised battle formats, most of them written in advance, while "off the top" improvisation survives in ciphers, street performance, livestreams and improvisational comedy-rap formats.
