Minesweeper

A minesweeper is a small warship designed to remove or neutralize naval mines. Using various mechanisms intended to counter the threat posed by individual mines, minesweepers keep waterways clear for safe shipping. The term can also refer to the computer puzzle game of the same name, popularized by Microsoft Windows.

Naval vessel

Minesweepers employ mechanical sweeps — wires towed behind the ship to cut the mooring cables of tethered mines so they float to the surface and can be destroyed — and influence sweeps that simulate the magnetic or acoustic signature of a larger ship to detonate mines at a safe distance. Early minesweepers were often converted trawlers; modern vessels are built with non-magnetic hulls of wood, fiberglass, or non-magnetic steel to reduce their own signature. Dedicated minehunters, which locate mines with sonar and destroy them individually, are a related class. Minesweeping was crucial in both World Wars and in clearing the vast minefields laid in conflicts since.

Computer game

The logic puzzle game Minesweeper presents a grid of hidden mines; numbers revealed by clicking squares indicate how many mines touch that square, and the player must deduce mine locations without detonating one. A version was included with Microsoft Windows from 1992 onward, making it one of the most widely played computer games ever. Despite its simple rules, determining whether a Minesweeper board is consistent is NP-complete.
