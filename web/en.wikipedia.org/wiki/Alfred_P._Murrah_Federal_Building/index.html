The Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building was a United States federal government office complex in downtown Oklahoma City, Oklahoma. Named for federal judge Alfred P. Murrah, a native Oklahoman, it housed offices of agencies including the Secret Service, the ATF, the DEA and a children's day-care centre.

Oklahoma City bombing

At 9:02 a.m. on 19 April 1995, a truck bomb detonated by anti-government extremist Timothy McVeigh exploded in front of the building, destroying its entire north face. The attack killed 168 people, including 19 children, injured more than 680 others, and remained the deadliest act of domestic terrorism in United States history. McVeigh was executed in 2001; his accomplice Terry Nichols received life imprisonment.

Memorial

The remains of the building were demolished a month after the bombing. The site is now occupied by the Oklahoma City National Memorial, whose rows of 168 empty chairs, reflecting pool and Gates of Time commemorate the victims, survivors and rescuers. A new, hardened federal building opened nearby in 2004.
