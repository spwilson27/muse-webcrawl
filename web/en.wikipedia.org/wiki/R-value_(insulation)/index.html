R-value (insulation)

QuantityThermal resistance
US unitft²·°F·h/BTU

The R-value is a measure of how well a two-dimensional barrier, such as a layer of insulation, a window or a complete wall, resists the conductive flow of heat. The higher the R-value, the greater the insulating effectiveness. It is the ratio of the temperature difference across the insulator to the heat flux density through it.

Units

In the United States, R-values are given in ft²·°F·h/BTU; the SI equivalent, the RSI value, is in m²·K/W. R-values of layers in series simply add: a wall assembly's total is the sum of its components, including surface air films. Because installed performance depends on installation quality, air leakage and thermal bridging, the rated R-value of a product is not by itself the performance of a whole building.

Typical values

Fibreglass batts provide roughly R-3 to R-4 per inch; cellulose about R-3.5 per inch; rigid polyisocyanurate foam up to about R-6.5 per inch; and vacuum insulated panels far more, at high cost. Building codes in cold climates commonly require attic insulation of R-38 to R-60.
