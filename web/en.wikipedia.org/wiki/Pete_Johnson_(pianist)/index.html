Pete Johnson (born Kermit H. Johnson; 25 March 1904 – 23 March 1967) was an American boogie-woogie and jazz pianist. Along with Meade Lux Lewis and Albert Ammons, he was one of the three leading boogie-woogie pianists whose performances at John Hammond's From Spirituals to Swing concerts at Carnegie Hall in 1938 and 1939 helped launch the boogie-woogie craze.

Career

Johnson began as a drummer in Kansas City before switching to piano in the early 1920s, developing a powerful two-handed style rooted in stride and blues. In Kansas City he worked with blues shouter Big Joe Turner; their partnership produced the 1938 recording "Roll 'Em Pete," often cited as an early rock and roll precursor, featuring Johnson's driving piano behind Turner's vocals.

After the Carnegie Hall concerts, Johnson, Ammons, and Lewis performed together frequently, including at Café Society in New York, and recorded in various combinations. Johnson continued to record and tour through the 1940s and 1950s, though the boogie-woogie fad faded. His playing also featured on sessions with Turner and other blues and jazz artists.

Later years and death

Ill health, including a stroke in 1958 that partially paralyzed him, and the loss of part of a finger, limited Johnson's later career. He died in Buffalo, New York, in 1967. His style — with its strong left-hand walking patterns and swinging right-hand figures — influenced generations of blues, jazz, and rock and roll pianists.
