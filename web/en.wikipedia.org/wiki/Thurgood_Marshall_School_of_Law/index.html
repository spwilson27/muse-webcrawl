The Thurgood Marshall School of Law (TMSL) is the law school of Texas Southern University, a historically Black public university in Houston, Texas. Founded in 1947 as a direct consequence of segregation-era litigation, it has grown into one of the most ethnically diverse law schools in the United States and has produced an outsized share of Texas's African American judges, legislators and public officials.

History

The school owes its existence to Heman Marion Sweatt, an African American postal worker who in 1946 applied to the University of Texas School of Law and was refused admission because of his race. Rather than admit him, the State of Texas hastily created a separate university and law school for Black Texans: in 1947 the legislature founded what became Texas Southern University, and its law school opened the same year in Austin before moving to Houston. Thurgood Marshall of the NAACP argued Sweatt's case, and in Sweatt v. Painter (1950) the U.S. Supreme Court held that the makeshift separate facilities could not provide an equal legal education — a key step toward Brown v. Board of Education four years later. In 1978 the law school was renamed in Marshall's honour.

Academics and reputation

TMSL is accredited by the American Bar Association and offers the JD as well as an LL.M. in immigration and naturalization law, among other programmes. It is regularly noted for the diversity of its student body — one of the highest proportions of African American and Hispanic students among American law schools — and for a tradition of public service and access, in keeping with its founding mission to open the legal profession to students historically excluded from it.
