Downsizing (business)

In business, downsizing is the permanent reduction of a company's workforce, typically by eliminating unproductive or redundant divisions or employees. It is a common organisational strategy undertaken to reduce costs, respond to falling demand, or improve competitiveness, and may be implemented through layoffs, redundancies, early retirement schemes, attrition, or the sale or closure of business units. The term became widespread in corporate vocabulary during the restructuring waves of the 1980s and 1990s.

Motivations

Companies downsize in response to economic downturns, technological change that automates work, mergers and acquisitions that create overlapping roles, or shareholder pressure to raise margins. Related euphemisms include "rightsizing", "restructuring" and "workforce optimisation".

Effects

Research on downsizing is mixed: while it can cut costs in the short term, studies frequently find that projected savings are not realised and that downsizing can damage morale, institutional knowledge and productivity among remaining staff — a phenomenon described as "survivor syndrome". Repeated rounds of cuts may also harm a firm's reputation with customers, investors and future recruits.

Alternatives

Alternatives to layoffs include reduced hours, pay freezes or cuts, hiring freezes, redeployment and voluntary redundancy programmes, which some firms adopt to preserve skills for recovery periods.
