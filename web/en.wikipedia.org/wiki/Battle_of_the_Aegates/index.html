Battle of the Aegates

The Battle of the Aegates (or Battle of the Egadi Islands) was a naval battle fought on 10 March 241 BC between the fleets of Carthage and the Roman Republic during the First Punic War, off the Aegates Islands (modern Egadi Islands) off the western coast of Sicily. The Roman victory ended the 23-year war in Rome's favour.

Background

By 242 BC Rome, its treasury emptied, raised a new fleet by loans from wealthy citizens. Its commander, the consul Lutatius Catulus, blockaded the Carthaginian strongholds of Lilybaeum and Drepanum in western Sicily, and trained his crews intensively through the winter.

The battle

A Carthaginian relief fleet under Hanno, laden with grain and supplies for the army of Hamilcar Barca in Sicily, attempted to reach the coast before fighting. Catulus intercepted it off the Aegates on a rough morning, attacking upwind. The Romans removed masts and excess gear and manned their ships with picked marines; the overloaded, under-trained Carthaginian ships were sunk or captured in large numbers.

Aftermath

With its fleet destroyed, Carthage could no longer supply its army in Sicily and authorised Hamilcar to negotiate. By the Treaty of Lutatius, Carthage evacuated Sicily and paid a large indemnity; Sicily became Rome's first overseas province, the foundation of its Mediterranean empire.
