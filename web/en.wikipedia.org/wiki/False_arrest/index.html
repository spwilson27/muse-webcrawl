False arrest

False arrest, also known as unlawful arrest or wrongful arrest, is a common law tort in which a plaintiff alleges that they were held in custody without probable cause or without an order issued by a court of competent jurisdiction. Although it is possible to sue law enforcement officials for false arrest, the usual defendants in such cases are private security firms and private persons.

Elements

A false arrest claim generally requires that the defendant intentionally confined the plaintiff, that the plaintiff was conscious of the confinement or harmed by it, and that the confinement was not legally authorized. False arrest is a species of the broader tort of false imprisonment, differing mainly in that the confinement is presented as being under legal authority.

Defenses

The most common defense is the existence of probable cause — a reasonable belief that the person committed a crime. In many jurisdictions, shopkeepers enjoy a limited "shopkeeper's privilege" permitting brief detention of suspected shoplifters for investigation. Police officers may additionally claim qualified immunity, which shields them unless they violated clearly established law.

Remedies

Victims of false arrest may recover damages for loss of liberty, humiliation, physical harm and economic losses. In the United States, claims against government officials are frequently brought under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 for violations of the Fourth Amendment.
