Pallet

A pallet (also called a skid) is a flat transport structure that supports goods in a stable fashion while being lifted by a forklift, pallet jack or front loader. It is the structural foundation of a unit load, permitting efficient handling, stacking, storage and transport of materials. Pallets are most often made of wood, but plastic, metal, paper and recycled materials are also used.

Design and types

The two common construction styles are the stringer pallet, which uses parallel boards ("stringers") between top and bottom decks and typically allows two- or four-way fork entry, and the block pallet, which uses cylindrical or rectangular blocks and allows true four-way entry. A skid lacks bottom deck boards. Reversible, flush and winged variants serve different stacking and conveyor applications.

Sizes and standardization

ISO 6780 recognizes six standard footprints. The most widely used are the 1200 × 800 mm EUR/EPAL pallet dominant in Europe, and the 48 × 40 inch (1219 × 1016 mm) GMA pallet dominant in North America. Regional standards also prevail in Australia (1165 × 1165 mm) and Asia (1100 × 1100 mm). Because warehouse racking, trucks and shipping containers are dimensioned around pallet footprints, size standardization is a major logistics issue.

Materials and treatment

Wooden pallets account for the large majority of the hundreds of millions of pallets produced annually; they are cheap and repairable but heavy and variable. For international trade, wood packaging must comply with ISPM 15, requiring heat treatment or fumigation to prevent the spread of plant pests, and is stamped with the IPPC mark. Plastic pallets are lighter, durable and hygienic — favoured in food and pharmaceutical supply chains — while metal pallets serve heavy industry. Pallet pooling services, such as CHEP and PECO, rent and recover pallets across supply chains.

History

Simple skids date to the early 20th century; the modern pallet emerged with the forklift truck in the 1920s–1940s, and palletization was massively expanded by military logistics during the Second World War.
