The Blue Army (Polish: Błękitna Armia), or Haller's Army, was a Polish military formation created in France during the First World War. It took its nickname from the horizon-blue French uniforms it wore, and was commanded by General Józef Haller.

Formation

Authorised by French President Raymond Poincaré in June 1917, the army recruited Polish volunteers from the diaspora — including tens of thousands of Polish-Americans and Polish-Canadians who crossed the Atlantic — as well as Polish prisoners of war and emigrants. It fought on the Western Front in 1918, including in Champagne and the Vosges, under French operational command while remaining a politically Polish force allied to the Polish National Committee of Roman Dmowski.

Return to Poland

After the Armistice, in spring 1919 the Blue Army was transported by rail through Germany to newly independent Poland, bringing some 68,000 trained and equipped men — then the largest single formation of the young Polish Army. It fought in the Polish-Ukrainian War in Galicia and later in the Polish-Soviet War of 1919–21, before being merged into the regular Polish armed forces.
