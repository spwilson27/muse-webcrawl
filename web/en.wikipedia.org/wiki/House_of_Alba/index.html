House of Alba

The House of Alba (Spanish: Casa de Alba) is one of the most prominent aristocratic families of Spain, whose head holds the title Duke of Alba, created in 1472 by Henry IV of Castile for García Álvarez de Toledo. Through inheritance and marriage the house accumulated one of the largest collections of titles in the Spanish nobility — more than forty at its height — together with great estates and art collections.

History

The family's best-known member is Fernando Álvarez de Toledo, 3rd Duke of Alba (1507–1582), the feared governor of the Spanish Netherlands during the Dutch Revolt. In the eighteenth century the dukedom passed by marriage into the Fitz-James Stuart family, descendants of James II of England, and later dukes combined the Alba inheritance with the titles of Duke of Berwick and Duke of Liria and Jérica.

The house's principal residence is the Liria Palace in Madrid, and its foundation administers palaces, archives and artworks including paintings by Goya, who famously painted Cayetana Fitz-James Stuart, 13th Duchess of Alba. The 18th Duchess, Cayetana Fitz-James Stuart (1926–2014), was one of the world's most titled aristocrats; she was succeeded by her son Carlos Fitz-James Stuart, 19th Duke of Alba.
