Kosrae

The Lelu ruins, seat of Kosrae's pre-contact kings

Kosrae (formerly Kusaie) is the easternmost island and state of the Federated States of Micronesia, in the Caroline Islands of the western Pacific. A single high volcanic island of about 110 km² surrounded by a fringing reef and mangrove swamps, it has a population of roughly 6,600 and its capital is Tofol. Kosrae is the only state of the FSM consisting of one main island, and its population is overwhelmingly Christian.

History

Kosrae was settled by Austronesian voyagers over two thousand years ago. Between roughly the 14th and 17th centuries its kings, based in the artificial stone city of Lelu — built of massive prismatic basalt logs stacked into compounds on islets in Lelu harbour — ruled a stratified society of nobles and commoners. European contact began in 1824 with the French expedition of Louis Isidore Duperrey. Whalers and traders followed; devastating introduced diseases reduced the population from perhaps 10,000 to a few hundred by the late nineteenth century, and Protestant missionaries from New England reshaped the culture. Spain, then Germany (1899), then Japan (1914) administered the island; after the Second World War it became part of the U.S. Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands, and in 1986 joined the Federated States of Micronesia in free association with the United States.

Geography and economy

The island's lush, steep interior is heavily forested, with Mount Finkol (634 m) the highest point. Kosrae is known for its relatively pristine marine environment: coral reefs, blue holes, hard-coral gardens, and the wreck-free harbour attract divers, and its mangroves support the endemic Kosrae flying fox. Limes, bananas, and taro are grown; the economy depends on government employment, fishing, small-scale tourism, and U.S. Compact assistance. Kosrae International Airport is built on an artificial island in the reef flat.
