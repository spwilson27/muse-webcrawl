Gender

[Image placeholder: Gender symbols]

Gender refers to the characteristics of women, men, girls and boys that are socially constructed — including norms, behaviours, roles, and identities. It varies from society to society and can change over time. In contemporary usage, gender is distinguished from sex, which refers to biological attributes such as chromosomes, hormones, and reproductive anatomy, although historically the terms were often used interchangeably and remain contested.

Concepts

The modern distinction between sex and gender was developed in the mid-20th century by researchers such as John Money and popularised by feminists including Ann Oakley. Gender identity is a person's internal sense of their own gender; gender expression is how it is presented outwardly; gender roles are the behaviours a culture deems appropriate. Many cultures recognise more than two genders — examples include hijra in South Asia, fa'afafine in Samoa, and Two-Spirit identities among some Indigenous North American peoples.

Theoretical perspectives

Scholars debate the relative contributions of biology and socialisation. Social constructionists such as Judith Butler argue gender is performative — constituted by repeated acts rather than an inner essence — while evolutionary psychologists emphasise inherited predispositions. Most contemporary accounts treat gender as the product of interaction between biological, psychological, and social factors. Gender is a central topic in sociology, psychology, anthropology, medicine, and public policy, and questions about gender categories are the subject of ongoing public and political debate in many countries.
