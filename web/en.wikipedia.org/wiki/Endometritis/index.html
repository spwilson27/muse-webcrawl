Endometritis

SpecialtyGynaecology
SymptomsFever, lower abdominal pain, abnormal vaginal bleeding or discharge
CausesBacterial infection (often polymicrobial)
TreatmentAntibiotics

Endometritis is inflammation of the inner lining of the uterus (the endometrium). Symptoms generally include fever, lower abdominal pain, and abnormal vaginal bleeding or discharge. It is the most common cause of infection following childbirth, and is also part of the spectrum of diseases that make up pelvic inflammatory disease.

Types

Endometritis is divided into acute and chronic forms. Acute endometritis is typically caused by infection that passes through the cervix, most often following childbirth, miscarriage, or medical procedures that breach the cervical barrier; it is characterised histologically by neutrophil infiltration of the endometrium. Chronic endometritis is a persistent low-grade inflammation characterised by plasma cells in the endometrial stroma, and may be associated with abnormal uterine bleeding, infertility and recurrent pregnancy loss.

Causes

The condition results from ascending bacterial infection, frequently polymicrobial, involving organisms such as streptococci, Escherichia coli, and anaerobes. Sexually transmitted infections due to Chlamydia trachomatis or Neisseria gonorrhoeae may also be implicated. Risk factors include caesarean section, prolonged rupture of membranes, retained products of conception, and intrauterine devices.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is based on clinical findings of fever and uterine tenderness, supported by laboratory tests; chronic endometritis is confirmed by endometrial biopsy showing plasma cells. Treatment consists of broad-spectrum antibiotics, for example combinations such as clindamycin and gentamicin in the postpartum setting, and removal of any retained tissue or infected foreign body. Untreated infection may progress to parametritis, peritonitis or sepsis.
