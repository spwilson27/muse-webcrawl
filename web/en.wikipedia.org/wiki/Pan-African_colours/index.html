Pan-African colours

The Pan-African colours are two sets of three colours: green, gold, and red (inspired by the flag of Ethiopia), and black, red, and green. They are used in the flags and other symbols of various African nations and organisations, and symbolise African unity, the land, its people, and the blood shed in the struggle for liberation.

Green, gold and red

The green-gold-red tricolour derives from the flag of Ethiopia, one of the few African countries to resist European colonisation. The colours were adopted by the African National Congress and by Ghana, the first sub-Saharan African colony to gain independence, in 1957. Ghana's flag — designed by Theodosia Okoh — combined the Ethiopian colours with a black star. Many subsequent independent African states adopted these colours in their national flags, including Mali, Senegal, Cameroon, Guinea, Togo, Benin, Burkina Faso, the Republic of the Congo, Mozambique, Zimbabwe, Zambia (with additions), and Lithuania-like arrangements appear also in the flag of Guinea-Bissau.

The colours are often given symbolic meanings: green for the land and fertility, gold for mineral wealth or the sun, and red for the blood of those who fought for freedom.

Red, black and green

The second set — red, black, and green — comes from the flag adopted by the Universal Negro Improvement Association (UNIA) of Marcus Garvey in 1920, often called the Black Liberation flag or Pan-African flag. In it, black represents the people, red the blood of martyrs, and green the land of Africa. This flag became a prominent symbol of the African diaspora and of Black liberation movements throughout the twentieth century and beyond.

Usage

Beyond national flags, the colours appear in the emblems of the African Union, in pan-African sporting events, and in the dress and iconography of independence movements across the continent and its diaspora.
