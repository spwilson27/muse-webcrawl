The Davao Region, designated as Region XI, is an administrative region in the Philippines, occupying the southeastern section of Mindanao. It comprises five provinces: Davao de Oro, Davao del Norte, Davao del Sur, Davao Occidental, and Davao Oriental. The region is enclosed by the Davao Gulf, and its regional center and largest city is Davao City, the largest city in Mindanao and the third-most populous metropolitan area in the Philippines.

History

The region was originally part of a single Davao province created in 1967 when the old province was divided into Davao del Norte, Davao del Sur, and Davao Oriental. Davao de Oro (originally Compostela Valley) was carved out of Davao del Norte in 1998, and Davao Occidental, the country's youngest province, was created in 2013 from Davao del Sur. Before Spanish colonization, the area was home to indigenous Lumad groups and Moro sultanates; the gulf area came under Spanish control only in the mid-19th century, later than most of the archipelago.

Geography

Davao Region lies on the southeastern coast of Mindanao, bordering Northern Mindanao to the northwest, Soccsksargen to the west, and facing the Philippine Sea to the east. The region surrounds the Davao Gulf, into which flow the Davao River and other waterways. Mount Apo, the highest peak in the Philippines at 2,954 metres, straddles the boundary between Davao del Sur and Cotabato province. The region is generally typhoon-free, with a climate favourable to agriculture throughout the year.

Administrative divisions

  • Davao de Oro – capital: Nabunturan
  • Davao del Norte – capital: Tagum
  • Davao del Sur – capital: Digos
  • Davao Occidental – capital: Malita
  • Davao Oriental – capital: Mati

In addition, Davao City is a highly urbanized city independent of any province.

Economy

Agriculture is the region's economic backbone. Davao is one of the world's largest producers and exporters of bananas, and is also a major producer of pineapples, coconuts, cacao, coffee, and the durian fruit for which Davao City is famous. The region is a leading gold mining area, particularly in Davao de Oro. Fishing, forestry, and growing business process outsourcing and tourism sectors in Davao City also contribute significantly to the regional economy.

Demographics

The region's population was 5,243,536 in the 2020 census. Davaoeños are predominantly Visayan-speaking, with Cebuano (locally called Bisaya or Davaoeño) as the lingua franca, alongside indigenous languages including Mandaya, Mansaka, Bagobo, and Kalagan, and Moro languages along the gulf coast. The population is majority Roman Catholic, with significant Muslim and indigenous communities.
