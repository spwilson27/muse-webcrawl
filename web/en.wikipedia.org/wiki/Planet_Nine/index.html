Planet Nine is a hypothetical planet in the outer region of the Solar System. Its gravitational effects could explain the peculiar clustering of orbits for a group of extreme trans-Neptunian objects (ETNOs), bodies beyond Neptune that orbit the Sun at distances averaging more than 250 times that of the Earth.

The hypothesis

In January 2016, astronomers Konstantin Batygin and Michael E. Brown of Caltech published evidence that the orbits of six known ETNOs — including Sedna — were clustered in physical space in a way that had less than a 0.007% probability of occurring by chance, and that the clustering could be maintained by a massive, distant planet in a highly eccentric, anti-aligned orbit. Their model predicted a planet of roughly ten Earth masses (later refined to around five) orbiting hundreds of astronomical units from the Sun, likely a cold ice giant core that was scattered outward early in the Solar System's history.

Evidence and debate

Further objects were found whose orbits match the prediction, including some on highly inclined and even retrograde paths, as the model predicted. However, critics have argued that the apparent clustering may arise from observational bias — the ETNOs are found only in the parts of the sky surveys actually searched — and analyses of the Dark Energy Survey and OSSOS samples have found distributions consistent with uniformity. Alternative explanations include a massive debris disk in the outer Solar System. No telescope has yet directly observed Planet Nine; searches with WISE, Pan-STARRS, and the Subaru Telescope have constrained but not found it, and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's survey is expected to test the hypothesis decisively.

Context

Planet Nine is unrelated to earlier hypothetical planets such as Vulcan or "Planet X" in the sense of Percival Lowell's searches (which led to the discovery of Pluto), though the name echoes that history. If confirmed, it would be the first planet discovered primarily through its gravitational fingerprint since Neptune (1846, found after predictions from Uranus's orbit).
