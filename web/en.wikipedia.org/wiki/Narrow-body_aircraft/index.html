Narrow-body aircraft

A narrow-body aircraft (also called a single-aisle aircraft) is an airliner with a fuselage diameter typically of 3 to 4 metres (10 to 13 ft), allowing a single aisle with seating of up to six seats abreast (3+3) in economy class. Narrow-body aircraft contrast with wide-body aircraft, which have two aisles. The highest-capacity narrow-bodies seat around 240–295 passengers, while typical types seat 100–200.

History and role

Narrow-bodies have dominated short- and medium-haul commercial aviation since the introduction of jets such as the Boeing 707 (technically narrow-body by fuselage width), the Douglas DC-9 and the Boeing 737 — the best-selling jet airliner family in history. The Airbus A320 family rivals the 737 in the 150-seat market, and newer types such as the Airbus A220 and the extended-range A321XLR have pushed single-aisle aircraft onto longer routes, including transatlantic services once reserved for wide-bodies.

Typical types

  • Airbus A320 family (A318–A321, neo variants)
  • Airbus A220 (formerly Bombardier CSeries)
  • Boeing 737 (Original, Classic, NG and MAX series)
  • Boeing 757 (large narrow-body)
  • Embraer E-Jets and E-Jets E2
  • McDonnell Douglas DC-9 / MD-80 / Boeing 717
  • COMAC C919
  • Historical: Sud Aviation Caravelle, BAC One-Eleven, Tupolev Tu-154

Characteristics

Single-aisle layouts speed boarding and suit high-frequency short sectors; modern narrow-bodies achieve ranges from about 3,000 km (regional variants) to over 8,700 km (A321XLR). Their low trip cost relative to wide-bodies underpins the low-cost carrier business model.
