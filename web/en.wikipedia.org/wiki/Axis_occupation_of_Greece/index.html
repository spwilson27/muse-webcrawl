Axis occupation of Greece
PeriodApril 1941 – October 1944
Occupying powersNazi Germany, Fascist Italy, Bulgaria
RegimeHellenic State (collaborationist government in Athens)
Major eventsGreat Famine (1941–42); deportation of Greek Jews (1943); mass reprisals; Greek Resistance

The occupation of Greece by the Axis powers began in April 1941 after the German and Italian invasion of Greece (with Bulgaria joining) defeated the Greek and Commonwealth forces. Greece was partitioned into German, Italian and Bulgarian zones of occupation: Bulgaria annexed eastern Macedonia and western Thrace in practice, Italy administered most of the mainland and islands, and Germany held Athens, Thessaloniki, Crete and strategically important areas directly.

The occupation regime

A collaborationist government under Georgios Tsolakoglou and his successors ruled in Athens under Axis supervision. Occupation policies of economic plunder—requisitioning, a forced "occupation loan" and runaway inflation—produced the Great Famine of the winter of 1941–42, in which tens of thousands (possibly hundreds of thousands) died, especially in Athens and Piraeus.

Resistance and repression

The Greek Resistance, led principally by EAM/ELAS alongside EDES and other groups, grew into one of Europe's largest resistance movements, tying down Axis troops and liberating much of the mountainous interior. Occupation forces responded with brutal reprisals, including the massacres at Kalavryta and Distomo, and in 1943 deported the ancient Jewish community of Thessaloniki and other cities to extermination camps; more than 80% of Greek Jews were murdered. After Italy's armistice in September 1943 Germany took over the Italian zone; German forces withdrew from mainland Greece in October 1944 as the Eastern Front collapsed.
