ESP32

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A typical ESP32 development board.

The ESP32 is a low-cost, low-power system-on-chip (SoC) microcontroller series designed by the Shanghai-based company Espressif Systems. Launched in September 2016 as the successor to the ESP8266, the ESP32 integrates Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz, 802.11 b/g/n) and dual-mode Bluetooth (classic Bluetooth and Bluetooth Low Energy) radios alongside one or two processor cores, making it one of the most popular platforms for Internet of Things devices, hobbyist electronics and embedded products.

Features

CPUXtensa dual-core (or single-core) LX6, up to 240 MHz
Memory520 KiB SRAM; external flash via QSPI
WirelessWi-Fi 802.11 b/g/n; Bluetooth v4.2 BR/EDR and BLE
PeripheralsUp to 34 GPIOs, ADC, DAC, capacitive touch, CAN, Ethernet MAC, Hall sensor
PowerDeep-sleep current around 10 µA (with ULP co-processor)

An ultra-low-power (ULP) co-processor can monitor peripherals while the main cores sleep, and hardware acceleration is provided for AES, SHA, RSA and ECC cryptography, together with secure boot and flash encryption features. Later variants include the ESP32-S2, ESP32-S3 (adding vector instructions aimed at machine learning), ESP32-C series based on RISC-V cores, and the ESP32-H series with Zigbee/Thread radios.

Software

Espressif provides the ESP-IDF development framework (C/C++). The chip is also supported by the Arduino core, MicroPython, CircuitPython, ESPHome and many other environments, which has made it a favourite of the maker community. Development boards such as the ESP32-DevKitC are sold for a few US dollars, and modules (WROOM, WROVER series) carry regulatory certification for embedding in commercial products.
