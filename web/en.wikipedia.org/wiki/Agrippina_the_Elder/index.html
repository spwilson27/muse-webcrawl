Agrippina the Elder

Agrippina the Elder (also Agrippina Major; c. 14 BC – AD 33) was a prominent member of the Julio-Claudian dynasty of the Roman Empire. She was the daughter of Marcus Vipsanius Agrippa and Julia the Elder, granddaughter of the emperor Augustus, wife of the general Germanicus, and mother of the emperor Caligula and of Agrippina the Younger.

Life

Agrippina married Germanicus around AD 5 and accompanied him on campaign in Germania and the East, an unusual public role that won her great popularity with the legions; Tacitus portrays her rallying troops at the Rhine bridge in AD 15. After Germanicus died at Antioch in AD 19, she accused the governor Gnaeus Calpurnius Piso of poisoning him with the connivance of the emperor Tiberius, and carried her husband's ashes back to Rome in a famous procession.

Exile and death

Her open enmity with Tiberius and the praetorian prefect Sejanus made her a focus of opposition. In AD 29 she was charged and exiled to the island of Pandateria, where she died in AD 33, reportedly of starvation. Her sons Nero Caesar and Drusus Caesar also perished in Tiberius's purges. When her son Caligula became emperor in AD 37, he brought her ashes back to Rome with great ceremony and placed them in the Mausoleum of Augustus.
