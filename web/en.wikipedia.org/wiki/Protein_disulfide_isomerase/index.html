Protein disulfide isomerase (PDI) is a family of enzymes in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) of eukaryotes that catalyze the formation and breakage of disulfide bonds between cysteine residues within proteins as they fold. This allows proteins to find the correct arrangement of disulfide bonds in their fully folded state, so the enzyme acts as a chaperone as well as a catalyst of oxidative folding.

Structure

The prototypical family member, PDIA1 (encoded by P4HB), consists of four thioredoxin-like domains arranged in a U shape (a–b–b′–a′), followed by a C-terminal extension bearing an ER-retention KDEL signal. The a and a′ domains each contain a CGHC active-site motif whose cysteine thiols cycle between reduced and oxidized states; the b′ domain provides the principal substrate-binding site.

Function

PDI introduces disulfide bonds into newly synthesized secretory and membrane proteins, reduces incorrect ones, and isomerizes them until the native pattern is achieved. It is re-oxidized in the ER largely by the sulfhydryl oxidase Ero1, which passes electrons ultimately to molecular oxygen. PDI also functions as the beta subunit of prolyl 4-hydroxylase and of microsomal triglyceride transfer protein. On the cell surface and in platelets, extracellular PDI participates in thrombus formation by modulating integrin and tissue-factor activity, making it a target of antithrombotic drug research.

Family members

Humans have more than 20 PDI-family proteins, including ERp57 (PDIA3), which works with the lectin chaperones calnexin and calreticulin on glycoproteins and in MHC class I antigen presentation; ERp72; PDIp, enriched in the pancreas; and the transmembrane TMX proteins.

Clinical relevance

PDI is up-regulated during the unfolded protein response. Inhibiting surface PDI reduces thrombosis in animal models, and certain flavonoid compounds that block PDI have entered clinical trials as antithrombotics. Aberrant PDI activity has also been linked to neurodegenerative diseases, in which S-nitrosylation of PDI impairs its protective function.
