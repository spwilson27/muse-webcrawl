Evapotranspiration (ET) is the combined process by which water is transferred from the Earth's surface to the atmosphere through evaporation from soil, water bodies, and intercepted precipitation, and transpiration from plants, in which water drawn from the soil is released as vapour through stomata in the leaves. It is a major component of the water cycle: on average, roughly 60% of precipitation falling on land is returned to the atmosphere by evapotranspiration.

Types and estimation

Potential evapotranspiration is the amount that would occur with unlimited water supply; reference evapotranspiration (ET₀) is defined for a standardised well-watered grass surface. The FAO-56 Penman–Monteith equation, which combines net radiation, temperature, wind speed, and vapour pressure deficit, is the standard method for estimating ET₀ from weather data. Actual crop evapotranspiration is obtained by applying crop coefficients (Kc) that vary with growth stage.

Applications

Evapotranspiration estimates are fundamental to irrigation scheduling, drought monitoring, watershed water-balance studies, and climate modelling. In agriculture, knowing crop water use allows precise irrigation that conserves water; in hydrology, ET is the largest outgoing flux in many catchment budgets. Satellite remote-sensing products such as MODIS-based MOD16 now map evapotranspiration globally.
