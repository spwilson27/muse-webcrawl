Mummification is the process of preserving a body — human or animal — after death by deliberate drying and chemical treatment, or by unusual natural conditions. It is most famously associated with ancient Egypt, where it was practised for some three thousand years as part of funerary religion centred on the afterlife.

The Egyptian process

In the classical process described by Herodotus and confirmed by modern study, embalmers first removed the internal organs most prone to decay — the lungs, liver, stomach and intestines — which were preserved separately in canopic jars; the brain was commonly extracted through the nose and discarded, while the heart was left in place as the seat of thought and soul. The body was then packed in and covered with natron, a natural salt mixture, for about 40 days to desiccate it completely. After washing, the body was anointed with oils and resins, stuffed to restore its shape, and wrapped in hundreds of metres of linen bandages, with amulets placed between the layers and a funerary mask over the head. The whole rite lasted about 70 days and concluded with the "Opening of the Mouth" ceremony before burial.

Other traditions

The oldest deliberate mummies are not Egyptian but those of the Chinchorro culture of the Atacama Desert coast (present-day Chile and Peru), dating from about 5000 BCE — some two millennia before Egypt's earliest artificial mummies. The Guanches of the Canary Islands embalmed their dead; Andean peoples preserved Inca royal mummies (mallqui) that continued to "own" property and be consulted. Natural mummification occurs in extreme dryness, cold or anaerobic bogs — producing finds such as Ötzi the Iceman and the bog bodies of northern Europe.

Modern study

Since the 19th century mummies have been studied by radiography and CT scanning, which reveal age, disease, diet and embalming technique without unwrapping; DNA analysis has reconstructed family relationships among Egyptian royal mummies. Experimental archaeology — notably the 1994 mummification of a donated human body at the University of Maryland using ancient methods — has tested the ancient accounts directly.
