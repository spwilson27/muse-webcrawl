Confusion

Confusion is the state of being bewildered or unclear in one's mind about something. In medicine, confusion is a symptom of disturbed orientation, attention and cognition, in which a person may be disoriented in time, place or identity, have difficulty following conversation, and show impaired memory or perception. Acute confusion is a hallmark of delirium, a medical emergency, while chronic confusion occurs in dementia and other neurological conditions.

Confusion denotes bewilderment in everyday life and a specific cognitive symptom in medicine.

Causes

Medical causes of confusion include infection (especially urinary tract infection and pneumonia in older people), metabolic disturbances such as hypoglycaemia or electrolyte imbalance, stroke, head injury, seizures, medication effects and drug or alcohol intoxication or withdrawal. Standard assessment tools include the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) for delirium and cognitive screening tests such as the Abbreviated Mental Test Score and the Mini-Mental State Examination.

In psychology

In ordinary psychological usage, confusion describes the subjective experience of not understanding — arising when information is contradictory, ambiguous or exceeds processing capacity. It plays a recognised role in learning: encountering confusion can signal a gap in knowledge and motivate inquiry, a theme explored in research on cognitive disequilibrium.
