Transport in London, the capital of England and the United Kingdom, has a complex and extensive network of road, rail, air and river services. Responsibility rests largely with Transport for London (TfL), the local government body controlled by the Mayor of London, which runs the London Underground, London Buses, the Docklands Light Railway, London Overground, the Elizabeth line (with MTR), London Trams, river services and the Santander Cycles hire scheme.

Rail and Underground

The London Underground — the "Tube" — opened in 1863 as the world's first underground railway and now has 11 lines serving 272 stations. Suburban and intercity rail radiates from termini including Waterloo, Euston, King's Cross and London Bridge, and the Elizabeth line (Crossrail), fully opened in 2023, added a high-capacity east–west route through the centre. London's buses were explicitly excluded from the deregulation of the Transport Act 1985 and operate as franchises specified by TfL, recognisable by their universal red livery.

Roads, air and river

London introduced the congestion charge in 2003 and the Ultra Low Emission Zone (ULEZ), expanded London-wide in 2023. Six airports serve the city, Heathrow being the busiest in Europe by passenger traffic. The Thames carries commuter river buses and freight, and cable cars (the IFS Cloud Cable Car) cross the river at Greenwich Peninsula.
