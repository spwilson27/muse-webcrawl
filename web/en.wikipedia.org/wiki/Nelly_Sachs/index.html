Nelly Sachs (10 December 1891 – 12 May 1970) was a German–Swedish poet and playwright. Her experiences resulting from the rise of the Nazis in World War II Europe transformed her into a poignant spokesperson for the grief and yearnings of her fellow Jewish inhabitants. Her best-known play is Eli: Ein Mysterienspiel vom Leiden Israels (1950); her poems include "O the Chimneys" (1947). In 1966 she was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature, jointly with Shmuel Yosef Agnon, for her "outstanding lyrical and dramatic writing, which interprets Israel's destiny with touching strength".

Biography

Leonie ("Nelly") Sachs was born into a wealthy, assimilated Jewish family in Berlin-Schöneberg in 1891. A delicate child, educated at home, she began writing poetry and prose as a teenager, corresponding for fifteen years with the Swedish novelist Selma Lagerlöf, whom she admired. Her early work was neo-Romantic and attracted little attention.

As Nazi persecution intensified, Sachs and her widowed mother prepared to leave Germany. In May 1940, helped by Lagerlöf's intervention with the Swedish royal family, they escaped to Stockholm on one of the last flights out — reportedly days before a summons to a labour camp arrived. In Sweden, living in poverty in a small apartment, Sachs learned Swedish, translated Swedish poetry into German, and — learning of the extermination camps — found her mature voice, writing the poems of In den Wohnungen des Todes ("In the Dwellings of Death", 1947), containing the famous "O die Schornsteine" ("O the Chimneys").

Later life and honours

Sachs became a Swedish citizen in 1952. Her work was championed in Germany by Hans Magnus Enzensberger and others; she received the Droste Prize (1960) and, in 1965, was the first woman to receive the Peace Prize of the German Book Trade. On her 75th birthday in 1966 she shared the Nobel Prize in Literature with the Israeli writer S. Y. Agnon. Troubled throughout her later years by severe mental illness and fears of persecution, she spent long periods in psychiatric care. She died of intestinal cancer in Stockholm on 12 May 1970.
