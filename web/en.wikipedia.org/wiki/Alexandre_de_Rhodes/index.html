Alexandre de Rhodes (15 March 1591 – 5 November 1660) was a French Jesuit missionary who worked in Vietnam (then divided between Cochinchina and Tonkin) and is credited with systematising the romanised writing system for the Vietnamese language, chữ Quốc ngữ, which eventually became Vietnam's national script.

Missions in Vietnam

De Rhodes arrived in Cochinchina in 1624 and, after being expelled, moved north to Tonkin in 1627, where he enjoyed initial success at the court in Hanoi before a royal edict expelled the missionaries in 1630. Returning to the south in 1640, he worked there until a death sentence for preaching — commuted to exile — drove him out definitively in 1646. He reported thousands of converts and trained Vietnamese catechists, an approach based on native clergy that shaped his later advocacy in Europe.

The Vietnamese dictionary and script

In Rome in 1651 de Rhodes published the Dictionarium Annamiticum Lusitanum et Latinum, a Vietnamese–Portuguese–Latin dictionary, together with a grammar and a catechism (Phép giảng tám ngày) printed in the romanised script with its full system of diacritics for tones. Building on the work of earlier Portuguese Jesuits such as Gaspar do Amaral and Francisco de Pina, de Rhodes codified the orthography that, refined over the following centuries and promoted under French colonial rule and by Vietnamese reformers, became the standard written form of Vietnamese.

Paris Foreign Missions and death

Sent back to Europe to plead for bishops and funds for the Asian missions, de Rhodes helped inspire the founding of the Paris Foreign Missions Society (Missions Étrangères de Paris) and the appointment of apostolic vicars for Vietnam and China — a landmark in moving missions away from Portuguese royal patronage. Prevented from returning to Vietnam, he was sent to Persia, where he died at Isfahan in 1660.
