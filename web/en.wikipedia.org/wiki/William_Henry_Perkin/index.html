William Henry Perkin

Born: 12 March 1838, London, England
Died: 14 July 1907, Harrow, England
Known for: Mauveine, the first synthetic dye; Perkin reaction; Perkin triangle
Field: Organic chemistry

Sir William Henry Perkin (12 March 1838 – 14 July 1907) was a British chemist best known for his accidental discovery, at the age of 18, of mauveine — the first commercial synthetic organic dye — an achievement that launched the synthetic dye industry and, with it, much of the modern chemical industry.

Discovery of mauveine

Perkin studied at the Royal College of Chemistry under August Wilhelm von Hofmann. In 1856, while attempting to synthesise quinine by oxidising aniline-derived compounds in his home laboratory during the Easter holidays, he obtained a black precipitate that dissolved in alcohol to give an intense purple solution which dyed silk effectively. He patented the dye, named mauveine (or aniline purple), in August 1856.

The dye industry

With his father and brother, Perkin built a factory at Greenford Green near London and began manufacturing the dye in 1857. "Mauve" became highly fashionable, and Perkin's success spurred the rapid growth of synthetic dyestuffs, which soon displaced natural dyes. He later developed other colours, including Britannia Violet and Perkin's Green, and a commercial route to alizarin.

Later work

Perkin sold the factory in 1873 and devoted himself to research, discovering the Perkin reaction for synthesising cinnamic acid derivatives and giving his name to the Perkin triangle distillation apparatus. He was knighted in 1906, the fiftieth anniversary of mauveine, and the Society of Chemical Industry created the Perkin Medal in his honour the same year.
