Longleat is an English stately home and the seat of the Marquesses of Bath, situated near the town of Warminster in Wiltshire. The house, built mainly between 1568 and 1580 for Sir John Thynne, is a leading example of Elizabethan prodigy house architecture, set in 1,000 acres of parkland landscaped by Capability Brown.

History

The site was originally an Augustinian priory. After the Dissolution, the estate was bought by John Thynne in 1541, and his great house rose over the following decades. The estate passed down the Thynne family, later Marquesses of Bath. In 1949 Longleat became one of the first stately homes in Britain opened commercially to the public.

Safari park

In 1966, the 6th Marquess opened the first drive-through safari park outside Africa in the grounds, famous for its lions ("The Lions of Longleat") and monkeys. The park has since expanded to include many other species and attractions, including a hedge maze, and Longleat remains a major visitor attraction.
