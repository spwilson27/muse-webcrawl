Insite

Location: 139 East Hastings Street, Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada
Opened: September 2003
Operated by: Vancouver Coastal Health / PHS Community Services

Insite is a supervised drug injection site in the Downtown Eastside of Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. The service, located at 139 East Hastings Street, has operated since September 2003 and was the first legal supervised injection facility in North America. Users bring their own pre-obtained drugs, and inject under the supervision of nurses who provide sterile equipment, monitor for overdoses, and offer referrals to treatment and other services.

History

Insite opened amid an epidemic of overdose deaths and HIV infections among injection drug users in the Downtown Eastside, following years of advocacy by groups including the Vancouver Area Network of Drug Users (VANDU). It initially operated under a federal exemption from Canada's Controlled Drugs and Substances Act granted for research purposes.

Legal battles

When the federal government declined to renew the exemption unconditionally, supporters sued. In 2011 the Supreme Court of Canada ruled in Canada (Attorney General) v. PHS Community Services Society that refusing the exemption violated users' rights to life, liberty and security of the person under section 7 of the Charter, and ordered the minister to grant the exemption. The decision was a landmark in Canadian harm-reduction law.

Research and impact

Dozens of peer-reviewed studies have evaluated Insite, finding associations with reduced public injecting, fewer discarded syringes, increased uptake of detox and treatment, and no increase in crime or drug use in the surrounding area. Research published in The Lancet in 2011 found a 35 percent reduction in overdose deaths in the immediate vicinity after the facility opened, compared with 9 percent in the rest of Vancouver. The model has since been adopted by other Canadian cities, especially during the opioid overdose crisis.
