Reflux

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Reflux is a technique in chemistry involving the condensation of vapours and the return of the condensate to the system from which it originated. A reaction mixture is heated in a vessel fitted with a condenser so that solvent can boil indefinitely without loss, allowing reactions to be run at the solvent's boiling point for extended periods.

FieldChemistry, chemical engineering
ApparatusFlask, reflux condenser, heat source
PurposeHeating a reaction without solvent loss

Laboratory use

In the laboratory, a reaction mixture in a round-bottomed flask is heated while a water-cooled condenser (such as a Liebig or Allihn condenser) mounted vertically above it condenses escaping vapour, which drips back into the flask. Reflux is used to carry out slow reactions at elevated temperature, to dissolve solids, and in the operation of Soxhlet extractors. Anti-bumping granules are often added to promote smooth boiling.

Industrial distillation

In industrial fractionation, reflux refers to the portion of condensed overhead product returned to the distillation column. The reflux ratio — returned liquid divided by withdrawn product — is a key operating variable: higher reflux improves separation between fractions but consumes more energy and reduces throughput. Refineries and petrochemical plants use reflux extensively in fractionating towers.
