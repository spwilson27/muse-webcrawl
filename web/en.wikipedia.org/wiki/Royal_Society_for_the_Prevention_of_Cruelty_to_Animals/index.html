The Royal Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (RSPCA) is a charity operating in England and Wales that promotes animal welfare. Founded in 1824, it is the oldest and largest animal welfare organisation in the world, and inspired counterpart societies in Scotland (SSPCA), Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa and the United States (ASPCA).

History

The society was founded at Old Slaughter's Coffee House in London by a group including Richard Martin MP — who had steered the first animal cruelty act through Parliament in 1822 — the anti-slavery campaigner William Wilberforce, and the Reverend Arthur Broome, its first secretary. Queen Victoria gave the society its Royal prefix in 1840. Its uniformed inspectors, first employed in the 1820s–30s, became the model for animal protection enforcement worldwide.

Work

The RSPCA operates animal centres and hospitals, rescues and rehomes neglected and abandoned animals, and its inspectorate investigates complaints of cruelty, historically bringing private prosecutions under the Animal Welfare Act 2006. It campaigns on issues including farm animal welfare (through the RSPCA Assured labelling scheme), wildlife, animals in research and pet ownership. The charity is funded entirely by donations and legacies.
