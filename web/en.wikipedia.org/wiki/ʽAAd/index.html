ʽAAd

This article is about the abbreviation rendered with an initial modifier letter reversed comma (ʽ). For the lower-case form, see ʽAad.

ʽAAd — in transliteration systems for South Arabian and related ancient languages, the form is used when citing material written with the letter ʿ (ʿayin) followed by the sequence conventionally printed in capitals in epigraphic publications. In databases of Ancient South Arabian inscriptions, ʽAAd may refer to catalogue entries in which the trigram is part of a museum or corpus siglum.

Usage

The initial character ʽ (U+02BD, MODIFIER LETTER REVERSED COMMA) is the standard transliteration sign for the voiced pharyngeal fricative /ʕ/ in Semitic studies. When combined with the capitalised sequence "AAd", the string appears in bibliographic and epigraphic contexts as a heading under which references to inscriptions, personal names and place names containing the consonantal skeleton ʿ-ʾ-d are collected.
