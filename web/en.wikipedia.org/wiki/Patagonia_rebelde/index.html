Patagonia rebelde

Patagonia rebelde (Spanish, "Rebel Patagonia"), also called Patagonia trágica ("Tragic Patagonia"), is the name given to the uprising and subsequent violent repression of striking rural workers (peones) in Argentina's Santa Cruz Province between 1920 and 1922. The workers, organized largely by anarcho-syndicalist unions affiliated with the Sociedad Obrera de Río Gallegos and the FORA, struck for better wages and humane working conditions on the great sheep ranches (estancias) of Patagonia. After two waves of strikes, the Argentine Army, under Lieutenant Colonel Héctor Benigno Varela, crushed the movement in a campaign of mass arrests and summary executions. Estimates of the number of workers killed range from about 300 to 1,500.

Background

After the First World War, the price of wool collapsed, and estancieros (ranch owners) cut wages that were already extremely low. Rural peons lived in harsh conditions — long hours, payment partly in vouchers redeemable only at company stores, crowded sleeping quarters, and no days of rest. The Sociedad Obrera, led by the Spanish anarchist Antonio Soto ("el Gallego Soto"), organized strikes in Río Gallegos and the countryside from late 1920. The first strike ended with a negotiated agreement in early 1921, but landowners largely failed to honour it, and friction continued through the year.

The second strike and repression

In October–November 1921 a general strike spread across Santa Cruz. President Hipólito Yrigoyen's government dispatched the 10th Cavalry Regiment under Varela. After an initial engagement and negotiations, many strikers surrendered on promises of fair treatment; instead, large numbers were shot at estancias such as El Cerrito, La Anita, and José Menéndez (Estancia Bella Vista). Others were executed after capture following the defeat of José Font's ("Facón Grande") column at the Tehuelches railway station in December 1921. Antonio Soto escaped across the mountains into Chile.

Aftermath and legacy

No one was prosecuted for the killings; Varela himself was assassinated in Buenos Aires in January 1923 by the anarchist Kurt Gustav Wilckens in revenge for the massacre. The events were largely suppressed in official history for decades. They were brought back to public attention by historian Osvaldo Bayer's multi-volume investigation Los vengadores de la Patagonia trágica (1972–1978) and by Héctor Olivera's 1974 film La Patagonia rebelde, which won the Silver Bear at the Berlin International Film Festival. Memorials at the sites of the executions, including at the La Anita estancia near El Calafate, commemorate the victims.
