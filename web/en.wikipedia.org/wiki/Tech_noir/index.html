Tech noir
Genre: Hybrid — science fiction / film noir
Themes: Dystopia, technology, moral ambiguity
Key works: Blade Runner, The Terminator

Tech noir (also known as future noir, science fiction noir, and cyber noir) is a hybrid genre combining film noir and science fiction, depicting technology as a destructive or dystopian force and featuring morally ambiguous protagonists in bleak, often urban futures.

Characteristics

Tech noir works borrow noir's low-key lighting, cynical antiheroes, crime plots, and fatalism, and science fiction's advanced technology, artificial humans, and surveillance states. Recurring questions concern what makes a person human and whether technological progress erodes identity and freedom.

History and examples

The term is associated with the nightclub named Tech Noir in James Cameron's The Terminator (1984). Ridley Scott's Blade Runner (1982) is the defining example. Other examples include Alphaville (1965), Dark City, Gattaca, Minority Report, and the anime Ghost in the Shell. The genre overlaps heavily with cyberpunk, sharing its high-tech, low-life settings.
