Douglas (Manx: Doolish) is the capital and largest city of the Isle of Man, a Crown Dependency in the Irish Sea. With a population of about 26,677 at the 2021 census, roughly a third of the island's residents live there. The city is the island's main centre of business, finance, shipping, transport, shopping and entertainment, and the seat of the island's government, the Tynwald.

The name comes from the Manx Dubh-ghlais, "black stream", referring to the dark waters of the River Douglas, at whose mouth the town grew up.

History

Douglas began as a small fishing and trading settlement around its natural harbour. It grew rapidly in the late 18th and 19th centuries, first through smuggling and the herring trade, then as a Victorian holiday resort served by steamships from Liverpool and Fleetwood. The sweeping two-mile promenade around Douglas Bay, lined with hotels and guesthouses, dates largely from this period. The Tower of Refuge, a small castellated structure built on St Mary's Isle (Conister Rock) in 1832 after a shipwreck, remains a landmark in the bay.

Landmarks and transport

Douglas is home to the Manx Museum, the Gaiety Theatre (designed by Frank Matcham, opened 1900), and the legislative buildings housing the House of Keys and Legislative Council. The Douglas Bay Horse Tramway, opened in 1876, runs along the promenade and is the oldest surviving horse tram service in the world. The city is also the southern terminus of the Manx Electric Railway to Ramsey and the Isle of Man Steam Railway to Port Erin, and hosts the island's main ferry terminal with services to Heysham and Liverpool.

Economy

Modern Douglas is an offshore financial centre; banking, insurance, e-gaming and fiduciary services dominate employment, while tourism remains significant, peaking during the annual TT motorcycle races, whose start/finish line and grandstand are on Glencrutchery Road in the city.
