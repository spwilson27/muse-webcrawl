Wild Geese

The Wild Geese were Irish soldiers who emigrated to serve in continental European armies after the Treaty of Limerick (1691), which ended the Williamite War in Ireland. The exodus — the "Flight of the Wild Geese" — carried Patrick Sarsfield and some 14,000 Jacobite troops to France.

Service abroad

Irish brigades served France for a century, fighting at Fontenoy (1745), where the Irish Brigade's charge was decisive; Irish regiments also served Spain, Austria, Russia and later the Papal States, and Irish officers rose to high rank, including Peter Lacy in Russia and numerous counts of the Habsburg and Bourbon courts. Recruitment continued despite English penal laws forbidding it.

Later echoes

The term extended to Irish military emigrants generally — in the American Civil War, the British Army's Irish regiments, and the 1960s Congo mercenary unit echoed in the 1978 film The Wild Geese.
