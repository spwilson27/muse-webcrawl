Beaked whales are cetaceans of the family Ziphiidae, the second-largest family of toothed whales, comprising more than twenty species in six genera, including Ziphius (Cuvier's beaked whale), Hyperoodon (bottlenose whales), Mesoplodon (mesoplodont whales) and Berardius. They are named for their distinct, dolphin-like beaks. Beaked whales are among the least known of all large mammals because they live in deep offshore waters, dive for long periods, and are shy of boats; several species are known mainly from stranded specimens, and new species have been described well into the twenty-first century.

Characteristics

Most beaked whales have greatly reduced dentition: females are effectively toothless, while adult males typically bear a single pair of tusks in the lower jaw, whose shape differs between species and is used in identification and probably in male combat — males are often heavily scarred. They feed mainly on deep-water squid and fish, captured by suction feeding. They are champion divers: tagged Cuvier's beaked whales have reached depths of nearly 3,000 metres and stayed submerged for over three hours, the deepest and longest dives recorded for any mammal.

Behaviour and ecology

Beaked whales live in small groups in deep water beyond the continental shelf edge, surfacing only briefly and inconspicuously, which makes them difficult to study at sea. They echolocate with unusual clicks while foraging at depth but are largely silent near the surface, possibly to avoid killer whales.

Conservation

Mass strandings of beaked whales have repeatedly coincided with naval exercises using mid-frequency active sonar, and necropsies have found gas-bubble injuries consistent with decompression-like damage, leading several countries to restrict sonar use in sensitive areas. Most species are listed as Data Deficient by the IUCN because so little is known of their populations. Other threats include plastic ingestion — a stranded Cuvier's beaked whale in the Philippines was found with 40 kg of plastic in its stomach.
