Battle of Lyndanisse

The Battle of Lyndanisse (also spelled Lindanise or Lyndanise) was fought on 15 June 1219 near present-day Tallinn, Estonia, during the Northern Crusades. A Danish expedition led by King Valdemar II of Denmark, accompanied by German crusaders and vassals, defeated the forces of the Estonian counties of Revalia and Harria. According to legend, the Danish flag, the Dannebrog, fell from the sky during the battle and turned defeat into victory.

Background

Valdemar II, at the height of Danish power in the Baltic, sailed to Estonia in 1219 with a large fleet — tradition says 1,500 ships — including contingents of Germans and Slavic allies, and Archbishop Anders Sunesen. The expedition landed at Lyndanisse, an Estonian stronghold on the hill of Toompea, where the Danes began building a castle, the Castrum Danorum, from which Tallinn ("Danish town") takes its name.

The battle

Estonian forces, having first appeared to negotiate and accept baptism, attacked the Danish camp from several directions on 15 June. The assault caught the crusaders off guard and at first drove them back; one body of Estonians mistook the camp of Bishop Theoderich for the king's and was thrown back when the fighting intensified around the Danish centre. Danish and German troops, aided by their Wenden allies holding a position near the shore, eventually counter-attacked and routed the Estonians, who lost, by chronicle accounts, around a thousand men.

Legacy

The victory founded Danish Estonia, ruled from Reval (Tallinn) until Denmark sold the territory to the Teutonic Order in 1346. The battle's enduring fame rests on the Dannebrog legend: as the Danes faltered, a red flag with a white cross was said to have fallen from heaven into the hands of Archbishop Sunesen, whose prayers sustained it; when the flag advanced, the Danes prevailed. The story, first recorded centuries later, made the Dannebrog — among the world's oldest national flags in continuous use — a central symbol of Denmark, and 15 June is celebrated as Valdemar's Day.
