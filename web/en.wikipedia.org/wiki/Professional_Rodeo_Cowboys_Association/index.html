Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association

The Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA) is the largest and oldest rodeo sanctioning body in the world, headquartered in Colorado Springs, Colorado. It sanctions roughly 650 rodeos annually, primarily in the United States and Canada, and its members compete for world championships decided at the National Finals Rodeo (NFR).

History

The organisation was founded in 1936 as the Cowboys' Turtle Association — the cowboys called themselves turtles for being slow to organise but willing to stick their necks out — after competitors struck at the Boston Garden rodeo over prize money and judging. It became the Rodeo Cowboys Association in 1945 and adopted the PRCA name in 1975. The first National Finals Rodeo was held in 1959; since 1985 it has taken place in Las Vegas, Nevada, each December.

Events and championships

Standard PRCA events are bareback riding, steer wrestling, team roping, saddle bronc riding, tie-down roping, barrel racing (sanctioned with the Women's Professional Rodeo Association), bull riding and steer roping. Season earnings determine the top 15 competitors per event who qualify for the NFR, where average and world titles are awarded. The PRCA also operates the ProRodeo Hall of Fame in Colorado Springs.
