Intellectual

An intellectual is a person who engages in critical thinking, research, and reflection about the nature of reality to guide transformation of society and propose solutions for its normative problems.

History

The modern term gained prominence during the Dreyfus Affair in France (1898), when writers such as Émile Zola intervened publicly. Earlier equivalents include the literati in China, ulama, and Enlightenment philosophes.

Public intellectuals

Public intellectuals address a general audience on political and cultural questions. Debates concern their independence from power, specialisation versus generalism, and the role of universities. Related concepts include tacit knowledge and the intelligentsia in Russia and Eastern Europe.

Criticism

Critics have accused intellectuals of abstraction, ideological bias, or detachment from practical life, while defenders stress their role in holding power to account.
