Baltic song festivals

Baltic song festivals (image placeholder)

The Baltic song festivals are large-scale choral song and dance celebrations held in Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania, generally every five years, drawing tens of thousands of singers performing together on enormous outdoor stages before audiences of similar size.

History

The tradition began during the 19th-century national awakening: the first Estonian Song Festival was held in Tartu in 1869, followed by the first Latvian festival in Riga in 1873 and the first Lithuanian festival in Kaunas in 1924. Under Soviet occupation the festivals were ideologically controlled but remained powerful expressions of national identity, culminating in the "Singing Revolution" of 1987–1991, when mass singing gatherings helped drive the Baltic states' restoration of independence.

Recognition

In 2003 UNESCO proclaimed the Baltic song and dance celebrations a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity; they were inscribed on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008.
