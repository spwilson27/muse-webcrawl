History of software

The history of software traces the instructions that run on computers, from Ada Lovelace's 1840s algorithm for the Analytical Engine to today's cloud and AI systems. Early programs were wired or entered on punched cards and paper tape.

Languages and operating systems

The 1950s produced the first high-level languages — Fortran (1957), Lisp (1958), and COBOL (1959) — and early operating systems. The term "software engineering" was popularised at a 1968 NATO conference amid concern over the "software crisis."

Personal computing

The microcomputer era brought packaged software, Microsoft and Apple, spreadsheets such as VisiCalc, and graphical user interfaces. The IBM PC standard and MS-DOS, then Windows, made software a mass-market industry.

Internet and open source

The World Wide Web, the free and open-source software movement (GNU, Linux), mobile apps, and cloud computing reshaped how software is written, distributed, and sold, culminating in software-as-a-service and machine-learning systems. See also Software.
