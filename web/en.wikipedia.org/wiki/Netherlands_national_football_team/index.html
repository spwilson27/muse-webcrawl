The Netherlands national football team represents the Netherlands in international association football and is administered by the Royal Dutch Football Association (KNVB). Nicknamed Oranje after the House of Orange-Nassau and the team's distinctive orange shirts, the Netherlands is regarded as one of the strongest national sides never to have won the FIFA World Cup, having finished as runners-up in 1974, 1978 and 2010.

History

The Netherlands played its first international match in 1905 against Belgium. The team's golden era arrived in the 1970s under coach Rinus Michels, when the 'Total Football' system, in which players fluidly exchanged positions, carried the side led by Johan Cruyff to consecutive World Cup finals, losing to hosts West Germany in 1974 and Argentina in 1978.

A second great generation, featuring Marco van Basten, Ruud Gullit and Frank Rijkaard, won the country's only major trophy at UEFA Euro 1988 in West Germany, defeating the Soviet Union 2–0 in the final.

Recent decades

The Netherlands reached the World Cup final again in 2010, losing to Spain in extra time, and finished third in 2014 under Louis van Gaal. The team has endured fluctuating fortunes since, missing Euro 2016 and the 2018 World Cup before returning to major tournaments under Ronald Koeman and reaching the semi-finals of Euro 2024.

Famous players associated with the team include Johan Cruyff, Marco van Basten, Dennis Bergkamp, Arjen Robben, Robin van Persie, Wesley Sneijder and Virgil van Dijk.

Style and culture

Dutch football is internationally associated with technically skilled, attack-minded play and with the tactical legacy of Total Football and the Ajax academy system. The team traditionally plays in orange shirts, white shorts and orange socks, and enjoys intense rivalries with Germany and Belgium.
