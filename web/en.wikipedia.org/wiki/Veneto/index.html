Veneto

Veneto is one of the 20 regions of Italy, in the north-east of the country, with a population of about 4.9 million. Its capital is Venice, while its largest city is Verona. It is one of Italy's wealthiest and most industrialised regions, and a major tourist destination.

Geography

Veneto stretches from the Dolomites in the north to the Adriatic Sea in the east, and from Lake Garda in the west to the Po delta in the south. The Po Valley plain covers much of the south, crossed by the Po, Adige, Brenta and Piave rivers.

History

The region was home to the ancient Veneti people. Much of it was ruled for centuries by the Republic of Venice, the Serenissima, whose mainland empire (the Domini di Terraferma) included Verona, Padua, Vicenza and Treviso. After the fall of Venice in 1797 the region passed between France and Austria before joining the Kingdom of Italy in 1866, confirmed by plebiscite.

Economy

Veneto's economy is based on small and medium-sized enterprises in textiles, footwear, eyewear (Luxottica is based in Agordo), furniture and machinery, alongside agriculture — including the wines of Valpolicella, Prosecco and Soave — and tourism, with Venice, Verona, Padua and the Dolomites drawing tens of millions of visitors a year.

Provinces

The region comprises the metropolitan city of Venice and the provinces of Belluno, Padua, Rovigo, Treviso, Verona and Vicenza.
