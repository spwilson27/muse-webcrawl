Afzal Khan (died 10 November 1659) was a prominent general of the Adil Shahi Sultanate of Bijapur in the Deccan, India. A veteran commander of campaigns in the Karnataka, he is best remembered for his final expedition against the Maratha leader Shivaji, which ended in his death at Pratapgad — one of the most famous episodes in Maratha history.

Career

Afzal Khan rose under the Bijapur court as a trusted sardar, suppressing revolts and campaigning against the nayakas of the south. Traditional accounts credit him with earlier hostility to Shivaji's family: he is said to have imprisoned Shahaji, Shivaji's father, and to have been involved in the death of Shivaji's elder brother Sambhaji at the siege of Kanakagiri.

Campaign against Shivaji and death

In 1659 Bijapur sent Afzal Khan with a large army to destroy Shivaji's growing power in the Sahyadris. After desecrating temples at Tuljapur and Pandharpur to draw Shivaji out, he camped below the fortress of Pratapgad. Shivaji proposed a personal meeting; both sides suspected treachery. At the meeting on 10 November 1659, according to the widely told account, Afzal Khan attempted to stab or crush Shivaji, who — wearing armour beneath his clothes — killed him with concealed wagh nakh ("tiger claws") and a dagger. The leaderless Bijapuri army was then routed by Maratha forces waiting in the forest at the Battle of Pratapgad.

Legacy

Afzal Khan's tomb stands at the foot of Pratapgad near Mahabaleshwar in Maharashtra. His defeat is celebrated in Maratha ballads (powadas) and remains a defining story of Shivaji's early career.
