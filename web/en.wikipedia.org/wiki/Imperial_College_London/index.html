Imperial College London
Established 1907
Type: Public research university
Location: South Kensington, London
Motto: Scientia imperii decus et tutamen

Imperial College London (legally the Imperial College of Science, Technology and Medicine) is a public research university in London, England. Its history began with Prince Albert, consort of Queen Victoria, who envisioned South Kensington as a district of museums, colleges and learned institutions—"Albertopolis". Imperial was chartered in 1907, joining the Royal College of Science, the Royal School of Mines, and the City and Guilds College. It was a constituent college of the University of London until becoming independent in 2007.

Imperial specialises in science, engineering, medicine and business, and is consistently ranked among the top universities in the world. As of recent counts, its community includes some 20,000 students and 8,000 staff, with alumni and faculty including 14 Nobel laureates, three Fields medallists, and figures such as Alexander Fleming, who discovered penicillin at St Mary's Hospital Medical School (now part of Imperial College School of Medicine).

Campuses and structure

The main campus sits in South Kensington, beside the Natural History Museum, the Science Museum, the Victoria and Albert Museum, and the Royal Albert Hall. Other campuses include White City (a growing innovation campus), Hammersmith, Charing Cross, Chelsea and Westminster, St Mary's in Paddington, the Royal Brompton, and Silwood Park for ecology and evolution research. The university is organised into the Faculties of Engineering, Natural Sciences, and Medicine, plus the Imperial College Business School.

History highlights

The Royal College of Chemistry (1845) and the Royal School of Mines (1851) were among Imperial's earliest forerunners; T. H. Huxley was among the noted scientists who taught in its predecessors. In the twentieth century Imperial figures contributed to the development of holography (Dennis Gabor), fibre optics, and monoclonal antibodies, and in the twenty-first century Imperial teams produced influential epidemiological modelling during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Admissions and reputation

Admission is highly competitive, with among the highest entry standards and student-to-staff ratios in the United Kingdom. Imperial is a member of the Russell Group, the League of European Research Universities, and the "golden triangle" of leading English universities.
