Balasagun

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Balasagun was an ancient Sogdian city in modern-day Kyrgyzstan, located in the Chuy Valley between Bishkek and Issyk-Kul. It was founded by the Sogdians, an Iranian people native to the region of Sogdia, and later became a thriving centre on the northern route of the Silk Road.

History

Balasagun was established by the Sogdians around the 9th century, though its origins may be earlier. It rose to prominence under the Kara-Khanid Khanate, becoming one of the khanate's capitals in the 10th century. The city was an important centre of trade and Islamic learning: the Turkic poet Yusuf Khass Hajib, author of the Kutadgu Bilig ("Wisdom Which Brings Good Fortune", c. 1070), came from Balasagun.

The city was later taken by the Qara Khitai (Western Liao), and in 1218–1219 it submitted to the Mongols of Genghis Khan without a fight, earning the name Gobalik ("good city"). It declined in the later Middle Ages as trade routes shifted, and was gradually abandoned; by the 14th–15th centuries it had ceased to exist as a city.

The site today

The principal surviving monument is the Burana Tower, a 25-metre minaret (originally about 45 m high before earthquake damage and restoration), near the modern town of Tokmok, about 80 km east of Bishkek. The site includes the remains of fortifications, mausoleums and a collection of balbal gravestones and petroglyphs gathered in an open-air museum. Excavations have uncovered evidence of a diverse religious life: mosques, a Nestorian Christian church, and Buddhist and Zoroastrian remains.
