Merchant Shipping Act

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Merchant Shipping Act is a stock short title used in multiple jurisdictions for legislation relating to merchant shipping — the registration, regulation, safety and crewing of merchant vessels. In the United Kingdom a series of Merchant Shipping Acts, beginning in the 19th century, formed the principal body of British maritime law, and similar Acts were enacted across the British Empire and later the Commonwealth.

United Kingdom

The Merchant Shipping Act 1854 consolidated earlier law and established the framework administered by the Board of Trade. The Merchant Shipping Act 1894 was a major consolidation, running to 748 sections, which remained the foundation of British shipping law for a century and was widely copied in other jurisdictions. Successive Acts addressed safety of life at sea, load lines (following Samuel Plimsoll's campaign against overloaded "coffin ships"), seamen's wages and conditions, and inquiries into shipping casualties.

The Merchant Shipping Act 1995 is the current principal consolidation. It covers British ships and registration, masters and seamen, safety, navigation, lighthouses and pilotage, wrecks and salvage, and liability and compensation including oil pollution.

Other jurisdictions

Many Commonwealth countries have their own Merchant Shipping Acts modelled on the British pattern, including India (Merchant Shipping Act 1958), Singapore, Malaysia, Ghana, Nigeria and South Africa. In Ireland, merchant shipping legislation begins with the Merchant Shipping Act 1894 as adapted, supplemented by later Acts. These statutes generally govern ship registration, safety certification, crew agreements and maritime liens in their respective flag states.
