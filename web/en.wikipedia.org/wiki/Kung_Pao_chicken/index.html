Kung Pao chicken

Kung Pao chicken (Chinese: 宮保雞丁; pinyin: Gōngbǎo jīdīng), also transcribed Gong Bao, is a spicy, stir-fried Chinese dish made with cubes of chicken, peanuts, vegetables and dried chili peppers. It is a classic of Sichuan cuisine, characterised by the numbing heat of Sichuan peppercorns combined with the fragrance of dried chillies — the flavour profile known as málà.

Origin

The dish is traditionally named after Ding Baozhen (1820–1886), a late Qing dynasty official and governor of Sichuan Province. His title was Taizi Gongbao, "the Crown Prince's tutor", shortened to Gongbao — hence "Gong Bao chicken", the dish he is said to have enjoyed. Because of this association with a Qing official, the dish's name was changed during the Cultural Revolution to "fast-fried chicken cubes" and later "spicy chicken cubes" (húlà jīdīng); the original name was restored in the 1980s.

Preparation

Diced chicken is briefly marinated, then stir-fried in a hot wok with dried chillies and Sichuan peppercorns that have been fried in oil until aromatic. Peanuts, spring onions and ginger are added, and the dish is finished with a sauce of soy sauce, black vinegar and sugar. Westernised versions, common in North American Chinese restaurants, often include bell peppers, celery, water chestnuts and a sweeter sauce, and are made without Sichuan pepper's numbing effect. In 2018 the Sichuan provincial government published an official standard for the dish as part of a campaign to codify classic Sichuan recipes.
