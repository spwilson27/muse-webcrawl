R. M. W. Dixon

R. M. W. Dixon
Born: Robert Malcolm Ward Dixon, 25 January 1939, Gloucester, England
Fields: Linguistics, typology, fieldwork
Known for: Work on Australian Aboriginal and Amazonian languages; ergativity
Institutions: Australian National University; James Cook University

Robert Malcolm Ward Dixon (born 25 January 1939), usually cited as R. M. W. Dixon, is a British-Australian linguist renowned for his fieldwork-based descriptive and typological studies, especially of Australian Aboriginal languages and the indigenous languages of Amazonia, and for his theoretical work on ergativity.

Career

Dixon took a degree in mathematics at Oxford before turning to linguistics. From the 1960s he carried out fieldwork in north-east Queensland with speakers of Dyirbal and other Aboriginal languages, producing the landmark grammar The Dyirbal Language of North Queensland (1972). He spent much of his career at the Australian National University in Canberra, where he co-founded the Research Centre for Linguistic Typology (later at La Trobe University and James Cook University), and conducted extended fieldwork on the Jarawara language of southern Amazonia in Brazil.

Contributions

Dixon's book Ergativity (1994) is a standard treatment of split case-marking systems, drawing on Dyirbal's famous morphosyntactic alignment. His three-volume Basic Linguistic Theory (2010–2012) sets out a framework for grammatical description grounded in detailed language documentation, and The Rise and Fall of Languages (1997) proposed a "punctuated equilibrium" model of language history. He also edited the four-volume Handbook of Australian Languages and wrote extensively on English semantics, including A New Approach to English Grammar, on Semantic Principles (1991). Under the pen name Charles Brice he has published crime fiction.
