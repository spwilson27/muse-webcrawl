Futarchy

Futarchy is a form of government proposed by economist Robin Hanson in 2000, in which prediction markets are used to select policies. In futarchy, democratic processes define welfare measures (the "ends"), while prediction markets decide which policy will best achieve them (the "means"). The slogan is "vote on values, bet on beliefs."

Mechanism

Under futarchy, citizens or their elected representatives choose one or more measures of national welfare — for example, GDP, health, or happiness indices. For each proposed policy, a prediction market is created in which participants trade securities that pay off based on the future value of the welfare measure if that policy is adopted. The policy with the highest market-predicted welfare value is enacted.

Hanson argued that this system improves on the typical democratic process because betting on outcomes gives traders financial incentives to be correct, whereas voting gives voters little incentive to become informed, since any single vote rarely matters.

Criticism and experiments

Critics raise concerns about manipulation of markets by wealthy participants, the difficulty of choosing welfare measures without gaming, the treatment of policies with long-term or hard-to-measure effects, and the moral objection to deciding public policy by betting. Limited experiments have been run in corporate settings (such as by Google and Microsoft) and in some small-scale governance trials. Futarchy remains a proposed system, not one implemented by any nation-state.
