Codex Bodley

MaterialDeerskin (prepared hide)
Size48 leaves
CreatedBefore 1521 (pre-Hispanic style)
PlaceTilantongo, Oaxaca, Mexico
Present locationBodleian Library, Oxford (MS Mex. d. 1)

The Codex Bodley is a Mixtec pictorial manuscript from pre-Hispanic Mexico, painted on deerskin in the traditional screenfold format. It records the dynastic history of Tilantongo, a major Mixtec city-state in the Mixteca Alta region of present-day Oaxaca, and is one of the few surviving pre-colonial Mesoamerican books. It is held by the Bodleian Library at the University of Oxford, from which it takes its name.

Content

The obverse of the codex traces the genealogy of the rulers of Tilantongo across five registers, beginning with the mythical origin of the dynasty and continuing through successive generations of lords and their marriages, births and deaths, all rendered in the Mixtec system of pictographic writing with place glyphs and calendrical name glyphs. The reverse continues the history with the biography of the celebrated ruler 8 Deer "Jaguar Claw" (1063–1115) and the rulers of Zaachila, linking the two dynasties through marriage alliances.

History

The manuscript's early history is obscure; it appears to have left Mexico during the colonial period and passed through European collections before entering the Bodleian Library in the seventeenth century, in the collection associated with Thomas Bodley. It was first identified and published in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, notably in Lord Kingsborough's Antiquities of Mexico, and has been extensively studied for its account of Mixtec dynastic history.
