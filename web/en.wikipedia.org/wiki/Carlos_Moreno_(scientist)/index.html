Carlos Moreno (born 16 April 1959) is a Colombian-French scientist, researcher and university professor specialising in complex systems and urban development. He is best known internationally as the originator of the 15-minute city concept, an urban planning model in which residents can reach daily necessities and services within a short walk or bicycle ride from their homes.

Career

Moreno built his career in France working on robotics, artificial intelligence and the control of complex systems before turning to the study of cities. He is a professor at the IAE Paris – Sorbonne Business School and directs the ETI chair (Entrepreneurship – Territory – Innovation). He has advised the Mayor of Paris, Anne Hidalgo, whose re-election programme in 2020 adopted the 15-minute city (the ville du quart d'heure) as a central plank.

The 15-minute city

The concept, developed by Moreno from 2016 onwards, proposes that urban life be organised around proximity: work, shops, healthcare, education, leisure and essential services should all be reachable within 15 minutes. It rests on four principles — proximity, diversity, density and ubiquity — and on the idea of "chrono-urbanism", organising the city by time of access rather than by car-based distance. The model has been adopted or discussed by cities including Paris, Milan, Melbourne and Portland, and popularised through the C40 Cities network. It has also attracted criticism and, in some quarters, conspiracy theories, which Moreno and city planners have rejected.

Recognition

Moreno was made a Knight of the Legion of Honour in 2010. In 2021 he received the Obel Award for the 15-minute city concept, and in 2024 he was among the recipients of the Global Award for Sustainable Architecture.
