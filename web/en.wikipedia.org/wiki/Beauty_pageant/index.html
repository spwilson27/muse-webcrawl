A beauty pageant is a competition that traditionally focuses on judging and ranking the physical attributes of contestants, though most modern pageants have evolved to also incorporate personality, intelligence, talent, and answers to judges' questions. The four largest international pageants for women — Miss World, Miss Universe, Miss International, and Miss Earth — are collectively known as the "Big Four".

History

Beauty contests became popular in the 19th century at festivals and fairs. The modern era is usually dated to the founding of the Miss America pageant in Atlantic City in 1921. International pageants expanded rapidly after the Second World War, with Miss World founded in the United Kingdom in 1951 and Miss Universe in the United States in 1952.

Modern pageants

Contestants typically compete in segments such as evening gown, swimwear or athletic wear, and an interview or on-stage question, while many pageants emphasise charitable platforms and community advocacy. There are also pageants for men, teenagers, and married contestants. Pageants have attracted criticism for objectification and for promoting narrow standards of beauty, which has led some contests to drop swimwear rounds and reframe themselves as scholarship or empowerment programmes.
