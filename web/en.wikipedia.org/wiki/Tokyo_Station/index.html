Tokyo Station (東京駅, Tōkyō-eki) is a major inter-city railway station in the Marunouchi business district of Chiyoda, Tokyo, Japan, near the Imperial Palace grounds. It is the busiest station in Japan in terms of number of trains per day and the principal terminal for most Shinkansen lines.

History and architecture

The station opened on 20 December 1914 with a grand red-brick, three-storey station building designed by Tatsuno Kingo, modelled loosely on Amsterdam Centraal station. Its two domes and facades were badly damaged in the firebombing of May 1945 and rebuilt in simplified form; a major restoration completed in 2012 returned the Marunouchi building to its original appearance, including the recreated domes, and the building is designated an Important Cultural Property of Japan.

Services

Tokyo Station is the terminus of the Tohoku, Joetsu, Hokuriku, Yamagata and Akita Shinkansen (JR East) and the Tokaido Shinkansen (JR Central), as well as conventional lines including the Yamanote, Keihin-Tohoku, Chuo, Yokosuka/Sobu rapid, Keiyo and Tokaido lines, and the Marunouchi subway line. The Narita Express links the station to Narita Airport. Extensive underground concourses connect to shops, restaurants and the Tokyo Station City commercial complex.
