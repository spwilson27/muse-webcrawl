Walls of Constantinople

The Walls of Constantinople are a series of defensive stone walls that surrounded and protected the city of Constantinople (modern Istanbul, Turkey) from its founding as the new capital of the Roman Empire by Constantine the Great. With numerous additions and modifications during their history, they were the last great fortification system of antiquity, and one of the most complex and elaborate systems ever built.

Theodosian Walls

The most famous component is the Theodosian land walls, built in the fifth century during the reign of Theodosius II. Stretching about 5.5 kilometres from the Sea of Marmara to the Golden Horn, they form a triple line of defence: a broad moat (some 20 metres wide and up to 7 metres deep), an outer terrace, a lower outer wall with towers, and a massive inner wall some 12 metres high and up to 5 metres thick, studded with 192 towers. Defenders could fight from wall walks at several levels.

Sea walls

Separate walls ran along the Golden Horn and the Propontis (Sea of Marmara) shores, lower and simpler than the land walls but protected by the sea and, across the mouth of the Golden Horn, by a great chain that could block hostile fleets.

Effectiveness and fall

For roughly a thousand years the walls repelled sieges by Avars, Arabs, Rus', Bulgars, and others, helping Constantinople survive as the Byzantine capital long after the fall of the Western Roman Empire. They were breached by the Fourth Crusade in 1204 — via the weaker sea walls — and finally by the Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II in 1453, when massed cannon fire, including the great bombards of the founder Orban, battered the land walls near the Gate of St. Romanus over a 53-day siege. The final assault on 29 May 1453 ended the Byzantine Empire.

Preservation

Long stretches of the Theodosian Walls survive today, extensively restored in places, and form one of Istanbul's most prominent monuments. Earthquakes and stone-robbing have damaged other sections, and the walls are part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the historic areas of Istanbul.
