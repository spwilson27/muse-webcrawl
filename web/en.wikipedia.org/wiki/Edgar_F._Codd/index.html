Edgar Frank "Ted" Codd (19 August 1923 – 18 April 2003) was an English computer scientist who, while working for IBM, invented the relational model for database management, the theoretical basis for relational databases and relational database management systems.

Biography

Codd was born in Dorset, England, studied mathematics and chemistry at Exeter College, Oxford, and served as a pilot in the Royal Air Force during the Second World War. He emigrated to the United States in 1948 and joined IBM in 1949, working on early computers including the IBM 701. He earned his PhD in computer science from the University of Michigan in 1965 and later worked at IBM's San Jose Research Laboratory in California.

The relational model

In 1970 Codd published the landmark paper "A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks", proposing that data be organised into relations (tables) manipulated by a declarative calculus and algebra, separating logical structure from physical storage. Initially resisted within IBM, the model was eventually embodied in products such as Oracle, DB2, and SQL-based systems generally. Codd went on to formulate his "twelve rules" defining a fully relational system and, with Chris Date and others, to popularise the term OLAP. He received the Turing Award in 1981.
