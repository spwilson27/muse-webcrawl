Détente

Détente (French for "relaxation") is the easing of strained relations, especially between states. The term is most often associated with the period of the Cold War from the late 1960s to the late 1970s, when the United States and the Soviet Union pursued reduced tensions through diplomacy, trade, cultural exchanges and arms control agreements.

Background and course

Strains of the 1960s — the Cuban Missile Crisis, the Vietnam War, the Sino-Soviet split and strategic nuclear parity — pushed both superpowers toward coexistence. Under Presidents Richard Nixon and Gerald Ford and National Security Adviser/Secretary of State Henry Kissinger, Washington pursued "linkage" and triangular diplomacy with China, while Leonid Brezhnev sought Western technology, grain and recognition of post-war European borders. Milestones included Willy Brandt's Ostpolitik, the 1972 Moscow summit producing SALT I and the ABM Treaty, the 1973 Paris Peace Accords, the 1975 Apollo–Soyuz mission, and the 1975 Helsinki Accords, whose human-rights provisions later empowered dissidents in the Eastern Bloc.

Decline

Détente was attacked in the US by both liberal critics of Soviet human-rights abuses — reflected in the Jackson–Vanik amendment — and conservative critics who saw it as rewarding Soviet expansion. Soviet interventions in Angola and Ethiopia, and finally the December 1979 invasion of Afghanistan, collapsed the policy; President Jimmy Carter withdrew SALT II from Senate consideration, imposed a grain embargo and boycotted the 1980 Moscow Olympics, ushering in the "Second Cold War" of the early 1980s.
