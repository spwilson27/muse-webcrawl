Point and figure chart

A point and figure (P&F) chart is a technique used in technical analysis of financial markets. Unlike conventional price charts, a point and figure chart plots only significant price movements and does not directly take time into account: the horizontal axis advances not by calendar intervals but whenever the direction of price movement reverses by a predetermined amount.

Construction

The chart is built from columns of X's and O's. A column of X's records rising prices; a column of O's records falling prices. Two parameters define the chart: the box size, the price increment each X or O represents, and the reversal amount, the number of boxes the price must move in the opposite direction before a new column begins. The classic chart uses a three-box reversal. Small movements that do not fill a box are ignored, so the chart filters out minor fluctuations ("noise").

History

Point and figure charting emerged in the late nineteenth century among floor traders and was popularised through the writings of Charles Dow's contemporaries and later analysts such as A. W. Cohen, whose 1940s–1960s books codified modern practice. Originally maintained by hand on graph paper, the charts are now generated by most technical-analysis software packages.

Interpretation

Traders look for patterns such as double tops and bottoms, triple tops, breakouts, and trendlines drawn at 45 degrees. Because P&F charts compress price action, support and resistance levels formed by repeated columns are considered especially visible. Price targets can be estimated by counting boxes horizontally or vertically from a breakout formation.
