Anastenaria

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anastenaria fire-walkers with the icons of Saints Constantine and Helen.

The Anastenaria (Greek: Αναστενάρια) is a ritual fire-walking tradition performed in a few villages of northern Greece (such as Langadas and Agia Eleni) and southern Bulgaria (where it is called nestinarstvo). Its central event is a barefoot dance over glowing embers by devotees carrying icons of Saints Constantine and Helen.

The ritual

The festival centers on May 21, the feast day of the two saints. After days of preparation, music (lyre and drum in Greece, bagpipe in Bulgaria), feasting, and animal sacrifice, participants known as anastenarides — who describe a calling, sometimes heralded by dreams or illness — enter a trance-like state and dance on the embers holding the sacred icons, emerging unburned. The icons are kept in a special shrine room, the konaki, the rest of the year.

Origins and interpretation

Local legend holds that the rite began when villagers rescued icons from a burning church and were unharmed by the flames. Folklorists have long debated whether the Anastenaria preserves ancient Thracian or Dionysian fire rituals beneath its Christian frame; the Greek Orthodox Church has periodically condemned it as pagan, while communities defend it as an expression of Orthodox folk piety. In 2009 the Bulgarian nestinarstvo was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
