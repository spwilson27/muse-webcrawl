Kurkh Monolith

The Kurkh Monolith is an Assyrian monument containing a description of the Battle of Qarqar and the preceding campaigns of the Neo-Assyrian king Shalmaneser III (reigned 859–824 BC). It was discovered in 1861 by the British archaeologist John George Taylor at Kurkh, near Bismil in south-eastern Turkey, and is now in the British Museum.

Content

The monolith records Shalmaneser's campaigns in his first six years, culminating in the battle of Qarqar (853 BC) against a coalition of Levantine kings including Hadadezer of Damascus and Ahab of Israel — the earliest historical reference to an Israelite king in an Assyrian source. The text claims an Assyrian victory, though the coalition's survival suggests the result was less decisive than stated. Ahab is said to have contributed 2,000 chariots and 10,000 infantry, the largest chariot contingent in the coalition.

Significance

Together with the Black Obelisk and the Mesha Stele, the Kurkh Monolith is a key extra-biblical source for the history of the ninth-century Levant and for the chronology of the kings of Israel and Judah.
