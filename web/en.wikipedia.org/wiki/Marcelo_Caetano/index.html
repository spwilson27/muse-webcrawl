Marcelo José das Neves Alves Caetano (17 August 1906 – 18 October 1980) was a Portuguese politician and scholar who served as prime minister of Portugal from 1968 to 1974, the last head of government of the authoritarian Estado Novo regime. A professor of administrative law and a long-serving minister under António de Oliveira Salazar, he succeeded the incapacitated Salazar in September 1968 and was overthrown by the Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974.

Career

Caetano rose through the regime as minister of the colonies, minister of the presidency, and rector of the University of Lisbon, and was a principal drafter of Portuguese administrative law. On taking office he promised renewal — the so-called "Marcelist spring" — relaxing censorship slightly, allowing limited opposition in the 1969 elections and readmitting some exiles. Reforms stalled, however: Caetano would not abandon Portugal's colonial wars in Angola, Mozambique and Guinea-Bissau, which consumed the budget and conscripted a generation, nor dismantle the political police.

Fall and exile

Frustration within the armed forces over the unwinnable wars produced the Armed Forces Movement, which overthrew the regime almost bloodlessly on 25 April 1974. Caetano surrendered to General António de Spínola and was exiled to Madeira and then Brazil, where he taught law in Rio de Janeiro and died in 1980.
