Léon Daudet

Léon Daudet
Born: 16 November 1867, Paris
Died: 30 June 1942, Saint-Rémy-de-Provence
Occupation: Writer, journalist, politician
Father: Alphonse Daudet

Léon Daudet (1867–1942) was a French writer, journalist, and monarchist politician, the son of the novelist Alphonse Daudet. A prolific and combative polemicist, he became, with Charles Maurras, a leading figure of the royalist movement Action française.

Career

Daudet studied medicine before turning to literature and journalism. He wrote novels, memoirs of the Parisian literary world, and the medical-political satire Les Morticoles. In 1908 he co-founded the newspaper L'Action française, whose blend of nationalism, monarchism, and anti-republican polemic he drove for decades; he was elected deputy for Paris in 1919.

Controversies

Daudet was repeatedly prosecuted for libel. After the still-disputed death of his son Philippe in 1923, he campaigned obsessively against the police and government; convicted of defamation in 1927, he was imprisoned, dramatically freed by supporters, and lived in exile in Belgium until pardoned.

Legacy

His memoirs remain vivid, if partisan, sources on Third Republic literary and political life, while his political legacy is inseparable from the far-right monarchism of Action française.
