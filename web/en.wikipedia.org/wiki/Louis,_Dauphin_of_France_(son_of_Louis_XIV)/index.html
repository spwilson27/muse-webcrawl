Louis, Dauphin of France (son of Louis XIV)

Louis, Dauphin of France (1 November 1661 – 14 April 1711), commonly known as le Grand Dauphin, was the eldest son and heir apparent of King Louis XIV of France and Queen Maria Theresa of Spain. As he died before his father, he never became king. He was the grandfather of Louis XV and, through his second son Philip, Duke of Anjou (Philip V of Spain), the founder of the Spanish Bourbon dynasty; he is thus an ancestor of the present Spanish royal family.

Life

Louis was born at Fontainebleau and baptised with his grandfather Louis XIII's chaplain officiating; his education was supervised by Bishop Bossuet, who wrote his Discours sur l'histoire universelle for the prince's instruction, and the classical editions ad usum Delphini ("for the use of the Dauphin") were prepared for him. In 1680 he married Maria Anna Victoria of Bavaria. Though permitted to attend and speak at the Council of State, he played no major political role, preferring hunting and his château at Meudon. He commanded French armies briefly in the Nine Years' War, taking Philippsburg in 1688.

Succession significance

In the War of the Spanish Succession his son Philip's accession to the Spanish throne realised Louis XIV's dynastic ambitions, but the Grand Dauphin died of smallpox at Meudon in April 1711, predeceasing his father by four years. His eldest son, the Petit Dauphin, died the following year, leaving the future Louis XV as heir.
