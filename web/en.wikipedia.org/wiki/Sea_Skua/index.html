Sea Skua


Type: Air-to-surface anti-ship missile
Origin: United Kingdom
Manufacturer: MBDA (formerly British Aircraft Corporation)
In service: 1982 onward

The Sea Skua is a British lightweight short-range air-to-surface missile (ASM) designed for use from helicopters against ships. It was primarily used by the Royal Navy on the Westland Lynx helicopter, and entered service in time for the Falklands War of 1982, in which Lynx helicopters used Sea Skuas to damage and disable Argentine vessels, including the patrol vessel ARA Alférez Sobral.

Design

The missile weighs about 145 kg (320 lb) and carries a 30 kg (66 lb) semi-armour-piercing warhead. It uses semi-active radar homing: the launch helicopter illuminates the target with its Seaspray radar, and the missile homes on the reflected energy while sea-skimming toward the target at high subsonic speed, with a range of roughly 15–25 km. Its small size allows four to be carried by a Lynx.

Operators

Besides the United Kingdom, Sea Skua has been exported to several navies including Brazil, Germany, India, Kuwait, Malaysia, Pakistan, South Korea, and Turkey, usually paired with Lynx or Super Lynx helicopters. It also saw combat use during the 1991 Gulf War, where Royal Navy Lynxes used it to sink or disable numerous Iraqi patrol craft and other vessels — one of the most successful air-launched anti-ship campaigns of the conflict.
