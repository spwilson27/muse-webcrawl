Spaceflight

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A rocket arcing into orbit above Earth's curved limb, satellites and a space station nearby]

Spaceflight is the use of space technology to attain the flight of spacecraft into or through outer space. It began with the Soviet Union's Sputnik 1 (1957), the first artificial satellite, and Yuri Gagarin's first human flight (1961), followed by the American Apollo landings on the Moon (1969-72).

Spaceflight serves communications, Earth observation, navigation (GPS and peers), astronomy, planetary exploration and crewed research stations such as the International Space Station (continuously occupied since 2000). Uncrewed probes have visited all eight planets, landed on Mars, comets and asteroids, and left the Solar System (Voyager 1 and 2).

Technology

Spacecraft are launched by rockets; they navigate under the laws of orbital mechanics, using gravity assists to gain speed. Reusable launch vehicles (notably SpaceX's Falcon family) have reduced costs in the 21st century.

Human spaceflight today

Agencies (NASA, ESA, Roscosmos, CNSA and others) and companies operate stations and capsules; China's Tiangong station was completed in the early 2020s.
