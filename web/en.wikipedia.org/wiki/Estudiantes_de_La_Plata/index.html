Estudiantes de La Plata

Club Estudiantes de La Plata

Nickname: El Pincha (The Pricker)

Founded: August 4, 1905

Ground: Estadio Jorge Luis Hirschi, La Plata

League: Argentine Primera División

Club Estudiantes de La Plata is an Argentine sports club based in La Plata, Buenos Aires Province, best known for its professional football team. Founded in 1905 by students and former members of the rival Gimnasia y Esgrima club, Estudiantes is one of the most successful clubs in Argentine football, with six league titles and a distinguished record in continental competition.

History

The club's golden era came under coach Osvaldo Zubeldía in the late 1960s. A famously disciplined and tactically innovative side, built around players such as Juan Ramón Verón and Carlos Bilardo, won the Copa Libertadores three consecutive times (1968, 1969, 1970) and the 1968 Intercontinental Cup, defeating Manchester United. The team was admired for its preparation and reviled by critics for its physical, unsentimental style, encapsulated in the maxim attributed to the club's philosophy of winning through work.

Under Carlos Bilardo — later coach of Argentina's 1986 World Cup winners — Estudiantes won the 1982 Metropolitano and 1983 Nacional league titles. In 2009, coached by Alejandro Sabella and captained on the field by Juan Sebastián Verón, the club won its fourth Copa Libertadores, defeating Cruzeiro in Brazil. Estudiantes has also won the Copa Argentina and other national cups, and remains one of the "big" clubs of the Buenos Aires region despite being based in La Plata. Its fierce local rivalry with Gimnasia y Esgrima La Plata is contested as the clásico platense.

Stadium

The club plays at the Estadio Jorge Luis Hirschi, on its historic site at 1st Avenue in La Plata, comprehensively rebuilt and reopened in 2019 after a long campaign by supporters to return home from the Estadio Único.
