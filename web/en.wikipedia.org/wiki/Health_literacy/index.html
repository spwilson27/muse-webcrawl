Health literacy

Health literacy is the degree to which individuals can obtain, process, and understand basic health information needed to make appropriate health decisions.

Health literacy is the degree to which individuals have the ability to find, understand, and use information and services to inform health-related decisions and actions for themselves and others. The definition used by the U.S. Healthy People 2030 initiative distinguishes personal health literacy from organisational health literacy — the degree to which organisations equitably enable people to use health information.

Prevalence and consequences

Surveys suggest that a large share of adults struggle with health information: the U.S. National Assessment of Adult Literacy (2003) found only about 12% of adults proficient in health literacy, and European surveys found comparable limitations in nearly half of respondents. Limited health literacy is associated with poorer management of chronic disease, lower use of preventive services, more hospitalisations, medication errors, and higher mortality among older adults. Numeracy — understanding doses, risks, percentages and nutrition labels — is a key component.

Interventions

Responses include plain-language writing, the "teach-back" method of confirming understanding, pictograms for medication instructions, professional interpreters, and universal-precautions approaches in which clinicians assume all patients may have difficulty and communicate accordingly.
