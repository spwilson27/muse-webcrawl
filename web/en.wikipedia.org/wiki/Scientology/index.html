Scientology is a set of beliefs and practices created by American author L. Ron Hubbard, launched as the self-help system Dianetics (1950) and reorganised as a church in the 1950s. Scientologists hold that humans are immortal spiritual beings (thetans) whose traumatic memories (engrams) can be cleared through counselling called auditing, progressing up a 'Bridge to Total Freedom'; advanced confidential materials describe a cosmic narrative involving a galactic ruler named Xenu — material the church neither confirms nor denies to outsiders and members may not discuss.

Controversy

The movement's celebrity members (Tom Cruise, John Travolta) and aggressive litigation brought fame; its tax status varies (religion in the US, contested elsewhere — banned or monitored at times in Russia and Germany), and defectors' accounts of disconnection and the Sea Org's discipline draw persistent criticism the church rebuts as religious bigotry. Membership figures are sharply disputed (church claims in millions; scholars suggest tens of thousands of active members).
