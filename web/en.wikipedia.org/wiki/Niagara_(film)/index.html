Niagara is a 1953 American film noir thriller directed by Henry Hathaway and starring Marilyn Monroe, Joseph Cotten, and Jean Peters. Unusually for a film noir, it was shot in Technicolor, on location at Niagara Falls. Monroe plays Rose Loomis, a femme fatale plotting with her lover to murder her unstable husband George (Cotten), a Korean War veteran suffering from mental illness; the scheme unravels when George survives, kills the lover instead, and turns on Rose. It was Monroe's breakthrough dramatic role and the first film in which she received top billing.

Plot

Honeymooners Ray and Polly Cutler arrive at a motel overlooking the Falls and find their cabin occupied by George and Rose Loomis. Polly discovers Rose kissing another man, Patrick, and overhears her plotting George's death during a sightseeing visit to the Falls. George kills Patrick in the bell tower of the Rainbow Tower carillon and is himself presumed drowned. When Polly realizes George is alive, she tries to warn Rose, but George strangles Rose beneath the carillon bells. Fleeing the police in a stolen boat, George is forced to take Polly along; as the boat drifts toward the brink of the Falls, he sacrifices himself to send her to safety, and the vessel goes over with him aboard.

Reception and legacy

The film was a substantial box-office success and established Monroe as a serious box-office star rather than a supporting player; Fox launched her biggest publicity campaign to date, and her performance as a scheming adulteress drew strong notices. Released the same year as Gentlemen Prefer Blondes, Niagara showed the range that made her the defining star of the late Golden Age of Hollywood, before her turn to dramatic training at the Actors Studio reshaped her ambitions.
