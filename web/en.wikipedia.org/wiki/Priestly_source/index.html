Priestly source

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
In the documentary hypothesis, the Priestly source (P) is one of the main strands of the Torah.

The Priestly source (or simply P) is one of the sources of the Torah/Pentateuch identified in the documentary hypothesis of biblical scholarship. It is characterised by an interest in priestly concerns—ritual law, cultic procedure, genealogies, and chronology—by a formal, repetitive style, and by a distinctive theology in which God is majestic and transcendent. The Priestly material includes much of Leviticus, large parts of Exodus and Numbers, and the first creation account of Genesis 1.

Characteristics

Priestly texts typically refer to God as Elohim in the period before Moses (to whom, in this source, the name YHWH is revealed in Exodus 6). Its narratives are sparse and structured: the flood story is built on precise dates, the covenant with Abraham (Genesis 17) centres on circumcision, and the account of the Tabernacle in Exodus 25–40 is written as detailed architectural instruction followed by its exact execution. P organises history into eras marked by covenants—with Noah, Abraham, and Moses—and emphasises holiness, purity, and the ordering of the world against chaos.

Dating and authorship

Julius Wellhausen, in the classic form of the documentary hypothesis (1878), placed P last among the sources, in the exilic or post-exilic period (6th–5th centuries BC), as the product of the priestly circles of the Second Temple. Later scholars, notably the Israeli school following Yehezkel Kaufmann, argued that P predates the Deuteronomist and belongs to the First Temple period. The dating remains debated. Many scholars also distinguish a later "Holiness" stratum (H), covering Leviticus 17–26.

Influence

Whether or not one accepts the documentary hypothesis in detail, the material conventionally assigned to P constitutes the ritual and legal core of the Pentateuch and shaped Judaism's understanding of worship, purity, and the Sabbath.
