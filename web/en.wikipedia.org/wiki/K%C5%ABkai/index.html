Kūkai (空海; 774–835), born Saeki no Mao and posthumously honoured as Kōbō Daishi, was a Japanese Buddhist monk, calligrapher, and poet who founded the esoteric Shingon school of Buddhism. He is one of the most revered figures in Japanese religious history.

Life

Born on Shikoku, Kūkai travelled to Tang China in 804, where he studied esoteric (Vajrayana) Buddhism under the master Huiguo in Chang'an and was initiated into the mandala traditions he would transmit to Japan. Returning in 806, he eventually received imperial permission to establish a monastic centre on Mount Kōya and was later granted the temple Tō-ji in Kyoto. His major doctrinal works include the Sangō Shiiki and the writings on the "ten stages" of the mind.

Legacy

Tradition credits Kūkai with inventing the kana syllabaries (an attribution modern scholars dispute), composing the famous Iroha poem, engineering works such as the Mannō reservoir, and countless miracles. Believers hold that he did not die but entered eternal meditation (nyūjō) on Mount Kōya, awaiting the future Buddha Maitreya. He is the central figure of the Shikoku Pilgrimage of 88 temples associated with his life.
