Particle size (grain size)

Particle size, also called grain size, is the diameter of individual grains of sediment, or of particles in clastic rocks and powders. For non-spherical particles it is expressed as an equivalent diameter. Grain size is a fundamental descriptor in sedimentology, soil science and materials engineering.

Scales

The Wentworth scale, widely used by geologists, divides sediment into boulders (>256 mm), cobbles, pebbles, granules, sand (2–0.0625 mm), silt and clay (<0.0039 mm). The phi (φ) scale expresses size logarithmically: φ = −log2(diameter in mm). Soil classifications such as USDA and international standards use related sand–silt–clay boundaries at 0.05 mm and 0.002 mm.

Significance

Grain size records the energy of the depositing environment — fast water or wind moves coarse grains — and controls porosity, permeability and soil texture. It is measured by sieving, settling (hydrometer) methods, laser diffraction and microscopy.
