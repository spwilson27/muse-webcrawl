Speech processing

Speech processing is the study of speech signals and the processing methods applied to them. Speech is the most natural form of human communication, and speech signals are processed for purposes including transmission and storage in telecommunications, automatic recognition by machines, synthesis of artificial speech, and identification of speakers.

Areas

Major subfields include speech recognition, which converts spoken words to text; speech synthesis, which generates artificial speech from text; speaker recognition; speech coding, which compresses speech for efficient transmission in systems such as mobile telephony; and speech enhancement, which removes noise from recordings. Modern systems are based largely on digital signal processing and, increasingly, deep neural networks trained on large speech corpora.

History

Early milestones include Homer Dudley's vocoder and Voder at Bell Labs in the 1930s, the linear predictive coding methods developed in the 1960s and 1970s, and hidden Markov model recognisers of the 1980s. From the 2010s, end-to-end deep learning systems greatly improved recognition accuracy and synthesis naturalness.
