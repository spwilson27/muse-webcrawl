Schwarzian derivative

The Schwarzian derivative measures deviation from a Möbius transformation

The Schwarzian derivative is a differential operator in complex analysis, named after Hermann Schwarz, that is invariant under Möbius transformations. For a holomorphic function f with nonvanishing derivative, it is defined by

{f, z} = (f′′′/f′) − (3/2)(f′′/f′)2

Properties

The Schwarzian derivative vanishes exactly on Möbius transformations f(z) = (az + b)/(cz + d); thus {f, z} = 0 if and only if f is (locally) a Möbius transformation. It obeys a chain rule involving the Schwarzians of the composed functions, and it determines f up to post-composition with a Möbius transformation. Conversely, given a holomorphic function q(z), the differential equation {f, z} = q(z) can be solved by taking f as a quotient of two independent solutions of the linear equation u′′ + (1/2) q u = 0.

Applications

The Schwarzian arises in the theory of univalent (schlicht) functions — Nehari's criterion bounds the Schwarzian of a univalent function on the unit disc — in conformal mapping, in the theory of Teichmüller spaces and projective structures, and in mathematical physics, where it appears in conformal field theory and in the study of the Korteweg–de Vries equation. A negative Schwarzian derivative also plays a role in one-dimensional dynamics, controlling the number of attracting cycles of unimodal maps (Singer's theorem).
