Göbekli Tepe

Göbekli Tepe
Location: Şanlıurfa Province, southeastern Turkey
Period: Pre-Pottery Neolithic, c. 9500–8000 BC
Excavated: from 1995, under Klaus Schmidt
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2018)

Göbekli Tepe (Turkish: [ɟœbecˈli teˈpe], "Potbelly Hill") is a Neolithic archaeological site in the Southeastern Anatolia Region of Turkey. Dated to around 9500 BC, it is among the oldest known monumental architecture in the world and contains large circular enclosures with massive carved stone pillars — the world's oldest known megaliths.

Archaeology

The site was first surveyed in the 1960s but its importance was recognised only in 1994 by German archaeologist Klaus Schmidt of the German Archaeological Institute, who led excavations until his death in 2014. The enclosures contain T-shaped limestone pillars up to 5.5 metres tall, many carved with reliefs of animals such as foxes, boars, snakes, cranes, and lions. Remarkably, the site was built by pre-agricultural hunter-gatherers, before pottery, metal tools, or the domestication of most plants and animals, and it was deliberately backfilled and abandoned around 8000 BC.

Significance

Göbekli Tepe has transformed understanding of the Neolithic Revolution: its construction suggests that large-scale organised ritual activity preceded — and may have helped drive — the adoption of farming, reversing the older assumption that agriculture and settlement came first. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2018.
