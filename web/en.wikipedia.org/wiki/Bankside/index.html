Bankside is a district of London, England, on the south bank of the River Thames between Blackfriars Bridge and London Bridge, in the London Borough of Southwark. Long a centre of entertainment outside the jurisdiction of the City of London, it is now one of the capital's principal cultural quarters.

History

The name derives from the "banke" or causeway along the river's edge. In medieval and Tudor times Bankside, as part of the Liberty of the Clink under the Bishop of Winchester, lay beyond the City's rules and hosted bear-baiting pits, taverns, brothels (whose workers were nicknamed "Winchester Geese"), and the great Elizabethan playhouses: the Rose (1587), the Swan, and the Globe (1599), where Shakespeare's company performed. After the theatres closed, the area industrialised, with wharves, breweries, and the massive Bankside Power Station built in the mid-20th century.

Regeneration

The power station was converted into Tate Modern, opened in 2000, anchoring a transformation of the district. Other landmarks include the reconstructed Shakespeare's Globe (1997), Borough Market, the Millennium Bridge to St Paul's Cathedral, the Golden Hinde replica, the remains of Winchester Palace, and the Clink Prison Museum. The Thames Path runs the length of the riverfront, which is lined with converted warehouses, offices, and apartment buildings.
