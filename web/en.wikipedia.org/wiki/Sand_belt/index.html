Sand belt

LocationSurrey, Berkshire and Hampshire, England
Also known asLondon's stockbroker belt (part)
GeologyBagshot Formation and related sandy deposits
CharacterHeathland, pine woodland, golf courses, large houses

The sand belt is an area of sandy soils to the south-west of London, England, running through parts of Surrey, Berkshire and Hampshire. The name derives from the light, acidic sands of the Bagshot Beds and related formations, which historically supported heathland rather than rich farmland. Sometimes described as part of the "stockbroker belt", the area is noted for large detached houses in leafy settlements, extensive woodland and some of Britain's most famous golf courses.

Landscape and golf

The free-draining, undulating sandy terrain proved ideal for golf, and the sand belt hosts a celebrated cluster of classic heathland courses — among them Sunningdale, Wentworth, St George's Hill, Walton Heath and Woking. Course architects of the golden age of golf design prized the firm turf, natural bunkering into sand and the heather-and-pine scenery.

Settlement

Because the thin soils were poor for agriculture, the land stayed relatively open until railways and the motor age made it commutable to London. Towns and villages such as Weybridge, Esher, Cobham, Virginia Water, Ascot and Woking grew into affluent suburbs with large plots, and the area acquired a reputation as one of England's most expensive residential districts.

Ecology

Remaining fragments of lowland heath on the sand belt are internationally important habitats, protected in places as part of the Thames Basin Heaths Special Protection Area, supporting rare birds such as the Dartford warbler, nightjar and woodlark, and managed to check the encroachment of pine and scrub.
