Ringatū

The Ringatū church is a Māori Christian faith founded in 1868 by Te Kooti Arikirangi Te Turuki, one of the most significant Māori religious movements in New Zealand. Its name, meaning "the upraised hand", recalls the gesture of prayer Te Kooti adopted after a vision, and the sign of Moses in the Book of Exodus.

History

Te Kooti had been exiled without trial to the Chatham Islands in 1866; while imprisoned there he developed the faith from intensive study of the Old Testament, seeing parallels between the Māori and the Israelites. After his escape in 1868 and years of conflict and exile, Ringatū became a faith of peaceful worship; Te Kooti was pardoned in 1883.

Practice

Services are conducted largely in te reo Māori, centred on memorized scripture recitation (kupu), hymns, prayer, and the raising of the hand. The faith's most important observances fall on the 12th of each month and at the twice-yearly gatherings (hui). Ringatū remains one of the largest Māori Christian denominations, with adherents numbering in the tens of thousands by census counts.
