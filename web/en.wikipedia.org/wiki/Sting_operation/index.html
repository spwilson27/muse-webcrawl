Sting operation

In law enforcement, a sting operation is a deceptive operation designed to catch a person attempting to commit a crime. A typical sting will have an undercover law enforcement officer, detective, or co-operative member of the public play a role as criminal partner or potential victim and go along with a suspect's actions to gather evidence of the suspect's wrongdoing. Mass media journalists occasionally employ similar tactics, with varying legality, to record video and broadcast it.

Examples

Common stings include "buy-bust" drug operations, posing as a hitman for hire, bait cars left for thieves, honeypot decoys for corrupt officials (as in the FBI's ABSCAM operation against members of the U.S. Congress in the late 1970s), and online personas used to catch predators or fraudsters. Sting operations are also used against fare evasion, illegal sales of alcohol or tobacco to minors, and wildlife poaching.

Legal issues

Stings raise the defence of entrapment: in the United States, entrapment may apply where government agents induce a person not predisposed to commit the crime. Merely providing an opportunity to commit a crime is not entrapment. In England and Wales, entrapment is not a substantive defence, but judges may stay proceedings for abuse of process or exclude evidence obtained by state agents who lured a defendant into crime (see R v Looseley).
