Phelonion

The phelonion (Greek: φαιλόνιον) is a liturgical vestment worn by a priest of the Eastern Christian tradition. It is a sleeveless cloak-poncho, tapering at the front and long at the back, worn over all other vestments.

In its modern Greek form, the phelonion is cut shorter in front to allow the priest freer use of his hands, while the Russian style is high-backed and covers more of the body. It corresponds broadly to the Western chasuble, though its origin and symbolism emphasise covering and protection, and the prayer said when putting it on refers to the robe of righteousness.
