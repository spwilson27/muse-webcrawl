Salary

A salary is a fixed amount of money paid by an employer to an employee at regular intervals — typically monthly or biweekly — in exchange for work performed, regardless of the exact number of hours worked in the period. Salaried employment contrasts with wages, which are usually calculated by the hour. The word derives from the Latin salarium, traditionally associated with payments to Roman soldiers for the purchase of salt.

A salary is periodic fixed pay for work performed.

Characteristics

Salaried employees are commonly professional, managerial, or administrative staff. In many jurisdictions they are classified as "exempt" from overtime-pay rules (for example, under the US Fair Labor Standards Act), meaning their pay does not increase for hours worked beyond the standard week. Salary packages may include benefits such as paid leave, pensions, health insurance, and bonuses, which form part of total compensation.

Determination

Salaries are influenced by labour-market supply and demand, the skills and experience required, industry norms, geographic cost of living, and collective bargaining. Many employers use salary bands or grades for comparable roles. Concepts related to salary include the minimum wage, gross and net pay (before and after taxes and deductions), and payroll administration.
