Jethou

Location: English Channel, off Guernsey
Archipelago: Channel Islands
Area: approx. 18 ha (44 acres)
Administration: Bailiwick of Guernsey (parish of St Peter Port)
Status: Privately leased; closed to the public

Jethou is a small island in the Channel Islands, lying about 1 kilometre (0.6 mi) east of Herm and southwest of Guernsey, to which it belongs constitutionally as part of the parish of St Peter Port. The island is privately leased from the Crown and is not open to the public.

Geography and wildlife

Jethou covers roughly 18 hectares (44 acres), rising to modest cliffs above rocky shores. A notable feature is its colony of Atlantic puffins, among the southernmost breeding groups of the species, which nest on the island's turf slopes in spring and early summer. Nearby islets include Crevichon and Fauconnière.

History

The name is thought to derive from Old Norse elements meaning an island or holm. In 1416 the island passed into ownership connected with the Crown. Like its neighbors, Jethou has seen tenants including, in the 20th century, the author Compton Mackenzie, who wrote of island life, and later tenants who maintained it as a private estate. During the German occupation of the Channel Islands (1940–1945) the island was garrisoned along with the rest of the Bailiwick.

Jethou is administered as part of Guernsey; its single residence and outbuildings serve the tenant's household, and landings are restricted, which has helped preserve the puffin colony and other wildlife.
