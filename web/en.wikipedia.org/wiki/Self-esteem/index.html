Self-esteem

Self-esteem is one's overall subjective evaluation of one's own worth — a judgement of oneself as well as an attitude toward the self. It encompasses beliefs about oneself (for example, "I am competent") and emotional states such as pride, shame, triumph, and despair. The psychologist Morris Rosenberg, whose 1965 Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale remains the most widely used measure, described it as a favourable or unfavourable attitude toward the self.

Development and measurement

Self-esteem is shaped by temperament, early relationships, competence experiences, and social feedback. It tends to be relatively high in childhood, drop during adolescence, rise gradually through adulthood, and decline in very old age. Distinctions are drawn between global and domain-specific self-esteem (academic, social, physical), between trait and state self-esteem, and between secure (stable, realistic) and fragile or contingent self-esteem, which depends on continual validation and success. Implicit measures attempt to tap automatic self-evaluations that may diverge from explicit self-reports.

Effects and debate

Higher self-esteem is associated with happiness, resilience, initiative, and better mental health, while low self-esteem correlates with depression, anxiety, and social difficulties. The popular "self-esteem movement" in education, prominent in the 1980s and 1990s, claimed that boosting self-esteem would improve achievement and social outcomes; subsequent reviews, notably by Roy Baumeister and colleagues (2003), found the causal benefits of high self-esteem to be modest — self-esteem is often the result rather than the cause of good performance — and warned that inflated, unjustified self-regard overlaps with narcissism and can foster defensiveness or aggression when threatened. Modern research therefore tends to treat self-esteem as an outcome worth caring about in itself, and as a social monitor (the "sociometer" theory of Mark Leary, which casts self-esteem as a gauge of relational acceptance), rather than as a cure-all.
