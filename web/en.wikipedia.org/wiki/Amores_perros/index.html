Amores perros (literally "Love's a Bitch") is a 2000 Mexican psychological drama film directed by Alejandro González Iñárritu and written by Guillermo Arriaga, in Iñárritu's feature directorial debut. It uses a hyperlink narrative structure of three interlocking stories united by a car crash in Mexico City and by the presence of dogs in each tale.

Plot

In the first story, teenager Octavio enters his brother's dog Cofi in illegal dogfights to earn money to run away with his sister-in-law. In the second, magazine publisher Daniel leaves his wife for model Valeria, whose career is destroyed when the crash breaks her leg and her beloved dog disappears beneath the floorboards. In the third, the vagrant hitman El Chivo — a former guerrilla who abandoned his family — witnesses the crash and rescues the wounded Cofi, setting him on a path toward attempted reconciliation with his daughter. The title puns on perros ("dogs") and the cruelty of love.

Reception and legacy

Premiering in Critics' Week at Cannes, the film won numerous international awards and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. It launched the international careers of Iñárritu and actor Gael García Bernal and is considered the first part of Iñárritu and Arriaga's informal "Trilogy of Death", followed by 21 Grams (2003) and Babel (2006). It is also credited with helping revive international interest in Mexican cinema at the turn of the century.
