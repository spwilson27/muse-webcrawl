Caribbean

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Caribbean is a region in the middle of the Americas centred on the Caribbean Sea and its islands and surrounding coasts, southeast of the Gulf of Mexico and the North American mainland, east of Central America and north of South America. It comprises more than 700 islands, islets, reefs and cays, including Cuba, Hispaniola (Haiti and the Dominican Republic), Puerto Rico, Jamaica, the Bahamas, Barbados, Trinidad and Tobago, and the Lesser Antilles chains, plus mainland territories such as Belize and Caribbean coasts of Mexico, Central America and Colombia and Venezuela.

History

Indigenous peoples — Taíno, Kalinago (Island Caribs) and others — inhabited the islands for millennia before Columbus reached the Bahamas in 1492, his first landfall in the Americas. Spanish colonisation, followed by British, French, Dutch and Danish competition, built plantation economies on enslaved African labour producing sugar, the region's defining and brutal export industry; the indigenous population was largely destroyed by disease, violence and enslavement. Haiti became the first independent black republic in 1804 after a successful slave revolution. In the 20th century the United States became the dominant external power; Cuba's 1959 revolution reshaped regional politics; and most islands gained independence from the 1960s onward.

Culture and environment

The Caribbean's culture blends African, European, Indigenous and South Asian influences — visible in its music (reggae, calypso, soca, salsa, dancehall), its carnivals, its creole languages and its cuisine. Tourism is now the economic mainstay, alongside finance and agriculture (bananas, rum, citrus). The region sits in the Atlantic hurricane belt (see hurricane), and low-lying islands are acutely vulnerable to climate change and sea-level rise. Its waters are home to the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, the second largest coral reef system in the world.
