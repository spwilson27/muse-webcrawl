Mohsen Makhmalbaf (born 29 May 1957) is an Iranian film director, writer, film editor, and producer. He is regarded as one of the leading figures of the Iranian New Wave and has made more than 20 feature films, winning numerous international awards. His daughter Samira Makhmalbaf and other family members are also acclaimed filmmakers, and together they founded the Makhmalbaf Film House in Tehran.

Early life and career

Born in Tehran, Makhmalbaf was imprisoned as a teenager for stabbing a policeman during the era of the Shah, and was released after the Iranian Revolution of 1979. He turned from militant activism to literature and then cinema, directing his first feature, Tobeh Nosuh, in 1982.

His early films reflected the ideology of the Islamic Republic, but his work evolved through successive phases toward realism, allegory, and poetic imagery. The Cyclist (1989) and Marriage of the Blessed (1989) brought him international attention.

International recognition

Gabbeh (1996), a lyrical film about nomadic carpet weavers, was screened at Cannes, and The Silence (1998), filmed in Tajikistan, won awards at Venice. Kandahar (2001), shot on the Iran–Afghanistan border, addressed the plight of Afghan women under the Taliban and gained worldwide prominence after the September 11 attacks.

Makhmalbaf left Iran in 2005 amid political pressure and has since lived and worked abroad, making films in Tajikistan, India, South Korea, Georgia, and the United Kingdom, including The Gardener (2012) and The President (2014).
