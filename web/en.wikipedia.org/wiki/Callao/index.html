Callao

The port of Callao, Peru's principal seaport.

Callao is a seaside city and constitutional province on the Pacific coast of central Peru, immediately west of the capital, Lima, with which it forms a continuous metropolitan area. It is Peru's chief seaport and home to the country's main airport, Jorge Chávez International Airport. Callao is the only province in Peru that constitutes its own region, enjoying a special constitutional status.

History

Callao was founded by Spanish colonists in 1537, just two years after Lima, and quickly became the principal port for Spanish trade on the Pacific coast of South America. Because of the wealth that passed through it, the port was repeatedly attacked by pirates and privateers, including Francis Drake in 1578. In 1746 a devastating earthquake and tsunami destroyed the city almost completely; it was rebuilt on a more defensible plan.

During the wars of independence, Callao's Real Felipe Fortress was the last Spanish stronghold in South America, holding out until 1826. The city also gave its name to the Battle of Callao of 2 May 1866, during the Chincha Islands War, in which Peruvian coastal defences repulsed a Spanish squadron. In 1857 Callao was granted the status of Constitutional Province, a distinction it retains today.

Geography

The province covers about 147 square kilometres, including the offshore islands of San Lorenzo, El Frontón, Cavinzas and Palomino, which lie just off the La Punta peninsula. The climate is mild and arid, influenced by the cold Humboldt Current, with frequent coastal fog known locally as garúa.

Economy and transport

The port of Callao handles the great majority of Peru's maritime imports and exports, including minerals, fishmeal and agricultural products. It is operated under modern concessions and is among the busiest container ports on the South American Pacific coast. Callao is also the base of the Peruvian Navy and a centre of the fishing industry.

Districts

The province is divided into seven districts: Callao (downtown), Bellavista, Carmen de la Legua Reynoso, La Perla, La Punta, Ventanilla and Mi Perú. La Punta, a narrow residential peninsula district, is known for its beaches and seafood restaurants.
