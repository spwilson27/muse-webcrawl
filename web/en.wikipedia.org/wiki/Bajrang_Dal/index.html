The Bajrang Dal (Hindi: बजरंग दल, "Hanuman's army") is a Hindu nationalist organisation in India and the youth wing of the Vishva Hindu Parishad (VHP). It belongs to the Sangh Parivar family of organisations associated with the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS). Named after Bajrangbali (the deity Hanuman), it was founded in Uttar Pradesh in 1984 during the Ram Janmabhoomi movement.

History

Bajrang Dal was established on 1 October 1984 in Ayodhya, initially to protect processions during the Ram Janaki Rath Yatra. It expanded rapidly across India during the campaign for a Ram temple at the disputed Babri Masjid site, and its members participated in the 1992 demolition of the mosque. The organisation runs training camps where recruits practise physical fitness, martial arts with sticks (lathi), and ideological instruction; it states the aim of serving and protecting Hindu society, cows, and religious sites.

Activities and controversies

The Bajrang Dal campaigns against cow slaughter, religious conversion, and what it terms "love jihad", and it conducts programmes such as re-conversion (ghar wapsi). It has been accused by human rights organisations, journalists, and opposition politicians of involvement in communal violence and attacks on minorities, including incidents during the 2002 Gujarat riots and attacks on churches in Odisha in 2008, allegations the organisation has denied or attributed to individual members. Several state governments have at times proposed or imposed bans, which courts have generally not upheld nationally. The Bajrang Dal maintains it is a volunteer service organisation engaged in blood donation, disaster relief, and temple activities.

Organisation

The group claims several million members organised into local units (shakhas) across India, coordinated under VHP leadership with conveners at national and state levels.
