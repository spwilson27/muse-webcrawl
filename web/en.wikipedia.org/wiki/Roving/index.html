Roving

A roving is a long and narrow bundle of fibre used in textile manufacture. It is an intermediate product between the sliver produced by carding and the yarn produced by spinning: fibres in a roving have been drawn out and given a slight twist, which strengthens the strand while keeping it loose enough to be drafted further on the spinning frame.

Production

In the traditional sequence for cotton and worsted wool, carded sliver is drawn out on a drawing frame and then passed through a roving frame (also called a flyer frame), which attenuates the sliver, inserts a small amount of twist with a rotating flyer, and winds the resulting roving onto bobbins. The bobbins are then mounted on the spinning frame — historically the spinning mule or ring frame — where the roving is drafted to its final fineness and given its full twist to become yarn.

Materials and uses

Rovings are made from wool, cotton, flax and synthetic fibres. Wool roving is also sold directly to hand spinners and felters, and pencil roving — a very fine, lightly twisted strand — can be knitted or crocheted without further spinning. In the composites industry, "roving" has a second meaning: continuous strands of glass fibre wound on spools, used as reinforcement in fibreglass products.
