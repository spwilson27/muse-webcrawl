Placeholder text

Placeholder text, also called dummy text or filler text, is text used in publishing, graphic design, and web development to occupy the space that finished content will eventually fill. It lets designers evaluate typography, layout, and visual balance without being distracted by the meaning of real copy.

Lorem ipsum

The most famous placeholder text is lorem ipsum, a scrambled passage of Latin-like text beginning "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, consectetur adipiscing elit." It derives from Cicero's De finibus bonorum et malorum (45 BC), with words altered, added, and removed over the centuries. It came into use in the printing trade by the 1500s and was popularised in the twentieth century through Letraset transfer sheets and, later, desktop publishing software such as Aldus PageMaker, which shipped with it as default filler.

Other forms

Alternatives include "greeking" (displaying text as grey bars or unreadable glyphs), pangrams such as "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog," repeated phrases like "text text text," and humorous themed generators that produce filler in the style of hipster slang, corporate jargon, or film dialogue. In languages using other scripts, placeholder conventions differ: Japanese designers, for example, may use standard sample passages of kana text. Good practice requires that placeholder text be removed before publication; accidentally published lorem ipsum is a familiar embarrassment in print and on the web.
