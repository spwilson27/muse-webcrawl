Israel lobby in the United States

The Israel lobby in the United States is a term used to describe a diverse coalition of individuals and organisations that seek to influence American foreign policy in support of Israel. It includes Jewish-American organisations, Christian Zionist groups, think tanks and political action committees.

Organisations

The best-known organisation is the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC), founded in the 1950s, which lobbies Congress on legislation concerning aid to Israel and sanctions on its adversaries. Other groups include the Conference of Presidents of Major American Jewish Organizations, Christians United for Israel, and liberal organisations such as J Street, founded in 2008, which supports a two-state solution.

Debate

The influence of pro-Israel lobbying became the subject of wide debate after the 2007 book The Israel Lobby and U.S. Foreign Policy by John Mearsheimer and Stephen Walt, which argued that the lobby had pushed American policy in directions harmful to American interests. Critics of the book disputed its claims, and discussion continues over how the lobby's role compares with that of other ethnic and foreign-policy lobbies.

References: Mearsheimer & Walt (2007); AIPAC and J Street publications.
