The Guildhall School of Music and Drama is a conservatoire in the City of London, England, offering undergraduate and postgraduate training in classical music, jazz, drama and production arts. It is one of the leading performing arts institutions in the United Kingdom.

History

The school was founded in 1880 as the Guildhall School of Music, the first municipal music college in Britain, established by the Corporation of London. It was initially housed in a warehouse in Aldermanbury before moving to purpose-built premises on John Carpenter Street in 1887. Drama was added to the curriculum in the 1930s, and the school moved to its current home in Silk Street, adjacent to the Barbican Centre, in 1977.

Facilities

The school's main building includes a 308-seat theatre, a concert hall, recital rooms and teaching studios. In 2013 the school opened Milton Court, a further building containing a 608-seat concert hall, a 223-seat theatre and a studio theatre, making it one of the largest arts education facilities in Europe.

Notable alumni

Alumni include actors Daniel Craig, Ewan McGregor, Orlando Bloom, Hayley Atwell and Michaela Coel; musicians include the conductor Sir Simon Rattle's contemporaries and composers such as Thomas Adès, and singers including Bryn Terfel and Anne Sofie von Otter.
