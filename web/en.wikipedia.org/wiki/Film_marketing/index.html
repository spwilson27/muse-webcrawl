Film marketing

Film marketing is the practice of promoting a motion picture, typically by film studios, distributors, and exhibitors. Because a film's opening weekend strongly influences its overall commercial success, marketing campaigns concentrate on building awareness and audience anticipation before release, using trailers, posters, publicity, merchandise, and media appearances.

Techniques

Trailers and teasers are the core of most campaigns, supplemented by posters, billboards, television spots, and online advertising. Publicity includes press junkets, interviews, film festival premieres, and advance screenings for critics. Merchandising tie-ins, product placement, and promotional partnerships extend a film's reach, especially for franchise and family films. Test screenings inform both edits and marketing positioning.

Digital era

Social media and viral marketing have become central since the 2000s, allowing targeted campaigns and fan engagement; some films have used alternate reality games and staged publicity stunts. Marketing budgets for major releases commonly rival or exceed production costs, making marketing one of the largest expenses a studio incurs.
