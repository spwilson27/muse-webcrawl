Jedburgh (Scots: Jeddart or Jethart; Scottish Gaelic: Deadard) is a town and former royal burgh in the Scottish Borders and the traditional county town of the historic county of Roxburghshire, the name of which was randomly chosen for the new council region. Jedburgh lies on the Jed Water, a tributary of the River Teviot, ten miles from the border with England. The town is dominated by the substantial ruins of Jedburgh Abbey. Other notable buildings in the town include Mary, Queen of Scots' House and Jedburgh Castle Jail, now a museum.

History

A religious community existed at Jedburgh by the 9th century, when Bishop Ecgred of Lindisfarne founded a church. King David I of Scotland established a priory of Augustinian canons around 1118, raised to an abbey in 1147. Because of its proximity to the English border, Jedburgh suffered repeatedly in the Anglo-Scottish wars: the town and abbey were burned or attacked on many occasions between the 13th and 16th centuries, including during the Rough Wooing in the 1540s, after which the ruined abbey was never rebuilt.

Jedburgh became a royal burgh in the 12th century and served as an occasional royal residence; its castle, a key border stronghold, was demolished by the Scots in 1409 to deny it to the English. In the 19th century the town developed woollen and tweed mills, knitwear, and agriculture. The phrase "Jeddart justice" — summary hanging followed by trial, supposedly characteristic of border reiving justice — derives from the town's name.

Jedburgh Abbey

Jedburgh Abbey was founded by David I for Augustinian canons brought from Beauvais in France. The great church, built in the 12th and 13th centuries in late Romanesque and early Gothic style, survives as an impressive roofless ruin in the care of Historic Environment Scotland. Its western front, rose window, and tall crossing tower are among the finest examples of medieval church architecture in the Borders. The abbey cloister site includes a museum displaying carved stones and artefacts, including the 8th-century Jedburgh comb.

Mary, Queen of Scots' House

Mary, Queen of Scots stayed in Jedburgh in October 1566 while holding a justice ayre (court) in the town. From here she made her celebrated ride to Hermitage Castle to visit the wounded Earl of Bothwell, falling ill on her return with a fever that nearly killed her. The fortified townhouse associated with her visit, traditionally the house of the Kerr family, is now a museum — Mary Queen of Scots Visitor Centre — displaying objects including her death mask and items relating to her life.

Sport and traditions

Jedburgh is known for the Jethart Hand Ba', a traditional ball game played through the streets around Shrove Tuesday, related to other Border ba' games. The town's rugby club, Jed-Forest RFC, founded in 1885, plays in Scotland's club leagues and has produced numerous Scotland internationals; Jedburgh is part of the Border League, the world's oldest established rugby league. The Jedburgh Border Games, founded in 1853, are the oldest of the Border games athletics meetings. The annual Callant's Festival includes a rideout to Redeswire, commemorating the Battle of Redeswire of 1575, the last major Anglo-Scottish border battle.
