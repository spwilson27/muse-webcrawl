Gregory of Nazianzus

Gregory of Nazianzus (Greek: Γρηγόριος ὁ Ναζιανζηνός; c. 329 – 25 January 390), also known as Gregory the Theologian or Gregory Nazianzen, was a 4th-century Archbishop of Constantinople and theologian. He is widely considered the most accomplished rhetorical stylist of the patristic age and one of the Cappadocian Fathers, alongside Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nyssa.

Life

Gregory was born near Nazianzus in Cappadocia to Gregory the Elder, the local bishop, and Nonna. He studied rhetoric in Athens, where he formed a lifelong friendship with Basil of Caesarea and knew the future emperor Julian. Reluctantly ordained by his father, he later assisted Basil in defending Nicene orthodoxy against Arianism, and in 379 he was called to Constantinople to lead the small Nicene congregation there.

Archbishop of Constantinople

His five Theological Orations, delivered in Constantinople, vindicated Nicene Trinitarian theology and earned him the title "the Theologian." After the emperor Theodosius I made Nicene Christianity the state religion, Gregory presided over the First Council of Constantinople (381), but resigned amid disputes over his appointment and retired to Cappadocia, where he wrote letters and poems until his death.

Legacy

Gregory's orations, letters, and poetry profoundly shaped both Greek and Latin theology. His phrase "what has not been assumed has not been healed" became a touchstone of Christology. He is a saint in both Eastern and Western Christianity and a Doctor of the Church in the Roman Catholic tradition.
