Rhys ap Gruffudd
Born: c. 1132
Died: 28 April 1197
Title: Prince of Deheubarth
Known as: The Lord Rhys (Yr Arglwydd Rhys)

Rhys ap Gruffudd (c. 1132 – 28 April 1197), usually known as The Lord Rhys (Welsh: Yr Arglwydd Rhys), was the ruler of the Welsh kingdom of Deheubarth in south Wales from 1155 until his death. He is widely regarded as one of the most successful and powerful Welsh princes of the Middle Ages, restoring Deheubarth's fortunes after the Norman advance and reaching an enduring settlement with Henry II of England.

Rise to power

Rhys was a grandson of Rhys ap Tewdwr, the last king of Deheubarth killed in 1093. After the death of his father Gruffudd ap Rhys in 1137 and the deaths of his elder brothers, Rhys campaigned through the 1150s and 1160s to recover his family's lands, capturing castles such as Cardigan and Cilgerran. In 1158 he submitted to Henry II, but renewed warfare followed until the Welsh campaign of 1165 ended in failure for the king.

Settlement with Henry II

In 1171–72 Rhys and Henry reached a lasting peace: Rhys was recognised in most of Deheubarth and was made the king's justice in south Wales, an unusual position for a Welsh prince. He rebuilt Cardigan Castle in stone and there, at Christmas 1176, held a great festival of music and poetry often described as the first recorded eisteddfod. He patronised the Cistercian abbeys of Strata Florida and Whitland and the bardic culture of his court.

Final years and legacy

After Henry's death in 1189, relations with the Marcher lords deteriorated and Rhys spent his last years campaigning to hold his lands. He died in 1197, reportedly of plague, and was buried at St Davids Cathedral. His death was followed by destructive disputes among his many sons, hastening Deheubarth's decline.
