Book of Sirach

The Book of Sirach, also called the Wisdom of Sirach or Ecclesiasticus, is a Jewish work of ethical teachings written by Jesus ben Sira (Yeshua ben Shimon ben Eleazar) in Jerusalem around 180 BC. It was translated into Greek by the author's grandson, whose prologue is the earliest datable evidence of a canon of the Prophets.

Status

Sirach is included in the canon of Scripture by the Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches, where it is classed among the deuterocanonical books, but it is not part of the Hebrew Bible and is regarded as apocryphal by most Protestants. Nevertheless it was widely quoted by early church fathers and in Jewish sources.

Content

The book is a collection of maxims and discourses on wisdom, ethics, family life, friendship, and worship, culminating in the famous "Praise of the Fathers" (chapters 44–50), a survey of Israel's heroes from Enoch to Simon the High Priest. Its teaching presents Wisdom as inseparable from the Law of Moses, a synthesis characteristic of Second Temple Judaism.
