Madtsoiidae

Madtsoiidae is an extinct family of snakes that lived from the Late Cretaceous to the late Pleistocene, with a distribution centred on the former Gondwanan continents of South America, Africa, Madagascar, India, and Australia. Madtsoiids were mostly large terrestrial constrictors, and the family survived for over 100 million years.

Notable genera

The family is named for Madtsoia from Madagascar and Argentina. Other well-known genera include Wonambi and Yurlunggur from Australia — among the last surviving members of the family, persisting into the Pleistocene and possibly known to Aboriginal Australians — and Sanajeh from the Late Cretaceous of India, which was found fossilized coiled around a sauropod dinosaur nest. The gigantic Gigantophis of Eocene Egypt, once considered the largest snake known, has sometimes been assigned to the family.

Classification

Madtsoiids are generally considered primitive snakes outside the crown group of living snakes, though their exact relationships have been debated. Fossils consist mainly of vertebrae, which show distinctive features used to define the family.
