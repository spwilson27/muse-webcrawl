Holger Danske

Holger Danske (Ogier the Dane) is a legendary hero of Denmark, first appearing in Old French chansons de geste as one of Charlemagne's paladins. In Danish tradition he sleeps in the casemates of Kronborg Castle, Helsingør, and will awaken to defend Denmark in its hour of greatest need.

Legend

In the French epics Ogier fights Saracens and giants. Danish ballads and Hans Christian Andersen's 1845 tale made him a national symbol: a bearded warrior asleep at a stone table, his beard grown into the marble.

Statue

A bronze statue by H.P. Pedersen-Dan in the Kronborg casemates (1907) popularised the image; a larger marble version stands at Skjern. During the Second World War, one of Denmark's largest resistance groups took the name Holger Danske.

References: Andersen, "Holger Danske" (1845); Danish folklore collections.
