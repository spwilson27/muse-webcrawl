Electric Boogaloos

The Electric Boogaloos, pioneers of popping
Electric Boogaloos
OriginLong Beach / Fresno, California, U.S.
Founded1977
FounderBoogaloo Sam (Sam Solomon)
StylesPopping, boogaloo, electric boogie

The Electric Boogaloos are an American dance crew credited with popularising the funk styles of popping and electric boogie, foundational forms of hip-hop dance. The group was founded in 1977 in Long Beach, California, by Sam Solomon, known as Boogaloo Sam, who had developed popping in Fresno in the mid-1970s, inspired by the locking of Don Campbell, the robot dances on television, and the music of funk bands.

Style and influence

Popping is built on quickly contracting and relaxing the muscles to create a jerk or "hit" in the body, combined with techniques such as the robot, waving, tutting, gliding and the Fresno. Boogaloo Sam combined these with the loose, rolling "boogaloo" style he had performed with the earlier West Coast group the Electronic Boogaloo Lockers. The crew — whose members over the years included Skeeter Rabbit, Poppin' Pete and Mr. Wiggles (in a later generation) — spread the styles through television appearances on programmes such as Soul Train and through tours, teaching and battles.

Legacy

The Electric Boogaloos are regarded as custodians of the original funk styles, emphasising that popping is a distinct dance and not simply "breakdancing". Members have worked with artists including Michael Jackson, and taught generations of dancers worldwide through camps and workshops. Despite periodic disputes over lineage and naming within street-dance culture, the crew's role in codifying and transmitting popping is widely acknowledged in hip-hop history.
