Food and Drug Administration Amendments Act of 2007

The Food and Drug Administration Amendments Act of 2007 (FDAAA), Public Law 110-85, is a United States federal law that amended the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act and the Public Health Service Act. It was signed into law on September 27, 2007, by President George W. Bush. The act is composed of several titles reauthorizing and expanding the authority of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA).

Provisions

The FDAAA reauthorized the Prescription Drug User Fee Act (PDUFA) and the Medical Device User Fee and Modernization Act (MDUFMA), allowing the FDA to continue collecting user fees from manufacturers. It also reauthorized the Best Pharmaceuticals for Children Act and the Pediatric Research Equity Act, which encourage and require studies of drugs in pediatric populations.

Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies

Title IX of the act substantially enhanced the FDA's authority over drug safety after approval. It authorized the agency to require Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies (REMS) for certain drugs, to mandate post-marketing studies and clinical trials, and to require safety-related labeling changes. The act also required the registration of clinical trials and the submission of their results to a public database, expanding the role of ClinicalTrials.gov.

Reagan-Udall Foundation

The act established the Reagan-Udall Foundation for the Food and Drug Administration, a nonprofit organization intended to advance the FDA's mission of modernizing the development, manufacture, and evaluation of drugs, devices, and biologics.
