The Trailways Transportation System is an American network of approximately 70 independent bus companies that have entered into a brand-licensing agreement. Founded in 1936 as the National Trailways Bus System by five regional operators — including Burlington Trailways and Adirondack Trailways — it grew into the principal competitor of Greyhound Lines in the United States intercity bus market.

History

The five founding companies formed Trailways in 1936 to coordinate schedules, through-ticketing and marketing under a common red-and-cream identity. The network expanded coast to coast during the post-war boom in bus travel, advertising routes that Greyhound did not serve. The association's members suffered along with the whole industry from airline competition and the Interstate Highway era's shift to private cars. In the 1980s and 1990s consolidation reshaped the network; several large members were acquired by Greyhound's parent or left the system. In 2023, Trailways announced an expanded partnership with Megabus for booking integration in the Northeast and beyond.

Operations

Unlike Greyhound, Trailways is not a single company: each member owns and operates its own buses and routes, while the association (Trailways Transportation System, headquartered in Fairfax, Virginia) maintains the brand, a central reservation and ticketing platform, and joint marketing. Member companies also provide charter services, tours and, in some regions, commuter services.
