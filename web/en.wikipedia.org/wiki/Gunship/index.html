Gunship

Type: Fixed-wing aircraft converted for ground attack

Origin: United States, Vietnam War era

Famous examples: AC-47, AC-119, AC-130

A gunship is a military aircraft armed with heavy guns, typically a transport aircraft converted to fire sideways at ground targets while flying a continuous banked circle (pylon turn) around them. The term is also loosely applied to heavily armed attack helicopters.

History

The concept was developed by the United States Air Force during the Vietnam War. The AC-47 "Spooky", a C-47 transport fitted with miniguns, demonstrated the idea in 1964–65; it was followed by the AC-119 and the AC-130 Spectre/Spooky family, which grew to carry 25 mm, 40 mm and 105 mm cannon. The AC-130, continuously updated (most recently the AC-130J Ghostrider), remains in service for close air support and armed reconnaissance, usually at night.
