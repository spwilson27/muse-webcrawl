Photoplethysmogram

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A PPG waveform shown on a pulse oximeter.

A photoplethysmogram (PPG) is an optically obtained plethysmogram that can be used to detect blood volume changes in the microvascular bed of tissue. It is typically obtained by using a pulse oximeter, which illuminates the skin and measures changes in light absorption. A conventional pulse oximeter monitors the perfusion of blood to the dermis and subcutaneous tissue of the skin.

With each cardiac cycle the heart pumps blood to the periphery. The pressure pulse distends the arteries and arterioles, increasing blood volume, which increases optical absorption; during diastole the absorbed light decreases. The resulting waveform therefore tracks the heartbeat and also carries signatures of respiration and vascular tone.

Applications

PPG is the basis of pulse oximetry for measuring blood oxygen saturation (SpO2). It is used in smartwatches and fitness trackers to measure heart rate, and smartphone apps can obtain a PPG from a fingertip placed over the camera. Analysis of pulse-wave shape is used to estimate features related to blood pressure, arterial stiffness, and heart-rate variability, though clinical accuracy outside oximetry remains an active research area. Motion artefacts are the main practical limitation.
