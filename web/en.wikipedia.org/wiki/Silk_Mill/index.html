Silk Mill

The Silk Mill, also known as Lombe's Mill, in Derby, England, was the first successful powered silk mill in England and one of the first fully mechanised factories in the world. It was built between 1717 and 1721 on an island in the River Derwent by the engineer George Sorocold for the Lombe brothers, John and Thomas Lombe.

History

John Lombe had travelled to Piedmont in Italy, where he worked in silk mills and, according to tradition, obtained the designs of the Italian silk-throwing machines by industrial espionage. He patented the machine in 1718. Because Lombe died in 1722 — legend holds that he was poisoned by an Italian agent — the business passed to his half-brother Thomas, who completed the mill complex on the Derby site.

The five-storey mill housed throwing machines powered by a large waterwheel driving a single rotating shaft, an arrangement that anticipated the factory system of the Industrial Revolution. The site now forms part of the Derwent Valley Mills World Heritage Site and houses the Museum of Making (formerly Derby Silk Mill museum), which opened in its redeveloped form in 2021.
