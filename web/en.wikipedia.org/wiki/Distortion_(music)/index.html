Distortion (music)

A distortion effects pedal, used to shape the sound of an electric guitar.

Distortion in music is a form of audio signal processing that alters the sound of amplified electric instruments, usually by increasing their gain, producing a "fuzzy", "growling", or "gritty" tone. It is most often associated with the electric guitar, but is also used with other instruments and with vocals. Distortion is produced when a signal is amplified beyond the point at which the amplifier or other equipment can reproduce it cleanly, causing the waveform to be clipped and generating additional harmonics.

History

Early guitar amplifiers were low-powered and prone to overloading; blues and rock and roll guitarists in the 1940s and 1950s discovered that pushing an amplifier into overdrive produced a desirable sustained, cutting tone. Damaged or deliberately mistreated equipment also contributed: the distorted guitar on the 1951 recording "Rocket 88" is often cited as an early example. In the 1960s, dedicated fuzz pedals such as the Maestro FZ-1 Fuzz-Tone made the effect widely available, and it became a defining sound of rock music, later evolving into the heavier timbres of hard rock and heavy metal.

Types

Distortion effects are commonly grouped into several overlapping categories. Overdrive simulates the mild clipping of an overdriven tube amplifier. Distortion pedals produce a harder, more aggressive clipping. Fuzz creates an extreme, square-wave-like tone. The underlying mechanisms include overdriving vacuum tubes, solid-state clipping circuits using diodes, and digital modelling.

Use

Distortion increases sustain and harmonic richness, and at high gain levels can produce controlled feedback. It is a foundational effect in genres from blues rock and punk to metal, and is also applied to bass guitar, keyboards and harmonica. Related topics include effects pedals, guitar amplifiers and fuzzboxes.
