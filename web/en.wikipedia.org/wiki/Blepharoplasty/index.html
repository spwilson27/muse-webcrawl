Blepharoplasty

Blepharoplasty (from Greek blepharon, "eyelid", and plassein, "to form") is the plastic surgery operation for correcting defects, deformities and disfigurations of the eyelids, and for aesthetically modifying the eye region of the face.

Procedure

The operation typically involves removing or repositioning excess skin, muscle and fat from the upper or lower eyelids. Upper blepharoplasty may be functional—restoring a field of vision obstructed by drooping skin—or purely cosmetic. Lower blepharoplasty commonly addresses under-eye bags.

Risks and recovery

Most patients recover within one to two weeks. Possible complications include dry eyes, haematoma, infection, scarring and, rarely, retrobulbar haemorrhage causing vision loss. Blepharoplasty is consistently among the most frequently performed cosmetic surgical procedures worldwide.
