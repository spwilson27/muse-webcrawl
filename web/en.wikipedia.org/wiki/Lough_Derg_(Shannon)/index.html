Lough Derg, historically Loch Deirgeirt, is a freshwater lake in the Shannon River Basin, Ireland. It is the third-biggest lough on the island of Ireland (and the second-biggest in the Republic of Ireland), after Lough Neagh and Lough Corrib. It is the southernmost of three large lakes on the River Shannon, the others being Lough Ree and Lough Allen.

The lake lies mostly in County Tipperary (to the east), with County Clare on its western shore and County Galway at its northern end. It is the largest lake in the Republic of Ireland by volume, and its waters are used by the ESB hydroelectric plant at Ardnacrusha, which was built in the 1920s as part of the Shannon Scheme.

Geography

Lough Derg is a long, relatively narrow lake, oriented roughly north–south. The River Shannon enters at its northern end near Portumna and leaves at its southern end near Killaloe, where it is crossed by a fine 18th-century stone bridge linking Killaloe with Ballina in County Tipperary.

History

The name derives from the Irish Loch Deirgeirt, from the mythological figure Derg. The lake was an important routeway in early Ireland, and its shores are dotted with early medieval ecclesiastical sites, castles and tower houses. Inish Cealtra (Holy Island), near Mountshannon, preserves a round tower and several early churches associated with Saint Caimín.

Recreation

The lake is a popular destination for boating, angling and watersports, with marinas at Dromineer, Garrykennedy, Terryglass and Killaloe. It is home to the Lough Derg Yacht Club, founded in 1835, one of the oldest yacht clubs in Ireland. The lake hosts trout, pike and perch fisheries, and its shores are also a habitat for the white-tailed eagle, reintroduced to Ireland in the area.
