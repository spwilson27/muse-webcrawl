Rashidun

The Rashidun (Arabic: الراشدون, "rightly guided") are the first four caliphs who led the Muslim community after the death of the Prophet Muhammad in 632 CE: Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, and Ali. In Sunni Islam they are revered as the "Rightly Guided Caliphs", and the state they governed, the Rashidun Caliphate (632–661), oversaw the first great wave of Muslim expansion.

The caliphs

Abu Bakr, Muhammad's father-in-law and close companion, was chosen as caliph at the Saqifa meeting and subdued the secessionist tribes in the Ridda Wars. Umar ibn al-Khattab presided over the conquests of Syria, Egypt, and Persia and established many administrative institutions. Uthman ibn Affan commissioned the standardized recension of the Qur'an but was assassinated in 656 amid grievances over his rule. Ali ibn Abi Talib, Muhammad's cousin and son-in-law, faced civil war (the First Fitna) and was assassinated in 661, after which Muawiya founded the Umayyad dynasty.

Significance

The Rashidun era is regarded in Sunni tradition as a golden age of pious, consultative rule. Shia Muslims, by contrast, hold that Ali was Muhammad's rightful successor from the start and do not regard the first three caliphs as legitimate. The period's conquests created one of the largest empires of the age, stretching from Arabia to Persia, Egypt, and North Africa.
