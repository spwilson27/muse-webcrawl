Japan Socialist Party

The Japan Socialist Party (JSP; Japanese: 日本社会党, Nihon Shakaitō) was a major socialist and social democratic political party in Japan that existed from 1945 to 1996. For most of the post-war period it was the largest opposition party to the governing Liberal Democratic Party (LDP).

History

The JSP was founded in November 1945, uniting pre-war socialist groups. In the 1947 general election it became the largest party and formed a coalition government under Tetsu Katayama, the only socialist prime minister in Japanese history. Thereafter the party settled into the role of permanent opposition under the "1955 system", defending the pacifist Article 9 of the Constitution, opposing the US–Japan security treaty, and advocating unarmed neutrality. Its support rested on the Sohyo trade union federation and public-sector workers.

Decline and dissolution

After a strong showing in 1989 under Takako Doi, the party's fortunes collapsed as it moderated its platform; in 1994 its leader Tomiichi Murayama became prime minister in coalition with the LDP, accepting policies the party had long opposed. In January 1996 the JSP reconstituted itself as the Social Democratic Party, and most of its members soon departed for the new Democratic Party of Japan.
