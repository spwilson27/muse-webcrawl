The 27 Club is a cultural phenomenon and informal list consisting of notable musicians — mainly rock and blues performers — who died at the age of 27. Although the "club" is a media construct, the deaths of several major artists at that age within a short period gave rise to a lasting popular myth that 27 is an unusually perilous age for famous musicians.

Origins

The notion took shape after the deaths of Brian Jones (1969), Jimi Hendrix (1970), Janis Joplin (1970) and Jim Morrison (1971) — all at 27, within two years of one another. Bluesman Robert Johnson, who died in 1938 at 27, was later counted retrospectively as an early member. The idea entered wider currency after Kurt Cobain's death in 1994, when his mother was quoted as saying he had joined "that stupid club". Interest revived strongly after Amy Winehouse died at 27 in 2011.

Statistical analysis

A 2011 study published in The BMJ examined mortality among famous musicians and found no peak in risk at age 27; musicians in their 20s and 30s did, however, show two to three times the mortality of the general population. The club is therefore generally regarded as a product of media attention and confirmation bias rather than a real statistical effect.
