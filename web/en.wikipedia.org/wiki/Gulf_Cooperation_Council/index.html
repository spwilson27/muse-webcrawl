Gulf Cooperation Council

The Cooperation Council for the Arab States of the Gulf, originally (and still colloquially) known as the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC; Arabic: مجلس التعاون الخليجي), is a regional, intergovernmental political and economic union that consists of Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates. The council's headquarters is in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. The Charter of the GCC was signed on 25 May 1981, formally establishing the institution.

Objectives

All of the GCC's current member states are monarchies, including three constitutional monarchies (Qatar, Kuwait, and Bahrain), two absolute monarchies (Saudi Arabia and Oman), and one federal monarchy (the United Arab Emirates, which comprises seven member states, each of which is an absolute monarchy with its own emir). According to its charter, the GCC's principal goals are to effect coordination, integration and interconnection among member states in all fields, strengthen ties, formulate similar regulations in economy, finance, trade, customs, tourism and other areas, and foster scientific and technical progress in industry, mining, agriculture, water and animal resources.

History

The GCC was established in Abu Dhabi on 25 May 1981 against the backdrop of the Iran–Iraq War and regional fears following the 1979 Iranian Revolution. Its founders sought collective security and economic coordination among the conservative Gulf monarchies. In 1984 the council created the Peninsula Shield Force, a joint military arm headquartered in Saudi Arabia. The 1990 Iraqi invasion of Kuwait tested the union; member states joined the international coalition that liberated Kuwait in 1991.

Economy

The GCC states together hold a large share of the world's proven crude oil reserves, and hydrocarbons dominate their exports and government revenues. A customs union was launched in 2003, and a common market, granting citizens freedom of movement, residence and economic activity across borders, came into effect in 2008. A monetary union was planned, with a single currency informally dubbed the "Khaleeji", but Oman withdrew in 2007 and the UAE in 2009; the Gulf Monetary Council was established in 2010 with its headquarters in Riyadh. Plans for a GCC-wide railway and a value-added tax framework were advanced in the 2010s.

Political disputes

The council has faced internal disputes, most seriously the Qatar diplomatic crisis of 2017–2021, when Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain and Egypt severed ties with Qatar and imposed a blockade, accusing it of supporting terrorism and interfering in their internal affairs. The dispute was formally resolved by the Al-Ula Declaration of January 2021 at a GCC summit in Saudi Arabia.
