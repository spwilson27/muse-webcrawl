Mania

Mania, also known as a manic episode, is a state of abnormally elevated arousal, affect, and energy level, often accompanied by a sense of euphoria or irritability. It is a defining feature of bipolar I disorder, in which episodes of mania alternate or coexist with episodes of depression. A milder form that does not cause marked impairment is called hypomania, characteristic of bipolar II disorder.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms include an abnormally elevated, expansive, or irritable mood lasting at least one week (or shorter if hospitalization is required), plus such features as decreased need for sleep, pressured speech, flight of ideas or racing thoughts, distractibility, increased goal-directed activity, inflated self-esteem or grandiosity, and excessive involvement in risky activities (spending sprees, sexual indiscretions, or foolish business investments). Severe mania may include psychotic features such as delusions or hallucinations.

Causes

Mania arises primarily in bipolar disorder but can also be triggered by substances (e.g., stimulants, corticosteroids, antidepressants) or medical conditions including hyperthyroidism, stroke, and multiple sclerosis ("secondary mania"). Genetics and disturbances of circadian rhythm and neurotransmitter systems are implicated.

Treatment

Acute mania is treated with mood stabilizers such as lithium and valproate and with antipsychotic medications; severe agitation may require hospitalization and short-term sedative use. Long-term management focuses on relapse prevention with maintenance pharmacotherapy, psychoeducation, and sleep regulation.
