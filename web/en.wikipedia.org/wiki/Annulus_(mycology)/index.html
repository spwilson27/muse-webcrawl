Annulus (mycology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The ring (annulus) on the stipe of a mature mushroom

In mycology, an annulus is a ring-like or collar-like structure on the stipe (stalk) of certain species of mushroom. It is the remnant of the partial veil, a membrane that encloses and protects the developing gills or pores of the young fruiting body; when the cap expands, the veil tears away from the cap margin and remains attached to the stipe as a ring.

Identification

The presence, position, texture and persistence of an annulus are important identification characters in agaric taxonomy. Genera such as Agaricus, Lepiota and Amanita typically possess a ring, whereas many others lack one entirely. In some species the annulus is double or fringed; in others it is fragile and disappears with age, making young specimens easier to identify. Some annuli are movable along the stipe, as in the parasol mushroom Macrolepiota procera.
