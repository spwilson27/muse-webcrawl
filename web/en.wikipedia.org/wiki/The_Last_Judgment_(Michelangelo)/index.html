The Last Judgment (Italian: Il Giudizio Universale) is a fresco by the Italian Renaissance artist Michelangelo covering the whole altar wall of the Sistine Chapel in Vatican City. It is a depiction of the Second Coming of Christ and the final and eternal judgment by God of all humanity.

Description

Christ stands at the centre, powerfully built and beardless, his raised right arm calling the saved upward while the damned sink at his left. The Virgin Mary turns away beside him. Around them swirl saints bearing the instruments of their martyrdom — Saint Bartholomew holds his own flayed skin, on which Michelangelo painted a distorted self-portrait. Below, angels sound the trumpets of judgment, the dead rise from their graves, Charon ferries the damned across the Styx, and the hell-mouth gapes at lower right.

History

The work was commissioned by Pope Clement VII and completed under Pope Paul III, painted between 1536 and 1541, more than two decades after Michelangelo finished the Sistine ceiling. It was unveiled on All Saints' Eve, 31 October 1541. Its turbulent, densely nude composition reflects the crisis of the Reformation era and a darker vision than the confident humanism of the ceiling.

Controversy and censorship

The painting's nudity provoked immediate criticism; the papal master of ceremonies Biagio da Cesena called it fitter for a tavern than a chapel, and Michelangelo retaliated by painting him as Minos, judge of the underworld, with donkey's ears. After the Council of Trent condemned nudity in religious art, the artist Daniele da Volterra was employed in 1564–65 to paint draperies over the most exposed figures, earning him the nickname Il Braghettone ("the breeches-maker"). A major restoration completed in 1994 removed centuries of grime and some later overpainting, while preserving Daniele's draperies.
