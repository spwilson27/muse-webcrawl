Mysore Dasara

Mysore Palace lit up for the festival.

Mysore Dasara is the state festival (Nadahabba) of Karnataka, India: a ten-day celebration of the Hindu festival of Dasara (Navaratri and Vijayadashami) centred on the city of Mysore (Mysuru). It commemorates the victory of the goddess Chamundeshwari over the buffalo demon Mahishasura — from whom Mysore takes its name — and is renowned for the illuminated Mysore Palace and the grand Jamboo Savari procession of decorated elephants.

Celebrations

The festival begins with rituals on Chamundi Hill, where the royal family worships at the Chamundeshwari Temple, and a ceremonial durbar in the palace: the scion of the Wadiyar dynasty sits on the golden throne and receives homage, continuing a tradition of the former Kingdom of Mysore. For ten nights the palace is lit with nearly 100,000 bulbs, and the city hosts music, dance, theatre, a food festival, flower show and an exhibition. Sporting events include a famous traditional wrestling (kusti) tournament.

Jamboo Savari

On Vijayadashami, the final day, the Jamboo Savari procession winds from the palace to Bannimantap. The lead elephant carries a golden howdah (ambari) weighing some 750 kg, bearing an idol of the goddess Chamundeshwari; caparisoned elephants, horses, folk dancers, music bands and tableaux from across Karnataka follow. The festivities conclude with a torchlight parade (Panjina Kavayatthu) and fireworks at Bannimantap. The Dasara celebrations at Mysore have been held, with interruptions, since the time of the Vijayanagara Empire and were patronised by the Wadiyar maharajas from the 17th century onward.
