Belonger status

Belonger status is a legal classification in several British Overseas Territories that confers the local rights of citizenship — the right to live and work without immigration restriction, to vote, and to stand for office — in that territory. It is distinct from British Overseas Territories citizenship and from British citizenship, and a person may hold belonger status in only one territory. The term reflects the idea of "belonging" to the territory.

Where it applies

Belonger status, under varying names, exists in Bermuda ("Bermudian status"), the British Virgin Islands, the Cayman Islands ("Caymanian status"), the Falkland Islands ("Falkland Islands status"), the Turks and Caicos Islands, Montserrat and Anguilla. The rules differ by territory but typically grant the status by birth to persons born in the territory to a belonger parent, by descent, by marriage to a belonger after a qualifying period, or by grant after long residence and good character.

Rights and controversies

Because most overseas territories have small populations and large expatriate workforces, belonger status controls access to the franchise, to land ownership in some territories, to government employment preferences and to business licensing. The criteria and the discretion exercised in grants are politically sensitive, and reforms — for example in the British Virgin Islands and Turks and Caicos — have repeatedly debated how easily newcomers, and the children of non-belonger residents, can acquire the status. Since 2002 most belongers also hold full British citizenship under the British Overseas Territories Act 2002, but that citizenship does not itself confer belonger rights in any territory.
