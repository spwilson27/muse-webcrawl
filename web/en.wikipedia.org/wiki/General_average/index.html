General average

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

General average is a principle of maritime law whereby all stakeholders in a sea venture proportionally share any losses resulting from a voluntary sacrifice of part of the ship or cargo to save the whole in an emergency. For instance, should the crew jettison some cargo overboard to lighten the ship in a storm, the loss would be shared pro rata by both the carrier and the cargo-owners.

In the exigencies of hazards faced at sea, crew members may have precious little time in which to determine precisely whose cargo bears the loss and whose is saved. Therefore, to avoid quarrelling that could waste valuable time, general average rules provide that the driver (or person in charge of the transport) undertakes extraordinary expenditure or sacrifice, and all stakeholders share the resulting losses in proportion to the value of their interest in the venture.

History

The principle has its origins in the ancient maritime law of Rhodes, the lex Rhodia de iactu, preserved in the Roman Digest of Justinian. The Rhodian sea law provided that when goods were thrown overboard to lighten a ship, that which had been given for all was replaced by the contribution of all. The rule survived through medieval sea codes such as the Rolls of Oléron and the Consolato del Mare, and was absorbed into the marine insurance practice of London and other trading centres.

The modern rules are codified in the York–Antwerp Rules, first agreed in 1890 and periodically revised, most recently in 2016. The Rules are not law in themselves but are routinely incorporated by contract into bills of lading and charter parties.

Requirements

For a general average act to be declared, classically three elements are required:

  • There must be a common peril endangering the ship, cargo and freight together;
  • There must be a voluntary and extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure, intentionally and reasonably made; and
  • The sacrifice must be successful, in whole or in part, in preserving the property involved in the common adventure.

Rule A of the York–Antwerp Rules states: "There is a general average act when, and only when, any extraordinary sacrifice or expenditure is intentionally and reasonably made or incurred for the common safety for the purpose of preserving from peril the property involved in a common maritime adventure."

Adjustment

The apportionment is calculated by a professional known as an average adjuster. Before cargo is released at destination, cargo interests are typically required to sign an average bond and provide security, such as a cash deposit or an insurer's guarantee. If a party fails to contribute, the carrier may exercise a lien over the cargo. Rights of recovery in general average may be barred by the passage of time, and under the York–Antwerp Rules a time bar applies unless proceedings are commenced within prescribed periods.

Modern relevance

General average is still declared in major casualties. After the container ship Ever Given blocked the Suez Canal in March 2021 and was refloated, its owners declared general average, requiring cargo interests to post security before delivery of their goods. The principle is criticised by some in the shipping industry as archaic and costly to administer, yet it remains a standard feature of maritime commerce.
