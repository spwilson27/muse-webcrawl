The Gambling Act 1845 (8 & 9 Vict. c. 109), often cited by its long title the Gaming Act 1845, was an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. Passed following an 1844 select committee report on gaming, it was the principal piece of legislation governing gambling in England and Wales for more than 150 years.

Provisions

Section 18 of the Act provided that all contracts or agreements, whether by parol or in writing, by way of gaming or wagering, were null and void, and that no suit could be brought in any court of law or equity to recover money won upon any wager. Section 17 made cheating at play an offence punishable as obtaining money by false pretences.

Legacy

The Act's unenforceability rule shaped English contract law on wagering for generations and was cited in cases on financial spread betting and derivatives. Most of its remaining provisions were repealed by the Gambling Act 2005, which for the first time made gambling contracts generally enforceable.
