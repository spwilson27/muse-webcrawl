Śakra (Sanskrit: शक्र) is a deity in Buddhism, the king of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven, the second of the heavens in Buddhist cosmology. He is often identified with the Vedic god Indra, though in Buddhist texts he is depicted as a devout follower and protector of the Buddha rather than a supreme creator.

Role in Buddhist texts

In the Pali Canon and Mahayana sutras, Śakra frequently descends to earth to question the Buddha, test monks, and offer protection to the sangha. The Śakra chapter of the Dhammapada commentary portrays him rewarding virtue. In the Vessantara Jātaka he grants boons to the prince. He is traditionally said to have descended with the Buddha from Trāyastriṃśa at Sankassa after the Buddha preached the Abhidharma to his mother.

Iconography

Śakra is usually shown as a regal figure riding the three-headed elephant Airāvata, holding a vajra. In Chinese Buddhism he is known as Di Shitian (帝釋天) and is one of the Twenty Devas; in Japan he is Taishakuten.
