William DeVries

William Castle DeVries (born December 19, 1943) is an American cardiothoracic surgeon, best known for implanting the first permanent total artificial heart, the Jarvik-7, into a human patient, retired dentist Barney Clark, on December 2, 1982, at the University of Utah.

Career

DeVries was born in Brooklyn and raised in Ogden, Utah. A former high-school track star and Eagle Scout, he earned his bachelor's and medical degrees at the University of Utah, where he trained under Willem Kolff, the pioneer of artificial organs, and worked with Robert Jarvik, inventor of the Jarvik-7 heart.

Barney Clark survived 112 days on the Jarvik-7 before dying of multi-organ failure; the operation drew worldwide attention and raised enduring ethical debate about artificial organs and quality of life. DeVries implanted the device in several further patients, including William J. Schroeder, who survived 620 days. In 1984 DeVries moved to Humana Hospital Audubon in Louisville, Kentucky, where the artificial heart programme continued. He later practised cardiothoracic surgery in Louisville and served in leadership roles in artificial-heart research and education.
