The Continental Army was the army of the United Colonies (later the United States) during the American Revolutionary War. It was formed by resolution of the Second Continental Congress on 14 June 1775 — a date now celebrated as the birthday of the United States Army — and was commanded throughout the war by General George Washington. It fought alongside state militias and French allied forces against the British Army and its German auxiliaries.

Despite chronic shortages of pay, food, clothing and trained officers, and short enlistment terms that repeatedly dissolved its ranks, the Continental Army survived eight years of war, won decisive victories at Trenton, Saratoga (through its northern department) and Yorktown, and was formally disbanded in 1783 after the Treaty of Paris recognised American independence.

Formation

After the battles of Lexington and Concord in April 1775, colonial militia besieged the British garrison in Boston. Congress adopted these forces as a continental army on 14 June 1775, appointed Washington commander-in-chief, and authorised the raising of riflemen companies from Pennsylvania, Maryland and Virginia. The army's first campaign secured the evacuation of Boston in March 1776 after Washington emplaced artillery captured from Fort Ticonderoga on Dorchester Heights.

Organisation

The army was organised into infantry regiments (later battalions) raised by quota from the thirteen states, supplemented by artillery, cavalry (light dragoons), engineers, and specialist corps such as sappers and miners. Washington's staff included adjutant, quartermaster and commissary departments, all hampered by a weak Congress that could requisition but not tax. From 1777 Congress authorised enlistments for three years or the duration of the war, with bounties of cash and land, which gradually produced a more professional core.

Foreign volunteers made major contributions: the Marquis de Lafayette, the Polish officers Tadeusz Kościuszko and Casimir Pulaski, and above all Friedrich Wilhelm von Steuben, whose drill regulations (the "Blue Book") standardised training from 1779.

Campaigns

The army's war fell into three broad phases. In 1776 it was driven from New York City and across New Jersey, before Washington revived the cause with surprise victories at Trenton (26 December 1776) and Princeton. In 1777 it lost Philadelphia but the separate Northern Army forced Burgoyne's surrender at Saratoga — the turning point that brought France into the war. The main army then endured the winter encampment at Valley Forge.

From 1778 the war shifted south. After hard campaigning in the Carolinas under Nathanael Greene, Washington seized the opportunity in 1781 to trap Lord Cornwallis at Yorktown, Virginia, where, with the French army of Rochambeau and the French fleet's victory at the Chesapeake, he forced the surrender of a second British army on 19 October 1781, effectively ending major operations.

Valley Forge and von Steuben

The winter of 1777–78 at Valley Forge, Pennsylvania, became the army's defining ordeal: roughly 2,000 soldiers died of disease and exposure. Yet the encampment also transformed the army, as von Steuben drilled the troops in a simplified, uniform system of manoeuvre and instilled professional pride, reforms demonstrated at Monmouth Court House in June 1778.

Demobilisation

Unpaid and restless, officers in 1783 flirted with pressure on Congress in the Newburgh Conspiracy, which Washington defused by a personal appeal. Most of the army was furloughed in June 1783 and formally disbanded by congressional resolution that autumn, Washington resigning his commission at Annapolis on 23 December 1783. A small remnant, the First American Regiment, continued in service and formed the nucleus of the later United States Army.

Legacy

The Continental Army established the American tradition of a citizen army subordinate to civil authority. Its privations — and Congress's inability to support it — shaped the debates that produced the United States Constitution, with its federal power to tax and to raise and support armies.
