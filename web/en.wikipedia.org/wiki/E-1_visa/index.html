The E-1 visa is a nonimmigrant visa of the United States available to nationals of countries with which the United States maintains a treaty of commerce and navigation (or an equivalent qualifying arrangement). It allows a treaty national — a "treaty trader" — to enter the United States to carry on substantial trade, principally between the United States and the treaty country.

Requirements

The applicant must be a citizen of a treaty country; the trading enterprise must have the nationality of the treaty country (at least 50% owned by treaty nationals); the trade must be "substantial", meaning a continuous flow of sizable transactions rather than a single exchange; and more than 50% of the enterprise's international trade must be between the US and the treaty country. Trade includes goods, services, technology, banking, insurance, tourism and other qualifying activities. Certain employees of a treaty trader — executives, supervisors and essential-skills workers of the same nationality — may also qualify.

Terms

E-1 status is typically granted in renewable increments (admissions are commonly for up to two years at a time, with extensions available and no fixed overall maximum for visa validity, which varies by country and can be up to five years for the visa itself). Spouses and unmarried children under 21 may accompany the principal in E dependent status, and spouses may obtain work authorisation. The E-1 is a nonimmigrant classification and does not by itself lead to permanent residence.
