Mechanical licence

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A phonograph record, the original medium of mechanical reproduction

A mechanical licence is a licence that grants certain permissions to work with, study, improve upon, reuse, or redistribute the creative contents of a copyrighted work — specifically, in music, the right to reproduce and distribute a copyrighted musical composition in audio form. The name derives from the era of mechanical reproduction: piano rolls and phonograph records.

United States law

Under United States copyright law, once a musical work has been recorded and distributed publicly, anyone may obtain a compulsory mechanical licence to make and distribute their own recording of the work (a "cover version"), provided they pay the statutory royalty rate set by the Copyright Royalty Board and comply with accounting requirements. The concept was introduced in the Copyright Act of 1909, partly in response to fears of a monopoly in the player-piano roll market.

Mechanical licences in the US are commonly administered through the Harry Fox Agency. The Music Modernization Act of 2018 extended the compulsory licensing system to digital music providers through a blanket licence administered by the Mechanical Licensing Collective.

In many other countries, mechanical rights are administered collectively by copyright collecting societies, which negotiate rates and distribute royalties to songwriters and publishers.
