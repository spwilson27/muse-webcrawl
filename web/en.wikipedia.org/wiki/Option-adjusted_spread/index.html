Option-adjusted spread

The option-adjusted spread (OAS) is the measurement of the spread of a fixed-income security rate and the risk-free rate of return, which is then adjusted to take into account an embedded option. Typically, an analyst uses Treasury yields for the risk-free rate. The spread is added to the fixed-income security price to make the risk-free bond price the same as the fixed-income security's price.

Concept

Where a security contains an embedded option — such as the prepayment option in a mortgage-backed security or a call option in a callable bond — its cash flows depend on the future path of interest rates, so a simple yield spread to a benchmark overstates or understates its relative value. The OAS corrects for this by modelling many possible interest rate paths (for example, with a binomial tree or Monte Carlo simulation), computing the security's cash flows along each path, and solving for the constant spread over the risk-free curve that makes the average present value equal to the market price.

Interpretation

The OAS measures the compensation investors receive for bearing credit, liquidity and other risks, after removing the value of the embedded option. The difference between the nominal (Z-) spread and the OAS is interpreted as the option cost. OAS is a standard relative-value tool for mortgage-backed securities and callable corporate bonds.
