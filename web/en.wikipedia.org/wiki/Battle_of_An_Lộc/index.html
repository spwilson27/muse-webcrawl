An Lộc under siege, 1972

The Battle of An Lộc was a major engagement of the Vietnam War fought between 13 April and 20 July 1972 at An Lộc, the capital of Bình Long Province, about 65 miles north of Saigon. During North Vietnam's Easter Offensive, three North Vietnamese and Viet Cong divisions attacked the town, defended principally by the Army of the Republic of Vietnam (ARVN) 5th Division under Brigadier General Lê Văn Hưng.

The siege

After taking Lộc Ninh to the north, North Vietnamese forces with tanks and heavy artillery assaulted An Lộc and encircled it, reducing the defenders' perimeter to a fraction of the town. The garrison survived on air-dropped supplies and depended on massive United States air support — tactical aircraft, B-52 Arc Light strikes, and AC-130 gunships coordinated by American advisors — to blunt repeated armoured attacks. The ARVN's use of the new M72 LAW rockets against North Vietnamese tanks was a notable feature of the fighting in April and May.

Outcome

By mid-July the North Vietnamese attacks had been exhausted, a relief force reopened Highway 13, and the siege was lifted. An Lộc was largely destroyed, and civilian casualties were heavy, but the defence blocked the northern approach to Saigon and is regarded as one of the ARVN's finest performances of the war.
