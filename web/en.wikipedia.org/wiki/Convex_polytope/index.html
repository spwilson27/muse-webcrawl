Convex polytope

A convex polytope is a special case of a polytope, having the additional property that it is also a convex set contained in n-dimensional Euclidean space. Most texts use the term "polytope" for a bounded convex polytope, and "polyhedron" for the more general, possibly unbounded, case.

Definitions

A convex polytope may be defined in several equivalent ways: as the convex hull of finitely many points (the V-description), or as a bounded intersection of finitely many half-spaces (the H-description). The equivalence of these definitions is a fundamental result of polytope theory associated with Minkowski, Weyl, and Farkas.

Faces and combinatorics

The boundary of a convex polytope is organized into faces of various dimensions: vertices (0-faces), edges (1-faces), and facets ((n−1)-faces). Euler's formula generalizes to higher dimensions through the Euler characteristic of the polytope's boundary. The combinatorial types of polytopes, recorded in face lattices, are a central topic of study.

Applications

Convex polytopes appear in linear programming, where feasible regions are polytopes and optima occur at vertices; in combinatorics; and in algebraic geometry through toric varieties.
