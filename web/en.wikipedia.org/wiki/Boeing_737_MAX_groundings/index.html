The Boeing 737 MAX groundings were a worldwide suspension of passenger operations of the Boeing 737 MAX from March 2019 to early 2021, following two fatal crashes in less than five months that killed 346 people. Lion Air Flight 610 crashed into the Java Sea on 29 October 2018, and Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302 crashed near Bishoftu on 10 March 2019. Within days of the second accident, regulators worldwide grounded the type, the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) acting last on March 13.

MCAS and investigations

Investigations centred on the Maneuvering Characteristics Augmentation System (MCAS), flight-control software added to counter the MAX's tendency to pitch up due to its larger, repositioned engines. MCAS could repeatedly command nose-down stabiliser trim based on a single angle-of-attack sensor; erroneous sensor data activated it in both accidents, and pilots had not been informed of the system's existence. Probes criticised Boeing's design and disclosure decisions and the FAA's delegation of certification work to the manufacturer.

Return to service

Boeing redesigned MCAS to use both angle-of-attack sensors, limit its authority, and added training requirements. The FAA rescinded its grounding order on 18 November 2020; other regulators followed through 2021, though China, the type's largest market, did not clear it until late 2021. The crisis cost Boeing more than $20 billion in compensation, fines, and lost orders, led to the firing of CEO Dennis Muilenburg, a deferred prosecution agreement with the US Department of Justice, and lasting reforms of aircraft certification.
