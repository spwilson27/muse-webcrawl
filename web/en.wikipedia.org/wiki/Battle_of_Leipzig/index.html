Battle of Leipzig

The Battle of Leipzig, also known as the Battle of the Nations, was fought from 16 to 19 October 1813 at Leipzig in Saxony between Napoleon's French Empire and the Sixth Coalition — the armies of Russian Empire, Prussia, Austria and Sweden. With some 600,000 soldiers engaged it was the largest battle in Europe before the First World War, and Napoleon's defeat there destroyed French power east of the Rhine and led directly to the invasion of France in 1814.

Course

After the failure of his 1812 invasion of Russia (see Napoleonic Wars), Napoleon rebuilt an army in Germany and won spring victories at Lützen and Bautzen, but an armistice let the coalition — guided by the Trachenberg Plan to avoid Napoleon himself and beat his marshals — gather overwhelming strength. In October the allied armies converged on Leipzig. Four days of fighting around the city culminated on 18 October, the war's largest single day of battle; the Saxons defected mid-battle. On 19 October the French retreat turned to disaster when the Elster bridge — the army's single escape route — was blown prematurely, trapping thousands, among them Prince Józef Poniatowski, who drowned.

Consequences

Napoleon fell back to France with perhaps 100,000 men; his German client states deserted him. The coalition crossed the Rhine on New Year's Day 1814. Leipzig is remembered in Germany as a war of liberation and commemorated by the enormous Völkerschlachtdenkmal.
