Accredited investor

An accredited investor is a person or entity that is permitted, under securities regulations, to invest in securities offerings that are not registered with financial authorities — such as private placements, hedge funds, venture capital and private equity. The concept is most developed in the United States, where the term is defined by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in Regulation D.

United States definition

For natural persons, the SEC definition has traditionally been based on wealth and income: an individual qualifies with a net worth exceeding US$1 million (excluding the value of the primary residence), or income exceeding US$200,000 in each of the two most recent years (US$300,000 jointly with a spouse), with a reasonable expectation of the same in the current year. Amendments adopted in 2020 added categories based on professional knowledge and experience, such as holders of certain securities licences and knowledgeable employees of private funds.

Rationale

The exemption rests on the assumption that accredited investors are financially sophisticated enough, or wealthy enough to bear the risk, that they do not need the protections of registered offerings, such as audited disclosure. Critics argue that wealth is a poor proxy for sophistication and that the thresholds exclude most households from private markets.

Other jurisdictions

Similar categories exist elsewhere under different names, such as "professional investors" under the European Union's MiFID framework and "sophisticated investors" or wholesale clients in Australia and Hong Kong.
