Zytglogge

The Zytglogge is a landmark medieval tower in Bern, Switzerland. Built in the early 13th century, it has served the city as a guard tower, prison, and clock tower. Its name is Bernese German for "time bell".

History

Raised around 1218–1220 as the western gate of Bern's fortifications, the tower became a women's prison after the city's expansion moved the walls outward. It was rebuilt after the great fire of 1405. Its famous astronomical clock and mechanical figures date largely from the 1527–1530 overhaul by Kaspar Brunner.

The clock

Shortly before each hour, a mechanical performance begins: a rooster crows, a procession of armed bears circles, a jester rings bells, and Chronos turns his hourglass while the great bell is struck. Until 1983, Bern's official standard measure of length was also marked on the tower. The Zytglogge is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Old City of Bern.
