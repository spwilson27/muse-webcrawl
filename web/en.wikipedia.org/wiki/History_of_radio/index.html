History of radio

FieldTelecommunications
Key figuresMaxwell, Hertz, Marconi, Tesla, Fessenden
MilestonesFirst transatlantic signal 1901; broadcasting from 1920

The history of radio covers the discovery of electromagnetic waves, their harnessing for wireless telegraphy and broadcasting, and radio's central role in 20th-century culture and war.

Foundations

James Clerk Maxwell predicted electromagnetic waves (1865); Heinrich Hertz demonstrated them (1887). In the 1890s Oliver Lodge, Alexander Popov, Nikola Tesla and Guglielmo Marconi developed wireless telegraphy; Marconi achieved a transatlantic signal in December 1901 and dominated maritime radio.

Voice and broadcasting

The audion tube (Lee de Forest, 1906) and Reginald Fessenden's early voice transmissions enabled telephony by radio. Commercial broadcasting began around 1920 (KDKA Pittsburgh); networks, the BBC (1922), and FM (Edwin Armstrong, 1930s) followed, and radio became the first mass electronic medium.

War and after

Radar, military communications and code-breaking made radio vital in the Second World War. Television eclipsed radio drama, but transistor portables, FM music formats and talk radio kept the medium strong; digital radio and podcasts extend it today.
