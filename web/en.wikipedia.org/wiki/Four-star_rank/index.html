Four-star rank

A four-star rank is the rank of any four-star officer described by the NATO OF-9 code. Four-star officers are usually the most senior commanders in the armed services, having ranks such as admiral, general, or air chief marshal. The designation derives from the United States system, in which the rank insignia of the senior-most officers bear four stars.

By country

In the United States, four-star ranks are general (Army, Marine Corps, Air Force, Space Force) and admiral (Navy, Coast Guard); the ranks are tied to specific senior billets, such as service chiefs, combatant commanders, and the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, and are held only while the officer occupies the position. In the United Kingdom the equivalent ranks (NATO OF-9) are full admiral, general, and air chief marshal. Many other countries follow similar conventions within the NATO rank code system.

Role

Four-star officers command major formations and entire services, serve on joint staffs, and act as the principal military advisers to their governments. Because of the small number of such posts, promotion to four-star rank is by nomination and, in the U.S., Senate confirmation.
