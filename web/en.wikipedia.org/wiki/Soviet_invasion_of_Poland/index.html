Soviet invasion of Poland

The Soviet invasion of Poland was a military conflict in September 1939 by the Soviet Union against the Republic of Poland, without a formal declaration of war. On 17 September 1939, sixteen days after Nazi Germany invaded Poland from the west, the Red Army invaded from the east. The campaign ended on 6 October 1939 with Germany and the Soviet Union dividing and annexing the whole of Poland under the terms of the German–Soviet Frontier Treaty.

Background

The secret protocol of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of 23 August 1939 divided Eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres of influence, assigning eastern Poland to the USSR. Soviet leader Joseph Stalin waited for Poland's military collapse and for France and Britain to focus on Germany before ordering the advance, claiming the Polish state had ceased to exist and that the USSR was protecting Ukrainians and Belarusians in the eastern territories.

The campaign

The Red Army's Belorussian and Ukrainian Fronts, under Mikhail Kovalyov and Semyon Timoshenko respectively, crossed the border against thin Polish resistance; the Polish commander-in-chief, Edward Rydz-Śmigły, had ordered forces to avoid battle with the Soviets except in self-defence. Clashes occurred at Wilno, Grodno, Lwów and Szack, but by early October organised resistance had ended. The USSR annexed over half of Poland's territory, home to around 13.5 million people.

Aftermath

The occupied territories were incorporated into the Ukrainian and Byelorussian Soviet republics after staged elections. Hundreds of thousands of Polish citizens were deported to Siberia and Kazakhstan, and in spring 1940 the NKVD murdered some 22,000 Polish officers and intelligentsia in the Katyn massacre. After Germany invaded the USSR in 1941, the Polish government-in-exile and Moscow briefly cooperated, but the border question remained unresolved until the postwar settlement moved Poland westward.
