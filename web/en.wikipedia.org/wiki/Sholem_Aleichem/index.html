Sholem Aleichem (Yiddish: שלום־עליכם, pronounced [ʃɔləm aˈlɛjxəm]; born Solomon Naumovich Rabinovich, March 2, 1859 – May 13, 1916) was a Yiddish author and playwright whose humorous, bittersweet stories of Jewish life in the small towns (shtetls) of the Russian Pale of Settlement made him the most popular and beloved writer in Yiddish literature — often compared to Mark Twain, an admiration that was mutual: Twain, on being introduced as "the American Sholem Aleichem", replied that he considered himself the American Sholem Aleichem. His tales of Tevye the dairyman inspired the musical Fiddler on the Roof (1964).

Life

Rabinovich was born in Pereiaslav and grew up in the shtetl of Voronkiv (immortalised in his fiction as Kasrilevke). His father's fortunes collapsed in his teens, and the young Rabinovich worked as a tutor — including for the wealthy landowner Elimelekh Loyev, whose daughter Olga he married in 1883. He inherited his father-in-law's estate near Kiev, wrote in Hebrew and Russian before turning to Yiddish, and published his first Yiddish work, the novel Stempenyu, in 1888. He lost his fortune in stock speculation in 1890, fled his creditors, and thereafter lived by his pen and by exhausting lecture tours.

After the pogroms of 1905 he left Russia, living variously in Switzerland, Germany and Denmark, and toured the United States in 1906–07. Falling health (tuberculosis) and the outbreak of the First World War drove him to New York in 1914, where he died on May 13, 1916. His funeral was among the largest New York had seen, with an estimated 100,000 mourners lining the route.

Works and style

Sholem Aleichem virtually created the classical canon of Yiddish fiction, in a monologue form — the character speaking in his own vernacular voice, with its mixture of comedy and pathos — that captured the speech, folk wisdom and predicaments of ordinary Eastern European Jews. His major creations include Tevye the Dairyman, the talmudically-quoting milkman negotiating tradition and modernity through his daughters' marriages; Menahem-Mendl, the hapless luftmensch speculator whose get-rich-quick schemes are narrated in letters to his long-suffering wife; The Railroad Stories (1909–11); and the unfinished autobiographical novel From the Fair. His pen name is a traditional Hebrew greeting, "peace be upon you".

Legacy

His work has been translated worldwide; Fiddler on the Roof made Tevye an international figure. A crater on Mercury is named Sholem Aleichem in his honour, and streets and schools across Israel, Ukraine and the Jewish diaspora bear his name.
