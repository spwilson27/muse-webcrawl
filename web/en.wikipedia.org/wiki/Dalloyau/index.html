Dalloyau

Type: Private
Industry: Gastronomy, pâtisserie
Founded: 1682 (origins); 1802 in Paris
Headquarters: Paris, France
Known for: Opéra cake

Dalloyau is a French gastronomy and pastry house based in Paris, tracing its origins to 1682, when Charles Dalloyau served at the court of Versailles. In 1802 Jean-Baptiste Dalloyau established the house in Paris as one of the first maisons de bouche, offering ready-made fine dishes and pastries to the Parisian bourgeoisie.

The Opéra cake

Dalloyau is credited with popularising the Opéra cake, a layered almond sponge, coffee buttercream and chocolate ganache creation associated with its pastry chef Cyriaque Gavillon in the 1950s — named, according to tradition, after a dancer of the Paris Opera whose husband exclaimed it reminded him of the stage of the Palais Garnier.

Today

The company operates boutiques in Paris, elsewhere in France and in Japan and other international markets, offering pastries, chocolates, catering and festive creations such as galettes des rois and bûches de Noël. Its flagship store stands on the Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré.
