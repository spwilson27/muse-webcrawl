Abraham Kuyper (29 October 1837 – 8 November 1920) was a Dutch Calvinist theologian, politician and journalist. He founded the Anti-Revolutionary Party, served as Prime Minister of the Netherlands from 1901 to 1905, and led the secession that formed the Reformed Churches in the Netherlands. He is known for the doctrines of sphere sovereignty and common grace.

Early life and theology

Kuyper studied theology at Leiden University. After serving as a pastor, he became a leading figure among the orthodox Calvinist opposition to liberal theology in the Dutch Reformed Church. In 1886 he led the Doleantie secession, and in 1892 the seceding churches united as the Reformed Churches in the Netherlands.

Institutions

In 1880 Kuyper founded the Free University of Amsterdam (Vrije Universiteit). He edited the newspaper De Standaard for nearly fifty years and authored the influential Stone Lectures at Princeton (1898), published as Lectures on Calvinism.

Politics

Kuyper's Anti-Revolutionary Party (founded 1879) was the Netherlands' first modern political party. As prime minister he pursued colonial reform and social legislation, though his government was marked by the harsh suppression of the 1903 railway strike. His concept of "pillarisation" shaped Dutch society for much of the twentieth century.
