Joanna, Duchess of Brabant (24 June 1322 – 1 November 1406), also known as Jeanne, was the hereditary Duchess of Brabant and Limburg from 1355 until her death. She was the first woman to rule Brabant in her own right, and her long reign — ending with the duchy's passage to the House of Valois-Burgundy — shaped the constitutional development of the Low Countries.

Accession and the Joyous Entry

The eldest daughter of Duke John III, Joanna had first been married to William IV, Count of Holland, who died in 1345, and then to Wenceslaus of Luxembourg, half-brother of Emperor Charles IV. On her father's death in 1355 she succeeded with Wenceslaus as co-ruler. To secure the loyalty of the Brabantine cities, the couple issued the Blijde Inkomste or Joyous Entry charter of 1356, a landmark document that limited ducal power and guaranteed the liberties of the towns and nobles; it remained a touchstone of Brabantine constitutionalism for centuries.

Reign

Joanna's succession was contested by her brothers-in-law, the counts of Flanders and Guelders, leading to the War of the Brabantine Succession, which cost her some territory. After Wenceslaus died in 1383 she ruled alone, defending her lands against Guelders and negotiating with France, England and the Empire. Childless, she arranged for her inheritance to pass to her niece Margaret of Male's line — ultimately to Antoine of Burgundy, grandson of the Count of Flanders — thereby bringing Brabant into the Burgundian sphere in 1404 and preparing the unification of the Netherlands under the Dukes of Burgundy.

Death

Joanna died in Brussels in 1406, aged 84, after a reign of more than half a century, and was buried in the Carmelite church there.
