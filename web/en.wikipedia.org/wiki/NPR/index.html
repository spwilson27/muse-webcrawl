NPR (National Public Radio) is an American non-profit public radio network, incorporated in 1970, which produces and distributes news and cultural programming to hundreds of member stations. Its flagship programmes include the drive-time newsmagazines Morning Edition and All Things Considered, among the most-listened radio programmes in the United States.

History and structure

NPR was created under the Public Broadcasting Act of 1967 and went on air in 1971. It is a membership organisation of independent local stations; NPR produces national programmes while stations produce local news and buy in network content. Shows such as Fresh Air, Wait Wait... Don't Tell Me! and the Tiny Desk Concerts are cultural institutions.

Funding and politics

NPR receives most revenue from member stations, corporate sponsorship and grants, with a small historic share indirectly from federal funding through the Corporation for Public Broadcasting. In 2025 the federal government moved to end CPB funding, a major upheaval for public media; details continued to develop.
