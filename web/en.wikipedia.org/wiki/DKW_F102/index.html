The DKW F102 is a passenger car that was produced by the West German manufacturer Auto Union under the DKW brand from 1963 to 1966. It was the last model developed by Auto Union under the DKW name and the last West German production car powered by a two-stroke engine.

Development and design

The F102 replaced the Auto Union 1000 S and introduced a modernised body with a self-supporting (unitary) construction — a first for the brand — together with front disc brakes, which were unusual in its class at the time. It retained DKW's traditional layout of a front-mounted three-cylinder two-stroke engine driving the front wheels. The engine displaced 1,175 cc and produced about 60 PS (44 kW), giving respectable performance, though the two-stroke unit was increasingly criticised for noise, exhaust smoke and poor fuel economy.

End of the marque

Sales fell short of expectations as the West German market turned decisively toward four-stroke cars. Auto Union was taken over by Volkswagen in 1964–65, and the F102's body shell was re-engineered with a four-stroke engine developed with Daimler-Benz. Relaunched in 1965 as the Audi F103, it became the first post-war car to carry the revived Audi name — and the DKW F102 thus marks the end of the DKW marque in 1966.
