The Needles Highway is a section of South Dakota Highway 87 in the Black Hills of western South Dakota, United States. It is renowned as one of the most scenic drives in America, passing through Custer State Park amid dramatic granite spires, narrow tunnels blasted through solid rock, and hairpin "pigtail" bridges.

Route description

The highway runs roughly 14 miles (23 km) between the towns of Custer and the junction with U.S. Route 16A near Sylvan Lake. Its most famous features are the single-lane tunnels — including the Needles Eye Tunnel, only about 8 feet 4 inches (2.54 m) wide and 12 feet (3.7 m) high — which restrict the size of vehicles that can pass. The road's granite formations, called "needles," rise sharply on either side of the roadway.

History

The road was championed by South Dakota governor and later U.S. Senator Peter Norbeck, who personally scouted much of the route on foot and horseback. Engineers initially declared the road impossible to build, but construction proceeded and the highway was completed in 1922. Norbeck also laid out the nearby Iron Mountain Road, and the two drives are often travelled together as a loop.

Tourism

The Needles Highway is a highlight of Custer State Park and is typically open from late spring to mid-autumn, weather permitting; it is closed in winter. It forms part of the Peter Norbeck Scenic Byway. Visitors frequently combine the drive with stops at Sylvan Lake and the Cathedral Spires trailhead.
