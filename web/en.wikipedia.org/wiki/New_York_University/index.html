New York University (NYU) is a private research university in New York City, United States. Founded in 1831 by a group of New Yorkers led by Albert Gallatin, then Secretary of the Treasury, NYU has grown into one of the largest private universities in the United States, with an enrollment of over 61,000 students across its schools and its global network of campuses and study-away sites. Its main campus is centred around Washington Square Park in Greenwich Village, Manhattan.

History

Albert Gallatin envisioned a university "in and of the city" open to all classes of society on the basis of merit. NYU opened its doors in 1831 and formally received its charter in 1832. In the late 19th century it moved much of its undergraduate activity to a campus in the Bronx (now Bronx Community College), returning to a consolidated Manhattan footprint after selling that campus in 1973. Under presidents such as John Sexton, NYU expanded into a self-described "global network university," opening degree-granting portal campuses at NYU Abu Dhabi (2010) and NYU Shanghai (2012, in partnership with East China Normal University) and acquiring engineering through the former Polytechnic Institute, now the Tandon School of Engineering in Brooklyn.

Academics

NYU comprises schools and colleges including the College of Arts and Science, Stern School of Business, Tisch School of the Arts (renowned for film and drama), School of Law, Grossman School of Medicine, Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences, Steinhardt School, Wagner School of Public Service, and Tandon School of Engineering. The university is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" and is consistently ranked among the top national universities in the United States. Its alumni and faculty include dozens of Nobel laureates, Pulitzer Prize winners, Academy Award winners, and heads of state.

Campus and student life

Lacking a traditional walled campus, NYU is woven into Manhattan — symbolized by its purple torch logo and violet flags — with Bobst Library on Washington Square South as its hub. Athletics teams, the Violets, compete in NCAA Division III. Student activism and the university's urban expansion, including controversies over its "2031" growth plan in Greenwich Village, have been recurring themes in its modern history.
