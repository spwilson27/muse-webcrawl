Six Thinking Hats
AuthorEdward de Bono
Published1985
SubjectDecision-making, group discussion, lateral thinking

Six Thinking Hats is a book by Edward de Bono and the name of a thinking tool used in group discussion and individual thinking. First published in 1985, it provides a means for groups to plan thinking processes in a detailed and cohesive way and to think together more effectively by "wearing" — in turn — one of six metaphorical coloured hats, each representing a distinct mode of thought.

The six hats

  • White hat — facts and information: what do we know, and what do we need to find out?
  • Red hat — emotions, feelings, intuition and hunches, without needing to justify them.
  • Black hat — caution, risks and critical judgement: what could go wrong?
  • Yellow hat — optimism and benefits: why will this work?
  • Green hat — creativity, alternatives and new ideas.
  • Blue hat — process control: organising the thinking, setting the agenda and summarising.

Method and use

Because everyone "wears" the same hat at the same time, the technique replaces adversarial debate with parallel thinking and allows participants to adopt modes — such as criticism — without personal confrontation. The method has been widely adopted in business meetings, education and facilitation, though controlled research on its effectiveness is limited.
