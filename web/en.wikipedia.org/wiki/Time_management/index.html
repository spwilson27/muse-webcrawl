Time management

Time management is the process of planning and exercising conscious control of time spent on specific activities—especially to increase effectiveness, efficiency and productivity. It involves demands relating to work, social life, family, hobbies and personal commitments.

Techniques

Popular methods include the Eisenhower matrix (urgent vs important), the Pomodoro Technique of timed work intervals, David Allen's Getting Things Done, time-blocking, ABC analysis, and Pareto analysis (the 80/20 rule). Common tools are to-do lists, calendars, prioritisation, goal-setting and delegation.

Concepts

Underlying ideas include Parkinson's law ('work expands to fill the time available'), the planning fallacy, procrastination, and the distinction in Stephen Covey's writings between clock-based scheduling and values-based 'compass' management.

Research and criticism

Studies link perceived control of time with job satisfaction and lower stress more strongly than specific techniques; critics note that relentless optimisation can itself become a source of anxiety and that structural workloads limit individual strategies.
