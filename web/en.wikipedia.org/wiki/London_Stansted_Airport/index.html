London Stansted Airport

London Stansted Airport (IATA: STN, ICAO: EGSS) is an international airport serving London, England. It is located near Stansted Mountfitchet, Essex, about 42 miles (68 km) northeast of central London. Stansted is a major base for low-cost airlines, particularly Ryanair, and is among the busiest airports in the United Kingdom.

History

The site began as RAF Stansted Mountfitchet, a United States Army Air Forces base in the Second World War. It was developed as a civil airport from the 1960s. The current terminal, designed by Norman Foster and notable for its lightweight roof and underfloor services, opened in 1991. Stansted became the centrepiece of debates over southeast England airport expansion; plans for a second runway were abandoned in 2010. In 2013 the airport was sold by Heathrow Airport Holdings to Manchester Airports Group.

Airlines and destinations

Stansted is dominated by short-haul European services. Ryanair has its largest base here, with easyJet, Jet2, and TUI also operating significant schedules. The airport is also a major UK air-freight hub and is designated as a diversion airport for aircraft involved in security incidents; it has hosted official state arrivals and is a preferred airport for deported-terrorist and hijack responses because of its relative isolation.

Ground transport

The Stansted Express rail service links the airport station to London Liverpool Street in about 47 minutes, with additional services to Tottenham Hale, Cambridge, and the Midlands. Coach services run to central London, and the M11 motorway provides road access.
