The Top End of Australia's Northern Territory is the northernmost section of the territory and the second-northernmost part of the Australian continent. It covers a largely tropical area of roughly 400,000 square kilometres bounded by the Timor Sea and the Arafura Sea to the north, the Gulf of Carpentaria to the east, and more arid country to the south. Its principal city is Darwin, the territorial capital.

Geography and climate

The region is dominated by the Arnhem Land plateau and its sandstone escarpment, extensive lowland plains, and great river systems including the Adelaide, South Alligator, East Alligator, Daly, and Katherine rivers. The climate is tropical savanna with two sharply distinct seasons: a hot, humid monsoonal wet season from about November to April, and a dry season with warm days and cool nights. Monsoon rainforests, eucalypt savanna, floodplains, and vast wetlands support exceptional birdlife, saltwater crocodiles, and other wildlife.

People and attractions

Aboriginal peoples have lived in the Top End for tens of thousands of years, and the region holds some of the world's richest rock-art sites. Kakadu National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site famed for sites such as Ubirr and Nourlangie, lies about 170 kilometres east of Darwin; Litchfield National Park and Nitmiluk (Katherine Gorge) are other major attractions. Much of the east of the region is Aboriginal land, including Arnhem Land. Major industries include tourism, mining (including uranium at Ranger), pastoralism, and defence, with Darwin hosting significant Australian Defence Force bases.
