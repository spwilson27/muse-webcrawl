Swedish (Swedish: svenska) is a North Germanic language spoken mainly in Sweden and in parts of Finland, where it is co-official with Finnish. With roughly ten million native speakers, it is the most widely spoken of the Scandinavian languages and is largely mutually intelligible with Norwegian and, to a somewhat lesser extent, Danish.

Features

Swedish uses the Latin alphabet with three additional vowels: å, ä and ö, sorted at the end of the alphabet. It has two genders (common and neuter), forms the definite article as a suffix (hus — "house", huset — "the house"), and is famous among learners for its pitch accent, which distinguishes otherwise identical words. Verb conjugation is simple: verbs do not change form according to person.

History

Swedish developed from Old Norse, diverging from the eastern branch of Norse during the Viking Age. Runic inscriptions are the earliest texts; a standard written language emerged in the 16th century with the Gustav Vasa Bible (1541). The great spelling reform of 1906 simplified orthography, and 20th-century changes — including the informalisation of pronouns in the 1960s du-reform — brought written and spoken Swedish closer together.
