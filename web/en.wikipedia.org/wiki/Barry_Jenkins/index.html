Barry Jenkins

Barry Jenkins (born November 19, 1979) is an American filmmaker. He gained wide acclaim for Moonlight (2016), which won the Academy Award for Best Picture and earned him, with Tarell Alvin McCraney, the Oscar for Best Adapted Screenplay. Raised in Miami's Liberty City neighbourhood, Jenkins studied film at Florida State University, where he formed long-lasting collaborations with cinematographer James Laxton and producers Adele Romanski and Dede Gardner's Plan B.

Career

His debut feature, Medicine for Melancholy (2008), a low-budget romance set in gentrifying San Francisco, was praised for its visual lyricism. After an eight-year gap in features, Moonlight — adapted from McCraney's unproduced play and made for about $1.5 million — told the coming-of-age story of Chiron in three chapters. Its Best Picture win at the 89th Academy Awards followed a famous envelope mix-up in which La La Land was first announced.

Jenkins next adapted James Baldwin's novel If Beale Street Could Talk (2018), and created the Amazon limited series The Underground Railroad (2021), based on Colson Whitehead's novel, which won widespread critical praise. He directed the photorealistic prequel Mufasa: The Lion King (2024) for Disney. His tender, natural-light-driven style is often compared to that of Wong Kar-wai and Claire Denis.
