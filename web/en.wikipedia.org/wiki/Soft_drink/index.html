Soft drink

A soft drink (see regional names below) is a drink that usually contains water (often carbonated), a sweetener, and a natural or artificial flavoring. The sweetener may be sugar, high-fructose corn syrup, fruit juice, a sugar substitute (in diet drinks), or some combination. Soft drinks may also contain caffeine, colorings, preservatives, and other ingredients. A drink is called "soft" in contrast with "hard" (alcoholic) drinks.

Terminology

In the United States, usage varies regionally: "soda" predominates in the Northeast, West, and South Florida; "pop" in the Midwest and Pacific Northwest; and "Coke" is used generically in much of the South. In Britain and Ireland the terms "fizzy drink" and "pop" are common; in Canada, "pop" is standard.

History

Effervescent drinks from naturally carbonated mineral springs were consumed for centuries for their supposed health benefits. In 1767, Joseph Priestley developed a method of infusing water with carbon dioxide. Torbern Bergman and later Jacob Schweppe (founder of Schweppes, 1783) commercialized carbonated water. Flavored sodas followed in the 19th century: ginger ale, root beer, Dr Pepper (1885, Waco, Texas), Coca-Cola (1886, Atlanta, created by pharmacist John Pemberton), and Pepsi (1893, Caleb Bradham). Soft drinks were sold at drugstore soda fountains before bottling and canning made them ubiquitous in the 20th century.

Production

Manufacturers prepare a flavored syrup, mix it with treated water, carbonate the mixture, and fill it into bottles or cans under pressure. Sweeteners account for roughly 7–12% of typical regular sodas by weight.

Health issues

Frequent consumption of sugar-sweetened soft drinks is associated with obesity, type 2 diabetes, dental caries, and gout. Phosphoric acid in colas and the drinks' acidity can erode tooth enamel. These concerns have driven declining per-capita soda consumption in the United States since the late 1990s, growth in bottled water and energy drinks, and the adoption of sugar taxes in jurisdictions such as Mexico (2014) and the United Kingdom (Soft Drinks Industry Levy, 2018).
