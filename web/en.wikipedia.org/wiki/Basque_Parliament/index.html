The Basque Parliament in Vitoria-Gasteiz

The Basque Parliament (Basque: Eusko Legebiltzarra; Spanish: Parlamento Vasco) is the legislative body of the Basque Autonomous Community in northern Spain. It sits in the former Ramiro de Maeztu secondary school building in Vitoria-Gasteiz, the community's capital.

Composition

The Parliament consists of 75 members, with 25 elected from each of the three historical territories — Álava, Biscay, and Gipuzkoa — regardless of population. Members are elected by proportional representation for four-year terms. This equal territorial representation is a defining feature of Basque institutions.

Functions

The Parliament enacts legislation in areas devolved under the Statute of Autonomy, approves the regional budget, and elects the lehendakari, the president of the Basque Government, whom it can remove through a vote of no confidence. Sessions are conducted in both Basque and Spanish, the community's co-official languages.

History

The first modern Basque Parliament was elected in March 1980 following the approval of the Statute of Gernika in a 1979 referendum. Basque nationalist parties, particularly the Basque Nationalist Party (PNV), have historically been leading forces in the chamber, alongside statewide Spanish parties and the Basque independentist left.
