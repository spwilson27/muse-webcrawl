The Prime Minister of Lebanon, officially the President of the Council of Ministers (Arabic: رئيس مجلس الوزراء), is the head of government of Lebanon. Under the unwritten National Pact of 1943 and the confessional system it codified, the prime minister is always a Sunni Muslim, while the president is a Maronite Christian and the speaker of parliament a Shia Muslim. The prime minister is designated by the president of the republic following binding consultations with members of parliament, and then forms a cabinet that must win a vote of confidence.

Powers

Since the Taif Agreement of 1989 ended the civil war and rebalanced the constitution, executive power is vested collectively in the Council of Ministers; the prime minister chairs the cabinet, signs decrees together with the president, sets the agenda, and speaks for the government in parliament. Nevertheless the office's effectiveness depends heavily on the balance among Lebanon's confessional blocs and on regional patrons. The prime minister's official residence is the Grand Serail in downtown Beirut.

Notable holders

Early prime ministers included Riad Al Solh, the first prime minister of independent Lebanon (1943), and Saeb Salam. Rafic Hariri, the billionaire businessman who dominated post-war reconstruction, served 1992–98 and 2000–04; his assassination by a massive car bomb in Beirut on 14 February 2005 triggered the Cedar Revolution and Syria's military withdrawal. His son Saad Hariri served two terms (2009–11, 2016–20), resigning amid the October 2019 protests. Hassan Diab's government (2020–21) resigned after the Beirut port explosion. Najib Mikati, a repeat holder of the office, formed a government in September 2021 and continued in a caretaker capacity after parliamentary elections in May 2022 and through the presidential vacuum that followed.
