Quinhydrone

Quinhydrone
Composition1:1 complex of quinone and hydroquinone
FormulaC12H10O4
AppearanceDark green crystals with metallic lustre

Quinhydrone is a charge-transfer complex consisting of equimolar amounts of benzoquinone and hydroquinone. It forms dark green crystals with a metallic lustre and dissolves in water to give a solution containing both components in equilibrium with a small amount of the semiquinone radical.

Quinhydrone is a 1:1 molecular complex held together by hydrogen bonds and charge-transfer interactions.

Quinhydrone electrode

The quinhydrone electrode, introduced by Einar Biilmann in 1921, is a redox electrode used to measure the pH of a solution. A platinum wire immersed in a solution saturated with quinhydrone develops a potential that depends linearly on hydrogen ion activity, because the quinone/hydroquinone couple involves two electrons and two protons. The electrode was widely used before the practical glass electrode became available; it is unreliable above pH 8, in strongly oxidising or reducing solutions, and in the presence of proteins.

Properties

In the solid, alternating quinone and hydroquinone molecules are linked by hydrogen bonds into chains, and the intense colour arises from charge transfer between the electron-rich hydroquinone and electron-poor quinone units.
