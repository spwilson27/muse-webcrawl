Maison du Roi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Meaning"The King's House"
ContextRoyal household of France; also a building on the Grand-Place, Brussels

The Maison du Roi ("King's Household") was the royal household of the King of France in the ancien régime. It comprised the monarch's domestic services, his military household troops, and the officers who administered the court.

Organisation

The Maison du Roi was divided into numerous departments covering the king's chapel, chamber, kitchens, stables, hunting establishments and guards. Great officers such as the Grand Master of France (Grand Maître de France) supervised whole branches of the household. The military part of the household included prestigious cavalry and infantry units such as the Musketeers of the Guard, the Gardes du Corps and the Cent-Suisses, who provided the king's bodyguard and much of the ceremonial display of the monarchy.

Under Louis XIV the household reached its greatest size and splendour, employing thousands of people at Versailles. Positions at court were much sought after by the nobility, and service in the Maison du Roi conferred status as well as income.

End

The household was progressively curtailed by reforms in the 1780s and was abolished during the French Revolution, though a reduced royal household was restored under the Bourbon Restoration.

The Brussels building

The name is also borne by the Maison du Roi (in Dutch, Broodhuis, "Bread House") on the Grand-Place in Brussels, a neo-Gothic building of 1873–1887 on the site of an older guildhall, now housing the Museum of the City of Brussels.
