Sostratus of Cnidus (fl. 3rd century BC) was a Greek architect and engineer in the service of the early Ptolemaic kings of Egypt. He is famous as the designer of the Lighthouse of Alexandria (the Pharos), one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, built on the island of Pharos at the entrance to Alexandria's harbour in the reigns of Ptolemy I Soter and Ptolemy II Philadelphus and completed around 280 BC.

The Pharos dedication story

According to a story retold by Lucian, Sostratus wished to sign his work but was forbidden to place his own name on it; instead, the official dedication named King Ptolemy. Sostratus carved his own inscription into the stone beneath — "Sostratus of Cnidus, son of Dexiphanes, to the Saviour Gods, for the sake of sailors" — and covered it with plaster bearing the king's dedication, knowing that the plaster would in time fall away and reveal his name. The anecdote may be embellished, but the dedicatory formula itself is attested in ancient authors.

Other works

Sostratus reportedly worked at Cnidus and Delphi (financing or designing a clubhouse for Cnidian merchants) and was associated with hydraulic works in Egypt, including diverting Nile channels. Little survives beyond the Pharos tradition. The lighthouse itself stood, in damaged form, for more than a millennium until earthquakes in the 10th–14th centuries destroyed it; its site is now occupied by the Citadel of Qaitbey in Alexandria.
