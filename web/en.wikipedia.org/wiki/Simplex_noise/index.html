Simplex noise

Simplex noise is a method for constructing an n-dimensional noise function comparable to Perlin noise ("classic" noise), but with fewer directional artifacts and, in higher dimensions, a lower computational overhead. Ken Perlin designed the algorithm in 2001 to address the limitations of his classic noise function, especially in higher dimensions.

Algorithm details

Instead of dividing space with an axis-aligned grid of hypercubes, simplex noise subdivides space into simplices — triangles in 2D, tetrahedra in 3D, and their n-dimensional analogues. Each simplex has only n + 1 corners, compared with 2n corners for a hypercube, so the number of gradient evaluations grows linearly rather than exponentially with dimension. Pseudo-random gradients at the corners are extrapolated and summed with a radially symmetric kernel, yielding smooth, band-limited noise.

Uses

Simplex noise is used in computer graphics and procedural generation for textures, terrain heightmaps, clouds, fire, water, and other natural phenomena, often summed in multiple octaves (fractal Brownian motion) to create detail at several scales.

Patent status and alternatives

Simplex noise was covered by U.S. patent 6,867,776 for uses in three or more dimensions for texture synthesis; the patent expired in 2022. The related open-source OpenSimplex noise (2014) and OpenSimplex2 were developed to provide similar quality while avoiding the patented approach.
