Chagai-I is the code name of the first public nuclear weapons test conducted by Pakistan. On 28 May 1998, Pakistan detonated five nuclear devices simultaneously in tunnels beneath the Ras Koh Hills in the Chagai District of Balochistan province. The tests made Pakistan the seventh country in the world to publicly test nuclear weapons, and the first Muslim-majority state to do so.

Background

Pakistan's nuclear weapons programme was initiated in 1972 under Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, following the loss of East Pakistan in the 1971 war. Research accelerated after India's first nuclear test, "Smiling Buddha," in May 1974. Under the leadership of the Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission and later Dr. Abdul Qadeer Khan's Kahuta Research Laboratories, Pakistan developed uranium enrichment capability and produced weapon designs through the 1980s. Pakistan is widely believed to have conducted "cold tests" (non-explosive criticality experiments) in the 1980s.

The immediate trigger for the 1998 tests was India's Pokhran-II series of five nuclear tests on 11 and 13 May 1998. Despite intense international pressure, particularly from the United States, the government of Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif decided to respond in kind.

The tests

At 14:16 PST on 28 May 1998, five devices were detonated simultaneously inside a horizontal tunnel bored into a granite mountain in the Ras Koh Hills. The devices were boosted-fission uranium implosion weapons. Pakistani officials reported a total yield of about 40 kilotons, though independent seismic estimates were lower, in the range of roughly 9 to 12 kilotons combined. The event registered approximately magnitude 5.0 on seismic scales.

The day is commemorated in Pakistan as Youm-e-Takbir ("Day of Greatness"). Two days later, on 30 May 1998, Pakistan conducted a sixth test, code-named Chagai-II, at the Kharan desert site.

International reaction

The tests were condemned by many countries and led to economic sanctions imposed by the United States, Japan, and others under legislation such as the Glenn Amendment. The UN Security Council adopted Resolution 1172 condemning both the Indian and Pakistani tests. In Pakistan, however, the tests were widely celebrated as a restoration of strategic balance with India.
