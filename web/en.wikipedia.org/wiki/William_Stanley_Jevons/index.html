William Stanley Jevons

William Stanley Jevons (1 September 1835 – 13 August 1882) was an English economist and logician, a founder of marginal utility theory and of the marginal revolution in economics.

Born: 1835, Liverpool
Died: 1882, Hastings (drowned)
Known for: Marginal utility, Jevons paradox

Work

In The Theory of Political Economy (1871) Jevons argued value depends on final degree of utility. The Coal Question (1865) foresaw resource limits and stated the Jevons paradox: efficiency gains can raise total consumption. He also built a logic piano, an early reasoning machine, and contributed to index numbers and sunspot theories of business cycles.

See Marginal utility, Carl Menger, Léon Walras.
