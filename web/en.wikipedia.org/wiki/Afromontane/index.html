Afromontane is a term describing the plant and animal species and biogeographical communities found in the highlands of Africa, which form a discontinuous archipelago of montane habitats running from the Ethiopian Highlands and the mountains of the Great Lakes region south to the Drakensberg of South Africa, with outliers in Cameroon, Angola, and the highlands of Yemen and Saudi Arabia.

Characteristics

Although the Afromontane patches are separated by hundreds of kilometres of lowland, they share a distinctive flora dominated by conifers such as Podocarpus and Afrocarpus species and African juniper (Juniperus procera), together with broadleaved trees such as Prunus africana, Olea, and wild olive relatives, and an understorey rich in giant lobelias and tree heaths (Erica) at higher elevations, where Afroalpine moorlands occur above the treeline. The botanist Frank White formalized the Afromontane as one of Africa's major phytochoria (regional centres of endemism).

Ecology and conservation

Because the montane "islands" are isolated, they harbour high levels of endemism; regions such as the Eastern Arc Mountains of Tanzania and the Albertine Rift are recognized biodiversity hotspots, home to endemic birds, chameleons, and primates including the mountain gorilla. Afromontane forests are heavily threatened by agricultural clearing, logging, and climate change, which squeezes cool-climate species toward the summits.
