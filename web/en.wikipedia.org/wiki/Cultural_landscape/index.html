Cultural landscape

A cultural landscape is a landscape that has been affected, influenced, or shaped by human involvement or activity. The term is used in geography and heritage conservation for the combined works of nature and humankind that express a long and intimate relationship between peoples and their natural environment.

Definitions

The American geographer Carl O. Sauer developed the concept in the 1920s, arguing that culture is the agent, the natural area the medium, and the cultural landscape the result. The UNESCO World Heritage Committee adopted cultural landscapes as a category of World Heritage Site in 1992, becoming the first international legal instrument to recognise and protect them.

Categories

UNESCO distinguishes three main categories:

  • Clearly defined landscapes designed and created intentionally by humans, such as gardens and parklands.
  • Organically evolved landscapes resulting from social, economic, administrative, or religious development over time, including relict (fossil) landscapes and continuing landscapes such as terraced farmland.
  • Associative cultural landscapes valued for their powerful religious, artistic, or cultural associations with nature rather than for material evidence.

Examples

Well-known cultural landscapes on the World Heritage List include the rice terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras, the vineyard landscape of the Alto Douro in Portugal, the sacred site of Uluru-Kata Tjuta in Australia, and the English Lake District.
