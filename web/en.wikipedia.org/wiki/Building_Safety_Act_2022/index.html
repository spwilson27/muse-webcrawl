Building Safety Act 2022

Building Safety Act 2022
Legislation: Parliament of the United Kingdom
Royal assent: 28 April 2022
Context: Response to the Grenfell Tower fire (2017)

The Building Safety Act 2022 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that overhauls the regulation of building safety in England, principally in response to the Grenfell Tower fire of June 2017, in which 72 people died, and the subsequent public inquiry and review led by Dame Judith Hackitt.

Key provisions

The Act establishes a new Building Safety Regulator within the Health and Safety Executive, introduces a stricter regime for "higher-risk buildings" (generally at least 18 metres or seven storeys with at least two residential units), and creates accountable persons responsible for the safety of occupied buildings. It also introduces gateways for design and construction approval, a "golden thread" of building information, and a new homes ombudsman.

Leaseholder protections

The Act protects qualifying leaseholders from much of the cost of remediating unsafe cladding and other historic building defects, placing responsibilities on developers and building owners, and extends the period for claims under the Defective Premises Act 1972.

Implementation

Major secondary legislation, including registration of high-rise residential buildings and new dutyholder roles, came into force during 2023 and 2024. The Act applies mainly to England, with some provisions extending across the UK.
