Dissolve (filmmaking)

In film and video editing, a dissolve (also called a lap dissolve) is a type of film transition in which one sequence fades over another. The terms fade-out and fade-in are used to describe a transition to and from a blank image (usually black). A dissolve overlaps two shots for the duration of the effect, usually at the end of one scene and the beginning of the next, although they are sometimes used elsewhere.

Use

Generally, dissolves are used to indicate the passage of time or a change of location, in contrast to the abrupt juxtaposition of a straight cut. A short dissolve may suggest a brief lapse of time, whereas a long, slow dissolve may indicate a dream, memory, or a gentle shift in mood. During the classical Hollywood era, dissolves were a standard punctuation mark in film grammar; many film theorists and editors noted their conventional association with the passage of time within a sequence of related scenes (montage sequences often use dissolves or a series of them to compress time).

In modern digital editing, dissolves are produced electronically or in software, but in the photochemical era they were created with an optical printer by overlapping two strips of film, or in-camera by fading out, rewinding the film, and fading in the next shot. The dissolve is one of the most common transitions available in non-linear editing systems, along with the cut, fade, and wipe.
