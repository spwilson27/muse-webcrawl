List of provincial parks in Canada

Canada's provinces and territories manage thousands of provincial parks protecting landscapes from coast to coast — in total a far larger area than the federal national park system. Each province maintains its own list and park agency (e.g. BC Parks, Ontario Parks, Alberta Parks, Sépaq in Quebec).

Notable provincial parks

  • Algonquin (Ontario, 1893) — Canada's oldest provincial park; canoe country and moose.
  • Wood Buffalo is national; large provincial counterparts include Atikaki (Manitoba) and Wabakimi (Ontario).
  • Strathcona (British Columbia, 1911) — BC's oldest provincial park, Vancouver Island.
  • Garibaldi, Tatlatui, Spatsizi Plateau — major BC wilderness parks.
  • Dinosaur Provincial Park (Alberta) — UNESCO site famous for fossils.
  • Cypress Hills Interprovincial Park (Alberta/Saskatchewan) — Canada's only interprovincial park.
  • Parc national du Mont-Tremblant and the Quebec "national parks" run by Sépaq are provincial in jurisdiction.
  • Sandbanks, Killarney, Bon Echo — popular Ontario parks.

Classification

Provincial systems classify parks as wilderness, natural environment, recreation, heritage, or conservation reserves, with rules varying by province.
