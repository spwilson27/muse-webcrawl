The Anakim (Hebrew: עֲנָקִים, ʿĂnāqîm) are a people described in the Hebrew Bible as formidable, tall inhabitants of the southern part of Canaan, in the hill country around Hebron, before the Israelite conquest. They are presented as descendants of Anak, himself the son of Arba, founder of Kiriath-arba (Hebron).

Biblical accounts

In Numbers 13, the spies sent by Moses report that the Anakim make them feel "like grasshoppers," and describe them as descended from the Nephilim — the source of the expression "sons of Anak." Deuteronomy calls them a people "great and tall" and groups them with the Rephaim. Joshua 11:21–22 states that Joshua cut off the Anakim from the hill country, leaving remnants only in Gaza, Gath, and Ashdod — a detail sometimes connected with the later Philistine giant Goliath of Gath. The named sons of Anak at Hebron were Ahiman, Sheshai, and Talmai, driven out by Caleb and Judah (Joshua 15:13–14).

Interpretation

The name is usually derived from a Hebrew word for "neck" or "necklace," suggesting "long-necked" or "necklace-wearing" people; some scholars have sought Egyptian connections, noting execration texts that mention a place or ruler "Iy-ʿanaq." In later tradition the Anakim became archetypal giants, and the term is used figuratively for something overwhelming.
