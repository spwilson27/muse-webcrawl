Drop spindle

A drop spindle is a hand tool used for spinning fibres such as wool, flax, hemp, and cotton into yarn or thread. It consists of a straight spike or shaft (the spindle) with a whorl — a disc-shaped weight — that acts as a flywheel, storing rotational energy so the spindle spins long enough for the spinner to draft out fibres and add twist.

Operation

The spinner attaches a length of already-spun yarn (a leader) to the spindle, drops or suspends the spindle so it hangs by the forming yarn, and sets it spinning with a flick of the fingers or a roll against the thigh. While the spindle turns, the spinner drafts fibres from a supply held in the hand or on a distaff, allowing twist to run into the drafted fibres to form new yarn. When the forming yarn grows too long to manage, the spinner stops, winds the finished length onto the spindle shaft in a cone or ball (the cop), and begins again.

Types

  • Top-whorl spindles — the whorl sits near the top of the shaft, usually with a hook above it.
  • Bottom-whorl spindles — the whorl is near the bottom; common in many historical and regional traditions.
  • Supported spindles — the tip rests in a bowl or on a surface; used for fine, short-staple fibres such as cotton (e.g., the takli of India).
  • Turkish spindles — have removable cross arms forming a centre-pull ball as yarn is wound.

History

Spindle whorls of stone, clay, and bone are among the commonest artefacts from Neolithic sites onward, attesting to the spindle's role as the principal spinning tool for thousands of years before the spinning wheel spread in the later Middle Ages. Drop spindles remain in everyday use in many parts of the world and have seen a revival among hand-spinners as a portable, inexpensive tool.
