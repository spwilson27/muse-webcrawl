Fujitsu

Fujitsu Limited (富士通株式会社) is a Japanese multinational information and communications technology equipment and services corporation, headquartered in Tokyo. Established in 1935, it is one of Japan's oldest computing companies and a major global provider of IT services.


Fujitsu headquarters, Tokyo

History

Fujitsu originated in 1923 as the telecommunications division of Fuji Electric, itself a joint venture involving Siemens. It was spun off in 1935 as Fuji Tsūshinki Seizō K.K. In 1954 it produced Japan's first relay-based computer, the FACOM 100, and went on to become a leading mainframe manufacturer, partnering with Britain's ICL and Germany's Siemens at various times. In the 2010s–2020s, Fujitsu developed, with the RIKEN research institute, the supercomputers Fugaku — the world's fastest from 2020 to 2022 — and its predecessor, the K computer.

Business

The company's offerings include servers, storage, personal computers (through Fujitsu Client Computing, part-owned by Lenovo), semiconductors (through subsidiaries), telecommunications equipment, and consulting and outsourcing services. Fujitsu was involved in the British Post Office Horizon scandal: the Horizon software it supplied was central to the wrongful prosecution of hundreds of subpostmasters, a matter examined by a public inquiry and widely described as one of the UK's worst miscarriages of justice.
