Peking University (PKU; Chinese: 北京大学) is a national public research university in Haidian, Beijing, China, and one of the country's most prestigious institutions. Founded in 1898 as the Imperial University of Peking during the Hundred Days' Reform, it was the first national comprehensive university in China and replaced the traditional imperial academy system.

History

Under president Cai Yuanpei from 1916, Peking University became a centre of the New Culture Movement, embracing academic freedom and intellectual diversity — "freedom of thought, inclusiveness" remains its ethos. The university was a cradle of the May Fourth Movement of 1919, and early faculty included figures such as Chen Duxiu, Li Dazhao, Hu Shih, and Lu Xun. In 1952 it moved to its present campus, the former site of Yenching University, beside the Summer Palace and the West Lake (Weiming Lake) with its Boya Pagoda.

Academics

PKU is consistently ranked at or near the top of Chinese universities and among the leading universities of Asia, with strengths across the humanities, sciences, medicine, law, and economics. Alumni include Nobel laureate Tu Youyou (affiliated with its health science centre), writer Lu Xun's contemporaries, and numerous leaders in Chinese politics, science, and culture. Its library is one of the largest academic libraries in Asia.
