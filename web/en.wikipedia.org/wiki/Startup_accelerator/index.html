Startup accelerator

A startup accelerator (also called a seed accelerator) is an organisation that supports early-stage, growth-driven companies through a fixed-term, cohort-based programme combining seed capital, mentorship, education and access to networks of investors and alumni. Programmes typically last around three months and end with a "demo day", at which the participating startups pitch to potential investors. In exchange for the investment and services, accelerators usually take a small equity stake.

History

The model was pioneered by Y Combinator, founded in 2005 in Cambridge, Massachusetts, by Paul Graham and partners, which backed companies such as Airbnb, Dropbox and Reddit. Techstars, founded in Boulder, Colorado, in 2006, developed a franchised network of city-based programmes, and the model spread worldwide through programmes such as Seedcamp (London), 500 Startups, and corporate and university accelerators.

Programme structure

Accelerators accept batches ("cohorts") of startups through competitive application processes, often with acceptance rates of a few percent. Typical features include a standard seed investment (for Y Combinator, historically structured through a "standard deal" using a post-money SAFE), intensive mentoring from experienced founders and operators, weekly talks and office hours, and help refining the product and fundraising story before demo day. Participants frequently relocate to the programme's city for its duration.

Distinctions and criticism

Accelerators differ from incubators, which generally provide longer-term workspace and support without a fixed cohort, equity-for-capital structure or demo day, and from angel investors and venture capital firms, which invest without an educational programme. Research on accelerator outcomes is mixed: graduates raise follow-on funding at higher rates, but selection effects make causal claims difficult, and critics note that most accelerated startups still fail and that the model suits some sectors better than others. Nevertheless, accelerators have become a standard institution of technology entrepreneurship ecosystems.
