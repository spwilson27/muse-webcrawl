Disputed territories of northern Iraq

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The disputed territories of northern Iraq are regions claimed both by the federal government of Iraq and by the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG), which administers the autonomous Kurdistan Region. The territories form a belt running from the Syrian border in the west to the Iranian border in the east, south of the officially recognised Kurdistan Region. They include the oil-rich city of Kirkuk, as well as parts of Nineveh, Diyala, and Saladin governorates – among them Sinjar, the Nineveh Plains, Tuz Khurmatu, Khanaqin, and Makhmur.

Background

The dispute has roots in the Ba'athist era "Arabisation" campaigns, during which Kurdish and other non-Arab residents were expelled from areas such as Kirkuk and replaced with Arab settlers, part of the same policy of repression that culminated in the Anfal campaign. After the 2003 invasion of Iraq, Kurdish Peshmerga forces moved into many of the territories.

Article 140

Article 140 of the 2005 Iraqi constitution provided a roadmap: normalisation (reversing Arabisation), a census, and a referendum to decide the territories' status, to be completed by the end of 2007. The deadline passed without implementation, and the referendum has never been held, leaving the dispute unresolved.

2014–2017 crisis

When the Islamic State (ISIL) offensive of 2014 collapsed Iraqi army positions in the north, Peshmerga forces took full control of Kirkuk and most of the disputed areas. In September 2017 the Kurdistan Region held an independence referendum that included the disputed territories; Baghdad rejected it, and in October 2017 Iraqi federal forces and allied militias retook Kirkuk and most of the disputed territories, which have since been under federal control or forms of contested joint administration.
