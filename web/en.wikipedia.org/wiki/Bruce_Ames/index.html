Bruce Nathan Ames (December 16, 1928 – October 5, 2024) was an American biochemist and professor at the University of California, Berkeley. He was the inventor of the Ames test, a cheap and rapid bacterial assay for screening chemicals for mutagenicity and hence potential carcinogenicity.

The Ames test

Developed in the early 1970s, the test uses strains of the bacterium Salmonella typhimurium engineered to require histidine; chemicals that cause mutations restoring the bacteria's ability to make histidine are flagged as mutagens. The test became a standard early screen in toxicology and regulation, and was used to show that many natural as well as synthetic chemicals are mutagenic.

Later work

Ames argued that the dose makes the poison and that natural pesticides in plants vastly outweigh synthetic residues in human diets. Later in his career he studied nutrition, aging, and mitochondrial decay, promoting the idea that micronutrient deficiencies accelerate aging and proposing the "triage theory" of nutrient allocation.
