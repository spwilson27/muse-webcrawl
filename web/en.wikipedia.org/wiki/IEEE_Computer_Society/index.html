IEEE Computer Society

The IEEE Computer Society (sometimes abbreviated Computer Society or CS) is a professional society of the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE). Its purpose and scope embrace "to advance the theory, practice, and application of computer and information processing science and technology" and the professional standing of its members. It is the largest of IEEE's 39 technical societies.

History

The society traces its origins to the Committee on Large-Scale Computing Devices founded in 1946 within the American Institute of Electrical Engineers, becoming the IRE Professional Group on Electronic Computers in 1951. After the AIEE–IRE merger formed IEEE in 1963, the group evolved into the Computer Society in 1971. It now has well over 300,000 members in more than 160 countries (counting IEEE members who elect society membership).

Activities

The Computer Society publishes more than 20 peer-reviewed journals and magazines, including Computer, IEEE Software, IEEE Security & Privacy, and the IEEE Transactions series, and sponsors or co-sponsors more than 200 technical conferences annually, among them the IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy and the International Conference on Software Engineering (jointly with ACM). It maintains the IEEE Computer Society Digital Library, develops standards such as POSIX (IEEE 1003), offers certifications, and administers awards including the Computer Pioneer Award.
