Shape of the universe

The shape of the universe concerns the geometry of space — its local curvature and global topology — in physical cosmology. In general relativity, spatial geometry may be positively curved (closed, like a sphere), negatively curved (open, saddle-shaped), or flat (Euclidean).

Observations

Measurements of the cosmic microwave background by WMAP and Planck indicate the observable universe is flat to within about 0.4% margin of error, consistent with cosmic inflation. Whether the whole universe is finite or infinite, and its topology (for example, a torus), remains undetermined, since a finite universe could be far larger than the observable part.

Density parameter

Geometry depends on the density parameter Ω: Ω > 1 implies a closed universe, Ω < 1 an open one, and Ω = 1 a flat one.
