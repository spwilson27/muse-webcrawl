IgA nephropathy (IgAN), also known as Berger's disease, is a kidney disease that occurs when the antibody immunoglobulin A (IgA) builds up in the glomeruli of the kidneys, causing inflammation that damages kidney tissue over time. It is the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide.

Signs and symptoms

The classic presentation is episodic visible haematuria (blood in the urine) occurring within a day or two of an upper respiratory tract infection — termed synpharyngitic haematuria. Many patients instead have microscopic haematuria and mild proteinuria found incidentally. A minority progress to nephrotic syndrome or rapidly progressive kidney failure; roughly a quarter to a third of patients reach kidney failure within 20–25 years.

Cause and diagnosis

The disease is thought to arise from galactose-deficient IgA1 molecules that form immune complexes depositing in the mesangium. Diagnosis requires a kidney biopsy, with immunofluorescence showing dominant or co-dominant IgA deposits.

Treatment

Management centres on blood-pressure control and proteinuria reduction with ACE inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers; immunosuppression with corticosteroids is reserved for high-risk patients. Targeted-release budesonide and sparsentan have been approved in some jurisdictions for adults at risk of rapid progression.
