Emergency power system

An emergency power system is an independent source of electrical power that supports important electrical systems on loss of normal power supply. A standby power system may include a standby generator, batteries, and other apparatus. Emergency power systems are installed to protect life and property from the consequences of loss of primary electric power supply.

Types

Systems range from small uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) that bridge brief interruptions for computers, through battery-backed lighting and alarm systems, to large diesel or gas turbine generator sets serving hospitals, data centers, water treatment plants, and high-rise buildings. Automatic transfer switches sense the failure of the utility supply, start the generator, and switch the load, typically within seconds.

Requirements

Building and life-safety codes — such as NFPA 70 (the National Electrical Code) and NFPA 110 in the United States — prescribe which loads must be served (egress lighting, fire alarms, smoke control, elevators, medical equipment) and how quickly power must be restored. Hospitals distinguish critical, life-safety, and equipment branches with restoration times as short as ten seconds.
