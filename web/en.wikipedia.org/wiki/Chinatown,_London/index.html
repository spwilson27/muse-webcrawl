Chinatown, London

Chinatown, London is an ethnic enclave in the City of Westminster, in the West End of London, centred on Gerrard Street and the surrounding streets of Soho. It is home to a concentration of Chinese restaurants, bakeries, supermarkets, souvenir shops and businesses serving London's Chinese community and visitors alike.

History

London's first Chinatown grew up in the Limehouse area of the East End in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, around Chinese seamen employed by shipping lines trading with the Far East. That community was heavily damaged by bombing during the Blitz and by post-war redevelopment. From the 1950s and 1960s, immigration from Hong Kong — including many farmers from the New Territories who opened restaurants — shifted the centre of Chinese London to Soho, where cheap premises were available in a district then known for nightlife. By the 1970s and 1980s, Gerrard Street and its neighbours had become the recognised Chinatown.

Features

The area is marked by ornamental Chinese gates (paifang), including a large gate on Wardour Street completed in 2016, street signs in Chinese characters, and red lanterns strung across the streets. It is a focal point for Chinese New Year celebrations, the largest outside Asia, which draw hundreds of thousands of visitors to the West End each year.
