Theodosius I

Theodosius I
Born: 11 January 347, Cauca (or Italica), Hispania
Died: 17 January 395 (aged 48), Mediolanum (Milan)
Reign: 379 – 395 (East from 379; sole emperor from 392)
Predecessor: Valens (East); Valentinian II (West)
Successors: Arcadius (East), Honorius (West)
Also known as: Theodosius the Great

Theodosius I (Greek: Θεοδόσιος; 11 January 347 – 17 January 395), also called Theodosius the Great, was Roman emperor from 379 to 395. He was the last emperor to rule the whole of the Roman Empire before its permanent division between his sons. He is remembered above all for making Nicene Christianity the official religion of the empire.

Rise to power

Theodosius was born in Hispania, the son of a distinguished general, Count Theodosius, under whom he campaigned in Britain and on the Danube. After his father's execution in 375/6, he withdrew to his estates in Spain, but the catastrophic Roman defeat at Adrianople in 378, in which the eastern emperor Valens was killed, led Gratian to recall him. Theodosius was proclaimed Augustus of the East on 19 January 379.

The Gothic War and settlement

Theodosius inherited a desperate situation on the Danube, where Gothic groups were at large inside the empire. Unable to defeat them outright, in 382 he concluded a settlement by which the Goths were established in Thrace and Moesia as foederati — semi-autonomous allies obliged to supply troops under their own chiefs. The policy bought peace but set a controversial precedent. Gothic soldiers thereafter played a major role in the eastern armies.

Religion

A devout Nicene Christian, Theodosius issued the Edict of Thessalonica in 380, together with Gratian and Valentinian II, declaring Nicene (Trinitarian) Christianity the faith of the empire. The Council of Constantinople in 381 reaffirmed the Nicene Creed and condemned heresies. In the 390s a series of laws went further, banning pagan sacrifice and closing temples; in this climate the Serapeum of Alexandria was destroyed by a Christian mob with official acquiescence.

After the massacre of citizens by the imperial garrison at Thessalonica in 390, Bishop Ambrose of Milan famously excluded Theodosius from communion until he performed public penance — a landmark moment in the relations between church and emperor.

Civil wars and death

Theodosius defeated the western usurpers Magnus Maximus (388) and Eugenius (at the Battle of the Frigidus, 394), briefly reuniting the empire under one ruler for the last time. He died at Milan in January 395. The empire was divided between his young sons: Arcadius took the East and Honorius the West, a division that became permanent.

Legacy

Theodosius' reign decisively Christianised the Roman state. In Constantinople he raised the Forum of Theodosius and erected the Egyptian obelisk still standing in the Hippodrome. The Theodosian dynasty he founded ruled the East until 457 and intermarried with the Valentinianic house in the West.
