The Department of Health and Social Care (DHSC) is a ministerial department of His Majesty's Government in the United Kingdom, responsible for government policy on health and adult social care matters in England, along with a few elements of policy in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. It oversees the National Health Service (NHS) in England and is led by the Secretary of State for Health and Social Care.

History

The department traces its origins to the Ministry of Health, created in 1919. In 1968 it merged with the Ministry of Social Security to form the Department of Health and Social Security (DHSS). In 1988 the DHSS was split into the Department of Health and the Department of Social Security. "and Social Care" was added to the department's name in January 2018, reflecting its responsibility for adult social care policy.

Responsibilities

The DHSC sets strategy and funding for the NHS, public health (through the UK Health Security Agency and the Office for Health Improvement and Disparities), and adult social care in England. Its arm's-length bodies include NHS England, the Care Quality Commission, the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE), and the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA).
