Inflation (cosmology)

In physical cosmology, cosmic inflation is a theory of exponential expansion of the early universe, in the first tiny fraction of a second after the Big Bang, driven by a hypothetical inflaton field.

Motivation

Proposed by Alan Guth in 1980, inflation solves the horizon problem (uniformity of the cosmic microwave background), the flatness problem, and the absence of magnetic monopoles. Quantum fluctuations stretched by inflation seed the large-scale structure of galaxies.

Evidence and models

Observations by COBE, WMAP and Planck of a nearly scale-invariant, Gaussian spectrum of fluctuations match inflationary predictions. Many models exist — slow-roll, eternal inflation and multiverse scenarios — and the search for primordial B-mode polarisation continues after the 2014 BICEP2 claim was attributed to galactic dust.

Criticism

Debates concern the theory's testability, initial conditions and measure problems in eternal inflation.
