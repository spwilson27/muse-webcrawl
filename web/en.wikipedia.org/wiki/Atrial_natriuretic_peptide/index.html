Atrial natriuretic peptide

Atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) or atrial natriuretic factor (ANF) is a hormone secreted from the cardiac atria in response to atrial stretch. It is a powerful vasodilator and reduces blood volume and blood pressure by increasing the renal excretion of sodium and water (natriuresis and diuresis). ANP is the founding member of the natriuretic peptide family, which also includes B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) and C-type natriuretic peptide (CNP).

Discovery

The existence of a cardiac hormone was demonstrated in 1981 by Adolfo de Bold and colleagues in Kingston, Ontario, who showed that injecting extracts of rat atrial (but not ventricular) tissue into rats produced a rapid, potent excretion of sodium and urine. The peptide was sequenced soon afterwards and named atrial natriuretic factor.

Synthesis and release

ANP is encoded by the NPPA gene and synthesised as a 151-amino-acid preprohormone, stored in atrial granules as the 126-amino-acid proANP. On release, triggered mainly by atrial wall stretch from increased blood volume, the enzyme corin cleaves proANP to yield the active 28-amino-acid C-terminal peptide.

Physiological actions

ANP acts mainly through the membrane receptor NPR-A, a particulate guanylyl cyclase that raises intracellular cGMP. In the kidney it dilates the afferent glomerular arteriole, raising glomerular filtration rate, and inhibits sodium reabsorption in the collecting duct. It also relaxes vascular smooth muscle, suppresses the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system and sympathetic outflow, and inhibits the secretion of aldosterone and vasopressin — a coordinated programme that opposes the salt- and water-retaining hormones.

Clinical significance

Circulating ANP rises in heart failure as atrial pressures increase. Its relative, BNP (and the diagnostic fragment NT-proBNP), became the standard blood biomarker for the diagnosis of heart failure. Recombinant BNP (nesiritide) was marketed for acute heart failure, and drugs that inhibit the ANP-degrading enzyme neprilysin — notably sacubitril, combined with valsartan — improve outcomes in chronic heart failure by potentiating the natriuretic peptides.
