PartiesNazi Germany and Fascist Italy (arbitrators); Kingdom of Hungary and Kingdom of Romania

The Second Vienna Award was the second of two territorial arbitrations made by Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy in 1940, through which Hungary gained territory from its neighbours. Signed at the Belvedere Palace in Vienna on 30 August 1940 by German foreign minister Joachim von Ribbentrop and Italian foreign minister Galeazzo Ciano, it granted Hungary Northern Transylvania, a territory of about 43,104 square kilometres (16,643 sq mi) with some 2.5 million inhabitants, which Romania had held since 1920.

Background

Hungary under Regent Miklós Horthy sought revision of the 1920 Treaty of Trianon, which had assigned all of Transylvania to Romania. After the First Vienna Award (1938) restored southern Slovakia, Hungary pressed its Transylvanian claim. Bilateral talks at Turnu Severin failed in August 1940, and Hitler — anxious to secure Romanian oil and keep both states in his orbit — imposed arbitration. Romania, already stripped of Bessarabia by Soviet ultimatum in June 1940, had no choice but to submit.

Terms and aftermath

The award divided Transylvania roughly along ethnic lines; Hungarian troops entered the territory in September 1940, greeted in Hungarian-majority areas and resented elsewhere, and episodes of violence against Romanians occurred. The award left both countries dissatisfied and dependent on Germany. After the Second World War, the 1947 Paris Peace Treaties annulled the Vienna Awards, and Northern Transylvania was returned to Romania.
