Wellington

Wellington is the capital city of New Zealand and the country's third-largest urban area, with about 215,000 residents in the city and 440,000 in the metropolitan area. It lies at the southwestern tip of the North Island, on Cook Strait, the water separating the North and South islands.

Wellington became capital in 1865, replacing Auckland. It is New Zealand's political centre (Parliament's 'Beehive' building), a cultural hub (Te Papa Tongarewa national museum, the Weta Workshop film studios of Peter Jackson's productions), and one of the windiest cities in the world, nicknamed 'Windy Wellington'. The city sits on major fault lines; the 1855 Wairarapa earthquake, among New Zealand's largest, uplifted its shoreline. A cable car climbs to the Botanic Garden above the harbour.
