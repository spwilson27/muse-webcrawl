The United States Penitentiary, Administrative Maximum Facility, commonly known as ADX Florence, is an American federal supermax prison in Colorado for male inmates deemed the most dangerous in the federal system. Opened in 1994, it is nicknamed "the Alcatraz of the Rockies".

Regime

Inmates at ADX spend about 23 hours a day in single, soundproofed concrete cells with a small slit window, eating meals inside the cell and exercising alone in a concrete pit. Contact with other prisoners is minimal, and the most restricted inmates are held under Special Administrative Measures. The regime is designed to incapacitate prisoners who have committed violent acts in other prisons or who pose exceptional security risks, including terrorists and organised-crime leaders.

Notable inmates

ADX has housed Ted Kaczynski, Ramzi Yousef, Dzhokhar Tsarnaev, Joaquín "El Chapo" Guzmán, Terry Nichols and Zacarias Moussaoui. The facility and its isolation regime have drawn criticism and litigation from human-rights advocates concerned about long-term solitary confinement and mental health.
