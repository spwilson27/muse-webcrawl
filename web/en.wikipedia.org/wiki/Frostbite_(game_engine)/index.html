Frostbite (game engine)

Frostbite is a game engine developed by DICE, the Swedish studio of Electronic Arts (EA). Originally created for first-person shooters, it became EA's standard in-house engine across most of its studios and genres, powering series including Battlefield, FIFA (later EA Sports FC), Need for Speed, Dragon Age, Mass Effect and Star Wars Battlefront.

History

The first version of Frostbite debuted with Battlefield: Bad Company (2008), notable for its highly destructible environments. Frostbite 2, introduced with Battlefield 3 (2011), expanded the scale of destruction, lighting and animation. From 2013, "Frostbite 3" powered most EA titles, ranging from shooters to role-playing games and sports titles, as EA consolidated development on a single engine to share technology and tools across studios.

The mandate to use Frostbite was controversial: development teams working on genres far removed from the engine's shooter origins, such as BioWare on Dragon Age: Inquisition (2014), Mass Effect: Andromeda (2017) and Anthem (2019), reported significant difficulties adapting its tools, which lacked features those games required. In later years EA relaxed the policy, allowing some studios — notably Respawn Entertainment — to use other engines such as Unreal Engine.

Features

Frostbite is designed for large-scale multiplayer environments, dynamic destruction of buildings and terrain, physically based rendering, and multi-platform deployment across PC and consoles. Development is centred at DICE in Stockholm with contributions from EA studios worldwide.
