Brown adipose tissue

SystemEndocrine / metabolic
FunctionNon-shivering thermogenesis
Key proteinUncoupling protein 1 (UCP1)
LocationsNeck, supraclavicular, paravertebral regions

Brown adipose tissue (BAT) or brown fat makes up the adipose organ together with white adipose tissue. It is found in almost all mammals and is especially important in small mammals and newborns, whose ability to generate heat by shivering is limited.

Mechanism

Brown fat cells are packed with mitochondria, giving the tissue its brown colour. Their inner mitochondrial membrane contains uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1, thermogenin), which short-circuits the proton gradient of oxidative phosphorylation so that the energy of respiration is released as heat rather than stored as ATP. This non-shivering thermogenesis is activated by cold exposure through sympathetic nervous stimulation and norepinephrine.

In humans

Adult humans retain metabolically active deposits of brown fat around the neck, collarbones, and spine, a fact firmly established by PET-CT imaging studies in 2009. Because activating brown fat increases energy expenditure, it is studied as a potential therapeutic target for obesity and type 2 diabetes. "Beige" adipocytes that appear within white fat can acquire a similar heat-producing phenotype.
