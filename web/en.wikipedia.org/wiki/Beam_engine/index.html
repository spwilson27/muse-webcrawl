Beam engine

Beam engine
Type: Reciprocating steam engine
Key feature: Pivoted overhead beam
Notable makers: Boulton and Watt

A beam engine is a type of steam engine in which a pivoted overhead beam transfers the motion of a vertical piston to a connecting rod, typically driving a pump or rotating machinery. Beam engines were widely used in the 18th and 19th centuries.

History

The atmospheric beam engine of Thomas Newcomen (1712) drained mines. James Watt's improvements, including the separate condenser and parallel motion, greatly increased efficiency and allowed rotative drive for factories and mills.

Operation

Steam acts on a piston in a vertical cylinder; the piston rod attaches to one end of the great beam, while the other end drives a pump rod or, via a flywheel and crank, rotary motion.

Preservation

Many beam engines survive in museums and pumping stations, such as Crofton Pumping Station and the engines of the London Museum of Water & Steam.
