André Léon Blum (9 April 1872 – 30 March 1950) was a French socialist politician and leader of the French Section of the Workers' International (SFIO). He headed the Popular Front government in 1936–1937 and served three times as Prime Minister of France.

Early life

Blum was born in Paris to a prosperous Jewish family from Alsace. Trained as a lawyer, he worked at the Conseil d'État while also making a name as a literary and theatre critic. The Dreyfus Affair drew him into politics and into the socialist movement led by Jean Jaurès.

Popular Front

After the far-right riots of February 1934, Blum helped unite socialists, radicals and communists in the Popular Front, which won the elections of May 1936. His government introduced the Matignon Agreements, the 40-hour working week, paid holidays, collective bargaining rights and the nationalization of the armaments industry and the railways (creating the SNCF). Faced with economic pressure and the Spanish Civil War, Blum resigned in June 1937.

War and postwar years

Arrested by the Vichy regime in 1940, Blum was put on trial at Riom in 1942, where his defense embarrassed the prosecution; he was then deported to Buchenwald and Dachau, surviving until liberation in 1945. He briefly headed a provisional government in 1946–1947 and helped negotiate American loans to France. He died in 1950.
