720p

720p (720 lines progressive) is a family of high-definition television (HDTV) resolutions with 720 horizontal scan lines and a progressive (non-interlaced) picture. The most common form is 1280×720 pixels with a 16:9 aspect ratio, giving a frame of about 0.92 megapixels.

Broadcasting

720p was adopted as a broadcast standard by a number of networks, including ABC and Fox in the United States, and was one of the original ATSC HDTV formats. Its progressive scanning was considered advantageous for fast-moving sports content, while the competing 1080i format offered higher spatial resolution at the cost of interlacing.

Streaming and devices

As internet streaming grew, 720p became a common "HD" tier on video platforms, balancing quality against bandwidth. Displays marketed as "HD ready" typically have a native resolution of 1280×720 or 1366×768. 720p has since been largely superseded in premium contexts by 1080p ("Full HD") and 4K resolutions, but remains widespread in streaming, webcams, and mobile video.
