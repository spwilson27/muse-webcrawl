Generation of 1927

The Generation of 1927 (Spanish: Generación del 27) was a group of Spanish poets who emerged in the 1920s and became one of the most influential movements in Spanish literature. The group's name derives from the tercentenary of the death of the Baroque poet Luis de Góngora, commemorated in Seville in December 1927, an event in which many of the poets participated.

Members

The ten poets conventionally listed as the core of the generation are Jorge Guillén, Pedro Salinas, Rafael Alberti, Federico García Lorca, Dámaso Alonso, Gerardo Diego, Luis Cernuda, Vicente Aleixandre, Manuel Altolaguirre and Emilio Prados. Women writers associated with the group, long overlooked, include Ernestina de Champourcín, Concha Méndez and Josefina de la Torre, sometimes grouped as Las Sinsombrero.

Style and influences

The poets combined avant-garde movements — surrealism, futurism, ultraism — with classical Spanish forms, folk song traditions and the Gongorine Baroque. They were influenced by Juan Ramón Jiménez and his ideal of "pure poetry", and several were closely connected with the Residencia de Estudiantes in Madrid, where they mixed with figures such as Salvador Dalí and Luis Buñuel.

Legacy

The Spanish Civil War shattered the group: Lorca was murdered in 1936, and most of the others went into exile. Vicente Aleixandre, who remained in Spain, received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1977. The Generation of 1927 is generally regarded as a second Golden Age (Edad de Plata) of Spanish poetry.
