Wooden roller coaster

A wooden roller coaster is a type of roller coaster classified by its wooden track structure, though the track itself is typically capped with a running strip of steel. Wooden coasters are known for a rougher, more rickety riding sensation and a characteristic rattling sound, qualities many enthusiasts prize.

Characteristics

The track is built of laminated wooden boards supported by a lattice structure of timber (or, in some modern designs, steel supports). Traditional wooden coasters cannot invert riders, because their trains use simple road wheels held to the track by side and underfriction wheels; however, modern hybrid designs by manufacturers such as Rocky Mountain Construction use steel track on wooden supports to perform inversions.

History

The first roller coasters in the 19th century used wooden structures. The classic era of the 1920s produced celebrated designs by builders like John C. Miller, including the Coney Island Cyclone (1927). Famous surviving wooden coasters include The Beast at Kings Island, once the world's longest, and El Toro at Six Flags Great Adventure.

Records

The tallest and fastest traditional wooden coaster is generally cited as El Toro (181 ft, 70 mph). Modern prefabricated wooden coasters, built with laser-cut factory-made track, deliver much smoother rides than classic designs.
