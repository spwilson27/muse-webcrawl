Pollux b

Pollux b, formally named Thestias, is an exoplanet orbiting the star Pollux (Beta Geminorum), approximately 34 light-years away in the constellation of Gemini. It is a gas giant with a minimum mass about 2.3 times that of Jupiter. The planet orbits its host star at a distance of about 1.64 astronomical units with an orbital period of roughly 590 days.

Discovery

The planet's existence was long suspected from periodic variations in Pollux's radial velocity, first reported in 1993, though the variations were initially attributed to stellar activity or rotation. In 2006, a team led by Artie P. Hatzes confirmed the planet using radial velocity measurements, distinguishing the signal from activity indicators on the star.

Name

In July 2014 the International Astronomical Union launched NameExoWorlds, a process for giving proper names to certain exoplanets. In December 2015, the IAU announced the winning name for this planet was Thestias, after the mother of Leda in Greek mythology — Pollux being one of the Dioscuri twins.

Host star

Pollux is an orange giant star of spectral type K0 III, the brightest star in Gemini. It is larger and more massive than the Sun, and the planet's orbit lies well outside the star's present-day habitable zone calculations at discovery, though the star's evolution complicates the question of past habitability.
