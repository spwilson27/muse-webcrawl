Dissolution of the Netherlands Antilles

Date10 October 2010
LocationCaribbean Netherlands

ResultCuraçao and Sint Maarten became constituent countries; Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba became special municipalities of the Netherlands

The dissolution of the Netherlands Antilles took effect on 10 October 2010, ending the existence of the Netherlands Antilles as a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. Under the restructuring, the island of Curaçao and the southern part of Sint Maarten became separate constituent countries within the Kingdom, joining Aruba (which had seceded in 1986) and the Netherlands itself. The islands of Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba — collectively known as the BES islands or Caribbean Netherlands — became special municipalities directly administered by the Netherlands.

Background

The Netherlands Antilles had been formed in 1954 from the former Dutch colony of Curaçao and Dependencies. Over the following decades, relations between the islands were frequently strained: Aruba left the federation in 1986 to become a separate country within the Kingdom, an event known as its status aparte. In the 2000s, referendums were held on each island about their constitutional future. Curaçao and Sint Maarten voted for a status comparable to Aruba's, while Bonaire and Saba voted for closer integration with the Netherlands, and Sint Eustatius voted to remain within the Netherlands Antilles — though it too was ultimately integrated directly.

Process

Negotiations between the Netherlands, the Netherlands Antilles and the individual islands produced a series of agreements beginning in 2005. Round Table Conferences were held, and the final declarations were signed in 2006 and 2007. Implementing legislation amended the Charter for the Kingdom of the Netherlands, and on 10 October 2010 the changes came into force. The Antillean guilder remained in use in Curaçao and Sint Maarten, while the BES islands adopted the United States dollar on 1 January 2011.

Aftermath

Curaçao and Sint Maarten established their own governments, parliaments and central bank arrangements, while the Netherlands assumed direct responsibility for public administration, policing and social security on the BES islands. The dissolution is an example of the peaceful constitutional restructuring of a state; see also the Netherlands Antilles and Kingdom of the Netherlands.
