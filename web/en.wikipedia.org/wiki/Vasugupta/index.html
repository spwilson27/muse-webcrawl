Vasugupta

Vasugupta (c. 860–925 CE) was a Kashmiri sage credited with authorship of the Shiva Sutras, the foundational aphorisms of the school of Hindu philosophy now known as Kashmir Shaivism (Trika). Little is known of his life; he is traditionally said to have lived near the Mahadeva mountain in Kashmir.

The Shiva Sutras

According to tradition, the god Shiva appeared to Vasugupta in a dream and directed him to a great rock on the mountain, on which the sutras were inscribed; the rock, called Shankaropala, was a site of pilgrimage. The Shiva Sutras comprise 77 aphorisms in three sections, setting out a non-dualistic vision in which universal consciousness (Shiva) manifests as the individual self and the world, and liberation consists in recognizing one's identity with that consciousness.

Legacy

Vasugupta is regarded by tradition as the founder of the Spanda school of Kashmir Shaivism. His pupil Kallata is associated with the Spanda Karikas (Verses on the Pulsation), which elaborate the doctrine that reality vibrates or pulsates as divine energy. Together the Shiva Sutras and the Spanda literature form the revealed (agama) basis of the Trika system later systematized by Somananda, Utpaladeva, and Abhinavagupta.
