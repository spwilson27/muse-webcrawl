Hypoparathyroidism

Hypoparathyroidism is a disorder of the parathyroid glands in which they produce insufficient parathyroid hormone (PTH). This leads to low blood calcium (hypocalcemia) and elevated blood phosphate, producing symptoms such as tingling in the lips, fingers and toes, painful muscle spasms and tetany, fatigue, and in severe cases seizures and laryngospasm.

Causes

The most common cause is inadvertent damage to, or removal of, the parathyroid glands during thyroid or other neck surgery; this may be transient or permanent. Other causes include autoimmune destruction of the glands (as in autoimmune polyendocrine syndrome type 1), genetic disorders such as DiGeorge syndrome, infiltration, and severe magnesium deficiency, which impairs both PTH secretion and action. Hypoparathyroidism must be distinguished from pseudohypoparathyroidism, in which PTH levels are high but the tissues are resistant to its effects.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on the combination of low serum calcium, low or inappropriately normal PTH, and high phosphate. Chronic management aims to relieve symptoms while keeping calcium in the low-normal range, using oral calcium supplements and active vitamin D analogues such as calcitriol or alfacalcidol; overtreatment risks hypercalciuria, kidney stones, and nephrocalcinosis, so calcium and renal function are monitored. Recombinant human PTH therapy is available for patients inadequately controlled on conventional treatment. Acute severe hypocalcemia is treated with intravenous calcium.
