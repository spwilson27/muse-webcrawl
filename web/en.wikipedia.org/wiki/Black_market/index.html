Black market

[Image: a dim alley exchange of unmarked goods, evoking clandestine trade]

A black market is economic activity that takes place outside government-sanctioned channels — trade in illegal goods (drugs, weapons, counterfeit products) or in legal goods conducted to evade taxes, Price controls, rationing or regulation. Black markets arise wherever prohibitions or controls create a gap between legal and willing-to-pay prices.

Features and examples

Classic cases include alcohol during US Prohibition (1920–33), rationed goods in wartime economies, foreign-currency black markets under exchange controls (the premium on dollars in many developing economies), and today's illicit drug trade, estimated in the hundreds of billions of dollars. Because contracts cannot be enforced legally, black markets substitute reputation, networks and sometimes violence, and prices include a risk premium. Economists study them as evidence about demand and the limits of prohibition: criminalisation can shrink supply but raise prices and criminal rents, the analysis behind legalisation debates. Black markets overlap with, but differ from, the wider informal economy, which includes legal activity merely unrecorded.
