South Dakota

South Dakota is a state in the North Central region of the United States. It is named after the Dakota Sioux Native American tribe, which comprises a large portion of the population and historically dominated the territory. South Dakota is the 17th largest by area, but the 5th least populous, and the 5th least densely populated of the 50 United States. Pierre is the state capital, and Sioux Falls, with a population of about 192,500, is South Dakota's most populous city.

Geography

The Missouri River bisects the state into two halves, "East River" and "West River". Eastern South Dakota is rolling prairie and farmland; the west features the rugged Badlands and the Black Hills, a mountain range home to Mount Rushmore, whose granite faces bear the sculpted heads of four US presidents, and to the Crazy Horse Memorial.

History

The region was inhabited by the Lakota and Dakota Sioux, and was acquired by the US in the Louisiana Purchase (1803). Dakota Territory was created in 1861; gold in the Black Hills in the 1870s brought conflict with the Sioux, including the events leading to the Wounded Knee Massacre of 1890. South Dakota became a state on November 2, 1889, simultaneously with North Dakota.

Economy

Agriculture (corn, soybeans, livestock), financial services (notably credit-card banking centred in Sioux Falls), tourism, and government services dominate the economy. The state has no personal or corporate income tax.
