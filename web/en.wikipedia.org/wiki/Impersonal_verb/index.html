Impersonal verb

An impersonal verb is a verb that lacks a determinate subject and is used with a dummy (expletive) subject, or with no subject at all. In English, weather verbs are typical examples: in "It rains", the pronoun it does not refer to anything but serves as a grammatical placeholder subject.

Across languages

Many languages mark impersonal verbs with fixed third-person singular forms. Latin used forms such as pluit ("it rains") and oportet ("it is necessary"), and impersonal passive constructions. In some languages, such as Finnish, weather expressions omit a subject entirely. Spanish uses constructions like llueve ("it rains") with no overt subject, and impersonal se constructions.

Types

Impersonal constructions commonly express weather, time, natural phenomena, necessity, and general statements about people in general. Some verbs are inherently impersonal in a given language, while others can be used impersonally in particular constructions, such as impersonal passives ("It is said that…").
