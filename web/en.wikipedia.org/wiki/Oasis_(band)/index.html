Oasis (band)

Oasis are an English rock band formed in Manchester in 1991. The band's core is the brothers Liam Gallagher (lead vocals) and Noel Gallagher (lead guitar, vocals, chief songwriter). The biggest act of the Britpop movement, Oasis have sold more than 75 million records worldwide, with signature songs including "Wonderwall", "Don't Look Back in Anger", "Live Forever", and "Champagne Supernova".

Rise and Britpop peak

Signed to Creation Records after label boss Alan McGee saw them in Glasgow in 1993, Oasis debuted with Definitely Maybe (1994), then the fastest-selling debut album in UK chart history. Their second album, (What's the Story) Morning Glory? (1995), sold over 22 million copies worldwide and made them global stars amid a chart rivalry with Blur dubbed the "Battle of Britpop". In August 1996 they played two nights at Knebworth to 250,000 people, with some 2.5 million ticket applications — a defining moment of "Cool Britannia". The cocaine-fuelled third album Be Here Now (1997) sold fast on release but was later critically reassessed as bloated.

Later years and split

The 2000s brought line-up changes (long-term bassist Gem Archer and guitarist Andy Bell succeeded the departing founders Bonehead and Guigsy), the albums Heathen Chemistry (2002), Don't Believe the Truth (2005), and Dig Out Your Soul (2008), and continuing stadium success, all shadowed by the Gallaghers' feuding. Noel quit in August 2009 after a backstage fight in Paris, ending the band; Liam's group continued as Beady Eye while Noel formed Noel Gallagher's High Flying Birds.

Reunion

In August 2024 the brothers announced Oasis's return for the Oasis Live '25 stadium tour, which began in Cardiff in July 2025 and drew enormous demand — one of the most anticipated reunions in rock history.
