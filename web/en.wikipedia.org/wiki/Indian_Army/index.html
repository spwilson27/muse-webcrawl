Indian Army

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indian Army is the world's largest standing volunteer army.

The Indian Army is the land-based branch and largest component of the Indian Armed Forces. The President of India is its Supreme Commander, and it is headed by the Chief of the Army Staff. Tracing its lineage to the armies of the East India Company and the British Indian Army, the modern force dates from independence in 1947. It is the second-largest standing army in the world and the largest volunteer force, with over 1.2 million active personnel.

History

Indian soldiers served in both world wars — some 1.5 million in the First and 2.5 million in the Second. After Partition, the army fought the wars of 1947–48, 1965, and 1971 against Pakistan, the Sino-Indian War of 1962, and the Kargil conflict of 1999, alongside decades of counter-insurgency and UN peacekeeping operations in which India is among the largest troop contributors.

Organisation

The army is organised into seven commands — Northern, Western, South Western, Eastern, Central, Southern, and Army Training Command (ARTRAC) — and into regiments (infantry, armoured corps, artillery, engineers, signals, and services) largely recruited on regional lines, a legacy of British practice. The Territorial Army forms a part-time reserve. Since 2022 most new soldiers are recruited under the Agnipath scheme as "Agniveers" on initial four-year terms.

Modernisation

Recent procurement centres on the Arjun and T-90 tanks, K9 Vajra self-propelled artillery, Pinaka rocket systems, and upgraded small arms, with increasing emphasis on indigenous production under the Atmanirbhar Bharat policy.
