Counterexample

Counterexample

[Image: diagram showing a single object satisfying a claim's premises but not its conclusion]
FieldLogic, mathematics, philosophy
PurposeDisproof of a universal claim

In logic and mathematics, a counterexample is an instance that disproves a general statement. To refute a claim of the form "for all x, if P(x) then Q(x)", it suffices to exhibit a single object that satisfies P but not Q. A single valid counterexample is enough to show that a universal claim is false, no matter how many confirming examples it may have.

In mathematics

Counterexamples play a central role in mathematical practice. Conjecture-and-refutation drives much research: proposed theorems are tested against potential counterexamples, which either kill the conjecture or reveal the need for additional hypotheses. Famous examples include the disproof of Euler's sum of powers conjecture (found in 1966 with fifth powers), the Weierstrass function as a counterexample to the intuition that continuous functions are mostly differentiable, and non-Euclidean geometries as counterexamples to the claim that Euclid's parallel postulate follows from the other axioms. Entire reference works, such as Counterexamples in Analysis and Counterexamples in Topology, catalogue instructive cases.

In philosophy

Philosophers use counterexamples to test definitions and theories. Edmund Gettier's 1963 three-page paper presented counterexamples to the definition of knowledge as justified true belief, reshaping epistemology. Thought experiments such as Nozick's "experience machine" serve as counterexamples to hedonism about welfare, and trolley problems test moral principles against hard cases.

Counterexamples and proof

A counterexample differs from a proof by contradiction: rather than deriving an absurdity from the claim, it directly exhibits a case the claim fails to cover. In formal logic, a countermodel plays the analogous role for arguments, showing an interpretation under which the premises are true and the conclusion false, thereby demonstrating invalidity.
