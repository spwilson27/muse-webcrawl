The Russian battleship Potemkin (Russian: Князь Потёмкин-Таврический, Kniaz Potemkin-Tavricheski, "Prince Potemkin of Taurida") was a pre-dreadnought battleship of the Imperial Russian Navy's Black Sea Fleet. She became famous for the mutiny of her crew in June 1905, during the Russian Revolution of 1905, an event later immortalised in Sergei Eisenstein's 1925 film Battleship Potemkin.

The mutiny

On 27 June [O.S. 14 June] 1905, sailors protesting over maggot-infested meat refused orders; when the captain threatened punishment, the crew, led by Afanasy Matiushenko, rose up, killed several officers including the captain, and raised the red flag. The ship sailed to Odessa, where a general strike was under way; the funeral of the slain sailor Grigory Vakulinchuk became a mass demonstration, and troops fired on crowds on the city's great staircase — the scene dramatised in Eisenstein's "Odessa Steps" sequence. After sailing to Constanța and Feodosia, the mutineers surrendered the ship to Romanian authorities, who returned it to Russia.

Later service

Renamed Panteleimon, the ship resumed service and took part in the First World War, fighting the Ottoman fleet at the Battle of Cape Sarych (1914). After the February Revolution she was renamed Borets za Svobodu ("Fighter for Freedom"). Captured in turn by German, Allied and White forces at Sevastopol, she was wrecked by retreating Allied troops in 1919 and scrapped by the Soviets in 1923.
