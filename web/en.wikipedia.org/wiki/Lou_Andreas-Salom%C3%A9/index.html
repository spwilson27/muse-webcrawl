Lou Andreas-Salomé

Born12 February 1861, St Petersburg
Died5 February 1937, Göttingen
OccupationWriter, psychoanalyst

Lou Andreas-Salomé (born Louise von Salomé; 1861–1937) was a Russian-born German writer, intellectual and psychoanalyst, celebrated for her friendships with Friedrich Nietzsche, Rainer Maria Rilke and Sigmund Freud, and for her own substantial body of fiction, essays and psychoanalytic writing.

Life

Born into a cosmopolitan family in St Petersburg, she studied theology and philosophy in Zurich, one of the few universities then admitting women. In Rome in 1882 she met Paul Rée and Nietzsche, who proposed marriage and was refused; the three planned a communal "trinity" of study that dissolved within the year. She married the orientalist Friedrich Carl Andreas in 1887, on terms that were unconventional for the time. Her intense relationship with the young Rilke, whom she mentored and renamed "Rainer", lasted from 1897 into the early 1900s.

Work

Andreas-Salomé wrote novels and novellas, a pioneering study of Nietzsche, essays on Ibsen's heroines, and, after joining Freud's circle in Vienna in 1911, psychoanalytic papers on narcissism, anal eroticism and female sexuality. Her memoirs, published posthumously as Lebensrückblick, remain a key document of European intellectual life around 1900.
