Kalinago people

The Kalinago, historically known to Europeans as the Island Caribs, are an Indigenous people of the Lesser Antilles in the Caribbean. They originated from mainland South America and settled the Windward Islands before the arrival of Europeans. Today the largest Kalinago community lives in the Kalinago Territory on the island of Dominica, with related Garifuna descendants in Central America and St. Vincent.

History

Kalinago ancestors migrated from the Orinoco basin of South America through the island chain, reaching the Lesser Antilles centuries before European contact. They called Dominica Wai'tu kubuli ("Tall is her body"). Fierce resistance made the Kalinago among the last Caribbean peoples to be subdued by Europeans: Dominica's rugged terrain sheltered them from French and British colonization longer than most islands.

In 1903 the British colonial administration formally set aside about 3,700 acres (15 km²) on Dominica's east coast as a reserve — today the Kalinago Territory — governed by an elected Kalinago Chief and Council.

Culture

Traditional Kalinago life centred on fishing, farming cassava and other crops, and skilled crafts including the waterproof gommier canoes (gli gli) used for inter-island voyaging, basketry woven from larouma reed, and calabash carving. The Kalinago language, an Arawakan language, fell out of everyday use in the 20th century but is the subject of revival efforts. The Kalinago Barana Autê (cultural village) at Crayfish River presents traditional practices to visitors.

Terminology

The name "Carib" derives from Spanish renderings of the people's name and gave rise to the word "Caribbean" and the Spanish caníbales, source of "cannibal". Community members increasingly prefer the endonym Kalinago, and Dominica officially renamed the Carib Territory and the Carib Council to Kalinago Territory and Kalinago Council in 2015.
