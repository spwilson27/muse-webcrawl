Salesforce, Inc. is an American cloud-based software company headquartered in San Francisco. It pioneered delivering customer relationship management (CRM) software as a subscription service over the internet, and is the world's largest provider of CRM applications.

History

Salesforce was founded in 1999 by former Oracle executive Marc Benioff with the slogan "the end of software", renting a San Francisco apartment as its first office. Its multi-tenant, browser-based CRM undercut installed rivals and defined the SaaS model; the company went public in 2004.

Growth came through acquisitions: Heroku (2010), ExactTarget (2013), MuleSoft (2018, ~$6.5 billion), Tableau (2019, ~$15.7 billion) and Slack (2021, ~$27.7 billion), its largest deal. The annual Dreamforce conference in San Francisco became one of the largest in tech. In 2023 activist investors pressed for cost discipline, prompting layoffs and a focus on margins; the company has since pivoted its pitch toward AI agents under the Agentforce brand.

Products and culture

The Customer 360 platform spans sales, service, marketing, commerce and analytics clouds. Salesforce is known for Benioff's "1-1-1" philanthropy model — donating 1% of equity, product and employee time — and for its mascot-filled "Trailhead" learning community.
