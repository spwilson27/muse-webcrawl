Fellowship of the Ring (characters)

The Fellowship of the Ring, also called the Company of the Ring or simply the Nine Walkers, is the group of nine companions formed at Rivendell in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings to carry the One Ring to Mordor and destroy it in Mount Doom. Chosen at the Council of Elrond, the nine were set deliberately against the nine Nazgûl, the Ringwraiths of Sauron.

Members

The Fellowship comprises the Ring-bearer Frodo Baggins and his hobbit companions Samwise Gamgee, Meriadoc Brandybuck and Peregrin Took; the wizard Gandalf the Grey, who leads the company until his fall in Moria; the Men Aragorn, heir of Isildur, and Boromir of Gondor; the Elf Legolas, son of Thranduil; and the Dwarf Gimli, son of Glóin.

Journey and breaking

The company sets out from Rivendell on 25 December, attempts the Redhorn Pass in snow, passes through the Mines of Moria — where Gandalf falls fighting a Balrog — and rests in Lothlórien before travelling down the Anduin. At Amon Hen the Fellowship breaks: Boromir, having tried to seize the Ring, dies defending Merry and Pippin from Orcs; Frodo and Sam cross the river alone toward Mordor; and Aragorn, Legolas and Gimli pursue the Orcs who have captured the younger hobbits. The scattered members nevertheless each play decisive parts in the War of the Ring, and the surviving company is reunited after Sauron's fall.
