Marrano

Marrano is a term for Spanish and Portuguese Jews who converted to Christianity, often under compulsion, in the late Middle Ages, and for their descendants — particularly those suspected of secretly practising Judaism (crypto-Jews). The word, originally a pejorative meaning "swine", is today widely avoided by scholars in favour of terms such as converso or crypto-Jew.

History

Following anti-Jewish riots in 1391 and further pressure culminating in the expulsion of Jews from Spain in 1492 and forced conversions in Portugal in 1497, tens of thousands of Sephardic Jews became nominal Christians. Many continued Jewish observance in secret and were targeted by the Spanish and Portuguese Inquisitions.

Legacy

Converso families spread across Europe and the Americas, founding open Jewish communities in places such as Amsterdam, Livorno and the Caribbean. Descendant communities with traces of crypto-Jewish tradition survive in Spain, Portugal (notably Belmonte) and Latin America.
