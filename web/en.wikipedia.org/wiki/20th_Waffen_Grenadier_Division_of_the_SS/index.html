The 20th Waffen Grenadier Division of the SS (1st Estonian) was a foreign infantry division of the German Waffen-SS during the Second World War, composed primarily of Estonian conscripts and volunteers. It was formed in 1944 from the Estonian SS Volunteer Brigade and earlier Estonian Legion units.

History

Estonia had been occupied by the Soviet Union in 1940 and by Germany from 1941. German authorities raised Estonian units for front-line and security service, and in early 1944 general mobilisation brought thousands of Estonians into German service, many motivated by the aim of resisting a renewed Soviet occupation.

Operations

The division fought on the Narva front in 1944, including the defence of the Tannenberg Line in the Sinimäed Hills during the summer. After the German evacuation of Estonia in September 1944, the division was withdrawn to Germany, rebuilt at Neuhammer, and deployed in Silesia in early 1945. It surrendered to Soviet and Czechoslovak forces in May 1945; some soldiers who reached Western lines were treated by the Western Allies as distinct from the SS proper for guard duties, a legacy that remains debated in Estonian memory politics.
