Capricornus is one of the constellations of the zodiac, located in the southern sky in an area often called the Sea, rich in watery constellations. It is commonly represented as a sea-goat: a creature with the body and head of a goat and the tail of a fish. One of the 48 constellations listed by Ptolemy, it remains among the 88 modern constellations, though it is relatively faint, containing no star brighter than third magnitude.

Mythology

The sea-goat figure is ancient: Babylonian astronomy knew the constellation as the goat-fish, associated with the god Ea. In Greek myth, Capricornus was connected with the goat Amalthea, who suckled the infant Zeus, or with the god Pan, who transformed himself partly into a fish to escape the monster Typhon.

Notable features

The brightest star, Deneb Algedi (Delta Capricorni), is a multiple system about 39 light-years away. Alpha Capricorni (Algedi) is a prominent optical double visible to the naked eye. The globular cluster Messier 30 lies within the constellation. The Tropic of Capricorn takes its name from the constellation, beside which the Sun stood at the December solstice in ancient times; because of precession, the solstitial Sun now lies in Sagittarius.

Astrology

In Western astrology the sign Capricorn, symbolised by the goat, spans roughly 22 December to 19 January in the tropical zodiac and is traditionally ruled by Saturn.
