Refinement (topology)

In topology, an open cover of a space X is a collection of open sets whose union is X. Given two covers C and D of X, the cover C is a refinement of D if every member of C is contained in some member of D. In that case D is said to be coarser than C, and C finer than D.

Uses

Refinements are fundamental in the definitions of compactness and its variants. A space is compact if every open cover has a finite subcover; paracompactness is defined by requiring that every open cover has a locally finite open refinement. A star refinement is a refinement in which the star of any member is contained within some member of the original cover, a notion used in the theory of uniform spaces and in the definition of fully normal spaces.

Point-finite and locally finite refinements

A refinement is point-finite if each point of the space belongs to only finitely many of its members, and locally finite if every point has a neighbourhood meeting only finitely many members. These notions underlie the metrization theorems of Nagata, Smirnov and Bing.
