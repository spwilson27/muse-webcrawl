Irving Berlin

BornIsrael Beilin, 11 May 1888, Tyumen, Russian Empire
Died22 September 1989 (aged 101), New York City, US
OccupationComposer, lyricist

Irving Berlin (born Israel Beilin; 11 May 1888 – 22 September 1989) was an American composer and lyricist, widely considered one of the greatest songwriters in American history. His music forms a large part of the Great American Songbook.

Life and career

Born into a Jewish family in Imperial Russia, Berlin emigrated with his family to New York in 1893 and grew up in poverty on the Lower East Side. He taught himself music, never learned to read notation fluently, and composed on a specially built transposing piano. His first international hit, "Alexander's Ragtime Band" (1911), helped ignite the ragtime craze. During the First World War he wrote the soldier revue Yip Yip Yaphank, featuring "Oh! How I Hate to Get Up in the Morning".

Over a 60-year career Berlin wrote an estimated 1,500 songs, including the standards "White Christmas", "God Bless America", "There's No Business Like Show Business", "Cheek to Cheek", "Blue Skies" and "Puttin' On the Ritz". He wrote scores for 19 Broadway shows — among them Annie Get Your Gun (1946) and Call Me Madam — and 18 Hollywood films, including Top Hat, Holiday Inn and Easter Parade. He founded his own music publishing company and was a co-founder of ASCAP and of the Music Box Theatre.

Legacy

Jerome Kern remarked that Berlin "has no place in American music — he is American music." Berlin received an Academy Award, a Tony Award, a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award and the Presidential Medal of Freedom, and died in 1989 at the age of 101.
