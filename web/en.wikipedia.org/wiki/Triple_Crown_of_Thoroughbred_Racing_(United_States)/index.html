The Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing, often shortened to the Triple Crown, is a series of horse races for three-year-old Thoroughbreds, consisting of the Kentucky Derby, Preakness Stakes and Belmont Stakes. Winning all three races is considered the greatest accomplishment in Thoroughbred racing.

The races

The Kentucky Derby is run over 1¼ miles at Churchill Downs in Louisville on the first Saturday in May; the Preakness over 1³⁄₁₆ miles at Pimlico in Baltimore two weeks later; and the Belmont Stakes over 1½ miles at Belmont Park in New York, three weeks after the Preakness. Fillies may compete, and three have won individual races, though none has won the Crown itself.

Winners

Thirteen horses have won the Triple Crown: Sir Barton (1919), Gallant Fox (1930), Omaha (1935), War Admiral (1937), Whirlaway (1941), Count Fleet (1943), Assault (1946), Citation (1948), Secretariat (1973), Seattle Slew (1977), Affirmed (1978), American Pharoah (2015) and Justify (2018). The 37-year gap between Affirmed and American Pharoah was the longest drought in the series' history. Secretariat still holds the stakes records in all three races.
