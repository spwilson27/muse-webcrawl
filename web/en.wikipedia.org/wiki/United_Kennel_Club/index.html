United Kennel Club

The United Kennel Club (UKC) is an American dog registry and event organization headquartered in Kalamazoo, Michigan. Founded in 1898 by Chauncey Z. Bennett, it is one of the oldest and largest all-breed dog registries in the United States. It registers purebred dogs and mixed-breed dogs for performance programs and sanctions events in conformation and working and companion sports.

History

Bennett created the UKC to emphasize the “total dog”—an animal that combines sound structure with working ability and temperament. Early registrations focused on American Pit Bull Terriers, but the club gradually expanded to many breeds and to hunting dogs, especially coonhounds. Unlike registries centered mainly on conformation shows, UKC developed extensive performance competitions.

Programs

UKC events include conformation, obedience, rally obedience, agility, dock jumping, lure coursing, weight pull, nose work, hunting tests, retriever events, beagle and coonhound field events. It maintains breed standards and recognizes hundreds of breeds in groups such as sporting, hound, herding, guardian, terrier, Northern breeds and companion dogs. Its mixed-breed Performance Listing Program allows altered mixed-breed dogs to compete in many sports.

Role

Alongside the American Kennel Club and other registries, the UKC forms part of the infrastructure of organized dog breeding and competition in North America. Pedigree records, DNA programs and event titles support its aim of recording ancestry while rewarding working performance as well as appearance.
