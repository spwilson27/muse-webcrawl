The discrete-time Fourier transform (DTFT) is a frequency-domain representation of a discrete-time sequence. Unlike the discrete Fourier transform (DFT), which produces a finite set of frequency samples, the DTFT produces a continuous function of frequency, which is periodic with period 2π. It is the discrete-time counterpart of the continuous-time Fourier transform and a special case of the Z-transform evaluated on the unit circle.

Definition

For a sequence x[n], the DTFT is

X(ejω) = Σn=−∞∞ x[n] · e−jωn

with inverse

x[n] = (1/2π) ∫−ππ X(ejω) · ejωn dω

Relation to sampling

When a continuous signal is sampled, its spectrum becomes a periodic summation of copies of the original spectrum; the DTFT of the samples is exactly this periodic spectrum, scaled. This explains aliasing: if the sampling rate is too low, the copies overlap (the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem). The DFT of a finite segment can be viewed as samples of the DTFT at N equally spaced frequencies, which is the basis of practical spectrum estimation.

Properties

The DTFT is linear and satisfies convolution, modulation and Parseval relations analogous to those of the continuous transform. Convergence of the defining sum requires the sequence to be absolutely summable for a uniformly convergent, continuous transform; finite-energy but non-absolutely-summable sequences (such as the unit step) have DTFTs understood in a distributional sense, containing impulses.
