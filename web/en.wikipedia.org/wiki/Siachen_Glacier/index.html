The Siachen Glacier is a glacier located in the eastern Karakoram range in the Himalayas, at the northeastern point of the Line of Control between India and Pakistan. At 76 km (47 mi) long, it is the longest glacier in the Karakoram and the second-longest in the world's non-polar areas. It lies in a region claimed by both India and Pakistan and administered in part by India as part of Ladakh.

The glacier has been the site of the world's highest battlefield since 1984, when India launched Operation Meghdoot and seized control of most of the glacier and the Saltoro Ridge passes. Pakistan maintains positions on the lower slopes to the west. A ceasefire has been in effect since 2003, but both countries maintain a costly military presence, and more soldiers have died from the extreme weather and terrain — with temperatures dropping below −50 °C (−58 °F) — than from combat.

Geography

The glacier originates near the Indira Col on the China border and descends southeastward. Its meltwater feeds the Nubra River, a tributary of the Shyok and ultimately the Indus. The Saltoro Ridge runs along the glacier's western side, with passes such as Sia La and Bilafond La held at altitudes above 5,500 m (18,000 ft).

Conflict

The 1949 Karachi Agreement and the 1972 Simla Agreement did not demarcate the boundary beyond grid point NJ9842, leaving the glacier in a cartographic ambiguity both countries exploited. India's pre-emptive occupation in April 1984 began the Siachen conflict. A major avalanche in 2012 buried a Pakistani battalion headquarters at Gayari, killing 140 people.

Environmental concerns

The military presence has raised concerns about pollution and accelerated glacial retreat; studies have reported the glacier to be thinning and receding, though the scale and causes remain debated.
