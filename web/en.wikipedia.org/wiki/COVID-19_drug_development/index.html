COVID-19 drug development
DiseaseCOVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2)
Key approved drugsRemdesivir, nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid), molnupiravir, dexamethasone
ApproachesAntivirals, immunomodulators, monoclonal antibodies, drug repurposing

COVID-19 drug development is the research effort to develop safe and effective therapeutic drugs for treating COVID-19, the disease caused by the SARS-CoV-2 coronavirus. Beginning in early 2020, the effort combined de novo antiviral development with large-scale repurposing of existing drugs, tested in platform trials such as the WHO Solidarity trial and the UK RECOVERY trial.

Researchers screened existing compounds and developed new antivirals against SARS-CoV-2.

Major therapeutic classes

Antivirals. Remdesivir, a nucleotide analogue developed by Gilead Sciences, became the first antiviral approved for COVID-19 in late 2020. Oral antivirals followed: molnupiravir (Merck/Ridgeback) and nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Pfizer's Paxlovid), which targets the viral main protease (3CLpro) and showed large reductions in hospitalization when given early to high-risk outpatients.

Immunomodulators. The RECOVERY trial showed in June 2020 that the corticosteroid dexamethasone reduced mortality in patients requiring oxygen or ventilation — the first proven life-saving treatment. IL-6 receptor blockers (tocilizumab) and JAK inhibitors (baricitinib) later showed benefit in severe disease.

Monoclonal antibodies. Products such as bamlanivimab, casirivimab/imdevimab, and sotrovimab were used for early treatment and prophylaxis, but successive variants — especially Omicron — progressively escaped them, and their authorizations were withdrawn.

Repurposing failures

High-profile repurposing candidates including hydroxychloroquine and ivermectin failed to show benefit in large randomized trials, illustrating the importance of platform trials in rapidly resolving which candidate therapies actually work.
