Vending machine

A vending machine is an automated machine that dispenses items such as snacks, beverages, cigarettes and lottery tickets to consumers after cash, a credit card, or another form of payment is inserted into the machine or otherwise made. The first modern vending machines were developed in England in the early 1880s and dispensed postcards and envelopes; the first commercial coin-operated machines in the United States, installed in New York City in 1888 by the Thomas Adams Gum Company, sold chewing gum on train platforms.

History

The earliest known reference to a vending mechanism is the coin-operated holy-water dispenser described by Hero of Alexandria in the 1st century CE. Coin-operated tobacco and snuff boxes appeared in English taverns in the 17th century. The industry expanded rapidly in the 20th century with machines for soft drinks, candy, coffee and cigarettes, and after the Second World War Japan developed an especially dense vending culture, selling everything from hot meals and umbrellas to electronics. More recently, machines accept banknotes, cards and mobile payments, and specialised machines dispense fresh food, pharmaceuticals and personal protective equipment.

Mechanism

After payment, the customer selects an item; the machine verifies the payment, then drives a motor that rotates a helical coil or conveyor to push the selected product into an open dispensing area, or releases a cup and fills it for drinks. Modern machines include refrigeration, telemetry for stock monitoring, currency validators that reject counterfeits, and accessibility features. Bulk vending machines dispense a handful of small items such as gumballs for a coin, using a rotary mechanism.

Industry

Vending is a major retail channel in some countries: Japan has one of the highest densities of machines per capita in the world, and the sector in the United States operates millions of machines in workplaces, schools and public spaces. Regulation covers health codes for food machines, restrictions on tobacco and alcohol sales, and nutritional rules in schools.
