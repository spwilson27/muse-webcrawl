Cooper Basin

LocationSouth Australia and Queensland, Australia
TypeSedimentary basin
AreaApproximately 130,000 km²
Named forCooper Creek
ResourcesNatural gas, crude oil

The Cooper Basin is a Permian–Triassic intracratonic sedimentary basin in Australia, located mainly in the north-eastern part of South Australia and extending into south-western Queensland. It is named after Cooper Creek, which drains the region. The basin is one of Australia's most important onshore petroleum provinces and supplies natural gas to markets in eastern Australia.

Geology

The Cooper Basin overlies the older Warburton Basin and is unconformably overlain by the Eromanga Basin. Its sedimentary succession, up to about 2,500 metres thick in the deepest troughs, consists mainly of fluvial and lacustrine sandstones, siltstones, shales and coals of the Gidgealpa Group and younger units. Source rocks are principally Permian coals and carbonaceous shales; reservoirs are fluvial sandstones sealed by intraformational shales and regional mudstones.

Exploration and production

Gas was first discovered in the basin at Gidgealpa in 1963, and commercial production began in 1969 with gas piped to Adelaide; a pipeline to Sydney followed in 1976 and to Brisbane in the 1990s. The town of Moomba is the main processing centre. The basin has produced large volumes of conventional gas and oil, and in the 21st century its deep coal and shale units have been investigated for unconventional gas resources.

Geothermal energy

The Cooper Basin also hosts some of the world's hottest identified granite bodies close to the surface, giving the region significant hot dry rock geothermal potential. Pilot projects near Innamincka have tested engineered geothermal systems in the high-temperature granites that underlie the basin's sediments.
