Hemp

Hemp, or industrial hemp, is a botanical class of Cannabis sativa cultivars grown specifically for industrial or medicinal use. Along with bamboo, hemp is among the fastest-growing plants on Earth. It was one of the first plants spun into usable fibre, some 50,000 years ago, and it can be refined into a variety of commercial items, including paper, rope, textiles, clothing, biodegradable plastics, paint, insulation, biofuel, food, and animal feed.

Uses

Hemp fibre from the stalk bast is strong and durable and historically vital for rope, canvas (the word itself derives from cannabis), sails, and sacking. The woody core (hurd) is used for animal bedding, paper, and hempcrete building material. Hemp seeds are eaten as a nutritious food rich in protein and omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids, and pressed for oil. Modern applications include bioplastics, insulation panels, and cannabidiol (CBD) extracts.

Legal status

Although chemotype I cannabis and hemp (chemotype III) are both Cannabis sativa, hemp varieties contain very little tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). Many countries regulate hemp by THC content — commonly a limit of 0.2 or 0.3 per cent. Cultivation was restricted in many places during the twentieth century because of association with marijuana, but has been progressively re-legalised; in the United States the 2018 Farm Bill removed hemp from the list of controlled substances.
