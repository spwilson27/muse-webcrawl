Green bond

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A green bond is a fixed-income instrument whose proceeds are earmarked exclusively to finance or re-finance projects with environmental benefits, such as renewable energy, energy efficiency, clean transport, sustainable water management, and green buildings.

The first green bonds were issued by the European Investment Bank (its "Climate Awareness Bond", 2007) and the World Bank (2008). Issuance grew rapidly in the 2010s; cumulative issuance passed one trillion US dollars in 2020 and annual volumes have since run in the hundreds of billions of dollars, with sovereign, municipal, corporate, and bank issuers.

Standards

The market is guided by the voluntary Green Bond Principles of the International Capital Market Association, covering use of proceeds, project evaluation, management of proceeds, and reporting. The Climate Bonds Standard provides certification, and the European Union adopted a European Green Bond Standard to combat "greenwashing" — claims of environmental benefit that are exaggerated or unverified.

Features

Green bonds are otherwise identical in credit terms to conventional bonds of the same issuer. Demand from ESG-minded investors sometimes allows issuers a small pricing advantage known as a "greenium".
