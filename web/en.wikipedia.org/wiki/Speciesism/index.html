Speciesism

Speciesism is the assignment of different rights, consideration, or moral status to individuals solely on the basis of their species membership — a prejudice, in the view of its critics, analogous to racism or sexism.

History of the term

British psychologist Richard Ryder coined 'speciesism' in 1970 and used it in Victims of Science (1975). Philosopher Peter Singer popularised it in Animal Liberation, arguing that equal consideration of interests should not depend on species, and that sentience — the capacity to suffer — is the relevant criterion.

Arguments

Critics of speciesism use the 'argument from marginal cases': if intelligence or rationality grounded moral status, some humans would rank below some animals. Defenders of differential treatment appeal to special relationships, reciprocity, or the view that species membership itself can be morally relevant. Debates connect to factory farming, animal experimentation, and environmental ethics.
