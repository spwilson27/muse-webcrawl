The Arts and Humanities Citation Index (A&HCI or AHCI) is a citation index covering scholarly literature in the arts and humanities. It was developed by the Institute for Scientific Information (ISI) and is now produced by Clarivate as one of the core indexes of the Web of Science platform, alongside the Science Citation Index and the Social Sciences Citation Index.

History

Eugene Garfield founded ISI in 1960 and launched the Science Citation Index in 1964, followed by the Social Sciences Citation Index in 1972. The Arts and Humanities Citation Index followed in 1978, extending citation searching to humanities scholarship, where it had been doubted that the method—devised for laboratory science—would work well, given the humanities' reliance on books, older literature, and non-English sources. The index was offered in print volumes and on magnetic tape before becoming part of the online Web of Science service.

Coverage and content

The A&HCI indexes the complete contents of more than 1,700 journals in fields such as archaeology, art and art history, classics, history, linguistics, literary studies, music, philosophy, religious studies, and theatre, film, and dance. It also selectively covers relevant articles from several thousand science and social-science journals. Records include not only research articles but also book reviews, editorials, letters, and reviews of performances and recordings, reflecting the reviewing culture of humanities disciplines. Coverage runs from 1975 to the present, with cited references enabling users to trace how works have been taken up by later scholarship.

Uses and criticism

Libraries and researchers use the index for literature searching and for mapping scholarly influence. Because inclusion in the index is widely treated as a mark of journal prestige, the A&HCI features in academic evaluation, hiring, and funding decisions. Critics note biases in coverage—toward English-language and journal-based publication over the monographs central to many humanities fields—and caution that citation counts capture only one dimension of humanities scholarship. Unlike journals in the Science and Social Sciences Citation Indexes, A&HCI journals do not receive Journal Impact Factors in the Journal Citation Reports.
