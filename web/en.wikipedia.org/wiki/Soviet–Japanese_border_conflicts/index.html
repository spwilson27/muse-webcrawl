Soviet–Japanese border conflicts

The Soviet–Japanese border conflicts, also known as the Soviet–Japanese border war, were a series of undeclared border clashes between the Soviet Union (and its ally Mongolia) and the Empire of Japan from 1932 to 1939. The fighting took place along the disputed frontier between Japan's puppet state of Manchukuo and the USSR and the Mongolian People's Republic, and ended in a decisive Soviet victory.

Background

After Japan occupied Manchuria in 1931–32, its Kwantung Army pressed against ill-defined borders with the Soviet Far East and Outer Mongolia, both of which Tokyo's planners viewed as future battlefields under the army's "strike north" (hokushin-ron) doctrine. Hundreds of small incidents escalated through the decade.

Major battles

The largest confrontations were the Battle of Lake Khasan (July–August 1938), in which Soviet forces repelled a Japanese attack on hills near Vladivostok, and the Battles of Khalkhin Gol (May–September 1939), where Soviet and Mongolian forces under Georgy Zhukov encircled and destroyed much of the Japanese Sixth Army on the Manchukuo–Mongolia border, inflicting tens of thousands of casualties. A ceasefire was signed in Moscow on 15 September 1939.

Consequences

The defeats discredited the strike-north strategy and pushed Japan toward war in the Pacific against the United States and the European colonial powers instead. The Soviet Union and Japan formalised the outcome in the Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact of April 1941, which held until the Soviet invasion of Manchuria in August 1945.
