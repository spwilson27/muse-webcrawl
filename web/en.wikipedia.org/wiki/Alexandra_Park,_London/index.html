Alexandra Park is a public park in the London Borough of Haringey, north London. Covering around 80 hectares (200 acres) of hillside between Wood Green, Muswell Hill and Crouch End, it surrounds Alexandra Palace and offers panoramic views over the capital.

History

The park was created by an Act of Parliament in 1863 and named after Alexandra of Denmark, who had married the Prince of Wales (the future Edward VII) that year. The Palace, nicknamed the "People's Palace", opened in 1873 as a north London counterpart to the Crystal Palace. The parkland was laid out with carriage drives, a racecourse (which operated until 1970), a boating lake and sports grounds.

Features and wildlife

Today the park contains a boating lake, a skate park, a farmers' market site, cricket and football pitches, and a small animal enclosure with deer. Its mixture of grassland and woodland supports notable wildlife, and the park is managed by the Alexandra Park and Palace Charitable Trust. Large events, concerts and exhibitions continue to be held at the Palace, and the park hosts the annual Red Bull Soapbox Race and fireworks festival.
