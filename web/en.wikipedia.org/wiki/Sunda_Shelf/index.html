Sunda Shelf

The Sunda Shelf is a geologically stable extension of the continental shelf of Southeast Asia, extending from the Asian mainland southward beneath the South China Sea, the Gulf of Thailand, and the Java Sea. It underlies the shallow seas surrounding the Greater Sunda Islands of Borneo, Java, and Sumatra, and the Malay Peninsula. A stable part of the Eurasian Plate known as the Sunda Plate or Sundaland Block, the shelf is characterized by low relief and slow tectonic subsidence compared with the volcanic arc at its southern margin.

Geography

The shelf covers an area of roughly 1.85 million square kilometres, with water depths generally less than 200 metres and much of it shallower than 100 metres. It is bounded to the south and west by the Java Trench along the Sunda Arc, and to the east by the deeper waters of Wallacea beyond the Makassar Strait. Drowned river valleys, mapped by Joseph Beete Jukes and later detailed by G.A.F. Molengraaff, cross the shelf floor and record the drainage of the exposed land surface during glacial lowstands.

Sundaland and human prehistory

During Pleistocene glacial maxima, when global sea level stood as much as 120 metres lower than today, the shelf was exposed as dry land — the subcontinent of Sundaland — which connected Borneo, Java, and Sumatra to mainland Asia. Large river systems, such as the Siam, Malacca, and North Sunda rivers, drained the exposed plain. This land bridge allowed early modern humans and Pleistocene fauna to disperse into the region; Homo erectus remains from Java ("Java Man") date to this connected landscape. Rising post-glacial seas flooded the shelf between roughly 14,000 and 7,000 years ago, in episodes including meltwater pulse 1A, isolating the present islands.

Significance

The shelf seas are major fishing grounds and petroleum provinces; offshore oil and gas fields of the Java Sea, Natuna, and Malay Basin sit in sedimentary basins on the shelf. Biogeographically, the exposed shelf explains the Asian character of the fauna of the Greater Sunda Islands, west of the Wallace Line.
