Surveyor 3

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Surveyor 3
AgencyNASA / JPL
Landed20 April 1967, Ocean of Storms
Famous forVisited by Apollo 12 crew, 1969
Conrad visits Surveyor 3.

Surveyor 3 was an American robotic lunar lander that soft-landed in the Ocean of Storms on 20 April 1967, bouncing twice on arrival after its engines failed to shut down on schedule. It operated for one lunar day, digging trenches with its soil-mechanics scoop and returning over 6,000 images — and then, uniquely, it got visitors.

On 19 November 1969 the Apollo 12 lunar module Intrepid landed within about 180 metres of the probe, and Conrad and Bean walked over to it — the only time humans have visited a spacecraft on another world. They cut off its camera and scoop and brought them home; analysis found the camera's parts still sound after 31 months on the Moon, and a long-running (probably contamination-based) story claimed terrestrial bacteria survived inside it — a claim modern reassessment doubts but which shaped planetary-protection thinking for decades.

Legacy

Surveyor 3's two landings — its own in 1967 and Apollo 12's pinpoint arrival beside it — bookend the robotic-to-crewed handover of lunar exploration. Its remains still sit in the Ocean of Storms, photographed from orbit by the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter.
