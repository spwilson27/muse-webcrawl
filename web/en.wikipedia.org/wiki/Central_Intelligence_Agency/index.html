Central Intelligence Agency

The Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) is a civilian foreign intelligence service of the federal government of the United States tasked with advancing national security through collecting and analysing intelligence from around the world, primarily through human intelligence (HUMINT).

Central Intelligence Agency
Formed: 18 September 1947
Headquarters: Langley, Virginia
Predecessor: Office of Strategic Services
Motto: "Ye shall know the truth and the truth shall make you free"

History

The CIA was created by the National Security Act of 1947, succeeding the wartime Office of Strategic Services. During the Cold War it conducted covert operations including the 1953 Iranian coup, the 1954 Guatemalan coup, and support related to the failed Bay of Pigs invasion (1961). Revelations in the 1970s led to the Church Committee and new oversight.

Organisation and role

Headed by a Director appointed by the President, the CIA is the only independent US intelligence agency reporting to the Director of National Intelligence. Its directorates cover analysis, operations, science and technology, and support. It has no domestic law-enforcement function, a role belonging to the FBI.
