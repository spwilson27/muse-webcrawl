Moderna COVID-19 vaccine

Brand name: Spikevax
Type: mRNA vaccine
Developer: Moderna / NIAID
First authorized: December 2020

The Moderna COVID-19 vaccine, sold under the brand name Spikevax and codenamed mRNA-1273, is a COVID-19 vaccine developed by the American company Moderna together with the U.S. National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID). It was one of the first vaccines authorized for use against SARS-CoV-2.

Technology

The vaccine uses lipid nanoparticles to deliver nucleoside-modified messenger RNA encoding the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2. After injection, cells temporarily produce the spike protein, provoking an immune response including antibodies and T cells.

Efficacy and use

In its Phase III trial of some 30,000 participants, the vaccine showed about 94% efficacy against symptomatic COVID-19 after two doses of 100 µg given 28 days apart. It received emergency use authorization from the U.S. FDA on 18 December 2020 and full approval in January 2022. Common side effects include injection-site pain, fatigue, headache, and muscle aches; rare cases of myocarditis, mainly in young males, have been reported. Updated formulations targeting later variants, such as bivalent and XBB.1.5 versions, were introduced from 2022 onward.
