Women's colleges in the United States

Women's colleges in the United States are institutions of higher education in the United States whose student populations are primarily women. They began to appear in the mid-nineteenth century, when they offered women educational opportunities then denied to them by most colleges and universities. More than 230 women's colleges were founded; today several dozen remain, coordinated in part by the Women's College Coalition.

Prominent examples

Well-known surviving women's colleges include Barnard College (affiliated with Columbia University), Bryn Mawr College, Mount Holyoke College (the oldest, founded in 1837), Smith College, Wellesley College, Scripps College, Spelman College, Mills College at Northeastern University, and the College of Saint Benedict. Historically Black women's colleges such as Spelman and Bennett College have played an important role in educating African American women.

Trends

The number of women's colleges peaked in the 1960s and declined as coeducation spread; many closed, merged, or admitted men. Those that remain emphasise small classes, alumnae networks and women's leadership, and some have adopted policies regarding transgender students.
