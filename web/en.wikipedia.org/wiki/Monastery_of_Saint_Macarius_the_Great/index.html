Monastery of Saint Macarius the Great

The Monastery of Saint Macarius the Great, also known as Dayr Abu Maqar, is a Coptic Orthodox monastery located in Wadi Natrun, in the desert about 92 kilometres (57 mi) northwest of Cairo, Egypt. Founded around 360 AD by Saint Macarius of Egypt, it is one of the oldest Christian monasteries in the world and one of the four surviving ancient monasteries of Wadi Natrun.

History

Saint Macarius the Great settled in the Wadi Natrun desert around 330 AD and gathered disciples around him; after his death in about 391 the community developed into a major centre of desert monasticism. The monastery was repeatedly damaged by raids and rebuilt over the centuries, and several of its monks became patriarchs of Alexandria. It preserves relics traditionally identified with Macarius and other saints, including John the Baptist and Elisha according to Coptic tradition.

Modern revival

In 1969 Pope Cyril VI sent a group of monks under Father Matta El Meskeen (Matthew the Poor) to restore the near-deserted monastery. The community grew to over a hundred monks, reclaimed large areas of surrounding desert for agriculture, and became a notable centre of Coptic scholarship and publishing. The monastery contains several ancient churches, a large keep, and an extensive modern library.
