Decimation (signal processing)

In digital signal processing, decimation is the process of reducing the sampling rate of a signal. It is the downsampling counterpart of interpolation, which increases the sampling rate. The term derives from the Latin decimatio, "removal of a tenth," though modern decimation factors are arbitrary integers.

Process

Decimation by a factor M consists of two steps: first, low-pass filtering the signal to limit its bandwidth to below half of the new (reduced) sampling rate, preventing aliasing; second, downsampling by keeping only every M-th sample and discarding the rest. The anti-aliasing filter is essential: without it, frequency components above the new Nyquist frequency would fold back into the band of interest and corrupt the signal.

Applications

Decimation is used in multirate signal processing systems, oversampling analog-to-digital and digital-to-analog converters, digital audio (for example converting high-rate one-bit streams to standard PCM rates), software-defined radio, and image scaling. Efficient implementations using polyphase filter structures perform the filtering at the lower output rate to save computation.
