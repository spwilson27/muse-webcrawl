Boston National Historical Park

Boston National Historical Park is a unit of the United States National Park Service that commemorates sites associated with the American Revolution and the early history of the United States Navy in Boston, Massachusetts. Established on 1 October 1974, the park is a partnership park: most of its sites are owned by other bodies — the City of Boston, the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, the U.S. Navy, and private non-profit organisations — and are linked by the Freedom Trail.

Sites

The park comprises eight sites: the Bunker Hill Monument in Charlestown, commemorating the 1775 battle; the Charlestown Navy Yard, home of the frigate USS Constitution ("Old Ironsides"), launched in 1797 and the world's oldest commissioned naval vessel afloat; Dorchester Heights in South Boston, fortified by Washington in March 1776; Faneuil Hall, the colonial marketplace and town hall; the Old North Church, from whose steeple signal lanterns were hung on 18 April 1775; the Old South Meeting House, where the Boston Tea Party meetings were held; the Old State House; and the Paul Revere House, the oldest house in downtown Boston.

Visiting

Visitor centres at Faneuil Hall and the Charlestown Navy Yard provide orientation, and park rangers offer guided walks along portions of the Freedom Trail. The park receives roughly three million visitors in a typical year.
