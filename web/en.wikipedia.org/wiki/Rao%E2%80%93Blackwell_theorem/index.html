Rao–Blackwell theorem

FieldStatistics (estimation theory)
Named afterC. R. Rao, David Blackwell
PublishedRao (1945); Blackwell (1947)

In statistics, the Rao–Blackwell theorem, sometimes called the Rao–Blackwell–Kolmogorov theorem, states that any estimator can be improved (or at least not worsened) by conditioning it on a sufficient statistic. The improved estimator — the Rao–Blackwellised estimator — is the conditional expectation of the original estimator given a sufficient statistic for the parameter.

Statement

If δ(X) is an estimator of a parameter θ and T(X) is a sufficient statistic for θ, then the estimator δ*(X) = E[δ(X) | T(X)] has mean squared error (and, more generally, expected loss for any convex loss function) no greater than that of δ(X), for every value of θ. The result was published independently by C. R. Rao in 1945 and David Blackwell in 1947.

Applications

Combined with the Lehmann–Scheffé theorem, Rao–Blackwellisation is a standard route to uniformly minimum-variance unbiased estimators: conditioning an unbiased estimator on a complete sufficient statistic gives the unique best unbiased estimator. The technique also underlies Rao–Blackwellised particle filtering in Monte Carlo methods.
