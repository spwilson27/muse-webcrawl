Hokey pokey (ice cream)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hokey pokey is a flavour of ice cream in New Zealand, consisting of plain vanilla ice cream with small, solid lumps of honeycomb toffee. It is one of the country's most iconic foods and among its most popular ice cream flavours, second only to vanilla by some surveys.

History and composition

The flavour dates to the late 19th or early 20th century; "hokey pokey" was a slang term for ice cream sold by Italian street vendors in Britain and America (possibly from ecco un poco, "here's a little"). In New Zealand the term came to denote honeycomb toffee — a sponge candy of sugar, golden syrup, and baking soda — and by the 1940s–50s producers such as Tip Top were folding pieces of it into vanilla ice cream. Exported under brands including Tip Top and Much Moore, hokey pokey is a staple of New Zealand summers and a marker of Kiwi identity abroad.

Variations

The flavour appears in ice cream bars, cones ("trumpets"), and as an ingredient in desserts. Australian manufacturers sell it as "honeycomb" ice cream, while in Japan and elsewhere it may be labelled "honeycomb crunch."
