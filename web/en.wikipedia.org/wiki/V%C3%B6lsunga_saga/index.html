Völsunga saga

LanguageOld Norse (Icelandic)
DateLate 13th century
SubjectThe Völsung clan; Sigurd the dragon-slayer

The Völsunga saga (Old Norse: Vǫlsunga saga) is a legendary saga written in Old Norse prose in Iceland around the late 13th century. It recounts the origin and decline of the Völsung clan, including the story of Sigurd the dragon-slayer, the cursed gold of Andvari, and the fall of the Burgundian kings.

Contents

The saga traces the descendants of Odin through Sigi, Rerir, and Völsung; tells of Sigmund and the sword Gram, drawn from the tree Barnstokkr; and follows Sigmund's son Sigurd, who kills the dragon Fáfnir, awakens the valkyrie Brynhildr (Sigrdrífa), and is drawn into the court of the Gjúkungar (Nibelungs). His murder, Brynhildr's suicide, and the later vengeance cycle involving Gudrun, Atli (Attila), and Hamdir and Sörli form the saga's tragic conclusion.

Sources and influence

The saga is based on older heroic poetry, much of it preserved in the Poetic Edda, and parallels the Middle High German Nibelungenlied. Together with that epic it formed the main narrative source for Richard Wagner's Der Ring des Nibelungen, and it influenced modern fantasy writers including William Morris, J. R. R. Tolkien, and Jorge Luis Borges.
