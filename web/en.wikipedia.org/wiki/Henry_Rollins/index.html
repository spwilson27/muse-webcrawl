Henry Rollins

Henry Rollins (born Henry Lawrence Garfield, February 13, 1961) is an American singer, writer, actor, spoken-word artist, and activist. He first gained prominence as the lead vocalist of the hardcore punk band Black Flag (1981–1986), and later fronted the Rollins Band. Rollins is also known for his extensive spoken-word tours, his radio and television work, and a large body of published writing.

Early life

Rollins was born in Washington, D.C., the only child of Iris and Paul Garfield. After his parents divorced, he was raised by his mother and attended the Bullis School, a military-style preparatory school in Potomac, Maryland, an experience he has frequently described in his spoken-word work. As a teenager he became involved in the Washington, D.C. hardcore punk scene, working at a Häagen-Dazs ice cream shop managed by future Fugazi and Minor Threat frontman Ian MacKaye.

Music career

Rollins briefly sang for the hardcore band State of Alert (S.O.A.), which released the EP No Policy in 1981. That same year, as a fan of the California band Black Flag, he was invited to join after singing with the band during an impromptu performance in New York. With Rollins as frontman, Black Flag released the landmark album Damaged (1981) and toured relentlessly until disbanding in 1986.

After Black Flag, Rollins formed the Rollins Band, which achieved commercial success with the album The End of Silence (1992) and the single "Liar" (1994) from Weight. The band's intense live performances and Rollins's muscular stage presence made him an emblem of 1990s alternative rock.

Spoken word and writing

Rollins has released numerous spoken-word albums and performs long, story-driven spoken-word tours worldwide. His publishing company, 2.13.61, has issued his journals and poetry collections, including Get in the Van (1994), an account of his Black Flag years that won a Grammy Award for Best Spoken Word Album.

Acting and media

Rollins has appeared in films such as Johnny Mnemonic, Heat, and Lost Highway, hosted television programs including The Henry Rollins Show, and played a recurring role in Sons of Anarchy. He has hosted a weekly radio show on KCRW and written columns for publications including LA Weekly.

Activism

Rollins is an outspoken human rights and social justice advocate, has performed on USO tours for American troops, and campaigns for LGBT rights and the release of the West Memphis Three, among other causes.
