Harrier (dog)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Harrier is a medium-sized breed of dog, a scent hound developed in England for hunting hares — hence its name. It resembles a smaller English Foxhound and a larger Beagle, standing roughly 48–55 cm at the shoulder, with a short, dense, weather-resistant coat, most commonly tricolour.

History

Packs of harrier-type hounds have been kept in England since at least the 13th century; the first recorded pack, the Penistone, was founded in 1260. Traditionally hunted on foot or with modest mounted fields, harriers were valued by farmers and smaller hunts. The breed is recognized by the UK Kennel Club and by the Fédération Cynologique Internationale, though it remains rare outside hunting circles and was briefly dropped from, then reinstated to, the American Kennel Club registry.

Temperament

Harriers are pack animals: friendly, outgoing and tolerant with people and other dogs, but with a strong nose and independent streak that demands exercise and secure fencing.
