The Dassault Mirage 2000 is a French multirole, single-engine, delta-wing, fourth-generation fighter aircraft manufactured by Dassault Aviation. It was designed in the late 1970s as a lightweight fighter to replace the Mirage III for the French Air Force, returning to the tailless delta configuration but with fly-by-wire controls, relaxed stability and modern avionics.

Design and variants

The single-seat Mirage 2000C interceptor entered service in 1984; the two-seat 2000N was developed for low-altitude nuclear strike with the ASMP missile, and the 2000D for conventional precision attack. The export-oriented Mirage 2000-5 added the RDY multi-target radar, a glass cockpit and MICA missile capability. Over 600 aircraft were built for nine air forces including India, Greece, Taiwan, Egypt, Qatar, Peru, Brazil and the UAE.

Service

Mirage 2000s flew combat missions in the Gulf War, over Bosnia and Kosovo, in Afghanistan, Libya in 2011 — where French 2000Ds and 2000Cs were among the first strike aircraft engaged — and in Indian service struck targets at Kargil in 1999 and Balakot in 2019. France is progressively replacing the type with the Rafale, though upgraded 2000Ds are to serve into the 2030s. See also Delta wing and Mirage III.
