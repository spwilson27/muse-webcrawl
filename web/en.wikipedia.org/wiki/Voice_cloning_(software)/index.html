Voice cloning (software)

Voice cloning is a software technology that uses artificial intelligence, typically deep neural networks, to replicate the voice of a specific person from samples of their speech, allowing a text-to-speech system to speak new utterances in a voice that closely resembles the original speaker.

Technology

Modern systems pair a speaker encoder, which extracts a compact embedding of vocal characteristics from a few seconds or minutes of audio, with a neural vocoder and sequence-to-sequence acoustic model that generate waveforms. Few-shot and zero-shot methods reduced the required sample from hours of studio recording to under a minute of speech.

Applications

Legitimate uses include restoring speech to people who lose their voice through illness, personalized digital assistants, audiobook and media production, accessibility tools, and localization of film and video. Companies such as Respeecher, ElevenLabs and others market cloning services, generally requiring consent verification.

Concerns

Because cloned voices can be nearly indistinguishable from real ones, the technology raises risks of fraud (for example, executive-impersonation scams), political disinformation, and unauthorized use of actors' and public figures' voices. Legislatures and regulators have responded with consent requirements and disclosure rules, and detection research aims to identify synthetic speech.
