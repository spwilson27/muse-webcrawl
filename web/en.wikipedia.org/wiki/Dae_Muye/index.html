Dae Muye (Korean: 대무예; Hanja: 大武藝), also known as King Mu of Balhae, was the second king of Balhae, reigning from 719 to 737. He was the eldest son of Dae Joyeong, the founder of the kingdom, which controlled territories in Manchuria and the northern Korean Peninsula after the fall of Goguryeo.

Reign

Dae Muye pursued an expansionist and assertive foreign policy, in contrast to his father's more cautious approach. Balhae forces extended the kingdom's frontiers, subduing peoples of the Amur region, and conflict erupted with Tang China: in 732 a Balhae fleet under Jang Mun-hyu raided the Shandong Peninsula, killing the Tang prefect of Dengzhou. Tang retaliated in alliance with Silla, attacking Balhae from the south, though severe weather frustrated the campaign. Dae Muye was succeeded by his younger brother Dae Heummu, who reversed his belligerent policies and restored friendly relations with Tang.
