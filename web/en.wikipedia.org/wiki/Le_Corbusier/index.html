Le Corbusier

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Villa Savoye at Poissy (1931): a white box on pilotis with ribbon windows and a roof garden, a period motor car beneath the curve of the ground floor]

Charles-Édouard Jeanneret (6 October 1887 – 27 August 1965), known as Le Corbusier, was a Swiss-French architect, designer, painter, urban planner and writer, who was one of the pioneers of what is now regarded as modern architecture. He was born in Switzerland and acquired French nationality by naturalisation in 1930.

His 'Five Points of Architecture' (pilotis, free plan, free façade, ribbon windows, roof garden) produced icons such as the Villa Savoye (1931); his Modulor system, the mass housing of the Unité d'Habitation in Marseille (1952), the sculptural chapel of Notre-Dame du Haut at Ronchamp (1955) and the planned city of Chandigarh in India made him the most influential — and contested — architect of the century. Seventeen of his works across seven countries form a UNESCO World Heritage listing.
