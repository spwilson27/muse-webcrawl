Well-behaved

In mathematics, the term well-behaved is an informal (but widely used) description of a mathematical object — such as a function, a set, a space or a system of equations — that satisfies the conditions needed for the standard theorems of a theory to apply, and that does not exhibit pathological behaviour. A well-behaved function, for example, might be continuous, differentiable, of bounded variation, and free of singularities in the region of interest.

Usage

The term has no single precise definition; its meaning depends on context. In analysis, well-behaved functions are those for which operations such as differentiation, integration and taking limits can be performed without special care. In physics and engineering, a well-behaved solution is one that remains finite and physically sensible. In statistics, well-behaved estimators and models satisfy regularity conditions such as identifiability and finite variance.

Mathematicians sometimes contrast well-behaved objects with deliberately pathological counterexamples — such as the Weierstrass function (continuous everywhere but differentiable nowhere), space-filling curves, or non-measurable sets — which show that weaker hypotheses are insufficient. The qualifier "well-behaved" in a proof therefore signals that such edge cases are being excluded, sometimes as a placeholder for a list of technical hypotheses that the author declines to spell out.

Related concepts

  • Nice – an even more informal synonym used for spaces and categories with convenient properties.
  • Regularity – in PDE theory, the smoothness of solutions, formalised in regularity theorems.
  • Pathological – the opposite: objects violating intuitive expectations.
