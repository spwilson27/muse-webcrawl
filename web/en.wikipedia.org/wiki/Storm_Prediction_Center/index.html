Storm Prediction Center

The Storm Prediction Center (SPC) is a United States government agency, part of NOAA's National Weather Service, responsible for forecasting severe thunderstorms across the contiguous United States. Based in Norman, Oklahoma, in the National Weather Center, the SPC issues convective outlooks, mesoscale discussions, and severe thunderstorm and tornado watches around the clock.

Storm Prediction Center
Parent agency: National Weather Service / NOAA
Headquarters: Norman, Oklahoma
Founded: 1952 (as Severe Local Storms Warning Service)
Role: Severe convective storm forecasting

Products

The SPC's best-known product is the convective outlook, a map categorising the risk of severe weather for today, tomorrow and the following day as marginal, slight, enhanced, moderate or high risk — a scale introduced in its current form in 2014. When severe weather is expected within hours, forecasters issue a watch covering a limited area for a few hours; a "particularly dangerous situation" (PDS) watch denotes an exceptional threat. Actual warnings for specific storms are issued by local National Weather Service forecast offices. The SPC also issues fire-weather outlooks, mesoscale discussions and a national storm report archive.

History

Systematic severe-storm forecasting grew out of the work of Ernest Fawbush and Robert Miller, who issued the first successful tornado forecast at Tinker Air Force Base in 1948. A civilian severe local storms unit was established in the U.S. Weather Bureau in 1952, moved through Kansas City and was renamed the Storm Prediction Center on its relocation to Norman, Oklahoma, in 1997, placing it beside the National Severe Storms Laboratory and the University of Oklahoma.
