Signal constellation (constellation diagram)

A constellation diagram for 16-QAM, showing the allowed symbol points.

A constellation diagram is a representation of a signal modulated by a digital modulation scheme such as quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) or phase-shift keying (PSK). It displays the sampled signal as points in a two-dimensional scatter plot in the complex plane: the horizontal axis is the in-phase (I) component and the vertical axis the quadrature (Q) component. Each point of the signal constellation corresponds to one symbol that the modulator can transmit.

Interpretation

In a clean channel the received points cluster tightly around the ideal constellation positions. Noise spreads the clusters, phase errors rotate them, and amplitude distortion compresses or expands them, so engineers use the diagram as a qualitative diagnostic of signal quality. The distance between adjacent points, set by the modulation order and average energy, determines how easily symbols can be confused and hence the bit error rate. Related measurements include error vector magnitude (EVM), computed from the displacement of received points from their ideal positions.

Common constellations

BPSK has two points on the real axis; QPSK has four at the corners of a square; 16-QAM arranges sixteen points in a 4×4 grid; Wi-Fi and cable standards use up to 1024-QAM and beyond. In optical communications, constellation shaping — choosing non-uniform point spacings or probabilities — improves performance toward the Shannon limit.
