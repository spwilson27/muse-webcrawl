Plagiocephaly

Specialty: Pediatrics, medical genetics

Plagiocephaly, also known as flat head syndrome, is a condition characterized by an asymmetrical distortion (flattening of one side) of the skull. It is broadly divided into synostotic plagiocephaly, caused by premature fusion of one or more cranial sutures (craniosynostosis), and the much more common deformational (positional) plagiocephaly, caused by external pressure on an infant's soft skull.

Causes

Positional plagiocephaly became more common after "back to sleep" campaigns recommended supine sleeping to reduce the risk of sudden infant death syndrome; prolonged time in car seats, swings, and other containers, and a preference for turning the head to one side (sometimes due to torticollis), also contribute. Synostotic plagiocephaly results from premature fusion of a coronal or lambdoid suture.

Treatment

Mild positional cases often resolve with repositioning, increased supervised "tummy time", and physical therapy for associated torticollis. Moderate to severe cases may be treated with a custom cranial orthosis (helmet). Synostotic plagiocephaly generally requires surgery.
