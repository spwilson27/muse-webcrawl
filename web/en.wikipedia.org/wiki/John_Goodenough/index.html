John Bannister Goodenough (July 25, 1922 – June 25, 2023) was an American materials scientist and solid-state physicist. He is credited with identifying and developing the cathode materials that made the rechargeable lithium-ion battery practical, and in 2019 he shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for that work, becoming at 97 the oldest person ever awarded a Nobel Prize.

Early career

Born in Germany to American parents, Goodenough studied mathematics at Yale and served as a meteorologist in the U.S. Army Air Forces during the Second World War. He earned a doctorate in physics from the University of Chicago in 1952 and worked at MIT's Lincoln Laboratory, where he contributed to magnetic memory and formulated the Goodenough–Kanamori rules of superexchange.

Battery research

At the University of Oxford in 1980, Goodenough's group demonstrated the lithium cobalt oxide cathode, which greatly increased the energy density of lithium cells and enabled the portable electronics revolution. Later, at the University of Texas at Austin, he identified the safer lithium iron phosphate cathode. He remained an active researcher into his late nineties, working on solid-state batteries, and died in 2023 at the age of 100.
