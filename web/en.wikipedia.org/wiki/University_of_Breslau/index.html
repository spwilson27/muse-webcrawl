The University of Breslau (German: Schlesische Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität zu Breslau) was a German university in Breslau, Silesia, founded in 1811 by King Frederick William III of Prussia by merging the city's Jesuit academy (the Leopoldina, founded 1702) with the Protestant Viadrina university transferred from Frankfurt (Oder).

History

The university united Catholic and Protestant theological faculties under one roof, unusual for its time. Through the 19th and early 20th centuries it became a leading centre of German scholarship in medicine, physics, chemistry, history and Oriental studies. Scholars associated with it included the physicists Max Born and Otto Stern, chemist Fritz Haber, and philosopher Edith Stein. Like other German universities it was brought into line after 1933, and Jewish faculty were expelled.

End and succession

During the siege of Breslau in 1945 much of the city was destroyed. After the war Silesia was transferred to Poland, the German population was expelled, and the university's collections were partly transferred to the new Polish University of Wrocław, founded in 1945 partly by academics displaced from Lwów. The Polish university today regards the merged traditions of 1702 and 1811 as part of its own heritage.
