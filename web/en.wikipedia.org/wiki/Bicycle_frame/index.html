Bicycle frame

A bicycle frame is the main component of a bicycle, onto which wheels and other components are fitted. The dominant modern design, the diamond frame, consists of a main triangle — top tube, down tube and seat tube — and a rear triangle of seat stays and chain stays. The frame supports the rider, transmits pedalling forces, and largely determines the bicycle's handling and comfort.

The diamond frame: main triangle and rear triangle.

Frame geometry

Key dimensions include seat tube length and angle, top tube length, head tube angle, fork rake and trail, chain stay length and bottom bracket drop. Steeper head angles and shorter wheelbases generally produce quicker steering, while slacker geometry favours stability. Frame sizes are traditionally given by seat tube length in centimetres or inches.

Materials

Frames have been built from many materials: steel (including chromoly and Reynolds tubing) is durable and repairable; aluminium alloys are light and stiff; carbon fibre composites allow highly engineered shapes and low weight; titanium combines light weight with corrosion resistance. Bamboo and wood are niche choices.

Other designs

Alternatives to the diamond frame include the step-through frame, which eases mounting; cantilever and mixte frames; recumbent frames; and folding frames that hinge for compact storage and transport.
