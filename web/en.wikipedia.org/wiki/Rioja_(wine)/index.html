Rioja (wine)

TypeDenominación de Origen Calificada (DOCa)
RegionLa Rioja, Basque Country and Navarre, Spain
Main grapesTempranillo, Garnacha, Graciano, Mazuelo; Viura (white)
Sub-regionsRioja Alta, Rioja Alavesa, Rioja Oriental

Rioja is a Spanish wine region and Denominación de Origen Calificada (DOCa) in north-central Spain, centred on the Ebro valley in the autonomous community of La Rioja and extending into Álava (Basque Country) and Navarre. It is Spain's most famous wine region, best known for red wines based on the Tempranillo grape and traditionally aged for long periods in oak barrels.

Sub-regions and grapes

Rioja is traditionally divided into three zones. Rioja Alta, in the west around Haro, produces elegant, age-worthy wines from higher, cooler vineyards. Rioja Alavesa, north of the Ebro in Álava province, has limestone soils favourable to Tempranillo. Rioja Oriental (formerly Rioja Baja), in the warmer, drier east, is the stronghold of Garnacha. Red Rioja blends Tempranillo with Garnacha, Graciano and Mazuelo (Carignan); white Rioja is based mainly on Viura (Macabeo), and rosado styles are also made.

Ageing classifications

Rioja pioneered a classification based on ageing: Genérico (young wines), Crianza (at least two years' ageing, one in oak), Reserva (at least three years, one in oak) and Gran Reserva (at least five years, two in oak, made only in the best vintages). The classic style — developed in the 19th century under French influence when phylloxera devastated Bordeaux — featured long ageing in American oak, yielding wines scented with vanilla, dill, leather and dried fruit. Since the 1990s many producers have adopted a more modern, fruit-driven style using French oak and estate-grown fruit; reforms in 2017–2019 added geographic categories such as Viñedo Singular for single vineyards.

History

Viticulture in the Ebro valley dates to Roman times, and Rioja's wines were regulated as early as the 16th century; the region received formal denominación status in 1925 and was promoted to the higher DOCa rank in 1991, the first in Spain. The region's capital of wine is Haro, whose Barrio de la Estación district concentrates several historic bodegas founded in the 19th century.
