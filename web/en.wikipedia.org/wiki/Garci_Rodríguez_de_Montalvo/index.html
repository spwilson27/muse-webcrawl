Garcí Rodríguez de Montalvo (c. 1450 – 1504) was a Spanish author and regidor (councilman) of Medina del Campo in the Crown of Castile. He is best known for his edition and continuation of the chivalric romance Amadís de Gaula and for its sequel, Las sergas de Esplandián, in which the name "California" first appears in literature.

Life

Little is known of Montalvo's life. He was a member of the municipal council of Medina del Campo, one of the most important commercial towns of Castile, and appears in records of the town in the late 15th century, during the reign of the Catholic Monarchs, Ferdinand and Isabella, whom he is reported to have served in the conquest of Granada.

Works

Amadís de Gaula

An older, lost version of the story of Amadís of Gaul circulated in Iberia from the 14th century. Montalvo reworked the first three books and added a fourth book of his own. His version was printed at Zaragoza in 1508 by Jorge Coci and became the definitive text of the romance and a model for the Spanish libros de caballerías (books of chivalry) that dominated 16th-century popular literature and were satirised a century later in Don Quixote.

Las sergas de Esplandián

Montalvo's sequel, Las sergas de Esplandián ("The exploits of Esplandián"), was published in 1510. It recounts the adventures of Esplandián, son of Amadís, and famously describes an island called California, ruled by the black queen Calafia and her Amazon warriors, "on the right hand of the Indies". The name — whether coined by Montalvo or adapted from earlier sources — was later applied by Spanish explorers to the peninsula and region of California.
