Frederic Edwin Church (May 4, 1826 – April 7, 1900) was an American landscape painter born in Hartford, Connecticut. He was a central figure in the Hudson River School of American landscape painting and is best known for painting vast, luminously detailed panoramas of mountains, waterfalls, and dramatic sunsets in North and South America and the Arctic. His large-scale works were exhibited as theatrical single-painting events that drew huge crowds.

Early career

Church was the only pupil of Thomas Cole, the founder of the Hudson River School, studying with him at Catskill, New York, from 1844 to 1846. By 1848 he was the youngest artist ever elected an associate of the National Academy of Design, becoming a full academician the next year. His early paintings of New England and the Catskills already showed his hallmark meticulous natural detail and radiant light.

Masterworks and travels

Inspired by the writings of the naturalist Alexander von Humboldt, Church travelled to Colombia and Ecuador in 1853 and again in 1857. The result was The Heart of the Andes (1859), a monumental canvas roughly five by ten feet, teeming with botanically accurate vegetation. Exhibited in a darkened room with theatrical framing, it drew more than 12,000 paying visitors in New York and sold for $10,000 — then the highest price paid for a work by a living American artist.

Other celebrated canvases include Niagara (1857), painted from the Canadian shore; The Icebergs (1861), based on a voyage to Labrador; and The Aegean Sea and The Parthenon after his 1867–69 journey to Europe and the Near East. Church's later years were quieter as arthritis limited his painting and tastes changed.

Olana

Church designed his home, Olana, a Persian-inspired villa on a hill above the Hudson River near Hudson, New York, where he also landscaped the grounds as a work of art. He died in 1900. Olana is now a New York State Historic Site and one of the best-preserved artists' homes in America. His major paintings are held by the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Corcoran Collection at the National Gallery of Art, and other leading museums.
