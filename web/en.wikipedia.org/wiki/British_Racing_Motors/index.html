British Racing Motors

British Racing Motors (BRM) was a British Formula One motor racing team founded in 1945 by Raymond Mays and Peter Berthon. Based at Bourne in Lincolnshire, BRM raced its own chassis and engines, won the Formula One Constructors' Championship in 1962, and supplied the car in which Graham Hill won that year's Drivers' Championship.

The BRM P57, 1962 World Championship winner.

History

BRM was conceived as a national prestige project — a British car to challenge Italian and German dominance — and was funded through a trust of motor-industry companies. Its first car, the ambitious supercharged V16 Type 15 of 1950, was spectacularly noisy but unreliable. Success finally came with the 1.5-litre V8 P57 "stackpipe" of 1962: Graham Hill won four Grands Prix and both championships.

The team remained competitive through the 1960s with drivers including Jackie Stewart, winning the 1970 Belgian Grand Prix with Pedro Rodríguez and the 1971 Italian Grand Prix with Peter Gethin — still the fastest race in Formula One history by average speed. Decline followed under Louis Stanley's management, and after the sponsorship of Stanley-BRM collapsed, the team withdrew from Formula One at the end of 1977.

Legacy

BRM engines powered other teams, notably Lotus, to race wins, and its H16 engine won the 1966 United States Grand Prix in a Lotus 43. The original cars survive in historic racing, and the Bourne workshops' heritage is preserved by collectors and the BRM Association.
