Big Brothers Big Sisters of Canada (BBBSC; French: Les Grands Frères Grandes Sœurs du Canada) is a Canadian non-profit federation of local agencies that provide mentoring programs for children and youth. Volunteer mentors ("Bigs") are matched with young people ("Littles") to build supportive one-to-one and group mentoring relationships.

History

The movement began in Canada in 1913, when the first Big Brothers agency was founded in Toronto, influenced by the earlier American Big Brothers movement started by Ernest Kent Coulter in New York in 1904. Big Sisters associations developed separately from 1912 onward. The national federation in its modern form brought the Big Brothers and Big Sisters organizations together; today the federation supports more than 100 local agencies serving over a thousand communities across Canada.

Programs

Core programs include traditional one-to-one community mentoring, school-based In-School Mentoring, group mentoring, and programs designed for specific communities, including mentoring for Indigenous youth, newcomers, and LGBTQ2+ youth. The organization cites research associating mentoring with improved self-confidence, school engagement, and reduced risk behaviours, and its best-known fundraising tradition is the annual "Bowl for Kids' Sake."
