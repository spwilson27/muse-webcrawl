"Strawberry Fields Forever" is a song by the English rock band the Beatles, written by John Lennon and credited to the Lennon–McCartney partnership. It was released on 13 February 1967 as a double A-side single with Paul McCartney's "Penny Lane". It represented a departure from the band's earlier recordings and from the conventions of pop music.

The song takes its name from Strawberry Field, a Salvation Army children's home near Lennon's childhood home in Liverpool. Lennon wrote it while filming How I Won the War in Spain in the autumn of 1966; its dreamlike lyrics reflect on his childhood and on the difficulty of being himself.

Recording

The Beatles recorded the song over five weeks in late 1966, producing two very different versions—one a sparse arrangement and one with cellos and brass. At Lennon's request, producer George Martin and engineer Geoff Emerick combined the beginning of one take with the end of another, in different keys and tempos, using varispeed to splice them—an early landmark in studio experimentation. The track also features Mellotron, swarmandal and extensive tape manipulation, ending with a false fade and a surreal coda in which Lennon mumbles "cranberry sauce".

Reception and legacy

The single reached number two on the UK charts (kept from number one, famously, by Engelbert Humperdinck's "Release Me") and number eight in the US. A promotional film shot at Sevenoaks, Kent, was among the first music videos. The song was later included on the US version of the Magical Mystery Tour album (1967). It is widely regarded as one of the Beatles' finest recordings and a defining work of psychedelic rock; the real Strawberry Field, its distinctive red gates now a Liverpool landmark, gives its name to the Strawberry Fields memorial in New York's Central Park.
