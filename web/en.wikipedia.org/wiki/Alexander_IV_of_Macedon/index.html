Alexander IV (c. 323 – 309 BC), sometimes erroneously given as Alexander Aegus, was the posthumous son of Alexander the Great and his wife Roxana. He was proclaimed king of Macedon and the empire at birth but never exercised real power, and his murder at about fourteen years of age extinguished the Argead dynasty.

Birth and nominal kingship

Alexander the Great died at Babylon in June 323 BC with Roxana pregnant. The army's settlement at Babylon made the unborn child joint king alongside Alexander's half-brother Philip III Arrhidaeus, under a series of regents. Control of the young king became a prize in the wars of the Diadochi: he passed through the hands of Perdiccas, Antipater, Polyperchon and Olympias.

Captivity and death

After Cassander seized power in Macedon, Alexander IV and Roxana were held under guard at Amphipolis. Around 309 BC, as the boy approached the age at which he might claim his inheritance and public support for him grew, Cassander had mother and son secretly murdered. Their deaths cleared the way for Alexander's former generals to assume royal titles themselves and divide the empire into Hellenistic kingdoms.

Legacy

A royal tomb at Vergina (Aigai) has been associated by some scholars with Alexander IV, though the attribution remains debated.
