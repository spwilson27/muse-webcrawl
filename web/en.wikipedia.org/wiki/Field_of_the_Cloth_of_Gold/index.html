Field of the Cloth of Gold

The Field of the Cloth of Gold (French: Camp du Drap d'Or) was a summit meeting between King Henry VIII of England and King Francis I of France from 7 to 24 June 1520. Held in the Pale of Calais, between Ardres in France and Guînes in the English Pale, it was a spectacular display of wealth and pageantry by both courts, intended to strengthen the bond of friendship between the two kings following the Anglo-French treaty of 1514. The name derives from the lavish use of cloth of gold — fabric woven with silk and gold thread — in the tents and garments on display.

Background

Two ambitious young kings dominated western Europe: Henry VIII, advised by Cardinal Wolsey, sought a leading role in European diplomacy, while Francis I wanted English neutrality in his rivalry with the Habsburg emperor Charles V. Wolsey arranged the summit as the centrepiece of a general peace following the Treaty of London (1518).

The meeting

Each king brought a retinue of roughly 6,000 attendants. Henry was accompanied by Queen Catherine of Aragon, Francis by Queen Claude. Henry's court occupied a temporary palace at Guînes covering some 10,000 square metres, with a chapel, great hall and apartments; fountains reportedly ran with wine. The French camp near Ardres used hundreds of tents of cloth of gold. The eighteen days featured jousts, tournaments, wrestling (in which Francis famously threw Henry), archery, banquets, masses and masques.

Outcome

Despite the splendour — which strained both treasuries — the summit produced little of substance. Within weeks Henry met Charles V at Gravelines, and by 1522 England and the Empire were at war with France. The event is remembered principally as a monument to Renaissance court spectacle and competitive display.
