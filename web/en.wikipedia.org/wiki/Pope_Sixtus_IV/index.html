Pope Sixtus IV (Italian: Sisto IV; 21 July 1414 – 12 August 1484), born Francesco della Rovere, was head of the Catholic Church from 9 August 1471 until his death in 1484. A Franciscan theologian, he is remembered for building the Sistine Chapel, advancing the Spanish Inquisition, and entangling the papacy in Italian power politics and nepotism on an unprecedented scale.

Early life

Della Rovere was born near Savona, joined the Franciscans, and became a celebrated teacher of theology at Padua and other universities, rising to minister general of his order in 1464. Paul II made him cardinal in 1467.

Pontificate

As pope, Sixtus transformed Rome: he built the Ponte Sisto across the Tiber, restored churches and streets, reorganised the Vatican Library, and constructed the Sistine Chapel (1473–1481), whose walls were frescoed by Botticelli, Perugino, Ghirlandaio and others; his nephew Julius II later commissioned Michelangelo's ceiling. The Sistine Chapel Choir was also established under him.

At the request of Ferdinand and Isabella, Sixtus authorised the Spanish Inquisition in 1478, though he later protested its excesses. He annulled the decrees of the Council of Constance, promoted crusades against the Turks after the Ottoman landing at Otranto, and was implicated in the Pazzi conspiracy of 1478 against the Medici in Florence, which led to war with Florence and Naples. His aggressive advancement of his della Rovere and Riario nephews set a pattern of papal nepotism. He died in August 1484, unpopular in Rome because of heavy taxation and the wars his policies had provoked.
