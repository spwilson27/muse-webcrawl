Cyanocobalamin


Cyanocobalamin structure (placeholder image)

Formula: C63H88CoN14O14P
Molar mass: 1355.4 g·mol−1

Cyanocobalamin is a synthetic form of vitamin B12, used in dietary supplements and fortified foods and to treat vitamin B12 deficiency. It is a dark red crystalline compound containing cobalt in a corrin ring; in the body it is converted to the active coenzyme forms methylcobalamin and adenosylcobalamin.

Uses

Cyanocobalamin is chemically stable and inexpensive to manufacture, which makes it the commonest supplemental form of B12. Deficiency — from pernicious anemia, malabsorption, or diets lacking animal products — causes megaloblastic anemia and neurological damage. The vitamin is given orally, by intramuscular injection, or as a nasal preparation. Hydroxocobalamin, a related form, is preferred in some countries and is also used as an antidote for cyanide poisoning; cyanocobalamin takes its name from the cyano ligand attached to the cobalt center, introduced during manufacturing.
