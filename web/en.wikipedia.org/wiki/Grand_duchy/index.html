Grand duchy

A grand duchy is a country or territory whose head of state or ruler is a monarch holding the title of grand duke or grand duchess. The title ranks in honour below a king or queen and above a sovereign duke or prince. Grand duchies were especially numerous within the Holy Roman Empire's successor states and the German Confederation in the 19th century.

History

The title of grand duke was first prominently used in Tuscany in 1569. Many grand duchies were created during and after the Napoleonic Wars — including Baden, Hesse, Mecklenburg-Schwerin, Mecklenburg-Strelitz, Oldenburg and Saxe-Weimar-Eisenach — most of which became constituent states of the German Empire in 1871 and were abolished as monarchies in 1918.

Present day

The only remaining sovereign grand duchy is Luxembourg, ruled since 2000 by Grand Duke Henri. The rulers of Liechtenstein and Monaco are princes, and Andorra is a co-principality, so Luxembourg stands alone in preserving the grand-ducal form.
