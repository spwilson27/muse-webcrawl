Decline of Greco-Roman polytheism

The decline of Greco-Roman polytheism describes the gradual disappearance of the traditional religions of the classical world between the 3rd and 6th centuries AD, as Christianity spread and gained imperial favour.

Constantine's conversion and the Edict of Milan (313) legalised Christianity; later emperors, especially Theodosius I, made Nicene Christianity the state religion and prohibited pagan sacrifices in the 390s. Temples were closed or converted, the Olympic Games were suppressed, and the Academy of Athens was shut under Justinian in 529. Pagan practices nevertheless survived for centuries in rural areas — hence the word "pagan", from paganus, "country-dweller".
