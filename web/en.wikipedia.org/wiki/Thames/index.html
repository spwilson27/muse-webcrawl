Thames

The Thames is a river flowing through southern England, including London, to the North Sea. At 346 kilometres it is the longest river entirely in England and the second-longest in the United Kingdom after the Severn. It rises in the Cotswolds at Thames Head in Gloucestershire and flows east through Oxford, Reading, Windsor and London.

The Thames has been London's artery since Roman times: a working river of docks and wharves, bridged by Tower Bridge and London Bridge, bordered by the Palace of Westminster and the Tower of London. Declared biologically dead in the 1950s, it recovered after sewage reforms and today supports seals, seahorses and over 100 fish species. The Thames Barrier, completed in 1982, protects London from storm-surge flooding. 'Old Father Thames' is celebrated in literature from Spenser to T.S. Eliot.
