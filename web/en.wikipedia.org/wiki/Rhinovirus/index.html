Rhinovirus

Rhinoviruses are the most common viral infectious agents in humans and the predominant cause of the common cold. They are small, positive-sense, single-stranded RNA viruses in the genus Enterovirus of the family Picornaviridae. More than 160 types have been classified into three species: rhinovirus A, B, and C.

Structure and transmission

The rhinovirus particle is an icosahedral capsid about 30 nanometres across, without an envelope. Infection is spread through respiratory droplets, direct contact, and contaminated surfaces. The virus replicates preferentially at the slightly cooler temperatures of the nasal passages and binds to cell receptors such as ICAM-1 (for most types), LDL receptor family members, or CDHR3 (rhinovirus C).

Clinical significance

Besides colds, rhinoviruses trigger exacerbations of asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. The large number of antigenically distinct types has made vaccine development impractical, and treatment remains symptomatic.
