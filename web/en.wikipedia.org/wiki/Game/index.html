Game

A game is a structured form of play, usually undertaken for entertainment or fun, and sometimes used as an educational tool. Games are distinct from work, which is usually carried out for remuneration, and from art, which is more often an expression of aesthetic or ideological elements. However, the distinction is not clear-cut, and many games are also considered to be work (such as professional sports) or art (such as jigsaw puzzles).

Definitions

Thinkers have offered many definitions. Johan Huizinga, in Homo Ludens (1938), characterised play as a free activity standing outside ordinary life within a "magic circle" of its own rules and order. Roger Caillois classified games into competition (agon), chance (alea), mimicry and vertigo (ilinx). Philosopher Bernard Suits defined game-playing as "the voluntary attempt to overcome unnecessary obstacles". Game designer Chris Crawford emphasised interactivity, goals, and conflict with an opponent or obstacle.

Components

Common elements of games include goals that players work to achieve, rules that define what is permitted, challenge in the form of opponents, puzzles or chance, and interactivity — the players' actions affect the outcome. Games may be skill-based, strategy-based or luck-based, and are played alone (solitaire, puzzles), in teams, or competitively between individuals.

Types

Major categories include tabletop games (board games, card games, dice games), video games, role-playing games, sports (often classed as games when amateur), children's games such as tag and hide-and-seek, and games of chance such as lotteries. The mathematical discipline of game theory studies formal models of strategic interaction, using "game" in a broader technical sense.

History

Games are among the oldest forms of human social interaction: the Royal Game of Ur and the Egyptian game Senet date to the third millennium BC, and dice and knucklebones are older still. Chess evolved in India by the 6th century AD. Organised sports were codified in the 19th century, and electronic games emerged in the mid-20th century, becoming a major global industry.
