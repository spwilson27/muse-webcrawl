Bag-of-words model

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A document converted into a histogram of word counts, word order discarded]

The bag-of-words model is a simplifying representation used in natural language processing and information retrieval. A text is represented as the multiset (bag) of its words, disregarding grammar and word order but keeping multiplicity — in effect, a vector of word counts or frequencies.

Example

The sentences "John likes to watch movies" and "Mary likes movies too" are represented over a vocabulary as vectors of counts for each word; the order in which words appeared is lost, so "dog bites man" and "man bites dog" produce the same representation.

Applications

Bag-of-words vectors underlie classic document classification, spam filtering and information retrieval systems, often weighted by tf–idf to down-weight common words. Related methods use bags of n-grams to retain short-range word order. In computer vision, the analogous bag-of-visual-words model represents images by histograms of local feature descriptors.

Limitations

The model ignores syntax and semantics: it cannot capture negation, word sense, or context. These limitations motivated distributed representations such as word embeddings, though bag-of-words remains a strong, simple baseline.
