Skidmore, Owings & Merrill LLP (SOM) is an American architectural, urban planning, and engineering firm founded in Chicago in 1936 by Louis Skidmore and Nathaniel Owings, joined in 1939 by engineer John O. Merrill. It is one of the largest and most influential architecture firms in the world and a pioneer of modernist high-rise design.

History

SOM's early fame came with Lever House in New York (1952), designed under Gordon Bunshaft — a glass curtain-wall slab that set the template for postwar corporate architecture. The firm organized itself as a partnership of autonomous studios bound by shared standards, and became known for integrating architecture with structural engineering, collaborating with engineers such as Fazlur Rahman Khan, inventor of the tubular structural systems used in the John Hancock Center and Willis (Sears) Tower in Chicago.

Major works

  • Lever House, New York (1952)
  • United States Air Force Academy, Colorado Springs (1962)
  • John Hancock Center, Chicago (1970)
  • Willis Tower (Sears Tower), Chicago (1973)
  • Jin Mao Tower, Shanghai (1999)
  • Burj Khalifa, Dubai (2010) — the world's tallest building
  • One World Trade Center, New York (2014)

Legacy

SOM was the first recipient of the AIA Architecture Firm Award twice (1962 and 1996) and has shaped the skylines of cities worldwide, alongside extensive work in master planning and sustainable design.
