n-Butyllithium

Chemical formulaC4H9Li
Molar mass64.06 g·mol−1
AppearanceColourless to pale yellow solution (usually in hexanes)
HazardsPyrophoric; reacts violently with water

n-Butyllithium (abbreviated n-BuLi) is an organolithium compound with the formula C4H9Li. It is widely used as a strong base and nucleophile in organic synthesis, and as a polymerisation initiator in the production of elastomers such as polybutadiene and styrene-butadiene rubber. It is the most commonly used of the three commercial butyllithium isomers, the others being sec-butyllithium and tert-butyllithium.

n-Butyllithium is supplied as a solution in hydrocarbon solvents such as hexanes.

Preparation

n-BuLi is prepared industrially by the reaction of 1-chlorobutane (or 1-bromobutane) with lithium metal dispersion in a hydrocarbon solvent. The product is standardised by titration before use, as its concentration decreases over time.

Structure

Like most organolithium compounds, n-BuLi is aggregated: it exists as a tetramer in hydrocarbon solvents and as tetramers or hexamers in ethers depending on conditions. Coordination by donor solvents and additives (such as TMEDA) deaggregates it and increases its reactivity.

Reactions

n-BuLi is a very strong base (the pKa of its conjugate acid, butane, is about 50) and deprotonates a wide range of weak acids, including terminal alkynes, arenes (directed ortho metalation), and heteroatom-stabilised positions. It undergoes lithium–halogen exchange with aryl and vinyl halides, transfers its butyl group to carbonyl compounds as a nucleophile, and initiates anionic polymerisation of dienes and styrene.

Safety

Concentrated solutions are pyrophoric, igniting on contact with air, and react violently with water and protic solvents, liberating flammable butane gas. Handling requires inert-atmosphere techniques such as Schlenk lines or gloveboxes.
