Conn Smythe Trophy

The Conn Smythe Trophy is awarded annually by the National Hockey League (NHL) to the most valuable player (MVP) of the Stanley Cup playoffs. It is named after Conn Smythe, the longtime owner, general manager, and coach of the Toronto Maple Leafs. The trophy was first awarded at the conclusion of the 1965 playoffs, when it was won by Jean Béliveau of the Montreal Canadiens — who was also the first winner whose team captured the Stanley Cup.

Selection

The winner is chosen in a vote by members of the Professional Hockey Writers' Association, with ballots cast during the deciding game of the Stanley Cup Final. Unlike most NHL awards, which are based on regular-season play, the Conn Smythe honours performance across the entire postseason. It is presented by the NHL commissioner immediately after the final game, before the Stanley Cup itself is awarded.

Notable winners

Goaltender Patrick Roy is the only three-time winner (1986, 1993, 2001). Wayne Gretzky, Mario Lemieux, Bobby Orr, Bernie Parent, and Sidney Crosby have each won it twice. A small number of winners have come from the team that lost the Final — including Jean-Sébastien Giguère (2003) and Connor McDavid (2024), only the second skater so honoured after Reggie Leach (1976). Both American-born and European-born players have won it, and the trophy is regarded as one of the most prestigious individual honours in hockey because it recognises performance when the championship is at stake.
