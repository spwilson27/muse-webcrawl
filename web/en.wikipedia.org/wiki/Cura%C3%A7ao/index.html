Curaçao is a Lesser Antilles island country in the southern Caribbean Sea, off the coast of Venezuela, and a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. Together with Aruba and Bonaire it forms the ABC islands. Its capital, Willemstad, is famous for the colourful Dutch colonial architecture of its waterfront, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The island was originally inhabited by Arawak peoples. Spain claimed it after 1499, and the Dutch West India Company seized it in 1634, developing it into a major centre of the Atlantic slave trade and commerce. Slavery was abolished in 1863. In the 20th century, oil refining of Venezuelan crude transformed the economy. Curaçao was part of the Netherlands Antilles until that country's dissolution on 10 October 2010, when Curaçao became a separate constituent country within the Kingdom.

Geography and climate

Curaçao has an arid savanna climate with a long dry season; lying outside the main hurricane belt, it is rarely hit by tropical storms. The landscape features cactus scrub, salt flats with flamingos, and coral-fringed bays. Mount Christoffel, at 372 metres, is the highest point.

Culture

Most Curaçaoans speak Papiamento, a Portuguese- and Spanish-based creole with Dutch and African influences, alongside Dutch (the language of administration and education). The island is known for its blue liqueur made from the peels of the laraha citrus fruit, its diving tourism, and a musical tradition including tumba, the official music of Carnival.
