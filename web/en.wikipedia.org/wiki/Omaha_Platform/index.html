Omaha Platform

The Omaha Platform was the party program adopted at the founding convention of the People's Party (Populists) held in Omaha, Nebraska, on 4 July 1892. Written largely by Ignatius L. Donnelly, its famous preamble denounced the corruption and inequality of the two major parties and declared that "the fruits of the toil of millions are boldly stolen to build up colossal fortunes for a few".

Demands

The platform called for the free and unlimited coinage of silver at a ratio of 16 to 1 with gold; a graduated income tax; government ownership of the railroads, telegraph, and telephone systems; a sub-treasury plan to provide low-cost credit to farmers; postal savings banks; the direct election of United States senators; the secret ballot; and the initiative and referendum. It also expressed sympathy with organised labour and demanded a shorter working day.

Legacy

Many of the Omaha Platform's demands were later enacted into law in the Progressive era and beyond, including the income tax (Sixteenth Amendment), direct election of senators (Seventeenth Amendment), and increased regulation and public ownership functions in transportation and finance.
