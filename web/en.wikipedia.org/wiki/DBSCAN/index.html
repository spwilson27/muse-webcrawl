DBSCAN (Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise) is a data clustering algorithm proposed by Martin Ester, Hans-Peter Kriegel, Jörg Sander and Xiaowei Xu in 1996. It groups together points that are closely packed in regions of high density, and marks points in low-density regions as outliers (noise). Unlike k-means, DBSCAN does not require the number of clusters to be specified in advance and can find arbitrarily shaped clusters.

The algorithm

DBSCAN uses two parameters: a radius ε (eps) and a minimum number of points minPts. A point is a core point if at least minPts points lie within distance ε of it. A point within ε of a core point but lacking enough neighbours of its own is a border point; all other points are noise. Clusters are formed by connecting core points that are within ε of each other (density-connectedness) together with their border points. The algorithm visits each point, expanding clusters from unvisited core points until all points have been classified.

Properties

DBSCAN runs in average time O(n log n) with a spatial index, or O(n²) without. Its strengths are discovering clusters of arbitrary shape, robustness to outliers, and not requiring a preset cluster count. Its weaknesses include difficulty with clusters of widely varying densities, sensitivity to the choice of ε and minPts, and degraded behaviour in very high-dimensional data where distance becomes less meaningful. Common rules of thumb suggest minPts ≥ dimensionality + 1 and selecting ε from a k-distance graph.

Variants and extensions

Well-known extensions include OPTICS, which produces a hierarchical ordering that handles varying densities, and HDBSCAN, which builds a density hierarchy and extracts stable clusters. The original paper received the 2014 SIGKDD Test of Time Award, and DBSCAN is implemented in libraries such as scikit-learn, ELKI and R packages.
