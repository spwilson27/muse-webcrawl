The 7th Cavalry Regiment is a United States Army cavalry regiment formed in 1866, shortly after the American Civil War. It is one of the most famous — and controversial — regiments in American history, associated both with the disaster at the Little Bighorn and with distinguished service in later wars.

Indian Wars

Under Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer, the 7th Cavalry fought in campaigns against the Plains tribes, including the attack on the Cheyenne village at the Washita River (1868). On 25 June 1876, at the Battle of the Little Bighorn, Custer and five companies — some 210 men — were annihilated by Lakota, Northern Cheyenne and Arapaho forces, the most famous US defeat of the Indian Wars. In December 1890 troops of the regiment were responsible for the Wounded Knee Massacre, in which hundreds of Lakota, many of them unarmed, were killed.

Later service

The regiment patrolled the Mexican border and served in the Second World War in the Pacific. In Korea it was implicated in the No Gun Ri massacre of 1950. Its 1st Battalion fought the first major battle of US ground forces in Vietnam, at Ia Drang in November 1965 — a story told in the book and film We Were Soldiers. Elements of the regiment later served in the Gulf War and the Iraq War, where its squadrons conducted armoured reconnaissance.
