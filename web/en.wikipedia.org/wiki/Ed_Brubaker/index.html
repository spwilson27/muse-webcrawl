Ed Brubaker (born November 17, 1966) is an American comic book writer and cartoonist known for his work in the crime and superhero genres. He is one of the most acclaimed writers in modern comics, having won multiple Eisner and Harvey Awards. His long collaborations with artist Sean Phillips on creator-owned crime series such as Criminal and Fatale are regarded as defining works of the modern crime comic.

Career

Brubaker began in the late 1980s drawing and writing semi-autobiographical and crime stories for independent publishers, including Lowlife. He moved into mainstream comics at DC, writing Batman, Detective Comics and the police procedural Gotham Central (with Greg Rucka), and co-created the espionage series Sleeper with Sean Phillips at WildStorm.

At Marvel, his run on Captain America beginning in 2005 relaunched the title as a spy thriller and famously resurrected Bucky Barnes as the Winter Soldier, a storyline later adapted in the film Captain America: The Winter Soldier. He also wrote Daredevil, Uncanny X-Men and The Immortal Iron Fist with Matt Fraction.

Creator-owned work

Through Image Comics, Brubaker and Phillips produced a series of acclaimed crime titles: Criminal, Incognito, the horror-noir Fatale, the Hollywood noir The Fade Out, Kill or Be Killed, and the Reckless series of original graphic novels. Brubaker also moved into television, working on HBO's Westworld and developing adaptations of his own comics.
