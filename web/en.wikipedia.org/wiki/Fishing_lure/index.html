Fishing lure

A fishing lure is a type of artificial fishing bait which is designed to attract the attention of predatory fish, using prey-like appearances, movements, bright colours, and flashy reflections. The lure is equipped with one or more hooks that snag the fish when it attacks. Lures are used in place of live bait because they can be reused, stored easily, and tailored to specific species and conditions.

History

Artificial lures have ancient origins: bone and bronze fish-shaped lures were used in China and Egypt, and the Macedonians were reported to use artificial flies two thousand years ago. Commercial lure manufacture began in the mid-nineteenth century in the United States, with makers such as James Heddon and the Pflueger company producing carved wooden minnows and metal spoons. Modern lures are made from wood, plastic, metal, rubber and composite materials.

Types

Spoons — curved metal lures that wobble and flash when retrieved, imitating an injured baitfish.

Plugs (crankbaits) — hard-bodied lures, often with a lip that makes them dive and wiggle on the retrieve.

Spinners and spinnerbaits — lures with rotating blades that produce flash and vibration.

Jigs — weighted hooks, often dressed with soft plastic bodies, fished with a lifting motion near the bottom.

Soft plastic baits — flexible imitations of worms, grubs, crayfish and baitfish, rigged in many ways including Texas and Carolina rigs.

Topwater lures — floating lures such as poppers and walkers that disturb the surface.

Flies — lightweight lures of thread, feather and fur used in fly fishing.

Technique

Lures are cast with a fishing rod and retrieved at varying speeds and with pauses, twitches or jerks to provoke a strike. Colour, size and action are selected to match local forage and water clarity.
