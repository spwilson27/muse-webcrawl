François Jacob

Born17 June 1920, Nancy, France
Died19 April 2013, Paris, France
Known forOperon model of gene regulation
AwardsNobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (1965)

François Jacob (17 June 1920 – 19 April 2013) was a French biologist who, together with Jacques Monod, demonstrated how the levels of certain enzymes in cells are controlled by regulation of transcription.

Research

Working at the Pasteur Institute, Jacob and Monod proposed the operon model in 1961, explaining how genes in bacteria are switched on and off. Their work, along with contributions from André Lwoff on lysogeny, earned them the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1965, shared with Lwoff.

Life

Jacob served with the Free French Forces during the Second World War and was severely wounded in Normandy. His autobiography, The Statue Within (1987), recounts both his wartime experience and his scientific career.
