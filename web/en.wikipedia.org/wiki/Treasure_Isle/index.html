Treasure Isle is a Jamaican record label founded by Arthur "Duke" Reid in 1962. Named after Reid's liquor store and bar at 33 Bond Street in Kingston, the label became one of the most influential in Jamaican music, central to the development of ska and especially rocksteady in the mid-to-late 1960s.

History

Duke Reid, a former policeman, had operated one of Jamaica's most powerful sound systems, "Duke Reid the Trojan", before entering record production. Treasure Isle initially released ska recordings by artists such as the Skatalites, Stranger Cole, and the Techniques. The label's house band included musicians drawn from the Skatalites after that group's breakup.

Rocksteady era

Treasure Isle is most celebrated for its rocksteady productions of 1966–1968, engineered by Reid's nephew (?) — recordings were made at the Treasure Isle studio with Duke Reid producing and, famously, Alton Ellis, the Paragons, the Melodians, Phyllis Dillon and the Techniques as leading artists. Signature hits included Alton Ellis's "Rocksteady" and "Girl I've Got a Date", the Paragons' "The Tide Is High" (later a hit for Blondie), and Phyllis Dillon's "Don't Stay Away". The slower, bass-led rocksteady sound produced at Treasure Isle bridged ska and reggae.

Legacy

Reid died in 1975, and the Treasure Isle catalogue passed through various owners; the studio masters remain highly prized, and the label's recordings have been extensively reissued. Treasure Isle's output is regarded as defining the rocksteady genre and a foundation of modern Jamaican music.
