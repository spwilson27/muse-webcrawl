Hudson Yards

Hudson Yards is a real estate development on the Far West Side of Manhattan, New York City, built largely on platforms over the West Side rail yards between 30th and 33rd Streets. It is one of the largest private real estate developments in United States history.

Development

The project, developed primarily by Related Companies and Oxford Properties, was enabled by the 2005 rezoning of the Hudson Yards area and the extension of the New York City Subway's 7 train to the 34th Street–Hudson Yards station, which opened in 2015. The eastern phase of the development opened in March 2019.

Buildings and attractions

Major structures include the office towers 10, 15, 30, 35, 50, and 55 Hudson Yards; 30 Hudson Yards hosts the Edge, a triangular sky-deck observation platform. The development also contains The Shops & Restaurants retail complex, the cultural venue The Shed, the Equinox Hotel, and the Vessel, a climbable honeycomb-like sculpture by Thomas Heatherwick that serves as its public centrepiece.

Criticism

Hudson Yards has been criticised for its scale of public subsidy, its luxury orientation, and its architecture, while supporters credit it with creating a new commercial district and funding the subway extension.
