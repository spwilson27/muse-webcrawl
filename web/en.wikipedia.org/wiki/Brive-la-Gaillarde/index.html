Brive-la-Gaillarde (Occitan: Briva la Galharda) is a commune of France. It is a sub-prefecture and the largest city of the Corrèze department. It has around 46,000 inhabitants, while the population of the urban area is around 75,000.

Geography

The city lies on the river Corrèze just east of its confluence with the Vézère, on the border between the sedimentary basin of Aquitaine to the west and the uplands of the Massif Central to the east. The name comes from the Gaulish briva (bridge) and gaillarde ("vigorous" or "valiant").

History

Brive began as a crossing point of the Corrèze, developing around a bridge on the ancient road between Lyon and Bordeaux. In the Middle Ages it was a fortified town contested between local lords and the kings of France. During the Second World War, Brive was a notable centre of the French Resistance; it was liberated in August 1944 largely by the action of its own resistance fighters before Allied troops arrived.

Culture and sport

The city is known for its rugby union club, CA Brive, which won the European Cup in 1997. Brive hosts a well-known book fair, the Foire du Livre de Brive, held each autumn, and is a centre of the walnut and foie gras trade of the surrounding countryside.
