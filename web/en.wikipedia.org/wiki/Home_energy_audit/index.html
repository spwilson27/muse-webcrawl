Home energy audit

A home energy audit (also called a home energy assessment) is an evaluation of the energy efficiency of a residence, performed to identify how energy is consumed and where it is wasted, and to recommend cost-effective improvements. Audits range from simple walk-through inspections and utility-bill analysis to comprehensive diagnostic assessments performed by certified professionals using specialized equipment.

Methods

A professional audit typically includes:

  • Blower-door test — a calibrated fan temporarily sealed into an exterior doorway depressurizes the house, revealing the rate of air leakage through the building envelope and pinpointing drafts and gaps.
  • Thermographic inspection — infrared cameras visualize temperature differences across walls, ceilings, and windows, indicating missing insulation, thermal bridges, and air leaks.
  • Assessment of systems — evaluation of heating and cooling equipment, water heating, duct leakage (sometimes with a duct blaster test), lighting, appliances, and insulation levels in attics, walls, and basements.
  • Utility bill analysis and, in detailed audits, computer modeling that ranks recommended measures by projected payback.

Outcomes

Common recommendations arising from audits include air sealing, adding insulation, sealing and insulating ducts, upgrading heating and cooling systems, installing programmable or smart thermostats, and replacing incandescent lighting. In the United States, audits are often prerequisites for weatherization assistance and for utility or government incentive programs; standardized rating systems such as the Home Energy Rating System (HERS) Index, developed by RESNET, express a home's modeled efficiency against a reference home.
