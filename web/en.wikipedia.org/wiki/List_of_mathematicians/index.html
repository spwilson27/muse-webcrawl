List of mathematicians

A mathematician is someone who uses an extensive knowledge of mathematics in their work, typically to solve mathematical problems. This article provides a list of notable mathematicians by century.

Euclid's Elements is the foundational text of geometry.

Antiquity

  • Thales of Miletus (c. 624–546 BCE) — early Greek geometry.
  • Pythagoras (c. 570–495 BCE) — Pythagorean theorem.
  • Euclid (c. 300 BCE) — author of the Elements.
  • Archimedes (c. 287–212 BCE) — anticipations of calculus, areas and volumes.
  • Apollonius of Perga — conic sections.
  • Diophantus — founder of algebra.
  • Hypatia (c. 360–415) — Alexandrine scholar and commentator.
  • Aryabhata (476–550) — Indian astronomy and mathematics.
  • Brahmagupta (598–668) — rules for zero and negative numbers.

Middle Ages

  • Al-Khwarizmi (c. 780–850) — father of algebra; origin of the word "algorithm".
  • Omar Khayyam (1048–1131) — geometric solutions of cubic equations.
  • Fibonacci (c. 1170–1250) — introduced Hindu–Arabic numerals to Europe.
  • Madhava of Sangamagrama (c. 1340–1425) — infinite series for π and trigonometric functions.

16th–18th centuries

  • Gerolamo Cardano, Niccolò Tartaglia — solutions of cubic and quartic equations.
  • René Descartes — analytic geometry.
  • Pierre de Fermat — number theory, Fermat's Last Theorem.
  • Blaise Pascal — probability theory, Pascal's triangle.
  • Isaac Newton and Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz — calculus.
  • Leonhard Euler — prolific across analysis, graph theory and notation.
  • Joseph-Louis Lagrange, Pierre-Simon Laplace — analytical mechanics and probability.
  • Émilie du Châtelet — translation and commentary on Newton.
  • Maria Gaetana Agnesi — calculus textbook and the "witch of Agnesi".

19th century

  • Carl Friedrich Gauss — "Prince of Mathematicians"; number theory, statistics, geometry.
  • Augustin-Louis Cauchy — rigorous analysis.
  • Niels Henrik Abel, Évariste Galois — group theory and solvability of equations.
  • Ada Lovelace — first computer algorithm, for Babbage's Analytical Engine.
  • Bernhard Riemann — Riemann hypothesis, Riemannian geometry.
  • Georg Cantor — set theory and transfinite numbers.
  • Sophie Germain — elasticity and Fermat's Last Theorem.
  • Sofia Kovalevskaya — partial differential equations.
  • David Hilbert — Hilbert's problems of 1900.
  • Srinivasa Ramanujan — extraordinary results in analysis and number theory.

20th–21st centuries

  • Emmy Noether — abstract algebra and Noether's theorem.
  • Kurt Gödel — incompleteness theorems.
  • Alan Turing — computability and the Turing machine.
  • John von Neumann — game theory, functional analysis, computing.
  • Paul Erdős — immensely prolific collaborator in combinatorics and number theory.
  • Andrew Wiles — proof of Fermat's Last Theorem (1995).
  • Grigori Perelman — proof of the Poincaré conjecture.
  • Terence Tao — Fields medallist across analysis and combinatorics.
  • Maryam Mirzakhani — first woman to win the Fields Medal (2014).
  • Karen Uhlenbeck — first woman to win the Abel Prize (2019).
