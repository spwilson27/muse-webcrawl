Prajapati

Prajapati (Sanskrit: प्रजापति, Prajāpati, "lord of creatures" or "lord of progeny") is a Vedic deity of Hinduism. In the late Vedic texts he emerges as the supreme creator god, the progenitor from whom all beings arise. In later Hindu literature, Prajapati becomes identified with the creator god Brahma, and the term is also applied as a title to a group of progenitors of humanity, the mind-born sons of Brahma.

In the Vedas

Prajapati appears in the later hymns of the Rigveda, most famously in the Hiranyagarbha Sukta (Rigveda 10.121), where the golden embryo that arose in the beginning is identified at the close as Prajapati, "the god above whom there is no other." In the Brahmanas, he becomes the central figure of cosmogony: alone in the beginning, he desires offspring, performs austerities (tapas), and emits creation from himself. His body is identified with the sacrifice and with the year, and the rituals of the Brahmanas repeatedly reconstitute his dismembered form.

Later usage

In epic and Puranic texts, "Prajapati" is a title borne by various progenitors — including Daksha, Kashyapa, and the ten mind-born sons of Brahma such as Marichi, Atri, Angiras, and Narada — who are charged with populating the world. The Manusmriti describes Brahma creating the Prajapatis to continue the work of creation. In this sense the word functions less as a single personal god than as an office: a lord of generation standing between the supreme creator and created beings.
