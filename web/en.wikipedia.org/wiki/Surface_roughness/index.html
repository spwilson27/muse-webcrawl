Surface roughness

Surface roughness, often shortened to roughness, is a component of surface texture defined as the small surface deviations from the nominal surface, usually resulting from the inherent action of the production process used to make the part. It is quantified by the deviations in the direction of the normal vector of a real surface from its ideal form: large deviations mean the surface is rough, and small deviations mean it is smooth.

Parameters

The most widely used roughness parameter in engineering is Ra, the arithmetic average of the absolute values of the profile height deviations from the mean line over an evaluation length. Other common parameters include Rq (root mean square roughness), Rz (maximum height of the profile), and the areal parameters Sa and Sq defined in ISO 25178.

Effects

Roughness plays an important role in determining how an object interacts with its environment. Rough surfaces usually wear more quickly and have higher friction coefficients than smooth ones; roughness is also often a good predictor of mechanical performance, and it affects phenomena such as hydrodynamic lubrication, contact mechanics, and corrosion initiation.
