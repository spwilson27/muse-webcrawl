Net energy analysis

Net energy analysis is a method for evaluating energy technologies by comparing the energy a facility or process delivers over its lifetime with all the energy consumed in constructing, operating, fuelling and decommissioning it. Only the surplus — the net energy — is available to do useful work for society.

Metrics

The most common measure is energy return on investment (EROI or EROEI), the ratio of energy delivered to energy invested. A related measure is energy payback time, the period a system must operate before it has "repaid" the energy used to make it. Net energy analysis was developed in the 1970s by researchers such as Howard T. Odum and Charles A. S. Hall, drawing on the field of energy economics and systems ecology.

Applications

The method has been applied to fossil fuels, nuclear power, photovoltaics, wind power and biofuels. Analysts note that declining EROI of conventional oil and gas raises the share of an energy system's output consumed by the energy sector itself; the "net energy cliff" describes how useful surplus falls steeply as EROI approaches 1:1.
