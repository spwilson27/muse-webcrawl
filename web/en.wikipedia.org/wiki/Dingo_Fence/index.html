Dingo Fence

Length5,614 km (3,488 mi)
LocationSouth-eastern Australia
Construction began1880s
PurposeExclusion of dingoes from sheep country

The Dingo Fence, also known as the Dog Fence, is a pest-exclusion fence in Australia built to keep dingoes out of the relatively fertile sheep-grazing country of the south-east of the continent. At about 5,614 kilometres (3,488 mi) long, it is one of the longest structures in the world.

History

The fence originated in the 1880s when individual pastoralists and state governments erected barriers against dingoes, rabbits and other pests. Over time the separate fences in Queensland, New South Wales and South Australia were joined into a single continuous barrier maintained by the states.

Route

The fence runs from the Darling Downs in Queensland westward and southward across the Strzelecki Desert, ending at the cliffs of the Nullarbor Plain on the Great Australian Bight coast in South Australia. It crosses some of the most remote country in Australia.

Maintenance

Boundary riders patrol sections of the fence, repairing damage caused by floods, camels, kangaroos and erosion. Parts of the fence are electrified, and its effectiveness remains a subject of ecological debate, since excluding dingoes can increase populations of kangaroos and emus inside the fence.
