Volkshalle


Model of the planned Volkshalle in Germania

The Volkshalle ("People's Hall"), also called the Große Halle ("Great Hall"), was a huge domed monumental building planned by Adolf Hitler and his architect Albert Speer for Germania, the projected renewal of the German capital Berlin. It was never built.

Design

Inspired by Hadrian's Pantheon in Rome, the hall was to be a vast assembly space for up to 180,000 people beneath a coffered dome about 290 metres high and 250 metres in diameter—so large, Speer later remarked, that clouds and rain might form inside. Its granite podium and portico were to face a grand north–south axis and parade square.

Context

The Volkshalle was the centrepiece of the Welthauptstadt Germania plans, alongside the expanded Reich Chancellery and a colossal triumphal arch. Speer reportedly worried the swastika-holding eagle atop the dome symbolised Nazi ambitions of world domination. Foundation tests, including the Schwerbelastungskörper concrete cylinder, were built in Berlin, but construction of the hall itself never began before the Second World War ended the project.
