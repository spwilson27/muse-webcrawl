Breast cancer awareness

The pink ribbon is the international symbol of breast cancer awareness.

Breast cancer awareness is an effort to raise awareness and reduce the stigma of breast cancer through education on symptoms and treatment. Supporters hope that greater knowledge will lead to earlier detection, which is associated with higher long-term survival rates, and that money raised for research will produce a reliable, permanent cure. The pink ribbon is the best-known symbol of the movement, and October is observed in many countries as Breast Cancer Awareness Month.

History

The modern movement grew out of the women's health movement and the activism of patients and survivors in the 1970s and 1980s, when treatments such as radical mastectomy were openly challenged. National Breast Cancer Awareness Month was founded in 1985. The pink ribbon was popularised in the early 1990s, notably by the Susan G. Komen Foundation's races and by a Self magazine–Estée Lauder campaign.

Criticism

Awareness campaigns have been criticised for "pinkwashing" — companies using the pink ribbon to market products while contributing little, or products that themselves contain carcinogenic ingredients — and for emphasising awareness and screening over prevention, treatment access, and the needs of patients with metastatic disease.
