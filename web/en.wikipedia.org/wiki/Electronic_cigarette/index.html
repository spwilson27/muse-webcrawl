Electronic cigarette

An electronic cigarette (e-cigarette), or vape, is a device that simulates smoking. It consists of an atomizer, a power source such as a battery, and a container such as a cartridge or tank. Instead of smoke, the user inhales vapor. Using an e-cigarette is known as "vaping". The atomizer is a heating element that vaporizes a liquid solution called e-liquid, which usually contains propylene glycol, vegetable glycerin, flavorings, and often—but not always—nicotine.

History

Early concepts date to the 1960s, but the modern e-cigarette is credited to Chinese pharmacist Hon Lik, who developed a device in 2003 after his father died of lung cancer; it was marketed internationally by the company Ruyan. E-cigarettes spread to Europe and the United States in the mid-2000s. Subsequent generations moved from cigarette-like "cigalikes" to refillable tank systems and, in the mid-2010s, to compact pod systems using nicotine salts—most prominently Juul—which achieved wide popularity, particularly among young people, prompting regulatory and public-health concern.

Health effects

E-cigarette aerosol generally contains fewer toxic chemicals than cigarette smoke, and public-health bodies in the United Kingdom have regarded vaping as substantially less harmful than smoking, while emphasizing that it is not risk-free. The long-term health effects remain uncertain because the products have not been in widespread use for long enough. Concerns include nicotine addiction, cardiovascular and respiratory effects, and the 2019 outbreak of e-cigarette or vaping product use-associated lung injury (EVALI), which was strongly linked to vitamin E acetate in illicit THC cartridges.

Regulation

Regulation varies widely: some countries treat e-cigarettes as tobacco products or medicines, others ban sales outright, and many restrict flavors and advertising. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration regulates them as tobacco products and requires premarket authorization. Many jurisdictions set a minimum sales age of 18 or 21 and prohibit use in smoke-free places.
