Saint Margaret of Scotland

Saint Margaret of Scotland (c. 1045 – 16 November 1093), also known as Margaret of Wessex, was an English princess and a Scottish queen. She was the daughter of Edward the Exile and granddaughter of Edmund Ironside, King of the English. After the Norman Conquest, her family fled to Scotland, where she married King Malcolm III around 1069. Renowned for her piety and charity, she reformed the Scottish church, promoted Benedictine monasticism and was canonised in 1250. She is Scotland's most famous female royal saint.

Life

Margaret was born in Hungary, where her exiled father Edward had settled, and came to England as a child in 1057. After the Battle of Hastings in 1066 her brother Edgar the Ætheling was proclaimed but never crowned king, and the family took refuge at the court of Malcolm III in Scotland. Her marriage to Malcolm united the Scottish crown with the old English royal line. Their children included three kings of Scotland — Edgar, Alexander I and David I — and Edith, renamed Matilda, who married Henry I of England.

Religious influence

Her confessor Turgot wrote her biography, the Life of St Margaret, at the request of her daughter. Margaret encouraged the Benedictines, invited monks to found Dunfermline Abbey, restored Iona Abbey, established the ferry across the Firth of Forth for pilgrims to St Andrews (commemorated in North and South Queensferry), and pressed the Scottish church to align its practices — including the observance of Lent and Easter and Sunday rest — with those of Rome. She was famous for personal austerity, prayer, feeding orphans and washing the feet of the poor.

Death and legacy

Margaret died at Edinburgh Castle on 16 November 1093, three days after learning that Malcolm and their son Edward had been killed at the Battle of Alnwick. She was buried at Dunfermline Abbey; her shrine there became a pilgrimage centre, and her remains were later partly transferred to the Escorial in Spain. St Margaret's Chapel in Edinburgh Castle is traditionally dated to her son David I and dedicated to her. She was declared patroness of Scotland in 1673.
