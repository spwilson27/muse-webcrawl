Descending aorta

The descending aorta is part of the aorta, the largest artery in the human body. It begins at the aortic arch and runs downward through the chest and abdomen, ending where it splits into the two common iliac arteries at about the level of the fourth lumbar vertebra. The descending aorta is divided into two portions: the thoracic aorta above the diaphragm and the abdominal aorta below it.

Structure

Thoracic aorta

The thoracic aorta lies in the posterior mediastinum. It begins at the lower border of the fourth thoracic vertebra, where it is continuous with the aortic arch, and ends at the aortic hiatus of the diaphragm at the level of the twelfth thoracic vertebra. Along its course it gives off bronchial arteries to the lungs, oesophageal arteries, posterior intercostal arteries to the chest wall, and branches to the pericardium and mediastinum.

Abdominal aorta

Below the diaphragm the vessel continues as the abdominal aorta, running slightly to the left of the midline in front of the lumbar vertebrae. Its major branches include the coeliac trunk, the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries, the renal arteries, and the gonadal arteries. It terminates by dividing into the right and left common iliac arteries.

Clinical significance

The descending aorta is a common site of atherosclerosis and of aneurysms, particularly in its abdominal segment below the renal arteries. Aortic dissection often involves the descending aorta; dissections confined to it (Stanford type B) are frequently managed medically or with endovascular stent grafts rather than open surgery.
