Ahmose-Nefertari

Consort: Ahmose I
Dynasty: 17th/18th Dynasty of Egypt
Burial: Dra' Abu el-Naga'; mummy later moved to the Deir el-Bahari cache

Ahmose-Nefertari was an ancient Egyptian queen of the late 17th and early 18th Dynasties, the wife (and probably full sister) of Pharaoh Ahmose I, founder of the New Kingdom, and the mother of Amenhotep I. She was one of the most influential royal women of Egyptian history and was deified after her death.

Life and titles

Ahmose-Nefertari was the daughter of Seqenenre Tao and Queen Ahhotep I. She held the powerful religious office of God's Wife of Amun, which she endowed with lands and goods in a famous donation stela, making it a hereditary office of great wealth and influence. She also bore titles including Great Royal Wife and King's Mother, and she appears to have acted as regent for her young son Amenhotep I. The workmen's village at Deir el-Medina worshipped her and Amenhotep I as patron deities for centuries.

Death and legacy

She lived to an advanced age, probably into the reign of Thutmose I. Her mummy was found in the royal cache at Deir el-Bahari (DB/TT 320) in 1881 and is now regarded as one of the identified royal mummies of Egypt. In tomb paintings she is sometimes depicted with dark skin, which has generated scholarly debate about its symbolic meaning.
