Isotretinoin

Trade namesAccutane, Roaccutane, Absorica, others
ClassRetinoid
Legal statusPrescription only
RoutesBy mouth, topical

Isotretinoin, also known as 13-cis-retinoic acid and sold under the brand name Accutane among others, is a medication used to treat severe acne. It is a retinoid, meaning it is related to vitamin A, and is found in small quantities naturally in the body. It is used for severe cystic or nodular acne that has not responded to other treatments, and can produce long-term remission after a single course.

Medical uses

Isotretinoin is prescribed primarily for severe recalcitrant nodular acne. A typical course lasts about four to five months, with dosing adjusted to reach a cumulative target dose. Many patients experience permanent clearing; others require a second course. It is also used, less commonly, for other skin conditions and certain cancers such as neuroblastoma (as maintenance therapy in high-risk cases).

Mechanism of action

Isotretinoin reduces the size and activity of the sebaceous glands, cutting sebum production dramatically. It also normalises the shedding of skin cells within follicles, has anti-inflammatory effects, and reduces colonisation by the acne-associated bacterium Cutibacterium acnes. It is the only acne therapy that acts on all four major pathogenic factors of the disease.

Adverse effects

Common side effects are dose-related and resemble those of vitamin A excess: dry lips (cheilitis), dry skin and eyes, nosebleeds, muscle aches and increased sensitivity to sunlight. The drug can raise blood lipids and liver enzymes, requiring monitoring.

Teratogenicity and pregnancy prevention

Isotretinoin is a potent teratogen: exposure during pregnancy carries a high risk of severe birth defects. For this reason its use in people who could become pregnant is tightly controlled — in the United States through the iPLEDGE programme, which requires pregnancy tests and contraception commitments, and similar pregnancy prevention programmes operate in Europe and elsewhere.

Debated associations

Possible links with depression and suicidal behaviour, and with inflammatory bowel disease, have been extensively studied; evidence for a causal association remains inconclusive, but monitoring for mood changes is commonly advised.

History

Isotretinoin was developed by Hoffmann-La Roche and approved in the United States in 1982. The original Accutane brand was withdrawn from the US market in 2009 amid litigation, but generic versions remain widely used.
