OpenDoc was a multi-platform component software and compound document framework developed by Apple Computer in the early 1990s with IBM and Novell, and released for Mac OS in 1995. It was intended as a document-centred alternative to monolithic applications and a competitor to Microsoft's OLE technology.

Concept

Under OpenDoc, a document was assembled from small, interchangeable "parts" — text, graphics, spreadsheets, media — each edited in place by its own part editor. Users were to buy small components rather than large applications, and developers, coordinated through the Component Integration Laboratories consortium, built parts using the System Object Model (SOM).

Decline

OpenDoc shipped with Mac OS and was demonstrated in Apple's Cyberdog internet suite, but it demanded substantial memory, few parts were released, and developers were slow to adopt it. After Steve Jobs returned to Apple, OpenDoc was cancelled in 1997 as part of sweeping product cuts, and Apple's strategy shifted to conventional applications and, later, web technologies.
