Doujinshi

Doujinshi (Japanese: 同人誌), often transliterated dōjinshi or dojinshi, are self-published works — most commonly manga — produced by amateurs (doujin circles) in Japan. Although the term originally covered literary coterie magazines from the Meiji era onward, today it chiefly denotes fan-made manga, frequently derivative works based on existing anime, manga, and games such as the franchises of Toei Animation or Gundam, sold at events like Tokyo's Comic Market (Comiket), the world's largest fan convention.

History

Early doujinshi were magazines published by literary circles — the magazine Arashi (1885) is often cited as a precursor, and modernist and proletarian writers circulated coterie publications. In the postwar manga boom, amateur circles flourished, aided by cheap offset printing in the 1970s; the first Comic Market was held in 1975. Many professional manga artists began in doujin, and many — including the group CLAMP — continued to move between the two worlds; fan conventions expanded to hundreds of events across Japan and inspired similar cultures worldwide.

Content and culture

Doujinshi range from original stories to parodies, alternative pairings of copyrighted characters (notably yaoi/boys' love works produced largely by women), gag manga, and art books. Print runs are small, sold at cost-recovery prices at events and through specialist shops such as Toranoana and Melonbooks; digital distribution has grown. The culture functions as a training ground and community for creators, and a substantial share of Comiket's tens of thousands of circles are run by women.

Legal status

Derivative doujinshi technically infringe copyright, but Japanese rights holders generally tolerate them under an informal understanding — works are produced in small runs, sold non-commercially at events, and are seen as promoting the source franchises. Rights holders have occasionally acted against mass-produced or pirated works, and the balance has been debated in connection with trade agreements and copyright-term reforms.
