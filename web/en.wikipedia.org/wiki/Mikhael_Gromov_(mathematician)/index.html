Mikhael Gromov (mathematician)

Mikhael Leonidovich Gromov (born 23 December 1943) is a Russian–French mathematician known for his work in geometry, analysis, and group theory. He is regarded as one of the founders of modern global Riemannian geometry and of symplectic geometry.

Work

Gromov introduced the h-principle for solving partial differential relations, founded metric geometry on the Gromov–Hausdorff distance, and created geometric group theory as a field through his work on hyperbolic groups. His theory of pseudoholomorphic curves (Gromov–Witten invariants) transformed symplectic topology, and his compactness theorem underlies much of modern symplectic geometry. Other contributions include Gromov's systolic inequality and the notion of a Gromov hyperbolic space.

Awards

He received the Wolf Prize in 1993 and the Abel Prize in 2009 "for his revolutionary contributions to geometry". Gromov has worked at the Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques in France and at the Courant Institute in New York.
