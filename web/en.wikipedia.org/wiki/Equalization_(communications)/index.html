Equalization (communications)

In telecommunication, equalization is the reversal of distortion incurred by a signal transmitted through a channel. Devices that perform this function are called equalizers. They are used in modems, telephone lines, and wireless receivers to combat intersymbol interference caused by the channel's limited bandwidth and multipath propagation.

Principle

A communication channel attenuates and delays different frequency components differently, smearing successive symbols into one another. An equalizer applies an approximate inverse of the channel response, often implemented as a transversal (FIR) filter whose tap weights are adjusted by algorithms such as least mean squares (LMS) or trained with a known training sequence. Decision feedback equalizers additionally use previously detected symbols to cancel post-cursor interference.

Applications

Equalization is essential in DSL modems, cable modems, cellular systems (LTE and 5G use OFDM with per-subcarrier equalization), high-speed serial links, and magnetic recording channels. Adaptive equalizers continuously track changes in the channel, such as fading in mobile radio.
