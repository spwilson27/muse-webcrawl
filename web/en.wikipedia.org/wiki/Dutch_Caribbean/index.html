The Dutch Caribbean (Dutch: Caribisch Nederland for the BES islands; Nederlandse Caraïben more broadly) comprises the islands in the Caribbean Sea that are part of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. These are the constituent countries of Aruba, Curaçao and Sint Maarten, and the special municipalities of Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba.

History

The islands were colonised by the Dutch in the 17th century, largely through the Dutch West India Company, and became integrated into Atlantic trade, including the transatlantic slave trade centred on Curaçao. For much of the 20th century the six islands were governed together as the Netherlands Antilles.

Constitutional status

Aruba left the Netherlands Antilles in 1986 to become a separate country within the Kingdom. On 10 October 2010 the Netherlands Antilles was dissolved: Curaçao and Sint Maarten became constituent countries, while Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba (the BES islands) became special municipalities of the Netherlands proper, using the US dollar as currency.

Geography

The islands fall into two groups: the Leeward Antilles (Aruba, Bonaire, Curaçao) off the coast of Venezuela, which are arid, and the northern Leeward Islands (Saba, Sint Eustatius, Sint Maarten), which are greener and lie in the hurricane belt. Dutch and Papiamento are official in the southern islands; English is widely spoken in the north.
