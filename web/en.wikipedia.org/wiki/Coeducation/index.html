Coeducation

[Image placeholder: A mixed-gender classroom]

Mixed-sex education, also known as mixed-gender education, co-education or coeducation (abbreviated co-ed or coed), is a system of education where males and females are educated together. Whereas single-sex education was more common up to the 19th century, mixed-sex education has since become standard in many cultures, particularly in Western countries.

History

Early advocates of coeducation included reformers in the 19th-century United States; Oberlin College admitted women from 1833 and is often credited as the first coeducational college. Many formerly single-sex universities, including Oxford and Cambridge colleges and the Ivy League schools, became coeducational during the 20th century.

Debate

Supporters argue coeducation reflects society, improves social skills and reduces stereotyping. Critics contend that single-sex settings may reduce distraction and better suit some students' learning. Research on academic outcomes shows small and inconsistent differences between the two systems once socioeconomic factors are controlled.
