3,000-hit club

Pete Rose holds the record with 4,256 career hits

The 3,000-hit club is the group of batters who have collected 3,000 or more regular-season hits in Major League Baseball (MLB). Reaching 3,000 hits is considered one of the sport's great career milestones and a near-certain credential for election to the National Baseball Hall of Fame.

History

Cap Anson is generally credited as the first player to reach 3,000 hits, doing so in the 1890s (his exact total remains debated because of 19th-century record-keeping). Honus Wagner, Ty Cobb and others followed in the early 20th century. Cobb held the career record of 4,189 hits until Pete Rose surpassed him in 1985; Rose finished with 4,256.

Membership

As of the early 2020s, 33 players had reached the mark. Notable members include Hank Aaron (3,771), Stan Musial, Derek Jeter, Albert Pujols and Miguel Cabrera, who joined in April 2022. Ichiro Suzuki collected 3,089 MLB hits in addition to 1,278 in Japan's Nippon Professional Baseball. Every eligible member who has reached 3,000 hits has been elected to the Hall of Fame except those barred or linked to performance-enhancing drugs; Rose is permanently ineligible because of gambling, and Rafael Palmeiro and Alex Rodriguez have not been elected.

Milestone hits

Several 3,000th hits were memorable in themselves: Roberto Clemente's came on 30 September 1972 in his final regular-season at-bat, while Derek Jeter and Wade Boggs each reached the mark with a home run.
