Ethel Barrymore (born Ethel Mae Blythe; August 15, 1879 – June 18, 1959) was an American actress and a member of the Barrymore family of actors, alongside her brothers John and Lionel Barrymore. Regarded as "The First Lady of the American Theatre", she commanded the Broadway stage for decades and later enjoyed a second career as a character actress in films, winning the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress for None but the Lonely Heart (1944).

Early life

Born in Philadelphia to actors Maurice Barrymore and Georgiana Drew, she was raised in a theatrical family that included her grandmother Louisa Lane Drew, a celebrated actress and theatre manager. She made her Broadway debut in 1894 and appeared in London before the turn of the century.

Career

Barrymore rose to stardom under producer Charles Frohman in productions such as Captain Jinks of the Horse Marines (1901), Ibsen's A Doll's House, and Barrie's plays. Her celebrated roles included Madame Trentoni, the title role in The Corn Is Green (1940) — one of her greatest stage triumphs — and appearances in works by Shakespeare, Somerset Maugham and Zoë Akins. The Ethel Barrymore Theatre on Broadway, opened in 1928 with her in The Kingdom of God, still bears her name.

Though she resisted films for years, from the 1940s she worked regularly in Hollywood, earning four Academy Award nominations in supporting roles and winning for None but the Lonely Heart opposite Cary Grant. Her later films include The Spiral Staircase (1945), The Paradine Case (1947) and Portrait of Jennie (1948). She also starred in the early television series Ethel Barrymore Theatre.
