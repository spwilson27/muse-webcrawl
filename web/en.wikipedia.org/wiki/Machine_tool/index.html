Machine tool

A machine tool is a machine for handling or machining metal or other rigid materials, usually by cutting, boring, grinding, shearing or other forms of deformation. Machine tools are operated by skilled workers or by computer numerical control (CNC), and they are fundamental to modern manufacturing, producing the components of almost every other machine.

Types

Common machine tools include lathes, which rotate the workpiece against a cutting tool; milling machines, which move a rotating cutter against a stationary workpiece; drilling machines; grinding machines; and saws. Multi-axis machining centres combine several operations in a single setup.

History

Machine tools emerged during the Industrial Revolution. Henry Maudslay's screw-cutting lathe of around 1800 made precision threads practical, and Joseph Whitworth standardised screw threads and precision measurement. In the twentieth century, numerical control and then CNC transformed the industry, allowing complex parts to be produced automatically from digital designs.

Accuracy

Modern machine tools achieve tolerances of a few micrometres. Their accuracy depends on the rigidity of the frame, the precision of guideways and bearings, thermal stability and the quality of the cutting tools used.
