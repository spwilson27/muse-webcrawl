Live2D is a software technology and the Japanese company behind it that allows two-dimensional illustrations to be animated as if they were three-dimensional models, without redrawing the artwork or building a 3D mesh. The original drawing is divided into layered parts (such as eyes, hair and limbs), and animators deform those layers with meshes and parameters to produce blinking, lip-sync, head turns and breathing motions while preserving the look of the original illustration.

Technology and uses

The flagship product, Live2D Cubism, consists of a Modeler for rigging artwork and an Animator for keyframing motion; a software development kit embeds the models in games and apps. Live2D became a standard tool in Japanese mobile games with anime-style characters, in visual novels and in digital signage. From the late 2010s it became central to the VTuber phenomenon: virtual YouTubers such as the members of Hololive and Nijisanji perform through Live2D avatars whose facial movements are captured from the performer's face in real time, and many independent streamers commission personal Live2D models from freelance riggers. A short-lived successor technology, Live2D Euclid, attempted fully rotatable models before being discontinued in favour of the Cubism line. The company was founded in 2006 by Tetsuya Nakashiro, growing out of his earlier work at Cybernoids.
