Turcot syndrome

Turcot syndrome
Named after: Jacques Turcot
Features: Colorectal polyposis with brain tumours
Genetics: APC or mismatch-repair gene mutations

Turcot syndrome is a rare hereditary condition characterised by the association of colorectal polyps and colorectal cancer with primary tumours of the central nervous system, such as medulloblastoma and glioblastoma. It was described by Canadian surgeon Jacques Turcot in 1959, based on cases in a brother and sister.

Genetics

Two genetic forms are recognised. In one, mutations in the APC gene — the same gene responsible for familial adenomatous polyposis — are associated particularly with medulloblastoma. In the other, mutations in DNA mismatch-repair genes (as in Lynch syndrome) are associated particularly with glioblastoma. Modern classifications often treat "Turcot syndrome" as a descriptive label spanning these two syndromes rather than a single disorder.

Clinical features

Patients develop numerous adenomatous polyps of the colon, usually in adolescence, with a high risk of colorectal cancer, together with malignant brain tumours. Management involves regular colonoscopic surveillance, consideration of prophylactic colectomy, and screening for central nervous system tumours.
