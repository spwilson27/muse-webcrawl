Warlord Era

The Warlord Era was a period in the history of the Republic of China from 1916 to 1928, when the country was fragmented among rival military commanders known as warlords. It began with the death of Yuan Shikai, whose Beiyang Army split into competing cliques, and ended nominally with the Northern Expedition of the National Revolutionary Army under Chiang Kai-shek, which reunified China under the Nationalist government.

Major cliques

The principal factions included the Zhili clique of Wu Peifu and Cao Kun, the Anhui clique of Duan Qirui, the Fengtian clique of Zhang Zuolin in Manchuria, and regional strongmen such as Yan Xishan in Shanxi and Feng Yuxiang, leader of the Guominjun. These groups fought a shifting series of wars — the Zhili–Anhui War (1920), the First and Second Zhili–Fengtian Wars (1922, 1924), and others — while nominal governments in Beijing changed hands repeatedly.

Society and legacy

Warfare among the cliques devastated large areas, disrupted agriculture and commerce, and imposed heavy taxation, while banditry and opium cultivation flourished. At the same time the period saw the May Fourth Movement, the founding of the Chinese Communist Party in 1921, and the reorganization of the Kuomintang in alliance with the Soviet Union. The Northern Expedition (1926–1928) defeated or absorbed the northern warlords, though many regional militarists retained de facto power into the 1930s.
