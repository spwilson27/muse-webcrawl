Stephanie Kwolek

Stephanie Louise Kwolek (July 31, 1923 – June 18, 2014) was an American chemist of Polish heritage best known for inventing Kevlar, the para-aramid fibre used in bulletproof vests, tyres, cables, spacecraft and hundreds of other products. She spent her entire career, from 1946 to 1986, at the DuPont company.

Early life and education

Kwolek was born in New Kensington, Pennsylvania, to Polish immigrant parents. She earned a Bachelor of Science in chemistry from Margaret Morrison Carnegie College (now part of Carnegie Mellon University) in 1946. Hoping eventually to attend medical school, she took a research job at DuPont's Buffalo laboratory — and stayed with the company for forty years.

Invention of Kevlar

In 1964, anticipating a gasoline shortage, Kwolek's group at DuPont's Wilmington laboratories was searching for a lightweight fibre strong enough to reinforce car tyres. Working with aromatic polyamides, she produced a cloudy, opalescent solution that appeared too thin and weak to be useful. Kwolek persuaded the technician to spin it anyway; to everyone's surprise, the resulting fibre was extraordinarily stiff and strong — five times stronger than steel by weight. The material, poly-paraphenylene terephthalamide, was commercialised as Kevlar in 1971. Kwolek received the patent in 1966 and later received numerous awards, including the National Medal of Technology (1996), election to the National Inventors Hall of Fame (1995), and the Perkin Medal (1997).

Later life

Kwolek continued consulting on polymer chemistry after retirement and devoted much time to encouraging girls to study science. She died in Wilmington, Delaware, in 2014, aged 90. The week of her death, the millionth bullet-resistant vest made from Kevlar was produced — one measure of an invention credited with saving thousands of lives.
