Field: Theoretical physics

Conserved current

In physics, a conserved current is a current, jμ, that satisfies the continuity equation ∂μjμ = 0. A conserved current is associated with a conserved quantity, obtained by integrating the time component of the current over all space, and such currents arise automatically in theories with continuous symmetries through Noether's theorem.

Examples

The canonical example is electric charge: the electromagnetic four-current is conserved, expressing local conservation of charge. Locally conserved currents arise from both global and local symmetries; a current arising from a local symmetry, such as the energy–momentum tensor, is automatically conserved once the equations of motion hold. In quantum field theory, conserved currents define charges that generate symmetry transformations, much as angular momentum generates rotations.

Canonical conserved currents

For fields charged under a U(1) group, the Noether current takes a standard form built from the field and its conjugate momentum. In non-Abelian gauge theories the corresponding currents obey covariant rather than ordinary conservation, a subtlety central to the Standard Model.
