Codling moth
Scientific nameCydia pomonella (Linnaeus, 1758)
FamilyTortricidae
Wingspan14–22 mm
Host plantsApple, pear, walnut, quince

The codling moth (Cydia pomonella) is the principal agricultural pest of apples worldwide and a member of the Lepidopteran family Tortricidae. Its larvae are the familiar "worms" found in apples; the species originated in Eurasia and has been introduced into North and South America, Australia and Africa.

Life cycle

Adults are small grey-brown moths with coppery markings at the wingtips. Females lay eggs on leaves and fruit; the hatching larvae bore into the fruit and tunnel to the core, feeding on the seeds. Depending on climate there may be one to four generations per year. Mature larvae leave the fruit and overwinter in cocoons under bark or in the soil.

Control

Management combines pheromone traps for monitoring, mating disruption, granulovirus (CpGV) biopesticides, insecticides timed to egg hatch, and sterile insect technique releases. The moth has evolved resistance to many insecticide classes, making integrated pest management essential.
