Comstock Lode

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Virginia City, Nevada, grew up around the Comstock Lode

The Comstock Lode is a lode of silver ore located under the eastern slope of Mount Davidson, a peak in the Virginia Range in Virginia City, Nevada, which was the first major discovery of silver ore in the United States and named after American miner Henry Comstock.

After the discovery was made public in 1859, it sparked a silver rush of prospectors to the area, scrambling to stake their claims. The discovery caused considerable excitement in California and throughout the United States, the greatest since the California Gold Rush in 1849. Mining camps soon thrived in the vicinity, becoming bustling commercial centers, most notably Virginia City.

History

Gold was first found in the area in 1850 by the Grosh brothers and others placer mining in Gold Canyon; the bluish-grey silver ore that clogged their rockers was at first discarded as a nuisance before being recognized as rich silver sulfide. Comstock purchased a claim in 1859 and promoted his interest tirelessly, lending his name to the lode.

The mines declined after 1874 when the ore bodies thinned, though a major bonanza — the "Big Bonanza" in the Consolidated Virginia Mine — produced enormous wealth in the mid-1870s. Between 1859 and 1878 the Comstock produced roughly $400 million in silver and gold (in period dollars). The wealth helped finance the Union during the Civil War, underwrote San Francisco's rise as a financial center, and speeded Nevada's admission to statehood in 1864.

Mining technology

The soft, crumbling ore bodies and great depths of the Comstock spurred innovations including square-set timbering, developed by Philipp Deidesheimer, the Sutro Tunnel for drainage and ventilation, and advances in hard-rock mining machinery manufactured in San Francisco and Virginia City.
