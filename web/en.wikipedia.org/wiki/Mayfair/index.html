Mayfair

Mayfair is an affluent area of the West End of London, in the City of Westminster. Roughly bounded by Oxford Street to the north, Piccadilly to the south, Park Lane to the west and Regent Street to the east, it is one of the most expensive districts in the world, famous for its Georgian townhouses, luxury hotels, gentlemen's clubs, art galleries and flagship shops.

History

The name comes from the annual May Fair, held from 1686 to 1764 on the site of what is now Shepherd Market; the fair was suppressed as a nuisance to the increasingly fashionable neighbourhood. From the early 18th century the great aristocratic estates — notably the Grosvenor Estate, which remains the dominant landowner — developed the area with grand squares and terraces (Grosvenor Square, Berkeley Square, Hanover Square) for the nobility and gentry. The saying "born in Mayfair" became shorthand for aristocratic birth.

Modern Mayfair

After the Second World War many great houses were converted to offices, embassies and clubs. The district contains the former United States Embassy on Grosvenor Square, the Royal Academy of Arts at Burlington House, Savile Row (famous for bespoke tailoring), Bond Street's jewellery and fashion houses, and hotels such as Claridge's, the Dorchester (on Park Lane) and the Ritz. On the London Monopoly board, Mayfair is the most expensive property — a status it retains in reality, with homes there routinely selling for tens of millions of pounds. In recent decades hedge funds, private equity firms and international buyers have made it a hub of global finance as well as luxury retail.
