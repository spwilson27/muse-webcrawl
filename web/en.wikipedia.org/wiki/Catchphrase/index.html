Catchphrase

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A catchphrase (alternatively spelled catch phrase) is a phrase or expression recognized by its repeated utterance. Such phrases often originate in popular culture and in the arts, and typically spread through word of mouth and a variety of mass media (such as literature and publishing, films, television and radio). Some become the trademark of the character or person who originated them, and can be used or referenced long after the original work has faded from popularity.

History

The term "catchphrase" dates to the mid-19th century, derived from the idea of a phrase that "catches" in the public imagination. In the theatrical tradition, actors and comedians cultivated signature expressions; in the 20th century, radio, cinema and television massively amplified the phenomenon, turning lines from programmes and advertisements into shared cultural shorthand. Vaudeville performers, stand-up comedians and later sitcom characters all contributed catchphrases to everyday speech.

Notable examples

Well-known catchphrases include "D'oh!" from The Simpsons, "I'll be back" from The Terminator, "May the Force be with you" from Star Wars, "Elementary, my dear Watson" associated with Sherlock Holmes, and "That's the way it is" from newsreader Walter Cronkite. Advertising slogans, such as "Just do it", function similarly as commercial catchphrases.

Cultural role

Catchphrases serve as in-group markers, allowing speakers to signal shared knowledge and generate humour through recognition. Linguists study them as examples of formulaic language and memetic transmission. Overuse can render a catchphrase a cliché, yet many remain durable elements of a language's idiom.
