Chancellor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Chancellor (from Latin cancellarius, a secretary at a barrier or lattice) is a title used for senior officials in various governments and institutions. Its most prominent modern use is as the head of government of Germany and Austria — the Federal Chancellor (Bundeskanzler), equivalent to a prime minister, who sets policy guidelines and is elected by the parliament (in Germany, the Bundestag, with a constructive vote of no confidence required to remove them mid-term).

Other uses

In the United Kingdom, the Chancellor of the Exchequer is the finance minister (the second most powerful cabinet office), and the Lord Chancellor is a senior cabinet office historically head of the judiciary, now the justice secretary. Many universities are headed (usually ceremonially) by chancellors, with vice-chancellors as chief executives. In the United States, chancellors head university systems and some state judiciaries (Delaware's Court of Chancery judges are chancellors). Historically the chancellor was the keeper of the royal seal and head of the chancery — the writing office of medieval monarchs — from which the modern ministerial senses descend.
