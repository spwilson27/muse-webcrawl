Chartered Engineer (UK)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Chartered Engineer (abbreviated CEng) is a professional registration grade awarded in the United Kingdom to engineers who have demonstrated, through education, training and professional review, that they meet the competence and commitment standards of the UK Standard for Professional Engineering Competence (UK-SPEC). Registration is regulated by the Engineering Council, which licenses professional engineering institutions to assess candidates.

Requirements

Candidates must typically demonstrate:

  • An accredited educational base — usually an integrated MEng degree, or a BEng (Hons) plus further learning to master's level (the "exemplifying academic standard" has evolved with the Washington Accord).
  • Initial professional development, generally several years of responsible engineering work.
  • Competence across five areas set out in UK-SPEC: knowledge and understanding; design, development and problem-solving; responsibility, management and leadership; communication and inter-personal skills; and professional commitment, including ethics and sustainability.
  • Success at a professional review, including an interview with trained assessors.

Licensed institutions

Assessment is carried out by licensed professional engineering institutions such as the Institution of Civil Engineers (ICE), the Institution of Mechanical Engineers (IMechE), the Institution of Engineering and Technology (IET) and the Royal Aeronautical Society. Registrants normally also become members (e.g. MICE, MIMechE, MIET) and may use the post-nominal letters CEng.

Related grades

The Engineering Council also maintains the grades of Incorporated Engineer (IEng) and Engineering Technician (EngTech), and ICT Technician (ICTTech). Chartered Engineer is recognised internationally through agreements such as the International Professional Engineers Agreement, and CEng registrants may be eligible for the European Engineer (EUR ING) title.
