The Regional Transportation Authority (RTA) is the financial oversight and regional planning body for public transportation in the Chicago metropolitan area in the U.S. state of Illinois. It oversees the three "service boards": the Chicago Transit Authority (CTA), Metra (commuter rail), and Pace (suburban bus), which together operate the second-largest public transit system in the United States.

History

The RTA was created by referendum in March 1974 in the six-county Chicago region (Cook, DuPage, Kane, Lake, McHenry and Will counties) after a financial crisis threatened the region's transit operators. A funding crisis in the early 1980s led the Illinois General Assembly to restructure the agency in 1983: the RTA was converted from an operating agency into a planning, funding and oversight body, and Metra and Pace were created as separate service boards.

Functions

The RTA collects a dedicated regional sales tax (the RTA sales tax), allocates operating and capital funds among the CTA, Metra and Pace, reviews and approves their budgets, and issues bonds for capital projects. It develops the regional transit strategic plan, coordinates fares and planning, and provides travel information services. Its governing board has sixteen directors appointed by county boards, the City of Chicago and the region's mayors, plus a chairman.

Funding challenges

The system has faced recurring fiscal pressures, including a major funding shortfall projected for the mid-2020s as federal pandemic relief funds expire — a "fiscal cliff" that prompted Illinois legislation debates on transit reform and new revenue.
