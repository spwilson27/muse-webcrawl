K2, at 8,611 metres (28,251 ft) above sea level, is the second-highest mountain on Earth, after Mount Everest. It lies in the Karakoram range, partially in the Gilgit-Baltistan region of Pakistan-administered Kashmir and partially in a China-administered area of Kashmir. K2 also became popularly known as the Savage Mountain after George Bell — a climber on the 1953 American expedition — told reporters "It's a savage mountain that tries to kill you."

The name K2 derives from the notation used by the Great Trigonometrical Survey of British India: K for Karakoram, numbered as the second peak surveyed. Unlike Everest, K2 has no widely used local name — a fact Thomas Montgomerie noted when labelling it.

Climbing

K2 is regarded as far more difficult and dangerous than Everest: it is steeper, more remote, more exposed to storms funnelling down the Baltoro Glacier, and has no comparably "easy" route. The standard route is the Abruzzi Spur on the Pakistani side. For many years roughly one climber died for every four who summited; the fatality rate has improved with modern support, but disasters — notably 1986 and 2008, when 11 climbers died in one August weekend — mark its history. A bottle-neck serac below the summit, and the "Bottleneck" couloir, are its most feared features. The first winter ascent, long considered one of mountaineering's last great prizes, was achieved in January 2021 by a team of ten Nepali climbers, including Nirmal Purja and Mingma Gyalje Sherpa, linking the Himalaya and Karakoram traditions of the Sherpa and other Nepali high-altitude workers celebrated in Nepal.

Geology and geography

K2 rises from the Baltoro Muztagh, its pyramid isolated by deep glacier valleys. The Godwin-Austen Glacier flows past its base camp. Its weather is governed by westerly storms rather than the monsoon that dominates the main Himalayas to the southeast.
