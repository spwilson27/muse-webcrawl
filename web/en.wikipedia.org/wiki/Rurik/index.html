Rurik (Old Norse probably Hrœríkr) was a Varangian chieftain who, according to the Primary Chronicle, was invited in 862 by the Slavic and Finnic tribes of the north to rule over them, traditionally founding the principality of Novgorod and the Rurik dynasty that ruled Kievan Rus' and its successor states until 1598.

The calling of the Varangians

The chronicle relates that the tribes, having expelled the Varangians, fell into discord and invited them back: "Our land is great and rich, but there is no order in it. Come and rule over us." Rurik settled in Novgorod (or, some evidence suggests, at nearby Gorodishche or Ladoga), his brothers Sineus and Truvor took Beloozero and Izborsk respectively — though the brothers' names may reflect a misunderstanding of Old Norse words for "his household" and "his loyal guard". Rurik's kinsman Oleg later seized Kiev and raised Rurik's son Igor as his ward.

Historicity

The details of Rurik's life are semi-legendary; scholars have proposed identifying him with Rorik of Dorestad, a Danish Viking active in Frisia, though this is unproven. The extent of the Scandinavian role in founding the Rus' state has been debated for centuries in the "Normanist controversy," with archaeological evidence confirming a substantial Norse presence along the eastern river trade routes.
