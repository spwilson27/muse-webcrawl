The Butterfly Lovers

The Butterfly Lovers is a Chinese legend of a tragic love affair between Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai, sometimes regarded as the Chinese equivalent of Romeo and Juliet. It is one of the Four Great Folktales of China, alongside the Legend of the White Snake, Lady Meng Jiang, and The Cowherd and the Weaver Girl. The story is set in the Eastern Jin dynasty (317–420).

Legend

Zhu Yingtai, the daughter of a wealthy family in Shangyu, Zhejiang, longs to study, which women of her era were forbidden to do. Disguised as a man, she travels to Hangzhou to attend school, where she meets the scholar Liang Shanbo. They study together for three years and become sworn brothers; Zhu falls in love with Liang, who remains unaware of her true sex. When Zhu is summoned home, Liang accompanies her part of the way, and she hints repeatedly at her identity, but he does not understand. She promises to propose her "sister" — herself — as his bride.

When Liang finally learns the truth and comes to propose, he finds Zhu's father has already betrothed her to Ma Wencai, the son of a wealthy family. Heartbroken, Liang falls ill and dies. On her wedding day, Zhu insists on visiting Liang's grave; the tomb splits open in a storm, she throws herself in, and the lovers emerge transformed into a pair of butterflies, flying away together.

Cultural legacy

The legend has been adapted into Yue opera, films, television series and music. The Butterfly Lovers' Violin Concerto (1959), composed by He Zhanhao and Chen Gang, is one of the most famous works in Chinese orchestral music. In 2006 the legend was inscribed on China's national list of intangible cultural heritage, and Liang and Zhu's story remains a symbol of free love triumphing over feudal convention.
