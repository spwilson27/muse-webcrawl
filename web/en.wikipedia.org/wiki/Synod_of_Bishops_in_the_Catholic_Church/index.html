Synod of Bishops in the Catholic Church

In the Catholic Church, the Synod of Bishops, considered as a permanent institution, is an advisory body for the Pope. It is described in the Code of Canon Law (1983) as "a group of bishops who have been chosen from different regions of the world and meet at fixed times to foster closer unity between the Roman Pontiff and bishops, to assist the Roman Pontiff with their counsel in the preservation and growth of faith and morals and in the observance and strengthening of ecclesiastical discipline, and to consider questions pertaining to the activity of the Church in the world."

Establishment

The Synod was established by Pope Paul VI with the motu proprio Apostolica sollicitudo of 15 September 1965, during the final session of the Second Vatican Council, whose fathers had expressed a desire for an ongoing body through which the world's bishops could share in the governance of the universal Church. Its norms were later set out in canon law and revised by Pope Francis in the 2018 apostolic constitution Episcopalis communio.

Assemblies

Synodal assemblies are convoked by the Pope and come in three forms:

  • Ordinary general assemblies, meeting roughly every three or four years on themes of universal concern — recent ones treated the new evangelization (2012), the family (2015), young people (2018), and synodality itself (2023–2024).
  • Extraordinary general assemblies, called to deal with urgent matters, such as the 1985 synod on the twentieth anniversary of Vatican II and the 2014 synod on the family.
  • Special assemblies, devoted to a particular region — for example Africa (1994, 2009), the Americas (1997), Asia (1998), Europe (1991, 1999), and the Pan-Amazon region (2019).

Most members are elected by episcopal conferences, with others appointed by the Pope or members ex officio. Assemblies traditionally concluded with propositions submitted to the Pope, who often responded with a post-synodal apostolic exhortation, such as Evangelii gaudium (following the 2012 synod) and Amoris laetitia (following the family synods).

Synod on Synodality

Under Pope Francis the institution was reshaped toward "synodality": the XVI Ordinary General Assembly (2023–2024) was preceded by a global consultation in dioceses and, for the first time, included lay men and women — including religious sisters — as voting members. Its final document was accepted by Francis directly as part of the Church's magisterium.
