Shades of brown

Brown is a composite colour produced by mixing red, yellow, and black pigments, or by darkening orange. In colour theory, browns are dark or muted shades of orange and red. The shades of brown include many named colours traditionally derived from earth pigments — such as umber, sienna, and ochre — as well as modern standardised colours used in design, fashion, and heraldry.

History and use

Brown earth pigments are among the oldest used by humans, appearing in prehistoric cave painting. Umber and sienna were staple colours of Renaissance and Baroque palettes. Because brown is the colour of soil and wood, it connotes nature, reliability, and simplicity; in heraldry it is uncommon, while in modern design it is used for warmth and neutrality.
