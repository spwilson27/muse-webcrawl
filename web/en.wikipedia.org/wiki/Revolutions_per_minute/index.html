Revolutions per minute

Revolutions per minute
Unit of: rotational speed
Symbol: rpm, r/min
SI equivalent: 1 rpm = 1/60 Hz = 2π/60 rad/s

Revolutions per minute (abbreviated rpm, RPM, rev/min, r/min, or r·min−1) is a unit of rotational speed or the frequency of rotation around a fixed axis. It measures the number of complete turns, or revolutions, made in one minute.

Use

The unit is ubiquitous in engineering. Vehicle tachometers display engine crankshaft speed in rpm, with a redline marking the maximum safe speed. Phonograph records are described by their nominal speeds of 33⅓, 45 and 78 rpm, and hard disk drives commonly spin at 5,400, 7,200 or 10,000+ rpm. Machine tools, centrifuges, turbines and electric motors are likewise rated in rpm.

Relation to SI units

The SI unit of rotational frequency is the hertz (cycles per second) and of angular velocity the radian per second. One revolution per minute equals 1/60 Hz, or 2π/60 ≈ 0.1047 rad/s. Although the minute is not an SI unit, rpm remains in widespread use; in many technical contexts angular frequency ω = 2π × (rpm)/60 is used for calculation.

Examples

  • A vinyl LP rotates at 33⅓ rpm.
  • An idling car engine typically turns at 600–1,000 rpm.
  • Aircraft and dental turbines can exceed 100,000 rpm.
  • The Earth completes one revolution per day, about 0.000694 rpm.
