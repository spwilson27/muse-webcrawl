Oprah Gail Winfrey (born January 29, 1954) is an American talk show host, television producer, actress, author, and media proprietor. She is best known for her talk show, The Oprah Winfrey Show, broadcast from Chicago, which ran in national syndication for 25 years, from 1986 to 2011.

Early life

Winfrey was born into poverty in rural Mississippi to a teenage single mother and later raised in Milwaukee and Nashville. She endured a difficult childhood but excelled academically, earning a scholarship to Tennessee State University, where she studied communication.

Career

After working in local media in Nashville and Baltimore, Winfrey took over the Chicago morning show AM Chicago in 1984; renamed The Oprah Winfrey Show, it became the highest-rated talk show in American television history. She founded Harpo Productions and later the Oprah Winfrey Network (OWN). Her film roles include Sofia in The Color Purple (1985), for which she received an Academy Award nomination.

Influence

Dubbed the "Queen of All Media," Winfrey was the richest African-American of the 20th century and North America's first Black multi-billionaire. Her book club popularized reading nationwide, and her endorsements have carried significant cultural and political weight.
