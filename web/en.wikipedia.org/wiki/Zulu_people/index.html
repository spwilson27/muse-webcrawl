The Zulu people (Zulu: amaZulu) are a Nguni ethnic group native to Southern Africa and the largest ethnic group in South Africa, numbering roughly 14–15 million, concentrated mainly in the province of KwaZulu-Natal. They speak isiZulu, the most widely spoken home language in South Africa.

History

The Zulu began as a small Nguni clan founded around 1709 by Zulu kaMalandela. Under King Shaka (r. 1816–1828), the clan was transformed through military and political innovation into a powerful kingdom — the Zulu Empire — that reshaped the region during the period known as the Mfecane. The kingdom resisted British expansion until its defeat in the Anglo-Zulu War of 1879, which included the famous Zulu victory at Isandlwana and the defence of Rorke's Drift. Under apartheid, Zulu people were assigned to the KwaZulu bantustan; Inkatha, led by Mangosuthu Buthelezi, became a major political force. Since 1994 the Zulu monarchy has continued as a traditional institution within South Africa.

Culture

Zulu culture features distinctive beadwork whose colours and patterns carry social meanings, ceremonial dances such as the reed dance (Umkhosi womHlanga), and a rich oral tradition of praise poetry (izibongo). Traditional religion venerates ancestors (amadlozi) and a creator, uNkulunkulu, while most Zulu today are Christian, including members of the Zion Christian Church and the Nazareth Baptist Church founded by Isaiah Shembe. Cattle remain central to wealth, marriage (lobola), and ritual.

The monarchy

The Zulu king is a constitutional figurehead in KwaZulu-Natal. Goodwill Zwelithini reigned from 1968 until his death in 2021 and was succeeded by his son Misuzulu kaZwelithini after a succession dispute.
