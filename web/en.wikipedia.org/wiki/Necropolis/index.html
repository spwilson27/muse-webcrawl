Necropolis
DefinitionA large, designed cemetery with elaborate tombs
EtymologyGreek nekropolis, "city of the dead"
Famous examplesSaqqara, Valley of the Kings, Tarquinia, Glasgow Necropolis

A necropolis (pl. necropolises, necropoles, necropoleis, or necropoli) is a large, designed cemetery with elaborate tomb monuments. The name stems from the Ancient Greek νεκρόπολις nekropolis (lit. "city of the dead"). The term usually implies a separate burial site at a distance from a city, as opposed to tombs within cities, which were common in various places and periods of history.

History

Ancient Egypt maintained elaborate necropoleis at Saqqara, Giza (site of the Great Sphinx and pyramids) and Thebes (the Valley of the Kings). The Etruscans built vast tomb cities such as Cerveteri and Tarquinia, whose painted chambers replicate domestic interiors. Greek and Roman necropoleis lined the roads outside cities, as at the Kerameikos in Athens and along the Appian Way. In the 19th century the term was revived for monumental landscaped cemeteries such as the Glasgow Necropolis (1832) and Père Lachaise in Paris.
