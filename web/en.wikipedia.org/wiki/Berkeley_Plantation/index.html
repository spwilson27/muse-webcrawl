Berkeley Plantation

Berkeley Plantation, also known as Berkeley Hundred, is a historic plantation on the north bank of the James River in Charles City County, Virginia. Founded in 1619, it was the site of one of the earliest recorded Thanksgiving observances in English America, and it is the ancestral home of the Harrison family, which produced two United States presidents — William Henry Harrison and Benjamin Harrison.

History

In December 1619, settlers aboard the ship Margaret, sent by the Berkeley Company of England, landed at Berkeley Hundred and, following their instructions, held a service of thanksgiving for their safe arrival — a commemoration promoted by the site's modern owners as "America's first Thanksgiving" (4 December 1619), predating the Plymouth celebration of 1621. The settlement was nearly wiped out in the Powhatan attack of 1622, and the hundred was abandoned before being resettled.

The property passed to the Harrison family in 1691. Benjamin Harrison IV built the three-storey brick Georgian manor house in 1726. His son Benjamin Harrison V was a signer of the Declaration of Independence and governor of Virginia. The house was the birthplace of William Henry Harrison, ninth president of the United States; his grandson Benjamin Harrison, the 23rd president, was born in Ohio but recognised Berkeley as his ancestral home.

During the Civil War, Berkeley was occupied by Union forces under George B. McClellan during the Peninsula Campaign of 1862; it was here that General Daniel Butterfield composed the bugle call "Taps" while camped at Harrison's Landing. Neglected afterward, the plantation was bought in 1907 by John Jamieson and restored by his son Malcolm Jamieson in the 20th century. It is now open to the public as a museum.
