Carol I of Romania

Reign: 20 April 1866 – 10 October 1914
Born: 20 April 1839, Sigmaringen
Died: 10 October 1914, Sinaia
Spouse: Elisabeth of Wied

Carol I (born Prince Karl of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen; 20 April 1839 – 10 October 1914) was the first king of Romania, reigning from 1866 until his death — first as prince (Domnitor) and from 1881 as king. His 48-year reign is the longest in Romanian history.

Accession and independence

Chosen as prince after the abdication of Alexandru Ioan Cuza, Carol arrived in Bucharest in 1866, travelling incognito through Austria due to the Franco-Prussian tensions. During the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78 he personally commanded the Romanian army at the Siege of Plevna, and Romania's independence from the Ottoman Empire was recognised by the Treaty of Berlin in 1878. He was crowned king in 1881.

Reign

Carol oversaw the modernisation of Romania: railways, industry, and the army expanded, financed in part by the Strousberg railway affair and later petroleum development. He built Peleș Castle at Sinaia as a summer residence. Secretly aligned with the Triple Alliance from 1883, he nevertheless kept Romania neutral at the outbreak of the First World War in 1914, against his own inclination to join the Central Powers.

He married Elisabeth of Wied, a writer under the name Carmen Sylva. As the marriage produced no surviving heir, he was succeeded by his nephew Ferdinand I.
