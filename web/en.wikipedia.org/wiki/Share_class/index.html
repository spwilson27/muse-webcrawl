Share class

A share class is a designation of a company's stock that distinguishes shares carrying different rights, such as voting power, dividend entitlement or conversion privileges. A company may issue, for example, Class A and Class B shares with different numbers of votes per share.

Company shares

Dual-class structures allow founders or insiders to retain control while holding a minority of the equity: technology companies such as Alphabet (Google) and Meta have issued classes with ten votes per share to founders alongside one-vote shares sold to the public. Critics argue such structures weaken shareholder accountability, while supporters say they protect long-term strategy.

Mutual fund share classes

The term is also used for mutual fund shares that represent the same portfolio but carry different fee and load structures, such as Class A (front-end load), Class B (back-end load) and Class C (level load) shares in the United States.
