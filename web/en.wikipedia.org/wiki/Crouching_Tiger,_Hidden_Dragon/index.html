Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (Chinese: 臥虎藏龍, Wòhǔ Cánglóng) is a 2000 wuxia film directed by Ang Lee. An international co-production, it is adapted from the fourth novel in Wang Dulu's Crane–Iron pentalogy. The film stars Chow Yun-fat, Michelle Yeoh, Zhang Ziyi, and Chang Chen, with action choreography by Yuen Woo-ping and an Oscar-winning score by Tan Dun featuring cellist Yo-Yo Ma.

A surprise international hit, it grossed over $213 million worldwide and became the highest-grossing foreign-language film in American box-office history at the time. It won four Academy Awards, including Best Foreign Language Film, and is credited with popularizing Chinese wuxia cinema for Western audiences and paving the way for films such as Hero and House of Flying Daggers.

Plot

In Qing-dynasty China, the renowned swordsman Li Mu Bai (Chow Yun-fat) asks his friend and long-unspoken love, the security-company owner Yu Shu Lien (Michelle Yeoh), to deliver his legendary sword, the Green Destiny, to Sir Te in Beijing. The sword is stolen by a masked thief — revealed to be Jen Yu (Zhang Ziyi), the governor's young daughter, who has been secretly trained by the fugitive criminal Jade Fox, the murderer of Li Mu Bai's master.

Jen, promised in an arranged marriage, longs for the free life of the jianghu (the martial-arts world) and for her bandit lover Lo (Chang Chen), whom she met in the desert. Pursuits and duels follow — including a celebrated fight in a bamboo forest — as Mu Bai tries to take Jen as his disciple, Jade Fox's revenge unfolds, and the older pair's restrained love contrasts with the reckless freedom of the younger. The film ends tragically: Jade Fox's poison kills Mu Bai, who dies confessing his love, and Jen makes an ambiguous leap from a bridge on Mount Wudang, echoing a legend of wishes granted.

Production

Ang Lee, known then for family dramas such as Sense and Sensibility, sought to make a film combining Chinese martial-arts spectacle with serious psychological drama — "a film with the plot of a drama and the action of a kung fu movie." Filming took place across China, including the bamboo forests of Zhejiang, the Gobi desert, and the historic town of Hongcun. The wire-fu sequences were staged by Yuen Woo-ping, famed for The Matrix; Michelle Yeoh performed despite tearing a knee ligament early in production. The cast spoke Mandarin with widely varying accents, a point criticized in China but unnoticed by most international audiences reading subtitles.

Themes

The title comes from a Chinese idiom (臥虎藏龍) meaning a place or person concealing formidable hidden talent. The film explores repressed desire and duty among its older heroes, female freedom and constraint (three generations of women — Jade Fox, Shu Lien, Jen — choose different paths), and the costs of the wandering swordsman's life.

Reception

Premiering at Cannes in 2000, the film was a critical triumph, widely praised for its cinematography, choreographed action "as ballet," and emotional depth. It won over 40 awards. In China and Taiwan its reception was cooler — accents and its novelistic pacing drew criticism — but its global impact on the international standing of Chinese-language cinema was enormous.

Accolades

At the 73rd Academy Awards the film won Best Foreign Language Film, Best Cinematography, Best Art Direction, and Best Original Score, from ten nominations including Best Picture and Best Director. It also won the Golden Globe for Best Foreign Language Film and four BAFTAs.

Sequel

A sequel, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon: Sword of Destiny, directed by Yuen Woo-ping and starring Michelle Yeoh and Donnie Yen, was released by Netflix in 2016 to mixed reviews.
