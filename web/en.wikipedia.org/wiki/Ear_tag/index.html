Ear tag

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An ear tag is a plastic or metal device attached to the ear of an animal for identification and tracking. Ear tags are widely used for livestock — cattle, sheep, goats and pigs — as well as in wildlife research, where they serve as visible markers, and for tissue sampling and electronic data collection.

Types

Common forms include two-piece plastic "button" or panel tags pierced through the ear with an applicator, metal clip tags, and electronic (RFID/EID) tags containing a transponder readable by scanners. In many jurisdictions the tag carries a printed unique number encoding the country, herd and individual animal; standardised schemes such as the EU's cattle identification rules and Australia's National Livestock Identification System require tags to remain with the animal for life, supporting traceability of meat and disease control.

Uses

Beyond official identification, farmers use colour-coded tags to record management groups, breeding and health treatments. Researchers tag wild animals to study movement, survival and population size, though studies must weigh the information gained against effects on the animal. Ear punches and notches can supplement tags.

Welfare considerations

Tagging causes brief pain and carries small risks of infection or tearing, especially if tags snag. Placement technique, tag size relative to the animal, and alternatives such as leg bands, microchips or photographic identification are debated in welfare literature.
