Turán graph

The Turán graph, denoted T(n, r), is a complete multipartite graph formed by partitioning a set of n vertices into r subsets as equal in size as possible, and connecting every vertex to all vertices not in its own subset. It is named after the Hungarian mathematician Pál Turán.

Turán's theorem

Turán's theorem (1941) states that the Turán graph T(n, r) has the maximum possible number of edges among all n-vertex graphs that do not contain an (r+1)-clique as a subgraph. Its number of edges is approximately (1 − 1/r)·n²/2. This result founded the field of extremal graph theory.

Special cases

For r = 2 the Turán graph is a complete bipartite graph with parts as equal as possible; the corresponding statement that a triangle-free graph has at most n²/4 edges is known as Mantel's theorem (1907), an earlier special case of Turán's result.
