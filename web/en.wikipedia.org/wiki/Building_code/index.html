Building code

A building code (also building control or building regulations) is a set of rules that specify the standards for constructed objects such as buildings and non-building structures. Codes set minimum requirements for structural integrity, fire safety, sanitation, accessibility, and increasingly energy efficiency, and are enforced by governments through permits and inspections.

History

Building regulation is ancient: the Code of Hammurabi prescribed consequences for builders whose houses collapsed. Rome imposed height limits after fires and collapses. Modern codes developed rapidly after disasters such as the Great Fire of London (1666) and the Great Chicago Fire (1871), which drove rules on fire-resistant materials and party walls.

Model codes

Most jurisdictions adopt model codes developed by standards organizations and amend them locally. In the United States the International Code Council publishes the International Building Code (IBC) and International Residential Code (IRC). Europe uses the Eurocodes for structural design. Specialized codes cover fire (e.g., NFPA 101 Life Safety Code), plumbing, electrical work (the National Electrical Code), and mechanical systems.

Scope

Building codes typically regulate structural loads (including seismic and wind design), means of egress, fire protection and alarm systems, plumbing and sanitation, ventilation and indoor air quality, accessibility for people with disabilities (often alongside laws such as the ADA), and energy conservation. Zoning ordinances, which govern land use, are legally distinct from building codes.

Performance vs. prescriptive codes

Traditional codes are prescriptive, specifying exact materials and dimensions. Performance-based codes instead state required outcomes (e.g., safe evacuation time) and let designers demonstrate compliance through engineering analysis, an approach common in fire safety engineering.
