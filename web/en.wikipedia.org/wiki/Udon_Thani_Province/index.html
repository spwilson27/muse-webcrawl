Udon Thani Province

Udon Thani (Thai: อุดรธานี) is one of Thailand's seventy-six provinces, located in the upper northeastern region of the country known as Isan. Its capital city, also called Udon Thani, is one of the major commercial centres of the northeast. The province is internationally known for the Ban Chiang archaeological site, a UNESCO World Heritage Site with evidence of prehistoric bronze-age settlement.

Geography

Udon Thani lies on the Khorat Plateau, about 560 km northeast of Bangkok. Neighbouring provinces include Nong Khai, Bueng Kan, Sakon Nakhon, Kalasin, Khon Kaen, and Loei. The province is largely flat farmland given to rice, cassava, and sugarcane, with the Phu Phan mountain range along its eastern edge.

History

The area is famous for Ban Chiang, settled from the Neolithic period onward, where distinctive painted pottery and early bronze metallurgy, dating back several thousand years, were excavated in the 1960s and 1970s. In the 20th century, Udon Thani grew rapidly during the Vietnam War era, when the United States Air Force operated the large Udorn Royal Thai Air Force Base, bringing an influx of American servicemen and investment. The city remains a regional transport, retail, and medical hub near the Lao border and the First Thai–Lao Friendship Bridge at Nong Khai.
