Nobel Prize

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A row of gold Nobel Prize medals under glass]

The Nobel Prizes are five separate prizes that, according to Alfred Nobel's will of 1895, are awarded to 'those who, during the preceding year, have conferred the greatest benefit to humankind' in Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature and Peace. A sixth, in Economic Sciences, was added in 1968 by Sweden's central bank.

The prizes are presented annually on 10 December, the anniversary of Nobel's death — in Stockholm for the sciences and literature (by the King of Sweden), and in Oslo for the Peace Prize. Each prize includes a gold medal, a diploma and a monetary award (11 million Swedish kronor in recent years).

Laureates and prestige

Over 1,000 individuals and organisations have been laureates; Marie Curie won in two sciences. The prizes are widely considered the most prestigious awards in their fields, though the choices (and omissions) attract perennial debate.
