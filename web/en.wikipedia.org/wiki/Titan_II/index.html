Titan II

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Titan II
TypeICBM / expendable launch vehicle
CountryUnited States
ManufacturerMartin Marietta
In service1963–2003 (variants)
A Titan II launches Gemini.

The Titan II was an American intercontinental ballistic missile and space launch vehicle developed from the Titan I in the early 1960s. The largest ICBM ever deployed by the United States, it carried a single multi-megaton warhead in a silo-based deterrent force — and, in its Gemini Launch Vehicle form, put all ten crewed Project Gemini missions into orbit in 1965–66.

Titan II used storable hypergolic propellants (Aerozine 50 and nitrogen tetroxide) that could remain loaded in the missile for years and ignite on command, a major advantage over earlier cryogenic ICBMs. Its space career continued long after Gemini: Titan II launchers flew from Vandenberg until 2003, mostly carrying weather and military satellites.

Accidents

The missile's deterrent service was marked by two notorious accidents: a 1978 fuel-leak explosion at a silo near Damascus, Arkansas that killed an airman, and the 1980 Damascus incident in which a dropped socket punctured a fuel tank and the subsequent explosion blew the 740-tonne silo door off and ejected the warhead, which was recovered intact nearby. One airman died; the warhead's safety systems prevented any nuclear explosion.
