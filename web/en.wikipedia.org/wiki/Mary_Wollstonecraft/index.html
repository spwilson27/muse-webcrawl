Mary Wollstonecraft (1759–1797) was an English writer and philosopher, author of A Vindication of the Rights of Woman (1792), one of the earliest works of feminist philosophy. Against Rousseau and the conventions of female education she argued that women appear intellectually inferior only because they are denied education: give them the same cultivation of reason and virtue, economic independence and civil standing, and the supposed inferiority would vanish.

Life and legacy

Wollstonecraft answered Burke's Reflections with A Vindication of the Rights of Men (1790), reported from revolutionary Paris, and scandalised Georgian England with her unconventional life — an irregular union, a child out of wedlock, marriage to William Godwin. She died of puerperal fever days after giving birth to her second daughter, Mary Shelley. Godwin's candid Memoirs wrecked her reputation for a century; 20th-century feminists restored her as a founding thinker.
