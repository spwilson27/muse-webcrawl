Newspaper

[Image: A press hall with newspaper webs running through a modern offset press]

A newspaper is a periodical publication of news, opinion and features, printed on newsprint or published digitally, usually daily or weekly. Newspapers emerged in 17th-century Europe (the Strasbourg Relation of 1605 is often cited as the first), became mass media with steam presses and advertising in the 19th century, and dominated news until broadcast, then digital, competition.

Formats and business

Broadsheet, tabloid and Berliner formats reflect different markets; the classic business model — circulation plus advertising — collapsed in the classifieds-and-print-ad era after 2000, driving closures, consolidation and the subscription turn.

Parts

A newspaper traditionally divides into news, business, sport, culture and opinion (editorials, op-eds, letters), produced by reporters, sub-editors and editors under a masthead.
