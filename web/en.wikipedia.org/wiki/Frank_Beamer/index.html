Frank Charles Beamer (born October 18, 1946) is an American former college football coach. He was the head football coach at Virginia Polytechnic Institute and State University (Virginia Tech) from 1987 through the 2015 season, and is widely regarded as one of the most successful coaches in the history of the Virginia Tech Hokies football program.

Early life

Beamer was born in Mount Airy, North Carolina, and grew up in Hillsville, Virginia. He attended Hillsville High School and later Virginia Tech, where he played cornerback for the Hokies from 1966 to 1968 under head coach Jerry Claiborne.

Coaching career

Beamer began his coaching career as a graduate assistant at the University of Maryland in 1972. He served as an assistant coach at The Citadel, Murray State, and Virginia Tech before becoming head coach at Murray State in 1981, where he compiled a 42–23–2 record over six seasons.

In 1987 he returned to Virginia Tech as head coach. After a slow start, Beamer built the Hokies into a national power. The 1999 team, led by quarterback Michael Vick, went undefeated in the regular season and played in the BCS National Championship Game (the 2000 Sugar Bowl), losing to Florida State. Virginia Tech won multiple conference championships, including four ACC titles after joining the Atlantic Coast Conference in 2004.

Beamer Ball

Beamer's teams became famous for "Beamer Ball," an emphasis on scoring in all three phases of the game — offense, defense, and especially special teams. Under Beamer and longtime defensive coordinator Bud Foster, Virginia Tech blocked an extraordinary number of punts and kicks, and its special teams units routinely scored touchdowns. Beamer won 238 games at Virginia Tech and 280 overall, retiring after the 2015 season as one of the winningest coaches in NCAA Division I FBS history.

Honors

Beamer was named the 1999 Associated Press College Football Coach of the Year and was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame in 2018. Virginia Tech retired his No. 25 jersey, an honor his teams awarded weekly to a standout special teams player.
