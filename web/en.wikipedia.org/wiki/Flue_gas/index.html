Flue gas

Flue gas
Main componentsNitrogen, carbon dioxide, water vapor, oxygen
PollutantsParticulates, NOx, SO2, CO, heavy metals
Major sourcesPower plants, industrial furnaces, boilers

Flue gas is the gas exiting to the atmosphere via a flue, which may be a pipe, channel, or chimney for conveying exhaust gases from a fireplace, oven, furnace, boiler, or steam generator. Most flue gas results from the combustion of fossil fuels such as coal, oil, natural gas, and wood.

Composition

The composition of flue gas depends on the fuel and combustion conditions. For most fossil fuels burned in air, the gas consists mainly of nitrogen from the combustion air, together with carbon dioxide and water vapor from the oxidation of the fuel, and a small amount of excess oxygen. Typical flue gas from the combustion of natural gas contains roughly 8–10% carbon dioxide and 17–18% water vapor by volume, with the remainder mostly nitrogen.

Pollutants

Flue gases from fossil fuel combustion contain pollutants including particulate matter (fly ash), sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, volatile organic compounds, and trace heavy metals such as mercury. Flue-gas emissions contribute to air pollution, acid rain, and climate change; carbon dioxide from flue gas is the largest single source of anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions.

Treatment

Industrial facilities treat flue gas before release using technologies such as electrostatic precipitators and baghouses for particulates, flue-gas desulfurization (scrubbers) for sulfur dioxide, selective catalytic reduction for nitrogen oxides, and activated carbon injection for mercury. Continuous emissions monitoring systems measure pollutant concentrations in the stack gas to verify compliance with air quality regulations.
