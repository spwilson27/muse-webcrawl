Stavisky affair

The Stavisky affair was a French financial and political scandal of 1934 centred on the financier Alexandre Stavisky, whose fraudulent bond scheme at the municipal pawnshop of Bayonne implicated prominent politicians of the Third Republic and triggered deadly riots in Paris.

Background

Stavisky, a Ukrainian-born émigré and serial fraudster, issued millions of francs in worthless bonds (bons de caisse) through the Crédit municipal of Bayonne, protected by political connections that repeatedly delayed his prosecution. When the fraud collapsed in December 1933, he fled and was found dead near Chamonix on 8 January 1934 — officially by suicide, though many believed he had been silenced.

Crisis of 6 February 1934

Accusations of a cover-up reaching the government of Camille Chautemps provoked massive demonstrations by far-right leagues in Paris on 6 February 1934. Rioting around the Place de la Concorde and the Chamber of Deputies left around fifteen people dead. The government fell, and Édouard Daladier was replaced by Gaston Doumergue's national-union cabinet. The crisis is often seen as a failed far-right challenge to the Republic and a precursor to the formation of the Popular Front.
