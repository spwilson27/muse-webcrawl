Reserve requirement

The reserve requirement (or cash reserve ratio) is a central bank regulation that sets the minimum amount that a commercial bank must hold in liquid assets as a proportion of its deposits. This minimum amount is generally determined by the central bank on the basis of commercial banks' deposits. The required reserves are normally held at the central bank or as vault cash, and generally cannot be lent out or invested.

Purpose and effects

Reserve requirements were traditionally used as an instrument of monetary policy: raising the ratio reduces the funds available for lending and so contracts the money supply, while lowering it does the opposite (through the money multiplier). In practice, changes in reserve requirements are blunt and disruptive, so many central banks adjust them rarely; China has used the required reserve ratio actively, while the United States reduced reserve requirements to zero in March 2020, and countries such as the United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia operate without binding requirements. Requirements may also serve to protect depositors and to ensure banks hold liquid reserves against withdrawals.
