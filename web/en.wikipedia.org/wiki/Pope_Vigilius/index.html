Pope Vigilius

Pope Vigilius (died 7 June 555) was bishop of Rome from 29 March 537 to his death, in a period overshadowed by the Gothic War and by the doctrinal politics of the Byzantine emperor Justinian I. His pontificate was dominated by the Three Chapters controversy, in which his vacillations under imperial pressure badly damaged his reputation in the West.

Rise to the papacy

Vigilius, a Roman aristocrat, served as papal apocrisiarius (legate) in Constantinople, where he formed ties with the emperor and empress Theodora. After the deposition of Pope Silverius by the general Belisarius, Vigilius was installed as pope in 537 — amid longstanding accusations that he had promised concessions to Theodora regarding the deposed patriarch Anthimus.

Three Chapters controversy

Justinian demanded condemnation of the writings of Theodore of Mopsuestia, Theodoret of Cyrrhus, and Ibas of Edessa — the "Three Chapters". Vigilius was seized in Rome in 545 and taken to Constantinople, where he alternately resisted and yielded, issuing and retracting judgments (Iudicatum of 548, then its withdrawal, then the Constitutum of 553). He refused to attend the Fifth Ecumenical Council (553) but eventually accepted its decrees in 554. Permitted at last to return, he died at Syracuse in Sicily on the journey home. Western churches, especially in Milan and Aquileia, broke communion over the affair.
