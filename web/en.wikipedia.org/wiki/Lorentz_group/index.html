Lorentz group
NotationO(1,3)
TypeLie group
Dimension6
Components4 (connected components)

The Lorentz group, denoted O(1,3), is the group of all Lorentz transformations of Minkowski spacetime — the linear transformations that preserve the spacetime interval. It is the symmetry group of special relativity and plays a fundamental role in modern physics: physical laws on Minkowski space are required to be invariant under it. It is named after the Dutch physicist Hendrik Lorentz.

Definition

A Lorentz transformation is a real 4×4 matrix Λ satisfying ΛT η Λ = η, where η is the Minkowski metric with signature (−,+,+,+) (or the opposite sign convention). The group consists of spatial rotations, Lorentz boosts (transformations between inertial frames in relative motion), and the discrete transformations of parity and time reversal.

Structure

The Lorentz group has four connected components, distinguished by whether a transformation reverses the direction of time and whether it includes a spatial reflection. The identity component — the proper orthochronous Lorentz group, SO+(1,3) — contains the rotations and boosts and is the part continuously connected to the identity. The group is non-compact, six-dimensional (three rotation parameters and three boost parameters), and its Lie algebra is generated by three rotation generators and three boost generators satisfying the Lorentz algebra commutation relations.

The proper orthochronous Lorentz group is doubly covered by the special linear group SL(2,ℂ), a fact of central importance in quantum mechanics: the representations of SL(2,ℂ) give rise to the spinor representations — Weyl and Dirac spinors — used to describe fermions. Wigner's classification of particles in relativistic quantum theory is based on the unitary representations of the Poincaré group, which combines the Lorentz group with spacetime translations.

Applications

Beyond special relativity, Lorentz symmetry is a tested foundation of the Standard Model of particle physics and of general relativity, where the Lorentz group is the local symmetry of the tangent space at each point of spacetime.
