Overview

Haile Selassie I (1892–1975) was regent of Ethiopia (1916–1930) and emperor from 1930 to 1974. Born Ras Tafari Makonnen, he modernised Ethiopia, brought it into the League of Nations (1923) and abolished slavery by decree. Exiled during the Italian occupation (1936–41) after his famous appeal to the League in Geneva, he was restored with British help in 1941.

Legacy

Haile Selassie centralised power, founded the Organisation of African Unity in Addis Ababa (1963) and made Ethiopia a symbol of African independence — the only African state (with Liberia) never formally colonised. But famine in Wollo (1973), feudal landholding and repression eroded his rule; the Derg military junta deposed him in 1974 and he died in custody in 1975. To Rastafari believers, Haile Selassie is God incarnate — the movement took his pre-coronation title, Ras Tafari; his 1966 visit to Jamaica is commemorated as Grounation Day.
