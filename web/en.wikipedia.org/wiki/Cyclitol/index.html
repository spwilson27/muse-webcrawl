Cyclitol

Placeholder image: Cyclitol

A cyclitol is a cycloalkane containing at least three hydroxyl groups, each attached to a different carbon atom of the ring. Cyclitols are a class of polyols that occur widely in plants and microorganisms, where they serve in osmoregulation, stress tolerance, and cell signaling.

Examples

The best-known cyclitols are the inositols — cyclohexanehexols of which myo-inositol is the most abundant and biologically important. Other notable cyclitols include:

  • Pinitol — a methyl ether of D-chiro-inositol found in legumes and pine trees
  • Quebrachitol — found in rubber tree serum and maple
  • Shikimic acid — a cyclohexene derivative that is a key biosynthetic intermediate and the starting material for the antiviral oseltamivir
  • Quinic acid — occurs in coffee beans, cinchona bark, and many fruits
  • Conduritols and inososes — partially oxidized cyclitols studied as glycosidase inhibitors
  • Valiolamine and related aminocyclitols — found in antibiotics and enzyme inhibitors such as voglibose

Biological roles

myo-Inositol is a precursor of phosphatidylinositol lipids and of inositol phosphates such as inositol trisphosphate (IP3), central second messengers in cell signaling. In plants, cyclitols act as compatible solutes protecting against drought and salinity, and as storage and transport carbohydrates. Some cyclitols and their derivatives have pharmaceutical relevance, including antibiotics of the aminoglycoside class built on aminocyclitol scaffolds such as 2-deoxystreptamine.
