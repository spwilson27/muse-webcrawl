Music of Trinidad and Tobago

The music of Trinidad and Tobago is best known for calypso and its descendants soca and chutney soca, and for the steelpan, the only acoustic musical instrument invented in the twentieth century and the national instrument of Trinidad and Tobago.

Calypso

Calypso grew out of African and French creole traditions in Trinidad, developing as a vehicle for social commentary, satire and news sung by calypsonians with stage names such as Lord Invader, Lord Kitchener and the Mighty Sparrow. Early recordings date from the 1910s, and calypso reached international audiences in the 1950s, notably with the success of "Rum and Coca-Cola" and Harry Belafonte's Calypso album. Calypsonians compete for the Calypso Monarch title during the carnival season.

Steelpan

After drumming was restricted under colonial rule, Trinidadians developed instruments from bamboo and then from discarded oil drums; by the 1940s the modern steelpan could play complete melodies. Steelbands compete in the Panorama competition at carnival, performing complex arrangements with dozens of players. The instrument is now played worldwide.

Soca and other genres

In the 1970s Lord Shorty fused calypso with Indian rhythms and soul to create soca, a faster dance music exemplified by artists such as Machel Montano and Bunji Garlin. Indo-Trinidadian chutney music and its hybrid chutney soca, parang (Spanish-influenced Christmas music), rapso, and the brass bands of carnival all contribute to a musical culture centred on the annual Port of Spain carnival.
