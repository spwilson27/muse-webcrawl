Climax locomotive

The Climax locomotive is a type of geared steam locomotive built by the Climax Manufacturing Company (later renamed the Climax Locomotive Works), of Corry, Pennsylvania. These had a design incorporating a central drive shaft with bevel gears driving multiple axles. Intended primarily for logging railroads, Climax locomotives could negotiate the rough, temporary track, sharp curves and steep grades typical of forest operations.

Design

Unlike the rival Shay locomotive, whose cylinders were mounted vertically on one side, the Climax used conventional horizontal or inclined cylinders on both sides driving a transverse crankshaft. A longitudinal driveshaft, geared down from the crankshaft, ran along the centreline of the locomotive and drove each truck through bevel gears. The gearing gave high tractive effort at low speed, suiting heavy log trains on grades of 10% or more.

Classes

Climax locomotives were produced in three broad classes. Class A engines were small two-truck locomotives with a vertical boiler and two-speed gearbox. Class B engines had a conventional horizontal boiler, inclined cylinders and two trucks. The larger Class C engines had three powered trucks and could weigh up to about 100 tons. In total, more than 1,000 Climax locomotives were built between the 1880s and 1928.

Preservation

A number of Climax locomotives survive. Notable operating examples include engines at Cass Scenic Railroad State Park in West Virginia and the Roaring Camp Railroads in California.
