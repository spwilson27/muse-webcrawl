Job analysis

Job analysis (also known as work analysis) is a family of procedures used in industrial and organizational psychology and human resource management to identify the content of a job in terms of the activities it involves and the attributes or requirements necessary to perform those activities. Job analysis provides information to organizations that helps them determine which employees are best fit for specific jobs.

Purpose

The products of job analysis are the job description, which sets out the tasks, duties and responsibilities of a role, and the person specification (or job specification), which describes the knowledge, skills, abilities and other characteristics (KSAOs) a job holder needs. These outputs underpin recruitment and selection, training needs analysis, performance appraisal, job evaluation and pay structures, and compliance with employment legislation.

Methods

Analysts gather data through observation of incumbents, structured and unstructured interviews, questionnaires such as the Position Analysis Questionnaire (PAQ), work diaries and logs, and critical incident techniques. Modern practice also draws on occupational databases such as O*NET in the United States.

Approaches

Task-oriented approaches catalogue the work performed, while worker-oriented approaches, sometimes called job element methods, focus on the human attributes the work demands. Competency modelling extends job analysis by linking job requirements to broader organisational strategy.
