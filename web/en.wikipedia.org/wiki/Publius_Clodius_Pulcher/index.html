Publius Clodius Pulcher (c. 93 – 52 BC) was a populist Roman politician and street agitator of the late Republic. A patrician of the great Claudian house who had himself adopted into a plebeian family so that he could hold the tribunate, he weaponised legislation and organised violence against his enemies — above all Cicero — and his murder by a rival gang in 52 BC plunged Rome into crisis.

Clodius was killed on the Appian Way near Bovillae.

Early career and the Bona Dea scandal

Clodius — he respelt his name from Claudius to signal popular sympathies — served in the East under his brother-in-law Lucullus, stirring up a mutiny among the troops, and was later captured by pirates in Cilicia. In December 62 BC he was caught disguised as a woman at the women-only rites of the Bona Dea, held at Julius Caesar's official residence; the ensuing sacrilege trial was a sensation, ending in acquittal amid open talk of bribed jurors. Caesar divorced his wife Pompeia over the affair, remarking that Caesar's wife must be above suspicion. The presiding pontiffs included Cicero, whose testimony destroyed Clodius's alibi — the origin of their lethal feud.

Tribunate

Adopted into a plebeian family in 59 BC with Caesar's connivance, Clodius became tribune for 58 BC and passed a programme of popular laws: free grain for the Roman poor, restoration of the guilds (collegia) that became his street gangs, limits on magisterial obstruction by omens — and a law exiling anyone who had executed citizens without trial, aimed at Cicero, who had put the Catilinarian conspirators to death. Cicero fled into exile and his Palatine house was demolished and its site consecrated to Liberty. Clodius soon turned against Pompey as well, harbouring the captured Armenian prince Tigranes and attacking Pompey's eastern settlement.

Violence and death

Through the 50s BC Clodius's gangs fought those of Titus Annius Milo for control of the streets. As aedile in 56 BC he prosecuted Milo; as candidate for the praetorship he continued the feud. On 18 January 52 BC the two retinues met by chance on the Appian Way near Bovillae; Clodius was wounded, carried into a tavern, and dragged out and killed on Milo's orders. His body was brought to Rome, where the mob burned the Senate house as his funeral pyre. The Senate responded with an emergency decree, Pompey was made sole consul, and Milo was convicted of murder and exiled despite Cicero's defence.
