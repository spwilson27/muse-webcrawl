Passion play

A Passion play or Easter pageant is a dramatic presentation depicting the Passion of Jesus Christ: his trial, suffering and crucifixion. Passion plays arose from medieval liturgical drama and mystery plays performed in churches and town squares during Holy Week.

The Oberammergau Passion Play in Germany, staged roughly every ten years since 1634.

History

The origins of the Passion play lie in the Quem quaeritis trope of the Easter liturgy and the expansion of church drama in the 12th–15th centuries, when cycles of mystery plays in vernacular languages were performed by guilds. During the Reformation, Passion plays were suppressed in Protestant regions and came under suspicion in Catholic ones as well, but the tradition survived in parts of Germany, Austria, and elsewhere.

Oberammergau

The most famous Passion play is that of Oberammergau in Bavaria. According to tradition, the villagers vowed in 1633, during the Thirty Years' War and an outbreak of plague, to stage the play every ten years if their village were spared; the first performance took place in 1634. It has since been performed roughly decennially (in years ending in 0, with exceptions), most recently in 2022 after postponement from 2020. Hundreds of villagers take part, and performances run for several months before audiences from around the world.

Other notable plays

Other long-standing Passion plays include those at Sordevolo in Italy, St. Margarethen in Austria, Škofja Loka in Slovenia (inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage), and numerous community productions in the Philippines, Mexico, Brazil, and the United States. Passion plays have historically sometimes been criticised for antisemitic portrayals, and many productions since the mid-20th century have revised their scripts in light of the Second Vatican Council's Nostra aetate (1965).
