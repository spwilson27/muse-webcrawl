Easy listening (also known as light music or mood music) is a popular music genre and radio format that was most popular during the 1950s to 1970s. It is related to middle-of-the-road (MOR) music and encompasses instrumental recordings of standards, hit songs, film themes, and non-rock popular music, arranged for lush orchestras and designed to be relaxing, unobtrusive background listening rather than the focus of attention.

Characteristics

Easy listening arrangements typically feature sweeping string sections, gentle rhythms, and soft, wordless vocal choruses, with familiar melodies drawn from the Great American Songbook, Broadway shows, Hollywood films, and instrumental versions of contemporary pop hits. Performers such as Percy Faith ("Theme from A Summer Place"), Mantovani, Henry Mancini, and Bert Kaempfert sold millions of albums, while singers including Andy Williams, Perry Como, and Jack Jones fronted the vocal side of the style.

Radio format

As FM radio expanded in the 1960s and 1970s, "beautiful music" stations programmed easy listening instrumentals in quarter-hour sweeps, aiming at an affluent adult audience attractive to advertisers. The format dominated FM ratings in many markets but declined from the late 1970s as its audience aged and tastes shifted toward soft rock and adult contemporary. Variants survive in nostalgia formats, online streams, and the retro "lounge" and exotica revival of the 1990s.

Related styles

Easy listening overlaps with lounge music, space-age pop, exotica, light orchestral music, and the programmed background music of Muzak, sometimes derisively called "elevator music." Its legacy persists in chill-out, downtempo, and the string arrangements of adult contemporary pop.
