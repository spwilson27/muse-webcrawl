The Northern Mariana Islands, officially the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands (CNMI), is an unincorporated territory and commonwealth of the United States in the northwestern Pacific Ocean. It consists of 14 islands in the Mariana archipelago, the southernmost of which, Guam, is a separate U.S. territory. Most of the population lives on Saipan, Tinian and Rota.

History

The islands were settled by the Chamorro people about 3,500 years ago, and their ancient latte stone pillars remain a national symbol. Claimed by Spain and named for Queen Mariana of Austria, the islands were sold to Germany in 1899, seized by Japan in 1914, and administered by Japan under a League of Nations mandate. In 1944 the United States captured Saipan and Tinian in fierce battles; Tinian was the departure point for the atomic bomb missions of August 1945. After the war the islands formed part of the U.S.-administered Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands.

Commonwealth status

In 1975 the islanders voted to enter a political union with the United States under a Covenant, becoming a commonwealth in 1978; residents became U.S. citizens in 1986. The economy depends on tourism — especially from Japan, Korea and China — and on federal support; a garment industry that once employed many migrant workers ended in 2009. The commonwealth suffered severe damage from Super Typhoon Yutu in 2018.
