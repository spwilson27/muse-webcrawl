Walther Penck

Walther Penck (August 30, 1888 – February 29, 1923) was a German geologist and geomorphologist known for his theories of landscape evolution, which he developed in opposition to the geographical cycle model of William Morris Davis. He was the son of geographer Albrecht Penck.

Work

Penck studied in Heidelberg and worked in Argentina's geological survey before returning to Germany, teaching at Leipzig. His major work, Die morphologische Analyse, was published posthumously in 1924. In it he argued that landforms reflect the relative rates of tectonic uplift and denudation: slopes evolve according to whether uplift is accelerating, constant, or decelerating, producing convex, straight, or concave slope profiles respectively.

Penck introduced terms such as Piedmonttreppen (piedmont steps) and emphasised that slope retreat and the development of broad erosion surfaces (endrumpf) depend on tectonic history rather than on a simple progression through "youth", "maturity", and "old age" as in Davis's cycle of erosion. His ideas, hampered by the difficult German of his posthumous book and its imperfect English translation, were long misunderstood in the Anglophone world but influenced later German geomorphology and modern studies of tectonic geomorphology.
