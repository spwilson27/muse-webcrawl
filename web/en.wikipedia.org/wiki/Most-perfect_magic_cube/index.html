Most-perfect magic cube

A most-perfect magic cube is a magic cube in which every line parallel to an axis, every space diagonal, and — in the stronger sense inherited from most-perfect magic squares — every pandiagonal section (including "broken" diagonals wrapped around the cube) sums to the magic constant. In addition, the pandiagonal property must hold in all three orientations of planar sections.

More precisely, a most-perfect magic cube of order n is a pandiagonal magic cube that is also "compact" and "complete": any 2×2×2 subcube (including those formed by wrapping around the faces) sums to the same constant, and pairs of cells a half-order apart along any diagonal sum to half the value of a complementary pair constant. For an order-n cube using the numbers 1 to n³, the magic constant is n(n³ + 1)/2.

Most-perfect magic cubes exist only for orders divisible by 4 (doubly even orders). They generalise the most-perfect magic squares studied in the plane, and constructions typically exploit binary (base-2) patterns and linear transformations from complementary number pairs. The smallest example has order 4 and magic constant 130.
