Kronborg

Kronborg Castle, Helsingør, Denmark

Kronborg is a castle and stronghold in the town of Helsingør, Denmark. Immortalised as Elsinore in William Shakespeare's play Hamlet, it is one of the most important Renaissance castles in Northern Europe. Standing at the narrowest point of the Øresund, the sound between Denmark and Sweden, Kronborg controlled the entrance to the Baltic Sea and the collection of the Sound Dues from passing ships. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000.

History

The site was first fortified in the 1420s by Eric of Pomerania, who built the stronghold Krogen to enforce the Sound toll. Between 1574 and 1585 Frederick II rebuilt it into a magnificent Renaissance castle with sandstone façades, spires and rich interiors. The castle burned in 1629 but was rebuilt under Christian IV, and in 1658 it was captured by Swedish forces during the Dano-Swedish War. It served as a barracks from 1785 to 1922 and now functions as a museum.

Architecture

Kronborg's four wings enclose a courtyard and include the 62-metre-long Knights' Hall, one of the largest banqueting halls of its era in Northern Europe, and a chapel that survived the 1629 fire with its Renaissance interior intact. Extensive casemates run beneath the castle, associated with the legend of Holger Danske, the sleeping hero said to awaken in Denmark's hour of need.

Hamlet

Shakespeare set Hamlet at Elsinore, though he never visited Denmark; the play has been performed in the castle courtyard regularly since 1816, with productions featuring actors such as Laurence Olivier, John Gielgud and Jude Law.
