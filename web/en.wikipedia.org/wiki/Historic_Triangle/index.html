Historic Triangle

The Historic Triangle is a region on the Virginia Peninsula in the United States formed by three historic colonial communities — Jamestown, Williamsburg, and Yorktown — which together span the history of British colonial America from the first permanent settlement in 1607 to independence. The three sites are connected by the scenic Colonial Parkway and are among the most visited heritage destinations in the country.

The three points of the Historic Triangle on the Virginia Peninsula

The three sites

Jamestown, founded in 1607 by the Virginia Company, was the first permanent English settlement in the Americas. It is commemorated by two neighbouring sites: Historic Jamestowne, the archaeological site of the original fort administered by Preservation Virginia and the National Park Service, and Jamestown Settlement, a living-history museum with replicas of the colonists' ships and fort.

Williamsburg became the capital of the Colony of Virginia in 1699 and was the scene of much of the political agitation leading to the American Revolution. From 1926 the philanthropist John D. Rockefeller Jr. funded the restoration of the town's colonial core as Colonial Williamsburg, now the largest living-history museum in the United States.

Yorktown is where the British army under Lord Cornwallis surrendered to George Washington and his French allies in October 1781, effectively ending the Revolutionary War. The battlefield is preserved as part of Colonial National Historical Park, and the American Revolution Museum at Yorktown interprets the war.

Tourism

The Historic Triangle is marketed as a single destination; combined annual visitor numbers for its attractions run into the millions. A free shuttle and the Colonial Parkway, built between 1930 and 1957, link the three points, and nearby attractions include Busch Gardens Williamsburg and the College of William & Mary's campus in Williamsburg. The 400th anniversary of Jamestown in 2007 was marked by a state-wide commemoration attended by Queen Elizabeth II.
