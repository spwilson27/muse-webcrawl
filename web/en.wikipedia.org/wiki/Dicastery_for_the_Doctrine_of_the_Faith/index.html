Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith

TypeDicastery of the Roman Curia
Established1542 (as the Congregation of the Roman and Universal Inquisition)
HeadquartersPalace of the Holy Office, Vatican City

The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith (Latin: Dicasterium pro Doctrina Fidei) is one of the oldest and most important departments of the Roman Curia, the central administration of the Holy See. Its mission is to promote and safeguard the doctrine and morals of the Catholic Church throughout the world.

History

The dicastery traces its origins to the Supreme Sacred Congregation of the Roman and Universal Inquisition, established by Pope Paul III in 1542 to combat heresy. Renamed the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith by Pope Paul VI in 1965, it took its present name under Pope Francis's 2022 apostolic constitution Praedicate evangelium.

Functions

The dicastery examines writings and teachings for conformity with Catholic doctrine, handles cases involving serious offences (delicta graviora) reserved to the Holy See, and oversees doctrinal commissions. Among its best-known prefects was Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger, who served from 1981 until his election as Pope Benedict XVI in 2005.
