The Chieftains

The Chieftains are a traditional Irish folk band formed in Dublin in 1962 by uilleann piper Paddy Moloney, fiddler Martin Fay, and tin-whistle player Seán Potts. Widely credited with popularising Irish traditional music throughout the world, they have won six Grammy Awards, recorded dozens of albums, and been designated Ireland's Musical Ambassadors. Following Moloney's death in October 2021, the band's future activities have been carried forward by long-serving members and guests.

History

The group grew out of the Ceoltóirí Chualann ensemble led by Seán Ó Riada, in which Moloney played. Their first album, The Chieftains (1964), was a modest release for the Claddagh label, but through the 1970s — after signing with Island Records and turning professional — albums such as The Chieftains 5 and the soundtrack to Stanley Kubrick's Barry Lyndon (1975), for which they won recognition including an Academy Award-associated success with "Women of Ireland", brought them international fame. Harpist Derek Bell joined in 1975, adding a distinctive colour; flautist Matt Molloy and singer/bodhrán player Kevin Conneff joined in 1979.

Collaborations

The Chieftains are famous for crossing musical borders: Irish Heartbeat with Van Morrison (1988), The Long Black Veil with rock and country stars (1995), Santiago exploring Galician music (Grammy winner), Tears of Stone with women artists, San Patricio with Ry Cooder and Mexican musicians, and Voice of Ages with a new generation of acts. They performed for Pope John Paul II in Dublin in 1979 before an audience of over a million, played on the Great Wall of China, and were the first Western group to do so.

Legacy

Over six decades the Chieftains transformed Irish music from a local tradition into a global genre and influenced countless folk and world-music ensembles. In 2012, marking their 50th anniversary, they were honoured in Ireland and abroad; the "Voice of Ages" tour included a performance whose recording was played aboard the International Space Station — fitting for a band that also supplied music for NASA-related tributes.
