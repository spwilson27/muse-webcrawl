IEEE 802.11ax

IEEE 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6 / Wi-Fi 6E)
Published: 2021 (work began 2014)
Bands: 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz; 6 GHz (Wi-Fi 6E)
Max data rate: ~9.6 Gbit/s
Predecessor: 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5)
Successor: 802.11be (Wi-Fi 7)

IEEE 802.11ax, marketed as Wi-Fi 6 by the Wi-Fi Alliance, is a wireless networking standard in the IEEE 802.11 set, designed to improve spectral efficiency and throughput in dense environments such as stadiums, airports, and apartment buildings. An extension called Wi-Fi 6E adds operation in the 6 GHz band. The standard was formally published in 2021, though certified products shipped from 2019.

Key features

The headline technology is OFDMA (orthogonal frequency-division multiple access), which subdivides a channel into small resource units so a single transmission can serve many clients at once, reducing latency and contention. 802.11ax extends multi-user MIMO to eight spatial streams and supports uplink MU-MIMO, uses 1024-QAM modulation for a 25% peak-rate increase over 802.11ac, and adds BSS coloring to reduce interference between neighboring networks. Target Wake Time lets access points schedule client wake-ups, saving battery in phones and IoT devices.

Performance

With 160 MHz channels and eight streams, the theoretical maximum PHY rate is about 9.6 Gbit/s. Typical real-world gains are roughly fourfold average throughput per user in crowded deployments compared with Wi-Fi 5, rather than large increases in single-client peak speed.

Adoption

Wi-Fi 6 became the mainstream standard in routers, laptops, and smartphones during the early 2020s. Wi-Fi 6E devices exploit the 1,200 MHz of new spectrum opened at 6 GHz in the United States and other countries. Its successor, Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be), was certified from 2024.
