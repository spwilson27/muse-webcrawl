Membranophone

A membranophone is any musical instrument that produces sound primarily through the vibration of a stretched membrane. It is one of the five main categories in the Hornbostel–Sachs system of musical instrument classification. Most drums are membranophones, as are instruments such as the kazoo, in which a membrane modifies the sound of the player's voice (a mirliton).

Classification

Within the Hornbostel–Sachs system, membranophones are class 2. They are subdivided according to how the membrane is set into vibration and the shape of the instrument:

  • Struck drums – the membrane is hit directly (by hand, stick, or beater) or indirectly; includes kettle-shaped, tubular, and frame drums such as the timpani, tabla, conga, and bodhrán.
  • Plucked drums – a string attached to the membrane is plucked, as in the Indian gopichand (ektara).
  • Friction drums – the membrane is made to vibrate by friction from a stick or cord passing through it, as in the cuíca and the lion's roar.
  • Singing membranes (mirlitons) – the membrane vibrates sympathetically with the voice or another sound, as in the kazoo.

Drums

Drums consist of at least one membrane, historically of animal skin and now often of synthetic material, stretched over a shell. They are among the oldest and most ubiquitous musical instruments in the world, used in ritual, communication, military signalling, and virtually every musical tradition. Membrane tension determines pitch, which is why drums such as the timpani and tabla can be tuned to definite pitches while others produce indefinite pitch.
