Ventspils

Ventspils is a city in northwestern Latvia in the historical region of Courland, situated on the Venta River and the Baltic Sea. It is one of Latvia's state cities, with a population of roughly 33,000, and is one of the country's busiest ports.

History

Ventspils developed around a Livonian Order castle built in the 13th century at the mouth of the Venta (German: Windau). It joined the Hanseatic League and flourished under the Duchy of Courland in the 17th century, when Duke Jacob Kettler built a major shipyard and trading fleet there. The city later passed through Swedish, Polish and Russian rule before becoming part of independent Latvia.

Port and economy

The ice-free port of Ventspils has long handled transit trade, especially oil and petroleum products from the east, as well as coal, grain and containers. The city is known for its flower sculptures, cow statues, beaches and the Ventspils Olympic Centre.

Culture

Landmarks include the Livonian Order Castle, now a museum; the narrow-gauge Seaside Open-Air Museum railway; and the Venta Rapid (Ventas Rumba) upstream, among the widest waterfalls in Europe.
