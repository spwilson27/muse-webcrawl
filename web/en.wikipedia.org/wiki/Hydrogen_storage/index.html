Hydrogen storage

Hydrogen storage is a key enabling technology for the advancement of hydrogen and fuel cell technologies in applications including stationary power, portable power, and transportation. Because hydrogen has very low density at ambient conditions, storing enough of it compactly and safely is challenging.

Methods

Established methods include compression in high-pressure tanks (350–700 bar), liquefaction at −253 °C, and storage in underground salt caverns for grid-scale quantities. Materials-based approaches under research include metal hydrides, chemical hydrides, liquid organic hydrogen carriers (LOHCs), and adsorption in porous materials such as metal-organic frameworks.

Challenges

Trade-offs involve energy density by weight and volume, cost, boil-off losses for liquid hydrogen, embrittlement of metals, and the energy cost of compression or liquefaction. Targets set by bodies such as the US Department of Energy guide research for on-board vehicle storage.
