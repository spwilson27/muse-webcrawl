Biedermeier

Biedermeier period
Years1815–1848
RegionCentral Europe, esp. German states and Austria
FieldsFurniture, painting, literature, music, interior design

The Biedermeier period was an era in German-speaking Central Europe between 1815 (the Congress of Vienna) and 1848 (the revolutions of 1848), in which the middle class grew in number and came to dominate the arts. The term derives from "Gottlieb Biedermaier," a satirical pseudonymous poet created by Ludwig Eichrodt and Adolf Kussmaul to mock the apolitical, domestic respectability of the age.

Characteristics

After the Napoleonic Wars and under the repressive Carlsbad Decrees, public political life was restricted, and culture turned inward toward the home. Biedermeier style emphasized cosiness (Gemütlichkeit), family life, nature walks, music-making in the home, and modest, functional elegance. Furniture featured light native woods such as birch and cherry, simple lines, and comfortable upholstery in place of Empire grandeur.

Arts

Painters such as Carl Spitzweg and Ferdinand Georg Waldmüller captured everyday middle-class scenes and landscapes. In literature, the period includes Eduard Mörike, Annette von Droste-Hülshoff, and the later work of Adalbert Stifter; in music, Franz Schubert's songs and salon pieces epitomize the domestic musical culture of the era. Biedermeier ideals later influenced Scandinavian design and the Arts and Crafts movement.
