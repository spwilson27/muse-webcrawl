Automatic train control


Image placeholder: ATC cab equipment

Automatic train control (ATC) is a general class of train protection systems for railways that involves a speed control mechanism in response to external inputs. ATC systems automatically apply the brakes if the driver fails to observe speed restrictions or signals, providing enforcement beyond warnings alone.

Description

ATC implementations range from simple automatic train stop devices at signals — used on rapid-transit systems such as parts of the New York City Subway — to continuous systems with in-cab signalling. In Japanese usage, ATC (自動列車制御装置) denotes the system developed for the Shinkansen, in which track circuits transmit digital (later analogue) speed indications to the train, and on-board equipment continuously supervises speed against the permitted limit for each block, replacing lineside signals entirely. Variants include ATC-NS and the digital D-ATC introduced on the JR East network.

Related systems

Related technologies include automatic train protection (ATP), which enforces signal and speed compliance; automatic train operation (ATO), which drives the train; and communications-based train control (CBTC) used on many metro systems. In Europe, the European Train Control System (ETCS) provides interoperable ATP/ATC functionality, while the United States mandates Positive Train Control (PTC) on most main lines following the Rail Safety Improvement Act of 2008.
