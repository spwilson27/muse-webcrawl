Row echelon form

In linear algebra, a matrix is in row echelon form if: all rows consisting entirely of zeros are at the bottom; and the leading entry (pivot) of each non-zero row occurs strictly to the right of the leading entry of the row above it. In reduced row echelon form, additionally, every leading entry is 1 and is the only non-zero entry in its column.

Gaussian elimination

Any matrix can be transformed into row echelon form by a sequence of elementary row operations — swapping rows, scaling a row, and adding a multiple of one row to another — a procedure known as Gaussian elimination. Continuing to reduced row echelon form (Gauss–Jordan elimination) yields a form that is unique for a given matrix.

Applications

Row echelon form reveals the rank as the number of pivots, makes systems of linear equations solvable by back-substitution, and reduces questions about linear independence, bases and determinants to straightforward computations.
