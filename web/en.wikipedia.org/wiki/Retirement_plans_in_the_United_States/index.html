Retirement plans in the United States

Retirement plans in the United States are arrangements that allow individuals to accumulate savings for retirement with various tax advantages, supplementing the federal Social Security programme. They are broadly divided into employer-sponsored plans and individual accounts, and into defined benefit plans (which promise a benefit based on salary and service) and defined contribution plans (which depend on contributions and investment returns).

Employer plans

The best-known defined contribution plan is the 401(k), named for a section of the Internal Revenue Code, under which employees defer salary into individual accounts, often with an employer matching contribution. Related plans include the 403(b) for employees of schools and non-profit organisations and the 457(b) for state and local government workers. Simplified arrangements for small employers include SEP IRAs and SIMPLE plans. Traditional defined benefit pensions, once common in large companies and the public sector, guarantee a lifetime annuity and place investment risk on the employer; most are regulated under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA).

Individual accounts

The Individual Retirement Account (IRA) lets individuals save independently of an employer. Traditional IRA contributions may be tax-deductible with tax deferred until withdrawal; Roth IRA contributions are made after tax but qualified withdrawals are tax-free. Withdrawals before age 59½ are generally penalised, and required minimum distributions apply in later life for pre-tax accounts. Annual contribution limits are set by statute and adjusted for inflation; legislation such as the SECURE Acts of 2019 and 2022 has adjusted ages, limits and catch-up contributions.

Social Security

Social Security's Old-Age and Survivors Insurance provides a basic, inflation-indexed benefit funded by payroll taxes and is the foundation of most Americans' retirement income, though it is formally a social insurance programme rather than an individual savings plan.
