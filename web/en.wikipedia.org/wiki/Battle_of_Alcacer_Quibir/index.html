Battle of Alcácer Quibir

The Battle of Alcácer Quibir (also known as the Battle of the Three Kings) was fought in northern Morocco, near the town of Ksar-el-Kebir (Alcácer Quibir), on 4 August 1578. The Portuguese king Sebastian I, allied with the deposed Moroccan sultan Abu Abdallah Mohammed II, was decisively defeated by the army of the reigning sultan Abd al-Malik, supported by the Ottoman Empire. All three kings — Sebastian, Abd al-Malik, and Abu Abdallah — died in or as a result of the battle.

Background

King Sebastian of Portugal, raised on crusading ideals, sought glory in Morocco after the deposed sultan Abu Abdallah Mohammed II appealed for help against his uncle Abd al-Malik, who had seized the throne with Ottoman support. Despite warnings from his uncle Philip II of Spain and his own nobles, Sebastian raised a large expedition — some 17,000 Portuguese troops plus foreign mercenaries — and landed in Morocco in the summer of 1578. Abd al-Malik, gravely ill, nevertheless assembled a much larger army and lured the Portuguese inland toward Ksar-el-Kebir.

Battle

On 4 August the armies met on a plain between two rivers. The Portuguese formed a large infantry square with cavalry on the wings. The Moroccan army, superior in cavalry and numbers, enveloped the Portuguese position. In the fighting, Abu Abdallah drowned fleeing the field, Abd al-Malik died of his illness (or, by some accounts, was poisoned), and Sebastian was killed — though his body was never conclusively identified. The Portuguese army was annihilated; thousands were killed and thousands more captured and ransomed.

Aftermath

The disaster left Portugal without a king or heir: Sebastian's successor, the aged Cardinal Henry, died in 1580, precipitating a succession crisis that ended with Philip II of Spain taking the Portuguese crown and creating the Iberian Union. In Morocco, Ahmad al-Mansur succeeded his brother Abd al-Malik and used the ransoms of Portuguese prisoners to finance his celebrated reign. The mystery of Sebastian's fate gave rise to Sebastianism, the belief that the king would one day return to save Portugal.
