Star (graph theory)

In graph theory, a star Sk is the complete bipartite graph K1, k: a tree with one internal node and k leaves (but no internal nodes when k ≤ 1). Alternately, some authors define Sk to be the tree of order k with maximum diameter 2; in which case a star with k ≥ 2 has k − 1 leaves.

Properties

A star with 3 edges is called a claw. Stars are the basic examples of complete bipartite graphs besides single edges, and they form the building blocks of star forests. Every tree is a star if and only if it has diameter at most 2. The star graphs are edge-graceful. Claw-free graphs are an important class in structural graph theory; stars also appear in network topology, where a star network has a single central hub connected to all other nodes.

Related concepts

A star forest is a forest whose connected components are stars. In chemistry, star graphs are used to model the structure of certain molecules. The centre of a star is the unique vertex of degree greater than one (for k > 2).
