Dungeons & Dragons

Dungeons & Dragons
Designers: Gary Gygax, Dave Arneson
Publisher: TSR (1974–1997), Wizards of the Coast (1997– )
First published: 1974
Genre: Fantasy tabletop role-playing game

Dungeons & Dragons (commonly abbreviated as D&D or DnD) is a fantasy tabletop role-playing game originally created and designed by Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson. It was first published in 1974 by Tactical Studies Rules (TSR). It has been published by Wizards of the Coast, a subsidiary of Hasbro, since 1997. The game was derived from miniature wargames, with a variation of the 1971 game Chainmail serving as the initial rule system. D&D's publication is commonly recognized as the beginning of modern role-playing games and the role-playing game industry, which also deeply influenced video games.

Game overview

D&D departs from traditional wargaming by allowing each player to create their own character to play instead of a military formation. These characters embark upon adventures within a fantasy setting. A Dungeon Master (DM) serves as referee and storyteller, maintaining the setting and playing the role of the inhabitants of the game world, known as non-player characters (NPCs). The characters form a party and interact with the setting's inhabitants. Together they solve dilemmas, engage in battles, explore, and gather treasure and knowledge. In the process, characters earn experience points (XP) to level up, becoming increasingly powerful over sessions.

Outcomes are often determined by rolling polyhedral dice, most iconically the twenty-sided die (d20). The game's success led to many other tabletop role-playing systems, yet D&D has remained the market leader for most of its history.

History

The first edition appeared in 1974 as a boxed set. Advanced Dungeons & Dragons followed from 1977. The second edition was published in 1989, the third in 2000 (revised as 3.5 in 2003), the fourth in 2008, and the fifth edition in 2014. The fifth edition, alongside renewed interest through actual-play series such as Critical Role and appearances in popular culture including Stranger Things, drove a major resurgence, making D&D more popular than ever by the late 2010s.

Editions and settings

Popular published settings include the Forgotten Realms, Eberron, Dragonlance, Dark Sun, Ravenloft, and Greyhawk — the earliest campaign setting, developed by Gygax himself.

Cultural impact

D&D has influenced fantasy literature, film, and especially video games; genres such as the computer role-playing game trace their conventions — hit points, character classes, levels — directly to it. The game was inducted into the National Toy Hall of Fame in 2016. See also Gary Gygax and tabletop role-playing game.
