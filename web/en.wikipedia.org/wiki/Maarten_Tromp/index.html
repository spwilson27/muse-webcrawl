Maarten Harpertszoon Tromp (23 April 1598 – 10 August 1653) was an officer and later admiral in the Dutch navy. One of the Netherlands' greatest naval heroes, he commanded the Dutch fleet through the final phase of the Eighty Years' War against Spain and the opening of the First Anglo-Dutch War against England.

Career

Tromp went to sea as a boy, was captured by English pirates and Barbary corsairs, and rose through service with the Dutch East India Company and the navy. In 1639, as lieutenant-admiral of Holland, he decisively defeated a large Spanish fleet at the Battle of the Downs, fought in English waters with the English fleet looking on — effectively ending Spanish sea power in northern Europe. In the First Anglo-Dutch War (1652–1654) he fought Robert Blake in a series of hard battles; legend has him hoisting a broom at his masthead to signify he had swept the English from the seas. Tromp was killed by a sharpshooter aboard his flagship Brederode during the Battle of Scheveningen in August 1653. His son Cornelis Tromp also became a celebrated admiral.
