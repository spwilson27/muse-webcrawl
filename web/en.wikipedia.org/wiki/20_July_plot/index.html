Date20 July 1944
LocationWolf's Lair, near Rastenburg, East Prussia
TargetAdolf Hitler

The 20 July plot was a failed attempt by German Army officers and conservative resistance figures to assassinate Adolf Hitler and overthrow the Nazi regime on 20 July 1944. Colonel Claus von Stauffenberg planted a bomb in a conference room at Hitler's Wolf's Lair headquarters; Hitler survived, shielded by a heavy table leg, and the associated coup — a modified version of the emergency plan Operation Valkyrie — collapsed within hours in Berlin.

The conspirators

The conspiracy drew on a long-standing circle of conservative and military opponents of Hitler, including Ludwig Beck, Carl Goerdeler, Henning von Tresckow and Friedrich Olbricht. The plotters intended to kill Hitler, seize power with the Reserve Army, and negotiate an end to the war with the Western Allies.

The attempt

Stauffenberg, newly appointed chief of staff of the Reserve Army, placed a briefcase containing a bomb under the map table near Hitler and left on a pretext. At 12:42 p.m. the charge exploded, killing four men, but Hitler escaped with ruptured eardrums and minor wounds. Stauffenberg, believing Hitler dead, flew to Berlin to trigger Valkyrie, but delays, hesitation, and the refusal of key officers — and the survival of Hitler, confirmed by phone — doomed the rising. Major Otto Ernst Remer, ordered to arrest Goebbels, was put on the phone to Hitler and instead crushed the coup.

Aftermath

Stauffenberg and several others were shot by firing squad that same night in the Bendlerblock courtyard. In the following months the Gestapo arrested around 7,000 people; nearly 5,000 were executed, many after show trials before Roland Freisler's People's Court. The plot has since become the central symbol of German resistance to Nazism, commemorated annually in Germany.
