The Iron Bridge

LocationIronbridge Gorge, Shropshire, England
Opened1 January 1781
Span100 ft 6 in (30.63 m)
MaterialCast iron, 384 tons
DesignerThomas Farnolls Pritchard; built by Abraham Darby III

The Iron Bridge is a cast iron arch bridge that crosses the River Severn in Shropshire, England. Opened in 1781, it was the first major bridge in the world to be made of cast iron, and became a symbol of the Industrial Revolution.

Construction

The bridge was designed by architect Thomas Farnolls Pritchard and cast and erected by Abraham Darby III at his Coalbrookdale works. Its five parallel ribs span just over 30 metres. Remarkably, the ironwork was assembled using carpentry joints — mortise and tenon, dovetails and wedges — rather than bolts or rivets, reflecting the woodworking traditions of the craftsmen involved. Roughly 384 tons of iron were used.

History

The gorge was a centre of ironmaking, and the bridge replaced a ferry linking the parishes of Broseley and Madeley, advertising the potential of cast iron to the world. It carried vehicles until 1934 and is now a pedestrian bridge, a scheduled monument, a Grade I listed structure and part of the Ironbridge Gorge UNESCO World Heritage Site. In 2017–2018 English Heritage carried out a major £3.6 million conservation project, repainting the bridge in its original red-brown colour.
