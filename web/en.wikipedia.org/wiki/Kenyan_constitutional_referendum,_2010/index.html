A constitutional referendum was held in Kenya on 4 August 2010. Voters were asked whether they approved of a proposed new constitution, which had been passed by the National Assembly on 1 April 2010. The new constitution was approved by approximately two-thirds of voters, and was promulgated on 27 August 2010, replacing the independence constitution of 1963 (as amended).

Background

Demands for constitutional reform had been a central theme of Kenyan politics since the 1990s. A previous draft constitution was rejected in a 2005 referendum. The violence that followed the disputed 2007 presidential election, in which more than 1,000 people were killed, gave new urgency to reform; constitutional change was a key element of the peace settlement brokered by Kofi Annan. A Committee of Experts produced a harmonised draft that went through parliamentary review before being put to the referendum.

The new constitution

The 2010 Constitution introduced a bill of rights, devolved government to 47 newly created counties, an independent judiciary with a Supreme Court, land reforms through a National Land Commission, and checks on presidential power, while retaining a presidential system. Contentious issues in the campaign included clauses on abortion, the retention of Kadhi courts for Muslim personal law, and land provisions. The "Yes" campaign, symbolised by a banana, was backed by President Mwai Kibaki and Prime Minister Raila Odinga; the "No" campaign, symbolised by an orange in earlier campaigns but here using other symbols, drew support from some church leaders and politicians including William Ruto.

Result

Approximately 67% of voters approved the constitution, with support exceeding 50% in most provinces; only the Rift Valley voted against. The vote was largely peaceful and was praised by international observers. The constitution was promulgated at a ceremony in Nairobi's Uhuru Park on 27 August 2010.
