Danmaku

Danmaku
Chinese弹幕 (dànmù)
Japanese弾幕 (danmaku)
Literal meaning"bullet curtain" / barrage
OriginNiconico (Japan, 2006)

Danmaku (Japanese: 弾幕; Chinese: 弹幕, literally "bullet curtain" or "barrage") is a feature on video-sharing websites in which user comments are overlaid directly on the video, scrolling across the screen synchronised with the playback time at which they were posted.

Origins and spread

The feature was popularised by the Japanese site Niconico (launched 2006). In China it became a defining characteristic of AcFun and Bilibili, where dense storms of comments — likened to the dense bullet patterns ("danmaku") of shoot-'em-up games — create a sense of shared, real-time viewing even for recorded video.

Characteristics

Danmaku comments typically scroll right to left, though they can be fixed at the top or bottom or styled with colours and positions. Platforms provide filtering, blocking and density controls. The system fosters communal jokes, translations, explanations and spoilers embedded in the video itself, and can also produce distraction and harassment, prompting moderation tools.

Related uses

The term is also used in gaming for "bullet hell" patterns in shooters such as the Touhou Project, from which the metaphor derives. Live-streaming platforms worldwide have adopted similar chat-overlay concepts.
