Round the Horne is a BBC radio comedy programme that ran for four series between 1965 and 1968, starring the urbane Kenneth Horne as the unflappable straight man amid a whirlwind of characters played principally by Kenneth Williams, Hugh Paddick, Betty Marsden, and Bill Pertwee. Written by Barry Took and Marty Feldman, it succeeded their earlier hit Beyond Our Ken and drew audiences of up to 15 million.

Format and characters

Each edition mixed parody film trailers, spoof folk songs by the dubious Rambling Syd Rumpo (Williams), and sketches featuring recurring characters: the camp pair Julian and Sandy (Williams and Paddick), who spoke in the gay slang Polari and ran a series of dubious "bona" businesses; J. Peasmold Gruntfuttock, the world's dirtiest old man; the melodious Charles and Fiona, parodying clipped stage romances; and Dr Chuffer's and Daphne Whitethigh's outrageous innuendo-laden exchanges with Horne.

Much of the humour rested on double entendre that sailed close to what BBC censors of the day would permit. Julian and Sandy were remarkable for putting Polari-speaking gay characters, played as cheerful insiders rather than figures of contempt, into mainstream entertainment years before male homosexuality was partially decriminalised in England and Wales in 1967.

Legacy

The programme ended with Horne's sudden death from a heart attack in February 1969, months after the fourth series; a planned fifth series became the short-lived Horne A'Plenty on television and radio spin-offs did not recapture the magic. Round the Horne remains one of the most beloved of all BBC radio comedies, endlessly repeated, issued on record and CD, and adapted for the stage. Its catchphrases and its stars' voices are woven into British comedy history.
