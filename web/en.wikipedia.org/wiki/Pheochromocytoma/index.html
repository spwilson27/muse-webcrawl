Pheochromocytoma

A pheochromocytoma is a rare tumour of the chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla that secretes excess catecholamines such as adrenaline and noradrenaline. Similar tumours arising outside the adrenal gland are called paragangliomas.

Signs and symptoms

Excess catecholamines cause paroxysmal or sustained hypertension, headache, palpitations, sweating, tremor and pallor — classically episodic spells. Although rare, it is an important treatable cause of secondary hypertension, and can cause dangerous hypertensive crises.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on demonstrating elevated plasma free metanephrines or urinary fractionated metanephrines, followed by imaging of the adrenal glands and screening for associated genetic syndromes such as MEN2, von Hippel–Lindau disease and neurofibromatosis type 1.

Treatment

Definitive treatment is surgical removal after adequate alpha-adrenergic blockade and volume expansion to prevent intraoperative catecholamine crises. Historically the tumour was summarised by a "rule of tens", though modern genetics has shown a much higher hereditary fraction than once thought.
