Henry Gantt

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Henry Laurence Gantt (May 20, 1861 – November 23, 1919) was an American mechanical engineer and management consultant best known for his work in the development of scientific management and for creating the Gantt chart in the 1910s. Gantt charts are still widely used today in project management to schedule tasks against time.

Career

Gantt worked closely with Frederick Winslow Taylor at Midvale Steel and Bethlehem Steel in the late 1880s and 1890s, developing methods of scientific management. He later became an independent consultant, emphasising the human element in industry more than Taylor did, and advocating that managers had a social responsibility toward their workers. During the First World War he advised the United States government on production.

Contributions

Gantt's contributions to management include the task-and-bonus wage system, which paid workers a bonus for completing a task within a set time; the daily balance sheet chart of production; and the famous bar chart bearing his name, which visualised planned work against completed work and became a foundational tool of production planning and, later, project management software.

Legacy

Gantt died in 1919 in Montclair, New Jersey. The Henry Laurence Gantt Medal, awarded by the American Society of Mechanical Engineers and the American Management Association, honours distinguished achievement in management and service to the community.
