London Wall

LocationCity of London, England
Builtc. AD 200 (Roman); medieval repairs thereafter
LengthAbout 3.2 km (2 miles) originally
MaterialKentish ragstone, tile courses

The London Wall is a defensive wall first built by the Romans around the strategic port town of Londinium in c. AD 200. It enclosed an area of roughly 330 acres (134 hectares) and remained the boundary of the City of London throughout the medieval period, repaired and heightened many times. Substantial sections survive, notably near Tower Hill, the Barbican and the Museum of London, and the modern street named London Wall follows part of its northern line.

Construction

The wall was built of Kentish ragstone bonded with courses of red tile, about 2.5–3 metres thick and up to 6 metres high, fronted by a ditch. It had gates at Aldgate, Bishopsgate, Newgate, Aldersgate, Cripplegate, Ludgate and Moorgate, and a riverside wall was added later in the Roman period. Bastions were added in the late Roman era, some reusing carved stone from demolished monuments.

Later history

After the Roman withdrawal the wall fell into disrepair, but Alfred the Great restored it when he reoccupied London in 886. Medieval London rebuilt and maintained it; the wall defined the City and its gates controlled the main roads. From the 17th century it was progressively built over and demolished as the city spread beyond it, though stretches were revealed again by bombing during the Blitz. Many surviving fragments are scheduled monuments.
