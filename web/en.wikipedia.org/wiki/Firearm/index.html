A firearm is any type of gun that uses an explosive charge to expel a projectile from a barrel through a controlled explosion, mainly serving as a ranged weapon for hunting, self-defence, law enforcement, and warfare. The first firearms were invented in China in the 13th century when the portable, bamboo (later metal) fire lance — a flame- and pellet-projecting device — was combined with projectiles such as shrapnel and the hand cannon was developed.

Mechanism

In a modern cartridge firearm, a trigger releases a hammer or striker that ignites the primer; the primer ignites the propellant, and expanding gas drives the bullet down the barrel, which is usually rifled with helical grooves that spin the projectile for stability. Firearms are fed by single loading, revolver cylinders, or detachable or fixed magazines, and operate manually (bolt, lever, pump), by self-loading semi-automatic action using recoil or gas, or fully automatically. Main categories are handguns (pistols and revolvers), long guns (rifles and shotguns), and automatic weapons such as submachine guns and machine guns.

History

Gunpowder weapons spread from China through the Islamic world to Europe by the 14th century, where hand cannons and then matchlock arquebuses (15th century), wheellock and flintlock mechanisms, rifled barrels, percussion caps (19th century), self-contained metallic cartridges, breech-loading and repeating actions, and smokeless powder (1880s) successively increased rate of fire, reliability, and range. Firearms progressively displaced bows and pikes in warfare, drove the design of fortifications and armour, and transformed hunting and personal defence; their military and civilian use is regulated to widely varying degrees around the world.

Terminology and law

Legal definitions vary: in the United States, the Gun Control Act of 1968 defines a firearm broadly as any weapon that expels a projectile by the action of an explosive, including its frame or receiver; antiques are partly exempted. International estimates (Small Arms Survey) put civilian-held firearms worldwide at roughly 850–1,000 million, with the United States accounting for a very large share per capita.
