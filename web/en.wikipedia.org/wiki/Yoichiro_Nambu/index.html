Nambu in 2008.

Yoichiro Nambu (18 January 1921 – 5 July 2015) was a Japanese-American physicist and professor at the University of Chicago, one of the most original theorists of the twentieth century. He shared the 2008 Nobel Prize in Physics 'for the discovery of the mechanism of spontaneous broken symmetry in subatomic physics' (with Makoto Kobayashi and Toshihide Maskawa, honoured for CP violation).

Nambu imported spontaneous symmetry breaking from superconductivity into particle physics (1960–61, the Nambu–Goldstone theorem and the Nambu–Jona-Lasinio model), showing how the vacuum can hide a symmetry and generate mass dynamically — the idea on which the Higgs mechanism builds. He also co-founded colour charge in Quantum chromodynamics (the Han–Nambu model) and gave the dual resonance model its string interpretation (1970), making him a grandfather of String theory as well. Many of his ideas were recognised only decades later.

Influence

Nambu's 1960 insight — that the strong interaction's near-massless pion is the Goldstone boson of a broken chiral symmetry — explained how mass can arise from dynamics rather than being fundamental. Jeffrey Goldstone, Abdus Salam and Steven Weinberg formalised the theorem for relativistic theories; Higgs, Brout–Englert and others extended it to gauge theories, 'eating' the Goldstone bosons to give W and Z mass.
