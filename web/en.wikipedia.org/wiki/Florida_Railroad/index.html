Florida Railroad

The Florida Railroad was the first railroad to cross the U.S. state of Florida, running from Fernandina on the Atlantic coast to Cedar Key on the Gulf of Mexico, a distance of about 155 miles (249 km). Chartered in 1853 with U.S. Senator David Levy Yulee as its president and chief promoter, it was completed in 1861, just before the outbreak of the American Civil War.

History

Yulee envisioned a cross-peninsula route that would let ships avoid the dangerous reefs of the Florida Straits by transferring cargo between Atlantic and Gulf ports. Construction began at Fernandina in 1855, aided by federal land grants and by Yulee's Internal Improvement Fund; rails reached Gainesville in 1859 and Cedar Key by March 1861. During the Civil War the railroad was a Confederate strategic asset and was attacked by Union forces — Fernandina and Cedar Key were both occupied — and parts of its track were removed for use elsewhere.

Successors

After the war the bankrupt line passed through foreclosure and a series of reorganizations and names — including the Florida Transit Railroad and the Florida Central and Peninsular Railroad — before becoming part of the Seaboard Air Line Railroad in 1903. Segments of the original route remain in service today under CSX Transportation, while much of the western portion was abandoned in the 20th century; part of the right of way near Cedar Key is now a nature trail and state park corridor.
