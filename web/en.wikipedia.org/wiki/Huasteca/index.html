La Huasteca is a geographical and cultural region located along the Gulf of Mexico in northeastern Mexico, spanning parts of the states of Veracruz, Tamaulipas, San Luis Potosí, Hidalgo, Querétaro and Puebla. It is named after the Huastec people, a Maya-speaking group that separated early from other Maya peoples and settled the northern Gulf coast.

Geography

The region is characterised by lush, humid lowlands watered by rivers such as the Pánuco, with the Sierra Madre Oriental rising to the west. The Huasteca Potosina, in San Luis Potosí, is noted for its waterfalls, turquoise rivers and the surrealist garden of Las Pozas at Xilitla, created by Edward James.

Culture

The region is famous for the son huasteco musical tradition, performed by a trío huasteco of violin, jarana huasteca and guitarra huapanguera, and for the falsetto singing style of the huapango. Huastec cuisine includes dishes such as zacahuil, a giant tamal wrapped in banana leaves.
