Holy trinity (cooking)

The holy trinity in Cajun and Creole cuisine of Louisiana is a combination of three aromatic vegetables — onion, bell pepper, and celery — used as the flavour base for many dishes. It is the regional counterpart of the French mirepoix (onion, carrot, and celery), with bell pepper substituted for carrot.

Use

Typically finely diced and cooked in oil, butter, or roux, the trinity forms the foundation of dishes such as gumbo, jambalaya, étouffée, sauce piquante, and many soups and stews. The usual proportions are roughly two parts onion to one part each of bell pepper and celery. Garlic, sometimes called "the pope", is frequently added, as are parsley and green onions to finish dishes.

Origins of the name

The term evokes both Louisiana's strong Roman Catholic heritage and the trio's near-universal presence in the region's savoury cooking. The chef Paul Prudhomme is widely credited with popularizing the expression beyond Louisiana in the 1980s.

Related bases

Similar aromatic bases include French mirepoix, Italian soffritto, Spanish sofrito, and the German Suppengrün.
