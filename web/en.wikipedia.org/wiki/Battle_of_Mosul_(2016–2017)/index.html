Battle of Mosul (2016–2017)

Battle of Mosul
Date16 October 2016 – 21 July 2017
LocationMosul, Nineveh Governorate, Iraq
ResultIraqi and coalition victory

The Battle of Mosul (Arabic: معركة الموصل) was a major military campaign launched by Iraqi government forces, allied militias, the Kurdistan Regional Government, and international forces to retake the city of Mosul from the Islamic State (IS), which had seized it in June 2014. The battle began on 16 October 2016 and Iraqi Prime Minister Haider al-Abadi declared the city liberated on 10 July 2017, although clearing operations continued afterwards.

Iraqi forces during the offensive to retake Mosul.

Background

Mosul, Iraq's second-largest city, fell to IS in June 2014 and became the group's de facto capital in Iraq; it was there that Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi proclaimed the "caliphate" from the Great Mosque of al-Nuri. By 2016 Iraqi forces, rebuilt with coalition support, had retaken Ramadi and Fallujah and advanced toward Mosul.

The battle

The offensive involved some 100,000 Iraqi and Kurdish personnel against an estimated 3,000–5,000 IS fighters. Iraqi Counter Terrorism Service units fought into eastern Mosul in November 2016 and cleared the east bank of the Tigris by January 2017. The assault on the densely built Old City in the west, launched in February 2017, was the fiercest phase: IS defended with snipers, suicide car bombs, tunnels, and civilians used as human shields. Coalition airstrikes and artillery caused extensive destruction and heavy civilian casualties; in June 2017 IS destroyed the Great Mosque of al-Nuri and its leaning al-Hadba minaret as Iraqi forces closed in.

Aftermath

The battle devastated large parts of Mosul, especially the Old City, displaced close to a million people, and killed thousands of civilians. Militarily it destroyed IS as a territorial power in Iraq and foreshadowed the fall of Raqqa in Syria later in 2017.
