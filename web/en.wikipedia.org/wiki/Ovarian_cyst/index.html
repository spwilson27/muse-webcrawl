Ovarian cyst

An ovarian cyst is a fluid-filled sac within the ovary. Often they cause no symptoms, though they may cause bloating, lower abdominal pain or pain during sex. Most cysts are harmless and disappear on their own within a few menstrual cycles.

Types include functional cysts (follicular and corpus luteum cysts), which are part of the normal menstrual cycle, and pathological cysts such as dermoid cysts, cystadenomas and endometriomas associated with endometriosis. Multiple cysts occur in polycystic ovary syndrome.

Complications include rupture, bleeding and ovarian torsion, which can cause sudden severe pain and require surgery. Diagnosis is typically by pelvic ultrasound, and treatment ranges from watchful waiting to laparoscopic removal.
