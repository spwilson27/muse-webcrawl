Role-playing video game

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A role-playing video game, commonly referred to as a role-playing game (RPG) or computer role-playing game (CRPG), is a video game genre where the player controls the actions of a character (or several party members) immersed in some well-defined world, usually involving some form of character development by way of recording statistics. Many role-playing video games have origins in tabletop role-playing games, particularly Dungeons & Dragons, and use much of the same terminology, settings, and game mechanics.

Major distinctions from other genres include the emphasis on narrative, character customization, and statistical character progression. Other common gameplay elements include exploration, quest completion, turn-based or real-time combat, and decision-making that can affect the story outcome.

Characteristics

Character creation and development

Players typically create or assume control of a character defined by numerical statistics such as strength, intelligence, and health. Through experience points earned in combat and quests, characters gain levels, unlocking new abilities and improving their attributes. Equipment — weapons, armor, and items — further modifies character performance.

Story and setting

RPGs usually feature extensive narratives, often set in fantasy or science fiction worlds. Players interact with non-player characters (NPCs), make dialogue choices, and undertake quests ranging from simple fetch tasks to elaborate branching storylines.

Combat systems

Combat may be turn-based, in which participants act in sequence; real-time, including action RPGs that emphasize reflexes; or hybrids such as active-time systems. Party-based games let players manage several characters with complementary roles such as tank, healer, and damage dealer.

History

The genre emerged in the mid-1970s on university mainframe computers, with early titles such as dnd (1974) and Rogue (1980) inspired by tabletop games. The early 1980s saw the influential Wizardry and Ultima series on home computers. Japanese developers brought the genre to consoles with Dragon Quest (1986) and Final Fantasy (1987), which popularized distinct conventions. The 1990s and 2000s produced landmark titles including Baldur's Gate, Diablo, Pokémon, and massively multiplayer online role-playing games (MMORPGs) such as World of Warcraft (2004).

Subgenres

  • Action RPG — real-time combat with RPG statistics, e.g. Diablo, Dark Souls.
  • MMORPG — persistent online worlds with thousands of players.
  • Tactical RPG — grid-based, strategy-focused combat, e.g. Fire Emblem, Final Fantasy Tactics.
  • Roguelike — procedurally generated levels and permanent character death.
  • JRPG / WRPG — informal labels for Japanese- and Western-developed conventions respectively.
