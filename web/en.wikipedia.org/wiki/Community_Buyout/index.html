Community buyout

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A community buyout is the purchase of land, buildings, or other assets by a local community — typically through a specially formed trust, company, or co-operative — so that the asset is owned and managed for the benefit of residents rather than by an absentee landlord or outside owner. The term is most strongly associated with Scotland, where community land buyouts became a prominent feature of land reform from the 1990s onwards.

Scotland

Land ownership in the Highlands and Islands was historically concentrated in large private estates, many assembled during the Highland Clearances. Early buyouts by crofting communities, such as the Assynt Crofters' Trust purchase of the North Lochinver Estate in 1992 and the Isle of Eigg Heritage Trust's purchase of Eigg in 1997, inspired legislation: the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 gave crofting communities a right to buy the land they occupy and gave other rural communities a pre-emptive right to buy land when it comes on the market. Subsequent purchases include the islands of Gigha (2002) and Ulva (2018), the South Uist estate (2006) — one of the largest, covering most of South Uist, Eriskay, and parts of Benbecula — and many smaller assets such as shops, piers, forests, and village halls. The Scottish Land Fund provides public grant support for acquisitions.

Beyond Scotland

Similar mechanisms include community shares and asset transfers in England (community pubs, shops, and sports grounds under the Localism Act 2011's "community right to bid"), community land trusts in the United States and elsewhere, and community buyouts of renewable-energy projects, in which residents purchase shares in local wind or hydro schemes.

Effects and debate

Supporters credit buyouts with reversing population decline, enabling affordable housing and local enterprise, and democratising land ownership. Critics note the management burden placed on small communities, dependence on grant funding, and variable long-term financial outcomes.
