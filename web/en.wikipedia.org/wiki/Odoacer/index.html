Odoacer

Reign476–493
Bornc. 433
Died15 March 493, Ravenna
TitleKing of Italy

Odoacer (c. 433 – 15 March 493), also spelled Odovacer, was a barbarian soldier and statesman who deposed the last Roman emperor of the West, Romulus Augustulus, on 4 September 476 and became the first King of Italy. His reign is traditionally taken to mark the end of the Western Roman Empire and the beginning of the Middle Ages in Western Europe.

Rise to power

Odoacer was of Germanic origin, partly Hunnic on his father's side, and rose through service among the federate troops of the Roman army in Italy. In 476 the federates demanded land settlements in Italy; when the patrician Orestes refused, they proclaimed Odoacer king. Odoacer captured Ravenna, deposed the young emperor Romulus Augustulus, and sent the imperial regalia to the Eastern emperor Zeno in Constantinople, requesting recognition as ruler of Italy under nominal Eastern suzerainty. Romulus was pensioned off to live in Campania — an unusually merciful end for a deposed emperor.

Reign

Odoacer ruled Italy competently for thirteen years. He preserved the Roman Senate, the civil administration, the coinage, and the legal system, granted land to his followers, and extended his authority over Sicily, Dalmatia, and Noricum. He generally ruled through existing Roman institutions and kept the peace with the Church, though he himself was an Arian Christian.

Downfall

Emperor Zeno, wary of Odoacer's strength, encouraged Theodoric, king of the Ostrogoths, to invade Italy. Theodoric defeated Odoacer in a series of campaigns beginning in 489 and besieged him in Ravenna. In 493 the two agreed to rule jointly, but at a banquet celebrating the settlement Theodoric personally killed Odoacer, and went on to found the Ostrogothic Kingdom of Italy.
