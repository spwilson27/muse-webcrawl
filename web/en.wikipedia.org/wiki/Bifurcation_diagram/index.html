Bifurcation diagram

A bifurcation diagram is a visual summary of the long-term behaviour of a dynamical system as one of its parameters is varied. For each parameter value, the diagram plots the values the system eventually settles into, revealing fixed points, periodic cycles, and chaotic regimes.

The logistic map

The most famous bifurcation diagram is that of the logistic map, x → r·x(1 − x). For small growth rates r, the population settles to a single value. As r increases past 3, the fixed point splits into a 2-cycle (the first bifurcation), then into cycles of period 4, 8, 16, and so on — a period-doubling cascade that accumulates at r ≈ 3.56995, beyond which chaos appears, interspersed with windows of periodicity such as the period-3 window near r ≈ 3.83.

Feigenbaum universality

Mitchell Feigenbaum discovered that the ratios of successive bifurcation intervals approach a universal constant, δ ≈ 4.6692, for a broad class of one-dimensional maps — a landmark result connecting bifurcation diagrams to universality in chaos theory.
