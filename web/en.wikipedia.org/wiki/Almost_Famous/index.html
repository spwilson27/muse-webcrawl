Almost Famous is a 2000 American comedy-drama film written and directed by Cameron Crowe. It is a semi-autobiographical account of Crowe's teenage years as a rock journalist for Rolling Stone magazine in the early 1970s. The film stars Patrick Fugit as William Miller, a 15-year-old reporter who is hired to tour with the fictional rock band Stillwater and write a cover story about them.

Plot

In 1973, William Miller, a sheltered teenager from San Diego whose strict mother Elaine (Frances McDormand) has banned rock music, is given his older sister's record collection when she leaves home. Inspired, he begins writing about music and sends his work to rock critic Lester Bangs (Philip Seymour Hoffman), who becomes his mentor. William is assigned to profile Black Sabbath but instead lands a Rolling Stone cover assignment on the rising band Stillwater after charming his way backstage.

On tour, William befriends the band's charismatic lead guitarist Russell Hammond (Billy Crudup) and Penny Lane (Kate Hudson), the leader of a group of devoted young women who call themselves "Band Aids". As William struggles to get an honest interview amid the band's internal feuds — particularly between Russell and singer Jeff Bebe (Jason Lee) — he witnesses Penny being traded to another band and overdosing, experiences he records with growing disillusionment. After a near-fatal plane trip confession scene, William finally writes his truthful story, which the band initially denies before Russell confirms it, and the article becomes the magazine's cover feature.

Production

Crowe drew directly on his own experiences touring with bands such as the Allman Brothers Band, Led Zeppelin, the Eagles and Lynyrd Skynyrd as a teenage correspondent. The fictional Stillwater was modelled on several of these acts; Crowe obtained permission from Led Zeppelin to use five of their songs on the soundtrack, a rare honour. The "tiny dancer" bus singalong scene, set to Elton John's "Tiny Dancer", became one of the film's most celebrated moments.

Reception and legacy

The film underperformed at the box office but was acclaimed by critics. Crowe won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and Kate Hudson and Frances McDormand were nominated for Best Supporting Actress. Almost Famous is frequently ranked among the greatest films of its era and was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 2025. An extended director's cut, Untitled, runs 162 minutes. A stage musical adaptation opened on Broadway in 2022.
