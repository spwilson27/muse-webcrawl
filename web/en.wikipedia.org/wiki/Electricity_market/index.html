Electricity market

An electricity market is a system enabling purchases of electricity through bids to buy and offers to sell, using supply and demand to set the price. Wholesale markets allow generators, retailers, and other financial intermediaries to trade electricity across transmission networks.

Structure

Markets typically include day-ahead and real-time (spot) energy markets, ancillary services such as reserves and frequency regulation, and sometimes capacity markets. Independent system operators (ISOs) or regional transmission organizations (RTOs) administer markets in much of North America, while European markets are coupled through power exchanges.

Features

Because electricity is costly to store, supply must balance demand instantaneously, producing distinctive features such as locational marginal pricing, price spikes, and negative prices during surplus renewable output.
