Wald's equation

In probability theory, Wald's equation, also called Wald's lemma, is an identity that relates the expected value of a sum of a random number of random variables to the expected value of one variable multiplied by the expected number of terms, under suitable independence and integrability conditions. It is named after the Hungarian mathematician Abraham Wald and is closely related to optional stopping theorems for martingales.

Statement

If X1, X2, ... are independent and identically distributed random variables with finite expectation, and N is a non-negative integer-valued random variable independent of the sequence (or, more generally, a stopping time with respect to a suitable filtration), then E[X1 + ... + XN] = E[N] · E[X1].

Applications

Wald's equation is used in sequential analysis, renewal theory, queueing theory, and the analysis of branching processes, among other areas. A more general version, Wald's second identity, concerns the variance of such random sums.
