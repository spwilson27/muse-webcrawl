MI5, commonly known as the Security Service, is the United Kingdom's domestic counter-intelligence and security agency. Along with the Secret Intelligence Service (MI6), the Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ), and Defence Intelligence, it forms part of the UK's intelligence community. MI5 is responsible for protecting the UK against threats to national security, including terrorism, espionage, and the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction. It is not a police force and has no powers of arrest; its officers work in support of law enforcement and other agencies.

History

The service originated in the Secret Service Bureau founded in 1909, whose home section, led by Captain Vernon Kell, became responsible for counter-espionage within Britain. During the First World War it was designated MI5 — Military Intelligence, Section 5. Between the wars it monitored communist and fascist movements. In the Second World War MI5, under the Twenty Committee, ran the celebrated Double-Cross System, turning captured German agents to feed disinformation to the Abwehr, a deception that contributed to the success of the Normandy landings.

During the Cold War MI5 concentrated on Soviet espionage, although the Cambridge Five scandal revealed deep KGB penetration of the British establishment. From the late 1960s its focus shifted heavily toward Irish republican terrorism, and in 1992 it took the lead intelligence role against the Provisional IRA on the British mainland. The Security Service Act 1989 placed MI5 on a statutory footing for the first time; the Intelligence Services Act 1994 extended its remit to supporting police against serious crime. After the 7 July 2005 London bombings, counter-terrorism — particularly Islamist terrorism — became its dominant priority, accounting for the great majority of its work.

Organisation and oversight

MI5 is headed by a Director General, who reports to the Home Secretary. Its headquarters is Thames House on Millbank in London, with a significant presence in Northern Ireland. The service is overseen by the Intelligence and Security Committee of Parliament and by judicial commissioners under the Investigatory Powers Act 2016. Famous Directors General include Stella Rimington (1992–1996), the first woman to hold the post and the first whose name was publicly announced, Jonathan Evans, Andrew Parker, and Ken McCallum, who became Director General in 2020.

In popular culture

MI5 features in the BBC television drama Spooks (broadcast as MI-5 in the United States) and in numerous spy novels; its headquarters and officers are frequent fixtures of British thriller fiction.
