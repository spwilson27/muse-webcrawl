The Italian Empire (Impero italiano) was the overseas colonial empire of the Kingdom of Italy. Formed late in the 19th-century "Scramble for Africa", it comprised colonies in East and North Africa — Eritrea, Somalia, Libya and, briefly, conquered Ethiopia — together with the Dodecanese islands, a concession at Tianjin in China, and after 1939 the protectorate of Albania. Fascist propaganda under Benito Mussolini framed the empire as the heir of ancient Rome.

Formation

Italy, unified only in 1861, acquired its first territory when the Rubattino shipping company bought Assab Bay on the Red Sea in 1869; the state took over in 1882 and expanded it into the colony of Eritrea (1890). In 1889–90 Italy established a protectorate over the Somali coast, and in 1896 its attempt to conquer Ethiopia ended in disaster at the Battle of Adwa — a landmark victory for an African state over a European power. Italy gained Libya and the Dodecanese from the Ottoman Empire in the Italo-Turkish War of 1911–12.

Fascist expansion

Under Mussolini the regime pursued a more aggressive imperialism: the brutal "pacification" of Libya (1923–32), involving concentration camps in Cyrenaica, and the Second Italo-Ethiopian War (1935–36), in which Italian forces used poison gas and proclaimed Victor Emmanuel III Emperor of Ethiopia. Ethiopia, Eritrea and Somalia were merged into Italian East Africa. In April 1939 Italy invaded and annexed Albania.

Collapse

Italy's empire disintegrated in the Second World War: British and Commonwealth forces liberated Ethiopia and conquered Italy's East African colonies in 1941, and Libya was lost by early 1943. The 1947 Treaty of Paris formally stripped Italy of its colonies, though Italy administered its former Somaliland as a UN trust territory until Somali independence in 1960.
