The Presidency of Religious Affairs (Turkish: Diyanet İşleri Başkanlığı), commonly known simply as the Diyanet, is a state institution established in 1924 in the Republic of Turkey to administer religious affairs. It replaced the Ottoman office of Shaykh al-Islām following the abolition of the Caliphate, and was intended to place Islam under state authority within the secular framework of the new republic founded by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk.

Functions

The Diyanet regulates the operation of Turkey's mosques (numbering over 80,000), appoints and pays imams and muezzins as civil servants, prepares the Friday sermons delivered nationwide, organizes Qur'an courses, and publishes religious literature. Through the Diyanet Foundation (Türkiye Diyanet Vakfı) and affiliated organizations such as DITIB in Germany, it also provides services to Turkish Muslim communities abroad.

Criticism

The institution has been criticized for serving only Sunni Islam of the Hanafi school, while Alevi and other communities receive no comparable state support, despite the Diyanet being funded from general taxation. Its budget — larger than that of several ministries — and its expansion under successive governments have also drawn debate about the relationship between religion and the state in Turkey.
