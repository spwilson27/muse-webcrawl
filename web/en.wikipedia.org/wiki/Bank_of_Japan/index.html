Bank of Japan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Bank of Japan (BOJ) is the central bank of Japan. Founded in 1882 (on the model of the National Bank of Belgium) and reorganised under the 1997 Bank of Japan Act (effective 1998), which granted formal independence, it issues the yen, conducts monetary policy, and oversees the payment and settlement system and financial stability.

The deflation fight

The BOJ's modern history is the world's longest experiment in combating deflation: the zero-interest-rate policy (1999), the first quantitative easing (2001–06), and from 2013 Governor Kuroda's "quantitative and qualitative easing" under Abenomics — massive purchases of government bonds, equities (via ETFs) and real-estate trusts, negative policy rates (2016) and yield-curve control pinning the 10-year yield near zero. These measures made the BOJ owner of roughly half the Japanese government-bond market and rewired debates about central-bank balance sheets and fiscal–monetary entanglement worldwide. Persistent below-target inflation for most of the period, then the 2022–24 global inflation shock reaching Japan, led the Bank to end negative rates and yield-curve control in March 2024 — the cautious beginning of normalisation watched by every market.
