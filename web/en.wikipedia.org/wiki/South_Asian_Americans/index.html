South Asian Americans

South Asian Americans are Americans with ancestry from South Asia, including India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, and the Maldives, as well as members of the South Asian diaspora who arrived via countries such as Fiji, Guyana, Trinidad and Tobago, and East Africa. Numbering over five million, they are among the fastest-growing groups within the broader category of Asian Americans.

History

Small numbers of South Asians, particularly Punjabi Sikhs and Bengali merchants and seamen, arrived in the 19th and early 20th centuries, settling mainly on the West Coast and in New York. Restrictive laws, including the Immigration Act of 1917 and a 1923 Supreme Court ruling (United States v. Bhagat Singh Thind) that South Asians were not "white" for naturalisation purposes, sharply limited immigration until reforms in 1946 and the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965, which opened the door to large-scale professional and family migration.

Demographics

Indian Americans form the largest subgroup, followed by Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan, and Nepali Americans. South Asian Americans have high average educational attainment and household incomes, though experiences vary widely by subgroup and immigration pathway. Religious traditions represented include Hinduism, Islam, Sikhism, Christianity, Buddhism, and Jainism.

Culture and politics

South Asian Americans have become increasingly visible in American politics, business, medicine, and entertainment, with figures serving in Congress, state governments, and as major-party presidential and vice-presidential candidates.
