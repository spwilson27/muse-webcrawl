Anode

An anode is an electrode through which conventional current enters a polarised electrical device, or at which oxidation occurs in an electrochemical cell. Its paired electrode is the cathode. The electrical sign of an anode depends on the device: in a discharging galvanic cell it is the negative terminal, whereas in an electrolytic cell driven by an external supply it is positive.

Electrochemistry

In electrochemical notation, oxidation takes place at the anode. In a battery being discharged, material at the anode gives up electrons to the external circuit. During electrolysis, anions or oxidisable species lose electrons at the anode surface. Sacrificial anodes made from more reactive metals are used to protect ships, pipelines and water heaters from corrosion.

Electronic devices

In vacuum tubes, X-ray tubes and semiconductor diodes, the anode is the electrode toward which electrons flow inside the device. In light-emitting diodes, the anode is normally the longer lead and must be at the higher potential for forward operation.
