The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art (Chinese: 九章算術; pinyin: Jiǔzhāng Suànshù) is a Chinese mathematics book, composed by several generations of scholars from the 10th–2nd century BCE and finalised during the Han dynasty. It is one of the earliest surviving mathematical texts from China and was the dominant textbook of Chinese mathematics for well over a thousand years, comparable in influence to Euclid's Elements in the West — though organised very differently, as a collection of practical problems with general solution procedures rather than axiomatic proofs.

Contents

The book contains 246 problems grouped into nine chapters: (1) surveying of land and areas of fields; (2) exchange of agricultural produce and proportions; (3) distribution by proportional division; (4) areas and volumes involving square and cube roots; (5) volumes of earthworks and constructions; (6) equitable taxation and transport problems; (7) excess and deficiency, including a rule for solving linear equations by double false position; (8) rectangular arrays (fangcheng), a method of solving systems of linear equations essentially equivalent to Gaussian elimination, in which negative numbers appear for the first time in mathematics; and (9) the "base and altitude" chapter, treating right triangles and including the Pythagorean theorem (gougu).

Methods and significance

Computations were performed with counting rods on a board, giving a decimal place-value system that made the array methods natural. The text treats fractions systematically, gives an accurate approximation of π, and handles negative numbers with explicit rules for their addition and subtraction — centuries before such numbers were accepted in Europe.

Commentaries

The most famous commentary was written by Liu Hui in 263 CE, who supplied derivations and justifications for the procedures, computed π to 3.14159, and added the appendix known as the Sea Island Mathematical Manual. Later commentaries by Zu Chongzhi and Li Chunfeng, who edited the text in the 7th century as part of the Ten Computational Canons, made it an official textbook for the imperial examinations.
