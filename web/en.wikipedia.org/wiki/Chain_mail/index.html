Chain mail

Mail or chain mail is a type of armour consisting of small metal rings linked together in a pattern to form a mesh. It was in use from antiquity through the Middle Ages and beyond, protecting against slashing and some piercing attacks.

History

Mail is attributed to the Celts in the 3rd century BCE and was adopted by the Romans as lorica hamata. In medieval Europe the hauberk – a knee-length mail shirt – was the principal armour of knights until plate armour became dominant in the 14th–15th centuries.

Construction

Rings were typically riveted or welded. Modern mail is made for reenactment, butcher's gloves, shark suits and stab-resistant protection.
