Beauty salon

A beauty salon (also called a beauty parlour) is an establishment that provides cosmetic and grooming treatments for men and women. Services commonly include hair cutting, colouring and styling; manicures and pedicures; facials and skin care; waxing and other hair removal; massage; and makeup application. Specialised establishments may focus on a single service category, such as nail salons or hair salons, while full-service salons and spas combine many treatments under one roof.

History

Personal grooming establishments date to antiquity, but the modern beauty salon emerged in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. In the United States, entrepreneurs such as Martha Matilda Harper — who opened her first salon in Rochester, New York, in 1888 and pioneered salon franchising — and Madam C. J. Walker and Annie Malone, who built major businesses serving African American women, helped establish the industry. Helena Rubinstein and Elizabeth Arden opened influential salons in the early 1900s, linking salon services to branded cosmetics.

Industry

Beauty salons are usually small businesses, often owner-operated or organised around chair rental, in which independent stylists rent workspace. Practitioners such as cosmetologists, barbers, estheticians and nail technicians are typically required to complete training and obtain licences, with regulations varying by jurisdiction. Salons also serve as social spaces, a role documented in cultural works such as the play and film Steel Magnolias and the barbershop and salon scenes common in cinema. Health and safety rules address sanitation of tools, ventilation, and the handling of chemical products used in colouring, straightening and nail services.
