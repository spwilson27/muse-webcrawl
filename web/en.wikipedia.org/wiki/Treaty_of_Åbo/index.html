Treaty of Åbo

The Treaty of Åbo (Swedish: Freden i Åbo) was a peace treaty signed at Åbo (Turku) on 18 August 1743 between the Russian Empire and Sweden, ending the Russo-Swedish War of 1741–1743, known in Sweden as the Hats' War.

Terms

Under the treaty, Sweden ceded to Russia territory in southeastern Finland east of the Kymijoki River, including the towns of Hamina, Lappeenranta, and Savonlinna, together with part of Karelia. The new border left Finland divided and moved Russia's frontier westward, though it fell short of the total annexation of Finland that had been feared during the Russian occupation.

Russia in turn supported the election of Adolf Frederick of Holstein-Gottorp as heir to the Swedish throne, and Russian forces withdrew from most of occupied Finland.

Aftermath

The treaty confirmed Sweden's decline as a great power and deepened Russian influence over Swedish politics. The remaining territorial questions between the two states were not resolved until the Finnish War of 1808–1809, after which all of Finland passed to Russia.
