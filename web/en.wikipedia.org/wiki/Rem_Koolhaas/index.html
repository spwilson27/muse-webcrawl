Rem Koolhaas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The CCTV Headquarters in Beijing: two leaning towers joined at the top into a continuous loop — the 'big pants' of local nickname]

Remment Lucas 'Rem' Koolhaas (born 17 November 1944) is a Dutch architect, architectural theorist and urbanist, founder of the Office for Metropolitan Architecture (OMA, 1975) and one of the most influential architectural thinkers of the past half-century. He won the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2000.

His books rewrote the profession's discourse: Delirious New York (1978), the retroactive manifesto for Manhattanism, and S,M,L,XL (1995), the brick-sized monograph that coined 'Bigness' and the Generic City. His buildings include the Kunsthal in Rotterdam (1992), the Seattle Central Library (2004), Casa da Música in Porto (2005), the CCTV Headquarters in Beijing (2012) and De Rotterdam (2013).

From theory to towers

Rotterdam-born, a journalist and screenwriter before architecture (he studied at London's AA), Koolhaas treats buildings as arguments about the contemporary city: congestion as culture, the shopping mall and the airport as the real public realms, China and Lagos as the urban future arriving early. His Harvard research studios (on shopping, on the countryside — the subject of his 2020 Guggenheim exhibition) extend the office's reach into sociology.

OMA and after

OMA and its think-tank twin AMO (which designs fashion shows, exhibitions and strategies) became the profession's talent refinery — Zaha Hadid, Bjarke Ingels and dozens of notable founders passed through. Koolhaas's own reputation oscillates between prophet and provocateur; his central claim, that architecture must engage the forces actually shaping cities rather than mourn them, has largely won.
