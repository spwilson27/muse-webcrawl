Foreigner (band)

Foreigner
Origin: New York City, U.S.
Genres: Hard rock, arena rock
Years active: 1976–present
Founders: Mick Jones, Lou Gramm, Ian McDonald

Foreigner is a British-American rock band formed in New York City in 1976 by guitarist Mick Jones, vocalist Lou Gramm, and multi-instrumentalist Ian McDonald, along with Dennis Elliott, Al Greenwood, and Ed Gagliardi. The name was chosen because the founding lineup mixed British and American members — no matter where they played, some of them would be foreigners. Foreigner is one of the best-selling bands of all time, with worldwide sales of more than 80 million records.

History

The band's self-titled debut album (1977) produced the hits "Feels Like the First Time" and "Cold as Ice". Double Vision (1978) added "Hot Blooded" and the title track, and Head Games followed in 1979. The album 4 (1981), produced by Robert John "Mutt" Lange, reached number one in the US and contained "Urgent" and "Waiting for a Girl Like You". Foreigner's signature song, the power ballad "I Want to Know What Love Is" from Agent Provocateur (1984), topped the charts in both the US and the UK and remains one of the most recognisable rock ballads of the era.

Lou Gramm left in 1990, returned in 1992, and departed again in 2003; he was succeeded by Kelly Hansen in 2005. Mick Jones is the sole remaining original member in the band's later touring lineups. Foreigner continued to record, releasing Can't Slow Down in 2009, and remained a major live draw. In 2024 Foreigner was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.

Musical style

Foreigner's sound blends hard rock guitar riffs with melodic, keyboard-supported choruses — a polished style closely associated with the arena rock and album-oriented rock (AOR) formats of the late 1970s and 1980s.
