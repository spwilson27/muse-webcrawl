The Graduate Institute of International and Development Studies (French: Institut de hautes études internationales et du développement), commonly called the Graduate Institute or IHEID, is a postgraduate institution of higher education in Geneva, Switzerland, devoted to the study of international affairs and development. Founded in 1927, it is regarded as one of the world's oldest schools of international relations.

History

The Institute was founded in 1927, in the milieu of the League of Nations, by the historians and diplomats William Rappard and Paul Mantoux, initially to train officials for the new international organisations seated in Geneva. In 2008 it merged with the Graduate Institute of Development Studies (IUED), adopting its present name and adding development studies to its historic focus on international law, politics, economics and history.

Academics

The Institute offers master's programmes in international and development studies and in disciplines including international law, international economics, international history and politics, and anthropology and sociology of development, as well as PhD programmes. Instruction is in English, with some courses in French. Its research centres address topics such as conflict and peacebuilding, global health, trade, finance and humanitarian action.

Campus

Since 2013 the Institute's main activities have been housed in the Maison de la Paix, a glass building near the Palais des Nations in Geneva's international district, alongside the Geneva Centre for Security Policy and other organisations.

Notable alumni

Alumni include former UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, several heads of state and government, central bankers, diplomats and leaders of international organisations.
