Scutum (constellation)

Scutum is a small constellation in the southern sky, one of the 88 modern constellations. It was introduced in 1684 by the Polish astronomer Johannes Hevelius as Scutum Sobiescianum, the Shield of Sobieski, honouring King John III Sobieski of Poland and his victory at the Battle of Vienna in 1683. Unusually, it is one of very few constellations named after a real historical person.

Features

Scutum contains no very bright stars — its brightest, Alpha Scuti, is of 4th magnitude — but it lies in a rich region of the Milky Way crossed by a prominent star cloud, the Scutum Star Cloud. It hosts the Wild Duck Cluster (Messier 11), one of the richest and most compact open clusters known, and Messier 26, as well as the red supergiant UY Scuti, once cited among the largest known stars by radius. The globular cluster NGC 6712 and planetary nebula IC 1295 also lie within its borders.
