Bilingualism

Bilingualism is the use of two languages by an individual or a community. Bilingual speakers have acquired and use two languages in daily life, to varying degrees of proficiency. Multilingualism is the use of more than two.

Definitions

Definitions range from minimal ability in a second language to balanced, native-like mastery of both. Researchers distinguish simultaneous bilinguals, who acquire two languages from infancy, from sequential bilinguals, who learn a second language later.

Cognitive aspects

Research has examined executive control, metalinguistic awareness, and a proposed bilingual advantage in some tasks, though findings are debated. Code-switching — alternating languages within conversation — is a normal bilingual practice governed by grammatical and social rules.

Education

Bilingual education models include immersion, dual-language and transitional programmes. Policies reflect goals of assimilation, maintenance of heritage languages, or enrichment.

Societal bilingualism

Many countries are officially or de facto bilingual or multilingual, including Canada, Belgium, Switzerland and India. Diglossia describes use of different varieties for different social functions.
