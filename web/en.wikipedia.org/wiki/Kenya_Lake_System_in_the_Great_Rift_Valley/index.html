The Kenya Lake System in the Great Rift Valley is a UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed in 2011, comprising three interlinked, relatively shallow alkaline lakes — Lake Bogoria, Lake Nakuru and Lake Elmenteita — in the Rift Valley Province of Kenya, together with their surrounding protected areas.

Description

The lakes are soda (alkaline) lakes whose nutrient-rich waters support immense growths of blue-green algae, which in turn sustain some of the most spectacular bird congregations on Earth. At times more than a million lesser flamingos — along with tens of thousands of greater flamingos — gather on the lakes, moving between them as conditions change, turning the shorelines pink. The site is the single most important foraging area for the lesser flamingo in the world.

The surrounding national parks and reserves support rich terrestrial wildlife, including black and white rhinoceroses at Lake Nakuru, Rothschild's giraffes, lions, leopards and the endangered greater kudu at Lake Bogoria. The area is also important for globally threatened bird species such as the great white pelican, which breeds on Lake Elmenteita, and lies on the African–Eurasian flyway used by hundreds of migratory species.

Conservation

Threats to the system include changing lake levels from drought and altered inflows, pollution from the growing town of Nakuru, deforestation in the catchment, and pressure from tourism and development, including geothermal expansion near Lake Bogoria. All three lakes are also designated wetlands of international importance under the Ramsar Convention.
