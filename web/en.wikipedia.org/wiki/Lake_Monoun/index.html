Lake Monoun is a crater lake in the West Region of Cameroon, lying in the Oku Volcanic Field of the Cameroon volcanic line. It is one of three known lakes in the world subject to limnic eruptions, together with Lake Nyos and Lake Kivu.

1984 disaster

On 15 August 1984, Lake Monoun released a cloud of carbon dioxide in a limnic eruption, suffocating 37 people in the low-lying area near the village of Njindoun. The event was initially mysterious — victims were found dead along a road with no signs of violence — and became the first recognised limnic eruption when, two years later, the far deadlier eruption at Lake Nyos (which killed about 1,746 people) led scientists to connect the two events. Magmatic CO₂ seeps into the lake bottom and accumulates in the stratified deep water until a disturbance triggers sudden release.

Degassing

Since 2003, scientists have operated degassing pipes at Monoun that siphon CO₂-rich deep water to the surface in a self-sustaining fountain, gradually reducing the gas inventory and the risk of another eruption. Similar degassing columns were installed at Lake Nyos.
