KeyHole

KeyHole is a geospatial data visualisation system developed by Keyhole, Inc., a Mountain View, California software company founded in 2001. Its principal product, Earth Viewer, allowed users to stream and explore satellite and aerial imagery of the Earth in three dimensions. Keyhole was acquired by Google in 2004, and its technology became the basis of Google Earth, launched in 2005.

History

Keyhole, Inc. was founded in 2001 and received early backing from Sony and, significantly, In-Q-Tel, the venture capital arm of the US Central Intelligence Agency. The company's name alluded to the KH "KeyHole" series of American reconnaissance satellites. Its Earth Viewer application gained wide attention during the 2003 invasion of Iraq, when television networks used it to display imagery of Baghdad and other locations.

Technology

Keyhole's system streamed compressed imagery and terrain data from central servers to a desktop client, which stitched the data into a continuously zoomable virtual globe. The markup language it developed for annotating geographic features, KML (Keyhole Markup Language), later became an open standard adopted by the Open Geospatial Consortium in 2008 and remains the standard format for Google Earth annotations.

Acquisition by Google

On October 27, 2004, Google announced its acquisition of Keyhole. Keyhole's Earth Viewer was reworked and relaunched on June 28, 2005 as Google Earth, while Keyhole's co-founder John Hanke went on to lead Google's Geo division and later founded Niantic.
