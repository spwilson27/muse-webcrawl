Red River of the North

The Red River, often called the Red River of the North to distinguish it from the Red River of the South, is a river in the north-central United States and central Canada. It originates at the confluence of the Bois de Sioux and Otter Tail rivers in Wahpeton, North Dakota, and flows generally northward for about 885 kilometres (550 mi) to Lake Winnipeg in Manitoba.

Course

The river forms most of the border between North Dakota and Minnesota, passing the Fargo–Moorhead and Grand Forks–East Grand Forks metropolitan areas. In Manitoba it flows through Winnipeg, where it is joined by the Assiniboine River at The Forks, and continues past Selkirk to Lake Winnipeg.

Flooding

The river drains a remarkably flat plain, the bed of glacial Lake Agassiz, and flows north into regions still frozen when spring melt begins in the south — a combination that produces frequent severe flooding. Major floods struck in 1997, devastating Grand Forks, and in 2009 and 2011. Winnipeg is protected by the Red River Floodway, a large diversion channel completed in 1968.
