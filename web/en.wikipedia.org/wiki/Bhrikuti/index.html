Bhrikuti Devi (Tibetan: བལ་བཟའ་ཁྲི་བཙུན་, bal bza' khri btsun) was a princess of the Licchavi kingdom of Nepal who, according to tradition, married the Tibetan emperor Songtsen Gampo in the 7th century CE and played a role in the introduction of Buddhism to Tibet.

Life and legend

Tibetan histories describe Bhrikuti as a daughter of the Nepalese king Amshuverma. Her marriage to Songtsen Gampo, along with that of the Chinese princess Wencheng, is traditionally credited with bringing Buddhist images and practices to the Tibetan court. Bhrikuti is said to have brought a revered statue of Akshobhya Buddha (the Mikyo Dorje), which was housed in the Jokhang temple complex in Lhasa. In later Tibetan tradition she is regarded as an incarnation of Green Tara, while Wencheng is associated with White Tara.

Historicity

Scholars debate the historical details of Bhrikuti's life; contemporary Nepalese and Tibetan records from the period are sparse, and much of the narrative comes from later chronicles. Nevertheless, the marriage alliance reflects the close political and religious ties between the Licchavi kingdom and the expanding Tibetan Empire in the 7th century.
