The Ukrainian War of Independence was a series of conflicts, lasting from 1917 to 1921, through which Ukrainian national movements attempted to establish an independent state in the aftermath of the Russian Revolution and the collapse of the Russian and Austro-Hungarian empires. It involved shifting struggles among the Ukrainian People's Republic, the Hetmanate of Pavlo Skoropadskyi, the Bolsheviks, the White armies, Poland, and peasant insurgent forces such as Nestor Makhno's Revolutionary Insurgent Army.

Course of the war

In 1917 the Central Rada in Kyiv proclaimed the Ukrainian People's Republic, first as an autonomous part of Russia and, in January 1918, as independent. Bolshevik forces invaded, briefly taking Kyiv; the government obtained German and Austrian intervention through the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, but Berlin soon backed Skoropadskyi's coup, creating the Hetmanate. After the Central Powers' defeat in November 1918, a Directory under Volodymyr Vynnychenko and Symon Petliura overthrew the Hetmanate and restored the Republic, while the West Ukrainian People's Republic was proclaimed in Galicia and formally united with it in January 1919.

From 1919 the Republic fought simultaneously against the Red Army, Denikin's Whites, and—after the Ukrainian–Polish alliance of 1920—alongside Poland against the Bolsheviks in the Polish–Soviet War. The Treaty of Riga (1921) partitioned Ukraine: the Soviet Ukrainian republic took the east and centre, while Poland received Galicia and Volhynia. Petliura's forces crossed into exile, ending organized resistance.

Consequences

The war failed to secure independence, but it established a precedent of modern Ukrainian statehood that was invoked again in 1991. The period also saw devastating violence, including pogroms against Jewish communities perpetrated by various armed forces, famine, and epidemic disease.
