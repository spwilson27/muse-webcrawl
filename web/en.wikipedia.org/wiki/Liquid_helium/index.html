Liquid helium

Boiling point (He-4)4.2 K at 1 atm
Density≈ 0.125 g/cm³ at boiling point
Lambda point2.17 K

Liquid helium is the liquid state of the element helium, existing only at extremely low temperatures. It was first produced by Heike Kamerlingh Onnes in Leiden on 10 July 1908, an achievement that opened the field of cryogenics.

Phases

Helium-4 boils at 4.2 K under atmospheric pressure. Below the lambda point of about 2.17 K it becomes helium-II, a superfluid with remarkable properties: zero viscosity, extremely high effective thermal conductivity, and the ability to flow through capillaries and climb walls as a thin film. Helium-3, the lighter isotope, boils at 3.2 K and becomes superfluid only below about 2.5 mK.

Applications

Liquid helium is used to cool superconducting magnets in MRI scanners and particle accelerators, infrared detectors, and low-temperature physics experiments. Because helium is a non-renewable resource on practical timescales, recovery and reliquefaction systems are widely used.
