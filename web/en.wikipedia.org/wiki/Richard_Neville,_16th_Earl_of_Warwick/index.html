Richard Neville, 16th Earl of Warwick (22 November 1428 – 14 April 1471), known as Warwick the Kingmaker, was an English nobleman, administrator and military commander — the richest and most powerful peer of his generation and a pivotal figure in the Wars of the Roses. Through inheritance and his marriage to Anne Beauchamp he controlled vast estates in the Midlands and the Welsh Marches, and served as Captain of Calais, the most important English military command abroad.

Role in the Wars of the Roses

Warwick was the chief supporter of Richard, Duke of York's claim against Henry VI. After victories at St Albans and Northampton (1460) and the Yorkist triumph at Towton (1461), he helped place Edward IV on the throne. The two men fell out over Edward's marriage to Elizabeth Woodville, his pro-Burgundian foreign policy (Warwick favoured a French alliance) and the rise of the Woodville family. In 1469–70 Warwick rebelled, captured Edward, and then, driven into exile, allied with Margaret of Anjou and restored Henry VI (the "Readeption"). Edward returned in 1471 with Burgundian support and defeated Warwick at the Battle of Barnet, where the Earl was killed fleeing the field.

Legacy

Warwick's daughters embodied his ambitions: Isabel married George, Duke of Clarence, and Anne — first wed to Edward of Westminster, Henry VI's son — became queen as wife of Richard III. His nickname "the Kingmaker", popularized in the 17th century, reflects his unique role in making and unmaking two kings.
