Community radio

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Community radio is a form of radio broadcasting that serves a local geographic community or a community of interest, is typically operated on a non-profit basis, and involves volunteers from that community in its ownership, management, and programming. It is often described as a "third tier" of radio alongside public (state) broadcasting and commercial radio.

History and models

Early models include the Bolivian miners' radio stations of the late 1940s and Colombia's Radio Sutatenza (1947), which pioneered educational broadcasting for peasants. In the United States, the Pacifica Foundation's KPFA (Berkeley, 1949) established listener-sponsored radio, and the FCC later created the Low Power FM (LPFM) service in 2000 for non-commercial local stations. The United Kingdom legalised community radio with licences administered by Ofcom from 2004–2005, following earlier "access radio" pilots; Australia and Canada developed large community broadcasting sectors from the 1970s, and community radio became a major medium across Africa and South Asia for local-language news, health, and agricultural information.

Characteristics

Stations commonly operate at low power, rely on grants, donations, and limited sponsorship, and are expected to deliver social gain — training, access for under-represented groups, and local content neglected by larger broadcasters. The World Association of Community Radio Broadcasters (AMARC, founded 1983) coordinates the movement internationally.
