Oppau explosion

The Oppau explosion occurred on 21 September 1921 at a BASF fertiliser plant in Oppau, near Ludwigshafen, Germany. A silo containing about 4,500 tonnes of a mixture of ammonium sulfate and ammonium nitrate fertiliser exploded, in one of the largest non-nuclear explosions in history.

The fertiliser had caked solid, and workers used small explosive charges to loosen it — a practice that had reportedly been used many thousands of times without incident. On this occasion a charge detonated the bulk material, creating a crater roughly 90 by 125 metres and 19 metres deep.

More than 500 people were killed and over 2,000 injured; most of Oppau's buildings were damaged or destroyed, and the blast was heard hundreds of kilometres away. The disaster led to stricter regulation of ammonium nitrate storage and handling.
