The Battle of Kwajalein was fought from 31 January to 3 February 1944 as the centrepiece of Operation Flintlock, the American invasion of the Marshall Islands during the Pacific War. US forces seized Kwajalein Atoll — Kwajalein Island at its southern end and the twin islands of Roi and Namur at its northern end — from the Japanese garrison, opening the Central Pacific drive toward the Marianas.

Background

Kwajalein, the world's largest coral atoll, had been developed by Japan under its South Seas Mandate into an important air and naval base. Following the costly victory at Tarawa in November 1943, Admiral Chester Nimitz chose to bypass the Marshalls' outer islands and strike directly at Kwajalein, applying the lessons of Tarawa: prolonged naval and air bombardment, ample amphibious tractors, and underwater demolition teams.

The battle

After days of shelling and bombing that devastated the defences, landings began on 31 January on islets flanking the main objectives. On 1 February the 4th Marine Division assaulted Roi and Namur, securing them within about a day despite fierce resistance around the airfield and the concrete structures on Namur — whose explosion, when a blockhouse containing torpedo warheads was demolished, caused many American casualties. In the south, the Army's 7th Infantry Division landed on Kwajalein Island on 1 February and methodically reduced the garrison, declaring the island secure on 4 February (fighting having effectively ended on the 3rd).

Outcome

Of roughly 8,700 Japanese and Korean defenders and labourers on the two objective areas, only a few hundred were taken prisoner. American battle deaths numbered about 370 — strikingly low compared with Tarawa — validating the new amphibious methods. Kwajalein became a major US base, and its capture was swiftly followed by the landings on Eniwetok (Operation Catchpole).
