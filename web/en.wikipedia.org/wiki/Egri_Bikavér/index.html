Egri Bikavér

Egri Bikavér ("Bull's Blood of Eger") is a red wine produced in Eger, Hungary. It is a blend of several grape varieties, traditionally including Kékfrankos (Blaufränkisch), and is known for its deep ruby colour, full body, and spicy, fruity character. The name "Bull's Blood" is also used for a similar wine from Szekszárd, known as Szekszárdi Bikavér.

History and legend

Legend links the name to the siege of Eger in 1552, when the defenders of Eger Castle, under István Dobó, repelled a much larger Ottoman army. The story holds that the soldiers drank large quantities of the local red wine, which stained their beards and armour; the Ottoman troops, believing the Hungarians had been drinking bull's blood, were demoralised. Historically, the region's red wines grew in reputation in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and Bikavér became Hungary's best-known red wine abroad.

Production

Modern regulations require Egri Bikavér to be a blend of at least three grape varieties grown in the Eger wine region, with no single variety exceeding a set proportion; Kékfrankos is typically the backbone. The wine is aged in oak barrels before release, and a higher category, Egri Bikavér Superior, is subject to stricter rules on yield and ageing.
