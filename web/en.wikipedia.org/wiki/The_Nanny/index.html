The Nanny is an American television sitcom that aired on CBS from November 3, 1993, to June 23, 1999, starring Fran Drescher as Fran Fine, a Queens-born fashionista who, after being fired from her job and dumped by her boyfriend, becomes the nanny to the three children of a wealthy widowed Broadway producer. The series was created by Drescher and her then-husband Peter Marc Jacobson, and was inspired in part by Drescher's own upbringing in Queens, New York.

Premise

Fran Fine, selling cosmetics door-to-door in Manhattan, is hired by widower Maxwell Sheffield (Charles Shaughnessy), a British Broadway producer, to care for his children Maggie, Brighton and Grace. Fran's unorthodox, warm-hearted approach to childcare clashes comically with the formality of the Sheffield household, which includes the deadpan butler Niles (Daniel Davis) and Maxwell's business partner C. C. Babcock (Lauren Lane), who regards Fran as a rival. Much of the comedy derives from the culture clash between Fran's working-class Jewish family from Flushing — her mother Sylvia and grandmother Yetta are frequent presences — and the Sheffields' upper-class world, and from the slow-burning romantic tension between Fran and Maxwell, who eventually marry in the fifth season. In the final season Fran gives birth to twins.

Cast and characters

  • Fran Drescher as Fran Fine (later Sheffield), the nanny.
  • Charles Shaughnessy as Maxwell Sheffield, the widowed producer.
  • Daniel Davis as Niles, the butler, famed for his sarcastic one-liners, usually aimed at C. C.
  • Lauren Lane as C. C. Babcock, Maxwell's associate.
  • Nicholle Tom, Benjamin Salisbury and Madeline Zima as Margaret "Maggie", Brighton and Grace Sheffield.
  • Renée Taylor as Sylvia Fine, Fran's perpetually hungry mother; Ann Morgan Guilbert as Grandma Yetta.

Reception and legacy

The series was a ratings success for CBS and became a substantial international hit, syndicated in dozens of countries and spawning numerous local adaptations, including versions in Russia (My Fair Nanny), Turkey, Poland, Greece and Argentina. Drescher was nominated for two Emmy Awards and two Golden Globe Awards for her performance. The show's distinctive theme song, "The Nanny Named Fran," was written and performed by Ann Hampton Callaway. The cast reunited for a virtual table read of the pilot episode in 2020, and a stage musical adaptation with music by Rachel Bloom was announced as in development.
