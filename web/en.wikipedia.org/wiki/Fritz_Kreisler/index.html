Fritz Kreisler

Fritz Kreisler (2 February 1875 – 29 January 1962) was an Austrian-born American violinist and composer, celebrated for his warm tone, expressive vibrato and charm, and regarded as one of the greatest violin masters of his era.

Career

A child prodigy from Vienna, Kreisler studied at the Vienna Conservatory and in Paris, winning the Premier Prix at the Paris Conservatoire at age twelve. After a period away from music, during which he studied medicine and served as an army officer, he resumed his career with a triumphant Berlin debut in 1899 and became one of the most beloved concert artists in the world.

Compositions and hoaxes

Kreisler wrote popular short pieces such as Liebesleid, Liebesfreud and Schön Rosmarin. In 1935 it was revealed that many works he had presented as arrangements of older composers — pieces attributed to Pugnani, Tartini and others — were in fact his own compositions, a scandal that caused a sensation but did little lasting damage to his reputation. He also wrote the operetta Apple Blossoms and a string quartet.

Later life

Kreisler became an American citizen in 1943 and lived in New York. He was famous for playing on fine instruments, including the 1733 Guarneri del Gesù now known as the "ex-Kreisler". He died in New York City in 1962.
