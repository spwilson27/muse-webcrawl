Voice over LTE

VoLTE carries voice as IP packets over the LTE network using IMS

Voice over LTE (VoLTE) is a standard for high-speed wireless voice communication for mobile phones and data terminals, including Internet of Things devices, based on the IP Multimedia Subsystem (IMS) network. It allows voice calls to be carried as data within the LTE bearer rather than falling back to the circuit-switched 2G or 3G networks, giving faster call setup, higher-quality audio and simultaneous voice and data.

Technology

The GSMA profile for VoLTE is defined in PRD IR.92. Calls are set up with SIP signalling through the IMS core and transported with RTP over a dedicated bearer with guaranteed quality of service (QCI-1). The default speech codec is AMR-NB and AMR-WB, the latter providing "HD Voice"; the newer EVS codec, offering super-wideband and fullband audio, is used in later deployments and in 5G Voice over NR (VoNR), for which VoLTE is the evolutionary basis. Emergency calling, supplementary services and SMS over IMS are supported, and Single Radio Voice Call Continuity (SRVCC) hands an ongoing call over to 2G/3G circuit-switched service when the user leaves LTE coverage.

History and deployment

The first commercial VoLTE services launched in August 2012 with MetroPCS in the United States and operators in South Korea (SK Telecom and LG U+). Adoption broadened through the mid-2010s as IMS cores were installed and handsets gained support, and VoLTE became essential as operators shut down 3G networks — in many markets a phone without VoLTE can no longer place calls at all. Benefits commonly cited are call setup times of around two seconds (against roughly eight for circuit-switched fallback), clearer wideband audio, improved battery life from avoiding network switching, and freed spectrum as legacy voice networks are retired.
