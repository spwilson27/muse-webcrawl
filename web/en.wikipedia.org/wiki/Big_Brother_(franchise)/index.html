Big Brother (franchise)

Big Brother is a reality television franchise created by John de Mol of Endemol, first broadcast in the Netherlands in 1999. In each edition, a group of contestants ("housemates" or "house guests") live together in a specially built house, isolated from the outside world and filmed continuously by cameras and microphones. Housemates are periodically evicted, usually by nomination and a public or house vote, and the last remaining contestant wins a cash prize. The name derives from the character in George Orwell's novel Nineteen Eighty-Four.

Housemates live under constant camera surveillance.

Format

Across its many national versions, core elements recur: weekly (or more frequent) tasks and competitions, a Head of Household or equivalent power in some versions, nominations, eviction nights, and a diary room where contestants speak privately to "Big Brother". The United States version, launched in 2000 and reshaped in its second season, emphasises strategic gameplay; many other versions rely primarily on public voting.

International reach

The format has been produced in more than 60 countries and territories, including the United Kingdom, Australia, India (as Bigg Boss), Brazil, Germany, Spain and the Philippines, with hundreds of seasons aired. Celebrity editions are common. The franchise is considered a landmark of reality television and has generated extensive academic debate about surveillance, privacy and fame.
