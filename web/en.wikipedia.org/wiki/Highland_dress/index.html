Highland dress

Highland dress is the traditional regional dress of the Highlands and Isles of Scotland. It is often characterised by tartan (plaid) patterns in specific colours, and its best-known garment is the kilt.

Men's dress

Traditional men's Highland dress includes a kilt or trews, a jacket (such as an Argyll or Prince Charlie jacket), a sporran worn at the front of the kilt, kilt hose with flashes, ghillie brogues, and often a sgian-dubh tucked into the hose. Day dress is plainer, while evening dress is more formal, with a bow tie and dress sporran.

Women's dress

Women's Highland dress traditionally includes tartan skirts or dresses, and the arisaid, a form of plaid garment. In modern formal wear, tartan sashes and brooches are common.

History

The belted plaid (féileadh mór) was the characteristic garment of Highlanders in the 16th to 18th centuries. After the Jacobite rising of 1745, the Dress Act 1746 banned Highland dress for men and boys, though it was repealed in 1782. In the 19th century, Highland dress was romanticised and popularised, partly through the works of Sir Walter Scott and the state visit of George IV to Edinburgh in 1822.
