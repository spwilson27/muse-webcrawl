Sikandar Bagh

Sikandar Bagh (Hindi: सिकन्दर बाग़, Urdu: سکندر باغ), formerly known by the British as Sikunder/Sikandra/Secundra Bagh, is a villa and garden enclosure located in Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh, India. It was built by the last Nawab of Awadh, Wajid Ali Shah (1822–1887), as a summer residence. The name of the villa signifies "Garden of Sikandar", perhaps after Sikandar Mahal Begum, the Nawab's favourite wife, or after Alexander the Great (whose name is rendered Sikandar in Persian and Urdu).

The enclosure is famous for the fierce fighting that took place there during the Indian Rebellion of 1857, when it was stormed by British forces under Sir Colin Campbell on 16 November 1857 during the relief of Lucknow. Several thousand sepoys who had taken position inside the walled garden were killed in the assault.

The battle of 1857

During the second relief of Lucknow, Sikandar Bagh stood on the line of advance toward the Residency. The garden, enclosed by a high wall with a single gateway, was held by an estimated 2,000–3,000 rebel sepoys. British troops of the 93rd Highlanders and other regiments breached the wall and stormed the enclosure; the fighting was exceptionally bloody and few of the defenders escaped. The event was photographed afterwards by Felice Beato, whose image of the gateway littered with skeletal remains became one of the most famous photographs of the rebellion.

Present day

Today the grounds house the National Botanical Research Institute of India. The original gateway, a pavilion and a portion of the wall survive, and a memorial commemorates the battle.
