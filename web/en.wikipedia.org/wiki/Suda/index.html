Suda

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A manuscript page of the Suda, the great Byzantine encyclopedia.

The Suda or Souda (Medieval Greek: Σοῦδα) is a large 10th-century Byzantine encyclopedia of the ancient Mediterranean world, written in Greek. It is an encyclopedic lexicon, written in alphabetical order, with 30,000 entries, many drawing from ancient sources that have since been lost, and often derived from medieval Christian compilers.

The derivation of the name is disputed: it may come from the Byzantine Greek word souda, meaning "fortress" or "stronghold", or from Latin or Greek words denoting a guide. The work was formerly attributed to a supposed author named "Suidas", but it is now understood to be the product of a team of compilers working under imperial patronage in Constantinople.

Content

The Suda contains entries on grammar, history, biography, geography, natural history, and literature. It preserves quotations from and summaries of classical authors — including Homer, Aristophanes, Thucydides, and many others — whose works have not survived in full, making it an invaluable source for classical scholarship. Between the ancient and Byzantine sources, the Suda also incorporates material from scholia (commentaries) on classical texts and from earlier lexica such as that of Hesychius.

Value and limitations

The Suda is of considerable value for the history of Greek literature and for the biographies of ancient authors, for which it is often the only surviving source. However, its entries are of uneven quality, contain errors and interpolations from later copyists, and give no systematic bibliographic references.

Editions

The first printed edition appeared in Milan in 1499. The standard modern edition is that of Ada Adler (1928–1938), and the text is available online through the Suda On Line project, a collaborative translation effort.
