Andrey Anatolyevich Zaliznyak (29 April 1935 – 24 December 2017) was a Russian linguist, a leading specialist in Slavic morphology, accentology and the language of medieval Novgorod. He was a full member of the Russian Academy of Sciences and one of Russia's most prominent public defenders of scholarly linguistics against pseudoscience.

Education and career

Zaliznyak studied Romance philology at Moscow State University and at the Sorbonne. He spent his career at the Institute of Slavic Studies of the Russian Academy of Sciences and taught at Moscow State University, as well as visiting positions at European universities.

Work

His Grammatical Dictionary of the Russian Language (1977) systematised Russian inflection with a rigorous index system widely used in computational linguistics. His studies of Old Russian and Common Slavic accentuation — the "Zaliznyak accent paradigm" — reshaped the field of Slavic historical phonology.

From the 1980s he was the chief linguistic analyst of the Novgorod birch bark letters, the medieval documents excavated in Novgorod; his multi-volume editions (with Valentin Yanin) reconstructed the Old Novgorod dialect, which proved markedly different from the Old Russian of Kiev and Suzdal.

In The Tale of Igor's Campaign: A Linguist's View (2004) he argued from the text's grammar that the 12th-century epic could not be an 18th-century forgery, since its language systematically matches Old Russian features only discovered by linguists in the 20th century. He was also a vocal critic of Anatoly Fomenko's "New Chronology" and hosted popular lectures on linguistics and pseudo-archaeology. In 2007 he received the State Prize of Russia.
