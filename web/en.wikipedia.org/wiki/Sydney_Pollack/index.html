Sydney Pollack

Born: July 1, 1934; Lafayette, Indiana, U.S.

Died: May 26, 2008 (aged 73); Los Angeles, California, U.S.

Occupations: Film director, producer, actor

Years active: 1955–2008

Sydney Irwin Pollack (July 1, 1934 – May 26, 2008) was an American film director, producer, and actor. Pollack directed more than 20 films and 10 television shows, acted in over 30 films and shows, and produced many films. He won the Academy Award for Best Director and Best Picture for Out of Africa (1985) and was also Oscar-nominated for They Shoot Horses, Don't They? (1969) and Tootsie (1982).

Career

Pollack trained as an actor with Sanford Meisner at the Neighborhood Playhouse and began directing television in the early 1960s before moving to features with The Slender Thread (1965). His major films include Jeremiah Johnson (1972), The Way We Were (1973), Three Days of the Condor (1975), Absence of Malice (1981), Tootsie (1982), Out of Africa (1985), The Firm (1993), and The Interpreter (2005). He frequently collaborated with Robert Redford, directing him in seven films.

Producing and acting

Through his company Mirage Enterprises, Pollack produced films including The Fabulous Baker Boys (1989), Sense and Sensibility (1995), and Cold Mountain (2003), several in partnership with Anthony Minghella. He also acted, appearing in his own Tootsie, Woody Allen's Husbands and Wives (1992), and Stanley Kubrick's Eyes Wide Shut (1999). Pollack died of cancer in 2008.
