Derby du Rhône

The Derby du Rhône is a football rivalry between the French clubs Olympique Lyonnais (OL) and AS Saint-Étienne (ASSE), two cities of the former Rhône-Alpes region separated by about 60 kilometres. Named after the River Rhône, it is considered one of the most intense and fiercely contested derbies in French football.

Background

The rivalry reflects a deeper social contrast: Lyon, a wealthy commercial and administrative city, against Saint-Étienne, a working-class industrial city built on coal mining and manufacturing. Saint-Étienne dominated French football in the 1960s and 1970s, winning ten league titles, while Lyon never won a championship until beginning a run of seven consecutive Ligue 1 titles from 2002 to 2008 — an era that shifted the balance of the derby decisively toward OL.

Notable matches

The derby has produced celebrated results and frequent crowd incidents, leading at times to bans on travelling supporters. Memorable fixtures include Lyon's 5–0 wins and Saint-Étienne victories at the height of their 1970s European runs. When the clubs meet, the fixture is treated as the defining match of the season by both sets of fans, irrespective of league position.
