Éomer, marshal and later king of Rohan.

Éomer is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's novel The Lord of the Rings. He is a rider of Rohan, nephew of King Théoden and brother of Éowyn, and after Théoden's death becomes the eighteenth King of Rohan.

Orphaned young, Éomer was raised in Théoden's household and rose to become Third Marshal of the Riddermark. In The Two Towers he defies orders to pursue the orc-band that has captured Merry and Pippin, destroys it at the edge of Fangorn Forest, and lends horses to Aragorn, Legolas and Gimli. For this disobedience he is briefly imprisoned by the manipulated Théoden, but he is freed when Gandalf heals the king, and he fights at the Battle of the Hornburg, in the charge at the Pelennor Fields, and at the Black Gate.

Character and reception

Tolkien drew the culture of the Rohirrim from the Anglo-Saxons; Éomer's name is from Old English eoh ("horse") and mǣre ("famous"), and a figure of the same name appears in Beowulf. Critics have noted Éomer's role as the hot-blooded but loyal foil to the more measured Aragorn, and his friendship oath with Aragorn, fulfilled throughout their long reigns. In Peter Jackson's film trilogy he is played by Karl Urban.
