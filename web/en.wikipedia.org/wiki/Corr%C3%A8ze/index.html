Corrèze

Corrèze (French pronunciation: [kɔʁɛz]) is a department in south-central France, in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region, named after the river Corrèze which runs through it. Its prefecture is Tulle, and its largest town is Brive-la-Gaillarde. The department is known for its rural landscapes on the western edge of the Massif Central.

History

Corrèze was one of the original 83 departments created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790, formed from part of the former province of Limousin. The department was the political base of Jacques Chirac, a native of Paris whose family was from Corrèze and who represented it in parliament for decades.

Geography

The department is crossed by the Corrèze and Dordogne river valleys. Its high plateaux, such as the Plateau de Millevaches, are among the most sparsely populated areas of France.
