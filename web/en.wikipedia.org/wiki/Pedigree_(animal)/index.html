Pedigree (animal)

A pedigree is a recorded genealogy of an animal, listing its ancestors — typically parents, grandparents, and further generations — together with identifying details such as registered names, dates of birth, and noteworthy achievements. Pedigrees are maintained for purebred domestic animals including dogs, cats, horses, and livestock, and are usually issued or recorded by a breed registry or kennel club.

Purpose

Pedigrees allow breeders to plan matings by tracking inherited traits, avoiding excessive inbreeding, and verifying that an animal belongs to a particular breed. In horse racing and showing, a documented pedigree is essential to an animal's value and eligibility to compete in restricted classes or races. A pedigree chart conventionally places the sire (father) on the upper portion and the dam (mother) on the lower, with each successive generation read back from the individual.

Registration

An animal is generally eligible for a registered pedigree only if both parents are themselves registered with the same registry and the mating and birth are reported according to the registry's rules. Registration papers typically record the animal's ancestors for three to five generations. Unregistered animals of known ancestry may be described as "pedigreed" informally, but the term properly implies registry documentation.

Genetic considerations

Pedigree records underpin pedigree analysis in genetics: the calculation of inbreeding coefficients, the tracing of inherited disorders through families, and the estimation of breeding values in livestock improvement programmes. Errors in recorded parentage, detectable by DNA testing, are a known source of inaccuracy in historical pedigrees.
