Holyrood Park is a royal park in Edinburgh, Scotland, immediately east of the Palace of Holyroodhouse and the city centre. Often described as a miniature Highlands landscape within the city, its 260 hectares of hills, lochs, glens and cliffs are dominated by Arthur's Seat, the remains of an ancient volcano, and are protected as a Site of Special Scientific Interest and a scheduled monument.

Arthur's Seat, the park's highest point, with the Salisbury Crags in the foreground.

Geology and landscape

The park's hills are the remnants of a Carboniferous volcano active about 350 million years ago, scoured by Ice Age glaciers into crags and tails. Arthur's Seat (251 m) is the highest of a group of hills including the Salisbury Crags — a noted landmark in the history of geology, where James Hutton found evidence for his theory of deep geological time ("Hutton's Section"). The park contains three lochs: St Margaret's Loch, Dunsapie Loch and Duddingston Loch, the last a bird sanctuary on the park's southern edge.

History

The hillfort on Arthur's Seat and other traces show human settlement from prehistory; the area has been linked (without proof) to the Gododdin stronghold of Din Eidyn. The park was enclosed as a royal hunting ground associated with Holyrood Abbey, founded by David I in 1128 — legend tells the king founded the abbey after being saved from a stag by a miraculous cross ("holy rood"). Mary, Queen of Scots knew the park well. In the 19th century Prince Albert planned paths and landscaping, and the Queen's Drive was laid out around the hills. Today the park is managed by Historic Environment Scotland on behalf of the Crown.

Features and recreation

Within the park stand the ruins of St Anthony's Chapel (15th century), St Margaret's Well, and the village of Duddingston with its Kirk — one of Scotland's oldest churches — where the minister John Thomson painted and the philosopher's "Skating Minister" portrait by Raeburn is set on Duddingston Loch. The park is popular for walking, running, climbing and views over Edinburgh, the Firth of Forth and the Pentland Hills; a road closure programme ("Holyrood Park roads" weekends) increasingly reserves it for recreation. It adjoins Dynamic Earth, the Scottish Parliament and the Palace of Holyroodhouse.
