Ötzi

Bornc. 3345 BC, near present-day Bolzano, Italy
Diedc. 3300 BC (aged about 45), Ötztal Alps, Austria–Italy border
Discovered19 September 1991, by Helmut and Erika Simon
Now atSouth Tyrol Museum of Archaeology, Bolzano

Ötzi, also called the Iceman, is the natural mummy of a man who lived between 3350 and 3105 BC. Discovered in the Ötztal Alps on the Austria–Italy border in September 1991, he is Europe's oldest known natural human mummy and has offered an unprecedented view of Chalcolithic (Copper Age) life.

Discovery

Two German tourists, Helmut and Erika Simon, found the body at an altitude of 3,210 metres near the Similaun mountain and the Tisenjoch pass. At first assumed to be a modern corpse, the body was recovered by Austrian authorities, and its great age was recognised by archaeologist Konrad Spindler of the University of Innsbruck.

Scientific analysis

Ötzi stood about 1.60 m tall, weighed roughly 50 kg in life, and was about 45 years old when he died. Analysis of his stomach contents revealed meals of ibex, red deer, and einkorn wheat. His body carries 61 tattoos, the oldest evidence of tattooing known. Genome sequencing published in 2012 showed he had brown eyes, type O blood, was lactose intolerant, and was predisposed to cardiovascular disease; he also hosted the oldest known case of Lyme disease infection.

Clothing and equipment

Ötzi carried a copper axe with a yew haft — a prestigious object — along with a flint dagger, a bow of yew, a quiver of arrows, a backpack frame, and a belt pouch containing a tinder fungus kit for making fire. His clothing included a bearskin cap, a woven grass cloak, and shoes made of bearskin and deerskin stuffed with grass for insulation.

Death

CT scans in 2001 revealed a flint arrowhead lodged in Ötzi's left shoulder; he is believed to have bled to death after being shot, having also suffered a head injury. Blood from several other individuals was found on his weapons, suggesting he died in or after a fight. His body was preserved by the glacier that covered it soon after his death.
