One Liberty Place is a skyscraper in Center City Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Completed in 1987, the 61-story tower rises 945 feet (288 m) to the top of its spire and was the first building in Philadelphia to exceed the height of the statue of William Penn atop City Hall — breaking a gentlemen's agreement that had governed the city's skyline for most of the twentieth century. It was the tallest building in Philadelphia until the completion of the Comcast Center in 2008.

Design

Designed by architect Helmut Jahn of Murphy/Jahn, the postmodern tower is clad in blue glass and granite and is crowned by a distinctive stepped, faceted spire often compared to New York's Chrysler Building. Its massing steps back in a series of setbacks, and the spire is illuminated at night, making it a defining element of the Philadelphia skyline. The complex includes a second, shorter tower (Two Liberty Place), a retail concourse (the Shops at Liberty Place), and a hotel.

The "gentlemen's agreement" and the curse

From the completion of Philadelphia City Hall in 1901, an informal agreement held that no building would rise above the 548-foot statue of William Penn. Developer Willard Rouse's decision to break that tradition with One Liberty Place was controversial. Local sports folklore subsequently blamed the broken agreement for the "Curse of Billy Penn," a championship drought that afflicted all four of Philadelphia's major professional sports teams from 1983 until 2008 — a drought said to end after a small Penn statue was placed atop the Comcast Center, and later the Comcast Technology Center, restoring him to the city's highest point.

Tenants and use

The tower contains primarily office space and has housed major law firms, financial companies, and the former headquarters of Cigna's Philadelphia operations. The observation deck "One Liberty Observation Deck" operated on the 57th floor from 2015 until its closure in 2020.
