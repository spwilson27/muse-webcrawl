Gustafsen Lake standoff

The Gustafsen Lake standoff was a month-long confrontation in 1995 between the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP) and a small group of Indigenous protesters and their supporters at Gustafsen Lake (Ts'peten) in the Cariboo region of British Columbia, Canada. It remains one of the largest police and military operations in Canadian history.

Gustafsen Lake, site of the 1995 standoff

Background and standoff

Members of the Secwepemc (Shuswap) nation had held a Sundance ceremony at the lake, on land claimed as unceded Indigenous territory but used by a local rancher. In the summer of 1995 tensions escalated after shots were reportedly fired, and the RCMP surrounded the camp. The ensuing standoff, from 18 August to 17 September 1995, involved hundreds of police officers, military armoured personnel carriers lent under Operation Wallaby, and tens of thousands of rounds of ammunition fired in exchanges of gunfire. Remarkably, no one was killed.

Aftermath

The standoff ended with the protesters' surrender. Fifteen Indigenous and three non-Indigenous defendants were convicted of various offences, and the group's leader, William "Wolverine" Jones Ignace, was imprisoned. The case became a landmark in debates over Indigenous land rights in British Columbia; participants and supporters have continued to call for a public inquiry, arguing that the land in question was never ceded and that the police response was disproportionate.
