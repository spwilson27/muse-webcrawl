The Institute of Mathematical Statistics (IMS) is an international professional and scholarly society devoted to the development, dissemination, and application of statistics and probability. Founded in 1935 — after an organizational meeting at the University of Michigan, with figures such as Harry C. Carver among its founders — the institute is one of the principal learned societies in its field, with several thousand members worldwide.

Publications

The IMS publishes or co-publishes leading research journals, including The Annals of Statistics, The Annals of Probability, The Annals of Applied Statistics, The Annals of Applied Probability, and Statistical Science, along with journals produced with partner societies such as the Annals of the Institute of Henri Poincaré and the Bernoulli journal. Its early flagship, the Annals of Mathematical Statistics (1930–1972), split in 1973 into the Annals of Statistics and the Annals of Probability. The IMS also issues lecture-note monograph series and the IMS Bulletin.

Activities

The institute organizes and co-sponsors conferences, most prominently the World Congress in Probability and Statistics held jointly with the Bernoulli Society. It elects Fellows in recognition of research distinction and awards honors including the Wald, Rietz, and Le Cam lectures and medals such as the Carver Medal. Membership grades range from student to Fellow.
