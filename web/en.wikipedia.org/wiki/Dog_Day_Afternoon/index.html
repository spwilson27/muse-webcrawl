Dog Day Afternoon

Dog Day Afternoon is a 1975 American biographical crime drama film directed by Sidney Lumet and written by Frank Pierson. It stars Al Pacino as Sonny Wortzik, based on the real-life bank robber John Wojtowicz, who in 1972 held up a Brooklyn branch of the Chase Manhattan Bank to pay for his lover's sex reassignment surgery.

Plot and reception

The film dramatizes the robbery turning into a hostage standoff and media circus, including Sonny's famous "Attica! Attica!" chant to the crowd. It was a critical and commercial success, won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and was nominated for Best Picture and Best Actor. In 2009 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant."
