The flag of the Faroe Islands is an offset cross flag, resembling the flags of the other Nordic countries. It is called Merkið in Faroese, meaning “the banner”. The design consists of a white field with a Nordic cross in red and blue: the blue cross is edged (fimbriated) and encloses a narrower red cross. White symbolises the creators of the flag and the sea foam, red and blue are colours found in traditional Faroese dress and also echo Norway and Iceland, with which the islands have historic ties.

History

The flag was devised in 1919 by Faroese students in Copenhagen, principally Jens Oliver Lisberg, Emil Joensen, and Dúsan Müller, and was first raised in Fámjin, Lisberg's home village, on 22 June 1919. For two decades it was used unofficially as a symbol of Faroese identity. Its official status came about during the Second World War: when British forces occupied the Faroe Islands in April 1940 (after Denmark was occupied by Germany), Faroese ships needed a flag distinguishing them from Danish vessels, and the British administration recognised Merkið on 25 April 1940. After the war, the flag's status was confirmed in the Home Rule Act of 23 March 1948, which gave the Faroes self-government within the Kingdom of Denmark. Flag Day is celebrated on 25 April; the original 1919 flag hangs in the church at Fámjin.
