Pliosauroidea is a suborder (or clade) of plesiosaurs, extinct marine reptiles that lived from the Early Jurassic until the end of the Cretaceous. Pliosaurs are characterized by a short neck and a large, elongated head—the opposite body plan to the long-necked plesiosauroids—together with powerful jaws and conical teeth, and large paddle-like flippers. They were among the apex predators of Mesozoic seas.

Description

Pliosauroids ranged from about 1.5 metres to well over 10 metres in length; the largest, such as Pliosaurus funkei ("Predator X") and some specimens of Kronosaurus and Liopleurodon, reached roughly 10–13 metres, though popular media often exaggerated their size. Their massive skulls—up to 2 metres or more in the biggest forms—housed strong jaw muscles and robust, sometimes carinated teeth suited to seizing large prey such as fish, ichthyosaurs, and other plesiosaurs. Bite-force modelling suggests pliosaurs had among the most powerful bites of any known animal.

Unlike long-necked plesiosaurs, pliosauroids likely relied on the large hind flippers and a cruising, "underwater flight" style of swimming, with the short, stiff neck and torpedo-shaped body suited to active pursuit and ambush of large prey.

Classification and evolution

Early pliosauroids include the longirostrine (long-snouted) rhomaleosaurids of the Early and Middle Jurassic, which were transitional in proportions. The thalassophonean pliosaurids of the Middle Jurassic to Cretaceous—Liopleurodon, Pliosaurus, Kronosaurus, Brachauchenius, and the polycotylids—developed the classic giant-headed form. The last pliosaurs disappeared at the end-Cretaceous mass extinction, 66 million years ago.
