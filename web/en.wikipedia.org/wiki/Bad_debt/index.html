Bad debt

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: An ageing schedule of accounts receivable, with long-overdue balances flagged for write-off]

Bad debt is debt owed to a creditor that is unlikely to be paid and is therefore worthless or uncollectible. In accounting, amounts owed by customers or borrowers that are judged irrecoverable are written off against income, and businesses commonly maintain an allowance for doubtful accounts based on the estimated share of receivables that will become bad debt.

Accounting treatment

Under the direct write-off method, a receivable is expensed only when it is determined to be uncollectible; this is simple but can distort period results. The preferred allowance method estimates bad debt expense in the same period as the related revenue, creating a contra-asset allowance that is adjusted as actual defaults occur. Estimates are usually based on historical default rates, ageing of receivables, and current economic conditions.

Causes and management

Bad debts arise when borrowers default through insolvency, disappearance, or refusal to pay. Lenders and businesses manage the risk through credit checks, credit limits, collateral, guarantees, prompt invoicing, and collection procedures. Unpaid debts may be sold to collection agencies at a discount or pursued through the courts.

Tax treatment

In many jurisdictions, bad debts that have been included in taxable income may be deducted when they become worthless, subject to statutory conditions.
