The Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta, or California Delta, is a vast inland river delta and tidal estuary in Northern California where the Sacramento River from the north and the San Joaquin River from the south meet and flow westward through Suisun Bay toward San Francisco Bay. Formed over thousands of years by sediment deposited at the Carquinez Strait, it is a maze of channels, sloughs and more than 50 leveed islands.

Water supply role

The Delta is the hub of California's water system. The federal Central Valley Project and the State Water Project pump water from the southern Delta into aqueducts serving Central Valley agriculture and more than 25 million people in Southern California and the Bay Area. Managing the competing demands of water export, flood protection, water quality and ecosystem health has made the Delta one of the most contested environmental and political issues in California, including long-running debates over a proposed conveyance tunnel.

Ecology and land subsidence

Historically a rich tule marsh supporting huge populations of fish and waterfowl, the Delta was reclaimed for farming from the 1850s onward by building levees. Drainage of the peat soils caused oxidation and subsidence, leaving many islands 3–8 metres below sea level and dependent on ageing levees, with risks of catastrophic flooding and saltwater intrusion. The estuary remains critical habitat for species such as the threatened Delta smelt and Chinook salmon, whose declines have repeatedly triggered restrictions on pumping.
