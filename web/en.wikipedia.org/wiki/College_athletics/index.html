College athletics encompasses non-professional sports played by students at colleges and universities. In the United States, college athletics is a major cultural and commercial institution: intercollegiate football and men's basketball draw television audiences and revenues rivalling professional leagues, and are organised principally by the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) into divisions based on institution size and athletic scholarships.

History

Intercollegiate competition began with a Harvard–Yale rowing regatta in 1852. The first intercollegiate American football game was played between Rutgers and Princeton in 1869. Concerns over injuries and amateurism led to the founding of the NCAA in 1906. Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972 greatly expanded women's participation, requiring equal opportunity in school athletics.

Organisation

Universities compete in conferences, mostly regional groupings, and in national championship tournaments such as the NCAA basketball tournament known as March Madness and the College Football Playoff. Athletic scholarships allow recruited students to attend at reduced cost. Recent reforms have transformed the landscape: athletes may now profit from their name, image and likeness (NIL), and conference realignment and transfer rules have reshaped rosters annually.

Outside the United States

In most other countries university sport is smaller in scale and organised through bodies such as BUCS in the United Kingdom, U Sports in Canada and FISU internationally, which runs the World University Games.
