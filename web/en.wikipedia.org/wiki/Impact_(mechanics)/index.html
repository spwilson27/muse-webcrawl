Impact (mechanics)

An impact involves a large force applied over a very short time

In mechanics, an impact is a high force or shock applied over a short time period when two or more bodies collide. Such a force or acceleration usually has a greater effect than a lower force applied over a proportionally longer period. The effect depends critically on the relative velocity of the bodies to one another.

Effects

At normal speeds, during a perfectly elastic collision, an object striking another object will deform it, and the deformation will then push both objects apart. This deformation and subsequent recovery account for the impact force. At high speeds, materials behave differently: stress waves propagate through the bodies, and solids may behave in some respects like fluids. Projectiles striking armour, for example, can cause spalling, in which fragments are ejected from the surface opposite the impact even if the projectile does not penetrate.

Applications

Impact analysis is central to vehicle crash safety, where crumple zones are designed to absorb impact energy and lengthen the collision time, reducing the peak force on occupants. It is also important in ballistics, pile driving, hammer and press tools, and in the study of meteorite impacts. Impact testing, such as the Charpy test, measures a material's toughness — its ability to absorb energy before fracturing.
