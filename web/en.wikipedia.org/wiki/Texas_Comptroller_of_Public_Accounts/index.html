The Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts is an executive branch position created by the Texas Constitution. The comptroller is the state's chief financial officer — in effect its treasurer, tax collector, chief accountant and chief revenue estimator in one office. The comptroller is elected statewide to a four-year term.

The Comptroller's headquarters in Austin

Duties

The Comptroller's office collects more than 60 separate taxes, fees and assessments, including the state sales tax and franchise tax, and administers the state's treasury operations. Before each regular session of the Texas Legislature, the comptroller issues the Biennial Revenue Estimate, projecting how much revenue will be available for the state budget. Under the Texas Constitution's pay-as-you-go provision, the Legislature may not appropriate more than the comptroller certifies will be collected, and the comptroller must certify that any appropriations bill passed is within projected revenue.

Other responsibilities include managing the state's unclaimed property program, publishing economic and fiscal reports, administering the Texas Bullion Depository, and overseeing state purchasing programs.

History

The office traces its origin to the Republic of Texas, and was re-established as an elected state office with Texas statehood in 1846. Notable comptrollers have included Albert Clinton Horton, Bob Bullock, Carole Keeton Strayhorn, Susan Combs and Glenn Hegar, who took office in January 2015.
