Rear derailleur

A rear derailleur is the mechanism at the rear wheel of a derailleur-equipped bicycle that moves the chain from one sprocket of the cassette to another, changing the gear ratio. Cable tension from the shifter moves a sprung parallelogram, and a cage with two pulley wheels — the guide pulley and tension pulley — feeds the chain and takes up slack.

Function

Because a bicycle chain has a fixed length, the derailleur cage swings to absorb the difference in chain wrap between large and small sprockets. The mechanism's capacity, cage length, and maximum sprocket size determine the gearing range it can support.

Adjustment

Limit screws set the innermost and outermost travel; a barrel adjuster trims cable tension so indexed shifters align the guide pulley with each sprocket. Electronic systems replace the cable with motors.
