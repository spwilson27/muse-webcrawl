Dario Argento, the "Master of the Thrill".

Dario Argento (born 7 September 1940) is an Italian film director, screenwriter and producer. His influential work in the horror genre during the 1970s and 1980s, particularly in the subgenre known as giallo, has led him to be referred to as the "Master of the Thrill" and the "Master of Horror".

Career

Argento began his career as a film critic before moving into screenwriting; he co-wrote Sergio Leone's Once Upon a Time in the West (1968), uncredited. His directorial debut, The Bird with the Crystal Plumage (1970), was a major success and helped popularize the giallo. He followed it with The Cat o' Nine Tails (1971) and Four Flies on Grey Velvet (1971), and achieved international acclaim with Deep Red (1975), often considered his masterpiece.

Supernatural horror

With Suspiria (1977), famous for its saturated Technicolor photography and progressive-rock score by Goblin, Argento turned to supernatural horror. Suspiria was the first of his "Three Mothers" trilogy, continued with Inferno (1980) and completed decades later by The Mother of Tears (2007). His other notable films include Tenebrae (1982), Phenomena (1985), and Opera (1987). He also produced George A. Romero's Dawn of the Dead (1978) and Lamberto Bava's Demons (1985).

Style

Argento's films are known for their virtuoso camera movement, bold use of colour and lighting, elaborate set-piece murders, and scores by Goblin and later Claudio Simonetti. His work has been highly influential on subsequent generations of horror filmmakers.
