Cost and freight (CFR)
CategoryIncoterm
AbbreviationCFR
ModeSea and inland waterway only
Former nameC&F (until 1990)

Cost and freight (CFR) is an Incoterm — one of the standardised trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce in the Incoterms rules — used in international commercial contracts for the sale of goods transported by sea or inland waterway. Under CFR, the seller delivers the goods on board the vessel at the port of shipment and pays the cost of carriage to the named port of destination.

Allocation of costs and risk

Despite paying the freight, the seller does not bear the risk of loss or damage during the voyage: risk transfers to the buyer when the goods are placed on board the vessel at the port of shipment. This divergence between the cost point and the risk point is the defining feature of CFR (and of its sibling term CIF). The seller must clear the goods for export, contract for carriage on usual terms, and provide the buyer with the transport document (typically a bill of lading). The buyer bears the cost of import clearance, and — unlike under CIF (cost, insurance and freight) — arranges and pays for insurance if it wants cover.

Usage

CFR is restricted to sea and inland waterway transport; for containerised or multimodal shipments the analogous term is CPT (carriage paid to). The term was known as "C&F" until the 1990 revision of Incoterms standardised the abbreviation CFR. Incoterms 2020 retained CFR unchanged in substance.
