Osedax

PhylumAnnelida
FamilySiboglinidae
Common namesBone-eating worms, zombie worms
HabitatDeep-sea whale falls

Osedax is a genus of deep-sea siboglinid polychaete worms, commonly called bone-eating worms or zombie worms. They specialise in consuming the bones of dead animals — above all the carcasses of whales that sink to the ocean floor, forming "whale falls". The genus was first described in 2004 from specimens collected on a grey whale carcass nearly 3,000 metres deep in Monterey Bay, California.

Biology

Female Osedax have no mouth or gut. Instead they grow root-like tissues into bone, where symbiotic bacteria (Oceanospirillales) break down the lipids and proteins locked in the bone matrix; the worm absorbs the released nutrients. A feathery plume of gills crowns the body and extracts oxygen from the water. Males are microscopic dwarfs: in many species they never develop beyond a larval-like form and live in harems inside the female's gelatinous tube, existing essentially to produce sperm.

Diversity and ecology

Dozens of species are now known, from polar to tropical seas, living on whale, seal, cow and even fish bones at depths from the shelf to the abyss. By dissolving bone, Osedax recycles nutrients and speeds the disappearance of whale falls. Fossil bite-like borings in Cretaceous marine-reptile bones suggest Osedax-like worms have exploited sunken vertebrate skeletons for tens of millions of years.
