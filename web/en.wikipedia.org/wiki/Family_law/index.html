Family law

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Family law (also called matrimonial law or the law of domestic relations) is an area of law that deals with family matters and domestic relations: marriage, civil unions and domestic partnerships; the termination of relationships (divorce, annulment, property settlements, alimony and child support); adoption and surrogacy; child custody, visitation and child protection; and domestic violence and protective orders.

Development

Historically governed by religious law and coverture doctrines subordinating wives' legal identity, family law was transformed in the 19th–20th centuries by married women's property acts, the liberalisation of divorce (notably no-fault divorce from the late 1960s), gender-neutral custody standards centred on the child's best interests (per the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, 1989), and the recognition of same-sex marriage in many jurisdictions from 2001 (Netherlands) onward — with continuing divergence worldwide, including plural systems where religious personal law governs family matters for different communities. Reproductive rights, assisted reproduction and cross-border child abduction (Hague Convention 1980) are active frontiers.
