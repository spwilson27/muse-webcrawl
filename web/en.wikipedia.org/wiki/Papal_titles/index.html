The papal titles are the official styles and dignities borne by the pope, the bishop of Rome and head of the Catholic Church. They accumulated over nearly two millennia and reflect the pope's roles as diocesan bishop, metropolitan, patriarch, head of the universal Church and temporal sovereign. The official list is published each year in the Annuario Pontificio.

The titles

The list traditionally printed in the Annuario Pontificio comprises: Bishop of Rome; Vicar of Jesus Christ; Successor of the Prince of the Apostles; Supreme Pontiff of the Universal Church; Primate of Italy; Archbishop and Metropolitan of the Roman Province; Sovereign of the Vatican City State; and Servant of the Servants of God (Servus Servorum Dei), a title emphasising humility first used prominently by Gregory the Great.

Patriarch of the West

From 1863 the Annuario also included Patriarch of the West, expressing the pope's patriarchal jurisdiction over the Latin Church. In 2006 Pope Benedict XVI had the title removed, a change the Pontifical Council for Promoting Christian Unity explained as dropping an obsolete and potentially misleading formula; some Orthodox and Catholic observers nonetheless regretted it. In April 2024 Pope Francis restored the title to the official list.

"Pope"

The word pope itself derives from Greek pappas and Latin papa, "father". Originally used of bishops generally — and still used by the Coptic and Greek Orthodox churches for the Patriarch of Alexandria — it became reserved in the Latin West to the bishop of Rome from roughly the 11th century, a reservation confirmed in the era of the Gregorian reform.

Use

Documents are signed with the pope's regnal name alone (e.g., "Franciscus"), and encyclicals traditionally begin with name and titles. In ecumenical dialogue, other churches most readily accept the titles "bishop of Rome" and "servant of the servants of God"; the Second Vatican Council and John Paul II's encyclical Ut unum sint (1995) invited reflection on how the ministry of the bishop of Rome might serve Christian unity.
