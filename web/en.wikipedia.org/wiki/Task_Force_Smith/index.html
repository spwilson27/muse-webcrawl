Task Force Smith was a United States Army task force of the 24th Infantry Division, formed hastily from occupation troops in Japan and dispatched to South Korea at the outbreak of the Korean War. Under Lieutenant Colonel Charles Bradford Smith, it was the first American ground unit to fight in the war, engaging North Korean forces at the Battle of Osan on 5 July 1950.

Formation

Following North Korea's invasion of 25 June 1950, General Douglas MacArthur ordered elements of the 24th Infantry Division — then serving as lightly equipped occupation forces in Japan — to Korea. Task Force Smith comprised two understrength infantry companies of the 1st Battalion, 21st Infantry Regiment, plus supporting artillery of the 52nd Field Artillery Battalion, totalling about 540 men. It was airlifted to Pusan on 1 July and moved north by rail and truck.

Battle of Osan

On the morning of 5 July the task force occupied hills astride the road north of Osan, confronting an advancing column of the North Korean 4th Infantry Division supported by T-34 tanks. The Americans' 2.36-inch bazookas and most of their artillery ammunition proved ineffective against the tanks, and their single battery's few high-explosive antitank rounds were soon exhausted. After delaying the North Koreans for roughly seven hours, the task force was outflanked and forced into a disorderly withdrawal, suffering about 60 killed and many more wounded or captured, with most of its heavy equipment abandoned.

Legacy

"No more Task Force Smiths" became a lasting maxim in the United States Army, symbolising the cost of military unpreparedness. The episode is cited in readiness debates to this day, and the battle is commemorated by a monument at the Osan battlefield site in South Korea.
