Lam Sai-wing

Lam Sai-wing (Chinese: 林世榮; 1861 – 1943) was a celebrated master of Hung Ga kung fu and the most famous disciple of the legendary martial artist Wong Fei-hung. Nicknamed "Magnificent Beard" for his long beard, he played a central role in systematizing and popularizing Hung Ga in southern China and Hong Kong.

Life

Born in Pingzhou, Nanhai, Guangdong, Lam came from a family of martial artists and worked in the pork trade, earning the additional nickname "Butcher Wing". He studied under his father and grandfather before becoming a disciple of Wong Fei-hung, eventually becoming his chief instructor.

Teaching and writings

Lam established schools in Guangzhou and later in Hong Kong. In the 1910s and 1920s he published illustrated manuals on the classic Hung Ga forms — including Taming the Tiger Fist (Gung Gee Fook Fu Kuen), Tiger and Crane Double Form Fist (Fu Hok Seung Ying Kuen), and Iron Wire Fist (Tit Sin Kuen) — among the earliest photographic records of Chinese martial arts forms, which greatly influenced the spread of Hung Ga worldwide.

Legacy

Most modern Hung Ga lineages trace their curricula through Lam Sai-wing and his students, including Lam Cho, who continued teaching in Hong Kong.
