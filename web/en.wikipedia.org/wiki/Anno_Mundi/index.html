Anno Mundi

Meaning"In the year of the world" (Latin)
Epoch (Hebrew)3761 BCE
Epoch (Byzantine)5509 BCE
SystemsHebrew calendar, Byzantine calendar, chronicles

Anno Mundi (Latin for "in the year of the world"; abbreviated AM), or "Year after Creation", is a calendar era based on the biblical accounts of the creation of the world and subsequent history. Years are counted from the moment of creation as computed from scriptural genealogies and chronology.

Hebrew calculation

The Hebrew calendar's epoch corresponds to 3761 BCE, derived from the chronology of the Seder Olam Rabbah (c. 2nd century CE). Under this reckoning, the year beginning at Rosh Hashanah in autumn 2024 CE is AM 5785. This system has been used by Jewish communities since the medieval period and remains the basis of the modern Hebrew calendar.

Christian calculations

Early Christian chronographers computed other creation dates. The Byzantine era, used in the Eastern Roman Empire and Orthodox world until 1453, placed creation on 1 September 5509 BCE. Alexandrian scholars such as Panodoros proposed dates around 5493 BCE, while James Ussher's influential 17th-century chronology dated creation to 4004 BCE, a figure printed in many English Bibles.

Usage

Anno Mundi dating appears in Jewish legal documents, on tombstones, and in Freemasonry, where it is called the "Year of Light" and is computed by adding 4,000 years to the common-era year.
