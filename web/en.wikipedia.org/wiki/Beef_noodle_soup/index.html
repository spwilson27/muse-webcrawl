Beef noodle soup is a noodle soup made of stewed or braised beef, beef broth, vegetables and Chinese noodles. It exists in various forms throughout East and Southeast Asia. One of the most famous versions is the red-braised beef noodle soup of Taiwan, widely regarded as a national dish of Taiwan.

History

Beef noodle soup has roots in mainland China, including the hand-pulled beef noodles of Lanzhou in Gansu province, served in a clear broth. The Taiwanese version is generally traced to veterans and their families who settled in Taiwan after the Chinese Civil War around 1949, particularly in military dependents' villages in places such as Gangshan, Kaohsiung. Sichuan-style seasonings — doubanjiang (spicy bean paste), soy sauce, five-spice and Sichuan pepper — were adapted to produce the rich, dark, spicy "red-braised" (紅燒, hóngshāo) broth that distinguishes the Taiwanese style from its clear-broth predecessors.

Variations

  • Red-braised (hong shao) — the most popular Taiwanese style; beef shank, brisket or tendon stewed in a soy-based, spiced, sometimes chilli-hot broth, served over wheat noodles with bok choy and pickled mustard greens.
  • Clear broth (qing dun) — a lighter, salt-seasoned broth closer to the Lanzhou tradition.
  • Tomato beef noodle soup — a modern variant adding tomatoes to the braise.
  • Lanzhou beef noodles — hand-pulled noodles in clear broth with sliced beef, radish, chilli oil and herbs; often eaten halal by Hui communities.

Cultural significance

Beef noodle soup is ubiquitous in Taiwan, from night-market stalls to specialist restaurants, and Taipei has hosted an annual beef noodle festival and competition since 2005. The dish has spread internationally with Taiwanese diaspora communities and is a staple of Taiwanese restaurants worldwide.
