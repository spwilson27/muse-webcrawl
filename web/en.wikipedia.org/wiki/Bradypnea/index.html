Bradypnea

SpecialtyPulmonology, emergency medicine
DefinitionAbnormally slow breathing rate
Threshold (adult)Fewer than 12 breaths per minute at rest
OppositeTachypnea

Respiratory rate is a core vital sign; bradypnea is a rate below the normal range.

Bradypnea is an abnormally slow breathing rate. In a resting adult the normal respiratory rate is about 12 to 20 breaths per minute, so bradypnea is usually defined as fewer than 12 breaths per minute; normal rates are higher in children and infants, so age-specific thresholds apply. The term comes from the Greek bradys (slow) and pnoia (breathing). It is the opposite of tachypnea, an abnormally fast rate, and differs from apnea, the complete cessation of breathing.

Causes

Bradypnea may be physiological — breathing slows naturally during sleep and in well-trained athletes at rest — but in a clinical setting it is most often a sign of central nervous system depression. Common causes include opioids (morphine, heroin, fentanyl) and other sedatives, alcohol intoxication, hypothyroidism, raised intracranial pressure or brainstem injury, hypothermia, and severe metabolic disturbances. Opioid-induced respiratory depression is a leading mechanism of death in opioid overdose.

Significance and management

A slow respiratory rate may be inadequate to clear carbon dioxide, producing respiratory acidosis and hypoxaemia; associated findings include reduced consciousness, pinpoint pupils in opioid toxicity, and eventually apnoeic episodes. Assessment uses the airway–breathing–circulation approach with pulse oximetry and, where indicated, blood gas analysis. Treatment targets the cause: the opioid antagonist naloxone can reverse opioid-induced bradypnea, sedative effects may be countered by stopping the drug and supporting the airway, assisted ventilation is given for inadequate breathing, and underlying conditions such as hypothyroidism or raised intracranial pressure are treated directly.
