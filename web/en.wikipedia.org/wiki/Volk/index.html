Volk


The German word Volk in historical usage

The German noun Volk (pronounced [fɔlk]) means "people" or "folk". Cognate with English folk, the word historically carried senses ranging from a crowd or army to a nation or ethnic community, and it acquired strong ideological connotations in nineteenth- and twentieth-century German nationalism.

Usage and compounds

Volk appears in many compounds: Volkslied (folk song), Volkswagen ("people's car"), Volkshalle, and Volksdeutsche, ethnic Germans living outside the Reich. The adjective völkisch described a nationalist movement that glorified an ethnically defined people.

Nazi era

In Nazi ideology, Volk was central: the regime claimed to embody the will of a racially unified German people, and the word pervaded propaganda, law and titles such as the Völkischer Beobachter newspaper. After 1945 the unqualified political use of the term declined, though it remains in everyday and constitutional language, as in the inscription "Dem deutschen Volke" on the Reichstag building.
