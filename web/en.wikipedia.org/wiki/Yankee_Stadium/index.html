Yankee Stadium is a baseball stadium in the Bronx, New York City, and the home field of the New York Yankees of Major League Baseball. It opened in 2009, replacing the original Yankee Stadium of 1923 — "The House That Ruth Built" — which stood across East 161st Street and was demolished after the 2009 season. The new stadium deliberately echoes its predecessor: the limestone exterior, the great hall with banners of Yankee greats, the recreated white frieze along the roofline, and Monument Park, where the retired numbers and plaques of Yankee legends, moved from the old park, are displayed beyond the centre-field fence.

History and use

The Yankees christened the new stadium by winning the World Series in its first season, defeating Philadelphia. Since 2015 it has also been the home of Major League Soccer's New York City FC, whose matches are played on a pitch laid over the baseball diamond. The stadium hosts the annual Pinstripe Bowl in college football, concerts, boxing and, in 2014, two outdoor NHL hockey games. Its construction, heavily subsidised by public financing and priced at about $2.3 billion including infrastructure, was controversial, but the building is designed to serve for generations as the cathedral of the Yankees franchise.
