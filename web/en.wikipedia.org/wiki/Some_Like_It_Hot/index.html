Some Like It Hot is a 1959 American crime comedy film directed, produced and co-written by Billy Wilder. It stars Marilyn Monroe, Tony Curtis and Jack Lemmon, with George Raft, Pat O'Brien, Joe E. Brown, Joan Shawlee and Nehemiah Persoff. The film is about two musicians who disguise themselves by dressing as women to escape from mafia gangsters whom they witnessed committing a crime.

Plot

In Prohibition-era Chicago, saxophonist Joe (Curtis) and bassist Jerry (Lemmon) witness the Saint Valentine's Day Massacre. Fleeing the gangster "Spats" Colombo (Raft), they join an all-female band heading to Florida, disguised as "Josephine" and "Daphne". Both fall for the band's singer, Sugar Kane (Monroe), while Jerry/Daphne attracts an ardent millionaire admirer, Osgood Fielding III (Brown). The film closes with one of the most famous last lines in cinema: when Jerry finally confesses "I'm a man!", Osgood replies, unfazed:

"Well, nobody's perfect."

Reception and legacy

Shot in black and white despite Monroe's contract calling for colour, the film was made without Production Code approval because of its cross-dressing theme, and its success helped weaken the Code. It was nominated for six Academy Awards, winning for Best Costume Design. In polls by the American Film Institute and the BBC it has repeatedly been named the greatest comedy film of all time; in 1989 it was among the first films selected for the National Film Registry.
