Homo is the genus that emerged in the otherwise extinct hominin tribe Hominini, comprising modern humans and species closely related to them. The genus is characterised by relatively large brains, reduced dentition and jaws compared with australopithecines, obligate bipedalism and an association with stone tool use. It appeared in Africa around 2.8 million years ago, and its members eventually dispersed across the entire globe; today only one species survives, Homo sapiens.

Species

Taxonomy is contested, but commonly recognised species include:

  • H. habilis ("handy man", ~2.3–1.65 Ma) — early, small-brained toolmaker of East Africa.
  • H. rudolfensis (~2.0 Ma) — larger-brained contemporary, sometimes merged into H. habilis.
  • H. erectus (~1.9 Ma–110 ka) — long-lived, fire-using species that spread across Eurasia.
  • H. ergaster — the African form of early H. erectus.
  • H. antecessor (~1.2–0.8 Ma) — from Atapuerca, Spain.
  • H. heidelbergensis (~700–200 ka) — large-brained Middle Pleistocene humans.
  • H. neanderthalensis (~430–40 ka) — the Neanderthals of Europe and western Asia.
  • Denisovans (~285–50 ka) — identified from DNA in Denisova Cave, Siberia.
  • H. naledi (~330–230 ka) — small-brained South African species from Rising Star Cave.
  • H. floresiensis and H. luzonensis — small island species from Indonesia and the Philippines.
  • H. sapiens (~300 ka–present) — anatomically modern humans, earliest fossils at Jebel Irhoud, Morocco.

Evolution and dispersal

Homo evolved from australopithecine ancestors in eastern or southern Africa as climates became cooler and drier and grasslands expanded. The genus shows trends toward larger brains (from ~600 cc in H. habilis to ~1,350 cc in H. sapiens and Neanderthals), smaller teeth, longer legs and delayed maturation. Members left Africa in several waves — H. erectus by ~1.8 Ma (Dmanisi, Georgia), archaic humans by the Middle Pleistocene, and H. sapiens in the major expansion of ~60–70 ka. Genetic evidence shows interbreeding between H. sapiens, Neanderthals and Denisovans, whose DNA persists in modern populations.

Relation to other hominins

For over a million years Homo coexisted with the robust Paranthropus lineage, which specialised in heavy chewing and went extinct about 1 Ma. By ~40,000 years ago all other Homo species had also disappeared, leaving H. sapiens as the sole human species.
