Sovereign Grant

The Sovereign Grant is the annual payment made by the government of the United Kingdom to the monarch to fund the Sovereign's official duties, including the maintenance of the occupied royal palaces, royal travel, and the salaries of the Royal Household. It was introduced by the Sovereign Grant Act 2011 and replaced the Civil List and several separate grants-in-aid from April 2012.

Calculation

The grant is set as a percentage of the net surplus of the Crown Estate for a preceding financial year. It began at 15 per cent; it was raised to 25 per cent for ten years from 2017 to fund the reservicing of Buckingham Palace, and subsequent reviews have adjusted the rate (to 12 per cent from 2024, following increased Crown Estate profits from offshore wind licensing). If the Crown Estate surplus falls, the grant is not reduced in cash terms — the "ratchet" provision — but the percentage is recalculated.

Accountability

The grant is audited by the National Audit Office and reported in the Sovereign Grant Annual Report. It does not cover the costs of security or of the Duchies of Lancaster and Cornwall, which fund the Sovereign's private income and the Prince of Wales respectively.
