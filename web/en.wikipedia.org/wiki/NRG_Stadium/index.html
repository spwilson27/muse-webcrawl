NRG Stadium, formerly known as Reliant Stadium, is a multi-purpose stadium in Houston, Texas, United States. Completed in 2002 at a cost of $352 million, it has a seating capacity of 72,220 and was the first NFL facility to have a retractable roof. The stadium is the home of the Houston Texans of the National Football League, the Houston Livestock Show and Rodeo, and the Texas Bowl, and it forms part of the NRG Park complex alongside the NRG Center, NRG Arena, and the former Astrodome site.

History

The stadium opened on 24 August 2002 and hosted its first regular-season NFL game on 8 September 2002, when the expansion Texans defeated the Dallas Cowboys. Naming rights were originally held by Reliant Energy; after NRG Energy acquired Reliant's retail electricity business, the venue was renamed NRG Stadium in 2014.

Major events

NRG Stadium has hosted two Super Bowls: Super Bowl XXXVIII in 2004 and Super Bowl LI in 2017, the latter famous for the New England Patriots' comeback from a 28–3 deficit against the Atlanta Falcons. It also hosted WrestleMania 25 in 2009, the NCAA Men's Final Four in 2011, 2016 and 2023, Copa América Centenario matches in 2016, and numerous international soccer fixtures. The stadium was selected as a venue for the 2026 FIFA World Cup. During Hurricane Katrina in 2005, the wider Reliant Park complex served as a shelter for evacuees from New Orleans.

Design

The stadium's retractable roof operates on a system of massive trusses and can open or close in about seven minutes. The playing surface was originally natural grass grown on trays, later replaced by artificial turf.
