Slack (software)

A Slack workspace with channels and messages

Slack is a cloud-based team communication platform developed by Slack Technologies, now owned by Salesforce. It offers persistent chat rooms organized by topic ("channels"), private groups, direct messaging, file sharing, search, and integrations with third-party services. It was launched in August 2013 and grew into one of the most widely used business messaging tools.

History

Slack originated as an internal tool at Tiny Speck, Stewart Butterfield's company, while it developed the game Glitch. After Glitch shut down in 2012, the team repurposed its IRC-based communication system into a standalone product. The name is an acronym for "Searchable Log of All Conversation and Knowledge." Slack Technologies went public through a direct listing on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2019 under the ticker WORK. Salesforce announced its acquisition of Slack in December 2020 for about $27.7 billion, completing the deal in July 2021; Butterfield stepped down as CEO in 2023 and was succeeded by Lidiane Jones.

Features

Workspaces are divided into public and private channels. Messages support threads, emoji reactions, mentions, and rich formatting. Slack integrates with services such as Google Drive, GitHub, and Trello, and provides voice/video "huddles," workflow automation (Workflow Builder), and Slack Connect for cross-organization channels. An enterprise tier, Slack Enterprise Grid, serves very large organizations.

Reception

Slack was praised for reducing internal email and for its design, but has also been criticized for information overload, notifications, and the blurring of work–life boundaries. It competes with Microsoft Teams, Discord, and Google Chat.
