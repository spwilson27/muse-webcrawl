Druze


The five-coloured star, a symbol of the Druze faith.

The Druze are an Arabic-speaking esoteric ethnoreligious group centred in the Levant, with communities in Lebanon, Syria, Israel and Jordan and a worldwide diaspora. Numbering roughly one million, they follow a monotheistic, syncretic religion that emerged in eleventh-century Fatimid Egypt and incorporates elements of Islam, Neoplatonism, Gnosticism, Christianity and other philosophies, including a doctrine of reincarnation.

History

The faith arose in Cairo during the reign of the Fatimid caliph al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah, around 1017, through the preaching of Hamza ibn Ali and others; its name derives from the early missionary al-Darazi. After al-Hakim's disappearance in 1021 and subsequent persecution, the mission was closed to new converts in 1043 under Baha al-Din al-Muqtana, and the community became endogamous and secretive — practices reinforced by the doctrine of taqiyya, protective dissimulation. Driven into the mountains of Lebanon, Syria and Galilee, the Druze developed a cohesive society led by families such as the Ma'ns and Shihabs; Emir Fakhr al-Din II briefly built a semi-autonomous principality in the seventeenth century. In 1860 Druze–Maronite conflict in Mount Lebanon drew European intervention. Druze played notable roles in the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925 under Sultan al-Atrash, and in Israel Druze men serve in the Israel Defense Forces under a separate arrangement from other Arab citizens.

Beliefs

Druze theology centres on the absolute oneness of God and the manifestation of the divine through a hierarchy of cosmic intellects, symbolised by five colours. Scripture consists of the Epistles of Wisdom (Rasa'il al-Hikma). The community is divided between initiated religious elders, the uqqal, who have access to the scriptures and follow strict codes of dress and conduct, and the uninitiated majority, the juhhal. Core ethical values include truthfulness, mutual protection and devotion to the community. Reincarnation (taqammus) — the immediate rebirth of the soul — is a central tenet.

Distribution

The largest populations are in Syria (centred on Jabal al-Druze around Suwayda), Lebanon (the Chouf, Aley and Hasbaya districts) and Israel (the Galilee and Mount Carmel), with significant emigrant communities in the Americas, Australia and West Africa.
