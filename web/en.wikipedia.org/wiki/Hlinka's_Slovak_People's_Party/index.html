Hlinka's Slovak People's Party

Hlinka's Slovak People's Party (Slovak: Hlinkova slovenská ľudová strana, HSĽS), also known simply as the Slovak People's Party, was a far-right, clerical-nationalist political party in Slovakia and Czechoslovakia from 1913 until its dissolution in 1945. Named after its long-time leader, the Catholic priest Andrej Hlinka, it advocated Slovak autonomy within Czechoslovakia and, after 1938, led the client state of the Slovak Republic under Jozef Tiso.

History

The party originated in the Slovak People's Party founded in 1913 within the Kingdom of Hungary. After the creation of Czechoslovakia in 1918, it became the principal voice of Slovak autonomism, complaining that the centralist state favoured Czechs. It won a large share of the Slovak vote in interwar elections but never entered the Prague government for long.

After Hlinka's death in August 1938, leadership passed to Jozef Tiso. Following the Munich Agreement, Slovakia gained autonomy in October 1938, and the party absorbed or suppressed rivals, becoming the dominant force. Its paramilitary wing, the Hlinka Guard, modelled itself on fascist movements.

Slovak Republic

On 14 March 1939, under German pressure, the Slovak Diet declared independence. Tiso became president of a one-party state aligned with Nazi Germany. The regime adopted anti-Jewish laws and, in 1942, deported the majority of Slovak Jews to German extermination and labour camps, paying Germany a fee per deportee. Deportations resumed after Germany occupied Slovakia following the Slovak National Uprising of 1944.

Dissolution

The party was banned and dissolved in 1945 after the defeat of Nazi Germany and the restoration of Czechoslovakia. Tiso was tried and executed in 1947.
