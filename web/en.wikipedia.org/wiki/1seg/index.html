1seg

1seg (Japanese: ワンセグ, wansegu) is a mobile terrestrial digital audio and video broadcasting service in Japan, Brazil and other countries using the ISDB-T standard. It began service in Japan on 1 April 2006. The name refers to the fact that the service uses one of the thirteen segments of a 6 MHz ISDB-T channel; the remaining twelve segments carry the high-definition service.

Technical overview

An ISDB-T broadcast channel is divided into 13 frequency segments. Twelve of these carry the fixed-reception HDTV or SDTV service, while the centre segment is reserved for 1seg mobile reception. 1seg transmits video using H.264/AVC at QVGA resolution (320×240) and around 15 frames per second, with AAC audio, in an MPEG-2 transport stream. Data broadcasting and electronic programme guide information can be sent alongside the programme.

Adoption

Because the signal is broadcast free-to-air, reception requires no subscription or mobile network connection, and 1seg tuners became a standard feature of Japanese mobile phones, car navigation systems and portable televisions. The service is valued for emergency broadcasting during disasters, when cellular networks may be congested. Variants of ISDB-T with 1seg were adopted in Brazil and several other Latin American and Asian countries. In Brazil, however, manufacturers later dropped support, and Japan itself planned high-definition mobile services as part of its broadcasting evolution.
