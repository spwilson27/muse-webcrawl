The Third Battle of Panipat took place on 14 January 1761 between the Maratha Empire and the Durrani Empire under Ahmad Shah Durrani (Ahmad Shah Abdali). It is among the largest and most consequential battles of the 18th century.

Background

Maratha expansion northward after the decline of the Mughal Empire collided with Ahmad Shah's repeated invasions of Punjab. The Maratha army under Sadashivrao Bhau, accompanied by a huge train of non-combatants, marched north in 1760, storming Delhi and Kunjpura before being cut off from supplies at Panipat.

Battle

Starved after months of blockade, the Marathas attacked on 14 January. Initial Maratha artillery and infantry assaults broke the Durrani left, but the death of Vishwasrao and then Sadashivrao Bhau, and Durrani reserves and zamburak camel guns, turned the day into a rout. Tens of thousands were killed in battle and in the massacre and enslavement that followed.

Aftermath

The defeat halted Maratha expansion for a decade and deepened India's political fragmentation, indirectly aiding the rise of the British East India Company, though the Marathas recovered under Mahadji Scindia and Madhavrao I. Ahmad Shah, weakened by losses, withdrew to Afghanistan.
