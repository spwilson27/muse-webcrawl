Natural Parks Act

The Natural Parks Act (自然公園法, Shizen-kōen-hō) is a Japanese law enacted in 1957 that provides the legal basis for the designation and management of Japan's national parks, quasi-national parks and prefectural natural parks. It replaced the earlier National Parks Law of 1931, under which Japan's first national parks had been established in 1934.

Provisions

The Act empowers the Minister of the Environment to designate national parks covering areas of outstanding natural beauty, and to regulate activities within them through a zoning system that ranges from special protection zones, where development is strictly limited, to ordinary zones. Quasi-national parks are designated by the minister on the application of prefectural governments, and prefectural natural parks are designated by the prefectures themselves.

Park system

Japan has more than thirty national parks, spanning volcanic landscapes such as Fuji-Hakone-Izu and Akan Mashu, the alpine scenery of Chūbu-Sangaku, and the subtropical islands of Ogasawara and Kerama Shotō. Unlike the parks of some countries, much of the land in Japanese parks is privately owned, so the Act relies heavily on regulation of land use rather than public ownership.
