Butcher

A butcher is a person who slaughters animals, dresses their flesh, and sells their meat, or who participates in any combination of these tasks. Butchers work in slaughterhouses, meat-processing plants, grocery stores, and independent butcher shops, preparing standard cuts of meat, poultry, and sometimes fish and game.

History

Butchery is among the oldest trades. In medieval Europe, butchers formed influential guilds, and markets in cities such as London (Smithfield) were organized around the trade. Industrialization in the 19th and 20th centuries shifted slaughter and cutting into large packinghouses — famously in Chicago and Cincinnati — while retail butchers increasingly received carcasses or boxed primals rather than live animals.

Work

Retail butchers break down carcasses into primal, subprimal, and retail cuts; trim and portion meat; grind meat; make sausages and cured products; and advise customers. The work requires knife skills, knowledge of anatomy, and strict attention to sanitation and food-safety rules, since cross-contamination and temperature abuse are constant hazards.

Modern developments

Supermarkets and centralized processing reduced the number of independent shops in many countries, but a revival of "whole animal" butchery, nose-to-tail cooking, and locally sourced meat has renewed interest in the craft, alongside growing alternatives such as plant-based and cultivated meat.
