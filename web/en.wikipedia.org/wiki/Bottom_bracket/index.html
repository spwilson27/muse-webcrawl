Bottom bracket

The bottom bracket on a bicycle is the component that connects the crankset (chainset) to the frame and allows it to rotate freely. It consists of a spindle (axle) to which the cranks attach, and the bearings that allow the spindle and cranks to rotate relative to the bottom bracket shell — the part of the frame where the down tube, seat tube, and chain stays meet.

History

Early bottom brackets used loose or caged ball bearings with adjustable cups and a square-taper spindle, serviced with grease. Cartridge designs sealed the bearings into a removable unit, simplifying maintenance. Later systems moved the bearings outside the shell (external-bearing cranksets) to allow a larger, stiffer hollow spindle, while press-fit standards seated bearings directly in oversized frame shells.

Standards

A profusion of standards exists for shell widths, diameters, and threading — English and Italian threaded shells, BB86/BB92 press-fit, BB30, PF30, and threaded T47 among them. The variety complicates compatibility between frames and cranksets, and creaking is a widely reported problem with press-fit installations.

Adjustment and wear

Bearings wear with mileage and exposure to water; symptoms of a worn bottom bracket include roughness, play, and noise under load. Replacement intervals depend on design and conditions.
