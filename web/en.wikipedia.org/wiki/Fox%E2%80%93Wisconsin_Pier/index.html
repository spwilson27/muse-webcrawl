Fox–Wisconsin Waterway

The Fox–Wisconsin Waterway is a water route in the U.S. state of Wisconsin linking the Great Lakes with the Mississippi River. It follows the Fox River northeastward from Portage to Green Bay on Lake Michigan, crosses a short continental-divide portage at Portage, and continues down the Wisconsin River to its confluence with the Mississippi at Prairie du Chien.

History

The route was used for centuries by Indigenous peoples and was travelled by French explorers Jacques Marquette and Louis Jolliet in 1673, becoming a principal fur-trade corridor. The roughly 1.5-mile (2.4 km) portage between the Fox and Wisconsin rivers gave the city of Portage its name. In the 19th century, canals and locks were built — including the Portage Canal — to create a navigable through-route for commerce.

The Fox River locks

The Upper Fox and the Lower Fox River systems contain a series of locks, most of them hand-operated historic structures. The waterway today is used mainly by recreational boaters; many of the Lower Fox locks have been restored by the Fox River Navigational System Authority, while the Portage Canal has been partially restored as a historic and recreational amenity.
