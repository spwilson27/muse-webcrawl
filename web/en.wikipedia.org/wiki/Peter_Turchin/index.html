Peter Turchin (born 1957) is a Russian-American scientist and historian known as a founder of cliodynamics, a field that applies mathematical modelling and statistical analysis to the study of historical societies. The son of Soviet human-rights activist Valentin Turchin, he emigrated to the United States in 1977 and trained in biology and zoology, initially working on population ecology.

Cliodynamics

Turchin applies models from population dynamics to history, arguing that large-scale historical patterns — the rise and fall of empires, cycles of political instability — can be explained by measurable demographic, economic, and social variables. His structural-demographic theory holds that instability grows when elite overproduction (too many aspirants for limited elite positions), popular immiseration, and state fiscal weakness coincide, producing cycles of disorder he calls secular cycles, typically lasting two to three centuries in agrarian states.

His books include Historical Dynamics (2003), War and Peace and War (2006), Secular Cycles (2009, with Sergey Nefedov), Ages of Discord (2016), which analysed instability in the United States, and End Times (2023), on elite overproduction and political disintegration in contemporary America. In 2010 he attracted attention for a letter in Nature forecasting heightened political instability in Western societies around 2020.

Seshat

Turchin co-founded the Seshat: Global History Databank, a large collaborative project encoding data on social complexity, religion, warfare, and institutions for hundreds of past societies, enabling statistical tests of theories of cultural evolution — including contested analyses of the origins of moralising gods and social complexity.
