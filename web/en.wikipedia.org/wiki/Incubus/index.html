Incubus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An incubus (plural incubi) is a demon in male form in folklore and demonology that seeks to have sexual intercourse with sleeping women. Its female counterpart is the succubus. The word derives from the Latin incubare, "to lie upon." Belief in such beings was widespread in medieval and early modern Europe and appears, under various names, in Mesopotamian, Greek, and African traditions as well.

Folklore and theology

Medieval theologians debated how demons — understood as incorporeal spirits — could engage in carnal relations and even father children; the witchcraft treatise Malleus Maleficarum (1487) proposed that a succubus could collect human seed and an incubus deliver it. Tales of the incubus are related to the night-hag and the experience of a crushing weight on the chest during sleep, a phenomenon now explained as sleep paralysis, in which vivid hallucinations of a menacing presence accompany temporary inability to move.

In culture

The incubus has remained a durable figure in Gothic literature, horror fiction, film, and games, typically symbolizing seduction, violation, and the uncanny. Explanations advanced by historians and psychologists link incubus traditions to sleep paralysis, night terrors, and social attempts to account for unexplained pregnancies or sexual experiences in repressive cultures.
