Eastern Rumelia (Bulgarian: Източна Румелия, Iztochna Rumeliya; Ottoman Turkish: روم الى شرقى, Rumeli-i Şarkî; Greek: Ανατολική Ρωμυλία, Anatolikí Romylía) was an autonomous province of the Ottoman Empire created in 1878 by the Treaty of Berlin, covering much of what is now southern Bulgaria, with its capital at Philippopolis (modern Plovdiv).

History

The Treaty of San Stefano (March 1878) had created a large Bulgarian state, but the Great Powers, fearing a Russian client state, scaled it back at Berlin. The Principality of Bulgaria was created north of the Balkan Mountains, Macedonia was returned to direct Ottoman rule, and the lands between the Balkans and the Rhodope Mountains became Eastern Rumelia — nominally Ottoman, but administered by a Christian Governor-General appointed by the Sultan with the assent of the Powers.

The province had its own assembly, militia and gendarmerie, and Bulgarian quickly became the dominant language of administration. On 6 September 1885, Bulgarian nationalist groups seized Plovdiv and proclaimed the unification of Eastern Rumelia with the Principality of Bulgaria under Prince Alexander of Battenberg. Serbia's subsequent attack on Bulgaria ended in defeat at Slivnitsa, and the unification was accepted internationally in 1886 through the fiction that the Bulgarian prince would serve as Ottoman Governor-General. Eastern Rumelia thereafter ceased to exist as a separate administration, and the date of the unification remains a Bulgarian national holiday.
