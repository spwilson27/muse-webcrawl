Biblical theology

Biblical theology is a discipline within Christian theology that studies the Bible from the perspective of understanding the progressive history of God's revelation of himself and his purposes. It differs from systematic theology, which organizes doctrine topically, by tracing theological themes as they develop across the biblical storyline and within the historical settings of the biblical books.

History of the discipline

The discipline is often traced to Johann Philipp Gabler's 1787 inaugural lecture at Altdorf, "On the proper distinction between biblical and dogmatic theology," which called for biblical theology to be a historical description of what the biblical authors believed in their own time. In the twentieth century the Biblical Theology Movement, associated with figures such as G. Ernest Wright and the journal Interpretation, emphasized God's acts in history. Brevard Childs later championed a canonical approach, while Geerhardus Vos's Biblical Theology (1948) shaped the Reformed tradition's redemptive-historical method.

Major themes

Biblical theologians commonly organize the narrative around themes such as covenant, the kingdom of God, creation and new creation, exile and exodus, temple and the presence of God, and promise and fulfilment. Old Testament theology and New Testament theology are often studied as subfields before being integrated into a whole-Bible biblical theology.

Relationship to other disciplines

Biblical theology draws on biblical criticism and biblical hermeneutics, and it presupposes some account of biblical inspiration. It in turn supplies material for systematic theology and preaching.
