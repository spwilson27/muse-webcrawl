Anarchism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anarchism is a political philosophy and movement that seeks to abolish all institutions that perpetuate authority, coercion or hierarchy, primarily the state, but also including other forms of domination such as patriarchy, racial hierarchy and coercive economic relations. Anarchists advocate self-governed, voluntary societies based on free association, mutual aid and decentralised organisation.

History

Ideas anticipating anarchism appear in ancient philosophy and radical religious movements, but anarchism as a named philosophy begins with Pierre-Joseph Proudhon's What Is Property? (1840) — "property is theft", and his declaration of himself as an anarchist. Mikhail Bakunin and Peter Kropotkin developed collectivist and communist variants within the First International, splitting with Marx over the state. Anarchist movements were strong in Spain, Italy, France, Russia and the Americas; anarcho-syndicalist unions organised millions, and anarchists governed much of Catalonia during the Spanish Civil War before defeat in 1939.

Currents

Major currents include anarcho-communism (Kropotkin's Mutual Aid), collectivist anarchism, anarcho-syndicalism (revolutionary trade unionism), mutualism, individualist anarchism, and contemporary green, feminist and post-left variants. A separate market-oriented tradition calling itself "anarcho-capitalism" is claimed by some to belong to the movement and rejected by most other anarchists. Common principles include federalism of communes and workplaces, free association, direct democracy, and prefigurative politics — embodying the desired society in present organisation.

Reputation and legacy

Anarchism acquired a reputation for violence from a wave of assassinations in the 1890s–1900s ("propaganda of the deed"), which many anarchists opposed. Its ideas nonetheless influenced labour movements, the New Left, anti-globalisation protests, Occupy, and cooperative and squatting movements; contemporary anarchist-influenced experiments range from municipalist projects to digital commons.
