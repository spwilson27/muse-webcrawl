Black wildebeest

SpeciesConnochaetes gnou
FamilyBovidae
Common namesBlack wildebeest, white-tailed gnu
RangeSouth Africa, Eswatini, Lesotho, Namibia (introduced)
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The black wildebeest or white-tailed gnu (Connochaetes gnou) is one of the two species that make up the genus Connochaetes, the wildebeests or gnus; its close relative is the larger blue wildebeest. It is native to the open grasslands and Karoo of southern Africa.

Description

Black wildebeest stand about 106–121 cm at the shoulder and weigh 110–180 kg. The coat is dark brown to black, with a shaggy mane that is dark at the roots and pale at the tips, a beard, and a distinctive long, flowing, almost pure white tail that gives the species its alternative name. Both sexes bear horns that curve forward from a heavy boss on the forehead, then up at the tips; males' horns and bosses are heavier.

Behaviour and ecology

The species is a grazer of short grasses, forming three kinds of social group: territorial bachelor males, territorial breeding bulls with female herds, and non-territorial mixed herds. Unlike blue wildebeest, black wildebeest are largely sedentary and do not undertake long migrations. Breeding is seasonal, with most calves born in the summer rains.

Conservation

Hunted almost to extinction by the early twentieth century — by 1900 only a few hundred remained — the black wildebeest recovered under protection on farms and reserves to a population estimated in the tens of thousands. The IUCN lists it as Least Concern, although most individuals occur on private land and genetic hybridisation with blue wildebeest on shared ranches is a management concern.
