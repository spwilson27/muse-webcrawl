The Austrian Civil War (German: Österreichischer Bürgerkrieg), also known as the February Uprising (Februarkämpfe), was a four-day armed conflict in February 1934 between the authoritarian government of Chancellor Engelbert Dollfuss and the paramilitary organisations of the Social Democratic Workers' Party of Austria.

Background

After the First World War, Austrian politics polarised between "Red Vienna", governed by the Social Democrats, and the conservative federal governments. Rival paramilitaries — the Social Democratic Republikanischer Schutzbund and the right-wing Heimwehr — repeatedly clashed, notably in the July Revolt of 1927. In March 1933 Dollfuss exploited a parliamentary crisis to suspend the National Council and rule by decree, moving toward an authoritarian "corporative state" (Ständestaat) modelled in part on fascist Italy.

The fighting

On 12 February 1934, police attempts to search the Social Democratic party headquarters in Linz for weapons triggered armed resistance by local Schutzbund commander Richard Bernaschek. Fighting spread to Vienna, where government forces used artillery against workers' housing estates such as the Karl Marx-Hof, and to industrial centres including Graz, Steyr and Bruck an der Mur. The government, supported by the army, police and Heimwehr, suppressed the uprising by 15 February.

Aftermath

The Social Democratic Party and its unions were banned; leading Schutzbund members were executed under martial law or driven into exile. Dollfuss promulgated a new authoritarian constitution on 1 May 1934, formalising the Austrofascist regime. In July of the same year Dollfuss was assassinated during an attempted Nazi coup.
