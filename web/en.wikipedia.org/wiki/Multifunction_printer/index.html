Multifunction printer

A multifunction printer (MFP), also called a multifunction device or all-in-one printer, combines several office functions — typically printing, scanning, copying, and sometimes faxing — in a single machine.

Features

Home and small-office MFPs are usually based on inkjet or laser print engines and include a flatbed scanner, often with an automatic document feeder for multi-page scanning and copying. Larger office devices add finishing options such as stapling and booklet-making, high-capacity paper trays, and user authentication.

Connectivity and software

Modern MFPs connect by USB, Ethernet, or Wi-Fi, support printing from mobile devices and cloud services, and can scan directly to email, network folders, or document management systems. Their embedded software and network access have also made them a recognised point of attention in office IT security.

History

Dedicated multifunction devices spread during the 1990s as scanner and fax components cheapened, displacing separate single-function machines in many offices.
