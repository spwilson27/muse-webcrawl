Michael Stipe

John Michael Stipe (born January 4, 1960) is an American singer-songwriter and artist, best known as the lead singer and lyricist of the alternative rock band R.E.M. Recognised for his distinctive vocal style and surreal, enigmatic lyrics, Stipe fronted the band from its founding in Athens, Georgia, in 1980 until its amicable disbandment in 2011, selling more than 90 million albums worldwide. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with R.E.M. in 2007.

Life and career

Stipe was born in Decatur, Georgia, the son of a United States Army officer, and moved frequently as a child, including to Germany and Texas. Studying art and photography at the University of Georgia in Athens, he met guitarist Peter Buck in the record store where Buck worked, and with Mike Mills and Bill Berry formed R.E.M. in 1980. Their early albums on I.R.S. Records — Murmur (1983) and Reckoning (1984) — defined the college-rock sound, while the Warner Bros. era brought worldwide hits with Out of Time (1991, including "Losing My Religion"), Automatic for the People (1992) and Monster (1994).

Art and other work

Stipe has pursued visual art and photography throughout his career, publishing volumes of his photographs and directing or producing film through his production companies C-00 and Single Cell Pictures, whose credits include Being John Malkovich (1999) and Man on the Moon (1999). He is known for political and environmental activism, campaigning for Democratic candidates and causes including gun control and Tibetan independence. After R.E.M.'s dissolution he returned to solo performance, releasing his first solo single, "Your Capricious Soul", in 2019.

Musical style

Stipe's early singing was famously mumbled and impressionistic — critics struggled to decipher the lyrics of Murmur — and his delivery grew clearer and more direct over the band's career. Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest singers of all time, and Bono and Thom Yorke are among those who cite him as an influence.
