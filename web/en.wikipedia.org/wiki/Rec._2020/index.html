Rec. 2020

ITU-R Recommendation BT.2020, commonly known as Rec. 2020 or BT.2020, is a standard for ultra-high-definition television (UHDTV) published by the International Telecommunication Union in 2012 and revised in 2015. It defines image parameters including resolutions of 3840×2160 ("4K") and 7680×4320 ("8K"), progressive scan frame rates up to 120 Hz, bit depths of 10 or 12 bits per channel, and a very wide color gamut.

Color space

The Rec. 2020 RGB primaries lie on the spectral locus of the CIE 1931 chromaticity diagram — corresponding to monochromatic light at 630 nm (red), 532 nm (green), and 467 nm (blue) — so its gamut covers about 75% of the visible color space, far more than Rec. 709 (HDTV) or DCI-P3. The white point is D65. Because the primaries are monochromatic, no current consumer display can reproduce the full gamut; content is typically mastered in P3 and carried in a Rec. 2020 container.

Transfer characteristics and usage

Rec. 2020 originally specified a conventional gamma EOTF, but amendments added the perceptual quantizer (PQ) and hybrid log-gamma (HLG) transfer functions used by HDR formats such as HDR10, Dolby Vision, and HDR10+. The standard underpins UHD Blu-ray, streaming HDR services, and UHDTV broadcasting.
