A junior college is a post-secondary educational institution offering vocational training and academic programmes, typically of two years' duration, designed to prepare students either for transfer to a four-year college or university or for direct entry into the workforce. In the United States the term was the standard name for such institutions through much of the 20th century and is now often used interchangeably with community college, though some older private institutions retain the name. History

The junior college movement began in the United States at the start of the 20th century, promoted by university presidents such as William Rainey Harper of the University of Chicago, who envisioned the first two years of undergraduate study being separated into "junior colleges". Joliet Junior College in Illinois, founded in 1901, is generally regarded as the oldest public junior college in the United States still operating. The sector expanded rapidly after the Second World War, aided by the GI Bill and, in California, by the 1960 Master Plan for Higher Education. From the 1970s the public institutions increasingly adopted the name "community college" to reflect their broader community mission, while a number of private, often liberal-arts or faith-based, two-year colleges kept "junior college".

By country

United States

Public junior colleges are usually governed by local districts and offer open-enrolment associate degrees (AA, AS, AAS), transfer pathways to state universities, and workforce certificates, at substantially lower tuition than four-year institutions. Private junior colleges include some of the oldest continuously operating two-year colleges in the country.

Japan

In Japan, junior colleges (tanki daigaku) are two- or three-year institutions whose students are predominantly women; they offer associate-level qualifications in fields such as early childhood education, nutrition, and nursing.

Other usages

In Singapore "junior college" refers to two-year pre-university institutions leading to the A-level examination, while in India the term denotes the two years of higher secondary schooling (classes 11–12) in several states.

Functions

Common functions include lower-division undergraduate education with transfer articulation, occupational and technical training aligned to local labour markets, remedial and adult education, and community enrichment courses. Critics historically worried about "cooling out" transfer aspirations; supporters emphasise affordability, access, and second-chance education.
