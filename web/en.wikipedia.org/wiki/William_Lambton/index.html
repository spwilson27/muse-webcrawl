William Lambton


Born c. 1753, Crosby Grange, North Yorkshire, England
Died 20 January 1823, Hinganghat, India
Known for Great Trigonometrical Survey of India

William Lambton (c. 1753 – 20 January 1823) was a British soldier and surveyor who initiated the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India, one of the most ambitious scientific mapping projects of the 19th century.

Early life

Little is known of Lambton's early life. He joined the army as an ensign, served in North America during the American Revolutionary War, and was taken prisoner at Yorktown. He later became barrack-master at Saint John, New Brunswick, before being posted to India in 1797 with the 33rd Regiment.

The Great Trigonometrical Survey

Convinced of the need for an accurate survey of the Indian subcontinent based on triangulation, Lambton began measurements in 1802 with a baseline near St. Thomas Mount, Madras, using a 36-inch theodolite. He measured an arc of the meridian in southern India and laid down the framework of triangles from which the whole survey grew. His successor, George Everest, extended the work northward to the Himalayas. Mount Lambton in British Columbia is named for him.

Death and legacy

Lambton died at Hinganghat in central India in 1823 while still on survey duty. The Great Trigonometrical Survey he founded continued for decades and ultimately determined the height of the world's highest peaks, including the mountain later named Mount Everest after his successor.
