Poorhouse

A poorhouse or poor farm is a government-run facility to support and provide housing for the dependent or needy. Poorhouses were common in the United States and parts of Europe during the 19th and early 20th centuries, often operated at the county level.

History

Under poor law systems modelled on the English tradition, communities were responsible for their indigent residents. "Indoor relief" in a poorhouse was deliberately made austere to discourage dependency: residents — including the elderly, orphans, the disabled, and the unemployed — were expected to work, often on an attached poor farm.

Decline

Conditions were frequently harsh and stigmatised, and reformers campaigned against housing children and the mentally ill in poorhouses. The institutions declined with the rise of specialised facilities, Social Security in the 1930s, and other welfare programmes; many former poor farms became county nursing homes or were demolished.

Terminology

Related terms include almshouse, workhouse (in Britain), and poor farm. The phrase "the poorhouse" survives idiomatically as a symbol of destitution.
