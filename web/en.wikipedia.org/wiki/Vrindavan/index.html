Vrindavan

Vrindavan (IAST: Vṛndāvana) is a historic city in the Mathura district of Uttar Pradesh, India. It is located in the Braj Bhoomi region and holds immense significance in Hinduism as the place where the god Krishna is believed to have spent most of his childhood. The city lies on the banks of the Yamuna river, about 15 km north of Mathura, Krishna's birthplace; together the twin cities of Mathura–Vrindavan are among the Sapta Puri, the seven sacred cities of Hinduism.

Religious significance

According to Hindu tradition, Krishna grew up in the pastoral village of Gokul and spent his youth in the forests of Vrindavan, dancing the rasa lila with Radha and the gopis (cowherd maidens) and performing many of his celebrated pastimes (lila). The town's groves, bathing ghats and sites such as Nidhivan and Seva Kunj are associated with these stories. In the 16th century theologians of the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, notably the Six Goswamis of Vrindavan sent by Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, "rediscovered" the lost holy places and established temples, making the town the heart of Krishna devotion. Vrindavan is also significant in other Krishna-centred traditions including the Radha Vallabha, Nimbarka and Vallabha sampradayas, and is known for its large population of widows who devote their lives to worship.

Temples

Vrindavan is said to have thousands of temples. The Banke Bihari Temple (1864) is among the most visited; the Radha Raman Temple houses a self-manifested deity; the Govind Dev Temple (1590), built by Raja Man Singh of Amber, was once a grand seven-storey red sandstone structure. Modern landmarks include the white marble Prem Mandir (opened 2012) and the Krishna Balaram Mandir of ISKCON (1975), which draws international devotees.

Festivals

Krishna Janmashtami and Holi are celebrated with particular fervour; Vrindavan's Phoolon Wali Holi (played with flowers) and the nearby Lathmar Holi of Barsana and Nandgaon are famous across India.
