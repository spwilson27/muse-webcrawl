Lucknow is the capital and largest city of the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh. Situated on the banks of the Gomti River, it is famed for its refined Urdu culture, Mughlai cuisine and the courtly etiquette (tehzeeb) of the Nawabs of Awadh, earning it the names "City of Nawabs" and "Golden City of the East".

The Rumi Darwaza, an emblem of Lucknow

History

Lucknow rose to prominence in 1775 when Asaf-ud-Daula, Nawab of Awadh, moved his capital here from Faizabad. The Nawabi court made the city a centre of poetry, music and architecture. During the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the British Residency in Lucknow was besieged for months; the ruined Residency remains a memorial. After independence, Lucknow became the capital of Uttar Pradesh.

Landmarks

  • Bara Imambara (1784) — a vast ceremonial hall built by Asaf-ud-Daula, containing the labyrinthine Bhulbhulaiya
  • Rumi Darwaza — a 60-foot gateway modelled on a gate in Constantinople
  • Chota Imambara — mausoleum of Muhammad Ali Shah
  • The Residency — scarred remains of the 1857 siege
  • Husainabad Clock Tower — among the tallest clock towers in India

Culture and cuisine

Lucknow is celebrated for Awadhi cuisine — galouti kebabs, biryani, and the dum pukht slow-cooking style — and for chikankari hand embroidery. It is a major centre of Urdu literature and of Kathak dance.

Economy and transport

The city is a growing centre for administration, education, IT and tourism. It is served by Chaudhary Charan Singh International Airport, a metro system, and major rail and road links to Delhi and eastern India.
