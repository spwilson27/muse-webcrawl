Estate agent

An estate agent is a person or firm that arranges the selling, renting, or management of properties and other buildings. An agent that specialises in renting is often called a letting or management agent. Estate agents are mainly engaged in the marketing of property available for sale, and a solicitor or licensed conveyancer is used to prepare the legal documents. In Scotland, however, many solicitors also act as estate agents, a practice that is rare in England and Wales.

The term originally referred to a person responsible for managing a landed estate, but in modern usage it corresponds broadly to the North American real estate broker or realtor.

Regulation

In the United Kingdom, estate agents are regulated by the Estate Agents Act 1979 and the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008, and must belong to an approved redress scheme. Unlike in the United States, there is no mandatory licensing examination for estate agents, though many belong to professional bodies such as the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS) or Propertymark.

Fees

Agents in England and Wales typically charge the seller a percentage of the sale price, commonly between 1% and 3% plus VAT, under sole-agency or multiple-agency agreements. Letting agents charge landlords fees for tenant-finding and management; fees charged to tenants in England were largely banned by the Tenant Fees Act 2019.

Online agents

Since the 2000s, online and hybrid estate agents, charging fixed fees rather than percentages, have taken a growing share of the market, and property portals such as Rightmove and Zoopla have become the main shop window for listings.
