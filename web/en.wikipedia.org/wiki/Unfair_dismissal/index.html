Unfair dismissal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Unfair dismissal is a statutory claim in labour law under which an employee may challenge a termination of employment as unjust, typically before an employment tribunal or similar body. It exists in many jurisdictions — including the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, and South Africa — and contrasts with the default rule in most of the United States, where employment is generally "at will" and either party may end the relationship for any lawful reason.

United Kingdom

In the UK, the right is principally governed by the Employment Rights Act 1996. Most employees need two years' continuous service to claim ordinary unfair dismissal, though no qualifying period applies for "automatically unfair" reasons such as pregnancy, whistleblowing, trade union activity, or asserting a statutory right. A dismissal is fair only if the employer shows a potentially fair reason — capability, conduct, redundancy, illegality, or "some other substantial reason" — and acted reasonably in treating it as sufficient, following a fair procedure of the kind set out in the Acas Code of Practice. Remedies include reinstatement, re-engagement, and a compensatory award; claims must usually be lodged within three months.

Australia

Under the Fair Work Act 2009, employees of national-system employers may apply to the Fair Work Commission if dismissed in a manner that was "harsh, unjust or unreasonable." Small-business employers follow a special code, and minimum employment periods apply (six months, or twelve months for small businesses). Australia also maintains a general-protections regime prohibiting dismissal for prohibited reasons such as exercising a workplace right. In all systems, unfair dismissal is distinct from wrongful dismissal, a common-law contract claim about notice, and from unlawful discrimination claims, which proceed under separate equality legislation.
