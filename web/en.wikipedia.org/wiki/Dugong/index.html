The dugong (Dugong dugon) is a marine mammal, one of four living species of the order Sirenia and the only living representative of the family Dugongidae. Unlike manatees, it has a fluked, dolphin-like tail and lives in salt water.

Biology and behaviour

Dugongs grow to about 3 metres and 400 kg, feeding almost entirely on seagrass, which they graze with a downturned, bristled snout—hence 'sea cow'. They are long-lived (up to ~70 years) with a slow reproductive rate, a calf every 3–7 years.

Distribution and threats

The largest population is in northern Australian waters; smaller remnant groups persist in the Persian Gulf, Red Sea, and Southeast Asia. Threats include seagrass loss, boat strikes, gillnet entanglement, and hunting; a Chinese population was declared functionally extinct in 2022. Dugongs likely inspired mermaid legends.
