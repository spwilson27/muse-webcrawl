Categorical distribution

In probability theory and statistics, the categorical distribution is a discrete probability distribution that describes the possible results of a random variable that can take on one of K possible categories, with the probability of each category separately specified. There is no inherent ordering to the categories.

Definition

P(X = i) = pi, where pi ≥ 0 and Σ pi = 1.

The categorical distribution is the generalisation of the Bernoulli distribution for a binary variable to a multi-valued variable. It is a special case of the multinomial distribution for a single trial (n = 1).

Properties

The conjugate prior of the categorical distribution is the Dirichlet distribution. It is widely used in machine learning, for example as the output of a softmax layer in classification, in latent Dirichlet allocation, and in Bayesian networks.
