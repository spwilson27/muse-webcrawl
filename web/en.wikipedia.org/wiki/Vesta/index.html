Vesta (asteroid)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Vesta (asteroid)
TypeAsteroid (differentiated protoplanet)
Diameter~525 km
Discovered29 March 1807 (Heinrich Olbers)
Explored byDawn (2011–2012)
Vesta from Dawn.

Vesta is one of the largest objects in the asteroid belt and the brightest asteroid — occasionally visible to the naked eye. It is not a rubble pile but a surviving protoplanet: differentiated, with a basaltic lava crust, mantle and iron core, essentially a small failed Earth preserved from the solar system's first millions of years.

NASA's Dawn orbited Vesta in 2011–12, mapping the colossal Rheasilvia impact basin at its south pole — nearly the asteroid's own diameter across, with a central peak rivalling Olympus Mons — and confirming that the common HED meteorites found on Earth are chips blasted off Vesta by that impact, giving laboratories a free sample-return from a protoplanet.

Character

Vesta's basaltic surface records lava flows older than any surviving rock on Earth, and its near-escape from destruction in the Rheasilvia impact makes it the most instructive intact building block of the planets we have.
