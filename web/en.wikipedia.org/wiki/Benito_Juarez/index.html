Benito Juárez

Benito Juárez (1806–1872), president of Mexico and national hero.

Benito Pablo Juárez García (21 March 1806 – 18 July 1872) was a Mexican statesman and lawyer who served as president of Mexico from 1858 until his death in 1872. A Zapotec from Oaxaca, he was the first president of Mexico of Indigenous origin and is regarded as a national hero for his defence of the republic against the French intervention and for the liberal reforms of La Reforma.

Early life

Juárez was born in the village of San Pablo Guelatao, Oaxaca, to a poor Zapotec peasant family and was orphaned as a child. Speaking only Zapotec until adolescence, he walked to the city of Oaxaca, where he worked as a servant, learned Spanish, and eventually studied law at the Institute of Sciences and Arts, graduating in 1831. He rose through local politics to become governor of Oaxaca in 1847. Exiled to New Orleans by the dictator Santa Anna, he returned to join the liberal Revolution of Ayutla.

La Reforma and the Reform War

As minister of justice in 1855, Juárez issued the Ley Juárez, abolishing church and military courts' special privileges. The Reform laws and the Constitution of 1857 provoked conservative revolt — the Reform War (1858–1861), during which Juárez, as president of the Supreme Court, succeeded to the presidency after President Comonfort's resignation. His government, based at Veracruz, issued the Laws of Reform nationalising church property and separating church and state, and ultimately defeated the conservatives.

The French intervention

In 1862 France invaded Mexico and installed the Austrian archduke Maximilian as emperor. Juárez led the republican resistance from the north of the country, famously refusing to surrender, and after the French withdrawal and Maximilian's defeat at Querétaro in 1867, restored the republic. His maxim became famous throughout the Americas:

"Among individuals, as among nations, respect for the rights of others is peace." (El respeto al derecho ajeno es la paz.)

Later years and legacy

Re-elected in 1867 and 1871, Juárez worked to modernise Mexico through railways, public education and fiscal reform, though his final re-election drew revolts, including that of Porfirio Díaz. He died in office in 1872. Juárez is commemorated across Mexico — in Ciudad Juárez, countless streets and monuments, and the Benito Juárez International Airport in Mexico City — and was named Benemérito de las Américas by several Latin American congresses.
