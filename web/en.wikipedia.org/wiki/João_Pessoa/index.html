João Pessoa

João Pessoa is the capital and largest city of the Brazilian state of Paraíba, in the northeast of the country. Founded in 1585, it is the third-oldest city in Brazil, and it contains the easternmost point of the Americas, Ponta do Seixas — making it the first place in the Americas to see the sunrise, and earning it the nickname "Gate of the Sun" (Porta do Sol). Among Brazilian capitals it is noted for its greenery, low-rise protected coastline, and quality of life; building heights near the beach are restricted by law.

History

Founded on 5 August 1585 as Filipéia de Nossa Senhora das Neves, the city passed through Portuguese, Dutch (1634–1654, as Fredrikstad), and again Portuguese hands. It was renamed Parahyba, then in 1930 took the name of state governor João Pessoa Cavalcanti de Albuquerque, whose assassination that year in Recife helped trigger the Revolution of 1930 that brought Getúlio Vargas to power; the state flag's motto "Nego" ("I refuse") commemorates João Pessoa's refusal to support the federal government's chosen presidential successor. The historic centre preserves colonial churches including the São Francisco Cultural Centre, considered one of the finest examples of Brazilian baroque.

Geography and tourism

The city lies between the Paraíba River and the Atlantic. Its urban beaches — Tambaú, Manaíra, Cabo Branco, and Bessa — face calm green water; offshore reef pools at Picãozinho and Seixas are visited by boat at low tide. South of the city, the Costa do Conde offers cliffs and beaches such as Coqueirinho and Tambaba, Brazil's first official naturist beach. The Estação Cabo Branco, a science and arts complex designed by Oscar Niemeyer, stands near the Ponta do Seixas lighthouse.
