January 6 Committee

The United States House Select Committee to Investigate the January 6th Attack on the United States Capitol, commonly known as the January 6 Committee, was a select committee of the U.S. House of Representatives established on July 1, 2021, to investigate the January 6 Capitol attack and the events leading to it, including efforts to overturn the 2020 presidential election.

January 6 Committee
Established: July 1, 2021
Dissolved: January 3, 2023
Chair: Bennie Thompson (D-MS)
Vice Chair: Liz Cheney (R-WY)
Final report: December 22, 2022

Formation and membership

After a proposed independent commission was blocked in the Senate, Speaker Nancy Pelosi created the select committee. Its nine members included Democrats Bennie Thompson (chair), Zoe Lofgren, Adam Schiff, Pete Aguilar, Stephanie Murphy, Jamie Raskin and Elaine Luria, and Republicans Liz Cheney (vice chair) and Adam Kinzinger — the latter two appointed by Pelosi after Minority Leader Kevin McCarthy withdrew his nominees.

Investigation and hearings

The committee interviewed over 1,000 witnesses, obtained extensive records, and held ten televised public hearings in summer and autumn 2022, presenting testimony largely from Republican officials and Trump aides. It subpoenaed Donald Trump, who did not comply, and made criminal contempt referrals for Steve Bannon and Peter Navarro.

Final report and referrals

The final report (845 pages) concluded that Trump had conspired to defraud the United States, obstructed an official proceeding, conspired to make false statements and incited insurrection, and referred him to the Justice Department for prosecution — the first such referral of a former president — alongside recommendations on the Electoral Count Act, which Congress reformed in 2022. The committee dissolved with the new Congress in January 2023.
