The Bernina railway is a single-track metre-gauge railway line forming part of the Rhaetian Railway (RhB). It links the spa resort of St. Moritz, in the Canton of Graubünden in eastern Switzerland, with the town of Tirano in the Valtellina region of Italy, via the Bernina Pass. The line is notable for crossing the Alps without the use of rack rails or tunnels at the summit, climbing to Ospizio Bernina station at 2,253 metres above sea level — among the highest adhesion railway crossings in Europe.

History

Construction of the line began in 1906, and sections opened progressively between 1908 and 1910. The railway was built by the Bernina-Bahngesellschaft and was electrified from the outset using direct current, an advanced choice for the time. In 1943 the financially struggling company was taken over by the Rhaetian Railway. The line was built both for tourist traffic and to connect the Engadin valley with Italy.

Route

From St. Moritz the line runs through the Upper Engadin, passing Pontresina and climbing past the Morteratsch Glacier to Bernina Suot and Ospizio Bernina, beside the Lago Bianco. It then descends through Alp Grüm, with views of the Palü Glacier, and down into the Val Poschiavo, passing Poschiavo and Brusio. Shortly before Tirano, trains traverse the famous Brusio spiral viaduct, a nine-arched circular viaduct built to limit the gradient on the descent. Famous structures on the route also include the Landwasser-like curved viaducts and numerous avalanche galleries.

World Heritage status

In July 2008, the Bernina line, together with the Albula railway, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List under the name "Rhaetian Railway in the Albula / Bernina Landscapes", in recognition of its outstanding engineering and its harmonious integration into the Alpine landscape.

Services

The line is served by regional trains and by the panoramic Bernina Express, which runs between Chur (via the Albula line) or St. Moritz and Tirano, where connections are available to Italian trains for Milan.
