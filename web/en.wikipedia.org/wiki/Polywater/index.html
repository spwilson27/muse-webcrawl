Polywater

Polywater was a hypothesised polymerised form of water that was the subject of much scientific interest during the late 1960s and early 1970s. It was later found to be a fictitious substance; the results that pointed to its existence were due to contamination of the experimental samples.

History

Soviet scientists, notably Nikolai Fedyakin and Boris Derjaguin, reported that water condensed in narrow quartz capillaries exhibited anomalous properties: higher density and viscosity, and a lower freezing point than ordinary water. Western scientists reproduced similar results, and hundreds of papers speculated about a polymeric structure of water.

Resolution

By the early 1970s careful analyses showed that the samples were contaminated with impurities — including silica and, famously, compounds matching human sweat — which explained the anomalous behaviour. Derjaguin acknowledged the problem in 1973, and polywater is now cited as a classic example of pathological science.

In popular culture

The idea of a dangerous modified form of water influenced science fiction, and the episode is frequently compared to Kurt Vonnegut's fictional "ice-nine," though polywater research preceded widespread reference to the novel.
