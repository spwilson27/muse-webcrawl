Power vertical

The "power vertical" (Russian: вертикаль власти, vertikal' vlasti) is a term used to describe the system of hierarchical, centralised executive authority in Russia, particularly as developed under President Vladimir Putin. The phrase denotes a chain of command extending from the president down through federal, regional and local authorities, in which lower levels are subordinated to, and accountable to, the levels above rather than to voters or local institutions.

Origins

Putin announced the construction of a "vertical of executive power" during his first presidential term, presenting it as a remedy for the decentralisation and regional separatism of the 1990s under Boris Yeltsin, when many regional leaders signed bilateral treaties with Moscow and defied federal law. Strengthening the vertical became a central theme of Putin's programme of recentralisation.

Measures

Key steps associated with building the power vertical included:

  • Creation in 2000 of seven (later eight) federal districts, each overseen by a presidential plenipotentiary envoy charged with enforcing federal authority over the regions.
  • Removal of regional governors from the Federation Council, weakening their role in national lawmaking.
  • Reform of gubernatorial selection: after the Beslan school siege in 2004, direct elections of governors were abolished in favour of presidential nomination (direct elections were partly restored in 2012, with filters).
  • Alignment of regional laws with the federal constitution and expansion of the ruling party United Russia as the organisational backbone of the hierarchy.

Interpretation

Supporters argue that the vertical restored governability and a single legal space to Russia after a decade of fragmentation. Critics describe it as a mechanism of authoritarian consolidation that hollowed out federalism, local self-government and political competition — sometimes termed "managed democracy". The concept is frequently cited in analyses of Russian politics alongside the idea of the "deep state" and siloviki networks within the administration.
