San Millán de la Cogolla

San Millán de la Cogolla
Country: Spain
Autonomous community: La Rioja
Population: about 200
UNESCO World Heritage Site: San Millán Yuso and Suso Monasteries (1997)

San Millán de la Cogolla is a sparsely populated municipality in La Rioja, Spain, situated in the valley of the Cárdenas River at the foot of the Sierra de la Demanda. It is celebrated for its two monasteries, San Millán de Suso ("above") and San Millán de Yuso ("below"), inscribed together as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997. The community is traditionally regarded as the birthplace of the written Spanish language.

History

The site grew around the hermitage of Saint Aemilianus (San Millán, 473–574), a shepherd turned hermit whose reputation drew disciples to the caves of the valley. The Mozarabic monastery of Suso, partly excavated in rock, preserves inscriptions and the tombs of the Seven Infantes of Lara. Almanzor's campaigns and the demands of a growing community led to the founding of the larger monastery of Yuso below, built from the 11th century onward in Romanesque and later Herrerian styles.

Language

The monasteries' scriptorium produced the Glosas Emilianenses, marginal glosses in an early Ibero-Romance vernacular (and, in some glosses, Basque) traditionally dated to the 11th century and long celebrated as the first written words in Spanish. The first known poet in Spanish, Gonzalo de Berceo, was born nearby and worked at the monastery in the 13th century, writing lives of saints including San Millán.
