Imam

Imam (Arabic: إمام, imām, plural أئمة aʾimmah) is an Islamic leadership position, most commonly the worship leader of a mosque and the Muslim community. The term has significantly different meanings in Sunni and Shia Islam.

Sunni Islam

In Sunni Islam, an imam is the person who leads Islamic formal prayers (salah), delivers the Friday sermon (khutba), and provides religious guidance. The role is not ordained; any knowledgeable, respected Muslim may serve, though mosques often employ a trained imam.

Shia Islam

In Twelver Shia Islam, the Imams are a specific line of divinely appointed, infallible leaders descended from the Prophet Muhammad through his daughter Fatima and son-in-law Ali ibn Abi Talib, the first Imam. Twelve Imams are recognised, the last being Muhammad al-Mahdi, believed to be in occultation. Ismaili branches recognise a continuing living Imam, the Aga Khan.

Other uses

Historically “imam” was also a title for caliphs and founders of the four Sunni schools of law — Abu Hanifa, Malik, al-Shafi'i and Ahmad ibn Hanbal are called imams.
