Battle of Cape Matapan

The Battle of Cape Matapan, fought on 27–29 March 1941 off the southern coast of Greece, was a decisive Royal Navy victory over the Italian Regia Marina in the Mediterranean theatre of the Second World War. Guided by Ultra intelligence from Bletchley Park, Admiral Sir Andrew Cunningham's Mediterranean Fleet intercepted an Italian sortie against British convoys to Greece. In a night action made possible by radar, British battleships annihilated an Italian cruiser squadron at point-blank range.

The battle

The Italian fleet, built around the new battleship Vittorio Veneto under Admiral Angelo Iachino, sailed to attack British convoys supplying Greece. On 28 March, British cruisers under Vice-Admiral Pridham-Wippell engaged Italian cruisers near Gavdos Island and were pursued toward the Italian battleship; aircraft from the carrier HMS Formidable then intervened, torpedoing Vittorio Veneto (which escaped) and crippling the heavy cruiser Pola. That night, unaware that the main British fleet was near, Italian cruisers Zara and Fiume with destroyers went back to assist Pola. Cunningham's battleships Warspite, Valiant and Barham, guided by radar, closed to within about 3,500 yards and opened fire in darkness, sinking Zara, Fiume and two destroyers in minutes; Pola was boarded and then sunk as well. Italy lost some 2,300 sailors; British losses were a single torpedo bomber and its crew.

Significance

Matapan confirmed British naval supremacy in the eastern Mediterranean, exposed Italian weaknesses in night fighting and radar, and — coming the same week as the raid on Taranto's aftermath and alongside later successes — badly damaged Italian fleet morale. It remains one of the clearest demonstrations of radar's and signals intelligence's impact on naval warfare.
