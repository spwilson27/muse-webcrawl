Maxwell (unit)

The maxwell (symbol: Mx) is the unit of magnetic flux in the centimetre–gram–second (CGS) system of units. It is defined as the flux through one square centimetre perpendicular to a magnetic field of one gauss: 1 Mx = 1 G·cm². In SI terms, one maxwell equals 10−8 weber (Wb), the SI unit of flux.

History

The unit honours the Scottish physicist James Clerk Maxwell, whose equations unified electricity and magnetism. The name was adopted for the CGS unit of flux by the International Electrical Congress at the start of the twentieth century and confirmed by the International Electrotechnical Commission in 1930, alongside the gauss for magnetic flux density and the oersted for magnetic field strength. In the CGS system, the maxwell relates to the abvolt: a flux changing at one maxwell per second induces an electromotive force of one abvolt (10−8 volt) in a surrounding circuit.

Use

Although the weber is preferred in modern scientific work, the maxwell persists in geophysics, astrophysics, and magnet engineering literature that uses CGS units — for example, in measuring the flux of sunspots and magnetic features on the Sun, whose fluxes are often quoted in maxwells.
