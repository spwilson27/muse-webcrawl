Overview

Salem is the capital of Oregon, in the Willamette Valley on the Willamette River, about 47 miles south of Portland. Methodist missionaries founded the settlement in the 1840s near Jason Lee's mission; the name Salem — from the Hebrew word for peace, also the root of Jerusalem — was chosen for the town, which became territorial and then state capital in the 1850s.

The Oregon State Capitol, completed in 1938 after two earlier capitols burned, is an Art Deco building in white marble topped by the gilded 'Oregon Pioneer' statue.

Economy

Salem is the centre of Oregon's government and of a rich agricultural region — the surrounding Marion County farmland and food-processing plants earned it the nickname 'Cherry City' for its orchards. Willamette University (1842), the oldest university in the western United States, stands across from the capitol.
