Wild Strawberries (Swedish: Smultronstället) is a 1957 Swedish drama film written and directed by Ingmar Bergman. The original title is a Swedish idiom for a place of special personal significance, often hidden from others. The film won the Golden Bear at the 1958 Berlin International Film Festival and is considered one of Bergman's greatest and most influential works.

Plot

Professor Isak Borg, a 78-year-old widowed physician, drives from Stockholm to Lund to receive an honorary degree, accompanied by his daughter-in-law Marianne, whose marriage is failing. Haunted by a dream in which he finds his own corpse in a coffin, Borg detours through places of his youth, slipping into dreams and memories of his family's summer house, his lost love Sara, and a lifetime of emotional coldness. Along the road he picks up young hitchhikers — including a lively Sara who doubles his youthful sweetheart — and a bickering married couple. By the journey's end, Borg has confronted his failings as husband, father, and son, and moves toward a quiet reconciliation with his son and with himself.

Production and themes

Bergman wrote the screenplay while hospitalised in 1957. He cast his idol, the veteran director Victor Sjöström, in what became his final screen role — a performance Bergman later described as the heart of the film. The film interweaves memory, dream, and reality to explore old age, regret, death, and the possibility of grace.

Reception

Wild Strawberries was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay and has appeared in numerous critics' polls of the greatest films ever made; Stanley Kubrick named it among his favourites.
