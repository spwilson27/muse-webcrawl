Australia Day is the official national day of Australia. Observed annually on 26 January, it marks the 1788 landing of the First Fleet at Sydney Cove and the raising of the Union Flag by Arthur Phillip following the voyage from England. On this day, ceremonies, family and community events occur across the nation, including citizenship ceremonies at which new Australian citizens affirm their commitment to the country.

History

On 13 May 1787, a fleet of 11 ships commanded by Captain Arthur Phillip set sail from Portsmouth, England, carrying convicts, marines and supplies to establish a penal colony in New South Wales. The fleet reached Sydney Cove on 26 January 1788, a date first marked by emancipists with anniversary dinners in the early 1800s. By 1935 all states celebrated 26 January as Australia Day (under different names), and it became a uniform national public holiday in 1994.

Celebrations

Events include the Australian of the Year Awards announced on the eve of the day, the Australia Day honours list, community barbecues, concerts, sporting events such as cricket and the Australian Open period, harbour regattas and fireworks. Large numbers of citizenship ceremonies are held, and the day is, by law, the day new citizens who attend ceremonies receive their citizenship certificates.

Criticism and "Invasion Day"

For many Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, 26 January marks the beginning of colonisation, dispossession and frontier violence, and the date is protested as "Invasion Day" or observed as a day of mourning — a protest tradition dating to the 1938 Day of Mourning. Proposals to change the date have been debated for decades; a growing number of local councils and organisations have moved or reframed their events, while national polling has generally shown majority support for retaining 26 January.
