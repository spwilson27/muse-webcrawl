Pilocarpine

Pilocarpine
Drug classMuscarinic agonist (parasympathomimetic)
FormulaC11H16N2O2
RoutesTopical (eye drops), oral
UsesGlaucoma, dry mouth, presbyopia

Pilocarpine is a medication used to reduce pressure inside the eye and to treat dry mouth. As eye drops it is used to manage several forms of glaucoma, including acute angle-closure glaucoma, and to cause constriction of the pupil (miosis). Taken by mouth it is used for dry mouth caused by Sjögren syndrome or radiation therapy. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism of action

Pilocarpine is a non-selective muscarinic acetylcholine receptor agonist. In the eye it contracts the ciliary muscle and the sphincter pupillae, opening the trabecular meshwork and increasing the outflow of aqueous humour, which lowers intraocular pressure. In the salivary glands it stimulates secretion.

Medical uses

  • Ophthalmic: treatment of open-angle and angle-closure glaucoma; reversal of mydriasis; since 2021, a low-dose formulation has been approved in the United States for the treatment of presbyopia.
  • Oral: treatment of xerostomia (dry mouth) resulting from Sjögren syndrome or head and neck radiotherapy.
  • Diagnostic: the pilocarpine sweat test is used in the diagnosis of cystic fibrosis.

Side effects

Common side effects of the eye drops include brow ache, blurred vision, and small pupils; oral use can cause sweating, nausea, diarrhoea and increased urination, reflecting its widespread parasympathomimetic action.

History

Pilocarpine is an alkaloid isolated in 1874–75 from the leaves of the South American shrub Pilocarpus (jaborandi). It was introduced for the treatment of glaucoma in the 1870s and is among the oldest drugs still used in ophthalmology.
