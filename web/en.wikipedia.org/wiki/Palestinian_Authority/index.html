Palestinian Authority

The Palestinian Authority (PA; Arabic: السلطة الوطنية الفلسطينية), officially the Palestinian National Authority (PNA), is the Fatah-controlled government body that exercises partial civil control over Palestinian population centres in the West Bank and, nominally, the Gaza Strip. Created by the Oslo Accords between Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) in 1994 as an interim self-governing body for a five-year transitional period, it has endured for three decades. Since the 2007 split in which Hamas seized control of Gaza, the PA's effective authority has been confined to parts of the West Bank, subdivided under the Oslo framework into Areas A, B and C.

Origins and structure

The PA was established under the 1993 Declaration of Principles and the 1995 Interim Agreement (Oslo II), which divided the West Bank into Area A (full Palestinian civil and security control), Area B (Palestinian civil control, joint security) and Area C (full Israeli control, about 60% of the territory). Yasser Arafat, who returned from exile in 1994, was elected president in 1996 and dominated the institution until his death in 2004; he was succeeded by Mahmoud Abbas. The PA runs ministries, schools, hospitals and a large public payroll funded by foreign aid and by tax revenues collected on its behalf by Israel, and fields substantial security forces that coordinate with Israel — a role supporters defend as stabilising and critics condemn as subcontracted occupation.

Split with Hamas and later years

Hamas's victory in the 2006 legislative elections, the brief unity government that followed, and Hamas's forcible takeover of Gaza in June 2007 split Palestinian governance in two. Abbas dismissed the Hamas-led government and appointed an emergency administration in Ramallah; repeated reconciliation agreements were never fully implemented, and no national elections have been held since 2006. The PA's popularity has been eroded by corruption allegations, authoritarian drift, economic dependence and the absence of progress toward statehood, even as foreign governments — especially after the 2023 Gaza war — have pressed for a "revitalised" PA to assume governance of a post-war Gaza, an outcome that remains uncertain.
