Tsipouro

Tsipouro
Type:Pomace brandy
Origin:Greece
Alcohol:40–45% ABV
Variants:With or without anise; tsikoudia (Crete)

Tsipouro (Greek: τσίπουρο) is a Greek pomace brandy, an unaged distilled spirit produced from the pomace (the residue of skins, pulp and seeds) left over after grapes are pressed for wine. Distillation takes place in copper stills, traditionally in the autumn after the grape harvest, in a festive communal process centred on village kazania (stills). The spirit is typically 40–45 per cent alcohol by volume.

Production and varieties

Tsipouro is produced across mainland Greece, especially in Thessaly, Macedonia, Epirus and Thrace. In some regions, notably Tyrnavos, anise and other aromatics are added during a second distillation, producing a spirit similar in flavour to ouzo; elsewhere it is distilled plain. The Cretan equivalent, made under its own protected designation, is called tsikoudia or raki and is likewise usually unflavoured. Tsipouro of Tyrnavos, tsikoudia of Crete, and tsipouro of Thessaly and Macedonia hold protected geographical status in the European Union.

Serving

Tsipouro is traditionally drunk in small glasses, neat or with ice or a little water, and always accompanied by meze — small dishes such as cheese, olives, cured meats and seafood. Dedicated tavernas called tsipouradika, particularly famous in Volos, serve successive small bottles with a progression of seafood dishes. The drink is strongly associated with hospitality and conviviality in Greek culture.
