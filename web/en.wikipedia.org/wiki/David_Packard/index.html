David Packard

David Packard (September 7, 1912 – March 26, 1996) was an American electrical engineer and businessman who co-founded the Hewlett-Packard Company (HP) in 1939 with Bill Hewlett. The company began in a one-car garage in Palo Alto, California — later regarded as the birthplace of Silicon Valley — with an initial investment of $538.

Career

Packard served as HP's president and chief executive and helped develop its distinctive management culture, "the HP Way," emphasizing openness and management by walking around. He served as U.S. Deputy Secretary of Defense from 1969 to 1971 under President Richard Nixon.

Philanthropy

With his wife Lucile, he founded the David and Lucile Packard Foundation in 1964, a major supporter of conservation, science, and the Monterey Bay Aquarium, which the Packards helped establish. He died in Stanford, California, in 1996.
