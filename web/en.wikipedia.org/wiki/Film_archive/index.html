Film archive

A film archive is an archive that collects, restores, preserves, and provides access to films and the documents related to the history of cinema. Film archives are responsible for rescuing, collecting, preserving, and screening moving images, which are recorded on film stock, videotape, or digital media.

History

The first film archives were established in the 1930s, when institutions such as the Museum of Modern Art in New York, the British Film Institute in London, and the Cinémathèque française in Paris began systematically collecting films that the commercial industry had treated as disposable. The International Federation of Film Archives (FIAF) was founded in Paris in 1938 to coordinate the work of these archives.

Preservation challenges

Early films were shot on cellulose nitrate stock, which is highly flammable and chemically unstable; a large majority of silent-era films are lost. Later acetate "safety film" suffers from vinegar syndrome, an irreversible decay process. Modern archives store film in cold, dry vaults to slow deterioration, and increasingly digitise collections — though digital files themselves require ongoing migration to survive format obsolescence. Major archives include the Library of Congress's National Film Registry in the United States, the BFI National Archive, and the George Eastman Museum.
