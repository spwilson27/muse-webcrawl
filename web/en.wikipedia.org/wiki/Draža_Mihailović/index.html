Draža Mihailović

Dragoljub 'Draža' Mihailović
Born: 26 April 1893, Ivanjica, Serbia
Died: 17 July 1946, Belgrade (executed)
Allegiance: Kingdom of Yugoslavia
Commands: Chetnik movement (Yugoslav Army in the Homeland)

Dragoljub Mihailović (1893–1946), known as Draža, was a Yugoslav Serb general who led the Chetnik resistance movement during World War II.

World War II

After the Axis invasion of Yugoslavia in April 1941, Mihailović organised royalist guerrillas at Ravna Gora and was recognised by the Yugoslav government-in-exile and initially supported by the Allies. His movement increasingly clashed with Tito's communist Partisans and factions collaborated tactically with Axis occupiers against them.

Trial and legacy

Captured in 1946, he was tried for treason and war crimes by the new communist government, executed, and buried in an unmarked grave. In 2015 a Serbian court politically rehabilitated him, a decision that remains controversial given documented Chetnik atrocities against Croat and Bosniak civilians.
