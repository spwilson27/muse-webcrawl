Polydipsia
SpecialtyEndocrinology, nephrology, psychiatry
DefinitionExcessive thirst and fluid intake

Polydipsia is excessive thirst and the consequent consumption of abnormally large quantities of fluid. It is a symptom rather than a disease, classically paired with polyuria (excessive urine production) in the medical triad that prompts investigation for diabetes and other disorders of water balance.

Polydipsia is characterised by persistent, excessive thirst.

Causes

The most important causes are diabetes mellitus, in which high blood glucose causes osmotic diuresis and dehydration, and diabetes insipidus, in which deficiency of, or resistance to, the hormone vasopressin (ADH) prevents the kidneys from concentrating urine. Other causes include chronic kidney disease, hypercalcaemia, hypokalaemia, certain medications such as lithium and diuretics, and dry mouth (xerostomia).

Psychogenic polydipsia

Primary or psychogenic polydipsia is compulsive water drinking seen particularly in people with schizophrenia and other psychiatric conditions. Intake can exceed the kidneys' ability to excrete water, causing dilutional hyponatraemia (water intoxication), which may lead to confusion, seizures, and death.

Diagnosis

Evaluation includes measuring fluid intake and urine output, blood glucose, serum and urine osmolality, and electrolytes. The water deprivation test, with response to desmopressin, distinguishes central from nephrogenic diabetes insipidus and primary polydipsia.
