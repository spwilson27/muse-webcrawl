Hyperbolic asteroid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A hyperbolic asteroid is a small Solar System body on a hyperbolic trajectory through the inner Solar System — that is, an object whose orbit has an eccentricity greater than 1, meaning it is not gravitationally bound to the Sun and will leave the Solar System (or, for interstellar objects, is merely passing through it).

Types

Some hyperbolic asteroids are native bodies flung onto escape trajectories by gravitational encounters with Jupiter or other planets, or by poorly determined orbits that only appear hyperbolic. The most famous examples, however, are interstellar objects originating around other stars. The first confirmed interstellar visitor, 1I/ʻOumuamua, discovered in 2017 by the Pan-STARRS survey, followed a strongly hyperbolic path (eccentricity about 1.2) and showed an unusual elongated or disc-like shape and a puzzling non-gravitational acceleration.

Other examples

The second interstellar object, 2I/Borisov (2019), was an active comet rather than an inert asteroid. Before the interstellar era, objects such as C/1980 E1 (Bowell) had been placed on hyperbolic trajectories by a close pass of Jupiter. Catalogues list a modest number of asteroids on apparently hyperbolic orbits, many of which revert to elliptical solutions as observations improve; genuinely hyperbolic small bodies remain rare, and each new detection refines estimates of the population of interstellar debris in the galaxy.
