Ray number


Rho (ρ) curves on the Theodorsen chart associated with Ray numbers.

In fluid dynamics, the Ray number (or Rho number) is a dimensionless quantity used to characterize the behaviour of fluid flow in certain specialized contexts, notably in connection with the classification of flows and the study ofrho curves relating parameters in aerodynamics and turbomachinery.

Definition

Dimensionless numbers such as the Ray number arise from dimensional analysis (the Buckingham π theorem) and allow experimental results obtained at one scale to be applied to geometrically similar systems at other scales. Like the better-known Reynolds number, Mach number, and Froude number, a Ray-type parameter combines fluid density, velocity, a characteristic length, and viscosity or compressibility properties into a single grouping.

Usage

The term appears in older aeronautical literature, where numbered families of dimensionless groups (sometimes denoted with Greek letters such as ρ, "rho") were used to plot performance characteristics of airfoils, cascades, and rotating machinery. In modern practice, the Reynolds and Mach numbers dominate most applications.
