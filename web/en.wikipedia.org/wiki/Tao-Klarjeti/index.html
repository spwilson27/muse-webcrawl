Tao-Klarjeti

Tao-Klarjeti (Georgian: ტაო-კლარჯეთი) is a historical region of medieval Georgia, now divided between north-eastern Turkey and Georgia. Comprising the provinces of Tao and Klarjeti in the Çoruh river basin, it was the cradle of the Bagrationi dynasty and of the unified Georgian kingdom.

History

From the late eighth century, as Arab rule weakened, Georgian princes of the Bagrationi family built a principality in Tao-Klarjeti under Byzantine protection. Ashot I (d. 826) founded its capital at Artanuji and took the Byzantine title of kouropalates. A monastic revival led by St. Grigol Khandzteli filled the region with churches and monasteries — Khandzta, Shatberdi, Bana, Oshki, and Khakhuli among them.

Legacy

In 1008 the inheritance of Tao-Klarjeti helped unite the Georgian lands under Bagrat III. The region's great cathedrals, such as Oshki and Bana, are masterpieces of medieval Georgian architecture; most now lie in Turkey's Artvin and Erzurum provinces, some in ruins, and remain central to Georgian cultural memory.
