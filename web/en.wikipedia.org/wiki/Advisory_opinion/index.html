Advisory opinion

[Image: The Peace Palace in The Hague, seat of the International Court of Justice, which issues advisory opinions at the request of UN organs]

An advisory opinion is a non-binding legal opinion issued by a court or tribunal in response to a question of law, without deciding a concrete dispute between parties. Advisory opinions clarify the law but, unlike judgments in contentious cases, they do not bind the parties to a dispute.

International Court of Justice

The International Court of Justice may give advisory opinions at the request of the UN General Assembly, the Security Council, and other authorised organs and specialised agencies. Well-known examples include the opinions on the legality of nuclear weapons (1996), the Israeli barrier in the occupied Palestinian territory (2004), Kosovo's declaration of independence (2010), the Chagos Archipelago (2019), and the climate change obligations of states (2025).

National courts

Practice varies by country. Federal courts in the United States may not issue advisory opinions, under the case-or-controversy requirement of Article III of the Constitution, a principle established in Muskrat v. United States (1911). Some US state courts, and the Supreme Court of Canada through the reference question procedure, may issue them.

European courts

The European Court of Human Rights acquired a limited advisory opinion procedure under Protocol No. 16, allowing highest national courts to request opinions on questions of principle.
