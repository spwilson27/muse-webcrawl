Archery had its debut at the Paris 1900 Summer Olympics and was contested in 1904, 1908, and 1920 under a variety of local formats. After a 52-year absence it returned as a standardised modern sport at Munich 1972 and has appeared at every Games since. Competition uses recurve bows only; compound bows are not part of the Olympic programme (though they are scheduled to debut with a mixed team event at Los Angeles 2028).

Format

Archers shoot at a target 122 cm in diameter from 70 metres. Individual events begin with a 72-arrow ranking round, followed by head-to-head elimination matches using set scoring (five sets of three arrows; two set points for a won set). Team events (since Seoul 1988) and the mixed team event (introduced at Tokyo 2020) use analogous formats, with teams of three and mixed pairs respectively.

History

Early Olympic archery reflected host-country traditions — Belgian pole archery in 1920, for example — and women's events were included as early as 1904. The modern era began with the FITA round at Munich. South Korea's women have dominated the sport: their team won every Olympic women's team gold from the event's introduction in 1988 through Paris 2024, and Korean archers top the all-time Olympic archery medal table by a wide margin. Notable individual champions include Darrell Pace (1976, 1984), Ki Bo-bae (2012), An San, who won three golds at Tokyo 2020 including the first mixed team title, and Kim Woo-jin of the Paris 2024 champion team.
