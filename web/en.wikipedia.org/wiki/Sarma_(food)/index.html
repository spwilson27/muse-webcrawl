Sarma (food)

Sarma (from Turkish sarmak, "to wrap" or "to roll") is a dish of filling wrapped in leaves or other wrappers, found across the Balkans, Central Europe, Anatolia, the Caucasus, Central Asia and the Middle East — cuisines shaped by the former Ottoman world. The filling most commonly combines minced meat (beef, pork or lamb) with rice, onion and spices, though meatless versions are made for fasting periods.

Varieties

Wrappers include pickled or fresh cabbage leaves (the classic winter sarma of Serbia, Croatia, Bosnia, Romania and Bulgaria), grape vine leaves (often called dolma in Turkish and tolma in Armenian, though usage of the two names varies by region), chard, sorrel and collard greens. In Slovenia and Croatia, sarma typically denotes the cabbage version cooked in a rich pot with smoked meats, a festive dish for New Year and weddings. Romanian sarmale, served with polenta (mămăligă) and sour cream, are regarded as a national dish.

Preparation

Leaves are blanched or, for cabbage, soured whole in brine. The filling is rolled tightly, the parcels packed in layers in a pot — often over sauerkraut and smoked ribs — and simmered slowly for several hours. Sarma is commonly said to improve on reheating over subsequent days.

Related dishes

The family of wrapped and stuffed dishes includes dolma, gołąbki (Poland), holishkes (Jewish), kåldolmar (Sweden, a legacy of Charles XII's Ottoman sojourn) and stuffed cabbage rolls worldwide.
