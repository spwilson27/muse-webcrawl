Rizal Park

Rizal Park
Also known as: Luneta Park
Location: Ermita, Manila, Philippines
Area: approx. 58 hectares (143 acres)
Named for: José Rizal
Features: Rizal Monument, Kilometer Zero marker

Rizal Park, also known as Luneta Park or simply Luneta, is a historic urban park in Ermita, Manila, Philippines. Situated along Manila Bay at the northern end of Roxas Boulevard, it is considered one of the largest urban parks in the Philippines, covering about 58 hectares. The park is named after the national hero José Rizal, who was executed by Spanish authorities on the site on 30 December 1896.

History

During the Spanish colonial period the area was the village of Nuevo Bagumbayan, later cleared to create an open field known as Bagumbayan, used for military parades and public executions. The crescent-shaped plaza laid out beside it gave the park its other name, Luneta, from the French lunette. Under American administration the park was developed as a civic centre following Daniel Burnham's 1905 city plan for Manila. In 1913 the Rizal Monument, designed by Swiss sculptor Richard Kissling, was inaugurated; it contains Rizal's remains and is guarded by ceremonial sentries.

Features

The park contains the Rizal Monument and the Independence Flagpole, in front of which stands the Kilometer Zero marker, the point from which road distances in the Philippines are measured. Other features include the National Museum complex nearby, the Manila Ocean Park, Quirino Grandstand, Japanese and Chinese gardens, the National Library, an orchidarium, and an open-air auditorium. A giant relief map of the Philippines was once a feature of the park's central lagoon area.

Significance

Rizal Park has been the site of major national events, including the declaration of Philippine independence from the United States on 4 July 1946, presidential inaugurations, large religious gatherings such as papal Masses, and public protests. It remains a popular leisure destination for families, especially on Sundays and holidays.
