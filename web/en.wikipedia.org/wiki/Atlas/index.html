Atlas

TypeBook of maps / reference work
First modern atlasOrtelius, 1570
Name fromAtlas (Greek mythology)
[Image: A printed world atlas open at a double-page map]
A printed world atlas open at a double-page map

An atlas is a collection of maps in book or digital form, typically showing the political and physical geography of the Earth or of a particular region. Modern atlases combine maps with gazetteers of place names, thematic maps of climate, population and economy, and reference data.

History

The first systematic map collections appeared in the 16th-century Low Countries: Abraham Ortelius's Theatrum Orbis Terrarum (1570) is regarded as the first modern atlas, and Gerardus Mercator's posthumous Atlas (1595) fixed the name — taken from Atlas of Greek mythology, often pictured holding up the heavens and, in later depictions, the globe. National mapping agencies and commercial publishers produced the great printed atlases of the 19th and 20th centuries, while today geographic information systems and online mapping have largely taken over the atlas's reference role, though printed world atlases remain in print.
