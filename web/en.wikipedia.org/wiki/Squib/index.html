Squib

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An electric squib, a miniature explosive device

A squib is a miniature explosive device used in a wide range of industries, from special effects in filmmaking to aerospace engineering. A squib consists of a small tube, typically about 1/4 to 1/2 inch in diameter, filled with a tiny quantity of explosive material and equipped with electrical leads; when current passes through it, the squib detonates with a small but reliable output. The term also describes other devices and failures named after this original device.

Uses

  • Special effects: In film and theatre, squibs simulate bullet impacts, often by bursting a small packet of stage blood attached to an actor's body or costume.
  • Pyrotechnics and blasting: Squibs serve as igniters for larger charges, fireworks, and ejection charges.
  • Aerospace and automotive: Electro-explosive devices derived from squibs activate parachutes, stage separations, and airbag inflators.
  • Firearms: A "squib load" is a malfunction in which a bullet receives too little force and lodges in the barrel — dangerous because a subsequent shot can burst the barrel.

Other meanings

The word "squib" originally referred to a short, witty piece of writing or a small firework. In sports, a squib kick in American football is a low, bouncing kickoff. In Harry Potter fiction, a Squib is a non-magical person born to magical parents. In linguistics, a squib is a short scholarly note discussing a minor point.

Safety

Because squibs are explosive initiators, their manufacture, transport, and use are regulated in most jurisdictions, and effects professionals require licensing to handle them on set.
