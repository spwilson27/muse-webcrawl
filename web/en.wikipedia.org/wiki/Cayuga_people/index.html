Cayuga people

The Cayuga people (Cayuga: Guyohkohnyo, "the People at the Great Swamp") are one of the five original constituents of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy), an alliance of Native American nations in what is now New York State. Their traditional homeland lies around Cayuga Lake in the Finger Lakes region, between the territories of the Seneca and Onondaga peoples.

History

When Europeans first encountered them, the Cayuga lived in villages near Cayuga Lake and engaged in agriculture based on maize, beans, and squash, supplemented by hunting and fishing. During the American Revolutionary War, most Cayuga sided with the British; the Sullivan Expedition of 1779 destroyed their villages, and afterwards many Cayuga dispersed to Canada, to the Seneca reservations in western New York, or westward. Under later treaties the Cayuga Nation of New York lost its reservation lands, a grievance pursued in land claims into the 21st century.

Present day

Today Cayuga citizens are enrolled principally in the Cayuga Nation of New York, the Seneca-Cayuga Nation in Oklahoma, and the Cayuga community at the Six Nations of the Grand River in Ontario, Canada. The Cayuga language, an Iroquoian language, is critically endangered but is the focus of revitalisation efforts, including language nests and immersion teaching at Six Nations.
