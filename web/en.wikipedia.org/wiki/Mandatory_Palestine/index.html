Mandatory Palestine was a geopolitical entity under British administration carved out of Ottoman Southern Syria after the First World War. The British Mandate for Palestine, incorporating the terms of the Balfour Declaration of 1917 in favour of a Jewish national home, was confirmed by the League of Nations in 1922 and came into effect in 1923. Britain administered the territory until the Mandate terminated at midnight on 14 May 1948, whereupon the State of Israel was proclaimed and neighbouring Arab states invaded, beginning the 1948 Arab–Israeli War.

History

British forces under General Allenby conquered Palestine from the Ottoman Empire in 1917–18. The Mandate's text committed Britain to facilitating Jewish immigration and settlement while safeguarding the rights of the existing non-Jewish population. Transjordan, east of the Jordan River, was administered separately from 1921 and became fully independent in 1946.

The Mandate years saw waves of Jewish immigration (aliyah), the growth of Jewish and Arab national movements, and recurring violence, including the 1929 riots and the Arab Revolt of 1936–39. The British Peel Commission (1937) first proposed partition; the 1939 White Paper sharply restricted Jewish immigration on the eve of the Holocaust. After the Second World War, Britain referred the problem to the United Nations, whose 1947 partition plan proposed Jewish and Arab states. Britain withdrew in May 1948 amid civil war between the territory's Jewish and Arab communities.

Demographics

In 1922 the population was about 757,000, roughly 78% Muslim, 11% Jewish and 10% Christian. By 1945 it had grown to about 1.76 million, of whom Jews formed about 31 per cent, the increase driven by immigration and natural growth in both communities.
