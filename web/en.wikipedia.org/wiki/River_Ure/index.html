The River Ure is a river in North Yorkshire, England, approximately 74 miles (119 km) long from its source to the point where it changes name to the River Ouse. It is the principal river of Wensleydale, one of the Yorkshire Dales, which is famous for its cheese named after the valley. Below Wensleydale the Ure flows through the Vale of Mowbray and the Vale of York before becoming the Ouse, which eventually reaches the Humber estuary.

Course

The Ure rises at Ure Head on Abbotside Common, near the border with Cumbria, and flows eastwards through Wensleydale, passing the towns of Hawes and Aysgarth, where it descends the celebrated Aysgarth Falls, a series of broad limestone waterfalls. It passes Middleham, with its castle, and Masham, known for its breweries, then continues south-east past Ripon, entering the flat vale near Boroughbridge, where it is crossed by the A1(M). Near Linton-on-Ouse it is joined by the River Swale—its largest tributary—whereupon the combined stream takes the name Ouse. Traditionally, which name continues downstream depends on the relative sizes at the confluence, and the convention is that the Ure ends there.

History and features

The Ure was navigable to Ripon in the 18th and 19th centuries via the Ripon Canal (opened 1773), which linked the city to the river below Boroughbridge; part of the canal closed in 1956 but was restored and reopened in 1996. The river is valued for salmon and trout fishing in its upper reaches and coarse fishing lower down. In the vale, the Ure is embanked in places against flooding of the low farmland, and gravel extraction has created lake complexes alongside its lower course.

Tributaries

The principal tributary is the River Swale. Smaller tributaries include the River Bain (one of England's shortest named rivers, flowing from Semerwater), Bishopdale Beck, Walden Beck, and the River Skell at Ripon.
