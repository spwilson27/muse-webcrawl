Elevator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Looking up a modern glass elevator shaft in a skyscraper atrium]
Glass elevators in an atrium.

An elevator (lift in British English) is a vertical transport system that moves people and goods between floors of buildings, mines and other structures. Elisha Otis\'s safety brake (demonstrated 1854) made passenger elevators trustworthy, and with them the skyscraper possible: buildings such as the Burj Khalifa operate lifts travelling at 10 m/s, while the fastest (Shanghai Tower) reach about 20.5 m/s. Escalators, Otis\'s other famous product line, complement them in stations and malls.

History

Hoists are ancient (Archimedes reputedly built one), and steam and hydraulic lifts served factories by the 1800s. Otis\'s 1852 safety gear — a spring brake engaging if the rope failed — was dramatised at New York\'s Crystal Palace in 1854; the first passenger elevator entered service in a New York store in 1857, and electric elevators followed from 1880 (Siemens) and 1889.

Technology

Traction elevators use ropes over a sheave with a counterweight; hydraulic types lift by piston for low rises. Modern group control dispatches cars intelligently; destination-control systems group passengers by floor. Machine-room-less designs (KONE MonoSpace, 1996) shrunk the plant room, and ropeless linear-motor concepts (MULTI) can move sideways.

Impact

Without safe elevators, steel-frame skyscrapers and dense cities are impractical; the elevator inverted building value, making top floors (penthouse) the most desirable instead of the least. Otis, KONE, Schindler and Mitsubishi dominate the industry, maintaining millions of units.
