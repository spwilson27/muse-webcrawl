Vince Lombardi

Vincent Thomas Lombardi (June 11, 1913 – September 3, 1970) was an American football coach and executive in the National Football League (NFL). He is best known as the head coach of the Green Bay Packers during the 1960s, leading the team to five NFL championships in seven years, including victories in the first two Super Bowls (Super Bowls I and II) at the end of the 1966 and 1967 seasons.

Early life and career

Lombardi was born in Brooklyn, New York, to Italian immigrant parents. He played football at Fordham University as a guard, one of the celebrated "Seven Blocks of Granite" offensive line. After coaching at high-school and college level and serving as an assistant at West Point under Earl "Red" Blaik, he joined the New York Giants in 1954 as offensive coordinator under Jim Lee Howell, working alongside defensive coordinator Tom Landry.

Green Bay Packers

Hired as head coach and general manager of the Packers in 1959, Lombardi inherited a team that had won one game the previous season and immediately made it a winner. Behind players such as Bart Starr, Paul Hornung, Jim Taylor and Ray Nitschke, Green Bay won NFL titles in 1961, 1962 and 1965, then won Super Bowls I and II following the 1966 and 1967 seasons — the latter championship decided by the famous "Ice Bowl" victory over Dallas. His signature play was the power sweep, and his demanding, motivational style made him a national symbol of discipline and winning; the famous line "Winning isn't everything, it's the only thing" is popularly associated with him.

Later years and legacy

Lombardi stepped down as Packers head coach after Super Bowl II, served a year as the team's general manager, then coached the Washington Redskins in 1969, leading them to their first winning season in years. He died of colon cancer in 1970 at the age of 57. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1971, and the Super Bowl trophy was renamed the Vince Lombardi Trophy in his honour. The stadium in Green Bay, Lambeau Field, stands on Lombardi Avenue.
