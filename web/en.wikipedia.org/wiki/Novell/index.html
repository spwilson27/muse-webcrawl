Novell

Novell, Inc. was an American software company headquartered in Provo, Utah, best known for the NetWare network operating system, which dominated office networking in the 1980s and early 1990s. Founded in 1979 and reshaped after 1983 under chief executive Ray Noorda, Novell later moved into Linux with its acquisition of SUSE, before being acquired itself by The Attachmate Group in 2011; its businesses subsequently passed to Micro Focus.

History

Novell's NetWare, released from 1983, let personal computers share files and printers over a local area network and at its peak held a large majority of the network operating system market. Under Noorda, Novell pursued an aggressive strategy of acquisitions and coined the ethos of “coopetition”. In the mid-1990s it bought WordPerfect and Unix System Laboratories, feuded with Microsoft—whose Windows NT and later bundled networking eroded NetWare's position—and sold off pieces of its empire.

In 2003 Novell acquired SUSE Linux, becoming a major Linux vendor, and in 2006 it controversially signed a patent cooperation agreement with Microsoft. Declining revenues led to the 2011 sale to Attachmate for about $2.2 billion, ending Novell's independence; its patent portfolio was partly sold to a consortium including Microsoft, Apple, EMC, and Oracle.

Novell's former headquarters tower in Provo, Utah.
