Benito Pablo Juárez García (21 March 1806 – 18 July 1872) was a Mexican lawyer and politician of Zapotec origin who served as the 26th president of Mexico, holding office intermittently from 1858 until his death in 1872. He is regarded as one of Mexico's most important national heroes and a symbol of liberal reform and indigenous pride.

Early life

Juárez was born in the village of San Pablo Guelatao, Oaxaca, to Zapotec peasant parents who died when he was a small child. Raised by relatives and later taken in by a bookbinder in Oaxaca City, he learned Spanish, studied at the seminary, and eventually trained as a lawyer at the Institute of Sciences and Arts of Oaxaca, graduating in 1834.

Political career

Juárez rose through local politics, becoming governor of Oaxaca and then Minister of Justice under President Juan Álvarez. In that role he authored the Ley Juárez of 1855, which abolished special courts (fueros) for the military and clergy. As a leading figure of the liberal movement, he championed the Constitution of 1857 and the Laws of Reform, which separated church and state and nationalised church property.

Presidency and the French intervention

When conservative forces rejected the constitution, the Reform War (1858–1861) followed, with Juárez leading the liberal government. After the liberals prevailed, financial crisis led France, Britain and Spain to intervene; Napoleon III installed Maximilian of Habsburg as emperor in 1864. Juárez's republican government resisted from the north of the country, and after French withdrawal, Mexican forces defeated the empire in 1867. Maximilian was executed at Querétaro, and Juárez famously declared that respect for the rights of others is peace (el respeto al derecho ajeno es la paz).

Death and legacy

Juárez died of a heart attack in office on 18 July 1872. His birthday, 21 March, is a national holiday in Mexico, and he remains the only indigenous person to serve as Mexico's head of state.
