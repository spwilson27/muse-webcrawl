Hammett acidity function

The Hammett acidity function (H0) is a measure of acidity that is used for very concentrated solutions of strong acids, including superacids. It was proposed by the physical organic chemist Louis Plack Hammett and is the best-known acidity function.

Definition

Whereas the pH scale is useful only for dilute aqueous solutions, the Hammett function extends the acidity scale using a series of weakly basic indicator dyes whose protonation can be followed spectrophotometrically. It is defined by an equation analogous to the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation, based on the equilibrium between an indicator base B and its conjugate acid BH+:

H0 = pKa + log([B]/[BH+])

Values

Concentrated sulfuric acid has an H0 of about −12. Superacids such as magic acid and fluoroantimonic acid reach values below −20; fluoroantimonic acid, the strongest known superacid system, has been estimated at around −28.
