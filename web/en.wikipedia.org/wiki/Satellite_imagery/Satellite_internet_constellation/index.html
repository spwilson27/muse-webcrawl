Satellite internet constellation

A broadband satellite constellation
TypeSatellite constellation
PurposeGlobal broadband internet access
Typical orbitLow Earth orbit (LEO)

A satellite internet constellation is a system of many artificial satellites working together to provide internet access to users on the ground. Modern systems place hundreds or thousands of satellites in low Earth orbit, whose low altitude reduces signal latency compared with traditional geostationary satellite internet and allows coverage of remote areas, ships, and aircraft.

Major systems

The largest operational system is Starlink, operated by SpaceX, which has launched several thousand satellites since 2019 and serves millions of customers. Other systems include OneWeb, which provides service via a constellation of roughly 630 satellites in polar orbit, and Amazon's Project Kuiper, which began deploying its constellation of over 3,000 planned satellites in 2025. China is developing national constellations such as Guowang and Qianfan.

Technology

Users connect through a phased-array terminal that tracks fast-moving satellites and hands over between them. Many newer satellites use optical inter-satellite laser links to route traffic in space, reducing dependence on ground stations. Because the satellites orbit close to Earth, they deorbit within a few years, which limits long-lived debris but requires continuous replenishment launches.

Concerns

The rapid growth of mega-constellations has raised concerns among astronomers about light pollution and interference with observations, and among space agencies about collision risk and orbital debris, prompting work on space-traffic coordination and mitigation guidelines.
