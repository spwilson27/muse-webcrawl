Tam Dalyell

Thomas Dalyell Loch (9 August 1932 – 26 January 2017), known as Tam Dalyell, was a Scottish Labour politician who served as a Member of Parliament from 1962 to 2005 — one of the longest-serving MPs of his era, and Father of the House from 2001 until his retirement.

Career

Dalyell was educated at Eton and King's College, Cambridge, and trained as a teacher before winning the West Lothian by-election of 1962. He became known as a famously independent-minded backbencher: a fierce critic of British involvement in the Falklands War settlement disputes (he persistently questioned the sinking of the Argentine cruiser General Belgrano), an opponent of the 2003 invasion of Iraq, and a campaigner on scientific and environmental issues, writing a long-running column for New Scientist.

The West Lothian question

Dalyell is best remembered for coining the West Lothian question during the devolution debates of the 1970s: if Scotland gained its own parliament, why should Scottish MPs at Westminster continue to vote on English domestic matters while English MPs could not vote on equivalent Scottish matters? The question remained unresolved through the establishment of the Scottish Parliament in 1999 and framed debates on English votes for English laws decades later.

Later life

He retired at the 2005 general election. His memoir, The Importance of Being Awkward, was published in 2011. He died in January 2017.
