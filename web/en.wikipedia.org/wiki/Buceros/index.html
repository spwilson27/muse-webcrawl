Buceros is a genus of large hornbills in the family Bucerotidae, native to tropical Asia. The genus was introduced by Carl Linnaeus in 1758. Members of the genus are among the largest hornbills, distinguished by their massive bills topped by a prominent casque.

Species

The genus contains three species:

  • Rhinoceros hornbill (Buceros rhinoceros) — Malay Peninsula and Greater Sunda Islands
  • Great hornbill (Buceros bicornis) — Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia
  • Rufous hornbill (Buceros hydrocorax) — endemic to the Philippines

Biology

Like other hornbills, Buceros species nest in tree cavities, the female being sealed inside with mud and droppings while the male delivers food through a narrow slit. They are primarily frugivorous and are important seed dispersers in Asian rainforests. The great and rhinoceros hornbills are threatened by habitat loss and hunting (the latter including demand for casques), and are listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List.
