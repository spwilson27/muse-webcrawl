Moshe Safdie (born July 14, 1938) is an architect, urban planner, educator, theorist, and author, of Israeli, Canadian, and American citizenship. He is known for incorporating principles of socially responsible design across typologies and geographies, and for such signature projects as Habitat 67 in Montreal, Marina Bay Sands in Singapore, and the Jewel Changi Airport.

Early life and education

Safdie was born in Haifa, then in British Mandate Palestine, to a Syrian-Jewish family. In 1953 the family immigrated to Montreal, Canada. He studied architecture at McGill University, graduating in 1960 with the university's highest honours, and apprenticed with Louis Kahn in Philadelphia before returning to Montreal.

Habitat 67

Safdie's master's thesis — a system of prefabricated modular housing — was selected to be built as a pavilion for Expo 67 in Montreal. Habitat 67, a stacked aggregation of 354 identical precast concrete modules forming 158 residences, each with a private roof garden, became an icon of modern architecture and a landmark of prefabricated design. It made Safdie internationally famous at the age of 29 and has shaped his lifelong inquiry into high-density, humane urban housing.

Later work

Safdie founded his practice in 1964; it now has offices in Boston (headquarters), Jerusalem, Toronto, Shanghai, and Singapore. Major built works include the National Gallery of Canada in Ottawa (1988), the Musée de la civilisation in Quebec City, the Vancouver Public Library's Library Square (1995), the Yad Vashem Holocaust History Museum in Jerusalem (2005), the United States Institute of Peace headquarters in Washington, D.C. (2011), Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art in Arkansas (2011), the SkyPark and integrated resort of Marina Bay Sands in Singapore (2010), the Jewel Changi Airport with its indoor waterfall (2019), and Raffles City Chongqing with its horizontal "Crystal" skybridge. In Jerusalem his projects, including the Mamilla development, have attracted both acclaim and political controversy.

Honours

Safdie is a Companion of the Order of Canada and a recipient of the Gold Medal of the American Institute of Architects (2015) and the Wolf Prize in Architecture (2019). His professional archive is held at McGill University.
