Elevation1,465 m (4,806 ft)
TypeLava dome
LocationAuvergne-Rhône-Alpes, France

The Puy de Dôme is a large lava dome and one of the youngest volcanoes in the Chaîne des Puys region of the Massif Central in south-central France, about 15 km from Clermont-Ferrand. It gives its name to the Puy-de-Dôme department.

History

In 1648 Florin Périer, at Blaise Pascal's urging, carried a barometer up the mountain and showed that air pressure decreased with altitude, a landmark experiment in the history of physics. In Gallo-Roman times a temple of Mercury stood on the summit; its remains were rediscovered in the nineteenth century.

Tourism and sport

The summit, accessible by the Panoramique des Dômes rack railway since 2012, offers views over the Chaîne des Puys. The mountain road famously hosted gruelling finishes of the Tour de France, including the 1964 duel between Jacques Anquetil and Raymond Poulidor, and returned to the race in 2023.
