Catalan solid

The Catalan solids are a family of convex polyhedra that are the duals of the Archimedean solids. They are named after the Belgian mathematician Eugène Catalan, who first described them in 1865. Whereas the Archimedean solids have regular polygon faces and identical vertices, the Catalan solids have identical (congruent) faces but vertices of more than one kind; they are therefore described as face-transitive (isohedral).

Properties

There are thirteen Catalan solids, matching the thirteen Archimedean solids. Their faces are not regular polygons but are all congruent to one another, and their dihedral angles are all equal. Examples include the triakis tetrahedron (dual of the truncated tetrahedron), the rhombic dodecahedron (dual of the cuboctahedron), the deltoidal hexecontahedron (dual of the rhombicosidodecahedron), and the pentagonal hexecontahedron (dual of the snub dodecahedron). Two of them — the pentagonal icositetrahedron and the pentagonal hexecontahedron — are chiral, existing in left- and right-handed forms.

List

  • Triakis tetrahedron — 12 faces
  • Rhombic dodecahedron — 12 faces
  • Triakis octahedron — 24 faces
  • Tetrakis hexahedron — 24 faces
  • Deltoidal icositetrahedron — 24 faces
  • Pentagonal icositetrahedron — 24 faces
  • Rhombic triacontahedron — 30 faces
  • Triakis icosahedron — 60 faces
  • Pentakis dodecahedron — 60 faces
  • Deltoidal hexecontahedron — 60 faces
  • Pentagonal hexecontahedron — 60 faces
  • Disdyakis dodecahedron — 48 faces
  • Disdyakis triacontahedron — 120 faces, the Catalan solid with the most faces

Applications

Because they are face-transitive, Catalan solids make fair dice, and several — especially the rhombic dodecahedron and rhombic triacontahedron — appear in crystal habits, architecture, and design. The disdyakis triacontahedron is used as the projection surface in some geodesic dome and planetarium systems.
