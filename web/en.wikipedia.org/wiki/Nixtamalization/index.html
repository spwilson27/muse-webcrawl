Nixtamalization

Nixtamalization (from Nahuatl nextli, "ashes" or "lime", and tamalli, "tamale") is a process for the preparation of maize (corn) in which the grain is soaked and cooked in an alkaline solution — usually limewater (calcium hydroxide) or lye from wood ash — washed, and hulled. The resulting product, nixtamal, is ground into masa dough for tortillas, tamales and other foods, or dried and cooked whole as hominy.

Process

Dried maize kernels are boiled briefly in the alkaline solution and left to steep for several hours or overnight. The alkali loosens the pericarp (hull), which is rubbed off during washing; the steeping liquid, called nejayote, is discarded. The softened kernels are then ground on a metate or in a mill to produce masa, or kept whole.

Nutritional and chemical effects

Nixtamalization dramatically improves the nutritional value of maize. It releases niacin (vitamin B3) from a bound, unavailable form, increases calcium content, improves the balance of essential amino acids, and reduces mycotoxins such as aflatoxins and fumonisins present in raw grain. It also partially solubilises hemicellulose in the hull and starches in the kernel, giving masa its characteristic cohesive texture and flavour.

History and spread

The process was developed in Mesoamerica several thousand years ago and was integral to the civilisations of the Maya and Aztecs. When maize was carried to Europe, Africa and other parts of the Americas after 1492, nixtamalization was often not adopted with it. Populations that came to depend on untreated maize suffered epidemics of pellagra, the niacin-deficiency disease — a stark demonstration of the process's importance. In the southeastern United States, Indigenous peoples' alkali treatment survived in the making of hominy.

Products

Nixtamal is the basis of corn tortillas, tamales, pozole, atole, pinole, and many regional dishes of Mexico and Central America. Industrially produced masa flour (such as that sold under the Maseca brand) is made by drying and milling nixtamal.
