Kallikratis reform

The Kallikratis reform (Greek: Πρόγραμμα Καλλικράτης) was a major restructuring of the administrative divisions of Greece, enacted by Law 3852/2010 and in force from 1 January 2011. Named after Callicrates, the ancient architect of the Parthenon, it succeeded the earlier Kapodistrias reform and was partly a response to the Greek government-debt crisis, aiming to cut costs and layers of government.

Main changes

The reform reduced the number of municipalities from 1,034 to 325 by compulsory mergers, abolished the 54 prefectures, and replaced them with 74 regional units within 13 self-governing regions whose governors and councils are directly elected. Above the regions it created seven decentralised administrations, whose heads are appointed by the central government, to exercise state supervision. Terms of local government were extended and elections aligned with those of the European Parliament.

Reception

The reform was controversial: supporters praised its rationalisation, while critics — including the opposition and many local communities — protested forced amalgamations, feared loss of local identity and services, and objected that savings were overestimated. It was subsequently amended by the Kleisthenis I programme of 2018–2019, which adjusted electoral rules and split some oversized municipalities.
