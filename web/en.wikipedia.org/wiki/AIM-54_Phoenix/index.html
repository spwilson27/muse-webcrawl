The AIM-54 Phoenix was a long-range American air-to-air missile carried exclusively by the Grumman F-14 Tomcat fighter. Developed by Hughes Aircraft for the United States Navy's fleet air defence mission, it was the first operational air-to-air missile with an active radar seeker for terminal homing, allowing the launching aircraft to guide several missiles against several targets simultaneously. The Phoenix was designed to destroy Soviet bombers and their cruise missiles at very long range, before they could threaten a carrier battle group.

Development

The Phoenix grew out of the cancelled Bendix AAM-N-10 Eagle programme for the Douglas F6D Missileer and the Hughes GAR-9/AIM-47 developed for the Lockheed YF-12 interceptor. When the Navy settled on the F-14 with the Hughes AWG-9 radar and fire-control system, the AIM-54 was developed to match it: the AWG-9 could track up to 24 targets and guide six Phoenix missiles against six of them at once. The missile entered fleet service in 1974 together with the Tomcat.

Design

The Phoenix was a large weapon, roughly 4 metres long and weighing about half a tonne, with a 60 kg high-explosive warhead and a solid-fuel rocket motor giving speeds around Mach 5. In flight it cruised at very high altitude — up to about 100,000 feet in a lofted profile — and used semi-active guidance supported by the Tomcat's radar for most of its flight before switching on its own active seeker for the final approach (a "track-via-missile" arrangement). Improved AIM-54C models of the 1980s added digital electronics and better electronic counter-countermeasures.

Service history

The United States never confirmed a Phoenix kill in combat; two were fired at Iraqi aircraft in January 1999 during Operation Southern Watch without success. The missile was exported only to Iran before the 1979 revolution, and Iranian F-14s used Phoenix extensively during the Iran–Iraq War, claiming numerous victories against Iraqi aircraft — making Iran the missile's only combat user and, after the US retirement in 2004, its only remaining operator. The Phoenix was retired from US service on 30 September 2004, two years before the Tomcat itself, and its fleet-defence role passed to successive versions of the AIM-120 AMRAAM.
