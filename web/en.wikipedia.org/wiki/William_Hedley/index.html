William Hedley (13 July 1779 – 9 January 1843) was an English colliery manager and engineer, one of the pioneers of the steam locomotive. As viewer (manager) of Wylam Colliery in Northumberland, he designed and built Puffing Billy (1813–14), the world's oldest surviving steam locomotive.

Adhesion experiments

Wylam's coal was hauled to the Tyne over a wooden waggonway, and Hedley was charged with replacing horse haulage by steam. Against the prevailing assumption that smooth wheels would slip on smooth rails, Hedley's experiments in 1812–13 demonstrated that the adhesion between smooth iron wheels and rails was sufficient to haul useful loads — a foundational insight for railway traction.

Puffing Billy and Wylam Dilly

Working with engine-wright Jonathan Forster and blacksmith Timothy Hackworth, Hedley built Puffing Billy in 1813–14 and a second engine, Wylam Dilly, shortly after. Puffing Billy worked at Wylam for nearly fifty years and is preserved in the Science Museum, London. Hedley patented his locomotive design in 1813.

Later life

Hedley went on to manage other collieries and remained a respected figure in the north-eastern coal trade. He died in Durham in 1843.
