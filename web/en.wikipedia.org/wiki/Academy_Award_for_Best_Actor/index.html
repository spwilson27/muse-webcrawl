The Academy Award for Best Actor is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS). It is given in honour of an actor who has delivered an outstanding performance in a leading role in a film released that year. The 1st Academy Awards were held in 1929, with Emil Jannings winning for his roles in The Last Command and The Way of All Flesh.

The Oscar statuette presented to Best Actor winners.

History and records

Daniel Day-Lewis holds the record with three wins, for My Left Foot (1989), There Will Be Blood (2007) and Lincoln (2012). Nine actors have won twice, including Spencer Tracy, Marlon Brando, Jack Nicholson, Gary Cooper, Fredric March, Dustin Hoffman, Tom Hanks, Sean Penn and Anthony Hopkins. James Dean is the only actor to receive two posthumous nominations. Adrien Brody became the youngest winner at age 29 for The Pianist (2002), while Anthony Hopkins became the oldest at 83 for The Father (2020). Humphrey Bogart won for The African Queen (1951), directed by John Huston.
