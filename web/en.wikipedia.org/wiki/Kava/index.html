Kava

Kava
Species: Piper methysticum
Origin: Pacific Islands
Active compounds: Kavalactones

Kava is a plant (Piper methysticum) of the Pacific Islands and the psychoactive beverage prepared from its roots. The drink has sedative and anaesthetic properties and has been consumed for centuries in ceremonies and social gatherings across Oceania.

Preparation and use

The root is ground or chewed and mixed with water, then strained to produce a muddy, peppery drink. Kava is central to social and ritual life in Fiji, Vanuatu, Tonga and Samoa, where it is shared from a communal bowl. Its active ingredients, the kavalactones, produce relaxation without impairing mental clarity at moderate doses.

Safety and regulation

Heavy use is associated with a scaly skin condition, and concerns about liver toxicity — linked mainly to extracts made from stems and leaves — led to restrictions in some countries, several of which were later lifted. Kava bars have become popular in the United States and elsewhere.

References: Pacific ethnobotany literature; WHO assessment of kava safety.
