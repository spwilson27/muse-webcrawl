Prescription drug

A prescription drug (also called a prescription medication or prescription-only medicine) is a pharmaceutical drug that is permitted to be dispensed only to those with a medical prescription from a licensed healthcare professional, such as a physician, dentist, or nurse practitioner. Drugs available without a prescription are termed over-the-counter (OTC) drugs.

Regulation

In the United States, the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act defines which substances require a prescription, and the Food and Drug Administration oversees approval and safety. Other jurisdictions maintain similar systems: in the United Kingdom, prescription-only medicines (POM) are distinguished from pharmacy and general-sale medicines.

Prescriptions

A prescription specifies the patient, drug, dosage, route, and duration of therapy, and may authorise refills. Electronic prescribing has largely replaced handwritten prescriptions in many countries, reducing errors from illegible handwriting.

Costs and access

Prices of prescription drugs vary widely between countries and are a recurring subject of public policy debate, particularly in the United States, where patients often pay more than in other developed nations.
