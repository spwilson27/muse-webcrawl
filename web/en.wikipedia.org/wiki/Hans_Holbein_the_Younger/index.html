Hans Holbein the Younger (c. 1497 – 1543) was a German artist and printmaker who worked in a Northern Renaissance style, and is considered one of the greatest portraitists of the 16th century. He also produced religious art, satire, and Reformation propaganda, and he designed for stained glass and metalwork. His portraits of the court of Henry VIII of England — and his famous double portrait The Ambassadors (1533) — fixed the visual image of the Tudor court for posterity.

Early life and Basel

Holbein was born in Augsburg, son of the painter Hans Holbein the Elder, and trained in his father's workshop. Around 1515 he moved to Basel, Switzerland, a centre of humanism and printing, where he designed woodcuts for books — including illustrations for Erasmus's In Praise of Folly and, later, the celebrated Dance of Death series — and painted portraits of the scholar Erasmus of Rotterdam, who became his patron and gave him letters of introduction to England.

England

Holbein first worked in England from 1526 to 1528, painting Sir Thomas More and his circle. After a second Basel period troubled by the Reformation's iconoclasm, he returned to London in 1532 and, through the patronage of Anne Boleyn's circle and then Thomas Cromwell, became King's Painter to Henry VIII. His portraits from this period include Henry VIII (whose most famous full-length image, from the destroyed Whitehall mural, survives in copies), Jane Seymour, Anne of Cleves (painted in 1539 before her marriage to the king), and Christina of Denmark. The Ambassadors (1533), depicting the French envoys Jean de Dinteville and Georges de Selve amid instruments and a distorted anamorphic skull, is among the most analysed paintings of the Northern Renaissance.

Death and legacy

Holbein died in London in the autumn of 1543, probably of plague, at about 46 years of age. His precise, unsparing portraiture — combining northern detail with Italianate monumentality — shaped English painting for generations, and the image he created of Henry VIII remains definitive.
