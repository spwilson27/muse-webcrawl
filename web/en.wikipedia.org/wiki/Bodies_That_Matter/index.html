Bodies That Matter

Bodies That Matter: On the Discursive Limits of "Sex" is a 1993 book by the philosopher Judith Butler. It is a sequel to Butler's influential Gender Trouble (1990) and responds to critics of that work, elaborating the theory of gender performativity and the idea that sex itself is materialised through regulatory discourses.

Argument

Butler argues that bodies become intelligible — that they come to "matter", in the double sense of materialising and being significant — through the repeated citation of norms. Drawing on Derrida, Foucault and psychoanalysis, the book examines drag culture, the film Paris Is Burning, and literary texts including Willa Cather's "The Professor's House" and Nella Larsen's Passing.

Reception

The book consolidated Butler's reputation in feminist theory, queer theory and philosophy, and remains a standard graduate text on the social construction of sex and the body.
