The River Waveney is a river in East Anglia, England, forming much of the boundary between Norfolk and Suffolk. It rises in the Redgrave and Lopham Fens near the source of the Little Ouse River, flowing close by — the two rivers rise within yards of each other and flow in opposite directions. The Waveney passes Diss, Harleston, Bungay and Beccles before joining Breydon Water and the River Yare at Great Yarmouth.

History

In Roman and Anglo-Saxon times the Waveney valley was an important routeway; the river is tidal as far as Beccles and was navigated by wherries carrying agricultural produce and malt. The name "Waveney" is thought to derive from Old English roots meaning the river by the water meadows or wagons.

Wildlife

The valley's grazing marshes, including Carlton Marshes and the fens at its source, support breeding waders, marsh harriers and otters. Upper reaches hold one of England's fenland reserves, Redgrave and Lopham Fen, home to the fen raft spider.
