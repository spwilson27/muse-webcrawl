Prague groschen

The Prague groschen (Czech: pražský groš; Latin: grossus Pragensis) was a silver coin issued in the Kingdom of Bohemia from 1300, following the monetary reform of King Wenceslaus II. It became one of the most widespread and trusted currencies of late medieval Central Europe.

Introduction

The reform drew on the rich silver output of the Kutná Hora mines and was carried out with the expertise of Italian financiers and mint masters from Florence. The groschen, struck at the Italian Court mint in Kutná Hora, replaced the earlier bracteate denars and was accompanied by a small denomination, the parvus.

Design

The obverse bears the Bohemian crown with the name of the king around it; the reverse shows the crowned Bohemian lion with the inscription GROSSI PRAGENSES. Early issues were of high-purity silver weighing about 3.8 grams, though weight and fineness declined over subsequent reigns.

Circulation and end

Prague groschen circulated widely in Poland, Hungary, Austria and the German lands and were frequently imitated, notably by the Meissen groschen. Production ceased in 1547 under Ferdinand I, when the coin was superseded by the thaler-type currency, but surviving groschen remained in use for decades and are common finds in medieval coin hoards.
