Complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial

In algebra, the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials are a specific kind of symmetric polynomials. Every symmetric polynomial can be expressed as a polynomial in complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials. The complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial of degree k in n variables, written hk(X1, …, Xn), is the sum of all monomials of total degree k in the variables. Formally,

hk(X1, …, Xn) = Σ1 ≤ i1 ≤ i2 ≤ … ≤ ik ≤ n Xi1 Xi2 ⋯ Xik

For example, in three variables the first few complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials are:

  • h0 = 1
  • h1(X1, X2, X3) = X1 + X2 + X3
  • h2(X1, X2, X3) = X12 + X22 + X32 + X1X2 + X1X3 + X2X3

The number of monomials of degree k in n variables is the binomial coefficient (n + k − 1 choose k).

Relations with other symmetric polynomials

The complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials are related to the elementary symmetric polynomials ek through the generating function identity

Σk≥0 hk tk = Πi 1/(1 − Xi t)

and by the Newton-type relations Σi=0k (−1)i ei hk−i = 0 for k ≥ 1. They also arise as the characters of finite-dimensional irreducible representations in the theory of Schur polynomials: each Schur polynomial sλ can be expressed in terms of complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials via the Jacobi–Trudi identities, which write sλ as a determinant in the hk.

Generating functions and power sums

Together with the elementary symmetric polynomials, the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials form two alternative systems of generators for the ring of symmetric polynomials. They are also related to the power sum symmetric polynomials through generating-function identities involving logarithms and exponentials.
