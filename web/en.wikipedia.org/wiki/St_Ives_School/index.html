St Ives School

The St Ives School refers to a group of artists living and working in the Cornish fishing town of St Ives and the surrounding area during the middle of the twentieth century. A epicentre of modern British painting and sculpture, the artists of the St Ives School combined abstract art with the landscape, light and maritime environment of West Penwith.

Origins

An artists' colony had existed at St Ives since the 1880s, drawn by the quality of the light and the working harbour. The modern movement is usually dated from the arrival in 1939 of Barbara Hepworth, Ben Nicholson and the Russian sculptor Naum Gabo, who moved to Carbis Bay at the outbreak of the Second World War.

Artists

Key figures associated with the St Ives School include:

  • Peter Lanyon — the only one of the group native to St Ives
  • Roger Hilton
  • Patrick Heron
  • Terry Frost
  • Bryan Wynter
  • Sven Berlin
  • Wilhelmina Barns-Graham
  • John Wells
  • Bernard Leach and the pottery tradition, including Shōji Hamada

Legacy

The work of the group is strongly represented in the collection of Tate St Ives, which opened in 1993 overlooking Porthmeor Beach, and at the Barbara Hepworth Museum and Sculpture Garden, based in Hepworth's former studio, Trewyn Studio.
