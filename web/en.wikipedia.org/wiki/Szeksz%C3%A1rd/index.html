Szekszárd is a town in southern Hungary and the capital of Tolna County. It lies near the Sió channel and the Danube floodplain, and is one of the smaller county seats in the country.

History

The town was first mentioned in 1015 in the founding charter of the abbey of Pécsvárad; King Béla I founded a Benedictine abbey there in 1061 and was buried in it. Szekszárd was occupied by the Ottoman Turks between 1541 and 1686, and later developed as a market town. It is the birthplace of the poet Mihály Babits, a leading figure of Hungarian literature, whose birthplace is now a museum.

Wine

Szekszárd is the centre of one of Hungary's historic wine regions, best known for its full-bodied red wines, including the Bikavér ("Bull's Blood") blend also produced in Eger, and for Kadarka and Kékfrankos varieties.
