Untermensch

Untermensch (German for "under-man" or "sub-human"; plural: Untermenschen) was a term used in Nazi racial ideology for people considered inferior, in contrast to the Übermensch ("over-man"). In Nazi usage it was applied above all to Jews, Slavs, and Roma, and it served to dehumanize the victims of Nazi persecution, mass murder, and plans for enslavement and extermination in Eastern Europe.

Origins

The word derived from Friedrich Nietzsche's concept of the Übermensch, inverted by later writers. It was popularized in its racial sense in the 1920s through the German translation of Lothrop Stoddard's American book The Revolt Against Civilization: The Menace of the Under Man (1922), rendered as Der Kulturumsturz: Die Drohung des Untermenschen.

Nazi use

Heinrich Himmler and other Nazi leaders used the term extensively in propaganda, most notoriously in the 1942 SS pamphlet Der Untermensch, which contrasted idealized "Aryan" imagery with degrading depictions of Eastern European and Jewish people. The concept underpinned the Hunger Plan, Generalplan Ost, and the treatment of Soviet prisoners of war, millions of whom died in captivity, and justified mass killings such as those carried out by the Einsatzgruppen.
