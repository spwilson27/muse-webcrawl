Edgar Degas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Dancers in blue tutus adjusting their costumes in the wings, cropped by the frame as if glimpsed in passing]

Edgar Degas (19 July 1834 – 27 September 1917), born Hilaire-Germain-Edgar De Gas, was a French Impressionist artist famous for his pastel drawings and oil paintings. Degas also produced bronze sculptures, prints and drawings. Degas is especially identified with the subject of dance; more than half of his works depict dancers.

Preferring to be called a 'Realist', Degas took modern Paris for his subject: ballet rehearsals glimpsed from odd angles, racehorses, cafés, laundresses and milliners, painted with daring cropping borrowed from photography and Japanese prints. In later life, with eyesight failing, he turned increasingly to pastel and to modelling wax dancers and horses, cast in bronze after his death.
