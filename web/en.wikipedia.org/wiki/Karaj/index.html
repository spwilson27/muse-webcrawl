Karaj

Karaj (Persian: کرج) is a city in north-central Iran, the capital of Alborz Province, lying about 20 kilometres west of Tehran at the foothills of the Alborz mountains. With a population of roughly 1.6 million (city) it is one of Iran's largest cities and functions largely as a western satellite and commuter city of the Tehran metropolis.

History

The area has ancient roots — the Tepe Khurvin mound shows occupation from the Bronze Age — and the Karaj River valley served as the route from Rey to Qazvin. The Safavid-era Soltanieh bridge and the Shah-Abbasi caravanserai in the old town date from the 17th century. Karaj remained a small town until the Pahlavi era, when an agricultural college (now part of the University of Tehran) and early industries were established; explosive growth after the 1960s, fed by proximity to Tehran and industry along the Tehran–Qazvin corridor, made it a major city. In 2010 it became capital of the newly created Alborz Province.

Geography and economy

Karaj sits on the Karaj River below the Amir Kabir (Karaj) Dam, a popular recreation area, and the road to Chalus on the Caspian coast begins here. Its economy mixes manufacturing, agriculture and orchards — the city was historically famed for its gardens — and large residential districts housing Tehran commuters, connected by motorway and by Tehran Metro Line 5.

Demographics

Rapid migration from across Iran has made Karaj ethnically diverse, with Persians, Azerbaijanis, Kurds, Lurs and others. The wider county includes the towns of Mohammadshahr, Meshkin Dasht and Kamal Shahr.
