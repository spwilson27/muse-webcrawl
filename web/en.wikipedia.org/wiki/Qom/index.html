Qom


The Shrine of Fatimah al-Masumeh in Qom

Qom (Persian: قم) is a city in the Central District of Qom County, Qom province, Iran, serving as the capital of the province, county and district. It is situated about 140 km south of Tehran, on the banks of the Qom River. With a population of over 1.2 million (2016 census), it is one of Iran's largest cities and, after Mashhad, its most important centre of Shia pilgrimage and learning.

Religious significance

Qom is the site of the Shrine of Fatimah al-Masumeh, sister of the eighth Imam, Reza, who died in the city in 816 CE. The city is the seat of the Hawza 'Ilmiyya, Iran's largest seminary, refounded by Ayatollah Abdolkarim Haeri Yazdi in the 1920s; Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini studied and taught there, and Qom's clergy played a central role in the Iranian Revolution of 1979. Many senior ayatollahs and the offices of the marjaʿ reside in the city.

History

Settled since ancient times, Qom became an Arab Shi'i settlement in the 7th–8th centuries and a refuge for Shi'a fleeing Abbasid persecution. It flourished under the Safavids, who adorned the shrine, and retained prominence under the Qajars and Pahlavis.
