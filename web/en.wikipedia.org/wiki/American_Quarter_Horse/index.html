American Quarter Horse

The American Quarter Horse is a breed of horse developed in the United States and renowned for sprinting short distances, especially a quarter of a mile. It is the most numerous registered breed in the world and is closely associated with ranch work, rodeo, western riding and quarter horse racing.

Characteristics

Quarter Horses are compact and heavily muscled, with a broad chest, powerful hindquarters and a generally calm temperament. They commonly stand about 14 to 16 hands high and appear in many coat colours recognised by the American Quarter Horse Association. Different breeding emphases produce racing, cutting, reining, halter and all-around performance types.

History and uses

The breed descends from colonial sprint horses crossed with English Thoroughbreds and later shaped by western stock work. The AQHA was founded in 1940 to preserve and register the breed. Quarter Horses excel at events requiring rapid acceleration, agility and “cow sense”, but inherited conditions such as HYPP and GBED have made genetic testing an important registry issue.
