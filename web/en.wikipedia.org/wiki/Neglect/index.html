Neglect

Neglect is a form of abuse in which a person or caregiver fails to provide needed care to a dependent person — such as a child, an elderly person, or a person with a disability — resulting in harm or risk of harm. Unlike physical or emotional abuse, which typically involve acts of commission, neglect is an act of omission: the failure to meet basic needs for food, shelter, clothing, medical care, supervision, education, or emotional support.

Child neglect

Child neglect is the most commonly reported form of child abuse in many jurisdictions. It may be physical (failure to provide food, shelter, or supervision), medical (failure to seek necessary treatment), educational (failure to enrol a child in school), or emotional (persistent inattention to a child's emotional needs). Chronic neglect in early childhood can impair brain development, attachment, and later mental health, and is associated with conditions such as reactive attachment disorder.

Elder neglect

Neglect of older adults, whether by family caregivers, paid carers, or institutions, can include failure to provide adequate nutrition, hydration, hygiene, medication, or protection from hazards. Self-neglect — in which an older person is unable or unwilling to care for themselves — is also recognised as a distinct concern in elder abuse research.

Animal and self-neglect

The term is also applied to animal neglect, the failure to provide animals with adequate food, water, shelter, and veterinary care, and to self-neglect, which may be associated with conditions such as Diogenes syndrome, depression, or dementia.

Legal aspects

Most jurisdictions impose a legal duty of care on parents and guardians; criminal neglect statutes penalise serious failures, and child protective services may intervene where neglect endangers a child. In professional settings, neglect by healthcare workers or care homes may lead to regulatory action and civil liability for negligence.
