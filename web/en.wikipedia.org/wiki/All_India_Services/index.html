The All India Services (AIS) are the three premier civil services of India: the Indian Administrative Service (IAS), the Indian Police Service (IPS), and the Indian Forest Service (IFS). Their members are recruited by the Union government but serve in state cadres, staffing senior administrative and police posts in both the central and state governments.

Constitutional basis

Article 312 of the Constitution of India empowers Parliament to create new All India Services. The All India Services Act, 1951 provides the statutory framework. The services succeeded the colonial Indian Civil Service, which Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel defended retaining, calling the new service the "steel frame" of India's administration.

Recruitment and training

Recruitment is by highly competitive examination conducted by the Union Public Service Commission, principally the Civil Services Examination. IAS officers train at the Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration in Mussoorie, IPS officers at the Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel National Police Academy in Hyderabad, and IFS officers at the Indira Gandhi National Forest Academy in Dehradun. Officers are allotted to state cadres, where they serve as district magistrates, superintendents of police, and divisional forest officers before rising to senior policy posts and possible deputation to the Union government.

Criticism and reform

The services have been praised for providing administrative unity across a diverse federation, and criticised for generalism, political interference, short tenures in post, and resistance to lateral entry and specialisation. Successive commissions, including the Second Administrative Reforms Commission, have proposed reforms to recruitment, mid-career training, and performance assessment.
