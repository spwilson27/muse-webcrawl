Acceptance testing

In software engineering, acceptance testing is a test conducted to determine if the requirements of a specification or contract are met. It may involve chemical tests, physical tests, or performance tests. In software, it typically involves a system test performed by the customer or end users to validate that the system meets their business needs before formal acceptance.

Types

User acceptance testing (UAT) is carried out by end users or clients to verify that the delivered system works for them in realistic scenarios. Operational acceptance testing checks readiness for deployment, including backup, recovery and maintenance procedures. In regulated industries, acceptance testing may also include compliance or certification testing against legal and contractual standards.

Acceptance criteria

Acceptance tests are usually derived from acceptance criteria defined in the requirements or contract. In agile software development, acceptance tests are often automated and written in the language of user stories; frameworks supporting acceptance test-driven development (ATDD) and behaviour-driven development (BDD) express tests as executable specifications shared by developers, testers and customers.

Relation to other testing

Acceptance testing occupies the highest level of the test pyramid commonly depicted in the V-model, corresponding to the user requirements phase, in contrast to unit testing (lowest level) and system testing.
