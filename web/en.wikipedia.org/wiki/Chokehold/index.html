Chokehold

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A chokehold, choke, stranglehold or, in judo, shime-waza (Japanese: 絞技, "constriction technique") is a general term for a grappling hold that critically reduces or prevents either air (choking) or blood (strangling) from passing through the neck of an opponent. The restriction may be of one or both and depends on the hold used and the reaction of the victim.

[Hero image: diagram of a rear naked choke application — image placeholder]

Types

Chokeholds can be divided into two primary categories: "blood chokes", which restrict blood flow to the brain, and "air chokes", which restrict breathing. Blood chokes, such as the rear naked choke and the triangle choke, apply pressure to the carotid arteries. Air chokes apply pressure to the trachea (windpipe) and are generally considered more dangerous.

Use in martial arts

Chokes are a fundamental part of grappling arts including Brazilian jiu-jitsu, judo, sambo and mixed martial arts, where they are used as submission holds. In judo competition, shime-waza are permitted for senior competitors; a successful choke may end a contest by ippon if the opponent submits or loses consciousness.

Safety and legality

Prolonged application can cause unconsciousness within seconds and, if maintained, brain damage or death. Because of this, chokeholds by law enforcement have been restricted or banned in a number of jurisdictions, particularly after a series of high-profile deaths in police custody. Many police departments classify the technique as a form of deadly or less-lethal force depending on whether the airway or the carotid arteries are compressed.
