A greenhouse gas is a gas that absorbs and emits infrared radiation, warming a planet's surface — the greenhouse effect. Earth's principal greenhouse gases are water vapour, carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and ozone.

Climate change

Human activity since the Industrial Revolution — burning fossil fuels, deforestation and agriculture — has increased greenhouse gas concentrations sharply (carbon dioxide by about half since 1750), driving modern climate change. Cutting emissions toward 'net zero' and expanding solar and wind power are central to international climate policy and sustainability efforts.
