Bovine

Bovine is an adjective pertaining to cattle and their close relatives. Scientifically, bovines constitute the subfamily Bovinae within the ruminant family Bovidae. The subfamily comprises about 30 species in some 10 genera, divided into the tribes Bovini (cattle, buffaloes and bison), Boselaphini (nilgai and four-horned antelope) and Strepsicerotini (spiral-horned antelopes such as kudus and elands).

Characteristics

Bovines are medium-sized to very large hoofed mammals; the gaur and wild water buffalo are among the largest land animals after elephants, rhinoceroses and hippopotamuses. Most species have horns in both sexes, though in some spiral-horned antelopes only males are horned. As ruminants, bovines possess a four-chambered stomach adapted to fermenting plant material, and most are grazers or mixed feeders inhabiting grasslands, savannas and open woodland in Africa and Asia, with bison extending into North America and Europe.

Domestication and usage

The most economically important bovines are domestic cattle (Bos taurus and Bos indicus), descended from the extinct aurochs and domesticated independently in the Fertile Crescent and the Indus Valley roughly 10,000 years ago; the domestic water buffalo is likewise a major dairy and draught animal in Asia. In agriculture and veterinary contexts, "bovine" is used in names such as bovine spongiform encephalopathy (BSE), bovine tuberculosis and bovine serum albumin.
