Doctor of Medicine

Doctor of Medicine (abbreviated M.D., from the Latin Medicinae Doctor) is a professional doctorate and the primary medical degree awarded by medical schools in the United States and in many other countries that follow the American model of medical education. Holders of the M.D. are licensed as physicians. In the United States, the M.D. is one of two doctoral degrees qualifying a person for physician licensure, the other being the Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine (D.O.).

Training

In the United States, the M.D. is typically a four-year graduate program following a bachelor's degree, comprising two years of preclinical study (basic sciences, anatomy, physiology, pharmacology) and two years of clinical rotations in hospitals and clinics. Graduates then complete residency training of three to seven years before independent practice.

International variations

Some countries award the title of Doctor of Medicine as a postgraduate research doctorate, while basic medical qualifications take other forms, such as the Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS) in the United Kingdom, Australia, India, and many Commonwealth countries.
