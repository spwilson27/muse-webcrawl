Mucin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Structure of a mucin: a protein backbone densely decorated with O-linked sugar chains.

Mucins are a family of high-molecular-weight, heavily glycosylated proteins produced by epithelial tissues in most animals. They are the principal organic component of mucus, the viscous secretion that coats and protects the surfaces of the respiratory, gastrointestinal, and reproductive tracts and the surface of the eye.

Structure

Mucin protein backbones (apomucins) contain tandem repeat domains rich in proline, threonine, and serine (PTS domains) that are densely O-glycosylated in the Golgi apparatus; sugars can account for up to 80 percent of the molecule's mass. The resulting "bottle brush" structure gives mucins their extended, gel-forming character. Mucins fall into two classes: secreted mucins, which form the mucus gel (including the large, polymerizing gel-forming mucins such as MUC2 in the intestine and MUC5AC and MUC5B in the airways, and smaller soluble secreted mucins), and membrane-bound (transmembrane) mucins such as MUC1 and MUC4, which sit in the cell surface glycocalyx with a cytoplasmic tail involved in signalling.

Function

Mucins lubricate and hydrate epithelial surfaces, form a physical and chemical barrier against pathogens and particles, and mediate cell adhesion and signalling. Mucus gels trap inhaled or ingested microbes, which are then cleared by cilia or peristalsis; the inner intestinal mucus layer is normally nearly sterile and its breakdown is implicated in inflammatory bowel disease.

Clinical significance

Abnormal mucin production features in cystic fibrosis (dehydrated, hyperconcentrated airway mucus), chronic bronchitis and asthma (mucus hypersecretion and plugging), and dry eye disease. Many adenocarcinomas overexpress or aberrantly glycosylate membrane mucins; MUC1 (detected as the CA 15-3 antigen) is used as a tumour marker, and mucinous carcinomas are a recognized histological subtype in several organs. Nearly twenty human mucin genes (MUC1 through MUC22) have been identified.
