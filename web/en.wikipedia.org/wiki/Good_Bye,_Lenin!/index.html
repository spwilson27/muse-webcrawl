Good Bye, Lenin! is a 2003 German tragicomedy film directed by Wolfgang Becker. It stars Daniel Brühl as Alex Kerner, a young East Berliner who goes to extraordinary lengths to convince his mother — a devoted socialist who fell into a coma shortly before the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989 — that East Germany still exists when she awakens eight months later, after reunification.

Plot

Christiane Kerner, a loyal citizen of the German Democratic Republic, suffers a heart attack and falls into a coma in October 1989. While she lies unconscious, the Berlin Wall falls, East Germany collapses, and her children adapt to capitalist West German life. When Christiane wakes in mid-1990, a doctor warns that any great shock could kill her. Alex therefore reconstructs the GDR inside her bedroom: he hunts down vanished East German food brands, re-labels Western products, and, with an aspiring filmmaker friend, fabricates episodes of the East German news programme Aktuelle Kamera explaining away every change Christiane notices — culminating in a fake broadcast claiming that West Germans are fleeing to the socialist East. The deception deepens even as Alex learns the hidden truths about his own family's past, including why his father fled to the West.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major commercial and critical success in Germany and internationally, winning the European Film Award for Best Film and numerous German Film Awards. It is widely cited as the defining cinematic treatment of Ostalgie — nostalgia for aspects of life in the former East Germany — and of the disorientation that followed German reunification. Yann Tiersen's piano-led score also attracted wide attention.
