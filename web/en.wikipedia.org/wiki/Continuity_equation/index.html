Continuity equation

A continuity equation is a mathematical statement of the conservation of some physical quantity, expressing that the amount of the quantity in any region changes only by the flux of that quantity across the region's boundary.

A control volume: flux in equals flux out plus accumulation.

General form

∂ρ/∂t + ∇·j = 0

where ρ is the quantity density and j its flux. The equation states that the local rate of decrease of density equals the divergence of the flux.

Fluid dynamics

For a fluid, ρ is mass density and j = ρv, giving the familiar form ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρv) = 0. For incompressible flow (ρ constant) this reduces to ∇·v = 0.

Electromagnetism

Charge conservation is expressed by ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·J = 0, where ρ is charge density and J is current density.

Quantum mechanics

The Schrödinger equation yields a continuity equation for probability density: ∂|ψ|²/∂t + ∇·J = 0.
