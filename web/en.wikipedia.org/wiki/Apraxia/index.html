Apraxia

Apraxia is a motor disorder caused by damage to the brain, in which a person has difficulty performing tasks or movements when asked, even though the request is understood and the muscles themselves function normally. It is distinct from weakness, sensory loss or poor comprehension; the problem lies in the planning and sequencing of movement.

Types

Several forms are recognised. Ideomotor apraxia is the inability to imitate gestures or perform actions on command, such as waving goodbye. Ideational apraxia involves difficulty sequencing multi-step tasks, such as making a cup of tea. Limb-kinetic, constructional, oculomotor and dressing apraxias describe deficits in specific domains. Apraxia of speech — including the developmental form, childhood apraxia of speech — affects the planning of the movements needed for speaking.

Causes and treatment

Acquired apraxia most often results from stroke, traumatic brain injury or neurodegenerative disease affecting the parietal and frontal lobes and their connections. Assessment is carried out by neurologists and occupational and speech therapists, and treatment focuses on rehabilitation strategies that retrain functional tasks and teach compensatory techniques.
