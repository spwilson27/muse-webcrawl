Jami al-Tirmidhi

Jami' al-Tirmidhi (Arabic: جامع الترمذي), also known as Sunan al-Tirmidhi, is one of the six major hadith collections (the Kutub al-Sittah) of Sunni Islam. It was compiled by the ninth-century scholar Muhammad ibn Isa al-Tirmidhi (824–892 CE), a student of al-Bukhari.

Contents and method

The collection contains roughly 4,400 hadiths arranged by chapters of law and practice. Al-Tirmidhi's distinctive contribution was his systematic grading of reports — marking them as sahih (sound), hasan (good), or da'if (weak) — and his practice of noting the differing opinions of the early jurists on each legal question and the evidence they used. He popularised the intermediate grade hasan, an important term in later hadith criticism.

Reception

The Jami' is generally ranked below the collections of al-Bukhari and Muslim in strictness, but it is prized by scholars for its combination of hadith, legal discussion, and criticism of transmitters. It attracted many commentaries, including the 'Aridat al-Ahwadhi of Ibn al-Arabi (the Maliki scholar Abu Bakr ibn al-Arabi) and the Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi of al-Mubarakfuri. It remains a standard text in Islamic seminaries worldwide.
