Aquifoliaceae is a family of flowering plants in the order Aquifoliales. In the APG IV classification it contains a single genus, Ilex — the hollies — with several hundred species of trees, shrubs and climbers distributed throughout temperate and tropical regions of the world.

Description

Members of the family are mostly dioecious evergreen or deciduous woody plants: male and female flowers are borne on separate individuals. The leaves are typically simple, alternate, leathery and often glossy, sometimes with spiny margins, as in the familiar European holly (Ilex aquifolium). The small, inconspicuous flowers are usually four-parted, and the fruit is a drupe — the bright red, black or yellow "berries" for which hollies are widely cultivated.

Taxonomy

Historically, other genera such as Nemopanthus (mountain holly) were sometimes placed in Aquifoliaceae, but molecular phylogenetic studies have led to their inclusion within Ilex in recent systems. The family is placed in the campanulid clade of the asterids, in the order Aquifoliales, alongside families such as Phyllonomaceae and Helwingiaceae.

Uses

Hollies are popular ornamental plants, and their foliage and berries are traditional Christmas decorations. The leaves of Ilex paraguariensis are used to prepare the caffeinated South American infusion yerba mate, while Ilex guayusa and the North American Ilex vomitoria ("yaupon") are also used for caffeinated drinks. The dense, fine-grained white wood of holly has been prized for inlay work and engraving blocks.
