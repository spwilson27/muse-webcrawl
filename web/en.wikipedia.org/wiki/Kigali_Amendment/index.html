Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol
Signed15 October 2016, Kigali, Rwanda
Effective1 January 2019
SubjectPhase-down of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)

Expected effectAvoid up to ~0.4–0.5 °C of global warming by 2100

The Kigali Amendment is an amendment to the Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer, adopted on 15 October 2016 in Kigali, Rwanda, which commits signatories to phase down the production and consumption of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) — potent greenhouse gases widely used as refrigerants.

Background

HFCs were introduced to replace the chlorofluorocarbons and hydrochlorofluorocarbons phased out for harming the ozone layer, following the research of Mario Molina and F. Sherwood Rowland. Although HFCs do not deplete ozone, many are thousands of times more powerful than carbon dioxide as greenhouse gases, and their use was growing rapidly with demand for air conditioning.

Terms

The amendment divides countries into groups with different schedules: most developed countries began cutting HFC use in 2019 aiming for an ~85% reduction by 2036; most developing countries freeze consumption in 2024 and cut by 80% by 2045; a smaller group including India and several Gulf states freezes in 2028. The amendment entered into force on 1 January 2019 and is estimated to avoid up to about half a degree Celsius of warming by 2100. The United States ratified it in 2022 with bipartisan Senate support.
