Article 140

Article 140 of the Constitution of Iraq, adopted in 2005, sets out a process for resolving the status of territories disputed between the federal government in Baghdad and the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG) — above all the oil-rich governorate of Kirkuk, along with parts of Nineveh, Diyala and Saladin governorates.

Provisions

The article incorporates the mechanism first set down in Article 58 of the Transitional Administrative Law of 2004. It prescribes three stages: normalisation — reversing the Arabisation policies of the Ba'athist era, under which Kurds and Turkmen were expelled and Arab settlers brought in; a census; and finally a referendum in Kirkuk and the other disputed areas to determine whether they join the Kurdistan Region or remain under federal administration. The constitution required the process to be completed by 31 December 2007.

Non-implementation

The deadline passed without a census or referendum, and successive Iraqi governments failed to implement the article, making it one of the most contentious unresolved issues in post-2003 Iraq. Kurdish parties regard Article 140 as a binding constitutional commitment and have repeatedly pressed for its application, while many Arab and Turkmen residents of the disputed areas, and politicians in Baghdad, resist steps that might transfer Kirkuk to the KRG.

2017 crisis

After the Kurdistan Region held an independence referendum in September 2017 — which included voting in Kirkuk and other disputed territories — federal forces and allied militias retook Kirkuk and most of the disputed areas in October 2017, reversing Kurdish control established after 2014. The status of the territories, and the fate of Article 140, remain formally unresolved.
