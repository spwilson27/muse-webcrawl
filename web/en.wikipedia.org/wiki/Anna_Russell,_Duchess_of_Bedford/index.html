Anna Russell, Duchess of Bedford

Anna Maria Russell, Duchess of Bedford (3 September 1783 – 3 July 1857), née Stanhope, was a British aristocrat, lady of the bedchamber to Queen Victoria, and the reputed originator of the custom of afternoon tea in 19th-century England.

Life

Daughter of Charles Stanhope, 3rd Earl of Harrington, she married Francis Russell, later 7th Duke of Bedford, in 1808. She was a lifelong friend of Queen Victoria, serving as a Lady of the Bedchamber from 1837 to 1841, and was caught up in the 1839 Lady Flora Hastings scandal at court.

Afternoon tea

Tradition holds that, finding the long gap between luncheon and the late evening dinner at Woburn Abbey produced a "sinking feeling", she began taking tea with bread, butter and cake in her rooms around 1840 and inviting friends to join, popularising afternoon tea as a fashionable social ritual among the aristocracy.
