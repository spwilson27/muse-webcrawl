Alfonso Cuarón Orozco (born 28 November 1961) is a Mexican filmmaker widely regarded as one of the most acclaimed directors of his generation. He is known for his versatility across genres and for long, elaborate tracking shots, often made with cinematographer Emmanuel Lubezki.

Career

Cuarón gained international attention with the road film Y tu mamá también (2001) and directed Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban (2004) and the dystopian thriller Children of Men (2006). His space drama Gravity (2013) won seven Academy Awards, including Best Director. The semi-autobiographical Roma (2018), which he also shot himself, won the Golden Lion at Venice and three Academy Awards, including Best Director and Best Cinematography.
