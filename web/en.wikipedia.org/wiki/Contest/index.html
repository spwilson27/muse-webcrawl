Contest

A contest is an event in which people compete for supremacy in a sport, an activity, or a particular quality — such as beauty, skill, knowledge or creativity — usually for a prize. Contests differ from ordinary competitive sports in that they are often judged subjectively against criteria rather than decided purely by objective performance, and they range from local talent shows to international design and architecture competitions.

Types

  • Beauty contests (pageants): competitions judged largely on physical appearance and presentation, such as Miss Universe and Miss World.
  • Talent and performance contests: singing, dance and variety competitions, from local shows to televised formats.
  • Knowledge contests: quizzes, spelling bees and academic olympiads.
  • Creative contests: design, architecture, writing, photography and programming competitions, in which entries are submitted and evaluated by a jury; architectural competitions have shaped many famous buildings.
  • Promotional contests and sweepstakes: marketing devices in which prizes are awarded for participation or by chance, often regulated to avoid constituting illegal lotteries (no purchase necessary rules derive from this distinction).
  • Eating contests: competitive eating events judged by quantity consumed in a fixed time.

Judging and rules

Contests typically publish eligibility rules, judging criteria and prize structures in advance. Outcomes may be determined by expert judges, public voting, measurable performance, or a combination. Disputes over fairness, judging bias and eligibility are a recurring theme in contest history.

In economics and biology

In economics, "contest theory" studies allocation mechanisms in which agents expend effort to win a prize (for example rent-seeking and patent races), modelled with contest success functions. In ethology, a contest is a competitive interaction between animals over a resource, studied in game-theoretic models such as hawk–dove and the war of attrition.
