George Edward Clinton (born 22 July 1941) is an American singer, songwriter, bandleader and record producer. As the mastermind of the bands Parliament and Funkadelic — collectively known as Parliament-Funkadelic, or P-Funk — he developed an eclectic style blending funk, psychedelic rock, soul and science-fiction mythology, and is regarded, alongside James Brown and Sly Stone, as one of the foremost innovators of funk music.

Career

Clinton began in the late 1950s with a doo-wop group, The Parliaments, which scored a hit with "(I Wanna) Testify" in 1967. Barred by contractual disputes from the name, he built two parallel groups: Funkadelic, a psychedelic rock-funk outfit, and Parliament, a horn-driven R&B band, both staffed by largely the same rotating cast of musicians. In the 1970s the P-Funk collective released landmark albums including Maggot Brain (1971), Mothership Connection (1975), One Nation Under a Groove (1978) and Uncle Jam Wants You (1979), and staged elaborate concerts featuring the landing of a prop "Mothership".

Solo work and legacy

As a solo artist Clinton released Computer Games (1982), whose single "Atomic Dog" topped the R&B chart. P-Funk's records were sampled extensively by hip-hop artists, especially in G-funk. Parliament-Funkadelic was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997, and Clinton received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2019. After decades of touring he announced his retirement from the road in 2019, though he has continued to perform occasionally.
