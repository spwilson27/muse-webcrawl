Amblin Entertainment, Inc. is an American film production company founded by director and producer Steven Spielberg and producers Kathleen Kennedy and Frank Marshall in 1981. Its headquarters are located in Bungalow 477 on the Universal Studios lot in Universal City, California. The company distributes all of the films produced by Amblin Partners, including Amblin Entertainment, DreamWorks Pictures, and Participant.

The company's name derives from Spielberg's first commercially released film, Amblin' (1968), a short independent film about a man and woman hitchhiking through the desert. Its logo, introduced with E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial (1982), features the silhouette of Elliott and E.T. flying in a bicycle across the face of the Moon.

History

Amblin was founded in 1981 by Spielberg, Kennedy, and Marshall, initially operating under an arrangement with Universal Pictures. The company quickly established itself with a string of commercially successful and critically acclaimed films in the 1980s, many of them family-oriented adventures or science fiction stories. Early productions included E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial (1982), Gremlins (1984), The Goonies (1985), and Back to the Future (1985).

In 1991, Kennedy and Marshall left to form their own production company, The Kennedy/Marshall Company. Amblin continued to produce films through the 1990s, including Jurassic Park (1993) and Schindler's List (1993). The company expanded into television with Amblin Television, which produced series such as ER, Animaniacs, and Tiny Toon Adventures.

In December 2015, Amblin became part of the newly formed Amblin Partners, a content creation company backed by Reliance Entertainment, Entertainment One, Participant, and Alibaba Pictures, with distribution through Universal.
