Geography of Oman

The Al Hajar Mountains rise behind the Batinah coastal plain.

Oman
Area: about 309,500 km²
Coastline: about 3,165 km
Highest point: Jebel Shams, about 3,009 m
Capital: Muscat

Oman is a country on the southeastern coast of the Arabian Peninsula, bordered by the United Arab Emirates to the northwest, Saudi Arabia to the west, and Yemen to the southwest, with coasts on the Arabian Sea and the Gulf of Oman. It covers about 309,500 square kilometres, making it the third-largest country on the peninsula. A notable feature is the Musandam Peninsula exclave, separated from the rest of Oman by UAE territory, which commands the southern shore of the Strait of Hormuz; the small exclave of Madha within the UAE contains in turn the counter-enclave of Nahwa.

Regions and terrain

The country divides into several natural regions. The fertile Al Batinah coastal plain runs southeast from the UAE border along the Gulf of Oman. Behind it rise the rugged Al Hajar Mountains, containing Jebel Shams, Oman's highest peak, and the high plateau of Jebel Akhdar, famous for terraced orchards. South of the mountains lie gravel desert plains and the central desert, grading into the Rub' al Khali (Empty Quarter) in the west. In the far south, the governorate of Dhofar is dominated by the Qara Mountains, which catch the Indian Ocean monsoon (the khareef), turning the coast around Salalah green each summer.

Climate and water

Most of Oman is hot and arid, with summer temperatures on the coast exceeding 40 °C combined with high humidity, while the interior desert is hotter and drier. Rainfall is scant and irregular except in the mountains and monsoon-affected Dhofar. Seasonal wadis carry flash floods after storms. With no permanent rivers, Oman relies on groundwater, traditional aflaj irrigation systems (several UNESCO-listed), and desalination plants.

Natural resources

Oman's economy rests on petroleum and natural gas, with significant reserves in the interior basins; other minerals include copper, chromite, and limestone. Ports at Sohar, Duqm, and Salalah exploit the country's position outside the Strait of Hormuz on the Arabian Sea.
