Hindu festivals

Hindu festivals are celebrations observed by Hindus, rooted in the Hindu lunisolar calendar and in the mythology, seasons and regional traditions of the Indian subcontinent. They range from vast pilgrim gatherings such as the Kumbh Mela to household observances honouring a family's chosen deity (ishta-devata).

Major festivals

  • Diwali — the festival of lights, celebrating the victory of light over darkness; marked by oil lamps, fireworks and Lakshmi puja.
  • Holi — the spring festival of colours, associated with Krishna and the arrival of spring.
  • Navaratri and Durga Puja — nine nights honouring the goddess Durga.
  • Dussehra (Vijayadashami) — celebrating Rama's victory over Ravana.
  • Ganesh Chaturthi — honouring Ganesha, especially prominent in Maharashtra.
  • Krishna Janmashtami — the birth of Krishna.
  • Pongal / Makar Sankranti — harvest festivals tied to the sun's northward journey.
  • Maha Shivaratri — the great night of Shiva.

Calendar and observance

Festival dates are fixed by tithi (lunar day) in regional variants of the Hindu calendar, so dates shift against the Gregorian calendar. Observances typically combine temple worship, fasting or feasting, charity, processions, music and dance, and the retelling of sacred stories from the epics and Puranas.

Kumbh Mela

The Kumbh Mela, held in rotation at Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik, is the largest religious gathering in the world, drawing tens of millions of pilgrims to bathe at sacred river confluences.
