The Plastic People of the Universe (PPU) is a Czech rock band formed in Prague in 1968, shortly after the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia. Taking their name from a song by Frank Zappa's Mothers of Invention, they became the leading voice of the Czech cultural underground during the communist era.

History

Managed by art historian Ivan Martin Jirous, the band lost its professional licence in 1970 and performed illegally. Their music drew on the Velvet Underground, Captain Beefheart and Czech poetry, including settings of Egon Bondy's texts. In 1976 the band members and associates were arrested and tried, an event that galvanised the dissident movement: playwright Václav Havel and others founded Charter 77 partly in response. The band split in 1988 and reunited in 1997 after Havel, by then president, invited them to perform.

Legacy

The Plastic People are regarded as a symbol of artistic resistance to totalitarianism; Tom Stoppard's play Rock 'n' Roll (2006) features the band prominently.
