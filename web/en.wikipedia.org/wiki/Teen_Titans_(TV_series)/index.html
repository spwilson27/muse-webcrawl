Teen Titans is an American animated superhero television series created by Glen Murakami and developed by Murakami, David Slack and Sam Register, based on DC Comics' superhero team of the same name. It premiered on Cartoon Network on July 19, 2003, and ran for five seasons over 65 episodes, followed by the television film Teen Titans: Trouble in Tokyo (2006).

Premise

The series follows five young superheroes living in Titans Tower in the fictional Jump City: Robin, the team leader; Starfire, an alien princess from Tamaran; Cyborg, a half-robot athlete and engineer; Raven, a half-demon empath from Azarath; and Beast Boy, a shapeshifter who can transform into animals. Together they fight villains such as Slade (Deathstroke), Trigon, and the H.I.V.E., while dealing with the ordinary pressures of adolescence.

Style and themes

The show blends superhero action with comedy and character-driven drama, and its visual style draws heavily on Japanese anime, including expressive reaction shots and occasional "super deformed" gags. Its opening theme was performed by Japanese duo Puffy AmiYumi. Major season arcs — including Terra's betrayal, Raven's conflict with her father Trigon, and Robin's obsession with Slade — gave the series a reputation for balancing light humour with darker emotional storylines.

Legacy

Teen Titans is regarded as one of Cartoon Network's most successful series of the 2000s. A comedic spin-off, Teen Titans Go!, premiered in 2013, and the original cast returned for the crossover film Teen Titans Go! vs. Teen Titans (2019).
