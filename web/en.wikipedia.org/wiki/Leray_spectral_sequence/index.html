Leray spectral sequence

In algebraic topology, the Leray spectral sequence is a spectral sequence that relates the cohomology of a topological space to the cohomology of a subspace via the higher direct images of a sheaf, or, in its original form, computes the homology of a space from that of a fibration-like mapping. It was developed by Jean Leray in the 1940s.

Statement

For a continuous map f : X → Y and a sheaf F on X, the Leray spectral sequence has second page given by the cohomology of Y with coefficients in the higher direct image sheaves Rqf*F, and it converges to the cohomology of X with coefficients in F.

History

Leray developed sheaf cohomology and spectral sequences while a prisoner of war at Oflag XVII-A in Austria, reportedly disguising his work as research on differential equations to avoid being assigned to war work. His ideas transformed algebraic topology and algebraic geometry.

Applications

Special cases include the Leray–Serre spectral sequence for fibrations and the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence in group cohomology. The construction generalises to derived functors in homological algebra.
