Triumphs of Caesar (Mantegna)

The Triumphs of Caesar are a series of nine large paintings created by the Italian Renaissance artist Andrea Mantegna between about 1484 and 1492 (with work continuing to about 1505). They depict a Roman triumphal procession celebrating the victories of Julius Caesar in the Gallic Wars. The canvases are held in the Royal Collection and displayed in the Lower Orangery at Hampton Court Palace near London.

History

Mantegna painted the series for the Gonzaga court at the Palazzo San Sebastiano in Mantua. The paintings were acquired by King Charles I of England in the 1620s as part of the purchase of the Gonzaga collection, and survived the Commonwealth sale of the king's goods. Vasari praised them as among the finest works of their kind.

Composition

Each canvas is approximately 2.7 metres square, painted in tempera on canvas — an unusual support for Italian painting of the period. Reading as a continuous frieze, the processions show trumpeters, standard-bearers, soldiers carrying spoils and models of conquered towns, sacrificial animals, prisoners, musicians, and finally Caesar himself on a triumphal chariot. Mantegna drew on classical sources including Appian, Plutarch, and Suetonius, and on antiquarian knowledge of Roman relief sculpture.

Condition and influence

The paintings have suffered from past restorations and relining, and remain fragile despite modern conservation. Engravings after the series spread its influence across Europe.
