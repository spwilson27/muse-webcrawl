Hugh Victor McKay (21 August 1865 – 21 May 1926) was an Australian inventor and industrialist best known for developing the first commercially successful combine harvester, the Sunshine Harvester, which stripped, threshed and winnowed grain in a single operation.

Life and career

Born on a farm near Drummartin in Victoria, McKay patented his stripper harvester in 1885 at the age of nineteen. He founded the Sunshine Harvester Works, which grew into one of Australia's largest agricultural machinery manufacturers. In 1907 he became a central figure in the Harvester Judgment, in which Justice H. B. Higgins established the concept of a fair and reasonable basic wage in Australian law.

Legacy

The Melbourne suburb of Sunshine, where his factory was located, took its name from his company. McKay's firm eventually merged into Massey Ferguson.
