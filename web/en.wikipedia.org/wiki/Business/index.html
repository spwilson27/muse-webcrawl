Business

[Image: a busy modern business district with glass office towers, photographed at street level]

A business is an organisation or enterprising entity engaged in commercial, industrial or professional activity — the production or sale of goods and services with the aim of earning a profit, though non-profit organisations are also sometimes described as businesses. Businesses range from a single street vendor to multinational corporations employing hundreds of thousands of people, and they form the basic producing units of a market economy.

Forms of ownership

The simplest form is the sole proprietorship (sole tradership), owned and run by one person with no legal distinction between owner and business. A Partnership is owned by two or more people who share profits and, usually, liability. A Corporation is a separate legal person owned by shareholders, whose liability is limited to their investment; large corporations are typically run by professional managers on behalf of shareholders. Other forms include cooperatives, limited liability companies, franchises and state-owned enterprises.

Activities and functions

Businesses are classified by sector: primary (extraction and agriculture), secondary (manufacturing) and tertiary (services), with "quaternary" knowledge activities sometimes added. Within a firm, core functions include Marketing and sales, Accounting (bookkeeping, financial reporting and auditing), finance (raising and allocating capital), supply chains and logistics, operations, and human resources (recruiting, training and managing staff). Management is the discipline of coordinating these activities.

Starting and running a business

Founding a business typically involves identifying a market opportunity, writing a business plan, choosing a legal form, registering with the authorities, and raising finance — from savings, bank loans, venture capital or investors. Entrepreneurship, the process of creating new ventures, is widely credited with driving innovation and job creation; small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) account for most firms and a large share of employment in most economies. Businesses are regulated through company law, taxation, competition law and sector-specific rules, and many publish accounts and are audited; publicly traded firms sell shares on a stock market.
