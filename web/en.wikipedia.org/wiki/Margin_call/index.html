Margin call

A margin call is a broker's demand that an investor deposit additional funds or securities into a margin account so that the account's equity rises to the maintenance margin requirement. Margin calls occur when the value of securities bought with borrowed money falls, reducing the investor's equity percentage below the minimum set by regulators or the broker. If the investor does not meet the call promptly, the broker may liquidate positions in the account without notice to restore the required margin.

Mechanics

In the United States, the Federal Reserve's Regulation T sets an initial margin of 50% for stock purchases, and FINRA rules require maintenance margin of at least 25%, though brokers commonly demand more ("house margin"). When account equity falls below the requirement, the broker issues a call for the shortfall. Because forced liquidations push prices down further, margin calls can amplify market declines — a dynamic implicated in the 1929 crash, the 2021 Archegos Capital collapse (in which banks issued margin calls on total return swaps), and deleveraging spirals in futures and crypto markets, where "margin calls" occur automatically as liquidation.
