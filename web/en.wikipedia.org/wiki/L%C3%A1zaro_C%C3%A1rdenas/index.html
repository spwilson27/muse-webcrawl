Lázaro Cárdenas del Río (21 May 1895 – 19 October 1970) was a Mexican army officer and politician who served as President of Mexico from 1934 to 1940. He is widely regarded as one of the most popular presidents in Mexican history, remembered for his sweeping land reform, his nationalization of the oil industry, and his support for republican Spain during the Spanish Civil War.

Early life and military career

Cárdenas was born in Jiquilpan, Michoacán, into a lower-middle-class family. He joined the Mexican Revolution in 1913, rising through the ranks of the Constitutionalist Army to become a general. He served as governor of Michoacán from 1928 to 1932, where he began experiments in agrarian reform and secular education that foreshadowed his national policies.

Presidency

Elected in 1934 under the Maximato-era arrangement dominated by Plutarco Elías Calles, Cárdenas broke with Calles in 1935 and exiled him, consolidating his own power. His administration redistributed nearly 20 million hectares of land to peasants through the ejido system, promoted socialist education, and strengthened labor unions such as the Confederation of Mexican Workers (CTM).

Oil expropriation

On 18 March 1938, Cárdenas announced the expropriation of foreign-owned oil companies, creating the state-owned company Petróleos Mexicanos (Pemex). The move, prompted by a labor dispute in which the companies refused to comply with a Supreme Court ruling, made him a national hero; ordinary Mexicans donated money and valuables to help pay compensation.

Later life and legacy

After leaving office, Cárdenas served as Secretary of National Defense during World War II and remained an influential elder statesman of the political left. His son Cuauhtémoc Cárdenas later became a prominent opposition politician. Cárdenas died in Mexico City in 1970.
