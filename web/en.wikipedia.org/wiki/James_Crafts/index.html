James Crafts

Born: March 8, 1839, Boston, Massachusetts, U.S.

Died: June 20, 1917 (aged 78), Ridgefield, Connecticut, U.S.

Known for: Friedel–Crafts reaction

James Mason Crafts (March 8, 1839 – June 20, 1917) was an American chemist, best known for co-discovering the Friedel–Crafts reaction. He later served as president of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology from 1898 to 1900.

Career

Crafts studied at Harvard and in Europe, working in Paris with Charles Friedel. In 1877 the two published their discovery of the Friedel–Crafts alkylation and acylation reactions, in which aluminum chloride catalyzes the substitution of an alkyl or acyl group onto an aromatic ring — a cornerstone of organic synthesis and industrial chemistry, including the manufacture of ethylbenzene and cumene.

Later work

Crafts also made important studies of the chemistry of iodine, of silicon compounds, and of the determination of the densities of elements at high temperatures. He returned to the United States in 1891 and taught at MIT, where he became president in 1898, before retiring to private research.
