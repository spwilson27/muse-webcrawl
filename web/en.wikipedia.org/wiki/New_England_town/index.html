New England town

The New England town is the basic unit of local government and local division of land in the six New England states of the United States: Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island and Vermont. Towns cover the entire territory of these states (unlike the unincorporated areas found elsewhere in the US) and perform functions similar to cities and counties elsewhere.

Government

Historically, towns were governed by open town meeting, in which all registered voters legislate directly, with day-to-day administration by an elected board of selectmen. Many larger towns have adopted representative town meetings or mayor/council governments. In Connecticut and parts of other states, counties have little or no governmental function, making towns the primary providers of local services.

Characteristics

Towns vary enormously in population, from a handful of residents to over 100,000. Unlike municipalities elsewhere, a New England town typically contains multiple named villages and settlements within a single municipal boundary, so the town's name may refer both to the municipality and to its principal village.
