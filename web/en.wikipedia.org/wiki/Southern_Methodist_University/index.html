Southern Methodist University (SMU) is a private research university in University Park and Dallas, Texas, United States. Founded in 1911 by the Methodist Episcopal Church, South, it opened in 1915 and is now nonsectarian in admissions while retaining its historic affiliation with the United Methodist Church. Its main campus is noted for Georgian Revival architecture, centred on Dallas Hall (1915), which is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Academics

SMU enrolls roughly 12,000 students across schools including Dedman College of Humanities and Sciences, the Cox School of Business, the Meadows School of the Arts, the Lyle School of Engineering, the Dedman School of Law and the Perkins School of Theology. The university hosts the George W. Bush Presidential Center, opened in 2013, containing the presidential library of George W. Bush.

Athletics

The SMU Mustangs compete in NCAA Division I. The football programme was famously given the "death penalty" by the NCAA in 1987 — cancellation of its season — for repeated recruiting violations, the most severe sanction ever imposed on a major programme; the team did not fully recover competitiveness for decades. The Mustangs joined the Atlantic Coast Conference in 2024 and reached the College Football Playoff in their first ACC season.
