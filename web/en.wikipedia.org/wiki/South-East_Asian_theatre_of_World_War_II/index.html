South-East Asian theatre of World War II

The South-East Asian theatre of World War II comprised the campaigns fought in Burma, India, Thailand, French Indochina, Malaya, Singapore, the Dutch East Indies and the Philippines' southern approaches, between the Japanese invasions of December 1941 and the surrender of Japan in September 1945. The Allies formed South East Asia Command (SEAC) in 1943 under Admiral Lord Louis Mountbatten to coordinate operations in the region.

Japanese conquests, 1941–42

Japan struck on 7–8 December 1941, invading Malaya and Thailand and bombing Pearl Harbor and Hong Kong. British, Indian and Australian forces were defeated in the Malayan campaign, culminating in the fall of Singapore on 15 February 1942 — the largest surrender in British military history, with some 80,000 troops captured. Japanese forces then conquered Burma, cutting the Burma Road supply route to China, and occupied the Dutch East Indies for its oil.

The Burma campaign

The longest British land campaign of the war followed. Orde Wingate's Chindits raided behind Japanese lines; American-led Chinese forces and Merrill's Marauders fought in the north, building the Ledo Road. In 1944 Japan's U-Go offensive toward India was defeated at the battles of Imphal and Kohima — the turning point in Burma. General William Slim's Fourteenth Army then reconquered Burma, recapturing Rangoon in May 1945.

End of the war

Allied plans for amphibious operations to liberate Malaya (Operation Zipper) were overtaken by Japan's surrender after the atomic bombings and Soviet entry into the war. Mountbatten accepted the formal Japanese surrender at Singapore on 12 September 1945. The war gravely weakened European colonial rule in the region, and independence movements in Indonesia, Vietnam, Burma and Malaya declared or fought for independence in its aftermath.
