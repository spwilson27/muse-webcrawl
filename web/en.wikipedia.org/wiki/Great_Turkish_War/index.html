Great Turkish War
Date14 July 1683 – 26 January 1699
LocationCentral and Eastern Europe, the Balkans
BelligerentsHoly League (Habsburgs, Poland-Lithuania, Venice, Russia, Papal States) vs Ottoman Empire
ResultHoly League victory; Treaty of Karlowitz (1699)

The Great Turkish War (German: Großer Türkenkrieg), also called the War of the Holy League, was a series of conflicts between the Ottoman Empire and the European powers of the Holy League — organised by Pope Innocent XI from 1684 and comprising the Habsburg Monarchy, the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Republic of Venice and, from 1686, the Tsardom of Russia. The war began with the Ottoman siege of Vienna in 1683 and ended with the Treaty of Karlowitz in 1699, marking the first major contraction of Ottoman power in Europe.

Course of the war

The siege of Vienna was broken on 12 September 1683 by a relief army under King John III Sobieski of Poland, whose winged hussars led one of the largest cavalry charges in history. The Holy League then went over to the offensive: imperial forces under Charles of Lorraine and Prince Eugene of Savoy recaptured Buda (1686) and Belgrade (1688), won at Mohács (1687) and crushed the Ottomans at Zenta (1697). Venice conquered the Morea (Peloponnese), while Russia captured Azov in 1696.

Treaty of Karlowitz

Signed on 26 January 1699, the treaty confirmed Austria in possession of Hungary and Transylvania, left the Morea to Venice, Podolia to Poland and Azov to Russia. Karlowitz is widely seen as the turning point after which the Habsburgs rose as the dominant power of Central Europe and the Ottoman Empire adopted a largely defensive posture in Europe.
