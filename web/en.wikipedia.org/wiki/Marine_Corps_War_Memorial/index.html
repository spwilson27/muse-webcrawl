Marine Corps War Memorial

The memorial at Arlington, Virginia

The United States Marine Corps War Memorial (also known as the Iwo Jima Memorial) is a national memorial located outside Arlington, Virginia, adjacent to Arlington National Cemetery. The memorial was dedicated in 1954 to all Marine Corps personnel who have died in the defense of the United States since 1775. Its design is based on the iconic photograph Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima.

History

The memorial depicts the second flag-raising on the summit of Mount Suribachi during the Battle of Iwo Jima on 23 February 1945, as photographed by Joe Rosenthal of the Associated Press. Rosenthal's image won the Pulitzer Prize and became one of the most reproduced photographs of the Second World War. Sculptor Felix de Weldon modelled the memorial from the photograph, working with the three surviving flag raisers — Rene Gagnon, Ira Hayes, and John Bradley — and the families of the other three, Michael Strank, Harlon Block, and Franklin Sousley.

The memorial

Cast in bronze, the figures stand 32 feet (9.8 metres) tall atop a granite base engraved with the names and dates of every principal Marine Corps engagement since the founding of the Corps. The flagpole flies a cloth United States flag 24 hours a day. President Dwight D. Eisenhower dedicated the memorial on 10 November 1954, the 179th anniversary of the Marine Corps. It is administered by the National Park Service.

Legacy

The memorial has become a landmark of the Washington area, the site of the weekly Marine Corps Sunset Parades in summer and of ceremonies honouring fallen service members. Replicas stand at the National Museum of the Marine Corps in Virginia and at Harlingen, Texas.
