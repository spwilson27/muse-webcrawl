Ford circle

In mathematics, a Ford circle is a circle in the upper half-plane associated with an irreducible fraction p/q (with q > 0), with center at (p/q, 1/(2q²)) and radius 1/(2q²). Ford circles are named after the American mathematician Lester R. Ford Sr., who introduced them in 1938.

Properties

Every Ford circle is tangent to the horizontal axis, and two distinct Ford circles are either disjoint or tangent to each other — they never cross. The Ford circle for p/q is tangent to those for its neighbors in Farey sequences. Ford circles can be viewed as the circles of curvature of the modular group acting on the upper half-plane, and they interrelate with Farey sequences, continued fractions, and Apollonian circle packings: between any two tangent Ford circles, infinitely many more can be inscribed.

Relation to Farey sequences

If p/q and p′/q′ are consecutive terms of a Farey sequence, their Ford circles are tangent, and the circle associated with their mediant (p+p′)/(q+q′) is tangent to both, fitting exactly in the curvilinear triangular gap between them and the axis.
