Abraham Robinson

Abraham Robinson (October 6, 1918 – April 11, 1974) was a German-American mathematician best known for the development of nonstandard analysis, a mathematically rigorous system whereby infinitesimal and infinite numbers were reincorporated into analysis. He also made major contributions to model theory and aerodynamics.

Biography

Robinson was born in Waldenburg, Germany (now Wałbrzych, Poland) to a Jewish family. In 1933 he emigrated to British Mandate Palestine, where he studied at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. During the Second World War he worked on aerodynamics in England at the Royal Aircraft Establishment at Farnborough. After the war he held positions in London, Toronto, Jerusalem and at the University of California, Los Angeles, before moving to Yale University in 1967, where he remained until his death.

Nonstandard analysis

In 1961 Robinson introduced nonstandard analysis, constructing a field of hyperreal numbers that extends the real numbers with infinitesimal and infinite elements. Using model theory — in particular the compactness theorem — he showed that statements about the reals could be transferred to the hyperreals, giving Leibniz's infinitesimals a rigorous foundation nearly three centuries after their introduction. His book Non-standard Analysis appeared in 1966.

Model theory

Robinson was one of the founders of modern model theory. He introduced the concept of model completeness and developed the theory of differentially closed fields, demonstrating how logic could be applied to algebra and analysis — a programme he called "formalism" applied to mathematics itself.
