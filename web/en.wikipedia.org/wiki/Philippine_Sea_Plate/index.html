Philippine Sea Plate

The Philippine Sea Plate or the Philippine Plate is a tectonic plate comprising oceanic lithosphere that lies beneath the Philippine Sea, to the east of the Philippines. Most segments of the Philippines, including northern Luzon, form part of the separate Philippine Mobile Belt rather than the plate itself. The Philippine Sea Plate is one of the largest plates composed predominantly of oceanic crust.

Boundaries

The plate is bordered mostly by convergent boundaries. To the east and southeast, the older, denser Pacific Plate subducts beneath it at the Izu–Bonin–Mariana system, forming the Mariana Trench, the deepest oceanic trench on Earth. To the west, the Philippine Sea Plate subducts beneath the Eurasian Plate along the Ryukyu Trench, the Nankai Trough, and the Philippine Trench system; Taiwan and the Philippine Mobile Belt lie in the complex collision zone between the two plates. To the south lies the Caroline Plate, and to the north the Amur and Okhotsk plates.

Features and motion

The plate contains several major submarine features, including the West Mariana Ridge, the Kyushu–Palau Ridge, the Shikoku and Parece Vela back-arc basins, and the Benham Rise. The plate is currently moving northwestward at roughly 6–9 centimetres per year relative to Eurasia, though its motion through time has included a substantial clockwise rotation and a more northerly direction earlier in the Cenozoic.

Geological history

The Philippine Sea Plate originated around 50 million years ago, and the Izu–Bonin–Mariana arc system began forming at about 48–49 million years ago, an initiation recorded by forearc basalts. Subduction around its margins has generated major earthquakes and volcanoes, including the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake on its northwestern boundary zone and the volcanoes of the Izu and Mariana islands, among them Iwo Jima.
