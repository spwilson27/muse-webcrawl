The Mid-Atlantic is a region of the United States located in the eastern part of the country. The United States Census Bureau defines the Middle Atlantic division as consisting of three states: New York, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania. In broader and historical usage the region often also includes Delaware, Maryland, Washington, D.C., and sometimes Virginia and West Virginia.

Geography and character

Stretching along the Atlantic seaboard between New England and the South, the Mid-Atlantic contains some of the nation's largest metropolitan areas — New York City, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Buffalo, and (in the broader definition) Baltimore and Washington, D.C. — alongside the Appalachian Mountains, the Great Lakes shore of New York and Pennsylvania, and the Chesapeake Bay. Historically it was the "breadbasket" of the early republic, settled by a diverse mix of Dutch, English, German, Scots-Irish, and other groups, and it hosted two early national capitals, New York and Philadelphia.

Economy and culture

The region is a centre of finance, media, higher education, pharmaceuticals, and manufacturing, and it forms the southern portion of the BosWash megalopolis. The Mid-Atlantic dialect and cuisine (such as the Philly cheesesteak and New York-style pizza) reflect its dense, immigrant-shaped cities.
