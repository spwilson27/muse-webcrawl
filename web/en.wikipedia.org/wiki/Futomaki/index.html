Futomaki

Futomaki (太巻き, "fat roll") is a thick type of Japanese makizushi (rolled sushi). A futomaki roll is typically about 5–6 centimetres (2–2.5 inches) in diameter and is made by rolling seasoned sushi rice and several fillings in a full sheet of nori seaweed, using a bamboo mat (makisu). It is cut into slices roughly 2 cm thick.

Fillings

Because of their size, futomaki contain multiple fillings chosen for flavour, texture, and colour — commonly tamagoyaki (sweet omelette), cucumber, kanpyō (dried gourd strips simmered in soy sauce and sugar), shiitake mushrooms, denbu (pink seasoned fish flakes), spinach, and sometimes tuna or eel. The arrangement of fillings can be designed so that the cross-section of each slice forms a decorative picture.

Ehomaki

Uncut futomaki are eaten as ehomaki ("lucky direction rolls") during the Setsubun festival in the Kansai region, a custom that spread nationwide: the whole roll is eaten in silence while facing the year's auspicious direction, for good luck.
