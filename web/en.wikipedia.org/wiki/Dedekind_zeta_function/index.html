Dedekind zeta function

In mathematics, the Dedekind zeta function of an algebraic number field K, generally denoted ζK(s), is a generalisation of the Riemann zeta function. It is a Dirichlet series defined, for complex arguments s with real part greater than one, as a sum over the nonzero ideals of the ring of integers of K:

ζK(s) = ΣI⊆OK (NK(I))−s,

where NK(I) denotes the absolute norm of the ideal I. It likewise admits an Euler product over the prime ideals of OK.

Properties

The Dedekind zeta function extends to a meromorphic function on the whole complex plane, with a single simple pole at s = 1. The residue at that pole is given by the analytic class number formula, which relates it to fundamental arithmetic invariants of the field: the class number, the regulator, the number of roots of unity, and the discriminant.

When K is the field of rational numbers, ζK is simply the Riemann zeta function. For abelian extensions of the rationals, the Dedekind zeta function factors as a product of Dirichlet L-functions. The function satisfies a functional equation relating its values at s and 1 − s, completed by gamma factors depending on the numbers of real and complex embeddings of K.

Special values

Values of Dedekind zeta functions at negative integers are rational numbers connected to higher K-theory through conjectures of Lichtenbaum and the theorems building on work of Borel and others. The behaviour near s = 0 is described by the Stark conjectures.
