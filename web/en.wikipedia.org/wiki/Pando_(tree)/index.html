Pando (Latin for "I spread"), also known as the Trembling Giant, is a clonal colony of a single male quaking aspen (Populus tremuloides) tree in Fishlake National Forest, Utah. All of its roughly 47,000 stems (trunks) arise from a single, massive root system and are genetically identical, making Pando a single organism — one of the largest and oldest known living organisms on Earth.

Description

The colony covers about 43 hectares near Fish Lake. Individual stems average about 130 years of age, rarely exceeding 150 years, but the root system is far older: estimates of the clone's age range from several thousand to as much as 80,000 years, having possibly originated as a seedling after the last glacial period. Genetic studies have confirmed that the stems constitute a single clone, with somatic mutations accumulating over its long life.

Threats and conservation

Since the late 20th century scientists have documented a decline in Pando's regeneration: mature stems are dying faster than new suckers replace them, largely because mule deer and cattle browse young shoots, while drought and disease add stress. Fencing of portions of the grove has allowed some regrowth to survive, and Pando has become a flagship example in debates about managing clonal organisms and the effects of herbivore pressure on aspen ecosystems.
