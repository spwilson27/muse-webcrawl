Dalbergia

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Fabales
Family: Fabaceae
Subfamily: Faboideae
Genus: Dalbergia L.f.

Dalbergia is a large genus of small to medium-size trees, shrubs and lianas in the pea family, Fabaceae, widely distributed in tropical regions of Central and South America, Africa, Madagascar and southern Asia. The genus has a fossil record extending from the late Eocene of the United States to the late Pliocene of Italy.

Rosewoods

Many species of Dalbergia are important timber trees valued for their fragrant, richly coloured wood, known generally as rosewood. The best-known are Brazilian rosewood (Dalbergia nigra), Honduran rosewood, East Indian rosewood (Dalbergia latifolia) and cocobolo (Dalbergia retusa). Rosewoods are used for fine furniture, musical instruments such as guitar backs and sides, chess pieces and veneer.

Conservation

Over-exploitation has driven many species toward endangerment. In 2017, all species of Dalbergia were listed under CITES Appendix II, requiring export permits for international trade, in an effort to control one of the most heavily trafficked wildlife commodities — rosewood furniture driving extensive illegal logging, particularly in Madagascar, West Africa and Southeast Asia.

Notable species

Notable species include Dalbergia sissoo (Indian rosewood or sheesham), Dalbergia melanoxylon (African blackwood, prized for clarinets and oboes), Dalbergia oliveri (Burmese rosewood) and Dalbergia stevensonii (Honduras rosewood, used for marimba bars).
