The Nesher ("eagle") was Israel's first indigenously produced jet fighter.

The IAI Nesher (Hebrew: נשר, "griffon vulture" or "eagle") was a multirole fighter aircraft built in Israel by Israel Aircraft Industries. It was an unlicensed copy of the French Dassault Mirage 5, produced after France embargoed the 50 Mirage 5J aircraft Israel had ordered and paid for following the Six-Day War of 1967. Israeli agents obtained the Mirage 5's manufacturing plans — famously, the Swiss engineer Alfred Frauenknecht was convicted of passing tens of thousands of technical drawings to Israel — allowing IAI to build the aircraft itself.

Design and development

The Nesher closely followed the Mirage 5, a simplified, ground-attack-optimised derivative of the Mirage III with additional fuel in place of radar avionics. IAI fitted some Israeli-made components and ejection seats. The first Israeli-built aircraft flew in 1969, and deliveries to the Israeli Air Force began in 1971; 61 were built in total.

Service

Neshers flew extensively in the 1973 Yom Kippur War in both air-to-air and strike roles; Israeli sources credit the type with over 100 aerial victories for relatively few losses. After the war the fleet was progressively replaced by the more advanced IAI Kfir, itself developed from the Nesher/Mirage line with a General Electric J79 engine. Surplus Neshers were sold to Argentina in the late 1970s, where, renamed Dagger, they saw combat in the 1982 Falklands War, flying long-range strike missions against the British task force; Argentina later upgraded surviving aircraft as the Finger.
