Alim Hamza oghlu Qasimov (born 14 August 1957) is an Azerbaijani musician and one of the most celebrated contemporary singers of mugham, the traditional modal art music of Azerbaijan. His impassioned, improvisatory singing — often performed with his daughter Farghana Qasimova — has brought mugham to concert halls around the world.

Life and career

Qasimov grew up in the village of Nabur near Shamakhi, where his parents worked on a collective farm; they encouraged his singing after recognising his exceptional voice. He studied at the Asaf Zeynally Music School and the Azerbaijan State Institute of Arts, graduating in 1989, and trained under leading khanandas including Hajibaba Huseynov. He began performing professionally in the early 1980s and won recognition at Azerbaijani music festivals.

International recognition

In 1999 Qasimov received the International Music Council–UNESCO Music Prize, awarded to musicians who have made outstanding contributions to musical life. He has recorded for European labels including the albums The Legendary Art of Mugham and collaborations with the Silkroad Ensemble of Yo-Yo Ma. His duets with his daughter Farghana, featuring the tar of his ensemble, became his signature international format. Mugham itself was proclaimed by UNESCO a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2003 (inscribed 2008).

Style

Qasimov's singing is marked by extreme vocal range, intense emotional delivery and daring improvisation within the mugham modes. He accompanies himself on the daf (frame drum) and is credited by critics with revitalising mugham for younger audiences while remaining rooted in classical form.
