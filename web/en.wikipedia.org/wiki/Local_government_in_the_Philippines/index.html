Local government in the Philippines

The Philippines is divided into local government units (LGUs) arranged in tiers: provinces, cities and municipalities, and barangays. Local autonomy is guaranteed by the 1987 Constitution and implemented through the Local Government Code of 1991 (Republic Act No. 7160), which devolved substantial powers, functions, and resources from the national government to LGUs.

Local government in the Philippines
Governing law: Local Government Code of 1991 (RA 7160)
Tiers: Provinces; cities; municipalities; barangays
Provinces: 82
Cities: 149
Municipalities: 1,493
Barangays: 42,001 (approx.)

Structure

Provinces are headed by a governor and a provincial board (Sangguniang Panlalawigan). Cities and municipalities are led by a mayor and a city or municipal council. The barangay, the smallest unit, is governed by a punong barangay (barangay captain) and a Sangguniang Barangay, with an elected Sangguniang Kabataan for youth representation. All local officials are elected for three-year terms and may serve at most three consecutive terms in the same office.

Classifications

Cities are classified as highly urbanized, independent component, or component cities, based on income, population, and charter provisions. Highly urbanized and independent component cities are administratively independent of their provinces and vote for their own congressional representatives.

Finance

LGUs receive a share of national taxes — historically the Internal Revenue Allotment, renamed the National Tax Allotment following the Supreme Court's Mandanas ruling, which enlarged the LGU share base to include customs duties — alongside locally levied real property and business taxes.

Regions and special areas

Above the provincial level, the national government groups LGUs into administrative regions (17 in total, including the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao, the country's only autonomous region with its own parliament).
