Classic female blues

Classic female blues was an early form of blues music popular in the United States in the 1920s. Unlike the rural, guitar-based country blues, it was an urban, theatrical style: professional women singers performed on the vaudeville and TOBA circuits and in theatres, backed by small jazz bands or pianists, and they were among the first blues artists to be recorded.

History

The craze began in 1920 when Mamie Smith recorded "Crazy Blues" for Okeh Records; its success persuaded record companies that there was a large Black market for "race records". Ma Rainey, the "Mother of the Blues", and her protégée Bessie Smith, the "Empress of the Blues", became the genre's biggest stars, alongside Alberta Hunter, Ethel Waters, Ida Cox, Victoria Spivey and Sippie Wallace. Their songs dealt frankly with love, independence, poverty, drinking and injustice.

The classic blues boom faded by the end of the 1920s as tastes changed and the Great Depression hit record sales, but the style laid the foundations for rhythm and blues and rock and roll, and its singers remain towering figures in American music.
