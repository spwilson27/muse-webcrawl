Mitogen-activated protein kinase

A mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK or MAP kinase) is a type of serine/threonine-specific protein kinase involved in directing cellular responses to a diverse array of stimuli, such as mitogens, osmotic stress, heat shock and proinflammatory cytokines. MAPKs regulate fundamental cellular processes including gene expression, mitosis, differentiation, proliferation, cell survival and apoptosis. They are found only in eukaryotes and form the terminal tier of three-kinase signalling cascades.

Activation and cascades

MAPKs are activated within MAP kinase cascades: a MAP kinase kinase kinase (MAPKKK, e.g. Raf) phosphorylates and activates a MAP kinase kinase (MAPKK, e.g. MEK), which in turn phosphorylates the MAPK on both a threonine and a tyrosine residue in its activation loop (a characteristic Thr-X-Tyr motif). Once activated, MAPKs phosphorylate target proteins — including transcription factors such as Elk-1 and c-Jun, other kinases, and cytoskeletal proteins — in the cytoplasm and nucleus. Signal termination is achieved by dual-specificity phosphatases (DUSPs/MKPs) that remove both phosphates.

Families

  • ERK (extracellular signal-regulated kinases) — ERK1/2 are activated mainly by growth factors and mitogens via the Ras–Raf–MEK pathway and promote proliferation and differentiation. Mutations that overactivate this pathway are among the most common in cancer.
  • JNK (c-Jun N-terminal kinases) — JNK1–3 respond to stress stimuli such as UV radiation, heat shock and inflammatory cytokines, and regulate apoptosis and immune responses.
  • p38 MAPKs — p38α–δ are likewise stress-activated, playing central roles in inflammation and cytokine production, and have been targets of anti-inflammatory drug development.
  • Atypical MAPKs — including ERK5 (BMK1), ERK3/4 and ERK7/8, which lack the canonical activation motif or activation pattern.

Clinical significance

Because the Ras–Raf–MEK–ERK cascade is hyperactivated in a large fraction of human cancers (through mutations in Ras, BRAF or other components), its kinases are important drug targets. Inhibitors of BRAF (vemurafenib, dabrafenib) and MEK (trametinib, cobimetinib) are approved cancer therapies, particularly in melanoma. MAPK signalling also contributes to inflammatory diseases, neurodegeneration and developmental syndromes known as RASopathies (such as Noonan syndrome), caused by germline mutations in pathway components.

Evolution

MAPK cascades are ancient and conserved across eukaryotes: yeast use them in mating-pheromone and osmosensing pathways (Fus3, Hog1), and plants possess numerous MAPKs involved in stress and immunity.
