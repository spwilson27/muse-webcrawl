Government of National Accord

The Government of National Accord (GNA; Arabic: حكومة الوفاق الوطني) was the interim government of Libya established under the Libyan Political Agreement signed in Skhirat, Morocco, on 17 December 2015 and endorsed by the United Nations. Led by Prime Minister Fayez al-Sarraj, who chaired its Presidency Council, the GNA arrived in Tripoli in March 2016 and was recognised internationally as Libya's legitimate government, but it never secured the endorsement of the rival Tobruk-based House of Representatives and faced an armed rival administration in the east backed by Khalifa Haftar's Libyan National Army. It governed until March 2021, when it handed power to the Government of National Unity.

Formation

After the 2014 elections Libya split between a Tripoli-based government and the internationally recognised House of Representatives in Tobruk. UN-brokered talks produced the Libyan Political Agreement, creating a nine-member Presidency Council and the GNA. When al-Sarraj sailed into Tripoli's Abu Sittah naval base on 30 March 2016, rival factions initially stood aside, and the GNA took over ministries and the central bank.

Rule and conflict

The GNA, supported by Misratan and western militias, led the 2016 campaign that expelled the Islamic State from Sirte with US air support. Its authority remained contested: the House of Representatives refused to approve its cabinet, and in April 2019 Haftar launched an offensive on Tripoli. With Turkish military support from late 2019 the GNA repelled the assault, a ceasefire followed in October 2020, and the Libyan Political Dialogue Forum selected a unified executive. On 10 March 2021 the GNA ceded power to the Government of National Unity under Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh.
