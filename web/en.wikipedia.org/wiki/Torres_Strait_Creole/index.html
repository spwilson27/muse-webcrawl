Torres Strait Creole (known to its speakers as Yumplatok, from "you-me fellow talk", and also called Pizin) is an English-based creole language spoken in the Torres Strait and on northern Cape York Peninsula in Queensland, Australia. It is the lingua franca of the Torres Strait Islands and is spoken by many Torres Strait Islanders and Aboriginal people of the region as a first or additional language.

History

The creole developed during the 19th century from the Pacific pidgin English used in the maritime industries — pearling, bêche-de-mer (trepang) fishing and sandalwood trading — which brought together Islanders, Aboriginal people, Europeans, Pacific Islanders, and Asian workers. Missionary activity from 1871 and the pearling fleets spread the language across the islands. Over generations it nativised into a full creole, stabilising in the early 20th century.

Varieties

Torres Strait Creole has regional varieties reflecting substrate languages, principally the Western-Central Torres Strait language (Kala Lagaw Ya) and the Eastern Torres Strait language (Meriam Mir), as well as a variety spoken on Cape York. Linguists sometimes describe "Broken" as the more basilectal (pidgin-like) end of a continuum and Yumplatok as the developed creole.

Features

Like its sister Pacific pidgins and creoles — Tok Pisin of Papua New Guinea, Bislama of Vanuatu and Pijin of Solomon Islands — Torres Strait Creole distinguishes inclusive and exclusive "we" (yumi vs mipla/yumpla distinctions by dialect), marks plurals and tense-aspect with separate particles (e.g. bin for past), and draws vocabulary mainly from English with words from Islander languages and other contact languages. An example: Wa'ia? — "How is it? / What's up?"

Status and use

The language is used in everyday life, local media, churches, and increasingly in print and broadcasting; a translation of the Bible and other materials exist in the language. It is distinct from both standard Australian English and from the traditional Indigenous languages of the Strait, which continue to be maintained alongside it.
