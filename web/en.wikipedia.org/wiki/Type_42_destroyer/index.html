Type 42 destroyer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Class overview
Operators: Royal Navy; Argentine Navy
Built: 1972–1985
Completed: 16 (14 UK, 2 Argentina)
Displacement: about 3,850–5,350 tonnes depending on batch
Armament: Sea Dart SAM system, 4.5-inch Mk 8 gun, Lynx helicopter

The Type 42 or Sheffield class was a class of guided-missile destroyers built for the Royal Navy and, in a pair of ships, the Argentine Navy. Designed in the late 1960s to provide area air defence for the fleet, they were armed principally with the Sea Dart surface-to-air missile system. A total of sixteen ships were built in three batches; the stretched Batch 3 ships were longer and more capable than the earlier vessels.

Design

The Type 42 was designed around the Sea Dart twin-arm launcher mounted forward, a 4.5-inch Mark 8 gun, and a flight deck and hangar for a Westland Lynx helicopter. Propulsion was by Rolls-Royce Olympus and Tyne gas turbines in a combined gas-or-gas arrangement. Cost constraints imposed by the government of the day forced a smaller, cheaper hull than the cancelled Type 82 design, which limited magazine capacity and seakeeping in the first two batches; the Batch 3 ships were lengthened by about 14 metres to correct some of these shortcomings.

Service history

Five Type 42s served in the Falklands War of 1982. HMS Sheffield was hit by an Exocet missile on 4 May 1982 and sank under tow six days later, the first Royal Navy warship sunk in action since the Second World War. HMS Coventry was bombed and sunk on 25 May. The losses exposed weaknesses in close-in defence that led to Phalanx CIWS and other improvements being fitted across the fleet. The two Argentine ships, built in Britain and Argentina respectively, also served in the war on the opposing side, although mechanical problems kept them largely in port.

Replacement

The class was progressively withdrawn from Royal Navy service between 1999 and 2013 and replaced by the Type 45 Daring-class destroyers, whose Sea Viper system succeeded Sea Dart in the air-defence role.
