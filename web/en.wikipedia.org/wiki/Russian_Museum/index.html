The State Russian Museum (Russian: Государственный Русский музей), formerly the Russian Museum of His Imperial Majesty Alexander III, is the world's largest depository of Russian fine art. It is also one of the largest museums in the country, located in Saint Petersburg.

History

The museum was established on 13 April 1895, following the coronation of Emperor Nicholas II, in honour of his father Alexander III, who had conceived the idea of a national museum of Russian art. The first collection was built up from works transferred from the Hermitage, the Imperial Academy of Arts, and the imperial palaces, and it opened to the public in 1898 in the Neoclassical Mikhailovsky Palace, designed by Carlo Rossi. Following the Russian Revolution, many nationalised private collections were added, vastly expanding the holdings.

Collection

The museum's holdings span the whole history of Russian visual art from the tenth century to the present: medieval icons (including works attributed to Andrei Rublev's school), eighteenth-century portraiture, and the great nineteenth-century canvases of Karl Bryullov (The Last Day of Pompeii), Ilya Repin (Barge Haulers on the Volga), Ivan Aivazovsky (The Ninth Wave), and Vasily Surikov. Its collection of the Russian avant-garde — Malevich, Kandinsky, Chagall, Filonov — is among the finest anywhere. The museum complex also includes the Marble Palace, St Michael's Castle (the Engineers' Castle), the Stroganov Palace, the Benois Wing, the Summer Garden with the Summer Palace of Peter I, and Peter I's Cabin.
