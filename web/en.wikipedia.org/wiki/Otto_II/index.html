Otto II

Otto II (955 – 7 December 983), called the Red (der Rote), was Holy Roman Emperor from 973 until his death in 983. The son and successor of Otto the Great, he had been crowned co-emperor in 967 and king of Germany in 961 during his father's lifetime.

As emperor, Otto II consolidated imperial authority in Germany, facing rebellions including the revolt of Henry II of Bavaria ("the Quarrelsome"), and pursued his father's Italian ambitions. In 972 he married the Byzantine princess Theophanu, strengthening ties with Constantinople. His attempt to conquer Byzantine southern Italy ended in disaster at the Battle of Stilo (982), where his army was destroyed by Muslim forces from Sicily in alliance against him; the defeat weakened imperial prestige and encouraged the great Slav uprising of 983 east of the Elbe.

Otto II died of malarial fever in Rome in 983, aged 28, leaving the throne to his three-year-old son Otto III under the regency of Theophanu and his mother Adelaide of Italy.
