History of Brazil

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Portuguese landing of 1500 began Brazil's colonial era.

The history of Brazil begins with the habitation of the territory by Indigenous peoples thousands of years before the arrival of Pedro Álvares Cabral's Portuguese fleet in 1500. Portuguese colonisation, built on sugar, gold and coffee and on the largest system of African slavery in the Americas, lasted until independence in 1822. Brazil then became an empire under the Braganza monarchy, a republic in 1889, and passed through populist, military and democratic regimes in the 20th and 21st centuries.

Colonial Brazil (1500–1822)

Portugal initially exploited brazilwood, then organised sugar plantations in the northeast worked by enslaved Africans — Brazil received roughly five million enslaved people, more than any other country. Gold discoveries in Minas Gerais in the 1690s shifted the colony's centre southward; Rio de Janeiro became capital in 1763. In 1808 the Portuguese court, fleeing Napoleon, transferred to Rio, making Brazil the seat of the empire.

Empire and abolition (1822–1889)

Dom Pedro I declared independence on 7 September 1822. His son Pedro II reigned from 1840 to 1889, a period of territorial consolidation, coffee expansion, and gradual abolition of slavery, completed by the Golden Law of 1888 — the last abolition in the Americas. Discontent among planters and the military led to the proclamation of the republic on 15 November 1889.

Republic, Vargas era and military rule

The Old Republic (1889–1930) was dominated by coffee oligarchies of São Paulo and Minas Gerais. Getúlio Vargas, in power 1930–1945 and again 1951–1954, industrialised the country and created its labour code. After democratic decades that produced Brasília (1960) under Juscelino Kubitschek, a 1964 coup installed a military dictatorship lasting until 1985, marked by repression and an "economic miracle".

New Republic

The 1988 constitution restored democracy. Fernando Henrique Cardoso's Real Plan (1994) ended hyperinflation; Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (2003–2010) expanded social programmes during a commodity boom. The following decade brought recession, the impeachment of Dilma Rousseff in 2016, the presidency of Jair Bolsonaro (2019–2022), and Lula's return in 2023.
