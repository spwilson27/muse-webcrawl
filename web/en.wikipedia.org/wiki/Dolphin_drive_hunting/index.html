Dolphin drive hunting
Locations: Japan (Taiji), Faroe Islands, Solomon Islands
Method: Herding cetaceans into coves
Notable coverage: The Cove (2009)

Dolphin drive hunting, also called drive fishing, is a method of hunting dolphins and other small cetaceans by driving them together with boats, usually into a bay or onto a beach, where they are slaughtered for meat or captured alive for sale to aquariums and marine parks.

By country

In Taiji, Japan, an annual drive hunt from September to March targets species including bottlenose and Risso's dolphins under government quota. In the Faroe Islands, the grindadráp drives long-finned pilot whales. Drive hunts also historically occurred in the Solomon Islands.

Controversy

The practice is criticised by animal welfare organisations and some governments for cruelty, sustainability, and high mercury levels in dolphin meat. The 2009 documentary The Cove brought international attention to Taiji. Defenders frame the hunts as traditional subsistence and pest control. See also whaling.
