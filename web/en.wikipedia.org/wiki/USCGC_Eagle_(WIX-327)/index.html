USCGC Eagle (WIX-327)

USCGC Eagle (WIX-327) is a 295-foot (90 m) barque and training ship of the United States Coast Guard. She is one of only two active commissioned sailing vessels in U.S. military service, along with USS Constitution, and she serves as the training vessel for cadets at the United States Coast Guard Academy in New London, Connecticut. Known as "America's Tall Ship," Eagle makes annual training cruises and goodwill voyages around the Atlantic.

German origins

The ship was built in 1936 at the Blohm & Voss shipyard in Hamburg as the German Navy training barque Horst Wessel, the second of the Gorch Fock-class sail training ships. She survived World War II and was taken by the United States as a war reparation, being commissioned into the Coast Guard on 15 May 1946 and renamed Eagle.

Service

Each summer, Eagle embarks roughly 150 Coast Guard Academy cadets and officer candidates for training cruises lasting several weeks, teaching seamanship, navigation, leadership, and teamwork under sail. She carries more than 21,000 square feet of sail on three masts and a crew augmented by trainees who work the rigging aloft. Eagle regularly participates in tall ships festivals and Operation Sail events, and she has crossed the Atlantic many times, visiting ports throughout Europe and the Americas.

Characteristics

The steel-hulled barque displaces about 1,800 tons, has a sparred length of 295 feet, and is also equipped with a diesel engine for auxiliary propulsion. Her hull designation WIX-327 reflects her role as an unclassified training vessel.
