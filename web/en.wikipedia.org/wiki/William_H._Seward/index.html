William H. Seward

Born: 16 May 1801, Florida, New York
Died: 10 October 1872, Auburn, New York
Offices: Governor of New York (1839–1842); US Senator (1849–1861); Secretary of State (1861–1869)
Known for: Alaska Purchase ("Seward's Folly")

William Henry Seward (May 16, 1801 – October 10, 1872) was an American politician who served as United States Secretary of State from 1861 to 1869, under Presidents Abraham Lincoln and Andrew Johnson. A leading anti-slavery figure, he is best remembered for negotiating the purchase of Alaska from Russia in 1867.

Early career

Seward practised law in Auburn, New York, and rose through the Anti-Masonic and Whig parties to become Governor of New York in 1839. As a US senator from 1849 he became a prominent Republican voice against the spread of slavery, speaking of a "higher law" than the Constitution and predicting an "irrepressible conflict" over the institution.

Secretary of State

Defeated by Lincoln for the 1860 Republican nomination, Seward served loyally as Secretary of State. During the Civil War he worked to prevent European recognition of the Confederacy. On the night of Lincoln's assassination in April 1865, Seward was himself attacked and seriously wounded at home by Lewis Powell, a co-conspirator of John Wilkes Booth.

Alaska Purchase

In 1867 Seward negotiated the purchase of Alaska from the Russian Empire for $7.2 million. Mocked at the time as "Seward's Folly" and "Seward's Icebox", the acquisition was later recognised as a major strategic and resource gain. Seward retired in 1869 and died in Auburn in 1872.
