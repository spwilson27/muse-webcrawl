Eugene Borisovich Dynkin (Russian: Евгений Борисович Дынкин; 11 May 1924 – 14 November 2014) was a Soviet and American mathematician. He made contributions to the fields of probability and algebra, especially semisimple Lie groups, Lie algebras, and Markov processes. The Dynkin diagram, the Dynkin system, and Dynkin's lemma are named after him.

Biography

Dynkin was born in Leningrad in 1924. His father was arrested during the Great Purge in 1937 and perished in the Gulag. Despite the difficulties faced by the son of an "enemy of the people", Dynkin was admitted to Moscow State University in 1940, where he studied under Andrey Kolmogorov and Israel Gelfand. He completed his doctorate in 1948 and became a professor at Moscow University, though his career advancement was hampered for political reasons and because of his Jewish background.

From 1968 Dynkin worked at the Central Economic Mathematical Institute of the Soviet Academy of Sciences, where he applied probability theory to economics. In 1977 he emigrated to the United States and became a professor at Cornell University, where he remained for the rest of his career. He became a US citizen and was elected to the National Academy of Sciences. In 1993 he received the Leroy P. Steele Prize of the American Mathematical Society for his cumulative research in probability theory and Lie algebras.

Mathematical work

As a student, Dynkin introduced the diagrammatic classification device now known as the Dynkin diagram, which he used to classify semisimple Lie algebras and which became a ubiquitous tool in algebra and geometry. His later work centred on probability theory: he developed the theory of Markov processes, proved fundamental results on boundary behaviour and superprocesses (measure-valued diffusions, sometimes called super-Brownian motion), and his "Dynkin formula" and characteristic operator remain standard tools of stochastic analysis. The Dynkin system (or λ-system) and the π-λ theorem are named for his work in measure theory.
