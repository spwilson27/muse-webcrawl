Hardening off

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hardening off is the process in horticulture of gradually acclimatizing plants raised indoors or under protection to outdoor conditions before they are transplanted into their final positions. Plants grown in a warm, sheltered, evenly watered environment have tender growth that can be damaged by direct sun, wind, and temperature swings; hardening off toughens them so they can survive outside.

Process

Typically, over a period of one to two weeks, the plants are placed outdoors for increasing lengths of time each day — beginning with an hour or two in shade and shelter — and brought back inside at night. Exposure to sunlight, breeze, and cooler temperatures is increased progressively. The plant responds by thickening cell walls, increasing cuticle thickness and root development, and adjusting its stomata and metabolism. Watering is often slightly reduced during the process.

Related concepts

In forestry and cold-climate physiology, "hardening" also describes the seasonal process by which trees and shrubs develop cold hardiness in autumn, partly through the hormone abscisic acid. Failure to harden off tender seedlings is a common cause of transplant shock and losses to late frost.
