Rendering (food processing)

Rendering is a process that converts waste animal tissue into stable, purified materials such as fats and protein meals. In food processing, rendering refers specifically to the gentle melting of fat from raw fatty tissue — for example, turning pork fat into lard, beef fat into tallow, or clarifying butter — by heating, which separates the pure fat from water and solids.

Methods

Wet rendering boils or steams the fatty material in water; the melted fat floats and is skimmed off. Dry rendering heats the material in a pan or jacketed vessel, allowing moisture to evaporate; the residual solids (cracklings or greaves) are pressed to extract remaining fat. Low, slow heat prevents scorching and yields a cleaner flavour.

Products and uses

Rendered fats include lard, tallow, schmaltz (chicken or goose fat) and clarified butter/ghee. Industrial rendering on a much larger scale processes slaughterhouse by-products into tallow, meat and bone meal, and other materials used in soaps, cosmetics, animal feed and biodiesel; this industry was central to managing the BSE ("mad cow disease") crisis, after which regulations restricted feeding rendered ruminant protein back to ruminants.
