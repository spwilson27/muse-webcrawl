Cooper Car Company

IndustryAutomotive, motor racing
Founded1947
FoundersCharles Cooper and John Cooper
HeadquartersSurbiton, Surrey, England

The Cooper Car Company was a British motorsport team and car manufacturer founded in 1947 in Surbiton, Surrey, by Charles Cooper and his son John Cooper. Cooper revolutionised Formula One in the late 1950s by placing the engine behind the driver, a mid-engine layout that became universal in single-seater racing. The team won the Formula One Constructors' Championship in 1959 and 1960, with Jack Brabham taking the Drivers' Championship in both years.

Origins

Charles Cooper ran a garage in Surbiton specialising in racing cars. After the Second World War he and his son John began building small single-seaters powered by 500 cc motorcycle engines for the new Formula 3 category. The lightweight chain-driven Cooper 500s were inexpensive and successful, and early drivers included Stirling Moss and a young Jack Brabham.

Rear-engine revolution

Cooper's Formula Two and Formula One cars of the 1950s placed the engine behind the driver, mainly because the original 500 cc cars were chain-driven. When the T51 of 1959, powered by a Coventry Climax engine, won the World Championship, the advantages of the layout — better traction, balance and aerodynamics — became undeniable, and rival constructors rapidly converted. The Cooper T53 continued the success in 1960.

Mini Cooper

John Cooper also persuaded the British Motor Corporation to let him develop a performance version of the Mini. The Mini Cooper, launched in 1961, and the later Cooper S became famous rally cars, winning the Monte Carlo Rally in 1964, 1965 and 1967.

Decline

Charles Cooper died in 1964 and the team's competitiveness faded as larger manufacturers dominated Formula One. The team closed in 1969, but the Cooper name remains synonymous with the rear-engine revolution and with the Mini Cooper.
