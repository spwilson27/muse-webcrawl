Landnámabók

Landnámabók (Icelandic for "The Book of Settlements") is a medieval Icelandic work that describes in considerable detail the settlement (landnám) of Iceland by Norsemen in the 9th and 10th centuries AD.

Content

The book lists more than 3,000 people and 1,400 settlements, naming the settlers (landnámsmenn), where they settled, and their descendants. It begins with the legendary first settler Ingólfr Arnarson, who founded Reykjavík, and proceeds district by district around the country. Many of the Icelandic sagas presuppose the genealogical information given in Landnámabók.

Versions and origins

The work is thought to originate in the 12th century, possibly with Ari Þorgilsson the Learned. Five versions survive in whole or part, including the Sturlubók, Hauksbók and Melabók redactions. It remains a fundamental source for the history and genealogy of early Iceland.
