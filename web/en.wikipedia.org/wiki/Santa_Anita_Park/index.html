Santa Anita Park is a thoroughbred horse racing track in Arcadia, California, United States. Opened on Christmas Day 1934, it is one of the most celebrated racetracks in North America, famous for its Art Deco architecture, its setting against the San Gabriel Mountains, and as the home of the legendary racehorse Seabiscuit, who won the Santa Anita Handicap there in 1940 in his final race.

History

The track was built on land that had been part of the Rancho Santa Anita of pioneer Elias J. "Lucky" Baldwin, who had operated an earlier Santa Anita track nearby from 1907 to 1909. The current facility was developed by a syndicate led by movie producer Hal Roach and Dr. Charles H. Strub, after California re-legalised pari-mutuel wagering in 1933. Its streamlined moderne grandstand, designed by Gordon B. Kaufmann, became an architectural landmark.

During the Second World War, the track grounds were used as the Santa Anita Assembly Center, one of the largest temporary detention centres for Japanese Americans under Executive Order 9066; some 18,000 people passed through it in 1942, many housed in converted horse stables, before transfer to permanent internment camps. Racing resumed after the war.

Racing

Santa Anita hosts the Santa Anita Derby, a major prep race for the Kentucky Derby, the Santa Anita Handicap ("the Big 'Cap"), one of the richest older-horse races in America, and the Sunshine Millions and California Cup for state-breds. It has hosted the Breeders' Cup World Championships a record number of times — ten through 2019 — including the 2019 meeting. The park also served as the equestrian venue for the 1984 Los Angeles Olympic Games.

The track's main dirt course is one mile; its turf course is noted for a unique downhill chute used for sprint races of about 6½ furlongs, crossing the dirt track. A statue of Seabiscuit stands in the walking ring, and the horse's story, and the track's, were retold in the 2003 film Seabiscuit, much of it filmed on location.
