Swiss franc

The Swiss franc (sign: CHF; German: Schweizer Franken, French: franc suisse, Italian: franco svizzero, Romansh: franc svizzer) is the currency and legal tender of Switzerland and Liechtenstein, and is also accepted in the Italian exclave of Campione d'Italia. It is issued by the Swiss National Bank (SNB). One franc is divided into 100 centimes (French), Rappen (German) or centesimi (Italian). CHF is the ISO currency code; the abbreviation Fr. and the symbol SFr. are also used.

History

The franc was introduced in 1850, replacing the myriad cantonal currencies, and was initially at par with the French franc as a member of the Latin Monetary Union. Switzerland's coinage — minted with a standing Helvetia or the famous William Tell-bust 5-franc piece — has remained remarkably stable in design. The franc is regarded as a safe-haven currency: during the European debt crisis, massive inflows forced the SNB in September 2011 to impose a minimum exchange rate of CHF 1.20 per euro, a cap it controversially abandoned on 15 January 2015, causing the franc to surge.

Coins and banknotes

Coins circulate in denominations of 5, 10 and 20 centimes and ½, 1, 2 and 5 francs. The eighth banknote series (1995–2021) depicted Swiss cultural figures; the ninth series, introduced from 2016, is themed on Swiss characteristics, with notes of 10, 20, 50, 100, 200 and 1000 francs — the high-value 1000-franc note being one of the world's most valuable circulating banknotes.
