Official languages of the United Nations

The UN conducts its work in six official languages, with simultaneous interpretation at meetings.

There are six official languages of the United Nations: Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, and Spanish. These languages are used at meetings of the various UN organs and are the languages into which official documents are translated. Delegates may speak in any of the six and are interpreted simultaneously into the others.

History

Five languages were chosen when the UN was founded in 1945, reflecting the victorious great powers of the Second World War. Arabic was added by the General Assembly in 1973 and extended to the Security Council in 1982. English and French are the two working languages of the UN Secretariat. The choice of Chinese shifted in practice from Traditional characters and the Republic of China to the People's Republic of China after 1971, with documents now issued in Simplified Chinese.

Language days and multilingualism

The UN observes a language day for each official language to celebrate multilingualism and cultural diversity. Proposals to add further languages — such as Hindi, Bengali, Portuguese, or Turkish — have been raised periodically but face the substantial cost of translation and interpretation services. The UN's Department for General Assembly and Conference Management runs the interpretation and translation services, and the organisation's multilingual website and the UN iLibrary publish in the six languages.
