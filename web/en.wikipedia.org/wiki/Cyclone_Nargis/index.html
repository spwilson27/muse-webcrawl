Cyclone Nargis

Cyclone Nargis approaching Myanmar in May 2008.

Extremely Severe Cyclonic Storm Nargis was a strong tropical cyclone that caused the worst natural disaster in the recorded history of Myanmar during early May 2008. The cyclone made landfall in the Ayeyarwady Delta region on 2 May 2008, sending a storm surge up to 40 kilometres inland and inundating the low-lying rice-growing region. Official figures put the death toll at about 84,500 killed with some 53,800 more missing — a total commonly cited as roughly 138,000 fatalities.

Meteorological history

Nargis formed in the Bay of Bengal in late April 2008, intensified to peak sustained winds of about 165 km/h (with higher estimates by some agencies) as it recurved eastward, and struck the delta coast near its peak strength. It weakened as it moved inland past Yangon, dissipating over the Myanmar–Thailand border region days later.

Damage and aftermath

The storm destroyed hundreds of thousands of homes, left millions homeless, flooded rice paddies with salt water, and caused damage estimated at more than US$10 billion. The ruling military government's slow acceptance of foreign aid drew international criticism and complicated relief efforts; aid access improved after ASEAN-brokered arrangements. Nargis remains the deadliest named storm in the North Indian Ocean basin and one of the deadliest tropical cyclones worldwide.
