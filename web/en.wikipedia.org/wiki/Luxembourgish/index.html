Luxembourgish (endonym: Lëtzebuergesch) is a West Germanic language spoken mainly in Luxembourg, where it is the national language and one of three administrative languages alongside French and German. It is a Moselle Franconian dialect of Central German, enriched with many loanwords from French.

Luxembourgish is the national language of Luxembourg

Status and use

Luxembourg uses a triglossic arrangement: Luxembourgish dominates everyday spoken life, broadcasting and informal writing; French predominates in legislation and much administration; German is used in much primary schooling and print media, with children literate first in German, then French, while Luxembourgish is the language of early childhood education. A 1984 law made Luxembourgish the national language and regulated its official spelling, standardised in reforms of 1946, 1975 and 1999.

Features

Like other Moselle Franconian varieties, Luxembourgish did not undergo the full High German consonant shift, and it preserves features that distinguish it from Standard German, alongside a heavy French lexical influence: merci ("thank you"), pardon ("sorry/excuse me"), and Buschauffer ("bus driver"), for example. Closely related dialects are spoken across the border in Germany's Bitburg–Trier region, Belgium's Arelerland and France's Lorraine.
