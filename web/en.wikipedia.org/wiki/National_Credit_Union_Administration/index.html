National Credit Union Administration

The National Credit Union Administration (NCUA) is one of two federal agencies in the United States that provide deposit insurance to depositors in U.S. depository institutions, the other being the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, which insures commercial banks and savings institutions. The NCUA is an independent federal agency created by the United States Congress to regulate, charter, and supervise federal credit unions. It insures deposits of account holders in all federal credit unions and the overwhelming majority of state-chartered credit unions through the National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund (NCUSIF).

History

Credit union regulation began with the Federal Credit Union Act of 1934, signed by President Franklin D. Roosevelt, which created the federal credit union system. Supervision was initially handled by the Bureau of Federal Credit Unions within the Farm Credit Administration, later moving to the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare, and then to the National Credit Union Administration, which became an independent agency in 1970. The National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund was created by Congress in 1970 to insure members' shares in credit unions.

Organization

The NCUA is governed by a three-member board appointed by the President of the United States and confirmed by the United States Senate. No more than two board members may belong to the same political party. The agency is headquartered in Alexandria, Virginia, and operates regional offices. It is funded entirely by fees paid by regulated credit unions and does not receive taxpayer appropriations.

Regulation

The NCUA charters and supervises federal credit unions, insures shares in federal and most state-chartered credit unions, and conducts regular examinations. As of the 2020s, the NCUA insured deposits at thousands of credit unions serving well over 100 million members. The standard share insurance coverage limit is $250,000 per depositor, per insured credit union, for each account ownership category, mirroring the coverage offered by the FDIC for banks.
