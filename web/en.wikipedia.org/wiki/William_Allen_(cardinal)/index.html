William Allen (1532 – 16 October 1594) was an English Catholic priest and cardinal, a leading figure of the English Counter-Reformation. Exiled under Elizabeth I, he founded seminaries abroad to train priests for the English mission and sponsored the Douay–Rheims translation of the Bible into English.

Early life

Allen was educated at Oriel College, Oxford, becoming a fellow and, in 1556, principal of St Mary Hall. Under Mary I he was ordained and held ecclesiastical office, but the accession of Elizabeth I and the restoration of Protestantism drove him abroad in 1561.

Douai and the English mission

In 1568 Allen founded the English College at Douai in the Spanish Netherlands, the first seminary of its kind, to train English priests for clandestine ministry at home; many of its alumni were executed in England and later venerated as martyrs. He later founded the English College in Rome. His college at Douai produced the Douay–Rheims Bible, whose New Testament appeared in 1582.

Cardinal

Allen was created a cardinal by Pope Sixtus V in 1587, partly in anticipation of England's return to Catholicism following the planned Spanish invasion. The failure of the Spanish Armada in 1588 ended those hopes, and Allen spent his final years in Rome, where he died in 1594.
