Shigeru Miyamoto (Japanese: 宮本 茂, born 16 November 1952) is a Japanese video game designer, producer and game director at Nintendo, where he serves as a representative director and fellow. Widely regarded as one of the most accomplished and influential game designers in the world, he is the creator of some of the most acclaimed and best-selling game franchises of all time, including Mario, Donkey Kong, The Legend of Zelda, F-Zero, Star Fox and Pikmin.

Early life

Miyamoto was born in the town of Sonobe, in Kyoto Prefecture, the son of schoolteachers. As a child he enjoyed exploring the countryside, hillsides and caves around his home — experiences he later cited as the inspiration for the exploration in The Legend of Zelda. He studied industrial design at the Kanazawa College of Art, graduating in 1975.

Career

Miyamoto joined Nintendo in 1977 as an apprentice in the planning department, initially designing artwork for arcade cabinets. After the commercial failure of the arcade game Radar Scope in North America, Nintendo president Hiroshi Yamauchi asked him to salvage the unsold units with a new game. The result was Donkey Kong (1981), introducing the characters of Donkey Kong and Jumpman — later renamed Mario — and one of the first games with a developed narrative.

He went on to design Super Mario Bros. (1985) and The Legend of Zelda (1986) for the Nintendo Entertainment System, games that helped revive the video game industry after the crash of 1983 and established enduring conventions of the platform and action-adventure genres. As a producer and general manager of Nintendo's Entertainment Analysis & Development division, he oversaw much of the company's software output, mentoring a generation of designers. He was closely involved in the design of the Wii console and games such as Wii Sports and Wii Fit, drawing on his hobbies of gardening, music and weighing himself daily.

Design philosophy

Miyamoto emphasises gameplay and intuitive controls over graphics and narrative complexity, often designing around a single novel mechanic or a personal interest — Pikmin grew from his gardening, and Nintendogs from his owning a dog. He is known for "upending the tea table" (chabudai gaeshi), overturning projects late in development when he feels they are not fun enough.

Honours

Miyamoto was the first inductee into the Academy of Interactive Arts and Sciences Hall of Fame (1998), received a star on the Walk of Game (2006), a BAFTA Fellowship (2010), and was named a Person of Cultural Merit by the Japanese government in 2019.
