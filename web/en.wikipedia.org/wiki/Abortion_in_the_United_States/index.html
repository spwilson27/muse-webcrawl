Abortion in the United States is subject to state regulation. The Supreme Court's 1973 decision in Roe v. Wade held that the Constitution protected a right to abortion, striking down many state bans. In 2022, Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization overturned Roe and returned the authority to regulate abortion to the individual states.

History

Abortion was generally legal in early American history under common law before "quickening". During the 19th century, states progressively criminalized it. By the mid-20th century, reform movements sought to liberalize state laws, and Roe v. Wade in 1973 established a national framework based on trimesters, later modified by the viability standard in Planned Parenthood v. Casey (1992).

After Dobbs

Following the Dobbs decision, numerous states enacted near-total bans or strict gestational limits, while other states expanded legal protections and access. The resulting patchwork has produced substantial interstate travel for abortion care and ongoing litigation over state laws and medication abortion.

Public opinion and politics

Abortion remains one of the most contested issues in American politics, with "pro-life" and "pro-choice" movements advocating restrictions or access respectively. Polling generally shows majority support for legal abortion in at least some circumstances.
