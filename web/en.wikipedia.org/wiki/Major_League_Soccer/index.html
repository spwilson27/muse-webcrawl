Major League Soccer

Major League Soccer (MLS) is the top professional men's soccer league sanctioned by the United States Soccer Federation, comprising clubs in the United States and Canada. Founded in 1993 as part of the United States' bid to host the 1994 FIFA World Cup, it began play in 1996 with ten teams. The league operates as a single entity — MLS owns all clubs and centrally negotiates player contracts — and uses a closed, franchise model with no promotion and relegation, a salary cap, and a season-ending playoff culminating in the MLS Cup.

History

After early financial struggles and the contraction of two Florida teams in 2001, MLS stabilised through soccer-specific stadiums, the designated player rule (2007) — which enabled the signing of David Beckham by the LA Galaxy — and steady expansion. Signing Lionel Messi to Inter Miami in 2023 brought unprecedented global attention. The league's media rights moved to a streaming model with Apple TV's MLS Season Pass in 2023.

Competition format

Clubs play a regular season from late February to October; the team with the best record receives the Supporters' Shield, and the leading teams enter the MLS Cup playoffs. MLS clubs also compete in the CONCACAF Champions Cup and, since 2023, jointly with Mexican clubs in the Leagues Cup. Average attendances rank among the highest in world football, and several clubs draw crowds rivalling major European leagues.
