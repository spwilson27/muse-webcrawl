Latin Quarter, Paris

The Latin Quarter of Paris (French: Quartier latin) is an area in the 5th and 6th arrondissements of Paris, situated on the left bank of the Seine, around the Sorbonne. Known for its student life, lively atmosphere, and many bistros, the area takes its name from the Latin language, which was widely spoken in and around the University of Paris during the Middle Ages.

History

The area was the site of the Roman city of Lutetia; remains include the Arènes de Lutèce amphitheatre and the Thermes de Cluny baths. From the 12th century, the University of Paris drew scholars from across Europe, and Latin was the common language of teaching and disputation. The quarter later became a centre of printing, bookselling, and intellectual life, and was a focus of the student protests of May 1968.

Landmarks

  • The Sorbonne and the Collège de France
  • The Panthéon, resting place of many French luminaries
  • The Luxembourg Gardens
  • The Musée de Cluny (Musée national du Moyen Âge)
  • The Théâtre de l'Odéon and the historic bookstalls along the Seine

Present day

Today the quarter is home to many institutions of higher education, including Paris-Sorbonne University, Paris Cité University, and the École Normale Supérieure nearby, as well as famous bookshops such as Shakespeare and Company. It remains one of the most recognisable neighbourhoods of Paris, although rising rents have displaced many students.
