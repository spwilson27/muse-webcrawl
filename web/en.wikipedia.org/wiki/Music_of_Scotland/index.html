Music of Scotland

The music of Scotland is an important part of the country's national identity, encompassing traditional folk music, piping, Gaelic song, classical composition and internationally successful rock and pop acts.

Traditional music

Scottish folk music features fiddles, accordions, harps (clàrsach), tin whistles and above all the bagpipes — the Great Highland bagpipe for outdoor and military music, and the Scottish smallpipes and border pipes for indoor playing. Dance forms include reels, strathspeys, jigs and the stately pibroch (ceòl mòr), the classical repertory of the Highland pipes. In Gaelic-speaking areas, traditions include waulking songs, puirt à beul (mouth music), and psalm singing in a distinctive heterophonic style. Social dancing at cèilidhs remains widespread.

Songs and ballads

Scotland is famous for its ballads — narrative songs such as "Tam Lin" and "The Bonnie Earl o' Moray" — and for the songs of Robert Burns, including "Auld Lang Syne," sung worldwide at New Year. Bothy ballads from the northeast describe farm labourers' lives, while Jacobite songs preserve the memory of the risings of 1715 and 1745.

Classical and modern music

Classical composers include Peter Maxwell Davies, James MacMillan and Thea Musgrave. Scottish folk groups such as The Corries, Runrig (singing partly in Gaelic), Capercaillie and Aly Bain and Phil Cunningham shaped the modern folk scene. In popular music, Scotland produced acts including Simple Minds, Annie Lennox, Franz Ferdinand, Calvin Harris, Lewis Capaldi and Susan Boyle.
