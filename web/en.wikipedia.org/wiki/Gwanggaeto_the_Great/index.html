Gwanggaeto the Great (374 – October 412, r. 391–412) was the nineteenth monarch of Goguryeo, the northernmost of the Three Kingdoms of Korea. Under his reign Goguryeo became a dominant power in East Asia, expanding deep into Manchuria and the Liaodong Peninsula and subjugating much of the Korean peninsula. His posthumous name, an abbreviation of Gukgangsang-gwanggaetogyeong-pyeongan-hotaewang ("Supreme King, broad expander of territory, buried in Gukgangsang"), records his reputation as an empire-builder; he adopted the independent era name Yeongnak ("Eternal Joy") in assertion of equality with the Chinese dynasties.

Conquests

The principal source is the inscription on the Gwanggaeto Stele, a 6.4-metre stone monument erected by his son Jangsu in 414 at the capital (modern Ji'an, Jilin). It credits Gwanggaeto with taking 64 walled cities and 1,400 villages in Manchuria, defeating the Khitan and the Later Yan and annexing much of Liaodong (by 404). In the south he intervened decisively in the war between Baekje and Silla, crushing a Wa (Japanese) army in 400 in response to Silla's plea, breaking Baekje's power (the Baekje king surrendered fealty in 402), and reducing Silla to a virtual protectorate; Goguryeo also accepted the submission of Buyeo.

Legacy

Gwanggaeto is one of only two Korean rulers honoured with the epithet "the Great" (the other being Sejong of Joseon). His campaigns are central to Korean national pride, while the Stele — rediscovered in the 1870s — remains politically sensitive: its passage concerning Wa activity in 391 has been disputed between Korean and Japanese scholars. The Republic of Korea Navy's first class of destroyers (the Gwanggaeto the Great-class, KDX-I) is named after him.
