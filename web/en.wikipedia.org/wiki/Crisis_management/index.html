Crisis management

Crisis management is the process by which an organisation deals with a disruptive and unexpected event that threatens to harm the organisation or its stakeholders. The study of crisis management originated with large-scale industrial and environmental disasters in the 1980s. It is considered to be the most important process in public relations.

Types of crisis

Common classifications include natural disasters, technological failures (such as chemical spills or data breaches), product defects and recalls, workplace violence, financial crises, and reputational crises arising from misconduct or social-media controversies. Crises may be sudden ("smoldering" crises that build over time without detection are a contrasting category emphasised by the Institute for Crisis Management).

Stages and models

Widely used frameworks divide crisis management into three phases:

  • Pre-crisis — prevention and preparation: risk assessment, crisis plans, trained spokespersons and simulation exercises.
  • Crisis response — the organisation's actual response: speed, accuracy and consistency of communication are prized; instructing information (telling people how to protect themselves) precedes adjusting information (helping them cope).
  • Post-crisis — evaluation, follow-up communication, and organisational learning.

W. Timothy Coombs' Situational Crisis Communication Theory (SCCT) matches response strategies — deny, diminish, rebuild, bolster — to the level of responsibility stakeholders attribute to the organisation. The 1982 Chicago Tylenol murders, in which Johnson & Johnson's rapid nationwide recall is still cited as exemplary, and the 2010 Deepwater Horizon spill, widely criticised, are standard contrasting case studies; research suggests companies with effective responses suffer smaller and less persistent reputational and share-price damage.

Crisis leadership

Scholarship on crisis leadership emphasises early recognition ("sensemaking"), decisiveness under uncertainty, visible and honest communication by senior leaders, and the "post-crisis learning" needed to prevent recurrence. Related fields include business continuity planning, emergency management and risk communication.
