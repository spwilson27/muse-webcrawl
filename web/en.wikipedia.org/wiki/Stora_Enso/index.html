Stora Enso

Stora Enso Oyj is a Finnish-Swedish manufacturer of pulp, paper, packaging and wood products, headquartered in Helsinki, Finland. Formed by the 1998 merger of the Finnish company Enso and the Swedish company Stora, it is one of the largest forest-products companies in the world, employing roughly 20,000 people. Its Swedish arm traces its origins to a copper-mining charter of 1288 and is often described as one of the world's oldest companies.

History

Stora Kopparberg in Falun, Sweden, received its charter in 1288 and operated the Falun copper mine for centuries; the company has sometimes been called the world's oldest limited-liability corporation, a share certificate from 1288 surviving in its archive. Enso grew out of Finnish timber and paper enterprises founded in the nineteenth century near the Russian border. Their merger created a forest-industry giant. In the 2000s the company expanded into North America and later restructured as demand for printing papers declined, repeatedly closing mills and selling divisions.

Operations

Stora Enso today organises its business into packaging materials, packaging solutions, biomaterials (pulp and new bio-based products), wood products, forest (its own forests, notably through a large stake in Sweden's Bergvik Skog) and a shrinking paper segment. It has invested in products such as cross-laminated timber, lignin-based materials, formed-fibre packaging and tree-to-textile fibres, positioning itself as a "renewable materials company".

Controversies

The company has faced criticism over eucalyptus plantations in Brazil (through its Veracel joint venture), land disputes with local communities, and the environmental effects of forestry. In 2010 a Chinese subsidiary was involved in a major accounting and reporting scandal that led to executive resignations.
