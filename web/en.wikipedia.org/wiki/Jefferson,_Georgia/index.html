Jefferson is a city in and the county seat of Jackson County, Georgia, United States. Located in the northeastern part of the state, roughly 55 miles (89 km) northeast of Atlanta, Jefferson is part of the fast-growing exurban fringe of the Atlanta metropolitan area while retaining a historic courthouse-square downtown.

History

The area was settled in the early 19th century following land cessions by the Cherokee and Creek peoples. The town was laid out and incorporated in 1803 and named in honor of Thomas Jefferson, then President of the United States. It became the seat of Jackson County, and its centerpiece, the Jackson County Courthouse, has anchored the town square through several rebuildings. Jefferson grew as an agricultural and trading center for the surrounding cotton country, later diversifying with manufacturing and, in recent decades, rapid residential growth tied to the expansion of metropolitan Atlanta and nearby Athens.

Geography

Jefferson lies in the Piedmont region of north Georgia, at an elevation of about 750 feet (229 m). U.S. Route 129 and Georgia State Route 11 pass through the city, and Interstate 85 runs just to its northwest. The North Oconee River flows nearby.

Demographics

The 2020 census recorded a population of roughly 13,000, a substantial increase from about 9,400 in 2010, reflecting the city's rapid suburban-style growth. The population is predominantly White, with growing Hispanic and Black communities.

Education

The city is served by the Jefferson City School District, including Jefferson High School, whose sports teams are known as the Dragons. The University of Georgia in nearby Athens provides higher-education access.

Notable people

Jefferson is the hometown of several professional football players who emerged from its high school program, continuing a strong local football tradition.
