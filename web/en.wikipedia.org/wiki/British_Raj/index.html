British Raj

British Raj / Indian Empire
Period: 1858–1947
Capital: Calcutta (to 1911), New Delhi
Sovereign: Queen/King-Emperor; Viceroy as representative

The British Raj (from Hindi rāj, "rule") was the rule of the British Crown on the Indian subcontinent from 1858 to 1947, established after the Indian Rebellion of 1857 ended East India Company rule. The territory — present-day India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and (until 1937) Myanmar — was administered as provinces plus some 584 princely states under British paramountcy.

History

Queen Victoria was proclaimed Empress of India in 1876. The Raj built railways, civil service and law, while extracting resources and presiding over devastating famines. The Indian independence movement — the Indian National Congress, and after 1920 Mahatma Gandhi's non-violent campaigns — grew steadily.

End

After World War II Britain agreed to independence; the Indian Independence Act 1947 partitioned the Raj into the Dominions of India and Pakistan on 14–15 August 1947, amid mass displacement and violence. See Partition of India, Mahatma Gandhi.
