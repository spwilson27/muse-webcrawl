Guru Nanak (1469–1539) was an Indian spiritual teacher who founded Sikhism and is its first Guru. After a transformative experience around 1499 — tradition says he disappeared in a river for three days and emerged declaring 'There is no Hindu, there is no Muslim' — he spent decades travelling across Asia teaching, through poetry and song, devotion to one God, honest labour, and the equality of all people regardless of caste or creed.

Life and teaching

Born in the village of Talwandi (now Nankana Sahib in Pakistan), Nanak worked in a granary store before his calling. His four great journeys (udasis) took him, by tradition, across India and to Mecca, Baghdad and Tibet; historical scrutiny finds firm ground mainly in Punjab, but the travels became foundational legend. He settled at Kartarpur on the Ravi, farming with a community that sang his hymns — some 974 of which are in the Guru Granth Sahib. Before dying he named a disciple, Guru Angad, as successor, beginning the line of ten Gurus.
