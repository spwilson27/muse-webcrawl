Carbene

In chemistry, a carbene is a molecule containing a neutral carbon atom with a valence of two and two unshared valence electrons. The prototypical carbene is methylene, :CH2. Carbenes are highly reactive intermediates in many organic reactions, though stable carbenes have been isolated since the late 20th century.

Carbene
General formula: R–(C:)–R' or C:
States: Singlet and triplet
Example: Methylene (:CH2)

Structure

Carbenes exist in singlet states, with paired electrons in one orbital and an empty p orbital, and triplet states, with two unpaired electrons. Their geometry and reactivity depend strongly on this electronic state and on substituents.

Reactions

Carbenes undergo cyclopropanation of alkenes, insertion into C–H and other bonds, and ylide formation. Transition-metal carbene complexes are catalysts for olefin metathesis. Stable N-heterocyclic carbenes (NHCs), first isolated by Anthony Arduengo in 1991, are widely used as ligands and organocatalysts.
