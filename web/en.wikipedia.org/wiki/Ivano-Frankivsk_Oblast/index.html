Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast

Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast (Ukrainian: Івано-Франківська область) is an administrative oblast in western Ukraine. Its capital and largest city is Ivano-Frankivsk, named after the writer Ivan Franko. The oblast covers much of the historic region of Pokuttia and the northern slopes of the Ukrainian Carpathians, including Hoverla, the highest mountain in Ukraine at 2,061 metres.

Geography

The oblast borders Lviv, Ternopil, Chernivtsi, and Zakarpattia oblasts and has a short border with Romania in the south. Its terrain rises from the Dniester and Prut river plains in the northeast to the forested Carpathian ridges in the southwest — the Gorgany, Chornohora, and Svydovets ranges. Major rivers include the Dniester, Prut, and Cheremosh. Part of the Carpathian National Nature Park, Ukraine's first and largest national park, lies within the oblast, and the ski resort of Bukovel near Yaremche is the country's largest.

History

The territory historically lay in the borderlands of Galicia; it was part of Kievan Rus', the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia, Poland, and from 1772 the Austrian Habsburg monarchy. Between the world wars it belonged to Poland as the Stanisławów Voivodeship. Annexed by the Soviet Union in 1939, it was organised as Stanislav Oblast and attached to the Ukrainian SSR; the region saw heavy fighting, the Holocaust destroyed its large Jewish population, and an anti-Soviet insurgency continued into the 1950s. In 1962 the city and oblast were renamed for Ivan Franko.

Demographics and culture

The population is overwhelmingly ethnic Ukrainian and includes the Hutsul highlanders of the Carpathians, known for distinctive wooden architecture, crafts, and music. Major cities besides the capital are Kalush, Kolomyia, Nadvirna, and Dolyna. The oblast's economy combines timber, oil and gas extraction around Dolyna and Nadvirna, chemical production at Kalush, agriculture, and a growing tourism sector centred on the mountains.
