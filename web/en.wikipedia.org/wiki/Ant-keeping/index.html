Ant-keeping

PracticeKeeping ant colonies as pets
HousingFormicarium
Popular speciesLasius niger, Messor barbarus, Camponotus spp.

Ant-keeping (or ant keeping) is the practice of keeping live ant colonies in captivity for observation, study or enjoyment. Colonies are housed in a specially designed enclosure called a formicarium, connected to a foraging area or "outworld". The hobby grew rapidly in the 2010s with online communities and commercial suppliers.

Starting a colony

Most keepers begin with a single newly mated queen, caught after a nuptial flight or purchased from a supplier. The queen is kept in a test tube with water held behind a cotton plug until her first workers ("nanitics") emerge. The young colony is fed small insects and sugar water or honey, and later moved into a formicarium of acrylic, plaster, ytong (aerated concrete) or 3D-printed material.

Care

Colony care involves controlling temperature and humidity, providing protein (insects) for brood development and carbohydrates for workers, and, for temperate species, a winter diapause period at low temperature. Escape prevention — using barriers such as fluon or talcum powder — is a central concern.

Popular species and concerns

Beginner favourites include the black garden ant Lasius niger, seed-harvesting Messor species, and large Camponotus carpenter ants. Conservationists caution against releasing non-native colonies and against trading invasive species such as fire ants; some jurisdictions restrict importing queen ants.
