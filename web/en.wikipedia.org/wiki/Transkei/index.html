Transkei

Transkei (Xhosa: iTranskei), officially the Republic of Transkei (Afrikaans: Republiek van Transkei), was a Bantustan and nominal parliamentary democracy in southeastern South Africa. Its capital was Umtata (now Mthatha). Transkei represented a significant precedent as the first Bantustan to be granted "independence" by the apartheid government on 26 October 1976, an independence recognized by no other country.

History

The territory, situated between the Great Kei River and the Mtamvuna River and inhabited largely by Xhosa-speaking people, was administered as a reserve under successive South African governments. Under the Promotion of Bantu Self-Government Act of 1959 it became a self-governing territory, and in 1976 Pretoria granted it nominal independence under Chief Kaiser Matanzima. It was the largest of the ten Bantustans, covering roughly 43,800 km² with a population of about 2.4 million at independence (plus millions of Xhosa assigned its citizenship while living elsewhere).

Reintegration

In 1987 a military coup led by Bantu Holomisa ousted the civilian government. Following the end of apartheid, Transkei was reincorporated into South Africa on 27 April 1994, becoming part of the new Eastern Cape province. The term "Transkei" continues to be used for the region, known for its rolling green hills and the Wild Coast.
