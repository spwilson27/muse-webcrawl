Drake Passage

The Drake Passage is the body of water between South America's Cape Horn, Chile, Argentina, and the South Shetland Islands of Antarctica. It connects the southwestern part of the Atlantic Ocean with the southeastern part of the Pacific Ocean and extends into the Southern Ocean.

History

The passage is named after English explorer and privateer Sir Francis Drake, who passed through it in 1578 during his circumnavigation, though Dutch navigator Willem Schouten first sailed it in 1616 naming Cape Horn.

Geography and climate

It is the shortest crossing from Antarctica to any other landmass. Powerful Antarctic Circumpolar Current flows through it, and with no land to slow winds, seas are famously rough — the 'Drake Shake' versus a calm 'Drake Lake'.

Ecology

Waters are rich in krill and support whales, dolphins, albatrosses and petrels. See Antarctica and Cape Horn.
