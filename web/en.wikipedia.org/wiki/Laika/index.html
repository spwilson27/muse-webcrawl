Laika

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Laika in her flight harness, 1957.

Laika was a Soviet space dog who became the first animal to orbit Earth, aboard Sputnik 2 on 3 November 1957. A stray mongrel from the streets of Moscow, probably about three years old, she was chosen because Soviet scientists believed stray dogs, hardened by hunger and cold, would cope best with the ordeal.

Laika's flight proved a living passenger could survive launch and weightlessness, paving the way for human spaceflight — but there was never a plan to bring her home, as the technology for safe re-entry did not yet exist for her capsule. For decades the Soviet account said she survived several days and was euthanised; in 2002 it was revealed she most likely died within hours, from overheating after a thermal control failure. Her sacrifice is commemorated by statues in Moscow, and her name became a byword for the animals who died in early space research.
