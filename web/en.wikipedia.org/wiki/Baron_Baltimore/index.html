Baron Baltimore, of Baltimore, County Longford, was a title in the Peerage of Ireland. It was created in 1625 for the English courtier and coloniser George Calvert, who had resigned as Secretary of State after declaring his conversion to Catholicism. The title takes its name from Baltimore, County Cork, in Ireland — a place-name from the Irish Baile an Tí Mhóir, "townland of the big house" — and later gave its name, through the family, to the city of Baltimore, Maryland.

History

George Calvert, 1st Baron Baltimore, obtained a charter for the Avalon colony in Newfoundland and, after visiting it, sought a more hospitable grant further south. He died in April 1632, weeks before the Charter of Maryland was sealed; it issued instead to his son Cecilius Calvert, 2nd Baron Baltimore, who governed the Province of Maryland as its proprietor from England and dispatched the first settlers aboard the Ark and the Dove in 1633–34. The barony and the proprietorship descended through the Calvert family, whose black-and-gold arms still appear on the flag of Maryland.

Barons Baltimore

  • George Calvert, 1st Baron Baltimore (1580–1632)
  • Cecilius Calvert, 2nd Baron Baltimore (1605–1675)
  • Charles Calvert, 3rd Baron Baltimore (1637–1715)
  • Benedict Calvert, 4th Baron Baltimore (1679–1715)
  • Charles Calvert, 5th Baron Baltimore (1699–1751)
  • Frederick Calvert, 6th Baron Baltimore (1731–1771)

The title became extinct on the death of the sixth Baron in 1771 without legitimate male heirs, shortly before the American Revolution ended the family's proprietary rule in Maryland.
