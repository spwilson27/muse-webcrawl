DPT vaccine
TypeCombination vaccine
TargetsDiphtheria, pertussis, tetanus
VariantsDTaP, Tdap, DTwP

The DPT vaccine or DTP vaccine is a class of combination vaccines against three infectious diseases in humans: diphtheria, pertussis (whooping cough), and tetanus. The vaccine components include diphtheria and tetanus toxoids, and either killed whole cells of the bacterium that causes pertussis (DTwP) or purified acellular pertussis components (DTaP).

History

The combined DTP vaccine was first licensed in the United States in 1948 and became a routine part of childhood immunisation worldwide, contributing to dramatic declines in all three diseases. Concerns in the 1970s and 1980s about reactions attributed to the whole-cell pertussis component led to the development of acellular pertussis vaccines, which were adopted for the full schedule in most high-income countries during the 1990s; subsequent large studies have not supported the alleged link between the vaccine and permanent neurological damage.

Usage

Children typically receive a series of DTaP doses in infancy and early childhood; reduced-dose formulations (Tdap and Td) are used for adolescent and adult boosters. In much of the developing world the whole-cell DTwP remains in use, often combined with hepatitis B and Haemophilus influenzae type b vaccines in the "pentavalent" vaccine.
