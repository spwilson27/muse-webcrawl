Latin American Boom

The Boom brought Latin American fiction worldwide attention in the 1960s.
Period1960s–1970s
Key figuresGarcía Márquez, Vargas Llosa, Cortázar, Fuentes

The Latin American Boom (Spanish: Boom latinoamericano) was a literary movement of the 1960s and 1970s in which the work of a group of relatively young Latin American novelists became widely celebrated in Europe and throughout the world.

Principal writers

The core figures were Gabriel García Márquez of Colombia, whose One Hundred Years of Solitude (1967) became the emblematic work of magic realism; Mario Vargas Llosa of Peru; Julio Cortázar of Argentina, author of Hopscotch (1963); and Carlos Fuentes of Mexico. Precursors included Jorge Luis Borges, Juan Rulfo, Juan Carlos Onetti and Alejo Carpentier.

Characteristics

Boom novels typically experimented with narrative chronology and point of view, blended the fantastic with the everyday, and engaged with the political turmoil of the region, including dictatorship and the aftermath of the Cuban Revolution. The movement's commercial success was aided by Barcelona publishers and the agent Carmen Balcells, and it permanently changed the international standing of Spanish-language literature. García Márquez and Vargas Llosa both later won the Nobel Prize in Literature.
