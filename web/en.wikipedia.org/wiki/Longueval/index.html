Longueval is a commune in the Somme department in Hauts-de-France in northern France. It is best known for its role in the Battle of the Somme in 1916, particularly the fighting for the adjacent Delville Wood, and for the memorials to South African and New Zealand soldiers that stand in and near the village.

First World War

Longueval occupied a strategically important position on the Somme battlefield. During the Battle of the Somme, British and Commonwealth forces attacked the village in July 1916 as part of the effort to break the German second position. The 1st South African Infantry Brigade was ordered to seize and hold Delville Wood, east of the village; in six days of ferocious fighting the brigade suffered some 80 per cent casualties, earning the wood the nickname "Devil's Wood" among the troops.

The village itself changed hands several times before finally being captured at the end of July 1916. In September 1916 the New Zealand Division fought in the area during the Battle of Flers–Courcelette.

Memorials

The Delville Wood South African National Memorial, unveiled in 1926, commemorates the South African soldiers who died in the First World War and later conflicts. A museum was added in 1986. The New Zealand memorial at Longueval marks the site from which the New Zealand Division launched its attack on 15 September 1916, the first day tanks were used in warfare. The Caterpillar Valley Cemetery nearby contains the grave from which the remains of New Zealand's Unknown Warrior were exhumed in 2004.
