John Michael "Ozzy" Osbourne (December 3, 1948 – July 22, 2025) was an English singer, songwriter, and media personality. He rose to prominence during the 1970s as the lead singer of the heavy metal band Black Sabbath, during which period he adopted the nickname "Prince of Darkness".

Black Sabbath

Osbourne co-founded Black Sabbath in Birmingham in 1968 with guitarist Tony Iommi, bassist Geezer Butler, and drummer Bill Ward. The band's early albums — Black Sabbath and Paranoid (both 1970), Master of Reality (1971), and Sabbath Bloody Sabbath (1973) — are now considered foundational to heavy metal. Osbourne's eerie, plain-spoken vocal style and chaotic stage persona contrasted with Iommi's riff architecture. He was dismissed from the band in 1979 owing to alcohol and drug problems.

Solo career

Managed by Sharon Arden, whom he married in 1982, Osbourne launched a successful solo career with Blizzard of Ozz (1980) — featuring guitarist Randy Rhoads and the signature song "Crazy Train" — and Diary of a Madman (1981). His 1980s career was marked by controversy, including the infamous 1982 incident in which he bit the head off a bat on stage in Des Moines. His 1991 ballad "Mama, I'm Coming Home" and the No More Tears album marked a commercial peak; he founded the Ozzfest touring festival in 1996.

Later life and death

Osbourne found a second fame as a reality television star with MTV's The Osbournes (2002–2005). He reunited with Black Sabbath several times, recording the album 13 (2013) and completing a farewell tour in 2017, and released the solo albums Ordinary Man (2020) and Patient Number 9 (2022) while living with Parkinson's disease, which he disclosed in 2020. His final performance, the "Back to the Beginning" charity concert with the original Sabbath line-up at Villa Park, Birmingham, took place on July 5, 2025; he died seventeen days later, on July 22, 2025.
