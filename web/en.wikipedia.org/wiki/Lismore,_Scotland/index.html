Lismore (Scottish Gaelic: Lios Mòr, "great garden") is an island of some 2,351 hectares (9.1 sq mi) in Loch Linnhe, within the council area of Argyll and Bute, off the west coast of Scotland. About 16 km long and up to 2 km wide, it lies between Oban and the Appin coast, and is predominantly of Dalradian limestone, which makes it unusually fertile for a Hebridean island.

Lismore seen across Loch Linnhe from Port Appin.

History

Lismore was an important early Christian centre. Saint Moluag founded a monastery there in the sixth century, and the island later became the seat of the medieval Bishop of Argyll, whose cathedral church, now the parish church at Clachan, dates largely from the fourteenth century. Brochs such as Tirefour Castle and the castles of Achanduin and Coeffin recall its Norse and medieval past. In the nineteenth century the island supported a large population engaged in farming, fishing and lime quarrying, with lime exported from kilns along the shore.

Today

Two ferry services link Lismore with the mainland: a vehicle ferry from Oban to Achnacroish and a passenger ferry from Port Appin to the north of the island. The Lismore Historical Society runs a heritage museum and a reconstructed nineteenth-century cottage. The island's mild climate and limestone soils support a rich flora unusual in the Highlands.
