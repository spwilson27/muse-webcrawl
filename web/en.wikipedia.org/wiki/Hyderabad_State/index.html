Hyderabad State

Hyderabad State (also known as the Princely State of Hyderabad) was a monarchy and princely state in south-central India, ruled by the sovereigns of the Asaf Jahi dynasty, who bore the title Nizam. It was the largest and wealthiest of the princely states of British India, covering about 82,000 square miles (212,000 km²) — roughly the size of Great Britain — with a population of some 16 million in the 1940s.

History

The state was founded in 1724 by Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan (Asaf Jah I), a Mughal viceroy of the Deccan who established de facto independence. Hyderabad entered a subsidiary alliance with the British East India Company in 1798 and remained a loyal princely state under British paramountcy. The last Nizam, Mir Osman Ali Khan, was reputedly among the richest men in the world in the 1930s.

Accession to India

At the partition of India in 1947, the Nizam sought independence or accession to Pakistan. After a standstill agreement, economic blockade, internal unrest (including the Telangana rebellion) and the activities of the Razakar militia, India launched a military action — Operation Polo, officially a "police action" — in September 1948. Hyderabad was incorporated into the Indian Union; the state survived as a federal unit until its linguistic reorganisation in 1956.

Society and culture

Hyderabad State was multilingual and religiously mixed: its population was majority Hindu while the ruling dynasty and much of the administration were Muslim, and Urdu served as the language of government. Its legacy includes Osmania University (1918), extensive irrigation works, and the distinct Deccani culture of the city of Hyderabad.
