Beatus of Liébana

Beatus of Liébana (c. 730 – c. 800) was a monk and theologian from the Monastery of Santo Toribio de Liébana in northern Spain. He is best known as the author of the Commentary on the Apocalypse, a compilation of earlier patristic interpretations of the Book of Revelation, and for his opposition to the Adoptionist heresy associated with Archbishop Elipandus of Toledo.

Commentary on the Apocalypse

Compiled around 776 and revised in later editions, Beatus's Commentary (Commentaria in Apocalypsin) stitched together passages from earlier writers including Tyconius, Jerome, Augustine and Isidore of Seville into a verse-by-verse exposition of Revelation. Beatus presented it modestly as a compilation rather than an original work.

The commentary became famous chiefly through the richly illuminated copies made in Iberian monasteries during the 10th to 13th centuries. These manuscripts, collectively called Beatus manuscripts (Beati), are among the masterpieces of Spanish Romanesque art, notable for their vivid, flat, highly coloured miniatures depicting the visions of the Apocalypse. Around two dozen illustrated copies survive, including the Morgan Beatus, the Girona Beatus and the Saint-Sever Beatus.

Adoptionist controversy

Beatus, together with Etherius of Osma, attacked the Adoptionist teaching of Elipandus of Toledo and Felix of Urgel in writings composed in the 780s–790s, defending the full divinity of Christ as the natural Son of God. The controversy drew in Alcuin and the Carolingian court, and Adoptionism was condemned at councils including Frankfurt in 794.
