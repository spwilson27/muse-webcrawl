Chang'e is the Chinese goddess of the Moon. She is the subject of several legends in Chinese mythology, most of which incorporate elements of the archer Hou Yi, an emperor, the elixir of life, the Moon, and a jade rabbit who serves as her companion and pounds the elixir of immortality.

The legend

In the best-known version, Hou Yi shot down nine of the ten suns that scorched the earth and received an elixir of immortality. Chang'e, his wife, consumed the elixir — in some versions to keep it from a thief, Peng Meng — and floated up to the Moon, where she remains, separated from her husband. Hou Yi is said to display her favourite foods on the anniversary of her ascension, a custom associated with the Mid-Autumn Festival, when families gather to worship the moon and eat mooncakes.

Cultural legacy

Chang'e is celebrated in classical poetry, opera and modern media. China's lunar exploration programme is named the Chang'e programme in her honour; its missions include the Chang'e 3 lander with the Yutu ("Jade Rabbit") rover and Chang'e 4, the first craft to soft-land on the far side of the Moon.
