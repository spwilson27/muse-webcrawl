Wimbledon Championships

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Wimbledon Championships
Founded: 1877
Venue: All England Club, Wimbledon, London
Surface: Grass (outdoor)

The Championships, Wimbledon — commonly known simply as Wimbledon — is the oldest tennis tournament in the world, held since 1877 at the All England Club in Wimbledon, London. It is one of the four Grand Slam tournaments, and the only one still played on grass, the game's original surface.

Traditions

Wimbledon is famous for its traditions: a strict predominantly-white dress code for players, strawberries and cream, the Royal Box, queues for tickets, and the absence (until recent years) of play on the middle Sunday. The Gentlemen's Singles trophy is a gilt cup; the Ladies' Singles champion receives the Venus Rosewater Dish. Centre Court gained a retractable roof in 2009, and No. 1 Court in 2019.

Records

Roger Federer holds the record for men's singles titles (eight); Martina Navratilova won nine women's singles titles. The longest match in tennis history was played at Wimbledon in 2010, when John Isner defeated Nicolas Mahut 70–68 in the fifth set over three days — a feat that eventually prompted final-set tiebreak rules across the Slams. In length of tradition Wimbledon rivals events such as the Tour de France and football's Super Bowl as a fixture of the sporting summer.
