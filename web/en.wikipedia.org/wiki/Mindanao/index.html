Mindanao is the second-largest island in the Philippines, after Luzon, and the name also applies to the island group comprising Mindanao itself plus surrounding islands such as the Sulu Archipelago. Located in the southern part of the archipelago, it covers about 97,530 square kilometres and is the eighth most populous island in the world. Its largest city is Davao City, and its highest point is Mount Apo, the highest mountain in the Philippines.

Geography

Mindanao is irregularly shaped, ringed by four seas: the Sulu Sea to the west, the Philippine Sea to the east, the Celebes Sea to the south, and the Mindanao Sea to the north. The island is mountainous, with several volcanic ranges; Mount Apo, an active stratovolcano near Davao, is its highest peak. Major river systems include the Rio Grande de Mindanao (Mindanao River) and the Agusan River. The island lies south of the main typhoon belt and is comparatively rarely struck by major storms, a fact that has favoured agriculture.

History

The island was inhabited by Austronesian peoples for millennia and traded with China, Borneo, and the wider region. Islam arrived in the late 14th and 15th centuries, giving rise to powerful sultanates such as Sulu and Maguindanao, which resisted Spanish colonization for centuries — the source of the term "Moro" for the Muslim peoples of the south. Spain established footholds, particularly around Zamboanga and the north coast, but never fully subdued the sultanates. Mindanao passed to the United States after the Spanish–American War, and the Moro resistance continued into the American period. In the later twentieth century it became a destination for government-sponsored migration from Luzon and the Visayas, shifting its demographics.

People and culture

Mindanao is home to the Moro peoples (among them the Tausug, Maguindanao, Maranao, and Yakan), indigenous non-Muslim groups collectively called Lumad, and a large Christian settler population speaking Cebuano, Hiligaynon, and other languages. The autonomous Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) was established in 2019 following the ratification of the Bangsamoro Organic Law, succeeding the earlier ARMM as part of a peace process with the Moro Islamic Liberation Front.

Economy

Agriculture dominates, with Mindanao producing much of the Philippines' bananas, pineapples, coffee, cacao, and other crops; it is often described as the country's food basket. Mining, timber, and fishing are also significant industries. Despite its resources, parts of the island have lagged national development indicators, a situation linked to decades of armed conflict in the west and south.
