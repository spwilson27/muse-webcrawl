Rosuvastatin

Rosuvastatin, sold under the brand name Crestor among others, is a statin medication used to prevent cardiovascular disease in people at high risk and to treat abnormal lipids. It is recommended together with dietary changes, exercise and weight loss, and is taken by mouth.

Trade names: Crestor, others

Class: Statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor)

Routes: By mouth

Medical uses

Rosuvastatin lowers LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and raises HDL cholesterol by inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis in the liver. It is among the most potent statins by dose. The JUPITER trial (2008) showed that rosuvastatin reduced cardiovascular events in people with normal LDL but elevated C-reactive protein, broadening the drug's use in primary prevention.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include headache, muscle pain (myalgia), nausea, constipation and weakness. Serious side effects may include rhabdomyolysis, liver problems and diabetes. Use during pregnancy may harm the baby. Rosuvastatin was patented in 1991 and approved for medical use in 2003; generic versions became available from 2016. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
