Magnate

MeaningA wealthy, powerful person, especially in business or landholding
EtymologyLate Latin magnas, "great man"
Synonyms in useTycoon, baron, mogul, oligarch

A magnate is a person of great wealth and power, particularly one who dominates an industry or holds extensive lands and corresponding political influence. The word derives from the Late Latin magnas (plural magnates), meaning a great or noble man, and historically it denoted the highest ranks of the nobility in several European states.

Historical usage

In the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, the magnateria were a small class of immensely wealthy noble families — such as the Radziwiłłs, Potockis and Czartoryskis — who owned vast latifundia in the eastern territories, maintained private armies and courts, and dominated the Sejm, at times overshadowing the elected king. In Hungary, "magnates" was the formal designation of the hereditary nobility entitled to sit in the upper house. In medieval England the term was applied loosely to the great barons who could constrain the Crown, the milieu that produced Magna Carta in 1215.

Business magnates

With industrialisation the word transferred to business leaders who controlled whole industries: the steel magnate Andrew Carnegie, the oil magnate John D. Rockefeller, railway and shipping magnates such as Cornelius Vanderbilt, and later press magnates, steel and coal magnates of the Ruhr, and in the present day technology and media magnates. Collocations like "property magnate" and "business magnate" are standard in journalism, alongside near-synonyms acquired from other languages — tycoon (from Japanese), mogul (from the Mughal emperors) and, in post-Soviet contexts, oligarch. The term usually implies not merely riches but durable dominance of a sector and influence over public life.
