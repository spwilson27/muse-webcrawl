Zemlyanka

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A zemlyanka (Russian: землянка; Ukrainian: землянка) is a type of dugout or earth-house found in Eastern Europe. It is built by excavating a pit, roofing it with logs, poles, or planks, and covering the whole structure with earth and sod, so that the finished shelter is largely underground and well insulated against cold winters.

Uses

Zemlyankas have served for centuries as peasant dwellings, storage cellars, and field shelters. In wartime they were ubiquitous: both Russian/Soviet and German forces on the Eastern Front in the two World Wars built zemlyankas as troop billets, headquarters, and aid stations, since a well-made dugout protected against shell splinters and retained the heat of a small stove. Soviet partisans living in the forests of Belarus and Ukraine during the Second World War famously sheltered in zemlyankas, some large enough for families and even printing presses.

Construction

A typical zemlyanka has a rectangular pit one to two metres deep, walls lined with timber, a pitched or flat roof of logs covered with earth, a single door, and a stove with a chimney. Because most of the structure is below grade, it stays warm in winter and cool in summer. Earth shelters of this general type correspond to dugouts and pit-houses known across northern Eurasia and North America.
