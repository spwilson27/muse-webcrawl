Invincible-class aircraft carrier

The Invincible class was a class of three light aircraft carriers operated by the Royal Navy. The ships — HMS Invincible, HMS Illustrious, and HMS Ark Royal — were built in the 1970s and commissioned in the early 1980s, displacing about 22,000 tonnes and operating Sea Harrier and later Harrier GR.7/9 aircraft from a distinctive "ski-jump" ramp.

Design

Conceived originally as "through-deck cruisers" for anti-submarine warfare in the North Atlantic, the class combined a helicopter and fixed-wing air group with Sea Dart area air-defence missiles (later removed). The ski jump allowed the Sea Harrier to take off with a heavier load than a flat deck of similar length would permit.

Service history

Invincible and Illustrious served in the 1982 Falklands War, providing the bulk of British air cover. The class later operated in the Adriatic during the Bosnian conflict, in the Gulf, and off Sierra Leone. Invincible was decommissioned in 2005, Ark Royal in 2011, and Illustrious in 2014, as the Queen Elizabeth class was prepared to replace them.
