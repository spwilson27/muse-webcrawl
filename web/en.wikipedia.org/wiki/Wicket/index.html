Wicket

In cricket, the term wicket has several related meanings. Most commonly it refers to the set of three stumps topped by two bails at each end of the pitch, and by extension to the dismissal of a batter, to the pitch itself, and to a partnership between two batters.

A cricket wicket: three stumps surmounted by two bails.

The physical wicket

Each wicket consists of three wooden stumps, 28 inches (71 cm) tall, placed in a line, with two small wooden bails resting in grooves on top. The stumps are positioned so that the ball can pass between them only if it is not to be considered to have hit the wicket. A wicket stands at each end of the 22-yard pitch, in front of which the batter stands.

Dismissal

To "take a wicket" means to dismiss a batter. A bowler's figures are recorded as wickets taken, and a team's innings ends when ten wickets have fallen (in eleven-a-side cricket). Common methods of dismissal involving the wicket itself include bowled (the ball hits the stumps) and run out (the fielding side breaks the wicket while the batter is out of their ground).

Other uses

"Wicket" is also used colloquially for the pitch ("a slow wicket", "a turning wicket"), and for a batting partnership: two batters batting together are said to be "at the wicket", and their partnership is described by its wicket number (first wicket, second wicket, and so on). In the Laws of Cricket, "wicket" additionally refers to the act of the wicket-keeper standing behind the stumps.
