International Force East Timor

International Force East Timor (INTERFET)
Active: 20 September 1999 – 28 February 2000
Lead nation: Australia
Commander: Major General Peter Cosgrove
Peak strength: ~11,500 personnel from 22 nations

The International Force East Timor (INTERFET) was a multinational peacemaking task force, organized and led by Australia, that intervened in East Timor from September 1999 to February 2000 to address the humanitarian and security crisis following the territory's independence referendum.

Background

East Timor, occupied by Indonesia since 1975, voted overwhelmingly for independence in the UN-supervised referendum of 30 August 1999. Pro-Indonesian militias, supported by elements of the Indonesian military, responded with a campaign of violence that killed over a thousand people, displaced hundreds of thousands, and destroyed much of the territory's infrastructure.

Deployment

Authorized by UN Security Council Resolution 1264 and with Indonesia's consent under international pressure, INTERFET began deploying on 20 September 1999 under Major General Peter Cosgrove. Troops came from 22 nations, with major contingents from Australia, New Zealand, Thailand, the Philippines, and others. The force restored security, protected humanitarian relief, and supported the return of refugees with remarkably few casualties.

Transition

On 28 February 2000 INTERFET handed over to the United Nations Transitional Administration in East Timor (UNTAET), which governed the territory until independence on 20 May 2002. INTERFET is frequently cited as a model of regional coalition peace enforcement and as a turning point in Australian defence policy.
