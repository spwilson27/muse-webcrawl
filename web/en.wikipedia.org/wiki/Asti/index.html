Asti

Country: Italy
Region: Piedmont
Province: Asti (AT)
Elevation: 123 m
Population: approx. 74,000

Asti's skyline, with its medieval towers and cathedral.

Asti (Piedmontese: Ast) is a city and commune of about 74,000 inhabitants in the Piedmont region of northwestern Italy, about 55 kilometres east of Turin in the valley of the Tanaro River. It is the capital of the province of Asti and is renowned worldwide for its sparkling wines, Asti Spumante and Moscato d'Asti.

History

The site was settled by the Ligurian Statielli people and became a Roman town known as Hasta Pompeia. In the Middle Ages Asti grew into one of the most powerful free communes of northern Italy, its bankers and merchants active across Europe, and it competed with rivals such as Alba and Alessandria. The city later passed among the Visconti, the House of Orléans and the House of Savoy, under whom it remained until Italian unification. Its medieval wealth is still visible in dozens of towers and fortified houses, including the Torre Troyana.

Main sights

Asti Cathedral, dedicated to Santa Maria Assunta, is one of the largest Gothic churches in Piedmont, built mainly in the 13th–14th centuries. Other landmarks include the Romanesque-Gothic church of San Secondo, housing the banner of the Palio, the crypt of Sant'Anastasio, and Palazzo Alfieri, birthplace of the dramatist Vittorio Alfieri.

Palio and wine

Each September the city stages the Palio di Asti, a bareback horse race around the triangular Piazza Alfieri, first documented in the 13th century and among the oldest in Italy. The surrounding Monferrato and Langhe hills produce Barbera d'Asti, Moscato d'Asti and Asti Spumante, and the vineyard landscapes of the area are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
