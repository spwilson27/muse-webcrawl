The Joint Institute for Nuclear Research (JINR; Russian: Объединённый институт ядерных исследований, ОИЯИ) is an intergovernmental research institute in Dubna, north of Moscow, Russia. Founded in 1956 by eleven founding states, principally the Soviet Union and its allies, it was modelled in part as an Eastern counterpart to CERN. The institute is internationally known for its role in the discovery and synthesis of new superheavy chemical elements.

History

JINR was established by an agreement signed in Moscow on 26 March 1956, building on two Soviet Academy institutes at Dubna that had operated the world's most powerful synchrocyclotron of the time. Membership grew to 18 member states plus associate members, though several Eastern European countries withdrew after the end of the Cold War and again following Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine. Bruno Pontecorvo and other prominent physicists worked at Dubna during the Cold War.

Research

The institute comprises several laboratories covering elementary particle physics, nuclear physics, neutron physics, condensed matter, theoretical physics, information technologies and radiation biology. Its flagship facilities include the Nuclotron superconducting synchrotron, the IBR-2 pulsed neutron reactor, cyclotrons for heavy-ion physics, and the Superheavy Element Factory, which began operation in 2020.

Element discoveries

Dubna teams are credited — sometimes jointly with laboratories in the United States and Germany, and sometimes amid priority disputes — with the synthesis of elements including nobelium (102), lawrencium (103, shared credit), rutherfordium (104), dubnium (105, named after Dubna), seaborgium (106), bohrium (107), flerovium (114) and livermorium (116, with Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory), moscovium (115), tennessine (117) and oganesson (118). The Flerov Laboratory's collaboration with American groups completed the seventh row of the periodic table.
