Art gallery


Inside a contemporary art gallery.

An art gallery is a room or a building in which visual art is displayed. In Western cultures from the mid-15th century, a gallery was any long, narrow covered passage along a wall, first used in the sense of a place for art in the 1590s. Two broad types are distinguished: the public art gallery or art museum, which exhibits works from a permanent collection for the benefit of the public, and the commercial gallery, which sells the work of artists it represents.

Commercial galleries

A commercial gallery sells art, typically taking a commission of around 50 percent on sales of work by the artists on its roster (the "primary market"). Galleries develop long-term relationships with artists, mounting regular solo and group exhibitions, promoting their work to collectors and museums, placing works in important collections, and representing them at art fairs such as Art Basel and Frieze. Major international galleries, sometimes called "mega-galleries", include Gagosian, David Zwirner, Hauser & Wirth and Pace, which operate spaces in several cities.

The modern commercial gallery system took shape in 19th-century Paris and London around dealer-critics such as Paul Durand-Ruel, who championed the Impressionists, and was developed in 20th-century New York by figures such as Leo Castelli. Since the 1960s the characteristic display style of the commercial gallery has been the "white cube": plain white walls, polished floors and even lighting intended to isolate the artwork from context.

Public galleries

Public art galleries and art museums, such as the National Gallery in London, the Uffizi in Florence and the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, hold collections in trust for the public, funded by governments, endowments and donors. Many began as royal or aristocratic collections opened to the public in the 18th and 19th centuries.

Other forms

Artist-run spaces and non-profit galleries exhibit work outside the commercial system; "vanity galleries" charge artists fees to exhibit and are generally frowned upon; and online galleries and virtual exhibitions have expanded rapidly since the 2010s. In architecture, the word gallery retains its older senses: a raised platform, a balcony in a theatre, or a long passage, as in the gallery of a church or palace.
