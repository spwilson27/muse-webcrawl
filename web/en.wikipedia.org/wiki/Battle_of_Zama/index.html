Battle of Zama

Battle of Zama
Date: 19 October 202 BC (traditional)
Location: Zama, near Carthage (modern Tunisia)
Combatants: Roman Republic vs Carthage
Commanders: Scipio Africanus vs Hannibal Barca
Result: Decisive Roman victory; end of the Second Punic War
[Image: Hannibal's war elephants charging the Roman lines at Zama, 16th-century engraving style depiction]

The Battle of Zama, fought in 202 BC in North Africa, was the final and decisive battle of the Second Punic War. A Roman army under Publius Cornelius Scipio defeated the Carthaginians commanded by Hannibal Barca, ending Carthage's challenge to Rome and confirming Roman supremacy in the western Mediterranean. Scipio received the honorific name "Africanus" for the victory.

Background

After defeating Carthaginian armies in Spain and invading Africa in 204 BC, Scipio won at Utica and the Great Plains and allied with the Numidian prince Masinissa, whose cavalry deserted Carthage. Carthage recalled Hannibal from Italy, where he had campaigned for fifteen years. The two generals famously met before the battle; negotiations failed, and both prepared for a decisive engagement on the plain of Zama.

The battle

Hannibal deployed some 80 war elephants in front, with mercenary, Carthaginian and veteran infantry in three successive lines. Scipio arranged his maniples in columns, leaving lanes through which the elephants could pass harmlessly, and his trumpets panicked many beasts back onto their own lines. The infantry fight was hard and even until the Roman and Numidian cavalry under Laelius and Masinissa, having driven off the Carthaginian horse, returned to strike Hannibal's veterans in the rear. Carthaginian losses were heavy; Polybius gives some 20,000 killed.

Aftermath

The peace of 201 BC stripped Carthage of its fleet, its overseas possessions and its right to make war without Roman permission, and imposed a huge indemnity. Hannibal, who urged acceptance, later fled into exile. Rome emerged as the dominant power of the Mediterranean world.
