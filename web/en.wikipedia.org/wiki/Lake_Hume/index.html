Lake Hume is a large reservoir on the Murray River in south-eastern Australia, formed by the Hume Dam near the twin cities of Albury (New South Wales) and Wodonga (Victoria). It is the principal water-storage and regulation structure of the Murray–Darling basin's River Murray system.

History

Construction of the Hume Dam began in 1919 under the River Murray Waters Agreement and was completed in 1936; the storage was enlarged in the 1950s and 1960s, submerging the township of old Tallangatta, which was relocated about 8 km west. The reservoir is named after the explorer Hamilton Hume, who passed through the area in 1824. At full supply it holds roughly six times the volume of Sydney Harbour.

Use

Lake Hume regulates flows for irrigation in Victoria and New South Wales, urban water supplies, and hydroelectric generation at the Hume Power Station (about 50–60 MW). It is a popular recreation area for boating, water skiing and fishing, particularly for Murray cod and golden perch. During drought, falling levels expose the stumps and foundations of the drowned old Tallangatta and surrounding farmland.
