Macitentan, sold under the brand name Opsumit, is an orally administered dual endothelin receptor antagonist developed by Actelion and used for the treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). It blocks both the ETA and ETB receptors for endothelin-1, a potent vasoconstrictor, thereby reducing pulmonary vascular resistance.

Medical uses

Macitentan is approved to delay progression of pulmonary arterial hypertension and reduce hospitalisation in adults with WHO functional class II or III disease. The pivotal SERAPHIN trial, a long-term event-driven study, showed that macitentan 10 mg once daily reduced the composite endpoint of morbidity and mortality (death, atrial septostomy, lung transplantation, initiation of intravenous or subcutaneous prostanoids, or worsening of PAH) by about 45 percent compared with placebo. It is given once daily by mouth.

Macitentan is also combined with tadalafil in a single-tablet regimen (sold as Opsynvi in some markets) approved for PAH.

Adverse effects

Common adverse effects include anaemia, headache, nasopharyngitis, bronchitis, urinary tract infection, and peripheral oedema. Like other endothelin receptor antagonists, macitentan is teratogenic and is contraindicated in pregnancy; in the United States it was distributed under a restricted programme (REMS) requiring pregnancy testing and contraception. It may cause fluid retention and a fall in haemoglobin, and liver enzyme monitoring is recommended, although it has less hepatotoxicity than the earlier antagonist bosentan, from whose structure it was derived.

Pharmacology

Macitentan is metabolised in the liver, mainly via CYP3A4, to an active metabolite (aprocitentan, ACT-132577) that contributes to its effect and allows once-daily dosing. Aprocitentan itself has been developed as a separate drug for resistant hypertension.
