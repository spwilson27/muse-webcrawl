Leopold Anthony Stokowski (18 April 1882 – 13 September 1977) was a British-born American conductor of Polish descent. One of the leading conductors of the early and mid-20th century, he is best known for his long association with the Philadelphia Orchestra, for his lush "Stokowski sound," and for appearing as himself in Walt Disney's film Fantasia (1940).

Career

Stokowski studied at the Royal College of Music and Oxford. After posts in New York, he became music director of the Cincinnati Symphony and then, in 1912, of the Philadelphia Orchestra, which he built into one of the world's finest ensembles over 29 years. He gave the American premieres of many major works, including Mahler's Eighth Symphony and Stravinsky's The Rite of Spring (as a staged ballet), and made pioneering recordings and radio broadcasts.

Famous for conducting without a baton and for rescoring orchestral seating and repertoire, Stokowski made celebrated orchestral transcriptions of Bach, including the Toccata and Fugue in D minor heard in Fantasia. Later he conducted the Houston Symphony, co-founded the American Symphony Orchestra in 1962, and continued recording into his nineties.

Personal life and legacy

Stokowski cultivated an air of mystery about his background, sometimes affecting a Slavic accent despite his London birth. He was married three times, including to the heiress Gloria Vanderbilt. His recordings and broadcasts did much to popularise classical music with a mass audience.
