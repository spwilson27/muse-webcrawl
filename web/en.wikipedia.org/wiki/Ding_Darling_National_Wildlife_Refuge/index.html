J. N. "Ding" Darling National Wildlife Refuge

Location: Sanibel Island, Lee County, Florida, United States
Area: approx. 6,400 acres (2,600 ha)
Established: 1945
Governing body: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service

The J. N. "Ding" Darling National Wildlife Refuge is part of the United States National Wildlife Refuge System, located in southwestern Florida on Sanibel Island in the Gulf of Mexico. The refuge is named after the popular Pulitzer Prize–winning cartoonist and conservationist Jay Norwood "Ding" Darling, who was instrumental in its founding and in the creation of the Federal Duck Stamp programme.

Description

The refuge covers roughly 6,400 acres (about 2,600 hectares), more than half of Sanibel Island's land area, comprising mangrove forest, subtidal seagrass beds, cordgrass marshes, and hardwood hammocks. It was established in 1945 as the Sanibel National Wildlife Refuge and renamed in Darling's honour in 1967. The refuge is famous for its migratory bird populations — more than 245 bird species have been recorded — including roseate spoonbills, herons, egrets, white ibis, osprey, and the mangrove cuckoo. West Indian manatees, American alligators, bobcats, and river otters are also found there.

Visiting

Wildlife Drive, a one-way scenic road of about four miles, is open to vehicles, bicycles and pedestrians (and closed on Fridays) and is the most popular way to view wintering birds at low tide. The refuge also offers walking and paddling trails, a visitor and education center, and fishing. Hurricane Ian in September 2022 caused extensive damage to Sanibel and the refuge, which has since been undergoing habitat and infrastructure recovery.
