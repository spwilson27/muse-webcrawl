Labrador is a geographic and cultural region within the Canadian province of Newfoundland and Labrador. It is the primarily continental portion of the province, constituting most of the eastern coast of the Labrador Peninsula, and is separated from the island of Newfoundland by the Strait of Belle Isle. With an area of about 294,330 square kilometres, Labrador is larger than the island portion but home to fewer than 30,000 people.

Geography

Labrador borders Quebec to the west and south, and its northern tip at Cape Chidley faces Ungava Bay and Hudson Strait. The Torngat Mountains in the north contain Mount Caubvick, the highest point in mainland eastern Canada. Major waterways include the Churchill River, site of the Churchill Falls hydroelectric generating station, one of the largest underground powerhouses in the world. The coastline is deeply indented with fjords and dotted with fishing communities called outports.

People and history

Indigenous peoples, including the Innu and the Labrador Inuit (whose homeland, Nunatsiavut, was established as an autonomous area in 2005), have inhabited the region for thousands of years. Norse explorers reached the coast around the year 1000, and Basque whalers operated at Red Bay in the 16th century. The boundary between Labrador and Quebec was settled by the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in 1927, a decision Quebec has never formally accepted. Labrador's economy rests on iron-ore mining in the west (Labrador City and Wabush), hydroelectricity, fishing, and the military air base at Happy Valley-Goose Bay.
