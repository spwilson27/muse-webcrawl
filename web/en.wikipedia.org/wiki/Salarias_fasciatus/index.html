Salarias fasciatus

Salarias fasciatus (Bloch, 1786)
Family: Blenniidae
Common names: jewelled blenny, lawnmower blenny
Habitat: Indo-Pacific reefs

Salarias fasciatus, commonly known as the jewelled blenny or lawnmower blenny, is a species of combtooth blenny found in the Indo-Pacific, from the Red Sea and East Africa to Samoa and the Great Barrier Reef. It inhabits shallow reef flats and lagoons, typically at depths of up to 8 metres.

Description and behaviour

The species reaches about 14 cm in length, with mottled brown patterning that provides camouflage among rocks and coral rubble. It is chiefly herbivorous, scraping filamentous algae and detritus from hard surfaces — behaviour that earns it the aquarium nickname "lawnmower blenny".

Aquarium trade

It is popular in marine aquaria for algae control, though individuals can become territorial and may nip at corals or clam mantles.
