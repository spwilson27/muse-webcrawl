Dynamic structure factor

In condensed matter physics, the dynamic structure factor (or dynamical structure factor), denoted S(q, ω), is a mathematical function that contains information about inter-particle correlations and their time evolution. It is a generalization of the (static) structure factor that considers correlations in both space and time.

Definition

The dynamic structure factor is the Fourier transform in space and time of the van Hove function G(r, t), the probability of finding a particle at position r at time t given a particle at the origin at time zero. It can be split into coherent and incoherent parts corresponding to distinct and self correlations.

Measurement

Experimentally, the dynamic structure factor can be measured directly by inelastic neutron scattering or inelastic X-ray scattering; the measured differential cross-section is proportional to S(q, ω), where ħq and ħω are the momentum and energy transferred in the scattering event. Its peaks reveal collective excitations such as phonons and magnons, as in the classic studies of liquid helium.
