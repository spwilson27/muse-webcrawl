Japanese school uniform

[Image: Japanese school uniforms]

The Japanese school uniform is modelled on European-style naval uniforms and was first introduced in Japan in the late nineteenth century. Today, most public Japanese secondary schools have adopted a distinct uniform for their students. The two best-known styles are the boys' gakuran and the girls' sailor fuku.

History

Japan's first school uniform was introduced at the Imperial University (now the University of Tokyo) in the 1880s, modelled on the uniforms of the Imperial Japanese Navy. The sailor suit for girls, introduced in the 1920s, was inspired by the uniforms worn by British Royal Navy personnel and by the dresses of European schoolchildren.

Styles

The gakuran is a dark, high-collared jacket with brass buttons, worn with matching trousers, and derived from Prussian army cadet uniforms. The sailor fuku consists of a blouse with a sailor collar, a ribbon or neckerchief, and a pleated skirt. In recent decades many schools have adopted blazers, tartan skirts, and other Western-style variations, and some allow students a choice of trousers or skirts regardless of gender.

Cultural significance

Japanese school uniforms are iconic in popular culture, appearing frequently in anime and manga. They are also popular as fashion items and costumes, and manufacturers market them abroad. Rules governing uniforms — including skirt length, hairstyles, and accessories — have been debated, with a number of schools relaxing restrictions in recent years.
