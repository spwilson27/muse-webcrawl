John Nash

John Forbes Nash Jr. (June 13, 1928 – May 23, 2015) was an American mathematician who made fundamental contributions to game theory, real algebraic geometry, differential geometry, and partial differential equations. He shared the 1994 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for the Nash equilibrium and received the Abel Prize in 2015. His life inspired the book and film A Beautiful Mind.

Work

Nash's 28-page Princeton dissertation (1950) introduced the Nash equilibrium for non-cooperative games. He also proved the Nash embedding theorem for Riemannian manifolds, solved problems in parabolic PDEs (Nash–Moser theorem, with De Giorgi's independent work), and contributed to Hilbert's nineteenth problem.

Illness and later life

From 1959 Nash experienced schizophrenia, with hospitalisations and delusions interrupting his career for decades. He gradually recovered from the 1980s, returned to work at Princeton, and married Alicia Lardé. He and Alicia died in a taxi crash on the New Jersey Turnpike returning from the Abel Prize ceremony in Norway.
