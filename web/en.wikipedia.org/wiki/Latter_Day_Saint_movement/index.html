Latter Day Saint movement

FounderJoseph Smith
Origin1820s–1830, western New York, United States
Key textsBook of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, Pearl of Great Price
Largest bodyThe Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

The Latter Day Saint movement (also called the LDS movement or Mormon movement) is a collection of independent church groups that trace their origins to the Church of Christ founded by Joseph Smith in the United States in 1830. Adherents believe Smith was a prophet who restored the original Christian church, and they accept the Book of Mormon as scripture alongside the Bible.

History

Smith reported visions beginning in the 1820s, published the Book of Mormon in 1830, and organized a church the same year. After Smith was killed by a mob in Illinois in 1844, a succession crisis split the movement. The largest faction followed Brigham Young west to the Salt Lake Valley, while others remained in the Midwest, eventually forming bodies such as the Community of Christ.

Denominations

The movement today comprises dozens of churches. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, headquartered in Salt Lake City, Utah, is by far the largest, with members worldwide. Other notable bodies include the Community of Christ and various smaller fundamentalist groups, which differ on doctrine and practice.
