Actuarial science

Actuarial science is the discipline that applies mathematical and statistical methods to assess risk in insurance, pensions, finance and other industries. Practitioners — actuaries — combine probability theory, statistics, financial mathematics and economics to model uncertain future events, especially those concerning mortality, morbidity, property damage and investment returns, and to calculate the premiums and reserves needed to cover them.

Actuarial science applies probability and statistics to quantify financial risk.

History

The foundations were laid in the seventeenth century with the development of probability theory by Pascal and Fermat, John Graunt's analysis of the London bills of mortality (1662), and Edmond Halley's construction of the first life table (1693). The first modern actuarial role is generally dated to 1762, when James Dodson and others founded the Society for Equitable Assurances on Lives and Survivorships in London, the first insurer to set premiums scientifically by age. Professional bodies followed: the Institute of Actuaries in London (1848) and the Faculty of Actuaries in Scotland (1856), now merged as the Institute and Faculty of Actuaries.

Fields of practice

  • Life insurance and pensions — mortality and longevity modelling, valuation of liabilities, pricing annuities.
  • General (property and casualty) insurance — claims reserving, catastrophe modelling, premium rating.
  • Health insurance — morbidity rates and the cost of medical care.
  • Investments and enterprise risk management — asset–liability modelling and solvency assessment.

Methods

Core tools include survival models and life tables, compound interest and present-value calculations, generalised linear models for pricing, stochastic simulation, and credibility theory for combining individual and population experience. Regulation such as the European Union's Solvency II framework requires insurers to hold risk-based capital, making actuarial functions legally central to insurance companies in many jurisdictions.

Qualification

Actuaries qualify through demanding professional examinations administered by bodies such as the Institute and Faculty of Actuaries (UK), the Society of Actuaries and the Casualty Actuarial Society (US), typically taking several years while working in the field. University degrees in actuarial science may grant exemptions from some examinations.
