Public housing in Canada

Public housing in Canada — more broadly termed social housing — encompasses housing owned or subsidized by governments and non-profit providers for low- and moderate-income households. Roughly 5% of Canadian households live in some form of subsidized housing.

History

Federal involvement began with postwar housing shortages. The Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC), founded in 1946, funded large public housing projects from the 1950s through the 1970s, often on the model of tower blocks and cleared "slum" districts — as with Regent Park in Toronto (begun 1948), Canada's oldest and largest public housing community. From the 1970s, policy shifted toward smaller mixed-income, non-profit, and co-operative housing.

Devolution and retrenchment

In the 1990s the federal government largely withdrew from funding new social housing and transferred administration of the existing stock to provinces and territories; Ontario in turn downloaded it to municipalities. Aging buildings accumulated large repair backlogs, a problem highlighted in Toronto Community Housing, one of North America's largest public landlords.

Recent policy

The National Housing Strategy (2017) renewed federal investment in affordable and community housing, and the National Housing Act framework continues to support rent-geared-to-income units for the lowest-income households.
