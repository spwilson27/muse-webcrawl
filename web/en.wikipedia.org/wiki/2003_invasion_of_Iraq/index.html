The 2003 invasion of Iraq was the opening phase of the Iraq War, conducted by a United States-led coalition against the government of Saddam Hussein. Beginning with air strikes on 20 March 2003 (Iraqi time) and a ground invasion from Kuwait, coalition forces overran Iraq in about three weeks; Baghdad fell on 9 April and President George W. Bush declared the end of major combat operations on 1 May 2003.

Background and rationale

The United States and United Kingdom justified the invasion principally by alleging that Iraq possessed weapons of mass destruction (WMD) and had links to al-Qaeda. UN weapons inspectors under Hans Blix had returned to Iraq under Security Council Resolution 1441 but found no stockpiles; the coalition proceeded without a further UN resolution expressly authorising force, a legality dispute that continues. No WMD stockpiles were found after the invasion, and official inquiries (including the US Iraq Survey Group's Duelfer Report) concluded Iraq's programmes had effectively ended in the 1990s.

Course of the invasion

The ground campaign combined a main drive on Baghdad by the US Army's V Corps and the 1st Marine Expeditionary Force, British operations around Basra in the south, and special forces and Kurdish peshmerga in the north. Key engagements included the battles of Nasiriyah, Najaf and Karbala. On 9 April US Marines entered central Baghdad and the regime collapsed; Saddam Hussein went into hiding and was captured in December 2003.

Aftermath

The speedy conventional victory gave way to a prolonged occupation, insurgency and sectarian civil war. The invasion remains one of the most controversial military actions of the modern era.
