Hubble's law

Hubble–Lemaître law
Formula: v = H₀ × D
H₀ (Hubble constant): ≈ 70 km/s/Mpc
Named after: Edwin Hubble, Georges Lemaître
Implication: Expansion of the universe

Hubble's law, also known as the Hubble–Lemaître law, is the observation in physical cosmology that galaxies are moving away from Earth at speeds proportional to their distance. In other words, the farther a galaxy is, the faster it moves away. This is expressed as v = H₀D, where H₀ is the Hubble constant.

The law was first derived by Georges Lemaître in 1927 and published observationally by Edwin Hubble in 1929, using distance measurements by Hubble and redshifts largely from Vesto Slipher. It is considered the first observational basis for the expansion of the universe and supports the Big Bang theory.

Interpretation

The recession is not motion through space so much as the expansion of space itself, described by general relativity and the Friedmann equations. The inverse of H₀ gives an approximate age of the universe. Precise measurement of H₀ remains contested — the "Hubble tension" between early-universe (≈67) and local distance-ladder (≈73 km/s/Mpc) values.
