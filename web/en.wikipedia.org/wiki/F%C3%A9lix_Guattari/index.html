Félix Guattari

Born30 April 1930, Villeneuve-les-Sablons, France
Died29 August 1992, Cour-Cheverny, France
OccupationPsychoanalyst, philosopher, activist

Pierre-Félix Guattari (30 April 1930 – 29 August 1992) was a French psychoanalyst, philosopher and radical activist. Best known for his collaborations with Gilles Deleuze, including Anti-Oedipus (1972) and A Thousand Plateaus (1980), he developed the concepts of schizoanalysis, deterritorialisation and ecosophy.

Life and work

Guattari trained under Jacques Lacan but broke with orthodox psychoanalysis, working for decades at the experimental La Borde clinic, where he pioneered institutional psychotherapy. Politically active from his youth, he participated in far-left movements and in the upheavals of May 1968.

Thought

With Deleuze, Guattari attacked Freudian and Lacanian psychoanalysis, arguing in Capitalism and Schizophrenia that desire is a productive, machinic force rather than a lack. In his later solo work, notably The Three Ecologies (1989), he extended his analysis to environmental, social and mental ecologies. He died of a heart attack in 1992.
