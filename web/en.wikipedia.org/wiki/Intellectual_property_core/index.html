Semiconductor intellectual property core

A semiconductor intellectual property core (IP core or IP block) is a reusable unit of logic, cell, or integrated circuit layout design that is the intellectual property of one party and is licensed to others for incorporation into their own chip designs. IP cores are fundamental to modern system-on-a-chip (SoC) design, in which processor cores, memory controllers, interfaces, and accelerators are assembled from pre-designed blocks.

Types

  • Soft cores – delivered as synthesizable register transfer level (RTL) code, usually in Verilog or VHDL, or as a generic gate-level netlist; flexible but performance and area depend on the target process.
  • Firm cores – partially optimized, sometimes including placement information.
  • Hard cores – delivered as fixed physical layout (e.g., GDSII) optimized for a specific fabrication process; predictable timing and area but not portable between processes.

Licensing

IP is commonly licensed for an up-front fee plus royalties per chip produced. Major vendors include Arm (processor cores), Synopsys and Cadence (interface and foundation IP), and Imagination Technologies (graphics). The business model lets fabless companies build complex chips without designing every block in-house.

Standards and verification

Interoperability standards such as AMBA bus protocols and the IP-XACT packaging format, together with verification IP and hardware description conventions, enable integration of blocks from multiple sources.
