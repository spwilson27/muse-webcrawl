The Trossachs

Location: Stirling, Scotland

Coordinates: approx. 56.23° N, 4.42° W

National park: Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park

Principal lochs: Loch Katrine, Loch Achray, Loch Venachar

The Trossachs (Scottish Gaelic: Na Tròiseachan) is a small woodland glen and popular tourist area in the Stirling council area of Scotland. The name originally applied only to a small wooded valley between Loch Katrine and Loch Achray, around the village of Brig o' Turk, but is now generally applied to the wider area of hills, lochs and glens lying between Ben A'an to the north and Ben Venue to the south, east of Loch Lomond. The area forms the eastern part of the Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park, established in 2002.

Geography

The Trossachs are bounded by Callander to the east and Aberfoyle to the south, and form part of the Queen Elizabeth Forest Park. Loch Katrine, the principal loch, supplies Glasgow with much of its drinking water; the steamship Sir Walter Scott has sailed on it since 1900. The Great Trossachs Path, a long-distance trail, runs through the area from Callander to Inversnaid on Loch Lomond.

Tourism and literature

The Trossachs became famous in the early nineteenth century through Sir Walter Scott's narrative poem The Lady of the Lake (1810), set around Loch Katrine, and his novel Rob Roy (1817), whose hero, the outlaw Rob Roy MacGregor, was born at Glengyle at the head of Loch Katrine and is buried at Balquhidder. The resulting influx of visitors made the Trossachs one of Scotland's first tourist destinations; the Wordsworths and many other writers followed. The area is sometimes described as "the Highlands in miniature".
