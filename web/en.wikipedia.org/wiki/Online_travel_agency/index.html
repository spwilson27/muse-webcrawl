Online travel agency

An online travel agency (OTA) is a travel website or application that sells tourism-related services to consumers, including flights, hotels, car rentals, cruises and package holidays. OTAs aggregate inventory from suppliers and allow customers to search, compare and book in a single transaction.

An online travel agency search results page

History

Online travel booking emerged in the mid-1990s as airlines and hotels began selling directly over the World Wide Web. Microsoft launched Expedia in 1996, and Travelocity, built on the Sabre reservation system, appeared the same year. Priceline.com (1998) popularised opaque "name your own price" inventory, while Booking.com, founded in the Netherlands in 1996, became the dominant hotel-focused OTA. Metasearch engines such as Kayak and Skyscanner later added price comparison across OTAs and suppliers.

Business model

OTAs typically earn commissions from hotels and other suppliers, commonly 15–25 per cent of the room rate for accommodation, or operate under a merchant model in which they set the retail price. Flight bookings yield smaller margins but drive traffic for higher-margin products. The sector is dominated by large groups including Booking Holdings and Expedia Group, alongside regional players such as Trip.com Group in Asia.

Impact and criticism

OTAs lowered distribution costs and increased price transparency, but have been criticised for rate-parity clauses restricting hotels from offering lower prices elsewhere, pressure-selling interface design, and drip pricing. Several competition authorities in Europe have restricted or banned wide parity clauses.
