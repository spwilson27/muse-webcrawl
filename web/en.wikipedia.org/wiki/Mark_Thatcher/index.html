Sir Mark Thatcher, 2nd Baronet (born 15 August 1953) is a British businessman. He is the son of Margaret Thatcher, who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1979 to 1990, and her husband Denis Thatcher. His business dealings repeatedly attracted controversy during and after his mother's premiership, most seriously in 2004–05, when he pleaded guilty in South Africa in connection with funding a planned coup in Equatorial Guinea.

Early life

Thatcher and his twin sister Carol were born in 1953. He was educated at Belmont School and Harrow School, and moved to Hong Kong in 1977 to work in business, later becoming involved in motor racing; in 1982 he was lost for several days in the Sahara Desert during the Paris–Dakar Rally before being found by an Algerian military search plane.

Business controversies

While his mother was Prime Minister, Thatcher acted as an intermediary on arms and construction deals in the Middle East, including the Al-Yamamah arms deal with Saudi Arabia and a cement plant contract in Oman. Official inquiries found no evidence that he had improperly used his mother's position, but the reports and press scrutiny made him a persistent political embarrassment.

Equatorial Guinea coup plot

In August 2004 Thatcher was arrested at his home in Cape Town, South Africa, and charged with contravening the Regulation of Foreign Military Assistance Act for allegedly financing the attempted coup in Equatorial Guinea led by Simon Mann. In January 2005 he pleaded guilty to negligently investing in an aircraft company whose helicopters were to be used by the plotters, receiving a four-year suspended sentence and a fine of 3 million rand. He subsequently left South Africa and has lived in Monaco and elsewhere.

Baronetcy

On his father's death in 2003 he succeeded to the Thatcher baronetcy of Scotney, a hereditary title created for Denis Thatcher in 1990 — the last baronetcy created in the United Kingdom.
