Visual prosthesis

A visual prosthesis, often called a bionic eye, is an experimental medical device intended to restore functional vision in people blinded by diseases of the eye, particularly degenerative conditions such as retinitis pigmentosa and age-related macular degeneration, in which the photoreceptor cells of the retina die while much of the downstream visual pathway survives. A camera captures the visual scene; the image is processed and converted into patterns of electrical stimulation delivered to surviving retinal neurons, the optic nerve, or the visual cortex, producing perceived spots of light (phosphenes) that the brain can learn to interpret as shapes.

Approaches

  • Epiretinal implants place an electrode array on the inner surface of the retina, stimulating ganglion cells. The best-known example is the Argus II system (Second Sight), approved in Europe (2011) and by the US FDA (2013) for retinitis pigmentosa.
  • Subretinal implants sit where the photoreceptors were, using light-sensitive microphotodiode arrays or photovoltaic pixels (e.g., the Alpha IMS and PRIMA devices) to stimulate bipolar cells, preserving more natural retinal processing.
  • Suprachoroidal and intrascleral implants place electrodes outside the retina for safer surgery at the cost of resolution.
  • Cortical prostheses (such as the Orion system) bypass the eye entirely, stimulating the visual cortex directly, and could treat blindness from eye or optic-nerve damage.

Performance and limitations

Current devices offer only crude vision: arrays of tens to a few hundred electrodes produce perception of light patterns, edges, and large high-contrast objects, aiding navigation and object localisation rather than reading or face recognition. Research focuses on higher electrode counts, better encoding of images into stimulation patterns, and biocompatibility. The field was shaken when Second Sight discontinued the Argus II around 2020, leaving implanted users with unsupported devices — a cautionary case in neuroprosthetics ethics.
