Orchid Pavilion Gathering

The Orchid Pavilion Gathering (Chinese: 蘭亭集會, Lanting jìhui) was a cultural event held in 353 CE, on the third day of the third month of the Chinese calendar, at the Orchid Pavilion near Shaoxing, Zhejiang, during the Eastern Jin dynasty. The renowned calligrapher Wang Xizhi hosted forty-one other literati for the spring purification ceremony (Shangsi Festival), at which the guests composed poems while seated along a winding stream, with cups of wine floated down to them.

The event

Guests sat on both banks of a meandering brook. Cups of wine were set afloat on lotus leaves or in small lacquered cups; when a cup stopped in front of a guest, he was required to compose a poem, or drink as a forfeit. By the end of the day, 37 poems had been composed and collected. Wang Xizhi, reportedly slightly drunk, wrote a preface for the collection in semi-cursive script — the Lantingji Xu (蘭亭集序), "Preface to the Poems Composed at the Orchid Pavilion."

The Preface and its legacy

The Preface reflects on the pleasures of the gathering and the transience of life, moving from celebration to a meditation on mortality. In Chinese calligraphy it is considered the greatest work in semi-cursive ("running") script, and Wang Xizhi is revered as the Sage of Calligraphy. The original was so prized that, according to tradition, the Tang emperor Taizong obtained it by ruse and had it buried with him; only copies — most famously the "Shenlong version" — survive. The Orchid Pavilion gathering became an enduring model for literati culture in China, Japan, and Korea, inspiring countless later gatherings, paintings, and pavilion replicas.
