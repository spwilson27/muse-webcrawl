Pirmin

Saint Pirmin
Bornc. 660–670, possibly in Visigothic Spain or southern Gaul
Died3 November 753, Hornbach, Germany
Feast day3 November
Known forFounding monasteries; author of the Scarapsus; planting vineyards on Reichenau

Saint Pirmin (c. 660/670 – 3 November 753), also called Pirminius, was a Merovingian-era monk and missionary in the Alemannic and Frankish lands. He founded and reformed numerous monasteries in southwestern Germany and the Upper Rhine region, and is remembered as an apostle of the Alemanni.

Life

Probably of Visigothic or southern Frankish origin, Pirmin appears in the record as a wandering bishop-monk (chorepiscopus) supported by the Carolingian mayor Charles Martel and by Alemannic nobles seeking to consolidate Christianity and Frankish influence in the region. In 724 he founded the abbey on the island of Reichenau in Lake Constance, which became a great centre of learning; tradition credits him with clearing the island of snakes and planting its first vines. Expelled from Reichenau after a few years amid political tensions, he went on to found or restore monasteries including Murbach in Alsace, and others at Amorbach, Gengenbach and Schwarzach, ending his life at Hornbach in the Palatinate.

Works

Pirmin is credited with the Scarapsus (also called the Dicta Pirminii), a short summary of Christian doctrine and moral instruction written for missionaries and new converts. One of the earliest texts of its kind from the Frankish church, it includes a renunciation formula closely related to the text of the Apostles' Creed and was long used to reconstruct early forms of the creed.

Veneration

Pirmin's feast day is 3 November. He is the patron saint of the Palatinate, Alsace, the island of Reichenau, and the city of Pirmasens, which takes its name from him.
