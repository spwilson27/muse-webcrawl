Battle of Gulnabad

The Battle of Gulnabad was fought on 8 March 1722 between the invading Afghan army of Mahmud Hotak and the forces of Safavid Persia, near the village of Golnabad (Gulnabad), east of Isfahan. Although heavily outnumbered, the Afghans won a crushing victory over the Safavid imperial army, opening the road to Isfahan and leading within months to the siege of the capital and the fall of the Safavid dynasty.

Background

The Ghilzai Afghans of Kandahar had revolted against Safavid rule under Mirwais Hotak in 1709. His son Mahmud, having consolidated power and raided Kerman, resolved in 1721–22 on a full invasion of Persia, whose government under the ineffectual Shah Soltan Hoseyn was paralysed by court faction. The Safavid court raised a large but ill-coordinated army stiffened with Arab auxiliaries from the southwest under the Vali of Arabistan, and Georgian contingents were awaited. Command was divided between the grand vizier Mohammad Qoli Khan and the qurchi-bashi Rostam Khan, who distrusted each other.

The battle

The armies met at Gulnabad on 8 March 1722. The Safavid right wing, the Arab auxiliaries, charged the Afghan line and initially drove it back, breaking through to plunder the Afghan baggage; but Mahmud's Afghan cavalry counter-attacked the disordered Safavid centre and left. The Persians' large numbers proved a liability as units masked each other's fire and command collapsed. When Rostam Khan fell and the grand vizier's troops gave way, the army disintegrated; the Arabs, laden with plunder, were cut off and heavily defeated as they tried to escape. Persian losses are estimated between 5,000 and 15,000 killed, while Afghan losses were comparatively light.

Aftermath

The road to Isfahan lay open. Mahmud advanced cautiously and laid siege to the capital in March 1722; after a six-month siege in which tens of thousands starved, Shah Soltan Hoseyn surrendered his crown to Mahmud in October 1722, ending more than two centuries of Safavid rule. Afghan rule over Persia lasted until Nader Shah expelled the invaders in 1729–30.
