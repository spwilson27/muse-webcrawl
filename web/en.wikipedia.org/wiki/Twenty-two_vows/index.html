Twenty-two vows
Administered byB. R. Ambedkar
Date14 October 1956
PlaceDeekshabhoomi, Nagpur, India
ContextConversion to Navayana Buddhism

The Twenty-two vows (Marathi: २२ प्रतिज्ञा) are a set of vows administered by B. R. Ambedkar to his followers at their mass conversion from Hinduism to Buddhism at Deekshabhoomi, Nagpur, on 14 October 1956. The vows mark a decisive rejection of Hindu doctrines — including belief in the Hindu gods, the caste system, and the authority of the Vedas — and an affirmation of the principles of Navayana Buddhism. They remain central to the identity of Dalit Buddhist (Ambedkarite) communities in India, and converts continue to take them at conversion ceremonies today.

Contents

The vows include renunciations of belief in Brahma, Vishnu, and Mahesh (Shiva) and in Rama and Krishna as gods; rejection of the doctrine of divine incarnation and of the rituals of shraddha and ancestor offerings; and a declaration of disbelief in Hindu teachings on karma and rebirth as taught by Hinduism. Positive vows affirm belief in the Buddha and his Dhamma, commitment to equality and human dignity, following the Noble Eightfold Path and the ten paramitas, and showing compassion toward all living beings.

Significance

Ambedkar framed the vows as the doctrinal boundary of his reinterpretation of Buddhism, Navayana, distinct from Theravada, Mahayana, and Vajrayana. Roughly 380,000 followers converted with him at Nagpur, with many more in the following days. The vows are recited at Buddhist conversion ceremonies and commemorations of Dhamma Chakra Pravartan Din each year.
