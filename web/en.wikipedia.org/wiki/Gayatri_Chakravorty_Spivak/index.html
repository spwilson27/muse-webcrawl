Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak (born 24 February 1942) is an Indian scholar, literary theorist, and feminist critic. She is a University Professor at Columbia University, where she was a founding member of the Institute for Comparative Literature and Society. Best known for her essay "Can the Subaltern Speak?" (1988) and her translation of Jacques Derrida's Of Grammatology (1976), Spivak is one of the foundational figures of postcolonial theory.

Early life and education

Spivak was born in Calcutta and studied English at Presidency College, University of Calcutta, graduating with first-class honours. She earned her PhD in comparative literature at Cornell University in 1967, with a dissertation on W. B. Yeats supervised by Paul de Man. She taught at the University of Iowa, the University of Texas at Austin, Emory University, and the University of Pittsburgh before joining Columbia in 1991; in 2007 she became a University Professor, Columbia's highest faculty rank.

Work

Spivak's translation of Derrida's Of Grammatology, with its long critical preface, introduced French deconstruction to much of the English-speaking world. In "Can the Subaltern Speak?" she examined whether the subaltern — those outside structures of power and representation, particularly colonized women — can make their voices heard, or whether they are always spoken for by others. Drawing on the abolition of sati (widow self-immolation) in British India, she concluded that "the subaltern cannot speak" within the structures that claim to represent her, a claim that has been endlessly debated and reinterpreted.

Her later work includes A Critique of Postcolonial Reason (1999), Death of a Discipline (2003), and An Aesthetic Education in the Era of Globalization (2012). She has translated the fiction of Mahasweta Devi and worked for decades on rural education and teacher training in West Bengal and elsewhere. In 2012 she received the Kyoto Prize in Arts and Philosophy, and India awarded her the Padma Bhushan in 2013.
