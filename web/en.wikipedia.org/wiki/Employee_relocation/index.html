Employee relocation

Placeholder image: a household goods move

Employee relocation occurs when an employer directs an employee to work in another location, or when a new hire moves to take up a job. Employers typically support the move through a relocation package administered in-house or by a relocation management company (RMC), covering some or all of the costs of moving house.

Relocation packages

Packages vary by seniority and distance but commonly include shipment of household goods, travel and temporary living expenses, home-sale and home-purchase assistance (sometimes through a guaranteed buyout of the old home), house-hunting trips, spousal employment assistance, school search help, and a miscellaneous expense allowance. International assignments add immigration support, tax equalisation, language and cultural training, and cost-of-living adjustments.

Industry

The relocation industry is represented by bodies such as Worldwide ERC, and services are supplied by relocation management companies that coordinate van lines, real estate agents, mortgage providers and immigration lawyers on the employer's behalf. Policies are often tiered, and lump-sum payments have become increasingly common in place of fully managed moves.

Tax treatment

In the United States, the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 suspended the exclusion and deduction for moving expenses for most employees through 2025, making employer-paid relocation benefits taxable wages, frequently "grossed up" by the employer. Rules differ widely in other countries.
