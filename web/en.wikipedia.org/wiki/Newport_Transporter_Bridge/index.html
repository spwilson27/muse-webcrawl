Newport Transporter Bridge

The Newport Transporter Bridge crosses the River Usk in Newport, Wales. Completed in 1906, it is one of only eight transporter bridges remaining in use worldwide, and a Grade I listed structure. Instead of a conventional deck, vehicles and pedestrians cross on a suspended platform, or "gondola", which is pulled by cables from one bank to the other beneath a high steel truss.

Design and construction

The bridge was designed by the French engineer Ferdinand Arnodin and built between 1902 and 1906 at a cost of about £98,000. Its two towers rise 73.6 metres (242 ft), and the horizontal truss spans 471 metres (1,545 ft) between anchorages, with a main span of 387 metres. The height was necessary to let the tall-masted sailing ships of the day pass beneath to reach the wharves upstream. The gondola, carrying up to six cars or about 120 pedestrians, crosses in about 90 seconds.

History

The bridge replaced a ferry that became dangerous at low tide. It was promoted by the Newport Corporation and the Alexandra Docks company to give workers access to the John Lysaght steelworks on the east bank. The bridge has closed for several extended periods for restoration, most recently undergoing a major refurbishment in the 2020s.
