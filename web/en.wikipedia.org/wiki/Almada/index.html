Almada is a city and municipality in Portugal, located on the southern bank of the Tagus estuary opposite Lisbon, in the district of Setúbal. It forms part of the Lisbon metropolitan area and is connected to the capital by the 25 de Abril Bridge, which lands at Almada, and by ferry services across the Tagus.

History

The area was inhabited in prehistoric and Roman times and was a Moorish settlement — its name derives from the Arabic al-ma'dan, "the mine" — before being taken in the Christian reconquest of 1147, the year Lisbon also fell. Almada developed as a fishing and agricultural town, and in the twentieth century grew rapidly as a dormitory city for Lisbon and as a centre of shipbuilding and ship repair at the Lisnave yards in Margueira.

Landmarks

Almada's best-known landmark is the Sanctuary of Christ the King (Cristo Rei), a monumental statue of Christ overlooking the Tagus, inspired by Rio de Janeiro's Christ the Redeemer and inaugurated in 1959 after a vow made during the Second World War. Below it, the old town of Cacilhas is known for its seafood restaurants and ferry terminal. The municipality also includes the Costa da Caparica, a long Atlantic beach resort popular with surfers, and the protected fossil cliffs of the Paisagem Protegida da Arriba Fóssil.

Transport

Besides the bridge and ferries (Transtejo services from Cacilhas and Trafaria), Almada is served by the Metro Sul do Tejo light rail network, opened in 2007–2008, linking Cacilhas, the university campus at Caparica and the surrounding parishes.
