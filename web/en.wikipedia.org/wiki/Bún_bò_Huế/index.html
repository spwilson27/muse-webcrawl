Bún bò Huế

Bún bò Huế is a Vietnamese rice noodle (bún) dish with sliced beef (bò) and sometimes pork, originating from the city of Huế, the former imperial capital of Vietnam. The dish is admired for its balance of spicy, sour, salty, and sweet flavours, and its broth is distinguished by lemongrass and fermented shrimp paste.

Description

The broth is prepared by stewing beef and pork bones with lemongrass, seasoned with fish sauce and sugar, and coloured and heated with chilli oil and annatto. It is served over thick round rice noodles with sliced beef shank, chunks of oxtail, pig's knuckles or trotters, and congealed pig's blood cubes. Accompaniments include shredded banana blossom, water spinach, bean sprouts, Vietnamese coriander, lime, and perilla.

History

The dish developed in Huế, whose cuisine reflects its imperial court heritage and the region's taste for spicy food. In its home city it may be served in smaller bowls and sometimes without beef (as bún giò heo, with pork knuckle). After 1975 the dish spread with Vietnamese emigration and is now common throughout Vietnam and overseas, though versions outside Huế are often adapted in saltiness and heat.

Comparison with phở

Bún bò Huế is frequently compared with phở: it uses thicker, round noodles, a spicier and more robustly flavoured broth based on lemongrass and shrimp paste, and a wider variety of meats and offal, whereas phở broth is clearer and flavoured with charred ginger, onion, and spices such as star anise.
