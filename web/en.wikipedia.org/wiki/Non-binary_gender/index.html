Non-binary gender

Non-binary (also spelled nonbinary) and genderqueer are umbrella terms for gender identities that are not solely male or female. Non-binary identities fall under the transgender umbrella, as non-binary people typically identify with a gender that is different from their sex assigned at birth, although some non-binary people do not consider themselves transgender.

Definitions and identities

Non-binary people may identify as having two or more genders (bigender or trigender), having no gender (agender), moving between genders (genderfluid), or as a third gender distinct from male and female. Gender identity is separate from sexual or romantic orientation. Many non-binary people use the singular pronoun set they/them/theirs, though others use she/her, he/him, neopronouns, or multiple sets.

History

Gender roles outside a male–female binary have been recognized in many cultures historically, including the hijra of South Asia, Two-Spirit people in some Indigenous North American cultures, and the fa'afafine of Samoa. The term "genderqueer" emerged in the 1990s, and "non-binary" gained wider use in the 2010s as public awareness and legal recognition grew.

Legal recognition

A growing number of jurisdictions allow non-binary gender markers on identity documents, often as an "X" alongside M and F. Such recognition exists in countries including Canada, Germany, Australia, New Zealand, the Netherlands, India, Pakistan, and Nepal, and in numerous US states. The United States issued its first passport with an X gender marker in 2021.

Discrimination and health

Surveys such as the US Transgender Survey have reported elevated rates of discrimination, harassment, poverty, and adverse mental-health outcomes among non-binary people, alongside legal and institutional barriers in healthcare, employment, and identification.
