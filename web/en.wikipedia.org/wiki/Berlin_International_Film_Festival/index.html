The Berlin International Film Festival (German: Internationale Filmfestspiele Berlin), usually called the Berlinale, is a major international film festival held annually in Berlin, Germany. Founded in West Berlin in 1951, it is one of the "Big Three" European film festivals alongside Venice and Cannes.

History

The festival was created during the Cold War as a "showcase of the free world" in the divided city. Since 1978 it has been held in February. The Berlinale attracts around half a million admissions and hundreds of thousands of visitors, making it one of the largest publicly attended film festivals in the world.

Awards

The main competition awards the Golden Bear for best film and Silver Bears for directing, acting and other categories. Alongside the competition, sections such as Panorama, Forum and Generation showcase independent and experimental cinema. The European Film Market runs parallel as a major industry marketplace.
