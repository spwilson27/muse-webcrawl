Holographic weapon sight

A holographic weapon sight on a rifle

A holographic weapon sight or holographic diffraction sight is a non-magnifying gunsight that allows the user to look through a glass optical window and see a holographic reticle image superimposed at a distance on the field of view. The hologram of the reticle is built into the window and is illuminated by a laser diode.

History

The first-generation holographic sight was introduced by EOTech—then an ERIM subsidiary—at the 1996 SHOT Show under the trade name HoloSight. The sight used a laser transmission hologram recorded in three dimensions, allowing the reticle to be projected so that it appears at the plane of the target. EOTech holographic sights such as the 512, 552, and the EXPS series were adopted widely by military and law enforcement users, including US special operations forces alongside red dot sights such as the Aimpoint CompM2/M4.

How it works

A laser diode illuminates a holographic grating in the viewing window; the recorded holographic image of the reticle is reconstructed and projected so that it appears focused at a distance, superimposed on the target. Because the reticle is holographic, it remains usable even if part of the window is obscured or damaged—the shooter can still see a complete reticle through any remaining clear portion of the window that catches the laser illumination. Unlike simple red dot (reflector) sights, the holographic sight uses true holography and a laser rather than an LED reflected off a coated lens.

Parallax and usage

Like other reflector sights, holographic sights are designed to be effectively parallax-free at typical engagement distances when the reticle is centred. Common reticles in EOTech sights consist of a large 65-MOA circle surrounding a 1-MOA central dot, intended to combine rapid target acquisition with a precise aiming point. Magnifiers can be mounted behind the sight for longer-range use.
