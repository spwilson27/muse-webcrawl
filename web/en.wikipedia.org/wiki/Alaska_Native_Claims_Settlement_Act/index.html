Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act

The Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act (ANCSA), signed into law by President Richard Nixon on 18 December 1971, is the largest aboriginal land-claims settlement in United States history. It extinguished Alaska Native claims to most of the state in exchange for fee-simple title to about 44 million acres (178,000 km²) of land and a payment of $962.5 million, and it created thirteen Alaska Native Regional Corporations — and more than 200 village corporations — to own and manage the land and money on behalf of Native shareholders.

Background and terms

The Act was driven by the 1968 discovery of oil at Prudhoe Bay: construction of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline required clear title to a corridor crossing Native-claimed land. Under ANCSA, Alaska Natives enrolled as shareholders in the corporations, receiving shares that initially could not be sold. The regional corporations — including Arctic Slope, Sealaska, Cook Inlet Region, Inc., and Doyon, Limited — became major economic actors in resource development, government contracting and tourism; a thirteenth corporation for non-resident Natives received money but no land. An 1988 amendment package ("the 1991 amendments") addressed fears that shares would fall into non-Native hands when restrictions expired.
