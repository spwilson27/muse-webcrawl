Asian Highway Network

Total length: approx. 141,000 km (88,000 mi)
Countries: 32
Coordinating body: UNESCAP
Agreement signed: 2003 (in force 2005)

Routes of the Asian Highway Network, spanning the continent from Japan to Turkey.

The Asian Highway Network (AH) is a cooperative project of the United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific (UNESCAP) to improve and standardise road networks across Asia, broadly analogous to the European E-road network. The network comprises some 141,000 kilometres of highways crossing 32 member states, intended to promote international trade, tourism and regional integration.

History

The project was first endorsed by UNESCAP in 1959, but languished for decades due to funding shortages and regional conflicts. It was revived in the 1990s as part of the Asian Land Transport Infrastructure Development (ALTID) project. The Intergovernmental Agreement on the Asian Highway Network was adopted in November 2003, signed in Shanghai in April 2004, and entered into force on 4 July 2005.

Routes

Routes are designated with the prefix "AH" followed by a number. Single-digit routes are the great transcontinental corridors:

  • AH1 — the longest route, running about 20,500 km from Tokyo, Japan, through Korea, China, Southeast Asia, India, Pakistan, Iran and Turkey to the border with Bulgaria.
  • AH2 — from Denpasar, Indonesia, through Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Myanmar, India, Nepal, Pakistan and Iran.
  • AH3, AH4, AH5, AH6, AH7, AH8 — north–south and east–west corridors linking Russia, Mongolia, China, Central Asia and South Asia.

Two- and three-digit numbers designate regional and national connecting routes. Signage standards use a distinctive AH route marker, and member states commit to bringing their sections up to minimum design classes specified in the agreement.

Relationship to other networks

The Asian Highway forms one leg of the combined Trans-Asian transport system alongside the Trans-Asian Railway. In Turkey and the Caucasus its routes connect with the European E-road network administered by the UNECE.
