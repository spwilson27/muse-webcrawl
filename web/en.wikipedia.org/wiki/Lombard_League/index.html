The Lombard League (Italian: Lega Lombarda) was a military alliance formed by the communes of northern Italy to resist the attempts of the Holy Roman Emperor Frederick I Barbarossa to assert imperial authority over the Kingdom of Italy. Founded traditionally by the Oath of Pontida on 7 April 1167, the League eventually included some thirty cities, among them Milan, Cremona, Bergamo, Brescia, Mantua, Ferrara, Verona, Padua, Treviso, Bologna, Modena and Parma, with the support of Pope Alexander III.

Background

Frederick's Italian expeditions, beginning in 1154, sought to restore imperial rights (regalia) defined at the Diet of Roncaglia in 1158, provoking resistance from the increasingly autonomous communes. Milan was destroyed by imperial forces in 1162, an act that galvanised the other cities into common cause; an earlier alliance, the Veronese League of 1164, foreshadowed the broader coalition.

War and victory

The League rebuilt Milan in 1167 and founded the fortress city of Alessandria (named for Pope Alexander) to guard the western passes. Its forces decisively defeated Frederick at the Battle of Legnano on 29 May 1176, where the communal infantry around the carroccio held against the imperial knights — a victory later celebrated as a founding moment of Italian civic identity. The ensuing Peace of Constance (1183) recognised the communes' rights of self-government, including electing their own consuls, while formally retaining imperial overlordship.

Second League

The League was revived in 1226 against Frederick II, whose campaigns in Lombardy continued intermittently until his death in 1250. The alliance gradually faded as the communes themselves passed under the rule of signori.

Legacy

The Lombard League is commemorated in the Italian national anthem and gave its name to the modern political movement Lega Lombarda, founded in 1984.
