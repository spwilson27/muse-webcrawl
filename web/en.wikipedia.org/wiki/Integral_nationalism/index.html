Integral nationalism

Integral nationalism (French: nationalisme intégral) is a type of nationalism that originated during the 19th century in France, most closely associated with Charles Maurras and the Action Française movement. It subordinated all individual and sectional interests to the supposed interests of the nation, combining extreme nationalism with monarchism.

Doctrine

Maurras's formula "Politique d'abord" ("politics first") placed the restoration of the French monarchy — as the guarantor of order and national unity — at the centre of the programme. Integral nationalism was anti-republican, anti-democratic and opposed to the legacy of the French Revolution, and it often incorporated antisemitism, notably during the Dreyfus affair, with which it emerged.

Influence

The doctrine influenced authoritarian and nationalist movements in several European and Latin American countries, and is distinguished from civic nationalism by its insistence on the nation as an organic, hierarchical whole to which citizens owe total allegiance.
