Adarga

The adarga was a shield of hard leather used originally by the Moors of North Africa (from Arabic ad-daraqa, "the shield") and adopted in medieval Iberia. Made of two or more layers of oxhide or the hide of the antelope pressed and sewn together, it was light, tough and resistant to sword cuts and arrows.

Use

Early adargas were round or oval; the later Spanish colonial form was characteristically heart-shaped or formed of two overlapping ovals. The adarga became emblematic of the frontier soldiers of New Spain — the soldados de cuera ("leather-jacket soldiers") of the presidios of northern Mexico and the Spanish Borderlands — who carried it alongside lance and escopeta into the 18th and early 19th centuries. Painted examples bearing coats of arms and devotional mottos survive as prized pieces of colonial art.
