Eco-industrial park

An eco-industrial park (EIP) is a community of manufacturing and service businesses located together on a common property that seek enhanced environmental and economic performance through collaboration in managing environmental and resource issues. The approach is the applied form of industrial ecology, often summarised as industrial symbiosis: the waste or by-product of one firm becomes the input of another.

Principles and examples

The classic example is Kalundborg, Denmark, where a network of exchanges grew up from the 1970s between a power station, oil refinery, pharmaceutical plant, plasterboard factory and the municipality, sharing steam, gas, cooling water, gypsum and fly ash. Eco-industrial parks are usually designed around such exchanges of energy, water and materials, plus shared infrastructure such as combined heat and power, common wastewater treatment and joint logistics.

Development

The concept was promoted in the 1990s by the U.S. President's Council on Sustainable Development and by UNIDO and the United Nations Environment Programme, and planned parks were developed in North America, Europe and Asia, including large programmes in China and South Korea. Results have been mixed: successful networks tend to grow incrementally around commercially viable exchanges rather than by comprehensive master-planning, and economic benefit is generally seen as the driver that sustains environmental gains.
