Roger Rabbit is a fictional animated anthropomorphic rabbit created by author Gary K. Wolf, who first appeared in Wolf's 1981 novel Who Censored Roger Rabbit?. The character reached his widest fame as the title character of the 1988 film Who Framed Roger Rabbit, directed by Robert Zemeckis, in which he was voiced by Charles Fleischer.

Roger Rabbit, animated star of Maroon Cartoons.

Character

Roger is a hyperactive, good-natured "toon" rabbit, star of the Maroon Cartoons studio, recognisable by his red overalls, yellow gloves, blue bow tie with yellow spots, and the tuft of red hair between his long ears. His famous catchphrase of delighted pleading — "P-p-p-please!" — accompanies his slapstick desperation. He is married to the sultry toon singer Jessica Rabbit, who explains her devotion with the line that he makes her laugh.

Film

In the 1988 film, set in a 1947 Hollywood where cartoons live alongside humans, Roger is framed for the murder of gag magnate Marvin Acme, and enlists hard-drinking private detective Eddie Valiant (Bob Hoskins) to clear his name. The film, a landmark in combining live action with hand-drawn animation, led to three theatrical Roger Rabbit shorts: Tummy Trouble (1989), Roller Coaster Rabbit (1990) and Trail Mix-Up (1993).
