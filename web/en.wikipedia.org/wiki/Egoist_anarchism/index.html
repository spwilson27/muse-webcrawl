Egoist anarchism

Egoist anarchism draws principally on the philosophy of Max Stirner

Egoist anarchism is a school of anarchist thought originating in the philosophy of Max Stirner, the 19th-century German philosopher whose 1844 book The Ego and Its Own is a foundational work of individualist philosophy.

Stirner's egoism

Stirner argued that individuals are constrained by "spooks" or "fixed ideas" — abstractions such as the state, morality, God, and even "humanity" — that demand self-sacrifice. Against them he set the "unique one" (der Einzige), the self-owning ego that treats everything, including other people, as its property to use or enjoy. Instead of society he proposed a "union of egoists," a voluntary and revocable association entered for each participant's own benefit.

Development

Stirner's ideas were taken up by individualist anarchists in Europe and the United States. Benjamin Tucker's journal Liberty published egoist writers; the American egoist James L. Walker (writing as Tak Kak) and the publisher of the journal Egoism developed the current, while figures such as Renzo Novatore and the illegalist current in France gave it an insurrectionary edge. In the 20th century, egoist themes reappeared in post-left anarchy, situationist-influenced writing, and critiques of ideology and morality within anarchism.

Criticism

Social anarchists have criticized egoism as indifferent to solidarity and collective liberation, while egoists reply that freely chosen association is the only honest basis for cooperation. Stirner's influence also extends beyond anarchism to existentialism and post-structuralist thought.
