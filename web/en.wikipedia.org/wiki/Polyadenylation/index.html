Polyadenylation

Polyadenylation is the addition of a poly(A) tail — a stretch of adenine nucleotides — to a messenger RNA molecule. It is part of the process that produces mature mRNA for translation in eukaryotes.

Mechanism

During transcription termination, the pre-mRNA is cleaved at a site specified by a polyadenylation signal (typically AAUAAA). The enzyme poly(A) polymerase then adds roughly 100–250 adenine residues to the 3′ end without using a template. Poly(A)-binding proteins coat the tail.

Function

The poly(A) tail protects the mRNA from enzymatic degradation, aids its export from the nucleus, and promotes translation. Its gradual shortening (deadenylation) is usually the first step in mRNA decay.
