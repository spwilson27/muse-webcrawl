Luxury car

A luxury car is a car that provides above-average to high-end levels of comfort, features and equipment, refined performance and prestigious brand image beyond those of mass-market vehicles, typically at a correspondingly higher price. The term is relative and somewhat subjective, and market segmentation varies by country; many regulators and market researchers classify vehicles by segment rather than by "luxury" status alone.

Characteristics

Luxury cars typically emphasise ride quality, quietness, high-quality interior materials (leather, wood, metal), generous interior space — especially rear legroom in flagship saloons — and advanced technology for infotainment, driver assistance and safety. Categories range from compact executive cars (such as the BMW 3 Series and Mercedes-Benz C-Class) through executive and full-size models (Mercedes-Benz S-Class, BMW 7 Series, Audi A8) to ultra-luxury marques such as Rolls-Royce, Bentley and Maybach, and increasingly include luxury SUVs and electric vehicles.

History

Early motoring was inherently luxurious, with bespoke coachbuilt cars from makers such as Rolls-Royce, Hispano-Suiza and Duesenberg. After the Second World War, luxury became a distinct market segment offered by both specialist brands and premium divisions of mass manufacturers (for example Toyota's Lexus, Honda's Acura and Hyundai's Genesis). Modern luxury increasingly emphasises technology, electrification and brand experience rather than size or engine power alone.
