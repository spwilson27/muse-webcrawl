Thalassemia

Thalassemia (also thalassaemia) is a group of inherited blood disorders characterised by reduced or absent synthesis of one of the globin chains of haemoglobin, leading to anaemia. The two main types are alpha thalassemia (deficient α chains, from deletions in the HBA genes on chromosome 16) and beta thalassemia (deficient β chains, from mutations in HBB on chromosome 11).

Signs and symptoms

Severity ranges from silent carrier states and mild microcytic anaemia to thalassemia major (Cooley's anaemia), which presents in infancy with severe anaemia, growth failure, skeletal deformities from marrow expansion and enlargement of the liver and spleen. Excess iron absorption and repeated transfusions can cause iron overload damaging the heart and endocrine glands.

Distribution and treatment

The condition is most common in Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, South and Southeast Asian and African populations, where carrier status confers protection against malaria. Management includes regular blood transfusions with iron chelation therapy, folate supplementation, and in some cases stem cell transplantation or splenectomy. Gene therapy and the CRISPR-based treatment exagamglogene autotemcel (Casgevy), approved in 2023, offer curative options for transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia.
