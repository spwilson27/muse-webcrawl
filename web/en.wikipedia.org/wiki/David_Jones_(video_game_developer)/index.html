David Scott Jones (born October 1965) is a Scottish video game programmer and entrepreneur who co-founded DMA Design (now Rockstar North) in 1987. He created the hit games Lemmings and Grand Theft Auto, which have both sold tens of millions of copies. Jones later founded Realtime Worlds, which developed Crackdown (2007) and the massively multiplayer game APB: All Points Bulletin (2010).

Career

DMA Design

Jones studied at the Dundee Institute of Technology and began programming games in his spare time. In 1987 he founded DMA Design in Dundee with friends, initially developing titles such as Menace and Blood Money for Psygnosis. DMA's breakthrough came with Lemmings (1991), a puzzle game featuring hordes of creatures that players guided to safety; it became one of the best-selling games of its era and was ported to dozens of platforms. In 1997 DMA released Grand Theft Auto, a top-down open-world action game whose emphasis on free-roaming exploration and criminal missions made it a landmark title and the foundation of one of the best-selling video game franchises of all time. DMA was acquired successively by Gremlin Interactive, Infogrames, and Take-Two Interactive, becoming Rockstar North.

Realtime Worlds

In 2002 Jones founded Realtime Worlds in Dundee. The studio's first game, Crackdown (2007) for the Xbox 360, was a critical and commercial success. Its next project, the ambitious online game APB: All Points Bulletin, consumed much of the studio's resources; after its release in 2010 it received a lukewarm reception, and Realtime Worlds went into administration within weeks, with heavy job losses in the Dundee games industry.

Later work

Jones went on to co-found Cloudgine, a cloud gaming technology company acquired by Epic Games in 2018, and has remained involved in games technology ventures and in advisory roles connected with the Scottish games sector centred on Dundee.

Recognition

Jones was appointed a fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh and received an honorary doctorate from the University of Abertay Dundee, where a games development competition and teaching facilities have been associated with his legacy in the city that also produced Rockstar North's formative work.
