Social enterprise

Social enterprise
Legal formsCommunity interest company (UK), benefit corporation (US), co-operative, non-profit trading arm
Key figuresMuhammad Yunus (Grameen Bank), Bill Drayton (Ashoka)

A social enterprise is an organisation that applies commercial strategies to make a positive difference to social or environmental well-being, trading for a social purpose rather than merely to maximise profit for shareholders. Social enterprises typically reinvest the majority of their profits into their mission, and form a core part of the wider social economy alongside co-operatives, mutuals, and charities with trading activities.

Definitions and forms

Definitions differ by country. In the United Kingdom, the government defines a social enterprise as "a business with primarily social objectives whose surpluses are principally reinvested for that purpose"; since 2005 the community interest company (CIC) has provided a dedicated legal form with an asset lock. In the United States similar goals are pursued through benefit corporations and L3Cs, while the microfinance institutions pioneered by Muhammad Yunus's Grameen Bank in Bangladesh are often cited as the paradigmatic social business.

History and debate

The modern movement is associated with Ashoka (founded 1980 by Bill Drayton, who popularised the term "social entrepreneur") and with the growth of fair trade, community development finance, and work-integration enterprises employing disadvantaged people. Critics debate "mission drift" and whether social goals can survive commercial pressures, while supporters point to hybrid models as a third way between charity and conventional business.
