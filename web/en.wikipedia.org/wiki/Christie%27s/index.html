Christie's is a British auction house founded in 1766 by James Christie and one of the world's two dominant fine-art auctioneers alongside Sotheby's. It conducts sales of art, jewellery, wine, watches, and collectibles from salerooms including London, New York, Paris, Hong Kong, and Geneva.

History

James Christie held his first sale on 5 December 1766 in Pall Mall. The firm grew with the London art market, selling the collections of Sir Robert Walpole's heirs (to Catherine the Great) and countless aristocratic estates. In 1998 it was acquired by François Pinault's Groupe Artémis. Christie's pleaded guilty in 2000 to price-fixing with Sotheby's in a scandal that shook the industry.

Record sales

Christie's sold Leonardo da Vinci's Salvator Mundi in 2017 for 50.3 million, the highest price ever paid for a painting at auction, and in 2021 sold Beeple's NFT Everydays for 9.3 million, a landmark for digital art.
