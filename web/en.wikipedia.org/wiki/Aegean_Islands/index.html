The Aegean Islands are the group of islands in the Aegean Sea, between the mainland of Greece to the west and north and Turkey to the east. There are more than a thousand of them, of which roughly 200 are inhabited; almost all belong to Greece, with a few — notably Imbros (Gökçeada) and Tenedos (Bozcaada) — belonging to Turkey. The largest is Crete, at the sea's southern edge, followed by Euboea, Lesbos and Rhodes. The islands have been a cradle of Greek civilisation since the Bronze Age Cycladic and Minoan cultures.

Groups

The islands are conventionally divided into several clusters: the Cyclades in the centre (including Santorini, Mykonos, Naxos and Delos, sacred in antiquity); the Dodecanese in the southeast (Rhodes, Kos, Patmos); the Sporades off the Thessalian coast (Skiathos, Skopelos); the North Aegean islands (Lesbos, Chios, Samos, Lemnos, Samothrace, Thasos); the Saronic islands near Athens (Aegina, Salamis, Hydra, Poros); Euboea, second largest, lying along the mainland; and Crete in the south with its surrounding islets.

History

The islands were settled by Neolithic farmers and produced the marble figurines of the Cycladic culture (c. 3200–2000 BC) and, on Crete, the Minoan civilisation. Colonised by Greeks in antiquity, fought over by Athens and Sparta, then ruled successively by Macedon, Rome, Byzantium, Venetians and Genoese (who held Chios and the Duchy of the Archipelago), Crusader states, and the Ottoman Empire, most passed to the modern Greek state in the 19th and early 20th centuries; the Dodecanese were Italian from 1912 until they were ceded to Greece in 1947. Sovereignty over islets and maritime zones in the Aegean remains a source of Greek–Turkish tension.

Economy and culture

Today the islands' economies rest heavily on tourism drawn by beaches, whitewashed villages and archaeological sites, supplemented by fishing, wine, olive oil, mastic (on Chios) and marble. Many islands lose population in winter and have distinctive musical, architectural and religious traditions, including Orthodox monasteries such as those of Patmos and Mount Athos's island-facing coast.
