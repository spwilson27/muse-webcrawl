Downtown Kitchener

Kitchener is a city in the Canadian province of Ontario, about 100 km west of Toronto. It is one of three cities that make up the Regional Municipality of Waterloo, together with Waterloo and Cambridge. Kitchener's metropolitan area, which includes Waterloo and Cambridge, had a population of 575,847 at the 2021 census, making it the tenth-largest metropolitan area in Canada.

History

The area was settled in the early 1800s by Pennsylvania German Mennonite farmers, and the growing community was incorporated as the village of Berlin in 1854. Strong German cultural roots shaped the town, which became an industrial centre known for furniture, leather, buttons and meat packing. During the First World War, anti-German sentiment led to a controversial 1916 referendum that renamed the city Kitchener, after Lord Kitchener, the British Secretary of State for War who had died that year when HMS Hampshire sank.

Economy and culture

Historically a manufacturing city, Kitchener is now part of the Toronto–Waterloo Region Corridor technology cluster, with a downtown innovation district anchored by the Communitech hub and offices of major technology firms. The city is known for Kitchener–Waterloo Oktoberfest, the largest Bavarian festival in North America, first held in 1969. Other landmarks include the Kitchener Memorial Auditorium, home of the Kitchener Rangers junior hockey team, Victoria Park, and the Joseph Schneider Haus museum. Since 2019 the city has been served by the ION rapid transit line connecting Kitchener and Waterloo.
