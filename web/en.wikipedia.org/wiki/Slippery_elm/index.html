Slippery elm

The slippery elm (Ulmus rubra), also called red elm, is a species of elm native to eastern and central North America. It is named for the mucilaginous inner bark, which becomes slippery when chewed or mixed with water.

Scientific name: Ulmus rubra Muhl.
Family: Ulmaceae
Range: Eastern and central North America

Description

Slippery elm is a medium-sized deciduous tree, typically 12–19 m tall, with rough, deeply furrowed bark and coarsely toothed leaves that feel rough above. It can be distinguished from American elm by its reddish-brown buds and mucilaginous inner bark.

Uses

The inner bark has a long history in traditional herbal medicine as a demulcent for coughs, sore throats and digestive complaints, and is sold in lozenges and supplements. The wood is used for furniture, boxes and fence posts. The species is susceptible to Dutch elm disease, though less severely than American elm.
