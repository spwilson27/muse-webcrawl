Hypercube graph

In graph theory, the hypercube graph Qn is the graph formed from the vertices and edges of an n-dimensional hypercube. Its vertices correspond to all binary strings of length n (2n vertices), and two vertices are adjacent when their strings differ in exactly one bit.

Properties

Qn is n-regular, bipartite, and has n·2n−1 edges. Distances in the graph equal Hamming distances between strings, so its diameter is n. Hypercube graphs are Hamiltonian and vertex-transitive, and contain Gray codes as Hamiltonian cycles.

Applications

Hypercubes served as interconnection networks in parallel computers, and the graph underlies the analysis of Boolean functions, coding theory, and the geometry of the discrete cube {0,1}n.
