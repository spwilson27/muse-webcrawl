Jackknife resampling

The jackknife is a resampling technique in statistics, especially useful for estimating the bias and variance of a statistic. It was introduced by Maurice Quenouille in 1949 and developed by John Tukey, who coined its name.

Method

The jackknife estimate is found by systematically leaving out one observation at a time from a sample and recalculating the estimate on the remaining observations. From these leave-one-out estimates one can compute an estimate of bias and of the standard error.

Applications

The jackknife is a precursor of the bootstrap and is still used for variance estimation in survey statistics and for robust inference with complex statistics.
