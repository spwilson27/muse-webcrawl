Earth observation satellite

An Earth observation satellite or Earth remote sensing satellite is a satellite used or designed for Earth observation (EO) from orbit, including spy satellites and similar ones intended for non-military uses such as environmental monitoring, meteorology, cartography, and geology. The first satellite images of Earth were obtained by Explorer 6 in 1959; the first dedicated Earth observation satellite, TIROS-1, launched in 1960 as a weather satellite.

Types and instruments

Earth observation satellites carry instruments such as multispectral and hyperspectral imagers, synthetic-aperture radar (SAR), altimeters, radiometers, sounders, and lidar. Most fly in low Earth orbit, often sun-synchronous orbits that image each location at the same local time; weather satellites also occupy geostationary orbit, staring continuously at one hemisphere.

Major programmes

The Landsat programme of the United States, running since 1972, provides the longest continuous record of Earth's land surface. Europe's Copernicus programme operates the Sentinel family of satellites with freely distributed data. Commercial operators such as Maxar and Planet Labs image the planet daily at high resolution. Reconnaissance satellites, such as the Keyhole series, conduct military Earth observation.

Applications

Data are used for weather forecasting, disaster response, agricultural monitoring, deforestation and climate-change tracking, oceanography, urban planning, and verification of arms-control treaties.
