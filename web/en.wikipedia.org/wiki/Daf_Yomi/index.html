Daf Yomi

Daf Yomi (Hebrew: דף יומי, "page of the day" or "folio of the day") is a daily regimen undertaken to study the Babylonian Talmud, in which each of the 2,711 pages of the standard Vilna edition is studied in sequence, one page (daf, consisting of two sides) per day. At this rate the entire Talmud is completed in a cycle of approximately seven and a half years. Tens of thousands of Jews around the world study the same page on the same day, in classes (shiurim), by telephone and online recordings, and in self-study.

Origins

The programme was proposed by Rabbi Meir Shapiro of Lublin at the First World Congress (Knessia Gedola) of Agudath Israel, held in Vienna in 1923, and the first cycle began on Rosh Hashanah of that year. Shapiro argued that a universal, synchronised syllabus would unite Jews across the world in shared study; he also founded Yeshivas Chachmei Lublin, whose students followed the programme. Rabbi Shapiro died in 1933, during the second cycle, but the regimen survived the near-destruction of European Jewry in the Holocaust and spread worldwide after the Second World War.

Siyum HaShas

Each completion of the cycle is celebrated at a Siyum HaShas ceremony, drawing mass gatherings. The 12th Siyum, in 2012, filled MetLife Stadium in New Jersey with over 90,000 attendees; the 13th Siyum, on 1 January 2020, again drew some 90,000 to MetLife Stadium, with parallel events in Jerusalem and other cities. The 14th cycle began on 5 January 2020 and is scheduled to conclude in 2027. In recent cycles, programmes such as Dirshu have added accountability testing, and women’s participation in Daf Yomi study has grown, with women-led classes and podcasts devoted to the daily page.
