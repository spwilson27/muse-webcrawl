Word metric

In group theory, a word metric on a finitely generated group is a metric measuring distance between group elements by the shortest word in the generators and their inverses that expresses their difference.

Definition

Given a generating set S, the word length of an element g is the minimum number of generators whose product equals g; the distance d(g,h) is the length of g⁻¹h. The resulting metric space is the geometry of the group's Cayley graph.

Properties

Changing the finite generating set changes the metric only up to quasi-isometry, making word metrics central to geometric group theory, and to results such as Gromov's theorem on groups of polynomial growth and the Milnor–Švarc lemma.
