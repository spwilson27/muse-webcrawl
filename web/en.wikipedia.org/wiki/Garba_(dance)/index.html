Garba (dance)

Garba is a form of Gujarati dance that originated in the state of Gujarat, India. The name is derived from the Sanskrit term Garbha ("womb") and Deep ("a small earthenware lamp"). Many traditional garbas are performed around a centrally lit lamp or a picture or statue of the Goddess Shakti, honouring the feminine form of divinity.

Tradition

Garba is traditionally performed during the nine-day Hindu festival of Navratri. Dancers move in a circle around a central lamp (the garba deep) placed inside a perforated earthen pot, or around an image of the goddess. The circular movement symbolises the Hindu view of time as cyclical, and the unchanging centre represents the divine.

Participants wear colourful traditional clothing: women typically wear the chaniya choli, a three-piece embroidered outfit, while men wear kediyu and pyjamas or dhotis. The dance is accompanied by rhythmic clapping and music from instruments such as the dhol, tabla and harmonium, along with folk singing.

Modern Garba

Today Garba is performed at large organised events in Gujarat, across India, and in diaspora communities worldwide, often with thousands of participants and modern amplified music. It is closely related to Dandiya Raas, which uses paired sticks, and the two are frequently performed together during Navratri festivities. In 2023, "Garba of Gujarat" was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
