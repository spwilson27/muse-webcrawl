Frank Finley Merriam (December 22, 1865 – April 25, 1955) was an American politician who served as the 28th governor of California from 1934 to 1939. A Republican, he rose to the governorship upon the death of Governor James Rolph and won election in his own right in the 1934 general election.

Early life and career

Merriam was born in Hopkinton, Iowa, and worked as a teacher and school principal before entering politics. He served in the Iowa House of Representatives and as Iowa State Auditor before moving to California in 1910. In California he was elected to the State Assembly and then the State Senate, eventually serving as lieutenant governor under Rolph.

Governorship

Merriam assumed office on June 2, 1934, following Rolph's death. His tenure coincided with the depths of the Great Depression. Weeks after taking office he faced the 1934 West Coast waterfront strike, during which he called out the National Guard in San Francisco. In the 1934 election he defeated the Democratic nominee, novelist Upton Sinclair, whose End Poverty in California (EPIC) campaign had alarmed business interests; Merriam's campaign benefited from one of the first modern media-driven negative campaigns in American politics.

As governor, Merriam signed California's first state sales tax to balance the budget, a measure that contributed to his declining popularity. He was defeated for re-election in 1938 by Democrat Culbert Olson.

Later life

Merriam retired to Long Beach, where he died on April 25, 1955, at the age of 89.
