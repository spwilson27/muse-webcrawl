Grammy Award

The Grammy trophy, a gilded gramophone, has been presented since 1959.

The Grammy Awards (stylised as GRAMMY), or simply known as the Grammys, are awards presented by the Recording Academy of the United States to recognise outstanding achievements in the music industry. They are regarded as among the most prestigious and significant awards in the music industry worldwide.

The first Grammy Awards ceremony was held on 4 May 1959, honouring musical accomplishments by performers of the previous year. The trophy depicts a gilded gramophone, handmade by Billings Artworks in Colorado.

General field categories

The four main "general field" categories, which are not restricted by genre, are:

  • Album of the Year
  • Record of the Year
  • Song of the Year
  • Best New Artist

Beyond these, dozens of genre-specific categories cover fields such as pop, rock, rap, R&B, country, jazz, classical, Latin, and production and songwriting crafts. As of recent ceremonies the total number of categories is in the mid-80s to low-90s, following periodic restructuring by the Academy.

Voting process

Members of the Recording Academy, along with record companies, submit recordings for consideration. Entries are reviewed by committees of genre experts who place them in the appropriate categories. Academy voting members then vote in up to ten categories within their fields of expertise, plus the general field. The accounting firm Deloitte tabulates the ballots, and winners are announced at the annual ceremony, which includes live performances and is broadcast on television.

Records

Beyoncé is the most awarded artist in Grammy history, with 32 wins. The Hungarian-British conductor Sir Georg Solti held the record for decades with 31 awards. Quincy Jones is among the most nominated and awarded figures, and artists such as Stevie Wonder, Taylor Swift and Frank Sinatra have won Album of the Year multiple times.

Criticism

The Grammys have periodically faced criticism over perceived snubs, the transparency of the nomination process, and under-representation of certain genres and demographics. In 2021 the Academy abolished its anonymous nomination review committees for most categories in response to controversy.
