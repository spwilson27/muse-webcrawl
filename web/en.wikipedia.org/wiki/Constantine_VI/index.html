Constantine VI (Greek: Κωνσταντῖνος, Kōnstantīnos; 14 January 771 – before 805), sometimes surnamed "the Blind", was Byzantine emperor from 780 to 797. As a child he reigned under the regency of his mother, the empress Irene of Athens, whose power he later contested. His reign is remembered above all for the Second Council of Nicaea (787), which restored the veneration of icons, and for his blinding and overthrow by his own mother.

Early reign and the regency of Irene

Constantine was crowned co-emperor in 776 and succeeded his father Leo IV at the age of nine. Irene governed as regent. In 781 he was betrothed to Rotrude, daughter of Charlemagne, though the engagement was broken off in 788. Irene's government, advised by the eunuch Staurakios, moved to end the iconoclasm established by Constantine's grandfather Leo III: after a failed attempt in 786, the Seventh Ecumenical Council met at Nicaea in 787 and restored icon veneration, with the young emperor and his mother presiding in name.

Personal rule and fall

By 790 Constantine, now nineteen, resented his mother's dominance. With military support he confined Irene to a palace and ruled in his own right, but his government proved unpopular: a campaign against the Bulgars ended in humiliating flight at the Battle of Marcellae (792), and his decision to divorce his wife Maria of Amnia in 795 and marry his mistress Theodote scandalised the Church, provoking the "Moechian controversy". Weakened, he allowed Irene to return to power as co-ruler in 792 and then joint ruler. In August 797 Irene's supporters seized Constantine and had him blinded — reportedly so brutally that he died of his injuries some years later — leaving Irene to rule alone as the first Byzantine empress regnant (797–802). Constantine's blinding shocked contemporaries and, in the West, was later cited in the context of the imperial vacancy that justified Charlemagne's coronation in 800.

Family

With Maria of Amnia he had two daughters, including Euphrosyne, who later married Emperor Michael II. With Theodote he had a son, Leo, who died in childhood.
