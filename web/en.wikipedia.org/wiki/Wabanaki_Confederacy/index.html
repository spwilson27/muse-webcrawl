The Wabanaki Confederacy (Wabanaki, translated as "People of the Dawn" or "People of the Dawnland") is a historic confederation of five principal Eastern Algonquian nations of what is now north-eastern North America: the Mi'kmaq, the Maliseet (Wolastoqiyik), the Passamaquoddy, the Penobscot and the Abenaki. Their homeland, the Dawnland, stretches across present-day Maine and the Canadian Maritimes into New Hampshire, Vermont and Quebec.

History

The member nations, linked by language, kinship and wampum diplomacy, formed a political and military alliance that took shape by the late seventeenth century, though its roots in councils and intertribal agreements are older. The Confederacy dealt collectively with European powers, especially the French, with whom the Wabanaki were generally allied, and the English colonies of New England, with whom they fought a series of wars — including King Philip's War, King William's War and Dummer's War — resisting encroachment on their lands.

Through the eighteenth century the Confederacy's member nations negotiated treaties with Britain and later the United States, while maintaining their own governance. Population losses from disease and war, land dispossession and, in Canada and the United States, assimilationist policies fragmented the nations' territories, but the Wabanaki peoples maintained their identities, languages and treaty rights.

Modern era

In the twentieth and twenty-first centuries, Wabanaki nations have pursued land claims and self-governance, including the Maine Indian Claims Settlement Act of 1980, one of the largest Native American land claim settlements in US history. The Wabanaki Confederacy has been formally revived as a forum bringing the five nations together, holding annual conferences and asserting shared positions on sovereignty, environmental protection and cultural preservation in the Dawnland.
