Siege of Ishiyama Hongan-ji

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Ishiyama Hongan-ji (1570–1580) was a ten-year campaign by the warlord Oda Nobunaga against the fortress-cathedral of the Ikkō-ikki movement in Osaka. It was the longest siege in Japanese history and a central episode of Nobunaga's wars of unification during the Sengoku period.

Background

The Ishiyama Hongan-ji, headquarters of the Jōdo Shinshū (True Pure Land) Buddhist sect under the abbot Kennyo, occupied a superb defensive site in the marshy delta of the Yodo River. Its congregation commanded networks of fortified temples and leagues (ikki) across central Japan, and its armed monks and lay followers resisted Nobunaga's expansion. In 1570 Kennyo refused Nobunaga's demand to surrender the fortress, opening a decade of war.

The siege

Nobunaga blockaded the fortress on land and, with the naval forces of Kuki Yoshitaka, by sea; the Mōri clan ran supplies through the blockade in the naval battles of Kizugawaguchi, winning in 1576 before Kuki's armoured ships prevailed in 1578. Nobunaga systematically destroyed supporting Ikkō strongholds, notably the Nagashima fortresses, with great loss of life. Cut off from allies — the Takeda were destroyed in 1575 and the Uesugi weakened after 1578 — Kennyo finally accepted an imperially mediated settlement in 1580.

Aftermath

Kennyo surrendered the fortress and withdrew to Kii Province; the Hongan-ji burned down shortly afterwards under suspicious circumstances. Toyotomi Hideyoshi later built Osaka Castle on the site. The sect survived but never again wielded military power on that scale.
