For Greater Glory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

For Greater Glory: The True Story of Cristiada (Spanish: Cristiada) is a 2012 English-language Mexican historical drama film directed by Dean Wright, chronicling the Cristero War (1926–1929), the Catholic uprising against the anticlerical policies of the Mexican government of President Plutarco Elías Calles.

Plot

Following the enforcement of the Calles Law, which criminalised public worship and closed churches, Catholics in west-central Mexico take up arms under the cry ¡Viva Cristo Rey! ("Long live Christ the King!"). Retired general Enrique Gorostieta (Andy García), an agnostic hired to lead the rebels, moulds peasants, priests and townspeople into an army. The film interweaves his campaign with the story of José Sánchez del Río (Mauricio Kuri), a teenage standard-bearer who is captured, tortured and executed for refusing to renounce his faith — a martyr canonised by the Catholic Church in 2016.

Cast and production

The cast includes Andy García, Eva Longoria, Peter O'Toole (in one of his last roles, as the priest Father Christopher), Rubén Blades as Calles, and Oscar Isaac. With a budget of roughly US$12 million, it was among the most expensive Mexican films ever made, shot on location in Mexico with large-scale battle sequences by Wright, a visual-effects supervisor making his directorial debut.

Reception

The film performed strongly in Mexico and drew praise from Catholic audiences for bringing a little-known episode to the screen; critics were more mixed, commending its scale and sincerity while finding its characterisation one-sided. It won several Ariel Awards from the Mexican Academy of Film.
