David Crockett (August 17, 1786 – March 6, 1836) was an American folk hero, frontiersman, soldier and politician. He is commonly referred to in popular culture as the "King of the Wild Frontier". He represented Tennessee in the US House of Representatives and died at the Battle of the Alamo during the Texas Revolution.

Early life

Crockett was born in what is now Greene County, Tennessee, of Scots-Irish and Huguenot stock. His father John, a Revolutionary War veteran and tavern keeper, hired the boy out as a labourer and drover to pay family debts; David ran away from an abusive master and spent nearly three years wandering before returning home. He married Polly Finley in 1806 and farmed in Tennessee, becoming a noted hunter — folklore credits him with killing more than a hundred bears in a single season — and a local militia officer and justice of the peace, despite having had only a few months of schooling.

Soldier and politician

Crockett served under Andrew Jackson in the Creek War (1813–14) and in the First Seminole campaign. Elected to the Tennessee legislature in 1821, he won a seat in Congress in 1827 as a Jacksonian but broke with Jackson over land policy and the Indian Removal Act of 1830, which he alone among the Tennessee delegation opposed — a stand that cost him his seat in 1831. Re-elected in 1833, he became the Whigs' favourite western celebrity, touring the East and publishing his autobiography, A Narrative of the Life of David Crockett (1834). Defeated narrowly in 1835, he famously told his constituents they could "go to hell", while he would "go to Texas".

The Alamo

Crockett reached San Antonio de Béxar in February 1836 and joined the Texian garrison of the Alamo. When Santa Anna's army stormed the mission at dawn on 6 March 1836, Crockett died with the other defenders. Whether he fell fighting or was captured and executed remains disputed; the account attributed to Mexican officer José Enrique de la Peña describes Crockett among a handful of survivors executed on Santa Anna's orders, a version historians continue to debate.

Legend

Even in his lifetime Crockett was transformed by almanacs, plays and tall tales into a superhuman backwoodsman who could "grin a raccoon out of a tree". The legend exploded again in the 1950s with Walt Disney's television series Davy Crockett starring Fess Parker, whose coonskin-cap craze and theme song, "The Ballad of Davy Crockett", made him an icon of the American frontier for a new generation.
