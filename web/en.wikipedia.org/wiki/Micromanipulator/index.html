Micromanipulator

A micromanipulator is a device which allows a scientist to interact with a sample at the microscopic level with precise, scaled-down movements. It translates the comparatively coarse motions of the operator's hand into minute motions of a tool — typically a micropipette, microelectrode, or probe — with resolutions ranging from micrometres down to nanometres in advanced systems.

Operation

Micromanipulators reduce hand movement mechanically (through levers, gears, or hydraulic linkages), pneumatically, or electronically (using stepper motors or piezoelectric actuators controlled by a joystick). The tool is usually mounted beside a microscope so the operator can watch the work in real time.

Applications

Micromanipulators are essential in:

  • Assisted reproduction — intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), in which a single sperm is injected into an egg.
  • Cell biology — microinjection of DNA, pronuclear transfer, and extraction of cellular contents.
  • Electrophysiology — positioning microelectrodes for patch-clamp recording of individual neurons and cells.
  • Semiconductor probing — contacting test points on wafers and dies.
  • Materials science and nanotechnology — handling individual nanowires and micro-scale components; combined with focused ion beam and scanning electron microscope systems.

History

Early micromanipulators were developed in the early 20th century, notably the Chambers and Barber instruments for single-cell surgery, and Hungarian botanist Peter von Fonbrune's pneumatic design of the 1930s. Modern computerized systems offer programmable, repeatable positioning.
