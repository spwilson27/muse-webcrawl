Electric toothbrush

An electric toothbrush is a toothbrush that makes rapid automatic bristle motions, either back-and-forth oscillation or rotation-oscillation (where the brush head alternates clockwise and counterclockwise rotation), in order to clean teeth. Motions at sonic speeds or higher are made by a motor.

History

The earliest electric toothbrush, the Broxodent, was developed in Switzerland in 1954 by Dr. Philippe-Guy Woog and produced by Squibb. General Electric introduced a cordless model in the early 1960s. The modern rechargeable oscillating-rotating brushes were later popularised by brands such as Oral-B and Philips Sonicare, the latter using high-frequency sonic vibration.

Types

Electric toothbrushes are classified by the motion of the head: vibrating, rotation-oscillation, side-to-side, circular, and ultrasonic. Sonic toothbrushes vibrate the head at high speed (typically 30,000–40,000 strokes per minute), creating fluid dynamics that help clean beyond the bristles' contact. Ultrasonic toothbrushes emit ultrasound intended to disrupt bacterial chains.

Effectiveness

Systematic reviews, including a 2014 Cochrane review, found that powered toothbrushes reduce plaque and gingivitis more than manual brushing over short and long term, with rotation-oscillation brushes showing a modest but consistent advantage. Dentists often recommend them for patients with limited manual dexterity.
