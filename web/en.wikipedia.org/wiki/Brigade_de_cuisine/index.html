Brigade de cuisine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

System: Kitchen hierarchy
Developed by: Georges Auguste Escoffier

Brigade de cuisine ("kitchen brigade") is a system of hierarchy found in restaurants and hotels in France and elsewhere, principally in fine-dining establishments. The system was developed by Georges Auguste Escoffier in the late 19th century, drawing on his experience of military organization and on the older traditions of French court and guild cooking.

By dividing the kitchen into specialized stations (parties), each with a defined responsibility, the brigade system allowed large hotel kitchens to produce elaborate meals for hundreds of diners with consistency and speed.

Stations

TitleResponsibility
Chef de cuisineHead of the kitchen; overall management and final responsibility.
Sous-chefSecond in command; supervises production and deputizes for the chef.
Chef de partieRuns a specific station.
SaucierSauces, stocks and braised meats; traditionally the most senior station after the sous-chef.
PoissonnierFish and seafood dishes.
RôtisseurRoasted and grilled meats.
EntremétierVegetables, soups and eggs.
Garde mangerCold preparations: salads, charcuterie, terrines.
PâtissierPastry, breads and desserts.
CommisJunior cook working under a chef de partie.
PlongeurDishwasher and kitchen porter.

Modern use

Few modern kitchens staff every classical station, but the brigade structure, its titles, and its discipline remain the foundation of professional culinary training worldwide. The term à la minute cooking and the "pass"—where the chef finishes and checks every plate—are enduring features of the system.
