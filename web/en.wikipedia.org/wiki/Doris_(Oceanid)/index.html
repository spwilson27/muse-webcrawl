Doris (Oceanid)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Doris (Ancient Greek: Δωρίς, "gift") is a sea goddess in Greek mythology, one of the three thousand Oceanids, the daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys. She is the wife of the sea god Nereus and by him the mother of the fifty Nereids.

Mythology

Hesiod's Theogony names Doris among the foremost Oceanids and lists her daughters, who include Thetis, mother of Achilles, and Amphitrite, queen of the sea as wife of Poseidon. Doris herself plays no active role in surviving myths; her importance is genealogical, linking the elder generation of sea deities with the Nereids who populate Greek poetry and cult.

Namesakes

The name Doris has been given to the asteroid 48 Doris, one of the largest main-belt asteroids, discovered in 1857, and to a genus of nudibranch molluscs. In ancient geography the region of Doris in central Greece and the Dorian Greeks take their names from the legendary Dorus rather than from the goddess.
