Plücker embedding

In mathematics, the Plücker embedding is a method of realizing the Grassmannian of all k-dimensional subspaces of an n-dimensional vector space as an algebraic variety in projective space. It is named after the German mathematician Julius Plücker.

Definition

Let V be a vector space of dimension n over a field, and let Gr(k, V) denote the Grassmannian of k-dimensional linear subspaces of V. The Plücker embedding is the map that sends a subspace W ⊆ V to the line spanned by the wedge product w1 ∧ … ∧ wk in the exterior power ⋀kV, where w1, …, wk is any basis of W. Since a change of basis rescales the wedge product by the determinant of the change-of-basis matrix, the resulting point in the projective space P(⋀kV) is well defined.

Plücker coordinates

The homogeneous coordinates of the image of W under this embedding are called the Plücker coordinates of W. Relative to a fixed basis of V, they are given by the k × k minors of a matrix whose rows (or columns) form a basis of W. For the classical case of lines in three-dimensional space (Gr(2, 4)), the six Plücker coordinates satisfy a single quadratic relation, the Plücker relation, which identifies the Grassmannian with a quadric hypersurface in P5 known as the Klein quadric.

Plücker relations

In general, the image of the Plücker embedding is cut out by a set of quadratic equations, the Plücker relations, which express the conditions under which a point of the projective space corresponds to a decomposable k-vector, and hence to a genuine k-plane. These relations make the Grassmannian a projective algebraic variety.

Applications

Plücker coordinates are widely used in computer vision and robotics, where they provide a convenient representation of lines in space for geometric computations, and in the theory of total positivity and cluster algebras.
