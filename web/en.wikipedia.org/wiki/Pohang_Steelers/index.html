Pohang Steelers

The Pohang Steelers (Korean: 포항 스틸러스) are a South Korean professional football club based in Pohang, North Gyeongsang Province, competing in the K League 1, the top tier of South Korean football. Founded in 1973 as the works team of the steel company POSCO, the club is one of the founding members of the K League (1983) and one of Asia's most successful clubs, having won the AFC Champions League three times — a record for a Korean club — in 1997, 1998, and 2009.

The Steelers play at the Pohang Steel Yard, Korea's first football-specific stadium.

History

The club was founded on 1 April 1973 as POSCO Football Club, one of the earliest company teams in Korean football. As the K League launched in 1983 the club became a founding professional side, known successively as POSCO Dolphins and POSCO Atoms before adopting the name Pohang Steelers in 1997, reflecting the steel industry of its home city. In 1990 the club opened the Pohang Steel Yard, the first football-specific stadium in South Korea.

Domestically, Pohang have won the K League championship five times (1986, 1988, 1992, 2007, and 2013) and the Korean FA Cup multiple times, including in 2023 and 2024. The 2013 season was especially celebrated: fielding an entirely Korean squad built around academy products — dubbed the "Steel Taka" style for its quick passing — the club won a league and FA Cup double on the final day.

Continental success

The Steelers won back-to-back Asian Club Championships in 1996–97 and 1997–98, defeating Cheonan Ilhwa Chunma and Dalian Wanda respectively. In 2009 they won the AFC Champions League, beating Al-Ittihad of Saudi Arabia 2–1 in the final in Tokyo, and went on to finish third at the FIFA Club World Cup in Abu Dhabi — still one of the best results by an Asian club in that tournament. They reached the Champions League final again in 2021, losing to Al-Hilal.

Identity

The club is famous for its youth development and its "made in Pohang" squads, and for the Steel Yard's intimate atmosphere. Its colours are red and black stripes. Pohang's traditional rivals include Jeonbuk Hyundai Motors and Ulsan Hyundai, with whom it contests the Donghaean derby, named for the East Sea coast both clubs share.
