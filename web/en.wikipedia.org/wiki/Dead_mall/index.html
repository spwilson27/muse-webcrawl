Dead mall

A dead mall (also greyfield) is a shopping mall with a high vacancy rate, low consumer traffic, or both, which has deteriorated into a state of disuse. The phenomenon is most associated with the United States, where thousands of enclosed malls were built from the 1960s to the 1990s, but has also occurred in Canada, China and elsewhere.

Causes

Malls decline when anchor department stores close or go bankrupt, when a newer competing centre opens nearby, when local employment and incomes fall, or when retail shifts to big-box power centres and e-commerce. Once vacancy passes a tipping point, remaining tenants' co-tenancy clauses allow them to leave, accelerating the spiral. Notorious examples include the vast, briefly world's-largest New South China Mall in Dongguan, which stood mostly empty for years, and numerous abandoned American malls documented by photographers and urban explorers. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated closures of already weak centres.

Afterlives

Dead malls are frequently demolished for redevelopment, but some are converted to new uses: mixed-use town centres, offices, medical facilities, schools, churches, warehouses and fulfilment centres, or housing. The Arcade Providence in Rhode Island, America's oldest indoor mall, was converted to micro-apartments with small shops. The term "dead mall" was popularised by the website deadmalls.com (founded 2000) and by documentary series such as Dan Bell's Dead Mall Series.
