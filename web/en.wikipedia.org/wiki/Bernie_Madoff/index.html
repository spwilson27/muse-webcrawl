Bernard Lawrence Madoff (April 29, 1938 – April 14, 2021) was an American financier who operated the largest Ponzi scheme in history, valued at approximately $64.8 billion. He was at one time the chairman of the NASDAQ stock exchange. He advanced the proliferation of electronic trading platforms and the concept of payment for order flow, which have been taken up by other market makers.

Madoff founded Bernard L. Madoff Investment Securities LLC in 1960, and in a parallel investment advisory business, he perpetrated a massive fraud that came to light in December 2008 amid the global financial crisis. Clients' account statements showed fictitious returns; withdrawals were paid from new investors' money rather than from genuine investment profits. The actual cash losses to investors were estimated at about $18 billion in principal.

The fraud and arrest

On December 10, 2008, Madoff confessed to his sons, Mark and Andrew, that the investment advisory business was "one big lie." They turned him in to federal authorities, and he was arrested the next day. On March 12, 2009, Madoff pleaded guilty to 11 federal felonies, including securities fraud, wire fraud, mail fraud, money laundering, and perjury. He admitted to operating the scheme since at least the early 1990s, though investigators and Madoff himself later suggested it may have begun in the 1970s or 1980s.

Victims included individual investors, charities, foundations, universities, banks, and celebrities. Charity organizations such as the Elie Wiesel Foundation for Humanity suffered major losses; many Jewish charities were affected because Madoff was a prominent figure in Jewish philanthropic circles.

Sentencing and aftermath

On June 29, 2009, Judge Denny Chin sentenced Madoff to 150 years in prison — the maximum allowable — and ordered $17.179 billion in restitution. Madoff served his sentence at the Federal Correctional Complex in Butner, North Carolina, and died there of natural causes on April 14, 2021, after suffering from chronic kidney disease.

A court-appointed trustee, Irving Picard, recovered billions of dollars for victims through lawsuits, including "clawback" actions against investors who had withdrawn more than they deposited. Madoff's wife Ruth was not charged, but she forfeited most of the couple's assets. His son Mark died by suicide on December 11, 2010, the second anniversary of his father's arrest; his son Andrew died of lymphoma in 2014. His brother Peter Madoff pleaded guilty and received a 10-year sentence.

The scandal badly damaged the reputation of the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, which had received repeated detailed warnings from analyst Harry Markopolos and others yet failed to uncover the fraud in multiple examinations.
