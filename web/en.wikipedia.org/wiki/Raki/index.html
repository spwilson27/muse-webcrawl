Raki or rakı (pronounced [ɾaˈkɯ]) is an alcoholic drink made of twice-distilled grape pomace and flavoured with aniseed. It is the national drink of Turkey, where it is most famously produced from grapes grown in the Aegean region. The drink is a member of a family of aniseed spirits found around the Mediterranean, including Greek ouzo, French pastis, Levantine arak, and Balkan rakia (though rakia is usually unflavoured).

Production

Rakı is produced by distilling fermented grape skins and pulp left over from winemaking; the resulting spirit (suma) is redistilled with aniseed in copper alembics, then diluted and rested. Adding water or ice to rakı causes the anise oils to emulsify into a milky-white liquid, leading to the nickname aslan sütü, "lion's milk".

Culture

Rakı is traditionally consumed slowly alongside meze at a long communal table — the rakı sofrası — in meyhanes (taverns), with white cheese, melon, grilled fish, and seafood. The canonical pairing is rakı-balık (rakı with fish). Its home consumption stretches back to Byzantine and Ottoman times, when it was distilled in taverns across the empire, often by Greek and Armenian producers.
