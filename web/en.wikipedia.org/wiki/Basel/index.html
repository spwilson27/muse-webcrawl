Basel

Basel is a city in northwestern Switzerland on the river Rhine, at the borders with France and Germany. With about 177,000 inhabitants it is Switzerland's third-most populous city and a global centre for the chemical and pharmaceutical industries, banking, and art fairs.

History

Settled since Roman times near the colony of Augusta Raurica, Basel became a prince-bishopric and a free imperial city. Its university, founded in 1460, is Switzerland's oldest; Erasmus taught and was buried here, and the city became a hub of humanism and printing. Basel joined the Swiss Confederation in 1501. The 1833 split of the canton produced Basel-Stadt and Basel-Landschaft. The Basel Peace of 1795 and the Congress-era settlement, plus the 1897 first Zionist Congress under Theodor Herzl, gave the name wider resonance.

Economy and culture

Pharmaceutical giants Novartis and Roche are headquartered here, as is the Bank for International Settlements. Basel hosts Art Basel, the world's leading modern art fair, museums such as the Kunstmuseum and Fondation Beyeler, and the Fasnacht carnival, inscribed by UNESCO. Its tram network and EuroAirport serve the tri-national region. See also Zurich and the Basel Committee.
