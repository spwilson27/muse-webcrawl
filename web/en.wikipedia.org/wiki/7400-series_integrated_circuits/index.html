7400-series integrated circuits

7400 series
Family of transistor–transistor logic integrated circuits
Introduced by Texas Instruments in the 1960s.

The 7400 series is a family of integrated circuits originally built using transistor–transistor logic (TTL). Introduced by Texas Instruments in the mid-1960s, the series supplied standardised digital building blocks—gates, flip-flops, counters, decoders, multiplexers and arithmetic units—in packages that engineers could combine into complete systems. The numbering scheme became so familiar that later CMOS and low-power Schottky families retained many of the same part numbers.

History

Before standard logic families became widespread, digital designers often had to work with incompatible parts from different vendors. The 7400 series offered a catalogue of functions with predictable electrical behaviour and broad second sourcing. Computers, industrial controllers, test equipment and military systems of the 1970s and 1980s commonly contained boards populated with dozens or hundreds of these chips. The original 7400 itself is a quad two-input NAND gate.

Families and variants

Letters inserted into part numbers indicate subfamilies and technologies: 74LS for low-power Schottky TTL, 74HC and 74HCT for high-speed CMOS, 74AC/ACT for advanced CMOS, and many others. These variants differ in supply voltage, speed, power consumption, input thresholds and drive capability. Pin-compatible naming made migration easier but did not make every variant electrically interchangeable in every circuit.

Legacy

Large-scale integration and programmable logic displaced most discrete logic design, yet 7400-series parts remain in education, prototyping, repair and simple control circuits. Their datasheets and logic diagrams are a standard introduction to digital electronics, and breadboard projects still use chips such as the 7400, 7404 inverter, 74161 counter and 74138 decoder.
