Second Epistle to Timothy

Paul writing to Timothy from imprisonment

The Second Epistle to Timothy, usually abbreviated 2 Timothy, is one of the three Pastoral Epistles of the New Testament, presented as a letter from the Apostle Paul to his younger co-worker Timothy at Ephesus. In the traditional account it is Paul's final letter, written during his last imprisonment in Rome shortly before his death, and it reads as a personal testament urging Timothy to faithfulness and endurance.

Content

Paul thanks God for Timothy and recalls his tears and his sincere faith, inherited from his grandmother Lois and mother Eunice (1:3–5). He exhorts Timothy to "fan into flame the gift of God" and not to be ashamed of the gospel, and uses the images of a soldier, an athlete and a farmer to commend steadfastness (chapters 1–2). Chapter 3 warns of godlessness "in the last days" and false teachers, appeals to Timothy's knowledge of the sacred writings, and contains the classic statement that "all Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness" (3:16).

Chapter 4 brings Paul's solemn charge — "preach the word; be ready in season and out of season" — and his valediction: "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith." He asks Timothy to come quickly, mentions Demas who deserted him and Luke who remained, and sends final greetings.

Authorship and scholarship

Like the other Pastoral Epistles, 2 Timothy has been debated since the 19th century. Many critical scholars regard the Pastorals as pseudepigraphal, written in Paul's name after his death, citing their vocabulary, church organisation and difficulty in fitting the letters into the chronology of Acts. Other scholars defend Pauline authorship, sometimes positing an amanuensis or a late, otherwise unattested period of ministry. Within the debate, 2 Timothy is frequently considered the most personal of the three and the closest to the undisputed letters in tone.

Significance

The letter's themes of perseverance, guarded tradition ("what you have heard from me... entrust to faithful men", 2:2), the inspiration of Scripture and the model of Paul's approaching death have made it influential in Christian teaching on ministry and discipleship.
