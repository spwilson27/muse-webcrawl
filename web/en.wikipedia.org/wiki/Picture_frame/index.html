Picture frame

A picture frame is a protective and decorative edging for a picture, such as a painting or photograph. It makes displaying the work safer and easier, and both sets the picture apart from its surroundings and aesthetically integrates it with them.

Construction

Traditional frames are made of wood, joined at the corners, and may be gilded, painted, or stained. Modern frames also use metal (commonly aluminium), plastic, and composite materials. The frame typically holds the artwork behind glazing — glass or acrylic — with a backing board, and often a mount (mat) of card that spaces the work from the glazing and provides a visual border. The visible profile of the frame is called the moulding; the inner lip that holds the picture is the rabbet.

History

Framed borders appeared in ancient Egyptian and Greek art, where architectural frames were painted around tomb paintings. Carved wooden frames developed in 12th- and 13th-century Europe, initially as part of church altarpieces. During the Renaissance, frames became independent works of craftsmanship, often designed by the same workshops that produced furniture. Gilded Baroque and Rococo frames were elaborately carved; the 19th century brought mass-produced composition ornament, and the 20th century minimalism favoured plain metal or wood strips.

Styles

Named styles include the tabernacle frame, the cassetta, the swept frame, and gallery frames with clean profiles. Colour, width, and ornament are chosen to complement both the picture and the room.
