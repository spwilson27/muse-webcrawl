Woonerf

A woonerf (Dutch for "living yard" or "residential yard") is a type of street in the Netherlands where pedestrians and cyclists have legal priority over motorists and techniques of shared space and traffic calming are used to slow vehicles. The concept was developed in the late 1960s by residents of Delft and urban designer Niek De Boer, and was incorporated into Dutch law in 1976.

Design

A woonerf typically removes the distinction between road and pavement: there are no kerbs, and the surface is paved with bricks, planters, benches and play equipment that force drivers to proceed at walking pace and negotiate the space as guests. Dutch regulations limit traffic in a woonerf to a maximum of 15 km/h, and pedestrians may use the full width of the street, with children permitted to play throughout.

Influence

The woonerf inspired "home zones" in the United Kingdom, shared zones in Australia and numerous complete-streets and living-streets schemes in North America and elsewhere. It is a foundational example of the shared space approach associated with traffic engineer Hans Monderman.
