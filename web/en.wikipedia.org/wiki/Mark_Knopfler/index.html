Mark Knopfler

Mark Freuder Knopfler
Born: 12 August 1949, Glasgow, Scotland
Genres: Rock, roots rock, blues rock
Instruments: Guitar, vocals
Years active: 1965–present

Mark Freuder Knopfler (born 12 August 1949) is a British guitarist, singer, songwriter and record producer. He was the lead guitarist, singer and songwriter for the rock band Dire Straits (1977–1995), which he co-founded with his younger brother David Knopfler.

Early life

Knopfler was born in Glasgow, Scotland, to an English mother and a Hungarian-Jewish father, an architect who had fled Hungary in 1939. The family moved to Newcastle upon Tyne when he was a child. He studied journalism at Harlow College and English at the University of Leeds, then worked as a journalist and later as a lecturer.

Dire Straits

Dire Straits formed in London in 1977. Their self-titled debut album (1978) produced the hit single "Sultans of Swing". The band's fifth album, Brothers in Arms (1985), became one of the best-selling albums of all time, with hits including "Money for Nothing" and "Walk of Life". Knopfler disbanded the group in 1995 to pursue a solo career.

Guitar style

Knopfler is renowned for his distinctive fingerstyle playing, often eschewing a pick, and for his association with the Fender Stratocaster and later Gibson Les Paul. Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time.

Solo career

His solo albums include Golden Heart (1996), Sailing to Philadelphia (2000), Shangri-La (2004) and One Deep River (2024). He has also composed film scores, including Local Hero and The Princess Bride.
