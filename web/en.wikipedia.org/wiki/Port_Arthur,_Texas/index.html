Port Arthur, Texas

Port Arthur is a city in Jefferson and Orange counties in the U.S. state of Texas, located on the western shore of Sabine Lake near the Gulf of Mexico. It is a major petroleum refining and petrochemical center, home to some of the largest oil refineries in the United States.

History

The city was founded in 1895 by railroad promoter Arthur Edward Stilwell, who envisioned it as a deep-water port terminus for his Kansas City, Pittsburgh and Gulf Railroad. An earlier settlement on the site, Aurora, had been established in the 1840s but declined after the Civil War. Port Arthur grew rapidly after the 1901 Spindletop oil discovery near Beaumont, which triggered the Texas oil boom and made the Sabine area a hub of refining and shipping.

Geography and economy

Port Arthur lies on the Gulf Coastal Plain, connected to the Gulf of Mexico by the Sabine-Neches Waterway. Its economy is dominated by oil refining, petrochemicals, and the Port of Port Arthur, one of the world's busiest petroleum ports. Major refiners with operations there include Motiva Enterprises (operator of the largest refinery in the United States), Valero, and TotalEnergies.

Notable people

Port Arthur is the birthplace of rock singer Janis Joplin and football coach Jimmy Johnson.
