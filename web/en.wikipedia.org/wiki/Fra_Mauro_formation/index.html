Fra Mauro formation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fra Mauro formation
LocationLunar highlands, east of Oceanus Procellarum
Named forFra Mauro crater
Explored byApollo 14 (1971)
Fra Mauro, Apollo 14's landing site.

The Fra Mauro formation is a lunar highland region on the near side of the Moon, named for the nearby crater Fra Mauro. It is composed largely of ejecta thrown out by the giant impact that excavated the Imbrium basin — material that, if dated, would anchor the chronology of the whole lunar surface. That scientific prize made it the target of Apollo 13's intended landing and, after that mission's failure, of Apollo 14.

On 5 February 1971 Alan Shepard and Edgar Mitchell landed the LM Antares among Fra Mauro's rolling hills and spent two moonwalks sampling its rocks, including at the rim of Cone crater. The samples they returned dated the Imbrium impact to about 3.85 billion years ago and included the "Big Bertha" breccia later found to contain a clast of probable terrestrial origin — a piece of the early Earth preserved on the Moon.

Geology

The formation's hummocky terrain challenged the crew's navigation — they struggled to recognise landmarks in the featureless light — but its rocks sampled the deep crustal material excavated by Imbrium, fulfilling the mission's purpose and validating the choice of a highland site over the smooth maria of the first two landings.
