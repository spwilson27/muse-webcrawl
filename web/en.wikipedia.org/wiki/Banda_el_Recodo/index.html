Banda el Recodo


Banda el Recodo performing.

Banda el Recodo de Cruz Lizárraga is a Mexican banda from Mazatlán, Sinaloa, founded in 1938 by Cruz Lizárraga. It is widely regarded as the pioneering and most influential group in the modern banda genre — nicknamed "La Madre de Todas las Bandas" ("The Mother of All Bands") — and is credited with popularising banda music in Mexico and internationally through tens of thousands of concerts across five continents.

History

Cruz Lizárraga, a clarinettist from the village of El Recodo near Mazatlán (hence the name), formed the band in 1938. Unlike earlier brass bands that played instrumental polkas and waltzes, el Recodo added vocalists and a repertoire of rancheras, corridos, boleros, and cumbias, defining the modern Sinaloan banda format: a large ensemble built around clarinets, trumpets, trombones, tuba (sousaphone), and percussion.

Under Cruz Lizárraga the band recorded prolifically and toured constantly, following Mexican migration to the United States. After his death in 1995, leadership passed to his sons Germán and Alfonso ("Poncho") Lizárraga, and the band modernised its sound and presentation. In 2003 the veteran singer Julio Preciado-era legacy continued with new vocalists; the group has won Latin Grammy Awards for Best Banda Album, including for Haciendo Historia (2013), and in 2018 celebrated its eightieth anniversary with tours and festival appearances.

Influence

Banda el Recodo established the template that later generations — including its own youth offshoot Banda Los Recoditos and acts such as Banda MS — followed: matched uniforms, choreographed stage presentation, star vocalists, and a repertoire mixing romantic ballads with dance music. The band recorded hundreds of albums over its career and remains based in Mazatlán, where a statue honours Cruz Lizárraga.
