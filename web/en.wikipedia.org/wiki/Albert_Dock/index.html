The Royal Albert Dock is a complex of dock buildings and warehouses in Liverpool, England, designed by Jesse Hartley and Philip Hardwick, and opened in 1846. It was Britain's first structure built from cast iron, brick and stone with no structural wood, making it the first non-combustible warehouse system in the world, and it revolutionised dock design by allowing ships to load and unload directly into the warehouses.

History

Prince Albert opened the dock in 1846. The warehouses, with their characteristic Doric cast-iron columns, handled valuable cargoes such as brandy, cotton, tea, silk, tobacco, ivory and sugar. The dock declined with changes in shipping after the Second World War and closed in 1972, falling into dereliction before being regenerated in the 1980s under the Merseyside Development Corporation.

Regeneration and today

Reopened in 1984, the dock became a major leisure and cultural destination, home to Tate Liverpool, the Merseyside Maritime Museum, the International Slavery Museum, and The Beatles Story. The site formed part of Liverpool's Maritime Mercantile City UNESCO World Heritage Site from 2004 until its removal in 2021. In 2018 the dock received royal status, becoming the Royal Albert Dock.

Architecture

The five-storey warehouses enclose a rectangular basin of about 7.75 acres. Hartley's innovations included hydraulic cranes and dock offices; the buildings rest on cast-iron columns more than four metres high.
