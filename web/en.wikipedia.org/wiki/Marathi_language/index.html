Marathi (मराठी, Marāṭhī) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken predominantly by the Marathi people in the Indian state of Maharashtra, where it is the official language, and in Goa, where it shares official status with Konkani. With roughly 83 million native speakers, it is among the ten most widely spoken native languages in the world.

History

Marathi descends from Maharashtri Prakrit, the literary Prakrit of ancient Maharashtra, through an intermediate Apabhramsha stage. Inscriptions in a form of Marathi appear from around the 11th century CE. The language flowered in the medieval period with the devotional poetry of the Varkari tradition: the Dnyaneshwari, a commentary on the Bhagavad Gita by Saint Dnyaneshwar (13th century), is a foundational work, followed by the abhangas of Eknath, Tukaram, and other saint-poets. Under the Maratha Empire and in the nineteenth-century renaissance centred on Pune and Bombay, Marathi journalism, theatre, and prose expanded rapidly.

Script

Marathi is written in the Balbodh style of the Devanagari script. Until the twentieth century it was also written in the cursive Modi script, which was standard for administration and commerce; Modi was officially displaced by Balbodh Devanagari in the 1950s, though it is still studied and used by specialists.

Features

Like other Indo-Aryan languages, Marathi is written left to right, uses postpositions rather than prepositions, and distinguishes three grammatical genders. Its vocabulary draws heavily on Sanskrit, with substantial borrowings from Persian, Arabic, Portuguese, and English. Marathi retains some features lost in its northern relatives, including an inclusive/exclusive distinction in the first-person plural ("we" including or excluding the hearer).

Literature and media

Marathi has one of the oldest literary traditions among modern Indian languages and a vigorous modern literature, theatre (including the musical sangeet natak tradition), and cinema centred in Mumbai and Pune. The first Indian feature film, Raja Harishchandra (1913), was made by the Maharashtrian pioneer Dadasaheb Phalke.
