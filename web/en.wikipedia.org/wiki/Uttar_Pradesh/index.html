Uttar Pradesh

Uttar Pradesh (literally "Northern Province") is a state in northern India. With over 240 million inhabitants, it is the most populous state in India as well as the most populous country subdivision in the world — more populous than all but a few countries. Its capital is Lucknow.

Geography

The state occupies a large part of the fertile Indo-Gangetic Plain, watered by the Ganges (Ganga), Yamuna, and their tributaries. It borders Nepal to the north and the Indian states of Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Delhi, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, and Bihar.

History

The region was the heartland of ancient kingdoms including Kosala and the Kuru realm, and the setting of the Ramayana and Mahabharata traditions. Ayodhya, Mathura, Varanasi, and Prayagraj are among Hinduism's holiest cities, and Sarnath is where the Buddha gave his first sermon. Successively ruled by the Mauryas, Guptas, the Mughals (Agra and Fatehpur Sikri were imperial capitals), and the Nawabs of Awadh, the territory became the British United Provinces and was renamed Uttar Pradesh in 1950. In 2000 its northwestern hill districts were separated to form Uttarakhand.

Economy and culture

Agriculture — especially wheat, rice, and sugarcane — dominates the economy, alongside growing industry and services in cities such as Noida and Kanpur. The state sends the largest contingent of members to India's Parliament and has produced many of the country's prime ministers.
