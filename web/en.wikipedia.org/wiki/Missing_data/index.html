Missing data

In statistics, missing data, or missing values, occur when no data value is stored for a variable in an observation. Missing data are common in surveys, clinical trials and observational studies, and mishandling them can bias conclusions.

Mechanisms

Following Donald Rubin's framework, data may be missing completely at random (MCAR), where missingness is unrelated to any values; missing at random (MAR), where missingness depends on observed values; or missing not at random (MNAR), where it depends on the unobserved value itself.

Methods

Simple approaches such as listwise deletion and mean substitution are easy but often biased or inefficient. More principled methods include maximum likelihood estimation, multiple imputation, and inverse probability weighting, chosen according to the assumed mechanism.
