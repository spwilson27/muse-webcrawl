Garry Kimovich Kasparov (born 13 April 1963) is a Russian chess grandmaster, former World Chess Champion, and writer and political activist. He is widely considered one of the greatest chess players of all time. From 1984 until his retirement in 2005, Kasparov was ranked world No. 1 for a record 255 months, and his peak rating of 2851, achieved in 1999, stood as the highest ever recorded until surpassed by Magnus Carlsen in 2013.

Chess career

Kasparov became the youngest ever undisputed World Chess Champion in 1985 at age 22, defeating Anatoly Karpov. Their five championship matches between 1984 and 1990 are among the most famous rivalries in chess. In 1993 Kasparov broke with FIDE to form the Professional Chess Association, splitting the world title; he lost the classical title to Vladimir Kramnik in 2000.

In 1996 Kasparov defeated the IBM supercomputer Deep Blue in a match, but lost the famous 1997 rematch — the first time a reigning world champion lost a match to a computer under standard time controls. He retired from professional chess in 2005 while still ranked world No. 1.

After chess

Kasparov became a prominent Russian opposition activist and a vocal critic of Vladimir Putin, chairing the Human Rights Foundation and writing books including Winter Is Coming (2015) and Deep Thinking (2017) on artificial intelligence. He took Croatian citizenship in 2014.
