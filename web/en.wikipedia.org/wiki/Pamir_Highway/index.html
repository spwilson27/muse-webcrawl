Pamir Highway

The Pamir Highway, officially known as the M41, is a road traversing the Pamir Mountains through Afghanistan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, and Kyrgyzstan in Central Asia. It is the only continuous route through the difficult terrain of the mountain range and is the main supply route to Tajikistan's Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous Region. Reaching elevations above 4,600 metres (15,300 ft) at the Ak-Baital Pass, it is among the highest-elevation international highways in the world.

Route

The best-known section runs from Osh in Kyrgyzstan south through the Alay Valley, crossing into Tajikistan at the Kyzyl-Art Pass (4,282 m), past Lake Karakul, over the Ak-Baital Pass, and down through the town of Murghab and the Bartang and Panj river valleys to Khorog, the capital of Gorno-Badakhshan. From Khorog, the road follows the Panj River — the border with Afghanistan — westward, eventually continuing to Dushanbe, the Tajik capital. A southern branch reaches the Wakhan Valley near Ishkashim.

History

Portions of the route follow one of the branches of the ancient Silk Road. The modern road was built by the Soviet Union in stages: a rough military track over the passes was constructed in the late 19th century during the Anglo-Russian "Great Game" rivalry, the Osh–Khorog section was built in the 1930s, and the western section to Dushanbe was completed around 1940. The highway has suffered damage from earthquakes, landslides, and avalanches, and sections remain unpaved or poorly maintained.

Tourism

The highway is a celebrated destination for adventure touring — cyclists, motorcyclists, and overland travellers cross it for its stark high-altitude landscapes, nomadic Kyrgyz settlements, and views of peaks such as Lenin Peak (Ibn Sina Peak) and the Hindu Kush across the Wakhan Corridor.
