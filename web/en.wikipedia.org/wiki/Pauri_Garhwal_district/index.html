Pauri Garhwal is a district in the Indian state of Uttarakhand. Its headquarters is in the town of Pauri. The district lies in the Garhwal division, in the foothills and higher ridges of the western Himalayas, and is bounded by the districts of Chamoli, Rudraprayag, Tehri Garhwal, Dehradun, Haridwar, Udham Singh Nagar, Nainital, Almora, and Bijnor (Uttar Pradesh).

Geography

The district covers rugged mountainous terrain drained by the Alaknanda and Nayar rivers and their tributaries, with elevations rising from the plains near Kotdwar to high ridges in the north. Much of the district is forested with oak, pine, and deodar, and its climate varies with altitude from subtropical in the low valleys to temperate and alpine conditions on the higher slopes.

History

The area formed part of the Garhwal Kingdom, ruled from Srinagar, and later came under Gorkha and then British rule following the Anglo-Nepalese War in the early 19th century. Under British administration it was part of British Garhwal, and after Indian independence it was incorporated into Uttar Pradesh before becoming part of the new state of Uttaranchal (now Uttarakhand) in 2000.

Demographics

According to the 2011 census, Pauri Garhwal district has a population of 687,271, giving it a ranking of 506th among India's districts at the time. The population is overwhelmingly Hindu, Hindi and Garhwali are the principal languages, and the district has experienced significant out-migration to the plains, a phenomenon widely discussed in Uttarakhand as a cause of depopulated "ghost villages".

Places of interest

Notable places include the hill station of Lansdowne, founded by the British as a cantonment; the town of Srinagar on the Alaknanda, home to Hemwati Nandan Bahuguna Garhwal University; the temple town of Devprayag at the confluence of the Alaknanda and Bhagirathi; and the Khirsu scenic area near Pauri.
