Elman Service

Elman Rogers Service (May 18, 1915 – November 14, 1996) was an American cultural anthropologist known for his work in ethnology, social evolution, and Latin American studies. He taught at the University of Michigan and the University of California, Santa Barbara.

Career

Service received his bachelor's degree from the University of Michigan in 1941 and his Ph.D. in anthropology from Columbia University in 1951. He conducted fieldwork among the Guarani of Paraguay and studied the social organization of Latin American peasant communities.

He is best known for his typology of political integration, classifying societies into four levels: the band, the tribe, the chiefdom, and the state. This scheme, presented in his works Primitive Social Organization (1962) and Origins of the State and Civilization (1975), became widely used in introductory anthropology, though later scholars have criticized its unilineal assumptions.
