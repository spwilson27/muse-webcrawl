Royal Dutch Shell was the name of the Anglo-Dutch oil and gas group formed in 1907 by the merger of the Netherlands' Royal Dutch Petroleum Company and the British Shell Transport and Trading Company. For over a century it was one of the world's largest companies and one of the "supermajor" oil companies, vertically integrated across exploration, production, refining, distribution and petrochemicals.

History

The Royal Dutch company grew from oilfields in the Dutch East Indies (Sumatra); the Shell side descended from Marcus Samuel's trading business, which shipped oil in bulk tankers through the Suez Canal. The 1907 combination — 60% Dutch, 40% British — operated through twin parent companies until 2005, when they were unified under Royal Dutch Shell plc, incorporated in the UK with headquarters and tax residence in The Hague.

Major episodes in its history include the acquisition of BG Group in 2016 for about £47 billion, controversies over operations in Nigeria and the Ogoni conflict of the 1990s, the Brent Spar dispute of 1995, and a 2004 reserves overstatement scandal. In 2021 a Dutch court ordered Shell to cut emissions 45% by 2030. In late 2021 Shell announced it would simplify its structure, move its headquarters to London, and drop "Royal Dutch" from its name; the rename to Shell plc took effect in January 2022, ending the company's historic Dutch royal designation.
