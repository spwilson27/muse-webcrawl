Questionnaire

A questionnaire is a research instrument that consists of a set of questions (or other types of prompts) for the purpose of gathering information from respondents through survey or statistical study. A research questionnaire is typically a mix of close-ended questions and open-ended questions. Open-ended, long-term questions offer the respondent the ability to elaborate on their thoughts. The research questionnaire was developed by the Statistical Society of London in 1838.

Although questionnaires are often designed for statistical analysis of responses, this is not always the case. Questionnaires are used in many fields, including market research, social science, psychology, health care, and education. Well-known standardised questionnaires include personality inventories such as the Big Five Inventory, clinical instruments such as the PHQ-9 depression scale, and consumer surveys such as national censuses and the American Community Survey.

Question types

  • Closed-ended questions: dichotomous (yes/no), multiple choice, and rating-scale questions, including Likert scales
  • Open-ended questions: free-text responses that allow elaboration
  • Contingency questions: follow-ups asked only of respondents who answered a screening question in a particular way

Design considerations

Good questionnaire design avoids leading, loaded, double-barrelled, and ambiguous questions; orders items to minimise context effects; and is tested through cognitive interviewing and pilot studies. Psychometric quality is assessed by reliability (consistency of measurement) and validity (whether the instrument measures what it claims). Mode of administration—face-to-face, telephone, mail, or online—affects responses, as do social desirability bias, acquiescence bias, and question-order effects.

Administration and analysis

Responses to closed questions are coded numerically for statistical analysis; open responses may be coded thematically. Response rates and non-response bias are major concerns in survey methodology, and weighting is often applied to adjust samples to population benchmarks.
