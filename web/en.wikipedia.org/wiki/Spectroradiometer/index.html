Spectroradiometer

A spectroradiometer is an instrument used to measure the radiance or irradiance of a light source as a function of wavelength — that is, its spectral power distribution in absolute radiometric units. It combines the wavelength-separating optics of a spectrometer with calibrated radiometric detection, distinguishing it from a simple spectrophotometer, which typically reports relative transmission or absorbance.

Design

Light enters through foreoptics (a lens, diffuser, or integrating sphere defining the measured quantity and field of view), passes to a monochromator or dispersive element such as a diffraction grating or prism, and falls on a photodetector array or scanning photomultiplier. The system is calibrated against traceable standard lamps so that readings are expressed in units such as W·m−2·nm−1 (spectral irradiance) or W·m−2·sr−1·nm−1 (spectral radiance). Instruments range from laboratory double-monochromator systems of very low stray light to portable field devices with diode-array detectors covering the ultraviolet, visible, and near-infrared, roughly 200–2500 nm in combined ranges.

Applications

Spectroradiometers are used to characterize lamps, LEDs, displays, and lasers; in photobiology and phototherapy dosimetry; in colour science to compute colour-rendering and chromaticity values from measured spectra; and in remote sensing, where ground-based field spectroradiometers measure surface reflectance to calibrate and validate satellite and airborne sensors. Satellite-borne imaging spectroradiometers, such as MODIS on NASA's Terra and Aqua spacecraft, observe the Earth in dozens of spectral bands.
