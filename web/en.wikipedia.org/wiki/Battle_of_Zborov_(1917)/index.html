The Battle of Zborov was fought on 1–2 July 1917 near the Galician town of Zborov (now Zboriv, Ukraine) during the Kerensky Offensive of the First World War. It is remembered above all for the performance of the Czechoslovak Rifle Brigade, fighting within the Russian Army, against Austro-Hungarian forces.

Background

The Czechoslovak Legion was formed from Czech and Slovak volunteers and prisoners of war who wished to fight Austria-Hungary in support of an independent Czechoslovak state. During the Kerensky Offensive the Czechoslovak Rifle Brigade, some 3,500 men, was assigned a sector opposite Austro-Hungarian positions held in part by regiments recruited in Bohemia.

The battle

After an artillery preparation, the legionaries attacked on 2 July, breaking through successive lines of trenches and capturing positions several kilometres deep. They took around 3,000 prisoners — many of them Czechs in Austro-Hungarian service — along with artillery and machine guns, at a cost of under two hundred killed. It was one of the few clear successes of the offensive, which otherwise collapsed amid the disintegration of the Russian Army.

Legacy

Zborov became a founding legend of the Czechoslovak independence movement: the victory persuaded the Allies and the Russian Provisional Government to permit the expansion of the Legion, and it strengthened the hand of Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk and the Czechoslovak National Council. The battle was commemorated as a national holiday in interwar Czechoslovakia.
