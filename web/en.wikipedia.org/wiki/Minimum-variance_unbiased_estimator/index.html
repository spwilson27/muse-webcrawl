Minimum-variance unbiased estimator

In statistics, a minimum-variance unbiased estimator (MVUE), also called a uniformly minimum-variance unbiased estimator (UMVUE), is an unbiased estimator that has lower variance than any other unbiased estimator for all possible values of the parameter being estimated. An MVUE is a "best" unbiased estimator in the sense that no other unbiased procedure is more precise on average.

Existence and theory

While the class of unbiased estimators may contain no best member in general, MVUEs exist in many common families. The Lehmann–Scheffé theorem states that if a statistic is complete and sufficient for the parameter and an unbiased estimator is a function of it, then that function is the unique MVUE. The Rao–Blackwell theorem provides a method of improving an unbiased estimator by conditioning on a sufficient statistic, and the Cramér–Rao bound gives a lower bound on the variance of any unbiased estimator, which an estimator may or may not attain.

Examples

Familiar examples include the sample mean as the MVUE of the mean of a normal distribution, and the sample variance with denominator n − 1 as the MVUE of the normal variance. For a Poisson distribution the sample mean is likewise the MVUE of the rate parameter. In some families, such as the uniform distribution, the MVUE differs sharply from the maximum likelihood estimator.
