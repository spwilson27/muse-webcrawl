The Riachuelo class is a Brazilian class of diesel-electric attack submarines developed by the Brazilian Navy in partnership with the French Naval Group (formerly DCNS). The class is based on the French Scorpène-class submarine design, enlarged and adapted to Brazilian requirements, and forms the conventional arm of Brazil's Submarine Development Programme (PROSUB), which also includes the nuclear-powered Álvaro Alberto.

Development

In December 2008 Brazil and France signed a strategic defence agreement that included the transfer of technology for the construction of four conventional submarines and support for the development of a nuclear-powered boat. The submarines are built at the Itaguaí Naval Complex in Rio de Janeiro state by the Itaguaí Construções Navais (ICN) consortium. The programme involved extensive technology transfer, with Brazilian engineers and technicians trained in France and French personnel supporting construction in Brazil.

Design

The Riachuelo class is longer than the baseline Scorpène, at about 70.6 metres, with a surfaced displacement of roughly 1,900 tonnes. The boats have a crew of about 35 and an endurance of more than 70 days. Armament centres on six 533 mm torpedo tubes capable of launching heavyweight torpedoes, submarine-launched anti-ship missiles and naval mines. The class does not use air-independent propulsion; instead it relies on a lengthened hull with additional battery capacity.

Boats in class

  • S40 Riachuelo — launched 14 December 2018; commissioned into the Brazilian Navy on 1 September 2022.
  • S41 Humaitá — launched 11 December 2020; entered service in January 2024.
  • S42 Tonelero — launched 27 March 2024.
  • S43 Almirante Karam (formerly Angostura) — under construction, renamed in honour of Admiral Alfredo Karam.

The class takes its name from the Battle of Riachuelo (1865), a decisive Brazilian naval victory in the Paraguayan War.
