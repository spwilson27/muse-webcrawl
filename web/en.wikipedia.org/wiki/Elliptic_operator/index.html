Elliptic operator

Elliptic operator
Field: partial differential equations
Prototype: Laplacian
Key results: elliptic regularity, maximum principle, index theorem

In mathematics, an elliptic operator is a differential operator that generalises the Laplace operator. It is defined by the requirement that its principal symbol — the highest-order part of the operator, viewed as a polynomial in the frequency variables — be invertible for every nonzero frequency.

Definition

For a second-order operator Lu = −Σ aij ∂i∂ju + lower-order terms, ellipticity means the coefficient matrix (aij) is positive definite at every point. The Laplacian corresponds to the identity matrix. Uniform ellipticity, where the eigenvalues are bounded above and away from zero, gives the cleanest theory.

Properties

Solutions of elliptic equations are remarkably regular: if the coefficients and data are smooth, solutions are smooth (elliptic regularity), and harmonic functions satisfy strong maximum principles. Boundary-value problems such as the Dirichlet problem are well-posed for elliptic operators, in contrast to elliptic equations' hyperbolic and parabolic counterparts, which describe waves and diffusion instead.

Applications

Elliptic operators govern steady-state phenomena — electrostatics, steady heat conduction, incompressible fluid equilibria — and appear in geometry through operators such as the Hodge Laplacian; the Atiyah–Singer index theorem computes the index of an elliptic operator on a compact manifold from topological data.
