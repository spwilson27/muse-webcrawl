Rule, Britannia!

"Rule, Britannia!" is a British patriotic song, originating from the poem "Rule, Britannia" by James Thomson and set to music by Thomas Arne in 1740. It is strongly associated with the Royal Navy and the British Army, and with the idea of Britain's maritime supremacy. The song is famously performed each year at the Last Night of the Proms in London.

Origin

"Rule, Britannia" was first performed as the final chorus of the masque Alfred, staged at Cliveden, the country home of Frederick, Prince of Wales, on 1 August 1740, to commemorate the accession of George I and the birthday of Princess Augusta. The masque was written by James Thomson and David Mallet, with music by Thomas Arne. The song soon took on a life of its own and became a rallying symbol of British patriotism, especially as Britain's naval power grew during the 18th century.

Lyrics

The most famous lines are the chorus:

Rule, Britannia! Britannia, rule the waves!
Britons never, never, never shall be slaves.

The verses describe Britannia emerging from the sea, "a strong arm'd cherub" guarding Britain's shores, and proclaim that "the nations, not so blest as thee, / Must, in their turns, to tyrants fall; / While thou shalt flourish great and free, / The dread and envy of them all."

Uses

The song is inseparably linked with the Last Night of the Proms, where it forms part of the traditional sequence of patriotic music alongside "Land of Hope and Glory" and "Jerusalem". It is also played at naval ceremonies and has been quoted in works by composers including Beethoven (in Wellington's Victory) and Wagner. In 2020 the BBC's consideration of performing the song without lyrics at that year's Last Night—later reversed—prompted national political debate, illustrating the song's continued cultural resonance. The phrase "Britannia rules the waves" is often a deliberate misquotation: the original is the imperative "rule the waves".
