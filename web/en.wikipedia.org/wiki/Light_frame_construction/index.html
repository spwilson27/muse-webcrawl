Light frame construction

Light frame construction is a building method that uses closely spaced, small-dimension structural members — typically sawn lumber (wood framing) or cold-formed steel — to form walls, floors, and roofs. It is the dominant method for low-rise residential construction in North America, Australia, and parts of Europe.

Methods

Two principal wood framing systems are balloon framing, in which wall studs run continuously through two storeys, and platform framing, in which each floor is built as a platform on which the next storey of walls is raised. Platform framing, using standardised 2×4 and 2×6 lumber connected with nails, largely superseded balloon framing during the 20th century.

Characteristics

Light frame buildings are quick and economical to erect, rely on widely available materials, and can be sheathed with plywood or oriented strand board to provide shear resistance. Disadvantages include susceptibility to fire, moisture damage, and termites compared with masonry construction, and building codes limit the height and area of combustible light-frame structures.
