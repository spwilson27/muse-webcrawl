Charge-transfer complex

Charge-transfer salts often form intensely coloured crystals

A charge-transfer complex, or electron donor–acceptor complex, is an association of two or more molecules in which a fraction of electronic charge is transferred from an electron donor to an electron acceptor. The resulting electrostatic attraction stabilizes the complex, and the electronic transition responsible for the transfer gives many such complexes intense colours.

Examples

A classic example is the deep violet complex of iodine with starch, and the dark green quinhydrone formed from quinone and hydroquinone. The organic salt TTF–TCNQ, formed from tetrathiafulvalene and tetracyanoquinodimethane, is a famous organic metal—a charge-transfer complex that conducts electricity.

Theory

Robert Mulliken developed the quantum-mechanical theory of charge-transfer complexes in the 1950s, describing the ground state as a mixture of a no-bond structure and a dative structure in which an electron has been transferred from donor to acceptor. The characteristic charge-transfer absorption band in the visible or ultraviolet spectrum is a signature of complex formation.

Applications

Charge-transfer complexes are important in organic electronics, in the chemistry of fullerenes, and in biochemistry, where charge-transfer interactions contribute to enzyme–substrate binding and the colour of some protein complexes.
