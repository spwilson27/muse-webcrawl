Oberstdorf

Oberstdorf is a municipality and skiing and hiking resort in the Oberallgäu district of Bavaria, Germany. At the southern edge of the district, close to the Austrian border, it is Germany's southernmost village and one of the country's best-known mountain resorts.

Geography

Oberstdorf lies in the Iller valley in the Allgäu Alps, surrounded by peaks including the Nebelhorn (2,224 m), reachable by cable car, and the Fellhorn. Several side valleys — the Oytal, Trettachtal, and Stillachtal — radiate from the village, offering hundreds of kilometres of hiking trails. The nearby Breitachklamm is one of the deepest rock gorges in Central Europe. The small exclave of Kleinwalsertal, Austrian territory accessible by road only through Germany, lies just to the south.

Winter sports

Oberstdorf is famous as the opening venue of the Four Hills Tournament in ski jumping, held each year around New Year at the Schattenberg ski jump. It has repeatedly hosted the FIS Nordic World Ski Championships (1987, 2005, 2021) and the Ski Flying World Championships on its Heini-Klopfer ski flying hill, one of only five such hills in the world. The Nebelhorn Trophy, a long-running figure skating competition, is also held in the town.

Tourism

Designated a climatic health resort (Heilklimatischer Kurort), Oberstdorf draws visitors year-round for hiking, alpine skiing, and cross-country skiing. The village centre is largely car-free, and the town has a reputation for clean air — the result of high altitude and restrictions on through traffic.
