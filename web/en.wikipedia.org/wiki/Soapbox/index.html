Soapbox

A soapbox is a raised platform on which one stands to make an impromptu speech, often about a political subject. The term originates from the days when speakers would elevate themselves by standing on a wooden crate originally used for shipment of soap, or other dry goods, from a manufacturer to a retail store.

Political speaking

From the late 19th century, soapbox oratory was a feature of street politics in industrial cities: labour organisers, suffragists, temperance campaigners and preachers addressed crowds from improvised platforms. In London, Speakers' Corner in Hyde Park — where orators traditionally may still use a soapbox or small ladder — remains the best-known venue. The phrase "to get on one's soapbox" means to expound at length, often proselytising, on a pet subject.

Other meanings

By extension, "soapbox" also refers to soapbox racers — gravity-powered homemade cars originally built from soap crates, raced in the Soap Box Derby founded in the United States in the 1930s — and figuratively to any platform used to air one's views, including blogs and opinion columns.
