Suzhounese

Old town canals of Suzhou

Native to: Suzhou, Jiangsu, China
Language family: Sino-Tibetan → Sinitic → Wu → Taihu
Speakers: ~5–6 million (Suzhou urban area)
Known for: Preserving voiced initials and entering tone

Suzhounese (苏州话, Sūzhōuhuà), also called the Suzhou dialect, is a variety of Wu Chinese spoken in and around the city of Suzhou in southern Jiangsu, China. It is traditionally the prestige variety of Wu, the dialect of the old cultural capital of the Jiangnan region, and is famous throughout China for its soft, melodic sound — the phrase "soft Wu speech" (Wu nong ruan yu) refers above all to Suzhounese.

Features

Like other Taihu Wu varieties, Suzhounese conserves the three-way distinction of Middle Chinese stop initials — voiceless aspirated, voiceless unaspirated, and voiced — which Mandarin has lost. It has seven or eight tones, including a checked (entering) tone on syllables ending in a glottal stop, and extensive tone sandhi across words. Its vowel system is large, with front rounded vowels, and differs noticeably from neighbouring Shanghainese, with which it is nonetheless largely mutually intelligible.

Status

Suzhounese is the language of Suzhou pingtan storytelling and Kunqu opera traditions. Since the late 20th century, Mandarin promotion in schools and in-migration have reduced its use among younger speakers, and much local broadcasting has shifted to Mandarin or the declining regional lingua-franca role once held by Suzhou speech within Jiangnan. Preservation groups and local media now promote dialect classes and recordings.
