Mononym

A mononym is a name composed of only one word. An individual who is known and addressed by a mononym is a mononymous person.

In some cases, a mononym selected by an individual may have originally been that person's given name, family name, or one of their middle names. In other cases, the name may have been chosen by the individual or imposed by others. Historically, mononyms have been common for royalty and nobility — monarchs and other royals are traditionally known by their first name with a regnal number or a place-based epithet — and in many societies, ordinary people historically had only one name, with additional descriptors added when needed to disambiguate.

History

The structure of persons' names has varied over time and place. In some societies, individuals have been known by a single name throughout, while in others a hereditary family name became a requirement at a particular point in history. Ancient Greek individuals typically bore a single name, sometimes supplemented by a patronymic or demonym. Many Indonesian persons, particularly of older generations, have mononyms by law and custom; likewise, Icelandic naming conventions produce single given names used with patronymics rather than surnames.

Modern usage

Mononyms are especially common among entertainers and athletes, who adopt a single distinctive name as a stage name. Examples include the singers Madonna, Cher, Prince, Björk, Adele, and Beyoncé; the footballers Pelé, Ronaldo, and Ronaldinho; and visual artists such as Banksy. Some mononyms are truncated or stylised forms of a fuller legal name.
