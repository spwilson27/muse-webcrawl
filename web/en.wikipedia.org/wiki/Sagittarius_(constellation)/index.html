The "Teapot" asterism of Sagittarius, pointing toward the centre of the Milky Way.

Sagittarius is one of the constellations of the zodiac and is located in the Southern celestial hemisphere. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. Its name is Latin for "archer", and it is commonly represented as a centaur drawing a bow. Sagittarius lies in the direction of the centre of the Milky Way, so it contains many globular clusters and nebulae.

Notable features

The constellation's brightest stars form the easily recognized Teapot asterism: a body (including Kaus Australis and Kaus Media), a handle topped by Kaus Borealis, a lid, and a spout marked by Alnasl, from which the "steam" of the Milky Way appears to rise. The arrow of the archer points toward Antares in Scorpius.

Deep-sky objects

Sagittarius is exceptionally rich in deep-sky objects because the galactic centre — including the radio source Sagittarius A*, which hosts a supermassive black hole — lies within it. Famous objects include the Lagoon Nebula (M8), the Omega Nebula (M17), the Trifid Nebula (M20), the globular clusters M22 and M55, and the Sagittarius Dwarf Elliptical Galaxy, a satellite of the Milky Way being torn apart by tides.

Mythology

In Greek mythology Sagittarius is often identified with a centaur — sometimes Chiron, though Chiron is more usually associated with Centaurus — and Babylonian astronomy linked the figure with the god Nergal. In the zodiac, Sagittarius is a fire sign ruled by Jupiter, spanning approximately 22 November to 21 December in tropical astrology.
