Félix Antoine Tshisekedi Tshilombo (born 13 June 1963) is a Congolese politician who has served as the fifth President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo since January 2019. He is the son of Étienne Tshisekedi, the long-time opposition leader and founder of the Union for Democracy and Social Progress (UDPS), whom he succeeded as party leader in 2018.

Path to the presidency

Tshisekedi spent much of his early life in Belgium, where his father was in exile or under restriction during the Mobutu era. He worked in various jobs in Brussels before entering politics full-time. In the December 2018 general election, the electoral commission declared him the winner over Martin Fayulu, a result contested by Fayulu's camp and questioned by observers, but upheld by the Constitutional Court. His inauguration marked the first transfer of power in the country's history without widespread violence.

Presidency

His administration moved to free political prisoners, reassert control over the armed forces, and re-engage with Western partners and international lenders. Persistent armed conflict in the eastern provinces, particularly with the M23 rebel group, has dominated his tenure. Tshisekedi was declared the winner of the December 2023 election, securing a second term, and has also chaired the African Union (2021–2022) and the Southern African Development Community.
