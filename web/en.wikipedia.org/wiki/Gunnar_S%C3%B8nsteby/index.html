Gunnar Fridtjof Thurmann Sønsteby DSO (11 January 1918 – 10 May 2012) was a member of the Norwegian resistance movement during the German occupation of Norway in the Second World War. Known by the code name Kjakan ("the Chin") and as Agent 24, he led the "Oslo Gang" (Oslogjengen), the most successful Norwegian sabotage group of the war, and became the most highly decorated citizen in Norway's history — the only person awarded the War Cross with three swords.

Resistance work

Sønsteby, an accountant from Rjukan, joined the resistance after the German invasion of April 1940. He travelled repeatedly to Britain and returned as a trained agent of the Special Operations Executive's Norwegian Independent Company 1 (Kompani Linge). Operating in Oslo under a web of aliases and safe houses, he gathered intelligence, ran an illegal press and forgery operation, and coordinated with the central resistance leadership Milorg. From 1943 he commanded the Oslo Gang, whose operations included sabotaging aircraft and machine-tool production, destroying records of forced-labour conscription (thereby preventing thousands of Norwegians from being sent to Germany), derailing transport, and the spectacular raid that blew up files at the Oslo police registry. Despite being near the top of the Gestapo's wanted list, he was never captured.

Later life

After the war Sønsteby studied at Harvard, worked in business, and spent decades lecturing to Norwegian schoolchildren about the war and the values of democracy. He received the United States Presidential Medal of Freedom and numerous other honours. A statue of him stands at Solli plass in Oslo, and his wartime story was dramatized in the 2024 film Number 24. He died in Oslo in 2012 at the age of 94 and received a state funeral.
