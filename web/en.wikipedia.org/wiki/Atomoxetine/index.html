Atomoxetine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Atomoxetine, sold under the brand name Strattera among others, is a medication used to treat attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). It is a selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor and, unlike stimulant medications, is not classified as a controlled substance and has lower abuse potential. It was approved for medical use in the United States in 2002.

Medical uses

Atomoxetine is approved for ADHD in children aged six and older, adolescents and adults. Its therapeutic effect builds over several weeks of daily dosing, in contrast to the immediate effect of stimulants such as methylphenidate. It may be preferred where there are concerns about stimulant misuse, tics, or anxiety.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include decreased appetite, nausea, dry mouth, insomnia, fatigue and dizziness. It carries a boxed warning in the United States regarding an increased risk of suicidal ideation in children and adolescents. Increases in heart rate and blood pressure can occur, and rare cases of liver injury have been reported.

Pharmacology

Atomoxetine selectively inhibits the norepinephrine transporter, increasing noradrenergic signalling, particularly in the prefrontal cortex, where it also indirectly raises dopamine levels. It is metabolised mainly by CYP2D6; poor metabolisers have substantially longer exposure.
