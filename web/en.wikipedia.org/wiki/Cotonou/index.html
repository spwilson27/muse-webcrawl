Cotonou

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cotonou (French pronunciation: [kɔtɔnu]) is the largest city in Benin and its economic capital. It is the seat of government, although the official capital is Porto-Novo. Located on the coastal strip between Lake Nokoué and the Atlantic Ocean, the city had a population of roughly 680,000 in the commune proper in the early 2020s, with more than a million people in the wider urban area.

History

The name Cotonou means "by the river of death" in the Fon language. What began as a small fishing village grew after 1851, when the Kingdom of Dahomey ceded the area to France, and developed rapidly in the colonial period as a deep-water port for Dahomey (renamed Benin in 1975).

Economy and transport

The Port of Cotonou is one of the most important in West Africa, handling trade for Benin and for landlocked neighbours such as Niger. The city hosts the Dantokpa Market, among the largest open-air markets in the region, and Cadjehoun Airport, the country's main international airport. Motorbike taxis, known locally as zémidjans, are a distinctive feature of transport in the city.
