Macquarie Dictionary

The Macquarie Dictionary is a dictionary of Australian English. First published in 1981, it is generally regarded as the standard reference on Australian English spelling, pronunciation and usage, and is used as the authority by Australian governments, publishers and media organisations.

History

The project began at Macquarie University in Sydney in the 1970s, with the goal of producing a dictionary that treated Australian English on its own terms rather than as a variant of British English. The first edition, edited under the direction of Arthur Delbridge and colleagues, appeared in 1981. Successive editions have added new words, etymologies and encyclopedic entries on Australian people, places and institutions.

Features

The dictionary records Australian pronunciations using the International Phonetic Alphabet, includes extensive coverage of colloquialisms and Aboriginal English, and selects preferred Australian spellings (for example, favouring "-ise" spellings and forms such as "organise" alongside acceptable variants). Its publisher, Macquarie Dictionary Publishers, also produces concise and pocket editions, thesauruses and an online subscription version.

Word of the Year

Since 2006 the Macquarie Dictionary has run an annual Word of the Year selection, chosen by committee and public vote, which has recorded terms such as "podcast", "mansplain" and "cancel culture" entering Australian usage.
