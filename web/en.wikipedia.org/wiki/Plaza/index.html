Plaza

A plaza is an open public space, typically a square or marketplace, found in cities and towns around the world. The word comes from Spanish plaza (from Latin platea, a street or courtyard) and in Spanish-speaking countries the plaza is traditionally the centre of community life.

The Spanish colonial plaza

Under the Laws of the Indies (1573), Spanish colonial towns in the Americas were laid out around a central plaza mayor, fronted by the cathedral, the town hall (cabildo), and the governor's residence, with a grid of streets extending from it. The plaza hosted markets, festivals, proclamations, bullfights, and public punishment. Famous examples include Mexico City's Zócalo and the Plaza Mayor of Madrid.

Modern usage

Modern plazas range from monumental civic squares to landscaped areas in front of office towers. In North American English, "plaza" also came to denote a shopping centre or strip mall, and a service area on a toll road. Urban designers value plazas as pedestrian gathering places framed by buildings, seating, trees, fountains, and monuments.
