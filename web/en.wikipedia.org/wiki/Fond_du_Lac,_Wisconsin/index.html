Fond du Lac is a city in and the county seat of Fond du Lac County, Wisconsin, United States. Its name is French for "bottom of the lake," referring to its location at the southern shore of Lake Winnebago. The population was 44,678 at the 2020 census.

History

The area was a center of Native American settlement long before European contact. French fur traders established a post here in the late eighteenth century. The city was founded in 1836 by James Doty and others, incorporated as a village in 1847 and as a city in 1852. The arrival of railroads and the lumber and manufacturing industries drove growth in the nineteenth century.

Geography

Fond du Lac lies at the south end of Lake Winnebago, Wisconsin's largest inland lake. The Fond du Lac River flows through the city into the lake. Lakeside Park, at the northern edge of the city, features a lighthouse and is a local landmark.

Economy

Major employers include Mercury Marine (Brunswick Corporation), which has a large outboard-motor plant in the city, along with healthcare, food processing, and machinery manufacturing. The city is served by U.S. Route 151 and Interstate 41.
