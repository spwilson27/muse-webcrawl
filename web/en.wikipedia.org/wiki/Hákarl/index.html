Hákarl

Hákarl (an abbreviation of kæstur hákarl, "treated shark") is a national dish of Iceland consisting of Greenland shark or other sleeper shark that has been cured with a particular fermentation process and hung to dry for four to five months. It has a strong smell rich in ammonia and a fishy taste, and is traditionally eaten as part of the midwinter þorrablót feast.

Preparation

Fresh Greenland shark flesh is poisonous because of its high content of urea and trimethylamine oxide. Traditionally the shark is beheaded, gutted and buried in a shallow pit in gravelly sand, weighted with stones so the fluids are pressed out, and left to ferment for six to twelve weeks depending on the season. It is then cut into strips and hung to dry for several months.

Consumption

Hákarl is available year-round in Icelandic stores and is served cut into small cubes, often speared on cocktail sticks and washed down with a shot of the Icelandic schnapps brennivín. First-time eaters are often advised to pinch their nose because of the powerful ammonia smell. The chef Anthony Bourdain famously described it as the worst thing he had ever eaten.
