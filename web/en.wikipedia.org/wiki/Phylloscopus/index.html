Phylloscopus

Phylloscopus is a genus of small insectivorous Old World birds known as leaf warblers. They were formerly included in the Old World warbler family Sylviidae, but are now placed in their own family, Phylloscopidae, of which they are the only genus. The name Phylloscopus combines the Ancient Greek phullon, "leaf", and skopos, "seeker".

Description

Species are small, typically greenish or brownish above and whitish or yellowish below, often with a pale supercilium and wing bars. They are active, constantly moving birds of woodland and scrub, feeding mainly on insects gleaned from foliage. Many species are very similar in appearance and are best distinguished by their songs.

Distribution and habitat

Leaf warblers breed across temperate and boreal Eurasia, with some species extending into Alaska, and winter in Africa, southern Asia, and Southeast Asia. Most are strongly migratory.

Species

The genus contains around 80 species, including the willow warbler (Phylloscopus trochilus), common chiffchaff (P. collybita), wood warbler (P. sibilatrix), Arctic warbler (P. borealis), and greenish warbler (P. trochiloides), the latter famous as an example of a ring species around the Tibetan Plateau.
