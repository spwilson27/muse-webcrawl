The 2010 NFC Championship Game (played for the 2009 NFL season) was contested on 24 January 2010 between the Minnesota Vikings and the New Orleans Saints at the Louisiana Superdome. The Saints won 31–28 in overtime on Garrett Hartley's 40-yard field goal, advancing to Super Bowl XLIV — the first Super Bowl appearance in franchise history, which they went on to win.

The game

The game pitted veteran quarterbacks Brett Favre, in his first season with Minnesota, against Drew Brees. It was a punishing, turnover-marred contest: the Vikings fumbled six times (losing three) and outgained the Saints substantially, yet kept the score level. With the score tied 28–28 and Minnesota driving into field-goal range in the final minute of regulation, the Vikings were penalised for having twelve men in the huddle, and Favre then threw an interception to Tracy Porter while rolling right — a pass that became one of the most scrutinised plays of his career. In overtime, the Saints won the coin toss, drove into range with the help of a pass-interference call, and Hartley's kick sent New Orleans into delirium.

Aftermath

The game later became central to the New Orleans Saints bounty scandal ("Bountygate"), in which Saints defensive players and coaches were accused of operating a fund that rewarded injurious hits; Favre was widely seen as a target of the scheme. The overtime format also drew criticism — Favre never touched the ball in the extra period — contributing to later changes to postseason overtime rules.
