Handkerchief

A handkerchief (also called a hanky or, historically, a kerchief) is a form of a kerchief or bandanna, typically a hemmed square of thin fabric which can be carried in the pocket or handbag and is intended for personal hygiene purposes such as wiping one's hands or face, or blowing one's nose. A handkerchief is also sometimes used as a purely decorative accessory in a suit pocket (a pocket square).

History

Kerchiefs were used in ancient Greece and Rome, and cloth handkerchiefs became popular status symbols in Renaissance Europe; the word derives from the French couvre-chef, "head covering", via "kerchief". The square shape is traditionally credited to Marie Antoinette, whose husband Louis XVI issued an 1785 decree that handkerchiefs be square.

Use and decline

The disposable paper tissue, marketed from the 1920s (e.g., Kleenex, originally a makeup remover), largely displaced the cloth handkerchief for nose-blowing, though cloth versions persist as fashion items and have seen some revival on sustainability grounds.
