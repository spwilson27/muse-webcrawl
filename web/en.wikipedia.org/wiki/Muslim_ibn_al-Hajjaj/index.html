Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj

Abū al-Ḥusayn ʿAsākir ad-Dīn Muslim ibn al-Ḥajjāj ibn Muslim ibn Ward ibn Kawshādh al-Qushayrī an-Naysābūrī (c. 815 – 875 CE), commonly known as Imam Muslim, was a Persian Islamic scholar from the city of Nishapur. He is best known as the compiler of Sahih Muslim, one of the most authoritative collections of hadith (reports of the sayings and actions of the Prophet Muhammad) in Sunni Islam, second in esteem only to Sahih al-Bukhari by his teacher Muhammad al-Bukhari.

Life

Muslim was born in Nishapur in the Abbasid province of Khurasan, in present-day northeastern Iran, around 204 AH (819/820 CE) — estimates of his birth year range from 202 to 206 AH. He travelled widely in search of hadith, visiting Iraq, the Hejaz, Syria and Egypt, and studied under leading scholars including al-Bukhari, Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Ishaq ibn Rahwayh. He died in Nishapur in 261 AH (875 CE).

Works

His magnum opus, al-Musnad as-Sahih al-Mukhtasar, known as Sahih Muslim, is said to have been selected from some 300,000 hadith he had collected, and contains roughly 7,500 narrations including repetitions (about 2,600 without repetition). It is distinguished by Muslim's careful attention to the exact wording of reports and to the chains of transmission (isnad), and by its celebrated introduction (Muqaddima) on hadith methodology. Sunni tradition places it alongside Sahih al-Bukhari as one of the "two Sahihs" (as-Sahihayn). Muslim also wrote works on hadith narrators and classification, including Al-Kuna wal-Asma and At-Tabaqat.
