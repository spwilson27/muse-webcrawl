The T2K experiment (Tokai to Kamioka) is a long-baseline particle physics experiment in Japan that studies neutrino oscillations. A beam of muon neutrinos (or antineutrinos) is produced at the J-PARC accelerator complex in Tokai on the east coast and aimed 295 km west to the Super-Kamiokande water Cherenkov detector under Mount Ikeno in Gifu Prefecture.

Design

The neutrino beam is directed 2.5 degrees off-axis, which produces a narrow-band beam peaked near 0.6 GeV, maximising oscillation effects at the 295 km baseline. A suite of near detectors at 280 m from the target—including the magnetised ND280 detector—measures the beam before oscillation so that fluxes and interaction rates can be predicted at the far detector.

Results

In 2011 T2K reported the first significant indication that muon neutrinos transform into electron neutrinos (measuring the mixing angle θ₁₃ in this channel), and in 2013 announced definitive observation of muon-to-electron neutrino appearance. Running with antineutrino beams, the collaboration has since gathered evidence bearing on CP violation in the lepton sector: in 2020 T2K published results in Nature strongly constraining the CP-violating phase δCP and disfavouring values around +π/2, suggesting neutrinos may violate CP symmetry—as may be required to explain the universe's matter–antimatter asymmetry. T2K is collaborating and competing with the NOvA experiment in the United States, and will feed into the future Hyper-Kamiokande programme.
