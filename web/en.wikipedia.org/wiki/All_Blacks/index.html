All Blacks

The New Zealand national rugby union team, commonly known as the All Blacks (Māori: Ōpango), represents New Zealand in men's international rugby union. Regarded as the most successful team in the sport's history, they have won the Rugby World Cup three times (1987, 2011, 2015) and hold a winning record against every other national side. The team performs a haka before each match.

History

New Zealand played its first international in 1903, beating Australia in Sydney. The famous 1905–06 "Originals" tour of Britain, France and North America lost only once in 35 matches and fixed the All Blacks name — from their all-black kit — in the public imagination. The 1924–25 "Invincibles" went unbeaten through their entire tour.

Relations with South Africa's Springboks were long shadowed by apartheid: tours were contested on racial grounds, and the 1981 Springbok tour of New Zealand provoked mass protest. In the professional era the All Blacks dominated the Rugby Championship (formerly the Tri Nations), winning the vast majority of editions, and enjoyed long reigns at the top of the world rankings.

The haka

Before matches the team performs the haka, a traditional Māori posture dance. The best-known version is Ka Mate, composed by the Ngāti Toa chief Te Rauparaha; a newer haka, Kapa o Pango, was written for the team in 2005.
