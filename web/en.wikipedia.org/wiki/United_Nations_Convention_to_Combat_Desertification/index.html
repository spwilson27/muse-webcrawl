The United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD), formally the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification in Those Countries Experiencing Serious Drought and/or Desertification, Particularly in Africa, is a convention to combat desertification and mitigate the effects of drought through national action programmes employing long-term strategies supported by international cooperation.

Background

The convention is the youngest of the three "Rio conventions" that emerged from the early-1990s environmental diplomacy, alongside the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change and the Convention on Biological Diversity; unlike its siblings, it was negotiated after the 1992 Rio Earth Summit, following a General Assembly resolution of December 1992. It was adopted in Paris on 17 June 1994—a date now observed as World Day to Combat Desertification and Drought—and entered into force in December 1996.

Implementation

Affected country parties prepare National Action Programmes emphasizing participatory, bottom-up approaches involving local communities and land users. Regional implementation annexes cover Africa (the convention's priority), Asia, Latin America and the Caribbean, the Northern Mediterranean, and Central and Eastern Europe. In 2015 the parties endorsed Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN) as a guiding goal, linked to Sustainable Development Goal target 15.3, and most affected countries have since set voluntary LDN targets. The Global Mechanism, hosted by the convention, helps countries mobilize finance, while the Global Environment Facility serves as a financial mechanism for land degradation projects. Conferences of the Parties are held roughly every two years; COP16 met in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, in December 2024.
