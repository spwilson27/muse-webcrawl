Midway Atoll

Midway Atoll (Hawaiian: Pihemanu Kauihelani) is a 2.4-square-mile (6.2 km²) atoll in the North Pacific Ocean, near the northwestern end of the Hawaiian archipelago. It lies about one-third of the way between Honolulu and Tokyo, and roughly halfway between North America and Asia — the origin of its name. Midway is an unorganized, unincorporated territory of the United States and is administered by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service as the Midway Atoll National Wildlife Refuge.

Geography

The atoll consists of a ring-shaped barrier reef and several sand islets, principally Sand Island and Eastern Island. It sits atop a submerged shield volcano, one of the oldest in the Hawaiian–Emperor seamount chain, formed roughly 28 million years ago.

History

Midway was claimed for the United States in 1867 under the Guano Islands Act. It became a cable station and later a seaplane stop on Pan American's trans-Pacific routes. The U.S. Navy developed Naval Air Facility Midway, and in June 1942 the atoll was the site of the Battle of Midway, the decisive naval battle of the Pacific Theater of World War II, in which U.S. forces sank four Japanese aircraft carriers. The naval air facility closed in 1993, and jurisdiction passed to the Fish and Wildlife Service in 1996.

Wildlife

Midway hosts one of the world's largest colonies of Laysan albatross (the "gooney bird"), including the famed individual Wisdom, the oldest known wild bird, banded in 1956. The refuge is also an important habitat for Bonin petrels, Hawaiian monk seals, green sea turtles, and spinner dolphins. The atoll lies within the Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument.
