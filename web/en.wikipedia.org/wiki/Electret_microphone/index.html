Electret microphone

Electret microphone
TypeCondenser microphone
Invented1962, Bell Labs (Sessler & West)
UsesPhones, laptops, hearing aids, lavalier mics

An electret microphone is a type of electrostatic capacitor-based microphone that eliminates the need for a polarizing voltage by using a permanently charged material — an electret — in the diaphragm or backplate. Invented at Bell Laboratories in 1962 by Gerhard Sessler and James West, it is the most widely produced microphone type in the world.

Principle

An electret is a dielectric material with a quasi-permanent electric charge. Sound waves move the diaphragm, changing capacitance and generating a voltage signal, which is buffered by a small built-in JFET amplifier requiring only modest power.

Types

Foil-type, back-electret, and front-electret designs differ in where the charged material sits. MEMS microphones are a modern silicon-micromachined relative used in smartphones.

Applications

Billions are manufactured annually for telephones, computers, cameras, hearing aids, and recording equipment, valued for small size, low cost, and good frequency response.
