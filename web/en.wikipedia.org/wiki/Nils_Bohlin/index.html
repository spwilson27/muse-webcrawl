Nils Ivar Bohlin (17 July 1920 – 21 September 2002) was a Swedish mechanical engineer and inventor who invented the three-point safety belt while working for the car manufacturer Volvo.

Biography

Born in Härnösand, Bohlin studied mechanical engineering and began his career at the aircraft manufacturer Saab, where he worked on ejection seats for fighter pilots. In 1958 he was recruited by Volvo's president Gunnar Engelau as the company's first safety engineer.

The three-point seat belt

At Volvo, Bohlin developed the modern three-point seat belt, which combined a lap strap with a diagonal shoulder strap anchored so that the geometry formed a "V" pointing down toward the buckle, keeping the belt in position and spreading crash forces across the chest and pelvis. Volvo introduced the belt as standard equipment in 1959 in the Amazon and PV544 models, and — recognising the invention's life-saving potential — opened the patent so that other manufacturers could adopt it without charge. The three-point belt went on to become standard in vehicles worldwide and is credited with saving well over a million lives; the German patent office later named it among the most significant inventions of the automobile's first century.

Later work and honours

Bohlin later headed Volvo's safety research and worked on additional protective systems. He received numerous honours, including induction into the Automotive Hall of Fame and the National Inventors Hall of Fame in the United States.
