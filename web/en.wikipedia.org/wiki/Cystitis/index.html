Cystitis

Cystitis is inflammation of the urinary bladder. The condition most often arises from a bacterial urinary tract infection (UTI), typically caused by Escherichia coli, but it can also result from irritation, certain drugs, or radiation.

Signs and symptoms

Common symptoms include a frequent and urgent need to urinate, burning pain during urination (dysuria), cloudy or strong-smelling urine, pelvic discomfort, and sometimes blood in the urine (hematuria). Fever and flank pain suggest the infection may have spread to the kidneys (pyelonephritis).

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually based on symptoms and urinalysis, with urine culture used in complicated or recurrent cases. Uncomplicated cystitis is commonly treated with short courses of antibiotics such as nitrofurantoin or trimethoprim. Interstitial cystitis (bladder pain syndrome) is a chronic, non-infectious form with different management.
