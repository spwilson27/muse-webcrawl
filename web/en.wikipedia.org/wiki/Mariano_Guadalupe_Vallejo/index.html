Mariano Guadalupe Vallejo

Don Mariano Guadalupe Vallejo (4 July 1807 – 18 January 1890) was a Californio military commander, politician, and rancher. Born in Monterey, Alta California, he rose to become commandant general of the northern frontier, founded the town of Sonoma, and amassed vast landholdings before and during the transition of California from Mexican rule to the United States.

Career

Vallejo entered the Monterey presidio as a cadet and rose through the ranks, leading campaigns against Native peoples and overseeing the secularization of Mission San Francisco Solano. In 1835 he established the pueblo of Sonoma and a military post to guard the northern frontier against Russian expansion from Fort Ross. During the Bear Flag Revolt of 1846 he was taken prisoner at his Sonoma home by American settlers and held for several weeks at Sutter’s Fort.

Later life

After the conquest, Vallejo favoured California’s admission to the Union, served in the state constitutional convention and the first California State Senate, and donated land for a proposed state capital. Litigation over his ranchos and squatters steadily eroded his fortune. He spent his later years at his Sonoma home, Lachryma Montis, writing a five-volume history of California, and died in 1890. The city of Vallejo and a US Navy submarine are named in his honour.
