Ab urbe condita

Ab urbe condita (Latin: "from the founding of the City"), often abbreviated AUC or a.u.c., is a calendar era based on the legendary founding of Rome by Romulus, an event conventionally dated to 753 BC. Dates given ab urbe condita count years from that foundation: for example, AD 1 corresponds to AUC 754. The expression is also the traditional Latin title of Livy's monumental history of Rome, Ab Urbe Condita Libri ("Books from the Founding of the City").

Usage

Roman writers occasionally dated events from the founding of the city, using the chronology worked out by the scholar Marcus Terentius Varro, who placed the foundation in what we call 753 BC. Different authorities gave slightly different foundation dates (the "Capitoline" or "Fabian" eras, for instance), but the Varronian era became standard in later scholarship. In practice, Roman official dating was normally by the names of the consuls of the year, and the imperial period was more often dated by regnal years; the AUC era was used mainly by historians and chronographers rather than in everyday documents.

Conversion

To convert an AUC year to a BC/AD year: if the AUC year is 753 or less, the BC year equals 754 minus the AUC year; if the AUC year is 754 or greater, the AD year equals the AUC year minus 753. Thus Romulus's foundation is 1 AUC (753 BC) and the traditional date of the expulsion of the kings and founding of the Republic, 509 BC, is 245 AUC.

Livy's history

As a book title, Ab urbe condita refers to Livy's 142-book history of Rome from its legendary origins to the reign of Augustus, of which 35 books survive complete.
