Resist dyeing

Resist dyeing (resist-dyeing) is a method of dyeing textiles in which parts of the cloth are prevented—"resisted"—from taking up the dye, producing patterns in which the undyed or differently dyed areas form the design. Resist methods have been used for centuries in Asia, Africa, and the Americas.

Techniques

  • Wax resist — hot wax, often beeswax or paraffin, is applied to cloth, which is then dyed; the wax is later removed. This is the basis of batik in Indonesia and Malaysia.
  • Paste resist — a rice-flour or similar paste is applied through a stencil or freehand; examples include Japanese yūzenzome and katazome.
  • Mechanical resist — stitching, binding, folding, or clamping the fabric, as in shibori, tie-dye, and South Asian bandhani.
  • Ikat — the yarns themselves are resist-dyed before weaving.
