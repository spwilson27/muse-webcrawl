Transition from Ming to Qing

Manchu forces entered China proper through Shanhaiguan in 1644.

The transition from Ming to Qing, also known as the Ming–Qing transition, was a period of conflict spanning the mid-17th century, in which the Ming dynasty collapsed and the Manchu-led Qing dynasty conquered China. The process began with the rise of the Jurchen leader Nurhaci, who unified the Jurchen tribes and founded the Later Jin (renamed Qing in 1636), and ended only in 1683 with the Qing conquest of Taiwan.

Fall of the Ming

The Ming state was weakened by fiscal crisis, famine, epidemic and the effects of the Little Ice Age. In 1644 the rebel leader Li Zicheng captured Beijing, and the Chongzhen Emperor hanged himself. The Ming general Wu Sangui, guarding the Shanhai Pass, allied with the Qing regent Dorgon; their combined forces defeated Li at Shanhaiguan and occupied Beijing, where the young Shunzhi Emperor was enthroned.

Conquest and resistance

The Qing conquest of the south involved brutal campaigns, including the Yangzhou massacre of 1645, and was resisted by Southern Ming regimes for nearly two decades. The last Ming claimant, the Yongli Emperor, was executed in 1662. Ming loyalist forces under Koxinga held Taiwan until his grandson surrendered to the Qing in 1683, completing the conquest. The transition was one of the most destructive episodes in Chinese history, with population losses estimated in the tens of millions.
