Maurice René Fréchet

Maurice René Fréchet (2 September 1878 – 4 June 1973) was a French mathematician. He made major contributions to topology and functional analysis, introducing the concept of metric spaces in his 1906 doctoral thesis, and is regarded as a founder of the theory of abstract spaces.

Career

Fréchet studied at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris, where Jacques Hadamard was an important mentor. He taught at the universities of Rennes, Poitiers, and Strasbourg, and from 1928 at the University of Paris. He was elected to the French Academy of Sciences in 1956.

Contributions

Fréchet's 1906 thesis Sur quelques points du calcul fonctionnel introduced metric spaces and abstracted the notions of convergence and compactness away from Euclidean space. He developed the theory of Fréchet spaces (locally convex F-spaces), the Fréchet derivative in functional analysis, and the Fréchet distance between curves. In probability and statistics he worked on convergence of random variables and gave his name to the Fréchet distribution and the Fréchet–Hoeffding bounds for copulas. He also contributed to the Riesz–Fréchet representation theorem's history. Many fundamental notions of modern analysis and topology trace to his program of studying functions as points in abstract spaces.
