Hermes 450

The Elbit Hermes 450 is a medium-sized, multi-payload unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) designed by the Israeli company Elbit Systems for tactical long-endurance reconnaissance, surveillance, and target acquisition missions. It is one of the most widely exported military drones in its class and has served as a backbone UAV of the Israel Defense Forces since the late 1990s.

Design and operation

The Hermes 450 is a single-engine aircraft with a pusher propeller, a length of about 6.1 m and a wingspan of about 10.5 m. It can carry electro-optical/infrared sensors, synthetic-aperture radar, and signals-intelligence payloads simultaneously, has an endurance of more than 20 hours, and operates at altitudes up to roughly 18,000 feet. It is controlled from a ground station, typically by a crew of two.

Users and derivatives

Besides Israel, the type has been operated by the United Kingdom as the Watchkeeper WK450 (a derivative developed with Thales), and by air forces and armies in Latin America, Southeast Asia, and elsewhere, including Brazil, Mexico, the Philippines, and Azerbaijan. The larger Hermes 900 was developed from the same family.
