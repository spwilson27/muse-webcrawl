Viacom Inc. (the second company to bear the name) was an American multinational mass media and entertainment conglomerate that existed from December 31, 2005, to December 4, 2019. It was created as a spin-off from the original Viacom, which was renamed CBS Corporation in the split, and was controlled by Sumner Redstone through National Amusements.

The company operated television networks and channels including MTV, Nickelodeon, Comedy Central, BET, VH1, CMT, TV Land, and the Paramount Network (formerly Spike), as well as the film studio Paramount Pictures and various international channel brands. Its headquarters were at One Astor Plaza in Midtown Manhattan.

History

The original Viacom announced in 2005 that it would split into two publicly traded companies, seeking to separate its faster-growing cable networks and film businesses from CBS's slower-growing broadcasting, radio, and publishing assets. The split was completed on December 31, 2005: the new Viacom retained MTV Networks and Paramount Pictures, while CBS Corporation kept the CBS network, UPN, Showtime, and Simon & Schuster.

During the 2010s Viacom faced declining ratings at several networks and disputes over carriage fees, including a high-profile 2014–2015 standoff with Suddenlink and a 2019 dispute with AT&T/DirecTV. Chief executives included Tom Freston (2006), Philippe Dauman (2006–2016), and Bob Bakish (2016–2019).

Merger with CBS

After several years of negotiations encouraged by Shari Redstone, CBS and Viacom announced on August 13, 2019, that they would re-merge. The merger closed on December 4, 2019, forming ViacomCBS, which was later renamed Paramount Global in February 2022.
