FHIR

Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR, pronounced "fire") is a standard describing data formats and elements ("resources") and an application programming interface (API) for exchanging electronic health records (EHRs). It is maintained by the standards organisation Health Level Seven International (HL7) and has become the dominant modern health-data interoperability standard.

Design

FHIR builds on familiar web technology: resources such as Patient, Observation, Condition and Medication are exchanged over RESTful APIs, serialized in JSON or XML, and identified by URLs. The standard was designed so that the core specification covers roughly the common 80% of use cases, with profiles and extensions (published as implementation guides) handling local variation. The SMART on FHIR framework adds OAuth2-based authorization so third-party apps can plug into EHR systems.

Adoption

Adoption was accelerated by regulation: the US 21st Century Cures Act rules require certified health IT to expose FHIR R4 APIs for patient access, and major EHR vendors and technology companies (through initiatives such as the Argonaut Project) support it. Successive releases — DSTU2 (2015), R4 (2019) and R5 (2023) — have progressively moved more content to normative status.
