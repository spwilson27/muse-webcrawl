Chang'e 5

Chang'e 5 (Chinese: 嫦娥五号; pinyin: Cháng'é wǔhào) was a robotic lunar exploration mission operated by the China National Space Administration (CNSA). Launched on 23 November 2020, it landed on the Moon on 1 December 2020 in the northern Oceanus Procellarum near Mons Rümker, collected approximately 1,731 grams of lunar material, and returned the samples to Earth on 16 December 2020. It was the first lunar sample-return mission since the Soviet Union's Luna 24 in 1976, and it made China the third country to return samples from the Moon after the United States and the Soviet Union.

Overview

Chang'e 5 was the fifth mission of the Chinese Lunar Exploration Program, named after the Chinese moon goddess Chang'e. The spacecraft had a launch mass of about 8.2 tonnes and consisted of four modules: a service module (orbiter), a lander, an ascender, and a sample return capsule. The mission profile closely resembled that of the crewed Apollo missions in miniature: the lander touched down, drilled and scooped samples, and transferred them to the ascender, which lifted off from the lunar surface, rendezvoused and docked with the orbiter in lunar orbit, and transferred the sample container. The orbiter then carried the return capsule back to Earth.

Scientific results

Analysis of the returned samples showed that the basalts at the landing site were about 2.03 billion years old, making them the youngest lunar rocks ever dated and filling a key gap in the Moon's crater-counting chronology. The samples also contained traces of water-bearing minerals and provided constraints on the late-stage volcanic activity of the Moon. After delivering the return capsule, the orbiter continued on an extended mission to the Sun–Earth L1 Lagrange point.
