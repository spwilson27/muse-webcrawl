Radial circuit

A radial circuit is an electrical circuit in which the wiring branches out from a single point, such as a consumer unit or distribution board, to serve sockets or other outlets, without the conductors returning to form a loop. Each branch is a separate feed, so the current in a branch serves only the loads connected to that branch.

Characteristics

Radial circuits are widely used in British electrical installations, where a radial circuit supplying socket outlets is typically protected by a 20 A or 32 A circuit breaker and wired in 2.5 mm² cable (for lower-rated radials) or 4 mm² cable. Unlike a ring circuit, only one path exists between the supply and each outlet, which makes fault-finding simpler but means the cable must be sized for the full load it carries.

Uses

Radial circuits are used for dedicated appliances such as cookers, showers and immersion heaters, and for socket outlets in smaller dwellings or outbuildings. Lighting circuits are also almost always wired radially.
