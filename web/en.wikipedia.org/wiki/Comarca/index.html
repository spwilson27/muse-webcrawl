Comarca
TypeRegional division
CountriesSpain, Portugal, Brazil, Panama and others

A comarca (Spanish and Portuguese: comarca) is a traditional region or local administrative division found in parts of Spain, Portugal, Brazil, Panama, and Nicaragua. The term roughly corresponds to a county, district, or shire in English, though its legal status varies widely by country and region.

Spain

In Spain, comarcas are traditional or legally recognised groupings of municipalities, especially in Catalonia, Aragon, Galicia, and the Basque Country. In Catalonia the comarca is an official tier of local government with its own council, while in other autonomous communities it may be merely a customary or service-provision grouping.

Portugal and Brazil

In Portugal a comarca historically denoted a judicial district. In Brazil the term survives principally in the judiciary, where a comarca is the territorial jurisdiction of a court, often comprising several municipalities.

Indigenous comarcas of Panama

In Panama, comarcas indígenas are semi-autonomous territories granted to Indigenous peoples, such as the Guna Yala and Ngäbe-Buglé comarcas, with their own congresses and internal governance.
