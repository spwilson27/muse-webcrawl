Joseph Antoine Ferdinand Plateau (14 October 1801 – 15 September 1883) was a Belgian physicist and mathematician. He was one of the first people to demonstrate the illusion of a moving image, using a device known as the phenakistoscope, and he made fundamental contributions to the study of soap films and bubbles.

Early life and education

Plateau was born in Brussels. His father, Antoine Plateau, was a talented flower painter. Joseph studied law briefly before turning to science, earning a doctorate in physical and mathematical sciences from the University of Liège in 1829. In 1835 he became professor of physics at Ghent University.

Persistence of vision

In 1829 Plateau submitted his doctoral thesis on impressions made by light on the retina. His later research on the persistence of vision led him, in 1832, to invent the phenakistoscope, a device with a spinning disc bearing sequential images that, viewed in a mirror through slits, created the illusion of motion. The device is regarded as a precursor of cinema and animation.

Plateau's eyesight deteriorated over years of experiments, including staring at the sun during his thesis research, and he eventually became completely blind. He nevertheless continued his scientific work with the assistance of colleagues and family.

Soap films and Plateau's laws

Working largely from memory and with assistants describing results to him, Plateau conducted extensive experiments on soap films stretched on wire frames. He observed that soap films meet in characteristic configurations, summarized in Plateau's laws: films meet three at a time along an edge at angles of 120 degrees, and edges meet four at a time at a vertex at the tetrahedral angle of about 109.47 degrees. The related mathematical question of finding the surface of least area spanning a given boundary is known as Plateau's problem.

Legacy

The Plateau-Rayleigh instability, describing how a stream of liquid breaks into droplets, is named partly for him. In 2019 Google commemorated his 218th birthday with a Doodle featuring phenakistoscope-style animation.
