The Jackson 5

The Jackson 5 (later known as the Jacksons) were an American pop band composed of members of the Jackson family: brothers Jackie, Tito, Jermaine, Marlon and Michael. Managed by their father Joe Jackson, they became one of Motown's most successful acts and launched Michael Jackson's solo career.

OriginGary, Indiana, U.S.
GenresPop, soul, funk, disco
Years active1964–1989 (reunions thereafter)
LabelsMotown, Epic

Career

Signed to Motown in 1968, the group opened with four consecutive US number-one singles: "I Want You Back", "ABC", "The Love You Save" and "I'll Be There". Their energetic performances, choreography and Saturday-morning cartoon series made them a cultural phenomenon in the early 1970s. In 1975 they moved to Epic Records, renamed themselves the Jacksons, and Jermaine was replaced by youngest brother Randy.

Legacy

The Jackson 5 were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997. Their sound bridged Motown soul and modern pop, and their success paved the way for later family and boy-band acts.
