Fuseki

Not to be confused with joseki.

In the game of Go, fuseki (Japanese: 布石) is the whole-board opening strategy, the phase of the game in which players stake out territory and influence across the board, typically lasting through the first 20 to 30 moves. As the game progresses into the middle game (chūban), fighting becomes more localized. Fuseki is contrasted with joseki, the studied local sequences played in the corners.

Principles

Opening theory emphasizes playing first in the corners, then the sides, and only later toward the center, because corners offer the most territory for the fewest stones. Early moves typically approach or enclose corners, extend along the sides, and establish frameworks (moyo) that later play will contest. Balance between territory and influence, and between solid and fast development, is a central strategic question.

Named patterns

Famous fuseki include the Shūsaku fuseki, associated with Honinbo Shusaku in the 19th century; the Chinese fuseki, popularized in the 1970s, which builds a formation parallel to one side; the mini-Chinese and Kobayashi fuseki; and the sanrensei, in which a player takes three star points along one side. In the 2010s, analysis by the artificial intelligence program AlphaGo revolutionized opening theory, reviving early 3-3 point invasions and other moves that professionals had long considered premature.
