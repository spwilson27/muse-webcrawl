In thermodynamics, the binodal, also known as the coexistence curve, is the curve on a phase diagram that marks the boundary between a region in which a mixture exists as a single homogeneous phase and a region in which it separates into two coexisting phases. On a temperature–composition diagram for a partially miscible liquid pair, the binodal is the dome-shaped curve enclosing the miscibility gap: any overall composition inside the dome splits into two liquid phases whose compositions are given by the points where a horizontal tie line meets the binodal.

A temperature–composition phase diagram: the binodal (outer curve) encloses the two-phase region.

Thermodynamic basis

The binodal is determined by the condition of phase equilibrium: within the two-phase region, the chemical potential (equivalently, the partial molar Gibbs free energy) of each component is equal in both phases. Geometrically, on a plot of Gibbs free energy versus composition, the coexisting compositions are the points joined by a common tangent to the free-energy curve. Compositions between the binodal and the inner spinodal curve are metastable — a homogeneous phase there can persist until nucleation initiates separation — whereas compositions inside the spinodal are unstable and separate spontaneously by spinodal decomposition.

Critical point

The binodal and spinodal meet at the critical (consolute) point, which caps the dome. Above an upper critical solution temperature (or below a lower critical solution temperature, for systems showing closed-loop miscibility) the components are miscible in all proportions. Near the critical point the two phases become identical and phenomena such as critical opalescence appear.

Applications

Binodal curves govern practical liquid–liquid extraction, polymer blending and membrane formation (where crossing the binodal by adding a non-solvent precipitates a polymer film), and the cloud-point behavior of surfactant and protein solutions. In polymer science, phase diagrams computed from Flory–Huggins theory predict binodals that guide the design of blends and porous materials.
