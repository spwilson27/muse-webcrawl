Bihar (Hindi: बिहार) is a state in eastern India, bordered by Nepal to the north and by the Indian states of Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand, and West Bengal. It is the third most populous state in India and one of its fastest-growing economically, though it remains among the poorest by per-capita income. Its capital and largest city is Patna, the ancient Pataliputra.

History

Bihar was a cradle of early Indian civilization: the Magadha kingdom produced the Maurya and Gupta empires, and the region is sacred to Buddhism and Jainism—the Buddha attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, and Mahavira was born at Vaishali. Ancient Nalanda and Vikramshila were world-renowned centres of learning. In the medieval period the region came under the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughals; under British rule it formed part of the Bengal Presidency until the province of Bihar and Orissa was created in 1912. The southern, mineral-rich part of the state became Jharkhand in 2000.

Geography and economy

The fertile Gangetic plain is bisected by the Ganges and drained by tributaries such as the Gandak, Kosi, and Son; the flood-prone north contrasts with the drier south. Agriculture—rice, wheat, maize, litchis, and makhana—employs most of the workforce, and large-scale out-migration for labour is a defining economic feature. Growth since the mid-2000s has been driven by construction, services, and improved governance, though industrialization remains limited.

Culture

Bihar's languages include Hindi, Maithili, Bhojpuri, and Magahi. Major festivals include Chhath, dedicated to the sun god Surya, and the Sonepur cattle fair is among Asia's largest. Madhubani (Mithila) painting is the region's most famous art form.
