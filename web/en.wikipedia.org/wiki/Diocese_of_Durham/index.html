Diocese of Durham

The Diocese of Durham is a Church of England diocese in North East England, covering most of County Durham, parts of Tyne and Wear, and a small area of North Yorkshire. Its cathedral is Durham Cathedral, seat of the Bishop of Durham, one of the most senior bishops in the Church of England.

The diocese traces its origins to the Anglo-Saxon see of Lindisfarne, founded by St Aidan in 635, whose community carried the body of St Cuthbert across Northumbria before settling at Durham in 995. For centuries the bishops of Durham ruled as Prince-Bishops, wielding secular palatine powers over the County Palatine of Durham as a buffer against Scotland; these powers were curtailed in the 1830s. The medieval diocese once stretched from the Tees to the Tweed and beyond; the Archdeaconry of Northumberland became the Diocese of Newcastle in 1882.

Today the diocese is in the Province of York and is led by the Bishop of Durham, assisted by the suffragan Bishop of Jarrow. Its administrative centre is in Durham, and its parishes include the great industrial towns of the Wear and the Tees as well as rural former mining communities.
