Adverse effect

FieldPharmacology, medicine
SynonymSide effect (broader usage)
MonitoringPharmacovigilance

An adverse effect is an undesired harmful effect resulting from a medication or other medical intervention, such as surgery. An adverse outcome may be indicated by symptoms, abnormal laboratory findings, or complications, and is caused by the intervention rather than by the underlying disease alone.

Terminology

The term is often used interchangeably with "side effect," though strictly a side effect is any effect — therapeutic or adverse — secondary to the intended one. An adverse drug reaction is a specific form of adverse effect in which a drug is causally implicated. Reactions are classified, in the Rawlins–Thompson system, into Type A reactions, which are dose-dependent and predictable from the drug's pharmacology, and Type B ("bizarre") reactions, which are dose-independent and unpredictable, such as allergies and idiosyncratic responses. Extended classifications add types C through F covering chronic, delayed, withdrawal, and failure-of-therapy effects.

Assessment and reporting

Severity is graded from mild to life-threatening or fatal, and causality may be assessed with tools such as the Naranjo algorithm. Clinical trials systematically record adverse events, and after approval, regulators rely on pharmacovigilance systems — for example the FDA's MedWatch and the Yellow Card scheme in the United Kingdom — to detect rare harms, which can lead to label changes, warnings, or withdrawal of a drug from the market.
