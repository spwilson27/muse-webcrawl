The Countryside Alliance is a British campaigning organisation that promotes the rural way of life, including field sports such as hunting, shooting and fishing, and campaigns on wider rural issues including farming, rural services, housing and broadband provision. It was formed in 1997–98 from the merger of the British Field Sports Society, the Countryside Business Group and the Countryside Movement.

The Alliance came to national prominence through mass demonstrations in London. The Countryside March of March 1998 drew more than 250,000 people, and the "Liberty and Livelihood" march of September 2002 attracted around 400,000 participants, protesting against the proposed ban on hunting with dogs in England and Wales and highlighting the foot-and-mouth crisis and rural decline. The hunting ban nevertheless became law with the Hunting Act 2004, and the Alliance was involved in unsuccessful legal challenges to it.

Since then the organisation has campaigned on issues including the protection of rural post offices and pubs, affordable rural housing, fly-tipping, metal theft, and the future of farming and food production, while continuing to seek amendment or repeal of the Hunting Act.
