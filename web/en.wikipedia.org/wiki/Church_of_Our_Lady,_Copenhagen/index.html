Church of Our Lady, Copenhagen

The Church of Our Lady (Vor Frue Kirke), Copenhagen Cathedral.

The Church of Our Lady (Danish: Vor Frue Kirke) is the cathedral of Copenhagen, the capital of Denmark. Located on Vor Frue Plads in the city centre, next to the University of Copenhagen, the present Neoclassical building was designed by the architect Christian Frederik Hansen and consecrated in 1829. It is the seat of the Bishop of Copenhagen of the Evangelical Lutheran Church of Denmark.

History

A church dedicated to the Virgin Mary stood on the site from the late twelfth century, and it served as the city's principal church and, from 1443, its cathedral. The medieval building hosted the coronations of several Danish monarchs and the wedding of Margrete I's son. It was repeatedly damaged by fire, including during the fires of 1728, and was largely destroyed when British rockets set the city ablaze during the bombardment of Copenhagen in 1807, the church tower being used as an aiming point.

Hansen's rebuilding (1817–1829) employed strict Neoclassicism: a temple-like pedimented portico, a barrel-vaulted interior, and a tower rebuilt in a plainer style. The sculptor Bertel Thorvaldsen decorated the interior with his famous figures of Christ and the twelve apostles in Carrara marble; the Christus, showing Christ with outstretched arms, became one of the most widely copied religious statues of the nineteenth century and the model for such works elsewhere.

Architecture

The church measures some 83 metres in length and seats around 1,000 people. Along the nave, Thorvaldsen's apostle statues stand in niches, and the altar is surmounted by the Christus. The baptismal font is an angel kneeling with a shell, also by Thorvaldsen. The building hosts royal weddings and funerals, and its bells include the largest in Denmark.
