Iron regulatory protein

Iron regulatory proteins (IRPs), also known as iron-responsive element-binding proteins, are cytosolic proteins that control the expression of genes involved in iron metabolism by binding to iron-responsive elements (IREs) in messenger RNA. The two IRPs in vertebrates are IRP1 (encoded by ACO1) and IRP2 (encoded by IREB2).

Mechanism

When cellular iron is scarce, IRPs bind IREs. Binding to IREs in the 5′ untranslated region of ferritin mRNA blocks its translation, reducing iron storage, while binding to IREs in the 3′ untranslated region of transferrin receptor mRNA stabilises the transcript, increasing iron uptake. When iron is abundant, IRP1 assembles a [4Fe–4S] cluster and functions instead as the enzyme cytosolic aconitase, while IRP2 is targeted for proteasomal degradation via the ubiquitin ligase adaptor FBXL5.

Physiology

The IRP–IRE system coordinates iron uptake, storage and use in response to cellular iron levels and also responds to oxygen, oxidative stress and nitric oxide. Disruption of IRP2 in mice causes neurodegeneration and anaemia, underlining the system's importance in systemic iron homeostasis.
