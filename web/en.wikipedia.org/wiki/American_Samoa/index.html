American Samoa (Samoan: Amerika Sāmoa) is an unincorporated and unorganized territory of the United States located in the Polynesian region of the South Pacific Ocean. It consists of the eastern part of the Samoan Islands archipelago: the inhabited volcanic islands of Tutuila, Aunuʻu, and the Manuʻa Islands (Ofu, Olosega, and Taʻū), plus the uninhabited Rose Atoll and Swains Island. The western islands of the archipelago form the independent nation of Samoa.

Geography and history

The islands were settled by Polynesian voyagers roughly 3,000 years ago. Contact with Europeans began in the 18th century. Under the Tripartite Convention of 1899, Germany and the United States partitioned the Samoan Islands, with the eastern islands passing to the United States. Tutuila's Pago Pago Harbor is one of the finest deep-water harbors in the South Pacific and was long valued as a naval station. The territory is administered by the U.S. Department of the Interior; its residents are U.S. nationals but not automatically U.S. citizens at birth.

Economy and culture

Tuna canning has historically dominated the economy, alongside government employment. Faʻa Sāmoa — "the Samoan way," centred on the extended family (ʻaiga) and the chiefly matai system — remains central to daily life, and the territory has one of the highest rates of military enlistment per capita in the United States. The National Park of American Samoa protects rainforest and coral reef ecosystems across three islands.
