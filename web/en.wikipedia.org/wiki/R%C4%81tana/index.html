Founded1918–1925, New Zealand
TypeMāori Christian church and political movement

Rātana is a Māori Christian church and political movement founded in New Zealand by Tahupōtiki Wiremu Rātana (1873–1939). Following a vision on 8 November 1918, Rātana began a faith-healing ministry that drew thousands of Māori followers, and in 1925 the Rātana Church was formally established.

Church

The movement's headquarters are at Rātana Pā near Whanganui, where a distinctive temple (Te Temepa Tapu) was opened in 1928. The church blends Christian teaching with Māori spirituality, emphasising the Holy Trinity and healing, and its symbols include the whetū mārama (star and crescent moon).

Political influence

Rātana also became a major political force. In the 1930s its candidates captured the Māori parliamentary seats, and in 1936 the movement formed an enduring alliance with the New Zealand Labour Party that shaped Māori politics for the rest of the century.
