Hybrid offence

A hybrid offence, in Canadian criminal law (dual procedure offence) and in similar Commonwealth systems, is an offence that the prosecution may elect to proceed with either summarily (as a summary conviction offence) or by indictment, depending on the seriousness of the circumstances.

Characteristics

The Crown's election determines the procedure, maximum penalties, limitation periods, and mode of trial available to the accused. Many offences under the Criminal Code of Canada are hybrid, including assault, theft under certain values, and impaired driving-related offences. Until the Crown elects, a hybrid offence is generally treated as indictable for certain procedural purposes.

Other jurisdictions

The analogous concept in England and Wales is an "either-way offence", triable either in the magistrates' court or the Crown Court.

References: Criminal Code, RSC 1985, c C-46.
