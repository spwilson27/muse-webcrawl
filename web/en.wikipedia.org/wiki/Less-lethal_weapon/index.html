Less-lethal weapon

A less-lethal weapon, also called a non-lethal weapon, is a weapon intended to be less likely to kill a living target than conventional weapons such as knives and firearms. It is understood that unintended or incidental casualties are risked wherever force is applied, but less-lethal weapons try to minimise the risk of casualties (e.g. serious/permanent injuries or death) as much as possible.

Less-lethal weapons are used in policing and combat situations to limit the escalation of conflict where employment of lethal force is prohibited or undesirable, where rules of engagement require minimum casualties, or where policy restricts the use of conventional force.

Types

Types include batons, pepper spray, tear gas, rubber and plastic bullets, bean bag rounds, water cannons, tasers and other conducted energy devices, and long-range acoustic devices. Rubber bullets and similar projectiles have caused serious injuries and deaths in some cases, leading to the preferred term "less-lethal" rather than "non-lethal".

Use and controversy

Use in crowd control and protests has been controversial, with human rights organisations documenting misuse and calling for restrictions on certain types, particularly kinetic impact projectiles aimed at the head or used at close range.
