Foundation (cosmetics)

Foundation is a liquid, cream, or powder makeup applied to the face and neck to create an even, uniform colour to the complexion, cover flaws and, sometimes, to change the natural skin tone. Some foundations also function as a moisturizer, sunscreen, astringent or base layer for more complex cosmetics. Foundation applied to the body is generally referred to as "body painting" or "body makeup".

History

The use of cosmetics to enhance the complexion dates to antiquity. Ancient Egyptians used ochre and other pigments on the skin, and in ancient Greece and Rome pale skin was fashionable; Greek women used white lead paint and chalk to lighten their faces, a practice that persisted, despite its toxicity, for centuries. In the Elizabethan era, a mixture of white lead and vinegar known as ceruse was used to achieve a pale complexion. Modern foundation developed in the 20th century: Max Factor introduced Pan-Cake makeup in 1937, originally developed for film, which became widely popular with the public. Estée Lauder, Revlon and other companies expanded foundation into a mass-market category after the Second World War.

Coverage and formulation

Foundations are classified by coverage: sheer, light, medium and full. Sheer foundation contains the least pigment and is used to even out skin tone slightly, while full-coverage products can conceal birthmarks, vitiligo, hyperpigmentation and scars. Formulations include oil-based, water-based and silicone-based liquids, cream foundations, mineral powders, mousse, stick and cushion compacts. Shades are matched to skin tone and undertone (warm, cool or neutral). Many modern foundations include sun protection factor (SPF) and skincare ingredients.

Application

Foundation is typically applied after moisturizer and primer, using fingers, a sponge (such as a damp beauty blender), or a brush, and is blended outward from the centre of the face. It is often set with translucent powder and followed by concealer, blush and other products.
