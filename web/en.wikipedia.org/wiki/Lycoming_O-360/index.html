Lycoming O-360

Lycoming O-360
Type: Four-cylinder piston aircraft engine
Manufacturer: Lycoming Engines
First run: 1955
Displacement: 361 cu in (5.92 L)
Power: 180 hp typical

The Lycoming O-360 is a family of four-cylinder, direct-drive, horizontally opposed, air-cooled, piston aircraft engines. The O-360 series engines are the four-cylinder counterparts in a family that also includes the six-cylinder O-540 and eight-cylinder O-720. The first O-360 was certified in 1955.

Design and development

All O-360 models share a bore of 5.125 in and a stroke of 4.375 in, giving 361 cu in of displacement. Most variants are rated at 180 hp (134 kW) at 2,700 rpm and run on 100LL avgas. The family includes carburetted (O-360), fuel-injected (IO-360), aerobatic (AEIO-360), and helicopter (HO-360, HIO-360) versions, with well over 160 certified models.

Applications

The O-360 powers the Cessna 172R/S Skyhawk SP, Piper PA-28-181 Archer, Robinson R22 helicopter (derated versions), Mooney M20, and many amateur-built aircraft including the Van's RV-6, RV-7, and RV-8.

Specifications (O-360-A1A)

  • Type: four-cylinder, air-cooled, horizontally opposed piston engine
  • Displacement: 361 cu in (5.92 L)
  • Dry weight: about 258 lb (117 kg)
  • Power output: 180 hp at 2,700 rpm
  • Time between overhaul: 2,000 hours (typical)
