Vertical transmission

Vertical transmission is the passage of a pathogen from a parent to its offspring — in medicine, most importantly from mother to child during pregnancy, childbirth or breastfeeding (mother-to-child transmission). It contrasts with horizontal transmission between individuals of the same generation.

Routes and examples

Transmission may occur across the placenta (as with rubella, cytomegalovirus, Zika virus and Toxoplasma), during delivery through contact with blood and secretions (hepatitis B, herpes simplex), or after birth through breast milk (HIV, HTLV-1). Infections acquired in utero are called congenital infections; the classic group is remembered by the acronym TORCH.

Prevention

Screening of pregnant women, vaccination (rubella, hepatitis B), antiviral treatment (which has cut HIV mother-to-child transmission from roughly a quarter of births to under 2% with full care), elective caesarean delivery in selected cases and advice on infant feeding are the main preventive tools. The term is also used in genetics and microbiology for inheritance of genes or symbionts from parent to offspring.
