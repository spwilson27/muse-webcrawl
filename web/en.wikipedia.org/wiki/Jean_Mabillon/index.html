Jean Mabillon

Image placeholder: Jean Mabillon

Jean Mabillon O.S.B. (23 November 1632 – 27 December 1707) was a French Benedictine monk and scholar of the Congregation of Saint Maur. He is considered the founder of the disciplines of palaeography and diplomatics, and is widely regarded as one of the great scholars of the 17th century.

Life

Mabillon was born at Saint-Pierremont in the Ardennes and entered the Benedictine abbey of Saint-Remi at Reims, later moving to the Abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés in Paris, a centre of Maurist scholarship. There he edited the works of Saint Bernard of Clairvaux and the Acta of the Benedictine saints, undertaking research journeys to the libraries of France, Germany, and Italy.

Work

His masterpiece, De re diplomatica (1681), written in response to the Jesuit Daniel van Papenbroeck's scepticism about Merovingian charters, established systematic criteria for authenticating medieval documents — comparing handwriting, seals, parchment, and formulas — and thereby founded diplomatics as a science. Mabillon also produced the Annales Ordinis Sancti Benedicti and, late in life, defended the Maurists' critical scholarship against critics such as the abbot de Rancé.

Legacy

Mabillon died in Paris in 1707. His methods laid the foundations of modern historical criticism of documents. The Paris Métro station Mabillon is named in his honour, as are streets and scholarly institutions in France.
