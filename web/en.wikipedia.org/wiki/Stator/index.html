Stator

Stator of a three-phase AC motor.

The stator is the stationary part of a rotary system, found in electric generators, electric motors, sirens, mud motors, and biological motors. Energy flows through a stator to or from the rotating component of the system, the rotor. In an electric motor, the stator provides a magnetic field that drives the rotating armature; in a generator, the stator converts the rotating magnetic field of the rotor into electric current.

Electric motors and generators

A stator typically consists of a frame or casing, a core of thin steel laminations insulated from one another to reduce eddy-current losses, and windings of insulated copper wire set into slots around the core. Depending on the machine, the stator may carry the main (armature) windings that produce or absorb power, or permanent magnets or field windings that set up the magnetic field. In most AC machines the stator carries the armature winding, while DC machines may place either winding on the stator.

Fluid systems

In turbines and other turbomachinery, stators are rows of fixed blades that guide and slow the fluid flow, converting kinetic energy into pressure and directing flow onto the rotating blades. Stator vanes are a key component of jet-engine compressors and of steam and gas turbines. In automotive torque converters, the stator is a one-way-clutched set of vanes that redirects fluid between the turbine and pump to multiply torque at low speeds.
