A Star Is Born is a 1954 American musical drama film directed by George Cukor, written by Moss Hart, and starring Judy Garland and James Mason. A remake of the 1937 film of the same name, it tells of alcoholic movie idol Norman Maine, whose career is in decline, and Esther Blodgett, the young singer he discovers, champions, and marries — her star rising as his collapses. It was Garland's comeback film after a four-year absence from the screen and is widely regarded as one of the greatest American musical films.

Plot

At a Hollywood benefit, the drunken Norman Maine stumbles on stage during a performance by Esther Blodgett and her band; she covers for him, and in gratitude he arranges a screen test. Renamed Vicki Lester, Esther becomes a major star. She and Norman marry, but his alcoholism and failing career drive him to despair; arrested for drunk driving and humiliated, he finally walks into the ocean and drowns so that he will not destroy her career. At a premiere soon after, Esther introduces herself to the audience: "This is Mrs. Norman Maine."

Production

The film was produced by Garland's husband Sid Luft through their Transcona Enterprises company for Warner Bros. It was Warner's first production in CinemaScope and was photographed in Technicolor. Songs by Harold Arlen and Ira Gershwin include "The Man That Got Away", written for the film, and the extended "Born in a Trunk" sequence. Production was troubled and expensive, and after poor preview responses the studio cut the film from its 182-minute premiere length to 154 minutes for general release, against Cukor's wishes.

Restoration and reception

In 1983, film historian Ronald Haver supervised a restoration to 176 minutes, reinstating cut footage and reconstructing missing scenes with production stills. The film received six Academy Award nominations, including Best Actor for Mason and Best Actress for Garland, whose loss to Grace Kelly is often cited among the Academy's most notorious upsets. In 2000 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry, and the American Film Institute ranked it among the greatest movie musicals.
