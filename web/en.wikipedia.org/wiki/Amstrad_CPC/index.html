The Amstrad CPC ("Colour Personal Computer") is a series of 8-bit home computers produced by Amstrad between 1984 and 1990. Designed to compete directly with the Commodore 64 and ZX Spectrum, the CPC was sold as a complete, all-in-one system with its own monitor and built-in cassette deck or floppy drive — a formula that made it especially successful in the United Kingdom, France, Spain and Germany.

Models

The original CPC 464 (1984) offered 64 KB of RAM and a built-in cassette recorder. The short-lived CPC 664 added a 3-inch floppy drive, and the CPC 6128 (1985) doubled memory to 128 KB and adopted CP/M Plus as a business operating system. In 1990 the range was refreshed as the "Plus" series (464 Plus and 6128 Plus) with enhanced graphics and a cartridge slot shared with the GX4000 games console.

Technical features

All CPCs use a Zilog Z80A processor and display hardware based on the Motorola 6845 CRTC, offering three graphics modes with a palette of 27 colours — unusually vivid for the time. Sound comes from the General Instrument AY-3-8912 chip, as in the ZX Spectrum 128. The machines run Locomotive BASIC from ROM, widely praised as fast and capable.

Legacy

Around three million CPCs were sold. The platform built a large library of games and serious software and retains an active retrocomputing and demoscene community, with new software still produced decades after production ended.
