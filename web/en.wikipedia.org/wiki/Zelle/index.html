Zelle

Launched: 2017

Operator: Early Warning Services

Type: Digital payments network (United States)

Zelle is a United States–based digital payments network owned by Early Warning Services, a consortium of major American banks. It allows users to send money directly between bank accounts, typically within minutes, using an email address or mobile phone number.

History

The service grew out of the clearXchange network founded by Bank of America, JPMorgan Chase and Wells Fargo in 2011, and was relaunched under the Zelle brand in 2017. Hundreds of banks and credit unions subsequently integrated Zelle into their mobile apps.

Criticism

Because payments are fast and generally irreversible, Zelle has been criticised over fraud and scam losses, prompting debate about reimbursement obligations for banks and increased consumer-protection scrutiny.
