Desmopressin, sold under the trade name DDAVP among others, is a medication used to treat diabetes insipidus, bedwetting, hemophilia A, von Willebrand disease, and high blood urea levels. It is a synthetic analogue of vasopressin, the hormone that reduces urine production, in which the amino group of the N-terminal cysteine is removed and the L-arginine at position 8 is replaced by D-arginine — changes that prolong its action and greatly reduce its blood-pressure-raising (pressor) effects.

Medical uses

Desmopressin is the treatment of choice for central (neurogenic) diabetes insipidus, replacing the missing antidiuretic hormone and concentrating the urine via V2 receptors in the kidney collecting ducts. It is used for primary nocturnal enuresis (bedwetting) in children, and for nocturia in adults. In haematology, desmopressin raises plasma levels of von Willebrand factor and factor VIII by releasing them from endothelial stores, making it useful in mild haemophilia A and type 1 von Willebrand disease, and as a test of renal concentrating ability.

Side effects and precautions

Common side effects include headache, flushing, nausea and abdominal pain. The principal risk is water retention leading to dilutional hyponatraemia — potentially causing seizures — so fluid intake must be restricted during treatment, especially in children treated for bedwetting. Desmopressin is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. Because it can mask dehydration and is misused in sport, it is prohibited by WADA as a masking agent.
