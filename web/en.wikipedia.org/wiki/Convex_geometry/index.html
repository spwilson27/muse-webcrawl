Convex geometry

FieldMathematics (geometry)
Key objectsConvex sets, convex bodies, polytopes
Key resultsHelly's theorem, Brunn–Minkowski theorem, Minkowski's theorems

Convex geometry is the branch of geometry concerned with the study of convex sets, principally in Euclidean space. A set is convex if, for any two points in the set, the line segment joining them lies entirely within the set. Convex geometry sits at the intersection of geometry, analysis, combinatorics, and optimisation, and has deep applications in functional analysis, number theory, and computer science.

Historical development

Although notions of convexity appear in the work of Archimedes, the theory took modern shape in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries through the work of Hermann Brunn, Hermann Minkowski, and others. Minkowski's geometry of numbers applied convex bodies to Diophantine approximation, and his mixed-volume theory laid the groundwork for the Brunn–Minkowski theorem, which relates the volumes of convex bodies to the volume of their Minkowski sum.

Main topics

Central topics include convex polytopes, support and separation theorems (such as the hyperplane separation theorem), Helly-type combinatorial results, the theory of valuations, integral geometry, and the study of extremal problems such as the isoperimetric inequality and Mahler's conjecture. The Banach–Mazur distance and the geometry of high-dimensional convex bodies are central to asymptotic geometric analysis, with applications to probability and theoretical computer science.

Applications

Convexity is foundational for convex optimisation, where local minima of convex functions are global, enabling efficient algorithms. Applications span economics (convex preferences and production sets), operations research (linear programming), crystallography, robotics (collision detection), and data science.
