Typhoon June (1975)

Typhoon June
Formed: November 1975
Dissipated: November 1975
Highest winds: ~295 km/h (185 mph) sustained
Lowest pressure: 875 hPa (mbar)
Areas affected: Pacific basin, at sea

Typhoon June, known in the Philippines as Super Typhoon June, was one of the most intense tropical cyclones on record at the time of its occurrence in November 1975. Developing over the open Pacific Ocean east of the Philippines during the 1975 Pacific typhoon season, June underwent rapid intensification and reached a minimum central pressure of about 875 hectopascals, with one-minute sustained winds estimated near 295 kilometres per hour.

Meteorological history

June originated from a tropical disturbance in the western North Pacific in mid-November 1975. Aided by warm sea surface temperatures and low wind shear, the system organized quickly, becoming a typhoon and then a super typhoon. At peak intensity its pressure of 875 hPa made it the most intense tropical cyclone yet measured by reconnaissance at that time, surpassing earlier records; the mark was later eclipsed by Typhoon Tip in 1979, which reached 870 hPa.

Remarkably, June shared the basin with two other simultaneous typhoons, forming a rare spectacle of three active tropical cyclones in close proximity — an event that drew considerable meteorological attention. June remained largely over open water, sparing heavily populated land areas from a direct catastrophic landfall, and gradually weakened as it recurved and moved into cooler waters and stronger shear, eventually dissipating.

Impact and records

Because June's core stayed mostly at sea, damage was limited compared with what a storm of its intensity might have caused over land. Its principal legacy is meteorological: for several years it held the record for the lowest central pressure reliably measured in a tropical cyclone, and it remains a benchmark case in studies of rapid intensification and of multi-storm interactions in the western North Pacific.
