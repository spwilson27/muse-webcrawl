Iran hostage crisis

Date4 November 1979 – 20 January 1981
Duration444 days
LocationTehran, Iran
Hostages52 Americans held longest

The Iran hostage crisis was a diplomatic standoff between Iran and the United States in which 52 American diplomats and citizens were held hostage after Iranian students seized the US Embassy in Tehran on 4 November 1979. The crisis lasted 444 days and ended on 20 January 1981, minutes after Ronald Reagan was inaugurated president.

Background

Relations had broken down after the Iranian Revolution overthrew Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, who had been restored by the US- and UK-backed 1953 coup. Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini returned from exile in February 1979. Anger intensified when the United States admitted the ailing Shah for cancer treatment in October 1979. The students, calling themselves Muslim Student Followers of the Imam's Line, demanded his extradition.

The crisis

Sixty-six Americans were initially taken; women and African Americans and one ill hostage were released in November and July, leaving 52. The Carter administration froze Iranian assets, broke diplomatic relations, and imposed sanctions. A rescue attempt, Operation Eagle Claw, on 24–25 April 1980 failed in the desert near Tabas, killing eight US servicemen, and led to Secretary of State Cyrus Vance's resignation.

Resolution

Negotiations mediated by Algeria produced the Algiers Accords of 19 January 1981, unfreezing assets and establishing the Iran–United States Claims Tribunal at The Hague. The hostages were released the next day.

Legacy

The crisis contributed to President Jimmy Carter's defeat in the 1980 election, deepened decades of US–Iran hostility, and shaped American politics and media — notably the launch of ABC's Nightline as a nightly crisis update. The former embassy in Tehran is now a museum.
