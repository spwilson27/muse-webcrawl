Hatikvah (Hebrew: הַתִּקְוָה, "The Hope") is the national anthem of Israel. Its lyrics are adapted from a poem by Naftali Herz Imber, a Jewish poet from Galicia, written in 1877 and first published in 1886. The anthem's theme revolves around the hope of the Jewish people to return to the Land of Israel and be a free nation in their own land.

History

Imber wrote the poem, originally titled Tikvatenu ("Our Hope"), while travelling in Romania. In 1888, in the farming community of Rishon LeZion in Ottoman Palestine, Samuel Cohen set the words to a melody derived from a Moldavian folk tune (also related to the theme of Smetana's Má vlast movement "Vltava"). The song became the anthem of the Zionist movement and was sung at Zionist congresses.

When the State of Israel was established in 1948, Hatikvah was proclaimed the national anthem unofficially; it was formally confirmed in law in 2004. Only the first stanza and refrain of Imber's nine-stanza poem are sung.

As long as in the heart, within,
A Jewish soul still yearns,
And towards the ends of the east,
An eye still looks toward Zion;
Our hope is not yet lost,
The hope of two thousand years,
To be a free nation in our land,
The land of Zion and Jerusalem.
