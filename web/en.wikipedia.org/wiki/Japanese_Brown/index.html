The Japanese Brown (Japanese: 褐毛和種, Akage Washu) is a breed of beef cattle from Japan and one of the four breeds collectively known as Wagyu. It is the second most numerous of the Wagyu breeds after the Japanese Black, and is raised principally in Kumamoto and Kōchi prefectures.

History

Like other Wagyu breeds, the Japanese Brown was developed from native Japanese cattle crossed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with imported European stock — in this case chiefly Simmental, Korean Hanwoo, Brown Swiss and Devon blood contributed to the breed. Two main strains exist: the Kumamoto strain, influenced by Simmental and Hanwoo, and the Kōchi strain, based on Korean cattle. The breed was formally recognised in 1944.

Characteristics

Japanese Brown cattle are reddish-brown in colour, hardy and fast-growing, and are noted for efficient feed conversion and good temperament. Their beef is marbled, though generally somewhat less intensely than that of the Japanese Black, and is sometimes marketed as "red Wagyu" (akaushi) abroad — a herd established in the United States in the 1990s supports an American Akaushi industry.
