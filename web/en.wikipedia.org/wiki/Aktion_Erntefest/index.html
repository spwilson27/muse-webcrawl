Aktion Erntefest

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Date3–4 November 1943
LocationMajdanek, Poniatowa and Trawniki camps, Lublin District, General Government (occupied Poland)
VictimsApproximately 42,000–43,000 Jewish prisoners
PerpetratorsSS and Police units under Friedrich-Wilhelm Krüger and Jakob Sporrenberg

Aktion Erntefest (German for "Operation Harvest Festival") was the mass murder of approximately 42,000 to 43,000 Jewish prisoners in the Lublin district of the General Government on 3 and 4 November 1943. Carried out by the SS at the Majdanek, Poniatowa and Trawniki camps, it was the largest single massacre of the Holocaust committed by German forces in a single operation: about 18,400 people were shot at Majdanek on 3 November alone.

Background

The operation was ordered by Heinrich Himmler in the aftermath of Jewish armed resistance in 1943 — the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising, the revolts at the Treblinka and Sobibor extermination camps, and the uprising in the Białystok Ghetto. Fearing further insurrections, Himmler decided to liquidate the remaining Jewish labor camps in the Lublin district, whose prisoners still worked for SS enterprises such as Ostindustrie. SS and Police Leader Jakob Sporrenberg organized the shootings after his predecessor Odilo Globocnik's departure.

The massacre

In the days before the operation, prisoners at Majdanek were forced to dig zigzag trenches, ostensibly air-raid defenses. On the morning of 3 November 1943 the camp's roughly 18,400 Jewish prisoners were marched to the trenches and shot in groups by SS and police units reinforced from outside the district. Loud dance music was broadcast over camp loudspeakers to drown the sound of gunfire. The same day, about 6,000 Jews were murdered at Trawniki and some 15,000 at Poniatowa, where prisoners who resisted by barricading themselves in a barracks were burned alive. Killings continued into 4 November at smaller subcamps.

Aftermath

After Aktion Erntefest, the Lublin district was declared judenfrei ("free of Jews") for reporting purposes, and the SS economic program in the district effectively collapsed for lack of labor. The operation marked the end of the mass-shooting phase of Operation Reinhard's broader program. Sporrenberg was tried and executed in Poland after the war. The trenches at Majdanek remain part of the camp memorial site.
