Puducherry

Puducherry, formerly known as Pondicherry, is a city and capital of the Union Territory of Puducherry in southeastern India, on the Coromandel Coast of the Bay of Bengal. It is known for its French colonial heritage, grid-planned French Quarter with mustard-coloured colonial villas, and the Sri Aurobindo Ashram.

History

The French East India Company established a trading post here in 1674, and the town changed hands repeatedly between French, Dutch and British forces before France ceded its Indian possessions to India de facto in 1954 and de jure in 1962. The name was officially changed from Pondicherry to Puducherry in 2006, reflecting the Tamil words for "new village".

Geography and culture

The territory consists of four non-contiguous districts — Puducherry, Karaikal, Mahé and Yanam — enclaved within neighbouring states. Tamil is the main language, with French cultural influence persisting in cuisine, architecture and institutions such as the Lycée français.
