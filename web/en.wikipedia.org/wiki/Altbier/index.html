Altbier (German for "old beer") is a style of beer brewed in the historical region of Westphalia and around the city of Düsseldorf on the Rhine. The name refers to the "old" method of brewing with top-fermenting yeast, which predates the bottom-fermenting lagers that became dominant in the 19th century.

Characteristics

Altbier is fermented warm with top-fermenting yeast like an ale, but is then cold-conditioned (lagered) for several weeks at near-freezing temperatures. The result is a copper-coloured, clean, crisp beer that combines ale fruitiness with lager smoothness. It is typically well-hopped with noble German hops such as Spalt, giving a firm bitterness balancing the malty body. Alcohol content is usually between 4.3% and 5.5% by volume.

History and culture

Düsseldorf's brewpubs (Hausbrauereien) such as Uerige, Füchschen, Schumacher and Schlüssel have brewed Altbier for centuries in the old town (Altstadt). It is traditionally served in a small, straight 0.25-litre glass. There is a famous friendly rivalry between Düsseldorf's Altbier and the Kölsch of neighbouring Cologne. A stronger, seasonal variant is known as Sticke Alt ("secret old"), originally brewed only occasionally and announced by word of mouth.
