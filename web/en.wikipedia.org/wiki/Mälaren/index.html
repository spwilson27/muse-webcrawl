Mälaren

Mälaren is the third-largest freshwater lake in Sweden, after Vänern and Vättern, with a surface area of about 1,070 km². It stretches roughly 120 km westward from Stockholm, where it meets the Baltic Sea through locks at the capital's waterfront.

Geography and history

The lake has many bays, inlets, and around 8,000 islands. Important towns on its shores include Västerås, Eskilstuna, Södertälje, and Uppsala (via the Fyris river). In the Viking Age and early medieval period, Mälaren was an arm of the Baltic Sea; post-glacial isostatic rebound gradually cut it off, making it a freshwater lake by around the 12th–13th centuries. The Viking-age trading town of Birka and the later settlement of Sigtuna stood on its shores, and the island of Björkö with Birka and Hovgården is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Use

Mälaren supplies drinking water to more than two million people in the Stockholm region and supports shipping, fishing, and recreation. Its outlet at Stockholm is regulated, and the Slussen locks manage the level difference between lake and sea.
