International Board on Books for Young People

The International Board on Books for Young People (IBBY) is an international non-profit organization committed to bringing books and children together. It was founded in Zurich, Switzerland, in 1953 by Jella Lepman, who believed that children's books could build international understanding after the Second World War.

Structure

IBBY is made up of more than 80 national sections, which include children's authors and illustrators, publishers, librarians, teachers, and literacy organizations. Its secretariat is in Basel, Switzerland, and it holds a biennial world congress.

Awards and activities

IBBY presents the Hans Christian Andersen Awards, often called the "Nobel Prize of children's literature", every two years to an author and an illustrator for their complete body of work. It also maintains the IBBY Honour List, sponsors International Children's Book Day (2 April, Andersen's birthday), and supports children in crisis through the IBBY Children in Crisis Fund.

History

Lepman had earlier founded the International Youth Library in Munich in 1949. IBBY's founding members included children's authors Astrid Lindgren and Erich Kästner. The organization has official relations with UNESCO and UNICEF.
