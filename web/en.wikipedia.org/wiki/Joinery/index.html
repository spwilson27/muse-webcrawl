Joinery

Common woodworking joints
CraftWoodworking
ProductsFurniture, doors, windows, stairs

Joinery is a part of woodworking that involves joining pieces of wood, engineered lumber, or synthetic substitutes to produce more complex items. Some wood joints employ mechanical fasteners, bindings, or adhesives, while others use only wood elements.

Types of joints

Common joinery joints include the mortise and tenon, dovetail, lap joint, bridle joint, box joint, dowel joint, and biscuit joint. Each distributes load and resists particular forces: dovetails, for example, resist being pulled apart, making them ideal for drawer construction.

Joinery and carpentry

Traditionally, joinery refers to the finer, workshop-based work of making doors, windows, stairs, and furniture, while carpentry refers to the structural work of building on site, though the terms overlap and vary between countries. The craft of joinery predates written history; complex jointed furniture survives from ancient Egypt.
