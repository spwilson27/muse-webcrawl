Plombières Agreement

TypeSecret alliance agreement
Signed21 July 1858 (oral agreement)
LocationPlombières-les-Bains, France
PartiesFrance (Napoleon III) and the Kingdom of Sardinia (Count Cavour)

The Plombières Agreement (Italian: Accordi di Plombières) was a secret verbal agreement reached on 21 July 1858 between the French emperor Napoleon III and Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour, prime minister of the Kingdom of Sardinia, at the spa town of Plombières-les-Bains in the Vosges. It laid the groundwork for the Franco-Sardinian alliance against Austria and for the Second Italian War of Independence.

Terms

Napoleon III pledged French military support if Austria attacked Sardinia, with the aim of expelling Austria from Italy. In the resulting settlement, Piedmont-Sardinia would annex Lombardy and Venetia along with Parma and Modena, creating a Kingdom of Upper Italy; France would receive the Duchy of Savoy and the County of Nice as compensation. A marriage was arranged between Prince Napoléon-Jérôme Bonaparte and Princess Maria Clotilde of Savoy to cement the alliance.

Consequences

The agreement led to the Treaty of Turin (January 1859) formalising the alliance and, after Austria's ultimatum, to the war of 1859. Napoleon III halted the campaign at the Armistice of Villafranca, disappointing Cavour, since Venetia remained Austrian; nevertheless Lombardy passed to Piedmont, and in 1860 Savoy and Nice were ceded to France following plebiscites, while the unification of Italy proceeded.
