Talcott Parsons

Born: 13 December 1902, Colorado Springs, USA

Died: 8 May 1979, Munich, West Germany

Talcott Parsons was an American sociologist, the dominant figure in post-war American sociology, who developed structural functionalism and a general theory of action synthesising Durkheim, Weber and Pareto.

Theory

The Structure of Social Action (1937) founded his "voluntaristic theory of action". The Social System (1951) presented society as systems meeting four functional imperatives — Adaptation, Goal attainment, Integration, Latency (AGIL) — and analysed socialisation, professions, and the family. With Edward Shils and others he proposed pattern variables describing choices in social action.

Influence and criticism

Based at Harvard, Parsons shaped sociology, political science and the study of medicine (the "sick role"). From the 1960s critics — conflict theorists, C. Wright Mills, and later interpretivists — attacked his abstraction and alleged conservatism, though a neo-functional revival followed.
