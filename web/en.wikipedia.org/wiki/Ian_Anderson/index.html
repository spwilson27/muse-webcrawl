Ian Scott Anderson MBE (born 10 August 1947) is a Scottish musician best known as the lead vocalist, flautist, acoustic guitarist, and founder of the British rock band Jethro Tull. He is the band's principal songwriter and its only constant member since its formation in 1967.

Anderson's signature stage pose: playing the flute while standing on one leg.

Career

Anderson was born in Dunfermline and raised in Edinburgh and Blackpool. In 1967 he co-founded Jethro Tull in Luton, teaching himself the flute after trading his electric guitar for one, and developing an overblown, percussive playing style influenced by jazz musician Rahsaan Roland Kirk. With albums such as Aqualung (1971), Thick as a Brick (1972), and Songs from the Wood (1977), Jethro Tull became one of the best-selling progressive rock acts, selling more than 60 million albums worldwide. In 1989 the band famously won the first Grammy Award for Best Hard Rock/Metal Performance over Metallica.

Anderson has released solo albums including Divinities: Twelve Dances with God (1995), Thick as a Brick 2 (2012), and Homo Erraticus (2014). Outside music he has business interests including salmon farming, and he was appointed MBE in 2006. In 2020 he revealed that he suffers from chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Jethro Tull returned with new studio albums The Zealot Gene (2022) and RökFlöte (2023).

Style

Anderson is instantly recognisable for playing the flute while standing on one leg, for his codpiece-era stage costumes, and for literate, wry lyrics mixing folk imagery, religious satire, and environmental themes.
