Ludwig II (25 August 1845 – 13 June 1886) was King of Bavaria from 1864 until his death. Remembered as the "Fairy Tale King" (Märchenkönig), he is famed for the extravagant palaces he built — Neuschwanstein, Linderhof, and Herrenchiemsee — and for his patronage of the composer Richard Wagner, as well as for his mysterious death.

Reign

Ludwig ascended the throne at eighteen on the sudden death of his father, Maximilian II. In 1864 he summoned the struggling Wagner to Munich, paying his debts and funding the premieres of Tristan und Isolde and other works, until scandal forced Wagner's departure. Bavaria fought on Austria's side in the Austro-Prussian War of 1866 and joined the Prussian-led German Empire in 1871, with Ludwig's "Kaiserbrief" nominally proposing the imperial crown for Wilhelm I. Ludwig thereafter withdrew from government, devoting himself to building projects financed from his own civil list.

Palaces

Neuschwanstein (begun 1869) is a romanticised medieval castle decorated with scenes from Wagner's operas. Linderhof (completed 1878), the only palace finished in his lifetime, is a rococo jewel with a Moorish kiosk and an artificial Venus grotto. Herrenchiemsee (begun 1878) was a vast, unfinished homage to Versailles.

Deposition and death

In June 1886, a government commission declared Ludwig insane — on the basis of witness reports gathered by a psychiatric team led by Bernhard von Gudden, without a personal examination — and deposed him in favour of his uncle Luitpold as regent. Days later, on 13 June 1886, Ludwig and von Gudden were found dead in the shallow water of Lake Starnberg near Berg Castle. The circumstances remain disputed; the official verdict was that Ludwig drowned, possibly after attacking his doctor.
