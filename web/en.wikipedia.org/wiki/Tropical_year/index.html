A tropical year, also known as a solar year, is the time the Sun takes to return to the same position in the cycle of seasons, as seen from Earth—for example, the time from one March equinox to the next. Because the seasons govern agriculture and civil calendars, the tropical year is the quantity that calendars aim to track.

Length

The mean tropical year is approximately 365.24219 ephemeris days. It differs slightly depending on the seasonal point chosen, because Earth's orbital speed varies and the axis precesses. It is about 20 minutes shorter than the sidereal year (365.25636 days), the time Earth takes to complete one orbit relative to the fixed stars; the difference arises from the precession of the equinoxes.

Calendar relevance

The Gregorian calendar uses a mean year of 365.2425 days, close enough to keep the calendar in step with the seasons for thousands of years. The Julian calendar's 365.25-day year drifted by about one day every 128 years, which motivated the Gregorian reform of 1582.
