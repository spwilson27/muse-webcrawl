Foreign relations of Israel

The foreign relations of Israel encompass the diplomatic, commercial, and cultural ties between the State of Israel and other countries. Israel was admitted to the United Nations in 1949 and has since established diplomatic relations with the large majority of UN member states, although a number of countries — mostly in the Arab and Muslim world — have historically withheld recognition, a position that has softened in recent decades.

Overview

Israel's foreign policy is shaped by its security environment, its alliance with the United States, trade and technology partnerships, and the ongoing Israeli–Palestinian conflict. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs, headquartered in Jerusalem, manages a network of embassies and consulates worldwide. Israel is a member of numerous international organisations and has particularly close ties with the United States, Germany, India, and several European states.

Relations with Arab states

For decades most Arab states refused to recognise Israel. Egypt became the first Arab country to sign a peace treaty with Israel in 1979, following the Camp David Accords, and Jordan followed in 1994. In 2020 the Abraham Accords normalised relations between Israel and the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain, with Morocco and Sudan also agreeing to normalisation steps. Israel has no diplomatic relations with a number of states, including Iran, which recognised Israel under the Shah but severed ties after the 1979 Islamic Revolution and remains a principal regional adversary.

United States

The United States was the first country to recognise Israel (de facto in 1948) and is its closest ally and largest provider of military assistance. Annual US military aid of around $3.8 billion is codified in a ten-year memorandum of understanding. The relationship also includes extensive intelligence cooperation, joint military exercises, and trade.

Europe and Asia

Israel has an association agreement with the European Union and participates in programmes such as Horizon Europe. Relations with Germany, founded in 1965, carry the historical weight of the Holocaust and include substantial trade and military cooperation. Since the 1990s Israel has deepened ties with India and China, and in 2022 it established full diplomatic relations with a growing list of states as part of a broader diplomatic expansion.

International organisations

Israel is a member of the United Nations, the OECD (joined 2010), the International Monetary Fund, the World Bank, and the World Trade Organization, among others. It has frequently been at odds with UN bodies over resolutions concerning its policies in the occupied territories.
