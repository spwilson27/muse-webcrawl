The National Reconnaissance Office (NRO) is a member of the United States Intelligence Community and an agency of the Department of Defense responsible for designing, building, launching, and operating the reconnaissance satellites of the United States federal government. It provides satellite imagery and signals intelligence to the CIA, NSA, NGA, and the military services.

History

The NRO was created in 1961 to consolidate the Air Force and CIA satellite reconnaissance programmes — including the CORONA film-return satellites — under a single covert organisation. Its very existence was classified until 1992, when it was declassified after the end of the Cold War. Successive generations of satellites, from the KH-series electro-optical systems to radar imaging and large signals-intelligence spacecraft, have been launched from Vandenberg and Cape Canaveral, often on classified missions designated NROL.

Mission

The agency's motto is Supra Et Ultra. The NRO works closely with the United States Space Force, which operates many of its constellations in orbit, and with the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, which processes much of its imagery. Its budget, long secret, has been estimated in the billions of dollars annually; the agency is headquartered in Chantilly, Virginia, near Washington Dulles International Airport.
