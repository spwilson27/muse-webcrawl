Linear regulator

In electronics, a linear regulator is a voltage regulator used to maintain a steady output voltage. It operates by using a pass device — such as a transistor — in its linear region, continuously adjusting its resistance to keep the output constant despite changes in input voltage or load current.

Unlike switching regulators, linear regulators dissipate the excess power as heat: the power lost is approximately the product of the voltage drop across the regulator and the load current. They are therefore simplest and quietest electrically, producing very low output noise and ripple, but can be inefficient when the input–output voltage difference is large.

Fixed and adjustable regulators

Classic three-terminal devices such as the 78xx series provide fixed positive outputs, while parts like the LM317 are adjustable via an external resistor divider. Low-dropout regulators (LDOs) are linear regulators designed to operate with a very small input–output differential, improving efficiency in battery-powered equipment.

Protection

Most integrated linear regulators include internal current limiting and thermal shutdown to protect the device and the load under fault conditions.
