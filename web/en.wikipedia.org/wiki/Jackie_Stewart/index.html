Sir John Young Stewart (born 11 June 1939), known as Jackie Stewart and nicknamed the "Flying Scot", is a British former Formula One racing driver from Scotland. He competed between 1965 and 1973, winning three World Drivers' Championships and 27 Grands Prix, then a record.

Racing career

Stewart began in sports cars and Formula Three before joining BRM in Formula One in 1965, winning the Italian Grand Prix in his first season. With Ken Tyrrell's team he won his championships in Matra and Tyrrell machinery in 1969, 1971 and 1973. He retired after the death of his team-mate François Cevert at Watkins Glen in 1973, having already decided to leave the sport at the season's end.

Safety campaigning and later life

Shaken by the deaths of fellow drivers and his own serious crash at Spa in 1966, Stewart became the most prominent campaigner for improved safety in motor racing, pressing for full-face helmets, seat belts, run-off areas and better medical facilities — reforms that were resisted at the time but later adopted wholesale. He later worked as a television commentator, co-founded Stewart Grand Prix (which became Jaguar Racing and then Red Bull Racing), and founded the charity Race Against Dementia after his wife Helen's diagnosis.
