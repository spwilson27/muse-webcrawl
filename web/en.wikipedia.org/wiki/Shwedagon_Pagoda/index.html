The Shwedagon Pagoda (Burmese: ရွှေတိဂုံဘုရား), officially named Shwedagon Zedi Daw and also known as the Great Dagon Pagoda and the Golden Pagoda, is a gilded stupa in Yangon, Myanmar. The 99-metre-tall (325 ft) pagoda is situated on Singuttara Hill and dominates the Yangon skyline. It is the most sacred Buddhist pagoda in Myanmar, believed to contain relics of the four previous Buddhas of the present kalpa.

History

According to tradition, the pagoda was built more than 2,600 years ago by the Mon people, when two merchant brothers, Tapussa and Bhallika, received eight strands of the Buddha's hair and enshrined them on Singuttara Hill. Historians and archaeologists maintain that the stupa was originally built between the 6th and 10th centuries CE and repeatedly enlarged; Queen Shin Sawbu raised it to about 40 metres in the 15th century, and King Hsinbyushin rebuilt it to its present height after a devastating earthquake in 1768. The spire is encrusted with thousands of diamonds, topped by a 76-carat diamond.

Significance

Shwedagon has been a centre of Burmese national identity as well as devotion: Aung San addressed a mass meeting there in 1946, and it was a focal point of the 1988 pro-democracy uprising and the 2007 Saffron Revolution.
