God Save the King

"God Save the King" (alternatively "God Save the Queen" when the monarch is female) is the national anthem of the United Kingdom and the royal anthem of several Commonwealth realms, their territories, and the British Crown Dependencies. It is also one of the two national anthems of New Zealand. The words and tune are anonymous, and may date to the seventeenth century.

In the United Kingdom, the anthem has no statutory status as the national anthem; it is used by tradition. The first verse is most commonly sung, though additional verses exist. When the monarch is a queen, "King" is replaced by "Queen" and masculine pronouns are changed accordingly. Since the accession of Charles III in September 2022, the male form has been in use.

History

The melody's origins are unclear. An early version of the present tune was published in Thesaurus Musicus in 1744. The anthem gained prominence in September 1745 during the Jacobite rising, when it was performed in London theatres in support of George II. By the early nineteenth century it had become generally regarded as the national anthem. The tune has been borrowed by other countries: it is used for the national anthem of Liechtenstein ("Oben am jungen Rhein"), the royal anthem of Norway, and formerly for the German imperial anthem "Heil dir im Siegerkranz" and the Swiss anthem "Rufst du, mein Vaterland". In the United States, the melody is used for the patriotic song "My Country, 'Tis of Thee".

Lyrics

The standard first verse begins: "God save our gracious King, / Long live our noble King, / God save the King! / Send him victorious, / Happy and glorious, / Long to reign over us, / God save the King!" Additional verses include the well-known prayer for the monarch's defence of the laws and, historically, verses expressing anti-Jacobite sentiments that are now rarely sung.

Use

The anthem is played at state occasions, at the close of BBC broadcasts historically, at sporting events involving teams from the United Kingdom or England, and on Remembrance Sunday. In Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales, other songs are often used at sporting events, but "God Save the King" remains the anthem of the United Kingdom as a whole.
