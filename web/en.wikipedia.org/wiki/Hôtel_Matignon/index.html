Hôtel Matignon

The Hôtel Matignon is a hôtel particulier in the 7th arrondissement of Paris, France, located at 57 Rue de Varenne. It serves as the official residence and workplace of the Prime Minister of France; by metonymy, "Matignon" is commonly used to refer to the prime minister's office and staff, much as "the Élysée" refers to the presidency.

History

The mansion was built between 1722 and 1725 to designs by the architect Jean Courtonne for Christian Louis de Montmorency-Luxembourg, prince de Tingry. It subsequently passed through several owners, including the dancer Anne Éléonore Francal and, in the nineteenth century, Talleyrand, who gave celebrated dinners there. The house later hosted the Austrian embassy and was the residence of the composer's widow before being acquired by the French state.

In 1935 the building became the official residence of the President of the Council of Ministers (the office now styled Prime Minister), and it has housed the head of government ever since. The Matignon Agreements of 1936, concluded under Léon Blum's Popular Front government, and the 1988 Matignon Accords on New Caledonia, take their names from the building. The hôtel is surrounded by a three-hectare park, the largest private garden in Paris, in which each departing prime minister traditionally plants a tree.
