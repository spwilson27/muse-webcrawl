Theater in der Josefstadt

The Theater in der Josefstadt is a theatre in Vienna, Austria, located in the city's 8th district, Josefstadt. Founded in 1788, it is the oldest theatre in Vienna still in operation and one of the most important German-language stages.

History

The theatre was founded in 1788 by Karl Mayer as a suburban stage; the present building, designed in its rebuilt form by Joseph Kornhäusel, opened in 1822 with an overture, The Consecration of the House, composed for the occasion by Ludwig van Beethoven, who lived nearby. In the 19th century the Josefstadt was a centre of Viennese popular theatre: Ferdinand Raimund acted there, and Johann Nestroy's plays were performed. Under the celebrated actor-director Josef Jarno (from 1899) and then under Max Reinhardt (director 1924–1937), the theatre gained international renown for productions of classics and contemporary drama, with stars of the German-speaking stage. Reinhardt's era is remembered as a golden age; he left after the Anschluss period approached and emigrated.

Present day

The theatre today operates as Vienna's leading boulevard and repertoire stage alongside the Burgtheater, presenting classics, modern drama and comedies with a permanent ensemble. It also operates the Kammerspiele as a smaller second stage. The building, seating around 700, retains its historic Biedermeier character.
