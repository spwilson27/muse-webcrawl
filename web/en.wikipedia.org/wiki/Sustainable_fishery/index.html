Sustainable fishery

A sustainable fishery is a fishery managed so that fish can be harvested indefinitely without depleting the stock or damaging the wider ecosystem: the catch rate does not exceed the rate at which the population reproduces and grows.

Management

Fisheries science assesses stocks and sets limits such as total allowable catches and quotas, often aiming at maximum sustainable yield — the largest catch that can be taken over the long term. Other tools include minimum fish sizes, closed seasons and areas, gear restrictions to reduce bycatch, marine protected areas, and individual transferable quotas ("catch shares"), which give fishers a stake in the long-term health of the stock.

Challenges and certification

Overfishing, illegal and unreported fishing, habitat damage and climate change threaten many fisheries; a substantial share of assessed global stocks are fished beyond biologically sustainable levels. Certification schemes such as the Marine Stewardship Council (MSC) eco-label aim to let consumers identify seafood from fisheries meeting sustainability standards, though their effectiveness is debated.
