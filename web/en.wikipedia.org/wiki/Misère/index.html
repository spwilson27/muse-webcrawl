Misère

Misère (French for "destitution") is a bid in various trick-taking card games in which the bidder undertakes to lose every trick, or to win no more than a stated number. A related concept, misère play in combinatorial game theory, reverses the usual winning condition so that the player who would normally win loses instead.

In card games

In solo whist and Boston, a misère contract commits the soloist to take no tricks at all, usually ranking between other bids; an "open misère", played with the hand exposed, scores more. Similar lose-every-trick contracts appear in many European games.

In combinatorial game theory

Misère versions of impartial games such as Nim can behave very differently from normal play: for Nim itself the misère strategy coincides with normal play except near the end, but for many games, analysed through misère quotients, the structure is far more complicated.
