Fortum Oyj is a Finnish state-owned energy company located in Espoo, Finland. It mainly focuses on the Nordic region. Fortum operates power plants, including co-generation plants, and generates and sells electricity and heat. The company also sells waste services such as recycling, reutilisation, final disposal solutions and soil remediation and environmental constructions services.

History

Fortum was founded in 1998 through the merger of Imatran Voima (IVO), the state-owned power company founded in 1932, and Neste Oy, the national oil company. The new company combined electricity generation and transmission assets with oil refining and marketing operations. In 2005, Fortum's oil business was spun off into a separate listed company, Neste Oil (later renamed Neste).

Through the 2000s and 2010s Fortum expanded across the Baltic region, Poland, and Russia, and acquired district heating networks in several European countries. In 2017 the company agreed to acquire a major stake in the German utility Uniper, completing a public takeover offer in 2020 and becoming Uniper's majority owner. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the German government nationalised Uniper, and Fortum recorded major losses on its Russian and Uniper investments before exiting Russia.

Operations

Fortum's generation portfolio includes hydroelectric power, nuclear power, combined heat and power (CHP) plants, wind and solar power. The company is a co-owner of the Loviisa Nuclear Power Plant in Finland and holds stakes in Swedish nuclear plants. Fortum is one of the largest emitters of carbon-free electricity in Europe by volume of hydro and nuclear generation.
