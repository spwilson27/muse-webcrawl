British Association for Shooting and Conservation

The British Association for Shooting and Conservation (BASC) is a British organisation that represents the interests of recreational shooters, gamekeepers and wildfowlers. Founded in 1908 as the Wildfowlers' Association of Great Britain and Ireland (WAGBI), it is the largest shooting organisation in the United Kingdom, with a membership well in excess of 150,000.

Wildfowling on coastal marshland was the founding pursuit of the association.

History

The association was established by Stanley Duncan, a journalist and keen wildfowler, who organised a meeting of wildfowlers concerned about threats to their sport and to the coastal habitats on which it depended. It adopted the name BASC in 1981 to reflect a membership that by then covered game shooting, deer stalking, target shooting and airgunning as well as wildfowling.

Activities

BASC provides members with third-party liability insurance, firearms licensing advice, and training courses in subjects such as deer stalking and shotgun proficiency. Its conservation work includes habitat management advice and participation in schemes such as the Green Shoots programme, which records the biodiversity value of land managed for shooting. The association also lobbies government on firearms legislation and rural affairs, and publishes the magazine Shooting and Conservation.
