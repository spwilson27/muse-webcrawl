The Battle of Vimy Ridge was fought from 9 to 12 April 1917 in the Pas-de-Calais region of northern France, as part of the Battle of Arras in the First World War. The Canadian Corps, fighting together for the first time with all four of its divisions, captured the German-held Vimy Ridge — a commanding escarpment that French and British attacks had failed to take since 1915. The victory is a defining event in Canadian national memory, though it cost over 10,000 Canadian casualties.

Background

Vimy Ridge dominated the Douai plain and the coalfields around Lens. Canadian preparations under Lieutenant-General Julian Byng and his subordinate Arthur Currie were meticulous: extensive tunnelling, rehearsal behind the lines with models and maps issued down to platoon level, massive counter-battery artillery work, and a carefully timed creeping barrage.

The battle

At 5:30 a.m. on Easter Monday, 9 April 1917, in snow and sleet, the four Canadian divisions attacked behind one of the largest artillery bombardments of the war. Most of the ridge was in Canadian hands by nightfall; the stubborn strongpoint known as "the Pimple" was taken on 12 April.

Aftermath

Four Canadians received the Victoria Cross. The Canadian National Vimy Memorial, designed by Walter Allward and unveiled in 1936, stands on the ridge's highest point on land granted to Canada by France; the battle is widely seen as a moment of Canadian nationhood.
