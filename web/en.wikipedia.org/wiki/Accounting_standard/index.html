Accounting standard

An accounting standard is a common set of principles, standards and procedures that define the basis of financial accounting policies and practices. Accounting standards improve the transparency of financial reporting and are applied to an organisation's financial statements so that investors, lenders and regulators can compare performance across entities and periods.

International standards

The most widely adopted framework is the International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) in London. IFRS is required or permitted in more than 140 jurisdictions. In the United States, listed companies follow Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (US GAAP), maintained by the Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB).

Purpose

Standards govern recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure of transactions such as revenue, leases, financial instruments and employee benefits. National standard-setters often adapt international standards to local law; for example, India uses Ind AS, converged with IFRS.
