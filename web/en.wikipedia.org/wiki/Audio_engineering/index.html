Audio engineering

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Audio engineering is a branch of engineering dealing with sound recording, reproduction and reinforcement. An audio engineer (also known as a sound engineer or recording engineer) works with the technical aspects of music, speech and acoustics, using equipment such as microphones, mixing consoles, signal processors and digital audio workstations to capture, manipulate and reproduce audio for media including music production, film, television, radio, live events and video games.

An audio engineer at a mixing console.

Areas of practice

  • Recording engineering — capturing performances in studios, including microphone selection and placement, gain staging and tracking.
  • Mixing — balancing levels, equalisation, dynamics processing, effects and stereo imaging of multitrack recordings.
  • Mastering — preparing final mixes for distribution.
  • Live sound — front-of-house and monitor engineering for concerts and theatre.
  • Broadcast engineering — audio for radio and television.
  • Post-production — dialogue editing, Foley, sound design and mixing for film and games.

Knowledge and tools

Audio engineering draws on acoustics, psychoacoustics, electronics and digital signal processing. Engineers study frequency response, phase, dynamic range, signal-to-noise ratio and room acoustics, and work with analogue tape machines, consoles, outboard processors and software plug-ins. Professional bodies such as the Audio Engineering Society (AES), founded in 1948, publish standards and research through the Journal of the Audio Engineering Society.

History

The discipline emerged with electrical recording in the 1920s, expanded with magnetic tape after World War II and multitrack recording in the 1960s, and was transformed by digital audio in the 1980s and software-based production from the 1990s onward.
