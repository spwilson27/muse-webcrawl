Beats Electronics is an American consumer audio company founded in 2006 by the music producer and rapper Dr. Dre and the record executive Jimmy Iovine. Marketed as "Beats by Dr. Dre," its premium headphones, earphones and speakers — characterised by heavy bass response and aggressive celebrity-driven marketing — came to dominate the premium headphone market in the early 2010s. In 2014 Apple acquired Beats for $3 billion, its largest acquisition at the time.

History

Beats was founded on the premise that listeners using cheap earbuds were missing the bass and dynamics artists intended. Its first product, the Beats Studio headphones (2008), manufactured initially with Monster Cable, was promoted heavily through musicians, athletes and product placement. The company bought back distribution rights in 2012; HTC took and later sold a major stake. In 2014 Beats launched the subscription streaming service Beats Music. Apple's acquisition, announced on 28 May 2014 and valued at $3 billion ($2.6 billion in cash and $400 million in stock), brought Dr. Dre and Iovine to Apple, closed Beats Music, and folded its team into the development of Apple Music (launched 2015). The Beats brand continues as an Apple subsidiary line alongside AirPods, with products such as the Studio Pro headphones, Beats Pill speaker and Powerbeats Pro earbuds.

Criticism

Reviewers have often judged Beats products to offer style and bass emphasis disproportionate to their price and measured audio fidelity, while acknowledging the brand's cultural influence in making headphones a fashion accessory.
