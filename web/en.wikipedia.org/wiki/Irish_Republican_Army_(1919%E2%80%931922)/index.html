Irish Republican Army (1919–1922)

Active: 1919–1922
Country: Irish Republic
Role: Guerrilla army of the Irish independence movement
Key figures: Michael Collins, Richard Mulcahy

The Irish Republican Army (IRA) of 1919–1922, retrospectively known as the Old IRA, was a guerrilla organisation that fought for Irish independence during the Irish War of Independence. It grew out of the Irish Volunteers, which had fought in the Easter Rising of 1916, and in 1919 was recognised by the Dáil Éireann as the army of the self-declared Irish Republic, to which its members swore allegiance.

War of Independence

From January 1919 the IRA waged a guerrilla campaign of ambushes, raids for arms, and assassinations against the Royal Irish Constabulary and British Army, whose reprisals — notably by the Black and Tans and Auxiliaries — escalated the conflict. Intelligence operations directed by Michael Collins, including the killing of British agents on Bloody Sunday (21 November 1920), were central to the campaign. A truce took effect on 11 July 1921.

Treaty split and Civil War

The Anglo-Irish Treaty of December 1921 split the IRA: the majority that accepted the Treaty became the nucleus of the National Army of the Irish Free State, while anti-Treaty forces retained the IRA name and fought against the Free State in the Irish Civil War (1922–1923). Later organisations using the IRA name trace their claimed lineage to this body.
