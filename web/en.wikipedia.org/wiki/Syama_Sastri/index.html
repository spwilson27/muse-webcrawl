Syama Sastri

Syama Sastri
Born: 26 April 1762, Thiruvarur
Died: 6 February 1827, Thanjavur
Occupation: Composer of Carnatic music
Era: Trinity of Carnatic music

Syama Sastri (26 April 1762 – 6 February 1827) was a leading composer of Carnatic music. He, along with Tyagaraja and Muthuswami Dikshitar, forms the Trinity of Carnatic music. He is the oldest among the three. His compositions are mostly in Telugu, with some in Sanskrit and Tamil, and are predominantly dedicated to the goddess Kamakshi.

Early life

Syama Sastri was born in Thiruvarur in present-day Tamil Nadu to a Telugu Brahmin family of temple priests. He received training in Sanskrit, Telugu and music, and was initiated into the Sri Vidya tradition of goddess worship. He later moved to Thanjavur, where he served as priest at the Bangaru Kamakshi temple. He was trained in music by his maternal uncle and refined his art under the guidance of the musician Paccimiriyam Adiyappayya.

Compositions

Around 300 of his compositions survive. Syama Sastri is especially admired for his mastery of rhythm (laya): he composed in intricate talas such as the Misra Chapu and introduced the svarajati form as a concert piece, most famously in Bhairavi, Yadukulakambhoji and Todi. His kritis, often addressed to Kamakshi of Kanchipuram, carry the signature (mudra) "Syama Krishna". Well-known works include "Devi Brova Samayamide", "Himadrisute Pahimam" and "O Jagadamba".

Legacy

Though the smallest in output of the Trinity, his compositions are prized for their emotional intensity, lyrical beauty and rhythmic sophistication, and remain staples of the Carnatic concert repertoire.
