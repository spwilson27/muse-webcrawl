Drilling mud

Drilling mud is the common name for drilling fluid, the circulating fluid used in rotary drilling operations for oil and gas wells, geothermal wells and exploratory boreholes. Mud is pumped from surface pits down through the drill string, exits through nozzles in the drill bit, and returns up the annular space carrying rock cuttings.

Composition

Most muds are built on a base of water, oil or synthetic fluid. Bentonite gives viscosity and suspension, barite adds weight to control formation pressure, and a large range of additives adjust properties such as filtration, alkalinity, lubricity and resistance to contaminants like salt, gypsum and hydrogen sulfide.

Monitoring

Mud properties — density, viscosity, gel strength, fluid loss and solids content — are tested routinely at the rig site, because changes can signal downhole problems such as kicks, lost circulation or unstable shale.
