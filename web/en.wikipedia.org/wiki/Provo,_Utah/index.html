Provo, Utah

Provo is a city in and the county seat of Utah County, Utah, United States. It lies about 43 miles (69 km) south of Salt Lake City along the Wasatch Front, between Utah Lake to the west and the Wasatch Range to the east. With a population of about 115,000, it is the third-largest city in Utah and the principal city of the Provo–Orem metropolitan area.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Ute people. In 1849, Mormon settlers from Salt Lake City established Fort Utah at the Provo River; the settlement was named Provo for Étienne Provost, an early French-Canadian trapper. Conflicts with the Utes led to the Walker War of 1853–54. Provo was incorporated in 1856.

Education and culture

Provo is home to Brigham Young University (BYU), operated by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and to the church's Provo City Center Temple and the original Provo Utah Temple. The city also hosts the Missionary Training Center, where LDS missionaries are prepared. The Provo–Orem area is a significant technology hub, sometimes called part of Utah's "Silicon Slopes".

Geography

The Provo River flows from the Uinta Mountains through Provo Canyon into Utah Lake. Peaks such as Y Mountain — bearing a large block "Y" for BYU — overlook the city, and nearby Provo Canyon gives access to Bridal Veil Falls and Sundance Resort.
