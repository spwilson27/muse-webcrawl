Detinue

In tort law, detinue (or detainer) is a common law action to recover for the wrongful taking of personal property (chattels). The action sought either the return of the goods themselves or their value, together with damages for the detention. It grew out of the medieval action of debt and was one of the old forms of action at common law.

A claim in detinue lay against a person who had lawfully come into possession of goods — for example, by finding or bailment — but wrongfully refused to return them on demand, provided the goods were still in the defendant's possession. This distinguished it from conversion, which required a wrongful act inconsistent with the owner's rights.

In England and Wales, detinue was abolished by the Torts (Interference with Goods) Act 1977, which replaced it (and conversion) with the statutory tort of wrongful interference with goods. Versions of the action survive in some other common-law jurisdictions.
