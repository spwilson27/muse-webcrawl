Geneva Free Ports

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Geneva Free Ports (French: Ports Francs et Entrepôts de Genève SA) are a warehouse complex and duty-free zone in Geneva, Switzerland, operating under the legal status of a free port. Goods stored there are legally considered to be outside Swiss customs territory, so import duties and taxes are suspended for as long as the goods remain in storage. The Geneva facility is the oldest freeport, founded in 1888, and is widely regarded as the largest tax-free warehouse for artworks and valuables in the world.

Contents and function

The complex offers some 140,000 square metres of storage used by art dealers, collectors, auction houses, wine merchants, and banks. It is estimated to hold around a million works of art, along with vast quantities of fine wine, precious stones, gold, antiques, and vintage cars. Because transactions can be conducted inside the freeport without the goods ever entering a tax jurisdiction, it functions as an offshore marketplace for art: works may change hands repeatedly, appreciating in value, while remaining in the same vault.

Controversies

Freeports have been criticised as havens for tax avoidance, money laundering, and the concealment of looted or illicitly traded cultural property. The Geneva freeport was central to the "Bouvier affair", in which the art dealer Yves Bouvier was accused of overcharging the Russian collector Dmitry Rybolovlev for works stored and sold through the facility. Scrutiny also followed the discovery of antiquities linked to looted Syrian and Italian sites. In response, Switzerland tightened its rules from 2016, requiring inventories of stored goods, identification of owners, and time limits on storage for certain cultural property, and the European Parliament has called for freeports to be phased out within the EU.

Other freeports

The Geneva model has been imitated in Singapore, Monaco, Beijing, Luxembourg (Le Freeport Luxembourg) and Delaware, forming a global network of high-security, tax-suspended storage for the art market and the wealth-management industry.
