Kakuei Tanaka (田中 角栄, Tanaka Kakuei, 4 May 1918 – 16 December 1993) was a Japanese politician who served as Prime Minister of Japan from 1972 to 1974. Rising from humble origins as a self-made construction entrepreneur, he became one of the most powerful and controversial figures of postwar Japanese politics, dominating the ruling Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) through an unmatched talent for fundraising and faction-building.

Early career

Born in rural Niigata Prefecture, Tanaka left school at fifteen, worked his way up in Tokyo's construction industry, and was elected to the Diet in 1947 at age 28. He held a succession of posts — including minister of posts and telecommunications, finance minister, and minister of international trade and industry — and as a minister championed vast infrastructure building.

Premiership

Tanaka became prime minister in July 1972. His bestselling policy book, Building a New Japan ("Plan for Remodeling the Japanese Archipelago"), proposed dispersing industry and population through a nationwide web of expressways and Shinkansen lines. In September 1972 he visited Beijing and normalised diplomatic relations between Japan and the People's Republic of China. His government was buffeted by the 1973 oil crisis and inflation, and he resigned in December 1974 amid reporting on his money politics.

Lockheed scandal and later influence

In 1976 Tanaka was arrested in the Lockheed scandal over bribes paid by the Lockheed Corporation in connection with aircraft sales. He was convicted in 1983 and sentenced to four years in prison, but remained free on appeal and continued to wield enormous influence as the LDP's kingmaker through his large faction — the so-called "Tanaka machine" — even after formally leaving the party. A stroke in 1985 ended his political career, and he died in 1993 while his appeal was still pending.
