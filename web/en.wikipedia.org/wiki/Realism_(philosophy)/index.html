Realism (philosophy)

Realism in philosophy is a family of positions that some subject-matter exists independently of minds and their representations. In medieval debates, realism about universals meant general terms name real entities (moderately, in things — Aquinas; or before things, in Plato's Forms), opposite to Nominalism. Modern forms include scientific realism (theories' unobservable posits, like electrons, really exist), moral realism (objective moral facts), and metaphysical realism about a mind-independent world.

Opponents and arguments

Anti-realisms — idealism, instrumentalism, constructivism, Dummett's semantic anti-realism — press how access to a mind-independent world could be described or verified. Putnam's internal realism tried a middle path. The realism question is less a single debate than the watermark of many.
