Gilbert Adrian (born Adrian Adolph Greenburg; March 3, 1903 – September 13, 1959), credited on screen simply as Adrian, was an American costume designer whose work for Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer defined Hollywood glamour in the 1930s. He designed the costumes for more than 250 films, dressed stars including Greta Garbo, Joan Crawford, Norma Shearer, Jean Harlow and Katharine Hepburn, and created one of the most famous costumes in cinema: the ruby slippers worn by Judy Garland in The Wizard of Oz (1939).

[Image: Adrian sketching a gown design at MGM, c. 1930s]

Career

Adrian studied at the New York School of Fine and Applied Art (now Parsons) and in Paris, and was designing for Broadway — including Irving Berlin's Music Box Revue — while still a student. Hired by Rudolph Valentino's wife Natacha Rambova, he went to Hollywood in the mid-1920s, worked for Cecil B. DeMille, and in 1928 became head costume designer at MGM, a position he held until 1941. His designs set fashions as well as dressing characters: the large-shouldered silhouette he created for Joan Crawford in Letty Lynton (1932) was copied by department stores nationwide, and his hats for Garbo popularised the slouch and turban styles. For The Wizard of Oz he changed Baum's silver shoes to ruby slippers to exploit Technicolor.

Later life

Leaving MGM in 1941, Adrian opened his own fashion house, Adrian Ltd., in Beverly Hills, becoming an influential figure in American ready-to-wear; he retired in 1952 after a heart attack. He returned to MGM to design the costumes for Lovely to Look At (1952) and for Grand Hotel-era retrospectives, and died of a heart attack in 1959 while working on the Broadway production of Camelot. He had married actress Janet Gaynor in 1939; they had one son.
