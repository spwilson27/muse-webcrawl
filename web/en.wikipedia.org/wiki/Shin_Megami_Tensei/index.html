Shin Megami Tensei

Shin Megami Tensei (真・女神転生, "True Goddess Reincarnation") is a series of role-playing video games developed and published by Atlus, and the flagship of the wider Megami Tensei franchise. Originating in 1992 for the Super Famicom, the series is known for its dark, often post-apocalyptic settings, its demon-summoning and negotiation mechanics, and its philosophical storylines built around conflicts between Law, Chaos, and Neutrality.

Gameplay

Players explore dungeons and a world map, fighting demons in turn-based battles. Rather than merely defeating demons, players can negotiate with them — choosing dialogue options to persuade them to join the party, give items, or leave peacefully. Collected demons can be fused together in places such as the Cathedral of Shadows to create stronger demons, a system designed by artist Kazuma Kaneko, whose distinctive demon designs define the series. Later entries use the "Press Turn" battle system, which rewards exploiting enemy weaknesses with extra actions. Character alignment choices throughout the story determine which ending — Law, Chaos, or Neutral — is reached.

Main entries

  • Shin Megami Tensei (1992) — post-apocalyptic Tokyo after a demonic invasion and nuclear strike.
  • Shin Megami Tensei II (1994) — set decades later in the theocratic Tokyo Millennium.
  • Shin Megami Tensei III: Nocturne (2003) — the Demi-fiend in the Vortex World; the first mainline entry widely released in the West.
  • Shin Megami Tensei IV (2013) and IV: Apocalypse (2016) — samurai of the Eastern Kingdom of Mikado.
  • Shin Megami Tensei V (2021) — a student becomes the Nahobino in a ruined Tokyo (Da'at).

Legacy

The franchise spun off numerous sub-series, most prominently Persona, as well as Devil Summoner, Digital Devil Saga, and Devil Survivor. The series' morally ambiguous narratives and challenging difficulty have made it one of the most influential Japanese RPG franchises.
