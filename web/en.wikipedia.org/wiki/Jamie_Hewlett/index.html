Jamie Christopher Hewlett (born 3 April 1968) is an English comic artist, illustrator and designer. He is best known as the co-creator of the comic book character Tank Girl, created with writer Alan Martin, and as the visual half of the virtual band Gorillaz, which he founded with musician Damon Albarn.

Early career and Tank Girl

Hewlett, from Horsham in West Sussex, studied at Northbrook College in Worthing. While still a student he contributed to the fanzine Atomtan and came to the attention of Brett Ewins, who invited him to contribute to the new magazine Deadline in 1988. For Deadline Hewlett and Alan Martin created Tank Girl, an anarchic, punk-influenced strip about a tank-driving outlaw in a surreal post-apocalyptic Australia. The character became a cult success and was adapted into a 1995 feature film, which Hewlett disliked but which raised his profile in the United States, leading to work for DC Comics and other publishers.

Gorillaz

Living in a flat in Notting Hill with Blur frontman Damon Albarn in the late 1990s, Hewlett conceived a "virtual band" as a comment on the manufactured pop of the MTV era. Gorillaz, launched in 2000, presents four animated members — 2-D, Murdoc Niccals, Noodle and Russel Hobbs — designed and drawn by Hewlett, with music performed by Albarn and collaborators. The project has sold millions of albums, won Grammy and Brit awards, and pioneered the use of animated and hologram-style stage performances. Hewlett provides the band's artwork, videos and visual mythology.

Other work

Hewlett has worked across media: he designed the stage visuals for Albarn's opera Monkey: Journey to the West (2007), created the animated sequences for the BBC's coverage of the 2008 Beijing Olympics, and produced the comic series The Invisibles covers and the Phoo Action series for the BBC. A major retrospective monograph of his art was published by Taschen in 2017. He lives and works in Paris.
