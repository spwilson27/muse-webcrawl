Telecommunications in Germany

Telecommunications in Germany encompasses the telephone, mobile, internet and broadcasting networks of Europe's most populous country. The market is one of the largest in Europe, with more than 80 million inhabitants and a high density of mobile subscriptions. The federal regulator is the Bundesnetzagentur (Federal Network Agency).

History

Telephony in the German Empire and later West Germany was operated as a state monopoly by the Deutsche Bundespost. Reunification brought the networks of East and West together, and a series of postal reforms in the 1990s split the Bundespost into separate companies. Deutsche Telekom was privatized and the market fully liberalized on 1 January 1998 under the Telecommunications Act, ending the state monopoly on voice telephony.

Fixed-line and broadband

Deutsche Telekom remains the largest fixed-line operator, competing with Vodafone, Telefónica's O2, 1&1 and regional providers. Broadband access is delivered mainly via DSL over copper lines — Germany was relatively slow to adopt full fibre-to-the-home — together with cable internet from Vodafone's Kabel Deutschland network and a growing fibre build-out.

Mobile

Three major mobile network operators dominate: Telekom Deutschland, Vodafone Germany and Telefónica Deutschland (O2). The former fourth operator, E-Plus, was acquired by Telefónica in 2014; 1&1 began building a fourth network after acquiring spectrum in the 2019 5G auction. Mobile penetration exceeds 100 subscriptions per 100 inhabitants.

Internet

Major internet exchange points, notably DE-CIX in Frankfurt, rank among the largest in the world by traffic. Internet use is widespread, with household broadband adoption among the highest in Europe, though average speeds have historically lagged some neighbouring countries due to the reliance on DSL.
