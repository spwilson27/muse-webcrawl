Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence

The Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence (known in India as Panchsheel) are a set of principles of international relations first formally enunciated in the Agreement on Trade and Intercourse between the Tibet Region of China and India, signed on 29 April 1954. They were promoted by Chinese Premier Zhou Enlai and Indian Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru, and became a touchstone of the foreign policies of both countries and of the Non-Aligned Movement.

The five principles

1. Mutual respect for each other's territorial integrity and sovereignty.
2. Mutual non-aggression.
3. Mutual non-interference in each other's internal affairs.
4. Equality and mutual benefit.
5. Peaceful coexistence.

History

Zhou Enlai first articulated the principles in December 1953 during negotiations with an Indian delegation over Tibet. The 1954 agreement's preamble enshrined them, and a joint statement by Zhou and Nehru in June 1954 expressed the hope that they would guide relations across Asia. The principles influenced the Bandung Conference of 1955 and were incorporated into the ten principles adopted there. They were subsequently written into China's constitution-era diplomacy and reaffirmed in numerous Chinese declarations, including the normalization of relations with other states.

The principles came under strain during the deterioration of Sino-Indian relations that culminated in the border war of 1962, yet both countries have continued to invoke Panchsheel, notably at its 60th and 70th anniversaries.
