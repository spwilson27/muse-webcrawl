Stephen Wolfram

Born: August 29, 1959, London, England

Fields: Computer science, mathematics, theoretical physics

Known for: Mathematica, Wolfram Language, WolframAlpha, A New Kind of Science

Stephen Wolfram (born August 29, 1959) is a British-American computer scientist, physicist, and businessman. He is known for his work in computer science, mathematics, and theoretical physics. He is the founder and CEO of the software company Wolfram Research, where he works as chief designer of Mathematica and the Wolfram Language. In 2009, he launched the WolframAlpha computational knowledge engine.

Wolfram is the author of the book A New Kind of Science (2002), which argues that simple computational systems, such as cellular automata, can exhibit complex behavior and could form the basis of a new approach to understanding nature. In 2020, he launched the Wolfram Physics Project, an effort to find a fundamental theory of physics based on simple computational rules.

Early life and education

Wolfram was born in London in 1959 and educated at Eton College. He published his first scientific paper at the age of 15 and received his PhD in particle physics from the California Institute of Technology (Caltech) at age 20. He joined the faculty of Caltech and, in 1981, became the youngest recipient of a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship.

Career

After work on cellular automata at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, Wolfram founded Wolfram Research in 1987. The first version of Mathematica was released in 1988. WolframAlpha, a computational knowledge engine, was launched in May 2009.
