The hepatic portal vein is a blood vessel that carries blood from the gastrointestinal tract, gallbladder, pancreas and spleen to the liver. This blood contains nutrients and toxins extracted from digested contents, which the liver processes before the blood returns to the heart via the hepatic veins and inferior vena cava.

Anatomy

The portal vein forms behind the neck of the pancreas by the union of the superior mesenteric vein and the splenic vein; the inferior mesenteric vein usually joins the splenic vein. It divides at the porta hepatis into right and left branches supplying the liver lobes. It carries roughly 75% of the liver's blood flow, though only about half its oxygen.

Clinical significance

Raised pressure in the portal system (portal hypertension), most often caused by cirrhosis, leads to splenomegaly, ascites and portosystemic collaterals such as oesophageal varices. The portal venous system lacks valves, so pressure changes are transmitted throughout its territory.
