OriginGermanic mythology and folklore

TraitsSupernatural, human-like, often beautiful, magical

An elf (plural: elves) is a type of humanoid supernatural being in Germanic folklore and mythology. In Norse mythology, elves (álfar) are associated with fertility, nature, and the god Freyr, and divided in some sources into light elves and dark elves.

Folklore

In medieval Germanic and Scandinavian tradition, elves could cause illness (e.g., "elf-shot"), help or hinder humans, and were offered sacrifices (álfablót). In early modern folklore they shrank into small, mischievous fairy-like beings; in Britain they merged with fairies, and Christmas elves later emerged in popular culture.

Literary revival

Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream miniaturized elves, while 19th-century Romanticism revived grander figures. J. R. R. Tolkien restored tall, wise, immortal Elves in The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings, profoundly shaping modern fantasy.

Modern fantasy

Pointed-eared, long-lived, magical elves are staples of fantasy literature, tabletop games such as Dungeons & Dragons, and video games, with variants like high elves, wood elves, and dark elves (drow).
