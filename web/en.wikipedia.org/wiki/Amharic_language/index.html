Amharic (አማርኛ, Amarəñña) is an Ethiopian Semitic language of the Afroasiatic family, spoken as a first language by the Amhara people and as a lingua franca throughout Ethiopia. With roughly 57 million speakers in total, it is the second most widely spoken Semitic language after Arabic, and it is the official working language of the Ethiopian federal government.

Writing system

Amharic is written with the Ge'ez script (Fidel), an abugida in which each character represents a consonant–vowel syllable; the script has 33 basic characters with seven vocalic orders each, plus labialised variants. Word order is subject–object–verb, and the language has a rich system of verb morphology inherited from its Semitic roots, along with Cushitic influence on vocabulary and syntax.

History

Emerging in the Amhara region around the 10th century, Amharic became the language of the Ethiopian imperial court under the Solomonic dynasty, and the language of royal chronicles from the 14th century onward. It remained Ethiopia's sole official language until the 1995 constitution made regional working languages possible, though it remains the federal working language.
