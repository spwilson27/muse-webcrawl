Jella Lepman

Jella Lepman
Born: 15 May 1891, Stuttgart, Germany
Died: 14 October 1970 (aged 79), Zurich, Switzerland
Known for: Founder of the International Youth Library and IBBY

Jella Lepman (15 May 1891 – 14 October 1970) was a German journalist, author and editor who founded the International Youth Library in Munich and was a driving force behind the International Board on Books for Young People (IBBY).

Life

Lepman worked as a journalist in Weimar Germany, including on the Stuttgarter Neues Tagblatt. Being Jewish, she emigrated in 1936 after the Nazi rise to power, working in London for the BBC and the American Broadcasting Station in Europe. After the Second World War she returned to Germany as an adviser on the cultural and educational needs of women and children in the American zone of occupation.

Children's books as a bridge

Convinced that children's books could build understanding across nations, Lepman organised an international exhibition of children's books in 1946 and in 1949 founded the International Youth Library in Munich, now housed in Blutenburg Castle and the world's largest library of international children's literature. She helped found IBBY in Zurich in 1953 and was instrumental in establishing the Hans Christian Andersen Award for children's writers and illustrators. Her memoir, A Bridge of Children's Books, recounts the project.
