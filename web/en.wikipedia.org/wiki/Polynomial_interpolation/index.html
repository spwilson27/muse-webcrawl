Polynomial interpolation

In numerical analysis, polynomial interpolation is the interpolation of a given data set by the polynomial of lowest possible degree that passes through the points of the dataset.

Definition

Given a set of n + 1 data points (x0, y0), ..., (xn, yn) with no two xj the same, a polynomial p of degree at most n is sought such that p(xi) = yi for every i. A theorem of algebra guarantees that such an interpolating polynomial exists and is unique.

Construction

Common constructions include the Lagrange polynomials, Newton polynomials (using divided differences), and interpolation at Chebyshev nodes. Solving the Vandermonde system directly is possible but often numerically unstable for large n.

Error and Runge's phenomenon

The interpolation error is proportional to the (n+1)-th derivative of the underlying function and to a product term in (x − xi). For equally spaced points, high-degree interpolation can oscillate badly near the ends of the interval — Runge's phenomenon — which is why piecewise methods such as spline interpolation are often preferred in practice.
