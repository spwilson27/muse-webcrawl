The 1944 World Series was the championship series of Major League Baseball for the 1944 season, played between the National League champion St. Louis Cardinals and the American League champion St. Louis Browns. The Cardinals defeated the Browns in six games. Because both clubs shared the city of St. Louis and the same ballpark, Sportsman's Park, the series is known as the "Streetcar Series" — every game was played in St. Louis, with the teams alternating as the nominal home club.

Background

The wartime 1944 season was played amid the manpower shortages of the Second World War. The Browns, perennial cellar-dwellers, won their only American League pennant in franchise history, finishing one game ahead of Detroit. The Cardinals were the dominant team of the era, winning 105 games and their third straight pennant. Many star players were away on military service, and both rosters included players classified 4-F or otherwise unavailable for the draft.

Summary of games

The Browns took the lead in the series by winning Games 1 and 3, behind pitching from Denny Galehouse and Jack Kramer, before the Cardinals' deeper pitching and defence prevailed. Max Lanier won the decisive Game 6, 3–1, as the Cardinals scored all three runs in the fourth inning. The series was notable for a record number of errors and strikeouts, reflecting the depleted wartime talent pool.

Legacy

The Browns never won another pennant in St. Louis; the franchise moved to Baltimore in 1954 and became the Orioles. The Cardinals' victory was their fifth World Series title and cemented the club's 1940s dynasty, which also won the 1942 and 1946 Series.
