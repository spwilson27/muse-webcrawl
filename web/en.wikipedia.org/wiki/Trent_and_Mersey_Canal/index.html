The Trent and Mersey Canal is a 93.5-mile (150 km) canal in Derbyshire, Staffordshire and Cheshire in north-central England. It is a "narrow canal" in the northern half of the country, linking the River Trent at Derwent Mouth in Derbyshire with the River Mersey via the Bridgewater Canal at Preston Brook in Cheshire.

History

The canal was promoted by Josiah Wedgwood and other Staffordshire pottery manufacturers, who needed a reliable route to carry fragile pottery and raw materials such as flint and china clay. James Brindley was appointed engineer, and the first sod was cut by Wedgwood at Brownhills in July 1766. The canal was completed in 1777, the year after Brindley's death. Its summit crosses the watershed between the Trent and Mersey through the Harecastle Tunnel — the original tunnel, 2,880 yards long, took eleven years to build and had no towpath, so boats were "legged" through.

Route

From Derwent Mouth the canal passes through Burton upon Trent, Rugeley and Stone to Stoke-on-Trent, where it serves the Potteries. North of Stoke, the flight of locks at Kidsgrove leads to the Harecastle Tunnel; beyond, the canal descends through the Cheshire salt towns of Middlewich and Northwich — the steep "Heartbreak Hill" flight of locks in Cheshire is famous among boaters — before reaching Preston Brook and the junction with the Bridgewater Canal. The Anderton Boat Lift, built in 1875, connects the canal to the River Weaver below.

Today

Commercial traffic ended in the 20th century, but the canal is now a popular leisure navigation and forms part of the Cheshire Ring and the Four Counties Ring cruising routes. It is managed by the Canal & River Trust.
