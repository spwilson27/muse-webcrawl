Rosalía de Castro

Rosalía de Castro (23 February 1837 – 15 July 1885) was a Spanish writer and poet from Galicia, considered one of the founders of modern Galician literature and a central figure of the Galician cultural revival, the Rexurdimento. Writing in both Galician and Spanish, she gave voice to the Galician poor and emigrants and expressed the melancholic longing known as morriña and saudade.

Born23 February 1837, Santiago de Compostela, Galicia, Spain
Died15 July 1885 (aged 48), Padrón, Galicia
OccupationPoet, novelist
Notable worksCantares gallegos (1863), Follas novas (1880), En las orillas del Sar (1884)

Life

Born in Santiago de Compostela, the daughter of a priest and a woman of minor nobility — a birth shadowed by social stigma — Rosalía married the historian Manuel Murguía in 1858 and had seven children. Her life was marked by poverty, ill health and grief; she died of uterine cancer at Padrón in 1885, asking that her daughters burn her unpublished manuscripts.

Works

Her Cantares gallegos (1863), the first major work in Galician in centuries, is celebrated each year on 17 May, Galician Literature Day, the date of its publication. Follas novas ("New Leaves", 1880) deepened her exploration of injustice, emigration and women's suffering, while her final Spanish-language collection, En las orillas del Sar (1884), is regarded as a masterpiece of Spanish poetry. Her novels include La hija del mar and El caballero de las botas azules.

Legacy

Rosalía is Galicia's most emblematic cultural figure; her image appeared on the Spanish 500-peseta banknote, and her house at Padrón is now a museum. Feminist and postcolonial readings have reinforced her standing as a major voice of nineteenth-century European literature.
