Lai Châu Province

Lai Châu
Country: Vietnam
Region: Northwest
Capital: Lai Châu
Area: approx. 9,060 km²
Population: approx. 460,000 (2019)

Lai Châu is a province in the Northwest region of Vietnam. It borders the provinces of Lào Cai, Yên Bái, Sơn La and Điện Biên, and shares a border with Yunnan province of China to the north. The provincial capital is the city of Lai Châu.

Geography

The province is mountainous, lying between the Hoàng Liên Sơn range and the Đà (Black) River. Much of the terrain is rugged highland, and the province includes part of the reservoir and catchment of major hydroelectric projects on the Đà River, notably the Lai Châu hydropower plant, inaugurated in 2016. The climate is tropical monsoon with a pronounced dry season, cooler at altitude.

Demographics

Lai Châu is one of Vietnam's most sparsely populated provinces and has a diverse ethnic composition. Besides the Kinh (Vietnamese) majority nationally, the province is home to large communities of Thái (White Thai and Black Thai), Hmong, Dao, Giáy, Lào and other ethnic minority groups, who together make up the majority of the provincial population.

History and administration

The modern province was re-established on 1 January 2004, when the former Lai Châu province was divided: the southern part became Điện Biên province, and the northern part retained the name Lai Châu, with its capital moved from the town of Lai Châu (now Mường Lay) to a new city built near Tam Đường. The province comprises the city of Lai Châu and seven rural districts, including Phong Thổ, Sìn Hồ, Tam Đường, Tân Uyên, Than Uyên, Nậm Nhùn and Mường Tè.
