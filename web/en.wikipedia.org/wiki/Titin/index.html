Titin

Titin
Gene: TTN (human, chromosome 2q31.2)
Also known as: Connectin
Length: up to ~34,350 amino acids
Mass: ~3–3.8 MDa, largest known protein

Titin (also called connectin) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the TTN gene. It is the largest known protein, and functions as a molecular spring in muscle, contributing largely to the passive elasticity of striated muscle.

Structure

Titin spans half a sarcomere, from the Z-disc to the M-line, in vertebrate striated muscle. A single titin molecule can be over 1 micrometre long. It is composed largely of repeated immunoglobulin-like and fibronectin type III domains, together with a unique PEVK region rich in proline, glutamate, valine and lysine that acts as an entropic spring.

Function

Titin limits the range of motion of the sarcomere in tension, acting as an elastic filament alongside the thick myosin and thin actin filaments. It also serves as a scaffold for myosin assembly and a signalling hub binding many sarcomeric proteins. See also Myosin and Actin.

Clinical significance

Mutations in TTN are associated with dilated cardiomyopathy, arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, and several myopathies including tibial muscular dystrophy. Truncating variants in TTN are among the most common genetic findings in dilated cardiomyopathy.

Name

The full chemical name of titin, starting methionyl- and ending -isoleucine, is sometimes claimed to be the longest word in English, at 189,819 letters, though lexicographers regard such systematic chemical names as verbal formulas rather than words.
