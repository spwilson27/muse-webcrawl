Flip-flop (electronics)

In electronics, flip-flops and latches are circuits that have two stable states and can be used to store state information — a bistable multivibrator. The circuit can be made to change state by signals applied to one or more control inputs and will output its state. It is the basic storage element in sequential logic. Flip-flops and latches are fundamental building blocks of digital electronics systems used in computers, communications, and many other types of systems.

Latches and flip-flops

A latch is level-sensitive: its output can change whenever its enable input is asserted. A flip-flop is edge-triggered: it samples its inputs and changes output only at the moment of a clock edge (rising or falling). The simplest circuit is the SR (set–reset) latch, built from cross-coupled NOR or NAND gates. Adding gating produces the gated D latch, and combining two latches in master–slave arrangement yields an edge-triggered flip-flop.

Types

  • SR flip-flop — set and reset inputs; the combination S=R=1 is forbidden in the simple form.
  • D flip-flop — captures the value on its D input at the clock edge and holds it; the most widely used type, forming registers and memory cells.
  • JK flip-flop — refines the SR type so that J=K=1 toggles the output.
  • T flip-flop — toggles its output on each clock pulse when T is high; used in counters and frequency dividers.

Timing parameters

Reliable operation requires respecting setup time (how long the input must be stable before the clock edge), hold time (how long it must remain stable afterward), and propagation delay. Violating setup or hold times can drive the circuit metastable, lingering between states — a key hazard when sampling asynchronous signals, mitigated by synchronizer chains of flip-flops.

History and uses

The flip-flop was invented in 1918 by William Eccles and F. W. Jordan, using vacuum tubes; it became the memory element of early computers and remains the basis of SRAM cells, registers, counters, and shift registers.
