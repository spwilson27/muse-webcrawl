KKR & Co. Inc., also known as Kohlberg Kravis Roberts & Co., is an American global investment company that manages multiple alternative asset classes, including private equity, energy, infrastructure, real estate, credit, and hedge funds. Founded in 1976 by Jerome Kohlberg Jr. and cousins Henry Kravis and George R. Roberts, KKR is one of the pioneers of the leveraged buyout (LBO).

History

Kohlberg, Kravis, and Roberts had worked together at Bear Stearns, where they carried out a series of early leveraged buyouts of family businesses whose founders wished to exit. Leaving in 1976, they founded KKR with their own capital. The firm rose to prominence in the 1980s with a succession of ever-larger buyouts, culminating in the 1988 acquisition of RJR Nabisco for about $25 billion — then the largest buyout in history, chronicled in the book and film Barbarians at the Gate. KKR's 2006 buyout of the hospital operator HCA (with Bain Capital and Merrill Lynch) and its 2007 acquisition of TXU (Energy Future Holdings), at roughly $45 billion the largest LBO ever, returned the firm to headline scale, though TXU later went bankrupt in 2014.

Business

KKR manages hundreds of billions of dollars in assets across private markets and liquid strategies, with offices in North America, Europe, and Asia. Its funds acquire companies mainly in the Americas, Europe and Asia-Pacific, and the firm has become a major investor in infrastructure, real estate, and growth equity. The insurance and reinsurance platform Global Atlantic, acquired in 2021, gave KKR a large base of permanent capital. In 2009 units of KKR's funds listed in Amsterdam moved to the New York Stock Exchange, and in 2018 the firm converted from a partnership to a corporation. In October 2021 Kravis and Roberts stepped down as co-CEOs, succeeded by Joseph Bae and Scott Nuttall, while remaining executive co-chairmen.

Criticism

Like other large private-equity firms, KKR has faced criticism over job cuts and asset sales at portfolio companies — notably in the retail sector with Toys "R" Us — as well as scrutiny of the tax treatment of carried interest. The firm has responded by promoting employee-ownership programmes at its industrial portfolio companies.
