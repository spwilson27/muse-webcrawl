Palazzo Ducale, Mantua

The Palazzo Ducale ("Ducal Palace") in Mantua, Lombardy, Italy, is a vast complex of buildings constructed between the 14th and 17th centuries mainly by the noble Gonzaga family as their royal residence in the capital of the Duchy of Mantua. The buildings are connected by numerous corridors and courtyards and are famous for the Camera degli Sposi ("Bridal Chamber"), frescoed by Andrea Mantegna between 1465 and 1474 for Ludovico III Gonzaga.

Description

The complex covers some 34,000 square metres with more than 500 rooms, making it one of the largest palace complexes in Europe, second only to the Vatican among Italian palatial cities. Its sections include the Corte Vecchia (Old Court), the Palazzo del Capitano and Magna Domus founded by the Bonacolsi family, the Corte Nuova built for Duke Guglielmo Gonzaga largely to designs by Giovan Battista Bertani, the Appartamento Ducale with its cycle of Fasti Gonzagheschi paintings, and small gems such as the studiolo apartments of Isabella d'Este and the Appartamento dei Nani, a suite of rooms built to miniature scale.

History

The Gonzaga ruled Mantua from 1328 until 1708. Under Ludovico III, Francesco II, Federico II (who commissioned Giulio Romano's Palazzo Te outside the walls) and the later dukes, the palace housed one of Europe's most celebrated courts and art collections, including works by Mantegna, Rubens and Pisanello. The sack of Mantua in 1630 and the sale of much of the collection to Charles I of England dispersed its treasures. Today the palace is a museum (Museo di Palazzo Ducale), and Mantua's historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
