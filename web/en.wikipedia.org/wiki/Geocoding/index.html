Geocoding

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Geocoding is the process of converting addresses (like a street address) into geographic coordinates (usually a latitude/longitude pair), which can then be used to place markers on a map or for spatial analysis within a geographic information system (GIS). The reverse process — finding an address or place description from coordinates — is called reverse geocoding.

How it works

A geocoder matches the components of a parsed address (house number, street name, locality, postal code) against a reference database of address ranges or address points. Where an exact address point is unavailable, the location is estimated by interpolation along the street segment: if a street is known to run from number 100 to number 200 along a block, number 150 is placed halfway along it. Results therefore carry different levels of precision — rooftop accuracy, interpolation along a street, or merely the centroid of a postal code or city — and responsible use requires attention to which level a result represents.

History and services

Geocoding developed with GIS in the 1960s–1980s, notably in the US Census Bureau's DIME files and later the TIGER system built for the 1990 census, which provided street-range data for the entire United States. Today geocoding is delivered as a web service by platforms such as Google Maps, OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, Esri, and national mapping agencies, and is embedded in emergency dispatch, logistics, ride-hailing, epidemiology, and marketing applications.

Issues

Addresses are not standardised worldwide: formats, languages, and transliteration vary, many rural and informal settlements lack formal addresses at all, and addresses change as streets are renamed. Errors propagate downstream — a mis-geocoded address can misdirect an ambulance or distort a disease map. Systems such as what3words and Google's Plus Codes offer coordinate-based alternatives in areas with poor address coverage. Privacy is also a concern, since geocoded health or customer data can re-identify individuals.
