Talent management

Talent management (TM) is the anticipation of required human capital for an organisation and the planning to meet those needs. The term, coined by McKinsey & Company following its influential 1997 study and 2001 book The War for Talent, describes the process of attracting, developing, motivating and retaining productive and engaged employees. Talent management is widely considered a function within human resources, closely linked to workforce planning, succession planning and employee engagement.

Scope

Definitions vary, but talent management generally encompasses:

  • Talent acquisition — employer branding, sourcing and recruitment of skilled staff;
  • Onboarding and integration of new hires;
  • Performance management — goal setting, appraisal and feedback;
  • Learning and development — training, coaching and career-pathing;
  • Succession planning — identifying and preparing internal candidates for critical roles;
  • Retention and engagement initiatives; and
  • Compensation and benefits aligned to scarce skills.

Approaches

Organisations differ in whether "talent" refers to all employees or only to a high-potential, high-performing subset ("exclusive" versus "inclusive" approaches). Exclusive approaches use devices such as talent pools, "A player" segmentation and nine-box grid assessments of performance and potential; critics argue these can demotivate the majority and rest on unreliable judgements of potential. Modern talent management increasingly relies on HR information systems and people analytics to track skills inventories, internal mobility and flight risks.

Criticism

Scholars note that the field suffers from definitional ambiguity — talent management is sometimes indistinguishable from strategic HRM — and that the "war for talent" framing can encourage excessive external hiring and pay inflation for executives at the expense of internal development.
