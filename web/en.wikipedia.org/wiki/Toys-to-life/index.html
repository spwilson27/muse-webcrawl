Toys-to-life

Toys-to-life is a video game genre and product category in which players use physical figurines or other toys to interact with a game. The toys contain near-field communication (NFC) chips — usually in their bases — that are read by a peripheral "portal" or by the console itself, allowing the corresponding character or item to appear in the game and, in many implementations, storing data such as experience and upgrades on the toy itself.

History

The genre was popularized by Activision's Skylanders: Spyro's Adventure (2011), whose "Portal of Power" and collectible figures generated enormous sales and spawned annual sequels. Disney entered the market with Disney Infinity (2013), combining figures of Disney and Pixar characters with a sandbox "Toy Box" mode. Warner Bros.' Lego Dimensions (2015) combined toys-to-life with physical Lego construction. Nintendo's amiibo figures, launched in 2014 for Wii U and Nintendo 3DS, used similar technology, typically to unlock extras rather than as the core of a single game.

Decline

The market contracted sharply in the mid-2010s: Disney Infinity was cancelled in 2016, Lego Dimensions ended in 2017, and the Skylanders series went on hiatus after 2016's Imaginators. Analysts attributed the collapse to market saturation, high prices, and the space consumed by figure collections. Amiibo continued as a durable, lower-key product line, and later games such as Ubisoft's Starlink: Battle for Atlas (2018) experimented with optional physical models.

Mechanics

Toys-to-life products blend collecting with gameplay: figures function both as game keys and as display collectibles. Because progress data is written to the figure, a player's character can be carried to a friend's console. Critics noted the business model's reliance on purchasing multiple physical items to unlock content already on the disc.
