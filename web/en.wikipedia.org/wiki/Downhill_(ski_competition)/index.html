Downhill (ski competition)

Downhill is a form of alpine skiing competition and the fastest of the alpine disciplines. It involves skiing down a steep, long course at the highest speeds attainable — top competitors exceed 130 km/h (81 mph), and speed records on courses such as Kitzbühel's Streif approach 160 km/h.


A downhill racer on the Streif course at Kitzbühel.

Format

Unlike the technical events (slalom and giant slalom), downhill courses have few turns, wide gates set far apart, and large vertical drops — up to about 1,100 m for men — over lengths of 2.5–5 km. A race is decided by a single run, with competitors starting at intervals after training runs on the course. Skiers adopt an aerodynamic tuck and use long, slightly curved skis.

Classic races

Famous downhill courses include the Streif at Kitzbühel (Hahnenkamm), the Lauberhorn at Wengen — the longest World Cup downhill — and the Birds of Prey course at Beaver Creek. Downhill has been an Olympic event since 1936 (combined) and as a standalone event since 1948. Because of the speeds and terrain, it is considered the most dangerous alpine discipline, and safety netting and course preparation are extensive.
