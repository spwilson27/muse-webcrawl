Organisation armée secrète

Organisation armée secrète (OAS)
Active: 1961–1963
Ideology: French Algerian nationalism; opposition to Algerian independence
Leaders: Raoul Salan, Edmond Jouhaud

The Organisation armée secrète (OAS, "Secret Army Organisation") was a far-right French paramilitary organisation active during the Algerian War. Founded in 1961 by French military officers and pied-noir settlers, it sought to prevent Algerian independence and keep Algeria French through a campaign of bombings and assassinations.

Activities

The OAS emerged after the failed Generals' Putsch of April 1961. It carried out thousands of attacks in Algeria and metropolitan France, attempted to assassinate President Charles de Gaulle — most famously in the Petit-Clamart ambush of August 1962 — and pursued a scorched-earth policy as independence approached.

Decline

The Évian Accords of March 1962 granted Algerian independence despite OAS violence. Its leaders were arrested, tried, or fled abroad, and the organisation dissolved by 1963, though it left a lasting mark on French politics and memory.
