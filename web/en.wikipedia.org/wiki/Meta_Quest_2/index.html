Meta Quest 2 (formerly and originally branded Oculus Quest 2) is a virtual reality headset developed by Meta Platforms' Reality Labs division, released on October 13, 2020. It is the successor to the original Oculus Quest and was succeeded by the Meta Quest 3 in 2023. The Quest 2 became one of the best-selling VR headsets of all time, with sales estimated in the tens of millions of units.

Hardware

The Quest 2 is a standalone, all-in-one headset powered by the Qualcomm Snapdragon XR2 system-on-chip with 6 GB of RAM. It uses a single fast-switch LCD panel with a resolution of 1832×1920 per eye and supported refresh rates rising from 72 Hz at launch to 90 Hz and experimentally 120 Hz via software updates. Inside-out tracking is handled by four cameras on the headset, which also track the included Oculus Touch controllers, and hand tracking without controllers was supported. It is lighter (503 g) than its predecessor and was offered initially with 64 GB or 256 GB of storage, later 128 GB or 256 GB.

Software and reception

The headset runs an Android-based operating system and the Quest Store library, and can also be connected to a PC via Oculus Link (USB) or wireless Air Link to play PC VR games. Its launch price of US$299 — lower than the original Quest — was widely credited with expanding the consumer VR market, aided by popular titles such as Beat Saber and the Quest 2 version of Resident Evil 4. In 2022 Meta raised the price by US$100. Criticism focused on the requirement (later relaxed) of a Facebook account, the stock head strap's comfort, and short battery life of about two to three hours.

Rebranding and discontinuation

Following Facebook's corporate rebranding to Meta in late 2021, the device was renamed Meta Quest 2 and the Oculus brand phased out. The Quest 2 was discontinued in September 2024, replaced at its price tier by the Meta Quest 3S.
