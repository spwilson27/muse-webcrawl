Morton Thiokol

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Morton Thiokol
IndustryChemical and aerospace propulsion
Known forSpace Shuttle solid rocket boosters
FateThiokol became part of Northrop Grumman (via ATK)
Building SRB segments in Utah.

Morton Thiokol was the American corporation whose Thiokol division designed and built the Space Shuttle's solid rocket boosters (SRBs) — and whose engineers became central figures of the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster. The Utah-based Thiokol had pioneered solid propellants and built rocket motors for missiles and the early space programme before winning the SRB contract.

On the night before Challenger's launch, with record cold forecast at Cape Canaveral, Thiokol engineers — prominently Roger Boisjoly and Allan McDonald — argued that the boosters' O-ring seals could fail in low temperatures and urged a delay. Under pressure from NASA managers, Thiokol executives reversed the recommendation in the infamous instruction to "take off your engineering hat and put on your management hat". The next morning the right booster's aft joint failed exactly as feared.

Aftermath

The Rogers Commission exposed the episode, which became a canonical case study in engineering ethics. Boisjoly later received the AAAS Award for Scientific Freedom and Responsibility. Morton Thiokol split in 1989; the propulsion business, as Thiokol/ATK, redesigned the booster joints (adding a capture feature and heaters) and went on to build the five-segment boosters flown today on the Space Launch System.
