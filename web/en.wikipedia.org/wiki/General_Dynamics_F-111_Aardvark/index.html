General Dynamics F-111 Aardvark

The General Dynamics F-111 Aardvark is a retired American medium-range supersonic strike aircraft developed in the 1960s by General Dynamics. It was the first production aircraft to feature variable-sweep wings ("swing wings"), and it pioneered terrain-following radar allowing automatic flight at very low altitude.

Role: Strike aircraft, strategic bomber
Manufacturer: General Dynamics
First flight: 21 December 1964
Retired: 1998 (USAF); 2010 (RAAF)
Number built: 563

Development

The F-111 grew out of the TFX program, which sought a common aircraft for the U.S. Air Force and Navy. The naval F-111B variant was cancelled, but the Air Force F-111A entered service in 1967. Early combat deployments to Vietnam in Operation Combat Lancer suffered losses, but the type matured into an effective all-weather strike aircraft.

Service

F-111s flew in the Vietnam War, the 1986 air strikes on Libya (Operation El Dorado Canyon), and the 1991 Gulf War, where they destroyed large numbers of armored vehicles using laser-guided bombs. The strategic FB-111A served with Strategic Air Command, and the EF-111A Raven provided electronic warfare support. The Royal Australian Air Force operated the F-111C until December 2010.

Design

The two-man crew sat side by side in an escape capsule rather than individual ejection seats. Two Pratt & Whitney TF30 turbofans gave a top speed above Mach 2.5 at altitude.
