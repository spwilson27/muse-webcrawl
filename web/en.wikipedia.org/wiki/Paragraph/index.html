Paragraph

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A paragraph (from the Ancient Greek παράγραφος, parágraphos, "written beside") is a self-contained unit of discourse in writing dealing with a particular point or idea. Though not required by the orthographic conventions of any language with a writing system, paragraphs are a conventional means of organizing extended segments of prose. A paragraph consists of one or more sentences, typically organized around a topic sentence that expresses the main idea, with supporting sentences that develop it.

History

In classical Greek and Latin manuscripts, a paragraphos was a short horizontal stroke in the margin marking a change of topic or speaker. Medieval scribes developed devices such as the pilcrow (¶), coloured initials, and rubrication to signal divisions. With the advent of printing, the convention of separating paragraphs by indentation or by line breaks became standardized in European typography.

Structure

School and style-guide models often describe a paragraph as containing:

  • A topic sentence stating the main point
  • Supporting sentences giving evidence, examples, or explanation
  • A concluding or transitional sentence linking to what follows

Paragraph length varies by genre: journalism typically uses short paragraphs, sometimes of a single sentence, while academic writing tends toward longer, more developed paragraphs.

Typography

The start of a paragraph is conventionally marked either by indenting the first line or by inserting vertical white space ("block paragraphs"), the latter being standard in web writing. Using both indentation and spacing at once is generally considered redundant. The pilcrow sign ¶ is called the "paragraph mark" and is used in proofreading, in legal drafting to refer to a numbered paragraph (as is the section sign §), and in desktop publishing to display hidden formatting characters.
