Work experience
DefinitionExperience gained while working in a specific field or occupation
FormsSchool placements, internships, apprenticeships, volunteering, part-time jobs
Common inUK, Australia, Ireland and other Commonwealth education systems

Work experience is any experience that a person gains while working in a specific field or occupation. In the United Kingdom and several other countries the term specifically denotes a short, usually unpaid placement undertaken by school or university students with an employer, intended to provide insight into working life and a particular career. Such placements typically last one or two weeks for school students and longer for university students, who may undertake sandwich placements lasting up to a year.

A student shadowing an employee during a placement

Purposes

Work experience is intended to help young people understand the expectations of employers, develop employability skills such as communication and teamwork, inform career choices, and build contacts. Employers may use placements to engage with their communities and identify future recruits. Evidence reviews generally find that good-quality placements are associated with improved employment outcomes, though unpaid placements have been criticised for favouring students with family connections and financial support.

Related forms

Related arrangements include internships (often for graduates), apprenticeships (longer, paid programmes combining work with structured training), traineeships, and volunteering. In the United States, comparable short school-based experiences are more often described as job shadowing or externships, while cooperative education (co-op) programmes integrate paid work terms into degree courses.
