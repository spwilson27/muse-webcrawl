The Tales of Ise

Japanese: 伊勢物語 (Ise Monogatari)

Period: Heian period, mid-10th century

Genre: Uta monogatari (poem tale)

Episodes: 125 (in the standard text)

The Tales of Ise (Japanese: 伊勢物語, Ise Monogatari) is a Japanese uta monogatari, or collection of waka poems and associated narratives, dating from the Heian period. The present text dates to the mid-tenth century, though some of its material is considerably older. It is one of the most famous works of classical Japanese literature and a foundational text of the Japanese poetic tradition.

Content

The work consists of 125 short episodes, each centred on one or more poems. The episodes loosely follow the life of an unnamed courtier, traditionally identified with the poet Ariwara no Narihira (825–880) — from his coming-of-age, through his amorous adventures at the capital and his journey into exile in the eastern provinces, to his death poem. Many episodes open with the formula mukashi otoko arikeri ("once there was a man").

Famous episodes include the hero's glimpse of two sisters at Kasuga, his forbidden affair with the Ise priestess, the journey east past Mount Fuji and across the Sumida River — where a poem about the capital birds (miyakodori) expresses his homesickness — and the episode of the irises at Yatsuhashi in Mikawa Province, where the traveller composes a poem whose lines each begin with a syllable of kakitsubata (iris).

Influence

The Tales of Ise profoundly influenced later Japanese literature, including The Tale of Genji, and its episodes became standard subjects for painting, from medieval picture scrolls to the Rinpa screens of Tawaraya Sōtatsu and Ogata Kōrin. Lady Murasaki's hero Hikaru Genji is in part modelled on Narihira.
