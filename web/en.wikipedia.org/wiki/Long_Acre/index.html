Long Acre is a street in the Covent Garden area of the City of Westminster in central London. It runs from St Martin's Lane in the west to Drury Lane in the east, parallel to and just north of the Strand. The street is known for its retail premises, theatres nearby, and its historical associations with the coachmaking and carriage trades.

History

The street was laid out in the early 17th century on land that had previously formed part of the Convent Garden of Westminster Abbey, from which the district of Covent Garden takes its name. The name "Long Acre" derives from the long, narrow strip of land — about an acre in extent — that the street follows.

By the 18th and 19th centuries Long Acre had become a centre of the London coachbuilding trade. Numerous carriage builders, wheelwrights and associated craftsmen had workshops along the street, supplying vehicles to the aristocracy and gentry. The connection survives in street names nearby and in the premises of businesses that once served the trade.

In the late 19th century the street also became associated with the motor trade, as coachbuilders adapted to producing bodies for early automobiles. Stanfords, the well-known map and travel bookshop founded by Edward Stanford, had its famous premises on Long Acre for many years.

Transport

Covent Garden tube station, on the Piccadilly line, is at the junction of Long Acre and James Street. The station is notable for being served by lifts rather than escalators, reflecting the short distance between the platforms and street level.
