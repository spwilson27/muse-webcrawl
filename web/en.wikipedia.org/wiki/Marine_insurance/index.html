Marine insurance

TypeProperty and liability insurance
CoversHulls, cargo, freight, and marine liabilities
Historic centreLloyd's of London

Marine insurance covers the physical loss of or damage to ships, cargo, terminals, and any transport by which property is transferred, acquired, or held between points of origin and final destination. It is generally regarded as the oldest form of insurance, with antecedents in Babylonian bottomry contracts and the sea loans of ancient Greece and Rome.

History

Standalone marine policies emerged in medieval Italy and spread through the trading cities of Europe. In London, merchants gathered at Edward Lloyd's coffee house from the late 17th century, giving rise to the Lloyd's of London insurance market and to the standard Lloyd's S.G. (ship and goods) policy form. English law was codified in the Marine Insurance Act 1906, which long governed practice across the Commonwealth.

Types and principles

Policies include hull and machinery cover for vessels, cargo insurance for goods in transit, freight insurance, and protection and indemnity (P&I) cover provided by mutual clubs for third-party liabilities. Distinctive principles include utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity, average — in particular general average, under which parties to a sea venture share losses from a voluntary sacrifice made for the common safety — and a traditional exclusion of ordinary wear and tear. Standard cargo wordings are organised through the Institute Cargo Clauses (A, B, and C) of decreasing breadth.
