Joshua

Joshua (Hebrew: יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, Yehoshua, "Yahweh is salvation"), son of Nun, is a central figure of the Hebrew Bible. The attendant and appointed successor of Moses, he leads the Israelites in the conquest of Canaan narrated in the Book of Joshua, and presides over the allotment of the land among the tribes of Israel.

In the Torah

Joshua first appears commanding Israel's forces in the battle against Amalek at Rephidim. He accompanies Moses partway up Mount Sinai, and is one of the twelve spies sent into Canaan; with Caleb alone he brings back a faithful report and urges the people to trust God, for which the two are the only men of their generation permitted to enter the promised land. In Deuteronomy and Numbers, Moses publicly commissions Joshua as his successor, laying hands on him before the congregation.

The conquest of Canaan

The Book of Joshua opens with God's charge to Joshua — "be strong and of a good courage" — and recounts the miraculous crossing of the Jordan on dry ground, the fall of Jericho after the procession of the ark and trumpets, the defeat at Ai and the sin of Achan, the victory over the southern and northern kings, and the famous episode at Gibeon where, at Joshua's word, "the sun stood still". Joshua then divides the conquered land by lot among the tribes, renews the covenant at Shechem, and dies at the age of 110, being buried at Timnath-heres.

Historical assessment and legacy

Modern scholarship and archaeology debate the historicity of the conquest narrative: several sites named, including Jericho and Ai, show no destruction layer matching a late Bronze Age conquest, and many scholars instead read the book as a later, theologically shaped account of Israel's origins. In Jewish tradition Joshua is Moses' exemplary disciple and the link in the chain of tradition between Moses and the elders. His Hebrew name shares its root with that of Jesus (Yeshua), and in Christian typology Joshua is read as a foreshadowing figure. He is also mentioned in the Qur'an's orbit of traditions, though not named there in connection with Moses' attendant in Sura 18, whom Islamic tradition identifies as Joshua son of Nun.
