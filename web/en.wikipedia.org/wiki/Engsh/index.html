Engsh

Engsh is a constructed, engineered variety of English proposed as part of the long tradition of English spelling reform. Its defining feature is a strictly phonemic orthography: each sound of spoken English is written with one consistent symbol or letter combination, and each written symbol always represents the same sound, so that reading and spelling become fully predictable from one another.

Design

Like other phonemic schemes, Engsh eliminates silent letters, removes duplicate spellings for the same sound (such as ph and f for /f/), and respells words according to pronunciation rather than etymology. The design restricts itself to the ordinary letters of the Latin alphabet, using digraphs where a single letter is insufficient, rather than inventing entirely new characters as the Shavian alphabet did. The goal is a system that a reader who already speaks English can learn in hours and that a learner can use to acquire correct spelling without memorising hundreds of exceptions.

Context

Engsh belongs to a family of twentieth-century rationalisation projects that treated English as an "engineered" language, similar in spirit to Basic English's restricted vocabulary but aimed at orthography rather than lexicon or grammar. None of these schemes achieved official adoption; their influence is visible mainly in linguistics discussions of orthographic depth, in simplified respellings used in language teaching, and in the informal phonetic spellings of text messaging.
