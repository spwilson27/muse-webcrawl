Social class

A social class is a grouping of people within a society based on shared socio-economic status, wealth, occupation, education and lifestyle. Class systems, unlike caste systems, in principle allow social mobility, though the degree varies widely.

Theories

Karl Marx defined classes by their relationship to the means of production: the bourgeoisie, who own capital, and the proletariat, who sell their labour, with class struggle as the engine of history. Max Weber added status (prestige) and party (political power) to economic class, producing a multidimensional picture. Pierre Bourdieu described economic, cultural and social capital, and habitus, the dispositions through which class is reproduced. Erik Olin Wright and others attempted syntheses for modern occupational structures.

Common models

Popular models divide societies into upper, middle and lower classes, often subdivided (upper-middle, lower-middle, working class, underclass). In Britain the 2013 Great British Class Survey proposed seven classes, from an "elite" to a "precariat". In the United States, income, occupation and education produce labels from the "one percent" to the working poor.

Effects

Class correlates strongly with health, life expectancy, educational attainment, voting and exposure to crime. Critics debate whether class remains the master cleavage in post-industrial societies or has been displaced by identity, region and education. Compare universal basic income, a policy aimed at the material floor beneath all classes.
