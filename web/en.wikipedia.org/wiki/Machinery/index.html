Machinery

Machinery is the collective term for machines and their parts: mechanical devices that use power to apply forces and control movement to perform an intended action. Machinery ranges from simple tools built from the classical simple machines — the lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge and screw — to complex industrial systems combining many mechanisms.

Overview

A machine typically consists of a power source or actuator, a transmission that modifies force and motion, sensors and a control system, and a structure or frame. Machinery is fundamental to manufacturing, agriculture, construction, mining and transport. Agricultural machinery includes tractors, harvesters and seed drills; industrial machinery includes machine tools, presses, pumps and conveyor systems; heavy machinery covers excavators, cranes and bulldozers.

History

The systematic study of machinery developed during the Industrial Revolution, when steam power and mechanised production transformed industry. In the 19th century Franz Reuleaux and others founded the theory of machines as a branch of mechanical engineering, classifying mechanisms by their kinematic pairs. The 20th century added electrical drives, hydraulics, pneumatics and eventually computer numerical control and robotics.

Safety and regulation

Because machinery can pose serious hazards, its design and use are regulated by standards and directives, such as the European Union's Machinery Directive, which require guarding, emergency stops and risk assessment.
