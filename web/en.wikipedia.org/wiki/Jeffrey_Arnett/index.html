Jeffrey Jensen Arnett (born 1955) is an American developmental psychologist best known for proposing the theory of emerging adulthood, a distinct developmental period between adolescence and young adulthood, roughly spanning ages 18 to 29, that he argues has emerged in industrialized societies as the transitions to stable work, marriage, and parenthood have been postponed. He is a professor in the department of psychology at Clark University in Worcester, Massachusetts.

Emerging adulthood

Arnett introduced the theory in a widely cited 2000 article in American Psychologist, "Emerging Adulthood: A Theory of Development from the Late Teens Through the Twenties." He identifies five features of the period: identity exploration, instability, self-focus, feeling in-between, and a sense of possibilities. The proposal drew on demographic shifts — rising ages of marriage and first parenthood, longer education — and on interviews with young people themselves. His 2004 book Emerging Adulthood: The Winding Road from the Late Teens through the Twenties developed the theory for a general audience.

Reception and debate

The concept has been hugely influential, prompting a dedicated scholarly society (the Society for the Study of Emerging Adulthood), a journal (Emerging Adulthood), and wide media coverage. Critics have questioned whether the period is a universal developmental stage or a phenomenon limited to affluent young people in wealthy societies, and whether it is better understood as a prolonged adolescence or simply young adulthood under changed economic conditions. Arnett has responded by studying emerging adulthood cross-culturally, while acknowledging its class and cultural boundaries.

Other work

Earlier in his career Arnett studied adolescents' risk behavior and sensation seeking, the influence of heavy metal music and media on youth, and the psychological effects of globalization on young people around the world, the subject of his book on the global generation. He has also written developmental textbooks on adolescence and adulthood.
