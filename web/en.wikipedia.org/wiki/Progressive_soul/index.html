Progressive soul

Progressive soul (often shortened to prog-soul) is a genre of soul and rhythm and blues music that developed in the late 1960s and 1970s, characterized by ambitious songwriting, extended compositions, sophisticated arrangements, and the influence of progressive rock and jazz fusion. Progressive soul artists treated the album as an artistic statement, incorporating orchestration, concept-album structures, social commentary, and studio experimentation.

Characteristics

The style features longer tracks, complex harmonic and rhythmic structures, layered instrumentation including strings and synthesizers, and lyrical themes ranging from politics and spirituality to Afrofuturism. It paralleled developments in progressive rock while remaining rooted in African-American musical traditions of gospel, soul, and funk.

Key artists and works

Landmark recordings include Marvin Gaye's What's Going On (1971), Stevie Wonder's albums of the 1970s such as Innervisions (1973) and Songs in the Key of Life (1976), Curtis Mayfield's Curtis (1970) and Superfly (1972), Isaac Hayes's Hot Buttered Soul (1969), and the work of Earth, Wind & Fire, Donny Hathaway, Minnie Riperton, and Parliament-Funkadelic. Sly and the Family Stone's There's a Riot Goin' On (1971) is also frequently cited.

Legacy

Progressive soul influenced later developments including neo soul of the 1990s and 2000s, whose artists consciously revived its album-oriented, musically eclectic approach.
