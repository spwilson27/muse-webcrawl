Mponeng Gold Mine

Location: Gauteng, South Africa
Owner: Harmony Gold (since 2020; previously AngloGold Ashanti)
Depth: Approx. 4.0 km (2.5 mi) below surface
Status: World's deepest mine

Mponeng is a deep-level gold mine near Carletonville in Gauteng province, South Africa, and is the deepest mine in the world, extending approximately 4.0 kilometres (2.5 mi) below the surface. The mine exploits the Ventersdorp Contact Reef of the Witwatersrand Basin, the richest goldfield ever discovered. Mponeng was developed by AngloGold (later AngloGold Ashanti) — sinking began in 1981 and production started in 1986 under the name Western Deep Levels South — and was sold to Harmony Gold in 2020.

Operations

The trip from the surface to the deepest levels takes over an hour by cage and on foot, and miners travel tens of kilometres of tunnels. Rock temperatures at depth reach about 66 °C (151 °F), so the mine operates an enormous refrigeration system — including ice slurry pumped from the surface — to cool the working air to below 30 °C. The surrounding rock is under extreme pressure, and "rockbursts" — small seismic events caused by mining — are a constant hazard managed by backfilling and seismic monitoring. Ore is hoisted to a gold plant that recovers gold by carbon-in-leach processing.

Science at Mponeng

Mponeng's depth makes it a major natural laboratory. Researchers studying deep subsurface life recovered the bacterium Candidatus Desulforudis audaxviator from fracture water 2.8 km down — an organism that lives entirely independently of sunlight, powered by radioactivity-driven chemistry, and was described as a complete ecosystem in a single species. The mine has also hosted deep-underground physics and geology research, including studies relevant to dark-matter detector siting.
