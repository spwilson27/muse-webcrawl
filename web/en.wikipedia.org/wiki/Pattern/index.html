Pattern

A pattern is a regularity in the world, in human-made design, or in abstract ideas. As such, the elements of a pattern repeat in a predictable manner. A geometric pattern is formed of geometric shapes and typically repeated like a wallpaper design. Any of the senses may directly observe patterns; conversely, abstract patterns in science, mathematics or language may be observable only by analysis.

Patterns in nature

Natural patterns include symmetries, spirals, waves, tessellations, cracks and stripes, arising from physical and biological processes. Examples include the Fibonacci spirals of sunflower heads and pinecones, the hexagonal cells of honeycomb, zebra stripes produced by reaction–diffusion processes, and the fractal branching of trees and rivers. Alan Turing's 1952 paper on morphogenesis explained how regular patterns can arise from uniform states.

Patterns in art and design

In design, a pattern is a decorative motif repeated across a surface — in textiles, tiles, architecture and graphic design. In sewing and garment-making, a pattern is also the template from which clothing is cut. In mathematics, patterns underlie sequences, tilings and symmetry groups; in computer science, design patterns are reusable solutions to common problems.

Pattern recognition

Humans and machines detect patterns to predict and classify; pattern recognition is a branch of machine learning, while the tendency to perceive patterns where none exist is called apophenia.
