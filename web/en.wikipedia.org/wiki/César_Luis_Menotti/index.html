César Luis Menotti (5 November 1938 – 5 May 2024), nicknamed El Flaco ("The Thin One"), was an Argentine football player and manager best known for leading the Argentina national team to its first FIFA World Cup title, at home in 1978. A leading figure of the romantic, possession-based school of Argentine football known as menottismo, he stood in famous contrast to the more pragmatic Carlos Bilardo.

Playing career

Born in Rosario, Menotti played as a striker for Rosario Central, Racing Club and Boca Juniors in Argentina, and had a spell in the United States with the New York Generals, before finishing his career in Brazil with Santos — where he played alongside Pelé — and Clube Atlético Juventus.

Managerial career

Menotti rose to prominence as coach of Huracán, whose stylish 1973 Metropolitano-winning team is still celebrated as one of the finest in Argentine club history. Appointed national team coach in 1974, he rebuilt Argentina around young talent, won the 1978 World Cup on home soil — beating the Netherlands 3–1 in the final — and also won the 1979 FIFA World Youth Championship with a team featuring Diego Maradona. He remained in charge through the 1982 World Cup.

He later managed Barcelona (winning the Copa del Rey, Copa de la Liga and Supercopa in 1983), Atlético Madrid, Boca Juniors, River Plate, Independiente, Sampdoria, the Mexico national team and others. From 2019 until his death he served as director of national teams for the Argentine Football Association, a role in which he supported Lionel Scaloni's appointment and Argentina's 2022 World Cup triumph.

Philosophy and legacy

Menotti was an articulate, chain-smoking intellectual of the game who argued that football was an expression of culture and that winning could not justify any means. The debate between menottismo and Bilardo's bilardismo framed discussion of Argentine football for decades. He died in Buenos Aires on 5 May 2024.
