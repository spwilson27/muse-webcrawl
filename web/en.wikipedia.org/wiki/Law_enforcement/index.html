Law enforcement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Law enforcement is the activity of some members of government who act in an organised manner to enforce the law by discovering, investigating, deterring, rehabilitating, or punishing people who violate the rules and norms governing that society. The term encompasses police, courts, and corrections. Modern law enforcement also includes private actors, such as security companies, who enforce rules in non-criminal matters.

Organisations

Most law enforcement is conducted by some type of law enforcement agency, with the most typical agency fulfilling this role being the police. In most countries, law enforcement agencies operate at multiple levels of government — national (federal), state or provincial, and local — with overlapping or exclusive jurisdictions. Specialised agencies may enforce particular bodies of law, such as customs, environmental protection, taxation, or immigration.

History

Although organised policing is largely a modern institution, mechanisms for enforcing law are ancient: kin-based retribution, communal watch systems such as the Anglo-Saxon frankpledge and the hue and cry, and officials such as sheriffs and constables preceded professional forces. The first modern police force is generally considered to be the Metropolitan Police of London, founded in 1829 under Sir Robert Peel, whose "Peelian principles" emphasised crime prevention and policing by consent.

Methods and issues

Law enforcement methods include patrol, criminal investigation, community policing, intelligence-led policing, and predictive policing. Officers are typically granted special powers — to search, arrest, detain, and use force — that are constrained by law and oversight mechanisms. Persistent debates concern accountability, use of force, racial and social disparities, surveillance, and the proper scope of enforcement.
