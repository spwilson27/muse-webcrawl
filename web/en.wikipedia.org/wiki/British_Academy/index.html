The British Academy, formally the British Academy for the Promotion of Historical, Philosophical and Philological Studies, is the United Kingdom's national academy for the humanities and social sciences. It was founded in 1902 and received its royal charter the same year; it is one of the UK's four national academies, alongside the Royal Society, the Royal Academy of Engineering and the Academy of Medical Sciences.

Activities

The Academy elects distinguished scholars as Fellows (postnominal FBA) across disciplines including history, law, economics, archaeology, linguistics, philosophy and sociology. It is a major funder of research: it distributes government research funding through grants and fellowships for early-career and senior researchers, supports the British International Research Institutes abroad, and publishes journals and reports on public policy questions such as the value of the humanities, language learning and social cohesion.

It should not be confused with the British Academy of Film and Television Arts (BAFTA), an unrelated arts charity best known for its film awards.
