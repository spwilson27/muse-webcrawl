Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports

The Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports is a ceremonial official in the United Kingdom. The post dates from at least the 12th century, when the title was Keeper of the Coast, but it may be older. The Lord Warden was originally in charge of the Cinque Ports, a group of port towns in south-east England that were grouped together for defence purposes. Today the role is a sinecure and an honorary title, held by a person of distinction as a mark of royal favour.

History

The Cinque Ports — Hastings, New Romney, Hythe, Dover and Sandwich, together with the later additions of Winchelsea and Rye — provided ships and men for the defence of the realm before the establishment of a standing navy. The Lord Warden exercised considerable authority over the confederation, including judicial and administrative powers, and commanded Dover Castle. By the 17th century the office had lost most of its practical functions as the Royal Navy developed, and it gradually became an honorary dignity.

The official residence of the Lord Warden is Walmer Castle in Kent. The holder of the office is also Constable of Dover Castle and Admiral of the Cinque Ports.

Notable holders

Among the most distinguished holders of the office were William Pitt the Younger, the Duke of Wellington, Sir Winston Churchill, and Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother, who held the post from 1978 until her death in 2002 and was the first woman to do so. Admiral Sir Michael Boyce, Baron Boyce, served as Lord Warden from 2004 until his death in 2022. He was succeeded in 2024 by Admiral Sir George Zambellas.

The Lord Warden is appointed by the monarch by letters patent and holds office during the sovereign's pleasure.
