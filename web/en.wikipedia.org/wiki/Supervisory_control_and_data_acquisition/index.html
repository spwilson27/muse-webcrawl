Supervisory control and data acquisition

Supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) is a control system architecture comprising computers, networked communications and graphical user interfaces for the high-level supervision of industrial processes, together with field devices such as programmable logic controllers (PLCs) and remote terminal units (RTUs) that interface directly with plant equipment.

Architecture

A SCADA system typically consists of:

  • Field devices — sensors and actuators connected to PLCs and RTUs, which execute local control and gather measurements.
  • Communications infrastructure — industrial networks and protocols (such as Modbus, DNP3 or IEC 60870-5-104) linking field devices to central servers, historically over leased lines and radio, increasingly over IP networks.
  • Supervisory computers and historians — servers that poll field devices, store process data, raise alarms and serve the operator displays.
  • Human–machine interfaces (HMIs) — graphical screens on which operators monitor processes and issue commands.

Applications

SCADA is used in electrical power generation, transmission and distribution; water treatment and distribution; oil and gas pipelines; railways; manufacturing; and building management. Systems range from small single-plant installations to networks spanning continents, such as national power grids.

Security

Systems designed when isolation was assumed proved vulnerable once interconnected with corporate networks and the internet. The Stuxnet worm, discovered in 2010, demonstrated that SCADA and PLC targets could be attacked to cause physical damage, prompting major programmes in industrial cybersecurity, network segmentation and standards such as IEC 62443.
