Michèle Hélène Raymonde Mouton (born 23 June 1951) is a French former rally driver. She is the most successful and well-known female rally driver of all time, as well as arguably the most successful female in motor racing. She was the first and remains the only woman to win a round of the World Rally Championship.

Career

Mouton began rallying in 1973 as a co-driver before moving behind the wheel, winning the French women's championship and competing at Le Mans in 1975 in an all-female team. Signed by Fiat France, she won the 1978 Tour de France Automobile. With the Audi works team from 1981, she made history by winning the 1981 Rallye Sanremo in the four-wheel-drive Audi Quattro.

In 1982, with co-driver Fabrizia Pons, Mouton won three further WRC rounds — Portugal, Greece (Acropolis) and Brazil — and finished runner-up in the World Championship to Walter Röhrl, losing the title battle only at the final round after a crash in Côte d'Ivoire weeks after her father's death. She also finished second at the 1982 RAC Rally.

Later career and legacy

Mouton won the Pikes Peak International Hill Climb in 1985, setting a record time, and retired after Group B was banned at the end of 1986. She co-founded the Race of Champions in 1988, later served as manager of the FIA's World Rally Championship commission and as president of the FIA Women in Motorsport Commission from 2009. She was inducted into the FIA Hall of Fame in 2019. Her duels with Röhrl and the Audi Quattro era remain landmarks in rallying history.
