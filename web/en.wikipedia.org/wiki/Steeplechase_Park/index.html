Steeplechase Park

LocationConey Island, Brooklyn, New York
Opened1897
Closed1964
OwnerGeorge C. Tilyou

Steeplechase Park was an amusement park in the Coney Island area of Brooklyn, New York, created by George C. Tilyou which operated from 1897 to 1964. It was the first of the three large Coney Island parks (the others being Luna Park and Dreamland) and took its name from its signature attraction, the Steeplechase, a mechanical horse race ride.

Features

The park was famous for its "Funny Face" logo — a grinning face with up to 44 teeth — and for attractions such as the Pavilion of Fun, an enormous enclosed structure housing rides and comic obstacles including the Human Roulette Wheel, the Dew Drop, and the Blowhole Theater, where clowns and air jets humiliated exiting riders to the amusement of onlookers. A 1907 fire destroyed much of the park, but Tilyou rebuilt it.

Decline and legacy

Attendance declined after the Second World War amid changing tastes and the area's deterioration. The park closed in 1964 and was sold to developer Fred Trump, who demolished it amid controversy. The site is now occupied by a minor-league baseball stadium (Maimonides Park), and the park's Parachute Jump tower, built for the 1939 World's Fair and later moved to the site, survives as a landmark nearby.
