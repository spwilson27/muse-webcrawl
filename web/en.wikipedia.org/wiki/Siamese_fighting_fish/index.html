Siamese fighting fish

Scientific nameBetta splendens
OriginSoutheast Asia

The Siamese fighting fish (Betta splendens), commonly known as the betta, is a freshwater fish native to the shallow waters and rice paddies of Thailand (formerly Siam), Cambodia, Laos, and Vietnam. Males are famously territorial and will fight rivals to the death, a trait exploited in staged matches in 19th-century Siam, where the fish was domesticated.

Biology

Bettas are labyrinth fish, able to breathe air at the surface. Males build bubble nests and care for the eggs. Selective breeding has produced vivid colours and elaborate finnage — halfmoon, crowntail, and veil tail forms — far beyond the drab green-brown wild type.

In aquaria

One of the world's most popular aquarium fish, the betta requires warm water (around 24–28 °C) and should not be housed with other males.
