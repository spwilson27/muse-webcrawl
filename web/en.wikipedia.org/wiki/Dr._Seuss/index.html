Theodor Seuss Geisel (2 March 1904 – 24 September 1991), known by his pen name Dr. Seuss, was an American children's author and cartoonist. He is known for his work writing and illustrating more than 60 books under the pen name Dr. Seuss, characterised by imaginative characters, rhyming verse — especially anapestic metre — and frequent use of invented words. His books have sold over 600 million copies and been translated into more than 20 languages.

Life and career

Geisel was born in Springfield, Massachusetts, studied at Dartmouth College and Oxford University, and worked in advertising and as a political cartoonist before publishing his first children's book, And to Think That I Saw It on Mulberry Street (1937). During the Second World War he made political cartoons and worked on Army training films. In 1957 he published The Cat in the Hat, written using a restricted vocabulary of 236 words as a response to debates about children's literacy; it revolutionised beginning reader books. Green Eggs and Ham (1960), written on a bet that he could not write a book using only 50 distinct words, became his best-selling title.

Themes and legacy

Many of his stories carry social and political messages: The Sneetches addresses racial discrimination, The Lorax environmental destruction, Yertle the Turtle authoritarianism, and Horton Hears a Who! the dignity of the marginalised ("a person's a person, no matter how small"). Geisel received a special Pulitzer Prize citation in 1984 for his contribution to children's literature. The main library at the University of California, San Diego, is named Geisel Library in his honour.
