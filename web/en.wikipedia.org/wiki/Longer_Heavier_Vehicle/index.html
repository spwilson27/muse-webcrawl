A Longer Heavier Vehicle (LHV) is a large goods vehicle combination that exceeds the standard legal limits for length and weight that apply to ordinary heavy goods vehicles (HGVs) in a given jurisdiction. In the United Kingdom the term is generally applied to articulated lorries or road trains longer than the standard 16.5 metres and heavier than 44 tonnes, such as combinations up to 25.25 metres long and 60 tonnes gross weight.

Rationale

Supporters argue that LHVs can carry the load of two standard lorries in a single vehicle, reducing the number of journeys, fuel consumption, and carbon dioxide emissions per tonne of freight moved. Studies in countries that permit them, and in trials, have suggested significant efficiency savings where loads are volume-limited rather than weight-limited.

Use internationally

Longer and heavier combinations are routine in several countries. In the Netherlands, combinations up to 25.25 m and 60 tonnes (known as LZVs or "ecocombis") have been permitted following successful trials. Sweden and Finland permit even longer combinations, historically up to 25.25 m and more recently beyond 30 m in Finland, reflecting their timber industries and sparse road networks. Australia uses road trains far exceeding these dimensions in remote areas.

United Kingdom

In the UK, a government-commissioned study in 2008 declined to authorise general LHV use, citing concerns about safety, road wear, and encouraging freight onto roads rather than rail. However, a long-running trial of "longer semi-trailers" (up to 18.75 m overall) began in 2012, and these trailers were subsequently permitted generally. In 2021–2023 the Department for Transport consulted on and then authorised a trial of LHVs up to 25.25 m and 60 tonnes on designated routes, with strict conditions.

Criticism

Critics, including rail freight groups and road safety campaigners, argue that LHVs increase the severity of collisions, cause disproportionate damage to road surfaces and structures such as bridges, and undermine rail freight. Supporters counter that the vehicles would be restricted to major roads and subject to additional safety requirements.
