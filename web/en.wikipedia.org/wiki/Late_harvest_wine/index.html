Late harvest wine

Late harvest wine is wine made from grapes left on the vine longer than usual. Late harvest is usually an indication of a sweet dessert wine, although wines range from dry to very sweet. The longer the grapes hang on the vine, the more sugar they accumulate through ripening and, in favourable conditions, through dehydration; many of the world's great sweet wines — German Spätlese and Auslese, French Sauternes, Hungarian Tokaji and Canadian ice wines — depend on late harvesting, often combined with the effects of noble rot (Botrytis cinerea).

Effects of late harvesting

Leaving grapes on the vine after normal ripeness increases sugar concentration in two ways: continued photosynthesis and import of sugars into the berry, and loss of water, which concentrates everything in the must. Risks grow with time: autumn rain can split berries and encourage grey rot and sour rot; birds and wasps attack the sweet fruit; and in warm regions acidity falls, producing flabby wines. Successful late-harvest sites combine a long, dry autumn with morning mists that encourage noble rot followed by sunny afternoons that dry the grapes.

Noble rot

Under the right alternating conditions, Botrytis cinerea infects ripe grapes as "noble rot": the fungus perforates the skins, water evaporates, and sugars, acids and flavour compounds are concentrated while the fungus adds its own honeyed, apricot-like metabolites and glycerol. The resulting shrivelled berries yield tiny quantities of intensely sweet must. Sauternes, Barsac, Tokaji Aszú and the German Beerenauslese/Trockenbeerenauslese categories all depend on this phenomenon.

Regional styles

  • Germany and Austria: the Prädikat system grades wines by must weight at harvest — Spätlese ("late harvest") and Auslese ("selected harvest") may be dry or sweet; Beerenauslese and Trockenbeerenauslese are always sweet botrytis wines.
  • Alsace: vendange tardive wines are made chiefly from Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot gris and Muscat.
  • Bordeaux: Sauternes and Barsac blend botrytised Sémillon, Sauvignon blanc and Muscadelle.
  • Hungary: Tokaji Aszú is made by adding botrytised berries (aszú) to a base must or wine.
  • New World: late-harvest Rieslings and ice wines (grapes frozen on the vine) are specialties of Canada, the Finger Lakes, Michigan and Australia.
