Business casual

Business casual is a loosely defined Western dress code that emerged in white-collar workplaces in the late 20th century, blending elements of traditional business wear with a more relaxed style. It typically excludes the full suit and necktie required by business formal attire while also excluding casual clothing such as jeans, shorts, and athletic shoes — though the exact boundaries vary widely between organizations and countries.

Typical garments

For men, business casual often consists of chinos or dress slacks, a button-down or polo shirt, optional blazer or sweater, and leather shoes such as loafers. For women, it may include tailored trousers or skirts, blouses, knit tops, cardigans, and closed-toe shoes. Ties and suit jackets are optional rather than expected.

History

The style spread from the technology and creative industries of the American West Coast and from the Hawaiian custom of "Aloha Friday", in which employees wore Hawaiian shirts on Fridays. In 1992 the clothing company Dockers launched a marketing campaign promoting khakis for the office and supplied employers with a "Guide to Casual Business Wear", greatly accelerating adoption. The dot-com era of the 1990s entrenched relaxed dress in many industries, though finance and law retained more formal standards, and some firms later tightened their policies.

Ambiguity

Because no universal standard exists, employers frequently publish detailed guidelines; surveys commonly find employees uncertain about what qualifies. The term "smart casual" is used in British English for a similar, slightly more polished code.
