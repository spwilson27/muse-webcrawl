Dezful (Persian: دزفول) is a city in Khuzestan province in southwestern Iran, situated on the banks of the Dez River near the foothills of the Zagros Mountains. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the region, long known for its distinctive brick architecture, its historic bridge, and its watermills.

History

The city's name is associated with a fortress (diz) guarding a bridge (pol) over the Dez. A bridge at the site is traditionally dated to the Sasanian era, built under Shapur I in the 3rd century CE, in part by Roman prisoners of war captured at the Battle of Edessa; sections of the present old bridge are among the oldest still in use in the world. Dezful was an important centre of the Khuzestan plain under successive Persian, Arab and Iranian dynasties, and was known for sugarcane, textiles and its underground houses and qanat-fed ventilation adapted to the extreme summer heat.

Modern city

Dezful suffered heavily in the Iran–Iraq War, when it was repeatedly struck by Iraqi missiles and became known in Iran as a symbol of resistance. Today it is an agricultural and trading centre for northern Khuzestan; the Dez Dam lies upstream. The city is served by Dezful Airport and is noted for its traditional brickwork, historic houses and citrus orchards.
