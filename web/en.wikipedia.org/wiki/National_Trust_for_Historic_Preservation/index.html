National Trust for Historic Preservation

Founded: 1949
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Type: Non-profit membership organisation
Purpose: Historic preservation in the United States

The National Trust for Historic Preservation is a privately funded non-profit organisation based in Washington, D.C., that works to save America's historic places. It was chartered by the U.S. Congress in 1949, inspired in part by Britain's National Trust.

Work

The Trust protects historic sites through advocacy, education, grants and direct stewardship. It owns or operates a portfolio of historic house museums and sites across the United States, maintains the National Trust Historic Sites collection, and supports local preservation through state and local partner organisations. Its best-known campaign is the annual list of America's 11 Most Endangered Historic Places, published since 1988 to raise awareness of threatened landmarks.

The Trust also helped establish the modern preservation framework in the United States, supporting the National Historic Preservation Act of 1966 and federal historic tax credits. It publishes the magazine Preservation and confers the National Preservation Awards. The organisation is distinct from the identically named British National Trust and from the National Trust for Scotland.
