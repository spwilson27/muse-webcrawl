Ögedei Khan

Ögedei Khan (c. 1186 – 11 December 1241) was the third son of Genghis Khan and the second khagan (supreme khan) of the Mongol Empire, reigning from 1229 until his death. Chosen as successor by his father, he consolidated the empire's administration and continued its expansion into China, Persia, and Eastern Europe.

Reign

Ögedei founded the imperial capital at Karakorum, established the Yam relay-post system across the empire, reformed taxation with the help of the Khitan adviser Yelü Chucai, and codified imperial decrees. Under his rule the Mongols completed the conquest of the Jin dynasty in northern China (1234), invaded Korea, and launched the great western campaign of 1236–1242 under Batu and Subutai that subjugated the Rus' principalities and reached Hungary and Poland.

Death and succession

Ögedei, known for heavy drinking despite his own attempts at moderation, died in December 1241. His death forced the Mongol armies to withdraw from Europe for the succession, and after a regency by his widow Töregene, his son Güyük was elected khagan in 1246. Ögedei's descendants later formed the Ögedeid branch, whose most famous figure, Kaidu, long contested the rule of Kublai Khan.
