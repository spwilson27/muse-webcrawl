Vero Beach, Florida

Vero Beach is a city in and the county seat of Indian River County, Florida, United States, on the Atlantic "Treasure Coast". Its population was about 16,000 at the 2020 census. Known for its beaches, citrus industry and quiet upscale character, it is nicknamed the "Hibiscus City".

History

The area was settled in the late 19th century; the town of Vero was incorporated in 1919 and renamed Vero Beach in 1925. The region gained archaeological fame from the "Vero Man" fossil finds near the Old Vero Site. The Brooklyn/Los Angeles Dodgers held spring training at "Dodgertown" in Vero Beach from 1948 to 2008.

Geography and attractions

The city straddles the Indian River Lagoon, with a barrier-island beachside across the Intracoastal Waterway. Attractions include McKee Botanical Garden, the Vero Beach Museum of Art and nearby Pelican Island National Wildlife Refuge, the first national wildlife refuge in the United States (1903). The surrounding county is a major producer of grapefruit and other Indian River citrus.
