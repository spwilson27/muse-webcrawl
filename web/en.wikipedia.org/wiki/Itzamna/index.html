Itzamna

Itzamna (Yucatec Maya: Itzamná) is one of the most important deities of the Maya pantheon, especially in the Postclassic Yucatán. A creator god and lord of the heavens, he was credited with inventing writing, the calendar, and books, and was regarded as a patron of learning, medicine, and priests.

Characteristics

In the codices Itzamna appears as "God D", an aged, toothless deity with a hooked or beak-like nose and sunken cheeks, sometimes shown seated on a celestial throne band. His name has been interpreted as containing itz, a term associated with wisdom, sorcery, and precious substances such as dew or resin. Maya kings on occasion identified themselves with Itzamna or bore his name, as in the Palenque king K'inich Kan Bahlam II's dedications at the Cross Group.

Role and associations

Itzamna was said in colonial sources to have been the first priest and the inventor of writing and calendrical knowledge, taught to humankind in mythic time. He could be identified with the sun god Kinich Ahau and appears as a medicine god consulted in curing rituals recorded in the Ritual of the Bacabs. At Izamal, a major pilgrimage shrine was dedicated to him under the aspect Itzamna Kauil, famed for healing. He is sometimes paired with the moon and childbirth goddess Ixchel.
