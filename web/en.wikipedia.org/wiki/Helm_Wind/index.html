Helm Wind

Helm Wind
TypeLocal katabatic / foehn-like wind
RegionCross Fell, Cumbria, England
DirectionFrom the north-east

The Helm Wind is a strong, cold north-easterly wind which blows down the south-western slope of the Cross Fell range in the Pennines of Cumbria, England. It is the only wind in the British Isles to have its own name, and is a rare example of a wind comparable to the foehn phenomena found in other mountainous regions.

Characteristics

When the Helm Wind blows, a distinctive stationary arc of cloud, known as the "Helm Bar," forms some distance to the leeward (south-west) of the escarpment, while a dense cap cloud — the "helm" — sits over the summit of Cross Fell. The wind can blow for days at a time, roaring down the Eden Valley and felling trees, and is traditionally said to drive local inhabitants to distraction. Villages such as Milburn and Knock at the foot of the fell are most affected.

Mechanism

The wind is produced when stable air is forced over the Pennine ridge and accelerates sharply down the steep lee slope, in a manner similar to the bora of the Adriatic. The Helm Bar is a standing-wave cloud formed in the lee of the ridge. The wind is most common in spring, when conditions favour its development.
