Emission (air pollution)

In the context of air pollution, an emission is the release of gases and particles into the atmosphere from a source such as a factory, a vehicle, or a natural event. Emissions are usually measured in mass per unit of time and are inventoried in emission inventories used for air quality management and climate policy.

Sources

Emission sources are commonly divided into stationary sources (power plants, industrial facilities, residential heating), mobile sources (cars, trucks, aircraft, ships), and area sources (agriculture, dust, wildfires). Natural emissions include volcanic gases, sea salt, pollen, and biogenic volatile organic compounds from forests.

Pollutants

The principal regulated air pollutants emitted by human activity include particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), nitrogen oxides (NOx), sulphur dioxide (SO2), carbon monoxide (CO), volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and ammonia. Greenhouse gas emissions — principally carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide — are tracked separately under climate frameworks such as the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement.

Measurement and control

Emissions are estimated from direct stack monitoring, emission factors applied to activity data, and remote sensing. Control technologies include scrubbers, catalytic converters, electrostatic precipitators, and low-emission fuels. Regulatory approaches range from emission standards for individual sources to cap-and-trade systems such as the European Union Emissions Trading System, which set a total ceiling on emissions and allow trading of allowances.
