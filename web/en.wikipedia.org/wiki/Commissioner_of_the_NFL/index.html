The commissioner of the NFL is the chief executive of the National Football League. The position was created in 1941, evolving from the earlier office of league president, and the commissioner is elected by, and accountable to, the owners of the league's franchises.

Powers

The commissioner oversees the league's business operations, negotiates collective bargaining and media agreements in concert with the owners, enforces league rules, and exercises disciplinary authority over players, coaches, and teams under the league constitution and bylaws.

Holders

Elmer Layden was the first commissioner (1941–1946), followed by Bert Bell, whose tenure saw the league's early television growth. Pete Rozelle (1960–1989) presided over the AFL–NFL merger, the creation of the Super Bowl, and football's rise to the top of American television. He was succeeded by Paul Tagliabue (1989–2006) and then Roger Goodell, who took office in 2006.
