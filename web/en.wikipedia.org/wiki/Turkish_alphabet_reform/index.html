Turkish alphabet reform

The Turkish alphabet reform of 1928 replaced the Ottoman Turkish alphabet, a version of the Perso-Arabic script, with a Latin-based Turkish alphabet of 29 letters. It was a central element of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk's reforms to modernise the Republic of Turkey.

Background

Ottoman Turkish in Arabic script poorly represented Turkish vowels — the script had essentially three vowel signs for Turkish's eight vowels — and literacy was estimated at only around 10 percent. Debate over script reform had continued since the 19th century.

Implementation

The Law on the Adoption and Implementation of the Turkish Alphabet was passed on 1 November 1928 and took effect on 1 January 1929. The new alphabet — a b c ç d e f g ğ h ı i j k l m n o ö p r s ş t u ü v y z — is highly phonemic. Atatürk personally toured the country teaching the letters, earning the title Başöğretmen (head teacher). "Nation's schools" were opened to teach adults, and literacy rose dramatically over the following decades.
