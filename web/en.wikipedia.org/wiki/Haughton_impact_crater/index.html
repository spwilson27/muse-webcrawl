Haughton impact crater

Haughton crater Location: Devon Island, Nunavut, Canada
Diameter: ~23 km (14 mi)
Age: ~39 million years (Eocene)
Coordinates: 75°23′ N, 89°40′ W

The Haughton impact crater is an impact crater on Devon Island in Nunavut, Canada, within the Canadian High Arctic. About 23 kilometres (14 mi) in diameter and some 39 million years old, it is one of the northernmost known impact structures on Earth and, because of the polar desert environment, one of the best preserved.

Haughton crater seen from orbit, in the polar desert of Devon Island.

Geology

The impactor struck sedimentary rocks overlying Precambrian basement, creating a structure filled with impact breccia and a central uplift. The cold, dry, sparsely vegetated terrain has slowed erosion dramatically. At the time of the impact the region was a temperate forest, and fossil remains preserved in the post-impact lake deposits — including an early seal ancestor, Puijila darwini — have provided insights into Eocene Arctic ecosystems.

Mars analogue research

Since 1997 the site has hosted the Haughton–Mars Project, an international programme using the crater and Devon Island's Mars-like environment — including the low-lying Haughton River valley — to test technologies and operational methods for the human exploration of Mars and the Moon. The Flashline Mars Arctic Research Station stands near the crater rim.
