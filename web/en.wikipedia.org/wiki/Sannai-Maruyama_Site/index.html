Sannai-Maruyama Site

Sannai-Maruyama Site
Location: Aomori, Aomori Prefecture, Japan
Period: Early–Middle Jōmon, c. 3900–2200 BC
Status: Special National Historic Site; UNESCO World Heritage (2021)

The Sannai-Maruyama Site (三内丸山遺跡) is an archaeological site in the city of Aomori, Japan, containing the remains of one of the largest and best-preserved Jōmon period settlements in the country. It was occupied for roughly 1,700 years, from about 3900 to 2200 BC.

Discovery

The site was discovered in 1992 during surveys for a planned baseball stadium. Excavations revealed such extensive remains that the stadium project was cancelled and the site preserved. In 2000 it was designated a Special National Historic Site, and in 2021 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the Jōmon Prehistoric Sites in Northern Japan.

Remains

Excavations uncovered hundreds of pit dwellings, adult and child burial pits, storage pits, longhouses, and roads. Its most famous feature is a large six-pillar building reconstructed from massive chestnut posts, thought to have served as a watchtower, monument, or shrine. Artefacts include clay figurines (dogū), lacquerware, jade beads, and pottery, as well as abundant remains of chestnuts, fish, and deer, indicating a large, sedentary community sustained by managed woodland resources rather than the small mobile bands once assumed for Jōmon hunter-gatherers.
