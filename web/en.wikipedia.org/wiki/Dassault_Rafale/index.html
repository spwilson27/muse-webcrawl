Dassault Rafale

The Dassault Rafale is a French twin-engine, canard delta-wing, multirole fighter aircraft designed and built by Dassault Aviation. Intended to perform air supremacy, interdiction, aerial reconnaissance, ground support, in-depth strike, anti-ship strike and nuclear deterrence, Dassault calls it an "omnirole" fighter.

Rafale
Manufacturer: Dassault Aviation
First flight: 4 July 1986 (demonstrator)
Introduction: 18 May 2001
Status: In service
Primary users: French Air and Space Force, French Navy
Engines: 2 × Snecma M88
Max speed: Mach 1.8

Development

France withdrew from the joint European fighter project that became the Eurofighter Typhoon over carrier and weight requirements, pursuing its own ACX programme. The Rafale A demonstrator flew in 1986; production variants are the single-seat Rafale C, two-seat B, and carrier-based M operating from Charles de Gaulle.

Design

The Rafale features a delta wing with close-coupled canards, fly-by-wire controls, the Thales RBE2-AA active electronically scanned array radar, SPECTRA electronic warfare suite, and OSF infrared search and track. Armament includes the MICA and Meteor air-to-air missiles, SCALP cruise missiles, Exocet anti-ship missiles, guided bombs and the ASMP-A nuclear missile.

Operational history and exports

French Rafales have flown combat in Afghanistan, Libya (2011), Mali, Iraq and Syria. After slow early export sales, orders came from Egypt, Qatar, India (36 aircraft plus naval Rafale M selection), Greece, Croatia, the UAE (80 aircraft, its largest export order), Indonesia and Serbia.
