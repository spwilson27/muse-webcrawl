Mirabilite is a hydrous sodium sulfate mineral with the chemical formula Na2SO4·10H2O. It is the hydrated form of thenardite and is better known in impure form as Glauber's salt, named after the Dutch-German chemist Johann Rudolf Glauber, who described its preparation in the 17th century.

Occurrence and properties

Mirabilite forms in evaporite deposits around saline lakes, playas, and hot springs, and as efflorescences in caves and mines. Large deposits occur around the Great Salt Lake in Utah, in Saskatchewan, and at Lake Karabogaz in Turkmenistan. The mineral is soft, colourless to white, and readily dehydrates in dry air to powdery thenardite; it melts in its own water of crystallisation at about 32 °C. Its seasonal dissolution and recrystallisation in porous stone also makes sodium sulfate one of the most destructive salts in the weathering of buildings and artwork.

Uses

Sodium sulfate is used in detergents, in the Kraft process for paper pulp, and in glass manufacture. Glauber's salt was historically valued as a purgative in medicine.
