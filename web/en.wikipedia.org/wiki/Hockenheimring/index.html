Hockenheimring

The Hockenheimring Baden-Württemberg is a motor racing circuit situated in the Rhine valley near the town of Hockenheim in Baden-Württemberg, Germany, located on the Bertha Benz Memorial Route. Amongst other motor racing events, it has hosted the German Grand Prix, most recently in 2019.

History

The Hockenheimring was built in 1932 by assistant timekeeper Ernst Christ using funds loaned by the German government. It was originally called "Dreieckskurs" (triangle course). The man-made track was used for motorcycle racing and was expanded to be used as test track for Mercedes-Benz and Auto Union in 1936.

Originally almost 8 km (5.0 mi) long, the track was used for German Grand Prix racing from 1970 to 1976, and again from 1977 to 2006, intermittently thereafter. Large portions of the track cut through a forest, including an extremely long, straight section going through the forest, then a twisty stadium section (so called because of the stands located there) at Hockenheim, back in view of the main stands. This long version was shortened in 2002 by a redesign credited to Hermann Tilke.

Layout

The circuit is almost completely flat. It has a capacity of 120,000 spectators. The modern circuit is 4.574 km (2.842 mi) long with 17 turns.
