Toponymy

Toponymy (from Greek tópos, "place", and ónoma, "name") is the study of place names — their origin, meaning, pronunciation and classification. A specialist is a toponymist, and an individual place name is a toponym. Toponymy is a branch of onomastics and draws on linguistics, history, geography and archaeology.

Types and themes

Names may be descriptive of landscape (e.g., Oxford, a ford for oxen), commemorative of people (Washington), derived from former languages of an area (Celtic river names across Europe), or transferred from elsewhere (New York). Categories include hydronyms (water), oronyms (relief), and hodonyms (streets). Generic elements — suffixes such as English -ham, -ton, -by, Scandinavian -stad, Slavic -grad — reveal settlement patterns and language history.

Applications

Place-name evidence reconstructs past environments, migration and land use where documents are lacking. Official standardisation is handled by bodies such as the UN Group of Experts on Geographical Names and national names boards; disputes over exonyms and renaming (e.g., decolonisation renamings) make toponymy politically sensitive.
