Tlacopan

Tlacopan
Region: Valley of Mexico
Period: Postclassic Mesoamerica
Status: Member of the Aztec Triple Alliance

Tlacopan (Classical Nahuatl: Tlacōpan, "on the plant of flint") was a pre-Columbian Tepanec altepetl (city-state) on the western shore of Lake Texcoco in the Valley of Mexico. Founded in the 14th century, it was conquered by the Mexica-led alliance and in 1428 became, with Tenochtitlan and Texcoco, one of the three members of the Aztec Triple Alliance.

History

Originally subject to the Tepanec empire of Azcapotzalco, Tlacopan was subdued during the Tepanec War. As the junior partner of the Triple Alliance, it received one-fifth of the tribute collected by the alliance, while Tenochtitlan and Texcoco each received two-fifths. A causeway from Tlacopan crossed Lake Texcoco to Tenochtitlan; along this route Hernán Cortés's forces suffered heavy losses during the Noche Triste in 1520.

Legacy

After the Spanish conquest, Tlacopan declined in importance. The modern Mexico City borough of Tacuba occupies roughly the site of the ancient city.
