Macy's (originally R. H. Macy & Co.) is an American department store chain founded in 1858 by Rowland Hussey Macy. As of recent years it operates several hundred stores across the United States, and is the largest department store chain in the country by retail sales. Its flagship store at Herald Square in Manhattan is one of the largest department stores in the world.

History

Macy opened his dry goods store on Sixth Avenue in New York in 1858 after a series of failed retail ventures in Massachusetts. The store pioneered practices such as fixed one-price selling, money-back guarantees, and newspaper advertising. Later owners Isidor and Nathan Straus expanded the business, moving it to Herald Square in 1902. Macy's became the flagship nameplate of the Federated Department Stores group, which absorbed regional chains nationwide and renamed itself Macy's, Inc. in 2007; the company also owns Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury.

Cultural presence

Macy's is famous for the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, held in New York since 1924 and renowned for its giant character balloons, and for the annual Fourth of July fireworks display. Its Herald Square store is the setting of the 1947 film Miracle on 34th Street. The red star logo derives from a tattoo Macy acquired as a young whaler.
