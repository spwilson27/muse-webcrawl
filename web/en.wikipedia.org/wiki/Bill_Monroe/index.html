William Smith Monroe (1911–1996) was an American mandolinist, singer and songwriter who created the bluegrass music style, and is known as the "Father of Bluegrass".

Career

From Rosine, Kentucky, Monroe first performed with his brothers as the Monroe Brothers. In 1939 he formed the Blue Grass Boys — named for Kentucky, the Bluegrass State — joining the Grand Ole Opry. With the classic 1945–48 lineup of Lester Flatt, Earl Scruggs, Chubby Wise and Cedric Rainwater, he forged the high, fast, virtuosic string-band sound that defined bluegrass: mandolin, banjo played in Scruggs's three-finger style, fiddle, guitar and bass, with "high lonesome" harmony singing.

Legacy

Songs such as "Blue Moon of Kentucky" (later covered by Elvis Presley) and "Uncle Pen" became standards. Monroe was inducted into the Country Music, Bluegrass and Rock and Roll halls of fame — the latter honouring bluegrass's influence on early rock and roll.
