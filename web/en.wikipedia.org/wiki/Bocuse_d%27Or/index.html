The Bocuse d'Or is a biennial world championship of cooking, widely regarded as the most prestigious culinary competition in the world. It was founded by French chef Paul Bocuse and first held in Lyon in 1987.

Format

Twenty-four national teams — each a chef, a commis and a coach — qualify through continental selections and compete in the final at the Sirha trade fair in Lyon. In about five and a half hours, working in open kitchens before a cheering crowd, teams prepare a meat dish and a fish (later, a themed platter and plate) presentation judged on technique, creativity and presentation.

History and winners

France, Norway and Denmark have been among the most successful nations. Winners cook their way through national and continental rounds over two years; victory historically transforms a chef's career and brings major national media attention.
