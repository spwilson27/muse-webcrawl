Atlantic horse mackerel

The Atlantic horse mackerel (Trachurus trachurus), also known as the European horse mackerel or common scad, is a species of jack mackerel in the family Carangidae, which includes the jacks, pompanos and trevallies. It is found in the eastern Atlantic Ocean off Europe and Africa and into the south-eastern Indian Ocean. It is an important species in commercial fisheries and is listed as a Vulnerable species on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species.

Description

The Atlantic horse mackerel has a fairly deep, compressed, silvery body with a large head and a strongly forked tail. A distinctive feature is the row of bony scutes along the lateral line. The upper parts are greenish-blue to grey, while the flanks and belly are silvery white. It typically reaches 40–60 cm in length, although larger specimens occur.

Distribution and habitat

This species occurs in the north-eastern Atlantic from Iceland and Norway south along the European and African coasts to Senegal and Cape Verde, throughout the Mediterranean Sea and the Black Sea, and there are also records from the south-eastern Indian Ocean off South Africa. It is a pelagic, schooling fish found over the continental shelf, usually at depths between 100 and 200 metres, though it may occur much deeper.

Biology

Atlantic horse mackerel form large schools, sometimes mixed with herring and other mackerels. They feed on small crustaceans, squid and small fish. Spawning takes place in the warmer months, with eggs and larvae drifting in the plankton. The fish undertake seasonal migrations, moving northwards and inshore in summer.

Fisheries

The Atlantic horse mackerel is caught in large quantities by trawlers and purse seiners, principally for human consumption and for fish meal. Major fisheries operate off north-west Africa, in the North Sea and off Iberia. Heavy exploitation has caused concern about declining stocks, and the species is assessed as Vulnerable by the IUCN.
