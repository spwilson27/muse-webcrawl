Sialolithiasis

Sialolithiasis is a condition in which a calcified mass, called a sialolith or salivary stone, forms within a salivary gland or its duct, obstructing the flow of saliva. It most commonly affects the submandibular gland, whose long, upward-curving duct and alkaline, mucin-rich saliva favour stone formation.

Symptoms and diagnosis

Typical symptoms are pain and swelling of the affected gland at mealtimes, when saliva production increases against the blockage. Stones may be felt on palpation and are confirmed by ultrasound, CT, or sialography.

Treatment

Small stones may pass spontaneously with hydration, gland massage, and sialogogues such as sour candies. Larger stones are removed by sialendoscopy, lithotripsy, or surgical excision of the stone or, rarely, the gland.
