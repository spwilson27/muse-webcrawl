Hugo Laurenz August Hofmann von Hofmannsthal (1 February 1874 – 15 July 1929) was an Austrian novelist, librettist, poet, dramatist, narrator, and essayist, and a leading figure of German-language literary modernism.

Life and work

A child prodigy, Hofmannsthal published exquisite lyric poetry under the pseudonym Loris while still a schoolboy, becoming a central member of the Young Vienna circle around Arthur Schnitzler and Stefan George. In 1902 he published the fictional "Letter of Lord Chandos," a landmark of modernism describing a crisis of language: the writer's loss of faith in words' ability to express experience.

His dramatic works include Elektra (1903), Jedermann (Everyman, 1911), performed annually at the Salzburg Festival—which Hofmannsthal co-founded in 1920 with Max Reinhardt and Richard Strauss—and the comedy Der Schwierige (1921).

Collaboration with Strauss

Hofmannsthal is celebrated as the librettist of Richard Strauss: Elektra (1909), Der Rosenkavalier (1911), Ariadne auf Naxos (1912/1916), Die Frau ohne Schatten (1919), Die ägyptische Helena (1928), and Arabella, premiered posthumously in 1933. Their correspondence is one of the great documents of operatic collaboration. Hofmannsthal died of a stroke in 1929, hours after the suicide of his son Franz.
