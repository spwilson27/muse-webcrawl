Motion capture

Motion capture (sometimes abbreviated mo-cap or mocap) is the process of recording the movement of objects or people so that it can be mapped onto digital models in computer animation. It is used in filmmaking, video game development, sports science, medicine, robotics and military training. When the recording includes an actor's face and fingers as well as body movement, it is often called performance capture.

Methods

In an optical motion capture system, the performer wears a suit fitted with markers — either reflective (passive) or light-emitting (active) — while an array of cameras around a calibrated capture volume tracks the markers' positions in three dimensions by triangulation. Software then reconstructs a moving skeleton from the marker data. Inertial systems instead use small sensors (accelerometers and gyroscopes) worn on the body, requiring no cameras and allowing capture over large areas, though they can drift over time. Markerless systems use computer vision and machine learning to estimate body pose directly from ordinary video.

Applications in entertainment

Motion capture underlies many of the most prominent digital characters in modern cinema, including Gollum in The Lord of the Rings films (performed by Andy Serkis), the Na'vi of Avatar, and Thanos in the Marvel Cinematic Universe. In video games it supplies realistic character animation, and it is also used in virtual production, where captured performances drive digital characters in real time on LED-volume stages. Earlier predecessors include rotoscoping, in which animators traced over live-action film.

Other applications

In biomechanics and clinical gait analysis, motion capture quantifies human movement for diagnosing disorders and designing rehabilitation. Sports teams use it to analyse technique and reduce injury risk. Engineers use it to test the ergonomics of products and workplaces, and robotics researchers use recorded human motion to teach robots movement by demonstration.
