Corpus Christi College (full name: The President and Scholars of the College of Corpus Christi in the University of Oxford) is one of the constituent colleges of the University of Oxford in England. Founded in 1517 by Richard Foxe, Bishop of Winchester, it is among the smaller Oxford colleges, and the 12th oldest surviving college in the university.

Foxe intended the college for the study of the humanities and sciences, and it was notably forward-looking for its time: its founding statutes provided for lectures in Greek and Latin literature, and its library — based on books donated by Foxe and the humanist circle around Erasmus — was one of the most advanced in early Tudor England. The college's first president, John Claymond, was a correspondent of Erasmus.

The college

The main quadrangle, built at the college's foundation, is famous for its central pillar sundial, the Pelican Sundial, erected in 1581 and topped by the college's pelican emblem. Corpus's symbol is the pelican in her piety, drawn from the college arms. The college occupies a site between Merton Street and Christ Church Meadow, adjacent to Merton College and Oriel College.

Despite its small size, Corpus has a strong academic reputation, particularly in classics, and has repeatedly performed near the top of Oxford's Norrington Table of undergraduate examination results. Notable alumni include the philosopher Isaiah Berlin, the writer John Fowles, and the former Labour Party leader Ed Miliband, as well as the art historian and spy Anthony Blunt, who studied at the college briefly.
