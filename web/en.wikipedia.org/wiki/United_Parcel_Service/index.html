United Parcel Service

United Parcel Service, Inc. (UPS) is an American multinational shipping and supply chain management company headquartered in Sandy Springs, Georgia. Founded in 1907 in Seattle as the American Messenger Company, it is one of the world's largest package delivery companies, delivering more than 20 million packages and documents per day to over 200 countries and territories, and is readily identified by its brown delivery vehicles and uniforms ("What can Brown do for you?").

History

Teenager Jim Casey borrowed $100 to start a messenger and delivery service in Seattle; the company adopted the name United Parcel Service in 1919 as it expanded into Oakland and adopted its signature brown colour. It built a consolidated system delivering packages for department stores, then, after mid-century changes in retail, shifted to common-carrier service for the general public—fighting long legal battles to operate nationwide. UPS Airlines, founded in 1988, operates one of the world's largest cargo fleets out of its "Worldport" hub in Louisville, Kentucky. UPS held one of the largest IPOs of its era in 1999.

Operations

Business segments include U.S. domestic package delivery, international package delivery, and supply chain and freight services. Its workforce is heavily unionised through the International Brotherhood of Teamsters; the 1997 strike and the 2023 contract negotiations drew national attention. Competitors include FedEx, DHL, and Amazon Logistics.
