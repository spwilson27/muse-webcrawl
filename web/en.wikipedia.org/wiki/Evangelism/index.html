Evangelism

Evangelism, or witnessing, is the act of sharing the Christian gospel — the good news of Jesus Christ — with the intention of converting others to Christianity. The term derives from the Koine Greek euangelion, meaning good news.

Methods

Evangelism takes forms including personal conversation, preaching, tracts and literature distribution, mass-media and televangelism, revival meetings and crusades such as those of Billy Graham, and missionary work abroad. See Great Commission and Missionary.

History

Rooted in the ministry of Jesus and the apostles, notably Paul, evangelism expanded through monastic missions in the Middle Ages, the Protestant Reformation and the Great Awakenings of the 18th and 19th centuries, and the modern missionary movement.

Related concepts

Evangelism is central to Evangelicalism and Pentecostalism, while other traditions emphasize different balances of proclamation, service, and dialogue. Proselytism is a related but sometimes pejoratively used term.
