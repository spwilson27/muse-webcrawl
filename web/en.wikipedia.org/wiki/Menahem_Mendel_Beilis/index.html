Menahem Mendel Beilis (1874 – July 7, 1934) was a Ukrainian Jew who was accused of ritual murder in a notorious 1913 trial in Kiev in the Russian Empire. The case, widely compared to the Dreyfus affair, became an international cause célèbre.

The trial

Beilis, a superintendent at a brick factory, was charged with murdering a 13-year-old Christian boy, Andrei Yushchinsky, whose blood was alleged to have been used to bake matzah — a revival of the medieval blood libel. Despite a case promoted by right-wing and antisemitic organisations with tsarist official support, the evidence collapsed at trial and an all-Christian peasant jury acquitted Beilis in November 1913 after a trial that drew worldwide attention.

Later life

Beilis emigrated first to Palestine and then to the United States, where he published his memoir, The Story of My Sufferings. He died in Saratoga Springs, New York, in 1934. The affair inspired Bernard Malamud's 1966 novel The Fixer.
