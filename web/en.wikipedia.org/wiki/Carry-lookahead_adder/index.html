Carry-lookahead adder

A carry-lookahead adder computes carries in parallel rather than waiting for a ripple.

A carry-lookahead adder (CLA) is a digital adder that speeds up binary addition by computing carry signals for groups of bits in parallel. Unlike a ripple-carry adder, where each full adder waits for the carry from the previous position, the CLA uses generate and propagate signals to predict carries with a shallower logic network.

Generate and propagate

For input bits Ai and Bi, a bit generates a carry when both are 1: Gi = Ai Bi. It propagates an incoming carry when at least one input is 1: Pi = Ai + Bi (or, in some formulations, XOR). The carry into the next position is Ci+1 = Gi + PiCi. Expanding this recurrence lets hardware compute all carries from the inputs and the initial carry.

Architecture

Because fan-in grows rapidly, wide CLAs are usually hierarchical: small blocks produce block generate and block propagate signals, and a lookahead unit combines them. This structure trades additional gates and wiring for much lower delay than a long ripple chain.

Comparison

Carry-lookahead adders are faster than ripple-carry adders for medium and wide words, but more complex. Related designs include carry-select adders, carry-save adders, and parallel-prefix adders such as Kogge–Stone and Brent–Kung, which generalize the carry computation.
