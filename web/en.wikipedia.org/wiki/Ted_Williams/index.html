Ted Williams

Theodore Samuel Williams (August 30, 1918 – July 5, 2002) was an American professional baseball player and manager. He played his entire 19-year Major League Baseball career, primarily as a left fielder, for the Boston Red Sox from 1939 to 1960; his playing career was interrupted by military service during World War II and the Korean War.

Career highlights

Nicknamed "Teddy Ballgame," "the Kid," and "the Splendid Splinter," Williams is regarded as one of the greatest hitters of all time. In 1941 he batted .406, the last player to hit .400 in a season. He won the American League batting title six times, hit 521 career home runs, and finished with a .344 lifetime average and a record .482 on-base percentage. He won the Triple Crown twice and was a 19-time All-Star and two-time MVP.

Military service and later life

Williams served as a naval aviator in World War II and flew 39 combat missions in the Korean War as a Marine Corps pilot. He was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1966. Later in life he was famous as a fisherman and hosted television programs on the sport.
