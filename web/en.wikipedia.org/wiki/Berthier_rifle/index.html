The Berthier rifles and carbines were a family of French bolt-action military arms designed by André Berthier, a civilian engineer with the Algerian railways, and adopted by the French Army from the 1890s. Alongside the Lebel Model 1886, they armed French infantry through both world wars.

Design

Berthier's design used a Mannlicher-style magazine loaded with an en-bloc clip of three cartridges (later five), which gave faster reloading than the Lebel's tube magazine. The first models were carbines for cavalry, cuirassiers, artillery and gendarmes, followed by a short rifle for colonial troops. The full-length Model 1907 Sénégalais and its wartime successor, the Model 1907/15, were produced in enormous numbers during the First World War, including by Remington in the United States under French contract, to supplement inadequate Lebel production.

Service history

Berthier rifles served throughout the First World War and were progressively modernised: the Model 1916 introduced a five-round magazine with a hinged cover, and many rifles were rebuilt in the 1930s. They remained in widespread service in the Second World War with French, colonial and Free French forces, and later with police and colonial units into the postwar decades. Total production of all Berthier variants exceeded two million.
