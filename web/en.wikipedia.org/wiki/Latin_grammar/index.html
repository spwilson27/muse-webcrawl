Latin grammar

Latin grammar is the grammar of the Latin language. Latin is a highly inflected language, with nouns, pronouns and adjectives changing form (declining) for case, number and gender, and verbs conjugating for person, number, tense, mood and voice. Word order is flexible, since grammatical relations are marked by endings rather than position, though subject–object–verb is common.

Nouns

There are five declensions and six or seven cases: nominative, vocative, accusative, genitive, dative and ablative, with vestigial locative. Nouns belong to three genders: masculine, feminine and neuter.

Verbs

Verbs fall into four conjugations, with six tenses in the indicative (present, imperfect, future, perfect, pluperfect, future perfect), plus subjunctive and imperative moods, infinitives, participles, gerunds and supines. The perfect system uses a separate stem; passive forms in the perfect are periphrastic with the perfect passive participle and esse.

Syntax

Features include the ablative absolute construction, indirect statements with accusative and infinitive, sequence of tenses in subjunctive clauses, and extensive use of participial clauses. Learning Latin grammar traditionally anchors the study of grammar in European education.
