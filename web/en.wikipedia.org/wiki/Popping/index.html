Popping

Popping
OriginFresno, California, United States
EraLate 1970s
Related stylesLocking, boogaloo, waving, tutting

Popping is a street dance style based on the technique of quickly contracting and relaxing muscles to cause a jerk, or "pop", in the dancer's body, performed in rhythm to music. It originated in Fresno, California, in the late 1970s, and is one of the major styles of the Californian funk styles family, alongside locking. Popping is often confused with breakdancing, but it is a distinct style within hip-hop and funk dance culture.

History

The style is credited largely to Sam Solomon, known as "Boogaloo Sam", who developed popping and its companion style, the electric boogaloo, in Fresno in the mid-to-late 1970s and founded the influential crew the Electric Boogaloos in 1977. The group brought popping to a national audience through television appearances such as Soul Train. Early influences included the robot dances of mime performers, the movements of cartoon characters, and earlier boogaloo dance traditions from Oakland.

Techniques and substyles

Popping encompasses many interrelated techniques and illusions, including hitting (the basic pop), waving, tutting (geometric arm positions inspired by Egyptian art), gliding and moonwalking, animation, strobing, ticking, robot, and dime stopping. Dancers combine these with freestyle improvisation, usually to funk, electro and hip-hop music.

In popular culture

Popping moves were popularised worldwide by Michael Jackson and by films of the 1980s such as Breakin'. Contemporary popping is sustained by international battles and events such as Juste Debout and the UK B-Boy Championships, where popping is contested as its own category.
