Moore–Penrose inverse

Moore–Penrose inverse
FieldLinear algebra
NotationA+
Introduced byE. H. Moore (1920); Roger Penrose (1955)
Also calledPseudoinverse

In mathematics, and in particular linear algebra, the Moore–Penrose inverse A+ of a matrix A — commonly called the pseudoinverse — is the most widely known generalisation of the inverse matrix. It exists uniquely for every matrix, including rectangular and singular ones, and gives the least-squares solution of a system of linear equations. It was described independently by E. H. Moore in 1920 and by Roger Penrose in 1955.

Definition

For a matrix A with real (or complex) entries, the Moore–Penrose inverse A+ is the unique matrix satisfying all four Penrose conditions:

  • A A+A = A
  • A+A A+ = A+
  • (A A+)* = A A+
  • (A+A)* = A+A

where * denotes the conjugate transpose. If A is invertible, then A+ coincides with the ordinary inverse A−1.

Construction via the singular value decomposition

If A = UΣV* is a singular value decomposition of A, then A+ = VΣ+U*, where Σ+ is formed by taking the reciprocal of each non-zero singular value and transposing. This is the basis of most numerical algorithms, such as the pinv functions in MATLAB and NumPy.

Applications

For a system Ax = b, the vector x = A+b is the solution of minimum Euclidean norm among all least-squares solutions: it minimises ‖Ax − b‖, and among all minimisers has the smallest ‖x‖. This makes the pseudoinverse fundamental in linear regression, signal processing, control theory, and statistics, where it underlies the ordinary least-squares estimator.
