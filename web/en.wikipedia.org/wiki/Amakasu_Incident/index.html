The Amakasu Incident (甘粕事件, Amakasu jiken) was the murder of the prominent Japanese anarchists Ōsugi Sakae and Itō Noe, together with Ōsugi's six-year-old nephew Tachibana Muneichi, by military police in the chaos following the Great Kantō earthquake of 1 September 1923. It remains one of the most notorious cases of state violence against the left in pre-war Japan.


Ōsugi Sakae and Itō Noe

Background

The Great Kantō earthquake devastated Tokyo and Yokohama and killed over 100,000 people. Martial law was declared, and amid rumours of arson and poisoning by Koreans and radicals, vigilantes and authorities killed thousands of Koreans (the Kantō Massacre) as well as socialists and labour activists, including the ten unionists murdered in the Kameido Incident.

The murders

On 16 September 1923, a squad of Kempeitai led by Lieutenant Amakasu Masahiko arrested Ōsugi, Itō and the child in Tokyo. The three were beaten and strangled at Kempeitai headquarters that night, and their bodies were thrown into a disused well. The crime was discovered, reported in the press, and caused a public scandal. Amakasu was court-martialled and sentenced to ten years' imprisonment, but he was released after serving only about three years, following a general amnesty on the accession of the Shōwa emperor.

Aftermath

Amakasu was later posted to Manchuria, where he became a senior operative in the Kwantung Army's intelligence apparatus and an executive of the Manchuria Film Association (Man'ei). He took his own life in August 1945 as the Soviet invasion of Manchuria began. The leniency shown to him has been cited as evidence of the growing impunity of the pre-war Japanese military. The incident has been dramatised in films including Ōshima Nagisa's and Yoshida Kijū's Eros + Massacre (1969), which interweaves the story of Ōsugi and Itō with student radicalism of the 1960s.
