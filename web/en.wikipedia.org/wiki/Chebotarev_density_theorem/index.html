Chebotarev density theorem

Splitting of primes in a Galois extension, described by Frobenius elements

The Chebotarev density theorem is a central result of algebraic number theory, proved by Nikolai Chebotarev in 1922. It describes how prime ideals split in a Galois extension of number fields, generalising both Dirichlet's theorem on prime numbers in arithmetic progressions and the Frobenius density theorem.

Statement

Let L/K be a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group G, and let C be a conjugacy class of G. For all but finitely many unramified prime ideals of K, one may attach a Frobenius conjugacy class. The theorem states that the set of primes whose Frobenius class equals C has natural (Dirichlet) density exactly |C| / |G|.

In particular, for an irreducible polynomial with Galois group G, the factorisation type of the polynomial modulo a prime p is governed by the cycle structure of the corresponding Frobenius element, and the frequency of each factorisation pattern is proportional to the number of group elements with that cycle structure.

Special cases

When L is a cyclotomic extension of the rationals, the Galois group is abelian, Frobenius elements correspond to residue classes modulo the conductor, and the theorem reduces to Dirichlet's theorem that each admissible arithmetic progression contains its fair share of primes. When G is trivial, it reduces to the prime number theorem for number fields.

History and applications

Ferdinand Frobenius had proved a weaker version in 1896, in which only the identity of densities in a looser sense was established. Chebotarev, a student of Dmitry Grave, proved the full theorem in his 1922 doctoral work using a clever reduction to the cyclotomic case combined with group-theoretic arguments; his proof was celebrated for its elegance, and alternative proofs using class field theory and L-functions came later.

The theorem is a workhorse of modern number theory: it underlies results on the inverse Galois problem, the distribution of Frobenius elements used in the Sato–Tate conjecture, criteria for a prime to be represented by quadratic forms, and effective bounds in analytic number theory (where effective versions due to Lagarias and Odlyzko are important in computational applications, including tests related to the generalised Riemann hypothesis).
