Barton Springs Pool is a recreational outdoor swimming pool in Zilker Park, Austin, Texas, filled entirely with water from nearby Barton Springs, the fourth-largest spring system in Texas. Measuring about three acres, the pool maintains a near-constant temperature of around 68–70 °F (20–21 °C) throughout the year and is one of Austin's most beloved public spaces, drawing around 800,000 visitors annually.

History

The springs were sacred to the Tonkawa people and were promoted in the 1830s by William Barton, for whom they are named. In 1917 Andrew Jackson Zilker deeded the springs and surrounding land to the city, and in the 1920s the pool took its present form with the construction of a dam and bathhouse; the art deco bathhouse followed in 1947. The pool was a centre of Austin's counterculture in the 1970s, when topless swimming was informally tolerated, and it remains a civic symbol.

Ecology

The springs issue from the Edwards Aquifer through limestone faults and are home to the endangered Barton Springs salamander, found nowhere else in the world, as well as the Austin blind salamander. Protecting the springs' water quality against development in the recharge zone drove Austin's landmark Save Our Springs ordinance of 1992 and continues to shape local politics. A bypass tunnel allows floodwater from Barton Creek to pass around the pool, and the pool is periodically closed for cleaning and after heavy rains.

Use

The pool has a grassy, tree-shaded hillside, a diving board, and a shallow end fed directly by the springs. It hosts lap swimming, the annual Splash! Into Spring events, and free early-morning "swim at your own risk" hours; actor Robert Redford learned to swim there as a child.
