Chicago Blackhawks

The Chicago Blackhawks (spelled Black Hawks until 1986) are a professional ice hockey team based in Chicago. The Blackhawks compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Central Division in the Western Conference and have won six Stanley Cup championships since their founding in 1926. They are one of the "Original Six" NHL teams, along with the Detroit Red Wings, Montreal Canadiens, Toronto Maple Leafs, Boston Bruins and New York Rangers.

History

The franchise was founded in 1926 by businessman Frederic McLaughlin, who named the team after the "Blackhawk Division" of the United States Army in which he had served during the First World War; the division was named for Black Hawk, a leader of the Sauk people. The team won its first Stanley Cup in 1934 and a second in 1938 with one of the weakest regular-season records of any Cup champion.

Led by Bobby Hull, Stan Mikita, Glenn Hall and Pierre Pilote, the Black Hawks won their third Stanley Cup in 1961. There followed a long championship drought — at 49 years, one of the longest in NHL history — during which the team nonetheless fielded stars such as Tony Esposito, Denis Savard, Chris Chelios and Jeremy Roenick, and lost the 1971, 1973 and 1992 Stanley Cup Finals.

Dynasty years

Under coach Joel Quenneville and a core of captain Jonathan Toews, Patrick Kane, Duncan Keith and Brent Seabrook, Chicago ended the drought by winning the Stanley Cup in 2010 against the Philadelphia Flyers. The Blackhawks won again in 2013 — after a lockout-shortened season in which they began with a record 24-game point streak — defeating the Boston Bruins, and in 2015 against the Tampa Bay Lightning, clinching the Cup at home for the first time since 1938. The three championships in six seasons are frequently described as a modern dynasty.

Later years

Following the dynasty period the team entered a rebuild. In 2021 an independent investigation found that members of the 2010 organisation had mishandled allegations of sexual assault against a video coach, leading to the resignations of senior executives and league sanctions. The Blackhawks won the 2023 NHL draft lottery and selected Connor Bedard first overall; Bedard won the Calder Memorial Trophy as rookie of the year in 2024.

The team played at Chicago Stadium from 1929 to 1994 before moving to the United Center, which it shares with the Chicago Bulls.
