John Foxe (1516/17 – 18 April 1587) was an English clergyman, theologian and historian, best remembered as the author of Actes and Monuments (1563), popularly known as Foxe's Book of Martyrs. The vast, vividly illustrated account of Christian martyrs — culminating in the Protestants burned under Mary I — became one of the most influential books in English history, shaping the nation's Protestant identity for centuries.

Early life and exile

Foxe was born at Boston, Lincolnshire, and educated at Magdalen College, Oxford, where he became a fellow. His Protestant convictions cost him his fellowship in 1545. He worked as a tutor, including in the household of the Duchess of Richmond, and was ordained deacon by Bishop Nicholas Ridley in 1550. When the Catholic Mary I came to the throne in 1553, Foxe fled abroad, working in Frankfurt and Basel — the latter as a proofreader for the printer Johannes Oporinus — and gathering testimony about the Marian persecutions.

Actes and Monuments

A shorter Latin martyrology appeared in Strasbourg in 1554, enlarged at Basel in 1559. After returning to England under Elizabeth I, Foxe published the English Actes and Monuments in 1563 with the printer John Day: over 1,800 pages, with 57 woodcuts. The second edition (1570), expanded to two volumes and more than 2,300 pages with 150 illustrations, was ordered to be placed in cathedral churches alongside the Bible. Later editions appeared in 1576 and 1583, and abridgements circulated for centuries; beside the Bible and The Pilgrim's Progress, it was the book most likely to be found in English Puritan homes.

The work recounts the sufferings of Wycliffe and the Lollards, the Hussites, and above all the nearly 300 Protestants burned between 1555 and 1558, including Bishops Latimer, Ridley and Archbishop Cranmer. Foxe drew on official registers, trial records and eyewitness letters; while partisan, modern scholarship regards his records of the Marian martyrs as broadly accurate.

Later life

Foxe preached and published extensively, pleaded (unsuccessfully) with Elizabeth against the execution of Anabaptists, and served as a prebendary of Salisbury Cathedral. He died in London in April 1587 and was buried at St Giles Cripplegate.

Influence

Foxe's Book of Martyrs fixed the "bloody Mary" image of Catholic persecution in the English imagination, fortified anti-Catholicism for generations, and remains a key source for Tudor history and a monument of Elizabethan printing.
