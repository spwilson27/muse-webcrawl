Free on board

Free on board (FOB)
TypeIncoterm / shipping term
Defined byInternational Chamber of Commerce (Incoterms); U.S. Uniform Commercial Code (domestic use)
Applies toSea and inland waterway transport (Incoterms)
Risk transferFrom seller to buyer when goods are on board the vessel at the port of shipment

Free on board (FOB) is a term in international commercial law specifying at what point the seller's obligations and the risk of loss transfer to the buyer. Under the Incoterms rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce, "Free on Board" means the seller delivers the goods on board the vessel nominated by the buyer at the named port of shipment; from that moment the buyer bears all costs and risks.


Cargo being loaded on board at the port of shipment

Incoterms usage

Under Incoterms 2020, FOB may be used only for sea or inland waterway transport. The seller must deliver the goods on board, clear them for export, and bear all risk until they are on board; the buyer contracts and pays for the main carriage, insurance (if desired) and import clearance. "FOB" must always be followed by the named port of shipment, e.g. "FOB Rotterdam." Because risk in container shipping typically passes when the container is handed to the carrier at a terminal, the ICC recommends the term FCA (Free Carrier) instead of FOB for containerised goods.

North American usage

In domestic U.S. and Canadian practice, governed historically by the Uniform Commercial Code, "FOB origin" (FOB shipping point) means title and risk pass to the buyer when the goods are given to the carrier at the seller's location, while "FOB destination" means they pass on arrival. This usage differs from the Incoterms meaning and also determines who pays freight charges and who records goods in transit in inventory accounting.
