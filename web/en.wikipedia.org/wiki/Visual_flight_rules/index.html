Visual flight rules

Visual flight rules (VFR) are a set of regulations under which a pilot operates an aircraft in weather conditions clear enough to see where the aircraft is going. The weather must meet minima known as visual meteorological conditions (VMC).

Principles

Under VFR the pilot is primarily responsible for seeing and avoiding other aircraft, terrain and obstacles, maintaining visual reference to the ground or horizon for attitude. VFR flight requires specified visibility and distance from clouds, which vary by airspace class and altitude. The counterpart regime, instrument flight rules (IFR), applies when flight by external visual reference is not safe.

Requirements

VFR pilots must hold appropriate certification; in many jurisdictions basic private pilots are VFR-only. Special VFR clearances may permit operations in controlled airspace below standard VMC minima. Night VFR is permitted in some countries with additional requirements.
