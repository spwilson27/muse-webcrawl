Caledonian Forest

The Caledonian Forest is the name given to the ancient temperate rainforest of Scotland. The forest today is a greatly reduced remnant of the post-glacial wildwood of Scots pine that once covered much of the Scottish Highlands.

History

Scots pine spread into Scotland around 8,500 years ago as the climate warmed after the last ice age. The forest reached its greatest extent about 5,000 years ago before a wetter climate, clearance for farming and grazing, and felling for timber steadily reduced it. By the twentieth century less than one percent of the original forest survived, in scattered remnants such as Abernethy, Rothiemurchus, Glen Affric and the Black Wood of Rannoch.

Ecology

The forest is dominated by Scots pine with birch, rowan, juniper and aspen, and supports species found almost nowhere else in Britain: capercaillie, Scottish crossbill, crested tit, red squirrel and pine marten, along with rare twinflower and other boreal plants. Its old "granny pines" can live for more than 500 years.

Restoration

Conservation bodies including Forestry and Land Scotland, the RSPB and Trees for Life work to restore the forest by excluding deer, planting native trees and reconnecting fragments, with the long-term aim of a self-sustaining wild forest ecosystem.
