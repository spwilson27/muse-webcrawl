Fold and thrust belt

A fold and thrust belt is a series of mountainous folds and thrust faults formed by horizontal compression in the upper crust along the front of a collisional or subduction-related orogenic belt.

Structure

Compression shortens the crust: layers fold into anticlines and synclines and are sliced by low-angle reverse (thrust) faults that stack sheets of rock, often above a weak basal detachment such as shale or salt.

Examples include the front ranges of the Canadian Rockies, the Appalachian Valley and Ridge, the Zagros, and the Sub-Andean belts.

Significance

Fold and thrust belts are important hydrocarbon provinces, since folds and fault traps can accumulate oil and gas, and they record the shortening history of mountain building.
