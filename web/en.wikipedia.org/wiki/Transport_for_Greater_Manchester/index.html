Transport for Greater Manchester (TfGM) is the local government body responsible for co-ordinating and delivering transport services across Greater Manchester, England. It is the passenger transport executive of the Greater Manchester Combined Authority and the successor to the Greater Manchester Passenger Transport Executive (GMPTE), itself descended from the SELNEC Passenger Transport Executive created in 1969.

Responsibilities

TfGM owns and manages the Manchester Metrolink light-rail network — the largest tram system in the United Kingdom, with over 100 km of track and 99 stops — and the region's bus stations and interchanges. It plans subsidised bus services, funds concessionary travel, and promotes cycling and walking, including the Bee Network active-travel programme championed by mayor Andy Burnham.

Bee Network and bus franchising

In 2021 Greater Manchester decided to use the franchising powers of the Bus Services Act 2017, reversing the deregulation of the Transport Act 1985 in the region. After surviving a legal challenge from bus operators, franchised yellow "Bee Network" buses began running in Wigan, Bolton and parts of Salford and Bury in September 2023, with the rollout completed across all ten boroughs by January 2025 — the first English region outside London to return buses to public control. The Bee Network brand unifies buses, trams and cycle hire under one identity, with integrated fares and tap-in payment.
