UPM (company)

UPM-Kymmene Corporation, branded as UPM, is a Finnish forest industry company headquartered in Helsinki. It is one of the world's largest producers of graphic papers and a growing producer of pulp, plywood, sawn timber, label materials, and biofuels. The company was formed in 1996 by the merger of Kymmene Corporation with Repola Oy and its subsidiary United Paper Mills (Yhtyneet Paperitehtaat), whose initials give UPM its name; its roots trace back to paper mills founded in the 1870s.

Business areas

UPM operates six business areas: UPM Fibres (pulp and timber), UPM Energy, UPM Raflatac (self-adhesive label materials, one of the world's largest producers), UPM Specialty Papers, UPM Communication Papers, and UPM Plywood. It owns hydroelectric and nuclear power assets in Finland, including a stake in the Olkiluoto nuclear power plant through TVO.

Expansion and transformation

As demand for newsprint and magazine paper declined in the digital era, UPM repeatedly closed mills in Europe and shifted investment toward pulp, labels, and biomaterials. Its largest single investment was the Paso de los Toros pulp mill in Uruguay, completed in 2023, among the largest pulp mills in the world. UPM also produces renewable diesel and naphtha at its Lappeenranta biorefinery and has invested in biochemicals at Leuna, Germany.

Sustainability

UPM markets itself under the "Biofore" concept, emphasizing recyclable, wood-based products as substitutes for fossil-based materials. It is a frequent constituent of sustainability indices, though its forestry practices and eucalyptus plantations in Uruguay have also drawn criticism from environmental groups.
