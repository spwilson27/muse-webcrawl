Eero Saarinen (August 20, 1910 – September 1, 1961) was a Finnish-American architect and industrial designer noted for his wide-ranging array of designs for buildings and monuments. The son of architect Eliel Saarinen, he emigrated to the United States in 1923.

Works

His major works include the Gateway Arch in St. Louis, the TWA Flight Center at New York's JFK Airport with its wing-like concrete shell, the main terminal at Washington Dulles International Airport, the General Motors Technical Center, and Yale's Ingalls Rink. He also designed celebrated furniture such as the Tulip chair and table for Knoll.

Style

Saarinen was a leading figure of mid-century modernism, known for sculptural, expressive forms tailored to each commission rather than a single signature style. He died at 51 during surgery for a brain tumour, with several projects completed by his associates.
