Danzig

Danzig
Polish: Gdańsk
Country today: Poland
Status historically: Hanseatic city; Free City (1807–14, 1920–39)

Danzig is the German name for Gdańsk, a port city on Poland's Baltic coast at the mouth of the Vistula. The article title reflects the city's long history under German-speaking rule and its international status between the world wars.

History

Founded in the 10th century, Gdańsk joined the Hanseatic League and grew rich on the grain trade, passing between Polish, Teutonic Order, Prussian and German control. After the First World War the Treaty of Versailles made it the Free City of Danzig (1920–1939), a League of Nations protectorate within Poland's customs frontier — a status contested by Nazi Germany.

Second World War and after

Germany's demand for Danzig and the attack on the Polish post office and Westerplatte on 1 September 1939 opened the Second World War. In 1945 the ruined city was transferred to Poland, its German population expelled, and it was rebuilt as Gdańsk. In 1980 its Lenin Shipyard strikes gave birth to the Solidarity movement.
