The Leaving Certificate (Irish: Ardteistiméireacht) is the final examination of secondary education in Ireland, taken at the end of the two-year Senior Cycle, usually at age 17–19. Established in 1924 and first examined in 1925, it is administered by the State Examinations Commission and serves both as a school-leaving qualification and as the principal basis for admission to universities and other third-level institutions.

Structure and grading

Students typically study six or seven subjects chosen from a list of over thirty, including Irish, English and mathematics alongside sciences, humanities, languages and applied subjects. Most subjects can be taken at Higher or Ordinary level. Since 2017 results are reported on a refined grading scale (H1–H8 at Higher level), and admission to higher education is allocated competitively through the Central Applications Office (CAO) "points" system, based on a student's best six subjects.

Related programmes

Alongside the established Leaving Certificate there are two alternative programmes: the Leaving Certificate Vocational Programme (LCVP), with a focus on enterprise and work preparation, and the Leaving Certificate Applied (LCA), a modular, practically oriented programme assessed largely by coursework. Reform of the Senior Cycle, including broader assessment components, has been under phased implementation in the 2020s.
