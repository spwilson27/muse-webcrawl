Lucknow Pact
SignedDecember 1916
LocationLucknow, British India
PartiesIndian National Congress; All-India Muslim League
Key figuresBal Gangadhar Tilak, Muhammad Ali Jinnah, Annie Besant

The Lucknow Pact was an agreement reached between the Indian National Congress and the All-India Muslim League at a joint session of both organisations held in Lucknow in December 1916. Muhammad Ali Jinnah, then a member of both parties, was the principal architect of the agreement, earning him Sarojini Naidu's epithet "the Ambassador of Hindu-Muslim Unity."

Terms

Under the pact the two parties agreed to present joint demands to the British government: self-government for India, expanded elected Indian representation in legislative councils, and provincial autonomy. Controversially, Congress accepted the principle of separate electorates for Muslims, which had been introduced by the Indian Councils Act 1909, and the parties agreed on fixed proportions of Muslim seats in each province—for example, one-third of the seats in the central legislature, half in Punjab and 30 per cent in the United Provinces.

Significance

The session also marked the reunion of the moderate and extremist wings of Congress, split since 1907, with Bal Gangadhar Tilak readmitted to a leading role. The Lucknow Pact represented the high point of Hindu–Muslim political cooperation in the independence movement; its acceptance of separate electorates, however, is often seen by historians as a step that entrenched communal representation in Indian politics. The joint demands influenced the Montagu Declaration of 1917 and the Government of India Act 1919.
