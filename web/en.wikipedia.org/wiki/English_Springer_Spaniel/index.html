The English Springer Spaniel is a breed of gun dog in the spaniel group, traditionally used for flushing and retrieving game. It descends from the spaniels of England and takes its name from its original function of "springing" — flushing birds from cover into nets, or later into the open for falcons and guns. It is among the most popular spaniel breeds both as a working dog and as a family companion.

History

Spaniels of a general type are documented in England from at least the 16th and 17th centuries. Springer and cocker spaniels were long produced in the same litters, with size alone deciding the role; the Kennel Club recognised the English Springer Spaniel as a distinct breed in 1902. The breed later diverged into distinct "bench" (show) and field-bred lines, with field-bred dogs generally smaller, lighter-coated, and selected for working style.

Description

The springer is a medium-sized, compact, and symmetrical dog, built for endurance in heavy cover. The coat is moderately long with feathering on the ears, chest, legs, and belly, in liver-and-white or black-and-white, sometimes with tan points. The expression is alert and kind, with long, lobular ears. The tail is traditionally docked in some countries where the practice remains legal, and left natural in others.

Temperament and work

Springers are eager, energetic, and highly trainable, with a strong desire to work closely with a handler. Besides gundog work they excel in detection roles — explosives, narcotics, and search-and-rescue — and in dog sports such as agility and flyball. They require substantial exercise and mental stimulation. Health concerns in the breed include hip dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy and other eye conditions, phosphofructokinase deficiency, and ear infections associated with their long, pendulous ears.
