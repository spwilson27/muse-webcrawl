Albireo is a double star designated Beta Cygni (β Cygni, abbreviated Beta Cyg, β Cyg). It lies at the head of the constellation Cygnus, the Swan, marking the "beak" of the swan, and forms the base of the Northern Cross asterism opposite Vega in the pattern. Despite its beta designation, it is fainter than Gamma Cygni, Delta Cygni and Alpha Cygni (Deneb).

Observing

To the naked eye Albireo appears as a single star of magnitude 3, but even a small telescope or binoculars split it into two components separated by about 34 arcseconds: a golden-yellow primary of magnitude 3.1 and a bluish-green companion of magnitude 5.1. The striking color contrast makes Albireo one of the most celebrated double stars in the sky, often described as the finest in the northern hemisphere for small instruments.

Components

The brighter component, Albireo Aa, is itself a close binary, a spectroscopic and interferometric pair consisting of an orange K-type bright giant and a hot B-type main-sequence star orbiting with a period of about 214 years. The more distant blue component, Albireo B, is a rapidly rotating B8 star with an equatorial bulge and a gaseous disc (a Be star). Whether Albireo B is gravitationally bound to the Aa pair or merely an optical companion along the same line of sight has long been debated; measurements from the Gaia mission suggest the two are at somewhat different distances, raising the possibility that Albireo is an optical double rather than a single system.

Name

The name Albireo has a convoluted history arising from mistranslations of Arabic star names through Greek and Latin texts; the star's Arabic name was al-Minhar al-Dajājah, "the hen's beak," appropriate to its position in the celestial swan (formerly drawn as a hen).
