South Korean won

The South Korean won (Korean: 원, symbol: ₩; code: KRW) is the official currency of South Korea. A single won is divided into 100 jeon, the monetary subunit; however, jeon are no longer used for everyday transactions and appear only in foreign exchange rates. The won is issued by the Bank of Korea, based in Seoul.

History

The first South Korean won was introduced in 1945 after the end of Japanese colonial rule, replacing the Korean yen. In 1953, during the Korean War era, it was replaced by the hwan at a rate of 100 won to 1 hwan. The second and present won was introduced on 10 June 1962 at a rate of 10 hwan to 1 won, initially pegged to the US dollar. South Korea moved through a series of pegs before allowing the won to float during the 1997 Asian financial crisis, when its value fell sharply before recovering.

Coins and banknotes

Coins are minted in denominations of 1, 5, 10, 50, 100, and 500 won, though the 1 and 5 won coins are rarely seen in circulation. The current series of banknotes, introduced beginning in 2006, features figures from the Joseon dynasty: the ₩1,000 note depicts the scholar Yi Hwang, the ₩5,000 note Yi I, the ₩10,000 note King Sejong the Great, and the ₩50,000 note — the highest denomination, introduced in 2009 — depicts the artist and calligrapher Shin Saimdang, the first woman to appear on a South Korean banknote.

Exchange rate and use

The won is a free-floating currency and ranks among the more actively traded currencies of Asia. Small prices are conventionally quoted in units of won without decimals, and everyday prices are often rounded to the nearest 10 won. North Korea has its own, separate won (KPW), which is not freely convertible.
