Magnetic reluctance

SymbolR (script R)
SI unitInverse henry (H−1)
Analogous toElectrical resistance

Magnetic reluctance, or magnetic resistance, is a concept used in the analysis of magnetic circuits. It is defined as the ratio of magnetomotive force (mmf) to magnetic flux, and represents the opposition to magnetic flux in a magnetic circuit, analogous to electrical resistance in an electrical circuit.

Definition

Reluctance is given by R = l / (μA), where l is the length of the circuit in metres, μ is the magnetic permeability of the material, and A is the cross-sectional area. The term was coined in May 1888 by Oliver Heaviside.

Applications

Reluctance calculations are used in transformer and electric motor design, in magnetic levitation, and in the design of loudspeakers and magnetic recording heads, where air gaps are deliberately introduced to shape flux paths.
