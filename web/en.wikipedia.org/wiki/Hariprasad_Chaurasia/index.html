Pandit Hariprasad Chaurasia (born 1 July 1938) is an Indian music director and classical flautist, the foremost exponent of the bansuri (Indian bamboo flute) in Hindustani classical music. He is credited with transforming the bansuri from a folk instrument into a vehicle for serious classical performance and expanding its technique, including playing the large bass flute.

Life and career

Born in Allahabad into a family of wrestlers, Chaurasia took up the flute in secret, later studying with Bholanath Bhatt and, decisively, with Annapurna Devi, daughter of Allauddin Khan, who required him to switch to playing left-handed. He worked as a staff artist and composer at All India Radio in Cuttack and Bombay, and in film music formed the successful duo Shiv–Hari with santoor player Shivkumar Sharma, composing for films such as Silsila and Chandni.

Collaborations and honours

Internationally he recorded and performed with artists across genres, including George Harrison, Yehudi Menuhin, Jean-Pierre Rampal and John McLaughlin (on the album Making Music with Zakir Hussain and L. Shankar). He founded the Vrindaban Gurukul schools in Mumbai and Bhubaneswar to teach the guru-shishya tradition. India honoured him with the Padma Shri (1984), Padma Bhushan (1992) and Padma Vibhushan (2000).
