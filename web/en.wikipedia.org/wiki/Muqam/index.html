Muqam

Muqam is a central tradition of classical music of the Uyghur people of Xinjiang in western China. Related in concept to the Arabic maqam and other modal systems of the Middle East and Central Asia, muqam combines suites of songs, dances and instrumental pieces governed by melodic modes, rhythmic cycles and classical poetry, much of it drawn from Sufi verse.

The Twelve Muqam

The best-known corpus is the Twelve Muqam (On Ikki Muqam), a grand cycle of twelve suites, each associated with a mode and consisting of a sequence of movements — instrumental preludes, slow songs, narrative songs (dastan) and dance pieces — that can together run to many hours of music. Each muqam traditionally includes dozens of individual pieces and verses, amounting to several hundred compositions and many thousands of lines of poetry in the full cycle.

Preservation

The tradition was systematised and notated in the 20th century, notably through the work of the musician Turdi Akhun and the composer Wan Tongshu, and regional forms survive in Kashgar, Ili, Qumul and Dolan communities. In 2005 UNESCO proclaimed the Uyghur Muqam of Xinjiang a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, and it was inscribed on the Representative List in 2008.
