Red Jacket

Red Jacket (c. 1750 – 20 January 1830), known in Seneca as Sagoyewatha ("He Keeps Them Awake"), was a Seneca orator and chief of the Wolf clan in western New York. He is famous for his speeches on Native American religion and rights, and took his English name from the embroidered red coat given to him by the British during the American Revolutionary War.

Life

Red Jacket fought on the British side during the Revolution and afterward became a leading diplomat for the Seneca Nation in negotiations with the new United States. He opposed the sale of Seneca lands and resisted the Treaty of Big Tree (1797), under which the Seneca ceded most of their territory in the Genesee country. During the War of 1812 he supported the United States and fought against the British at battles including Fort George and Chippawa, though he grew disillusioned with the war.

Oratory and beliefs

Red Jacket defended Iroquois religion against Christian missionaries; his 1805 reply to the missionary Jacob Cram, arguing that Native peoples had their own legitimate faith given by the Great Spirit, became one of the most celebrated speeches in early American records. He also strongly opposed alcohol, which he blamed for devastating his people. He died in 1830 at the Seneca village near Buffalo Creek.
