VP9 is an open and royalty-free video coding format developed by Google. It is the successor to VP8 and competes principally with the High Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC/H.265) standard. VP9 was first released on June 17, 2013, and is best known for its use in streaming video on YouTube and in the WebM container format.

History

Development of VP9 began in the third quarter of 2011 under the internal codename "Next Gen Open Video" (NGOV). Its design goals included reducing the bit rate by 50% compared with VP8 at the same video quality, and catching up with the efficiency of HEVC. Work on the bitstream was largely frozen in June 2013, and support was added to the Chromium web browser and the libvpx reference implementation.

Google deployed VP9 widely on YouTube beginning in 2014. In September 2015, a group of companies including Google, Microsoft, Netflix, Amazon, Intel, Cisco and Mozilla founded the Alliance for Open Media, which developed the successor format AV1, incorporating technologies from VP9, Daala and Thor.

Technical details

VP9 is a traditional block-based transform coding format. It supports resolutions up to 16384×16384, 4:2:0 chroma subsampling in its base profiles (with 4:2:2 and 4:4:4 in later profiles), 8, 10 and 12-bit colour depth, and superblocks of up to 64×64 pixels. Its entropy coding uses a multi-symbol arithmetic coder. Reference implementations are collected in the libvpx library, which also encodes VP8.

Adoption

VP9 is natively supported by most major web browsers, including Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Opera, and by Android. Apple added VP9 decoding support in iOS 14 and macOS Big Sur for WebRTC and WebM playback. Streaming providers such as Netflix and YouTube use VP9 to serve higher-quality video at lower bandwidth on supported devices.

Licensing

Google releases VP9 under a permissive BSD-style licence and grants users a royalty-free patent licence for patents it holds that are necessarily infringed by the format, in an effort to avoid the patent-pool licensing fees associated with HEVC.
