Anzio (Italian: [ˈantsjo]) is a town and comune on the coast of the Lazio region of Italy, about 51 kilometres (32 mi) south of Rome. It is a fishing port and a popular seaside resort, well known internationally as the site of the Allied amphibious landing in the Battle of Anzio during the Second World War.

History

In antiquity, Antium was the capital of the Volsci until it was conquered by the Romans. It became a favoured resort of wealthy Romans and emperors: both Caligula and Nero were born at Antium, and Nero built a celebrated villa and a new harbour there, the ruins of which are still visible. The famous Apollo Belvedere statue was found among the ruins of a villa at Antium.

The modern town was rebuilt near the ancient harbour and developed under the popes in the 17th century. Anzio and neighbouring Nettuno grew as fishing ports and resorts.

Battle of Anzio

On 22 January 1944, Allied forces under Operation Shingle landed at Anzio and Nettuno, aiming to outflank the German Gustav Line. The landing achieved surprise, but the beachhead was contained by German counterattacks, and heavy fighting continued for months. The Allies finally broke out in May 1944, shortly before the liberation of Rome on 4 June. The Beach Head War Cemetery and the Anzio War Cemetery commemorate the Commonwealth dead.

Main sights

  • The ruins of Nero's villa and the ancient harbour
  • The Paradiso sul mare, an Art Nouveau building on the seafront
  • Villa Adele and the Archaeological Museum
  • The beachhead museum (Museo dello Sbarco di Anzio)
  • The nature reserve of Tor Caldara
