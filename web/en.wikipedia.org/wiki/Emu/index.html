Emu

The emu (Dromaius novaehollandiae) is a species of flightless bird endemic to Australia, where it is the tallest native bird. It is the only extant member of the genus Dromaius and the third-tallest and second-heaviest living bird after its African ratite relatives, the common and Somali ostriches. The emu's native ranges cover most of the Australian mainland, but the Tasmanian, King Island and Kangaroo Island subspecies became extinct after the European settlement of Australia in 1788.

The emu is featured on the Australian coat of arms alongside the red kangaroo, and the two animals are popularly held to have been chosen because they are the only two Australian animals that move only forward, never backward.

Description

The emu is a large, flightless ratite with a long neck and legs. Adults stand between 150 and 190 cm (59 and 75 in) tall and weigh between 18 and 60 kg (40 and 132 lb). Females are usually larger than males. Emus have soft, brown, shaggy feathers, small vestigial wings, and long, powerful three-toed feet adapted for running; they can travel long distances at a fast, economical trot and can sprint at about 48 km/h (30 mph).

Distribution and habitat

Emus live in most habitats across Australia, although they avoid heavily populated areas, dense forest, and arid areas. They are commonly found in pairs, and while they can form large flocks, this is an atypical social behaviour that arises from the common need to move towards a food source.

Behaviour and ecology

Emus are diurnal birds whose diet consists of a variety of native and introduced plant species, as well as insects and other arthropods. They swallow small stones (gastroliths) to help grind up and digest plant material. Emus breed in the cooler months; the male incubates the dark green eggs and cares for the chicks, which have distinctive striped plumage for camouflage.

Relationship with humans

Emus were an important source of meat and oil for Indigenous Australians. After European settlement, emus were hunted as agricultural pests, most famously in the 1932 Emu War in Western Australia, in which military personnel attempted unsuccessfully to curb the population of emus damaging crops. Emus are now farmed commercially for meat, leather, and oil.
