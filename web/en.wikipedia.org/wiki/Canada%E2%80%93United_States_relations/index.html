Canada–United States relations

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Canada–United States relations are among the closest and most extensive bilateral relationships in the world. The two countries share the world's longest international land border (8,891 km including Alaska), cooperate through NATO and the joint NORAD air-defence command, and are each other's largest trading partners in most years, with goods and services trade of well over a trillion dollars annually.

History

Relations began with conflict: American forces invaded Canada during the Revolutionary War and the War of 1812. Thereafter the border was progressively demilitarised — the Rush–Bagot Agreement of 1817 limited naval forces on the Great Lakes — and boundary disputes were settled by treaty, notably the Oregon Treaty of 1846 and the Alaska boundary settlement of 1903. Canada and the United States fought as allies in both world wars and the Korean War, and co-founded NATO in 1949 and NORAD in 1958.

Trade

The Canada–United States Free Trade Agreement of 1989 and NAFTA (1994) created a continental free-trade area, succeeded by the United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA) in 2020. Periodic disputes — over softwood lumber, dairy, energy, and steel and aluminium tariffs — punctuate an otherwise deeply integrated economic relationship.

Culture and movement

Millions of citizens cross the border each year, and the two countries share media markets, sports leagues such as the NHL, and similar cultural lives, while Canada maintains distinct political institutions and a strong emphasis on a separate national identity. In 2025 the relationship entered a period of strain after the imposition of US tariffs and remarks about annexation, which provoked a sharp Canadian public backlash.
