Salmon as food

Salmon is a popular food fish, classified as an oily fish and considered a health food due to its high protein and omega-3 fatty acid content and high vitamin D content. Salmon flesh is generally orange to red in colour, although there are also white-fleshed wild salmon.

Species used

The most commonly consumed species are Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar) and Pacific salmon species of the genus Oncorhynchus, including Chinook (king), coho (silver), sockeye (red), chum (dog), and pink salmon. The majority of Atlantic salmon available on the world market is farmed, while a large share of Pacific salmon is wild-caught. Norway, Chile, Scotland, and Canada are major producers of farmed salmon.

Nutrition

Salmon is rich in long-chain omega-3 fatty acids EPA and DHA, high-quality protein, B vitamins, potassium, and selenium. Its red-orange colour comes from carotenoid pigments, principally astaxanthin, which wild salmon obtain from eating krill and other crustaceans; farmed salmon are typically given astaxanthin in their feed to produce the same colour.

Preparations

Salmon may be eaten raw in sushi and sashimi, cured as gravlax, cold- or hot-smoked (smoked salmon and lox), grilled, baked, poached, or canned. Salmon roe (ikura) is used in Japanese cuisine, and the milt and other parts are eaten in some cultures.

Health concerns and sustainability

Raw salmon can carry parasites such as anisakis, so fish intended for raw consumption is normally frozen to kill them. Concerns have also been raised about contaminant levels, sea lice and escapes from salmon farms, and the sustainability of wild stocks; consumer guides such as seafood watch lists rate different salmon fisheries accordingly.
