Iron-deficiency anaemia

CauseInsufficient iron for haemoglobin production — blood loss, poor intake, malabsorption, increased demand
SignsLow haemoglobin with small (microcytic), pale red cells; low ferritin

Overview

Iron-deficiency anaemia is a common type of anaemia — a condition in which blood lacks adequate healthy red blood cells — that occurs when the body does not have enough iron to produce haemoglobin. It is the most common nutritional deficiency disorder in the world and a leading cause of anaemia globally, affecting particularly women of reproductive age, young children and people in low-income countries.

Causes and effects

Causes include menstrual and gastrointestinal blood loss (in adults, GI blood loss mandates investigation for ulcers or colorectal cancer), pregnancy, rapid growth, vegetarian diets low in bioavailable iron, hookworm infestation, and malabsorption (e.g. coeliac disease, after gastric surgery). Symptoms include fatigue, pallor, breathlessness, pica (craving non-food items such as ice or clay), koilonychia (spoon nails) and restless legs. Treatment is iron replacement — oral ferrous salts first-line, intravenous iron when needed — plus treatment of the underlying cause.
