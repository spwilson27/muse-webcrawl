Brake-specific fuel consumption

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A BSFC contour map ("island diagram") for a diesel engine.

Brake-specific fuel consumption (BSFC) is a measure of the fuel efficiency of any prime mover that burns fuel and produces rotational (shaft) power. It is the rate of fuel consumption divided by the power produced, with "brake" referring to the dynamometer brake used to measure the engine's output. BSFC allows the efficiency of engines of different sizes and types to be compared directly: the lower the value, the more efficiently the engine converts fuel into work at that operating point.

BSFC = ṁf / P

where ṁf is the fuel mass flow rate and P the brake power. In SI units BSFC is commonly expressed in grams per kilowatt-hour (g/(kW·h)); in Imperial units, pounds per horsepower-hour (lb/(hp·h)).

Typical values

BSFC varies strongly with speed and load. Engines generally reach their minimum BSFC at moderate speed and high (but not full) load—around the point of peak torque—and efficiency worsens sharply at light load. Representative best-point values are approximately:

Engine typeTypical best BSFC (g/(kW·h))
Large marine two-stroke diesel155 – 175
Heavy-duty truck diesel190 – 210
Passenger-car diesel200 – 240
Passenger-car gasoline (direct injection)230 – 270
Small utility gasoline engine300 – 400
Two-stroke gasoline (small)350 – 500+

BSFC is inversely proportional to thermal efficiency for a given fuel. For diesel fuel (lower heating value about 42.5 MJ/kg), a BSFC of 200 g/(kW·h) corresponds to a brake thermal efficiency of roughly 42%; the most efficient large marine diesels exceed 50% efficiency. When comparing engines running on different fuels, thermal efficiency is the more meaningful measure because fuels differ in energy content.

Measurement and representation

BSFC is measured on an engine dynamometer by recording fuel consumption and torque over the engine's speed and load range. The results are often plotted as a contour map with engine speed on one axis and brake mean effective pressure (or torque) on the other; the concentric contours resembling islands indicate regions of equal BSFC, with the innermost island the "sweet spot" of maximum efficiency. Such maps guide gear-ratio selection and hybrid control strategies so that the engine operates as often as possible near its minimum-BSFC region.

Related measures

Related quantities include indicated specific fuel consumption (ISFC), based on indicated rather than brake power, and thrust-specific fuel consumption (TSFC), used for jet engines, which expresses fuel flow per unit thrust. For vehicles, BSFC links directly to fuel economy once gearing and road load are known.
