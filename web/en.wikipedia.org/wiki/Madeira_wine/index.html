Madeira wine

Madeira is a fortified Portuguese wine made on the Madeira Islands in the Atlantic Ocean. It is produced from grapes including Sercial, Verdelho, Bual and Malvasia (Malmsey), fortified with grape spirit, and deliberately heated and oxidised during ageing — a process called estufagem that gives the wine its characteristic flavour and extraordinary longevity.

History

Madeira was a standard port of call for ships bound for the Americas and the East Indies, and casks carried as ballast were found to improve after long tropical voyages — the prized vinho da roda. The wine was fashionable in colonial America and was used to toast the United States Declaration of Independence in 1776.

Production and styles

Styles range from dry Sercial, served as an aperitif, to sweet Malmsey drunk as a digestif. Bottles of Madeira are famously durable: opened bottles keep for months, and 18th-century vintages can still be drinkable today.
