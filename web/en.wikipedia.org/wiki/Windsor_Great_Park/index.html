Windsor Great Park

Windsor Great Park is a Royal Park of 2,020 hectares (5,000 acres) south of the town of Windsor in Berkshire, England, adjoining the border with Surrey. It was historically the private hunting ground of Windsor Castle and is now largely open to the public, managed by the Crown Estate. The park is a Site of Special Scientific Interest and part of the Windsor Estate.

Features

The park's most famous feature is the Long Walk, a 2.64-mile (4.25 km) straight avenue of horse chestnut and elm trees running from Windsor Castle to the Copper Horse statue of George III on Snow Hill. Other landmarks include Virginia Water lake, created in the eighteenth century with its cascade and ornamental "ruins"; the Savill Garden and Valley Gardens, celebrated for ornamental plantings; and the Royal Lodge, residence of Prince Andrew. The park is home to a herd of red deer, reintroduced by the Duke of Edinburgh in his role as Ranger.

History

The area was a royal hunting reserve from the time of William the Conqueror. Successive monarchs landscaped the park: Charles II laid out the Long Walk's predecessor avenue, Queen Anne created Ascot Racecourse nearby, and George IV commissioned extensive works at Virginia Water. Many ancient oaks survive, some over 800 years old, making the park one of Britain's most important sites for veteran trees and the fungi and insects that depend on them.
