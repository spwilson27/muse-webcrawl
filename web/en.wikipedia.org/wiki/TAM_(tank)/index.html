TAM (tank)

The Tanque Argentino Mediano (TAM; English: Argentine Medium Tank) is a main battle tank — classed as a medium tank — of the Argentine Army. Developed in the 1970s by the German company Thyssen-Henschel in cooperation with Argentina, the TAM was designed to meet Argentine requirements for a modern but relatively light armoured vehicle suited to the country's terrain and limited bridge capacities.

Development and design

Argentina lacked experience in tank design, so the government contracted Thyssen-Henschel, which based the TAM on the chassis of the Marder infantry fighting vehicle. The resulting vehicle weighs about 30 tonnes, carries a 105 mm rifled gun (a licence-produced version of the British L7), and is powered by an MTU diesel engine giving a top speed of around 75 km/h. Its light weight sacrifices armour protection in favour of mobility.

Service

Some 256 TAMs were built at a purpose-built plant in Boulogne, Buenos Aires Province. The tank never saw combat — deliveries were still under way during the 1982 Falklands War — and it has remained Argentina's main tank for decades, with the TAM 2C modernisation programme adding new fire-control systems, thermal sights and an auxiliary power unit.

Family of vehicles

The TAM chassis spawned a family including the VCI infantry fighting vehicle, the VCA 155 self-propelled howitzer firing the Palmaria turret, mortar carriers, command vehicles and an armoured recovery vehicle.
