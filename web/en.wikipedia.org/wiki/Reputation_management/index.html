Reputation management

Reputation management is the practice of monitoring and influencing how an individual, company or brand is perceived by the public. Originating in the field of public relations, it expanded greatly with the rise of the internet and search engines, where it typically focuses on the content visible in search results, review sites and social media — often called online reputation management (ORM).

Techniques

Practitioners track mentions of a client across news, blogs, forums and review platforms, respond to criticism, and try to push favourable content up and negative content down in search engine results pages, using methods related to search engine optimisation — publishing positive articles, websites and profiles that outrank unwanted material. Legal routes, such as defamation actions or the European "right to be forgotten" established in the Google Spain case, are also used to remove or de-list content.

Ethics and criticism

Reputation management sits on a spectrum from legitimate public relations and customer-service response to deceptive practices such as astroturfing (fake grassroots support), posting fake reviews, and suppressing truthful criticism. Platforms and regulators have acted against fake reviews and undisclosed paid advocacy, and campaigns that are exposed can backfire through the Streisand effect, drawing more attention to the material they sought to bury.
