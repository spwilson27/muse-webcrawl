Widsith

Widsith ("far traveller", lit. "wide journey") is an Old English poem of 143 lines preserved in the Exeter Book, a tenth-century manuscript of Old English poetry. The poem is narrated by a fictional scop (minstrel) named Widsith, who recounts his travels among the peoples and rulers of heroic-age Europe and the gifts he received from them.

Content

The poem consists largely of three catalogues (thulas): lists of famous kings, peoples and tribes, and heroes whom the narrator claims to have visited. Widsith says he travelled with Eormanric (Hermanaric) of the Goths and visited peoples from the Baltic to Italy and beyond, mentioning figures also known from Germanic legend, including Ælfwine (Alboin) of Lombardy, Attila the Hun, Theodoric, and many tribal groups. The poem closes by reflecting on the wandering life of minstrels and their dependence on generous lords.

Significance

Although the persona is fictional — the journeys would have spanned impossibly many generations — the poem is an invaluable record of the oral heroic traditions of the Germanic world, preserving names and groupings found nowhere else. Most scholars date the poem's composition to the seventh century or earlier in some form, making it among the earliest Old English poems. Its lists overlap with traditions known from Norse and continental sources and from poems such as Beowulf and the Poetic Edda.
