Miranda (The Tempest)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Miranda is a fictional character in William Shakespeare's play The Tempest (c. 1610–1611). She is the daughter and only child of Prospero, the exiled Duke of Milan, and one of only two female characters who appear on stage in the play (the other being the spirit Ariel, often played by a woman, while Sycorax and Claribel are only mentioned).

Role in the play

Miranda was three years old when she and her father were set adrift by his usurping brother Antonio; they survived and reached an island, where Prospero raised her for twelve years, teaching her language and learning. She has therefore never seen another human being besides her father and the deformed slave Caliban. When Prospero raises the storm that wrecks the King of Naples' ship, Miranda begs him to calm the sea, showing her compassion: "If by your art, my dearest father, you have / Put the wild waters in this roar, allay them."

She falls instantly in love with Ferdinand, Prince of Naples, the first young man she has ever seen, exclaiming at the sight of the Neapolitan nobles: "O, wonder! / How many goodly creatures are there here! / How beauteous mankind is! O brave new world, / That has such people in't!" Prospero pretends to oppose the match to test Ferdinand, but the couple are betrothed by the play's end, celebrated with a masque of goddesses.

Reception

Miranda has been read as an emblem of innocence and natural purity, but feminist and postcolonial critics have also examined her sheltered upbringing, her father's control over her sexuality and marriage, and her attempted assault by Caliban in the backstory. Her "brave new world" line gave Aldous Huxley the title of his 1932 dystopian novel.
