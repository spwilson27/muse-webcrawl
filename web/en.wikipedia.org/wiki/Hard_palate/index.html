The hard palate is a thin horizontal bony plate made up of two bones of the facial skeleton, located in the roof of the mouth. It forms the anterior two-thirds of the palate, separating the oral cavity from the nasal cavity, while the soft palate forms the posterior third. The bones are the palatine process of the maxilla and the horizontal plate of the palatine bone.

Structure

The hard palate is covered by mucous membrane continuous with that of the mouth; its surface bears transverse ridges (palatine rugae) toward the front that help grip food during chewing. The greater palatine foramen and the incisive foramen transmit vessels and nerves to the palate. Along the midline of some adults a bony ridge, the torus palatinus, may be present.

Function

Working with the tongue, the hard palate is essential in the production of speech sounds — alveolar and palatal consonants are articulated against it — and in mastication and swallowing, providing a rigid surface against which food is pressed.

Clinical significance

Failure of the palatine shelves to fuse in development produces cleft palate, a congenital defect repaired surgically in infancy. The palate can also be involved in infections, tumours of the minor salivary glands, and traumatic perforations.
