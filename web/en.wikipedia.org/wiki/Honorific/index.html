Honorific

An honorific is a title or word implying or expressing respect, politeness or honour. Honorifics may be used as prefixes ("Mr.", "Dr.", "His Excellency"), suffixes, or as complete grammatical systems within a language — some languages, such as Japanese and Korean, encode social hierarchy in specialised honorific vocabulary and verb forms (keigo in Japanese).

Titles in English

English honorifics include Mr, Mrs, Miss, Ms, Dr, Professor, and forms of address for nobility, royalty, judges ("Your Honour", "My Lord/Lady") and clergy. Styles such as "His Majesty" and "Her Royal Highness" follow strict protocol in monarchies.

Honorific languages

In Japanese, honorific speech uses distinct verb conjugations and vocabulary depending on the relative status of speaker, listener and subject, divided into respectful (sonkeigo), humble (kenjōgo) and polite (teineigo) forms. Javanese and Korean have similarly elaborate systems of speech levels.

Suffixes and particles

Many languages add honorific suffixes to names: Japanese -san, -sama, -sensei; Korean -ssi and -nim; Thai khun; Hindi -ji. Academic and professional post-nominals (such as PhD, OBE) are also honorifics in a broad sense.
