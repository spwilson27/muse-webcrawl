Jamaican cuisine includes a mixture of cooking techniques, flavours, and ingredients influenced by the island's history — from the indigenous Taíno people through Spanish and British colonists, enslaved West Africans, and Indian and Chinese indentured labourers. It is known worldwide through Jamaica's diaspora.

Jerk

The most famous Jamaican cooking style is jerk, in which meat — traditionally pork, now most often chicken — is dry-rubbed or marinated in a hot spice mixture dominated by allspice (pimento) and fiery Scotch bonnet peppers, then slow-cooked over pimento wood. The technique descends from the island's Maroon communities, adapting Taíno and African methods.

Dishes

The national dish is ackee and saltfish: the ackee fruit, introduced from West Africa, sautéed with salted cod, onions, and peppers. Other staples include rice and peas (rice cooked with kidney beans or gungo peas in coconut milk), curry goat, oxtail stew, escovitch fish, mannish water soup, and the flaky, turmeric-tinted Jamaican patty. Breakfasts may feature callaloo, fried dumplings ("Johnny cakes"), plantain, and breadfruit.

Drinks and sweets

Jamaica is famed for Blue Mountain coffee, rum, ginger beer, and sorrel drink made from hibiscus. Desserts include gizzada (coconut tart), toto coconut cake, and sweet potato pudding.
