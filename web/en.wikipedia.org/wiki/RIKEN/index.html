RIKEN (Japanese: 理化学研究所, Rikagaku Kenkyūjo) is Japan's largest comprehensive scientific research institution, a national research and development agency conducting work across physics, chemistry, biology, medicine, engineering and computational science. Founded in 1917 in Tokyo, it is headquartered in Wakō, Saitama, with centres across Japan.

Research

RIKEN operates major facilities including the SPring-8 synchrotron and SACLA X-ray free-electron laser in Harima, the Radioactive Isotope Beam Factory at Nishina Center, and powerful supercomputers — the K computer and its successor Fugaku, jointly developed with Fujitsu, which was for a time the world's fastest supercomputer. In 2016 a RIKEN-led team won naming rights for element 113, named nihonium, the first element discovered in Asia.

History

Founded with support from industry, government and the Imperial household, RIKEN nurtured figures such as physicist Yoshio Nishina. After the Second World War it was dissolved and re-established, becoming an independent administrative institution in 2003.
