The Golden Gate Bridge is a Suspension bridge spanning the Golden Gate, the strait connecting San Francisco Bay with the Pacific Ocean. Opened in 1937, it links the city of San Francisco with Marin County, California, and carries U.S. Route 101 and California State Route 1. With a main span of 1,280 m (4,200 ft), it was the longest suspension bridge in the world until 1964, and its 227 m towers made it the tallest bridge of its time. It is one of the most internationally recognised symbols of the United States.

Design and construction

The bridge was designed under chief engineer Joseph B. Strauss, with significant contributions from engineer Charles Alton Ellis and architect Irving Morrow, who chose the distinctive Art Deco styling and the colour, known as international orange, selected partly for visibility in San Francisco's frequent fog. Construction began in January 1933 and finished ahead of schedule and under budget; the bridge opened to pedestrians on 27 May 1937 and to vehicles the following day. A safety net suspended below the deck saved the lives of 19 workers, who became known as the Halfway to Hell Club.

Suicide barrier

The bridge long had the grim distinction of being one of the most-used suicide sites in the world. After decades of debate, a stainless-steel deterrent net extending below and outward from the deck was completed in January 2024.

Traffic

The bridge carries six reversible traffic lanes and pedestrian and bicycle paths. Tolls are collected electronically in the southbound direction only.
