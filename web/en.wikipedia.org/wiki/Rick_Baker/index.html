Richard A. Baker (born 8 December 1950) is an American retired special make-up effects artist, widely considered the most honoured practitioner of his craft. He won the Academy Award for Best Makeup a record seven times — beginning with the award's inaugural competitive presentation for An American Werewolf in London (1981), whose on-screen transformation sequence set a new standard for practical effects.

Career

Baker grew up in Covina, California, building monsters in his garage and studying under veteran make-up artist Dick Smith, who became his mentor. He assisted Smith on The Exorcist (1973) and began creating his own creature suits for low-budget films such as Schlock (1973) and The Incredible Melting Man (1977); he also built the ape suit worn in the 1976 remake of King Kong (sharing credit with Carlo Rambaldi) and designed cantina creatures for Star Wars (1977).

His breakthrough came with An American Werewolf in London, whose transformation effects — achieved with mechanical "change-o" heads and inflatable bladder prosthetics — astounded audiences and earned the first competitive Oscar for make-up in 1982. Further Academy Awards followed for Harry and the Hendersons (1987), Ed Wood (1994), The Nutty Professor (1996), Men in Black (1997), How the Grinch Stole Christmas (2000) and The Wolfman (2010). Other major credits include Michael Jackson's Thriller (1983), Gremlins 2 (1990), Mighty Joe Young (1998) and Planet of the Apes (2001).

Retirement and legacy

Baker announced his retirement from large-scale film work in 2015, citing the industry's shift toward digital effects and compressed schedules, and auctioned much of his studio's collection — though he continues to create art and smaller projects. He received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2012. Generations of effects artists, including many of his former Cinovation staff, regard him as the defining figure of the modern creature-effects era.
