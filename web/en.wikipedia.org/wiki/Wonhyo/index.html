Wonhyo

Wonhyo (Korean: 원효, 元曉; 617–686) was one of the most important Buddhist philosophers and commentators of Korean history. A monk of the Silla kingdom, he wrote more than 80 works in over 200 volumes, systematising the Mahayana Buddhist teachings that had reached Korea from China and articulating a philosophy of harmony (hwajaeng) among doctrinal disputes.

Life

Wonhyo was born in present-day Gyeongsan, North Gyeongsang Province. Ordained as a monk, he set out in 661 with his friend Uisang to study in Tang China. According to the famous story, sheltering in a cave during a storm, he drank sweet water from a vessel in the dark; in the morning he saw it was a human skull filled with rainwater, and realised that purity and impurity are creations of the mind — "the mind is everything". Concluding that enlightenment did not require travel to China, he abandoned the journey. He later broke his monastic vows, fathering a son (Seol Chong, who became a noted Confucian scholar), and taught Buddhism among common people as a lay practitioner, chanting and dancing in the streets.

Thought

Wonhyo's commentaries covered virtually the whole Mahayana canon, notably the Awakening of Faith, the Nirvana Sutra, and Huayan texts. His central doctrine of ilshim ("one mind") and his method of reconciling contradictions (hwajaeng) profoundly shaped East Asian Buddhism, and his writings were studied in China and Japan as well as Korea. He is venerated as one of the Ten Saints of Korean Buddhism, and modern honours include the Wonhyo Bridge in Seoul and the ROKS Wonhyo, a South Korean destroyer.
