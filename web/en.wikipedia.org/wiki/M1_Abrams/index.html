M1 Abrams

The M1 Abrams is a third-generation American main battle tank designed by Chrysler Defense (now General Dynamics Land Systems) and named for General Creighton Abrams. It entered U.S. service in 1980, replacing the M60 Patton, and remains the main battle tank of the United States Army, the United States Marine Corps (until the divestment of its tank battalions), and the armies of several allied nations. More than 10,000 have been built in three major versions: the M1, the M1A1 and the M1A2.

History

The M1 emerged from the failed joint U.S.–West German MBT-70 programme and the subsequent XM1 competition, in which Chrysler's gas-turbine design beat General Motors' diesel entry in 1976. Production began in 1979 at the Lima Army Tank Plant in Ohio and the Detroit Arsenal Tank Plant. The tank was named for Creighton Abrams, former Army Chief of Staff and commander of U.S. forces in Vietnam.

Design

The Abrams is notable for its composite armour — originally the British-developed Burlington (Chobham) array, later incorporating depleted uranium mesh in U.S. variants — and for its multi-fuel AGT1500 gas turbine engine, which gives high power and quiet running at the cost of heavy fuel consumption. Ammunition is stored in a separate compartment with blowout panels to protect the crew. The M1A1 (1985) introduced the 120 mm M256 smoothbore gun derived from the German Rheinmetall Rh-120, NBC protection and improved armour. The M1A2 (1992) added a commander's independent thermal viewer, digital architecture and improved fire control; the SEP (System Enhancement Package) upgrades continue to modernise the fleet.

Combat history

The Abrams first saw combat in Operation Desert Storm (1991), where it decisively outmatched Iraqi Soviet-built tanks, and served extensively in the 2003 invasion of Iraq and the ensuing occupation, where urban fighting and improvised explosive devices posed new threats countered by the TUSK survivability kit. Export versions serve with Egypt (co-production), Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Iraq, Morocco, Australia and Poland, and M1A1s transferred to Ukraine from 2023 saw action in the Russo-Ukrainian War.
