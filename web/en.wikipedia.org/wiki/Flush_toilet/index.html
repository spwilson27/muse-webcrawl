Flush toilet

A flush toilet is a toilet that disposes of human waste by using a large volume of water to carry it through a drainpipe to another location, typically a sewer system or septic tank, using a cistern or flush valve.

History

Flushing devices date to ancient civilisations, and modern designs developed in 19th-century Britain with pioneers such as John Harington (an early inventor), Alexander Cumming, who patented the S-trap in 1775, and Thomas Crapper, who popularised improvements.

The trap retains water that blocks sewer gases from entering buildings.

Operation and impact

Typical flushes historically used several gallons, while modern low-flow and dual-flush models reduce water consumption.

Flush toilets transformed urban sanitation but require reliable water and waste treatment infrastructure.
