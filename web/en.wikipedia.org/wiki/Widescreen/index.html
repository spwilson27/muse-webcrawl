Widescreen

Widescreen images are images displayed within a set of aspect ratios (relationship of image width to height) that are wider than the conventional 4:3 aspect ratio used in traditional television and computer displays. Since the 1990s, the most common widescreen format for television and video is 16:9 (1.78:1), while motion pictures commonly use 1.85:1 or 2.39:1.

Comparison of standard and widescreen aspect ratios.

History

Widescreen formats were experimented with in the earliest days of cinema, but the near-square Academy ratio of 1.37:1 dominated for decades. In the 1950s, facing competition from television, the film industry introduced wide formats such as CinemaScope (2.35:1), VistaVision, and Cinerama. Television remained 4:3 until the development of high-definition television, which standardised 16:9 as a compromise between film and television ratios. By the 2000s and 2010s, 16:9 became standard for televisions, computer monitors, and mobile devices.

Presentation methods

Displaying widescreen material on narrower screens (or vice versa) requires techniques such as letterboxing, pillarboxing, pan and scan, cropping, or stretching, each with trade-offs in image fidelity.
