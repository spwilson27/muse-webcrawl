Calcium channel

A calcium channel is an ion channel which shows selective permeability to calcium ions. It is sometimes synonymous with voltage-gated calcium channel, although there are also ligand-gated calcium channels.

Voltage-gated channels

Voltage-gated calcium channels (VGCCs) open in response to membrane depolarization and mediate calcium entry into excitable cells, triggering muscle contraction, neurotransmitter release, hormone secretion and gene expression. They are classified by their electrophysiological and pharmacological properties into L-, N-, P/Q-, R- and T-types.

Ligand-gated and store-operated channels

Other calcium entry pathways include the IP3 receptor and ryanodine receptor, which release calcium from the endoplasmic or sarcoplasmic reticulum, and store-operated channels such as Orai, activated when intracellular stores are depleted.

Pharmacology

Calcium channel blockers such as amlodipine, verapamil and diltiazem inhibit L-type channels and are widely used for hypertension and angina. Mutations in calcium channel genes cause a family of disorders called channelopathies, including familial hemiplegic migraine, episodic ataxia and Timothy syndrome.
