Level 5 leadership

Level 5 leadership is a concept developed by Jim Collins in his book Good to Great (2001). It describes the highest level in a hierarchy of executive capabilities, characterised by a paradoxical blend of personal humility and professional will.

The hierarchy

Collins described five levels: Level 1, the highly capable individual; Level 2, the contributing team member; Level 3, the competent manager; Level 4, the effective leader who catalyses commitment to a compelling vision; and Level 5, the executive who builds enduring greatness through humility and fierce resolve. In Collins's research, companies that transitioned from good to great performance were led by Level 5 leaders during the transition.

Characteristics

Level 5 leaders channel their ego needs away from themselves and into the larger goal of building a great company. They attribute success to factors outside themselves and take responsibility for poor results. They are described as modest, quiet, reserved and even shy, yet fanatically driven to produce sustained results.

Criticism

Critics have questioned the methodology of the Good to Great study, including survivorship bias and the subsequent performance of some of the featured companies.
