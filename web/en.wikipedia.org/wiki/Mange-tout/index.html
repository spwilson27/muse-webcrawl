Mange-tout

Mange-tout
SpeciesPisum sativum
TypesSnow pea, sugar snap pea

Mange-tout (French for "eat all") are pea varieties eaten whole, pod and all, while the seeds inside are still immature. The term covers both the flat-podded snow pea (var. saccharatum) and the plump-podded sugar snap pea (var. macrocarpon), both cultivars of Pisum sativum.

Characteristics

Unlike ordinary garden peas, mange-tout pods lack the tough, fibrous inner membrane (parchment) that makes mature pods inedible. Snow peas are picked flat, before the seeds swell, and are a staple of stir-fries in East Asian cooking; sugar snap peas, bred in the 20th century from a cross between snow peas and garden peas, are eaten crisp and sweet when fully rounded.

Culinary use

Mange-tout are usually eaten raw in salads or lightly cooked to preserve their crunch and sweetness. They are a good source of vitamin C, vitamin K, and fibre.
