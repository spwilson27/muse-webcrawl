Marc is a pomace brandy produced in France, Italy (as grappa), and other wine-producing countries. It is distilled from the pomace — the skins, seeds, and stems left after grapes are pressed for wine — and is a distinct spirit from wine brandy, which is distilled from wine itself.

Production

The pomace is fermented if necessary and then distilled, traditionally in copper pot stills. The raw spirit is sharp and fiery; good marc is aged in oak barrels, which rounds it and imparts colour and complexity. Marc de Champagne and Marc de Bourgogne are the best-known French styles; in Champagne, the regulations require the marc to be produced in the region, and it is often made from the pomace of the same grapes used for champagne. In Alsace, Marc de Gewurztraminer is valued for its aromatic, spicy character.

Character and serving

Marc is usually served as a digestif, neat or with coffee (the French café-marc tradition pours marc into coffee in place of cognac). It is also used as an ingredient in sauces and desserts, and in Burgundy the marc-soaked sugar cube sucre de marc is a traditional accompaniment to coffee. Unaged marc can be pungent; aged examples resemble fine brandy.

Related spirits

Italian grappa is the same spirit produced from Italian pomace, and it has its own protected appellations. The Portuguese bagaço and Spanish aguardiente de orujo are similar pomace brandies.
