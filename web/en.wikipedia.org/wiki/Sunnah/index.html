Sunnah

The Sunnah is preserved principally in the hadith literature

Sunnah (Arabic: سُنَّة, literally "habitual practice" or "trodden path") is the body of traditions and practices of the Islamic prophet Muhammad that constitute a model for Muslims to follow. Together with the Quran and hadith — the transmitted reports of Muhammad's words, actions and tacit approvals — the Sunnah forms the primary textual basis of Islamic law (sharia) and theology. In Sunni Islam, the term also gives the branch its name: Ahl as-Sunnah wa'l-Jama'ah, "the people of the Sunnah and the community".

Meaning and usage

In pre-Islamic Arabic, sunnah meant the customary practice or precedent of a tribe or person. In Islamic jurisprudence it came to denote the normative example of Muhammad, encompassing what he said (qawl), did (fi'l) and approved by silence (taqrir). Jurists also classify recommended acts of worship as sunnah — performed for reward but not obligatory — in contrast to fard (obligatory) duties, giving the word a second, technical legal sense.

Sources

The Sunnah is known chiefly through hadith collections compiled in the 8th and 9th centuries CE, above all the canonical Six Books (Kutub al-Sittah) of Sunni Islam, including Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. Each report is accompanied by a chain of transmitters (isnad), whose reliability Muslim scholars assessed in an elaborate science of hadith criticism. Shia Islam accepts the Sunnah as authoritative but draws on a different corpus, including the sayings of the Twelve Imams, while Quranist movements reject the authority of hadith altogether.

Role in law and life

The Sunnah supplements and explains the Quran: it specifies, for example, how to perform the five daily prayers, which the Quran commands but does not describe in detail. Beyond law, the Sunnah shapes everyday Muslim practice — dress, eating, prayer posture and personal conduct — and devotional imitation of the Prophet's example is regarded as an expression of love for him. Debates over the authenticity and scope of the Sunnah have been central to Islamic reform movements from the classical period to modern Salafism and Islamic modernism.
