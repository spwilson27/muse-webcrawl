Wends


Medieval Wendish territory stretched along the southern Baltic shore.

Wends (Old English: Winedas; German: Wenden; Latin: Veneti) is a historical name used by Germanic peoples for the Slavic peoples living near Germanic settlement areas. The term most often referred to the West Slavic tribes of the southern Baltic coast and the Elbe valley — including the Obotrites, Veleti (later Lutici), Hevelli, and Sorbs — but was applied at different times to Slavs more generally.

History

During the early Middle Ages, Wendish tribes controlled much of the land east of the Elbe. From the tenth century onward, German expansion eastward — the Ostsiedlung — combined with crusades against the pagan Wends, notably the Wendish Crusade of 1147, brought these territories under German and Danish rule. Over the following centuries most Wends were assimilated into German-speaking populations.

Sorbs and modern usage

The clearest surviving descendants of the Wends are the Sorbs of Lusatia in eastern Germany, who still speak Sorbian languages. In older German usage, "Wenden" could refer specifically to Sorbs. The label "Wendish" is also used by descendants of Sorbian emigrants to the United States, notably the Wends of Texas, who founded the community of Serbin in the 1850s, and by a Wendish community in Australia.
