GenBank

GenBank is the NIH genetic sequence database, an annotated collection of all publicly available DNA sequences. GenBank is part of the International Nucleotide Sequence Database Collaboration, which comprises the DNA Data Bank of Japan (DDBJ), the European Nucleotide Archive (ENA), and GenBank at NCBI. These three organizations exchange data daily to ensure worldwide coverage.

History

GenBank grew out of the Los Alamos Sequence Database created in the late 1970s by Walter Goad and colleagues at Los Alamos National Laboratory, in collaboration with Bolt, Beranek and Newman. By 1982 it was publicly funded as GenBank, and responsibility later moved to the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) after its creation in 1988–1989.

Growth and releases

GenBank is released in full every two months, with daily updates available by FTP and web services. Its size has grown exponentially: from under a million bases in the early 1980s to hundreds of billions of bases from tens of millions of sequences, plus the much larger Whole Genome Shotgun (WGS) sequence collections. The sequence identifiers known as accession numbers (and the GI numbers, retired in 2016) provide stable references for records.

Submission and access

Laboratories submit sequences directly using tools such as BankIt and Sequin; most journals require deposition of new sequences as a condition of publication. Records carry annotations — feature tables describing genes and coding regions — largely supplied by submitters. Data are freely available without restriction on use.
