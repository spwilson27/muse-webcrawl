Ifá

Ifá is a Yoruba religion and system of divination originating among the Yoruba people of West Africa, in what are now Nigeria, Benin, and Togo. Its literary corpus, the Odu Ifá, comprises 256 odù containing thousands of verses (ese Ifá) of stories, poems, and guidance consulted by diviners.

Practice

Divination is performed by a priest called a babalawo ("father of secrets") for men or iyalawo for women, using sacred palm nuts (ikin) or a divining chain (opele) to generate one of the 256 odù, whose verses are recited and interpreted for the client. Ifá is presided over by the orisha Orunmila, regarded as the witness of creation and knowledge.

Spread and recognition

Through the African diaspora, Ifá took root in the Americas in traditions such as Cuban Santería (Regla de Ocha) and Brazilian Candomblé. UNESCO proclaimed the Ifa divination system a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2005 (inscribed on the Representative List in 2008).
