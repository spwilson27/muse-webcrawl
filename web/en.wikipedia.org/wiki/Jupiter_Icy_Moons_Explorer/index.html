Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer

OperatorEuropean Space Agency (ESA)
Launch14 April 2023, Ariane 5, Kourou
Arrival at JupiterPlanned July 2031
TargetsGanymede, Callisto, Europa

The Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer (JUICE) is an interplanetary spacecraft developed by the European Space Agency to study Jupiter and three of its large icy moons — Ganymede, Callisto, and Europa — which are thought to harbour subsurface oceans and potentially habitable conditions.

Mission

JUICE launched on 14 April 2023 aboard an Ariane 5 from French Guiana. Its eight-year cruise uses gravity assists at Earth, Venus, and the Moon — including the first lunar–Earth flyby in August 2024 — to reach Jupiter in July 2031. After touring the system and making flybys of Europa and Callisto, it will enter orbit around Ganymede in late 2034, becoming the first spacecraft to orbit a moon of another planet, before an impact on Ganymede ends the mission around 2035.

Instruments

The payload of ten instruments includes cameras (JANUS), spectrometers (MAJIS, UVS, SWI), a laser altimeter (GALA), ice-penetrating radar (RIME), a magnetometer (J-MAG), and radio science experiments, designed to characterise the moons' surfaces, interiors, oceans, and tenuous atmospheres, and Jupiter's magnetosphere.

JUICE complements NASA's Europa Clipper, launched in 2024.
