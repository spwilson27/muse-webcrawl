Seyfert galaxy

TypeActive galaxy
Named forCarl Seyfert
ExamplesCircinus Galaxy, NGC 1068, Messier 77

Seyfert galaxies are a class of active galaxies whose nuclei are extremely bright, powered by accretion of material onto a supermassive black hole at their centres. They are named after the American astronomer Carl Keenan Seyfert, who first systematically described them in 1943.

Characteristics

Seyfert galaxies resemble normal spiral galaxies in overall appearance, but their cores outshine the rest of the galaxy and show strong emission lines in their spectra. Their luminosities can rival those of quasars while their host galaxies remain clearly visible, making them important nearby laboratories for studying active galactic nuclei. Many Seyferts show broad spectral lines from fast-moving gas near the central black hole.

Classification

Following the scheme introduced by Khachikian and Weedman in 1974, Seyferts are divided into Type I and Type II. Type I Seyferts display both broad and narrow emission lines, indicating gas moving at thousands of kilometres per second close to the nucleus. Type II Seyferts show only narrow lines; in the unified model of active galaxies, this is explained by a dusty torus that hides the broad-line region when viewed edge-on.

Examples

Well-known Seyfert galaxies include Messier 77 (NGC 1068), the Circinus Galaxy, NGC 4151 and NGC 1275. X-ray observations, notably of the variable source NGC 4151, have provided key evidence for supermassive black holes in galactic nuclei.
