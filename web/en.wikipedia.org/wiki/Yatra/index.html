Yatra

Yatra (Sanskrit: यात्रा, yātrā, 'journey', 'procession') in Hinduism and other Indian religions generally means a pilgrimage to holy places such as confluences of sacred rivers, places associated with the epics such as the Mahabharata and Ramayana, and other sacred pilgrimage sites. Visiting a sacred place is believed by the pilgrim to purify the self and bring one closer to the divine. The journey itself is as important as the destination, and the hardships of travel serve as an act of devotion in themselves.

Etymology and meaning

The Sanskrit word yātrā derives from the root yā, meaning 'to go' or 'to move'. In a religious context it denotes a procession or journey undertaken for sacred purposes, often in a group, and frequently accompanied by chanting, singing of bhajans, and ritual observances along the way. The term is used across Hinduism, Jainism, Buddhism and Sikhism for pilgrimage journeys of many kinds.

Major yatras

Among the most famous yatras is the Kumbh Mela, a mass pilgrimage in which Hindus gather to bathe in a sacred river; it is considered the largest religious gathering in the world. The Char Dham Yatra in Uttarakhand visits the four shrines of Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath and Badrinath in the Himalayas. The Amarnath Yatra in Kashmir leads pilgrims to a cave shrine associated with Shiva, while the Rath Yatra at Puri features the great chariot procession of the deity Jagannath. The Kailash Mansarovar Yatra journeys to Mount Kailash and Lake Manasarovar in Tibet, sacred to several religions.

Processions and other uses

Beyond long-distance pilgrimage, yatra also refers to temple festival processions in which a deity's image is carried through the streets, and in modern Indian politics to campaign journeys undertaken by leaders to gather support. In Jainism, organised pilgrimages to sites such as Shatrunjaya are also called yatras.
