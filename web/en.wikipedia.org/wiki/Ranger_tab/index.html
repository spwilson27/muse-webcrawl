The Ranger tab is a qualification tab of the United States Army, authorized for soldiers who successfully complete the U.S. Army Ranger School at Fort Moore (formerly Fort Benning), Georgia. It is one of four permanent individual skill tabs, alongside the Special Forces, Sapper, and President's Hundred tabs.

Description

The tab is an arched cloth patch embroidered with the word "RANGER" in yellow-gold letters on a black (or subdued olive drab) background, worn on the upper left sleeve above the unit patch and any combat or special-skill badges.

Ranger School

Ranger School is a 62-day course conducted in three phases — Darby (Fort Moore), Mountain (Dahlonega, Georgia), and Swamp (Florida) — testing small-unit leadership under conditions of hunger, fatigue, and stress. Historically about half of candidates fail or are recycled.

Distinction from the 75th Ranger Regiment

The tab denotes graduation from Ranger School, not service in the 75th Ranger Regiment. Regiment members wear the tan beret and the Ranger scroll; many are Ranger-qualified, but the tab alone does not make a soldier a member of the Regiment.
