A flameless ration heater (FRH), colloquially a "MRE heater", is a form of self-heating food packaging included in the United States military's Meal, Ready-to-Eat (MRE) field rations. It is a flat plastic pouch containing a pad impregnated with finely powdered magnesium and iron alloy and salt. Adding a small amount of water triggers an exothermic oxidation reaction that heats the ration entrée placed inside the pouch, without any flame or external heat source.


An MRE entrée heating in an FRH, "propped against a rock or something"

How it works

When water reaches the pad, the magnesium–iron alloy forms a galvanic cell: the magnesium oxidises rapidly, releasing heat, while small amounts of hydrogen gas are evolved. The reaction can raise the temperature of a standard entrée by about 100 °F (56 °C) in ten minutes. The pouch instructions famously advise the user to prop the heating ration "against a rock or something" while it heats, a phrase that has become an in-joke among service members.

Safety and use

Because the reaction produces hydrogen, heaters must not be used in sealed or confined spaces, and used heaters are restricted on aircraft — unused FRHs are classed as dangerous goods for air transport and have occasionally been mistaken for hazards at airport security. The technology has been adapted for civilian self-heating meals, emergency rations and disaster relief kits, and similar heaters are issued in the rations of several other armed forces.
