Philippine languages

Philippine languages
Region: Philippines and northern Sulawesi
Family: Austronesian → Malayo-Polynesian → Philippine
Number of languages: roughly 130–180, depending on classification
Major languages: Tagalog, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Bikol, Waray, Kapampangan, Pangasinan
Official: Filipino (based on Tagalog) and English

The Philippine languages are a group of roughly 130 to 180 languages spoken in the Philippines and parts of northern Sulawesi, Indonesia. They form a subgroup of the Malayo-Polynesian branch of the Austronesian language family and are characterised by a distinctive verb-initial word order and a complex voice (focus) system.

Classification

Linguists such as Robert Blust classify the Philippine languages into several primary subgroups, including Batanic, Northern Luzon, Central Luzon, North Mangyan, Greater Central Philippine, Kalamian, South Mindanao (Bilic), and the South Philippine (Manobo, Danao, Subanen) groups. The Greater Central Philippine subgroup includes Tagalog, the Bikol languages, the Visayan languages (such as Cebuano, Hiligaynon and Waray), and Mamanwa.

Major languages

The most widely spoken languages are Tagalog — the basis of the national language Filipino — Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Central Bikol, Waray, Kapampangan and Pangasinan. According to the constitution of 1987, Filipino and English are the official languages, while the major regional languages serve as auxiliary official languages in their regions. Spanish, once official, declined sharply in the twentieth century, though the Spanish-based creole Chavacano survives in Zamboanga and elsewhere.

Features

Philippine languages typically have small phoneme inventories, predicate-initial syntax, and an Austronesian alignment system in which affixes on the verb mark the semantic role of the "topic" noun phrase. Historically they were written in indigenous Indic-derived scripts such as Baybayin; today the Latin alphabet is universal.
