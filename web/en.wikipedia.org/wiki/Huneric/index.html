Huneric or Hunneric (died 23 December 484) was King of the Vandals and Alans in North Africa from 477 to 484. The eldest son of the kingdom's founder, Gaiseric, he succeeded in accordance with his father's law of succession by seniority within the royal family (the tanistry-like system later chronicled as succession by the oldest male agnate).

Life and reign

As a young prince, Huneric was sent as a hostage to Ravenna under the treaty of 435, and in 455, following the Vandal sack of Rome, he was married to Eudocia, daughter of the emperor Valentinian III—taken to Carthage by Gaiseric as a captive. Eudocia later fled to Jerusalem (c. 471/472) after sixteen years of marriage, leaving behind their son Hilderic.

As king, Huneric initially sought better relations with the Nicene (Catholic) population, allowing the election of a Catholic bishop of Carthage, Eugenius, in 481 after a vacancy of twenty-four years. But in 484, after a failed conference between Arian and Catholic bishops (1–4 February 484), he launched the most severe persecution of Catholics in the Vandal kingdom: an edict of 25 February 484 ordered all Catholic churches closed and their property transferred to the Arians, and on 1 June 484 a further decree required all Catholics to convert to Arianism by 1 June 485. Bishops were exiled to Corsica and the desert, and thousands were banished, as recorded by Victor of Vita in his History of the Vandal Persecution. Huneric also purged members of his own family and the Arian clergy. He died in December 484—according to hostile sources, of a plague of worms—and was succeeded by his nephew Gunthamund.
