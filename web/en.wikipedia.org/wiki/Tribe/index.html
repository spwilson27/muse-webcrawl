Tribe

A tribe is a human social group that, in popular usage, comprises families or communities linked by social, economic, religious, or blood ties, with a common culture and dialect, typically led by a recognised chief. In anthropology, the term's precise meaning is debated, and it is sometimes criticised as a colonial-era category.

Historical uses

The English word derives from the Latin tribus, one of the three original divisions of the Roman people. In evolutionary anthropology, "tribe" was used in the band–tribe–chiefdom–state sequence of political organisation. In the United States, "tribe" has a legal meaning: federally recognised Native American tribes are sovereign entities with a government-to-government relationship with the United States.

Modern usage

Many Indigenous peoples today use "tribe" or "tribal nation" as a term of self-description and legal identity, while some scholars prefer terms such as "kin group" or "ethnic group".
