Gambling Act 2005

The Gambling Act 2005 (c. 19) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. It mainly applies to England and Wales and to Scotland, and is designed to control all forms of gambling. It transferred authority for licensing gambling from magistrates' courts to local authorities (specifically unitary authorities, and district councils in non-metropolitan areas with two tiers of local government), and created the Gambling Commission to replace the Gaming Board for Great Britain. The Act received Royal Assent on 7 April 2005, and most of its provisions came into force on 1 September 2007.

Objectives

The Act sets out three licensing objectives, which the Gambling Commission and local licensing authorities must aim to permit gambling in a manner reasonably consistent with:

  • preventing gambling from being a source of crime or disorder, being associated with crime or disorder or being used to support crime;
  • ensuring that gambling is conducted in a fair and open way; and
  • protecting children and other vulnerable persons from being harmed or exploited by gambling.

Provisions

Amongst other things the Act:

  • sets out the functions of the Gambling Commission;
  • defines a range of gambling offences, including cheating;
  • for the first time regulates remote gambling, including gambling conducted via the internet, telephone, and other electronic means;
  • introduces new categories of casino, including "large" and "small" casinos, and originally permitted one "regional" casino (or super casino), a provision later abandoned;
  • allows local licensing authorities to license gambling premises in their areas.

Super casino

The Act originally provided for the creation of one regional casino, popularly known as a "super casino". An independent panel recommended Manchester as the location in January 2007, but the proposal was dropped by the government of Gordon Brown later that year.
