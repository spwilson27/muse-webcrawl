Battle of the Gates of Trajan

The Battle of the Gates of Trajan was fought on 17 August 986 in the Ihtiman Pass (the "Gates of Trajan") in Bulgaria. Tsar Samuel of Bulgaria ambushed and destroyed the Byzantine army of Emperor Basil II — the young emperor's worst defeat, which prolonged Bulgarian resistance for another generation.

Background

After the Byzantine conquest of eastern Bulgaria in 971, the Cometopuli brothers — Samuel foremost — rebuilt Bulgarian power in the west. In 986 Basil II, aged about 28, marched to capture Serdica (Sofia) and reassert imperial rule. The siege failed after some 20 days through poor logistics and inexperience.

Battle

Retreating toward Philippopolis through the wooded pass, the Byzantines were harassed and their column straggled. Samuel's Bulgarians blocked the route and attacked from the heights; the Byzantine army panicked and was destroyed, losing its baggage, siege train and the imperial tent. Basil escaped with difficulty, aided by Armenian infantry of the guard. The general Kontostephanos was killed.

Aftermath

The victory secured Samuel's position — he was soon crowned tsar — and emboldened Bulgarian expansion into Greece and Serbia. Basil II learned hard lessons, returned methodically year after year, and finally crushed Bulgaria after Kleidion (1014), earning the epithet "Bulgar-slayer".
