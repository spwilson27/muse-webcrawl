Nelson Piquet Souto Maior (born 17 August 1952), known as Nelson Piquet, is a Brazilian former racing driver and businessman. He won the Formula One World Drivers' Championship three times, in 1981 and 1983 with Brabham and in 1987 with Williams. He is regarded as one of the greatest Formula One drivers of his era.

Early life and career

Piquet was born in Rio de Janeiro, the son of Estácio Gonçalves Souto Maior, a physician and government minister. Against his father's wishes he took up karting as a teenager, using a false name to hide his activities. He won the Brazilian karting championship in 1971 and 1972 and the Brazilian Formula Vee title in 1976, then moved to Europe, winning the British Formula Three championship in 1978 with a record number of wins.

Formula One

Brabham (1978–1985)

Piquet made his Formula One debut with Ensign at the 1978 German Grand Prix and soon joined Brabham. He finished runner-up in 1980 and won his first championship in 1981. In 1983 he became the first driver to win the title with a turbocharged car, the BMW-powered Brabham BT52, beating Alain Prost in the final races.

Williams (1986–1987)

Moving to Williams, Piquet entered a fierce rivalry with teammate Nigel Mansell. After losing the 1986 title narrowly, he won the 1987 championship through consistent points finishes despite Mansell winning more races, a campaign ended early when Mansell was injured in qualifying in Japan.

Lotus and Benetton (1988–1991)

Piquet spent two uncompetitive seasons at Lotus before joining Benetton in 1990, where he won three further races, including back-to-back victories in Japan and Australia at the end of 1990 and the 1991 Canadian Grand Prix, his final win. He retired from Formula One at the end of 1991 with 23 wins from 204 starts.

Later life

In 1992 Piquet suffered serious leg and foot injuries in a crash practising for the Indianapolis 500, but returned to compete at Indianapolis in 1993. He later built successful business interests in Brazil and managed the racing careers of his sons, including Nelson Piquet Jr., who also raced in Formula One. Piquet was inducted into the International Motorsports Hall of Fame in 2000.
