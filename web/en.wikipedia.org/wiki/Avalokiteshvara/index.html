Avalokiteshvara

Avalokiteshvara (Sanskrit: अवलोकितेश्वर, "the lord who looks down [with compassion]") is a bodhisattva in Mahayana Buddhism who embodies the compassion of all Buddhas. He is one of the most widely revered bodhisattvas, venerated throughout Asia in numerous forms — as Guanyin in China, Kannon in Japan, Gwan-eum in Korea, Quán Thế Âm in Vietnam, and Chenrezig in Tibet, where he is regarded as the patron deity of the land and the spiritual ancestor of the Dalai Lamas.

Origins and scriptures

Avalokiteshvara appears prominently in the Lotus Sutra, whose 25th chapter — often circulated independently as the Guanyin Sutra — describes how the bodhisattva hears the cries of suffering beings and assumes whatever form is needed to save them. The Heart Sutra is framed as his teaching to the monk Shariputra, and the Karandavyuha Sutra elaborates his cosmic role and his mantra, Om mani padme hum.

Forms

Iconography varies widely. Indian and Tibetan forms are usually male, often with multiple arms — most famously the thousand-armed, eleven-headed form representing his vow to help all beings. In East Asia, Guanyin gradually took on female forms, becoming a goddess of mercy associated with childbirth and protection of sailors. Other forms include the esoteric Cundi, the horse-headed Hayagriva (a wrathful emanation), and Lokeshvara, "Lord of the World", prominent in Cambodia's Angkor period.

Worship

Devotees recite the bodhisattva's name and mantra seeking protection from danger, healing and rebirth in Amitabha's Pure Land; Avalokiteshvara is an attendant of Amitabha along with Mahasthamaprapta. Major pilgrimage sites include Mount Putuo in China, dedicated to Guanyin, and the Potala Palace in Lhasa, named for his mythical mountain abode, Potalaka.
