Vowel length

In phonetics, vowel length is the perceived duration of a vowel sound. In many languages, vowel length is a distinctive (phonemic) feature: two words can differ only in whether a vowel is short or long. In the International Phonetic Alphabet, length is marked with the triangular colon ⟨ː⟩ for long vowels and ⟨ˑ⟩ for half-long vowels.

Languages with length contrasts

Classical Latin distinguished long and short vowels (as in malus "evil" vs. mālus "apple tree"). Japanese has a five-vowel system in which each vowel can be short or long (e.g., obasan "aunt" vs. obāsan "grandmother"), and Finnish, Hungarian, Arabic, Thai and many Australian Aboriginal languages also use length phonemically. Some languages, such as Estonian and some dialects of Finnish, distinguish three degrees of length.

Non-phonemic length

Even in languages where length is not phonemic, vowels vary in duration: in English, vowels are longer before voiced consonants (bead vs. beat) and in stressed syllables. Historically, the long vowels of Middle English were transformed by the Great Vowel Shift, so that modern English "long vowels" differ mainly in quality rather than duration.

Chroneme

The abstract unit of length — applying to both vowels and consonants — is sometimes called the chroneme, measured in morae: a short vowel counts as one mora, a long vowel as two.
