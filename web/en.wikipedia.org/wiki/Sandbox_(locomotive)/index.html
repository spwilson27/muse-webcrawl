Sandbox (locomotive)

A sandbox is a container on most locomotives, multiple units, and trams that holds sand, which is dropped onto the rail in front of the driving wheels in wet and slippery conditions, and when starting or climbing a steep gradient, to improve traction and reduce wheel slip.

Operation

Sanders (sanding gear) deliver sand by gravity or compressed air through pipes aimed at the point where the wheel contacts the rail. The driver operates the sanders manually, or automatic systems apply sand when wheel slip is detected, and in some cases during emergency braking to shorten stopping distances. Sand must be dry — locomotives carry means to dry it, and sandboxes on steam locomotives were often placed on top of the hot boiler, which kept the sand warm and dry.

Placement

On steam locomotives, sandboxes were commonly mounted on top of the boiler as prominent domes, or on the running boards. Diesel and electric locomotives typically have sandboxes at the ends of the bogies or in the body near the driven axles. Multiple sandboxes are fitted so that sand can be applied for both directions of travel.

Limitations

Sanding is of limited benefit at speed and can contaminate track circuits used for signalling if excessive; modern practice, together with wheel-slide protection systems and railhead treatment trains (which apply a sand-and-gel mixture called Sandite), manages adhesion in leaf-fall season. Excess sand on the rail can also insulate the wheels electrically and increase rolling resistance for following trains.
