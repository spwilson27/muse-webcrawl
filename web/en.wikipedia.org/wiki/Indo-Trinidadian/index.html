Indo-Trinidadians

Indo-Trinidadians, or Indo-Trinidadians and Tobagonians, are Trinidadians and Tobagonians of Indian origin. They are descendants of indentured labourers brought from India by the British between 1845 and 1917 to work on sugar and cocoa plantations after the abolition of slavery. Indo-Trinidadians form one of the two largest ethnic groups in Trinidad and Tobago, constituting roughly 35 per cent of the population.

History

The first ship, the Fatel Razack, arrived on 30 May 1845 with 225 Indian labourers — a date commemorated as Indian Arrival Day, a public holiday. Over the period of indenture, about 147,000 Indians came to Trinidad. Most came from the Bhojpuri-speaking regions of present-day Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, with a significant minority of Tamil and Telugu speakers from South India.

Culture and politics

Indo-Trinidadians have preserved and transformed Indian traditions, creating distinctive forms such as chutney music, pichakaree, and local celebrations of Diwali, Eid, and Hosay. Notable Indo-Trinidadians include Nobel laureate V. S. Naipaul and Basdeo Panday, the country's first prime minister of Indian descent (1995–2001), as well as Kamla Persad-Bissessar, its first female prime minister (2010–2015).
