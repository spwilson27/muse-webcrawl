Basic hypergeometric series


The general rφs series

In mathematics, a basic hypergeometric series, or q-hypergeometric series, is a q-analogue of the ordinary hypergeometric series. It is a power series in which the ratio of successive coefficients is a rational function of qk rather than of k itself. The theory was created by Eduard Heine in the 19th century, who introduced the basic analogues of the Gauss hypergeometric function; such series are therefore sometimes called Heine series.

Definition

The series is written using the q-Pochhammer symbol (a;q)n = (1−a)(1−aq)···(1−aqn−1). The general rφs series has numerator parameters a1,…,ar and denominator parameters b1,…,bs, with general term built from these symbols times zk and sign factors. As q → 1 (with parameters suitably rescaled), the series reduces to the ordinary hypergeometric series rFs.

Key identities

The q-binomial theorem sums the 1φ0 series, while Heine's summation and transformation formulas generalise Gauss's sum and Euler's transformations. The Rogers–Ramanujan identities are evaluations of basic hypergeometric series with deep connections to partitions, modular forms and statistical mechanics; bilateral generalisations (rψs) were studied by Ramanujan and W. N. Bailey. The standard modern reference is Gasper and Rahman's Basic Hypergeometric Series.
