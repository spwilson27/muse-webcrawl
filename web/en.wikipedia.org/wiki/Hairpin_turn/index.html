Hairpin turn

A hairpin turn (also hairpin bend or hairpin corner) is a bend in a road with a very acute inner angle, making it necessary for an oncoming vehicle to turn about 180° to continue on the road. It is named for its resemblance to a bent metal hairpin. Such turns are most often built where a road climbs a steep slope in a limited space, with the road zigzagging up in a series of switchbacks.

Uses

Hairpin turns allow a road or trail to gain elevation gradually over short horizontal distances, reducing the gradient to a manageable level. They are a defining feature of mountain passes in the Alps, Pyrenees, Rockies, and Himalayas, and of famous climbs in cycling races such as the 21 hairpin bends of Alpe d'Huez in the Tour de France and the Stelvio Pass in Italy.

Motorsport

In motor racing, a hairpin is a very slow, tight corner following a long straight, often providing one of the best overtaking opportunities on a circuit. Famous examples include the Fairmont Hairpin (Loews) at the Monaco Grand Prix circuit and the hairpin at Circuit Gilles Villeneuve in Montreal.

Railways

Very rarely, railways also use hairpin or switchback alignments to climb steep grades, though tight turning radii make them impractical for most lines; examples historically existed on narrow-gauge and mountain railways, sometimes requiring trains to reverse direction.

Safety

Hairpin bends demand low speeds and careful braking. Warning signs, mirrors, guardrails, and widened shoulders are commonly installed, and descent requires attention to brake overheating on long mountain roads.
