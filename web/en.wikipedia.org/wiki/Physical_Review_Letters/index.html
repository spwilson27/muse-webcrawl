Physical Review Letters (PRL), established in 1958, is a peer-reviewed, scientific journal that is published 52 times per year by the American Physical Society. The journal is considered one of the most prestigious in physics and related fields, and is intended as a venue for rapid publication of short reports of significant fundamental research in all areas of physics.


Cover of a 2017 issue of Physical Review Letters.

As also confirmed by various measurement standards, which include the Journal Citation Reports impact factor and the journal h-index proposed by Google Scholar, many physicists and other scientists consider Physical Review Letters to be one of the most prestigious journals in the field of physics. Its impact factor has typically been around 8–9.

PRL publishes papers it judges to be of especially broad importance and interest, across subject areas including condensed matter physics, particle physics, astrophysics, atomic and molecular physics, plasma physics, and interdisciplinary areas such as biological physics and quantum information. Articles are limited in length (historically four pages, later slightly longer formats), which encourages concise presentation of key results.

History

The journal grew out of The Physical Review, founded in 1893. In the 1950s, the editor Samuel Goudsmit proposed a separate letters journal for the rapid communication of important new results; Physical Review Letters began publication on 1 July 1958. Its first issue included work that quickly became landmarks of postwar physics.

Famous papers published in PRL include the 1964 papers by Peter Higgs and by François Englert and Robert Brout proposing the mechanism now associated with the Higgs boson; the experimental papers on the J/ψ meson in 1974; foundational papers in quantum information; and the 2016 paper by the LIGO Scientific Collaboration reporting the first direct detection of gravitational waves.

Scope and format

PRL is a letters journal: manuscripts must present important new results concisely and be comprehensible to the broad physics readership. Papers undergo peer review by experts, and accepted articles are published online and in weekly print issues organized by subject section.

Abstracting and indexing

The journal is abstracted and indexed in major bibliographic databases, including Chemical Abstracts, INSPEC, PubMed (for relevant content), Scopus, and the Science Citation Index.
