Roulette (curve)

In differential geometry, a roulette is a curve traced by a point attached to a given curve (the generator or pole) as that curve rolls without slipping along a second, fixed curve. The rolling curve is sometimes called the polode pair: the moving curve rolls on the fixed directrix.

Examples

Familiar roulettes include the cycloid (a point on a circle rolling on a straight line), the epicycloid and hypocycloid (circles rolling on circles), the involute of a circle, nephroids and astroids. If the generator is a line, the resulting roulette is called a Sturm roulette; if the point lies on the rolling curve itself the curves include trochoids.

History

Roulettes were studied by 17th-century mathematicians including Pascal, Roberval and de la Hire, the latter coining usage around 1706. They arise in the design of gear teeth, in Wankel engine housings, and in mechanisms such as the Spirograph toy.
