Minister for Foreign Affairs (Japan)

StyleHis/Her Excellency
Member ofCabinet of Japan
AppointerPrime Minister of Japan
Formation22 December 1885

The Minister for Foreign Affairs (外務大臣, Gaimu Daijin) is a member of the Cabinet of Japan and the head of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The minister is responsible for the conduct of Japan's foreign policy and diplomacy, including its embassies, consulates, treaties and participation in international organisations.

History

The post was established with the cabinet system in 1885, when Itō Hirobumi formed Japan's first modern cabinet and Inoue Kaoru became the first foreign minister under the new system. The minister is appointed by the Prime Minister and confirmed by the Emperor. Foreign ministers have often been senior figures within the governing party, and several — including Shigeru Yoshida, Mamoru Shigemitsu and, in later years, Tarō Asō and Fumio Kishida — went on to become prime minister or are counted among the most influential postwar politicians.

Role

The foreign minister directs Japan's diplomatic service, negotiates treaties, represents Japan at bodies such as the United Nations and G7, and manages policy toward major partners including the United States, China and South Korea, as well as issues such as territorial disputes and official development assistance.
