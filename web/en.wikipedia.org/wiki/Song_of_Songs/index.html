Song of Songs

The Song of Songs (Biblical Hebrew: שִׁיר הַשִּׁירִים, Shir HaShirim), also called the Canticle of Canticles or the Song of Solomon, is a book of the Hebrew Bible and the Christian Old Testament. A collection of love poetry between a woman and a man, it is unique among biblical books in celebrating erotic love without mentioning God, the Law, or Israel's covenant history.

Contents

The book consists largely of a dialogue between two lovers — the woman (called the Shulammite) and the man — along with a chorus of the "daughters of Jerusalem". The poems exchange praise of each other's beauty using vivid imagery from nature and gardens ("Thy name is as ointment poured forth"; "I am the rose of Sharon, and the lily of the valleys"), describe longing and seeking through the night streets, and include a dream sequence and a description of a royal wedding procession. Its most famous lines declare that "love is strong as death" and that "many waters cannot quench love".

Authorship and date

The superscription attributes the song to Solomon, but modern scholars regard this as a later ascription; features of the language, including Persian and possibly Greek loanwords, lead most to date the work to the post-exilic period, perhaps the 4th–3rd centuries BCE, though it may incorporate older material. Its secular character made its place in the canon debated, but Rabbi Akiva famously defended it as "the Holy of Holies".

Interpretation

Jewish tradition read the book allegorically as God's love for Israel, and Christian tradition as Christ's love for the Church (or the soul) — interpretations that dominated for two millennia and inspired commentaries by Origen and Bernard of Clairvaux, as well as much medieval mysticism. Modern scholarship generally reads it literally, as love poetry related to ancient Near Eastern love songs and wedding songs (wasfs). In Jewish liturgy it is read on the Sabbath during Passover.
