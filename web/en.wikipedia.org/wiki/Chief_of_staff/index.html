Chief of staff

Chief of staff
ContextsGovernment, military, corporations, hospitals
RolePrincipal aide and manager of a leader's office and staff
Prominent exampleWhite House Chief of Staff

A chief of staff is the leader of a supporting staff or the principal aide to a person or organization, such as a head of government, military commander, legislator, or corporate executive. The role typically involves managing the leader's schedule and communications, coordinating subordinate staff, filtering information, and acting as a trusted adviser and gatekeeper.

Government

The best-known example is the White House Chief of Staff, the highest-ranking member of the Executive Office of the President of the United States, overseeing the president's staff, controlling access to the president, and advising on policy and politics. Similar posts exist for governors, mayors, members of legislatures, and ministers in many countries.

Military origin

The term originates in military staff systems of the 18th and 19th centuries, in which a chief of staff coordinated the specialised branches (operations, intelligence, logistics) serving a commander. Senior service chiefs, such as the Chief of Staff of the Army, remain examples of the title.

Corporate and other sectors

In business, chiefs of staff support CEOs by running strategic initiatives and coordinating executive teams; in healthcare the title also denotes the elected head of a hospital's medical staff. In all settings the office's influence depends on the incumbent's proximity to the principal.
