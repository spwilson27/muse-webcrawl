The Wars of Scottish Independence were a series of military campaigns fought between the Kingdom of Scotland and the Kingdom of England in the late 13th and 14th centuries. They began after the death of Alexander III of Scotland in 1286 and the disputed succession that followed, which Edward I of England sought to exploit to establish overlordship over Scotland.

The First War (1296–1328) saw the resistance led first by William Wallace and Andrew de Moray, victors at the Battle of Stirling Bridge (1297), and then by Robert the Bruce, whose victory at the Battle of Bannockburn (1314) secured his kingship. The Declaration of Arbroath (1320) asserted Scotland's independence to the Pope, and the war ended with the Treaty of Edinburgh–Northampton (1328), by which England recognised Robert I as king of an independent Scotland.

The First War (1296–1328)

Edward I invaded in 1296, deposed King John Balliol, and carried the Stone of Scone to Westminster. Wallace's uprising in 1297 was crushed at the Battle of Falkirk (1298), and Wallace himself was captured and executed in 1305. Robert the Bruce crowned himself king in 1306 and, after years of guerrilla warfare, won a decisive victory over Edward II at Bannockburn in June 1314. Scottish raids into northern England and the diplomatic campaign culminating in the Declaration of Arbroath eventually compelled recognition of independence.

The Second War (1332–1357)

War resumed when Edward Balliol, backed by Edward III of England, invaded in 1332 and won at Dupplin Moor and Halidon Hill (1333). The young David II was sent to safety in France, and the conflict became a theatre of the Hundred Years' War. David's invasion of England ended in his capture at the Battle of Neville's Cross (1346); he was released eleven years later under the Treaty of Berwick (1357), which ended the wars without resolving the underlying rivalry.

Legacy

The wars fixed Scotland's status as an independent kingdom until the Union of the Crowns in 1603 and the Acts of Union in 1707, and produced enduring national symbols in Wallace and Bruce.
