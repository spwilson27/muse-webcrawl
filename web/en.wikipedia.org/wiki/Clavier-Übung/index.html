Clavier-Übung

Clavier-Übung (sometimes Germanized as Klavierübung, "keyboard practice" or "keyboard exercise") is the title of a series of works by Johann Sebastian Bach, published between 1726 and 1741 and containing some of his most significant music for keyboard instruments. The title derives from earlier works by Johann Kuhnau and others.

The four parts

Part I (1726–1731) consists of the six Partitas, BWV 825–830, Bach's first published works. Part II (1735) contains the Italian Concerto, BWV 971, and the French Overture, BWV 831. Part III (1739), written for organ, includes the Prelude and Fugue in E-flat major and chorale preludes framed by the "German Organ Mass". Part IV (1741) is the Goldberg Variations, BWV 988, an aria with thirty variations for two-manual harpsichord.

Significance

Taken together, the four parts present a compendium of keyboard styles and techniques of Bach's time. The series is among the most ambitious keyboard publications of the Baroque era.
