Coping saw

A coping saw with its thin blade held in a deep C-shaped frame.

A coping saw is a type of bow saw used to cut intricate external shapes and interior cut-outs in woodworking or carpentry. It is widely used to cut moldings to create coped rather than mitered joints. It is also occasionally used to cut metal, in which case it is known as a piercing saw.

Construction

A coping saw consists of a thin, hardened steel blade, typically about 150 mm (6 in) long, stretched between the ends of a square, C-shaped ("deep-throated") frame, with a handle at one end. The blade is easily removed from the frame and can be passed through a drilled hole in the workpiece to make interior cuts. The blade is held under tension, and its orientation relative to the frame can be rotated so the saw can cut in any direction.

Use

Coping saws are traditionally used to cut the coped joints that join skirting boards (baseboards) and crown moldings in interior corners: one piece is cut square, and the adjoining piece is coped — cut with the coping saw to follow the profile of the first. The fine blade also makes the saw suitable for fretwork, marquetry and fine curved cuts in thin stock.

Comparison with other saws

The coping saw is similar to the fretsaw, but the fretsaw has a much deeper frame and finer blade for very delicate work, whereas the coping saw is sturdier and intended for somewhat heavier cuts. Both differ from the hacksaw, whose frame keeps the blade fixed in one orientation and which is intended primarily for metal.
