List of shipwrecks in the Great Lakes

The Great Lakes, a collection of five freshwater lakes in North America with a reputation for severe autumn storms, have been the site of more than 6,000 shipwrecks over the past three centuries of navigation. This list of shipwrecks in the Great Lakes catalogues notable vessels lost on lakes Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie and Ontario.

Notable wrecks

The first recorded loss is often taken to be Le Griffon, René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La Salle's vessel, which vanished on Lake Michigan in 1679. The 19th century saw heavy losses of schooners and steamers, including the side-wheel steamer Lady Elgin (1860, Lake Michigan, about 300 lives lost) and the Eastland disaster of 1915 in the Chicago River, in which 844 people died. The Great Lakes Storm of 1913 ("the White Hurricane") destroyed 19 ships and killed more than 250 sailors, mainly on Lake Huron. In the modern era the most famous loss is the ore carrier SS Edmund Fitzgerald, which sank with all 29 crew in Lake Superior on 10 November 1975 and was immortalised in Gordon Lightfoot's song "The Wreck of the Edmund Fitzgerald". Other well-known wrecks include the Carl D. Bradley (1958), Daniel J. Morrell (1966), Cedarville (1965) and John M. Osborn, as well as innumerable wooden schooners preserved in the cold fresh water.

Preservation

Because the lakes are cold and fresh, many wrecks survive in exceptional condition, and several are protected within state underwater preserves and national marine sanctuaries such as the Thunder Bay National Marine Sanctuary in Lake Huron.
