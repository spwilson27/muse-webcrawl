Mali Lošinj

Mali Lošinj (Italian: Lussinpiccolo) is a town on the island of Lošinj in the Primorje-Gorski Kotar County of western Croatia, in the northern Adriatic Sea. Despite the name (mali means "small"), it is larger than the nearby town of Veli Lošinj ("big") — a naming inversion dating from when Veli Lošinj was the larger settlement.

History and tourism

The town was first mentioned in 1398. In the nineteenth century, under Habsburg rule, Lošinj became a noted shipbuilding and maritime centre and a fashionable health resort for the Austro-Hungarian aristocracy, prized for its mild climate and pine forests. Today tourism is the main industry. Mali Lošinj is also known for the Apoxyomenos, a remarkably preserved ancient Greek bronze statue of an athlete recovered from the sea nearby in 1999 and now displayed in a dedicated museum in the town.
