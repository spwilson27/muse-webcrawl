Greater London is the administrative area of London, England, and a ceremonial county and region of England. Created in 1965, it comprises the historic City of London and 32 London boroughs, covering 1,572 square kilometres and home to about 8.9 million people — the most populous city-region in the United Kingdom and one of the largest in Europe.

Governance

Local government in Greater London is two-tier: the 32 borough councils and the City of London Corporation deliver most services, while the Greater London Authority — a directly elected Mayor of London and a 25-member London Assembly, created in 2000 after the earlier Greater London Council was abolished in 1986 — holds strategic powers over transport (Transport for London), policing oversight, fire, and planning. Greater London also functions as a ceremonial county (excluding the City, which has its own Lord Lieutenant) and returns 73 Members of Parliament.

Geography and demography

Greater London stretches about 58 km east–west along the Thames valley, from Heathrow and Uxbridge in the west to Havering in the east, and is encircled by the M25 motorway and the Metropolitan Green Belt. It is one of the world's most ethnically diverse urban areas: the 2021 census recorded that around 37% of residents were born abroad and over 300 languages are spoken.

History

The County of London (1889) covered only the inner urban core; as the built-up area sprawled into Middlesex, Kent, Surrey, Essex and Hertfordshire, reform became unavoidable. The Herbert Commission led to the London Government Act 1963, creating Greater London in 1965, since when its boundaries have been essentially stable.
