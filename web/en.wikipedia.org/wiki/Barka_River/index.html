The Barka River (Arabic: Nahr Baraka) is a seasonal river that flows from the Eritrean Highlands westward into the lowlands of Eritrea and across the border into Sudan. Rising south of Asmara, it joins with its principal tributary, the Anseba River, in the western lowlands before dissipating into the inland Barka Delta near Tokar in Sudan, short of the Red Sea.

Course

From its headwaters in the central Eritrean highlands, the Barka descends steeply to the western escarpment and crosses the arid Gash-Barka region — an administrative region of Eritrea named for the Barka and the neighbouring Gash (Mareb) rivers. At Agordat the river passes one of the principal towns of the Eritrean lowlands. Its flow is highly seasonal: dry for much of the year, it can turn into a powerful torrent during the summer rains, carrying heavy loads of fertile silt down from the highlands.

Delta and agriculture

In Sudan the river spreads into an inland delta where floodwaters are used for sorghum and cotton cultivation, and where the rich alluvial deposits support grazing for the herds of the Beni-Amer and other local peoples. The Tokar Delta scheme is one of Sudan's older irrigation developments.

History

The Barka valley was contested during the colonial period between Italian Eritrea and Anglo-Egyptian Sudan, and again during the Eritrean War of Independence, when the lowlands formed a key theatre of operations. The river gives its name to the former province of Barka and to the modern Gash-Barka Region.
