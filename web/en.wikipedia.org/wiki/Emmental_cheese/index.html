Emmental, Emmentaler, or Emmenthal is a yellow, medium-hard cheese that originated in the Emme valley in the canton of Bern, Switzerland. It is classified as a Swiss-type cheese and is distinguished by its large internal holes ("eyes") and its mild, slightly nutty and buttery flavor.

Production

Emmental is made from raw cow's milk with three bacterial cultures, including Propionibacterium freudenreichii, which consumes lactic acid during warm-room ripening and releases carbon dioxide, forming the characteristic bubbles that become the holes. Wheels typically weigh 75–120 kg (165–265 lb) and are aged at least four months; wheels labeled Emmentaler AOP must meet strict Swiss standards for origin, feed, and manufacture. Imitation "Swiss cheese" is produced worldwide, notably in France (where Emmental is the most-produced cheese), Germany, and the United States.

Uses

Emmental melts smoothly and is used in gratins, sandwiches, and, traditionally mixed with Gruyère, in fondue.
