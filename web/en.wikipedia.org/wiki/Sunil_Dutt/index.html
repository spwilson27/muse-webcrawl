Sunil Dutt (born Balraj Dutt; 6 June 1929 – 25 May 2005) was an Indian actor, film producer, director, and politician. One of the major stars of Hindi cinema in the 1950s and 1960s, he later served five terms as a Congress Member of Parliament and was Union Minister for Youth Affairs and Sports in the government of Manmohan Singh.

Early life and film career

Born in what is now Pakistan, Dutt's family resettled in Haryana after Partition. He came to Bombay and worked as a radio announcer at Radio Ceylon while studying at Jai Hind College. He made his film debut in Railway Platform (1955). His breakthrough came with Mehboob Khan's Mother India (1957), in which he played Birju, the rebellious son of the heroine played by Nargis; during filming he rescued Nargis from a fire on set, and they married in 1958.

He went on to star in classics including Sadhna (1958), Sujata (1959), Mujhe Jeene Do (1963) — playing a dacoit, a type of role he made his own — Waqt (1965), Hamraaz (1967), and Padosan (1968), which showcased his comic talent. As producer-director he made films such as Yaadein (1964), notable as a one-actor film, and launched his son Sanjay Dutt in Rocky (1981). His final screen appearance was a cameo in Munna Bhai M.B.B.S. (2003), alongside Sanjay.

Public life

Dutt was known for humanitarian work: he led peace marches, including a 1987 march from Mumbai to Amritsar during the Punjab insurgency, and founded the Nargis Dutt Foundation for cancer patients after his wife's death from pancreatic cancer in 1981 — an experience that also led him to support his son through widely reported struggles with drug addiction and legal troubles. Elected to the Lok Sabha from Mumbai North West in 1984, he was re-elected repeatedly and became Minister of Youth Affairs and Sports in 2004. He received the Padma Shri in 1968 and the Dadasaheb Phalke Award in 1995. He died of a heart attack in Mumbai on 25 May 2005; his daughter Priya Dutt succeeded him in Parliament.
