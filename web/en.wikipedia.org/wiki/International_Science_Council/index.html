The International Science Council (ISC) is a non-governmental organization that unites scientific bodies at the international, regional and national levels. It was formed in 2018 by the merger of the International Council for Science (ICSU, formerly the International Council of Scientific Unions) and the International Social Science Council (ISSC), making it the largest organization of its type and the only global NGO bringing together the natural and social sciences.

Mission

The ISC's mission is to act as the global voice for science, catalysing and convening scientific expertise on issues of major concern to science and society, including sustainable development, global environmental change, science policy and the defence of the scientific endeavour. It works closely with the United Nations system and other international bodies.

Activities

The Council sponsors international research programmes, networks and committees, including bodies formerly under ICSU such as the Scientific Committee on Antarctic Research (SCAR) and committees on space research and oceanic research. It plays a leading role in science for the Sustainable Development Goals and in coordinating international science advice to policymakers.

History

ICSU was founded in 1931 (succeeding the International Research Council of 1919) and the ISSC in 1952. After years of increasing cooperation, the members of both organizations voted in 2017 to merge, and the ISC was formally launched at a general assembly in Paris in July 2018.
