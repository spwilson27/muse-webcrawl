Constituent Assembly of Nepal

The Constituent Assembly of Nepal (Nepali: Saṁvidhāna Sabhā) was the body elected to draft a new constitution for Nepal following the end of the civil war and the abolition of the monarchy. Two successive assemblies sat: the first from 2008 to 2012, which failed to promulgate a constitution, and the second from 2013 to 2015, which adopted the Constitution of Nepal on 20 September 2015, transforming the assembly into the Legislature Parliament.

First Constituent Assembly (2008–2012)

Elections were held on 10 April 2008 as part of the peace process that ended the decade-long civil war. The Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist) emerged as the largest party. The assembly's first meeting on 28 May 2008 declared Nepal a federal democratic republic, ending the 240-year-old Shah monarchy. Repeated extensions of its term failed to resolve disputes over federalism and the form of government, and the assembly was dissolved on 27 May 2012 without a constitution.

Second Constituent Assembly (2013–2015)

A second election on 19 November 2013 returned the Nepali Congress as the largest party, followed by the CPN (UML). Drafting accelerated after the April 2015 earthquake, and the new constitution was promulgated on 20 September 2015 by President Ram Baran Yadav. Nepal was declared a federal democratic republic divided into seven provinces. The assembly then converted itself into the Legislature Parliament.
