Walther Nernst

Walther Hermann Nernst (25 June 1864 – 18 November 1941) was a German physicist and chemist who helped establish the modern field of physical chemistry. He won the 1920 Nobel Prize in Chemistry "in recognition of his work in thermochemistry". He formulated the Nernst equation and the third law of thermodynamics, known as the Nernst heat theorem.

Career

Nernst studied at Zurich, Berlin, Graz, and Würzburg, worked with Ludwig Boltzmann and Friedrich Kohlrausch, and founded the Institute for Physical Chemistry at Göttingen in 1895. In 1905 he moved to Berlin. He invented the Nernst lamp, an early incandescent light source, and contributed to electrochemistry, including the theory of the electrolytic dissociation and the galvanic cell potential that bears his name.

Later years

Both of Nernst's sons were killed in the First World War. Under the Nazi regime he retired from university life to his country estate, where he died in 1941.
