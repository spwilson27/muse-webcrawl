Tarot de Marseille

The Tarot de Marseille (French: Tarot de Marseille, "Tarot of Marseille") is one of the standard patterns of tarot cards, originating in Italy and popularised in France, from which most modern tarot decks descend. It is a 78-card deck: 56 cards in four suits (the pièces or minor arcana), 21 numbered trump cards (atouts) and one unnumbered card, the Fool (Le Mat).

Design

The Marseille pattern is produced by woodblock printing with colours applied through stencils, in a limited palette of blue, red, yellow, green and flesh tones. Its trumps follow the order and iconography of early Italian tarot: I the Juggler (Le Bateleur), II the Popess (La Papesse), III the Empress, IV the Emperor, V the Pope, VI the Lovers, VII the Chariot, VIII Justice, IX the Hermit, X the Wheel of Fortune, XI Strength, XII the Hanged Man, XIII (unnamed) Death, XIV Temperance, XV the Devil, XVI the Tower, XVII the Star, XVIII the Moon, XIX the Sun, XX Judgement, XXI the World. The suit cards use Latin suits — batons, coins, cups and swords — with pip arrangements and floral decoration.

Use

The deck was created for tarot card games, which are still played in France (Jeu du Tarot) and elsewhere. From the late 18th century, occultists such as Court de Gébelin and Etteilla promoted the Marseille tarot as a tool of divination, and it remains, alongside the Rider–Waite deck, one of the most used decks for cartomancy; influential 20th-century restorations include those of Paul Marteau (1930) and Alejandro Jodorowsky with Philippe Camoin (1997).
