Carsharing

Carsharing is a model of car rental in which people rent cars for short periods of time, often by the minute or hour, typically through a membership scheme and smartphone app. It differs from traditional car rental in its self-service access, distributed neighbourhood locations, short rental periods, and pricing that bundles fuel, insurance, and parking. Carsharing is a form of the sharing economy and of mobility-as-a-service.

Models

  • Round-trip (station-based): vehicles are picked up and returned to the same designated parking spot; Switzerland's Mobility cooperative, founded in 1997, is a leading example.
  • One-way / free-floating: vehicles can be left anywhere within a service zone, pioneered by services such as car2go and DriveNow (merged as Share Now).
  • Peer-to-peer: private owners rent out their own cars through a platform, e.g. Turo and Getaround.

History

The earliest carsharing scheme was Sefage in Zurich in 1948. Modern carsharing took off in Switzerland and Germany in the late 1980s and 1990s, then spread worldwide; Zipcar, founded in Boston in 2000, popularised the model in North America. By the 2010s, major car manufacturers and rental companies operated large free-floating fleets, many of them electric.

Effects

Studies commonly find that members drive less and shed personal vehicles: each shared car is estimated to replace roughly 9–13 private cars, and carsharing households show reduced vehicle-kilometres travelled and emissions, particularly when combined with public transit use.
