Kirchenaustritt

In Germany, formally leaving a church is an act performed before a state authority.

Kirchenaustritt (German for "leaving the church") is the formal act by which a member resigns membership of a religious community, particularly in Germany, Austria, and Switzerland, where church membership has civil and tax consequences. A person who has declared a Kirchenaustritt is no longer liable for the church tax (Kirchensteuer) and generally loses rights such as church weddings, godparenthood, and burial by the church.

Procedure in Germany

In most German states the declaration is made in person at the local registry office (Standesamt) or district court, for a fee of roughly €25–€60; in Baden-Württemberg and some other states a notary may be involved. The state notifies the church and the tax office, and the church tax ceases from the end of the month of resignation. The Catholic Church regards formal resignation as also having canonical consequences, excluding the person from the sacraments, while the person remains baptised and, in Catholic theology, retains an indelible character of baptism.

Numbers

Resignations rose sharply in Germany during the 2010s and 2020s: in 2022 more than 500,000 people left the Catholic Church alone, a record figure, with several hundred thousand more leaving the Protestant churches, amid abuse scandals and declining religiosity.
