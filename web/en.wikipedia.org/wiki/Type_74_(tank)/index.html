The Type 74 (74式戦車, Nanayonshiki sensha) is a main battle tank of the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force (JGSDF). It was built by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries as a replacement for the earlier Type 61 tank and entered service in 1975. Designed during the Cold War to counter contemporary Soviet armour such as the T-62, it combined a licence-built 105 mm Royal Ordnance L7 rifled gun with a distinctive hydropneumatic suspension that allowed the hull to tilt and adjust its ground clearance.

Design

The Type 74 used a conventional layout with a four-man crew and a cast steel turret and welded hull. Its Mitsubishi 10ZF diesel engine produced about 750 horsepower, giving a road speed of roughly 53 km/h. The adjustable suspension, a feature shared with few other production tanks, let the vehicle kneel, lean, or raise itself to suit Japan's mountainous terrain and to increase gun depression when firing from ridges. Later production vehicles and upgrades added a laser rangefinder, a ballistic computer, thermal imaging for the gunner, and NBC protection.

Service and retirement

A total of 873 Type 74s were produced between 1975 and 1989. The tank never saw combat. Although progressively supplemented and then superseded by the Type 90 and Type 10 tanks, upgraded Type 74s remained in service for decades in reconnaissance and armoured units. The JGSDF formally retired the type in 2024, ending nearly fifty years of service.
