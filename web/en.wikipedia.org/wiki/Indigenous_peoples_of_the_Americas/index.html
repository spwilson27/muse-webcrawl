Indigenous peoples of the Americas

The Indigenous peoples of the Americas are the peoples who inhabited the Americas before European colonisation in the 15th century, and their descendants today.

Total populationAbout 60 million (estimates vary)
RegionsNorth, Central and South America, Caribbean
LanguagesHundreds, incl. Quechua, Navajo, Maya, Guaraní

History

Ancestors migrated from Asia across Beringia at least 15,000 years ago. Societies ranged from nomadic hunter-gatherers to empires such as the Aztec, Maya and Inca, with agriculture centred on maize, beans and squash.

Colonisation and impact

European colonisation brought warfare, enslavement and diseases such as smallpox that caused catastrophic population decline, alongside displacement from land. Resistance and cultural survival continued everywhere.

Contemporary peoples

Today Indigenous nations pursue sovereignty, language revival and land rights. Terms vary by country: Native American, First Nations, American Indian, pueblos indígenas. See Migration.
