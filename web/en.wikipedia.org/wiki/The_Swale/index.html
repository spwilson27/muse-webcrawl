The Swale

The Swale is a tidal channel in Kent, England, separating the Isle of Sheppey from the mainland. About 14 miles (23 km) long, it stretches from the confluence with the River Medway near Queenborough westward... rather eastward to Faversham and the Swale estuary, where it meets the Thames Estuary. Despite its name, it is not a swale in the geomorphological sense but a strait of the sea.

Geography

The Swale is crossed by the Kingsferry Bridge and the Sheppey Crossing, which carry road and rail traffic to the Isle of Sheppey. Its mudflats and saltmarshes are an internationally important habitat for wading birds and wildfowl. Much of the channel and its shores form part of The Swale Site of Special Scientific Interest and a Ramsar wetland.

History

In Roman and Anglo-Saxon times The Swale was an important sheltered shipping route between the Thames and the Kent ports. The town of Faversham, at the head of a navigable creek off The Swale, was a member of the Cinque Ports confederation. The channel was historically noted for its oyster fisheries. During the Second World War, defences were built along its shores; the wreck of the explosives-laden SS Richard Montgomery lies near its western entrance, off Sheerness.

Ecology

The extensive intertidal flats support large wintering populations of birds including brent geese, shelduck, avocets and dunlin, and the area is managed in part as nature reserves by conservation bodies.
