Valar

The Valar (singular Vala) are characters in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, described in The Silmarillion. They are the fourteen powerful Ainur who entered the world of Arda after its creation to prepare it for the coming of Elves and Men, acting as its guardians and rulers under the authority of Eru Ilúvatar. In function they resemble gods or archangels of Tolkien's mythology.

The fourteen Valar

The Valar comprise seven Lords and seven Queens. The Lords are Manwë, lord of the winds and King of the Valar; Ulmo, lord of waters; Aulë the smith; Oromë the huntsman; Mandos, judge of the dead; Lórien, master of dreams; and Tulkas, the strongest and most warlike. The Queens, the Valier, are Varda, kindler of the stars and wife of Manwë; Yavanna, giver of fruits; Nienna, lady of pity; Estë the healer; Vairë the weaver; Vána the ever-young; and Nessa the dancer. A fifteenth Ainu, Melkor, mightiest of all, was cast out from their number for his rebellion and became the first Dark Lord, Morgoth; his servant Sauron is the antagonist of The Lord of the Rings.

Role in the mythology

The Valar dwelt first in Middle-earth, then withdrew to the blessed realm of Valinor in the continent of Aman, where they raised the Two Trees that gave light to the world. Their history in The Silmarillion is dominated by the long war with Melkor, the summoning of the Elves to Valinor, and the eventual War of Wrath that ended the First Age. Thereafter they intervened only rarely in Middle-earth, most notably by sending the Istari (Wizards) to aid the free peoples against Sauron. Maiar are lesser Ainur of the same order, among them Gandalf, Saruman, and the Balrogs.
