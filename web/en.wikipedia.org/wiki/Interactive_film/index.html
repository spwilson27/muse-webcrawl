Interactive film

An interactive film is a work of cinema or video in which viewers make choices that influence the course of the narrative, branching the story into different scenes and endings.

History

Early experiments include the Czechoslovak film Kinoautomat (1967), where audiences voted at decision points. The format developed through laserdisc arcade games such as Dragon's Lair (1983), full-motion video games of the 1990s, and streaming releases such as Netflix's Black Mirror: Bandersnatch (2018), which popularised branching narratives for mainstream audiences.

Characteristics

Interactive films sit between cinema and video games. Critics debate whether viewer choices deepen engagement or merely create an illusion of agency, since most productions converge their branches onto a limited number of endings.
