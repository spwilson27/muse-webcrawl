Areas of mathematics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mathematics can be broadly divided into the study of quantity, structure, space, and change.

Mathematics is a broad discipline that can be divided into many areas. Traditionally, mathematics was grouped into pure mathematics, studied for its own sake, and applied mathematics, studied for direct practical use. There is no general consensus among mathematicians about a common definition for their academic discipline, nor a universally accepted division of the subject into areas; the boundaries between fields are fluid and shift as new connections are discovered.

Overview

A traditional division of mathematics is into the study of quantity (arithmetic and number theory), structure (algebra), space (geometry), and change (mathematical analysis). In addition, there are subdivisions devoted to exploring links to other fields: logic and set theory, which study the foundations of mathematics; statistics and decision sciences, of an empirical character; and computer science, which studies computation and algorithms.

Foundations

The foundations of mathematics include mathematical logic, set theory, and category theory. Logic studies formal systems, proof, and computability; set theory provides the standard framework in which most of mathematics is formulated (usually Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with the axiom of choice). Category theory, developed in the mid-20th century, describes mathematical structures and their relationships at a high level of abstraction.

Number theory

Number theory is the study of the integers and arithmetic functions. Once called simply "arithmetic", it includes elementary topics such as divisibility and prime numbers as well as advanced branches like analytic number theory, algebraic number theory, and Diophantine geometry.

Algebra

Algebra is the study of mathematical structures such as groups, rings, fields, and vector spaces. It grew from elementary equation-solving to abstract algebra, which studies axioms and operations in full generality. Linear algebra, the study of vector spaces and linear maps, is fundamental throughout mathematics and its applications.

Geometry

Geometry studies shapes, sizes, and the properties of space. It includes Euclidean geometry, differential geometry, topology, algebraic geometry, and discrete geometry. Topology, sometimes called "rubber-sheet geometry", studies properties preserved under continuous deformations.

Analysis

Mathematical analysis deals with limits and related notions such as differentiation, integration, measure, and infinite series. It includes real analysis, complex analysis, functional analysis, and the theory of differential equations.

Discrete mathematics and combinatorics

Discrete mathematics studies structures that are fundamentally discrete rather than continuous, including combinatorics (the study of counting and arrangements), graph theory, and the theory of computation.

Applied mathematics

Applied mathematics concerns mathematical methods used in science, engineering, business, and industry. It includes probability and statistics, numerical analysis, optimization, mathematical physics, financial mathematics, and operations research.

Classification

The Mathematics Subject Classification (MSC), maintained by Mathematical Reviews and zbMATH, is the most widely used scheme for classifying mathematical literature, dividing the subject into over 60 top-level areas. Similarly, the Library of Congress and Dewey Decimal classifications organize mathematical works for libraries.
