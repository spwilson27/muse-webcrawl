Xuan paper

Xuan paper (Chinese: 宣紙; pinyin: xuānzhǐ) is a traditional handmade paper originating in Jing County, Anhui Province, China, named after the Tang-dynasty prefecture of Xuanzhou that administered the area. For over a thousand years it has been the preferred support for Chinese calligraphy and ink painting because of its softness, absorbency, durability, and resistance to insects and aging.

Manufacture

Genuine Xuan paper is made from the bark fiber of the blue sandalwood tree (Pteroceltis tatarinowii) mixed with rice straw, in proportions that vary by grade. Production involves more than a hundred steps over a year or more: soaking, fermenting, steaming, bleaching in the sun, pulping, sheet-forming by hand on bamboo moulds, pressing, and drying sheet by sheet on heated walls. The craft was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2009.

Types and use

Unsized "raw" Xuan (shengxuan) absorbs ink freely and is used for expressive ink-wash painting; alum-sized "ripe" Xuan (shuxuan) resists bleeding and suits detailed gongbi work and small-script calligraphy. Because well-made Xuan can survive for centuries, it is also used for mounting, rubbings, and archival documents.
