Transport for Wales (TfW; Welsh: Trafnidiaeth Cymru) is a not-for-profit company owned by the Welsh Government and established in 2016 to oversee public transport in Wales. TfW originally acted as the procurement agency for the Wales and Borders rail franchise; since 7 February 2021 it has operated the franchise directly, through its subsidiary Transport for Wales Rail, after the Welsh Government took the franchise into public ownership in response to the collapse in passenger revenue during the COVID-19 pandemic.

History

The Welsh Government established Transport for Wales in 2016 as an arms-length body to provide expertise on transport projects, beginning with the procurement of the new Wales and Borders franchise and the South Wales Metro. In 2018 the franchise was awarded to KeolisAmey Wales, trading as Transport for Wales Rail Services. In October 2020, the Welsh Government announced it would take the franchise under public control, with operations transferring to the public-sector operator of last resort in February 2021.

Responsibilities

  • Operation of Wales and Borders rail services (Transport for Wales Rail).
  • Development of the South Wales Metro, an integrated rail and bus network centred on Cardiff, including electrification of the Core Valley Lines and new fleets such as the Class 231, 756 and 398 trains.
  • Strategic planning for bus services and active travel in Wales, and management of the TrawsCymru long-distance bus network.
  • The fflecsi demand-responsive bus pilots.
