Andrew Bonar Law (16 September 1858 – 30 October 1923) was a British statesman and Conservative politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from October 1922 to May 1923. Born in the British colony of New Brunswick (now in Canada), he was the first prime minister born outside the British Isles and the first to serve entirely in the 20th century whose time in office is remembered primarily for his leadership of the Conservative Party between 1911 and 1923.

Early life

Law was born in Kingston, New Brunswick, the son of a Presbyterian minister of Ulster Scots descent. After his mother's death, he was sent to Scotland at the age of twelve to be raised by wealthy cousins, the Kidstons, and was educated at the High School of Glasgow. He entered the family iron-merchant business, William Kidston & Sons, and became a prosperous businessman and a director of the Clydesdale Bank.

Political career

Law entered Parliament in 1900 as MP for Glasgow Blackfriars and Hutchesontown. He served as Parliamentary Secretary to the Board of Trade under Arthur Balfour. Following the Conservatives' defeat in 1906 he became a leading figure on the tariff reform wing of the party. When Balfour resigned the leadership in 1911, Law was elected as a compromise candidate.

During the First World War he served in the coalition governments of H. H. Asquith and David Lloyd George, as Colonial Secretary and then as Chancellor of the Exchequer (1916–1919) and Leader of the House of Commons. Known for his austere manner and prodigious memory, he was nicknamed "the unknown Prime Minister"—a phrase later borrowed as the title of his biography by Robert Blake.

Premiership

In October 1922 Law led the revolt of Conservative backbenchers at the famous Carlton Club meeting, which ended the Lloyd George coalition. He became prime minister and won the 1922 general election. His government was short-lived: diagnosed with terminal throat cancer, he resigned in May 1923 and died that October.
