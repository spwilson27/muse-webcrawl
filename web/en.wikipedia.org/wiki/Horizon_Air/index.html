Horizon Air is an American regional airline headquartered in SeaTac, Washington, near Seattle–Tacoma International Airport. It is a wholly owned subsidiary of the Alaska Air Group and flies as Alaska Horizon for Alaska Airlines, operating regional routes primarily in the Pacific Northwest and western United States.

History

Horizon Air was founded in May 1981 by Seattle-area entrepreneur Milt Kuolt II and began operations in September 1981 with Fairchild F-27 aircraft, initially serving Yakima, Pasco, and Pullman from Seattle. The airline grew rapidly through the 1980s, serving communities across Washington, Oregon, and Idaho.

In 1986 Horizon was acquired by the Alaska Air Group. It later pioneered the introduction of the De Havilland Canada Dash 8 in the United States and became the launch customer for the Bombardier Q400 turboprop, which for many years formed the backbone of its fleet.

Operations

Since 2011, Horizon's aircraft have been painted in Alaska Airlines colours under the "Alaska Horizon" brand, while Horizon retains its own operating certificate and crews. The airline gradually retired its Q400 turboprops and transitioned to an all-Embraer 175 jet fleet, with the last Q400 flights taking place in January 2023.

Incidents

On 10 August 2018, a Horizon Air Q400 was stolen from Seattle–Tacoma International Airport by an airline ground service agent, Richard Russell, who took off without authorisation and later crashed the aircraft on Ketron Island in Puget Sound, killing himself. The incident prompted reviews of airport and airline ground-crew security procedures.
