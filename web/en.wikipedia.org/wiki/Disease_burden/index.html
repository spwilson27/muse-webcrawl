Disease burden

Disease burden is the impact of a health problem as measured by mortality, morbidity, financial cost, or other indicators. It is often quantified in terms of quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) or disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), which combine years lost due to premature death with years lived with disability.

Global burden of disease

The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study, launched by the World Bank and the World Health Organization in the early 1990s and now coordinated by the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME), comprehensively assesses mortality and disability from hundreds of diseases, injuries, and risk factors worldwide. It allows health burdens to be compared across conditions, countries, and time.

Uses

Estimates of disease burden inform public health priorities, resource allocation, and cost-effectiveness analyses of interventions. Burden may be expressed overall or attributable to specific risk factors such as tobacco use, air pollution, or high blood pressure. Environmental and occupational disease burdens are also estimated for particular exposures.
