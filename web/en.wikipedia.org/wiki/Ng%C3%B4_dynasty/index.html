The Ngô dynasty (Vietnamese: Nhà Ngô) was the first Vietnamese dynasty to rule an independent Vietnam after nearly a millennium of Chinese domination. It was founded by Ngô Quyền, whose victory at the Battle of Bạch Đằng in 938 defeated the Southern Han fleet and ended the third period of Chinese rule.

History

Ngô Quyền proclaimed himself king in 939 and established his capital at Cổ Loa, the ancient citadel near modern Hanoi. After his death in 944, his brother-in-law Dương Tam Kha usurped the throne; Ngô Quyền's sons eventually recovered power but ruled jointly and weakly, and the kingdom fragmented into the rival warlords of the Anarchy of the Twelve Warlords after 965, until reunification by Đinh Bộ Lĩnh.

Legacy

Though short-lived and unstable, the dynasty marks the restoration of Vietnamese independence, commemorated ever since in Vietnamese historiography.
