Boroughs of Montreal

The city of Montreal is divided into 19 boroughs (French: arrondissements), each with its own borough council and mayor, responsible for local services such as parks, recreation, road maintenance, and urban planning permits. The borough system took its modern form following the municipal mergers of 2002, when the suburbs on the Island of Montreal were amalgamated into a single city, and the subsequent partial demergers of 2006.

List of boroughs

The boroughs include Ahuntsic-Cartierville, Anjou, Côte-des-Neiges–Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, Lachine, LaSalle, Le Plateau-Mont-Royal, Le Sud-Ouest, L'Île-Bizard–Sainte-Geneviève, Mercier–Hochelaga-Maisonneuve, Montréal-Nord, Outremont, Pierrefonds-Roxboro, Rivière-des-Prairies–Pointe-aux-Trembles, Rosemont–La Petite-Patrie, Saint-Laurent, Saint-Léonard, Verdun, Ville-Marie, and Villeray–Saint-Michel–Parc-Extension. Ville-Marie, which contains the downtown core and Old Montreal, has a special status: the mayor of Montreal serves as its borough mayor.

Governance

Borough councils are elected by borough residents and manage local budgets, while the central city council handles island-wide services such as police, fire protection, public transit, and major infrastructure. This two-tier arrangement within a single city reflects the political compromise reached after the controversial merger and demerger referendums.
