New Youth

New Youth (Xin Qingnian, 新青年) was an influential Chinese magazine founded in Shanghai in 1915 by Chen Duxiu. It became the leading journal of the New Culture Movement, advocating science, democracy, vernacular literature and the rejection of Confucian tradition.

History

Chen launched the magazine in September 1915 under the title Youth Magazine, renaming it New Youth the following year. Contributors included Hu Shi, whose 1917 essay proposed reforming literature in favour of the vernacular baihua; Lu Xun, whose first major story, "Diary of a Madman" (1918), appeared in its pages; and Li Dazhao, who introduced Marxist ideas to many readers. After Chen moved to Peking University, the magazine became the centre of the intellectual ferment that culminated in the May Fourth Movement of 1919.

Legacy

Several editors and readers, including Chen and Li, went on to found the Chinese Communist Party in 1921, and the magazine moved toward Marxist politics in its later years, ceasing publication in 1926. New Youth is remembered as the spark of China's literary revolution and modern cultural awakening.
