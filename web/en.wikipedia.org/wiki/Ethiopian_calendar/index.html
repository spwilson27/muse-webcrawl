Ethiopian calendar

The Ethiopian calendar is the principal calendar used in Ethiopia and serves as the liturgical calendar of the Eritrean and Ethiopian Orthodox churches. It is a solar calendar derived from the Coptic calendar, itself based on the ancient Egyptian calendar, but with its own era and reckoning that place it roughly seven to eight years behind the Gregorian calendar.

Structure

The Ethiopian year has thirteen months: twelve months of thirty days each, followed by a short thirteenth month, Pagumen, of five days (six in a leap year). The new year, Enkutatash, falls on 11 September in the Gregorian calendar (12 September before a leap year).

Era

The calendar's era is based on a calculation of the Annunciation placed in 7/8 CE relative to the Gregorian count, so the Ethiopian year number is seven years behind the Gregorian one from January to September, and eight years behind from September to January. Ethiopia thus celebrated its millennium in 2007–2008 Gregorian time.

Months

The months are Meskerem, Tikimt, Hidar, Tahsas, Tir, Yekatit, Megabit, Miyazya, Ginbot, Sene, Hamle, Nehase, and Pagumen. Many Ethiopian holidays and the Ethiopian Orthodox liturgical cycle follow this calendar, which remains in everyday official use in Ethiopia alongside the Gregorian calendar.
