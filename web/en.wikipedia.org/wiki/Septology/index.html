Septology (Norwegian: Septologien) is a seven-part novel by the Norwegian author Jon Fosse, published in three volumes between 2019 and 2021: The Other Name: Septology I–II, I Is Another: Septology III–V, and A New Name: Septology VI–VII. The English translation by Damion Searls was published by Fitzcarraldo Editions and Transit Books. The work is widely considered Fosse's masterpiece and a major factor in his receiving the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2023.

Plot and themes

The novel is narrated by Asle, an ageing painter and widower living on the west coast of Norway. Asle has a double, another Asle—a doppelgänger who is also a painter but whose life has been marked by alcoholism and decline. Over the days leading up to Christmas, the narrator's reflections move between the present and memories of childhood, his marriage to his late wife Ales, his conversion to Catholicism, and his encounters with his double, whose failing health he must tend to.

The narrative is written without sentence breaks or full stops—Fosse's signature "slow prose"—in a single flowing stream of consciousness that interweaves prayer, memory, and observation. Central themes include identity and doubling, death, faith, art, and the mystery of the self: the conviction, expressed in the third volume's title, that "I is another".

Reception

The English translation received extensive critical acclaim. The Other Name was shortlisted for the International Booker Prize in 2020, and A New Name was shortlisted in 2022; the latter also won the Norwegian Brage Prize. Critics praised the work's meditative rhythm, its exploration of faith and mortality, and Searls's translation.

Structure

Although divided into seven parts and three volumes, Septology is conceived as one single book and one single continuous sentence of more than a thousand pages in total. Fosse wrote it over several years, largely in his characteristic state of intense, trance-like concentration.
