The Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing, often shortened to Triple Crown, is a series of three horse races for three-year-old Thoroughbreds. Winning all three of these races is considered the greatest accomplishment in Thoroughbred racing. The term originated in mid-19th-century England, and Thoroughbred racing countries each have their own series.

United States

The American Triple Crown consists of the Kentucky Derby (1¼ miles, Churchill Downs, first Saturday in May), the Preakness Stakes (1³⁄₁₆ miles, Pimlico, two weeks later), and the Belmont Stakes (1½ miles, Belmont Park, three weeks after the Preakness). Only thirteen horses have won the series, including Sir Barton (1919, before the term was popularised), Secretariat (1973), Seattle Slew (1977), Affirmed (1978), American Pharoah (2015, ending a 37-year drought), and Justify (2018).

United Kingdom

The English Triple Crown, dating to the 19th century, comprises the 2,000 Guineas, the Derby at Epsom, and the St Leger. Fifteen horses have won it, the most recent being Nijinsky in 1970; no horse has seriously attempted the feat in recent decades because the St Leger's stamina test conflicts with the commercial value of milers and middle-distance horses in the breeding shed. A fillies' version — the 1,000 Guineas, the Oaks, and the St Leger — has been won by nine fillies, including Oh So Sharp in 1985.
