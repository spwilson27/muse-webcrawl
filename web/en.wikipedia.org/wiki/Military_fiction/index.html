Military fiction

Military fiction is a genre of fiction focusing on warfare, soldiers and military life, in novels, short stories, films and other media. Works may depict historical conflicts, contemporary operations or imagined future wars, and range from patriotic adventure to bleak anti-war narratives.

History

War narratives are among the oldest literary forms, from the Iliad to historical epics. The modern military novel developed with works such as Stephen Crane's The Red Badge of Courage (1895), and the First World War produced classics including All Quiet on the Western Front. The Second World War and Vietnam War generated major bodies of fiction by authors such as Norman Mailer, James Jones, Joseph Heller and Tim O'Brien.

Subgenres

Subgenres include naval fiction (such as C. S. Forester's Hornblower novels and Patrick O'Brian's Aubrey–Maturin series), military science fiction, techno-thrillers by authors such as Tom Clancy, and alternate-history war stories.
