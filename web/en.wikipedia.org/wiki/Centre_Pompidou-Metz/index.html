The Centre Pompidou-Metz is a museum of modern and contemporary art in Metz, France, and the first decentralised branch of the Centre Pompidou in Paris. Opened on 12 May 2010, it presents temporary exhibitions drawn largely from the Paris institution's collection, one of the largest collections of modern art in Europe, and holds no permanent collection of its own.

Architecture

The building was designed by Japanese architect Shigeru Ban with Jean de Gastines. Its most striking feature is a vast hexagonal roof of woven laminated timber, inspired by a Chinese bamboo hat Ban found in Paris, curving over three stacked, cantilevered gallery boxes that project through the roof. A central spire rises 77 metres, echoing the 1977 opening date of the Paris centre. The translucent membrane roof covers a large forum space intended for performances and public events.

Exhibitions and role

The inaugural exhibition, "Chefs-d'œuvre?" (Masterpieces?), drew around 800 works and attracted some 800,000 visitors in its first year, making the museum one of the most visited cultural venues in France outside Paris. Subsequent programming has ranged across modern masters, thematic shows and large-scale installations, contributing to the cultural regeneration of Metz's Amphithéâtre district near the railway station.
