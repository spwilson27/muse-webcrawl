Saint Laurent (fashion house)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Saint Laurent fashion house]
The YSL monogram

Yves Saint Laurent SAS, trading as Saint Laurent and still commonly known as Yves Saint Laurent or YSL, is a French luxury fashion house founded in 1961 by designer Yves Saint Laurent and his partner Pierre Bergé. It is a subsidiary of the Kering group. The house is renowned for revolutionising women's fashion with creations such as the Le Smoking tuxedo suit (1966), the Mondrian dress (1965), ready-to-wear under the Rive Gauche label, and the popularisation of safari jackets, thigh-high boots, and narrow trousers.

History

Yves Saint Laurent had succeeded Christian Dior as head designer of Dior at age 21 in 1957. After leaving Dior, he and Bergé founded their own house in 1961, financed by American businessman J. Mack Robinson. The YSL logo — interlocking initials designed in 1961 by the artist Cassandre — became one of fashion's most recognisable marks.

The house launched Rive Gauche ready-to-wear in 1966, among the first couturiers to do so, and expanded into menswear in 1969. Fragrances such as Opium (1977) became major commercial successes. In 1993 the fashion house was sold to Sanofi, and in 1999 it was acquired by the Gucci Group (now Kering). Tom Ford designed the ready-to-wear lines until 2004; Stefano Pilati followed, and in 2012 Hedi Slimane renamed the ready-to-wear line "Saint Laurent Paris," reviving a rock-influenced aesthetic. Anthony Vaccarello became creative director in 2016.

Products and presence

Saint Laurent produces ready-to-wear, leather goods, shoes, jewellery, eyewear, and cosmetics (YSL Beauté, under licence to L'Oréal). The brand operates hundreds of boutiques worldwide and is one of Kering's largest revenue contributors. Its permanent home is at 5 Avenue Marceau in Paris, the site where Yves Saint Laurent worked for nearly thirty years and which now houses the Musée Yves Saint Laurent Paris.
