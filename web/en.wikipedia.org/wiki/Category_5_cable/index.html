Category 5 cable (Cat 5) is a twisted pair cable standard for computer networks, defined in the ANSI/TIA-568 standard. Since its introduction in the 1990s it has been the workhorse of structured cabling, carrying Ethernet and other signals over four pairs of copper wires, each pair twisted to reduce crosstalk and electromagnetic interference.

Characteristics

Cat 5 cable is specified for frequencies up to 100 MHz and was originally intended for Fast Ethernet (100BASE-TX) at 100 Mbit/s, though it can also carry telephone, video, and token ring signals. The maximum recommended run for a permanent link is 90 metres, with up to 10 metres of patch cords giving a 100-metre channel. The cable is normally terminated with 8P8C ("RJ45") connectors wired to the T568A or T568B pin assignments.

Category 5e and successors

The enhanced Category 5e (Cat 5e) standard, ratified in 1999, tightened crosstalk specifications and supports Gigabit Ethernet (1000BASE-T) over the full 100-metre channel; it has almost entirely replaced plain Cat 5. Later categories — Cat 6, Cat 6a, Cat 7, and Cat 8 — offer progressively higher bandwidths and data rates up to 40 Gbit/s, but Cat 5e remains widely installed in homes and offices.
