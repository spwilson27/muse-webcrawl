Housing tenure

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Housing tenure is the financial arrangement under which a household has the right to live in a house or apartment. The most common forms are owner-occupation (in which the household owns the dwelling outright or with a mortgage), private renting (tenancy from a private landlord), and social renting (housing provided by a public authority or non-profit housing association, usually at below-market rent). Other forms include shared ownership, cooperative housing, leasehold, life tenancy, and employer-provided or tied accommodation.

Types of tenure

Tenure typeDescription
Owner-occupiedThe occupant owns the home, with or without a mortgage; includes freehold and, in some systems, leasehold ownership.
Private rentedThe occupant rents from a private landlord under a tenancy agreement, at market rent.
Social rentedThe occupant rents from a local authority or housing association, typically at subsidised rent and with greater security of tenure.
Shared ownershipThe occupant buys a share of the home and pays rent on the remainder, often with the option to "staircase" to full ownership.
CooperativeThe dwelling is owned by a cooperative of which the occupant is a member.
Informal / otherIncludes squatting, living with family rent-free, and employer-provided housing.

Patterns

Tenure mixes vary greatly between countries. Homeownership rates are high in much of Eastern Europe and in countries such as Spain and Ireland, while Germany and Switzerland have large private rented sectors with strong tenant protections. Tenure is associated with wealth, household income, age, and housing policy: owner-occupation typically accumulates housing equity, while renters have greater mobility but less security. Governments influence tenure through mortgage regulation, tax treatment, rent control, social housing construction, and policies such as the United Kingdom's Right to Buy.
