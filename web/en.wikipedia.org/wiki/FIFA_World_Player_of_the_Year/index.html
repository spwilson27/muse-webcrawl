FIFA World Player of the Year

The FIFA World Player of the Year was an association football award presented annually by FIFA, the sport's governing body, to the male and female players judged the best in the world. The men's award was presented from 1991 to 2009; a women's award was added in 2001 and ran to 2015. In 2010 the men's award was merged with France Football's Ballon d'Or to create the FIFA Ballon d'Or, an arrangement that lasted until 2015. From 2016 the honor has been presented as The Best FIFA Men's Player as part of The Best FIFA Football Awards.

Voting

Winners were selected by votes from the coaches and captains of national teams, who each chose three players and awarded points (five, three, and one). In later years media representatives were also included in the electorate. The system was sometimes criticized for favoritism toward teammates and prominent leagues.

Women's winners

The women's award was dominated by Mia Hamm of the United States (2001, 2002), Germany's Birgit Prinz (2003–2005), and Brazil's Marta, who won five consecutive awards from 2006 to 2010. Other winners included Homare Sawa (2011), Abby Wambach (2012), Nadine Angerer (2013), Nadine Keßler (2014), and Carli Lloyd (2015), after which the women's honor continued within The Best FIFA Football Awards.

See also

The award is historically compared with the Ballon d'Or, awarded by France Football since 1956, and with World Soccer's World Player of the Year. George Weah remains the only African player to have won the FIFA men's award, and Fabio Cannavaro the rare defender and goalkeeper-era exception in a list dominated by attacking players.
