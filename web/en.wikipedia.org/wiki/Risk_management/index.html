Risk management

Risk management is the identification, evaluation, and prioritization of risks (defined in ISO 31000 as the effect of uncertainty on objectives) followed by coordinated and economical application of resources to minimize, monitor, and control the probability or impact of unfortunate events or to maximize the realization of opportunities.

Process

Standard frameworks such as ISO 31000 and COSO ERM describe a cycle: establish context, identify risks, analyse likelihood and consequences (often with a risk matrix), evaluate against risk appetite, treat risks, and monitor and review, with communication throughout.

Risk treatment

Strategies include avoiding the activity, reducing likelihood or impact, sharing or transferring risk (for example through insurance or outsourcing), and retaining/accepting risk within tolerance. Financial risk management uses instruments such as hedging and diversification; project management addresses schedule, cost and safety risks; and health and safety practice applies hazard controls.

Fields

Specialised branches include enterprise risk management, clinical risk management, information security risk management and environmental risk assessment. Critics note limits of quantitative models, highlighted by the 2008 financial crisis and the problem of "black swan" events emphasised by Nassim Nicholas Taleb.
