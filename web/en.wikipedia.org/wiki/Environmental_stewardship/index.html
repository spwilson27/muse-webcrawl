Environmental stewardship

Environmental stewardship refers to the responsible use and protection of the natural environment through active participation in conservation efforts and sustainable practices by individuals, small groups, nonprofit organizations, or governmental agencies. It draws on the older concept of stewardship — managing property or resources on behalf of another — applied to the Earth, which is held in trust for future generations and, in many religious formulations, for God or creation itself.

Origins and perspectives

The idea appears in many religious and ethical traditions. In Christianity, stewardship has been derived from the Genesis accounts of humanity's dominion over creation and the command to "till and keep" the garden; Pope Francis's 2015 encyclical Laudato si' restated this duty in terms of care for "our common home". The conservationist Aldo Leopold articulated a secular version in his "land ethic" in A Sand County Almanac (1949), arguing that people should see themselves as members of a biotic community rather than conquerors of it.

Within public policy, stewardship is invoked by land-management agencies and programs that enlist private landowners and volunteers in habitat restoration, water-quality protection and species conservation. Businesses use the term in corporate social responsibility and product stewardship programs, including take-back and recycling schemes for electronics and packaging.

Criticism

Some commentators argue that the stewardship model remains anthropocentric, casting humans as managers standing outside nature, and contrast it with ecocentric approaches that assign intrinsic value to ecosystems. Others note that the term's breadth allows it to be used loosely in marketing, a form of greenwashing.
