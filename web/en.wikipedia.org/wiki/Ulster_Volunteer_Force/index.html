Ulster Volunteer Force

The Ulster Volunteer Force (UVF) is an Ulster loyalist paramilitary group based in Northern Ireland. Formed in 1965–66, it first emerged in 1966 to fight the Irish Republican Army (IRA) — whom the UVF believed were being revived to start a campaign — and to retain Northern Ireland's place in the United Kingdom. During the 30-year conflict known as the Troubles, the UVF carried out bombings, shootings and other attacks, killing more than 400 people, the great majority of them Catholic civilians. It is a proscribed organisation in the United Kingdom and an unlawful organisation in the Republic of Ireland.

Active: 1966–present (on ceasefire since 1994)
Allegiance: Ulster loyalism; Northern Ireland within the UK
Strength: Several hundred at peak
Status: Ceasefire 1994; weapons decommissioned 2009

Origins

The modern UVF took its name from the original Ulster Volunteers of 1912–13, formed to resist Home Rule. In 1966, led by Gusty Spence, it declared war on the IRA and carried out attacks including the bombing of a Catholic-owned pub and murders that led to its proscription. Spence was convicted of murder; the group's first victims included civilians unconnected to republicanism.

The Troubles

The UVF was responsible for some of the conflict's worst atrocities, including the McGurk's Bar bombing (1971, 15 killed), the Dublin and Monaghan bombings (1974, 33 killed — the deadliest day of the Troubles), the Miami Showband killings (1975), and numerous sectarian assassinations, including of solicitor Pat Finucane (1989), a case marked by proven state collusion. Mid-Ulster commander Billy Wright was expelled in 1996 and founded the breakaway Loyalist Volunteer Force. The UVF's political wing evolved into the Progressive Unionist Party (PUP).

Ceasefire and after

The UVF announced a ceasefire in October 1994 as part of the Combined Loyalist Military Command, and supported aspects of the peace process. In May 2007 it renounced violence and put arms "beyond reach"; in June 2009 it confirmed full decommissioning of weapons before independent witnesses. Elements of the organisation have nevertheless been linked to continuing criminality and occasional violence, and its legacy remains contested in investigations into collusion and unsolved killings.
