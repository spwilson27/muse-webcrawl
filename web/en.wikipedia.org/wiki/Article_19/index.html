Article 19 (stylised ARTICLE 19) is an international human rights organisation that works to defend and promote freedom of expression and freedom of information worldwide. Founded in 1987, it takes its name from Article 19 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, which states: "Everyone has the right to freedom of opinion and expression; this right includes freedom to hold opinions without interference and to seek, receive and impart information and ideas through any media and regardless of frontiers."

History

The organisation was founded in London in 1987, in a period when censorship campaigns—including the fatwa against Salman Rushdie, whose defence the organisation prominently supported—were bringing questions of free expression to international attention. Early financial support came from the Rowntree Social Service Trust. From its London base the organisation grew into a global network with regional offices in, among other places, Bangladesh, Brazil, Kenya, Mexico, Senegal, Tunisia, and the United States.

Activities

Article 19 monitors threats to free expression, campaigns against censorship and restrictive media laws, and produces legal analyses of draft legislation measured against international human rights standards. It publishes the Global Expression Report, an annual assessment of the state of freedom of expression across more than 160 countries, and develops policy standards on issues including defamation, hate speech, media regulation, internet governance, and the protection of journalists. It also participates in international advocacy at the United Nations and regional human rights bodies, and runs training and capacity-building programmes for journalists, lawyers, and civil-society groups.

Principles

The organisation is associated with soft-law instruments such as the Johannesburg Principles on national security and freedom of expression and the Camden Principles on freedom of expression and equality, which it helped to develop with partner organisations and experts.
