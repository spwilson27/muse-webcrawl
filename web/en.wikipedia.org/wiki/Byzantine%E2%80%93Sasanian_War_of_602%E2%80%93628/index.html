Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628

The Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628 was the final and most devastating of the series of wars fought between the Byzantine (Eastern Roman) Empire and the Sasanian Empire of Persia. Lasting 26 years, it began when the shah Khosrow II declared war to avenge the murder of the emperor Maurice, who had helped him regain his throne in 591.

Course of the war

Persian armies overran Mesopotamia, Syria, Palestine and Egypt; Jerusalem fell in 614 and its True Cross was carried off to Ctesiphon. In 626 Constantinople itself was besieged by Avars and Persians but held. Meanwhile the emperor Heraclius, allied with the Western Turks (Khazars), campaigned deep into Persian territory, defeating the Persians decisively at Nineveh in December 627.

Aftermath

Khosrow II was overthrown and executed in 628 by his son Kavad II, who made peace, restoring the pre-war borders and returning the True Cross. Both empires were left exhausted — a condition that facilitated the Arab Muslim conquests of the following decade.
