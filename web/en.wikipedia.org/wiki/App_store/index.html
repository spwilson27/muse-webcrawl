App store

An app store, also called an app marketplace or app catalog, is a type of digital distribution platform for computer software applications, usually for mobile devices. Apps provide specific sets of functions which, by definition, do not include the running of the computer itself. App stores usually organise the offered software by category, present user ratings and reviews, and handle payment for paid software.

History

Precursors included software repositories for Linux distributions and electronic marketplaces of the 1990s such as the Electronic AppWrapper for NeXT. The concept became widespread with the launch of Apple's App Store for the iPhone in 2008, which launched with 500 applications and grew into an ecosystem of millions of apps. Google's Android Market (later Google Play), Microsoft's Windows Store, and stores for games consoles and desktop operating systems followed.

Business model and criticism

App store operators typically take a commission — historically 30 percent — on sales of apps and in-app purchases. This commission, along with operators' review policies and their control over distribution, has drawn criticism from developers and scrutiny from regulators and antitrust litigation, including cases concerning Apple and Google.
