Asian Low
TypeSeasonal thermal low (semi-permanent low)
RegionSouth Asia — centred over northwestern India/Pakistan (Thar Desert area)

The Asian Low is a large, semi-permanent low-pressure area that develops over South Asia during the northern summer. It is a thermal low: intense solar heating of the vast Asian landmass — particularly the arid northwest of the Indian subcontinent and the elevated Tibetan Plateau — warms the air column, lowers surface pressure, and draws in moist air from the surrounding oceans. It is one of the principal engines of the Asian monsoon system.

Formation and structure

From about April, surface pressure falls steadily over northwestern India and Pakistan, and by June–July the low is centred approximately over the lower Indus valley and Thar Desert region, with central pressures commonly around 995–1000 hPa on mean sea-level charts, though as a shallow thermal low it weakens rapidly with height. Heating over the Tibetan Plateau reinforces the system aloft, where the Tibetan anticyclone forms in the upper troposphere. The low, together with the Mascarene High in the southern Indian Ocean, establishes the cross-equatorial pressure gradient that drives the southwesterly monsoon flow into India.

Effects

The Asian Low governs the onset, strength, and northward extent of the Indian summer monsoon rains. Fluctuations in its intensity are linked to monsoon "breaks" and active spells. As summer wanes, the land cools, the low fills, and pressure rises — giving way in winter to the Siberian High, which drives the dry northeasterly winter monsoon. The annual reversal between these two pressure centres is the classic textbook example of a continental monsoon circulation.
