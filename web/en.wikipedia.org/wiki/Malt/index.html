Malt

Malt is a cereal grain, most commonly barley, that has been made to germinate by soaking in water and then stopped from germinating further by drying with hot air — a process known as malting. Malted grain is used to make beer, whisky, malted milk, malt vinegar, confectionery such as Maltesers and Whoppers, flavoured drinks such as Horlicks and Ovaltine, and some baked goods.


Malted barley.

Malting process

Malting traditionally involves steeping the grain in water to raise its moisture content, allowing it to germinate on malting floors or in germination boxes, and finally kilning it. During germination, enzymes — particularly alpha-amylase and beta-amylase — develop and modify the grain's starches and proteins, producing the sugars that yeast can ferment. Kilning temperature determines the malt's colour and flavour, from pale lager malts through crystal and chocolate malts to black patent malt.

Malt extract

Malt extract is produced by mashing malted grain and evaporating the resulting wort to a syrup or powder. It is used by homebrewers, in breakfast cereals and as a nutritional supplement historically given to children.
