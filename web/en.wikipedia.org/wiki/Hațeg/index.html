Hațeg

Hațeg (Romanian pronunciation: [haˈt͡seɡ]; German: Wallenthal; Hungarian: Hátszeg) is a town in Hunedoara County, Transylvania, Romania, with a population of roughly ten thousand. It is the centre of the Hațeg Country (Țara Hațegului), a historic and ethnographic region in a depression between the Retezat, Șureanu and Poiana Ruscă mountains.

History

The Hațeg Basin was part of Roman Dacia; the capital of the province, Ulpia Traiana Sarmizegetusa, lies nearby, and medieval records first mention the settlement in the thirteenth century. The region's Romanian noble families, fortified churches — including the stone church at Densuș, one of the oldest in Romania — and its role in Transylvanian history give it a distinctive heritage.

Hațeg Island and dinosaurs

In the Late Cretaceous the area formed part of Hațeg Island, an island in the Tethys Sea famous for its fauna of dwarf dinosaurs, described by the palaeontologist Franz Nopcsa — including the titanosaur Magyarosaurus and the hadrosaur Telmatosaurus — a classic case of insular dwarfism. The Hațeg Country Dinosaurs Geopark, recognised within UNESCO's geopark network, protects and interprets the region's geological and fossil heritage.

Geography

The town lies on the Strei River valley, near the entrance to Retezat National Park, one of Romania's most visited mountain reserves, making Hațeg a gateway for tourism in the Southern Carpathians.
