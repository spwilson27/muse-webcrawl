The Circuit de Nevers Magny-Cours is a motor racing circuit near the towns of Magny-Cours and Nevers in the Nièvre department of central France. It is best known as the home of the Formula One French Grand Prix from 1991 to 2008.

History

The track began in 1960 as a kart circuit built by farmer Jean Bernigaud and grew into a national racing school and club circuit, hosting the Winfield Racing School attended by several future Grand Prix drivers. Under a modernization programme championed by regional and national politicians — notably François Mitterrand — the circuit was rebuilt to Formula One standards and took over the French Grand Prix from Paul Ricard in 1991.

Michael Schumacher won the French Grand Prix at Magny-Cours a record eight times. After 2008 the race was dropped from the Formula One calendar amid financial difficulties, and the Grand Prix eventually returned to France at Paul Ricard in 2018. Magny-Cours continues to host the French round of the Superbike World Championship, the Bol d'Or motorcycle endurance race in some years, national series and historic events.

Layout

The Grand Prix layout is 4.411 km long with 17 corners and a very smooth surface. Corners borrow names from other famous circuits — Estoril, Adelaide, Imola, Nürburgring, Château d'Eau — and the circuit combines a long straight into the Adelaide hairpin with slow technical sections. The track is also known for unpredictable weather and its rural, comparatively remote setting.
