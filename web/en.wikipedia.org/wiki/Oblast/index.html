Oblast

An oblast (from Russian and Ukrainian область, "region") is a type of administrative division in a number of Slavic and formerly Soviet countries, including Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Kazakhstan, and Kyrgyzstan. The word is often translated as "province" or "region." In the Russian Federation, oblasts are federal subjects with their own charters and legislatures, headed by governors; there are 48 oblasts, the most numerous type of federal subject.

History

The term was used in the Russian Empire for administrative units from the eighteenth century, but the modern system was formed in the Soviet Union, where oblasts were the principal first-level subdivisions of the union republics, themselves divided into districts (raions). Upon the dissolution of the USSR in 1991, successor states largely retained the inherited structure: Ukraine is divided into 24 oblasts plus the Autonomous Republic of Crimea and cities with special status; Belarus has six oblasts (voblasts); Kazakhstan has 17 regions officially termed oblasts.

Oblasts in Russia

Russian oblasts range enormously in size and population, from Kaliningrad Oblast — an exclave on the Baltic Sea — to the vast Siberian oblasts such as Tyumen and Irkutsk. Unlike the republics of Russia, oblasts are not associated with a titular ethnic group. Four autonomous okrugs are administratively subordinated to oblasts. Under the 1993 Constitution of Russia, all federal subjects are equal in status, though they differ in their governing instruments: oblasts adopt charters (ustav) rather than constitutions.

Other uses

Bulgaria's provinces are called oblasti, a legacy of the term's wider Slavic usage. In Serbia, the cognate word oblast was used for administrative divisions in the Kingdom of Yugoslavia between 1922 and 1929, when nine large oblasts briefly replaced the older counties before being reorganised into banovinas.
