Silence=Death

The Silence=Death poster (1987): a pink triangle above the slogan.

"Silence = Death" is one of the most famous slogans of the AIDS activist movement. It appeared on a poster created in 1986–87 by the Silence=Death Project, a six-person New York collective (Avram Finkelstein, Brian Howard, Oliver Johnston, Charles Kreloff, Chris Lione and Jorge Socarrás), and became closely associated with ACT UP, the AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power, founded in 1987. The poster pairs the equation "SILENCE = DEATH" with an upright pink triangle, reclaiming the badge the Nazis forced homosexual prisoners to wear.

Design and meaning

The design is stark: white text beneath a fuchsia-pink triangle on a black field, with smaller text below urging action against government inaction. The triangle — inverted in the Nazi camps — is flipped point-up as a symbol of defiance and reclamation. The equation argued that silence about the epidemic, by officials and by the gay community alike, was itself deadly, and that only open, angry activism would save lives. The collective deliberately left the work uncopyrighted so it could be freely reproduced.

Legacy

Wheat-pasted across New York in early 1987, the poster became the visual emblem of AIDS activism and one of the most recognisable protest graphics of the 20th century. ACT UP adopted the slogan and triangle on banners, T-shirts and stickers at demonstrations worldwide. The image is held in museum collections, including the Museum of Modern Art, and remains a touchstone in discussions of graphic design and political art.
