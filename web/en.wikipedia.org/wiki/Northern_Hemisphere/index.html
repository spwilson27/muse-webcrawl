Northern Hemisphere

The Northern Hemisphere is the half of Earth that is north of the Equator. It contains most of the planet's land area — about 68% of Earth's land — including all of North America and Europe, most of Asia and Africa, and the northern part of South America. Approximately 90% of the world's human population lives in the Northern Hemisphere.

Geography and climate

Because of the concentration of land, the Northern Hemisphere's climate is more strongly affected by continental temperature swings than the more oceanic Southern Hemisphere. Its seasons are opposite to those in the south: summer occurs around June to August, when the North Pole is tilted toward the Sun, and winter around December to February. The Arctic Ocean occupies the far north, largely surrounded by land.

Characteristics

In the Northern Hemisphere, the Coriolis effect deflects moving air and water to the right, so large weather systems rotate counter-clockwise (cyclones) while high-pressure systems rotate clockwise — the reverse of the Southern Hemisphere. The bright star Polaris, the North Star, is visible only from this hemisphere, while familiar northern constellations include Ursa Major and Cassiopeia. The Arctic Circle (66.5° N) marks the latitude of the polar night and midnight sun.
