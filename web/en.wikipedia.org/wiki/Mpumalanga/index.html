Mpumalanga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Blyde River Canyon and Three Rondavels, on Mpumalanga's Panorama Route.

Mpumalanga (/əmˌpʌməˈlɑːŋɡə/) is one of the nine provinces of South Africa. The name, in the Nguni languages (Zulu, Swati, Xhosa, and Ndebele), means "place where the sun rises". The province was formed in 1994 out of the eastern part of the former Transvaal Province and parts of the former Bantustans of KaNgwane, KwaNdebele, and Lebowa; it was initially called the Eastern Transvaal and was renamed Mpumalanga in August 1995. Its capital and largest city is Mbombela (formerly Nelspruit).

Geography

Mpumalanga borders the South African provinces of Limpopo to the north, Gauteng to the west, the Free State to the southwest, and KwaZulu-Natal to the south, and shares international borders with Eswatini and Mozambique. The Drakensberg Escarpment divides the province into the cooler, high-lying grasslands of the Highveld in the west and the hot, subtropical Lowveld in the east, dropping from over 2,000 m to a few hundred metres above sea level. The eastern Lowveld contains the southern half of the Kruger National Park, one of Africa's largest game reserves.

Tourism and nature

Major attractions line the Panorama Route along the escarpment: the Blyde River Canyon — among the largest canyons on Earth — the Three Rondavels, God's Window, Bourke's Luck Potholes, and the historic gold-rush town of Pilgrim's Rest. The province is also known for the Sudwala Caves, the Barberton Makhonjwa Mountains (a UNESCO World Heritage Site of some of the Earth's oldest rocks), and abundant safari tourism in and around Kruger.

Economy and demography

Mpumalanga's economy rests on mining (it produces a large share of South Africa's coal, much of it feeding Eskom power stations around eMalahleni and Secunda), agriculture and forestry in the Lowveld (citrus, sugarcane, macadamia nuts, timber), stainless steel, and tourism. The population is roughly 4.7 million. The most widely spoken languages are siSwati, isiZulu, isiNdebele, Northern Sotho, and Afrikaans; the province is also the traditional home of the Ndebele people, renowned for their colourful geometric house painting.
