Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina

The Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina (Bosnian/Croatian: Federacija Bosne i Hercegovine) is one of the two political entities that compose Bosnia and Herzegovina, the other being Republika Srpska. The Federation, often called the Bosniak-Croat Federation, covers about 51% of the country's territory and is itself divided into ten cantons.

History

The Federation was created by the Washington Agreement of March 1994, which ended the Croat–Bosniak War and united the territories held by the Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina and the Croatian Defence Council. The Dayton Agreement of 1995 confirmed it as one of the two entities of the Bosnian state, with its boundary against Republika Srpska following the Inter-Entity Boundary Line. Brčko District, disputed between the entities, was later constituted as a separate self-governing district.

Government and subdivisions

The Federation has its own president, government and bicameral parliament (House of Representatives and House of Peoples), headquartered in Sarajevo, which is also the state capital. Powers are extensively devolved to ten cantons — including Sarajevo, Herzegovina-Neretva, Tuzla, Zenica-Doboj and Central Bosnia — each with its own assembly and government, producing a famously complex, multi-layered administration. Bosniaks form the majority of the population, with Croats the second-largest constituent people and smaller Serb and other communities.
