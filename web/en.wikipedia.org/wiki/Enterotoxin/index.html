Enterotoxin

TypeProtein exotoxin
TargetIntestinal mucosa
EffectsDiarrhea, vomiting, food poisoning

An enterotoxin is a protein exotoxin released by a microorganism that targets the intestines. Enterotoxins are frequently responsible for the symptoms of food poisoning and diarrhoeal diseases, acting on the mucosal cells of the intestine to alter membrane permeability and fluid secretion.

Mechanism

Many enterotoxins work by increasing cyclic AMP or cyclic GMP levels in intestinal epithelial cells, driving chloride and water secretion into the gut lumen and producing profuse watery diarrhea. Others, such as the staphylococcal enterotoxins, act as superantigens and potently stimulate the immune system.

Examples

Well-known enterotoxins include cholera toxin from Vibrio cholerae, the heat-labile and heat-stable toxins of enterotoxigenic Escherichia coli, staphylococcal enterotoxins from Staphylococcus aureus, and toxins produced by Clostridium perfringens and Bacillus cereus.
