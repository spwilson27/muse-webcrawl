Eco-anxiety

Eco-anxiety is a term for distress, worry or anxiety caused by the perceived threats of climate change and environmental degradation. The American Psychological Association has described it as "a chronic fear of environmental doom." Related terms include climate anxiety, eco-grief for sorrow over ecological losses, and solastalgia, distress caused by environmental change to one's home environment.

Prevalence

Surveys in the 2010s and 2020s found substantial proportions of young people reporting worry about climate change. A widely reported 2021 study of 10,000 people aged 16–25 in ten countries (published in The Lancet Planetary Health) found 59 percent very or extremely worried about climate change and 45 percent saying such feelings affected their daily life. Direct experience of extreme weather, wildfire smoke or flooding is associated with stronger symptoms.

Responses

Psychologists generally regard eco-anxiety as a rational response to a genuine threat rather than a disorder in itself, though it can exacerbate existing mental-health problems. Coping approaches discussed in the literature include collective action and activism, community support, limiting distressing media exposure, and therapies adapted from anxiety treatment. Critics of the term worry about medicalising political concern, while clinicians argue naming the experience validates sufferers and can motivate engagement.
