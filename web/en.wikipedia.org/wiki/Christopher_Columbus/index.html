Christopher Columbus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Posthumous portrait of Christopher Columbus (no contemporary likeness survives)]

Christopher Columbus (1451 – 20 May 1506) was an Italian explorer and navigator from Genoa who completed four voyages across the Atlantic Ocean for the Catholic Monarchs of Spain (1492–93, 1493–96, 1498–1500, 1502–04). Seeking a westward sea route to Asia, he instead made landfall in the Bahamas on 12 October 1492 and reached the Caribbean islands and the coasts of Central and South America — opening the Americas to sustained European contact, colonisation and the Columbian exchange. He died maintaining that he had reached the outskirts of Asia. His legacy is among the most contested of any historical figure: celebrated for centuries as a heroic discoverer, he is now also judged for the enslavement, violence and demographic catastrophe that followed his arrival for the Caribbean's peoples.

The enterprise of the Indies

Columbus, an experienced Atlantic sailor in Portuguese service, proposed sailing west to 'the Indies' (Asia), estimating the ocean far narrower than it is — his critics on the Portuguese and Spanish commissions were right about the distance, wrong only because a continent lay in between. After years of lobbying, Ferdinand and Isabella agreed in the Capitulations of Santa Fe (April 1492), promising him titles (Admiral of the Ocean Sea, viceroy) and a tenth of revenues. With the Niña, Pinta and Santa María and about 90 men, he sailed from Palos on 3 August 1492, made landfall on an island he named San Salvador, and explored Cuba and Hispaniola before returning in triumph in 1493.

Later voyages and reputation

His subsequent voyages founded Santo Domingo, charted the South American mainland (1498, the Orinoco delta) and Central America, and descended into misgovernment: complaints from colonists led to his arrest and return in chains (1500), and he lost his governorship though not his titles or wealth. He died at Valladolid in 1506. The peoples of the Caribbean — Taíno above all — suffered enslavement, famine and epidemic collapse within two generations, a catastrophe Columbus' own harsh governorship and the system he inaugurated set in motion; the modern reassessment has removed statues and renamed holidays, while his navigational feat and its world-changing consequences remain undisputed.
