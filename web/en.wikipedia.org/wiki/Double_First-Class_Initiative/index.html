Double First-Class Initiative

The Double First-Class Initiative (Chinese: 双一流; pinyin: shuāngyīliú) is a Chinese government programme launched in 2015 to develop a group of elite Chinese universities and academic disciplines into world-class institutions by the middle of the 21st century. It succeeded the earlier Project 985 and Project 211 schemes for fostering leading universities.

Background

In October 2015 the State Council of China announced the "Overall Plan for Promoting the Construction of World First-Class Universities and First-Class Disciplines", aiming to raise the international competitiveness of Chinese higher education. Implementation measures were issued in 2017, when the first official list was published.

First list (2017)

The 2017 list named 42 universities targeted for development as world-class universities — 36 in Class A, including Peking University and Tsinghua University, and 6 in Class B — together with 465 first-class discipline programmes at 140 institutions in total.

Second round (2022)

A second round of the initiative was announced in February 2022, covering 147 universities. The A/B classification of universities was abolished, and institutions were warned that disciplines failing to meet standards could be removed, introducing a dynamic adjustment mechanism instead of permanent status.

Goals and criticism

The programme channels additional state funding to selected universities and disciplines, with the stated goals of producing world-class research, faculty and graduates. Critics have noted the concentration of resources in already advantaged institutions and regions, and the risk of distorting university priorities towards ranking metrics.
