Past participle

Placeholder image

A past participle is a form of a verb used, among other things, to form perfect tenses and the passive voice, and as an adjective. In English, regular verbs form the past participle with the suffix -ed (for example, walked), while irregular verbs have varied forms (sung, written, gone).

Uses

In English, the past participle combines with the auxiliary have to form perfect constructions ("she has finished") and with forms of be to form the passive ("the window was broken"). Past participles also function as adjectives: "a broken window", "the spoken word".

In many languages, past participles show agreement in gender and number with the noun they modify, and historically they have often been a source of new adjectives and nouns. See also participle, perfect, and English verbs.
