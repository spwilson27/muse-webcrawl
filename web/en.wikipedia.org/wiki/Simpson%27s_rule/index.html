Simpson's rule

FieldNumerical analysis
Named afterThomas Simpson
Exact forPolynomials up to degree 3

Simpson's rule is a method for numerical integration that approximates the integral of a function by fitting parabolas through triples of points. Named after the English mathematician Thomas Simpson (1710-1761), it is exact for polynomials of degree three or less and is widely used in engineering and the sciences when an antiderivative is unavailable or data comes from discrete samples.

Formula

Over an interval [a,b] divided into an even number n of subintervals of width h, Simpson's rule gives the integral as h/3 times a weighted sum of function values, with weights 1, 4, 2, 4, ..., 2, 4, 1. The simplest case, with a single pair of subintervals, uses the endpoints and the midpoint.

Error

The error of the composite rule is proportional to h^4 times the fourth derivative of the integrand, making Simpson's rule considerably more accurate than the trapezoidal rule for smooth functions, whose error is proportional to h^2.

Variants

Simpson's 3/8 rule uses cubic interpolation over three subintervals at a time. Adaptive Simpson's method recursively subdivides intervals where the estimated error is large, concentrating effort where the function varies rapidly.
