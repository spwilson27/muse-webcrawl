Ballast water discharge and the environment

Ballast water is taken aboard ships to maintain stability and trim, and is discharged when cargo is loaded. Because ballast is drawn from coastal waters and released in distant ports, its discharge is a major vector for the transfer of aquatic organisms and pathogens between ecosystems—widely regarded as one of the greatest threats to the world's oceans.

Ecological impacts

A single ship can carry thousands of species—bacteria, plankton, larvae, and small animals—in its ballast tanks. Survivors released abroad may become invasive species with no natural predators. Notorious examples include the comb jelly Mnemiopsis leidyi, which devastated Black Sea fisheries in the 1980s; the zebra mussel, introduced to the North American Great Lakes around 1988, causing billions of dollars of damage; the toxic dinoflagellates linked to harmful algal blooms; and the cholera bacterium Vibrio cholerae, implicated in a 1991 Latin American epidemic traced to ballast discharge. An estimated 7,000 species are in transit in ballast water at any time, and global shipping moves several billion tonnes of ballast water annually.

Regulation

The International Maritime Organization adopted the Ballast Water Management Convention in 2004; it entered into force on 8 September 2017. The convention's D-2 standard sets limits on viable organisms per volume of discharge, effectively requiring onboard treatment systems using filtration, ultraviolet light, electrochlorination, or deoxygenation. Earlier practice relied on mid-ocean ballast water exchange (the D-1 standard), which reduces but does not eliminate risk. The United States regulates ballast under Coast Guard rules and the VIDA statute, and several ports conduct inspections and sampling.

Management technologies

Treatment systems must operate at high flow rates and kill or remove organisms from large viruses to fish larvae while producing discharge safe for the environment, creating an active market for approved equipment and port-side reception facilities.
