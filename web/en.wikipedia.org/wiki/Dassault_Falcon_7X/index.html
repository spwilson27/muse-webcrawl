Dassault Falcon 7X

The Dassault Falcon 7X is a large-cabin, long-range business jet manufactured by Dassault Aviation of France. Announced at the Paris Air Show in 2001 as the successor in spirit to the Falcon 900 line, it first flew on 5 May 2005 and entered service in 2007. It was the first fully fly-by-wire business jet and the first aircraft designed entirely on a virtual platform by Dassault.

Design and development

The 7X is a trijet, powered by three Pratt & Whitney Canada PW307A engines mounted at the rear of the fuselage, giving a range of about 5,950 nautical miles (11,020 km) and a maximum speed of Mach 0.90. Its wing, with winglets, was a new design optimised for transonic efficiency. The flight deck uses the Honeywell-based EASy avionics suite with head-up displays and large LCD screens.

Variants and successors

The stretched Falcon 8X, announced in 2014, offers a longer cabin and increased range. The 7X later gained approval for steep approaches, including operations at London City Airport.

Specifications

The aircraft typically seats 12–16 passengers, has a maximum takeoff weight of about 31,750 kg, a service ceiling of 51,000 ft, and a typical cruise speed of Mach 0.80.
