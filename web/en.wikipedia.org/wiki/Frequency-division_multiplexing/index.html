Frequency-division multiplexing

In telecommunications, frequency-division multiplexing (FDM) is a technique by which the total bandwidth available in a communication medium is divided into a series of non-overlapping frequency bands, each of which is used to carry a separate signal. This allows a single transmission medium such as a cable or a band of the radio spectrum to be shared by multiple independent signals.

Applications

FDM was historically used in analogue telephone networks, where groups of twelve voice channels were multiplexed into a group, and groups into supergroups, for long-distance trunk lines. It is also used in radio and television broadcasting, cable television, and DSL broadband. Orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM), used in Wi-Fi, LTE and digital broadcasting, is a related digital method in which sub-carriers are spaced so as to be mathematically orthogonal.
