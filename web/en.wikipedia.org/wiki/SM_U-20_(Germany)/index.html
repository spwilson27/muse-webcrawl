SM U-20 was a German Type U 19 U-boat built for the Imperial German Navy at the Kaiserliche Werft in Danzig. She is best known for sinking the British ocean liner RMS Lusitania on 7 May 1915, an event that killed 1,198 people, including 128 Americans, and sharply turned American public opinion against Germany.

Career

Commissioned shortly before the First World War, U-20 operated in the North Sea and around the British Isles under Kapitänleutnant Walther Schwieger. On 7 May 1915, off the Old Head of Kinsale, Ireland, Schwieger fired a single torpedo at the Cunard liner Lusitania, which sank in only 18 minutes. The sinking provoked a diplomatic crisis between Germany and the United States and became one of the most controversial episodes of the unrestricted submarine warfare campaign.

Loss

On 4 November 1916, in thick fog, U-20 ran aground on the Jutland coast of Denmark near Vrist, north of Thorsminde. Attempts to refloat her failed, and the crew destroyed the boat with explosives the following day; the crew were interned in neutral Denmark for the remainder of the war. The wreck remained visible in the surf for decades, and parts of the boat, including its deck gun, were later salvaged and preserved in Denmark.
