Language disorder

Language disorders or language impairments are disorders that involve the processing of linguistic information. Problems may involve grammar (syntax or morphology), semantics (meaning), or other aspects of language, and may be receptive (difficulty understanding language), expressive (difficulty producing language), or both.

Types

Developmental language disorder (DLD) is diagnosed when language difficulties occur in childhood without a known differentiating condition such as autism or hearing loss, and significantly affect everyday communication and schooling. Acquired language disorders, such as aphasia, result from brain injury, stroke or disease in people who had previously developed normal language.

Assessment and treatment

Assessment is carried out by speech-language pathologists using standardised tests and language sampling. Intervention typically involves targeted therapy for vocabulary, grammar and narrative skills, classroom accommodations and, where relevant, augmentative and alternative communication. Early identification improves outcomes.
