Michelin

Founded: 28 May 1889, Clermont-Ferrand, France
Founders: André Michelin and Édouard Michelin
Industry: Tyres and rubber; travel guides
Mascot: Bibendum (the "Michelin Man")

Michelin (Compagnie Générale des Établissements Michelin SCA) is a French multinational tyre manufacturer based in Clermont-Ferrand, one of the largest tyre makers in the world. It is also famous for its travel guides, road maps and the Michelin Guide to restaurants, whose stars are a benchmark of fine dining.

History

Brothers André and Édouard Michelin took over a failing rubber factory in 1889. Their breakthrough came in 1891 with the patented detachable pneumatic bicycle tyre, which dramatically shortened repair time in races. The company went on to promote motoring itself — publishing maps and the first Guide in 1900, given away free to encourage travel — and introduced innovations including the radial tyre (patented 1946, as the "X" tyre), which transformed handling and tyre life. Its mascot Bibendum, a figure built of stacked tyres, dates from 1898 and is one of the world's oldest trademarks.

Guides and stars

The red Michelin Guide began awarding a single star to notable restaurants in 1926 and the present one-to-three-star hierarchy in the 1930s: one star "a very good restaurant in its category", two "excellent cooking, worth a detour", three "exceptional cuisine, worth a special journey". Anonymous inspectors' verdicts can make or break reputations. Michelin also publishes the green tourist guides and detailed road maps, and has supplied tyres to Formula One, endurance racing and MotoGP.

Present day

Michelin employs well over 100,000 people worldwide and manufactures tyres for cars, trucks, aircraft, agricultural and earth-moving machinery and bicycles, alongside ventures in hydrogen fuel cells, high-tech materials and mobility services.
