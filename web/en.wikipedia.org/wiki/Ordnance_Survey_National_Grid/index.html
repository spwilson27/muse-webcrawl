Ordnance Survey National Grid

The Ordnance Survey National Grid (OSGB) is the standard system of geographic grid references used in Great Britain, distinct from latitude and longitude. It is based on the OSGB36 datum and a transverse Mercator projection with its origin off the Scilly Isles, so that all of Britain lies within a single grid of squares.

Grid references

The grid is divided into squares 100 km × 100 km, each identified by a two-letter code (such as "TG" for the Norwich area). Within each square, a location is given by an easting and a northing measured from the square's south-west corner. A full grid reference such as TG 51409 13177 pinpoints a spot to within 10 metres or better; shorter references give progressively larger squares. The famous reference for Land's End sits in square SW, while the origin's "false" coordinates avoid negative numbers anywhere in the country.

Use

Introduced with the retriangulation of Great Britain in the 1930s, the National Grid underlies all Ordnance Survey mapping, including the Landranger and Explorer series used by walkers. With the advent of GPS, OSGB36 coordinates are routinely converted to and from WGS84 latitude and longitude, which differ by up to about 200 metres in Britain.
