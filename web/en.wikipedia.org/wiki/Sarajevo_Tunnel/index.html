The Sarajevo Tunnel, known in Bosnia as the Tunel spasa ("Tunnel of Hope"), was a tunnel constructed between March and June 1993 during the Siege of Sarajevo, in the midst of the Bosnian War. It was built by the Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina to link the besieged city of Sarajevo with Bosnian-held territory on the other side of Sarajevo Airport.

A preserved section of the tunnel, now part of the Tunnel of Hope museum in Butmir.

Background and construction

During the siege, which lasted from 1992 to 1996, Sarajevo was encircled by Bosnian Serb forces, and its only connection to the outside world was across the runway of Sarajevo Airport, a dangerous crossing under sniper fire. The tunnel ran from the suburb of Dobrinja, inside the city, beneath the airport runway, to Butmir outside the siege lines. Dug by hand around the clock in shifts, it was roughly 800 metres long, about 1.6 metres high and 1 metre wide, reinforced with steel and timber, and fitted with rails for small carts.

Use

The tunnel became the city's lifeline. Through it passed food, humanitarian aid, medicine, weapons and fuel, as well as telephone and power cables laid along its length. An estimated 2 to 3 million crossings were made during the war, and the tunnel allowed the Bosnian government to sustain the defence of the city and its roughly 300,000 remaining inhabitants until the siege was lifted in 1996.

Museum

A 20-metre section of the tunnel has been preserved at the house in Butmir whose cellar formed its entrance, owned by the Kolar family. The site now operates as the Tunnel of Hope museum, one of the most visited memorial sites in Sarajevo.
