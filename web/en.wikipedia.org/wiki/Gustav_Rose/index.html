Gustav Rose (18 March 1798 – 15 July 1873) was a German mineralogist, a leading figure in 19th-century crystallography and the study of meteorites. He was the brother of the chemist Heinrich Rose.

Life and work

Rose studied in Berlin and worked under the crystallographer Christian Samuel Weiss; he also spent time in Stockholm with Jöns Jacob Berzelius. In 1829 he accompanied Alexander von Humboldt on his expedition to the Russian Empire, collecting minerals across Siberia and the Altai, and published the mineralogical results of the journey. Rose became professor at the University of Berlin and director of its Mineralogical Museum.

Contributions

Rose made fundamental studies of crystal form and twinning, of quartz and feldspar, and classified meteorites into chondrites, achondrites and iron types — a scheme still recognisable today. He described several new minerals, and the mineral roselite was named in his honour.
