Genetic marker

A genetic marker is a gene or DNA sequence with a known location on a chromosome that can be used to identify individuals or species, or to track the inheritance of a nearby gene. Markers are useful because variation at the marker locus — polymorphism — can be detected by laboratory assays even when the marker itself has no effect on the trait being studied.

Types

Common types of DNA markers include restriction fragment length polymorphisms (RFLPs), amplified fragment length polymorphisms (AFLPs), microsatellites (short tandem repeats), and single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). Earlier generations of geneticists used visible phenotypes, blood groups and protein (allozyme) variants as markers.

Uses

Genetic markers are used to build linkage maps, perform association studies that locate genes for diseases and agricultural traits, select breeding stock (marker-assisted selection), and conduct forensic identification and paternity testing. Because a marker is inherited together with adjacent DNA, co-inheritance patterns reveal the physical position of genes on chromosomes.

Properties

A useful marker should be polymorphic, co-dominant (distinguishing heterozygotes from homozygotes), reproducible, and cheap to assay at high throughput; SNP arrays and sequencing-based genotyping now supply millions of markers per individual.
