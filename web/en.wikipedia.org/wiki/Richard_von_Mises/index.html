Richard von Mises

Born: 19 April 1883, Lemberg, Austria-Hungary
Died: 14 July 1953 (aged 70), Boston, Massachusetts
Fields: Applied mathematics, probability, aerodynamics
Institutions: University of Berlin; Istanbul University; Harvard University

Richard Edler von Mises (19 April 1883 – 14 July 1953) was an Austrian scientist and mathematician who worked on fluid mechanics, aerodynamics, aeronautics, statistics and probability theory. He was the younger brother of the economist Ludwig von Mises.

Life

Born in Lemberg (now Lviv), von Mises studied at the Vienna University of Technology and became professor at Strasbourg in 1909. During the First World War he served as a test pilot and aircraft designer for the Austro-Hungarian army. In 1920 he became director of the Institute of Applied Mathematics at the University of Berlin. Dismissed under Nazi racial laws, he moved to Istanbul in 1933 and then to Harvard in 1939, where he remained for the rest of his life.

Work

Von Mises developed the frequency theory of probability, defining probability as the limiting relative frequency in a "collective". In mechanics he formulated the von Mises yield criterion for ductile materials and contributed to boundary-layer theory and airfoil design. He founded the journal Zeitschrift für Angewandte Mathematik und Mechanik (ZAMM).
