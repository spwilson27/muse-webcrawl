Kumārajīva

Kumārajīva (Sanskrit: कुमारजीव; Chinese: 鳩摩羅什, Jiūmóluóshí; 344–413 CE) was a Buddhist monk, scholar and translator from the kingdom of Kucha in Central Asia. He is regarded as one of the greatest translators of Buddhist texts from Sanskrit into Chinese, and his elegant renderings — including of the Lotus Sutra, the Diamond Sutra and the treatises of Nāgārjuna — profoundly shaped East Asian Buddhism.


Statue of Kumārajīva near the Kizil Grottoes in Xinjiang.

Life

Kumārajīva was born in Kucha to an Indian father, Kumārāyaṇa, and a Kuchan princess. He studied Sarvāstivāda Buddhism in Kashmir and later converted to Mahāyāna. In 383 the Chinese general Lü Guang conquered Kucha and carried him off to Liangzhou, where he was held for some 17 years — during which he mastered Chinese. In 401 the Later Qin ruler Yao Xing brought him to Chang'an, where he headed an imperial translation bureau with hundreds of assistants.

Translations

In Chang'an, Kumārajīva and his team produced translations celebrated for their fluency and fidelity, among them the Lotus Sutra (the version still recited throughout East Asia), the Vimalakīrti Sutra, the Amitābha Sutra, and the Madhyamaka treatises Mūlamadhyamakakārikā and related texts that founded the Chinese Three Treatise school (Sanlun). Tradition credits him with 35 works in 294 scrolls. His disciples included Sengzhao and Daosheng.

Legacy

Kumārajīva reportedly vowed that if his translations were correct, his tongue would remain unburned at his cremation — a legend later cited as proof of their accuracy. His work set the standard for Chinese Buddhist translation until Xuanzang.
