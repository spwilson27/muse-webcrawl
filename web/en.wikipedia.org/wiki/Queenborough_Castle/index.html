Queenborough Castle

LocationQueenborough, Isle of Sheppey, Kent
Built1361–1368 (completed c. 1377)

Queenborough Castle, also known as Sheppey Castle, was a medieval castle near the mouth of the River Medway at Queenborough on the Isle of Sheppey, Kent, England. Built by King Edward III between 1361 and 1368, it was the last true concentric castle built in England and the first royal castle designed specifically to resist artillery.

Design

The castle had a perfectly circular plan: a round inner court ringed by buildings, surrounded by a circular curtain wall with six towers, an outer court, and a moat fed by a creek. Its innovative concentric geometry provided overlapping fields of fire for guns. Edward named the castle and the adjoining planned town after his queen, Philippa of Hainault.

Later history

The castle guarded the Swale channel and the approach to the Medway but saw little action. Repairs were made under Henry VIII. After the Civil War, Parliament sold it in 1650 and it was demolished; only earthworks and traces of the moat remain, within a public park. Excavations have confirmed the circular layout.
