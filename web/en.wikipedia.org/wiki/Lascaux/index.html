Lascaux (French: Grotte de Lascaux) is a network of caves near the village of Montignac, in the Dordogne department of southwestern France, containing some of the best-known examples of Upper Palaeolithic cave art. The paintings, nearly 600 in number, are estimated to be around 17,000 years old and consist mainly of large animals, most notably the great bulls of the Hall of the Bulls, horses, stags and aurochs, rendered in mineral pigments.

Discovery

The cave was discovered on 12 September 1940 by four teenagers — Marcel Ravidat, Jacques Marsal, Georges Agnel and Simon Coencas — who followed Ravidat's dog Robot down a hole left by an uprooted tree. After the Second World War the cave was opened to the public; by 1955 some 1,200 visitors a day were damaging the art through the carbon dioxide they exhaled, and the cave was closed in 1963. The original paintings deteriorated further from mould and lichen outbreaks in the 2000s, which conservators continue to manage.

The art

The most famous chamber, the Hall of the Bulls, contains 36 animals including four enormous bulls, one over 5 metres long — the largest animal figure in Palaeolithic art. Other sections include the Axial Gallery, the Nave, the Apse and the Shaft, which holds the cave's only depiction of a human figure, apparently falling beside a bison and a bird on a pole. The artists used the natural contours of the rock, scaffolding to reach the ceilings, and pigments of iron oxide, charcoal and manganese, applied by blowing through tubes as well as by brush and pad. The meaning of the imagery remains debated: interpretations have included hunting magic, shamanic visions and mythological narrative.

Replicas

To protect the original, France built full replicas nearby: Lascaux II (1983), reproducing the Hall of the Bulls and Axial Gallery, and Lascaux IV (2016), the International Centre for Cave Art at Montignac, which replicates the entire cave. Lascaux was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1979 as part of the Prehistoric Sites and Decorated Caves of the Vézère Valley.
