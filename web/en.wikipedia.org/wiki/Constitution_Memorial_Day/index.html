Constitution Memorial Day (憲法記念日, Kenpō Kinenbi) is a national holiday in Japan celebrated every year on 3 May in commemoration of the promulgation of the Constitution of Japan in 1947. It forms part of Golden Week, the cluster of holidays in late April and early May that includes Shōwa Day (29 April), Constitution Memorial Day, Greenery Day (4 May), and Children's Day (5 May) — one of the busiest travel periods of the Japanese year.

History

The post-war constitution, drafted under the Allied occupation and adopted by the Imperial Diet, was promulgated on 3 November 1946 and came into effect on 3 May 1947. It introduced popular sovereignty, guaranteed fundamental human rights, and renounced war in the famous Article 9. When the Act on National Holidays was enacted in 1948, 3 May was designated Constitution Memorial Day, intended — in the words of the law — as a day to commemorate the enforcement of the constitution and to nurture hopes for the nation's future growth. (The promulgation day, 3 November, became Culture Day.)

Observance

The day is marked by public lectures, open-house events, and press features reflecting on the constitution's meaning. The National Diet building has traditionally been opened to the public around this date. Because the constitution — never amended since 1947 — remains a subject of political debate, especially over Article 9 and proposals for revision advanced by some governments, Constitution Memorial Day often sees rallies both for protecting and for revising the constitution, and newspapers customarily publish opinion polls on amendment.
