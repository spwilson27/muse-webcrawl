Stylistic originsHouse, disco, funk, soft rock, dub, world music
Cultural originsMid-1980s, Ibiza, Balearic Islands, Spain

Balearic beat, also known as Balearic house, is an eclectic blend of DJ-led dance music that emerged in the mid-1980s on the Spanish island of Ibiza. Named after the Balearic Islands, it describes less a single genre than an open-minded approach to DJing: mixing house, disco, funk, rock, pop, reggae, dub and world music into a sun-soaked, anything-goes soundtrack for open-air clubbing.

The Balearic sound was born in Ibiza's clubs in the 1980s

History

The style is most associated with DJ Alfredo (Alfredo Fiorito), an Argentine DJ at the Amnesia nightclub, whose sets combined early Chicago house with unlikely records by artists such as Kate Bush, Chris Rea, The Woodentops and Talking Heads. British DJs including Paul Oakenfold, Danny Rampling and Nicky Holloway encountered the sound on holiday in Ibiza in 1987 — the famous "summer of love" trips — and exported it to London, where it helped ignite the acid house and rave explosion of 1988.

Characteristics

Balearic sets typically favour tempos around 110–125 BPM, soulful vocals, Mediterranean and flamenco guitars, pianos, dubby basslines and an emphasis on melody and atmosphere over aggression. The guiding principle is eclecticism: a record "works" if it fits the mood of a sunrise terrace, regardless of genre.

Legacy

The term "Balearic" has been applied ever since to any eclectic, summery DJ style. The sound influenced chill-out music, the Ibiza café compilations of the 1990s and 2000s, and contemporary "Balearic" revivals and reissue labels.
