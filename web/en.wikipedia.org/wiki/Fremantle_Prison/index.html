Fremantle Prison

Fremantle Prison
Location: Fremantle, Western Australia
Built: 1850s
Status: Museum; UNESCO World Heritage Site (2010)
Operated: 1855–1991

Fremantle Prison, sometimes referred to as Fremantle Gaol or Fremantle Jail, is a former Australian prison and World Heritage Site in Fremantle, Western Australia. The 15-acre (6 ha) site includes the prison cell blocks, gatehouse, perimeter walls, cottages, and tunnels.

History

The prison was built by convicts in the 1850s from limestone quarried on the site, after Western Australia accepted transportation in 1850 to relieve a labour shortage. It received its first convicts in 1855 and remained in continuous use as Western Australia's main maximum-security prison until its closure in 1991. Forty-four prisoners were executed at the prison, all by hanging; the last was serial killer Eric Edgar Cooke in 1964. Notorious inmates included bushranger Moondyne Joe, who escaped twice, and the prison saw major riots in 1968 and 1988, the latter causing extensive fire damage.

Heritage

Since closure the prison has been conserved as a museum and tourist attraction offering tours, including torchlight and tunnel tours. In 2010 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as one of the eleven Australian Convict Sites.
