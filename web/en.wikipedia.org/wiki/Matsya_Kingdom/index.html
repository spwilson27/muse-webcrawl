Matsya Kingdom

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Matsya was one of the sixteen Mahajanapadas (great realms) of ancient India.

Matsya (Sanskrit: Matsya, "fish") was an ancient Indian kingdom, counted among the solasa (sixteen) Mahajanapadas. It lay south of the Kuru kingdom and west of the Yamuna river, in territory corresponding roughly to the former Jaipur state of modern Rajasthan, encompassing the whole of present-day Alwar with portions of Bharatpur. Its capital was Viratanagara (modern Bairat), said to have been founded by King Virata.

In the Mahabharata

Matsya is best known from the epic Mahabharata. During the thirteenth year of their exile, the Pandavas were required to live incognito; they took service at the court of King Virata of Matsya. Yudhishthira became a courtier under the name Kanka, Bhima a cook named Vallabha, Arjuna a dance and music teacher named Brihannala, Nakula and Sahadeva tended horses and cattle, and Draupadi served Queen Sudeshna as a maid named Sairandhri. The year culminated in the Kaurava raid on Matsya's cattle, defeated by Arjuna with Prince Uttara as his charioteer. The royal city of Virata is also where Abhimanyu married Virata's daughter Uttara, whose son Parikshit continued the Kuru line.

History

Matsya appears in early Buddhist and Jain texts among the great realms of the 6th century BC. To the west it was bordered by the hill country associated with painted grey ware culture settlements. Over time the kingdom lost its independence, coming successively under the influence of Magadha and later empires; by the early centuries AD the region was absorbed into larger polities, and the Matsya tribal confederacy is also remembered in later tradition around Alwar and the Matsya Union formed briefly at Indian independence in 1948.
