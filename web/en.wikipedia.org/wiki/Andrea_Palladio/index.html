Andrea Palladio

Andrea Palladio (30 November 1508 – 19 August 1580) was an Italian Renaissance architect active in the Venetian Republic. Influenced by Roman and Greek architecture, primarily by Vitruvius, he is widely considered one of the most influential individuals in the history of architecture. The term Palladian derives from his name.

Born1508, Padua
Died1580, Vicenza / Maser
Known forVilla Rotonda, Basilica Palladiana, I quattro libri dell'architettura

Life and works

Born Andrea di Pietro della Gondola in Padua, he trained as a stonemason and was patronized by Gian Giorgio Trissino, who gave him the name Palladio. In Vicenza he built palazzi and the Basilica Palladiana; across the Veneto he designed villas such as Villa Rotonda and Villa Barbaro, and in Venice the churches of San Giorgio Maggiore and Il Redentore.

The Four Books

His treatise I quattro libri dell'architettura (1570) codified his system of proportion and his designs, spreading Palladianism to England (Inigo Jones, Lord Burlington), and later to the United States, where Thomas Jefferson drew on it for Monticello and the University of Virginia.

Legacy

The City of Vicenza and the Palladian Villas of the Veneto are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
