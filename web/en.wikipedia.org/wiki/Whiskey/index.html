Whisky

Whisky or whiskey is a type of distilled alcoholic beverage made from fermented grain mash. Various grains (which may be malted) are used for different varieties, including barley, corn, rye, and wheat. Whisky is typically aged in wooden casks, which are typically made of charred white oak. The spelling whisky is used in Scotland, Canada, and Japan, while whiskey is used in Ireland and the United States.

History

The art of distillation spread to Ireland and Scotland by the 15th century; the first written record of whisky in Ireland dates to 1405 and in Scotland to 1494. The name derives from the Gaelic uisce beatha, "water of life", itself a calque of the Latin aqua vitae.

Production

Grains are mashed and fermented to produce a beer-like wash, which is distilled — in pot stills for malt whisky, or in column stills for grain whisky — and the resulting spirit matured in oak casks. Maturation gives whisky much of its colour and flavour; Scotch whisky must be aged in Scotland for at least three years.

Styles

Major styles include Scotch whisky (single malt and blended), Irish whiskey, American bourbon and rye (made in charred new oak), Canadian whisky, and Japanese whisky. Many countries regulate the production and labelling of the spirit; the global market is worth tens of billions of dollars annually.
