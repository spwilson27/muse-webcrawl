The Champlain Canal is a canal in eastern New York State that connects the Hudson River to Lake Champlain. It runs roughly from Waterford, near the confluence of the Hudson and Mohawk rivers, north to Whitehall at the southern end of Lake Champlain.

History

Authorized alongside the Erie Canal, the canal opened in 1823 and provided a navigable water route from New York City via the Hudson to Lake Champlain and onward toward the St. Lawrence River, boosting trade in lumber, iron and agricultural goods.

Today

The modern canal, rebuilt as part of the New York State Barge Canal system in the early 20th century, has 11 locks and remains open to commercial and recreational traffic as part of the New York State Canal System.
