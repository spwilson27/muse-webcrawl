Alan of Galloway

Alan fitz Roland
Lord of Galloway; Constable of Scotland
Born: c. 1175
Died: 1234
Buried: Dundrennan Abbey
Father: Roland of Galloway
Mother: Elena de Morville

Alan of Galloway (c. 1175 – 1234), also known as Alan fitz Roland, was a leading Scottish magnate of the early 13th century. As hereditary Lord of Galloway and Constable of Scotland, he was one of the most powerful men in the British Isles, commanding a large fleet and army in the Irish Sea world and ruling a semi-independent lordship in south-western Scotland.

Career

Alan inherited Galloway from his father Roland (Lochlann) in 1200 and the constableship through his mother Elena, heiress of the de Morville family. He served both the Scottish crown and King John of England, receiving lands in England and Ireland. He campaigned in Ulster, where he was granted a large lordship in Antrim, and his fleets dominated the Irish Sea. In 1215 he contributed a large contingent — reportedly 1,000 men — to the baronial side against John, and Galloway's knights were among the Scottish forces in the Barons' War. He was a benefactor of the church, confirming his ancestors' foundation of Dundrennan Abbey, where he was buried.

Death and succession

Alan died in 1234 leaving only daughters by his marriages (his son Thomas, by a daughter of David of Huntingdon, also predeceased him in some accounts). King Alexander II insisted on dividing Galloway among Alan's three daughters and their husbands — including John de Balliol and Roger de Quincy — contrary to Galwegian custom, provoking a revolt that was crushed in 1235, effectively ending Galloway's separate identity.
