The equatorial coordinate system is a celestial coordinate system widely used to specify the positions of celestial objects. It uses the projection of the Earth's equator onto the celestial sphere as its fundamental plane, so coordinates do not change as the Earth rotates.

Coordinates

Declination (δ) is measured in degrees north (+) or south (−) of the celestial equator, analogous to latitude. Right ascension (α) is measured eastward along the equator from the vernal equinox, usually in hours, minutes and seconds of time (24 h = 360°).

Epoch and precession

Because the Earth's axis precesses, the equinox and equator slowly move, so published coordinates are tied to a standard epoch such as J2000.0. Observers convert catalogue positions to the epoch of date for accurate telescope pointing.

Use

Star catalogues, planetarium software and equatorial telescope mounts all use this system; an equatorial mount aligned on the celestial pole can track objects by rotating about one axis at the sidereal rate.
