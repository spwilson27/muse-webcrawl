History of Poland (1945–1989)

Period1945 – 1989
RegimePolish People's Republic (communist)
Key eventsSolidarity (1980), Martial law (1981), Round Table (1989)

The history of Poland from 1945 to 1989 covers the Polish People's Republic, the communist state established after the Second World War under Soviet domination, ending with the negotiated transition of 1989.

Establishing communist rule

Poland's borders shifted west: the USSR annexed the eastern territories while Poland gained former German lands, and millions of people were expelled or resettled. The communist Polish United Workers' Party consolidated power through a rigged referendum (1946) and elections (1947); Stalinist repression followed.

Crises and opposition

Worker protests in Poznań (1956) brought Władysław Gomułka to power, but renewed unrest in 1968, 1970 and 1976 showed the regime's fragility. In August 1980 strikes at the Gdańsk Shipyard produced Solidarity, the first independent trade union in the communist bloc, led by Lech Wałęsa. General Jaruzelski imposed martial law in December 1981.

The road to 1989

Economic collapse and renewed strikes forced the Round Table Talks of early 1989. Semi-free elections on 4 June 1989 delivered a landslide to Solidarity, and Tadeusz Mazowiecki became the first non-communist prime minister in the Soviet bloc, opening the way to the Third Polish Republic.
