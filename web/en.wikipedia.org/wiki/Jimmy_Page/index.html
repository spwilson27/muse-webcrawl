Jimmy Page

James Patrick Page (born 9 January 1944) is an English musician and producer who achieved international success as the guitarist and founder of the rock band Led Zeppelin. He is widely considered one of the greatest and most influential guitarists of all time.

Early career

Page began as one of London's most in-demand session guitarists in the 1960s, playing on records by the Who, the Kinks, and Donovan, before joining the Yardbirds in 1966, first on bass and then sharing lead guitar with Jeff Beck.

Led Zeppelin

In 1968 Page formed Led Zeppelin with Robert Plant, John Paul Jones, and John Bonham. As the band's guitarist and producer he crafted its signature sound on albums including Led Zeppelin II (1969), Led Zeppelin IV (1971) — featuring "Stairway to Heaven," partly played on his double-neck Gibson — and Physical Graffiti (1975). The band disbanded in 1980 after Bonham's death.

Later work

Afterwards Page formed the Firm with Paul Rodgers, collaborated with David Coverdale, and reunited with Plant for the No Quarter project and the album Walking into Clarksdale. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice, with the Yardbirds (1992) and Led Zeppelin (1995).
