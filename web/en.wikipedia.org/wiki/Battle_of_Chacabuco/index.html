The Battle of Chacabuco, fought on 12 February 1817, was a decisive engagement of the Chilean War of Independence. The patriot Army of the Andes, commanded by General José de San Martín, had just completed its famous Crossing of the Andes from Argentina and descended into the Chacabuco Valley north of Santiago, where it met a royalist force under Colonel Rafael Maroto.

Background

After the patriot defeat at Rancagua in 1814, Chile had been reconquered by Spain and its patriot leaders exiled to Mendoza. There San Martín organized the Army of the Andes, crossing the cordillera in January 1817 by several passes. Maroto, sent to block the advance, took up a position on the heights of Chacabuco overlooking the road to Santiago.

Battle

San Martín divided his army into two columns. Bernardo O'Higgins led a frontal attack that was initially repulsed, while a second column under Soler turned the royalist flank. When O'Higgins renewed his assault in coordination with the flanking movement, the royalist line collapsed. The patriots lost relatively few men; the royalists suffered several hundred killed and captured, and Maroto barely escaped.

Aftermath

The victory opened the road to Santiago, which the patriots entered two days later. O'Higgins was proclaimed Supreme Director of Chile, and the battle marked the effective end of Spanish rule in central Chile, though royalist resistance continued until the Battle of Maipú in April 1818.
