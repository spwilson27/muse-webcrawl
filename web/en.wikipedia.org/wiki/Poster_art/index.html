Poster art

Poster art is a form of graphic art in which images and text are combined on a printed sheet intended for public display, typically for advertising, propaganda, or decoration. The modern artistic poster emerged in the late 19th century with the development of colour lithography.

History

Jules Chéret is often called the father of the modern poster: his brightly coloured lithographs advertising Parisian entertainments of the 1870s–1890s turned billboards into public art galleries. Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec, with iconic works such as Moulin Rouge: La Goulue (1891), compressed figures into bold flat shapes influenced by Japanese woodblock prints. Alphonse Mucha's Art Nouveau posters, beginning with Gismonda (1894) for Sarah Bernhardt, defined an international style. The Sachplakat ("object poster") of Lucian Bernhard and the Swiss Style of Josef Müller-Brockmann later pushed toward minimal, typographic design.

Uses

Posters have served commercial advertising, theatre and film promotion, political propaganda in both world wars and the Cold War, travel marketing by railway and airline companies, and protest movements from May 1968 to the present. The film poster and the gig poster remain vibrant subgenres, and vintage posters are widely collected.
