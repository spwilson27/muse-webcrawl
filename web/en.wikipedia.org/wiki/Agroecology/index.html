Agroecology
Approach: Ecology applied to food systems; also a science, practice, and movement

Agroecology is an academic discipline, a set of agricultural practices, and a social movement that applies ecological principles to the design and management of sustainable food systems. It studies agroecosystems holistically, including human and environmental dimensions.

Principles

Key principles include recycling nutrients, enhancing biodiversity and soil health, minimising external inputs and losses, intercropping, agroforestry, integrated pest management, and valuing farmers' local knowledge. It is often contrasted with industrial agriculture that followed the later phases of the Agricultural Revolution / Green Revolution.

History

The term appeared in the 1930s, and the field developed through work by ecologists and agronomists and Latin American movements such as La Vía Campesina. The FAO has promoted agroecology as central to sustainable food security and climate adaptation.
