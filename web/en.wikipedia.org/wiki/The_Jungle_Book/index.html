The Jungle Book

The Jungle Book is an 1894 collection of stories by the English author Rudyard Kipling. Most of the characters are animals such as Shere Khan the tiger and Baloo the bear, though a principal character is the boy or "man-cub" Mowgli, who is raised in the jungle by wolves. The stories are set in a forest in India; one place mentioned repeatedly is Seoni (Seonee), in the central Indian state of Madhya Pradesh.

A major theme in the book is abandonment followed by foster care, as in the life of Mowgli, echoing Kipling's own childhood. The theme is echoed in the triumph of protagonists including Rikki-Tikki-Tavi and The White Seal over their enemies, as well as Mowgli's. Another important theme is of law and freedom; the stories are not about animal behaviour, still less about the Darwinian struggle for survival, but about human archetypes in animal form. The stories are moral fables, drawing on the conventions of the Indian Panchatantra tradition.

Stories

The best-known stories follow Mowgli: "Mowgli's Brothers", in which the infant Mowgli is adopted by the Seeonee wolf pack and taught the Law of the Jungle by Baloo and Bagheera while being hunted by the tiger Shere Khan; "Kaa's Hunting", in which he is kidnapped by the Bandar-log monkeys; and "Tiger! Tiger!", in which he returns to a human village and finally kills Shere Khan with a buffalo stampede. Other stories in the collection include "Rikki-Tikki-Tavi", about a brave mongoose protecting a family from cobras; "Toomai of the Elephants"; "The White Seal", set in the Bering Sea; and "Her Majesty's Servants". Each story is framed by a short poem or song.

Adaptations and influence

The book has never been out of print and has strongly influenced children's literature and the Scout movement—Robert Baden-Powell drew its names and themes for the Wolf Cubs programme. It has been adapted many times, most famously in Disney's animated film of 1967 and the live-action/CGI film of 2016 directed by Jon Favreau.
