Barnaby Rudge

Barnaby Rudge: A Tale of the Riots of Eighty is a historical novel by British author Charles Dickens. Published in 1841, first in serial form in Dickens's weekly periodical Master Humphrey's Clock, it was Dickens's fifth novel and his first historical novel (the second being A Tale of Two Cities). The story is set during the Gordon Riots of 1780, anti-Catholic disturbances that devastated parts of London.

Plot

The gentle, intellectually disabled Barnaby Rudge lives with his mother and his pet raven, Grip. A long-unsolved murder at the Warren, a country house, entwines the Rudges with the Haredale and Chester families. As anti-Catholic agitation in London erupts into the Gordon Riots of June 1780, Barnaby is swept up in the mob and nearly hanged before being pardoned. Secrets about the old murder are revealed, the villains meet their ends, and the surviving lovers are united. Grip the raven is credited with inspiring Edgar Allan Poe's poem "The Raven."

Characters and themes

Major characters include Barnaby and his mother Mary Rudge; Gabriel Varden, a locksmith; his apprentice Sim Tappertit; Dolly Varden; the steward Rudge senior; Mr. Chester and his son Edward; Geoffrey Haredale; the hangman Dennis; and Hugh, the hostler. Themes include mob psychology, religious intolerance, the abuse of authority, and the contrast between order and chaos. Though less popular than many of Dickens's later novels, Barnaby Rudge is admired for its vivid depiction of the riots.
