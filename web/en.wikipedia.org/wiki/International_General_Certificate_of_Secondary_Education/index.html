International General Certificate of Secondary Education

The International General Certificate of Secondary Education (IGCSE) is an English-language-based examination similar to the GCSE examinations taken by students in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. It was developed by Cambridge Assessment International Education (then University of Cambridge International Examinations) in 1988 to meet the needs of international schools and students educated outside the British system. It is recognised by universities and employers worldwide as evidence of secondary education, and is commonly taken by students aged 14 to 16 as preparation for A Levels, the International Baccalaureate or other advanced study.

Providers and subjects

Cambridge Assessment International Education is the largest provider, offering Cambridge IGCSE in more than 70 subjects in over 150 countries. Edexcel (Pearson) also offers an International GCSE, reformed in the 2010s to align with the revised English GCSEs, including the 9–1 grading scale. OxfordAQA provides a third variant. Subjects range across languages, humanities, social sciences, mathematics, sciences, and creative and vocational areas.

Assessment

Assessment is typically by written examination at the end of the course, though some subjects include coursework or practical components. Many subjects are offered at Core and Extended levels (Cambridge) or Foundation and Higher tiers, allowing differentiation by ability. Cambridge grades IGCSE on an A* to G scale, while reformed Edexcel International GCSEs use the 9–1 scale adopted from the English GCSE reforms.

Comparison with GCSE

The IGCSE was originally designed to be more flexible than the domestic GCSE for students outside Britain: it avoids coursework requirements that are difficult to administer internationally and historically placed less emphasis on specifically British content. In England, IGCSEs were once widely offered by independent schools, which regarded them as more rigorous; following government reforms, most state-funded schools returned to the reformed GCSE, while IGCSE remains common in the independent sector and internationally.
