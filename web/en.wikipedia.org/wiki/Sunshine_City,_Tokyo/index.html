Sunshine City (サンシャインシティ, Sanshain Shiti) is a large commercial and entertainment complex in Ikebukuro, Toshima, Tokyo, Japan. Opened in 1978 on the site of the former Sugamo Prison — where Japanese war criminals were held after the Second World War — it was one of Japan's first "city within a city" developments, combining offices, shops, restaurants, a hotel, an aquarium, a planetarium, a museum and indoor theme parks.

Buildings and attractions

The centrepiece is Sunshine 60, a 60-storey, 240-metre skyscraper that was the tallest building in Asia when completed in 1978 and the tallest in Japan until 1985; its elevators were once among the fastest in the world. At its top is the Sky Circus Sunshine 60 Observatory, offering panoramic views of Tokyo and virtual-reality attractions. The complex also includes the Prince Hotel, the World Import Mart and Bunka Kaikan buildings, the Sunshine Aquarium — notable for its rooftop "Sunshine Aqua Ring" tank in which sea lions appear to swim through the sky — the Ancient Orient Museum, the Konica Minolta planetarium, and Namco's indoor theme park Namja Town.

History

The site was occupied by Sugamo Prison from 1895; after the war it was used by the Allied occupation to detain suspected war criminals, and seven were executed there in 1948. The prison was demolished in 1971 and the land redeveloped, with Sunshine City opening in October 1978 as a symbol of Japan's postwar economic resurgence.
