Gore-Tex

Gore-Tex is a waterproof, breathable fabric membrane and registered trademark of W. L. Gore & Associates. Invented in 1969, Gore-Tex was originally designed for use in a wide spectrum of applications, including those in the medical, industrial and apparel fields. Due to its enduring performance, Gore-Tex technology is routinely used in manufacturing consumer goods, particularly active- and outer-wear, and is perhaps best known for its use in garments designed for outdoor recreation.

History

Gore-Tex was co-invented by Wilbert L. Gore and his son, Robert W. (Bob) Gore. In 1969, Bob Gore stretched heated rods of polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) and found that the polymer could be "expanded," creating a microporous structure that was about 70 percent air. This material, expanded PTFE (ePTFE), was extremely strong and contained billions of microscopic pores per square inch — small enough to block liquid water droplets but large enough to let water vapor pass through. The discovery led to a 1970 patent, and the Gore-Tex brand was introduced commercially in 1976, initially in outdoor apparel.

The first large-scale use of Gore-Tex fabric in consumer clothing came through garments produced with Early Winters, a Seattle outdoor company, in the mid-1970s. The material quickly became a standard in high-performance rainwear, mountaineering clothing and footwear. In 1989, Gore introduced a guarantee program ("Guaranteed to Keep You Dry"), strengthening the brand's association with waterproof performance.

Structure and properties

The Gore-Tex membrane is a thin film of expanded PTFE with a microporous structure — roughly 1.4 billion pores per square centimeter. Each pore is about 20,000 times smaller than a droplet of liquid water but about 700 times larger than a molecule of water vapor, which explains the membrane's combination of waterproofness and breathability. In apparel, the membrane is typically laminated between an outer face fabric and a lining, with seams sealed by tape to maintain waterproof integrity. Contamination by oils and dirt can compromise performance, so later versions added a protective polyurethane layer at the cost of some breathability ("Gore-Tex Paclite" and other product lines balance these trade-offs differently).

Applications

Beyond clothing and footwear, ePTFE and Gore-Tex technology are used in:

  • Medical implants, vascular grafts and sutures
  • Industrial filtration and venting
  • Cable insulation and seals
  • Musical instrument strings (Elixir brand) and dental floss (Glide)
  • Protective clothing for firefighters, military personnel and workers in hazardous environments

Competing products and criticism

Gore-Tex competes with other waterproof-breathable membrane systems, including eVent, SympaTex, and various proprietary laminates marketed by outdoor brands. Critics have noted that breathability in real-world conditions can fall short of marketing claims, especially in warm, humid weather. The use of fluoropolymers has also drawn environmental scrutiny: PTFE belongs to the broader family of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), and Gore announced plans to transition its consumer fabrics to non-PFAS membranes, introducing an expanded polyethylene (ePE) membrane in products from 2022 onward.
