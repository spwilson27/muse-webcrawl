Russian empire

This is a redirect in some navigations to the article on the Russian Empire, the state that existed from 1721, when Peter the Great was proclaimed Emperor of All Russia, until the abdication of Nicholas II in 1917 during the February Revolution.

Overview

The Russian Empire was the third-largest empire in history by land area, stretching across Eastern Europe, Northern Asia and parts of North America, from the Baltic Sea to the Pacific Ocean. At its peak in the late nineteenth century it covered some 22.8 million square kilometres and contained more than 125 million subjects of many ethnicities and religions.

History

Peter the Great's reforms westernised the state and military, founding St Petersburg as a new capital. Catherine the Great expanded the empire into Crimea and partitioned Poland. The nineteenth century saw victory over Napoleon (1812), the emancipation of the serfs (1861), defeat in the Crimean War, expansion into Central Asia and the Caucasus, and the sale of Alaska (1867). Industrialisation under Sergei Witte, defeat by Japan in 1905, and the creation of the Duma followed. Defeats and privations in the First World War precipitated the revolutions of 1917 that ended the empire.
