Panamax

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Panamax bulk carrier squeezed into a Panama Canal lock chamber]
A ship in the Miraflores Locks.

Panamax and Neopanamax are size limits for ships transiting the Panama Canal, set by the lock chambers and water draft. Original Panamax (from the canal\'s 1914 opening) allows maximum dimensions of about 294.1 m length, 32.3 m beam and 12.0 m tropical fresh-water draft — roughly 65,000–80,000 dwt. The expanded locks opened in 2016 define Neopanamax (366 m × 49 m × 15.2 m), admitting container ships up to about 14,000–15,000 TEU.

Influence on design

For a century, Panamax was a primary design constraint: bulkers, tankers and container ships were sized to the locks, and \'post-Panamax\' ships knowingly traded canal access for scale. The 2016 expansion reset the economics of Asia–US East Coast container routes and enabled large LNG carriers to transit.

Limits and drought

Beam is limited by lock width, length by chamber length and gate geometry, and draft by Gatun Lake water levels — in the 2023–24 drought, draft restrictions and daily transit cuts (from ~36 to as low as 24 slots) caused queues and diversions via Suez and the US land bridge, showing the canal\'s climate vulnerability.

Other \'max\' sizes

The naming pattern spread: Suezmax, Seawaymax (St. Lawrence), Handymax, Aframax, Chinamax (Valemax ore carriers) and Malaccamax each describe the largest ship able to use a route or trade.
