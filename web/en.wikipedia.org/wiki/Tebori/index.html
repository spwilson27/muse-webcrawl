Tebori

Tebori (Japanese: 手彫り, literally "hand carving") is the traditional Japanese method of tattooing by hand, without the use of an electric machine. The term also describes the resulting style of tattoo. Tebori is closely associated with irezumi, the full-body Japanese tattoo tradition.

Technique

The tebori artist uses a tool called a nomi: a long rod of bamboo or metal tipped with a bundle of needles. Holding the rod in one hand, the artist stretches the skin with the other and rhythmically pushes the needles into the skin, depositing ink — traditionally sumi ink, often the blue-green Nara black that changes colour as it settles. Shading is achieved by varying the angle, depth and speed of the strokes.

Training and practice

Traditional tebori masters (horishi) undergo long apprenticeships, historically involving household service before being permitted to tattoo clients. Practitioners often adopt professional names prefixed with hori ("to carve"). Large works, such as full back pieces, may take years of regular sessions to complete.

Comparison with machine tattooing

Advocates argue that tebori produces softer gradations and colours that age distinctively, and that the hand process preserves a craft lineage. Machine tattooing is faster and more common in modern studios; some contemporary Japanese artists combine both methods, using machines for outlines and tebori for shading.
