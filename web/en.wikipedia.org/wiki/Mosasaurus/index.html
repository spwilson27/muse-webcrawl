Mosasaurus (meaning "lizard of the Meuse River") is a genus of mosasaurs, giant aquatic carnivorous lizards that lived during the Late Cretaceous period, roughly 82 to 66 million years ago. Mosasaurus was among the last and largest of the mosasaurs, with the biggest species reaching an estimated 13–15 metres (43–49 ft) in length. Its fossils, first found near the Meuse River in the Netherlands in the 18th century, played a historic role in the development of palaeontology and the recognition of extinction.

Discovery and history

In 1764, a partial skull was discovered in a chalk quarry near Maastricht, and a second, more complete skull was found around 1770–1774. These specimens were studied by Petrus Camper and his son Adriaan, and the second skull was seized as war booty after French revolutionary troops captured Maastricht in 1794 and taken to Paris, where Georges Cuvier later identified it as a giant marine lizard related to monitor lizards — a landmark conclusion. William Conybeare named the genus Mosasaurus in 1822, and Gideon Mantell named the type species M. hoffmannii in 1829. The Maastricht fossils helped establish that species could become extinct, a radical idea at the time.

Description

Mosasaurus had a robust skull with conical, slightly curved teeth suited to gripping and crushing prey, and — unlike many earlier mosasaurs — jaws lined with teeth also on the palate (pterygoid teeth). Its body was streamlined, with four paddle-like flippers and a long tail ending in a downward bend supporting a two-lobed caudal fin, an adaptation confirmed by exceptional fossils preserving soft tissues. Studies of its anatomy suggest it was a powerful swimmer that relied on its tail for propulsion, probably patrolling surface waters as an apex predator.

Palaeobiology

Mosasaurus preyed on fish, seabirds, turtles, plesiosaurs, sharks, and other mosasaurs; fossil stomach contents and bite-marked bones document this diet, and healed injuries show that individuals also fought one another. Like other mosasaurs it breathed air and gave birth to live young at sea. Fossils of Mosasaurus are known from Europe, North America, and elsewhere, reflecting its wide distribution in the shallow seas of the Late Cretaceous. The genus disappeared in the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago.

Classification

Mosasaurus belongs to the family Mosasauridae within the order Squamata, the group containing lizards and snakes. Commonly recognized species include M. hoffmannii, M. missouriensis, M. conodon, M. lemonnieri, and M. beaugei, though the taxonomy of the genus has been repeatedly revised.

In popular culture

A giant mosasaur featured as an attraction in the film Jurassic World (2015) greatly raised the animal's public profile, though the film's animal far exceeds the real genus's size.
