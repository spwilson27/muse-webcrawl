Eagle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eagles are large birds of prey of the family Accipitridae — not a single natural group but a name for the bigger hawks and eagles, including sea eagles, snake eagles, harpy and Philippine eagles, and the 'booted' true eagles such as the golden eagle. They share heavy hooked beaks, powerful talons, broad wings for soaring, and the keenest eyesight in the animal kingdom — acuity estimated at several times a human's, with eyes nearly as large as ours in a far smaller head.

Eagles are apex aerial predators and scavengers: golden eagles take prey up to foxes and young deer, harpy eagles snatch monkeys and sloths from the canopy, and bald eagles — North America's emblem — steal fish from ospreys as readily as they catch their own.

Conservation stories

The bald eagle's recovery from DDT-driven collapse (a few hundred nesting pairs in the contiguous US in the 1960s) to delisting (2007) is a landmark of environmental law. The Philippine and harpy eagles remain threatened by forest loss; lead poisoning from ammunition fragments is a modern, fixable cause of eagle deaths.

Symbolism

Eagles have symbolised power since Rome's legionary standard (the aquila) and on the arms of empires and republics from Byzantium's double-headed eagle to the United States' Great Seal — the bald eagle was formally designated the US national bird by statute only in 2024, after 240 years as its emblem.
