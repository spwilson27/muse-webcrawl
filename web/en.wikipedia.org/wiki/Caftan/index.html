Kaftan

TypeGarment
OriginAncient Mesopotamia / Persia
VariantsMoroccan takchita, Ottoman kaftan, Russian kaftan

A kaftan or caftan is a variant of the robe or tunic worn in many cultures around the world for thousands of years. Typically a long, loose garment with wide sleeves, belted at the waist, the kaftan is associated with the Ottoman Empire, Morocco, Persia, and West Africa, and was adopted into European fashion in the 20th century.

Ottoman sultans wore lavish silk kaftans, many preserved in the Topkapı Palace collection, and the robe was presented as a gift of honour (hilat) within court culture.

History

Garments of kaftan type appear in ancient Persian and Mesopotamian dress. Under the Ottomans, the fabrics, colours, and fur linings of kaftans signalled rank; in Morocco, the caftan became an elaborate ceremonial garment for women, richly embroidered and worn at weddings.

Regional forms

In Russia, the kaftan was a long coat worn by peasants and merchants. In West Africa, related robes such as the grand boubou share the flowing silhouette. Moroccan caftans today are showcased at fashion events and were inscribed in cultural-heritage discussions.

Modern fashion

Designers such as Yves Saint Laurent popularised the kaftan as glamorous resort wear in the 1960s and 1970s, and it remains a staple of warm-weather and bohemian fashion.
