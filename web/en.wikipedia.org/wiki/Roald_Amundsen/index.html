Roald Engelbregt Gravning Amundsen (16 July 1872 – c. 18 June 1928) was a Norwegian explorer of the polar regions. A key figure of the Heroic Age of Antarctic Exploration, he led the first expedition to reach the South Pole, arriving on 14 December 1911 with four companions, five weeks ahead of Robert Falcon Scott's British party. He was also the first to traverse the Northwest Passage by ship (1903–1906) and, in 1926, flew over the North Pole in the airship Norge, making him arguably the first person to reach both poles.

Early life

Amundsen was born in Borge, near Fredrikstad, into a family of shipowners and captains. Against his mother's wishes he pursued exploration, gaining experience as first mate on the Belgian Antarctic Expedition (1897–1899), the first expedition to winter in Antarctica.

Northwest Passage

From 1903 to 1906, Amundsen led a crew of six aboard the small sloop Gjøa through Canada's Arctic archipelago, completing the first single transit of the Northwest Passage. The expedition spent two winters at Gjøa Haven, where Amundsen learned Arctic survival and dog-sledging techniques from the local Netsilik Inuit—knowledge crucial to his later success in Antarctica.

South Pole expedition

Amundsen originally planned to sail for the North Pole, but on learning that Frederick Cook and Robert Peary claimed to have reached it, he secretly changed his objective to the South Pole. Sailing in Fridtjof Nansen's ship Fram, he established a base, Framheim, at the Bay of Whales on the Ross Ice Shelf in January 1911. Using skis and Greenland dog teams, and laying supply depots along the route, his party of five reached the Pole on 14 December 1911 and returned safely after 99 days.

Later expeditions and death

In 1918 Amundsen began a drift across the Arctic Ocean in the ship Maud, completing a transit of the Northeast Passage. In 1926 he, Lincoln Ellsworth, and Umberto Nobile flew the airship Norge from Svalbard over the North Pole to Alaska. In June 1928 Amundsen disappeared while flying a rescue mission for Nobile's crashed airship Italia in the Barents Sea; wreckage of his flying boat was later found, but his body was never recovered.
