Malcolm Timothy Gladwell CM (born 3 September 1963) is a Canadian journalist, author and public speaker. He has been a staff writer for The New Yorker since 1996 and is known for books that apply social-science research to everyday phenomena in an accessible narrative style.

Early life and career

Gladwell was born in England to a Jamaican mother, psychotherapist Joyce Gladwell, and an English father, mathematics professor Graham Gladwell, and grew up in Ontario, Canada. After graduating from the University of Toronto, he worked at The American Spectator and then as a reporter at The Washington Post, where he covered business and science.

Books

  • The Tipping Point: How Little Things Can Make a Big Difference (2000) — on how ideas spread like epidemics
  • Blink: The Power of Thinking Without Thinking (2005) — on rapid cognition and intuition
  • Outliers: The Story of Success (2008) — popularised the "10,000-hour rule"
  • What the Dog Saw (2009) — collected essays
  • David and Goliath (2013) — on underdogs and advantages of disadvantage
  • Talking to Strangers (2019) — on misjudging people we do not know
  • The Bomber Mafia (2021) — on precision bombing in the Second World War
  • Revenge of the Tipping Point (2024)

Gladwell also co-founded the podcast company Pushkin Industries and hosts the podcast Revisionist History. His work has been praised for narrative skill and criticised by some academics for oversimplifying research; several of his books have been international bestsellers.
