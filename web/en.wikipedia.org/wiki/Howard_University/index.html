Howard University is a private, federally chartered historically Black research university in Washington, D.C. Chartered by Congress in 1867, it is named after General Oliver Otis Howard, a Civil War hero and commissioner of the Freedmen's Bureau. Howard is one of the most prestigious historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity".

History

Howard was founded shortly after the Civil War to educate newly freed African Americans, and from the start admitted students of all races and sexes in principle. Its law school, under dean Charles Hamilton Houston in the 1930s, became the training ground for the civil-rights litigation strategy that culminated in Brown v. Board of Education (1954); its most famous graduate was Thurgood Marshall, later the first Black US Supreme Court justice. Mordecai Wyatt Johnson, appointed in 1926, was Howard's first Black president and greatly expanded the university.

Academics and campus

The university comprises more than a dozen schools and colleges, including medicine, law, business, engineering, divinity and the College of Arts and Sciences, enrolling roughly 12,000 students. Its main campus occupies about 256 acres in northwest Washington, anchored by Founders Library. Howard University Hospital serves the surrounding community.

Notable alumni

Alumni include Vice President Kamala Harris, Nobel laureate Toni Morrison, Supreme Court justice Thurgood Marshall, civil-rights figures such as Vernon Jordan and (historian) Carter G. Woodson taught there; many members of the "Divine Nine" Black Greek-letter organisations were founded or chartered at Howard, including Alpha Kappa Alpha (1908), Omega Psi Phi (1911), Delta Sigma Theta (1913), Phi Beta Sigma (1914) and Zeta Phi Beta (1920).
