Polish language

Polish text written in the Latin alphabet with diacritics

Polish (Polish: język polski) is a West Slavic language of the Lechitic group, spoken primarily in Poland, where it is the official language. It is one of the most widely spoken Slavic languages, with around 50 million speakers worldwide, including large communities in Germany, the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, and Brazil.

Features

Polish is written in the Latin alphabet with additional letters formed with diacritics: ą, ć, ę, ł, ń, ó, ś, ź, ż, together with digraphs such as sz, cz, dż, and rz representing its characteristically rich set of sibilant and affricate consonants. The language is highly inflected: nouns have seven cases and three genders (with masculine further divided by animacy), verbs mark aspect, and word order is relatively free. Polish retains nasal vowels, a feature lost in most other Slavic languages.

History

Polish began to emerge as a distinct language in the tenth century, with the earliest written sentences dating to the twelfth century, such as the famous sentence in the Book of Henryków (c. 1270), often regarded as the oldest sentence in Polish. Literature flourished in the Renaissance and again in the nineteenth century with poets such as Adam Mickiewicz, whose epic Pan Tadeusz (1834) is regarded as the Polish national epic.
