Krišjānis Barons

Krišjānis Barons (October 31, 1835 – March 8, 1923) was a Latvian writer remembered as the "father of the dainas" (Dainu tēvs) for his systematic collection and publication of Latvian folk songs (dainas). His monumental work Latvju dainas (1894–1915) published over 35,000 daina texts and stands as a cornerstone of Latvian national culture.

Life

Barons was born in Strutele, Courland Governorate, and studied mathematics and astronomy at the University of Dorpat (Tartu) from 1856 to 1860. He became a leading figure of the Young Latvian movement and edited the newspaper Pēterburgas Avīzes in St. Petersburg. From 1867 he worked as a tutor in the family of the landowner Ivan Stankevich in Russia, and later in Moscow, where he spent much of his life.

The dainas

In 1878 Barons took over the folk-song collection begun by Fricis Brīvzemnieks. He devised a meticulous classification system, copying and sorting songs on small slips of paper which he stored in a specially built cabinet — the "Cabinet of Folksongs" (Dainu skapis), now in the Archives of Latvian Folklore in Riga and inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register. In total the archive drew on more than 218,000 song texts; Barons personally reviewed and edited each one considered for publication.

Legacy

Barons died in Riga in 1923. His portrait appeared on the Latvian 100-lats banknote, and streets and schools bearing his name are found throughout Latvia. The Cabinet of Folksongs remains a symbol of the Latvian national awakening.
