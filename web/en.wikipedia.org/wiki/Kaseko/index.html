Kaseko

Kaseko is a music genre from Suriname, derived from the traditional Afro-Surinamese music called kawina and strongly influenced by jazz, calypso and other Caribbean styles. The name is thought to derive from the French casser le corps ("break the body"), referring to the dancing it inspires.

History

Kaseko developed in Paramaribo in the decades after the Second World War, as big bands combined kawina percussion — including the skratji drum — with brass and reed sections playing jazz-influenced arrangements. It became especially popular among Surinamese communities in the Netherlands after Suriname's independence in 1975.

Style

The music features call-and-response singing, usually in Sranan Tongo, over interlocking percussion rhythms and punchy horn lines. Leading performers have included Lieve Hugo, known as the king of kaseko.
