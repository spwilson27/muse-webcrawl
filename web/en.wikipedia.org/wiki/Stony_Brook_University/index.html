The State University of New York at Stony Brook, commonly known as Stony Brook University (SBU), is a public research university in Stony Brook, New York, on Long Island. It is one of four university centres of the State University of New York system and a member of the Association of American Universities.

History

Founded in 1957 as the State University College on Long Island at Oyster Bay, the institution moved to its present 1,454-acre campus in 1962 under founding president John Francis Lee. It grew rapidly into a major research university, particularly in the sciences, mathematics, and health professions, and helps manage Brookhaven National Laboratory for the U.S. Department of Energy through Brookhaven Science Associates.

Academics and research

Stony Brook comprises colleges of Arts and Sciences, Business, and Engineering and Applied Sciences, schools of journalism, marine and atmospheric sciences, and professional development, and a health sciences centre with schools of medicine, dental medicine, health professions, nursing, and social welfare. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity." The university operates Stony Brook University Hospital, Long Island's only tertiary care and Level I trauma centre for the region.

Athletics

The Stony Brook Seawolves compete in NCAA Division I, primarily in the Coastal Athletic Association. The men's lacrosse and baseball programmes are notable; the 2012 baseball team famously advanced to the College World Series.
