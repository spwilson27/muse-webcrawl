Polish population transfers (1944–1946)

The Polish population transfers of 1944–1946 were the forced migrations of ethnic Poles from the eastern territories of pre-war Poland (the Kresy), annexed by the Soviet Union, to the reduced post-war Polish state, including the "Recovered Territories" in the west taken from Germany.

Background

At the Tehran and Yalta conferences, the Allies agreed that Poland's eastern border would follow the Curzon Line and that Poland would be compensated with German territory in the west. Agreements between the Polish Committee of National Liberation and the Ukrainian, Byelorussian, and Lithuanian Soviet republics in 1944 provided for the "evacuation" of Poles from the annexed lands.

Course and scale

Approximately 1.5 million Poles were transferred westward between 1944 and 1946, often in harsh conditions and with little property. A second wave of transfers followed in 1955–1959. The transfers were accompanied by the expulsion of Germans from the western territories and the resettlement of Ukrainians within Poland.
