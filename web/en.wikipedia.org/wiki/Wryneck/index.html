Wryneck

The Eurasian wryneck (Jynx torquilla).
FamilyPicidae (woodpeckers)
GenusJynx
SpeciesEurasian wryneck (J. torquilla); rufous-necked wryneck (J. ruficollis)

The wrynecks are a small group of Old World woodpeckers in the genus Jynx, comprising two species: the Eurasian wryneck (Jynx torquilla) and the rufous-necked (or red-throated) wryneck (Jynx ruficollis) of Africa. They form the subfamily Jynginae. Wrynecks take their name from their ability to twist and snake their heads through nearly 180 degrees; when disturbed at the nest they use this snake-like head movement and hissing sounds as a threat display, a behaviour linked in folklore to witchcraft — the Eurasian species' genus name and its old English name associations gave us the word "jinx".

Description and behaviour

Unlike typical woodpeckers, wrynecks have softer, more cryptically patterned plumage in browns and greys, weaker bills unsuited to excavating wood, and they do not brace themselves with stiff tail feathers. They nest in existing holes in trees or walls rather than chiselling their own. Their diet consists chiefly of ants and other insects, which they gather from the ground and bark with long, sticky tongues.

Distribution

The Eurasian wryneck breeds across temperate Europe and Asia in open woodland and orchards and winters in southern Europe, Africa and southern Asia; it was once a familiar breeding bird in England but declined to extinction there as a regular breeder in the 20th century, now occurring mainly as a scarce passage migrant. The rufous-necked wryneck is resident in sub-Saharan Africa in open savanna woodland.
