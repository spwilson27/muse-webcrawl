Chrysops is a genus of horse flies in the family Tabanidae, commonly known as deer flies (or yellow flies in Florida). The genus, described by Johann Wilhelm Meigen, contains well over a hundred species distributed worldwide, recognizable by their boldly patterned wings and bright green or golden eyes with dark markings in life.

Biology

Adult females bite mammals — including humans — to obtain blood for egg development, slicing the skin with blade-like mouthparts and lapping up the blood; males feed on nectar and pollen. Larvae live in wet soil, mud at pond margins, and marshy ground, where they prey on other invertebrates.

Medical importance

Chrysops species transmit the filarial worm Loa loa (loiasis, "African eye worm") in Central and West African rainforests, and can mechanically transmit tularemia and anthrax. Their persistent, painful biting makes them significant pests of livestock and outdoor recreation.
