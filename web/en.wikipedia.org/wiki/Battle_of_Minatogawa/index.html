Battle of Minatogawa

The Battle of Minatogawa (Japanese: 湊川の戦い, Minatogawa no tatakai) was fought on 5 July 1336 (Japanese calendar: 25th day of the 5th month, Kenmu 3) near the Minato River in Settsu Province (modern Kobe, Hyōgo), Japan. The loyalist hero Kusunoki Masashige, fighting for Emperor Go-Daigo, was defeated and killed by the forces of Ashikaga Takauji. It is one of the most famous battles of the Nanboku-chō period.

Kusunoki Masashige

Background

After briefly restoring imperial rule in the Kenmu Restoration, Emperor Go-Daigo fell out with his leading general Ashikaga Takauji, who rebelled in 1335. Driven from Kyoto, Takauji regrouped in Kyushu, won at Tatarahama, and marched east again with a large army advancing both by land and sea along the Inland Sea coast.

The battle

Kusunoki Masashige advised Go-Daigo to abandon Kyoto and wage guerrilla war from the mountains; the court rejected the plan and ordered him to fight. Knowing the cause hopeless, Kusunoki marched out with his brother Masasue and a small force — tradition puts his men at only some 700 — and joined Nitta Yoshisada near the Minato River. Attacked on land by Ashikaga Tadayoshi and from the sea by Takauji's fleet, the loyalist army was crushed. Kusunoki, wounded many times, withdrew with his brother; the two, exchanging the famous vow to be reborn seven times to destroy the enemies of the throne (shichishō hōkoku), killed themselves together.

Legacy

Takauji entered Kyoto and installed a rival emperor, beginning the Northern and Southern Courts period and the Ashikaga shogunate. Kusunoki Masashige became the supreme Japanese symbol of loyalty; his statue stood outside the Imperial Palace in Tokyo, and Minatogawa Shrine (founded 1872) enshrines him at the battle site.
