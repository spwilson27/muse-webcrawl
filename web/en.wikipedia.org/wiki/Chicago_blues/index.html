Chicago blues is a form of blues music developed in Chicago, Illinois, in the 1940s and 1950s. It is a type of urban blues in which Southern Delta blues musicians, who had migrated north during the Great Migration, electrified their sound, adding amplified guitar, harmonica, bass, and drums to the acoustic country blues tradition.

Development

As African American workers moved to Chicago for industrial jobs, musicians such as Muddy Waters, Howlin' Wolf, and Willie Dixon developed a harder, ensemble-based sound suited to noisy clubs. Labels such as Chess Records and its Aristocrat predecessor recorded the music and distributed it nationally. Little Walter revolutionized the blues harmonica by playing it amplified through a microphone.

Influence

Chicago blues was a foundational influence on rock and roll and on the British blues boom of the 1960s; bands such as the Rolling Stones (named after a Muddy Waters song) and Led Zeppelin drew heavily on its repertoire and style. The city remains a centre of the genre, hosting the annual Chicago Blues Festival.
