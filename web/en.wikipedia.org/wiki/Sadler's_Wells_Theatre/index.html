Sadler's Wells Theatre

Location: Rosebery Avenue, Islington, London
Capacity: 1,500 (main auditorium)
Opened: 1683 (original); 1998 (current building)
Type: Dance theatre

Sadler's Wells Theatre is a performing arts venue in Clerkenwell, Islington, London, and the world's leading venue dedicated to international dance. The present theatre, the sixth on the site, opened in 1998. Sadler's Wells presents a wide range of dance, from contemporary performance to ballet, flamenco, hip hop and musical theatre, and is home to a number of associate companies and artists.

History

The theatre takes its name from Richard Sadler, who established a "musick house" on the site in 1683, and from the medicinal wells discovered there. The venue became popular for entertainments and was rebuilt several times. In the early nineteenth century it was famous for aquatic dramas performed in a huge water tank, and for the performances of the clown Joseph Grimaldi. It later served as a music hall, cinema and even a skating rink before being rescued by Lilian Baylis, who reopened it in 1931.

Companies

Under Baylis, Sadler's Wells became the home of the Vic-Wells Ballet, directed by Ninette de Valois — the seed of both The Royal Ballet and Birmingham Royal Ballet — and of an opera company that evolved into English National Opera. In the post-war period the theatre shifted toward dance, and since the opening of the current building in 1998 it has developed as an international producing house for dance, commissioning new work and touring productions worldwide. The complex also includes the smaller Lilian Baylis Studio and, since 2025, a second venue, Sadler's Wells East in the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park, Stratford.
