Strontium-90

Symbol⁹⁰Sr
Half-life28.8 years
Decay modeBeta decay to yttrium-90
SourceNuclear fission product

Strontium-90 (⁹⁰Sr) is a radioactive isotope of strontium produced by nuclear fission, with a half-life of 28.8 years. It decays by beta emission to yttrium-90, which itself beta-decays to stable zirconium-90. Strontium-90 was a major component of the fallout from atmospheric nuclear weapons testing and of the releases from the Chernobyl accident.

Health effects

Chemically similar to calcium, strontium-90 is absorbed into bone and bone marrow after ingestion, where its radiation increases the risk of bone cancer and leukaemia. Concern about children absorbing ⁹⁰Sr from milk led to the Baby Tooth Survey in the United States, whose findings helped build support for the Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty of 1963.

Uses

The decay heat of strontium-90 has been used in radioisotope thermoelectric generators, especially in the former Soviet Union for remote lighthouses and navigation beacons, and in small medical and industrial sources.
