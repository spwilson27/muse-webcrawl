Jammu and Kashmir (princely state)

The princely state of Jammu and Kashmir was a princely state under British suzerainty from 1846 to 1947, ruled by the Dogra dynasty. It comprised the regions of Kashmir, Jammu and Ladakh, and at Partition became the subject of the Kashmir conflict between India and Pakistan.

History

The state was created when the British sold Kashmir to Gulab Singh, the Dogra ruler of Jammu, under the Treaty of Amritsar following the First Anglo-Sikh War. Successive maharajas consolidated control over Ladakh, Baltistan and Gilgit.

Partition and accession

On the Partition of India in 1947, Maharaja Hari Singh initially remained independent. After a tribal invasion from Pakistan, he signed the Instrument of Accession to India on 26 October 1947, triggering the Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–1948.
