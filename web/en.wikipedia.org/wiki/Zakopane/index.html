Zakopane

Zakopane is a town in southern Poland, in the Lesser Poland Voivodeship, with a population of about 27,000. Situated at the foot of the Tatra Mountains near the border with Slovakia, it is Poland's principal mountain resort and is often called the country's "winter capital."

Tourism and sport

Zakopane is a centre for skiing, ski jumping, and mountaineering. The Wielka Krokiew ski jump hosts World Cup events, and the nearby Kasprowy Wierch cable car gives access to high alpine terrain. The town has hosted the FIS Nordic World Ski Championships three times, most recently in 1962, and bid unsuccessfully for the Winter Olympics. In summer, hikers use the town as a base for the trails of Tatra National Park, including ascents of Giewont and Rysy, Poland's highest peak.

Culture

The town is the heartland of the Górale (Polish highlander) culture, known for distinctive wooden architecture. The Zakopane Style, developed by the artist Stanisław Witkiewicz around the turn of the 20th century, drew on local folk building traditions. The main pedestrian street, Krupówki, is lined with shops and restaurants serving regional specialities such as oscypek, a smoked sheep's cheese.
