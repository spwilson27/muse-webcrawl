Tom and Jerry is an American animated media franchise and series of comedy short films created in 1940 by William Hanna and Joseph Barbera. Best known for its 161 theatrical short films produced by Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, the series centres on the rivalry between a house cat, Tom, and a mouse, Jerry, whose chases and conflicts produce slapstick violence and comic mayhem. Tom rarely succeeds in catching Jerry, whose cleverness and luck — along with Tom's own overconfidence — turn the tables.

History

The pair debuted in Puss Gets the Boot (1940), in which the cat was named Jasper and the mouse was unnamed. The short was nominated for an Academy Award, and after a studio contest the characters were renamed Tom and Jerry. Hanna and Barbera went on to direct 114 shorts for MGM between 1940 and 1958, seven of which won the Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film, tying with Walt Disney's Silly Symphonies as the most awarded cartoon series.

After MGM closed its cartoon studio in 1957, shorts were produced by Gene Deitch in Prague (1961–1962) and by Chuck Jones at Sib Tower 12 (1963–1967). Television versions followed, beginning with The Tom and Jerry Show (1975) from Hanna-Barbera, and later series including Tom & Jerry Kids (1990–1993), Tom and Jerry Tales (2006–2008), and The Tom and Jerry Show (2014–2021).

Other media

The franchise includes feature films beginning with Tom and Jerry: The Movie (1992), a long run of direct-to-video films, and the live-action/animation hybrid Tom & Jerry (2021). The characters rarely speak in the classic shorts, though Tom's agonised yells — performed by Hanna himself — are famous. Supporting characters include the bulldog Spike and his son Tyke, the black cat Butch, and the housekeeper Mammy Two Shoes, whose portrayal has been criticised as a racial stereotype.

Legacy

Tom and Jerry is one of the most popular and enduring cartoon franchises in the world, and the phrase "Tom and Jerry" has entered several languages as a byword for an endless, antagonistic rivalry.
