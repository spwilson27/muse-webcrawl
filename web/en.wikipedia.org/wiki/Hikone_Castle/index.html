Hikone Castle (彦根城, Hikone-jō) is a Japanese Edo-period castle in the city of Hikone, Shiga Prefecture, Japan. It is considered the most significant historical building in Shiga. The site has been protected as a National Historic Site since 1951, and the main keep (tenshu) is designated a National Treasure of Japan.

Hikone is one of only twelve Japanese castles with an original keep, and one of only five castles listed as National Treasures — the others being Himeji, Matsumoto, Inuyama, and Matsue. The castle's keep, attached turrets, and some of its walls incorporate structures moved from other castles, including the keep of Ōtsu Castle.

History

Construction of the castle began in 1603 on the orders of Ii Naokatsu, son of the Tokugawa retainer Ii Naomasa, on the shore of Lake Biwa. The keep was completed in 1604 using a five-story tower relocated from Ōtsu Castle, and the castle as a whole was finished in 1622 under Ii Naotaka, incorporating stone walls and buildings moved from the demolished Sawayama Castle, the former stronghold of Ishida Mitsunari, which stood on a neighbouring hill.

The Ii clan ruled Hikone Domain from the castle for the rest of the Edo period, as fudai daimyō closely trusted by the Tokugawa shogunate; members of the family repeatedly served as tairō (great councillor), most famously Ii Naosuke, the regent who signed the Harris Treaty in 1858.

When the feudal system was abolished in the Meiji era, many castles were dismantled. Hikone Castle was scheduled for demolition, but an intervention by Emperor Meiji, who visited the area in 1878, saved the castle. It remained in the possession of the Ii family and later the Imperial Household before being given to the city of Hikone.

Layout and structures

The castle is a hirayama (hilltop on a plain) castle built on Konki-yama hill between Lake Biwa and the Nakasendō highway. Its defences include multiple baileys, stone walls, moats, arrow slits, and "nightingale floors" in some corridors. Surviving original structures include the three-story keep, the Tenbin turret with its distinctive balanced appearance, the Taikomon and other gates, the Umaya (stables), and a bell tower housing the time bell. The Genkyū-en garden, created in 1679 in the style of a Tang-dynasty imperial garden, lies at the foot of the hill.

Today

Hikone Castle is a major tourist attraction in Shiga Prefecture, celebrated for cherry blossoms in spring. The Hikone Castle Museum, opened in 1987, displays treasures of the Ii family, including armour, manuscripts, and items relating to Ii Naosuke. The castle's mascot, the cat character Hikonyan, created for the castle's 400th anniversary in 2007, became one of Japan's most popular local mascots.
