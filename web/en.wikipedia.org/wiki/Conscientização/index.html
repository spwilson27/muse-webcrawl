Conscientização

Freire's literacy circles in 1960s Brazil aimed at conscientização — learning to read the word and the world.

Conscientização (Portuguese for "consciousness-raising" or "critical consciousness"; often rendered in English as conscientization) is a concept developed by the Brazilian educator Paulo Freire, most fully in his book Pedagogy of the Oppressed (1968). It denotes the process by which oppressed people develop a deepening awareness of the social, political and economic contradictions that shape their lives, together with the capacity to act against the oppressive elements of that reality.

Meaning in Freire

Freire described levels of consciousness moving from a "magical" or naive acceptance of one's situation, through naive transitivity, toward critical transitivity: perceiving problems in their causal and historical depth and recognising oneself as a subject capable of transforming the world. Conscientização is inseparable from praxis — the unity of reflection and action — and from dialogue: it cannot be "deposited" in learners by the "banking model" of education that Freire rejected, but emerges through problem-posing education in which teacher and students learn together, beginning from learners' own words and life situations ("generative themes").

History and influence

The idea grew out of Freire's adult-literacy work in north-eastern Brazil in the early 1960s (notably the 1963 Angicos experiment, in which some 300 sugarcane workers became literate in about 40 hours of study) and was cut short by the 1964 military coup, which imprisoned and exiled Freire. The concept influenced liberation theology, Latin American social movements, critical pedagogy (Henry Giroux, Peter McLaren), community development and participatory research worldwide; in English it informs the modern psychological literature on "critical consciousness" among marginalised youth. Critics have charged the term with vagueness and with romanticising the oppressed, while Freire himself later warned against diluting conscientização into mere awareness without transformative action.
