Deng Yingchao (Chinese: 邓颖超; 4 February 1904 – 11 July 1992) was a Chinese revolutionary and politician, a leader of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and the wife of Premier Zhou Enlai. She was one of the most prominent women in twentieth-century Chinese politics, serving as Chairperson of the National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC) from 1983 to 1988 and as a member of the CCP Politburo.

Early life and revolutionary career

Born in Nanning to a family from Guangshan, Henan, Deng was educated in Tianjin, where she became active in the May Fourth Movement in 1919 and helped found the Awakening Society with Zhou Enlai. After teaching and women's organising work, she joined the CCP in 1925 and married Zhou that year in Guangzhou. She worked underground in Shanghai, participated in the Long March (1934–1935) as one of the few women to complete it, and during the Second Sino-Japanese War and the renewed civil war she directed much of the party's women's work, serving as a CCP representative in Chongqing during the united front period.

After 1949

Following the founding of the People's Republic of China, Deng Yingchao became a vice-chairperson of the All-China Women's Federation and a leading figure in drafting marriage law and policies on women's and children's welfare, though she deliberately avoided holding offices that appeared to derive from her husband's position. She was a member of the 11th and 12th Central Committees and the Politburo (1978–1985), Vice Chairperson of the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress, and honorary president of the All-China Women's Federation. During the Cultural Revolution she helped protect a number of cadres and intellectuals within the limits of her influence.

Later years and legacy

After Zhou Enlai's death in 1976, Deng supported Deng Xiaoping's reform programme and, as CPPCC chairperson from 1983, worked on united front affairs, including contacts with Taiwan and overseas Chinese. She retired from the Central Committee in 1985 and from the CPPCC in 1988. Widely respected in China for her integrity and frugality, she is remembered alongside Soong Ching-ling as one of the two pre-eminent women statespersons of modern China.
