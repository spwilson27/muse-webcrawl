OSCAR 2 (Ocean Surface Current Analysis Real-time 2) is a satellite-derived oceanographic data product and mission concept centred on measuring surface currents and winds over the world ocean in near real time. OSCAR products combine satellite altimetry, scatterometry and sea surface temperature observations to estimate ocean surface velocity fields on a global grid.

Overview

The OSCAR data set, developed and distributed by NOAA and NASA's Physical Oceanography Distributed Active Archive Center (PO.DAAC), provides global maps of ocean surface currents computed from satellite measurements of sea surface height, surface wind stress, and sea surface temperature. The fields are typically provided at one-third-degree horizontal resolution with a temporal resolution of five days.

Applications

OSCAR currents are widely used in fisheries oceanography, marine debris tracking, search-and-rescue drift modelling, climate research, and the validation of numerical ocean circulation models. Because the products are available in near real time, they are also used operationally by mariners and by agencies monitoring oil spills and harmful algal blooms.
