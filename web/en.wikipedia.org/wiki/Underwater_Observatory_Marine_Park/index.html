Underwater Observatory Marine Park

The Underwater Observatory Marine Park is a public aquarium and observatory located in Eilat, Israel, on the shore of the Red Sea, a few kilometres south of the city near the Egyptian border. Founded in 1974 by zoologist and businessman Morris Kahn, it was one of the first attractions of its kind in the world.

Attractions

The park's centrepiece is an observatory tower situated about 100 metres offshore, reached by a pier. Its underwater viewing halls, sunk some six metres below the surface, allow visitors to observe a natural coral reef and its fish through large windows. Onshore, the park maintains more than fifty aquarium tanks displaying Red Sea species, including sharks, sea turtles, stingrays, and rare fish, together with Amazonian and other exotic creatures. Other attractions have included a shark pool with a transparent viewing tunnel, turtle and ray pools, a 3D oceanarium cinema, and glass-bottom boat tours of the reef.

Conservation

The park participates in breeding and rehabilitation programmes for endangered Red Sea species, including the hawksbill sea turtle, and works with the adjacent Coral Beach Nature Reserve to monitor reef health in the Gulf of Aqaba.
