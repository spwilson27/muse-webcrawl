Radfan

Radfan is a mountainous region of southern Yemen, north of the port city of Aden. Inhabited largely by the Qutaibi tribe, the area became strategically important during the period of British rule in the Aden Protectorate because the main road from Aden to Dhala and the north ran through it.

The Radfan campaign

In 1964, during the Aden Emergency, British forces mounted a campaign in the Radfan hills to restore control over the Aden–Dhala road, which had been cut by dissident tribesmen supported by Egyptian-backed rebels in North Yemen. The operation, which involved units including the Parachute Regiment and Royal Marines, became one of the best-known counter-insurgency operations of the late colonial period. Fighting in the steep, waterless hills was intense, and the campaign was later the subject of official controversy at home.

Later history

After South Yemeni independence in 1967 the region formed part of the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen. Local grievances persisted into the unified Republic of Yemen, and Radfan saw renewed fighting during the 1994 civil war and in later unrest in the south.
