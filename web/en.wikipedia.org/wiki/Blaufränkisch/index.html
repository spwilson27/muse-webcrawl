SynonymsLemberger, Kékfrankos, Gamé
Notable regionsBurgenland (Austria), Hungary, Württemberg (Germany)

Blaufränkisch is a dark-skinned variety of wine grape used for red wine, grown principally in Central Europe. In Austria it is the signature red grape of the Burgenland region, while in Hungary it is known as Kékfrankos and in Germany as Lemberger or Limberger.

Characteristics

The vine is late-ripening and yields deeply coloured wines with firm tannins, refreshing acidity and aromas of dark berries, sour cherry and spice. High-quality examples can age well and are often compared in structure to Pinot Noir with greater colour and body.

Wine regions

In Austria, Blaufränkisch accounts for the majority of plantings in Mittelburgenland, sometimes nicknamed "Blaufränkischland". It is also grown in Hungary (notably around Sopron and Eger, where it forms part of the Bull's Blood blend), Slovakia, Croatia (as Borgonja), Slovenia, Serbia, and the Finger Lakes region of New York.
