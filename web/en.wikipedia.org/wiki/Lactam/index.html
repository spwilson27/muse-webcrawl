Lactam


Lactam ring sizes

A lactam is a cyclic amide: a compound in which the amide nitrogen and carbonyl carbon form part of a ring. Lactams are named with Greek prefixes according to ring size — α-lactams have three-membered rings, β-lactams four, γ-lactams five, δ-lactams six, and ε-lactams seven. The word is a portmanteau of lactone and amide.

Synthesis

Lactams are commonly prepared by the Beckmann rearrangement of cyclic ketoximes, by the Schmidt reaction of cyclic ketones with hydrazoic acid, and by ring-closing reactions of amino acids and their derivatives. The industrial synthesis of ε-caprolactam from cyclohexanone oxime proceeds on a scale of millions of tonnes per year.

Applications

The most famous lactams are the β-lactam antibiotics, including the penicillins and cephalosporins, whose strained four-membered ring is essential to their antibacterial activity. ε-Caprolactam is the monomer for nylon-6, while 2-pyrrolidone (a γ-lactam) and its N-vinyl derivative are used in pharmaceuticals and polymers. Lactam rings also appear in many drugs and natural products.
