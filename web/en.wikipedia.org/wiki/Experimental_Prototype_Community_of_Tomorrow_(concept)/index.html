Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow (concept)

The Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow (EPCOT) was a utopian city concept developed by Walt Disney in the 1960s, to be built on the Florida land that became Walt Disney World. Conceived as a real, functioning city of about 20,000 residents, EPCOT was intended as a test bed for city planning and organization, continually introducing new technologies in cooperation with American industry.

The plan

In Disney's 1966 "EPCOT film", the city was laid out radially: a dense urban centre with a climate-controlled dome and a 30-storey hotel, ringed by apartments, greenbelts with single-family homes, and industrial parks. Transport would be by monorail and PeopleMovers, with automobiles and trucks confined to underground levels. Residents would rent rather than own, hold jobs within the community, and have no retirees, so the city would always remain part of the "world of tomorrow".

After Disney's death

Walt Disney died in December 1966; his company, lacking his personal commitment and wary of running a city, abandoned the residential concept. Landmark legislation creating the Reedy Creek Improvement District (1967) had nonetheless been passed. The EPCOT name survived in the Epcot theme park, opened in 1982, which reflects the concept only in its Future World section and its international World Showcase. The planned community of Celebration, Florida, built in the 1990s, inherited some of the original ideas.
