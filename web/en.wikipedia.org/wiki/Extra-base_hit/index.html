Extra-base hit

Image placeholder: A gap shot resulting in a double

In baseball, an extra-base hit (EB, XBH, or extra-base) is a hit by the batter that allows the batter to advance beyond first base without the benefit of a fielder's error or a fielder's choice. It includes doubles, triples, and home runs — any hit other than a single. Extra-base hits are a key component of slugging percentage and are used to measure a hitter's power.

Statistics

A player's extra-base hit total is simply the sum of his doubles, triples, and home runs for a season or career. The Major League Baseball single-season record for extra-base hits is 119, set by Babe Ruth in 1921 (44 doubles, 16 triples, and 59 home runs). The career record is held by Hank Aaron, with 1,477 extra-base hits, ahead of Barry Bonds and Albert Pujols.

Extra-base hits also drive related statistics: total bases, isolated power (slugging percentage minus batting average), and a team's extra-base hit percentage all derive from them.
