Constitution of the German Empire

The Constitution of the German Empire (German: Verfassung des Deutschen Reiches) was the basic law of the German Empire from 1871 to 1919. It came into force on 4 May 1871 and was a modified version of the constitution of the North German Confederation of 1867. It established Germany as a federation of 25 constituent states under the permanent presidency of the King of Prussia, who bore the title of German Emperor.

Structure

The constitution created two legislative bodies: the Bundesrat, representing the federated states, and the Reichstag, elected by universal male suffrage. The Emperor appointed the Imperial Chancellor, who presided over the Bundesrat and directed government; the Chancellor was responsible to the Emperor rather than to parliament. Prussia held 17 of the 58 votes in the Bundesrat, enough to block constitutional amendments.

Powers

The Empire controlled foreign affairs, the military, customs, coinage, banking, and much commercial law, while the states retained authority over education, religion, and direct taxation. The constitution contained no catalogue of fundamental rights, although citizenship and some civil freedoms were addressed in ordinary legislation and state constitutions.

End

Reforms in October 1918, amid the closing phase of the First World War, made the Chancellor dependent on the confidence of the Reichstag. The constitution effectively ended with the proclamation of the republic on 9 November 1918 and was formally replaced by the Weimar Constitution on 11 August 1919.
