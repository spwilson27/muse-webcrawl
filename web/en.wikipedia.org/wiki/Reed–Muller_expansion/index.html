Reed–Muller expansion

Field: Boolean algebra, coding theory
Named after: David E. Muller, Irving S. Reed
Also called: Positive polarity Reed–Muller expansion

A Reed–Muller expansion (or Reed–Muller form) is a representation of a Boolean function as an exclusive sum (XOR) of conjunctions of Boolean variables, in which every variable appears only in its positive (uncomplemented) form. It is also known as the positive polarity Reed–Muller expansion (PPRM) when all literals are positive, and as the algebraic normal form when variables may appear complemented.

Definition

Any Boolean function f(x1, …, xn) can be written uniquely as an XOR sum of products of distinct positive literals, with coefficients in the field GF(2). The degree of the expansion is the size of the largest product term. Unlike the sum-of-products form, the Reed–Muller expansion is canonical: each Boolean function has exactly one such representation.

History

The form is named after David E. Muller, who introduced the related Reed–Muller codes in 1954, and Irving S. Reed, who proposed a decoding algorithm for them. The expansion became important in logic synthesis because XOR-based representations can be much more compact than AND/OR forms for certain functions, such as parity and arithmetic circuits.

Applications

Reed–Muller expansions are used in the design of programmable logic devices, in testability analysis of digital circuits, and in the study of Boolean function complexity. Fixed-polarity variants, in which each variable is assigned a single polarity throughout, generalise the concept and are widely studied in logic minimisation.
