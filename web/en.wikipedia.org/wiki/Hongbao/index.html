Hongbao

Hongbao (simplified Chinese: 红包; traditional Chinese: 紅包; pinyin: hóngbāo) are traditional Chinese gifts of money enclosed in red paper envelopes. Known in Cantonese as lai see (利是) and in Mandarin also as yāsuìqián (壓歲錢, "suppressing-age money") when given at New Year, they are presented on occasions such as Lunar New Year, weddings, graduations, and the birth of a child.

Customs

The red colour of the envelope symbolizes good luck and prosperity and is believed to ward off evil spirits. According to legend, the New Year money protected children from the demon Sui, who could be frightened away by the noise of coins wrapped in red paper. Traditionally, married couples give hongbao to children and to elderly parents; recipients, especially children at New Year, may kneel and offer formal good wishes before accepting. The amount given varies with the relationship and the giver's means, and crisp new banknotes are preferred.

Uses beyond New Year

At weddings, guests give hongbao to the couple as a contribution toward the banquet and their new life together; at funerals, money is instead given in white envelopes. Employers in China and overseas Chinese communities commonly distribute hongbao as year-end bonuses. In Malaysia, Singapore, and other parts of Southeast Asia, similar customs exist among Chinese communities, and green packets (duit raya) are given by Muslims at Eid al-Fitr.

Digital hongbao

In 2014 the messaging app WeChat launched virtual red packets, allowing users to send monetary gifts individually or into group chats where the amount is split randomly among members who tap to claim a share. The feature was promoted through the CCTV New Year's Gala in 2015 and became a phenomenon, spurring rivals such as Alipay to follow. Digital hongbao are now a major channel for holiday greetings, marketing promotions, and small payments in China, though authorities have periodically warned against their use as disguised gambling or bribery.
