Tilikum

Tilikum (c. 1981 – January 6, 2017), nicknamed Tili, was a male orca (killer whale) held at SeaWorld Orlando in Florida. At about 6.9 metres (22.5 feet) long and 5,700 kilograms (12,500 lb), he was one of the largest orcas in captivity and sired a large proportion of SeaWorld's orcas. Tilikum was involved in the deaths of three people — more than any other captive orca — which made him the central subject of the 2013 documentary film Blackfish and a focus of debate over cetacean captivity.

Life

Tilikum was captured in Berufjörður, Iceland, in November 1983 at about two years of age, together with two other young orcas. He was first held at the Hafnarfjörður aquarium and then transferred to Sealand of the Pacific in British Columbia, Canada. On February 21, 1991, twenty-year-old trainer Keltie Byrne slipped into the pool and was dragged underwater by Tilikum and the two females held with him; she drowned. Sealand closed, and Tilikum was sold to SeaWorld Orlando in 1992. In 1999, the body of Daniel P. Dukes, a visitor who had remained in the park after closing, was found draped over Tilikum's back; he had drowned and been mauled by the whale.

Death of Dawn Brancheau and aftermath

On February 24, 2010, during a "Dine with Shamu" show, Tilikum pulled veteran trainer Dawn Brancheau into the water by her ponytail and killed her. The incident led to an Occupational Safety and Health Administration investigation and a ruling that required SeaWorld trainers to cease water work with orcas. Tilikum returned to performances in 2011 but was mostly kept apart from shows thereafter. The Blackfish documentary, built around his story, caused a sharp drop in SeaWorld attendance and profits and contributed to the company's 2016 decisions to end orca breeding and phase out theatrical orca shows.

Death

SeaWorld announced in March 2016 that Tilikum was suffering from a persistent bacterial lung infection resistant to treatment. He died on January 6, 2017, at an estimated age of 36; the infection was given as the cause of death.
