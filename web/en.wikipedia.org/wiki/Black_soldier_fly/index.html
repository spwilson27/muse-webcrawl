Black soldier fly

SpeciesHermetia illucens
FamilyStratiomyidae (soldier flies)
OrderDiptera
Native rangeAmericas (Neotropics)
Present rangeCosmopolitan in warm regions worldwide

The black soldier fly (Hermetia illucens) is a common and widespread fly of the family Stratiomyidae. Native to the Neotropical region of the Americas, it has spread during the twentieth century to warm regions on every continent, partly through the movement of goods and partly through its use in industry.

Description and life cycle

Adults are black, wasp-like flies 15–20 mm long; despite the resemblance they cannot bite or sting and do not feed, living off fat stores accumulated as larvae. Females lay several hundred eggs near decaying organic matter. The cream-coloured larvae are voracious feeders on manure, food waste and other decaying material, growing through six instars before the final "prepupal" stage migrates to a dry site to pupate. The whole cycle can complete in a few weeks under warm conditions.

Uses

Black soldier fly larvae (BSFL) are farmed commercially on a large scale. They convert low-value organic waste into protein- and fat-rich biomass used as feed for poultry, pigs, aquaculture fish and pets, while the residue, called frass, serves as a fertiliser. Larvae have also been studied for rendering, biodiesel production from their fats, and the suppression of house fly populations and harmful bacteria in manure. Because adults do not feed and avoid human habitation, the species is not considered a pest or disease vector.
