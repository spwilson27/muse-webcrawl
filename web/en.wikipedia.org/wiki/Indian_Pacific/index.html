Indian Pacific

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indian Pacific is a weekly experiential tourism-oriented passenger train service in Australia operated by Journey Beyond since 1970. It runs between Sydney, on the Pacific Ocean coast, and Perth, on the Indian Ocean coast, a distance of 4,352 kilometres (2,704 miles), making it one of the longest train journeys in the world. The train's route includes the world's longest straight stretch of railway track, a 478-kilometre (297 mi) stretch across the Nullarbor Plain.

History

The first transcontinental standard-gauge service completed the Sydney–Perth run on 23 February 1970, following the completion of gauge standardisation projects across New South Wales, South Australia and Western Australia. It was the first direct train to cross the continent from coast to coast on a single gauge. The service was originally operated by the state and federal government railways, later by Australian National and then Great Southern Rail, before passing to Journey Beyond.

Route

Westbound services depart Sydney, cross the Blue Mountains, and travel via Broken Hill into South Australia, stopping at Adelaide before crossing the Nullarbor Plain with a stop at the remote siding of Cook. In Western Australia the train stops at Kalgoorlie before terminating in Perth. Eastbound services generally follow the same route in reverse, with off-train experiences offered at several stops.

Service

The train operates in Gold and Platinum service classes, with sleeping cabins, dining cars serving meals featuring regional produce, and lounge cars. Its emblem is the wedge-tailed eagle. In recent years the journey has been marketed as a multi-day experiential tour, with the full Sydney–Perth itinerary taking around four days including extended stops.
