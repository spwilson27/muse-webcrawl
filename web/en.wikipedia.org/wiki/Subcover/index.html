Subcover

In mathematics, and in particular in topology, a subcover of a cover of a set or topological space is a subcollection of that cover which still covers the whole space. The concept is central to the definition of compactness: a topological space is compact if every open cover of it has a finite subcover.

Definition

Let X be a set and let C = {Uα} be a collection of subsets of X whose union contains (covers) X. A subcover is a subcollection C′ ⊆ C such that the union of the members of C′ still covers X. In topology, when the members of the cover are open sets, one speaks of an open subcover; a subcover with finitely many members is a finite subcover.

Role in compactness

The existence of finite subcovers is the standard modern definition of a compact space, generalizing the Heine–Borel theorem: a subset of Euclidean space is compact exactly when it is closed and bounded, which is equivalent to every open cover having a finite subcover. Many fundamental theorems — such as the extreme value theorem for continuous functions — follow readily from this definition.

Related notions include the Lindelöf property, in which every open cover has a countable subcover, and paracompactness, in which every open cover has a locally finite open refinement.
