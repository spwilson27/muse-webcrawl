National Academy of Engineering

National Academy of Engineering (NAE)
Founded: December 5, 1964
Headquarters: Washington, D.C., United States
Members: about 2,500 (including international members)
Parent organization: National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine

The National Academy of Engineering (NAE) is an American nonprofit learned society and one of the three National Academies of the United States, alongside the National Academy of Sciences and the National Academy of Medicine. Founded in 1964 under the congressional charter of the National Academy of Sciences, the NAE honors outstanding engineers and provides independent engineering expertise and analysis to the federal government and the public.

Membership

Election to the NAE is among the highest professional distinctions accorded to an engineer. Members are elected by their peers for outstanding contributions to engineering research, practice, or education, including the pioneering of new technologies and significant advances in traditional fields. New members are announced annually, typically in February.

Programs and prizes

The NAE conducts studies and symposia on engineering and technology policy, promotes engineering education and the engineering workforce, and convenes the Frontiers of Engineering programs for early-career engineers. It administers some of the profession's most prestigious awards, including the Charles Stark Draper Prize for Engineering—often described as the "Nobel Prize of engineering"—the Fritz J. and Dolores H. Russ Prize for bioengineering, and the Gordon Prize for innovation in engineering education. In 2008 the NAE announced its Grand Challenges for Engineering, a list of 14 broad goals for the 21st century ranging from making solar energy economical to engineering better medicines and securing cyberspace.
