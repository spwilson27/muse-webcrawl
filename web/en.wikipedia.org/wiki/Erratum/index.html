Erratum

An erratum or corrigendum (plurals: errata, corrigenda) is a correction of a published text. As a general rule, publishers issue an erratum for a production error (i.e., an error introduced during the publishing process) and a corrigendum for an author error, although modern practice often uses the terms loosely.

In book publishing

Traditionally, an erratum is a list of errors and their corrections inserted, usually on a loose sheet or at the back, in a printed book. Typical entries give the page and line of the error and the corrected reading. Errata sheets were common in early printing, when correcting the type after printing had begun was impractical.

In scholarly journals

In academic publishing, an erratum is a formal, citable notice published by a journal that corrects errors in an earlier article by the same authors or by the publisher. Errata are distinct from retractions, which withdraw a paper whose findings are invalid, and from "expressions of concern". Corrected articles are often amended in their online versions with a link to the erratum notice so that the two are read together.
