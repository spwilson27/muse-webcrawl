Reichskommissariat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The Reichskommissariats of occupied Europe, 1942

Reichskommissariat (plural Reichskommissariate) is the German designation for a type of administrative entity headed by a government official known as a Reichskommissar. Although most frequently associated with the quasi-colonial occupation regimes established by Nazi Germany in parts of German-occupied Europe during the Second World War, the term and office also existed in other contexts, notably in Germany's overseas colonies before the First World War.

Nazi occupation regimes

In the west, the Reichskommissariat Niederlande (Netherlands, under Arthur Seyss-Inquart) and Reichskommissariat Norwegen (Norway, under Josef Terboven) were established in 1940; Belgium and northern France were projected as a Reichskommissariat Belgien-Nordfrankreich, formally created in 1944 shortly before liberation. In the east, following the invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941, the enormous Reichskommissariat Ostland (the Baltic states and Belarus, under Hinrich Lohse) and Reichskommissariat Ukraine (under Erich Koch) were placed under Alfred Rosenberg's Reich Ministry for the Occupied Eastern Territories. Further eastern commissariats — Moskowien, Kaukasien and others — were planned under the Generalplan Ost but never fully established.

Character

The eastern Reichskommissariate were instruments of exploitation, Germanisation and mass murder: their administrations cooperated in the Holocaust, the starvation policies of the Hunger Plan, forced labour deportations and anti-partisan reprisals. The western commissariats were civilian occupation administrations superimposed on retained local bureaucracies, also involved in persecution and deportation of Jews and forced labour conscription. All ceased to exist with Germany's defeat in 1945.
