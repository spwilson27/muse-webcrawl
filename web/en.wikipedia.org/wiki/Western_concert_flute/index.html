Western concert flute
ClassificationWoodwind, aerophone
RangeAbout three octaves, from middle C (C4) upward
Key systemBoehm system

The Western concert flute is a transverse flute made of metal (or, historically, wood) that is the standard flute of Western classical music and a common instrument in orchestras, wind ensembles and jazz. The player blows across an embouchure hole, and pitch is changed by keys operated by the fingers.

History

The modern instrument descends from the Baroque one-keyed flute. In the 19th century, Theobald Boehm redesigned the flute with a cylindrical bore, large tone holes and a system of ring keys and rods — the Boehm system — that greatly improved intonation and facility and remains standard today. Instruments are commonly made of silver, with gold and platinum used for professional models.

Family

The concert flute in C is joined by the smaller piccolo, and by alto and bass flutes used in flute choirs and occasionally in orchestral scores.
