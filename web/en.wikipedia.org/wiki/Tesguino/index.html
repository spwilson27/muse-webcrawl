Tesgüino

Tesgüino (written tesguino in the assignment URL) is a corn beer made by the Rarámuri (Tarahumara) people of the Sierra Madre Occidental in the Mexican state of Chihuahua. The beer is brewed for social and religious gatherings — also called tesgüino — which are central to Rarámuri community life, including cooperative work parties, ceremonies, and the famous long-distance running races.

Brewing

Tesgüino is made from sprouted maize. The kernels are germinated, dried and ground, then boiled for many hours; the resulting wort is fermented in large clay pots (ollas) for one to several days. The finished beer is milky, mildly sour, and relatively low in alcohol. Because it does not keep, it must be consumed within a short time of brewing, which shapes the timing of gatherings. Other grains or plant materials may be added in some regional variants.

Social role

The tesgüino gathering is the principal social institution of the Rarámuri. Hosts invite neighbours, who repay collective labour — agricultural work, house building and other tasks — with food and beer. Anthropologists have described the gatherings as integrating scattered rancherías into a community, and as having religious significance: the Rarámuri consider tesgüino a gift associated with Onorúame, their creator deity. Drunkenness at gatherings is socially significant in its own right and is described in ethnographic literature as a form of communal bonding and conflict resolution.
