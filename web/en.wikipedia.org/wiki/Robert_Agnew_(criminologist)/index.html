Robert Agnew (criminologist)


Born: December 9, 1953, Atlantic City, New Jersey, U.S.
Occupation: Criminologist, sociologist
Known for: General strain theory

Robert Agnew (born December 9, 1953) is an American criminologist and sociologist, long a professor at Emory University. He is best known for developing general strain theory, one of the leading explanations of crime and delinquency.

General strain theory

First presented in 1992, Agnew's general strain theory broadened earlier strain theories by identifying three major sources of strain: failure to achieve positively valued goals, the removal of positively valued stimuli, and the presentation of negative stimuli. Such strains generate negative emotions — especially anger — which create pressure for corrective action, sometimes in the form of crime. Agnew expanded the theory in Pressured into Crime (2005), specifying the strains most likely to result in offending and the factors that condition their effects.

Career

Agnew has also written on the causes of juvenile delinquency and on criminological theory more broadly, including work toward a general, integrated theory of crime. He served as president of the American Society of Criminology.
