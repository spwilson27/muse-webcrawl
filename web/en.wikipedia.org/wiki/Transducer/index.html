Transducer

A transducer is a device that converts energy from one form to another. Usually a transducer converts a signal in one form of energy to a signal in another, most often converting a physical quantity (such as pressure, temperature, displacement, or sound) into an electrical signal, or vice versa. Transducers are widely used in sensors, measurement and control systems, communications, and audio equipment.

Types

Transducers may be classified by the energy domains they connect. Common examples include:

  • Electromechanical: microphones, loudspeakers, piezoelectric transducers, strain gauges, accelerometers, and potentiometers.
  • Electroacoustic: hydrophones and sonar transducers used underwater.
  • Optoelectronic: photodiodes, LEDs, and solar cells.
  • Thermal: thermocouples, thermistors, and resistance temperature detectors.
  • Chemical: pH electrodes and gas sensors.

Sensors and actuators

A transducer that produces an electrical output in response to a physical stimulus is a sensor; one that converts an electrical signal into physical action is an actuator. Some devices, such as ultrasonic transducers used in medical imaging and sonar, act as both, emitting energy and detecting echoes. Important performance characteristics include sensitivity, linearity, frequency response (bandwidth), dynamic range, and hysteresis.
