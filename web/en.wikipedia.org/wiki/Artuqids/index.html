Artuqids

The Malabadi Bridge (1146–47), an Artuqid construction

The Artuqids (Turkish: Artuklular) were a Turkoman dynasty of Oghuz origin that ruled parts of eastern Anatolia and Upper Mesopotamia (the Jazira) from the 11th to the 15th century. The dynasty was founded by Artuk Bey, a commander in the service of the Seljuk sultan Malik-Shah I, who was appointed governor of Jerusalem in 1086.

Branches

After Artuk's death (1091), his sons Sökmen and Ilghazi established three branches of the family: at Hisn Kayfa (Hasankeyf) and Amid (Diyarbakır), at Mardin, and at Harput. Ilghazi, ruler of Mardin and briefly of Aleppo, won a celebrated victory over the Crusaders at the Battle of Ager Sanguinis in 1119. The Artuqids navigated between the Crusader states, the Zengids, the Ayyubids, the Sultanate of Rum, the Mongols and the Timurids, surviving through diplomacy as much as war.

Culture

The Artuqid courts were notable patrons of architecture and metalwork, producing mosques, madrasas, caravanserais and great bridges such as the Malabadi Bridge. The polymath Ismail al-Jazari served the Hisn Kayfa branch and completed his Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices in 1206 under Artuqid patronage. The last branch, at Mardin, endured until 1409, when it was absorbed by the Qara Qoyunlu.
