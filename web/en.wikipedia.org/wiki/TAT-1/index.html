TAT-1

Type: Submarine communications cable

Route: Oban, Scotland – Clarenville, Newfoundland

In service: 25 September 1956

Retired: 1978

Capacity: 35–36 telephone channels

TAT-1 (Transatlantic No. 1) was the first submarine transatlantic telephone cable system. It was laid between Gallanach Bay, near Oban, Scotland, and Clarenville, Newfoundland, between 1955 and 1956 by the cable ship Monarch. It was inaugurated on 25 September 1956, initially carrying 36 telephone channels.

History

The first transatlantic telegraph cable had been laid in 1858, but reliable telephone service across the Atlantic depended on radio until the development of rugged submerged repeaters (amplifiers) using vacuum tubes. TAT-1 was a joint project of AT&T, the British Post Office, and the Canadian Overseas Telecommunications Corporation. The system consisted of two cables, one for each direction of transmission, each containing 51 repeaters spaced about 37 nautical miles apart.

Service

In its first 24 hours of public service, 588 calls were placed between London and North America. Capacity was soon increased, and the system also carried the famous Moscow–Washington hotline link for part of its route. Three voice circuits were allocated as talkback circuits for the system control. TAT-1 was retired in 1978, by which time later cables such as TAT-6 and early satellites carried the transatlantic traffic.

Legacy

The site of TAT-1's landing at Clarenville is commemorated as a historic site, and the cable system is widely regarded as one of the great engineering achievements of 20th-century telecommunications, ushering in the era of modern international telephony.
