Hilbert's problems

Hilbert's problems are 23 problems in mathematics published by the German mathematician David Hilbert in 1900. They were a turning point in mathematical research and remain influential, with most now solved or partially solved.

History

Hilbert presented the problems at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Paris on 8 August 1900. Ten were presented at the congress; the full list of 23 appeared in print later that year.

Notable problems

The list includes the continuum hypothesis (problem 1), the consistency of arithmetic (problem 2), the Riemann hypothesis (part of problem 8, still open), and the tenth problem on Diophantine equations, shown undecidable in 1970.
