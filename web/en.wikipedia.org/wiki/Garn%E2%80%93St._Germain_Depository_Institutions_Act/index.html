Garn–St. Germain Depository Institutions Act

Garn–St. Germain Act
EnactedOctober 15, 1982
Signed byPresident Ronald Reagan
Named forSen. Jake Garn; Rep. Fernand St. Germain

The Garn–St. Germain Depository Institutions Act of 1982 is a United States federal law that substantially deregulated savings and loan associations and other depository institutions during the savings and loan crisis. Signed by President Ronald Reagan on October 15, 1982, it was named for its sponsors, Senator Jake Garn of Utah and Representative Fernand St. Germain of Rhode Island.

Provisions

The Act phased out interest-rate ceilings on deposits (Regulation Q), authorized money market deposit accounts to compete with money market funds, expanded the lending and investment powers of savings and loans into commercial and consumer loans, and raised federal deposit insurance coverage. It also preempted state restrictions on due-on-sale clauses in mortgages and authorized emergency acquisitions of failing institutions across state lines.

Effects

Intended to rescue a thrift industry battered by high interest rates, the Act's deregulation — combined with weak supervision and expanded powers — is widely blamed for enabling the risky lending that deepened the savings and loan crisis of the late 1980s, which ultimately cost taxpayers over 00 billion and led to corrective legislation in the Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act of 1989.
