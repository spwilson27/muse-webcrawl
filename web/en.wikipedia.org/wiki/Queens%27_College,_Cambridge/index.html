Queens' College, Cambridge

Queens' College

Founded: 1448
University: Cambridge
Students: c. 1,100

Queens' College is a constituent college of the University of Cambridge. It was founded in 1448 by Margaret of Anjou, wife of Henry VI, and refounded in 1465 by Elizabeth Woodville, wife of Edward IV — the source of the plural "Queens'" in its name.

Buildings

The college stands on both banks of the River Cam, linked by the Mathematical Bridge, a wooden footbridge first built in 1749 and popularly (and falsely) said to have been designed by Isaac Newton without bolts. Old Court and Cloister Court contain some of the oldest buildings on the river, and the President's Lodge is among the oldest buildings in Cambridge still in use.

People

Erasmus of Rotterdam was a resident scholar at Queens' in the early 16th century. Notable alumni include the actor and writer Stephen Fry, the journalist Emily Maitlis, and the naturalist and broadcaster's contemporaries in science and politics. The college admits around 1,100 students, undergraduate and postgraduate combined.
