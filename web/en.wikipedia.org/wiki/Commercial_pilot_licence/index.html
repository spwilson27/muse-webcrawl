A commercial pilot licence (CPL), also known as a commercial pilot license, is a type of pilot licence that permits the holder to act as a pilot of an aircraft and be paid for their work. It sits above the private pilot licence (PPL), which does not permit remuneration, and below the airline transport pilot licence (ATPL).

Requirements

Under ICAO standards, applicants must generally be at least 18 years old, hold a current medical certificate, and complete a minimum of about 200 hours of flight time, including specified cross-country, night, and instrument experience, together with written examinations and a flight test. Exact requirements differ among licensing authorities such as the FAA and EASA.

Privileges

A CPL holder may fly for hire in commercial operations such as charter, aerial work, and flight instruction (with additional ratings), and may act as co-pilot in air transport. Command of large airliners in scheduled service requires the ATPL, for which the CPL with instrument and multi-engine ratings is the usual stepping stone.
