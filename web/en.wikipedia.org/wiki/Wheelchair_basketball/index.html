Wheelchair basketball

Wheelchair basketball is basketball played by people with physical disabilities in sports wheelchairs. It is one of the most popular Paralympic sports, governed internationally by the International Wheelchair Basketball Federation (IWBF), and has been part of the Paralympic Games since the first edition in Rome in 1960 (with a women's tournament added in 1968).

History

The sport began in the 1940s in veterans' hospitals in the United States, where wounded Second World War servicemen adapted the running game to wheelchairs; teams such as the Flying Wheels toured to demonstrate the sport. It spread through rehabilitation centres in Britain and elsewhere, and Stoke Mandeville in England became an early centre of wheelchair sport.

Rules

The game is played on a regulation basketball court with hoops at the standard 10 ft (3.05 m) height. Most rules mirror FIBA basketball. A player may push their chair at most twice before they must dribble, pass, or shoot; a third push is a travelling violation. The wheelchair is considered part of the player's body for fouls, and deliberate chair contact can draw technical fouls.

Classification

Players are classified by functional ability from 1.0 (least function) to 4.5 (most function). A team's five players on court may not exceed a combined 14.0 points, which ensures mixed-ability teams and gives low-point players a vital tactical role in screening and defence.

Competitions

Besides the Paralympics, major events include the IWBF World Championships and continental championships. The United States, Canada, Australia, the Netherlands, and Great Britain have been among the leading nations; the American men's and women's teams and the Dutch women's team have won multiple Paralympic titles.
