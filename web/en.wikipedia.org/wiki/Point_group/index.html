Point group

In geometry, a point group is a group of geometric symmetries (isometries) that keep at least one point fixed. Point groups exist in any Euclidean space, and their elements are rotations, reflections, inversions, and improper rotations about axes passing through the fixed point. They are fundamental in crystallography, where they describe the symmetry of crystal structures and molecules, and in spectroscopy.

Overview

A point group in two dimensions is either a cyclic group Cn (rotations by multiples of 360°/n) or a dihedral group Dn (rotations combined with reflections). In three dimensions, point groups include the axial, polyhedral (tetrahedral, octahedral, icosahedral), and other families. The complete classification of three-dimensional point groups yields 32 crystallographic point groups when only symmetries compatible with a periodic lattice are allowed; molecules, unconstrained by lattice periodicity, may also exhibit fivefold and other symmetries, as in the icosahedral group of fullerenes and virus capsids.

Notation

Two main notation systems are used. Schoenflies notation, common in spectroscopy and molecular chemistry, labels groups such as C2v, D3h, Td, and Oh. Hermann–Mauguin (international) notation, standard in crystallography, describes groups with symbols such as 4/mmm or m3̄m.

Applications

Point-group symmetry determines many physical properties: whether a molecule has a permanent dipole moment, which vibrational modes are infrared- or Raman-active, and the degeneracy of electronic energy levels. Character tables of point groups are a standard tool in group theory applied to chemistry.
