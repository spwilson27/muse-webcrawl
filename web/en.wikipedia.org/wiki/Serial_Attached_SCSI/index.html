Serial Attached SCSI


Abbreviation: SAS
Type: Computer bus / storage interface
Standard body: INCITS T10 (SCSI Trade Association)

Serial Attached SCSI (SAS) is a point-to-point serial protocol that moves data to and from computer storage devices such as hard drives and tape drives. SAS replaces the older parallel SCSI bus technology first appearing in the mid-1980s, but uses the same SCSI command set. It offers backward compatibility with second-generation Serial ATA (SATA) devices: SATA drives can be connected to SAS backplanes, although SAS drives cannot be used on a SATA bus.

Technical overview

SAS uses point-to-point links, so each device gets dedicated bandwidth without the shared-bus contention and termination problems of parallel SCSI. Successive generations raised link rates: SAS-1 at 3 Gbit/s (2004), SAS-2 at 6 Gbit/s (2009), SAS-3 at 12 Gbit/s (2013), and SAS-4 at 24 Gbit/s ("24G", standardized around 2017). SAS expanders allow a single host adapter to address very large numbers of devices — theoretically over 16,000 — across multiple levels.

SAS drives typically support dual ports for redundant paths to storage, full-duplex communication, and long cable lengths relative to SATA, making SAS the interface of choice for enterprise servers and storage arrays, while SATA is favoured for capacity-oriented bulk storage. The protocol is maintained by the INCITS T10 committee and promoted by the SCSI Trade Association.
