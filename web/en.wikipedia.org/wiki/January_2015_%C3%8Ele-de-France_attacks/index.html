January 2015 Île-de-France attacks

Date7–9 January 2015
LocationParis and surroundings, France
Deaths17 victims (plus 3 perpetrators)
PerpetratorsSaïd and Chérif Kouachi; Amedy Coulibaly

The January 2015 Île-de-France attacks were a series of coordinated terrorist attacks in and around Paris, France, between 7 and 9 January 2015. Seventeen people were killed in three connected incidents, which were among the deadliest attacks in France since the Second World War.

Charlie Hebdo shooting

On 7 January, brothers Saïd and Chérif Kouachi forced their way into the offices of the satirical weekly newspaper Charlie Hebdo and murdered 12 people, including editor Stéphane "Charb" Charbonnier and cartoonists Cabu, Wolinski, Tignous and Honoré, in retaliation for the paper's cartoons of Muhammad. The gunmen, who claimed allegiance to al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula, escaped and were tracked to a printing works in Dammartin-en-Goële, where they were killed in a police assault on 9 January.

Montrouge and Hypercacher attacks

On 8 January, Amedy Coulibaly shot dead a policewoman in Montrouge. On 9 January he took hostages at a kosher supermarket, the Hypercacher at Porte de Vincennes, murdering four Jewish customers before being killed when police stormed the store.

Aftermath

On 11 January, around 1.5 million people marched in Paris, joined by dozens of world leaders, in defence of free expression; the slogan "Je suis Charlie" spread worldwide. The attacks led to heightened security across France and the deployment of soldiers under Opération Sentinelle.
