Harpoon (missile)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Harpoon is an all-weather, over-the-horizon, anti-ship missile developed and manufactured by McDonnell Douglas (now Boeing Defense, Space & Security). Introduced in 1977, it has become one of the most widely used anti-ship missiles in the world, in service with the United States Navy and the armed forces of more than 30 countries.

Characteristics

The Harpoon is a sea-skimming cruise missile powered by a small turbojet engine, with a range of roughly 90–240 km depending on launch platform and variant. It navigates by inertial guidance with GPS updates and homes on its target in the terminal phase using active radar. The missile carries a 221 kg penetrating blast-fragmentation warhead. Variants include the ship-launched RGM-84, the air-launched AGM-84, and the submarine-launched UGM-84, fired from torpedo tubes in a capsule.

Service

The missile saw combat during the Iran–Iraq War era, including the 1988 Operation Praying Mantis, when U.S. forces used Harpoons against Iranian naval vessels. The SLAM-ER land-attack derivative extends the design. Successive upgrades, culminating in the Harpoon Block II and Block II+, have kept the weapon in service for nearly five decades.
