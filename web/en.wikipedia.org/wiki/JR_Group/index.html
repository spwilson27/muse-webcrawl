JR Group

The Japan Railways Group, commonly known as the JR Group (JRグループ), is a group of seven Japanese railway companies that together took over most of the assets and operations of the government-owned Japanese National Railways (JNR) when it was privatised on 1 April 1987.

Member companies

The group consists of six regional passenger companies — JR Hokkaido, JR East, JR Central, JR West, JR Shikoku, and JR Kyushu — plus the nationwide JR Freight and associated research and IT bodies. The passenger companies operate the Shinkansen high-speed network, which is divided among JR East, JR Central, JR West, JR Kyushu, and JR Hokkaido, as well as dense urban and regional conventional lines.

Privatisation

JNR was broken up because of mounting debt exceeding ¥37 trillion. The three largest Honshu companies were subsequently listed on the stock exchange and became fully private, while the island companies of Hokkaido, Shikoku, and Kyushu received stabilisation funds. The JR companies cooperate on through services, the nationwide Japan Rail Pass, and shared branding while competing in some markets.
