Battle of Arnhem

The Battle of Arnhem was a battle of the Second World War fought in and around the Dutch city of Arnhem from 17 to 26 September 1944, as the northernmost element of Operation Market Garden. The British 1st Airborne Division, with the Polish 1st Independent Parachute Brigade, dropped to seize the road bridge over the Lower Rhine at Arnhem, the final crossing needed to open a route into northern Germany. Only one battalion — Lieutenant Colonel John Frost's 2nd Battalion — reached the bridge. Surprised by the presence of the II SS Panzer Corps refitting nearby, and with ground relief delayed at Nijmegen and beyond, the division was cut off and largely destroyed in the suburb of Oosterbeek; the survivors, about 2,000 of some 10,000 men, were withdrawn across the river on the night of 25–26 September. Arnhem thus became famous as the "bridge too far".

Background and landings

Market Garden aimed to use three airborne divisions to capture a chain of bridges so that XXX Corps could advance into Germany. The 1st Airborne dropped on 17 September on landing zones up to 13 km from the bridge — a compromise forced by terrain and air-planning concerns — and radio failures crippled coordination from the start. Frost's battalion reached the northern end of the bridge by evening and held it against repeated attacks for nearly four days. The rest of the division was stopped by German blocking lines, including SS reconnaissance and panzer units that Allied intelligence had dismissed as depleted.

The Oosterbeek perimeter

Driven back into Oosterbeek, the division formed a defensive thumb-shaped perimeter around the Hartenstein Hotel divisional headquarters. The Polish brigade dropped south of the river at Driel on 21 September, but ferry crossings failed. Meanwhile XXX Corps, having taken the Nijmegen bridge late, was halted by German counterattacks along the single raised highway ("Hell's Highway"). On the night of 25/26 September, Operation Berlin evacuated roughly 2,400 survivors by boat across the Rhine; thousands of wounded and the rest of the division were left to be captured.

Aftermath and memory

Arnhem remained in German hands until April 1945, and its Dutch population was forcibly evacuated; the winter that followed brought famine to the still-occupied western Netherlands. The battle was immortalised by Cornelius Ryan's book A Bridge Too Far (1974) and the 1977 film of the same name. The Airborne Museum at Oosterbeek and the annual commemorations, attended by veterans and Polish and Dutch communities, keep the battle at the centre of the region's remembrance culture.
