Sean Connery

Connery in 1964.

Sir Thomas Sean Connery (25 August 1930 – 31 October 2020) was a Scottish actor. He was the first actor to portray the fictional British secret agent James Bond on film, starring in seven Bond films between 1962 and 1983. Connery's portrayal defined the character and made him one of the most popular film stars of the 20th century; he went on to a long career in films such as The Untouchables (1987), for which he won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor.

Born: Thomas Sean Connery, 25 August 1930, Edinburgh, Scotland

Died: 31 October 2020 (aged 90), Nassau, Bahamas

Occupation: Actor

Years active: 1954–2003, 2012

Known for: James Bond (1962–1971, 1983), The Untouchables, Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade, The Hunt for Red October, The Rock

Honours: Knighted 2000; Academy Award winner

Early life

Connery was born in Fountainbridge, Edinburgh, to a working-class family. He worked as a milkman, lorry driver, lifeguard and coffin polisher, served in the Royal Navy, and competed as a bodybuilder before turning to acting in the early 1950s, taking minor stage and television roles.

James Bond

Cast by producers Albert R. Broccoli and Harry Saltzman, Connery debuted as Bond in Dr. No (1962) and starred in From Russia with Love, Goldfinger, Thunderball, You Only Live Twice and Diamonds Are Forever, returning once more for the unofficial Never Say Never Again (1983). His blend of toughness, wit and suavity made Bond a global phenomenon.

Later career

Connery broadened his range in The Man Who Would Be King (1975), The Name of the Rose (1986), The Untouchables (1987), Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade (1989) as Henry Jones Sr., The Hunt for Red October (1990), The Rock (1996) and Finding Forrester (2000). He retired from acting after The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen (2003). He was knighted in 2000 and died at his home in the Bahamas in 2020.
