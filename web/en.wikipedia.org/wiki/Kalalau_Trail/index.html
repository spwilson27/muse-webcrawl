Kalalau Trail

The Kalalau Trail is a hiking trail along the Nā Pali Coast of the island of Kauai in the U.S. state of Hawaii. Running approximately 11 miles (18 km) from Keʻe Beach to Kalalau Beach, it is the only land access to the Kalalau Valley and is widely regarded as one of the most beautiful — and most demanding — trails in the United States.

Route

The trail begins at Keʻe Beach in Hāʻena State Park and climbs and descends a series of five valleys along the rugged volcanic coastline. Major landmarks include:

  • Hanakāpīʻai Beach (2 miles) — the most popular day-hike destination, with a side trail to Hanakāpīʻai Falls.
  • Hanakoa Valley (6 miles) — a former terraced agricultural valley with a camping area.
  • Crawler's Ledge — a narrow, exposed section near mile 7 crossing steep sea cliffs above the Pacific.
  • Kalalau Beach (11 miles) — the terminus, backed by the Kalalau Valley.

Permits and hazards

Access beyond Hanakāpīʻai requires an overnight camping permit issued by Hawaii State Parks, and day use of Hāʻena State Park requires advance reservations. The trail crosses several streams that can become impassable during flash floods, and the unprotected beach at Hanakāpīʻai has been the site of numerous drownings; hikers are warned not to swim there.

History

The path follows ancient Hawaiian footpaths used by the inhabitants of the remote valleys, who cultivated taro in terraced fields. The modern trail was built in the late 19th and early 20th centuries and is maintained as part of the Nā Pali Coast State Wilderness Park.
