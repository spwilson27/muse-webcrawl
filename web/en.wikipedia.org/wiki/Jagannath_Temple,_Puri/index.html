Jagannath Temple, Puri

Location: Puri, Odisha, India

Deity: Jagannath (a form of Krishna)

Completed: 12th century CE

Festival: Ratha Yatra

The Jagannath Temple is a Hindu temple dedicated to the deity Jagannath, a form of the god Vishnu/Krishna, located in the coastal city of Puri in the state of Odisha, India. It is one of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites and one of the most revered temples in Hinduism.

History

The present temple complex was rebuilt beginning in the 10th century and completed in its main form in the 12th century under the Eastern Ganga king Anantavarman Chodaganga. The temple's towering shikhara rises about 65 metres (214 feet) above the surrounding city.

Deities and worship

The principal deities are the triad of Jagannath, his elder brother Balabhadra, and their sister Subhadra, whose wooden images are carved anew at intervals in the Nabakalebara ritual. Worship in the temple follows distinctive traditions, and entry is restricted to Hindus of Indian origin.

Ratha Yatra

The temple is famous for its annual Ratha Yatra, or chariot festival, in which the three deities are drawn through the streets of Puri on enormous wooden chariots. The festival draws millions of devotees and gave rise to the English word "juggernaut". The temple kitchen is among the largest in the world, preparing the sacred food offering known as Mahaprasad.
