Siege of Cawnpore

The siege of Cawnpore was a key episode of the Indian Rebellion of 1857. From 6 to 25 June 1857, rebel forces led by Nana Sahib besieged the British entrenchment at Cawnpore (modern Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh), defended by Major-General Sir Hugh Wheeler. The siege ended with a negotiated evacuation by river — and the massacre of most of the evacuees at the Satti Chaura Ghat, an event that seared itself into British memory of the rebellion.

Background

Cawnpore was an important East India Company garrison station on the Ganges and the Grand Trunk Road. When the sepoy regiments there mutinied in early June 1857, they were joined by Nana Sahib, the dispossessed adopted heir of the last Maratha Peshwa, who assumed leadership of the revolt locally. Wheeler, expecting a brief crisis and intending to move toward Allahabad, had prepared only a shallow, poorly sited entrenchment around two barrack buildings, into which some 900 soldiers, civilians, women, and children crowded.

The siege

For three weeks the defenders endured constant bombardment, extreme heat, collapsing buildings, and desperate shortages of food, water, and medicine; several hundred died in the entrenchment. On 25 June Nana Sahib offered terms: safe passage by boat down the Ganges to Allahabad. Wheeler, his position hopeless, accepted.

The massacre and aftermath

On the morning of 27 June, as the survivors boarded boats at the Satti Chaura Ghat, firing broke out — whose side fired first remains disputed — and nearly all the men were killed; around 200 women and children were taken prisoner. These captives were murdered on 15 July at the Bibighar as British relief forces under Henry Havelock approached, one of the most notorious atrocities of the rebellion. Havelock retook Cawnpore on 16–17 July. The events at Cawnpore became the rallying cry of British reprisals and were commemorated by the Memorial Well erected on the site of the Bibighar; after Indian independence, the memorials were reinterpreted and Nana Sahib honoured as a hero of the freedom struggle.
