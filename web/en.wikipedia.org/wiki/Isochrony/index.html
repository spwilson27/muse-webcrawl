Isochrony

Isochrony (from Greek isos, "equal", and chronos, "time") is the property of a periodic system whose period is independent of its amplitude. The term is used in several distinct fields, most famously in horology and mechanics, and in linguistics and phonetics.

Physics and horology

Galileo Galilei is traditionally credited with observing, around 1602, that a pendulum's period is approximately independent of the arc through which it swings (for small amplitudes). This property made the pendulum practical as a timekeeper, first exploited by Christiaan Huygens in the pendulum clock of 1656. Huygens showed that a perfectly isochronous pendulum bob must follow a cycloid rather than a circular arc — the tautochrone curve — and designed "cheeks" to constrain the suspension accordingly. Isochronism is also sought in the balance springs of watches, where the spiral hairspring is shaped with an overcoil (Breguet) terminal curve to keep the rate equal at large and small oscillations. A linear harmonic oscillator is exactly isochronous, since its period depends only on mass and stiffness, not amplitude.

Linguistics

In phonetics, isochrony denotes the hypothesised rhythmic division of speech into equal time intervals. Kenneth Pike proposed in 1945 that languages fall into types: in stress-timed languages (such as English and German), stressed syllables are said to recur at roughly equal intervals; in syllable-timed languages (such as Spanish and French), syllables are said to take roughly equal time; and in mora-timed languages (such as Japanese), the mora is the equal timing unit. Instrumental research since the 1980s has found little evidence for literal equal timing; modern work (notably by Dauer, and by Ramus, Nespor and Mehler) treats rhythm as a continuum measured by the variability of vocalic and consonantal intervals rather than as strict isochrony.

Other uses

In geology, an isochron is a line on a graph used in isochron dating, joining points representing samples of the same age; in communications and computing, "isochronous" describes data transfer with guaranteed timing, such as the isochronous mode of USB used for audio and video streaming.
