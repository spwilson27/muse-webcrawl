Canonical commutation relation

In quantum mechanics, the canonical commutation relation is the fundamental relation between canonical conjugate quantities, such as position and momentum: the commutator of the position and momentum operators equals iħ.

Field: Quantum mechanics
Relation: [x, p] = iħ

Significance

The relation, formulated in the 1920s by Born, Heisenberg, Jordan and Dirac, implies the Heisenberg uncertainty principle and distinguishes quantum from classical mechanics, where the corresponding Poisson bracket structure differs in interpretation.

Generalisation

Canonical commutation relations extend to field operators in quantum field theory and to angular momentum components.
