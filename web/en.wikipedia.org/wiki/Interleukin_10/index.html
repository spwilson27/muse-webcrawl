Interleukin 10 (IL-10), also known as human cytokine synthesis inhibitory factor (CSIF), is an anti-inflammatory cytokine. In humans, interleukin 10 is encoded by the IL10 gene and is produced by a wide range of immune cells, including monocytes, macrophages, regulatory T cells, and Th2 cells.

Function

IL-10 binds a tetrameric receptor of two IL-10R1 and two IL-10R2 subunits, activating JAK1/TYK2 and STAT3 signalling. It suppresses macrophage and dendritic-cell production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF, IL-1, IL-6 and IL-12, downregulates MHC class II and co-stimulatory molecules, and thereby limits tissue damage from excessive inflammation while also modulating B-cell survival and antibody production.

Clinical significance

Mice lacking IL-10 develop chronic enterocolitis, and in humans, loss-of-function mutations in IL-10 or its receptor cause severe early-onset inflammatory bowel disease. Polymorphisms affecting IL-10 production are associated with several autoimmune and infectious diseases. Some viruses, notably Epstein–Barr virus, encode an IL-10 homologue that dampens host immunity.
