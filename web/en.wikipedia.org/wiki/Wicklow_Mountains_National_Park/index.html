Wicklow Mountains National Park

Wicklow Mountains National Park (Irish: Páirc Náisiúnta Shléibhte Chill Mhantáin) is a protected area in County Wicklow, Ireland, covering upland blanket bog, heath, glacial valleys and lakes south of Dublin. At around 22,000 hectares it is Ireland's largest national park.

Geography

The park centres on the Wicklow Mountains, including Lugnaquilla, at 925 m the highest peak in Leinster. The Glendalough valley, with its early medieval monastic site founded by St Kevin, contains the Upper and Lower Lakes. Other features include the Glenmacnass Waterfall, Lough Tay and Lough Dan.

History

The park was established in 1991 around Glendalough and expanded by later land purchases, including Luggala. It is managed by the National Parks and Wildlife Service.

Wildlife and recreation

Habitats support red deer and sika hybrids, peregrine falcons and rare plants. The Wicklow Way long-distance trail passes through the park, which is popular for hillwalking, though erosion and deer overgrazing are management concerns.
