Black rice is a range of rice types of the species Oryza sativa, some of which are glutinous rice, whose grains have a deep black or purple husk and bran layer. When cooked, the rice typically turns a deep purple colour owing to its high content of anthocyanin pigments, the same antioxidants found in blueberries and blackberries.

History

In imperial China black rice was known as "forbidden rice," supposedly because it was reserved for the emperor and the nobility, who valued it for longevity and health. Varieties have long been cultivated across Asia: in Indonesia, the Philippines (where it is used in the rice cake puto bumbong-style desserts and the drink tinumis), Thailand, and the Manipur region of India, where the variety Chak-hao is prized. The Italian cultivar Venere, created by crossing a Chinese black variety with an Italian one, is grown in the Po valley.

Nutrition and uses

Black rice is a whole grain: its bran layer, retained in the unmilled grain, supplies fibre, iron, vitamin E, and anthocyanins. Its flavour is mild and nutty. It is used in porridges, desserts such as Indonesian bubur ketan hitam, risottos, salads, and as a colourful substitute for white or brown rice.
