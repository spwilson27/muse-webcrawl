Captiva, Florida

County: Lee County
State: Florida, United States
Type: Census-designated place
Area: About 27 km² (incl. water)
Population: Under 1,000 permanent residents
Island: Captiva Island

Captiva is an unincorporated community and census-designated place in Lee County, Florida, United States, occupying the southern portion of Captiva Island, a barrier island in the Gulf of Mexico off the coast of Fort Myers. It is reached by road via the Sanibel Causeway and Sanibel Island, and lies just south of North Captiva Island, from which it was separated by a hurricane-created inlet in 1921.

History

Local legend holds that the island's name derives from the pirate José Gaspar ("Gasparilla"), who is said to have held captives for ransom there. The island was homesteaded in the late 19th century; a small village developed around fishing, farming and, later, tourism. The 'Tween Waters Inn, a historic resort dating to 1931, hosted guests such as Anne Morrow Lindbergh. The poet Anne Morrow Lindbergh reportedly found inspiration for her book Gift from the Sea on the island.

Geography and character

Captiva is known for its beaches — famous for shelling, like neighbouring Sanibel — lush tropical vegetation, upscale resorts such as South Seas Island Resort, and a small, picturesque village centre along Andy Rosse Lane with restaurants including the Bubble Room, known for its Christmas-themed décor and large cakes. The historic Captiva Chapel by the Sea and its small cemetery stand nearby. Strict building codes limit heights and preserve the island's laid-back character.

Storms

The island is vulnerable to hurricanes. Hurricane Charley made landfall near Captiva in August 2004, causing severe damage and cutting a new inlet through North Captiva. Hurricane Ian in September 2022 devastated the wider Sanibel–Captiva area, destroying the Sanibel Causeway temporarily and causing extensive damage on the island.
