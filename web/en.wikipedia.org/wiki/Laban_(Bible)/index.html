Laban (Bible)

Laban (Hebrew: לָבָן, "white") is a figure in the Hebrew Bible's Book of Genesis. He is the brother of Rebekah, the father of Leah and Rachel, and thus the father-in-law — and uncle — of the patriarch Jacob. Laban lived in Paddan Aram (Haran) in upper Mesopotamia and was a wealthy herdsman and Aramean.

Biblical narrative

Laban first appears welcoming Abraham's servant, who came seeking a wife for Isaac, and agreeing to Rebekah's departure. Years later, Jacob fled to Haran to escape Esau's anger and to find a wife. Laban welcomed him and agreed that Jacob would work seven years to marry Rachel — but at the wedding substituted the elder daughter, Leah, defended with the claim that custom forbade marrying off the younger first. Jacob then worked a further seven years for Rachel, and six more for flocks, during which Laban repeatedly changed his wages.

After twenty years Jacob secretly departed with his wives, children, and livestock. Laban pursued and overtook him in Gilead, accused him of stealing his household gods (teraphim, which Rachel had in fact hidden), but the two ultimately made a covenant at Mizpah, marked by a heap of stones, and parted in peace. Through Leah and Rachel, Laban is the grandfather of the twelve tribes of Israel.

Interpretation

In rabbinic tradition Laban is portrayed as a schemer and idolater; the Passover Haggadah reads the line "an Aramean sought to destroy my father" as referring to Laban, said to have sought Jacob's ruin more thoroughly than Pharaoh.
