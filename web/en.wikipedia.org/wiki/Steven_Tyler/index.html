Steven Tyler

Born: Steven Victor Tallarico, March 26, 1948, New York City, U.S.

Occupation: Singer, songwriter, musician

Years active: 1964–present

Steven Victor Tallarico (born March 26, 1948), known professionally as Steven Tyler, is an American singer, best known as the lead singer of the Boston-based rock band Aerosmith, in which he also plays harmonica, piano and percussion. He has been called the "Demon of Screamin'" due to his high screams and his powerful wide vocal range.

During the 1970s, Tyler rose to prominence as the frontman of Aerosmith, which released rock albums such as Toys in the Attic and Rocks, along with a string of hit singles, including "Dream On", "Sweet Emotion" and "Walk This Way". After struggles with drug addiction in the late 1970s and early 1980s, Aerosmith made a major comeback in the late 1980s with the albums Permanent Vacation, Pump, and Get a Grip.

Career

Tyler formed Aerosmith in Boston in 1970 with guitarist Joe Perry and others. The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2001. Tyler later served as a judge on the television singing competition American Idol and released a solo country-influenced album, We're All Somebody from Somewhere (2016).
