Oprah's Book Club

Host: Oprah Winfrey

Launched: 1996 (The Oprah Winfrey Show)

Relaunched: 2012 (Book Club 2.0); 2019 (Apple TV+)

Effect: Selections became instant bestsellers ("the Oprah effect")

Oprah's Book Club is a book discussion club founded by talk show host Oprah Winfrey in 1996 as a segment of The Oprah Winfrey Show. A selection typically sent the chosen title to the top of bestseller lists, often selling a million or more additional copies — a phenomenon publishers called the "Oprah effect".

History

The club debuted on 17 September 1996 with Jacquelyn Mitchard's The Deep End of the Ocean. Selections mixed contemporary fiction, classics such as East of Eden and Anna Karenina, and memoirs including Elie Wiesel's Night. Toni Morrison, whose Song of Solomon was an early pick, had four novels chosen, more than any other author.

Controversies

In 2001 novelist Jonathan Franzen expressed ambivalence about his The Corrections being selected, and Winfrey withdrew his invitation to appear. In 2005 James Frey's memoir A Million Little Pieces was revealed to contain fabrications; Winfrey first defended Frey, then famously confronted him on air and apologised to viewers, prompting a national debate about truth in memoir.

Later versions

The original club ended with the show in 2011. Oprah's Book Club 2.0 launched in 2012 with OWN and O, The Oprah Magazine, beginning with Cheryl Strayed's Wild. In 2019 the club moved to Apple TV+ as a series of author interviews, opening with Ta-Nehisi Coates's The Water Dancer.

Impact

Scholars credit the club with popularising literary reading groups and diversifying the commercial canon, while critics argued its selections favoured emotionally accessible, redemptive narratives.
