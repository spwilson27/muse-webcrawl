Bell Textron

Bell Textron Inc., doing business as Bell and formerly Bell Helicopter, is an American aircraft manufacturer headquartered in Fort Worth, Texas, and a subsidiary of Textron. Founded as the Bell Aircraft Corporation in Buffalo, New York in 1935 by Lawrence Dale Bell, the company is best known for its helicopters — including the UH-1 Iroquois "Huey" of the Vietnam War, the Bell 47 that pioneered commercial helicopter certification, and the V-22 Osprey tiltrotor developed with Boeing — but it also built famous fixed-wing aircraft such as the P-39 Airacobra and the Bell X-1, the first aircraft to break the sound barrier in level flight.

History

Bell Aircraft produced the P-39 and P-63 fighters during the Second World War and built America's first jet aircraft, the P-59 Airacomet. On 14 October 1947 the rocket-powered X-1, piloted by Chuck Yeager, exceeded Mach 1. In 1946 the Bell 47 became the first helicopter certified for civilian use in the United States; its bubble canopy and exposed framework became an icon, familiar from the television series M*A*S*H. Textron acquired the company in 1960 and spun its helicopter operations into Bell Helicopter. Production moved to Texas, and the UH-1 family became one of the most widely produced helicopters in history.

Products and programmes

Current and recent products include the Bell 206 JetRanger family, the 407, 412 and 429 civil helicopters, the AH-1Z Viper and UH-1Y Venom for the U.S. Marine Corps, and the V-280 Valor tiltrotor, selected by the U.S. Army in 2022 as its Future Long-Range Assault Aircraft to succeed the Black Hawk. Bell also developed the Eagle Eye UAV and the Nexus air-taxi concept. The company employs roughly 10,000 people at facilities in Texas, Tennessee, Canada and elsewhere.
