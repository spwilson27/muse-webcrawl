Agnes of Rome

Saint Agnes
Bornc. 291, Rome
Diedc. 304, Rome
Feast day21 January
AttributesLamb, palm branch of martyrdom

Agnes of Rome (c. 291 – c. 304) was a young Roman Christian virgin and martyr, venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church, and other traditions. She is one of the most famous of the early Roman martyrs, remembered in the Roman Canon of the Mass, and her feast day is 21 January.

Legend

According to tradition, Agnes was a beautiful girl of noble birth who consecrated her virginity to Christ. Rejecting suitors — in some accounts the son of the prefect of Rome — she was denounced as a Christian during the Diocletian persecution. Brought before the authorities, she refused to sacrifice to the gods or accept marriage. Various miracles are said to have thwarted attempts to shame or burn her, and she was finally executed by the sword, at about twelve or thirteen years of age.

Veneration

Agnes is the patron saint of virgins, girls, and victims of sexual abuse. Her symbol is a lamb, both because her name resembles the Latin agnus (lamb) and as an emblem of purity. The basilica of Sant'Agnese fuori le mura in Rome stands over her tomb in the catacombs on the Via Nomentana. On her feast day, two lambs are traditionally blessed at the basilica, and their wool is used to make the pallia given to new archbishops.
