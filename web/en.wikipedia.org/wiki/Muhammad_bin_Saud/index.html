Muhammad bin Saud ibn Muhammad ibn Muqrin Al-Maridah Al-Ad-Diriyah (Arabic: محمد بن سعود; c. 1687–1765), also known as Ibn Saud, was the emir of Diriyah and the founder of the House of Saud and of the Emirate of Diriyah, retrospectively called the first Saudi state.

Life

Muhammad bin Saud became ruler of the oasis town of Diriyah in Najd in 1727. In 1744 he received the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, who had been expelled from neighbouring Uyayna, and concluded with him the famous Diriyah pact: Ibn Saud would protect and propagate the reformer's doctrine — a strict, puritanical reading of Islam later called Wahhabism — and in return the reformer recognised Ibn Saud's political authority.

Expansion

From Diriyah, Muhammad bin Saud and his son and successor Abdulaziz campaigned across Najd, uniting much of central Arabia under Saudi rule allied with Wahhabi religious legitimacy. He died in 1765. The state he founded endured until its destruction by Ottoman-Egyptian forces in 1818, and his descendants later founded the second and third Saudi states, culminating in the modern Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.
