Linda Thompson (born Linda Pettifer, 23 August 1947) is an English folk singer, widely regarded as one of the finest interpreters of traditional and contemporary British song. She is best known for her work in the 1970s and early 1980s as one half of the duo Richard and Linda Thompson, whose albums — including I Want to See the Bright Lights Tonight (1974) and Shoot Out the Lights (1982) — are classics of British folk rock.

Career

Born in Hackney, London, Linda Pettifer sang in folk clubs from the late 1960s (briefly as Linda Peters), recording advertising jingles and appearing with groups such as The Bunch. She married guitarist Richard Thompson in 1972; together they made six studio albums as a duo, joining the Sufi Muslim community in the mid-1970s and withdrawing from touring for a period. Their final album, Shoot Out the Lights — recorded as their marriage collapsed, with songs such as "Walking on a Wire" and "Wall of Death" — was acclaimed on release in 1982 and frequently appears in critics' all-time lists.

After the divorce, Thompson developed spasmodic dysphonia — "hysterical dysphonia" — a stress-related condition that left her literally unable to sing for long periods, and she largely disappeared from music for some 17 years. She returned with the solo album Fashionably Late (2002), followed by Versatile Heart (2007) and Won't Be Long Now (2013), collaborating with her children Teddy and Kamila Thompson and other family members in the "Thompson family" musical clan. A career retrospective, Dreams Fly Away, gathered her recordings. In 2024 she released Proxy Music, an album of her songs sung by other artists (including her family, Rufus Wainwright and The Proclaimers) because her voice no longer permitted her to perform them herself — a project widely praised as both poignant and witty.
