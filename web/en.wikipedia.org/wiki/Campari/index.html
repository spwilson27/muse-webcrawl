Campari is an Italian alcoholic liqueur — a bitter apéritif (amaro) — obtained from the infusion of herbs and fruit, including chinotto and cascarilla, in alcohol and water. It is known for its intense bitter flavour and striking crimson colour, and is a defining ingredient of classic cocktails such as the Negroni, the Americano, and the Garibaldi.

History

Gaspare Campari created the recipe in 1860 in Novara, Piedmont, and later served it at his Caffè Campari in Milan's Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, where his son Davide was born in 1867. Davide Campari built the brand internationally, opening a factory at Sesto San Giovanni in 1904 and promoting innovations such as the single-serve Campari Soda (1932), sold in its distinctive cone-shaped bottle designed by Fortunato Depero. The company grew into Gruppo Campari, now one of the world's largest spirits groups, owning brands including Aperol, Skyy vodka, and Wild Turkey.

Production

The exact recipe is a trade secret; the company states that only company presidents have known it in full. More than 60 ingredients are said to be involved. The red colour traditionally came from carmine dye derived from cochineal insects; since the mid-2000s many markets have used artificial colorants instead. Alcohol content ranges from about 20.5% to 28.5% ABV depending on the country of sale.

Serving

Campari is served as an apéritif, on the rocks with soda, with orange juice (the Garibaldi), or in cocktails. The Negroni — equal parts Campari, gin, and sweet red vermouth — reputedly created in Florence around 1919, became a global classic and the centrepiece of the annual Negroni Week charity event.

Advertising

Campari is famous for its poster art, commissioning artists and designers including Leonetto Cappiello (Spiritello, 1921), Marcello Nizzoli, and Depero, making its campaigns landmarks of Italian graphic design.
