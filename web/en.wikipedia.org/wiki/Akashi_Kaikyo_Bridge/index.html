Akashi Kaikyo Bridge

The Akashi Kaikyo Bridge (Japanese: 明石海峡大橋, Akashi Kaikyō Ōhashi) is a suspension bridge linking the city of Kobe on the Japanese mainland of Honshu to Iwaya on Awaji Island. It is part of the Kobe–Awaji–Naruto Expressway and crosses the busy and turbulent Akashi Strait. With a main span of 1,991 metres (6,532 ft), it was the world's longest suspension bridge from its opening in 1998 until the completion of Turkey's 1915 Çanakkale Bridge, whose main span is 2,023 metres, in 2022. The bridge is also known as the Pearl Bridge for the appearance of its lights at night.

History

Before the bridge was built, ferries carried passengers across the Akashi Strait, a route subject to severe storms; in 1955 two ferries sank in the strait, killing 168 people, many of them children, and the disaster helped spur national support for a fixed crossing. The original plan included both road and railway, but the design was eventually limited to a six-lane highway. Construction began in 1988 in extraordinarily difficult conditions: the strait carries heavy shipping traffic, strong currents of up to 4.5 metres per second, and water some 110 metres deep.

The 1995 earthquake

On 17 January 1995 the Great Hanshin earthquake struck Kobe while the towers and foundations were complete but the deck not yet hung. The earthquake shifted the two main towers apart, lengthening the main span by about 0.8 metres; the bridge's design was adjusted without major difficulty and construction proceeded. The completed bridge is designed to withstand winds of 286 km/h, earthquakes up to magnitude 8.5 on the Richter scale, and the strait's harsh currents.

Structure

The bridge has three spans — 960 m, 1,991 m, and 960 m — suspended from two steel towers 297 metres high. Its main cables are among the strongest ever made, using high-strength galvanised wire developed for the project; each cable is 1.12 metres in diameter and contains some 36,830 wires, with a total cable wire length of about 300,000 km. The stiffening girder is a steel truss, with tuned mass dampers in the towers to counter wind-induced oscillation. The total cost was roughly ¥500 billion.
