Cargo aircraft

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A freighter aircraft being loaded with palletised cargo at night]
Loading a cargo jet.

A cargo aircraft (freighter) is a fixed-wing aircraft designed or converted to carry goods rather than passengers. Dedicated freighters have main-deck cargo doors, reinforced floors and (on the 747F) nose loading; \'preighters\' and passenger-to-freighter conversions (P2F) add capacity. The largest cargo aircraft flying is the Antonov An-124; the one-off An-225 Mriya, destroyed in Ukraine in 2022, was the largest ever built.

Operations

Integrators (FedEx, UPS, DHL) run hub-and-spoke overnight networks centred on Memphis, Louisville and Leipzig; general cargo moves in freighter networks and in passenger belly holds. Air freight carries under 1% of trade by weight but about a third by value — electronics, pharmaceuticals, fashion and perishables.

Types

Production freighters include the 777F, 747-8F, A350F (entering service mid-2020s) and turboprops for feeders; conversions of 737s, A321s and 767s serve e-commerce growth. Military airlifters (C-17, A400M, C-130) double as humanitarian workhorses.

History

Airmail was the original air cargo (1910s–30s); the Berlin Airlift (1948–49) proved air logistics at scale. Purpose-built freighters followed the jet age, and e-commerce from the 2010s refreshed fleets and converted thousands of passenger jets.
