Wittman Regional Airport (IATA: OSH, ICAO: KOSH) is a county-owned public-use airport located two nautical miles south of the central business district of Oshkosh, in Winnebago County, Wisconsin, United States. It is named after pioneering air racer and aircraft designer Steve Wittman.

The airport is best known as the home of EAA AirVenture Oshkosh, the world's largest fly-in convention, organized annually by the Experimental Aircraft Association (EAA), whose headquarters and museum are on the field. During the week-long event each July, Wittman Field becomes the busiest air traffic control tower in the world, handling more than 10,000 arriving aircraft.

History

The airport was established in 1927 and was for many years known as Winnebago County Airport. In 1972 it was renamed for S. J. "Steve" Wittman, a famed air racer, aircraft designer, and longtime airport manager. The EAA moved its annual convention to Oshkosh in 1970, and the event grew steadily into a gathering drawing more than 600,000 attendees in peak years.

Facilities

Wittman Regional Airport covers about 1,392 acres and has four runways, the longest being runway 18/36 at 8,002 feet. The airport supports general aviation, corporate, and charter operations and is home to flight schools, maintenance businesses, and Basler Turbo Conversions, which rebuilds Douglas DC-3 aircraft into turboprop BT-67s on the field.
