Muhammad Ali Pasha

Muhammad Ali Pasha
Wali of Egypt and Sudan
Reign: 1805–1848
Born: c. 1769, Kavala, Ottoman Greece
Died: 2 August 1849, Alexandria, Egypt

Muhammad Ali Pasha (c. 1769 – 2 August 1849) was an Ottoman Albanian commander who rose to become the wali (governor) of Egypt and Sudan, ruling from 1805 to 1848. He is widely regarded as the founder of modern Egypt and of the Muhammad Ali dynasty, whose descendants ruled Egypt until 1952.

Early life and rise to power

Born in Kavala in Ottoman Macedonia to an Albanian family from Korçë, Muhammad Ali arrived in Egypt in 1801 as second-in-command of an Albanian contingent sent to expel the French after Napoleon's invasion. Amid the power struggle between the Ottomans and the Mamluks, he maneuvered himself into power and was confirmed as wali by Sultan Selim III in 1805. In 1811 he destroyed the Mamluk elite at the Massacre of the Citadel in Cairo.

Reforms and campaigns

Muhammad Ali modernized Egypt's army, administration, agriculture, and industry along European lines. He introduced long-staple cotton as a cash crop, built factories, canals, and schools, and sent educational missions to Europe. His son Ibrahim Pasha led campaigns in Arabia, Greece, and Syria; the Egyptian invasion of Syria brought confrontation with the Ottoman Empire and intervention by the European powers, formalized in the Convention of London (1840), though the governorship of Egypt was made hereditary in his family.

Death and legacy

Senile in his last years, Muhammad Ali died in Alexandria in 1849 and was buried in the mosque he built at the Cairo Citadel. His reforms laid the foundations of the modern Egyptian state.
