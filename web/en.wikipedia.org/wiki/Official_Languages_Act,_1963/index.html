Official Languages Act, 1963

Enacted10 May 1963
JurisdictionIndia
StatusIn force, amended 1967

The Official Languages Act, 1963 is an Act of the Parliament of India that provides for the languages to be used for the official purposes of the Union and for the transaction of business in Parliament. It implemented the constitutional scheme under which Hindi in the Devanagari script is the official language of the Union, while English was to continue for a transitional period.

Background

Article 343 of the Constitution of India provided that English would continue to be used for official purposes for fifteen years from 1950 — that is, until 26 January 1965 — after which Hindi would take over. Strong opposition in non-Hindi-speaking states, especially Tamil Nadu, led Parliament to enact the 1963 Act, which allowed the continued use of English in addition to Hindi after 1965.

Provisions and the 1967 amendment

As originally passed, the Act used permissive language ("may"), which many feared would still allow English to be dropped. Following widespread anti-Hindi agitations in 1965, the Act was amended in 1967 to provide that English would continue to be used in addition to Hindi until resolutions for its discontinuance were passed by the legislatures of all the non-Hindi states and by both Houses of Parliament — in effect guaranteeing bilingualism at the Union level indefinitely. The Act also deals with the languages of High Court judgments, authoritative texts of laws, and the Official Language Commission.
