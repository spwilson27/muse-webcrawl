Hans Axel von Fersen

Count Hans Axel von Fersen
Born: 4 September 1755, Stockholm, Sweden
Died: 20 June 1810, Stockholm
Occupation: Soldier, courtier, Marshal of the Realm
Known for: Relationship with Marie Antoinette; the Flight to Varennes

Hans Axel von Fersen (4 September 1755 – 20 June 1810) was a Swedish count, Marshal of the Realm of Sweden, a soldier, and a courtier. He is famous for his close relationship with Queen Marie Antoinette of France and for organising the royal family's ill-fated Flight to Varennes in June 1791 during the French Revolution.

Life

The son of the statesman Fredrik Axel von Fersen, he travelled widely in Europe, served as aide-de-camp to the comte de Rochambeau in the American Revolutionary War, and became a favourite at the French court. During the Revolution he acted as an intermediary for the royal family and helped plan their escape, driving the coach himself for the first leg.

Death

Back in Sweden, Fersen became Marshal of the Realm. After the sudden death of Crown Prince Christian August in 1810, rumours falsely accused Fersen of involvement; on 20 June 1810, during the prince's funeral procession in Stockholm, he was beaten and stomped to death by a mob in what is known as the Fersen murder (the "June Days" lynching).
