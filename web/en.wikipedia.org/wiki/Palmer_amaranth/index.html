Palmer amaranth

Palmer amaranth
Scientific nameAmaranthus palmeri
FamilyAmaranthaceae
Native rangeSouthwestern US and Mexico
StatusMajor agricultural weed

Palmer amaranth (Amaranthus palmeri) is an annual flowering plant in the amaranth family, native to the Sonoran Desert region and southwestern United States and northern Mexico. It is named for botanist Edward Palmer. A fast-growing dioecious species — separate male and female plants — a single female can produce several hundred thousand small seeds.

As a weed

Palmer amaranth has become one of the most damaging weeds in American cotton, soybean, and corn production. It grows rapidly (up to several centimetres a day), competes aggressively for water and light, and has evolved resistance to multiple herbicide modes of action, most consequentially glyphosate resistance documented in Georgia in the mid-2000s and since spread widely. Populations resistant to five or more herbicide groups have been reported.

Control

Management relies on integrated strategies: cover crops, tillage, residual herbicides, crop rotation, hand weeding, and harvest weed-seed control. Its edible leaves and seeds were traditionally used by some Native American peoples, though the plant accumulates nitrates and is toxic to livestock in quantity.
