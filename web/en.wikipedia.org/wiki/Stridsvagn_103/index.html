Stridsvagn 103


Type: Main battle tank
Place of origin: Sweden
In service: 1967–1997
Main armament: 105 mm L/62 rifled gun, fixed in hull

The Stridsvagn 103 (Strv 103), also known as the S-Tank, is a Swedish main battle tank developed in the late 1950s and early 1960s. It was unique in being turretless, with its main gun fixed in the hull and aimed by adjusting the hull itself, and it was the first production tank to use a gas turbine engine.

Design

The gun, a licence-built 105 mm L7 variant, was fixed and elevated by raising or lowering the hull on its hydropneumatic suspension, while traverse was achieved by turning the whole vehicle on its tracks. This allowed an autoloader, a crew of three, an extremely low profile, and operation even by a single crewman, since commander and driver/gunner both had driving and firing controls. A diesel engine provided normal propulsion with a Boeing gas turbine for extra power. A dozer blade was fitted, and the tank could swim with a flotation screen.

Service

Built by Bofors, 290 vehicles served with the Swedish Army from 1967 until 1997 in A, B, and C versions. The design reflected Swedish defensive doctrine — ambush from prepared positions — but the turretless layout, inability to fire on the move, and vulnerability of its aiming method to suspension damage meant it was never exported, and Sweden eventually replaced it with the Leopard 2 (Strv 121/122).
