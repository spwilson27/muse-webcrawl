Bimal Gurung (born 17 July 1964) is an Indian politician from the Darjeeling hills of West Bengal and the founder of the Gorkha Janmukti Morcha (GJM), a political party that revived the demand for a separate state of Gorkhaland for the Nepali-speaking Gorkha population of northern West Bengal. One of the most influential hill politicians of his generation, he rose as a close aide of Subhash Ghisingh before breaking away to eclipse him.

Early political career

Gurung began his career in the Gorkha National Liberation Front (GNLF) of Subhash Ghisingh, the party that had led the violent Gorkhaland agitation of 1986–88 and subsequently ran the Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council. Gurung served as a councillor and became Ghisingh's close associate before the two fell out over Ghisingh's acceptance of Sixth Schedule tribal status arrangements for the hills, which Gurung argued betrayed the statehood demand.

Gorkha Janmukti Morcha

In October 2007 Gurung founded the Gorkha Janmukti Morcha and rapidly displaced the GNLF through mass mobilization, cultural campaigns — including promotion of traditional Nepali dress — and repeated bandhs (shutdowns) in the hills. The movement's pressure, combined with the 2011 change of government in West Bengal, led to the tripartite agreement creating the Gorkhaland Territorial Administration (GTA) in 2011–12, with Gurung as its chief executive. He later resigned from the GTA in 2013, charging the state government with obstructing its functioning.

2017 agitation and exile

In mid-2017, protests over the perceived imposition of the Bengali language in hill schools escalated under Gurung's leadership into a renewed statehood agitation and a 104-day general strike that shut down the Darjeeling hills, accompanied by violence and a heavy security response. Facing a series of criminal cases, including under anti-terror legislation, Gurung went underground and remained in hiding for over three years, while Binoy Tamang led a rival GJM faction that cooperated with the state government.

Return

Gurung resurfaced publicly in Kolkata in October 2020, announcing his support for Mamata Banerjee's Trinamool Congress ahead of the 2021 West Bengal assembly elections and withdrawing the GJM's earlier support for the BJP. He subsequently resumed political activity in the hills, contesting for influence within the divided Gorkhaland movement.
