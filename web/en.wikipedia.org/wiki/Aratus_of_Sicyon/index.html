Aratus of Sicyon

Aratus of Sicyon
Born:271 BC, Sicyon
Died:213 BC, Aegium
Occupation:Statesman, general (strategos) of the Achaean League

Aratus of Sicyon (Ancient Greek: Ἄρατος ὁ Σικυώνιος; 271–213 BC) was a politician and military commander of Hellenistic Greece. He was elected strategos of the Achaean League seventeen times and led it for a generation, transforming it from a minor regional federation into a major power in the Peloponnese. Though Plutarch praised his political skill, Aratus was better as a statesman than a battlefield general, preferring diplomacy and surprise attacks by night.

Early life

Aratus was the son of Cleinias of Sicyon. When Aratus was seven years old, his father was murdered during a coup by Abantidas, and the boy was smuggled out of the city and raised in exile at Argos. In 251 BC, aged twenty, he led a band of exiles in a daring night escalade of Sicyon's walls, overthrew the tyrant Nicocles, and restored freedom to the city, which he then brought into the Achaean League.

Career

In 243 BC Aratus seized the Acrocorinth, the fortress commanding Corinth, by another surprise night attack, a feat that made his reputation throughout Greece and brought Corinth, Megara, Troezen, and Epidaurus into the League. He campaigned repeatedly against Macedonian influence in Greece, but, threatened by the social-revolutionary kings of Sparta — first Agis IV and then Cleomenes III — he controversially invited Antigonus III Doson of Macedon into the Peloponnese, leading to the Battle of Sellasia in 222 BC, in which Cleomenes was crushed.

Death and legacy

Aratus died in 213 BC at Aegium; ancient sources report, perhaps apocryphally, that Philip V of Macedon, whose conduct Aratus had criticized, had him slowly poisoned. His memoirs, which ran to at least thirty books, were a principal source for Plutarch's Life of Aratus and for Polybius. Sicyon honored him with hero cult and annual sacrifices.
