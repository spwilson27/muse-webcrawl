Casualty evacuation

Casualty evacuation, often shortened to CasEvac, is the movement of casualties from a battlefield or incident site to medical care, using whatever transport is available — ground vehicles, aircraft, or boats — as distinct from dedicated medical evacuation (MedEvac) platforms staffed and equipped for en-route care.

Practice

Speed is prioritised within the "golden hour" concept: point-of-injury first aid and rapid transport to surgical care sharply improve survival. Modern doctrine uses categories of urgency and the nine-line CasEvac request format standardised in NATO forces to report location, casualties and hazards.

History

Organised evacuation dates to Larrey's "flying ambulances" in the Napoleonic Wars; helicopters transformed the practice in Korea and Vietnam. See Medical evacuation and Triage.
