Capricorn (astrology)

Capricorn (♑︎; Greek: Αιγόκερως, Aigókerōs, Latin for "horned goat") is the tenth astrological sign in the zodiac, originating from the constellation of Capricornus, the horned goat or sea-goat. It spans the 270th to 300th degrees of celestial longitude. Under the tropical zodiac, the Sun transits this sign from about December 22 to January 19; under the sidereal zodiac, roughly from January 15 to February 12. Individuals born during these periods may be called Capricorns.

Background

Capricorn is one of the four cardinal signs, associated with the beginning of a season — in the Northern Hemisphere it begins at the winter solstice, when the Sun reaches its southernmost point, marked by the Tropic of Capricorn. It is an earth sign, along with Taurus and Virgo, and is ruled by the planet Saturn in traditional astrology. The sign is symbolised by the sea-goat, a mythical creature with the front half of a goat and the tail of a fish, a figure derived from Babylonian imagery of the god Ea and associated in Greek myth with the god Pan, who transformed to escape the monster Typhon.

Associations

In astrological tradition, Capricorn is associated with ambition, discipline, patience, practicality, and mastery over the material world; its colour associations include dark browns, greys, and black, and its body part is the knees and skeletal system. As with all astrological characterisations, these associations belong to the astrological tradition and are not supported by empirical evidence.

Mythology

The constellation Capricornus was one of the 48 listed by Ptolemy and is among the faintest zodiacal constellations. In Babylonian astronomy it was the "Sea-Goat" (SUḪUR.MAŠ), linked to Ea, god of waters and wisdom. In Greek tradition the figure was sometimes identified with Amalthea, the goat that suckled Zeus, whose horn became the cornucopia.
