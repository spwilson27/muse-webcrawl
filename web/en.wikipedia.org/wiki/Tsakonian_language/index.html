Tsakonian language

Tsakonian
Native to:Greece (Tsakonia, eastern Peloponnese)
Speakers:~300 native speakers (severely endangered)
Family:Indo-European → Hellenic → Greek → Doric
Descended from:Laconian Doric Greek

Tsakonian (Greek: Τσακωνικά) is a highly distinctive variety of Greek spoken in the region of Tsakonia on the eastern coast of the Peloponnese in Greece. It is notable among modern Greek varieties because it descends not from the Hellenistic Koine, like Standard Modern Greek, but from ancient Doric, specifically the Laconian dialect spoken in ancient Sparta. Tsakonian is therefore often described as the modern Greek variety most divergent from the standard language, and the two are not mutually intelligible.

Characteristics

Tsakonian preserves features of Doric lost elsewhere, such as the long vowel /aː/ where other Greek has /i/ (for example Tsakonian maté "mother" corresponding to forms preserving Doric mātēr), and archaic vocabulary including words traced to ancient Laconian. It has also developed innovations of its own, including characteristic consonant changes and the analytic use of particles. Dialects include Northern Tsakonian (around Kastanitsa and Sitaina), Southern Tsakonian (around Leonidio and Tyros), and the now extinct Propontis Tsakonian once spoken on the Sea of Marmara.

Status

Tsakonian is classified by UNESCO as severely endangered. The number of fluent native speakers has fallen to a few hundred, mostly elderly, though local cultural associations, publications, and limited teaching support revival efforts. Its survival is of great interest to historical linguists studying the continuity of Greek dialects from antiquity.
