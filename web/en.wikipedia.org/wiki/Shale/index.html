Shale

Shale
TypeSedimentary rock
CompositionClay minerals, quartz silt, organic matter
TextureFine-grained, fissile (clastic)

Shale is a fine-grained, clastic sedimentary rock formed from mud that is a mix of flakes of clay minerals and tiny fragments (silt-sized particles) of other minerals, especially quartz and calcite. Shale is characterized by its tendency to split into thin layers (laminae) less than one centimeter in thickness. This property is called fissility. Shale is the most common sedimentary rock.

The term shale is sometimes applied more broadly, as essentially a synonym for mudrock, rather than in the more narrow sense of clay-rich fissile mudrock.

Shale outcrop showing characteristic fissility

Texture and composition

Shale typically exhibits varying degrees of fissility. Because of the parallel orientation of clay mineral flakes, shale has a tendency to split into thin layers parallel to the bedding plane surface. Shale usually has a high clay mineral content and may contain variable amounts of silt-sized quartz grains, chert, feldspar, and carbonates.

Colour

Shales are typically grey in colour but can range through black, brown, green, and red depending on composition. Black shales contain significant amounts of organic matter (often more than 1% by weight) and formed in oxygen-poor environments. Red and purple shales owe their colour to hematite coatings; green shales contain chlorite or mica.

Formation

Shale forms in environments where mud accumulates in quiet water — deep marine basins, lagoons, floodplains, and lake bottoms. Compaction and burial expel water and lithify the mud into rock. Over geological time, deeply buried organic-rich shale may generate petroleum and natural gas, which is why shale is the most common source rock for hydrocarbons.

Economic importance

Shale is the source rock for most conventional oil and gas, and since the early 2000s, shale gas and tight oil have been produced directly from shale formations by hydraulic fracturing and horizontal drilling. Shale is also used in the manufacture of brick, tile, and cement, and certain oil shales can be retorted to yield synthetic crude oil.
