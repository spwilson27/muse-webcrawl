Paramilitary

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A paramilitary is a military that is not part of a country's official or legitimate armed forces. The term derives from the Greek prefix para- ("beside") and the Latin militaris ("of soldiers"). Paramilitary organizations are organized with a military structure — ranks, uniforms, and training — but are distinct from the regular armed forces, and may be state-controlled forces, private organizations, or non-state armed groups.

Types

State paramilitary forces

Many states maintain paramilitary forces that supplement the military and police. Examples include gendarmeries such as the French National Gendarmerie and Italian Carabinieri, border forces such as India's Border Security Force and the Pakistan Rangers, coast guards, and internal security forces such as China's People's Armed Police and Russia's National Guard (Rosgvardiya). Such forces may be responsible for border security, counter-insurgency, riot control, and rural policing.

Non-state and auxiliary forces

The term is also applied to armed wings of political movements and insurgent groups, to militias, and to loyalist and republican paramilitary organizations in Northern Ireland, such as the Provisional IRA, the Ulster Volunteer Force, and the Ulster Defence Association. Private military companies and volunteer auxiliaries are sometimes described as paramilitary as well.

Legal status

Under international humanitarian law, the status of paramilitary members varies: forces incorporated into or operating alongside the armed forces of a party to a conflict may be lawful combatants, whereas members of non-state armed groups are generally targetable as participants in hostilities but do not enjoy combatant immunity. The ambiguity of paramilitary status is a recurring issue in modern conflicts, including cases of state deniability for actions by affiliated groups.
