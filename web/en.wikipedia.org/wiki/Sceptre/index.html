Sceptre

A sceptre (or scepter in American English) is a staff or wand held in the hand by a ruling monarch as an item of royal or imperial insignia, symbolising authority. Figuratively, it has come to mean royal authority itself.

A sceptre as an emblem of sovereign power.

Antiquity

The sceptre is among the oldest symbols of power. In ancient Egypt, sceptres such as the was and the heka (crook) were associated with gods and pharaohs. In Greek tradition the sceptre was borne by kings and by speakers in assemblies, a custom echoed in Homer's Iliad, where the sceptre of Agamemnon is described as an heirloom made by Hephaestus. Roman consuls and emperors also carried sceptres, often topped with an eagle.

Medieval and modern use

In medieval Europe the sceptre became a standard item of regalia, typically a gold rod surmounted by a cross, orb, dove or fleur-de-lis. The British Crown Jewels include the Sceptre with the Cross and the Sceptre with the Dove; the former was remodelled in 1910 to hold the Cullinan I diamond, the largest clear-cut diamond in the world. Sceptres remain part of coronation ceremonies and state openings of parliament in several monarchies.

Ecclesiastical use

Bishops and abbots carry a crosier, a pastoral staff related in form and symbolism; Eastern Orthodox bishops may carry a staff topped with a cross between serpents or doves.
