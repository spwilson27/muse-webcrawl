Guerrilla marketing

Guerrilla marketing is an advertising strategy that uses unconventional, low-cost tactics to promote a product or brand through surprise, spectacle and direct public engagement. The term was popularised by Jay Conrad Levinson's 1984 book Guerrilla Marketing.

Characteristics

Guerrilla campaigns rely on creativity and timing rather than large media budgets. Common forms include ambient advertising in public spaces, flash mobs, street art, viral stunts and "ambush marketing" around major events. The goal is a memorable, personal interaction that prompts word-of-mouth and media coverage.

Criticism

The approach can backfire when stunts are mistaken for threats or vandalism, and some methods raise legal issues around unauthorised use of public space and event branding.
