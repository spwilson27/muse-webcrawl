The Bernese Mountain Dog (German: Berner Sennenhund) is a large working breed of dog from the canton of Bern in Switzerland. It is one of four breeds of Sennenhund (Swiss mountain dogs), distinguished by its long, silky tricolour coat of jet black, clear white, and rich rust markings. Traditionally a farm dog, it was used to drive cattle, guard property, and pull carts of milk and produce to market, earning the nickname "cheese dog" country.

History

The breed was developed in the Dürrbach region south of Bern, where it was known as the Dürrbächler. By 1900 the farm dogs were declining; fancier Franz Schertenleib and professor Albert Heim championed them, a breed club was founded in 1907, and the Swiss Kennel Club recognised the breed (renamed Berner Sennenhund) in 1912. The breed was exported to the United States in the 1920s and recognised by the American Kennel Club in 1937.

Characteristics and health

Bernese Mountain Dogs are calm, good-natured, affectionate, and eager to please, though they mature slowly and require socialisation and moderate exercise. They are notably prone to certain health problems, especially histiocytic sarcoma and other cancers, hip and elbow dysplasia, and gastric torsion; their lifespan, commonly around seven to ten years, is shorter than that of most breeds of comparable size. They excel at drafting (carting), obedience, herding, and therapy work.
