Halton sequence

In statistics, the Halton sequence is a sequence used to generate points in a multidimensional space for numerical methods such as Monte Carlo simulations. It was introduced by John Halton in 1960 and is an example of a low-discrepancy sequence, also called a quasirandom sequence, because its points fill space more uniformly than uncorrelated random points.

Construction

The Halton sequence is built from the van der Corput sequences in different bases. For each dimension, a coprime base is chosen—usually the successive prime numbers 2, 3, 5, 7, and so on. To generate the one-dimensional van der Corput sequence in base b, an integer n is written in base b, and the digits are reflected about the radix point to give a number between 0 and 1. The n-th point of the multidimensional Halton sequence is the vector of the van der Corput values in each base.

For example, in base 2 the first values are 1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/8, 5/8, 3/8, 7/8, …, obtained by reversing the binary digits of 1, 10, 11, 100, … after the binary point.

Properties and applications

Halton sequences have low discrepancy in low dimensions and are widely used in quasi-Monte Carlo integration, computer graphics, and simulation. In high dimensions, however, sequences built from large prime bases can show strong correlations and striations, so improved variants such as scrambled Halton sequences and the leaped Halton sequence are often used instead. The related Faure and Sobol sequences offer alternatives with better high-dimensional behaviour.
