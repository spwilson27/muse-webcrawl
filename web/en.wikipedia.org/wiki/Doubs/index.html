Doubs (French: [du]; Arpitan: Dubs) is a department in the Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region in eastern France, named after the Doubs River. Its prefecture is Besançon, a historic watchmaking centre, and other important towns include Montbéliard and Pontarlier.


The loop of the Doubs at Besançon.

Geography

The department lies along the French-Swiss border and is dominated by the Jura Mountains, with forests, pastures and the Doubs river carving deep gorges such as the Gorges du Pont-de-Roide. It has a continental climate with snowy winters, and is known for cross-country skiing at Métabief and other Jura resorts.

Economy and culture

Doubs was historically central to French watchmaking (Besançon and Morteau), and the Montbéliard area hosted Peugeot's automotive industry. Gastronomic specialities include Comté and Morbier cheeses, Morteau sausage, and Pontarlier anise spirits. Victor Hugo was born in Besançon in 1802.
