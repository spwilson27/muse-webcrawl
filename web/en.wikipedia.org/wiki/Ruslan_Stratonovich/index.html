Ruslan Leont'evich Stratonovich (Russian: Руслан Леонтьевич Стратонович; 31 May 1930 – 13 January 1997) was a Soviet and Russian physicist, one of the founders of the theory of stochastic differential equations.

Life and career

Stratonovich studied physics at Moscow State University, where he spent his entire career, becoming a professor in the physics faculty. Working on problems of noise in radio-physics and signal detection, he developed mathematical tools for describing systems driven by random fluctuations.

Contributions

Stratonovich introduced a calculus for stochastic integrals — now called the Stratonovich integral — which, unlike the alternative Itô integral, obeys the ordinary rules of calculus and is the natural formulation when noise arises as the limit of smooth, coloured processes. His interpretation is standard throughout the physical sciences, where his stochastic differential equations model phenomena from Brownian motion to fluctuating circuits. He also made fundamental contributions to the theory of optimal non-linear filtering (independently of, and in parallel with, work leading to the Kalman–Bucy filter), to the statistical theory of information, and to what is now called stochastic thermodynamics: his 1950s work on the value of information anticipated later theories of optimal invention and control. His monographs, including Topics in the Theory of Random Noise, introduced many Western scientists to the Soviet school of random-process theory.
