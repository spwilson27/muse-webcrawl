The Periodic Table (book)

The Periodic Table (Italian: Il sistema periodico) is a 1975 book by the Italian chemist and writer Primo Levi. It consists of 21 short stories, each named after a chemical element, mixing memoir, fiction and essay around Levi's life as a chemist in Fascist and post-war Italy.

AuthorPrimo Levi
LanguageItalian
Published1975 (Einaudi)
GenreShort stories, memoir

Contents

Chapters such as "Argon", "Zinc", "Iron" and "Carbon" trace Levi's ancestry, education under the racial laws, work in a mine and a paint factory, his arrest and survival of Auschwitz (in "Cerium", where he stole cerium rods to trade for bread), and his post-war career and retirement.

Recognition

In 2006 the Royal Institution named it the best science book ever written in a celebrated poll. It is regarded as a masterpiece of both literature and science writing.
