La Maison Rose

La Maison Rose ("The Pink House") is a small pink-painted house and restaurant at 2 Rue de l'Abreuvoir, on the corner of Rue des Saules, in the Montmartre district of the 18th arrondissement of Paris, France. It is one of the most photographed buildings in Montmartre and a landmark of the neighbourhood's artistic heritage.

History

The house dates from the mid-19th century, when Montmartre was still a semi-rural village of vineyards and windmills. In the early 20th century it was acquired by Germaine Gargallo, a friend and former model of Picasso, who with her husband, the Catalan painter Ramon Pichot, turned it into a small café and inn frequented by the artists of Montmartre. It was she who had the house painted pink. The painter Maurice Utrillo, who lived nearby, depicted the house in several of his works, helping fix its image in the popular imagination.

The restaurant

La Maison Rose has operated as a restaurant for much of its life, serving traditional French cuisine on a terrace beneath the vineyards of the Clos Montmartre across the street. After periods of closure and changes of ownership, the restaurant was taken over and renovated by descendants of the Pichot family and continues to trade under the historic name.

In popular culture

The house appears in numerous paintings and photographs of Montmartre and featured prominently in the Netflix series Emily in Paris, which brought renewed tourist attention to the site.
