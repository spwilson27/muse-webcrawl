Speaker of the House of Commons (United Kingdom)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Speaker of the House of Commons is the presiding officer of the lower house of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. Elected by MPs at the start of each parliament (by secret ballot since 2001), the Speaker chairs debates, maintains order, selects amendments, calls members to speak, certifies money bills under the Parliament Act, and represents the Commons to the Crown and the Lords. On election the Speaker renounces party affiliation and sits above politics; by convention the major parties do not contest the Speaker's seat at general elections, and the Speaker does not vote except to break a tie (by the casting-vote conventions associated with Speaker Denison: to preserve the status quo and further debate).

The office's long arc runs from medieval spokesmen executed for their messages to the Crown, through the seventeenth-century Speakers caught between king and Commons, to the modern impartial chair (the convention of impartiality hardened in the nineteenth century). Recent Speakers made the office newly salient: Betty Boothroyd (1992–2000, the first woman Speaker), John Bercow (2009–19), whose procedural rulings during the Brexit crisis made him a protagonist in the politics he umpired, and his successor Lindsay Hoyle (from 2019). The Speaker also heads the House of Commons Commission and holds ceremonial precedence second only to the Lord Chancellor among commoners.
