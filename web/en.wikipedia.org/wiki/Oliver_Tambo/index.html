Oliver Tambo

Oliver Reginald Kaizana Tambo (27 October 1917 – 24 April 1993) was a South African anti-apartheid politician and activist. A central figure in the African National Congress (ANC), he was its president from 1977 to 1991 and led the movement in exile during the imprisonment of Nelson Mandela and many other leaders.

Early life

Born in Nkantolo in the Eastern Cape, Tambo studied at the University of Fort Hare, where he met Mandela. After being expelled for participating in a student strike, he taught science and mathematics, then studied law and in 1952 co-founded Mandela & Tambo, the first Black-owned law firm in South Africa. He was a founding member of the ANC Youth League (1944) and served as the ANC's secretary-general and deputy president.

Exile

After the Sharpeville massacre and the banning of the ANC in 1960, Tambo left South Africa to build international support and direct the external mission of the liberation movement, building alliances and lobbying for sanctions against the apartheid regime. He authorised the turn to armed struggle through Umkhonto we Sizwe and maintained the ANC's cohesion through three decades of exile.

Return and death

Tambo returned to South Africa in 1990 after the ANC was unbanned, and handed the presidency to Mandela at the ANC's 1991 conference, becoming national chairperson. He died of complications from a stroke in Johannesburg in April 1993, a year before South Africa's first democratic election. Johannesburg's international airport is named O. R. Tambo International Airport in his honour.
