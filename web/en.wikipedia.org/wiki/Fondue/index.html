Fondue

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A cheese fondue pot with bread cubes and forks]

Fondue is a Swiss dish of melted Cheese in a communal pot (caquelon) over a small flame, eaten by dipping bread cubes on long forks; the name comes from French fondre, to melt. The classic mixture blends Gruyère and Emmental (or Vacheron) with white wine, garlic, a little kirsch and cornstarch, seasoned with nutmeg and pepper.

Promoted by the Swiss cheese industry in the 20th century ("fondue creates good spirits" was its slogan), fondue became Switzerland's national dish and a byword for convivial winter eating. Variants include fondue chinoise (meat in broth), bourguignonne (meat in oil), and chocolate fondue (an American invention of the 1960s).

1. Etiquette

Etiquette

Stir in a figure of eight; the crusty layer at the bottom (la religieuse) is a delicacy. Customs charge a forfeit — a song, a round of drinks — to whoever loses their bread in the pot.
