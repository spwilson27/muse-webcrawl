Trail hunting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Trail hunting is an equestrian field sport in which hounds follow an artificially laid scent trail, usually of an animal-based scent (often fox urine or a synthetic substitute), rather than pursuing a live quarry. It was developed in England and Wales after the Hunting Act 2004 banned the hunting of wild mammals with dogs, as a way for hunts to continue operating lawfully while preserving the structures, employment and rural traditions of mounted hunting.

Practice

Before or during the meet, a trail layer — on foot, horseback, bicycle or quad bike — drags a scent-impregnated rag along a route across country. The huntsman then casts the hounds across the line of the trail, and the pack follows it, with the mounted field riding behind. A day's trail hunting normally involves several separate trails. Unlike drag hunting, in which the line is laid to be straightforward and fast and the route is known to the huntsman, trail hunting aims to mimic live hunting: the trail is laid in loops and across varied ground, and neither the huntsman nor the followers know its exact course in advance.

Legal status and controversy

Trail hunting is lawful in England and Wales, provided no wild mammal is intentionally hunted. Critics, including the League Against Cruel Sports and some wildlife campaigners, allege that trail hunting is used as a cover ("a smokescreen") for illegal hunting, since hounds following an animal-based scent may pick up the scent of a live fox, and prosecutions of hunts since 2004 have relied on evidence gathered by hunt monitors and saboteurs. Hunts and the Countryside Alliance deny this and maintain that trail hunting is a legal, distinct activity.

The controversy intensified in 2020 when leaked webinars from the Hunting Office, in which trail hunting was discussed alongside the phrase "smokescreen", led to the suspension of licences for trail hunting on land owned by the National Trust, Forestry England, United Utilities and some other major landowners, and to the conviction of a Hunting Office director for encouraging offences. In 2021 National Trust members voted to ban trail hunting on Trust land. The Labour Party has proposed banning trail hunting, and consultations on strengthening the Hunting Act have been held in England, Wales and Scotland. Scotland's Hunting with Dogs (Scotland) Act 2023 separately tightened the law there.

Related activities

Related lawful alternatives to banned hunting include drag hunting, bloodhounding ("hunting the clean boot"), in which hounds follow the natural scent of a human runner, and exempt hunting under the schedules to the Hunting Act, such as the use of dogs to flush a wild mammal to be shot.
