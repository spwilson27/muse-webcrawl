Block and tackle

A block and tackle is a system of two or more pulleys (blocks) with a rope or cable threaded between them, used to lift or pull heavy loads with a mechanical advantage. The system trades distance for force: the load can be lifted with a fraction of its weight in effort, but the rope must be pulled through proportionally further.

ComponentsTwo or more blocks (pulleys), rope or cable

UsesSailing rigging, cranes, hoists, theatre rigging, rescue systems

Mechanics

In an ideal (frictionless) system, the mechanical advantage equals the number of rope parts supporting the moving block and its load. Blocks are rigged "to advantage" when the hauling part is pulled in the direction of the load's movement and "to disadvantage" otherwise. Named configurations include the gun tackle (advantage 3), luff tackle (3 or 4) and double tackle (4 or 5). In practice, friction in the sheaves reduces the advantage, sometimes substantially with many pulleys.

History and use

Pulley systems were known in the ancient world — Archimedes was credited with using compound pulleys to move a ship — and they became essential in sailing, where tackles raise sails and yards, tension rigging and handle cargo. Block and tackle systems remain in use in construction, vehicle recovery, sailing, and mountain and cave rescue, where compact pulley systems create large mechanical advantages from portable equipment.
