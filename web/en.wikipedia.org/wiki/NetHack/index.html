NetHack

NetHack is a single-player roguelike video game originally released in 1987 with continued development by the NetHack DevTeam. The player character descends through a procedurally generated dungeon to retrieve the Amulet of Yendor from its deepest level and offer it to their deity to achieve demigod status — "ascension". NetHack is renowned for the depth and interactivity of its systems, summarised by the developer motto "The DevTeam Thinks of Everything".

Gameplay

The player chooses a role (such as wizard, valkyrie, tourist, or archaeologist), a race, alignment, and gender, and explores dozens of dungeon levels filled with monsters, traps, shops, altars, and treasure. Characters are traditionally displayed in ASCII graphics, though tilesets exist. Death is permanent ("permadeath"): when a character dies, the game ends and the save file is deleted. Success requires managing food, identifying magical items by experimentation, and exploiting the game's rich simulation — nearly every object can interact with every other.

History

NetHack descends from Hack (1982), itself inspired by Rogue (1980). Mike Stephenson coordinated the first NetHack release in 1987, and a shifting group of volunteer developers — the DevTeam — has maintained it since. Major versions include 3.4.3 (2003), long the stable standard, and 3.6.x (from 2015), which modernised the codebase. Numerous variants, such as SLASH'EM and UnNetHack, extend the game.

Culture

The community developed rituals and terminology such as "YASD" (Yet Another Stupid Death) and "ascension streaks" on public servers, where players can watch others play and find the bones files of previous characters. The annual /dev/null tournament and Junethack celebrate the game. NetHack has been cited as one of the greatest video games of all time and influenced countless roguelikes and dungeon-crawlers.

Reception

Critics praise NetHack's emergent complexity and replayability; despite (or because of) its austere interface, the game remains actively played decades after release, and winning is considered a significant achievement for players.
