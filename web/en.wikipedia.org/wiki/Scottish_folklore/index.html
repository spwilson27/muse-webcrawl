Scottish folklore

Scottish folklore refers to the folklore of the people of Scotland, a body of oral tradition encompassing myths, legends, ballads, charms and folk tales passed down through Gaelic, Scots and Norse-influenced cultures. It is notable for its rich bestiary of supernatural creatures, its belief in fairies and second sight, and its strong regional variation between the Highlands, Islands, Lowlands and Borders.

Creatures and beings

Well-known beings include the kelpie, a shape-shifting water spirit said to haunt rivers and lochs; the selkie, a seal-folk creature able to shed its skin on land; the brownie, a helpful household spirit; and the bean-nighe, a washerwoman whose appearance at a ford was an omen of death. The Shetland and Orkney islands contributed the trow and the Nuckelavee, while the Highlands preserved tales of the cat sìth and the wulver. The Loch Ness Monster, popularised in the twentieth century, draws on older traditions of water beasts in Highland lochs.

Traditions and festivals

Seasonal festivals such as Samhain, Beltane, Hogmanay and Up Helly Aa retain elements of older folk practice. First-footing at New Year, the burning of clavie barrels, and fire festivals across the country are among its best-known customs. Highland games, ceilidh dancing and Gaelic psalm singing preserve other strands of traditional culture.
