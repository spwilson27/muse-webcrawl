Melanocortin 5 receptor

MC5R (melanocortin 5 receptor)

Gene: MC5R, chromosome 18p11.21 (human)

Family: G protein-coupled receptors

Ligands: α-MSH, ACTH

The melanocortin 5 receptor (MC5R) is a G protein-coupled receptor for melanocortin peptides, most potently α-MSH. It is the most widely distributed of the melanocortin receptors, found in many peripheral tissues including exocrine glands, skin, adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, and immune cells.

Function

MC5R is best known for regulating exocrine gland secretion. Mice lacking the receptor show defective sebaceous, lacrimal (tear), and Harderian gland function: they produce less sebum, have reduced water repulsion of the coat, and show impaired tear production. The receptor also contributes to immune and inflammatory modulation, to thermoregulation, and to lipid mobilisation in adipocytes.

Distribution and significance

Unlike MC1R (pigmentation), MC2R (adrenal), and MC3R/MC4R (energy balance), no common human disease has been attributed to MC5R mutation, and it remains the least studied member of the family. Its involvement in sebum production makes it of interest in acne and dry-eye research, and MC5R signalling has been investigated in retinal and immune-cell (B and T lymphocyte) function.
