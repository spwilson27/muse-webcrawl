Crab Nebula

The Crab Nebula (catalogue designations M1, NGC 1952) is a supernova remnant and pulsar wind nebula in the constellation Taurus. It is the remnant of a supernova observed by Chinese and other astronomers in 1054 AD, which was visible in daylight for weeks. At its centre lies the Crab Pulsar, a neutron star rotating about 30 times per second.

History

English astronomer John Bevis recorded the nebula in 1731; Charles Messier rediscovered it in 1758, making it the first entry in his catalogue. Lord Rosse's drawings in the 1840s suggested a crab-like shape, giving the nebula its name. In the 20th century its expansion was traced back to the 1054 "guest star," and in 1968 the pulsar was detected, confirming the nebula is powered by a rotating neutron star.

Properties

The nebula, roughly 11 light-years across, expands at about 1,500 km/s. Its glow combines synchrotron radiation from electrons spiralling in the pulsar's magnetic field with emission from ionised filaments of gas. It is a bright source across the spectrum, from radio waves to gamma rays, and long served as a standard calibration source in X-ray astronomy.
