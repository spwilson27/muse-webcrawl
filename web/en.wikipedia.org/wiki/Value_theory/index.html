Value theory

Value theory, also called axiology, is the branch of philosophy that studies the nature of value and valuation. It encompasses questions about what things are good or bad, valuable or worthwhile, and how such judgements can be justified — spanning ethics, aesthetics, and other domains of worth.

Key distinctions

A central distinction is between intrinsic value (valued for its own sake, as an end) and instrumental value (valued as a means to something else). Philosophers also distinguish types of value — moral, aesthetic, epistemic, prudential — and debate monism (a single fundamental value, as in classical utilitarianism's happiness) versus pluralism (many incommensurable values, associated with Isaiah Berlin).

History

The systematic study of value emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries through thinkers such as Franz Brentano, G. E. Moore — whose Principia Ethica (1903) argued that "good" is a simple, indefinable property — and the Austrian "value school". Value theory remains closely connected to metaethics, decision theory and welfare economics.
