Trachurus

Trachurus is a genus of marine fish in the jack family, Carangidae, commonly known as jack mackerels and horse mackerels. The genus contains around 15–20 recognised species found in temperate and tropical waters of the Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian Oceans.

Characteristics

Species of Trachurus are streamlined, schooling pelagic fish with a distinctive row of bony scutes along the lateral line, a forked tail, and two dorsal fins. They feed on small crustaceans, fish larvae, and juvenile fish, and are themselves important prey for larger predators, seabirds, and marine mammals.

Species and fisheries

Well-known species include the Atlantic horse mackerel (T. trachurus), Mediterranean horse mackerel (T. mediterraneus), Japanese jack mackerel (T. japonicus), Chilean jack mackerel (T. murphyi), and Cape horse mackerel (T. capensis). Several support major commercial fisheries for human consumption, fishmeal, and bait; the Chilean jack mackerel fishery in the South Pacific was historically one of the world's largest single-species fisheries and has been the subject of international management efforts after severe depletion.
