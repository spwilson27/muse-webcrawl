Eusebio Kino

Eusebio Francisco Kino (10 August 1645 – 15 March 1711), born Eusebio Chini, was a Tyrolean Jesuit missionary, explorer, cartographer, geographer, and astronomer active in what is now northwestern Mexico and the southwestern United States. He is best known for founding a chain of missions in the Pimería Alta region of the Sonoran Desert, spanning present-day Sonora and Arizona.

Life and expeditions

Born near Trento, Kino joined the Society of Jesus in 1665 and studied mathematics and astronomy. Sent to New Spain, he arrived in 1681 and first served in Baja California before beginning work on the mainland frontier in 1687. Over the next 24 years he founded some twenty missions, including Mission San Xavier del Bac near present-day Tucson, introduced European livestock and crops, and travelled extensively among the Tohono O'odham and neighbouring peoples.

Through a series of overland expeditions between 1698 and 1702, Kino demonstrated that Baja California was a peninsula, not an island, overturning a long-standing cartographic error; his map of the region, published in 1705, remained authoritative for decades. He died at Magdalena, Sonora, in 1711, where his remains, rediscovered in 1966, are displayed.

Legacy

Kino is honoured with statues in the United States Capitol's National Statuary Hall (representing Arizona) and in Mexico; the town of Bahía Kino and numerous schools and streets bear his name. His cause for canonisation in the Catholic Church has advanced, and he was declared Venerable in 2020.
