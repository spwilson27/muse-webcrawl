The Pine Ridge Indian Reservation (Lakota: Wazí Aháŋhaŋ Oyáŋke), also called the Pine Ridge Agency, is an Oglala Lakota Indian reservation located in the U.S. state of South Dakota, with a small portion extending into Nebraska. It is one of the largest reservations in the United States, encompassing all of Oglala Lakota County and parts of Jackson and Bennett counties in South Dakota, and the site of the former White Clay district in Sheridan County, Nebraska.

History

The reservation was established as part of the Great Sioux Reservation under the Fort Laramie Treaty of 1868. Following the discovery of gold in the Black Hills and the Great Sioux War of 1876, the United States seized the Black Hills in 1877, and in 1889 the Great Sioux Reservation was broken up into smaller reservations; Pine Ridge was allotted to the Oglala. The Wounded Knee Massacre of 29 December 1890, in which U.S. cavalry killed some 250–300 Lakota, many of them followers of the Ghost Dance, took place on the reservation near Wounded Knee Creek; it is often regarded as the end of the Indian Wars.

In 1973 the reservation was the site of the Wounded Knee Occupation, a 71-day standoff between members of the American Indian Movement and the Oglala Sioux Civil Rights Organization on one side and federal law-enforcement agencies on the other, which drew national attention to treaty rights and reservation conditions. The following years were marked by conflict under tribal chairman Dick Wilson, including the 1975 Pine Ridge shootout in which two FBI agents and one Native activist were killed; AIM member Leonard Peltier was convicted of the agents' murders.

Geography

The reservation includes part of the South Dakota Badlands; a portion lies within Badlands National Park's Stronghold Unit, co-managed with the tribe. The largest community is Pine Ridge, the tribal capital. Other communities include Oglala, Kyle, Manderson, Porcupine, Allen, Martin (adjacent), Batesland and Wounded Knee.

Demographics and conditions

Pine Ridge is frequently cited as one of the poorest places in the United States: Oglala Lakota County has ranked among the lowest counties in per-capita income, unemployment has been estimated at very high levels, and life expectancy has been reported as among the lowest in the Western Hemisphere. Residents face shortages of housing, high rates of diabetes and alcoholism, and related health problems. Alcohol sales have long been prohibited on the reservation, a policy reaffirmed in referendums, while the border town of Whiteclay, Nebraska, was long a controversial source of beer sales until its liquor stores were closed in 2017.

Government and education

The Oglala Sioux Tribe is governed by an elected tribal council and president under a constitution adopted in the Indian Reorganization Act era. Red Cloud Indian School (now Mahpiya Luta) and Little Wound School serve the community, and Oglala Lakota College, headquartered in Kyle, is one of the oldest tribal colleges.
