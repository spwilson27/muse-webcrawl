We'll Meet Again

"We'll Meet Again" is a 1939 British song written by Ross Parker and Hughie Charles. It became famous through Vera Lynn's recording and is one of the best-known songs of the Second World War era, expressing hopeful parting between servicemen and their loved ones.

History

Vera Lynn recorded the song in 1939 with Arthur Young on Novachord, and it resonated deeply with British audiences during the war. Lynn performed it for troops around the world, and it became closely associated with her image as the "Forces' Sweetheart." The song regained prominence near the end of her life: in 2020, a duet version with Katherine Jenkins reached the charts amid the COVID-19 pandemic, and Queen Elizabeth II alluded to its title in a televised address that April.

Cultural references

The song is used ironically over the closing nuclear-apocalypse montage of Stanley Kubrick's Dr. Strangelove (1964). It has been covered by many artists, including Perry Como, and appeared in numerous films, television programmes and commemorations of the war.
