Formal semantics of programming languages

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The formal semantics of programming languages is the study of the rigorous mathematical study of the meaning of programming languages and models of computation. Whereas the syntax of a language specifies the form of its programs, its semantics specifies what those programs do. Formal semantics provides the foundation for program verification, compiler correctness, language standardisation and reasoning about program equivalence.

Approaches

Operational semantics defines the meaning of a program by describing how it executes, typically as transitions of an abstract machine or through inference rules over program configurations. It comes in small-step (structural) and big-step (natural semantics) varieties.

Denotational semantics, developed by Christopher Strachey and Dana Scott, assigns to each program a mathematical object (its denotation), usually a function between domains, compositionally: the meaning of a compound phrase is built from the meanings of its parts.

Axiomatic semantics describes programs through logical assertions — preconditions and postconditions — as in Hoare logic, and underpins deductive program verification. Other approaches include action semantics, game semantics and categorical semantics.

History

The field grew from attempts in the 1960s to define languages such as ALGOL 60 and ALGOL 68 rigorously; the Vienna Definition Language and the work of Peter Landin, John McCarthy, Robert Floyd and Tony Hoare were formative. Semantics now plays a central role in verified compilation (as in CompCert) and in the formal specification of real languages.
