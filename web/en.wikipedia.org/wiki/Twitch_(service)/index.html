Twitch is an American video live-streaming service that focuses on video game live streaming, including broadcasts of esports competitions, in addition to offering music broadcasts, creative content, and "in real life" streams. Twitch is operated by Twitch Interactive, a subsidiary of Amazon. It was introduced in June 2011 as a spin-off of the general-interest streaming platform Justin.tv.

Content on the site can be viewed either live or via video on demand. The games shown on Twitch's current homepage vary, but popular titles have included League of Legends, Fortnite, Grand Theft Auto V, Minecraft and Counter-Strike. Individual streamers build audiences through live chat interaction, emotes and channel points, and monetise through subscriptions, donations and advertising.

History

When Justin.tv was launched in 2007 by Justin Kan and Emmett Shear, the site was divided into several content categories. The gaming category grew especially fast. In June 2011, the company decided to spin off the gaming content as TwitchTV. The public beta launched on June 6, 2011.

Twitch's popularity soared; by 2013 it was the largest live-streaming platform for games. In August 2014, Amazon acquired Twitch Interactive for approximately US$970 million in cash. The platform became deeply integrated with Amazon's services, including Prime Gaming benefits for subscribers.

Platform and culture

Twitch streams are organised into channels, each with a live chat notorious for its own slang, copypastas and emotes. The site runs Partner and Affiliate programs for creators, and its Partner Program gives streamers a share of subscription and advertising revenue. "Twitch Plays Pokémon", a 2014 social experiment in which thousands of viewers collectively controlled a game, became a landmark of the platform's culture. Annual conventions were held under the name TwitchCon.

Content moderation controversies

Twitch has faced recurring controversies over copyright — notably muting recorded audio under the DMCA in 2020 — inconsistent enforcement of its community guidelines, "hot tub" and gambling streams, and the revenue split with creators, which was changed in 2022–2023 amid criticism and competition from YouTube and Kick.
