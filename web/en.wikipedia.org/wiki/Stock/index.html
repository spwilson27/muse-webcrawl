Stocks (also capital stock, or simply equity) consist of all the shares into which ownership of a corporation or company is divided. A single share of the stock means fractional ownership of the corporation in proportion to the total number of shares. This typically entitles the shareholder (stockholder) to that fraction of the company's earnings, proceeds from liquidation of assets (after discharge of all senior claims such as secured and unsecured debt), and voting power, often divided by the number of shares owned.

Types of stock

Common stock usually carries voting rights and a claim on dividends, which are variable and not guaranteed. Preferred stock generally does not carry voting rights but has a higher claim on assets and earnings: dividends are paid to preferred shareholders before common shareholders, and preferred holders have priority if the company is liquidated. Companies may also issue multiple classes of shares with different rights, such as the dual-class structures used by many technology firms.

History

The Dutch East India Company, founded in 1602, is widely credited with issuing the first modern shares of stock traded on a formal exchange, the Amsterdam Stock Exchange. Shares allowed the spreading of risk among many investors and enabled large-scale commercial ventures.

Stock markets

Stocks of publicly traded companies are bought and sold on stock exchanges such as the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq. Prices are set by supply and demand and are tracked by stock market indices. Investors may seek returns through capital gains and dividends, while markets are subject to periodic bubbles and crashes.
