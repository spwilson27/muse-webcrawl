Imidazole

The imidazole ring, a five-membered aromatic heterocycle

Imidazole (ImH) is an organic compound with the formula C3N2H4. It is a white or colourless solid that is soluble in water, producing a mildly alkaline solution. In chemistry, it is an aromatic heterocycle, classified as a diazole, and has non-adjacent nitrogen atoms in meta-substitution.

Many natural products, especially alkaloids, contain the imidazole ring. These imidazole derivatives share a 1,3-C3N2 ring system but feature varied substituents. This ring system is present in important biological building blocks, such as histidine and the related hormone histamine. Many drugs contain an imidazole ring, including certain antifungal drugs, the nitroimidazole series of antibiotics, and the sedative midazolam.

Structure and properties

Imidazole is a planar 5-membered ring with 3 carbons and 2 nitrogens. It exists in two equivalent tautomeric forms because the hydrogen atom can be located on either of the two nitrogen atoms. Imidazole is a highly polar compound, as evidenced by its electric dipole moment of 3.67 D. It is highly soluble in water. The compound is classified as aromatic due to the presence of a sextet of π-electrons, consisting of a lone pair from the protonated nitrogen atom and one from each of the remaining four atoms of the ring.

Preparation

Imidazole was first reported in 1858 by the German chemist Heinrich Debus, although various imidazole derivatives had been discovered as early as the 1840s. It was synthesized from glyoxal and ammonia. The Debus synthesis uses glyoxal, formaldehyde, and ammonia to form imidazole. Industrial methods largely involve the reaction of glyoxal or other 1,2-dicarbonyl compounds with aldehydes and ammonia.

Applications

Imidazole is used as an intermediate in the manufacture of pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, dyes, and photographic chemicals. Derivatives such as ketoconazole and clotrimazole are widely used as antifungal agents, while imidazolium salts serve as precursors to N-heterocyclic carbene catalysts and ionic liquids.
