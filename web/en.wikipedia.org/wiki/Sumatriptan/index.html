Sumatriptan

Trade namesImitrex, Imigran, others
ClassTriptan (serotonin 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist)
RoutesOral, subcutaneous, intranasal
Legal statusPrescription-only in most countries
Bioavailability~15% (oral); 96% (subcutaneous)
Elimination half-life~2.5 hours

Sumatriptan, sold under the brand names Imitrex and Imigran among others, is a medication used to treat migraine headaches and cluster headaches. It was the first of the triptan class of drugs, developed by Glaxo and introduced in 1991, and it revolutionised the acute treatment of migraine. Sumatriptan is not a painkiller and does not prevent attacks; it is taken at the onset of an attack to abort it. It is available as tablets, a nasal spray, and a subcutaneous injection, the injection being the fastest-acting formulation and the standard acute therapy for cluster headache.

Medical uses

Sumatriptan is effective for the acute treatment of migraine attacks, with or without aura, relieving headache pain and associated nausea and sensitivity to light and sound within about two hours in a majority of patients, although headache recurs within 24 hours in roughly a third. Subcutaneous sumatriptan is also a first-line treatment for acute cluster headache attacks, often combined with high-flow oxygen.

Mechanism of action

Sumatriptan is a selective agonist at serotonin 5-HT1B and 5-HT1D receptors. Activation of 5-HT1B receptors constricts the dilated cranial blood vessels associated with migraine, while 5-HT1D activation on trigeminal nerve endings inhibits the release of vasoactive neuropeptides such as CGRP and substance P, dampening neurogenic inflammation and the transmission of pain signals in the trigeminovascular system.

Side effects and contraindications

Common side effects include tingling, warmth or flushing, dizziness, fatigue, and a characteristic sensation of tightness or pressure in the chest and throat ("triptan sensations"). Because it constricts blood vessels, sumatriptan is contraindicated in people with coronary artery disease, uncontrolled hypertension, previous stroke, or peripheral vascular disease, and it must not be combined with ergotamine or, within two weeks, monoamine oxidase inhibitors. Overuse (on 10 or more days per month) can cause medication-overuse headache.

History

Sumatriptan grew out of Glaxo's research in the 1980s on selective serotonin receptor agonists, led by Patrick Humphrey and colleagues, and launched in 1991. Its success established the triptan class — followed by zolmitriptan, rizatriptan, naratriptan and others — and it became one of the best-selling migraine drugs in the world before losing patent protection in 2009.
