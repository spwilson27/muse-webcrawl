Katoomba, New South Wales

Katoomba is a town in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, Australia, and the administrative centre of the City of Blue Mountains. Situated about 100 kilometres west of Sydney at an elevation of roughly 1,017 metres, it is the principal tourist hub of the Greater Blue Mountains World Heritage Area, famous for the Three Sisters rock formation and the dramatic Jamison Valley.

History

The area is the traditional land of the Gundungurra and Darug peoples; the name Katoomba derives from an Aboriginal word often interpreted as "shining falling water", referring to the falls below the escarpment. Coal and oil shale were mined in the valley from the 1870s, served by the remarkable Katoomba Scenic Railway — originally a mining incline and now among the steepest passenger railways in the world. With the railway from Sydney, Katoomba boomed as a cool-climate resort in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, crowned by grand guesthouses and the Hydro Majestic and Carrington hotels.

Attractions

Echo Point Lookout offers the classic view of the Three Sisters — Meehni, Wimlah and Gunnedoo in Gundungurra tradition — lit by changing light over the valley. The Scenic World complex operates the Scenic Railway, Skyway and Cableway. Katoomba Falls, the Giant Stairway walking track to the valley floor, and the art-deco main street with its cafés, bookshops and antique stores draw visitors year-round. The town has a cool mountain climate with occasional winter snow.
