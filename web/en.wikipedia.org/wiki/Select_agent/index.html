Select agent

Select agents are biological agents and toxins that have been determined by the United States Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) or the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) to have the potential to pose a severe threat to public health and safety, to animal or plant health, or to animal or plant products. The agents are regulated under the Federal Select Agent Program, administered jointly by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS).

Regulation

Entities that possess, use, or transfer select agents or toxins in the United States must register with the Federal Select Agent Program and comply with detailed requirements covering security, biosafety, personnel reliability, record-keeping, and incident reporting. The legal basis includes the Public Health Security and Bioterrorism Preparedness and Response Act of 2002 and regulations at 42 CFR Part 73, 7 CFR Part 331, and 9 CFR Part 121.

The lists

The HHS select agents and toxins list includes viruses such as Ebola virus and Marburg virus, bacteria such as Bacillus anthracis (which causes anthrax) and Yersinia pestis (plague), and toxins such as botulinum neurotoxins and ricin. The USDA lists cover plant and animal pathogens, and some agents appear on both lists as "overlap" agents. Australia Group lists of biological agents serve a related export-control purpose internationally.
