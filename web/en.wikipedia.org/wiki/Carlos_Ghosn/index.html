Carlos Ghosn (Arabic: كارلوس غصن; born 9 March 1954) is a Lebanese-French-Brazilian businessman who led the Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance, one of the world's largest automotive groups. Celebrated for two decades as the turnaround artist who saved Nissan from bankruptcy, he was arrested in Japan in November 2018 on charges of financial misconduct, and in December 2019 fled to Lebanon while awaiting trial—an escape that made him an international fugitive.

Career

Born in Porto Velho, Brazil, to Lebanese parents, Ghosn was educated in Lebanon and at the École Polytechnique and École des Mines in Paris. He joined Michelin in 1978 and rose to run its North American operations before moving to Renault in 1996, where his cost-cutting earned him the nickname "Le Cost Killer." In 1999 Renault took a stake in the near-bankrupt Nissan, and Ghosn's Nissan Revival Plan—factory closures, job cuts, and the breaking of keiretsu supplier ties—returned the company to profit ahead of schedule, making him a celebrity in Japan, even the hero of a manga comic series. He became CEO of Nissan in 2001 and of Renault in 2005, later adding the chairmanship of Mitsubishi Motors in 2016.

Arrest and escape

On 19 November 2018 Ghosn was arrested at Tokyo's Haneda Airport on allegations of under-reporting his compensation and misusing company assets. He was repeatedly rearrested and held in detention, then released on bail under strict conditions. On 30 December 2019, while under house arrest in Tokyo, he was smuggled out of Japan—by most accounts hidden in a large equipment case aboard a private jet via Osaka and Istanbul—and surfaced in Beirut. Lebanon has no extradition treaty with Japan, and Ghosn, who maintains his innocence and calls himself the victim of a "hostage justice" system and a corporate coup, has remained there since. Interpol has issued a Red Notice for him, and France has also issued arrest warrants in connection with investigations into his conduct at Renault.
