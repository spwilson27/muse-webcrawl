Brangus

Brangus
Country of origin: United States
Cross: Brahman × Angus
Traits: Polled, black or red coat
Use: Beef production
A Brangus bull

The Brangus is an American breed of beef cattle developed from crosses between the Brahman and the Angus. Breeding began in the 1930s, including work at the United States Department of Agriculture experiment station at Jeanerette, Louisiana, with the aim of combining the Brahman's tolerance of heat and resistance to disease and insects with the Angus's superior carcass quality, fertility and mothering ability. The breed association, now the International Brangus Breeders Association, was founded in 1949.

Characteristics

To be registered, a Brangus must be 3/8 Brahman and 5/8 Angus. The cattle are solid black — a related red variety, Red Brangus, is registered separately — and are naturally polled. They show a slight hump and loose skin inherited from the Brahman, medium to large frame, and are noted for hardiness, good disposition and performance in hot climates. Brangus cattle are now raised in the southern United States and in Australia, South Africa and Latin America.
