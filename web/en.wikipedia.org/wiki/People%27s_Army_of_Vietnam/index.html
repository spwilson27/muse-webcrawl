People's Army of Vietnam

People's Army of Vietnam

Founded: 22 December 1944

Country: Vietnam

Headquarters: Hanoi

Active personnel: about 482,000

Branches: Ground Force, Navy, Air Force, Border Guard, Coast Guard

The People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN; Vietnamese: Quân đội nhân dân Việt Nam) is the military force of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam and the armed wing of the ruling Communist Party of Vietnam. It comprises the Vietnam People's Ground Force, the Vietnam People's Navy, the Vietnam People's Air Force, the Border Guard and the Coast Guard.

History

The PAVN traces its founding to 22 December 1944, when Võ Nguyên Giáp established the Armed Propaganda Unit for National Liberation with 34 soldiers in Cao Bằng Province. It grew into the main-force army that defeated the French at Điện Biên Phủ in 1954, ending the First Indochina War. During the Vietnam War it fought alongside the Viet Cong against South Vietnam and the United States, launching campaigns such as the Tet Offensive (1968) and the final Ho Chi Minh Campaign that captured Saigon in April 1975, reunifying the country.

Later conflicts

The PAVN invaded Cambodia in 1978, overthrowing the Khmer Rouge, and fought the brief Sino-Vietnamese War against China in 1979. Today it focuses on territorial defence, including in the South China Sea, and contributes to UN peacekeeping missions.

Organisation

The army is controlled by the Central Military Commission of the Communist Party and the Ministry of National Defence. Military service is compulsory for men, and Vietnam maintains one of the largest standing armies in Southeast Asia.
