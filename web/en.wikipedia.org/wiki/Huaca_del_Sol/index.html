Huaca del Sol

Huaca del Sol is an adobe brick pyramid built by the Moche culture (100–800 CE) on the northern coast of modern-day Peru. Together with the nearby Huaca de la Luna, it formed the ceremonial and administrative centre known as Huacas de Moche, near present-day Trujillo. It is one of the largest pre-Columbian adobe structures in the Americas.

Construction

The pyramid was built in stages from an estimated 130 million mud bricks, many stamped with makers' marks suggesting levies of labour from subject communities. The stepped structure originally rose about 40 metres. Spanish colonial looters diverted the Moche River in the seventeenth century to wash away part of the pyramid in search of gold, destroying much of it.

Function

The Huaca del Sol is thought to have served administrative and possibly royal residential functions, while the Huaca de la Luna opposite it held the principal temples and elite burials. The complex remains an important archaeological site and symbol of Moche civilisation.
