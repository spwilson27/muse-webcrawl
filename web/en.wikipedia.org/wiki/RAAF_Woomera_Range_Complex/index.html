RAAF Woomera Range Complex

The RAAF Woomera Range Complex (WRC) is a major Australian military and civil aerospace facility and operation located in South Australia, approximately 450 km (280 mi) north-west of Adelaide. The WRC is operated by the Royal Australian Air Force (RAAF) and is the largest land-based test range in the western world, covering some 122,000 km².

The range supports the testing of military systems including rockets, missiles, unmanned aerial vehicles, and aircraft weapons systems, as well as scientific and commercial space launches. It includes the Woomera Test Range, RAAF Base Woomera, and the village of Woomera, which supports range operations.

History

The range was established in 1947 as a joint project between the United Kingdom and Australia (the Joint Project) for the testing of long-range weapons systems following the Second World War. It hosted rocket tests including the Black Knight and Blue Streak programmes, and the launch of Australia's first satellite, WRESAT, in 1967. In the 21st century the range has supported hypersonics research (such as the HIFiRE programme), trials of the F-35 Joint Strike Fighter, and the return of the Japanese Hayabusa asteroid sample capsules, which landed within the range in 2010 and 2020.
