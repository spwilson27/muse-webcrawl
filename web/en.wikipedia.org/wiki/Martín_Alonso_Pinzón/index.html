Martín Alonso Pinzón

Martín Alonso Pinzón
Born: c. 1441, Palos de la Frontera, Spain
Died: c. 31 March 1493, Palos de la Frontera, Spain
Occupation: Mariner, explorer
Known for: Captain of the Pinta on Columbus's first voyage

Martín Alonso Pinzón (c. 1441 – c. 31 March 1493) was a Spanish mariner, shipbuilder, and explorer from Palos de la Frontera, Huelva. He was the captain of the Pinta, one of the three ships of Christopher Columbus's first voyage to the Americas in 1492–1493, and the eldest of the Pinzón brothers.

Role in the first voyage

A wealthy and respected shipowner, Pinzón's support was crucial in recruiting crews in Palos for Columbus's expedition. He co-commanded the fleet, captaining the Pinta, while his brother Vicente Yáñez Pinzón captained the Niña. On 6 October 1492 Pinzón suggested a change of course to the southwest, a decision credited with leading the fleet to its landfall in the Bahamas on 12 October; a lookout aboard the Pinta, Rodrigo de Triana, made the first sighting of land.

Separation and return

In November 1492 Pinzón separated from Columbus and explored independently, seeking gold and a route to Asia, before rejoining the fleet in January 1493. He returned to Spain separately, arriving at Bayona in Galicia shortly before Columbus reached Palos. Pinzón died within weeks of his return, reportedly of an illness contracted on the voyage (often identified as syphilis, though this is disputed).

Legacy

Historians continue to debate the relative contributions of Columbus and Pinzón to the success of the 1492 voyage; in Palos he is honoured as a co-discoverer of America.
