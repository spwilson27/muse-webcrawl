Inclisiran, sold under the brand name Leqvio, is a medication used to lower low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol in adults with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia or established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. It was the first small interfering RNA (siRNA) therapy approved for cholesterol lowering.

Mechanism

Inclisiran works by RNA interference: the synthetic double-stranded siRNA, conjugated to a sugar molecule (GalNAc) that targets liver cells, directs the destruction of the messenger RNA encoding PCSK9, a protein that promotes degradation of LDL receptors. With less PCSK9, more LDL receptors remain on the surface of liver cells to clear cholesterol from the blood.

Use and approval

The drug is given by subcutaneous injection, administered by a health care professional, with a distinctive twice-yearly maintenance schedule after initial doses at day 0 and three months. In the ORION-10 and ORION-11 trials, inclisiran reduced LDL cholesterol by roughly 50 percent compared with placebo. It was approved in the European Union in December 2020 and by the US Food and Drug Administration in December 2021. The most common adverse effects are mild injection-site reactions.
