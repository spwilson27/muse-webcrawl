Sharefarming

Sharefarming is an arrangement in agriculture in which a landowner and a working farmer agree to share the costs of running a farm and the returns from it, rather than the farmer paying a fixed rent. It is common in dairy farming in New Zealand and Australia, where it is known as sharemilking.

Arrangements

In a typical sharemilking contract, the sharefarmer supplies labour and often the herd and machinery, while the farm owner supplies the land and fixed infrastructure. Milk income is split in negotiated proportions; in New Zealand the "50/50" sharemilking agreement, in which the sharemilker owns the herd, is a longstanding pathway for farm workers to build equity and eventually buy land.

Related systems

Sharefarming differs from sharecropping, historically associated with the post-Civil War American South, in which tenants farmed an owner's land for a share of the crop and often fell into debt. Modern sharefarming is usually a formal business contract between independent parties.
