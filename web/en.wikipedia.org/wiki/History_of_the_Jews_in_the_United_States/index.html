History of the Jews in the United States

The history of the Jews in the United States begins in 1654, when 23 Sephardic Jews fleeing Portuguese Brazil arrived in New Amsterdam, founding the community that became New York's Shearith Israel.

Immigration waves

German Jews arrived in large numbers in the mid-19th century, founding Reform Judaism in America and firms such as Goldman Sachs. Between 1881 and 1924, some two million Eastern European Jews fled pogroms and poverty, settling in cities such as New York, where many worked in the garment industry.

Twentieth century

The Immigration Act of 1924 sharply restricted entry. American Jews faced quotas and antisemitism but integrated rapidly after the Second World War, moving to suburbs and excelling in academia, law, medicine, and entertainment. Holocaust survivors and, later, Soviet Jews added new waves.

Contemporary community

The United States is home to one of the world's two largest Jewish populations, broadly divided among Reform, Conservative, Orthodox, and unaffiliated movements, with ongoing debates over identity, intermarriage, and Israel. See also American Jews.
