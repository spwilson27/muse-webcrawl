Hydroelectric power in Norway

Hydroelectric power in Norway supplies the overwhelming majority of the country's electricity — typically around 88–92% of generation — one of the highest shares of any country in the world. Norway's mountainous terrain, high precipitation, and numerous natural lakes used as reservoirs give it about half of Europe's total reservoir storage capacity.

Overview

Norway has more than 1,700 hydropower plants with a combined installed capacity on the order of 33–34 GW and average annual production around 140 TWh, varying with precipitation. Most production is publicly owned: the state-owned company Statkraft is the largest producer, alongside municipal and county utilities. Large stations include Kvilldal (1,240 MW), part of the Ulla-Førre complex, and Sima. Historical landmark schemes include the Rjukan plants of Norsk Hydro, once used for fertiliser production and heavy-water manufacture at Vemork.

Grid and trade

Norway is interconnected with Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands (NorNed), Germany (NordLink), and the United Kingdom (North Sea Link, opened 2021). Its reservoirs act as a flexible "green battery" for north-western Europe, balancing variable wind power. High electricity exports during the 2021–22 European energy crisis, and the resulting domestic price rises in southern Norway, sparked political debate about export limits and interconnector policy.
