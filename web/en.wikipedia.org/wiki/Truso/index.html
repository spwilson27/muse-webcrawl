Truso

Truso
Location:Near modern Elbląg, Poland
Period:8th–11th centuries
Cultures:Old Prussian, Scandinavian (Viking)
Key source:Wulfstan of Hedeby's voyage account (c. 890)

Truso was a Viking Age port and trading town (emporium) on the south-eastern shore of the Baltic Sea, near the mouth of the Vistula, on or near Lake Drużno in present-day northern Poland, close to the modern city of Elbląg. It was a major centre of Baltic trade between roughly the 8th and 11th centuries, connecting Scandinavian, Slavic, Prussian and more distant trading networks, including routes towards the Black Sea and the Islamic world.

Wulfstan's account

Truso is best known from the account of the seafarer Wulfstan of Hedeby, who around 890 sailed from Hedeby in Denmark to Truso in seven days. His report, preserved in the Old English translation of Orosius's history, describes the lands of the Eastseaxons, the Slavs, and the Ests (Prussians), and the funeral customs of the local population. Wulfstan's Truso is one of the earliest named trading places on the Polish Baltic coast.

Archaeology

The site of Truso was long sought and was identified with excavations at Janów Pomorski near Elbląg beginning in the 1980s. Archaeologists uncovered a large settlement of perhaps 5–20 hectares with craft workshops producing amber, bronze, horn and glass goods, weights and Arabic dirham coins attesting to long-distance exchange, and evidence of both Scandinavian and Old Prussian inhabitants. The emporium declined in the 11th century, possibly after a fire and as river channels silted and political centres shifted; its role was later partly succeeded by Gdańsk and by Elbląg, founded by the Teutonic Order in 1237.
