Vega C, or Vega Consolidation, is a European expendable small-lift launch vehicle developed by the Italian company Avio for the European Space Agency. It is an evolution of the original Vega rocket with larger first and second stages and a wider fairing, increasing payload performance by roughly 60% at comparable launch cost.

Design

Vega C has four stages. The first two are solid-propellant motors: the P120C — shared with the Ariane 6 boosters — and the Zefiro 40. The third stage is the solid Zefiro 9, and the upper stage, AVUM+, is a liquid-propellant module that can reignite to place multiple payloads in different orbits. The rocket stands about 35 metres tall and can deliver around 2,300 kg to a 700 km sun-synchronous orbit.

Operational history

Vega C's maiden flight on 13 July 2022 successfully carried the Italian LARES 2 satellite and six CubeSats. Its second flight, in December 2022, failed because of a defective nozzle insert in the Zefiro 40 motor, destroying two Pléiades Neo satellites and grounding the vehicle for two years. After redesign of the nozzle, Vega C returned to flight on 5 December 2024, successfully launching the Copernicus Sentinel-1C radar satellite.
