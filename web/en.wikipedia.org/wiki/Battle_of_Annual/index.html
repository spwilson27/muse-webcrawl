Battle of Annual

The Battle of Annual was a battle fought in Spanish Morocco between the Spanish Army and Rifian Berber irregulars on 22 July 1921 at Annual, in northeastern Morocco — remembered in Spain as the Disaster of Annual (Desastre de Annual). The Spanish suffered one of the worst defeats of a European colonial army by indigenous forces, losing roughly 13,000 soldiers, and the disaster had profound political consequences in Spain.

Background

Spain's high commissioner in Morocco, General Fernández Silvestre, advanced rapidly from Melilla into the Rif in early 1921, establishing a chain of weak, poorly supplied outposts (blocaos) culminating at Annual. The Riffian leader Abd el-Krim, a former qadi and journalist, united the tribes against the incursion. After Riffian forces overran the outpost of Abarrán (1 June) and besieged Igueriben, Silvestre ordered a general retreat from Annual on 22 July.

The disaster

The withdrawal collapsed into a rout. Spanish units disintegrated under attack, Silvestre died at Annual (probably by suicide), and retreating columns were massacred along the road to Monte Arruit, where some 3,000 survivors surrendered on 9 August and most were killed. Total Spanish and allied losses are commonly estimated between 8,000 and 13,000 dead. Abd el-Krim used the victory and vast captured weaponry to proclaim the Republic of the Rif.

Consequences

The scandal of the Picasso file investigation into responsibilities shook the Spanish monarchy and parliamentary system; the crisis contributed to the 1923 coup of Primo de Rivera. Spain eventually subdued the Rif only after the Alhucemas landing (1925) in cooperation with France, ending the Rif War in 1926.
