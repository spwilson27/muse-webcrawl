Sir Michael Anthony Epstein CBE FRS (18 May 1921 – 4 February 2024) was a British pathologist and virologist. Together with Yvonne Barr and Bert Achong, he discovered the Epstein–Barr virus (EBV), the first virus shown to cause cancer in humans, in cells cultured from Burkitt lymphoma samples.

Discovery of Epstein–Barr virus

Epstein trained at St Paul's School and Trinity College, Cambridge, and at the Middlesex Hospital Medical School, where he became a lecturer in pathology. In 1961 he attended a lecture by the surgeon Denis Burkitt on the childhood lymphoma common in equatorial Africa. Using electron microscopy on lymphoma cells flown from Uganda and cultured by his research student Yvonne Barr, Epstein, Barr and Achong identified herpesvirus-like particles in 1964 — the first evidence of a virus associated with a human cancer. EBV was later shown to cause infectious mononucleosis (glandular fever) and to be implicated in several cancers, including Burkitt lymphoma, Hodgkin lymphoma and nasopharyngeal carcinoma.

Career and honours

From 1968 to 1985 Epstein was professor of pathology at the University of Bristol. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1979, appointed CBE in 1985 and knighted in 1991. He received the Paul Ehrlich and Ludwig Darmstaedter Prize in 1973 (shared) and numerous other honours. Epstein died in February 2024 at the age of 102.
