The Talpiot program (Hebrew: תוכנית תלפיות) is an elite training program of the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) for recruits who have demonstrated outstanding academic ability in science. Founded in 1979, it recruits a small cohort — typically the top fraction of a percent of Israeli high-school graduates — and trains them as military research and development leaders rather than combat officers.

History

The program was proposed by Professors Felix Dothan and Shaul Yatziv of the Hebrew University after the Yom Kippur War, which exposed technological and doctrinal gaps. The idea was to give a select group of gifted young scientists a broad education in physics and mathematics, combined with exposure to the IDF's branches and needs, so that they could later lead interdisciplinary R&D projects. The first class began in 1979.

Training and service

Cadets complete an accelerated bachelor's degree in physics and mathematics (with additional computer science) at the Hebrew University, alongside military training including a parachuting course and rotations through IDF units. They then serve about six years in R&D positions in bodies such as MAFAT (the Directorate of Defense Research and Development), the Air Force, and intelligence units. The name "Talpiot" is biblical, from the Song of Songs, meaning a tower built in courses of stone.

Alumni and impact

Although small — around a thousand graduates over its history — the program's alumni have had an outsized influence on Israel's defence industries and high-tech sector, founding or leading numerous technology companies after their service. Talpiot is frequently cited as a model of elite technical education and is among the most competitive programs in Israel, with applicants undergoing extensive testing in mathematics, physics and leadership.
