A biomolecule or biological molecule is a molecule that is present in living organisms, including large macromolecules such as proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, and nucleic acids, as well as small molecules such as metabolites, vitamins, and hormones. Biomolecules are composed primarily of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus, and sulfur.

Classes of biomolecules

Carbohydrates are composed of monosaccharides such as glucose and serve as energy sources and structural materials. Lipids are hydrophobic molecules including fats, phospholipids, and steroids that form membranes and store energy. Proteins, built from amino acids, perform most of the cell's work as enzymes, structural elements, and signaling molecules. Nucleic acids—DNA and RNA—store and transmit genetic information using sequences of nucleotides.

Small molecules

Many biomolecules are small organic compounds involved in metabolism. Primary metabolites are directly involved in growth and reproduction, while secondary metabolites—such as alkaloids and terpenes in plants—often serve ecological roles like defense against herbivores. Other important small biomolecules include neurotransmitters, pigments, and cofactors.

Structure and study

The structure of biomolecules ranges from primary sequence to the folded three-dimensional conformations of macromolecules. Techniques such as X-ray crystallography, NMR spectroscopy, and cryo-electron microscopy are used to determine these structures, while mass spectrometry and chromatography identify and quantify small molecules.
