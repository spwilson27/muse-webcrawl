Isère (French pronunciation: [izɛʁ]) is a department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of southeastern France, named after the Isère river. Its prefecture is Grenoble, a major university, research and high-technology city at the foot of the Alps.

Geography

Isère spans varied terrain: the Rhône plain around Vienne in the west, the limestone plateaus of the Dauphiné, the Chartreuse and Vercors pre-Alpine ranges (partly protected as regional natural parks), and high Alpine massifs including Belledonne and the Écrins around the resort of Alpe d'Huez, famed for its Tour de France stage finishes. Grenoble hosted the 1968 Winter Olympics; other ski resorts include Les Deux Alpes and Chamrousse.

History

The department was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790 from part of the former province of Dauphiné. The meeting of the Assembly of Vizille (1788), sometimes called the opening act of the Revolution, took place at Vizille in Isère. During the Second World War the Vercors plateau was a stronghold of the French Resistance, brutally suppressed in 1944.

Economy

The Grenoble area is a hub of microelectronics, software and scientific research (including the European Synchrotron Radiation Facility and Institut Laue–Langevin), earning it the nickname "capital of the Alps". Agriculture includes walnut production (the noix de Grenoble AOC) and the Chartreuse liqueur is distilled near Voiron.
