Sam Phillips

Born: Samuel Cornelius Phillips, 5 January 1923, Florence, Alabama, U.S.

Died: 30 July 2003 (aged 80), Memphis, Tennessee, U.S.

Occupation: Record producer, record label owner

Known for: Founder of Sun Records and Sun Studio

Samuel Cornelius Phillips (January 5, 1923 – July 30, 2003) was an American record producer and founder of Sun Records and Sun Studio in Memphis, Tennessee. He was instrumental in the development of rock and roll during the 1950s, recording landmark sessions by Elvis Presley, Johnny Cash, Carl Perkins, Jerry Lee Lewis and Roy Orbison.

Early life

Phillips grew up on a farm near Florence, Alabama, where he picked cotton alongside Black labourers and absorbed the blues and gospel music of the region. He worked as a radio announcer and engineer before moving to Memphis, where he opened the Memphis Recording Service at 706 Union Avenue in January 1950 with the slogan "We Record Anything—Anywhere—Anytime."

Sun Records

Phillips first recorded blues and rhythm-and-blues artists including B.B. King, Howlin' Wolf and Ike Turner, whose 1951 session produced "Rocket 88", often cited as the first rock and roll record. In 1952 he launched Sun Records.

In July 1954 he recorded Elvis Presley's "That's All Right", a breakthrough that helped ignite the rockabilly movement. Financial pressures led him to sell Presley's contract to RCA Victor in 1955 for $35,000, enabling Sun to promote its other stars. The "Million Dollar Quartet" session of December 1956 brought Presley, Cash, Perkins and Lewis together in the studio.

Later life and legacy

Phillips later invested in radio stations and the Holiday Inn hotel chain. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in its first class of 1986, and is widely regarded as one of the most important figures in 20th-century popular music.
