Dual-coding theory

Dual-coding theory is a theory of cognition proposed by Allan Paivio of the University of Western Ontario in 1971. It proposes that human cognition uses two distinct but interconnected systems for representing and processing information: a verbal system specialised for language, and a non-verbal (imagery) system specialised for spatial and pictorial information.

Core claims

According to the theory, information encoded in both systems — for example, a word together with a mental image — is remembered better than information encoded in only one, which helps explain the picture superiority effect and the concreteness effect in memory. The two systems contain representational units called logogens (verbal) and imagens (non-verbal), connected by referential and associative links.

Applications

The theory has influenced research and practice in education, multimedia learning, advertising and the treatment of phobias and post-traumatic stress.
