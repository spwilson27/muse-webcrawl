Reciprocal lattice

In physics, the reciprocal lattice emerges from the Fourier transform of a (direct) crystal Bravais lattice and is fundamental to X-ray crystallography and solid-state theory. It represents wavevectors of waves that can propagate in the periodic structure.

Reciprocal lattice
Field: Crystallography, solid-state physics
Related: Brillouin zone, Miller indices, Bragg diffraction
Construction: b₁ = 2π (a₂×a₃)/V, etc.

Definition

For primitive vectors a1, a2, a3, reciprocal primitive vectors are b1=2π(a2×a3)/a1·(a2×a3), cyclically, satisfying ai·bj=2πδij. The reciprocal of the reciprocal lattice is the original direct lattice.

Applications

Diffraction patterns are maps of the reciprocal lattice (Ewald sphere construction, Bragg's law): each diffraction spot corresponds to a reciprocal lattice point and a set of lattice planes with Miller indices (hkl). The first Brillouin zone is the Wigner–Seitz cell of the reciprocal lattice, defining electronic band structure.
