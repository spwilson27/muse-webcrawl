Gilbert and Marshall Islands campaign

DateNovember 1943 – February 1944
LocationGilbert and Marshall Islands, Pacific Ocean
ResultAmerican victory
BelligerentsUnited States vs Empire of Japan

The Gilbert and Marshall Islands campaign was a series of battles fought in the Pacific theater of World War II between November 1943 and February 1944. United States forces seized Japanese-held atolls in the Gilbert and Marshall island groups as part of the island-hopping drive across the Central Pacific toward Japan.

Background

After the Guadalcanal campaign, Admiral Chester W. Nimitz's Central Pacific forces began advancing through the island chains. Air bases in the Gilberts and Marshalls would support the subsequent invasion of the Marianas.

Operations

The campaign opened with the Battle of Tarawa (Betio) and the Battle of Makin in November 1943, where U.S. Marines and soldiers faced fierce resistance; Tarawa became notorious for its heavy casualties in just 76 hours. In early 1944, Operation Flintlock captured Kwajalein and Roi-Namur, followed by the seizure of Eniwetok in Operation Catchpole, aided by fast carrier task force strikes that neutralized the Japanese base at Truk.

Significance

Lessons from Tarawa about amphibious assault, naval gunfire support, and logistics shaped later operations. The captured atolls provided airfields and anchorages that supported the Mariana and Palau Islands campaign in mid-1944.
