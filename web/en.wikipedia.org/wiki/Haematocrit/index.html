Haematocrit

The haematocrit (Ht or HCT), also known as packed cell volume (PCV), is the volume percentage of red blood cells in blood. Normal values are typically about 40–54% for adult men and 36–48% for adult women.

Measurement

Haematocrit is measured by centrifuging a sample of blood in a capillary tube and measuring the proportion occupied by packed red cells, or automatically by haematology analysers as the product of red cell count and mean corpuscular volume.

Clinical significance

A low haematocrit indicates anaemia, blood loss, or overhydration. A high haematocrit occurs in polycythaemia, dehydration, chronic lung disease, and at high altitude. Haematocrit forms part of a full blood count and is used to guide transfusion and monitor treatment.

History

The term derives from Greek haima (blood) and kritēs (judge). The centrifuge method was developed in the late 19th century.
