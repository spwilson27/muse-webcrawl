Oulipo

The Oulipo (short for Ouvroir de littérature potentielle, "workshop of potential literature") is a loose gathering of French-speaking writers and mathematicians founded in Paris in 1960 by Raymond Queneau and François Le Lionnais. Its members seek to create literature using constrained writing techniques — self-imposed rules such as lipograms, palindromes and combinatorial structures — in order to generate new forms and possibilities.

Famous Oulipian works include Queneau's Cent mille milliards de poèmes (One Hundred Thousand Billion Poems), a book of ten sonnets whose interchangeable lines can be combined into 1014 poems; Georges Perec's lipogrammatic novel La Disparition (A Void), written entirely without the letter "e", and his Life: A User's Manual, structured by a knight's tour of a Paris apartment block; and Italo Calvino's combinatorial fictions.

The group describes its members as "rats who build the labyrinth from which they plan to escape." Sister groups applying constraints to other arts (Oupeinpo for painting, Oumupo for music) followed. The Oulipo remains active and influential in experimental writing worldwide.
