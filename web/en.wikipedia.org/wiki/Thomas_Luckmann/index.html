Thomas Luckmann (1927–2016) was an Austrian-American sociologist best known as the co-author, with Peter L. Berger, of The Social Construction of Reality: A Treatise in the Sociology of Knowledge (1966), one of the most influential sociology books of the twentieth century. Drawing on the phenomenological sociology of Alfred Schutz, under whom both men studied at the New School for Social Research, the book described how social reality is produced and maintained through habitualisation, institutionalisation, legitimation, and internalisation.

Work

Luckmann's own research ranged widely: The Invisible Religion (1967) argued that religion in modern societies had retreated from public institutions into private life rather than simply declining; his studies of language and communication treated communication as the fundamental process by which social worlds are built, and his later work on moral communication and on the structures of the life-world extended Schutz's phenomenology. After teaching in the United States, he returned to Europe as professor at the University of Konstanz, Germany.
