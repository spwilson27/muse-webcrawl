Self-managed superannuation fund

A self-managed superannuation fund (SMSF) is a private superannuation fund in Australia, regulated by the Australian Taxation Office (ATO), whose members act as its trustees and control its investment decisions. SMSFs are the largest sector of the Australian superannuation industry by number of funds and hold a substantial share of total superannuation assets.

Structure

An SMSF may have up to six members (increased from four in 2021). Each member must generally be a trustee of the fund, or a director of its corporate trustee. The fund must pass the sole purpose test—being maintained exclusively to provide retirement benefits to members or benefits to dependants on a member's death—and must comply with the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993 and its regulations.

Investments

Trustees may invest in listed shares, property, managed funds, term deposits and, subject to strict rules, collectables and personal-use assets. SMSFs may borrow to acquire assets only under limited-recourse borrowing arrangements. Residential property held by the fund generally cannot be acquired from, or rented to, a related party, though business real property is an exception.

Administration and compliance

Trustees bear personal responsibility for compliance, including annual audits by an approved SMSF auditor, annual returns to the ATO, record-keeping and investment-strategy documentation. Breaches can result in penalties, disqualification of trustees, or the fund being made non-complying, which attracts severe tax consequences.
