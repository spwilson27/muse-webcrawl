Magdalene College is a constituent college of the University of Cambridge. Founded in 1428 as a hostel for Benedictine monks and refounded in 1542 as the College of St Mary Magdalene, it stands on the north bank of the River Cam in Cambridge. The college name is pronounced "Maudlin".

History

The college originated as Monk's Hostel, established for monks studying at Cambridge. It was refounded by Thomas Audley, Lord Chancellor under Henry VIII, who gave it its dedication to Mary Magdalene. Magdalene was the last Cambridge college to admit women, in 1988, ending a long-standing tradition that had become a focus of protest by reformers.

Buildings and collections

The college's courts run along the river and include the Pepys Building, which houses the Pepys Library: the book collection and diaries of Samuel Pepys, an alumnus, preserved in his own bookcases. The Fellows' Garden and riverside grounds are notable features. The New Library, opened in 2021, won the RIBA Stirling Prize in 2022.

Notable alumni

Alumni include Samuel Pepys, the diarist; Charles Kingsley, author; A. C. Benson; and the former Archbishop of Canterbury Rowan Williams, who served as Master from 2013 to 2020.
