1988 Writers Guild of America strike

Dates7 March – 7 August 1988
Duration153 days (22 weeks)
PartiesWriters Guild of America vs Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers
Main issuesResiduals for hour-long television shows and foreign markets

The 1988 Writers Guild of America strike was a labour dispute between screen and television writers, represented by the Writers Guild of America (WGA), and the studios and networks represented by the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers (AMPTP). Lasting 153 days from 7 March to 7 August 1988, it was at the time the longest strike in the Guild's history, a record later surpassed by the 2023 strike.

Issues

The central dispute concerned residuals — payments to writers when their work was re-run or sold to new markets. Writers sought a larger share of revenue from the growing foreign markets and an improved residual formula for one-hour television dramas, which were disadvantaged relative to half-hour comedies under the existing calculation. The studios resisted, arguing that foreign and syndication markets were uncertain.

Effects on television

The strike delayed the start of the 1988–89 American television season by weeks, forced networks to air reruns, specials, and hastily revived old series such as Mission: Impossible, and contributed to audience drift toward cable television. Late-night programmes improvised without writers, and the strike is often cited as a turning point in the economics of American television production, strengthening independent producers who made interim deals with the Guild.

Settlement

The eventual agreement gave writers a new residual structure tied more closely to producers' actual foreign revenues, along with modest gains in other areas. Though many writers felt the settlement fell short after five months on strike, the deal established principles that shaped later negotiations in 2007–08 and 2023.
