La Bamba (song)

"La Bamba" (pronounced [la ˈβamba]) is a Mexican folk song, originally from the state of Veracruz, best known in the United States from a 1958 adaptation by Ritchie Valens, a Top 40 hit in the US charts. Valens's version is ranked number 345 on Rolling Stone magazine's list of the 500 Greatest Songs of All Time, and is the only song on the list not in English.

Ritchie Valens (1941–1959)

Origins

The song is a traditional son jarocho from Veracruz, on Mexico's Gulf coast, traditionally performed on harp, jarana, and requinto and danced as a courtship dance — couples famously tie a ribbon into a bow with their feet. Its exact age is unknown; it is often dated to the late 17th or 18th century, and the title is thought to derive from the Spanish verb bambolear, "to sway" or "to shake". The refrain — "Para bailar la Bamba / se necesita una poca de gracia" ("To dance the Bamba, you need a bit of grace") — is universally known in Mexico.

Ritchie Valens and Los Lobos versions

Valens, a Mexican-American rock and roll pioneer, recorded "La Bamba" as the B-side of "Donna" in 1958, giving the folk song an electric rock arrangement. After Valens's death in the 1959 plane crash that also killed Buddy Holly and the Big Bopper, the song remained an emblem of Chicano rock. In 1987, Los Lobos recorded the song as the title track of the Valens biopic La Bamba; their version reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 and in several other countries, and won the MTV Video Music Award for Best Video from a Film.

Legacy

"La Bamba" was inducted into the Latin Grammy Hall of Fame and the Grammy Hall of Fame, and Valens's recording was selected for the National Recording Registry of the Library of Congress. The song has been recorded in dozens of languages and remains one of the best-known Spanish-language songs worldwide.
