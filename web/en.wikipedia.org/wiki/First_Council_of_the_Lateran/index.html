First Council of the Lateran

The First Council of the Lateran was the ninth ecumenical council recognised by the Catholic Church, convoked by Pope Callixtus II in December 1122 and held in Rome from 18 March to 6 April 1123 at the Lateran Palace. It was the first ecumenical council held in the West, and its principal business was to ratify the Concordat of Worms, which had ended the Investiture Controversy between the papacy and the Holy Roman Empire, and to enact reforms of clerical discipline.

Background

The Investiture Controversy, the struggle since the 1070s over whether lay rulers could invest bishops with their offices, had been provisionally settled by the Concordat of Worms (23 September 1122) between Callixtus II and Emperor Henry V. Under the concordat the emperor renounced investiture with ring and staff and conceded free episcopal elections, while the pope allowed elections to take place in the emperor's presence and investiture with the temporal regalia by the sceptre. Callixtus summoned a general council to confirm the settlement and complete the Gregorian Reform programme.

Decrees

The council issued 22 canons. It solemnly ratified the Concordat of Worms and proclaimed the Peace and Truce of God. Its disciplinary canons condemned simony (the purchase of ecclesiastical office), forbade laymen to dispose of church property, renewed the requirement of clerical celibacy by declaring the marriages of clergy in major orders invalid, prohibited the ordination of the sons of priests to the priesthood without dispensation, restricted the charging of fees for chrism and holy oil, and condemned the theft of offerings and the fortification of churches. It also offered indulgences to those who went to the aid of Christians in Spain and the Holy Land.

Significance

The First Lateran Council marks the emergence of the medieval papal monarchy at its height: for the first time an ecumenical council was summoned and presided over by the pope in the West, and its canons were received as universal law for the Latin Church. It was followed in the same palace by the Second (1139), Third (1179), Fourth (1215) and Fifth (1512–17) Lateran councils.
