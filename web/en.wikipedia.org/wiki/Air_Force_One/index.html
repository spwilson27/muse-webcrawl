Air Force One is the official air traffic control call sign for any United States Air Force aircraft carrying the president of the United States. In popular usage it refers to the two specially configured Boeing 747-200B airliners, military designation VC-25A, with tail numbers 28000 and 29000, that serve as the presidential aircraft.

The aircraft

The VC-25As entered service in 1990. Their 4,000 square feet of interior space on three decks includes a presidential suite, office, conference and dining room, medical facility with operating table, communications centre, and quarters for staff, Secret Service, and press. The aircraft can be refuelled in flight, has a range of about 12,600 km, and carries hardened communications and self-defence systems. It is maintained by the Presidential Airlift Group of the 89th Airlift Wing at Joint Base Andrews.

History

Presidential air travel began with Franklin D. Roosevelt's flights in 1943–45. The call sign Air Force One was adopted in 1953 after a mix-up between Eastern Air Lines Flight 8610 and the presidential aircraft (Air Force 8610) over New York. Famous predecessors include the Boeing 707-based VC-137s, one of which carried John F. Kennedy — and returned his body from Dallas in 1963, with Lyndon B. Johnson sworn in aboard.

Replacement

A replacement programme based on the Boeing 747-8, designated VC-25B, has been underway since the 2010s, using two aircraft originally built for a defunct Russian airline.
