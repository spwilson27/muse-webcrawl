Singapore Changi Airport (IATA: SIN) is the main international airport serving Singapore and one of the largest transportation hubs in Asia. Built on reclaimed land at the eastern end of the island, it opened in 1981 to replace the older airport at Paya Lebar and has grown to four main passenger terminals, with a fifth planned. It is a hub for Singapore Airlines, Scoot and numerous international carriers, connecting to well over 100 cities worldwide in normal times.

Facilities and reputation

Changi is consistently ranked among the world's best airports in the Skytrax awards, famous for amenities unusual in aviation: butterfly and orchid gardens, a rooftop swimming pool, cinemas, a giant slide, and extensive art and retail. Its centrepiece since 2019 is Jewel Changi Airport, a glass-domed complex linking Terminals 1–3 that contains the world's tallest indoor waterfall, the 40-metre Rain Vortex, surrounded by a terraced indoor forest. A fourth terminal opened in 2017 on the site of the former budget terminal, and a third runway has been developed from the adjacent Changi Air Base, part of a long-term plan for a huge Terminal 5.

Operations

Before the COVID-19 pandemic the airport handled about 68 million passengers in 2019; traffic recovered strongly in the following years. The airport is operated by Changi Airport Group, corporatised in 2009 from the Civil Aviation Authority of Singapore. It is served by the Mass Rapid Transit (East West line), expressways and taxis, lying about 17 km from the city centre.

History

The decision to build at Changi was taken in the 1970s as Paya Lebar, hemmed in by housing, could not expand. Construction involved extensive land reclamation from the sea. The airport opened with a single terminal and runway and expanded in phases: Terminal 2 in 1990, Terminal 3 in 2008, Terminal 4 in 2017. Changi also played a role in aviation history as an early adopter of the Airbus A380 with Singapore Airlines' inaugural commercial flight in 2007.
