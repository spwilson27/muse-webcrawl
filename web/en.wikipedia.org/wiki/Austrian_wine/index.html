Austrian wine

Austrian wine
Key regions: Lower Austria, Burgenland, Styria, Vienna
Signature grape: Grüner Veltliner
Style focus: Dry white wines; sweet wines of Burgenland
Vineyard area: ~45,000 ha

Austrian wine is produced in the eastern part of Austria, principally in Lower Austria, Burgenland, Styria and Vienna — one of the few capital cities with significant vineyards within its limits. Austria is best known for dry white wines, above all from the indigenous Grüner Veltliner grape, and for the noble sweet wines produced around Lake Neusiedl.

Regions and grapes

The Danube valley regions of Wachau, Kremstal and Kamptal are noted for Grüner Veltliner and Riesling grown on terraced slopes. Burgenland produces Blaufränkisch and other reds as well as botrytised sweet wines; Styria is known for Sauvignon Blanc. The 1985 diethylene glycol wine scandal severely damaged exports but led to some of the strictest wine laws in Europe and a lasting reorientation toward quality and dry styles.
