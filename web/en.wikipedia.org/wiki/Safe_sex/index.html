Safe sex

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Safe sex (also called safer sex) is sexual activity using methods or devices, such as condoms, to reduce the risk of transmitting or acquiring sexually transmitted infections (STIs), especially HIV. The concept developed in response to the AIDS crisis of the 1980s, when community health campaigns — notably the 1983 pamphlet How to Have Sex in an Epidemic — promoted barrier protection and changes in sexual practice.

Practices

Correct and consistent use of external (male) condoms greatly reduces transmission of HIV and many other infections; internal condoms and dental dams provide barriers for other acts. Additional measures include mutual monogamy between tested partners, regular STI testing, vaccination against HPV and hepatitis B, and pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) against HIV. Safe sex measures reduce but do not eliminate risk: infections transmitted by skin-to-skin contact, such as herpes and HPV, can spread despite condom use.

Public health

Safe sex education forms part of comprehensive sex education programmes, contrasted with abstinence-only approaches. Public health authorities including the World Health Organization recommend condom promotion as a central element of STI and HIV prevention strategy.
