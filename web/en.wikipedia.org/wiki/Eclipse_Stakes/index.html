The Eclipse Stakes is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to horses aged three years or older. It is run at Sandown Park over a distance of 1 mile, 1 furlong and 209 yards (2,002 metres), and it is scheduled to take place each year in early July. It is one of Britain's most prestigious weight-for-age races and is often the first major clash between the Classic generation of three-year-olds and their older rivals.

History

The race was established in 1886 and named after Eclipse, the celebrated eighteenth-century racehorse and sire. Its first running carried prize money of £10,000, then the richest ever offered for a race in Britain, funded by a levy on racecourses under a scheme devised by Leopold de Rothschild. Early winners included the Triple Crown winner Ormonde's rival Bendigo and the great mare Sceptre. The Eclipse has been won by many champions, including Mill Reef (1971), Brigadier Gerard (1972), Dancing Brave (1986), Sea the Stars (2009), and Enable (2019). Sponsored for many years by Coral bookmakers, the race was long known as the Coral-Eclipse.

Records

The leading jockey in the race's history is Lester Piggott, with seven victories, and the leading trainer is Sir Michael Stoute, with six wins. The race is part of a midsummer programme that links the Derby at Epsom with the King George VI and Queen Elizabeth Stakes at Ascot later in July.
