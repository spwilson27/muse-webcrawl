Research fellow

A research fellow is a position in academia, normally held by a person who has completed a doctorate, in which the holder is employed to conduct research — usually on a fixed-term contract funded by a grant, fellowship scheme or institution — rather than holding a permanent teaching post. The exact meaning of the title varies considerably between countries and institutions.

United Kingdom and Commonwealth

In the UK, research fellowships are often prestigious, competitively awarded grants that allow an early- or mid-career researcher to pursue an independent programme: examples include Royal Society University Research Fellowships, UKRI Future Leaders Fellowships and college junior research fellowships at Oxford and Cambridge. Grades range from postdoctoral research associate and research fellow up to senior and principal research fellow, the latter roughly equivalent to senior lecturer or professor in research standing. At some universities "fellow" instead denotes membership of a college's governing body.

Other systems

In the United States, the rough equivalents are postdoctoral fellow or research scientist, though "fellow" is also used for holders of named fellowships in think tanks and professional societies. In India, research fellows (junior and senior) are funded through agencies such as the CSIR and UGC while working toward or after a PhD. In Australia, fellowships from the ARC and NHMRC structure much of the research workforce.

Role

Research fellows typically design and carry out studies, publish results, supervise students and apply for further funding, with fewer teaching and administrative duties than lecturers — though the security of permanent academic posts makes fellowship-holding a competitive and often precarious career stage.
