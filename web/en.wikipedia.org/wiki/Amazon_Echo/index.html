Amazon Echo, often shortened to Echo, is a brand of smart speakers developed by Amazon. Echo devices connect to the voice-controlled intelligent personal assistant service Alexa, which will respond when a user speaks its "wake word", typically "Alexa". The devices can be used for voice interaction, music playback, making to-do lists, setting alarms, streaming podcasts, playing audiobooks, and providing weather, traffic, sports, and other real-time information.

History

Work on the Echo began at Amazon's Lab126 subsidiary in Silicon Valley. The device was announced on November 6, 2014, initially available by invitation to Amazon Prime members, and released generally in the United States on June 23, 2015. It later expanded to the United Kingdom and Germany in 2016, and to many other countries thereafter.

Features

Echo devices use far-field voice recognition through an array of microphones. Users can extend Alexa's capabilities by installing "skills", additional functionality developed by third-party vendors, covering categories such as news, games, smart home control, and ordering services. Echo devices can control compatible smart home devices including lights, thermostats, and locks.

Variants

The Echo line has grown to include the smaller Echo Dot, the Echo Show with a touchscreen display, the Echo Studio with high-fidelity audio, and various other form factors. The Echo has been widely credited with popularizing the smart speaker category, competing with products such as Google Nest and Apple's HomePod.

Privacy concerns

Echo devices have raised privacy concerns related to their always-listening microphones and recordings of user conversations, which have at times been reviewed by Amazon employees as part of quality improvement programs, and have occasionally been sought in criminal investigations.
