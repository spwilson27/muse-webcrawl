Heimweh

Heimweh is a German word meaning homesickness: a deep, painful longing for one's home or homeland. Unlike the English "nostalgia", to which it is related, Heimweh is directed at a place and the people and security associated with it, and may be experienced while the home still exists and could in principle be returned to.

History of the concept

The term became prominent in the seventeenth century in connection with Swiss mercenaries serving abroad, whose affliction was studied by the Basel physician Johannes Hofer, who coined the medical term "nostalgia" in 1688 as a Greek rendering of Heimweh. The condition was taken seriously as a potentially fatal disease among soldiers; Swiss troops were famously said to be forbidden to sing alpine herdsmen's songs (Kuhreihen) lest they desert or fall ill with longing.

Cultural significance

Heimweh became a central theme of German Romanticism, intertwined with Sehnsucht (yearning) and Fernweh (longing for distant places, its opposite). It remains a common theme in folk music and literature of emigration.
