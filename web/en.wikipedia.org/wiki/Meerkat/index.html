Meerkat

Scientific name: Suricata suricatta
Family: Herpestidae (mongooses)
Range: Kalahari Desert, southern Africa

The meerkat (Suricata suricatta) or suricate is a small mongoose found in southern Africa. It is characterised by a broad head, large eyes, a pointed snout, long legs, a thin tapering tail, and a brindled coat pattern.

Behaviour

Meerkats are highly social, living in groups called mobs of 20–50 individuals in extensive burrow systems in the Kalahari and Namib deserts. They forage for insects, scorpions, eggs and small vertebrates; while the group feeds, sentries stand upright on mounds scanning for eagles and jackals, calling alarm barks specific to the threat. Pups are raised cooperatively, with helpers babysitting and teaching them to handle venomous prey.

In culture

Long-running studies of habituated Kalahari groups and television series such as Meerkat Manor have made the species one of the best-known mongooses, and meerkats are popular zoo animals worldwide.
