Llanelli

Llanelli
Country: Wales
Principal area: Carmarthenshire
Population: about 25,000 (town)

Llanelli (Welsh pronunciation roughly "Hlan-eth-lee") is a market town and community in Carmarthenshire, Wales, on the Loughor estuary about 16 kilometres (10 miles) north-west of Swansea. It is the largest town in the county and has a long history as an industrial centre.

History

The name means "church (of) Saint Elli", referring to the parish church of St Elli. Llanelli grew rapidly during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries on the strength of coal mining and metalworking; it became especially famous for tinplate manufacture, earning the nickname "Tinopolis". Steel production also flourished, and the town's docks shipped coal and industrial goods. The heavy industries declined in the later twentieth century, and the town has since shifted toward services, retail, and light industry.

Sport

Llanelli is renowned for rugby union. The Scarlets regional team and Llanelli RFC are based at Parc y Scarlets; the town's rugby heritage includes the famous 1972 victory of Llanelli RFC over the touring New Zealand All Blacks at the old Stradey Park ground.

Geography and economy

The town faces the Burry Inlet across to the Gower Peninsula. The Millennium Coastal Park, created on reclaimed industrial land along the coast, provides a traffic-free path toward Burry Port and Pembrey. Nearby attractions include the National Wetland Centre Wales at Llanwern (Penclacwydd).
