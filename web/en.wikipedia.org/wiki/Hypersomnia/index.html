Hypersomnia

Hypersomnia is a neurological disorder of excessive time spent sleeping or excessive daytime sleepiness. It can have many possible causes, including other sleep disorders, head injury, and certain medications, and can severely impair functioning at work and in social life. Sufferers may fall asleep repeatedly throughout the day, often at inappropriate times such as during a meal or in conversation, and experience prolonged, unrefreshing night-time sleep and sleep inertia on waking.

Hypersomnia is characterised by excessive daytime sleepiness despite adequate sleep.

Classification

The International Classification of Sleep Disorders distinguishes idiopathic hypersomnia, in which no cause is found; narcolepsy types 1 and 2, in which hypersomnia accompanies REM-sleep dysregulation; Kleine–Levin syndrome, with recurrent episodes of hypersomnia and behavioural changes; and hypersomnia due to medical disorders, psychiatric disorders, or substances. Idiopathic hypersomnia is characterised by long sleep times, severe sleep drunkenness and unrefreshing naps, with normal or prolonged sleep latency findings on the multiple sleep latency test.

Diagnosis

Assessment uses sleep history, actigraphy, polysomnography to exclude sleep apnoea and other causes, the multiple sleep latency test, and questionnaires such as the Epworth Sleepiness Scale. A mean sleep latency of eight minutes or less supports a central disorder of hypersomnolence.

Treatment

Wake-promoting agents such as modafinil, armodafinil, solriamfetol and pitolisant are used, together with traditional stimulants. Lower-sodium oxybate is approved for idiopathic hypersomnia. Behavioural measures include regular sleep schedules and planned naps.
