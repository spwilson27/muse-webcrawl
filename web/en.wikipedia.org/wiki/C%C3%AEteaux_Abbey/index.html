Cîteaux Abbey (French: Abbaye de Cîteaux) is a Catholic abbey in Burgundy, France, founded in 1098 by a group of Benedictine monks under Robert of Molesme who sought to follow the Rule of Saint Benedict more strictly. It is the mother house of the Cistercian Order, which takes its name from Cistercium, the Latin name of the site.

History

The new monastery flourished under abbots Alberic and Stephen Harding, who drafted the Carta Caritatis governing the order. In 1112 or 1113 the young Bernard of Clairvaux arrived with thirty companions, greatly boosting the young order's growth. The abbey was suppressed during the French Revolution and its buildings largely demolished or sold, but Trappist monks repurchased the site in 1898 and restored monastic life.

Today

The present community of Trappist monks supports itself through farming and the production of Cîteaux cheese. Surviving medieval buildings include the library and a 15th-century structure; the abbey remains the symbolic head of the worldwide Cistercian family.
