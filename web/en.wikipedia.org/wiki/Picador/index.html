Picador

A picador (Spanish: picador, plural picadores) is one of the team of horse-mounted bullfighters in a Spanish bullfight who performs the second of the three stages (tercios) of the fight, known as the tercio de banderillas's predecessor, the tercio de varas ("third of lances"). The picador rides into the ring on a heavily padded and blindfolded horse, armed with a long lance called a vara or puya.

Role in the bullfight

When the bull charges, the picador drives the lance into the bull's neck muscles (morillo). This weakens the muscles that raise the bull's head, causing the bull to lower its head and charge more in a straight line, which allows the matador to work more closely and artistically in the final stage, the faena. The picador's work also tests the bull's bravery and strength.

Dress and equipment

The picador wears a distinctive costume quite different from the matador's embroidered traje de luces: a wide-brimmed hat, a short jacket, a leather apron, and heavy protective leg armour called monas or gregoriana — steel guards covering the right leg, which faces the bull. The horse is protected by a heavy padded blanket (peto) and is blindfolded so that it does not shy away from the bull.

Reception

The picador's role is often the least popular part of the spectacle with the public, who sometimes whistle their disapproval if they judge the lance work excessive. Even so, skilled picadors are highly respected within the profession, since their judgement of how much punishment a bull needs shapes the quality of the whole fight.
