Valencia orange

The Valencia orange is a variety of sweet orange (Citrus × sinensis) first hybridized by pioneer American agronomist William Wolfskill in the mid-19th century on his farm in Santa Ana in present-day Orange County, California. It is one of the most important commercial orange varieties, prized especially for juicing.

History

Wolfskill named the fruit for Valencia, Spain, famous for its sweet oranges. Before his death the Valencia became the leading orange in California. It was later widely planted in Florida and around the world.

Characteristics

The fruit is of medium size, round to slightly oval, with a thin, smooth rind that is orange at maturity but may regreen in warm climates. The flesh is juicy, sweet with some acidity, contains few seeds, and ripens late — in spring and summer in the Northern Hemisphere, after navel oranges have finished. Because it ripens in summer it is sometimes called a summer orange.

Cultivation and use

Valencias are grown in California, Florida, Arizona, Texas, Mexico, Brazil, South Africa, Australia and the Mediterranean. A large share of the harvest is processed into orange juice; the fruit is also eaten fresh. Valencia juice is valued for its colour and ability to be stored and blended.
