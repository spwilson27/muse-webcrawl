767 Fifth Avenue, originally known as the General Motors Building, is a 50-story, 705-foot (215 m) office tower located at the southeast corner of Fifth Avenue and 59th Street in the Midtown Manhattan neighborhood of New York City, across from the Plaza Hotel and the southeast corner of Central Park.

History

The building was completed in 1968 on the site of the Savoy-Plaza Hotel, which was demolished in 1964 to make way for the tower—a loss that helped galvanize New York's landmarks preservation movement. Designed by Edward Durell Stone in association with Emery Roth & Sons, the tower is clad in white Neoparián marble and is notable for its sunken plaza facing Fifth Avenue.

General Motors sold the building in 1982, though it retains offices there. In 1999 the building was sold to Macklowe Properties, and in 2003 it was acquired by a group led by Boston Properties in a transaction valued at roughly $1.4 billion, then the largest single-asset real estate sale in North American history.

Tenants and features

At street level, the sunken plaza is home to a glass cube entrance to an Apple Store, opened in 2006, which has become one of the most photographed retail sites in the city. The building has also housed the FAO Schwarz toy store and the CBS morning program's studio space at its base.
