Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Point

A Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Point (GSSP) is an internationally agreed reference point within a stratigraphic section that defines the lower boundary of a stage on the geologic time scale. GSSPs are established by the International Commission on Stratigraphy (ICS) and ratified by the International Union of Geological Sciences.

The "golden spike"

The point itself is informally called a golden spike, by analogy with railway spikes, and many GSSP sites are marked with a physical plaque or spike. The first GSSP, for the Silurian–Devonian boundary at Klonk in the Czech Republic, was ratified in 1972. To be accepted, a candidate section must show continuous sedimentation, abundant fossils or other correlatable markers, and be accessible for study.

Purpose

Because the geological record is incomplete in any one place, stage boundaries are defined by a single reference point rather than a time; other sections worldwide are correlated to the GSSP using fossils, magnetic reversals, chemostratigraphy and radiometric dates. Most stage boundaries of the Phanerozoic now have ratified GSSPs, though some Precambrian boundaries are instead defined by absolute ages (GSSAs).
