Environmental economics

Environmental economics is a sub-field of economics concerned with environmental issues, distinct from ecological economics in its roots in neoclassical welfare economics. It studies how environmental resources are allocated, how externalities such as pollution cause market failure, and which policy instruments—taxes, tradable permits, regulation—best correct them.

Core concepts

Key ideas include Pigouvian taxes (after A. C. Pigou), the Coase theorem on bargaining and property rights, valuation of non-market goods (contingent valuation, hedonic pricing), the social cost of carbon, and discounting of future damages, debated in the Stern Review (2006).

Policy instruments

Instruments include carbon taxes, cap-and-trade schemes such as the EU Emissions Trading System, subsidies for clean technology, and command-and-control standards. Cost–benefit analysis is used to compare them.

Topics

Research covers climate change economics (integrated assessment models, e.g. William Nordhaus's DICE), fisheries and common-pool resources (Elinor Ostrom), biodiversity valuation, and environmental Kuznets curves.
