Frédéric Mistral (French: [fʁedeʁik mistʁal]; Occitan: Frederic Mistral, 8 September 1830 – 25 March 1914) was a French writer and lexicographer of the Occitan language and a Provençal poet who received the 1904 Nobel Prize in Literature "in recognition of the fresh originality and true inspiration of his poetic production, which faithfully reflects the natural scenery and native spirit of his people, and, in addition, his significant work as a Provençal philologist". He shared the prize with the Spanish dramatist José Echegaray.

Mistral was the leading figure of the Félibrige, the literary and cultural movement he co-founded in 1854 with six fellow poets to revive and promote the Provençal language and literature of southern France.

Works

His epic poem Mirèio (1859), written in Provençal over eight years, tells of the thwarted love of the farmer's daughter Mirèio and the basket-weaver Vincèn; it won him acclaim, including the praise of Alphonse de Lamartine, and was later adapted by Charles Gounod into the opera Mireille (1864). His other major poems include Calendau (1867), Lis Isclo d'Or (1875), Nerto (1884), La Reino Jano (1890) and Lou Pouèmo dóu Rose (1897).

As a lexicographer he compiled Lou Tresor dóu Felibrige (1878–1886), the most comprehensive dictionary of the Occitan language to date. He used his Nobel prize money to help found the Museon Arlaten, an ethnographic museum in Arles. The French secondary-school award and the Chilean poet Gabriela Mistral's pen name both commemorate him — Lucila Godoy Alcayaga adopted the surname "Mistral" partly in his honour.
