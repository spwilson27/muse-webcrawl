Human experimentation

Human experimentation is medical research conducted on human subjects, ranging from clinical trials of new medicines to psychological and physiological studies. When performed within an ethical framework — with informed consent, independent review and a favourable risk–benefit balance — it is essential to medical progress; when performed coercively or without consent it constitutes a grave abuse.

Ethical framework

Modern safeguards derive from the Nuremberg Code (1947), the Declaration of Helsinki (1964) and the Belmont Report (1979), which established principles of respect for persons, beneficence and justice. Institutional review boards and research ethics committees oversee protocols in most countries.

Historical abuses

Notorious cases include Nazi medical experiments on concentration-camp prisoners, Japan's Unit 731 programme, the Tuskegee syphilis study in the United States, and various Cold War radiation and mind-control experiments. Such abuses drove the development of international law and professional codes governing research on humans.
