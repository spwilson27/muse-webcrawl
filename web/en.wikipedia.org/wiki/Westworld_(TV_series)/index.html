Westworld (TV series)

Westworld
Creators: Jonathan Nolan and Lisa Joy
Network: HBO
Seasons: 4 (2016–2022)
Based on: Westworld (1973 film) by Michael Crichton

Westworld is an American science fiction dystopian television series created by Jonathan Nolan and Lisa Joy for HBO. It is based on the 1973 film of the same name written and directed by Michael Crichton. The series ran for four seasons from 2016 to 2022, when it was cancelled before a planned fifth season.

Premise

The story begins in Westworld, a vast, technologically advanced Wild West–themed amusement park operated by the Delos company, populated by android "hosts" who cater to wealthy human guests' every desire without consequence. As the hosts begin to achieve consciousness — led by Dolores Abernathy (Evan Rachel Wood) and Maeve Millay (Thandiwe Newton) — narrative loops give way to revolution. Later seasons move beyond the parks into the human world of the 2050s, governed by the predictive supercomputer Rehoboam, and explore free will, data and control.

Cast and reception

The ensemble included Anthony Hopkins as park founder Robert Ford, Ed Harris as the Man in Black, Jeffrey Wright as Bernard Lowe, Tessa Thompson and Aaron Paul. The first season was acclaimed as one of the best of its year, and the series won multiple Emmy Awards, though later seasons drew more mixed reviews for their complexity. In 2022 the series was removed from the HBO Max streaming catalogue.
