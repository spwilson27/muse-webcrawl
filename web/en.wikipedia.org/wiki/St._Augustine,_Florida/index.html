St. Augustine (Spanish: San Agustín) is a city in and the county seat of St. Johns County, Florida, United States. Founded on 8 September 1565 by Spanish admiral Pedro Menéndez de Avilés, it is the oldest continuously occupied European-established settlement in the contiguous United States, predating the English settlement at Jamestown by 42 years.

History

Spain established St. Augustine to defend the route of its treasure fleets and to counter the French presence at Fort Caroline. The town survived attacks by English privateers including Francis Drake in 1586, and repeated sieges, protected from 1695 by the masonry Castillo de San Marcos, built of coquina stone. By the Treaty of Paris (1763), Florida passed to Britain; it returned to Spain in 1783 and was ceded to the United States in 1821 under the Adams–Onís Treaty. In the 1880s, Henry Flagler developed St. Augustine as a winter resort for wealthy tourists, building grand hotels such as the Ponce de León Hotel and reshaping the city's skyline.

Geography and tourism

The city lies on the Atlantic coast of northeastern Florida, between the Matanzas River and the Atlantic Ocean, southeast of Jacksonville. Its historic district preserves Spanish colonial and Spanish Revival architecture, including St. George Street, the cathedral, and the Lightner Museum. Heritage tourism, centred on the Castillo, the Fountain of Youth site, and the 450th anniversary commemorations in 2015, is the mainstay of the local economy. The city was significantly affected by flooding from Hurricanes Matthew (2016) and Irma (2017), prompting ongoing resilience planning.
