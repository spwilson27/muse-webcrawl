Stonehaven Tolbooth

The Stonehaven Tolbooth is a late sixteenth-century stone building at the harbour of Stonehaven, Aberdeenshire, Scotland. It is among the oldest buildings in the town and originally served as a storehouse and later as the tolbooth — the courthouse and prison — of the burgh. It now houses a museum and a restaurant.

Stonehaven Tolbooth
Location: Old Pier, Stonehaven, Aberdeenshire
Built: late 16th century (with later additions)
Original use: storehouse; later courthouse and jail
Current use: museum and restaurant

History

The building is traditionally associated with George Keith, 5th Earl Marischal, founder of Marischal College, who is said to have built it around 1590–1600 as a storehouse for the nearby Dunnottar Castle estates. In the seventeenth century it was given over to burgh use as the tolbooth, housing court sessions on an upper floor and prison cells below. After the Jacobite rising of 1745, Episcopalian clergy were imprisoned in the tolbooth for the offence of holding services for more than a handful of worshippers; three clergymen confined there in 1748 famously baptised infants through the prison bars. The building later served as a courthouse and council chamber before falling into other uses; it was restored in the late twentieth century.

Museum

The Tolbooth Museum, run by volunteers, displays artefacts of Stonehaven's fishing and maritime heritage, local history and the tolbooth's own past, including material relating to Dunnottar Castle and the town's role in the story of the Honours of Scotland.

Architecture

The tolbooth is a two-storey rubble-built structure with a distinctive outside stair and crow-stepped gables, typical of Scottish burgh architecture of its period, standing directly on the old pier at the harbour mouth.
