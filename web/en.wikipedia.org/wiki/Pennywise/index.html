Pennywise

This article is about the fictional entity. For the American punk rock band, see Pennywise (band).


First appearance: It (1986)
Created by: Stephen King
Portrayed by: Tim Curry; Bill Skarsgård

Pennywise the Dancing Clown is the primary guise of It, the shapeshifting antagonist of Stephen King's 1986 horror novel It and its adaptations. It is an ancient, malevolent entity that arrived on Earth in a meteor strike in what became Derry, Maine, and awakens roughly every 27 years to feed on the town's children, preferring to terrify them first because fear "salts the meat".

Character

It's true form is incomprehensible to humans; the closest approximation is a gigantic female spider, while its essence resides in the "deadlights", swirling orange lights in another dimension. The clown form—with red balloons, a ruffled costume, and a painted face—is used to lure children. It is opposed by the Losers' Club, a group of seven children who confront It in 1958 and again as adults in 1985, ultimately destroying it through the Ritual of Chüd and the strength of their belief.

Portrayals

Tim Curry played Pennywise in the 1990 television miniseries, a performance widely praised for its menace beneath the clown makeup. Bill Skarsgård took the role in the films It (2017) and It Chapter Two (2019), which together grossed over a billion dollars and cemented Pennywise as one of horror's most recognisable figures. Skarsgård returns in the prequel series It: Welcome to Derry.

Legacy

Pennywise is credited with popularising the "evil clown" in modern popular culture and intensifying public coulrophobia; King has said the character grew from asking what children feared most: "clowns scared children more than anything else".
