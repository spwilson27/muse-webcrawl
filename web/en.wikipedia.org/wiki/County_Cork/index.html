County Cork (Irish: Contae Chorcaí) is a county in the province of Munster in southern Ireland. It is the largest county in the country by area and the third most populous. Named after its county town, Cork (from corcach, "marsh"), the county stretches from the rich farmland of the Golden Vale fringe and the Blackwater valley in the north to the peninsulas of West Cork — Beara, Sheep's Head and Mizen Head — in the south-west, washed by the Atlantic Ocean and the Celtic Sea. It is nicknamed "the Rebel County" for its history of resistance, from the Desmond Rebellions to its central role in the Irish War of Independence.

Geography

Cork is drained by the rivers Lee, Bandon and Blackwater. Cork Harbour, on the east side of the city, is one of the world's largest natural harbours and hosts the towns of Cobh — the last port of call of the RMS Titanic in 1912 and an embarkation point for generations of emigrants — and the naval base at Haulbowline. The county's islands include Cape Clear, Ireland's southernmost inhabited island and a noted bird observatory, and Bere Island. Gougane Barra, the MacGillycuddy's Reeks foothills near the Kerry border, and the Old Head of Kinsale are among its scenic landmarks; Blarney Castle, north of the city, is famed for the Blarney Stone.

History

The county was formally shired in the thirteenth century. In the sixteenth century much of it was devastated in the Desmond Rebellions and subsequently planted with English settlers under the Plantation of Munster. During the War of Independence (1919–1921) Cork was among the most active theatres of conflict — the city was partly burned by British forces in December 1920, and the Kilmichael Ambush and the death on hunger strike of Lord Mayor Terence MacSwiney drew international attention. Michael Collins, the revolutionary leader, was born near Clonakilty and killed at Béal na Bláth in the county during the Civil War in 1922.

Economy and culture

Cork city is Ireland's second city, a university and port city with major pharmaceutical, technology and food industries; the Apple European headquarters at Hollyhill and the "Cork harbour pharma cluster" at Ringaskiddy are major employers. Agriculture, fishing (notably at Castletownbere) and tourism are central to the rural economy. The port of Kinsale is known as a gourmet centre, Bantry and Baltimore as sailing hubs, and the county has strong traditions in Gaelic games — Cork teams compete in both hurling and Gaelic football at the highest level — and in music, from the sean-nós singing of the Múscraí Gaeltacht to the composer Seán Ó Riada.
