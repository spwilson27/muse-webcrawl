Energy in Norway

Energy in Norway describes energy and electricity production, consumption and import in Norway. Norway is unusual among developed countries in that almost all of its electricity is generated from renewable sources, overwhelmingly hydroelectricity, while the country is simultaneously one of the world's largest exporters of oil and natural gas.

Electricity

Hydropower accounts for roughly 90% of Norwegian electricity production, with wind power providing most of the remainder. Norway's numerous mountain reservoirs allow it to store large amounts of energy and to trade electricity with neighbouring countries through interconnectors to Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands, Germany, and the United Kingdom. Because electricity is cheap and abundant, electric heating is widespread and Norway has among the highest per-capita electricity consumption in the world, as well as very high adoption of electric vehicles.

Oil and gas

Norway is a major producer of petroleum and natural gas from fields on the Norwegian continental shelf in the North Sea and Norwegian Sea. Oil and gas exports form a large share of national income, much of which is saved in the Government Pension Fund Global (the Norwegian sovereign wealth fund). The state-owned company Equinor is the dominant operator on the shelf.

Climate policy

Norway's domestic electricity system produces very low emissions, but its petroleum exports are a major source of global emissions, which has led to debate about the country's climate policy. Norway aims to reduce its own greenhouse gas emissions substantially by 2030 and invests heavily in carbon capture and storage.
