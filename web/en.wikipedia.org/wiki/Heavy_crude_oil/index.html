Heavy crude oil

Heavy crude oil
API gravity: typically below 20°
Viscosity: high
Examples: Orinoco Belt extra-heavy oil, Canadian oil sands bitumen

Heavy crude oil (or extra-heavy crude oil) is highly viscous oil that cannot easily flow to production wells under normal reservoir conditions. It is referred to as "heavy" because its density or specific gravity is higher than that of light crude oil. Heavy crude oil has been defined as any liquid petroleum with an API gravity less than 20°.

Properties

Physical properties that differ between heavy crude oils and lighter grades include higher viscosity and specific gravity, as well as higher molecular weight hydrocarbon composition. Heavy crude often contains high concentrations of sulfur and nickel, and requires additional refining to produce lighter products such as gasoline and diesel.

Production

Producing heavy oil is more difficult and expensive than producing light oil. Methods include cold heavy oil production with sand (CHOPS), steam-assisted gravity drainage (SAGD), cyclic steam stimulation, and mining for shallow oil sands deposits. Major resources are found in Venezuela's Orinoco Belt and Canada's Athabasca oil sands.

Environmental issues

Heavy oil production generally has a larger environmental footprint per barrel than light oil, owing to the energy needed for extraction and upgrading, and greater greenhouse gas emissions.
