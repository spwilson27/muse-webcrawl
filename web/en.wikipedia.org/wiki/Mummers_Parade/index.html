Mummers Parade

The Mummers Parade is held each New Year's Day in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. It is believed to be the oldest folk festival in the United States. Local clubs — themselves Mummers, costumed performers with roots in British and Scandinavian mumming traditions — compete in categories including Comics, Wench Brigades, Fancies, String Bands, and Fancy Brigades, performing in elaborate costumes and, for the string bands, with banjos, saxophones, drums, and other instruments.

History

The parade's roots lie in working-class neighborhood celebrations of the 19th century, when residents of South Philadelphia marked the New Year with masquerades, noise-making, and house-to-house visits. The city officially recognized the parade in 1901. The route traditionally runs along Broad Street, with performances judged in front of City Hall.

Organization

Mummers belong to clubs organized into divisions. The String Bands are known for themed musical performances with elaborate feathered costumes and sets, while the Fancy Brigades perform choreographed indoor routines. Preparation of costumes and choreography occupies clubs for much of the year. The parade's signature dance and step is known as the "Mummers' Strut," and its unofficial anthem is "Oh! Dem Golden Slippers."

Culture and controversy

The parade has periodically been criticized over routines perceived as offensive, leading to stricter rules and sensitivity training in recent years. It has also faced funding and weather challenges, but remains one of Philadelphia's signature annual events and a major television broadcast in the region.
