Cruel Intentions

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Theatrical release poster.

Cruel Intentions is a 1999 American teen romantic drama film written and directed by Roger Kumble. A modern retelling of the 1782 French novel Les Liaisons dangereuses by Pierre Choderlos de Laclos, it is set among wealthy students at an elite preparatory school in New York City. The film stars Sarah Michelle Gellar, Ryan Phillippe, Reese Witherspoon, and Selma Blair.

Plot

Kathryn Merteuil and her stepbrother Sebastian Valmont are wealthy, manipulative students at an exclusive Manhattan prep school. Kathryn, bitter after being dumped by her boyfriend for the naive Cecile Caldwell, persuades Sebastian to seduce and ruin Cecile as revenge. Sebastian, a notorious womanizer, is more interested in a challenge: Annette Hargrove, the headmaster's daughter, who has publicly pledged to remain a virgin until marriage.

The step-siblings make a wager: if Sebastian fails to seduce Annette, Kathryn gets his prized vintage Jaguar; if he succeeds, Kathryn — the one girl Sebastian cannot have — will sleep with him. As Sebastian pursues Annette, he unexpectedly falls genuinely in love with her, undermining the game. Kathryn's manipulations escalate, leading to betrayal, blackmail, and tragedy, and Sebastian's private journal — documenting all of their schemes — becomes the instrument of Kathryn's public downfall.

Production and reception

Kumble adapted Laclos's epistolary novel, previously filmed as Dangerous Liaisons (1988) and Valmont (1989), into a contemporary high-school setting. The film was produced by Neal H. Moritz and released by Columbia Pictures on March 5, 1999. It was a commercial success, grossing over $75 million worldwide against a modest budget, though reviews were mixed: critics praised the performances of Gellar and Phillippe while finding the film's tone uneven.

The film developed a lasting cult following, noted for its soundtrack — including "Bitter Sweet Symphony" by The Verve in the finale — and for Blair and Gellar's kiss, which won Best Kiss at the MTV Movie Awards. It spawned two direct-to-video follow-ups, Cruel Intentions 2 (2000), originally produced as a television pilot, and Cruel Intentions 3 (2004), as well as a stage musical adaptation that premiered in 2015.
