Amer (also spelled Amber) is a town in Jaipur district, Rajasthan, India, about 11 km northeast of Jaipur. Once the capital of the Kachhwaha Rajputs, it is dominated by the Amber Fort, a vast palace-fortress of red sandstone and marble rising over the Maota Lake, and is today one of India's most visited heritage sites.

History

Amer was a Meena settlement before passing to the Kachhwaha clan of Rajputs, who made it their capital. The present fort-palace was begun in 1592 by Raja Man Singh I, a trusted general of the Mughal emperor Akbar, and enlarged by his successors, notably Mirza Raja Jai Singh I, who added the celebrated Sheesh Mahal (Hall of Mirrors) with its intricate mirror-and-glass inlay, the Diwan-i-Aam hall of public audience, and the Ganesh Pol gateway painted with frescoes. In 1727 Sawai Jai Singh II founded the new planned city of Jaipur and moved the capital there, after which Amer declined, though the fort remained the ceremonial heart and treasury of the state, guarded by neighbouring Jaigarh Fort — the two linked by a subterranean passage.

Amber Fort

The palace is a classic example of Rajput architecture blending Hindu and Mughal elements: courtyards, audience halls, zenana apartments, the cool Sukh Niwas with its water channel, and the small temple of the goddess Sila Devi. In 2013 Amber Fort was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as one of the Hill Forts of Rajasthan. Visitors ascend the fort on foot, by vehicle or traditionally by elephant — a practice long criticised by animal-welfare campaigners — and the site anchors the tourism economy of the Jaipur region along with the Hawa Mahal, City Palace and Jantar Mantar.
