Trichotillomania

Trichotillomania (TTM), also known as hair-pulling disorder, is a mental disorder characterised by a long-term urge that results in the pulling out of one's own hair, most commonly from the scalp, eyebrows, or eyelashes. It is classified in the DSM-5 among obsessive-compulsive and related disorders.

Signs and symptoms

Episodes of pulling are often preceded by rising tension and followed by a sense of relief or gratification; many people describe pulling as automatic or trance-like. The behaviour causes noticeable hair loss and significant distress or impairment. Some individuals also eat the pulled hair (trichophagia), which can lead to a hairball (trichobezoar) in the stomach.

Treatment

The main treatment is habit reversal training, a form of cognitive behavioural therapy. Evidence for medication is limited; N-acetylcysteine and certain antidepressants have been studied. Onset is typically in late childhood or adolescence. See obsessive–compulsive disorder.
