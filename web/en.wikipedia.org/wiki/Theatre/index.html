Theatre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The reconstructed Globe Theatre in London: a circular timber-framed playhouse with a thatched roof and open yard

Theatre or theater is a collaborative form of performing art that uses live performers, usually actors, to present real or imagined events before a live audience in a specific place, often a stage. It encompasses spoken drama, musical theatre, opera, ballet, mime, puppet theatre and improvisation, communicating through speech, gesture, music, dance and spectacle.

Western theatre originates in classical Athens, where tragedy and comedy were performed at festivals of Dionysus; playwrights such as Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides and Aristophanes founded the Western canon. Later flowerings include the English Renaissance theatre of William Shakespeare, French classical drama, Japanese Noh and Kabuki, and the modern realism of Ibsen, Chekhov and Stanislavski.
