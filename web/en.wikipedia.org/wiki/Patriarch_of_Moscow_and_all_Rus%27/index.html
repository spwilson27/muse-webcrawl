Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus'

Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus'
Church: Russian Orthodox Church
Established: 1589; restored 1917
Incumbent: Kirill (since 2009)
Residence: Moscow, Russia

The Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus' is the primate (first hierarch) of the Russian Orthodox Church, the largest autocephalous church in Eastern Orthodoxy. The patriarchate is headquartered in Moscow, and the patriarch is elected by the Local Council of the Church and serves for life.

History

The Moscow Patriarchate was established in 1589, when Metropolitan Job became the first Patriarch of Moscow with the agreement of the Ecumenical Patriarch Jeremiah II of Constantinople, recognizing the growing importance of the Russian Church after the fall of Constantinople. In 1721 Peter the Great abolished the patriarchate, replacing it with the Most Holy Synod under state control. The office was restored by the Local Council of 1917–18, which elected Tikhon as patriarch just as the Bolshevik Revolution began; after Tikhon's death in 1925, the Soviet authorities prevented a successor until 1943, when Sergius was elected during a wartime thaw in church-state relations.

Recent patriarchs

Post-Soviet patriarchs include Alexy II (1990–2008), who oversaw a broad revival of church life, and Kirill, elected in 2009. The patriarch leads the Holy Synod, represents the Church in relations with other churches and the Russian state, and since the 2018 rupture with Constantinople over the autocephaly of the Orthodox Church of Ukraine, the Moscow Patriarchate has been in broken communion with the Ecumenical Patriarchate.
