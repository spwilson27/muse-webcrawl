Royal Border Bridge

The Royal Border Bridge is a railway viaduct over the River Tweed at Berwick-upon-Tweed, Northumberland, England. Designed by Robert Stephenson, it was opened by Queen Victoria on 29 August 1850 and carries the East Coast Main Line between England and Scotland.

Construction

Built between 1847 and 1850 for the York, Newcastle and Berwick Railway, the bridge is 659 metres (2,162 ft) long with 28 semicircular arches, the river section built of brick faced with ashlar. Its completion, together with the Royal Albert Bridge and other links, created a continuous rail route from London to Edinburgh. Queen Victoria's opening ceremony gave the bridge its "Royal" name, marking its position just south of the Anglo-Scottish border.

Today

The Grade I listed viaduct remains in daily use by long-distance and local trains, and its evening floodlighting makes it a landmark of Berwick, alongside the older Berwick Bridge and the Royal Tweed Bridge.
