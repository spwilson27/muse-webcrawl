The government of China, officially the Central People's Government, is the state authority of the People's Republic of China. Under the constitution, the National People's Congress (NPC) is the highest organ of state power; in practice, political leadership is exercised by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), which stands above the state institutions in a unified party-state system.

Structure

The NPC, a unicameral legislature of about 3,000 delegates, elects the President and approves the Premier. Its Standing Committee exercises legislative power when the NPC is not in session. The State Council, headed by the Premier, is the chief administrative organ — the cabinet of the government. Other state organs include the Central Military Commission, the Supreme People's Court, the Supreme People's Procuratorate, and the National Supervisory Commission.

Party-state relationship

The General Secretary of the CCP is the country's paramount leader and typically also holds the presidency and the chairmanship of the Central Military Commission. Party committees are embedded at every level of government, and leading state officials are almost invariably senior party members.

Local government

China is a unitary state divided into provinces, autonomous regions, municipalities, and special administrative regions (Hong Kong and Macau), each with its own people's government and people's congress mirroring the central structure.
