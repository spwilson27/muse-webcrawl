Palermo protocols

The Palermo protocols are three protocols adopted by the United Nations in 2000 to supplement the Convention against Transnational Organized Crime, which was opened for signature in Palermo, Italy. They address trafficking in persons, smuggling of migrants, and illicit manufacturing and trafficking in firearms.

The three protocols

The Protocol to Prevent, Suppress and Punish Trafficking in Persons, Especially Women and Children was the first global legally binding instrument with an agreed definition of human trafficking, built on the "three Ps" of prevention, protection of victims, and prosecution. The Protocol against the Smuggling of Migrants by Land, Sea and Air requires criminalisation of migrant smuggling while protecting the rights of smuggled migrants. The Protocol against the Illicit Manufacturing of and Trafficking in Firearms controls marking, record-keeping, and transfer of firearms and ammunition.

Significance

The Trafficking in Persons Protocol in particular has shaped national anti-trafficking legislation worldwide and underlies the U.S. State Department's annual Trafficking in Persons Report assessments.
