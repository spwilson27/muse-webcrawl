Zürich

Zürich is the largest city in Switzerland and the capital of the canton of Zürich. It is located in north-central Switzerland, at the northwestern tip of Lake Zürich, where the Limmat river flows out of the lake. With a population of roughly 430,000 in the city proper and about 1.5 million in the metropolitan area, Zürich is Switzerland's main financial, cultural and industrial centre.

History

The area was settled in Roman times as Turicum. Zürich joined the Swiss Confederacy in 1351 and became a leading city of the Reformation under Huldrych Zwingli in the 1520s. Guilds governed the city for centuries, and from the nineteenth century Alfred Escher and others built Zürich into a centre of railways, banking and insurance — home to institutions such as UBS, Credit Suisse (now part of UBS), Swiss Re and Zurich Insurance Group, and ranked among the world's foremost financial centres.

Culture and education

Zürich hosts the University of Zürich and ETH Zürich, one of the world's top technical universities, whose alumni include Albert Einstein. Cultural landmarks include the Grossmünster and Fraumünster churches (the latter with stained-glass windows by Marc Chagall), the Kunsthaus art museum, the Tonhalle orchestra and the Bahnhofstrasse shopping street. The Cabaret Voltaire gave birth to the Dada art movement in 1916. The city consistently ranks near the top of global quality-of-life surveys.

Transport

Zürich Hauptbahnhof is one of Europe's busiest railway stations, and Zürich Airport, Switzerland's largest, lies about 10 km northeast in Kloten. An extensive tram network serves the city, and the S-Bahn connects the wider region.
