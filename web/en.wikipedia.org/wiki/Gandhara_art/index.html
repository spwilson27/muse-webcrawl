Gandhara art

Gandhara art, or Greco-Buddhist art, is the artistic manifestation of Greco-Buddhism, a cultural syncretism between Ancient Greek art and Buddhism. It developed over a period of close to 1,000 years in Gandhara, in what is now north-western Pakistan and eastern Afghanistan, between the 1st century BCE and the 7th century CE.

The style was strongly characterized by the idealized realism of Hellenistic art, and produced the first representations of the Buddha in human form, which had previously been depicted only through symbols such as the empty throne, the Bodhi tree, or footprints. Gandharan sculpture in schist and stucco became a major vehicle for the transmission of Buddhism along the Silk Road to Central and East Asia.

History

Greek artistic influence in the region dates back to the conquests of Alexander the Great and the establishment of the Greco-Bactrian and Indo-Greek kingdoms. The style flourished especially under the Kushan Empire, whose rulers, notably Kanishka, patronized Buddhist art and architecture. Gandharan workshops produced large numbers of statues of the Buddha and bodhisattvas, narrative relief panels depicting scenes from the Buddha's life and the Jataka tales, and architectural ornament for stupas and monasteries.

Style and iconography

The Gandharan Buddha typically wears a heavy monastic robe resembling a Roman toga, covering both shoulders, with deep, naturalistic folds. The hair is wavy and often drawn up into a topknot (ushnisha), and the face has classical Mediterranean features. Bodhisattvas are shown as princely figures with elaborate jewelry and mustaches, while attendant figures include the thunderbolt-bearing Vajrapani, often modeled on Heracles. Corinthian columns, acanthus leaves, cherubs, and garlands abound in architectural decoration.

Legacy

Gandharan art was foundational for the Buddhist art of Central Asia, China, Korea, and Japan, transmitting classical forms far to the east. Major collections are held in the museums of Lahore and Peshawar, the British Museum, and the Musée Guimet in Paris.
