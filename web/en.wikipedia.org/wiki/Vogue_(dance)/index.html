Vogue (dance)

Vogue, or voguing, is a highly stylised, modern house dance originating in the Harlem ballroom scene of New York City. Created by African American and Latino LGBTQ+ communities — particularly Black and Latina trans women and queer youth — beginning in the 1960s and maturing in the 1980s, voguing imitates the poses of fashion models in magazines such as Vogue, performed with angular arm and leg movements, walks, spins, dips and dramatic freezes.

History

Ballroom culture in Harlem dates to the early 20th century, with drag balls growing through the 1960s. Within the "house" system — chosen families led by "mothers" and "fathers" that sheltered queer youth of colour — dancers developed competitive categories judged on dance, costume and "realness". Paris DuPree is credited with pioneering voguing, inspired by model poses in Vogue magazine; Willi Ninja, of the House of Ninja, became its most famous ambassador in the 1980s.

Styles

Three major styles are recognised. Old way emphasises graceful poses, lines and Egyptian- and martial-arts-inspired shapes. New way features extreme flexibility, contortions and illusions with the limbs. Vogue fem, the most widely performed today, exaggerates feminine movement through five elements: hand performance, catwalk, duckwalk, floor performance, and spins and dips (the dramatic "death drop"). Battles are scored by panels of judges while MCs chant over house music.

Mainstream exposure and legacy

Voguing entered mainstream awareness with Madonna's 1990 hit single "Vogue" — which employed dancers from the ballroom scene — and Jennie Livingston's documentary Paris Is Burning (1990). Later, ballroom culture reached new audiences through the television series Pose and the competition show Legendary. Voguing language and aesthetics have permeated global pop, fashion and advertising, prompting ongoing discussion about credit and compensation for its originators in marginalised communities. Kiki balls — a younger, community-focused offshoot of the scene — continue the tradition worldwide.
