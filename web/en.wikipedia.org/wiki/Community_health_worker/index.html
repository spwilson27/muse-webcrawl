Community health worker

Community health workers deliver frontline primary care and health education in their own communities.

A community health worker (CHW) is a frontline public health worker who is a trusted member of, or has a close understanding of, the community served, and who provides culturally appropriate health education, informal counselling, social support, advocacy, and basic preventive and curative services. CHWs typically receive shorter, focused training rather than full professional qualifications, and form a bridge between communities and formal health systems.

History

The concept is often traced to China's "barefoot doctors" of the 1960s and 1970s, farmers given basic medical training who delivered much of rural China's primary care. The 1978 Alma-Ata Declaration on primary health care promoted CHWs worldwide. Large national programmes include Brazil's Family Health Strategy, Ethiopia's Health Extension Workers, Pakistan's Lady Health Workers, India's ASHAs (Accredited Social Health Activists) — numbering around a million, among the largest such workforces in the world — and Bangladesh's BRAC community health workers.

Roles and evidence

CHWs conduct household visits; promote immunisation, sanitation, and maternal and child health; screen for malnutrition and infectious diseases such as malaria, tuberculosis, and HIV; distribute medicines and contraceptives; collect health data; and refer patients to clinics. Systematic reviews find that well-supported CHW programmes can reduce child mortality, improve treatment adherence, and extend services to populations poorly reached by facilities. The World Health Organization issued a guideline in 2018 on optimising CHW programmes, recommending fair remuneration, training, supervision, and integration into health systems rather than reliance on unpaid volunteers.

Challenges

Common issues include low or irregular pay (many CHWs, predominantly women, have historically been unpaid or underpaid stipend workers), heavy workloads, weak supply chains, and unclear career pathways. Labour organising by CHWs, such as strikes by India's ASHAs for employee status, has drawn attention to these questions.
