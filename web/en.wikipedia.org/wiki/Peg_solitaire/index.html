Peg solitaire

Peg solitaire, also called brainvita (in India), solo noble or simply solitaire, is a board game for one player involving movement of pegs on a board with holes. Some sets use marbles in a board with indentations. The game is known in the United Kingdom as solitaire and in the United States as peg solitaire or Hi-Q.

Boards and rules

The standard English board is cross-shaped with 33 holes; the French (European) board has 37 holes. The game begins with every hole filled except the centre. A move consists of jumping one peg orthogonally over an adjacent peg into an empty hole immediately beyond, removing the jumped peg — as in draughts (checkers). The goal is to finish with a single peg remaining, ideally in the centre hole. On the English board the central game (centre vacant, finish in centre) is solvable and has been studied extensively.

History

The game is first depicted in an engraving of 1697 showing the Princess de Soubise playing it at the court of Louis XIV of France. It became a popular pastime in England and France, and was commercialised in the twentieth century under names such as Hi-Q.

Analysis

Peg solitaire has been analysed mathematically using invariants, "pagoda functions", and computer search; researchers have catalogued which starting vacancies can be reduced to a single survivor and where that survivor can end. Generalisations include other board shapes, triangular boards (as in the restaurant version played with 15 pegs), and versions permitting diagonal jumps.
