FishBase

FishBase is a global biodiversity information system on finfishes (i.e. opposed to crustaceans, molluscs, other sea animals also known as seafood/shellfish, and to sea plants). It is the largest and most extensively accessed online database on adult finfish on the web. Over time, it has "evolved into a dynamic and versatile ecological tool, widely cited in scholarly publications".


A FishBase species summary page, showing taxonomy, distribution, and life-history data.

History

FishBase originated in 1987–88, when ichthyologist Daniel Pauly recruited programmer Rainer Froese at the International Center for Living Aquatic Resources Management (ICLARM, now WorldFish) in the Philippines to build software synthesising key life-history data needed for fisheries assessment — notably parameters of the von Bertalanffy growth function — into a single database. The first version, distributed on floppy disks and later CD-ROM, appeared in the early 1990s; the web version followed and grew to include taxonomy, distribution, ecology, diet, reproduction, common names in many languages, and hundreds of thousands of images and references.

Content and use

FishBase covers essentially all described finfish species — over 35,000 species — with data drawn from tens of thousands of references, curated with the help of hundreds of collaborators worldwide. It is used by fisheries managers, researchers, teachers, and the aquarium trade, and its standardised parameters underpin meta-analyses of fish growth, trophic levels, and climate vulnerability. A companion system, SeaLifeBase, extends the same approach to non-fish marine organisms. FishBase is coordinated by a consortium; the database is hosted with support from institutions including the Helmholtz Centre for Ocean Research Kiel (GEOMAR) and is freely accessible at fishbase.se.

Criticism

Reviewers note that although FishBase is indispensable, users should treat individual entries with care: some records propagate old or erroneous literature values, and trait estimates may be generated by models rather than direct measurement. The maintainers respond that entries cite their sources so users can verify data quality.
