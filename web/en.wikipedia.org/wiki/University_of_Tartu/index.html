The University of Tartu (Estonian: Tartu Ülikool; Latin: Universitas Tartuensis) is a public research university located in the city of Tartu, Estonia. It is the country's national university, its largest and oldest institution of higher education, and consistently ranked among the leading universities of Northern and Eastern Europe. Founded in 1632 by King Gustavus Adolphus of Sweden as the Academia Gustaviana, it is one of the oldest universities in Northern Europe.

History

The university was founded in 1632 during the period of Swedish rule over Livonia, on the initiative of Governor Johan Skytte. It operated intermittently during the 17th century and closed in 1710 during the Great Northern War. Reopened in 1802 by Emperor Alexander I of Russia as the Kaiserliche Universität Dorpat, it became a leading German-language university of the Russian Empire; the astronomer Friedrich Georg Wilhelm Struve worked at its observatory, where he measured the first stellar parallax-related distances and directed the Struve Geodetic Arc. After Estonian independence, the university reopened in 1919 as an Estonian-language institution — a milestone of national culture. During the Soviet period it was known as Tartu State University.

Structure and academics

The university is organized into four faculties: Arts and Humanities, Social Sciences, Medicine, and Science and Technology. It operates the University of Tartu Museum in the former cathedral on Toome Hill, a botanical garden founded in 1803, and the Estonian Genome Center, whose gene bank is a major resource for population genetics. The university is a member of the Coimbra Group and has produced notable scholars including semiotician Juri Lotman, founder of the Tartu–Moscow Semiotic School.

Campus

The neoclassical main building, completed in 1809 to designs by Johann Wilhelm Krause, anchors the historic campus in central Tartu. Student traditions centered on the university — including the monument to Johan Skytte and the Kissing Students fountain in Town Hall Square — define Tartu's identity as Estonia's intellectual capital and a quintessential university town.
