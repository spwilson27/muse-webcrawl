Bunker Hill, Los Angeles

Bunker Hill
Location: Downtown Los Angeles, California
Known for: Victorian mansions (historical); modern skyscrapers (today)
Features: Angels Flight funicular

Bunker Hill is a neighbourhood and historic district in Downtown Los Angeles, California, centred on a prominent hill west of Hill Street. Named after the Battle of Bunker Hill, it developed in the late 19th century as one of the city's most fashionable residential districts, lined with ornate Victorian mansions.

Decline and redevelopment

By the mid-20th century the mansions had largely become rooming houses, and noir writers and filmmakers — including Raymond Chandler and John Fante, whose Ask the Dust is set there — made the seedy, atmospheric hill famous. The Los Angeles Community Redevelopment Agency's Bunker Hill Urban Renewal Project, adopted in 1955, demolished nearly all the historic housing and lowered the hill's summit; residents were displaced and the area was rebuilt as a modern office and cultural district.

Today

Modern Bunker Hill hosts skyscrapers along Grand Avenue, the Walt Disney Concert Hall, The Broad museum, the Museum of Contemporary Art, and the Cathedral of Our Lady of the Angels. The Angels Flight funicular, dating to 1901 and billed as the world's shortest railway, climbs the hill's eastern slope on Olive Street.
