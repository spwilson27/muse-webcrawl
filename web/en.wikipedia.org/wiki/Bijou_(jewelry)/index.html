Bijou (jewelry)

Bijou
Origin of termFrench: "jewel" or "trinket"
MeaningA small, delicately crafted piece of jewelry
Related tradeBijouterie — fine jewelry making and retail

A bijou (from the French word for a jewel or trinket) is a small and delicately made piece of jewelry, often valued as much for its craftsmanship and design as for the intrinsic worth of its materials. The term is used both for individual ornaments — such as a pendant, brooch, or ring — and, in the French trade, as part of the word bijouterie, which denotes the making and selling of fine jewelry.

A bijou: a small, finely worked jewel.

Usage

In English the word is most often applied to items that are small, elegant, and intricate, and it carries a connotation of refined taste: a "bijou residence" borrows the sense metaphorically for something small but exquisite. In the antiques and auction trades, bijou pieces — miniature enamelled, gem-set, or filigree objects — form a recognized collecting category alongside vertu.

Bijouterie

In France, the trade distinguishes bijouterie (jewelry worked in precious metals and stones) from joaillerie (high jewelry using exceptional gems). Paris, and particularly the Place Vendôme, became the historic center of the bijouterie trade, home to houses such as Cartier, Boucheron, and Van Cleef & Arpels. The Union Française de la Bijouterie, Joaillerie, Orfèvrerie, des Pierres et des Perles remains the industry's professional body.
