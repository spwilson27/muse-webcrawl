Linji Yixuan (Chinese: 臨濟義玄; Wade–Giles: Lin-chi I-hsüan; Japanese: Rinzai Gigen; died 866/867) was a Chinese Chan (Zen) master of the Tang dynasty and the founder of the Linji school of Chan, transmitted to Japan as the Rinzai school. His recorded sayings, compiled by disciples as the Record of Linji (Linji lu), became one of the most influential texts of Zen Buddhism.

Life

Little is reliably known of Linji's early life. Tradition says he studied under Huangbo Xiyun, whose blows and shouts were famous, and attained awakening after a period of severe training and a pilgrimage in which he repeatedly questioned Huangbo about the meaning of Bodhidharma's coming from the west. He later settled at a small temple in Zhenzhou (in present-day Hebei), near the Hutuo River, from whose crossing (Linji, "overlooking the ford") he took his name.

Teaching

Linji's teaching style was fierce and direct, employing shouts (katsu), blows with a stick, and paradoxical demands to cut off conceptual thinking. His sermons insist that the ordinary person is already complete — the "true person of no rank" (wuwei zhenren) who is listening to the Dharma right now — and denounce dependence on scriptures, masters, and even the Buddha: "If you meet the Buddha on the road, kill him." He classified students and teachings with devices such as the "Three Mysterious Gates" and the "Fourfold Guest and Host".

Legacy

The house of Linji became the dominant stream of Chan in later Chinese Buddhism and, carried to Japan by Eisai in the 12th century, formed the Rinzai school, famed for koan training. The Record of Linji was canonised as one of the classic recorded-saying collections of Zen.
