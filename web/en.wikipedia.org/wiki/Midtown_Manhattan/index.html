Midtown Manhattan

Midtown Manhattan is the central portion of the New York City borough of Manhattan. It is home to some of the city's most famous buildings, including the Empire State Building, the Chrysler Building, the United Nations headquarters, and Broadway theatre district around Times Square. Midtown is the largest central business district in the world and one of the most expensive pieces of real estate.

Geography

Midtown is generally taken to span from about 14th Street in the south to 59th Street (Central Park South) in the north, river to river. Its core — the dense office district — is often defined more narrowly, from around 31st Street to 59th Street between Third and Ninth Avenues. Neighborhoods within or overlapping Midtown include Hell's Kitchen, Chelsea, Murray Hill, Koreatown, and the Garment District.

Economy

Midtown contains roughly 400 million square feet of office space and the headquarters of numerous global companies in media, finance, law, and advertising — Madison Avenue long being synonymous with the advertising industry. Major landmarks of commerce include Rockefeller Center, the Grand Central Terminal district, Pennsylvania Station and Madison Square Garden, and the newer Hudson Yards development on the far west side.

Landmarks

  • Empire State Building (1931) — for decades the world's tallest building.
  • Times Square — the "Crossroads of the World", at Broadway and Seventh Avenue.
  • Chrysler Building (1930) — an Art Deco icon.
  • New York Public Library main branch and Bryant Park.
  • St. Patrick's Cathedral and Radio City Music Hall.
  • United Nations headquarters on the East River.

Transportation

Midtown is served by Penn Station, Grand Central Terminal, the Port Authority Bus Terminal, and numerous subway lines, making it the most transit-accessible business district in the United States.
