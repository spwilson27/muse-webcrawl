Empress Genmei (元明天皇, Genmei-tennō, 660 – December 29, 721) was the 43rd monarch of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession, and the fourth of eight women to take the role of empress regnant. She reigned from 707 to 715.

Reign

Genmei was the daughter of Emperor Tenji and the wife of Crown Prince Kusakabe, son of Emperor Tenmu. When her son Emperor Monmu died young in 707, she ascended the throne as a placeholder until her grandson (the future Emperor Shōmu) came of age. Her reign is notable for the founding of the permanent capital at Heijō-kyō (Nara) in 710, to which the court moved from Fujiwara-kyō, and for the completion of the Kojiki chronicle, presented to her in 712 by Ō no Yasumaro. The era also saw the compilation of the Fudoki provincial gazetteers, ordered in 713.

Abdication

In 715 Genmei abdicated in favour of her daughter, Princess Hidaka, who reigned as Empress Genshō—a rare mother-to-daughter succession while the male heir was still a minor. She died in 721.
