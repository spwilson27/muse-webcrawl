Tang poetry

Tang poetry (Chinese: 唐詩; pinyin: Tángshī) refers to poetry written in or around the time of China's Tang dynasty (618–907) or in the characteristic style of that period. The Tang era is traditionally regarded as the golden age of Chinese poetry: verse composition was an element of the imperial civil-service examinations, an expected accomplishment of every educated person, and a central medium of social and political life.

Corpus

The scale of surviving Tang poetry is enormous. The 18th-century anthology Complete Tang Poems (Quan Tangshi) collects some 48,900 poems by more than 2,200 authors. Countless additional works were lost. Poets were often officials, and poems circulated through examinations, banquets, exile postings, and correspondence.

Forms

The major Tang forms are the shi styles: gushi ("old-style" verse, relatively free in form) and jintishi ("modern-style" regulated verse), the latter governed by strict rules of line length (five or seven characters), tonal patterning, parallelism, and rhyme. Regulated verse includes the eight-line lüshi and the four-line jueju (quatrain). The era also saw the beginnings of the ci (lyric) form, which flowered in the Song dynasty.

Major poets

The best-known Tang poets include Li Bai (Li Po), famed for romantic, Daoist-inflected verse on wine, nature, and transcendence; Du Fu, the "poet-sage", whose technically masterful poems document the devastation of the An Lushan Rebellion; Wang Wei, the Buddhist landscape poet-painter; Bai Juyi, whose accessible style was read across East Asia; and Li Shangyin, known for dense, allusive love poetry. The rebellions and decline of the late 8th century gave Tang verse much of its characteristic themes of transience, exile, and lost grandeur.

Legacy

Tang poetry set the standard against which later Chinese verse was measured and profoundly influenced the literatures of Japan, Korea, and Vietnam. Anthologies such as the Three Hundred Tang Poems remain standard school texts in China, and many Tang lines have become everyday proverbs.
