Cambrian explosion

The Cambrian explosion (or Cambrian radiation) is the relatively rapid appearance, over a few tens of millions of years in the early Cambrian about 538 million years ago, of most major animal phyla in the fossil record, accompanied by major diversification of other organisms.

WhenEarly Cambrian, c. 538–515 Ma
Key sitesBurgess Shale, Chengjiang, Sirius Passet

Evidence

Fossils from the Burgess Shale, Chengjiang biota and Emu Bay Shale show animals with shells, limbs, eyes and guts: trilobites, anomalocaridids, Opabinia, Hallucigenia, Wiwaxia and early chordates. Trace fossils also become complex.

Causes

Proposed drivers include rising oxygen, the evolution of predation and arms races, developmental genetic toolkits (Hox genes), eyes and vision, biomineralised skeletons, and ecological feedbacks. Whether the event was truly sudden or partly an artefact of fossilisation of hard parts remains debated.

History of interpretation

Charles Darwin saw the abrupt appearance of Cambrian fossils as a problem for gradual evolution. Stephen Jay Gould's Wonderful Life (1989) popularised the Burgess Shale fauna and debates about disparity and contingency.
