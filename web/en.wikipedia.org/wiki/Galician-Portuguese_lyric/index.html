Galician-Portuguese lyric

In the Middle Ages, the Galician-Portuguese lyric, also known in Portuguese as trovadorismo, was the dominant poetic tradition of the northwest of the Iberian Peninsula. Between roughly 1189 and the mid-14th century, poets composed in Galician-Portuguese — then the prestige language for lyric poetry throughout Christian Iberia, used even at the courts of Castile and León — producing a corpus of some 1,680 surviving songs (cantigas) attributed to about 170 known authors.

Genres

The corpus is conventionally divided into three main genres:

  • Cantigas de amor — courtly love songs in the Provençal tradition, voiced by a male lover addressing his lady (senhor).
  • Cantigas de amigo — songs of a woman longing for her absent lover (amigo), a genre considered indigenous to the northwest of Iberia, characterised by refrains and parallelistic structure and often set by the sea at Vigo or in other Galician landscapes.
  • Cantigas de escárnio e maldizer — satirical and mocking songs directed at courtiers, clergy, rival poets and social types.

A fourth, smaller group consists of religious songs, the Cantigas de Santa Maria, composed in the same language at the court of Alfonso X of Castile, "the Wise", who was himself a prolific practitioner of the secular genres, as was his grandson King Denis of Portugal.

Transmission

The songs survive principally in three large songbooks (cancioneiros): the Cancioneiro da Ajuda, the Cancioneiro da Biblioteca Nacional (Colocci-Brancuti) and the Cancioneiro da Vaticana, along with smaller parchment fragments including the Vindel Parchment, which preserves seven cantigas de amigo by Martín Codax with musical notation.
