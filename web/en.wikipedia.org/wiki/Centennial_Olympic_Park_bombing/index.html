Centennial Olympic Park bombing

Centennial Olympic Park, site of the 1996 bombing.

The Centennial Olympic Park bombing was a domestic terrorist pipe-bomb attack on Centennial Olympic Park in Atlanta, Georgia, on 27 July 1996, during the Summer Olympic Games. The blast killed one spectator directly and injured 111 others; a cameraman rushing to the scene died of a heart attack.

The attack

Security guard Richard Jewell discovered a suspicious backpack beneath a bench near a concert sound tower and alerted police; as officers began clearing the area, the bomb — a steel pipe bomb surrounded by nails, weighing about 40 pounds — detonated. A 911 call warning of the bomb had been placed shortly before the explosion.

Investigation

Jewell was initially hailed as a hero but then became a suspect in media reports, enduring intense scrutiny before being cleared by the FBI after 88 days. In 2003, Eric Rudolph was arrested and identified as the bomber; he had also carried out attacks on abortion clinics and a nightclub. Rudolph pleaded guilty in 2005 and is serving multiple life sentences without parole, stating the attacks were motivated by his anti-abortion and anti-government views.

Legacy

The case became a widely cited example of trial by media; Jewell received settlements from several news organisations and was formally thanked by the state of Georgia in 2019. The episode was dramatised in the 2019 film Richard Jewell.
