Bus shelter

TypeStreet furniture
PurposeWeather protection at bus stops
Common featuresRoof, seating, timetable display, advertising panel

A typical contemporary bus shelter.

A bus shelter is a small structure at a bus stop that provides seating and protection from the weather for passengers waiting for a bus. Shelters range from simple roofed benches to enclosed glass structures with lighting, real-time departure displays, and advertising panels.

Design and funding

Many bus shelters are installed and maintained by outdoor advertising companies under contracts with municipalities: in exchange for maintaining the shelter, the company sells the advertising space on its panels. This model was pioneered on a large scale by the French company JCDecaux from the 1960s onward and is now common in cities worldwide. Design considerations include sightlines for approaching buses, accessibility for wheelchair users, resistance to vandalism, and, in some designs, features intended to deter sleeping or loitering, which have drawn criticism on grounds of hostile architecture.

Variations

In rural areas, shelters may be built of brick, stone, or timber by local communities. Some historic shelters are protected as listed structures, particularly in the United Kingdom. Modern shelters increasingly incorporate solar-powered lighting, USB charging, and electronic paper timetables.
