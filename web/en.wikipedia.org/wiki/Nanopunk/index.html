Nanopunk

Nanopunk is a subgenre of science fiction that explores the potential of nanotechnology — including self-replicating nanobots, molecular assemblers, grey goo, smart matter and radical life extension — alongside cyberpunk-derived themes of punk rebellion, decentralisation and transgression against established order.

GenreScience fiction
PrecursorCyberpunk
ThemesNanotechnology, posthumanism, grey goo, rebellion

Origins and works

Nanopunk emerged in the 1990s–2000s as nanotechnology entered popular discourse. Kathleen Ann Goonan's Nanotech Quartet (1997–2008) is frequently cited, alongside Neal Stephenson's The Diamond Age (1995), which envisions a matter-compiler post-scarcity society, and works by Linda Nagata, Greg Bear (Queen City Jazz, Blood Music) and Michael Crichton's Prey (2002) — the latter popularising the grey goo/ecophagy scenario. In manga and anime, works such as Ghost in the Shell incorporate nano-scale augmentation themes.

Themes

Where biopunk stresses biotechnology, nanopunk stresses molecular manufacturing, programmability of matter, post-scarcity economics, mind uploading via nanobots, and the runaway risks of replication. Politically it tends to foreground corporate control, grey goo accidents and punk resistance to tech monopolies.
