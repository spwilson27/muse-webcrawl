Sittwe

Sittwe (formerly romanised as Akyab) is the capital city of Rakhine State, Myanmar (Burma). It is a port city situated on an estuarine island at the confluence of the Kaladan, Mayu and Lay Mro rivers where they meet the Bay of Bengal.

History

The site's importance grew after the First Anglo-Burmese War, when the British annexed Arakan in 1826 and developed Akyab into a major rice-exporting port; by the early 20th century it ranked among the busiest rice ports in the world, drawing migrant labour from across British India. During the Second World War the city changed hands in the Burma campaign, and its airfield figured in Allied operations.

Demographics

The population is predominantly Rakhine Buddhist, with a historically large Rohingya Muslim population. Communal violence in 2012 displaced most of the city's Muslim residents into camps outside the city, an arrangement that persisted for years afterwards.

Economy and transport

Sittwe remains a fishing and trading port. India has invested in developing its deep-sea port as part of the Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project, intended to connect India's landlocked northeast to the sea through Myanmar. The city is served by Sittwe Airport and road and river links to the interior.
