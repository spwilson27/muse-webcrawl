Vector field

Vector field
A vector field on the plane, shown as a grid of arrows

In vector calculus and physics, a vector field is an assignment of a vector to each point in a space, most commonly Euclidean space. A vector field on the plane, for instance, can be visualised as a collection of arrows, each attached to a point of the plane and having a given magnitude and direction. Vector fields are used to model quantities that have both magnitude and direction distributed through space, such as the speed and direction of a moving fluid, or the strength and direction of a force such as the magnetic or gravitational force generated by a material object.

Definition

Given a subset S of n-dimensional Euclidean space, a vector field is represented by a vector-valued function V: S → Rn in standard Cartesian coordinates (x1, …, xn). If each component of V is continuous, then V is a continuous vector field; more generally, a vector field is Ck if each component is k times continuously differentiable. Vector fields can be generalised to manifolds, where they are sections of the tangent bundle.

Operations

Two fundamental differential operations act on vector fields: the divergence, which yields a scalar field measuring the tendency of the field to flow away from each point, and the curl, which yields another vector field measuring local rotation. Integral curves (field lines) follow the direction of the field from point to point; line integrals of a vector field along a curve measure the work done by the corresponding force in moving a particle along that curve.

Examples

  • The velocity field of a flowing liquid, such as air passing over an aircraft wing.
  • The gravitational field around a planet, pointing toward the planet's centre.
  • The electric and magnetic fields of electromagnetism, described jointly by Maxwell's equations.
  • Gradient fields, the gradients of scalar potentials.
