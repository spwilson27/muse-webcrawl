John Boyd (military strategist)

John Richard Boyd (23 January 1927 – 9 March 1997) was a United States Air Force colonel and military strategist whose theories have been highly influential in military, business, and legal strategy. He is best known for the OODA loop, a model of decision-making, and for his work on energy–maneuverability theory.

Career

Boyd served as a fighter pilot in the Korean War, where he flew F-86 Sabres and earned the nickname "Forty Second Boyd" for his claimed ability to defeat any opponent in aerial combat in under forty seconds. After the war he became an instructor at the Fighter Weapons School at Nellis Air Force Base.

Energy–maneuverability theory

With mathematician Thomas Christie, Boyd developed energy–maneuverability theory, a quantitative method for comparing the performance of fighter aircraft. The theory influenced the design of the F-15 and F-16 fighters.

OODA loop

Boyd's later work on patterns of conflict produced the OODA loop — Observe, Orient, Decide, Act — a cycle describing how a combatant processes information and reacts. The concept has been widely adopted in military doctrine and business strategy.
