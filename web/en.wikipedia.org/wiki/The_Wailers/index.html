The Wailers

The Wailers, originally called the Teenagers, then the Wailing Rudeboys, the Wailing Wailers, and finally the Wailers, were a Jamaican ska, rocksteady, and reggae band formed in Kingston in 1963. The group is best known as the original band of Bob Marley, Peter Tosh, and Bunny Wailer, who went on to become reggae's three most famous solo artists.

History

The group was formed in the Trench Town area of Kingston by Bob Marley, Peter Tosh, Bunny Wailer (Neville Livingston), Junior Braithwaite, Beverley Kelso, and Cherry Smith, under the guidance of vocal coach Joe Higgs. They recorded their first single, "Simmer Down", for Clement "Coxsone" Dodd's Studio One label in late 1963; it reached number one in Jamaica. The Wailers recorded extensively at Studio One through the mid-1960s, including such classics as "One Love" and "Rude Boy".

Rocksteady and reggae years

As ska gave way to rocksteady and then reggae, the group—reduced to the core trio of Marley, Tosh, and Wailer—recorded with producer Leslie Kong and then with Lee "Scratch" Perry at his Upsetter label, producing landmark tracks such as "Soul Rebel", "Duppy Conqueror", and "Small Axe". In 1972 they signed with Chris Blackwell's Island Records, releasing Catch a Fire (1973) and Burnin' (1973), the latter including "Get Up, Stand Up" and "I Shot the Sheriff".

Breakup and legacy

Wailer and Tosh left the group in 1974 to pursue solo careers, after which Marley continued with a new lineup under the name Bob Marley and the Wailers. The original Wailers are regarded as foundational to reggae music; their harmonies and songwriting helped define the genre's sound and international appeal.
