Gloucester is a city in Essex County, Massachusetts, United States, on Cape Ann on the Atlantic Ocean. It is one of America's oldest seaports: settled in 1623 by the Dorchester Company as a fishing station and incorporated as a town in 1642, it has been a centre of the fishing industry for four centuries and is the oldest fishing port in the United States still in operation.

History

Gloucester's economy was built on the cod, haddock and other fisheries of Georges Bank and the Grand Banks; generations of fishermen sailed from its harbour, and more than 10,000 Gloucester men are recorded as lost at sea, commemorated by the famous Man at the Wheel — the Gloucester Fisherman's Memorial, erected in 1925. The city was the setting of Rudyard Kipling's novel Captains Courageous (1897) and of Sebastian Junger's The Perfect Storm (1997), about the loss of the Andrea Gail in 1991.

Culture and landmarks

Gloucester is home to the Rocky Neck Art Colony, one of the oldest operating art colonies in America, and to Beauport, the Sleeper–McCann House, and Hammond Castle. The Gorton's of Gloucester seafood company, founded in 1849, is headquartered here. The neighbourhoods of Annisquam, Lanesville, Folly Cove, Bay View, Magnolia and East and West Gloucester lie within the city.
