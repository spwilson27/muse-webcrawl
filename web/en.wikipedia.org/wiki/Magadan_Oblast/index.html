Magadan Oblast

Magadan Oblast (Russian: Магаданская область) is a federal subject (an oblast) of Russia, located in the Far Eastern Federal District. Its administrative centre is the port city of Magadan. The oblast has an area of about 462,000 square kilometres — larger than California or Sweden — but a population of only around 140,000, making it one of the most sparsely populated regions of Russia. It borders Chukotka to the north, Kamchatka Krai to the east, Khabarovsk Krai to the south and the Sakha Republic (Yakutia) to the west.

History

The region corresponds closely to the territory administered from 1931 by Dalstroy, the Soviet trust that developed the Kolyma goldfields using forced labour from an extensive network of Gulag camps. Magadan Oblast was formally created in 1953, the year of Stalin's death, initially including Chukotka, which became a separate subject in 1992. The population peaked at over 550,000 in the late Soviet period and fell dramatically after 1991 as subsidised northern settlements were abandoned.

Geography and climate

The oblast is mountainous, dominated by the Chersky and Kolyma ranges, with the upper Kolyma River crossing its interior. Much of the land is permafrost taiga and tundra, and the climate is severe subarctic, with some of the coldest inhabited places in the northern hemisphere along the upper Indigirka near the Yakutian border. Wildlife includes brown bears, reindeer, snow sheep and large salmon runs in the rivers flowing to the Sea of Okhotsk.

Economy

Mining is the dominant industry: Magadan Oblast is among Russia's leading producers of gold and silver, with large mines such as Natalka and Pavlik, and historically also produced tin, tungsten and coal. Fishing and fish processing are significant along the coast. Most freight arrives through the port of Magadan, and the R504 Kolyma Highway is the region's only land link to the rest of Russia's road network.
