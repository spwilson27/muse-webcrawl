Girl Guides

Girl Guides taking part in an outdoor activity

Girl Guides (known as Girl Scouts in the United States and some other countries) is a worldwide movement for girls and young women, founded in the United Kingdom in 1910 in response to the popularity of Scouting among girls. Its international umbrella body is the World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts (WAGGGS), which represents around 8–10 million members in over 150 countries.

History

At the 1909 Crystal Palace Scout Rally in London, a group of girls in Scout uniform demanded a place in the new movement. Robert Baden-Powell, Scouting's founder, asked his sister Agnes Baden-Powell to organise a parallel movement for girls, and The Girl Guides Association was formed in 1910; his wife Olave Baden-Powell later became its leader and World Chief Guide. Juliette Gordon Low founded the Girl Scouts of the USA in 1912 after meeting the Baden-Powells.

Programme

Guiding is organised by age sections — for example Rainbows, Brownies, Guides and Rangers in the United Kingdom, and Daisies, Brownies, Juniors, Cadettes, Seniors and Ambassadors in the US. Members make a Promise, follow a Guide Law, earn interest badges, and take part in camping, community service and international camps. The movement's motto is "Be Prepared"; its symbol is the trefoil, and World Thinking Day is celebrated on 22 February, the shared birthday of Robert and Olave Baden-Powell.
