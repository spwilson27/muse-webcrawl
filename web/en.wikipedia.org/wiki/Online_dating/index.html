Online dating

Online dating, also known as internet dating or mobile dating, is a method used by people with the goal of searching for and interacting with potential romantic or sexual partners via the internet. Online dating services allow users to create profiles, browse or be matched with others, and communicate before meeting in person.

History

Precursors include newspaper personal ads and computer dating services of the 1960s, such as Operation Match at Harvard. The first major web dating sites appeared in the mid-1990s: Match.com launched in 1995, followed by sites such as eHarmony (2000) and OkCupid (2004), which used questionnaires and compatibility algorithms. The arrival of smartphones and location-based apps — Grindr (2009), Tinder (2012), and others — made swiping-based dating mainstream.

Prevalence and research

Pew Research Center surveys have found that a growing share of couples meet online — by the late 2010s, online had become the most common way American couples met — and that around three in ten US adults have used a dating site or app. Research examines self-presentation, deception in profiles, matching algorithms, and outcomes such as relationship satisfaction, finding results broadly comparable to offline-formed relationships on average.

Criticism and safety

Online dating has faced criticism over superficial selection, "ghosting", harassment, romance scams and fraud, privacy of sensitive profile data, and the commercialization of intimacy. Platforms and regulators have responded with verification, reporting tools, and safety guidance.
