Kamadeva

Kamadeva (Sanskrit: कामदेव, "god of desire"), also known as Kama, Manmatha, and Madana, is the Hindu god of erotic love, desire, and pleasure, comparable to the Greek Eros and Roman Cupid. He is depicted as a handsome young man wielding a bow made of sugarcane strung with bees, and arrows tipped with flowers, riding a parrot.

Mythology

In the best-known myth, the gods send Kama to rouse Shiva from deep meditation so that Shiva might father a son able to defeat the demon Taraka. Kama shoots Shiva with his flower arrows; the enraged Shiva opens his third eye and burns Kama to ash, leaving him bodiless (Ananga). On the plea of Kama's wife Rati, Shiva later restores him — in some versions as Pradyumna, son of Krishna.

Worship and culture

Kama is celebrated in poetry and temple art, and the festival of Holi is in part associated with his legend. The Kama Sutra takes its name from the realm of desire (kama) over which he presides, one of the four aims of human life in Hindu thought.
