Number of the beast

The number of the beast is a number associated with the Beast of Revelation in chapter 13 of the Book of Revelation in the New Testament. In most manuscripts it is given as 666 — written in Greek as χξϛ (chi, xi, stigma) or in words as "six hundred and sixty-six". Revelation 13:18 introduces it with a riddle: "Let the one who has understanding calculate the number of the beast, for it is the number of a man, and his number is 666."

Textual variants: 666 and 616

Some early witnesses give the number as 616 (χιϛ): these include Papyrus 115 — among the oldest surviving fragments of Revelation — and the Codex Ephraemi Rescriptus; Irenaeus knew of the 616 reading but rejected it as a scribal error. Modern critical editions print 666, noting 616 as a variant. The difference likely reflects different spellings of the name being encoded.

Interpretations

The dominant scholarly interpretation uses gematria, the assignment of numerical values to letters. In Hebrew, "Neron Caesar" (נרון קסר) sums to 666, identifying the Beast with the emperor Nero, who persecuted Christians and whose name, transliterated from Latin (Nero Caesar, נרו קסר), sums to 616 — neatly explaining the variant. Other readings include: a symbol of imperfection (falling short of 777, a number of completeness); the "preterist" view that Revelation describes first-century events; "historicist" identifications with the papacy or other powers; and futurist readings expecting a coming Antichrist. Throughout history the number has been attached to figures from Roman emperors to modern politicians, and to technologies such as barcodes and microchips.

Cultural impact

Fear of the number is called hexakosioihexekontahexaphobia. 666 recurs in popular culture — for example the Iron Maiden album The Number of the Beast (1982) and horror films such as The Omen (1976) — and in curiosities such as the former US Highway 666 (renumbered 491 in 2003) and sums derived from roulette wheels (0–36 totalling 666).
