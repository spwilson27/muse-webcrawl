Motor neuron disease


The upper and lower motor neuron pathways affected in MND.

Motor neuron diseases or motor neurone diseases (MNDs) are a group of rare neurodegenerative disorders that selectively affect motor neurons, the cells which control voluntary muscles of the body. They include amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), progressive bulbar palsy, pseudobulbar palsy, progressive muscular atrophy, primary lateral sclerosis, spinal muscular atrophy and monomelic amyotrophy, as well as some rarer variants resembling ALS.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms typically include progressive muscle weakness and wasting, fasciculations, spasticity, and increasing difficulty with speaking, swallowing and breathing. Sensation, eye movement and (usually) cognition are spared, although a minority of ALS patients develop frontotemporal dementia.

Causes

Most cases are sporadic. Around 5–10% are familial, with mutations in genes such as SOD1, C9orf72, TARDBP and FUS identified as causes. Diagnosis is clinical, supported by electromyography, and requires excluding mimicking conditions.

Treatment

There is no cure. The drug riluzole modestly prolongs survival in ALS; edaravone and multidisciplinary supportive care, including non-invasive ventilation and nutritional support, improve quality of life. Physicist Stephen Hawking, who lived with ALS for over 50 years, was an unusually long-lived case; most patients die from respiratory failure within two to four years of symptom onset.
