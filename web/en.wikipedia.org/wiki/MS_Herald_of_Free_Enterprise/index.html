MS Herald of Free Enterprise

MS Herald of Free Enterprise was a roll-on/roll-off (RORO) ferry operating across the English Channel between Dover and Zeebrugge for Townsend Car Ferries. On 6 March 1987, soon after leaving Zeebrugge with her bow doors still open, the ship took on water and capsized in about 90 seconds, coming to rest on a sandbank in shallow water. Of the 539 people on board, 193 passengers and crew died, making it the worst British maritime disaster in peacetime since the sinking of HMY Iolaire in 1919.

The disaster

The assistant bosun, who was responsible for closing the bow doors, was asleep in his cabin and did not hear the call to harbour stations. The bosun did not check, and the captain could not see the doors' status from the bridge because the ship lacked indicator lights. Water flooded the open car deck, and the free surface effect destroyed the ship's stability.

Aftermath

A public inquiry chaired by Mr Justice Sheen found the immediate cause to be negligence, but concluded that the company, Townsend Car Ferries, was infected "from top to bottom" with the "disease of sloppiness". Corporate manslaughter charges against P&O European Ferries and seven individuals ended in acquittal in 1990, though the case established that a company could be indicted for manslaughter. The disaster led to major reforms in ferry safety, including mandatory door indicator lights, and to the creation of the Marine Accident Investigation Branch (MAIB) in 1989. The salvage operation raised the ship, which was later sold for scrap and broken up in Taiwan in 1988.
