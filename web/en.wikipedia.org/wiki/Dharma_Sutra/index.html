Dharma Sutra

The Dharmasutras are a genre of Sanskrit texts on dharma, the religious and social duties of individuals in ancient Indian society. They form part of the Kalpa Sutras and are among the earliest sources of Hindu law.

Content

The Dharmasutras deal with the duties of the four varnas (social classes) and the four ashramas (stages of life), covering topics such as ritual purity, marriage, inheritance, diet, penances and the duties of kings. They are written in a terse, aphoristic sutra style.

Major texts

The best-known Dharmasutras are those attributed to Apastamba, Gautama, Baudhayana and Vasishtha, generally dated to between roughly the 6th century BCE and the early centuries CE. They were later expanded and versified into the Dharmashastra literature, most famously the Manusmriti (Laws of Manu).

Significance

Although largely normative rather than descriptive, the Dharmasutras are important sources for the social and legal history of early India.
