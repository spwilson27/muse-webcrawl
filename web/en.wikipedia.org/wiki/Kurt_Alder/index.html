Kurt Alder

Kurt Alder (10 July 1902 – 20 June 1958) was a German chemist and Nobel laureate, best known for discovering, together with his doctoral advisor Otto Diels, the Diels–Alder reaction.

Life and work

Alder was born in Königshütte, Upper Silesia (now Chorzów, Poland), and studied chemistry at the University of Berlin and the University of Kiel, where he obtained his doctorate in 1926 under Diels. In 1928 Diels and Alder published the "diene synthesis": the [4+2] cycloaddition of a diene and a dienophile to form a substituted cyclohexene — one of the most useful carbon–carbon bond-forming reactions in organic chemistry. Alder worked at IG Farben in Leverkusen from 1934 and was appointed professor at the University of Cologne in 1940.

Honours

Alder shared the 1950 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Otto Diels "for their discovery and development of the diene synthesis". He died in Cologne in 1958, aged 55. The lunar crater Alder is named after him.
