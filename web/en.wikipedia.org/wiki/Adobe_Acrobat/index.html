Adobe Acrobat is a family of application software and web services developed by Adobe Inc. to view, create, manipulate, print, and manage Portable Document Format (PDF) files. The family comprises Acrobat Reader (formerly Adobe Reader), which is free, and the commercial Acrobat Standard and Acrobat Pro editions, which add authoring, editing, conversion, and security features. Acrobat was launched on June 15, 1993, together with the PDF format itself, as part of co-founder John Warnock's "Camelot" project for device-independent document exchange.

History

Early versions of Acrobat were commercial products, and the reader (Acrobat Reader) initially cost money as well; Adobe soon made the reader free, a strategy that helped PDF become the dominant format for electronic document distribution. Successive versions added support for forms (Acrobat Forms / FDF), digital signatures, commenting and review workflows, accessibility tagging, preflight tools for print production, and the ability to convert PDFs to and from Microsoft Office formats. In 2008 PDF was standardized as ISO 32000, and Adobe progressively opened the format while retaining Acrobat as its flagship implementation. The product line shifted to the subscription-based Document Cloud model in 2015 (Acrobat DC, "DC" for Document Cloud), continuous updates replacing numbered releases, alongside mobile apps and a browser-based version.

Features

Acrobat Reader can display, print, annotate, and fill in PDF documents and forms. The paid editions can create PDFs from almost any printable application, edit text and images within PDFs, combine files, apply OCR to scanned pages, redact sensitive content, encrypt and password-protect files, apply digital signatures, and prepare documents meeting accessibility and archival (PDF/A) standards. Acrobat integrates with Adobe's cloud storage and with third-party services for sending, tracking, and collecting signatures via Adobe Acrobat Sign.

Security

Because of its ubiquity, Acrobat has historically been a frequent target for exploits embedded in malicious PDFs, prompting Adobe to adopt a regular patch cycle (quarterly, later monthly alongside Microsoft's) and to add sandboxing ("Protected Mode") in Reader X (2010). Support for the embedded JavaScript engine in PDFs remains a common attack vector, and administrators often disable it in hardened environments.
