Naval Reactors

Full name: Naval Nuclear Propulsion Program
Founded: 1948 (as a joint Navy–AEC programme)
Associated with: U.S. Department of Energy / National Nuclear Security Administration and U.S. Navy
Long-serving director: Admiral Hyman G. Rickover (1949–1982)

Naval Reactors (NR) is the United States government office responsible for all aspects of naval nuclear propulsion, including the design, construction, operation, and safety of the reactors that power the Navy's submarines and aircraft carriers. It is an unusual dual agency: part of the National Nuclear Security Administration within the Department of Energy, and simultaneously an office of the United States Navy.

History

The programme began after the Second World War, when Captain (later Admiral) Hyman G. Rickover championed nuclear propulsion for submarines. Under his direction the land-based prototype S1W reactor was built in Idaho, leading to USS Nautilus, the world's first nuclear-powered vessel, which got underway on nuclear power in January 1955 with the famous signal "Underway on nuclear power". Reactors followed for surface ships, including the carrier USS Enterprise and the cruiser USS Long Beach.

Role and culture

Naval Reactors oversees reactor design by its prime contractor laboratories (Knolls Atomic Power Laboratory and Bettis Atomic Power Laboratory), trains all nuclear operators through the Naval Nuclear Power Training Command and prototype plants, and sets exacting standards for personnel selection and engineering practice. The director serves an exceptionally long eight-year term and holds the rank of four-star admiral. The programme is noted for its record of reactor safety: no U.S. naval reactor accident involving core damage has occurred, a record attributed to Rickover's conservative engineering culture.
