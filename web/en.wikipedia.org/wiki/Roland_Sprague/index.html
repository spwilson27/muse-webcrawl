Roland Percival Sprague (11 July 1894 – 1 August 1967) was a German mathematician best known for the Sprague–Grundy theorem in combinatorial game theory, which he published in 1935, independently and a few years before Patrick Michael Grundy's 1939 proof.

Roland Sprague, co-discoverer of the Sprague–Grundy theorem.

Life and work

Sprague was born in Unterliederbach (now part of Frankfurt am Main). In his 1935 paper "Über mathematische Kampfspiele" ("On mathematical combat games"), published in the Tohoku Mathematical Journal, he showed that every impartial game under the normal play convention is equivalent to a nim-heap of a certain size — the result now called the Grundy value or nimber of the game — thereby generalising the theory of Nim to all impartial games. He also contributed work on the squaring of the square: his 1939 paper showed how to dissect a square into distinct smaller squares using a construction related to electrical networks, a problem he pursued alongside R. L. Brooks, C. A. B. Smith, A. H. Stone and W. T. Tutte.

Sprague was a grandson of the English mathematician and game inventor Thomas Pennington Kirkman on his mother's side of the family line recorded in his biography, and spent his career largely outside academic mathematics, working in business in Berlin.
