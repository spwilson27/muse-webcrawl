Ruby Ridge

Ruby Ridge standoff
DateAugust 21–31, 1992

Ruby Ridge is a mountain ridge in northern Idaho that was the site of an eleven-day siege in August 1992 between federal law-enforcement officers and the family of Randy Weaver, a former Green Beret and survivalist. The confrontation left three people dead and became a defining controversy over federal use of force, later linked in public debate to the Waco siege of 1993.

Background and siege

Weaver, who had moved his family to a remote cabin to live apart from society, was indicted after an ATF informant said he had sold him sawn-off shotguns; Weaver believed the government sought to recruit him to infiltrate the Aryan Nations and refused to appear in court (a letter gave a wrong date). On August 21, 1992, a reconnaissance by US Marshals turned into a firefight in which Marshal William Degan and Weaver's 14-year-old son Sammy were killed. The FBI Hostage Rescue Team deployed under rules of engagement later ruled unconstitutional; on August 22 FBI sniper Lon Horiuchi fired two shots, wounding Weaver and Kevin Harris and killing Weaver's wife Vicki as she stood in the cabin doorway holding her infant daughter. Civilian negotiator Bo Gritz helped end the standoff on August 31.

Aftermath

At trial in 1993, Weaver was convicted only of failing to appear and a bail violation; Harris was acquitted of all charges, and defence lawyer Gerry Spence attacked the government's conduct. Senate hearings in 1995 criticised the FBI's rules of engagement and led to reforms; the government settled the Weavers' wrongful-death suit for $3.1 million, and an Idaho prosecutor charged Horiuchi with manslaughter, a case ultimately dropped. Ruby Ridge remains a rallying reference in debates about government overreach and the American militia movement.
