Ingleborough

Ingleborough
Elevation: 723 m (2,372 ft)
Prominence: 428 m
Location: Yorkshire Dales, England
Range: Pennines

Ingleborough is the second-highest mountain in the Yorkshire Dales, England, at 723 metres (2,372 ft) above sea level. It is one of the Yorkshire Three Peaks, along with Whernside and Pen-y-ghent, and forms part of the Three Peaks Challenge walking route.

Geography

The mountain lies in the west of the Yorkshire Dales National Park, near the village of Ingleton. Its distinctive stepped profile results from alternating bands of Carboniferous limestone, shale, and gritstone: a cap of millstone grit crowns a broad limestone plateau. Notable features on its flanks include the limestone pavements of the Ingleborough National Nature Reserve, Gaping Gill pothole, and White Scar Cave.

History

The summit plateau is ringed by the remains of a large Iron Age hill fort, with stone ramparts enclosing about 6 hectares, once containing hut circles. The name is thought to derive from Old English burh, a fortified place.

Access

Popular ascent routes start from Ingleton, Clapham, Chapel-le-Dale, and Horton-in-Ribblesdale. The summit has a trig point, a wind shelter, and extensive views over Morecambe Bay and the Lake District.
