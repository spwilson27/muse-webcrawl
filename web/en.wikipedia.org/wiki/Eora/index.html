Eora

The Eora (also spelled Iora or Yura) are an Aboriginal Australian people of the Sydney region. "Eora" derives from a Dharug-language word meaning "people" or "from this place". The name has been used broadly for the coastal Aboriginal groups around Sydney, whose lands extended roughly between the Hawkesbury River, the Nepean River, and the Georges River, although the term's precise ethnographic status — whether it denoted a single group or a language — is debated.

People and language

The Eora spoke dialects of the Dharug language. They comprised numerous clans, each identified with a place and a suffix -gal ("people of"): among them the Cadigal around Sydney Cove, the Wanegal, the Wallumedegal, and the Borogegal. Notable individuals at the time of colonisation included Bennelong, a Wangal man who acted as an intermediary with the British, and Barangaroo, a Cameragal woman.

Contact and colonisation

The Eora were the first Aboriginal people to encounter the First Fleet and the penal settlement founded at Sydney Cove in 1788 under Governor Arthur Phillip. Initial exchanges were followed by conflict over land and resources, resistance led in part by figures such as Pemulwuy (a Bidjigal man), and a devastating smallpox epidemic in 1789 that killed a large proportion of the population. Dispossession continued through the 19th century, yet Eora descendants and community members remain in Sydney today, and Eora is commemorated in names such as the Eora Centre for visual and performing arts and in the phrase "Eora Nation" used in acknowledgements of country.
