Galcanezumab

Galcanezumab, sold under the brand name Emgality, is a humanised monoclonal antibody used for the prevention of migraine in adults and for the treatment of episodic cluster headache. It was developed by Eli Lilly and Company and approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2018.

Emgality is given as a monthly subcutaneous injection.

Mechanism of action

Galcanezumab binds to calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), a neuropeptide involved in migraine pathophysiology, preventing it from activating its receptor. It belongs to the first class of preventive migraine therapies designed specifically around CGRP biology, alongside erenumab, fremanezumab, and eptinezumab.

Medical use

For migraine prevention, galcanezumab is given subcutaneously once a month, after a double loading dose, using a prefilled pen or syringe. In clinical trials (EVOLVE-1, EVOLVE-2, and REGAIN), it reduced monthly migraine days by roughly half in a substantial share of patients with episodic or chronic migraine. It was subsequently approved for episodic cluster headache, the first drug approved for that indication.

Side effects

The most common side effects are injection-site reactions, including pain, redness, and itching; hypersensitivity reactions can occur. As an IgG antibody, it is cleared by proteolysis rather than hepatic metabolism.
