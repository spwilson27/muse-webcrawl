Kodály method

The Kodály method, also referred to as the Kodály concept, is an approach to music education developed in Hungary during the mid-20th century by Zoltán Kodály (1882–1967). His philosophy of education served as the basis for the method, which has been adapted and used in many countries.

Principles

Kodály believed that music education should begin early and that singing should be the foundation of musical learning, since the voice is the most accessible instrument. The method uses folk songs from the child's own culture as teaching material, movable-do solfège, rhythm syllables, and hand signs adapted from the work of John Curwen. Musical concepts are introduced in a carefully sequenced, experience-based order moving from the known to the unknown.

Hand signs and solfège

Each scale degree is associated with a distinctive hand position, giving students a visual and kinetic representation of pitch relationships. Rhythm durations are spoken with syllables such as "ta" for quarter notes and "ti-ti" for paired eighth notes.

Influence

The Kodály method influenced music curricula worldwide, particularly in Hungary, where music-focused primary schools were established from 1950 onward, and was recognized by UNESCO with the inscription of the Kodály concept on its Register of Good Safeguarding Practices for intangible cultural heritage in 2016.
