In the Slavic religious tradition, the domovoy (Russian: домовой; Ukrainian: домовик, domovyk) is the household spirit of a given kin. The term literally means "[he] of the house". The domovoy is the guardian and sometimes trickster spirit of the home, believed to protect the family, its animals, and its property, provided he is treated with respect and receives small offerings of food.

Description

The domovoy is usually described as a small, hairy old man with a long grey beard, sometimes wearing the clothes of the household's master and sometimes said to resemble the head of the family or a deceased ancestor. He lives behind the stove, under the threshold, or in the attic, and is rarely seen, though his presence may be heard at night. A feminine counterpart, the domovikha or kikimora, is sometimes said to inhabit the house as well.

Behaviour

A contented domovoy helps with chores, tends the livestock, and warns the family of danger — his groans or weeping might portend a death in the household, while laughter and music foretold good fortune. An offended domovoy could become a nuisance: hiding objects, making noises at night, or tormenting the cattle. Families leaving a house would ritually invite the domovoy to accompany them to their new home. Offerings traditionally consisted of bread, salt, milk, or porridge left out overnight.

Related spirits

The domovoy belongs to a wider family of Slavic household and place spirits, including the bannik of the bathhouse, the ovinnik of the threshing barn, and the dvorovoy of the farmyard. Comparable beings appear across European folklore, such as the English brownie and the Scandinavian tomte.
