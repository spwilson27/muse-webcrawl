Piazza di Spagna

The Piazza di Spagna with the Spanish Steps rising to Trinità dei Monti.

The Piazza di Spagna is one of the most famous squares in Rome, Italy. It owes its name to the Palazzo di Spagna, the seat of the Embassy of Spain to the Holy See, which has been located there since the 17th century. The square lies at the foot of the Spanish Steps in the historic centre of the city, within the Campo Marzio district.

Monuments and places of interest

  • The Spanish Steps (Scalinata di Trinità dei Monti) — the monumental stairway of 135 steps, built in 1723–1725 to designs by Francesco de Sanctis, linking the square to the church of Trinità dei Monti at the top of the Pincian Hill.
  • Fontana della Barcaccia — the "Fountain of the Ugly Boat", a Baroque fountain by Pietro Bernini and his son Gian Lorenzo Bernini (1627–1629), shaped like a half-sunken ship.
  • Colonna dell'Immacolata — the Column of the Immaculate Conception (1857), topped by a statue of the Virgin Mary.
  • Keats–Shelley Memorial House — at the foot of the steps, where the poet John Keats died in 1821, now a museum.
  • Babington's tea room — founded in 1893 and still open.

The square is also a centre of luxury shopping: the Via dei Condotti, lined with fashion houses, leads away from it toward Via del Corso. In spring the steps are traditionally decorated with azaleas.
