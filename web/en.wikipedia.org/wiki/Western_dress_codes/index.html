Western dress codes

Western dress codes are a set of social conventions governing clothing for occasions in Western culture, ranging from the most formal ceremonial dress to everyday casual wear. The principal categories, in descending order of formality, are formal wear, semi-formal wear, informal wear (business suits), business casual and casual wear.

Formal wear

Formal dress comprises white tie (tailcoat, white waistcoat and white bow tie for men; full-length evening gowns for women), worn for state occasions, balls and certain ceremonies, and black tie (dinner jacket/tuxedo with black bow tie; evening dress). Morning dress — morning coat, waistcoat and striped trousers — is formal day wear for weddings and royal events in Britain.

Semi-formal and informal

Semi-formal attire includes cocktail dress and the lounge suit; "black tie optional" invitations sit here. Informal wear in the dress-code sense means a matching business suit and tie, distinct from everyday casual clothing. Business casual — trousers or chinos with a collared shirt, typically no tie — became standard office dress in many industries from the 1990s.

Casual

Casual wear encompasses jeans, T-shirts, sportswear and sneakers. Invitations may specify gradations such as "smart casual" or "dressy casual". Dress codes have generally relaxed over the 20th and 21st centuries, though formal codes persist for courts, legislatures, the military and ceremonial occasions.
