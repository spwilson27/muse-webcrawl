Servile Wars

The Servile Wars (Latin: Bella Servilia) were a series of three slave revolts in the late Roman Republic, each of which required substantial Roman military effort to suppress. They reflected the strains created by the massive influx of enslaved prisoners of war who worked the great estates (latifundia) of Sicily and Italy.

First Servile War (135–132 BC)

The first revolt broke out in Sicily under the Syrian slave Eunus, who took the royal name Antiochus, and the Cilician Cleon. The rebels seized Enna and other towns and defeated several Roman armies before the consul Publius Rupilius finally reduced their strongholds.

Second Servile War (104–100 BC)

Again in Sicily, slaves rose under Salvius, who took the name Tryphon, and Athenion. The revolt was suppressed by the consul Manius Aquillius after hard campaigning.

Third Servile War (73–71 BC)

The third and most famous revolt was led by the Thracian gladiator Spartacus, who escaped from a gladiatorial school at Capua with some seventy followers. His army of escaped slaves defeated multiple Roman forces and ranged over Italy for two years before being destroyed by Marcus Licinius Crassus; Pompey annihilated the remnants, and some 6,000 captured slaves were crucified along the Appian Way.
