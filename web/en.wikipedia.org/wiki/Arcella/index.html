Arcella is a genus of testate amoebae in the phylum Amoebozoa. Its species live in a dome- or umbrella-shaped shell (test) that the cell secretes from proteinaceous material, with a single central ventral opening through which finger-like pseudopods extend for feeding and movement.

Description

The test is typically transparent to brownish and ranges from about 50 to 200 micrometres across. Arcella inhabit freshwater ponds, sphagnum mosses and damp soils, feeding on bacteria, algae and organic detritus. They are classic subjects in introductory microscopy and indicators of water quality.

Common species

Well-known species include Arcella vulgaris, A. discoides and A. hemisphaerica.
