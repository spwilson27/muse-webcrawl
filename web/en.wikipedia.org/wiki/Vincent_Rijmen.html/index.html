Vincent Rijmen

Vincent Rijmen (born 16 October 1970) is a Belgian cryptographer and one of the two designers of the Rijndael block cipher, which was selected as the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) by the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in 2000. Rijndael was developed together with Joan Daemen, with whom Rijmen has also worked on many other designs.

Biography

Rijmen was born in Leuven, Belgium. He received a degree in electronics engineering from the Katholieke Universiteit Leuven in 1993 and a Ph.D. in 1997, both from the same university, where he worked in the Computer Security and Industrial Cryptography (COSIC) research group. His doctoral advisor was Joos Vandewalle.

After his Ph.D., Rijmen continued working at COSIC, often in collaboration with Joan Daemen. One of their joint papers introduced the design strategy that became known as the wide trail strategy, which underlies the structure of Rijndael. In 2001 he joined Proton World as chief cryptographer, and later worked at Cryptomathic. He has also been a professor at the Katholieke Universiteit Leuven and at Graz University of Technology in Austria.

Work

Rijmen's best-known work is the Rijndael cipher, submitted to the AES process in 1998 and selected in 2000 as the successor to the Data Encryption Standard. Other designs he contributed to include the SHARK and Square block ciphers, the Whirlpool hash function, and the KHAZAD and Anubis ciphers developed in the NESSIE project.

He has published widely on cryptanalysis and on the design of symmetric-key primitives, including work on related-key attacks and the algebraic structure of block ciphers.
