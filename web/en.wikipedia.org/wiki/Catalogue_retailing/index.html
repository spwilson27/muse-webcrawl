Catalogue retailing

Catalogue retailing is a form of retail in which customers select goods from a printed or digital catalogue and order by mail, telephone or in a showroom, rather than taking goods directly from shop shelves. Variants include mail-order houses and catalogue showrooms, where display items are viewed in store but purchased stock is fetched from a back warehouse.

History

The model flourished from the late 19th century with firms such as Sears, Roebuck and Montgomery Ward in the United States and Littlewoods and Great Universal Stores in Britain. Argos, founded in 1972, became the leading British catalogue showroom chain. E-commerce from the 1990s absorbed the model, as printed catalogues gave way to websites offering the same order-and-collect logistics.
