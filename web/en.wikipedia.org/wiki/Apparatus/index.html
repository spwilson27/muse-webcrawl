Apparatus

Apparatus (plural: apparatus or apparatuses) is a general term for a set of materials, instruments, equipment, or machinery assembled for a particular purpose. The word derives from the Latin apparatus, meaning "equipment" or "preparation," and is used wherever a complex arrangement of tools serves a function rather than a single device.

Uses of the term

In science, "apparatus" commonly denotes laboratory equipment — from distillation setups and vacuum chambers to the Golgi apparatus, a cellular organelle named for its structural appearance. In gymnastics, apparatus refers to the equipment on which routines are performed, such as the pommel horse, rings, and balance beam; artistic gymnastics competition is divided into apparatus finals. In politics and sociology, the term describes the organizational machinery of a state or party — the bureaucracy and institutions through which power is exercised — a sense popularized by writers such as Franz Kafka and by theorists of the "state apparatus." In textual scholarship, a critical apparatus (apparatus criticus) is the apparatus of notes listing variant readings at the foot of an edited text.
