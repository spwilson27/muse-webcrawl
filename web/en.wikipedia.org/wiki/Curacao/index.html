Curacao (properly spelled Curaçao) is a Lesser Antilles island in the southern Caribbean Sea, about 65 kilometres north of the Venezuelan coast. It is a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands, having gained that status on 10 October 2010 when the Netherlands Antilles was dissolved. Its capital, Willemstad, is noted for its colourful Dutch colonial architecture; the historic area of Willemstad is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Geography and history

The island covers about 444 square kilometres and has a dry, savanna-like climate lying outside the main hurricane belt. Originally inhabited by Arawak peoples, it was claimed by Spain in 1499 and seized by the Dutch West India Company in 1634. It became an important trading entrepôt—including, notoriously, in the transatlantic slave trade—and in the twentieth century a major oil-refining centre serving Venezuelan crude. Its population, of roughly 150,000, is of mixed African, European, and other descent, and the creole language Papiamento is widely spoken.

The liqueur

The name also refers to curaçao liqueur, flavoured with the dried peel of the Laraha citrus fruit grown on the island, a descendant of the bitter Seville oranges introduced by the Spanish. Blue curaçao, coloured a vivid blue, is a common cocktail ingredient.
