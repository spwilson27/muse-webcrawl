Coupé

A coupé or coupe (from the French verb couper, "to cut") is a passenger car with a sloping or truncated rear roofline and, traditionally, two doors. The term was first applied to horse-drawn carriages for two passengers without forward-facing seats in the rear.

History and definition

The earliest coupés were closed carriages derived from the berline, cut down to a single row of seats. When the term transferred to automobiles in the early twentieth century, it denoted a two-door body style, often with a single bench seat or a small occasional rear seat. International standards have variously defined a coupé by a fixed roof, and a rear interior volume below a specified size.

Since the 1960s the term has been used loosely for stylish two-door cars, and manufacturers have marketed four-door cars and even crossovers as "four-door coupés" or "coupé SUVs" when they have a sloping rear roofline, a usage that purists dispute.

Variants

  • Club coupé — a two-door car with a larger rear seating area.
  • Business coupé — a basic two-door model with a storage area instead of a rear seat, popular in the United States in the 1930s and 1940s.
  • Opéra coupé — fitted with small folding jump seats.
  • Three-window and five-window coupés — hot rod terminology counting the side and rear windows.
