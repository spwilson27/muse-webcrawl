Hamilton Mausoleum

The Hamilton Mausoleum is a mausoleum in Hamilton, South Lanarkshire, Scotland. It was built as the tomb of Alexander Douglas-Hamilton, 10th Duke of Hamilton (1767–1852), one of Scotland's wealthiest landowners, and stands in what were the grounds of the now-demolished Hamilton Palace. One of the most elaborate mausoleums in Britain, it is a domed rotunda about 37 metres (121 ft) high, designed by the architect David Hamilton and completed after his death by David Bryce and others; construction ran from 1842 to 1858.

Description

The building combines a tall domed chapel above with a crypt below, entered through bronze doors modelled on those of the Baptistery in Florence. Its interior is famed for its acoustics: the dome produces one of the longest echoes of any building in the world — a whisper or a slammed door reverberates for many seconds. The 10th Duke, noted for his pride and his rivalry with other collectors, was interred in an ancient Egyptian sarcophagus he had acquired for himself.

History

Hamilton Palace was demolished in the 1920s–30s after coal mining undermined the area and the family sold its contents. Because of subsidence, the remains of the dukes were removed from the mausoleum's crypt in 1921 and reburied in Hamilton's Bent Cemetery. The mausoleum, a category A listed building, was restored in the 21st century and is open to visitors on guided tours.
