Coregonus hoyi

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Actinopterygii
Order: Salmoniformes
Family: Salmonidae
Genus: Coregonus
Species: C. hoyi

Coregonus hoyi, commonly known as the bloater, is a species of freshwater whitefish in the family Salmonidae native to the Great Lakes of North America. It was described by James Ellsworth De Kay in 1842 and named for Charles F. Hoy. The common name refers to the way the fish swells when brought rapidly to the surface from deep water, as its swim bladder expands.

Description

The bloater is a silvery fish with a pale underside and a moderately deep body, reaching about 28 cm (11 in) in length. It has a small head with a slightly projecting lower jaw and fine gill rakers suited to filtering zooplankton from the water column.

Distribution and habitat

Bloaters inhabit deep, cold waters of lakes Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie and Ontario, typically at depths of 30 to 100 metres or more. They feed mainly on small crustaceans such as Mysis and amphipods and are in turn eaten by lake trout and burbot.

Fisheries and status

Bloaters were once an important commercial catch in the Great Lakes, sold fresh, smoked, and as "smoked chubs." Populations crashed in the mid-20th century because of overfishing, competition and predation involving introduced alewife and rainbow smelt, and sea lamprey predation. Numbers have since partially recovered, especially in Lake Michigan, where the bloater is again a significant prey fish and a modest commercial fishery exists. The species is assessed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List in some assessments of Great Lakes ciscoes.
