Tomás Luis de Victoria

Bornc. 1548, Ávila, Spain
Died27 August 1611, Madrid, Spain
OccupationComposer, organist, choirmaster, Catholic priest
EraRenaissance / Counter-Reformation

Tomás Luis de Victoria (sometimes Italianised as Tommaso Luigi da Vittoria; c. 1548 – 27 August 1611) was the most famous Spanish composer of the Renaissance and, alongside Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina and Orlande de Lassus, one of the great masters of late Renaissance polyphony. He devoted himself exclusively to sacred music and was also an organist and a priest.

Life

Born near Ávila, the seventh of eleven children, Victoria sang as a choirboy at Ávila Cathedral. Around 1565 he went to Rome to study at the Collegium Germanicum, where he may have been a pupil of Palestrina, whom he succeeded as maestro at the Roman Seminary. He held posts at several Roman churches and was ordained a priest in 1575. In 1587 he returned to Spain as chaplain to the Dowager Empress María, sister of Philip II, at the convent of Las Descalzas Reales in Madrid, serving as her choirmaster and later organist until his death in 1611.

Works

Victoria published twenty masses, among them the celebrated Missa pro defunctis (Requiem) of 1603 and the Officium Defunctorum of 1605, written for the Empress's funeral and often considered his masterpiece. His motets — including O magnum mysterium, O quam gloriosum, Ave Maria and the Holy Week responsories of the Officium Hebdomadae Sanctae (1585) — are admired for their intense, mystically expressive character, frequently linked to the Spanish spirituality of his age. Unusually for his time, he published his works in handsome editions throughout his life and never wrote secular music.
