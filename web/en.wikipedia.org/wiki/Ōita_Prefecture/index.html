Ōita Prefecture

Ōita Prefecture (Japanese: 大分県, Ōita-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located on the island of Kyushu, facing the Seto Inland Sea. Its capital is the city of Ōita. The prefecture is famed throughout Japan for its hot springs (onsen), particularly the resort towns of Beppu and Yufuin.

Geography

Ōita occupies the northeastern corner of Kyushu. Much of the prefecture is mountainous and forested, including parts of the Aso-Kuju and Setonaikai national parks. The Kunisaki Peninsula projects into the Seto Inland Sea in the northeast. Rivers such as the Ōita and Ōno have carved fertile plains along the coast, where most of the population lives.

History

The prefecture corresponds largely to the former provinces of Bungo and part of Buzen. In the 16th century the Ōtomo clan ruled from Funai (modern Ōita City); Ōtomo Sōrin welcomed Portuguese traders and Jesuit missionaries, making the region an early centre of Western contact — the local Christian lord Ōmura Sumitada in neighbouring Hizen was part of the same milieu. Francis Xavier visited in 1551. The prefecture took its present form in the Meiji era.

Hot springs

Ōita has the largest number of hot spring sources and the greatest volume of hot spring water of any prefecture in Japan. Beppu alone has thousands of sources; its "Eight Hells" (jigoku) — vividly coloured boiling ponds such as the blood-red Chinoike Jigoku — are a major tourist attraction. Yufuin, beneath Mount Yufu, is a smaller, fashionable resort town.

Economy and culture

Industry is concentrated along the coast at Ōita City, with steel, petrochemicals, semiconductors and the CANON and other electronics plants. Agriculture includes shiitake mushrooms — Ōita is a leading producer — and kabosu citrus. The prefecture hosted matches of the 2002 FIFA World Cup and the 2019 Rugby World Cup at its Ōita Stadium ("Big Eye"). The Usuki Stone Buddhas, carved into cliffs in the late Heian period, are designated a National Treasure.
