Closed list

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Closed list describes the variant of party-list proportional representation in which voters cast a vote for a political party without any influence over the order in which the party's candidates are elected. Each party publishes a ranked list of candidates before the election; if the party wins, say, ten seats, the first ten candidates on its list are elected. The system contrasts with the open list, where personal votes reorder the list.

Characteristics

Closed lists maximise the role of the party: candidate selection and list placement are controlled by party organs, which can use them to ensure the election of experts, balance gender or regional representation, and enforce cohesion. Critics argue that closed lists make representatives accountable upward to party leadership rather than to voters, and that voters cannot remove an unpopular individual candidate. Seat allocation between parties uses divisor methods or quotas such as the Droop quota.

Usage

Closed lists are used in many party-list systems, including Spain, Portugal, Israel, South Africa, Argentina, Russia and (in a regional-list form) for devolved and European elections in Great Britain. Some systems, such as Germany's mixed-member proportional system, combine a party list tier with constituency seats; in New Zealand's MMP the list tier is closed, though the overall result remains proportional — see electoral system of New Zealand and additional-member system.
