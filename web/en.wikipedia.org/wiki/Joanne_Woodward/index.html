Joanne Woodward

Born: Joanne Gignilliat Woodward, 27 February 1930, Thomasville, Georgia, U.S.

Occupation: Actress

Years active: 1952–present

Spouse: Paul Newman (m. 1958; died 2008)

Joanne Gignilliat Woodward (born February 27, 1930) is an American actress. One of the last surviving major stars of the Golden Age of Hollywood, she won the Academy Award for Best Actress for The Three Faces of Eve (1957) and was nominated three further times, for Rachel, Rachel (1968), Summer Wishes, Winter Dreams (1973), and Mr. & Mrs. Bridge (1990).

Career

Woodward studied at the Actors Studio and began in television before her film breakthrough. She was the first performer to win an Academy Award for playing three different personalities in a single role, portraying a woman with dissociative identity disorder in The Three Faces of Eve. She frequently collaborated with her husband, Paul Newman, who directed her in Rachel, Rachel and The Glass Menagerie (1987), among other films.

Personal life and philanthropy

Woodward married Newman in 1958; their marriage lasted fifty years until his death in 2008 and was considered one of Hollywood's most enduring. Together they founded Newman's Own, the food company whose profits go to charity, and the Hole in the Wall Gang Camp for seriously ill children. Woodward later served as artistic director of the Westport Country Playhouse in Connecticut. In 2022 she was the subject, with Newman, of the documentary series The Last Movie Stars.
