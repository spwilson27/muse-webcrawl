The Old Mill

Series: Silly Symphonies
Directed by: Wilfred Jackson
Produced by: Walt Disney
Released: 5 November 1937

The Old Mill is a 1937 American animated short film produced by Walt Disney Productions and released by RKO Radio Pictures. Part of the Silly Symphonies series, it depicts a community of animals — birds, mice, owls, and frogs — sheltering in an abandoned windmill as a violent storm breaks over it at night.

Significance

The film is celebrated as a landmark of animation technique. It was the first Disney cartoon to use the multiplane camera, which moved artwork on separate glass layers to create a convincing illusion of depth, and it showcased advances in the realistic animation of water, rain, lightning, and animal behaviour. These experiments directly prepared the studio for its first feature, Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs, released weeks later.

Awards and legacy

The Old Mill won the Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film at the 10th Academy Awards. Its mood-driven, story-light approach influenced later Disney nature pieces, and a homage to the mill appears in the Disney parks and in subsequent films.
