Bayan (accordion)

The bayan (Russian: баян, IPA: [bɐˈjan]) is a type of chromatic button accordion developed in the Russian Empire and Soviet Union since the early 20th century. It is named after the 11th-century bard Boyan. The bayan differs from the Western button accordion in its range, its construction, and its systems of bass buttons.

A Russian bayan with its characteristic rows of small round treble buttons.

Characteristics

The bayan has a much wider range than Western accordions, a different tuning (typically drier, with little tremolo), and buttons that are smaller, recessed into the soundboard, and arranged in three to five rows on the treble side. Two bass systems are used: the older Stradella system and the free-bass converter system, which allows the left hand to play independent melodic lines in a full chromatic range. These features let the bayan perform transcriptions of classical keyboard and orchestral music as well as folk repertoire.

History

The instrument emerged around 1907, when the harmonica maker Vasily Beloborodov and the player Orlandsky developed a chromatic button instrument in Russia. In the Soviet era the bayan was standardised, manufactured in large factories such as those at Tula, and taught in conservatories, where virtuosi elevated it to a concert instrument. The instrument is central to Russian, Ukrainian, and other East European folk ensembles, and its repertoire now includes original works by composers such as Sofia Gubaidulina and Vladislav Zolotaryov.
