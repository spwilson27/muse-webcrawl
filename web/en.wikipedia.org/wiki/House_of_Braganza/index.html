House of Braganza

The House of Braganza (Portuguese: Casa de Bragança) is a Portuguese and Brazilian dynasty that ruled the Kingdom of Portugal from 1640 to 1910 and the Empire of Brazil from 1822 to 1889. It originated as a cadet branch of the House of Aviz.

Origins

The house descends from Afonso, 1st Duke of Braganza (1377–1461), an illegitimate son of John I of Portugal. The dukes of Braganza became the kingdom's wealthiest and most powerful noble family, and in 1640 — during the Portuguese Restoration War against Spain — John, 8th Duke, was acclaimed King John IV of Portugal.

Portugal

Braganza monarchs reigned until the abolition of the monarchy on 5 October 1910. Notable rulers include John V, Joseph I (whose minister was the Marquis of Pombal), Maria I, John VI, Pedro IV, Maria II, and the last king, Manuel II. In 1826 the senior line briefly held the Brazilian and Portuguese crowns together under Pedro.

Brazil

When Pedro, prince royal, declared Brazilian independence in 1822, he became Emperor Pedro I, founding the Brazilian branch. His son Pedro II reigned until the proclamation of the republic in 1889. The Brazilian line merged with Orléans-Braganza descendants.

Today

The current head of the Portuguese house, Duarte Pio, Duke of Braganza, is the recognized claimant to the defunct Portuguese throne.
