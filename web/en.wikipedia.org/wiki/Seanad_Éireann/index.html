Seanad Éireann (the Senate of Ireland) is the upper house of the Oireachtas, Ireland's national parliament, the lower house being Dáil Éireann. It consists of 60 senators (Seanadóirí) and sits at Leinster House in Dublin. Its powers are considerably weaker than those of the Dáil: it may delay and amend legislation but cannot ultimately block bills passed by the lower house.

Composition

Senators are not directly elected by the general public. Eleven are nominated by the Taoiseach. Forty-three are elected by panels representing vocational interests — culture and education, agriculture, labour, industry and commerce, and public administration — from an electorate of TDs, outgoing senators and councillors. Six are elected by university graduates: three by the National University of Ireland and three by the University of Dublin (Trinity College).

History and reform

A senate existed in the Irish Free State (1922–1936) before its abolition, and the present Seanad was established by the Constitution of Ireland in 1937. Its composition and electoral system have long been criticised, and a 2013 referendum proposing its abolition was narrowly defeated, after which governments committed to reform rather than abolition.
