Isentropic efficiency

Isentropic efficiency is a measure used in thermodynamics to assess the performance of processes in devices such as turbines, compressors, nozzles and pumps. It compares the actual performance of a device to the performance that would be achieved under idealized isentropic (reversible adiabatic) conditions for the same inlet and exit states.

An isentropic process on a temperature-entropy diagram.

Definition

The efficiency is defined differently depending on whether the device produces work (such as a turbine) or consumes work (such as a compressor or pump). For a turbine, the isentropic efficiency is the ratio of the actual work output to the work output that would be obtained if the expansion were isentropic between the same pressures:

ηturbine = (h1 − h2a) / (h1 − h2s),

where h1 is the enthalpy at the inlet, h2a the actual enthalpy at the exit, and h2s the enthalpy at the exit for an isentropic process.

For a compressor, the efficiency is the ratio of the isentropic work input to the actual work input:

ηcompressor = (h2s − h1) / (h2a − h1).

Applications

Typical isentropic efficiencies range from about 70–90% for well-designed turbines and compressors. The concept is widely used in the analysis of gas turbine cycles, steam power plants and refrigeration systems, where departures from the ideal cycle are caused by irreversibilities such as friction and turbulence.
