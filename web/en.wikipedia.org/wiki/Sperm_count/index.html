Sperm count

Sperm count is the number of spermatozoa in a given volume of semen, usually expressed as concentration (million sperm per millilitre) or as the total number in an ejaculate. It is a central parameter of semen analysis, used in evaluating male fertility. Low sperm count is termed oligospermia; the complete absence of sperm in the ejaculate is azoospermia.

Reference values

The World Health Organization's laboratory manual for semen analysis gives a lower reference limit of about 16 million sperm per millilitre (or 39 million per ejaculate) based on fertile populations, in its 2021 sixth edition. Counts are highly variable between individuals and between samples from the same person, and are assessed together with motility, morphology, and ejaculate volume.

Factors affecting count

Sperm production (spermatogenesis) takes roughly 64–74 days in humans and is sensitive to heat, illness and fever, varicocele, hormonal disorders, certain medications, chemotherapy, heavy alcohol use, smoking, obesity, and environmental exposures. Abstinence interval also affects measured concentration and total count.

Reported declines

Meta-analyses by Levine and colleagues (2017, updated 2022) reported substantial declines in average sperm concentration among men from Western countries over recent decades, and suggested the decline may be broadening geographically and accelerating; the causes and clinical significance remain debated among researchers.
