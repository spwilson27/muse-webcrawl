G&L Musical Instruments is an American guitar manufacturing company founded by Leo Fender and George Fullerton in 1980. Based in Fullerton, California — in the same Fender Avenue factory associated with Leo Fender's earlier work — G&L produces electric guitars and basses that Fender regarded as the final evolution of his classic designs. The initials stand for "George and Leo".

History

After selling Fender Musical Instruments to CBS in 1965 and later co-founding Music Man, Leo Fender joined his old associate George Fullerton — a collaborator since the late 1940s — in the new venture, initially called G&L Music Sales. Leo Fender served as chairman and continued designing instruments and pickups until his death in 1991, famously working at his bench in the Fullerton factory. His widow Phyllis Fender later became honorary chair. In 1991 the company was acquired by BBE Sound, which continues to manufacture G&L instruments in Fullerton.

Designs

G&L instruments refine Fender's templates with innovations from Leo Fender's later research, including Magnetic Field Design (MFD) pickups, whose adjustable pole pieces sit in a ceramic-magnet structure for higher output and clarity; the Dual-Fulcrum Vibrato bridge, pivoting on two points for stability and smooth action; the Saddle-Lock bridge for basses and some guitars, which locks the saddles together to transfer string energy into the body; and the Passive Treble and Bass (PTB) tone system.

Models

Well-known models include the ASAT (a Telecaster-style guitar), the Legacy (a Stratocaster-style model), the Comanche, the Invader, and basses such as the L-2000 and SB-2. The more affordable Tribute series, introduced in 2003, is manufactured in Asia to G&L specifications.
