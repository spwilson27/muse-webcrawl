Philippa Foot


Philippa Ruth Foot, née Bosanquet (3 October 1920 – 3 October 2010), was a British philosopher known for inventing the trolley problem and for reviving Aristotelian virtue ethics.

Philippa Ruth Foot (3 October 1920 – 3 October 2010) was an English philosopher and one of the founders of contemporary virtue ethics, influenced by the philosophy of Aristotle. She taught for most of her career at Somerville College, Oxford, and later at the University of California, Los Angeles.

The trolley problem

In a 1967 paper, "The Problem of Abortion and the Doctrine of the Double Effect", Foot proposed the thought experiment that became the famous trolley problem: a runaway tram is heading toward five people; you may pull a lever redirecting it to another track where it will kill one person instead. Foot's original discussion concerned the doctrine of double effect and the contrast with a related case in which a surgeon might kill one patient to save five. The problem was later elaborated extensively by Judith Jarvis Thomson and became a staple of moral philosophy and, more recently, of debates about self-driving cars.

Natural goodness

Foot challenged the fact–value distinction and non-cognitivism that dominated mid-twentieth-century ethics. In her book Natural Goodness (2001) she argued that moral evaluations are a species of natural evaluation, like judgements about the roots of an oak or the hunting of a lion: human goodness is goodness of the human will, judged by the standards of the human life form. Her 1958 paper "Moral Arguments" and 1972 paper "Morality as a System of Hypothetical Imperatives" were likewise landmarks.

Foot was a granddaughter of U.S. President Grover Cleveland. She was elected a Fellow of the British Academy and was a co-founder of Oxfam's early circle of supporters in Oxford.
