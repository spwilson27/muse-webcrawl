Endocannabinoid system

The endocannabinoid system (ECS) is a biological system composed of endocannabinoids, which are endogenous lipid-based retrograde neurotransmitters that bind to cannabinoid receptors (CBRs), and cannabinoid receptor proteins that are expressed throughout the vertebrate central nervous system (including the brain) and peripheral nervous system. The ECS is still being studied, but it is known to be involved in regulating a variety of physiological and cognitive processes including fertility, pregnancy, pre- and postnatal development, various immune activities, appetite, pain sensation, mood, and memory, as well as mediating the pharmacological effects of cannabis.

The ECS modulates signalling throughout the nervous and immune systems.

Components

The ECS consists of three core components: endocannabinoids, receptors, and enzymes. The two best-characterised endocannabinoids are anandamide (N-arachidonoylethanolamine, AEA), identified in 1992 by Lumír Hanuš and William Devane in Raphael Mechoulam's laboratory, and 2-arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG), identified in 1994–95. Unlike classical neurotransmitters, endocannabinoids are synthesised on demand from membrane lipid precursors and act as retrograde messengers: released from the postsynaptic cell, they travel backwards across the synapse to suppress neurotransmitter release from the presynaptic terminal.

Receptors

The CB1 receptor, cloned in 1990, is one of the most abundant G protein-coupled receptors in the brain, concentrated in the basal ganglia, hippocampus, cerebellum and cerebral cortex; it mediates most of the psychoactive effects of tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). The CB2 receptor, cloned in 1993, is expressed mainly on immune cells and in the spleen and is involved in immunomodulation and inflammation. Other proposed targets include the orphan receptor GPR55 and the TRPV1 channel.

Enzymes

Fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH) breaks down anandamide, while monoacylglycerol lipase (MAGL) is principally responsible for degrading 2-AG. Inhibiting these enzymes raises endocannabinoid tone and has been investigated as a strategy for treating pain, anxiety and inflammatory disorders without the psychoactivity of direct CB1 agonists.

Physiological roles

Research since the 1990s has implicated the ECS in energy balance and metabolism (the origin of the "munchies" effect of cannabis), stress responses via the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis, synaptic plasticity underlying learning and memory, neuroprotection, bone remodelling, and reproductive events including implantation of the early embryo, which requires a precise anandamide gradient.
