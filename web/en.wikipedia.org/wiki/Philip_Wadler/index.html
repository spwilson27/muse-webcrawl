Philip Lee Wadler (born 8 April 1956) is an American computer scientist known for his contributions to programming language design and type theory, in particular to the functional programming language Haskell and to the theory of monads. He is Professor of Theoretical Computer Science at the University of Edinburgh and is associated with the Laboratory for Foundations of Computer Science.

Contributions

Wadler's 1992 paper "The essence of functional programming" popularised the use of monads — a structure borrowed from category theory — for structuring functional programs, structuring effects such as input/output and state in a purely functional style. He was a central figure in the design of Haskell, contributing to its type classes and list comprehensions, and co-authored the Haskell 98 report. With Martin Odersky and others he designed Generic Java, which became the generics facility of the Java programming language, and co-wrote the book Java Generics and Collections (2006).

His other work includes the theory of deforestation (listless programming) for optimising functional programs, the use of linear logic in session types for concurrent communication, and the application of the Curry–Howard correspondence — summarised in his slogan "Propositions as Types". More recently he has worked on languages for blockchains and smart contracts, including Plutus for the Cardano platform. Wadler is a Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh and an ACM Fellow, and is noted for his energetic lecturing style and his advocacy of functional programming in industry.
