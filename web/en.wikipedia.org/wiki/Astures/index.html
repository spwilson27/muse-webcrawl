Astures

The Astures were a Celtic tribe of northwestern Hispania, inhabiting the mountainous region roughly corresponding to the modern Spanish province of Asturias, as well as parts of León and northern Portugal. Their territory, known to the Romans as Asturia, was divided by the Cantabrian Mountains into Transmontane and Cismontane Astures.

History

The Astures were known for their fierce resistance to Roman expansion. During the Cantabrian Wars (29–19 BC), they fought alongside the Cantabri against the legions of Augustus. The Romans eventually subdued the region and exploited its rich gold mines, including those at Las Médulas, using the labour of the local population.

Culture

The Astures lived in fortified hill settlements called castros, practised transhumant pastoralism, and rode small sturdy horses known as asturcones. Their language and customs retained strong Celtic elements long after Romanisation.
