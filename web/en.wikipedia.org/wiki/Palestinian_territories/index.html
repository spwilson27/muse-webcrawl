The Palestinian territories (Arabic: الأراضي الفلسطينية) are the two regions of the former British Mandate for Palestine that have been occupied by Israel since the Six-Day War of 1967: the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) and the Gaza Strip. The International Court of Justice and the United Nations refer to the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and Gaza as the Occupied Palestinian Territory.

The territories are claimed by the State of Palestine, which was proclaimed in 1988 and is recognised by a majority of UN member states. Following the Oslo Accords of 1993–1995, the Palestinian Authority (PA) was established to exercise limited self-rule in parts of the West Bank and Gaza. Since the 2007 Battle of Gaza, the Gaza Strip has been administered by Hamas, while the PA, dominated by Fatah, governs the Palestinian enclaves of the West Bank.

History

From 1948 to 1967, the West Bank was annexed by Jordan and the Gaza Strip was administered by Egypt, following the 1948 Arab–Israeli War and the creation of the State of Israel. Israel captured both territories, together with the Sinai Peninsula and the Golan Heights, in the Six-Day War of June 1967. Military occupation followed, accompanied from the late 1970s onward by the construction of Israeli settlements, which the international community considers illegal under international law.

The First Intifada (1987–1993) led to the Oslo peace process. The 1993 Oslo I Accord and the 1995 Oslo II Accord divided the West Bank into Areas A (full Palestinian civil and security control), B (Palestinian civil control, joint security) and C (full Israeli control, roughly 60% of the territory). The agreements envisaged a final-status settlement within five years, which was never concluded. Israel unilaterally withdrew its settlers and ground forces from Gaza in 2005 but retained control of its airspace, coastline and most crossings.

Political status

On 29 November 2012 the UN General Assembly granted Palestine non-member observer state status. The International Criminal Court and other bodies treat "Palestine" as comprising the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and Gaza as a single territorial unit. Israel disputes Palestinian sovereignty claims and regards the territories' final status as subject to negotiation; it annexed East Jerusalem in 1980, a move not recognised internationally.

Geography

The West Bank is a landlocked territory of hills and the Jordan Valley, bordered by Israel to the north, west and south and by Jordan across the Jordan River to the east. The Gaza Strip is a narrow coastal enclave on the Mediterranean Sea, bordering Israel and Egypt's Sinai Peninsula.

Demographics

The population is overwhelmingly Palestinian Arab, predominantly Sunni Muslim with a small Christian minority. A large share are refugees of the 1948 war and their descendants, many living in camps administered with assistance from UNRWA. Israeli settlers number in the hundreds of thousands in the West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Economy

The Palestinian economy depends heavily on foreign aid, remittances, employment in Israel, and clearance revenues collected by Israel on the PA's behalf. Movement restrictions, closures and periodic conflicts have constrained growth; unemployment, especially in Gaza, is among the highest in the world.
