Sunburn

Sunburn with characteristic redness and peeling

Sunburn is a form of radiation burn that affects living tissue, such as skin, as a result of overexposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation, usually from the Sun. Described medically as erythema following exposure, sunburn ranges from mild redness to severe blistering classed as a second-degree burn. UV exposure also damages DNA in skin cells, and repeated sunburn — especially in childhood — substantially increases the risk of skin cancers including melanoma.

Signs and symptoms

Redness typically begins 3–5 hours after exposure and peaks at 12–24 hours. The skin feels hot and painful; in more severe burns, blisters form, followed by peeling after several days. Extensive sunburn can cause fever, chills, nausea and dehydration — sometimes called "sun poisoning". Healing of mild burns takes a few days; severe burns may take weeks.

Cause

UVB radiation (290–320 nm) is the principal cause of sunburn, directly damaging DNA and triggering apoptosis of keratinocytes ("sunburn cells"), while UVA contributes through oxidative damage. Susceptibility varies with skin phototype: people with fair skin, red hair and freckles burn fastest. Sunburn can also result from artificial sources such as tanning beds and welding arcs, and some medications (e.g. doxycycline) increase photosensitivity.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention relies on limiting midday sun, shade, hats and clothing, and broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher, reapplied after swimming. Treatment is symptomatic: cool compresses, moisturisers such as aloe vera, hydration and NSAID painkillers; blistered skin should be left intact. Medical care is advised for infants, extensive blistering, or signs of infection or heatstroke.
