Pope Sylvester II

Pope Sylvester II (c. 946 – 12 May 1003), born Gerbert of Aurillac, was a French scholar and teacher who served as Bishop of Rome from 999 to his death. A prolific scientist, philosopher, and mathematician, he was the first French pope and one of the most learned men of his age.

Scholarship

Gerbert studied in Catalonia, where he encountered Arabic learning, and taught at the cathedral school of Reims. He reintroduced the abacus to Western Europe, promoted the use of Hindu–Arabic numerals, constructed astronomical instruments and a hydraulic organ, and is sometimes credited with inventing or improving the mechanical clock and with early work toward a calculating machine.

Papacy

Tutor and adviser to Emperor Otto III, Gerbert became Archbishop of Ravenna and then pope in 999, taking the name Sylvester II in homage to Sylvester I, adviser to Constantine. He supported Otto's vision of a renewed Roman Empire, combated simony and clerical marriage, and sent a crown to Stephen I of Hungary, recognising Hungary as a Christian kingdom.

Legends

After his death, his learning gave rise to legends that he had been a sorcerer in league with the devil — tales of a brazen talking head and a pact made in exchange for the papacy. His tomb inscription famously read "Iste locus Silvestris membra sepulti..."
