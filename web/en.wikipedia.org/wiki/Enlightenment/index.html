The Age of Enlightenment was an intellectual and philosophical movement that dominated Europe in the 17th and 18th centuries, exalting reason, science, individual liberty and toleration against tradition and religious authority. Immanuel Kant's 1784 essay defined it as humanity's emergence from self-imposed immaturity: Sapere aude — dare to use your own understanding. Its vehicles were salons, coffeehouses, academies and Diderot's great Encyclopédie.

Legacy and critique

Enlightenment ideas undergird modern democracy, human rights, the separation of powers (Montesquieu) and scientific institutions; they also coexisted with, and were used to justify, colonialism and hierarchies of race and sex that its own universalism later helped to attack. Adorno and Horkheimer's Dialectic of Enlightenment (1944) indicted instrumental reason; postcolonial and feminist scholarship continues to contest and reclaim its promises.
