RWTH Aachen University

Established: 1870
Type: Public
Location: Aachen, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany
Students: about 47,000
Motto: Zukunft denken ("Thinking the future")

RWTH Aachen University (German: Rheinisch-Westfälische Technische Hochschule Aachen) is a public research university located in Aachen, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. With more than 45,000 students enrolled in 144 study programs, it is the largest technical university in Germany.

History

The university was founded in 1870 as the Royal Rhenish-Westphalian Polytechnic School, intended to train engineers for the industries of the Rhineland and Ruhr. It received the right to award doctorates in 1899 and was renamed a Technische Hochschule in 1908. After expansion through the twentieth century it adopted the name RWTH Aachen University in 1980.

Profile

RWTH is organised into nine faculties, including mathematics, computer science and natural sciences; architecture; civil engineering; mechanical engineering; georesources and materials engineering; electrical engineering and information technology; arts and humanities; business and economics; and medicine (with the University Hospital Aachen). It has been selected in every round of Germany's Excellence Initiative and Excellence Strategy, and is a member of the TU9 alliance of German technical universities and the IDEA League.

Notable alumni and faculty include aerospace pioneer Theodore von Kármán and several Nobel laureates in physics and chemistry.
