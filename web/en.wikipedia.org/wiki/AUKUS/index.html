AUKUS

TypeTrilateral security partnership
MembersAustralia, United Kingdom, United States
Announced15 September 2021
Announced byScott Morrison, Boris Johnson, Joe Biden
PillarsI: nuclear-powered submarines; II: advanced capabilities

AUKUS (an acronym of Australia, the United Kingdom, and the United States) is a trilateral security partnership announced on 15 September 2021, focused on the Indo-Pacific region. Its best-known element is a plan for Australia to acquire a fleet of conventionally armed, nuclear-powered submarines.

Pillar I: Submarines

Under the "optimal pathway" announced in March 2023, the United States will sell Australia three to five Virginia-class submarines in the 2030s, the United Kingdom will design and build a new class — SSN-AUKUS — based on its Astute-class replacement with American combat systems, and Australia will build its own boats at Osborne, South Australia, with the first Australian-built SSN-AUKUS expected in the early 2040s. Rotational deployments of US and UK submarines to HMAS Stirling near Perth (Submarine Rotational Force – West) began in 2027.

Pillar II: Advanced capabilities

The second pillar covers cooperation on emerging technologies including artificial intelligence, quantum technologies, hypersonics and counter-hypersonics, electronic warfare, cyber capabilities, and undersea robotics.

Reactions

The announcement caused a diplomatic crisis with France, whose Naval Group lost a contract worth tens of billions of Australian dollars to build diesel-electric submarines for Australia; France temporarily recalled its ambassadors to Australia and the United States. China criticised the pact as threatening regional stability and raising nuclear-proliferation concerns, since it involves transferring naval nuclear propulsion technology to a non-nuclear-weapon state. Supporters argue the arrangement strengthens deterrence and is fully consistent with the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty because the reactors use sealed, weapons-grade fuel that is never removed.
