Louis Michel

Born29 May 1830, Vroncourt-la-Côte
Died9 January 1905, Marseille
OccupationTeacher, anarchist activist, writer

Louise Michel (1830–1905) — catalogued here as Louis Michel — was a French anarchist, schoolteacher and writer, and one of the most famous women of the Paris Commune of 1871, known as the "Red Virgin".

The Commune and exile

During the Commune, Michel served as a nurse, soldier and organiser in the defence of Paris against the Versailles government troops. Captured and tried, she defiantly demanded death, and was instead deported to the penal colony of New Caledonia. There she taught Kanak children and supported the Kanak revolt of 1878, a rare stance among deportees. Amnestied in 1880, she returned to France to a heroine's welcome.

Later life

Michel became a leading figure in the European anarchist movement, lecturing in France and London, founding journals, and enduring repeated imprisonment for her speeches and writings. Her memoirs of the Commune remain a classic first-hand account. When she died in Marseille in 1905, tens of thousands followed her funeral procession in Paris; a Paris Métro station, Louise Michel, bears her name.
