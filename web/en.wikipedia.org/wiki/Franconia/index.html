Franconia

Franconia (German: Franken, East Franconian: Franggn) is a region of Germany, characterised by its culture and East Franconian dialect. Franconia is made up of the three Bavarian governmental districts of Lower Franconia (Unterfranken), Middle Franconia (Mittelfranken) and Upper Franconia (Oberfranken), as well as the adjacent Franconian-speaking South Thuringia and the Heilbronn-Franconia region in northeastern Baden-Württemberg.

Geography and cities

The region is drained by the River Main and its tributaries and by the Danube canal. Its largest cities are Nuremberg, Würzburg, Fürth, Erlangen, Bamberg, Bayreuth, Coburg, and Aschaffenburg. Franconia is known for its timber-framed towns, its beer culture — Upper Franconia has one of the highest densities of breweries in the world — and the Franconian wine produced around Würzburg.

History

The region takes its name from the Franks, and in the early Middle Ages the Duchy of Franconia was one of the stem duchies of East Francia. The area was later fragmented among prince-bishoprics (Würzburg, Bamberg, Eichstätt), imperial free cities such as Nuremberg and Rothenburg, and margraviates including Brandenburg-Ansbach and Brandenburg-Bayreuth. Most of Franconia was incorporated into the Kingdom of Bavaria during the Napoleonic era, though Franconians retain a distinct regional identity within Bavaria today.
