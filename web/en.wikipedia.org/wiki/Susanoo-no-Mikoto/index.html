Susanoo-no-Mikoto

Susanoo (スサノオ; historical orthography: スサノヲ), formally Susanoo-no-Mikoto (須佐能乎命), is a kami in Japanese mythology, the younger brother of the sun goddess Amaterasu and of the moon god Tsukuyomi. Born from the nose of the creator god Izanagi, he is the tempestuous storm god, associated with the sea, storms, and serpents, and famed for slaying the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi.

Birth and exile

Susanoo was born when Izanagi, returning from the land of the dead, purified himself in a river; Amaterasu came from his left eye, Tsukuyomi from his right, and Susanoo from his nose. Given dominion over the sea plain, Susanoo instead raged and wept, desiring to follow his mother Izanami to the underworld; Izanagi banished him. Before leaving he visited Amaterasu in Takamagahara — in a contest of divination they produced children from each other's possessions — but then went on a rampage, flaying a heavenly horse and throwing it into her weaving hall, killing one of her attendants. Amaterasu hid in the Heavenly Rock Cave, plunging the world into darkness, until the other gods lured her out; Susanoo was expelled from heaven.

Slaying the Orochi

Descending to Izumo, Susanoo found an elderly couple whose seven daughters had been devoured by the eight-headed, eight-tailed serpent Yamata no Orochi, with the eighth, Kushinadahime, about to be taken. Susanoo transformed her into a comb, set out eight vats of sake, and when the drunken serpent's heads dipped to drink, cut it to pieces. In its tail he found the sword Ame-no-Murakumo, later called Kusanagi no Tsurugi, which he presented to Amaterasu; it became one of the Three Imperial Regalia of Japan. He married Kushinadahime and built a palace at Suga, composing what tradition calls the first waka poem.

Worship

Susanoo is enshrined at Yasaka Shrine in Kyoto (center of the Gion Matsuri, originally a rite to appease him as a plague deity) and across the Izumo region. His descendant Ōkuninushi, builder of the land, links him to the Izumo cycle of myths rivaling the Amaterasu-centered Yamato cycle.
