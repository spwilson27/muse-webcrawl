Utilitarianism is a family of normative ethical theories holding that the morally best action is the one that maximises utility — usually understood as overall well-being or happiness minus suffering, for everyone affected. Systematised by Jeremy Bentham and John Stuart Mill, it is the best-known form of consequentialism and a major influence on economics, public policy, animal ethics and effective altruism.

Variants and debates

Act utilitarians judge each act by its consequences; rule utilitarians judge rules whose general acceptance does best. Mill distinguished higher (intellectual) from lower pleasures to answer charges that utilitarianism is a 'doctrine worthy only of swine'. Critics object that it can licence injustice (punishing the innocent, violating rights) or demand superhuman self-sacrifice; defenders reply with two-level thinking and rights-protecting rules. Peter Singer's work on global poverty and animal liberation is its best-known modern continuation.
