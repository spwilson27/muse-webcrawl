The Ilyushin Il-2 (Russian: Ильюшин Ил-2) is a ground-attack aircraft produced by the Soviet Union in large numbers during the Second World War. Known as the Shturmovik (Штурмовик, "stormer" or "attacker"), it was designed by Sergey Ilyushin's bureau and is the most-produced military aircraft in aviation history, with 36,183 built. German troops nicknamed it the "Black Death".

Design

The Il-2's defining feature was its armoured shell of steel plate, up to 12 mm thick in places, that surrounded the engine, cockpit, and fuel tanks as an integral part of the structure, making it exceptionally resistant to ground fire. Armament typically included two 23 mm cannon and two machine guns in the wings, plus bombs and RS-82 or RS-132 rockets under the wings. Early single-seat versions suffered heavy losses to fighters; from 1942 the two-seat Il-2M added a rear gunner.

Operational history

The Il-2 entered service in 1941 just before the German invasion and became the backbone of Soviet tactical air power, attacking tanks, artillery, and troop concentrations at low altitude. Joseph Stalin famously declared the Il-2 "as essential to the Red Army as air and bread". The aircraft played a major role in battles from Stalingrad to Kursk and Berlin.

Variants and legacy

The later Il-10 was a refined successor introduced in 1944. The Il-2 was exported to several Eastern Bloc air forces after the war and remained in service into the 1950s. Numerous examples survive in museums, and a few have been restored to flight.
