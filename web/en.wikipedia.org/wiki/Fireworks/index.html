Fireworks

Fireworks are low-explosive pyrotechnic devices used for aesthetic and entertainment purposes. They produce combinations of light, colour, smoke, noise, and floating or falling effects through controlled combustion of pyrotechnic compositions. The most common form of display is the firework show, in which aerial shells, rockets, Roman candles, fountains, Catherine wheels, and sparklers are ignited in sequence, often choreographed to music ("pyromusicals"). Fireworks are central to celebrations such as Chinese New Year, Diwali, Independence Day in the United States (4 July), Bonfire Night in Britain (5 November), and New Year's Eve worldwide.

History

Fireworks originated in China, where bamboo stalks thrown into fires exploded with a bang ("baozhu") and, after the invention of gunpowder in the 9th century, paper tubes filled with powder became firecrackers used in festivals and warfare. Knowledge of gunpowder spread along trade routes to India, the Islamic world, and Europe by the 13th century; Italian and German pyrotechnists of the Renaissance and Baroque eras turned displays into high art for royal courts. Coloured flames became practical in the 1830s with the adoption of potassium chlorate, which allowed metal salts to burn with intense colour. Liuyang in China's Hunan province remains the world's largest centre of fireworks manufacture.

How fireworks work

An aerial shell consists of a paper or plastic casing containing a bursting charge and many small pellets of composition called stars; a lift charge of black powder launches the shell from a mortar, and a time fuse ignites the burst at altitude, scattering the burning stars into patterns such as peonies (trailing-less spheres), chrysanthemums (with trails), willows, palms, and rings. Colours come from metal salts: strontium for red, barium for green, sodium for yellow-orange, copper compounds for blue (the hardest colour to achieve), and calcium for orange; aluminium, magnesium, and titanium produce white light, sparks, and crackling effects. Safety rules govern manufacture, storage, transport, and display distances, and consumer fireworks are restricted in many jurisdictions because of injury, fire, noise, and air-pollution concerns.

Environmental and social concerns

Displays release particulate matter and metal residues, disturb wildlife and pets, and can aggravate respiratory conditions; these concerns have prompted some cities to substitute drone light shows or laser displays. Injuries — burns and eye damage chiefly — remain a recurring public-health issue around major festivals.
