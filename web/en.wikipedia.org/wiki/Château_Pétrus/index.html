Château Pétrus is a wine estate in the Pomerol appellation on the Right Bank of Bordeaux, France. Its wine, made almost entirely from Merlot grapes, is regarded as one of the most celebrated and expensive red wines in the world, notable for its richness, concentration and longevity.

History

Vineyards on the site date to the 18th century, and the estate took its name from Saint Peter (Petrus). For much of its history Pétrus was an obscure property, but under the hotelier Madame Loubat, who acquired it in the mid-20th century, and the négociant Jean-Pierre Moueix, who became its exclusive distributor in 1945, its reputation rose sharply. The wine became famous in the United States after being served at prominent restaurants and was reportedly a favourite at the White House during the Kennedy administration.

Vineyard

The estate covers about 11.4 hectares at the top of the Pomerol plateau. Its distinctive feature is a topsoil of dark clay, unusually rich in iron, over a subsoil of gravel; a small central parcel of blue clay is credited with giving Pétrus its characteristic texture and ageing capacity. Since 2010 the vineyard has been planted almost entirely to Merlot, the last Cabernet Franc vines having been pulled up after the 2010 harvest.

Winemaking

Grapes are harvested by hand and sorted with unusual rigour; in difficult years a large share of the crop is declassified. Fermentation takes place in temperature-controlled cement vats, and the wine is aged in new French oak barrels for roughly 12 to 16 months. Annual production is small — around 30,000 bottles — and no second wine is regularly produced.

Reputation and prices

Pétrus consistently ranks among the most expensive wines sold at auction and through merchants, with mature vintages such as 1945, 1961, 1982, 1989, 1990, 2000 and 2010 fetching very high prices. A bottle of Pétrus 2000 known as the "Cuvée de l'espace", aged aboard the International Space Station, was offered for sale in 2021 with an estimate of around one million dollars.
