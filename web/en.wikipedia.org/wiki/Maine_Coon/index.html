The Maine Coon is one of the largest domesticated breeds of cat and one of the oldest natural breeds in North America. Native to the U.S. state of Maine, where it is the official state cat, the breed is distinguished by its large size, rugged appearance, long shaggy coat, tufted ears, and bushy tail. Maine Coons are known for their sociable, dog-like temperament and their soft chirping and trilling vocalisations, which has earned them the nickname "gentle giants".

History

The breed developed from longhaired cats in Maine during the nineteenth century, adapted by natural selection to the state's harsh winters. Folk tales claimed impossible ancestry from raccoons (hence "Coon") or from Marie Antoinette's longhaired cats supposedly sent ahead to America, but the practical explanation is that longhaired cats brought by seafarers interbred with local shorthairs. Maine Coons were shown at early American cat shows in the 1860s and 1890s — a Maine Coon named Cosey won best cat at the 1895 Madison Square Garden show — before falling out of fashion with the arrival of Persian and Siamese imports. The breed was revived by fanciers from the 1950s onward and was restored to championship status by the Cat Fanciers' Association in 1976.

Characteristics

Maine Coons mature slowly, reaching full size at three to five years. Males typically weigh 6–8 kilograms (13–18 lb), with some individuals larger. The body is muscular and rectangular, with a long, flowing tail at least as long as the body. Adaptations to winter include a heavy, water-resistant double coat longer on the ruff, belly, and britches; large, well-furred paws that act as snowshoes; and tufted, lynx-tipped ears. The breed occurs in most colours and patterns, with brown tabby the classic look. Polydactyly (extra toes) was common in the original population and remains accepted by some registries.

Temperament

Maine Coons are noted for intelligence, playfulness retained into adulthood, and loyalty to their families. They often follow owners around, enjoy water more than most cats, and communicate with chirps and trills as much as with meows.

Health

Breed predispositions include hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), for which a breed-specific gene variant in MYBPC3 can be tested, hip dysplasia, and spinal muscular atrophy. Responsible breeders screen breeding stock by echocardiography and DNA testing.
