Focus (optics)

In geometrical optics, a focus, also called an image point, is a point where light rays originating from a point on an object converge. Although the focus is conceptually a point, physically the focus has a spatial extent, called the blur circle, due to the effects of diffraction and aberrations. When a lens is used to form an image of an object, the distance between the lens and the image sensor or film determines where the object will be in focus; the image is said to be "in focus" when the converging rays meet at the plane of the sensor.

Principal focus

The principal focus (or focal point) of a lens or mirror is the point where collimated light parallel to the axis is focused. The distance from the lens or mirror to this point is the focal length. Because light can travel in either direction through a lens, a lens has two focal points, one on each side, provided the surrounding medium is the same.

Focus in imaging

An image, or image point, is said to be in focus if light from the object points is converged almost as much as possible in the image, and out of focus otherwise. The concept of the "sharpest" focus is complicated by aberrations such as spherical aberration and by the circle of confusion criterion used to define acceptable sharpness and depth of field. In cameras, focus is adjusted by moving the lens relative to the image plane, either manually or by an autofocus system; the human eye focuses by changing the shape of its lens through accommodation.

Defocus and bokeh

Deliberate defocus can be an artistic tool: out-of-focus highlights take the shape of the aperture and produce the aesthetic quality known as bokeh. Conversely, techniques such as focus stacking combine multiple images taken at different focus distances to achieve a greater depth of field than any single exposure.
