Kleisli category

Composition in the Kleisli category uses the monad's multiplication to chain effectful functions.

In category theory, a Kleisli category is a category naturally associated to any monad T on a category C. It is named after the Swiss mathematician Heinrich Kleisli, who originally introduced it to study homological invariants of AT-algebras. Equivalently, the Kleisli category can be seen as a category whose objects are the objects of C but whose morphisms are maps from an object X to T(Y): a morphism X → Y in the Kleisli category is a morphism X → T Y in C.

Definition

Let ⟨T, η, μ⟩ be a monad on a category C. The Kleisli category CT has the same objects as C. A morphism f : X ⇢ Y in CT is a morphism f : X → T Y in C. Composition uses the monad multiplication: given f : X → T Y and g : Y → T Z, their composite is μZ ∘ T(g) ∘ f : X → T Z. The identity morphism on X is the unit ηX : X → T X. The monad laws ensure associativity and the identity laws.

Kleisli triples

An equivalent formulation, often used in computer science, is the Kleisli triple ⟨T, η, (–)*⟩, in which T is an operation on objects, η is a family of "return" maps, and (–)* extends a map f : X → T Y to f* : T X → T Y (the "bind" operation). The Kleisli category can be built directly from a Kleisli triple, and every monad arises this way.

Adjunction

Every monad arises from an adjunction. The Kleisli category gives the initial such adjunction: there is an adjunction between C and CT whose induced monad is T, and any other adjunction inducing T factors through it. The other canonical choice is the Eilenberg–Moore category of algebras, which is terminal among such adjunctions.

Use in computer science

In functional programming, Kleisli arrows are the effectful functions X → T Y for a monad T (for example, partial functions for the maybe monad, or nondeterministic functions for the powerset monad). Composition of Kleisli arrows is the Kleisli composition operator, written >=> in Haskell, and underlies do-notation and monadic programming generally.
