Phasianidae

The Phasianidae are a family of heavy, ground-living birds in the order Galliformes, including pheasants, partridges, junglefowl, chickens, turkeys, Old World quail, grouse, and peafowl. The family contains well over 180 species in more than 50 genera, mostly of the Old World, with turkeys and New World grouse extending its range to the Americas.

Characteristics

Phasianids are typically plump-bodied birds with short, rounded wings suited to brief bursts of flight, strong legs for scratching the ground, and short stout bills. Many species are strongly sexually dimorphic, the males being larger and spectacularly coloured — as in the Indian peafowl and the golden pheasant — while females are drab and cryptic. Males of many species bear spurs on their legs.

Relationship with humans

The family includes the most economically important of all birds: the domestic chicken, descended principally from the red junglefowl (Gallus gallus). Turkeys were domesticated in the Americas, and pheasants, partridges, and grouse are major game birds worldwide; several, such as the ring-necked pheasant, have been introduced far beyond their native ranges for hunting.

Classification

Modern classifications divide the family into subfamilies including Phasianinae (pheasants, junglefowl, peafowl), Pavoninae, and Rollulinae (partridges and spurfowl); grouse and turkeys, once treated as separate families, are now generally included within Phasianidae.
