Daron Acemoglu

Kamer Daron Acemoglu (born September 3, 1967) is a Turkish-American economist who has taught at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) since 1993, where he is an Institute Professor. In 2024 he shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Simon Johnson and James A. Robinson for studies of how institutions are formed and affect prosperity.

Research

Acemoglu's research spans political economy, economic development, growth theory, labour economics and network economics. With James A. Robinson he wrote Economic Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy (2006) and the bestseller Why Nations Fail (2012), which argues that inclusive political and economic institutions — rather than geography or culture — are the fundamental cause of long-run prosperity, while extractive institutions enrich narrow elites and stifle growth. Their later book The Narrow Corridor (2019) examines the balance between state power and society.

With Simon Johnson, Acemoglu wrote Power and Progress (2023), on the political economy of technology. He has also written influential papers on directed technical change, automation and robots' effects on employment, and the role of institutions in development, including the widely cited "colonial origins of comparative development" study with Johnson and Robinson.

Honours

Acemoglu received the John Bates Clark Medal in 2005 and is among the most cited economists in the world. He was born in Istanbul to Armenian parents and studied at the University of York and the London School of Economics, where he earned his PhD in 1992.
