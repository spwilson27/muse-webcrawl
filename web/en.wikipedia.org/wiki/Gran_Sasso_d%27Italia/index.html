Gran Sasso d'Italia is a massif in the Apennine Mountains of central Italy, located in the Abruzzo region. Its highest peak, Corno Grande, at 2,912 metres (9,554 ft), is the tallest mountain in the Apennines. The massif forms the centrepiece of the Gran Sasso and Monti della Laga National Park, established in 1991.

Geography

The massif includes the Calderone glacier, one of the southernmost glaciers in Europe, and the high plateau of Campo Imperatore, sometimes called "Little Tibet". The Gran Sasso tunnel, carrying the A24 motorway beneath the massif, is among the longest road tunnels in Italy and also houses the Laboratori Nazionali del Gran Sasso, a major underground physics laboratory.

History

In September 1943, the deposed Italian dictator Benito Mussolini, held prisoner at the Campo Imperatore hotel, was freed in the daring Gran Sasso raid by German paratroopers and SS commandos led by Otto Skorzeny.
