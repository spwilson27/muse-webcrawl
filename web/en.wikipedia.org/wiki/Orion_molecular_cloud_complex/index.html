The Orion molecular cloud complex (often called the Orion complex) is a vast star-forming region of interstellar gas, dust, and young stars in the constellation Orion. It is the nearest region of massive star formation to Earth and one of the most extensively studied objects in astronomy.

Components

The complex comprises two giant molecular clouds, Orion A and Orion B, together containing hundreds of thousands of solar masses of cold molecular gas. Notable features include the Orion Nebula (Messier 42), the brightest part of Orion A and visible to the naked eye in Orion's Sword; the Horsehead Nebula and Flame Nebula in Orion B; the emission nebulae M43 and M78; and Barnard's Loop, a huge arc of glowing gas surrounding much of the complex.

Star formation

Within the clouds, gravity is collapsing dense cores into new stars and clusters, most famously the Trapezium Cluster at the heart of the Orion Nebula, whose massive hot stars ionise the surrounding gas. Successive generations of star formation, and feedback from supernovae and stellar winds, have sculpted the complex over the past roughly 12 million years; the familiar Orion OB1 association of bright blue stars records this history.
