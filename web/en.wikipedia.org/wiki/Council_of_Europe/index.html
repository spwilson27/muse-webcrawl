Council of Europe

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Council of Europe is an international organisation founded in the wake of the Second World War to uphold human rights, democracy and the rule of law in Europe. Founded by the Treaty of London on 5 May 1949 and headquartered in Strasbourg, it has 46 member states (after Russia's expulsion in March 2022 following its invasion of Ukraine, the first expulsion of a member) — all European states except Belarus, plus observer states beyond Europe. It is distinct from the European Union (different membership, institutions and flag-sharing quirks aside).

Its flagship instrument is the European Convention on Human Rights (1950), enforced by the European Court of Human Rights, whose judgments bind member states and have reshaped domestic law from decriminalisation of homosexuality to prisoners' rights. The Council also produces conventions (on corruption, cybercrime — the Budapest Convention, violence against women — the Istanbul Convention, data protection), monitoring bodies (GRECO, the Venice Commission on constitutional law, the CPT on torture prevention) and the Parliamentary Assembly (PACE), whose delegations are national parliamentarians. Its authority rests on soft power and the Convention system, strained by non-execution of judgments and democratic backsliding among members.
