Thenardite

[Hero image placeholder: white crusts of thenardite crystals on a dry lake bed.]

Thenardite is an anhydrous sodium sulfate mineral with the chemical formula Na2SO4. It occurs in arid evaporite environments, especially in and around dried-up soda lakes and playas. It was named in 1826 for the French chemist Louis Jacques Thénard (1777–1857).

Properties

Thenardite crystallises in the orthorhombic system, forming tabular or prismatic crystals, though it is more commonly found as powdery crusts and efflorescences. It is colourless to white or greyish, transparent to translucent, with a vitreous to resinous lustre, a hardness of 2.5 on the Mohs scale, and a bitter, saline taste. It is soluble in water.

Occurrence

Notable localities include Soda Lake in California, Searles Lake, Lake Balkhash, the Atacama in Chile, and deposits in Spain and Siberia. Thenardite alters readily to the hydrous mineral mirabilite (Na2SO4·10H2O) in moist air; the repeated crystallisation and hydration cycle is a significant cause of stone decay in monuments.
