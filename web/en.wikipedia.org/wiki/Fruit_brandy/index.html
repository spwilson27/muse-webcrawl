Fruit brandy

A fruit brandy is a distilled spirit produced from fermented fruit juice. The term typically excludes brandies made from grapes, which are simply called brandy, and covers spirits distilled from apples, pears, plums, cherries, apricots, berries, and other fruit. Fruit brandies are usually clear (unless aged in wood) and are typically bottled at 40–45% alcohol by volume.

Production

Ripe fruit is crushed and fermented, and the resulting fruit wine is distilled, traditionally in copper pot stills. Because fruit yields less sugar than grapes, large quantities are required — roughly ten kilograms of fruit per litre of finished spirit. Quality fruit brandies are made from whole fermented fruit rather than from flavourings, and many regional varieties are protected by geographical indications.

Varieties

Well-known fruit brandies include slivovica (plum) from Central and Eastern Europe, kirschwasser (cherry) from Germany, Switzerland, and Alsace, Williams (pear) brandy, applejack and Calvados from apples (Calvados being an aged apple brandy from Normandy), and pálinka from Hungary. In the Balkans, rakia covers a broad family of fruit brandies, often home-produced.

Consumption

Fruit brandies are commonly served neat as a digestif, chilled or at room temperature, and in Central Europe they accompany celebrations and hospitality rituals. Unaged examples are known in German-speaking regions as Obstler or Schnaps.
