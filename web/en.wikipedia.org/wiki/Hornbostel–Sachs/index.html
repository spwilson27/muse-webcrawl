Hornbostel–Sachs is a system of musical instrument classification devised by Erich Moritz von Hornbostel and Curt Sachs, first published in 1914 in the Zeitschrift für Ethnologie. It is the most widely used system for classifying instruments in ethnomusicology and museum collections, modelled on the Dewey Decimal Classification.

The five classes

Instruments are classified by what vibrates to produce the sound: idiophones (the instrument body itself, e.g., bells), membranophones (a stretched membrane, e.g., drums), chordophones (strings, e.g., violins and guitars), aerophones (the air itself, e.g., flutes and trumpets), and electrophones (added in 1940 for electrically produced or amplified sound). Successive digits refine the category by how the instrument is played and its structure.

Revisions

The scheme has been repeatedly revised—by Sachs himself, by the MIMO (Musical Instrument Museums Online) project, and others—to accommodate new instruments and clarify ambiguous cases. Critics note difficulties with instruments combining sound sources, but the system remains the scholarly standard.
