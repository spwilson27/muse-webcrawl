Real estate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Real estate is property consisting of land and the buildings on it, along with its natural resources such as crops, minerals or water; immovable property of this nature; and the business of buying, selling, developing and managing such property. In law, "real property" (or "realty") is distinguished from personal property, which is movable. The term derives from the Latin res ("thing") via the concept of property in land as distinct from chattels.

Categories

  • Residential — single-family homes, apartments, condominiums, townhouses and vacation homes.
  • Commercial — office buildings, retail premises, hotels and shopping centres, held for income.
  • Industrial — warehouses, factories, logistics and research facilities.
  • Land — undeveloped land, farms, ranches and early-development sites.
  • Special purpose — schools, places of worship, government buildings and infrastructure.

The industry

Real estate is among the world's largest asset classes and a primary store of household wealth. Transactions typically involve brokers or estate agents, lawyers or conveyancers, surveyors and mortgage lenders. Property values depend on location, supply and demand, interest rates, zoning and broader economic conditions; housing markets are prone to cycles and bubbles, most consequentially the United States subprime mortgage crisis of 2007–08, which triggered the global financial crisis. Residential prices are tracked by indices such as the Case–Shiller index.

Investment

Investors access real estate directly, through rental property and development, or indirectly via listed property companies and real estate investment trusts (REITs), which trade like shares and distribute rental income. Appraisal methods include comparable sales, capitalisation of income and replacement cost.
