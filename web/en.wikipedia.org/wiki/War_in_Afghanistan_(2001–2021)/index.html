The War in Afghanistan was a conflict that took place from 2001 to 2021 in the Central Asian country of Afghanistan. It began when the United States and its allies invaded Afghanistan and toppled the Taliban-ruled Islamic Emirate in response to the September 11 attacks, as part of the "war on terror". The war ended with the Taliban regaining power after a rapid offensive in 2021 and the final withdrawal of US forces on 30 August 2021. It was the longest war in United States history.

Invasion and fall of the Taliban (2001)

After the Taliban refused to hand over al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden, US and British forces began airstrikes on 7 October 2001 under Operation Enduring Freedom, supporting the Northern Alliance on the ground. Kabul fell on 13 November and Kandahar in December; the Taliban government collapsed, and an interim administration under Hamid Karzai was established following the Bonn Agreement. Bin Laden escaped the battle of Tora Bora into Pakistan.

Insurgency and nation-building

The NATO-led International Security Assistance Force (ISAF) expanded across the country while the Taliban regrouped in Pakistan's border regions and launched an insurgency. Fighting intensified after 2006; in 2009–2010 President Barack Obama ordered a "surge" that raised US troop levels to about 100,000. In May 2011, US Navy SEALs killed Osama bin Laden in Abbottabad, Pakistan. NATO ended its combat mission in 2014, handing security responsibility to the Afghan National Security Forces, while a smaller Resolute Support mission continued training and advising.

Peace process and Taliban victory (2020–2021)

In February 2020 the United States and the Taliban signed the Doha Agreement, providing for the withdrawal of US forces. After the Biden administration confirmed a complete withdrawal by August 2021, the Taliban launched a nationwide offensive; provincial capitals fell in rapid succession, Kabul was captured on 15 August 2021, and President Ashraf Ghani fled the country. A chaotic evacuation from Hamid Karzai International Airport followed, and the last US military aircraft departed on 30 August 2021, ending the war.

Costs and casualties

The war killed an estimated 176,000 people, including about 46,000 civilians, according to the Costs of War Project; more than 2,400 US service members and some 66,000–69,000 Afghan security personnel died. The United States spent an estimated $2.3 trillion on the war. The conflict displaced millions of Afghans and left the country facing economic collapse and humanitarian crisis after the Taliban's return.
