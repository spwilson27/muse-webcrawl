Spinet

A spinet is a smaller type of harpsichord or piano. As a harpsichord, a spinet is a compact instrument in which the strings run at an oblique angle to the keyboard, saving space; the name is usually traced to Giovanni Spinetti, a Venetian maker around 1500, or to the Latin spina ("thorn"), referring to the quills that pluck the strings.

Harpsichord spinets

Bentside spinets were popular domestic instruments in 17th- and 18th-century England, France, and Italy, where makers such as Stephen Keene and the Hitchcock family built them in large numbers. Their small triangular or polygonal cases made them cheaper and more portable than full harpsichords, though with a more modest sound.

Spinet pianos

In the 1930s American manufacturers applied the name to very low upright pianos (about 36–38 inches tall) with a drop action located partly below the keys. Spinet pianos were inexpensive and widely sold for home use, though their short strings and indirect action give them inferior tone and touch compared with taller uprights.
