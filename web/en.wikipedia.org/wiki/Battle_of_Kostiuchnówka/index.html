Battle of Kostiuchnówka

The Battle of Kostiuchnówka was fought from 4 to 6 July 1916 near the village of Kostiuchnówka (Kostyukhnivka, in present-day Volyn Oblast, Ukraine) between the Polish Legions in Austro-Hungarian service and the Imperial Russian Army during the Brusilov Offensive of the First World War. It was the largest and most famous battle of the Polish Legions, who held their positions in the Polish redoubt line at heavy cost before withdrawing in good order.

Background

The Brusilov Offensive, launched in June 1916, shattered the Austro-Hungarian front in Galicia and Volhynia. The Polish Legions — three brigades of volunteers fighting for Polish independence alongside the Central Powers — were holding a sector along the Styr River around Kostiuchnówka, with their line anchored on fortified positions including the Polish Hill (Polska Góra) and the Polish Redoubt. Russian forces, ordered to break through toward Kovel, concentrated heavily against the Legion sector.

The battle

After a massive artillery preparation on 4 July 1916, Russian infantry assaulted the Legion lines in repeated waves. The fighting around Polish Hill and the redoubt was exceptionally fierce, with positions changing hands amid shellfire and bayonet fighting; the Hungarian Honvéd units on the flank gave way, exposing the Legions, who nonetheless counter-attacked locally and held the main line through 5 July. On 6 July, with the front collapsing on both sides, the Legions withdrew across the Styr in good order, having delayed the Russian advance toward Kovel at the cost of roughly 2,000 men — about a third of their strength.

Legacy

Kostiuchnówka became the foundational battle myth of the Polish Legions and of Józef Piłsudski's independence movement. The battlefield's Polish military cemetery, restored after 1991 by Polish–Ukrainian cooperation, is maintained by Polish scouts each summer, and the battle is commemorated in Polish military tradition as proof of Polish arms in the struggle for statehood.
