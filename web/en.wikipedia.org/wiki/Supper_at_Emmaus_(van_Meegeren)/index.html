Supper at Emmaus (van Meegeren)

The Supper at Emmaus is a painting by the Dutch forger Han van Meegeren, created in 1936–1937 and passed off as a work by Johannes Vermeer. It became the centrepiece of one of the most famous art forgery scandals of the 20th century.

Reception

Van Meegeren, embittered by critics who dismissed his own work, set out to prove he could fool the art world. He painted the Supper at Emmaus on a genuine 17th-century canvas using period pigments mixed with Bakelite resin to simulate aged paint, and hardened it in an oven. The painting was authenticated by the leading Vermeer scholar Abraham Bredius, who hailed it as Vermeer's masterpiece, and was purchased in 1938 by the Museum Boijmans in Rotterdam for the enormous sum of 520,000 guilders.

Exposure

After the Second World War, van Meegeren was arrested for allegedly selling Dutch cultural property — another "Vermeer", Christ with the Adulteress — to Hermann Göring. To defend himself against the collaboration charge, he confessed that the paintings were his own forgeries and demonstrated his technique by painting another "Vermeer" under supervision. He was convicted only of forgery in 1947.
