Yigal Allon

Born: 18 October 1918, Kfar Tavor, Ottoman Syria
Died: 29 February 1980 (aged 61), Afula, Israel
Occupation: Military commander, politician
Office: Interim Prime Minister of Israel (1969); Deputy Prime Minister; Foreign Minister

Yigal Allon (Hebrew: יגאל אלון; 18 October 1918 – 29 February 1980) was an Israeli military commander and politician. One of the leading commanders of the Palmach and the Israel Defense Forces during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, he later became a senior minister in Labour governments, serving as interim prime minister in 1969 and as foreign minister under Yitzhak Rabin.

Early life and Palmach

Allon was born in Kfar Tavor in the Lower Galilee, the son of a farmer who had served in the Jewish Legion. He joined the Haganah as a teenager and in 1941 became one of the founding commanders of its strike companies, the Palmach, rising to command the force in 1945. He led operations against British targets during the Jewish insurgency in Mandatory Palestine and against Vichy French forces in Syria and Lebanon.

1948 war

During the 1948 war Allon commanded some of the largest operations of the conflict, including Operation Yiftah in the Galilee, Operation Dani in the central front, and Operation Yoav, which broke the Egyptian siege lines in the Negev. As commander of the Southern Command he pursued Egyptian forces into the Sinai, withdrawing under American pressure. He was passed over for chief of staff despite his record and left the army in 1950.

Political career

Allon entered the Knesset in 1955 with the Ahdut HaAvoda party and held a succession of ministries, including labour, immigrant absorption, and education. After the assassination-level political crisis following the death of Levi Eshkol in 1969, Allon served briefly as interim prime minister before Golda Meir was chosen. He is best known in policy terms for the Allon Plan, his proposal that Israel retain a security belt along the Jordan Valley while returning densely populated Arab areas of the West Bank to Jordanian administration — a framework that shaped Israeli settlement strategy for decades. As foreign minister (1974–1977) he negotiated the Sinai Interim Agreement with Egypt.
