Roper River

Country: Australia

State: Northern Territory

Length: approx. 150 km (93 mi)

Mouth: Limmen Bight, Gulf of Carpentaria

The Roper River is a perennial river in the Roper Gulf region of the Northern Territory, Australia. It is formed by the confluence of the Elsey and Beswick creeks near Mataranka and flows eastward for roughly 150 kilometres to Limmen Bight on the Gulf of Carpentaria. Unusually for a river in the seasonally dry tropics, the Roper flows all year, fed by large limestone springs in its upper catchment.

Geography

The river rises in the low ranges near Mataranka, where warm spring water from the Tindall Limestone aquifer sustains famous thermal pools. Its major tributaries include the Waterhouse River, Beswick Creek, Elsey Creek, the Phelp River, and the Rose and Hodgson rivers. Downstream the valley widens into wetlands and floodplains fringed with paperbark forest, and the river becomes tidal well before reaching the mangrove-lined coast of Limmen Bight.

History

The Roper valley is the traditional country of several Aboriginal groups, including the Mangarrayi, Yangman, and Alawa peoples. Ludwig Leichhardt crossed and named the river in 1845 during his overland expedition from Moreton Bay to Port Essington, naming it for John Roper, a member of his party. The area was later made famous internationally by Jeannie Gunn's 1908 memoir We of the Never Never, set on Elsey Station along the river's tributaries.

Ecology and use

The Roper is renowned among anglers for barramundi fishing, and its waters support saltwater crocodiles, sawfish, and rich birdlife. The perennial flow has attracted repeated proposals for irrigation development and water extraction, which have been contested by Traditional Owners, fishing interests, and conservation groups concerned about the river's environmental flows and the Gulf's prawn and barramundi fisheries.
