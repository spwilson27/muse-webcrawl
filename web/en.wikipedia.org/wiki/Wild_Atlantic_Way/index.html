The Wild Atlantic Way (Irish: Slí an Atlantaigh Fhiáin) is a long-distance touring route along the west coast of Ireland, running about 2,600 km from the Inishowen Peninsula in County Donegal to Kinsale in County Cork. Marketed by Fáilte Ireland as one of the longest defined coastal touring routes in the world, it passes through nine counties and connects dozens of towns, headlands, and islands along the Atlantic seaboard.

Route

The route is divided into six zones: the Northern Headlands (Donegal), the Surf Coast (Sligo and Mayo), the Bay Coast (Galway), the Cliff Coast (Clare), the Southern Peninsulas (Kerry), and the Haven Coast (Cork). Along it are 188 "discovery points", of which 15 are designated Signature Discovery Points — including Malin Head, the Slieve League cliffs, the Cliffs of Moher, the Blasket Islands viewpoint at Dunmore Head, the Ring of Kerry's Bray Head on Valentia Island, and the Old Head of Kinsale. Ferry-linked island points include the Aran Islands and Dursey Island, reached by Ireland's only cable car.

Development and impact

The Wild Atlantic Way was developed by Fáilte Ireland to bundle existing coastal roads into a single branded itinerary, modelled partly on routes such as South Africa's Garden Route and Scotland's North Coast 500 (which followed later). Launched in 2014, it has been credited with increased visitor numbers and spending in western coastal communities, and it anchors further initiatives such as greenways and the Ireland's Hidden Heartlands and Ancient East brands for other regions.
