Franz Boas

Franz Uri Boas (1858–1942) was a German-American anthropologist and a pioneer of modern anthropology, often called the “Father of American Anthropology”. He founded the first US PhD programme in anthropology at Columbia University and argued for cultural relativism, historical particularism and a four-field approach (cultural, linguistic, biological anthropology and archaeology).

Franz Boas
Born: 9 July 1858, Minden, Germany
Died: 21 December 1942, New York City
Known for: Cultural relativism, four-field anthropology
Students: Margaret Mead, Ruth Benedict, Zora Neale Hurston

Life and work

Trained in physics and geography, Boas did fieldwork on Baffin Island and among Indigenous peoples of the Pacific Northwest, especially the Kwakwa̱ka’wakw (Kwakiutl). Emigrating to the US, he worked at the American Museum of Natural History before Columbia. His 1911 book The Mind of Primitive Man attacked scientific racism, arguing culture, not biology, explains group differences; his immigrant head-form studies challenged racial typology.

Legacy

Boas trained a generation that shaped American anthropology and public understanding of race and culture. He also opposed Nazi racial science and advocated academic freedom, while his collecting and salvage ethnography are now critically re-examined by Indigenous scholars.
