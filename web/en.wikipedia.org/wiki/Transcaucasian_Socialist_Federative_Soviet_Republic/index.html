Transcaucasian Socialist Federative Soviet Republic

The Transcaucasian Socialist Federative Soviet Republic (Transcaucasian SFSR or TSFSR), also known as the Transcaucasian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic, was a republic of the Soviet Union that existed from 1922 to 1936, uniting the Armenian, Azerbaijani and Georgian Soviet republics in the South Caucasus. Its capital was Tbilisi (Tiflis).

History

The federation originated in March 1922, when the three republics formed a federative union, strongly promoted by Joseph Stalin over Georgian communist objections — the dispute became the "Georgian Affair" that occupied Lenin's last months. In December 1922 the TSFSR joined Russia, Ukraine and Belarus as a founding member of the USSR.

Through the 1920s the union republics retained their own governments, while the federation coordinated economic policy, transport and finance. It issued its own currency (the Transcaucasian rouble) and stamps.

Dissolution

Under the 1936 Soviet Constitution the federation was abolished, and Armenia, Azerbaijan and Georgia entered the USSR directly as union republics on 5 December 1936. The three remained Soviet republics until they declared independence in 1991.
