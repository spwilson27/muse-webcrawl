Kiss of the Spider Woman (musical)

Kiss of the Spider Woman Music: John Kander
Lyrics: Fred Ebb
Book: Terrence McNally
Basis: Novel Kiss of the Spider Woman (1976) by Manuel Puig
Premiere: 1990 (New Musicals, SUNY Purchase); Broadway 1993
Honours: Tony Award for Best Musical (1993)

Kiss of the Spider Woman is a musical with a book by Terrence McNally, music by John Kander and lyrics by Fred Ebb, adapted from Manuel Puig's 1976 novel of the same name. It tells of two cellmates in a Latin American prison: Molina, a gay window dresser imprisoned for indecency, who escapes into fantasies of old movies starring the glamorous Aurora, and Valentin, a Marxist revolutionary who initially despises him.

Productions

The musical was first workshopped and staged in 1990 at the State University of New York at Purchase as part of the New Musicals programme, in a version that was extensively revised afterwards. Directed by Harold Prince, the revised production opened in Toronto and London in 1992 and transferred to Broadway's Broadhurst Theatre on 3 May 1993.

The Broadway production starred Brent Carver as Molina, Anthony Crivello as Valentin and Chita Rivera as Aurora/the Spider Woman. It won seven Tony Awards in 1993, including Best Musical, Best Score, and acting awards for Carver, Crivello and Rivera. Vanessa Williams later took over the role of Aurora on Broadway.

Themes and songs

The score contrasts dark prison scenes with the lush, escapist film fantasies embodied by Aurora, whose most seductive persona is the Spider Woman, whose kiss means death. Well-known numbers include "Dressing Them Up," "Dear One," "Over the Wall" and the title song. The story explores political commitment, sexuality, dignity and the redemptive power of fantasy and storytelling.
