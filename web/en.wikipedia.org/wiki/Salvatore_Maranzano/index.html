Salvatore Maranzano

Born: 31 July 1886, Castellammare del Golfo, Sicily, Italy
Died: 10 September 1931 (aged 45), New York City, U.S.
Cause of death: Gunshot and stab wounds
Known for: Organised crime boss; "boss of all bosses"

Salvatore Maranzano (Italian: [salvaˈtoːre maranˈtsaːno]; July 31, 1886 – September 10, 1931) was an Italian-American mobster from Sicily and an early Cosa Nostra boss who led a criminal organisation in New York City during Prohibition. He won the Castellammarese War of 1930–31 against Joe Masseria and briefly became capo dei capi ("boss of all bosses"), reorganising the American Mafia into the Five Families before being murdered on the orders of Lucky Luciano.

Early life

Maranzano was born in Castellammare del Golfo, Sicily. He briefly studied for the priesthood and studied law before emigrating to the United States in the 1920s. In Brooklyn he built interests in real estate and Prohibition-era bootlegging, backed by the Castellammarese clan that included Joseph Bonanno.

Castellammarese War

In 1930 open conflict broke out between Maranzano's Castellammarese faction and the larger organisation of Giuseppe "Joe the Boss" Masseria. The war ended on 15 April 1931 when Masseria was shot dead in a Coney Island restaurant, betrayed by his lieutenant Charles "Lucky" Luciano, who had made a secret deal with Maranzano. At a Chicago banquet Maranzano declared himself boss of all bosses and set out the structure of the Five Families of New York, with ranks modelled on a Roman legion.

Death

Maranzano's rule lasted only a few months. Suspicious of the ambitious younger, largely non-Sicilian generation, he planned to have Luciano, Vito Genovese, Frank Costello and others killed. Luciano struck first: on 10 September 1931, Jewish gunmen supplied by Meyer Lansky, posing as tax investigators, stabbed and shot Maranzano in his Park Avenue office. His death ended the title of capo dei capi and opened the way for the creation of the Commission.
