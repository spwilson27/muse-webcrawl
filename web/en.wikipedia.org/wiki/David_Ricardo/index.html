David Ricardo (18 April 1772 – 11 September 1823) was a British political economist, stockbroker and member of Parliament, among the most influential of the classical economists alongside Adam Smith, Thomas Malthus and John Stuart Mill.

Contributions

His Principles of Political Economy and Taxation (1817) introduced the theory of comparative advantage: countries gain by specialising in goods they produce at lower opportunity cost and trading — his example of English cloth and Portuguese wine remains the standard defence of Free trade. Ricardo developed a labour theory of value, a theory of rent as payment for the use of land's "original and indestructible powers", and the proposition now called Ricardian equivalence — that debt- and tax-financed government spending may be equivalent in effect. He debated Malthus on the Corn Laws (which he opposed as a free trader, speaking as MP for Portarlington), population and the possibility of general gluts, the question later taken up by John Maynard Keynes.
