Country: Morocco

Essaouira (Arabic: الصويرة, aṣ-Ṣawīra; Berber: ⵜⴰⵚⵚⵓⵔⵜ, Taṣṣuṛt), known until the 1960s by its Portuguese name Mogador, is a city in the western Moroccan region of Marrakech-Safi, on the Atlantic coast. It is a port and resort town noted for its fortified old town (medina), its fishing harbour, and its strong trade winds, which make it a centre for windsurfing and kitesurfing.

History

The bay of Essaouira, sheltered by the island of Mogador, has been used since ancient times; the Phoenicians and later the Romans established trading posts and a purple-dye works on the island, exploiting the murex shells of the area. The Portuguese built the Castelo Real fortress in 1506 but abandoned it within a few years.

The modern town was founded in 1764 by the Alaouite sultan Mohammed ben Abdallah, who decided to build a royal port to rival Agadir and channel the trade of southern Morocco. He hired the French engineer Théodore Cornut, a pupil of Vauban, to design the town — one of the first planned cities in Morocco — with broad straight streets inside European-style ramparts. Essaouira became Morocco's principal port for trade with Europe through the late 18th and 19th centuries, handling goods such as gold, ostrich feathers, and almonds carried by caravan from Timbuktu; a large Jewish merchant community (the tujjar as-sultan, "merchants of the sultan") played a central role in its commerce. Its importance declined after the French protectorate developed Casablanca as the main port.

The medina and landmarks

The medina of Essaouira was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2001 as an outstanding example of a late-18th-century fortified town built according to contemporary European military architecture in a North African context. Landmarks include the Skala de la Ville sea bastion with its row of bronze cannons, the Skala du Port by the harbour, the Mellah (former Jewish quarter), and the port with its fleet of blue wooden fishing boats. The offshore Île de Mogador, now a protected falcon sanctuary for Eleonora's falcons, is closed to visitors.

Culture

Essaouira is known for thuya-wood marquetry, argan oil produced in the surrounding countryside, seafood, and the annual Gnaoua World Music Festival, held since 1998, which draws hundreds of thousands of visitors. The town's windy beach has earned it the nickname "the Wind City of Africa".
