Wilhelm I (22 March 1797 – 9 March 1888) was King of Prussia from 1861 and the first German Emperor from 1871 until his death in 1888. Under his reign, and the chancellorship of Otto von Bismarck, Prussia achieved the unification of Germany and the establishment of the German Empire through the wars against Denmark (1864), Austria (1866), and France (1870–71).

Life and reign

The second son of Frederick William III, Wilhelm served in the Wars of Liberation against Napoleon and gained a reputation as a conservative soldier — his role in suppressing the revolutions of 1848 earned him the nickname "the Grapeshot Prince". He became regent for his incapacitated brother Frederick William IV in 1858 and king in 1861. A conflict over army reform led him to appoint Bismarck as minister president in 1862. Wilhelm was proclaimed German Emperor (Deutscher Kaiser) in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles on 18 January 1871. He survived several assassination attempts, one of which Bismarck used to pass the Anti-Socialist Laws of 1878. His death in 1888 began the "Year of the Three Emperors".
