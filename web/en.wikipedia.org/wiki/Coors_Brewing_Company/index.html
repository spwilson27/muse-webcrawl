Coors Brewing Company

The Coors Brewing Company is an American brewing company founded in 1873 in Golden, Colorado, by German immigrants Adolph Coors and Jacob Schueler. Its flagship beer, Coors Banquet, and its later light beer, Coors Light, became among the best-known lager brands in the United States. The company is now the US division of the Molson Coors Beverage Company.

History

Coors survived Prohibition (1920–1933) by producing malted milk, near beer, and porcelain products through its ceramics division. For much of the 20th century the company distributed only west of the Mississippi, refusing to pasteurize its beer and relying on refrigerated transport; its limited availability added to its cachet, dramatized in the 1977 film Smokey and the Bandit. Nationwide distribution was achieved by the early 1990s. Coors Light, introduced in 1978, grew into one of the top-selling beers in America.

Mergers

In 2005 Coors merged with Canada's Molson to form Molson Coors. Through the subsequent MillerCoors joint venture (2008) and its full acquisition in 2016, the Coors brands became part of one of the world's largest brewing groups. The Golden brewery remains the largest single-site brewery in the world, and the company also operates the historic Coors brewery in Colorado and facilities across the United States.
