Maryport is a town and civil parish in Cumberland, Cumbria, England. It lies on the coast of the Solway Firth at the mouth of the River Ellen, historically in Cumberland. The town had a population of 10,865 at the 2021 census.

History

The site was the location of the Roman fort of Alauna, established around AD 122 as part of the coastal defences linked to Hadrian's Wall; the Senhouse Roman Museum displays one of the largest collections of Roman altars in Britain, excavated near the fort. The modern town was founded in the mid-18th century by Humphrey Senhouse, who developed a coal port and named it after his wife Mary. Maryport grew with the coal trade, shipbuilding and iron industry, exporting coal to Ireland and beyond.

Industry and decline

The Elizabeth Dock was added in 1857. In the 20th century the closure of pits and the decline of heavy industry hit the town hard, and the last deep mine in the area closed in 1966. Today the harbour is used by fishing vessels and leisure craft, and the town is a centre for tourism on the Cumbrian coast, with the Lake District Coast Aquarium and the Wave Centre among its attractions. The Maryport and Carlisle Railway historically linked the town inland.

Notable people

Thomas Henry Ismay, founder of the White Star Line, was born in Maryport in 1837. Fletcher Christian, the Bounty mutineer, was born nearby at Cockermouth.
