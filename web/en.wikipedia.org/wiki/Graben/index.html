Graben

A graben (German for "ditch" or "trench"; plural Gräben) is a depressed block of the Earth's crust bounded on both sides by parallel normal faults. Graben form where the crust is being pulled apart — extensional tectonics — so that the central block drops down relative to the flanking blocks, which are called horsts. A very large graben with tectonic boundaries is called a rift valley.

Formation

When crustal extension begins, faulting often creates a symmetric or asymmetric (half-graben) depression. The downthrown block forms a valley that commonly fills with sediment eroded from the horst shoulders, and may host lakes or rivers — the Rhine flows along the Upper Rhine Graben between the Vosges and the Black Forest. Graben range in width from a few kilometres to hundreds of kilometres, as in the Basin and Range Province of western North America and the East African Rift.

Half-graben

Many rifts are asymmetric: a half-graben is bounded by a major normal fault on one side only, with a more gently tilted margin on the other. Half-grabens are characteristic of the East African Rift's lake basins and of many continental rift systems, and their sedimentary architecture strongly influences hydrocarbon and groundwater reservoirs.
