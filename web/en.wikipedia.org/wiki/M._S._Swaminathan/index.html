M. S. Swaminathan

Mankombu Sambasivan Swaminathan (7 August 1925 – 28 September 2023) was an Indian agronomist, agricultural scientist, geneticist and administrator, widely regarded as the principal architect of India's Green Revolution. Working with Norman Borlaug, he helped introduce and develop high-yielding varieties of wheat and rice in India in the 1960s, transforming the country from a food-importing nation facing famine into one self-sufficient in cereals. He was awarded the first World Food Prize in 1987.

Early life and education

Swaminathan was born in Kumbakonam, Tamil Nadu, into a family of doctors and scholars. Witnessing the Bengal famine of 1943, he turned from medicine to agriculture. He studied zoology at Maharaja's College, Trivandrum, and agricultural science at Madras Agricultural College, then pursued genetics at Wageningen in the Netherlands and earned a PhD from the University of Cambridge in 1952, followed by postdoctoral work at the University of Wisconsin.

Green Revolution

At the Indian Agricultural Research Institute in New Delhi, Swaminathan worked on wheat genetics, including induced mutagenesis and cytogenetics of potato and jute. In the early 1960s, he invited Norman Borlaug to India and organised trials of Borlaug's semi-dwarf Mexican wheat varieties, adapting them to Indian conditions. The resulting high-yielding varieties, supported by irrigation and fertiliser, powered the dramatic rise in Indian wheat production from the late 1960s. Swaminathan served as director of IARI, director-general of the Indian Council of Agricultural Research, and principal secretary in the Ministry of Agriculture.

Later work

From 1982 to 1988 Swaminathan was director-general of the International Rice Research Institute in the Philippines, the first Asian to hold the post. In 1988 he founded the M. S. Swaminathan Research Foundation in Chennai, dedicated to sustainable agriculture, coastal systems and rural development, and he promoted an "Evergreen Revolution" — productivity improvement without ecological harm. As chairman of the National Commission on Farmers (2004–2006), he recommended that minimum support prices for crops be set at cost plus 50 per cent, a formula that became a longstanding farmers' demand in India. He also served a term in the Rajya Sabha (2007–2013).

Death and honours

Swaminathan died in Chennai on 28 September 2023, aged 98. In February 2024 the Government of India announced he would be conferred the Bharat Ratna, the country's highest civilian award, posthumously. Time magazine had listed him among the 20 most influential Asians of the 20th century.
