Air services agreement

An air services agreement (ASA) is a treaty between two countries that governs scheduled international air services between their territories. Because the 1944 Chicago Convention left the exchange of commercial traffic rights to negotiation, international aviation grew up around a web of thousands of bilateral ASAs specifying routes, capacity, frequencies, designated airlines, and pricing rules.

History

The most influential early model was the 1946 Bermuda Agreement between the United States and the United Kingdom, which balanced liberal route access with government oversight of capacity and fares; its successor, Bermuda II (1977), was more restrictive. From the 1990s, the United States and later the European Union promoted liberalised "Open Skies" agreements that remove most restrictions on routes, capacity, and pricing. Multilateral arrangements, such as the EU single aviation market and the EU–US Open Skies agreement of 2007, have replaced many bilateral deals within their regions.

Contents

ASAs operate within the framework of the freedoms of the air. Typical provisions cover the designation and authorisation of airlines, safety and security standards, fair competition, and dispute resolution; ownership and control rules usually require designated airlines to be substantially owned by nationals of the designating state, a constraint that has shaped the structure of the international airline industry.
