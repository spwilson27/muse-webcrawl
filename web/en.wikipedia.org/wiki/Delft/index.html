Delft

Delft (Dutch pronunciation: [dɛlft]) is a city and municipality in the province of South Holland, Netherlands. It lies between Rotterdam and The Hague and has a population of roughly 100,000. Delft is known for its historic canals, its blue-and-white Delftware pottery, the painter Johannes Vermeer, and the Delft University of Technology (TU Delft).

History

Delft received city rights in 1246. In 1536 a great fire destroyed much of the town, and in 1654 the explosion of a gunpowder store — the "Delft Thunderclap" — devastated the centre and killed the painter Carel Fabritius. William of Orange, leader of the Dutch Revolt, resided in Delft and was assassinated there in 1584 in the Prinsenhof; he is buried in the Nieuwe Kerk, which became the traditional burial place of the House of Orange-Nassau.

Delftware

From the mid-17th century Delft's potteries produced the tin-glazed earthenware known as Delftware or "Delft blue", imitating Chinese porcelain imported by the Dutch East India Company. At its height the town had more than thirty factories; the Royal Delft factory (De Porceleyne Fles, founded 1653) still operates.

Education

TU Delft, founded in 1842, is the oldest and largest Dutch public technical university, and the city also hosts UNESCO-IHE, the institute for water education.
