Lubumbashi (loo-buum-BAH-shee; formerly Élisabethville in French, or Elisabethstad in Dutch) is the second-largest city in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, located in the country's southeasternmost part, along the border with Zambia. It is the capital and principal city of Haut-Katanga Province and the hub of the country's mining industry.

History

The city was founded in 1910 by Belgian colonists near the copper mine at Étoile du Congo ("Star of the Congo") and named Élisabethville after Queen Elisabeth of Belgium. It grew rapidly as the centre of the copper-rich Katanga region, administered by the mining giant Union Minière du Haut Katanga, whose smelters and camps shaped the segregated colonial city.

Lubumbashi played a central role in the turmoil following Congolese independence in 1960. It was the capital of Moïse Tshombe's secessionist State of Katanga (1960–1963), during which the deposed prime minister Patrice Lumumba was flown to Élisabethville and murdered there in January 1961 — one of the defining episodes of the Congo Crisis. In 1966, under President Mobutu Sese Seko's policy of authenticité, the city was renamed Lubumbashi. The collapse of the state mining company Gécamines in the 1990s devastated the economy, and the city saw fighting during the First and Second Congo Wars.

Economy

Lubumbashi remains the centre of Congolese copper and cobalt production, the lifeblood of the national economy. Mining, smelting and refining dominate formal employment, and the city is a rail and road junction linking the Copperbelt to ports in Angola, Zambia and South Africa. It is also a commercial and educational centre, home to the University of Lubumbashi (founded 1955) and the football club TP Mazembe, one of Africa's most successful teams.
