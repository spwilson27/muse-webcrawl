Suspension (topology)

In topology, the suspension of a topological space X, denoted SX, is the quotient space of the product X × [0, 1] in which the top end X × {1} is collapsed to a single point and the bottom end X × {0} is collapsed to another single point. Intuitively, the suspension stretches the space into a cylinder and then crimps both ends shut.

Suspension
FieldAlgebraic topology
NotationSX or ΣX

Definition

Formally, SX = (X × [0,1]) / ~, where (x, 0) ~ (x′, 0) for all x, x′ and (x, 1) ~ (x′, 1) for all x, x′. The two collapsed ends are sometimes called the north and south poles of the suspension.

Examples

The suspension of a point is an interval; the suspension of two points is a circle; the suspension of a circle is a 2-sphere. In general, the suspension of an n-sphere is an (n+1)-sphere.

Reduced suspension

For a pointed space, the reduced suspension ΣX collapses the segment joining the two poles as well, and is the version most used in homotopy theory, where it participates in the suspension–loop space adjunction.
