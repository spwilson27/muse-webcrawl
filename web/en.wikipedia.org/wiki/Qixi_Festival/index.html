Qixi Festival

Also calledQiqiao Festival; Chinese Valentine's Day
Date7th day of the 7th lunar month

The Qixi Festival (Chinese: 七夕), also known as the Qiqiao Festival, is a Chinese festival celebrating the annual meeting of the cowherd and weaver girl in Chinese mythology. It falls on the seventh day of the seventh lunisolar month of the Chinese calendar and is sometimes called Chinese Valentine's Day.

Legend

The festival derives from the legend of Zhinü (the weaver girl, symbolising the star Vega) and Niulang (the cowherd, symbolising Altair), lovers separated by the heavenly river (the Milky Way) by the Queen Mother of the West. Once a year, on the seventh night of the seventh month, a bridge of magpies forms across the river so the lovers can meet.

Customs

Traditionally, young women demonstrated their domestic skills — threading needles by moonlight, carving melons, and praying to Zhinü for skill in needlework and for a good husband. In modern times the festival is celebrated as a romantic holiday, with couples exchanging gifts and flowers.
