Altruism

Altruism is the principle and practice of concern for the well-being of other humans or animals. It is a virtue in many cultures and religions, and the opposite of selfishness. The term was coined by Auguste Comte from French altruisme.

Philosophy and ethics

Philosophers debate whether truly selfless action is possible (psychological egoism) and whether altruism is obligatory. Effective altruism applies evidence and reason to maximise the good done by charitable giving.

Biology

In evolutionary biology, altruism refers to behaviour that reduces an individual's fitness while increasing another's. Explanations include kin selection (W. D. Hamilton's rule), reciprocal altruism (Robert Trivers), and group selection. Examples include alarm calls and sterile worker insects.

Psychology

Social psychologists study bystander intervention, empathy-altruism (C. Daniel Batson), and prosocial behaviour. See compassion, sympathy and empathy.
