Slash (punctuation)

The slash is a punctuation mark, /, also known as a stroke, oblique, virgule, or forward slash. It is distinguished from the backslash (\) in computing and from the solidus (⁄), a typographically distinct fraction slash. The name "slash" entered common use with computing; traditionally the mark was called an oblique or stroke, and in British usage "stroke" remains common when reading out letters and numbers.

Uses

The slash most often denotes alternatives or a choice ("and/or", "writer/director"), stands in for per in rates (km/h), separates the elements of dates (27/10/2026), marks line breaks when poetry is quoted inline, and indicates fractions or division. In linguistics it encloses phonemic transcriptions (/s/), and in computing it separates directories in Unix-style paths and elements of URLs. In proofreading and informal writing it can join related terms, as in "the writer/director."

History

The virgule descends from a medieval scribal mark used for pauses similar to the modern comma, and by the early modern period served as a comma, period, or abbreviation mark before settling into its present roles. Its mathematical use for division spread with the development of fractional notation in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

Typography

Style guides differ on spacing: generally no spaces surround the slash in compounds and abbreviations (24/7, c/o), while spaces are used when separating full phrases or lines of poetry. The fraction slash (⁄) is designed to set superscript numerators and subscript denominators for elegant built fractions.
