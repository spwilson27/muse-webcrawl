Osaka Prefecture (大阪府, Ōsaka-fu) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Kansai region of Honshu. Its capital and largest city is Osaka, one of Japan's largest metropolitan centres. With roughly 8.8 million inhabitants in an area of about 1,905 square kilometres, it is the second-smallest prefecture by area but among the most densely populated.

Geography

Osaka Prefecture lies on the northeastern shore of Osaka Bay, part of the Seto Inland Sea. It is bordered by Hyōgo and Kyoto prefectures to the north, Nara to the east and Wakayama to the south. The Yodo River, flowing from Lake Biwa, crosses the prefecture and empties into the bay. Much of the terrain is a fertile alluvial plain, ringed by low mountains including Mount Kongō on the Nara border.

History

The area corresponds largely to the former provinces of Settsu, Kawachi and Izumi. Osaka city grew as a commercial hub during the Edo period, when it was known as the "nation's kitchen" for its role in the rice trade. The modern prefecture was established in 1868 during the Meiji Restoration. In 2025 the prefecture hosted Expo 2025 on the artificial island of Yumeshima in Osaka Bay.

Economy and cities

Osaka is a major centre of commerce, manufacturing, pharmaceuticals and electronics, and forms the core of the Keihanshin metropolitan area together with Kyoto and Kobe. Besides Osaka city, major municipalities include Sakai, Higashiōsaka, Toyonaka, Takatsuki and Hirakata.
