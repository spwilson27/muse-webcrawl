Yueyang (simplified Chinese: 岳阳; traditional Chinese: 岳陽; pinyin: Yuèyáng) is a prefecture-level city on the eastern shores of Dongting Lake and on the Yangtze River in northeastern Hunan Province, China. It has a 3,000-year history and borders Hubei and Jiangxi provinces.

History and landmarks

The city is famous for the Yueyang Tower (岳阳楼), first built in the Three Kingdoms period and repeatedly rebuilt, most notably in the Song dynasty, when the statesman Teng Zongliang commissioned the scholar Fan Zhongyan to write the celebrated essay Memorial to Yueyang Tower (岳阳楼记). Its famous lines — "Be the first to bear the world's cares, and the last to enjoy its pleasures" — made the tower an enduring symbol of public service. Junshan Island in Dongting Lake is another historic site, noted for its tea and temples.

Yueyang is an important Yangtze port and industrial city, with petrochemical, paper, and feed industries, and a busy container port at Chenglingji. The broader prefecture administers several counties and districts including Yueyanglou District, Junshan District, and the county-level city of Miluo, home of the dragon boat festival traditions linked to the poet Qu Yuan.
