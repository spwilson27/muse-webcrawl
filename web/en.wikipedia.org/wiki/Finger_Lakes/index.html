Finger Lakes

The Finger Lakes are a group of eleven long, narrow, roughly north–south lakes located in the Finger Lakes region of New York state in the United States. The lakes are glacial in origin, carved into valleys by retreating ice sheets during the last ice age, and their elongated shapes resemble fingers on a map — hence the name.

The lakes

From west to east, the eleven Finger Lakes are Conesus, Hemlock, Canadice, Honeoye, Canandaigua, Keuka, Seneca, Cayuga, Owasco, Skaneateles, and Otisco. The two longest and deepest are Seneca Lake (38 miles, maximum depth 618 feet) and Cayuga Lake (about 40 miles). Seneca, Cayuga, and Skaneateles are particularly notable for their depth and clarity.

Geology

The lakes occupy U-shaped glacial troughs. Because their surfaces lie higher than their bottoms in some cases below sea level, they are striking examples of glacial overdeepening. The region also features gorges and waterfalls, such as those at Watkins Glen and Taughannock Falls.

Wine and tourism

The Finger Lakes region is one of the premier wine-producing areas of the eastern United States, especially known for Riesling and other cold-climate varieties; the deep lakes moderate the local climate, extending the growing season along their shores. The region is also a major tourism destination, and is home to institutions such as Cornell University in Ithaca.
