Casting (performing arts)

In the performing arts, casting is the pre-production process of selecting actors, singers, dancers, or other performers for the roles in a play, film, television production, or other performance work.

Process

A casting director assembles a breakdown of roles, solicits submissions from agents and managers, and holds auditions — first rounds, callbacks, and chemistry or screen tests. Directors, producers, and (for major roles) financiers make the final decisions. Casting notices specify type, age range, and special skills; "typecasting" describes repeatedly casting an actor in similar roles.

History and profession

The dedicated casting director emerged as a distinct craft in mid-20th-century Hollywood and theatre; the Casting Society of America was founded in 1982. Major film productions may use casting teams across several countries for location shoots.

Issues

Casting practices have been debated over representation — including "colorblind" and "color-conscious" casting, whitewashing controversies, and the casting of cisgender actors in transgender roles — as well as the use of "stunt casting" of celebrities to attract audiences.
