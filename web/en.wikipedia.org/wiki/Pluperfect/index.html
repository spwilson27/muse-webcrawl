Pluperfect

The pluperfect (shortening of plusquamperfect, from Latin plus quam perfectum, "more than perfect") is a type of verb form, generally treated as a tense in certain languages, used to describe an action that occurred prior to another past action or point in time. In English, the pluperfect is formed with the auxiliary verb had followed by the past participle: "I had walked to the store before it rained."

Meaning

The pluperfect expresses anteriority relative to a reference point that is itself in the past. Where the simple past describes an event at the reference time, the pluperfect describes an event completed before that time. For example, in "When I arrived, the train had already left," the leaving of the train precedes both the arrival and the moment of speech.

In various languages

Latin had a fully inflected pluperfect tense, both active (e.g. amāveram, "I had loved") and passive. The Romance languages developed analytic pluperfects: Spanish uses había + participle, French uses the imperfect of avoir or être + participle (the plus-que-parfait), and Italian uses the imperfect of avere or essere + participle (the trapassato prossimo). German forms the Plusquamperfekt with the preterite of haben or sein plus the past participle. Ancient Greek possessed a synthetic pluperfect, used relatively rarely.

English

In English grammar, the pluperfect is often called the past perfect. It combines the past tense of the auxiliary have (had) with the past participle of the main verb. It also appears in reported speech and in counterfactual conditionals: "If I had known, I would have acted differently."
