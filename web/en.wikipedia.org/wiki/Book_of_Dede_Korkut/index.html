Book of Dede Korkut

The Book of Dede Korkut (Azerbaijani: Kitabi-Dədə Qorqud; Turkish: Dede Korkut Kitabı) is the most famous epic of the Oghuz Turks, telling heroic tales of the semi-nomadic Oghuz of Central Asia and their struggles against neighbouring peoples. It is a central monument of Azerbaijani, Turkish and Turkmen literature and oral tradition.

Contents

The book consists of a preface and twelve heroic stories (boy) centred on figures such as Dirse Khan's son Bughach, Bamsi Beyrek, Salur Kazan and the one-eyed giant Tepegöz, a monster closely paralleling the Cyclops of the Odyssey. The wise elder Dede Korkut frames the tales as narrator, counsellor and name-giver to heroes.

Manuscripts and recognition

Two main manuscripts survive, held in Dresden and the Vatican, copied in the 16th century from much older oral material. In 2018 the heritage of Dede Qorqud was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity for Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan and Turkey.
