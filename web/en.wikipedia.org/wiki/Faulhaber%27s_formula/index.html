Faulhaber's formula

Faulhaber's formula, named after the German mathematician Johann Faulhaber (1580–1635), expresses the sum of the p-th powers of the first n positive integers — 1p + 2p + … + np — as a polynomial in n of degree p + 1 whose coefficients involve the Bernoulli numbers.

Familiar cases

For p = 1 the formula gives the triangular numbers, n(n+1)/2. For p = 2 it gives the square pyramidal numbers, n(n+1)(2n+1)/6, and for p = 3 it gives [n(n+1)/2]2 — the sum of the first n cubes equals the square of the sum of the first n integers, a result known since antiquity. Faulhaber published formulas for powers up to 17 without, however, stating the general form.

The general formula

Jacob Bernoulli, in his posthumous Ars Conjectandi (1713), gave the general formula using the sequence now called Bernoulli numbers, and remarked that he computed the sum of the tenth powers of the first thousand integers in "half of a quarter of an hour." A striking feature discovered by Faulhaber is that for odd powers greater than 1, the sum is a polynomial in the triangular number n(n+1)/2.
