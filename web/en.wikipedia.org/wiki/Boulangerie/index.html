Boulangerie

A boulangerie is a French bakery specialising principally in bread, especially baguettes, as distinct from a pâtisserie, which specialises in pastries. Under French law, the title boulangerie may be used only by shops that select their raw materials, knead their dough, control its fermentation, and bake their bread on the premises.

Legal definition

A 1998 French law (the "Fischer law") reserves the name boulangerie and the title boulanger for artisans who make bread from scratch on site. Shops that merely bake off frozen or industrially produced dough must call themselves a point chaud or dépôt de pain. The rules protect the traditional trade, which still numbers tens of thousands of shops across France.

Products

Alongside baguettes — including the protected baguette de tradition française, made only with flour, water, yeast, and salt — boulangeries sell pains de campagne, wholemeal loaves, brioche, and viennoiserie such as croissants and pains au chocolat, which the baker may also make in-house.

Culture

The morning trip to the boulangerie is a fixture of French daily life, and the quality of a village's bakery is a point of local pride. In 2022, the artisanal know-how and culture of baguette bread was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
