Djibouti, officially the Republic of Djibouti, is a country in the Horn of Africa, bordered by Somalia to the south, Ethiopia to the southwest, Eritrea to the north, and the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden to the east, across the Bab el-Mandeb strait from Yemen. Strategically located at one of the world's busiest shipping lanes, Djibouti's economy is dominated by its deep-water port, which handles most of landlocked Ethiopia's trade, and by the foreign military bases it hosts — including those of France, the United States, China, Japan and Italy.

History

The region was inhabited by nomadic Afar and Somali (Issa) pastoralists and formed part of ancient trading networks with Egypt, Arabia and India. In the nineteenth century France acquired the territory through treaties with Afar sultans, founding the colony of French Somaliland (later the French Territory of the Afars and the Issas). Following a referendum, Djibouti became independent on 27 June 1977, the last French colony on mainland Africa to do so, with Hassan Gouled Aptidon as its first president. A civil war between the Issa-dominated government and Afar rebels in the early 1990s ended in a negotiated peace in 1994–2001.

Geography

Djibouti is mostly stony desert and volcanic highlands. Lake Assal, at about 155 metres below sea level, is the lowest point in Africa and one of the saltiest bodies of water in the world. The climate is hot and arid year-round, among the hottest inhabited places on Earth. The Day Forest in the Goda Mountains shelters remnant juniper woodland.

Politics and military bases

Djibouti is a presidential republic dominated since independence by the People's Rally for Progress. Ismaïl Omar Guelleh succeeded his uncle Hassan Gouled in 1999 and has been re-elected repeatedly. The country's stability and location have made it a hub for foreign forces: Camp Lemonnier hosts the largest U.S. military presence in Africa, and in 2017 China opened its first overseas naval support base near the port of Doraleh.

Economy

With almost no arable land or rainfall, Djibouti imports nearly all its food and fuel. Services — port operations, logistics, banking and base rents — dominate GDP. The Djibouti franc has been pegged to the U.S. dollar under a currency board since 1949. Major infrastructure projects, financed largely by Chinese investment, include a standard-gauge railway to Addis Ababa opened in 2018.
