Spandau Prison was a military prison in the Spandau borough of West Berlin, built in 1876. It is best known as the place of imprisonment of the seven Nazi war criminals sentenced to prison terms at the Nuremberg trials, who were held there under the joint administration of the four Allied powers from 1947 to 1987. After the death of its last prisoner, Rudolf Hess, the prison was demolished and replaced by a shopping centre, to prevent it becoming a neo-Nazi shrine.

History

The fortress-like brick prison was constructed in 1876 as a central military detention facility for the Prussian army and later held political prisoners, including after 1933 opponents of the Nazi regime. It survived the Second World War and lay in the British sector of occupied Berlin.

The Nuremberg prisoners

On 18 July 1947 the seven defendants sentenced to imprisonment at Nuremberg with terms were transferred to Spandau: Konstantin von Neurath (15 years, released 1954), Erich Raeder (life, released 1955), Karl Dönitz (10 years), Baldur von Schirach (20 years), Albert Speer (20 years), Walther Funk (life, released 1957) and Rudolf Hess (life). The prison, built for hundreds, was run for these few men by rotating detachments of American, British, French and Soviet guards, each nation taking charge for a month at a time — a unique surviving institution of four-power cooperation throughout the Cold War.

Speer and Schirach were released at the end of their sentences in 1966, leaving Hess sole prisoner for the remaining 21 years of his life. The Soviet Union vetoed all Western proposals for his release. Hess died on 17 August 1987, officially by suicide, aged 93.

Demolition

Within weeks of Hess's death the building was demolished; the rubble was dumped at sea or buried, and a British forces supermarket and car park complex — nicknamed "Hessco's" by soldiers, after the Tesco chain — was built on the site, ending four decades of four-power administration in Berlin.
