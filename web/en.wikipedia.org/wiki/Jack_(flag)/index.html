Jack (flag)

A jack is a flag flown from a short jack staff at the bow of a vessel. The jack is typically flown only when the ship is not under way, and in most navies it differs from the ensign flown at the stern. In British usage the term is bound up with the Union Jack, which originated as the jack of British warships.

Usage

Naval jacks often consist of the canton or a distinctive device from the national flag: the jack of the United States, for example, was for most of its history the blue canton of the ensign bearing the stars. Some countries use a distinct naval jack unrelated to the ensign. Merchant vessels may fly a house flag or a pilot jack in the jack position. The rules for when jacks are hoisted and lowered form part of naval ceremonial, traditionally following the making and breaking of colours each day.
