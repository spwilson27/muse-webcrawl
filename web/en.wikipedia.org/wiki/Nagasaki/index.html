Nagasaki

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nagasaki
Country: Japan (Kyushu)
Atomic bombing: 9 August 1945

Nagasaki is a port city on the northwest coast of Kyushu, Japan, historically Japan's principal window on the outside world and, on 9 August 1945, the second city destroyed by a nuclear weapon.

History

Founded as a trading port in the sixteenth century, Nagasaki hosted Portuguese and then Dutch traders, and during Japan's two centuries of seclusion the artificial island of Dejima in its harbour was the country's only point of contact with Europe. Nagasaki also has a long history of Japanese Christianity, including the martyrdom of the Twenty-Six Martyrs in 1597 and the "hidden Christians" who preserved their faith in secret. Its dramatic natural harbour and hills made it a major shipbuilding centre.

The atomic bombing

On 9 August 1945 the plutonium bomb "Fat Man" was dropped on Nagasaki after clouds obscured the primary target, Kokura. It exploded over the Urakami valley, north of the city centre; the hills shielded parts of the city but the industrial valley was devastated, and deaths by the end of 1945 are commonly estimated at 60,000–80,000. The Nagasaki Atomic Bomb Museum and Peace Park commemorate the victims, and the surviving Urakami Cathedral ruins became a symbol of the city's Catholic community.

Modern city

Today Nagasaki is a city of roughly 400,000 people, known for its night view from Mount Inasa, its Chinese-influenced cuisine (champon and castella cake), and continued peace activism alongside Hiroshima.
