Azo compound

Azo compound
Functional group–N=N– (azo group)
General formulaR–N=N–R′
Typical useDyes and pigments

Azo compounds are organic compounds bearing the functional group diazenyl R−N=N−R′, in which R and R′ can be either aryl or alkyl groups. IUPAC defines azo compounds as "derivatives of diazene (diimide), HN=NH, wherein both hydrogens are substituted by hydrocarbyl groups." The more stable derivatives contain two aryl groups. The N=N group is called an azo group.

Azo dyes

As a consequence of π-delocalization, aryl azo compounds have vivid colors, especially red, orange, and yellow. They are therefore used as dyes, known as azo dyes, which account for roughly 60–70% of all dyes used in the textile and food industries. Well-known examples include methyl orange, butter yellow, and the pigment para red.

Synthesis

Aryl azo compounds are usually synthesized by azo coupling, the reaction of a diazonium salt with an electron-rich coupling partner such as a phenol or an aniline derivative. The diazonium component is prepared by diazotization of a primary aromatic amine with nitrous acid, typically generated in situ from sodium nitrite and mineral acid at low temperature.

Chemistry and isomerism

Azo compounds can exist as cis and trans isomers about the N=N double bond; the trans form is usually the more stable. Many azo compounds undergo photochemical isomerization, a property exploited in molecular switches and photopharmacology. Reduction of the azo group cleaves the molecule to two amines, a reaction important both in dye degradation and in the activation of prodrugs such as sulfasalazine.

Safety

Some azo dyes can release carcinogenic aromatic amines upon reductive cleavage, and their use in consumer products is regulated in the European Union and elsewhere.
