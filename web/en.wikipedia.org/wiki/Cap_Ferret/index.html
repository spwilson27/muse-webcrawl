Cap Ferret

Cap Ferret (French pronunciation: [kap feʁɛ]; Occitan: Cap de Herrèt) is a headland at the southern tip of the commune of Lège-Cap-Ferret in the department of Gironde, southwestern France. It separates the Atlantic Ocean from Arcachon Bay, facing the resort town of Arcachon across the bay's entrance.

Geography

The narrow, sandy Cap Ferret peninsula extends south from Lège; its western shore is exposed Atlantic beach, while the sheltered eastern shore on Arcachon Bay hosts oyster-farming villages such as L'Herbe, Le Canon and Piraillan. The Cap Ferret lighthouse, rebuilt after wartime destruction and commissioned in 1947, stands 53 metres tall near the tip.

Tourism

Long a discreet resort popular with Bordeaux families and Parisian celebrities, Cap Ferret is known for its pine forests, oyster tasting huts, beaches and views to the Dune of Pilat across the bay. The area is largely within protected natural zones, limiting development.
