Subliminal stimuli

Subliminal stimuli (from Latin sub, "below", and limen, "threshold") are sensory stimuli presented below the threshold of conscious perception. A subliminal visual stimulus, for example, may be flashed so briefly — often for only a few milliseconds — that the viewer reports seeing nothing, yet it can still influence subsequent judgments, feelings, or behavior through priming.

Types

Subliminal stimuli may be visual, auditory, or in other modalities. Visual stimuli are commonly embedded by masking: a target image is presented very briefly and immediately replaced by a random pattern (the mask), preventing conscious identification while the brain still registers the target. Auditory subliminal stimuli include very quiet messages or sounds masked by louder material; claims about "backmasking" in music are a popular-culture variant with little scientific support.

Research findings

Laboratory studies show that subliminal stimuli can bias simple, immediate responses — for instance, briefly presented happy or angry faces can shift evaluations of subsequently shown pictures, and masked words can speed recognition of related words. However, effects are generally weak, short-lived, and limited to situations where the primed response is already relevant or the person is already motivated (for example, a subliminal drink-related prime affecting only thirsty participants). Robust "subliminal persuasion" that changes major real-world behavior has not been demonstrated.

Subliminal advertising controversy

Public anxiety about subliminal influence dates to 1957, when market researcher James Vicary claimed to have boosted popcorn and Coca-Cola sales in a cinema by flashing "Eat popcorn" and "Drink Coca-Cola" messages — a claim he later admitted was fabricated. Despite the hoax, several countries banned subliminal advertising, and the idea remains a durable fixture of popular culture. Double-blind studies of commercial "subliminal self-help" audio tapes found improvements attributable to placebo effects rather than the subliminal content.
