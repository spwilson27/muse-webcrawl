A textboard is a simple kind of Internet forum that allows users to post text messages without requiring registration or uploading images. Textboards are a text-only counterpart to imageboards: where imageboards (such as 4chan) require or emphasise an image with each new thread, a textboard thread consists purely of written posts, often by anonymous users.

Origins

The format descends from Japanese bulletin board culture. Ayashii World (1996) and especially 2channel (2ch, founded 1999 by Hiroyuki Nishimura) defined the model: many topical boards, threads ordered by most recent post ("bumping"), default anonymity (posts appear under a generic name like "Nanashi" — "nameless"), and minimal moderation tooling. English-language textboards followed, including the now-defunct world2ch — regarded as the first English textboard — and sites such as 4-ch (iichan) and Scheme BBS-style boards. Software such as Kareha (modelled on 2ch), Shiichan, and Wakaba (best known in its imageboard form) let anyone run a textboard.

Culture and features

Anonymity and ephemerality are defining traits: old threads stop being bumped and are deleted, there are no persistent accounts or reputations, and quoting conventions (such as the ">" greentext adapted from textboard quoting) developed within the culture. Textboards influenced later anonymous communities and fed memes and terminology into imageboard and wider internet culture. Although far less prominent than imageboards and social media today, textboards persist as lightweight, low-bandwidth forums, and the tradition continues at 5channel (the renamed 2channel) in Japan.
