ʻOumuamua

1I/ʻOumuamua
Discovered19 October 2017
DiscovererRobert Weryk (Pan-STARRS)
Designation1I/2017 U1
OriginInterstellar

ʻOumuamua is the first known interstellar object detected passing through the Solar System. Discovered by Robert Weryk using the Pan-STARRS telescope at Haleakalā Observatory, Hawaii, on 19 October 2017, it was already heading away from the Sun on a strongly hyperbolic trajectory.

Characteristics

ʻOumuamua showed no cometary coma yet exhibited a small non-gravitational acceleration, a puzzle that prompted explanations ranging from hydrogen or nitrogen ice outgassing to the controversial suggestion of a solar-sail-like artificial object. Its light curve indicated an elongated, tumbling body, possibly several hundred metres long, with a reddish colour resembling outer Solar System objects.

Name

The name, chosen by the Pan-STARRS team, is Hawaiian for "scout" or "messenger from afar arriving first". Its formal designation 1I/2017 U1 created a new "I" class for interstellar objects.
