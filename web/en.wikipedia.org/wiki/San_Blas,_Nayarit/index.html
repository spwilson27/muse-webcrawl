San Blas is a port town and municipality on the Pacific coast of the Mexican state of Nayarit. Founded in 1768 as a Spanish naval base and shipyard, it was New Spain's principal Pacific port for expeditions to Alta California and the Pacific Northwest. Today it is a quiet fishing and tourist town celebrated for its estuaries and birdlife.

History

The Spanish Crown established San Blas as a naval department in 1768 under the direction of Visitador José de Gálvez. From its shipyards and wharves sailed supply ships and expeditions: the San Carlos and San Antonio to found San Diego and Monterey in 1769, voyages to Nootka Sound and Alaska in the 1770s–90s, and the annual Manila galleon connections. The hilltop fortress ( Fuerte de la Contaduría ) and the old customs house survive from this era. The bell of the original church of Nuestra Señora del Rosario "La Marinera" inspired Henry Wadsworth Longfellow's last poem, "The Bells of San Blas" (1882). After Mexican independence the port declined as its harbour silted, and in the Mexican Revolution and Cristero War eras the town saw fighting around its customs house.

Geography and nature

San Blas lies amid river estuaries and mangrove wetlands of the San Cristóbal and other rivers. The La Tovara nature reserve, accessible by boat through mangrove channels, protects crocodiles and hundreds of bird species — the region is one of Mexico's premier birdwatching sites, with herons, boat-billed herons, wood storks and endemic species. Nearby beaches such as Playa Las Islitas are known for long, gentle surf; a ride at Matanchén Bay was once famously listed among the world's longest waves. The town is also notorious for biting sandflies (jejenes).

Modern town

Fishing, shrimp farming, agriculture (mangoes, bananas) and growing eco-tourism sustain the modern economy. San Blas is home port of a small naval base and a departure point for whale-watching trips in winter.
