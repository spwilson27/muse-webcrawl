People meter

A people meter is an audience measurement tool used to measure the viewing habits of TV and cable audiences. The system was originally developed by the British company Audits of Great Britain (AGB), and its American arm, AGB Television Research, first provided service in 1988. People meters are the basis of television ratings systems in many countries, most famously the Nielsen ratings in the United States.

How it works

The people meter is a box attached to each television set in a sample household. It records when the set is on and what channel is being watched. Each member of the household is assigned a personal viewing button on the unit, and viewers are required to press their button when they begin and end watching. The device stores the data and transmits it, typically overnight via a telephone line or internet connection, to the measurement company, which combines it with data from other sample households to produce ratings estimates.

History

Before people meters, audiences were measured with paper diaries and, earlier, with meters that recorded only when a set was on and tuned to a channel, without identifying who was watching. Nielsen introduced the people meter in the United States in 1987. The change was controversial because the new method initially produced lower ratings than the diary system it replaced, particularly for network programming. Later developments include the Portable People Meter (PPM), a pager-like device developed by Arbitron that detects inaudible codes embedded in audio broadcasts and can measure radio as well as television exposure.

Criticism

People meters have been criticized for sample bias, for reliance on viewers' compliance in pressing their buttons, and for the accuracy of the demographic data collected, particularly among younger and minority audiences. Ratings from people meter panels nevertheless remain the standard currency for buying and selling television advertising in most markets.
