Senyera

The Senyera is a vexillological symbol based on the coat of arms of the Crown of Aragon: four red horizontal stripes (pallets) on a gold (yellow) field. It is the flag of Catalonia and, in versions with local charges, of Aragon, the Balearic Islands, and the Valencian Community, and it forms part of the flag of Spain.

Origins

The arms are among the oldest heraldic symbols in Europe, documented from the twelfth century in the seals of the counts of Barcelona. Their origin is debated: one tradition links them to Wilfred the Hairy, Count of Barcelona in the ninth century, who according to a legend popularized centuries later received the arms when the Frankish king drew four bloody fingers across his golden shield. Heraldic scholarship generally treats the story as a later invention, though the arms' association with the House of Barcelona is genuine and medieval.

Modern use

The plain striped banner is the official flag of Catalonia, where it is a powerful national symbol displayed on public buildings and at sporting and civic events. A variant with a blue star in a white triangle — the Estelada — is used by supporters of Catalan independence. Related striped flags appear across the former territories of the Crown of Aragon, including in Provence and Roussillon in southern France.
