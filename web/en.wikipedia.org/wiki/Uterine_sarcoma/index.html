Uterine sarcoma

SpecialtyGynaecologic oncology
Frequency~3% of uterine cancers
Leiomyosarcoma is the most common type of uterine sarcoma in adults

Uterine sarcomas are a group of rare malignant tumours that arise from the smooth muscle or connective tissue of the uterus. They account for roughly 2–5% of all uterine malignancies and behave more aggressively than the much more common endometrial carcinomas, with a tendency to spread through the bloodstream, particularly to the lungs.

Types

The principal types are leiomyosarcoma (arising from the myometrium), endometrial stromal sarcoma, and undifferentiated uterine sarcoma; carcinosarcoma, formerly grouped with them, is now classified as a type of endometrial carcinoma. Symptoms often mimic benign fibroids — abnormal bleeding, pelvic pain, and a rapidly enlarging uterus — which makes diagnosis before surgery difficult.

Treatment

The mainstay of treatment is surgery, usually total hysterectomy; adjuvant chemotherapy or radiation may be considered depending on type and stage. Because diagnosis is frequently made only after removal of a presumed fibroid, the use of power morcellation in such surgery has been restricted over concerns about disseminating unsuspected sarcoma.
