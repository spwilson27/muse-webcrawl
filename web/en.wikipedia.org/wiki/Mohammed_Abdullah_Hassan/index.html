Mohammed Abdullah Hassan (April 7, 1856 – December 21, 1920) was a Somali religious and political leader who founded the Dervish movement and led a twenty-year armed resistance against British, Italian, and Ethiopian colonial forces in the Horn of Africa. British accounts dubbed him the "Mad Mullah", while Somali tradition remembers him as the Sayyid, a national hero and accomplished poet.

Dervish movement

A member of the Salihiyya Sufi order, Hassan began preaching against foreign influence and Christian missionaries in the 1890s. In 1899 he proclaimed a holy war and built the Dervish state, with capitals including Taleh, whose distinctive stone fortifications still stand. The colonial powers mounted repeated campaigns against the Dervishes; Hassan's forces won notable victories but also suffered defeats, including at Jidballi in 1904.

Defeat and death

In early 1920 the British launched a final combined offensive, using Royal Air Force aircraft to bomb Taleh — one of the earliest uses of air power in Africa. Hassan fled to the Ogaden, where he died in December 1920, probably of influenza. His poetry, composed in Somali, remains central to Somali literature, and he is commemorated by statues in Mogadishu.
