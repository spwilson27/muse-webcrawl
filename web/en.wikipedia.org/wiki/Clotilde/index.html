Clotilde

Clotilde (c. 474 – 3 June 545), also known as Clotilda or Saint Clotilde, was a Burgundian princess and queen consort of the Franks as the wife of Clovis I. She is credited in Frankish tradition with persuading Clovis to convert to Catholic Christianity, a decision of lasting consequence for the history of France, and she is venerated as a saint.

Life

Clotilde was the daughter of the Burgundian king Chilperic II, who was murdered by his brother Gundobad. Raised a Catholic in a kingdom ruled by Arian Christians, she married Clovis around 493. According to Gregory of Tours, she repeatedly urged her pagan husband to convert; he finally did so after attributing his victory at the Battle of Tolbiac to Christ, and was baptised at Reims, traditionally in 496 or 508.

Widowhood and legacy

After Clovis's death in 511, Clotilde's later years were marred by wars among her sons and grandsons, including the murder of her son Chlodomer's children. She retired to Tours, devoted herself to prayer and charity, and founded churches and monasteries. French tradition honours her, with Clovis, as a founder of the French monarchy; the basilica of Sainte-Clotilde in Paris bears her name.
