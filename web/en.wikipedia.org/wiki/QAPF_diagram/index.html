QAPF diagram

A QAPF diagram is a double ternary diagram used to classify igneous rocks based on their mineralogical composition. The acronym QAPF stands for the four mineral groups used for classification: Quartz, Alkali feldspar, Plagioclase, and Feldspathoid (foid). The percentages of these minerals are recalculated so that their sum is 100%, and the rock is plotted on one of the two triangles: the QAP triangle for silica-oversaturated and saturated rocks, and the APF triangle for silica-undersaturated rocks.

Use

The QAPF classification, based on the work of Albert Streckeisen and adopted by the International Union of Geological Sciences (IUGS), is the standard for naming plutonic rocks when their modal mineralogy can be determined under the microscope. Separate fields on the diagram define rock names such as granite, granodiorite, tonalite, syenite, monzonite, gabbro, and foid syenite. For volcanic rocks, where mineral modes are often hard to determine, the chemical TAS classification is usually used instead.
