Super Mario Bros. 3 is a 1988 platform game developed and published by Nintendo for the Nintendo Entertainment System. It was released in Japan in 1988 and in North America in 1990, and is regarded by many critics as one of the greatest video games of all time. Mario (and Luigi, in two-player mode) must travel through eight themed kingdoms of the Mushroom World to defeat Bowser's seven children, the Koopalings, restore each kingdom's transformed king, and finally rescue Princess Toadstool.

Gameplay

The game greatly expanded on its predecessors: an overworld map screen connects the levels and lets players choose paths and use collected items; new power-ups include the Super Leaf, which gives Mario a raccoon tail for flight, the Tanooki Suit, the Frog Suit for swimming, and the Hammer Suit; and Mario can pick up and throw shells and slide down slopes. Themed worlds — Grass Land, Desert Land, Water Land, Giant Land (where enemies are enormous), Sky Land, Ice Land, Pipe Land and Dark Land — introduced a scale and variety new to the series, capped by airship and fortress stages. The game also introduced "warp whistles" that let players skip ahead, now famous secrets of early game culture.

Release and reception

In North America the game was famously previewed in the 1989 film The Wizard, which built enormous anticipation ahead of its February 1990 release. Super Mario Bros. 3 sold more than 17 million copies worldwide, making it the best-selling NES game not bundled with the console. It was remade as part of Super Mario All-Stars (1993) and ported to Game Boy Advance as Super Mario Advance 4 (2003). Critics then and since have praised its level design, graphics, music and sheer inventiveness, and it routinely appears at or near the top of "greatest games" lists.
