Matriculation

Matriculation is the formal process of entering a university, or of becoming eligible to enter by fulfilling certain academic requirements, such as passing a matriculation examination. The term derives from the Latin matricula, a register or roll, reflecting the act of entering a student's name on the university's official list of members.

University matriculation

At older universities, such as Oxford and Cambridge, matriculation is marked by a ceremony at which new students formally become members of the university and of their college. At Cambridge the ceremony is held by colleges; at Oxford incoming students attend a ceremony in the Sheldonian Theatre, wearing academic dress. Similar formal ceremonies, often called matriculation or convocation, are held at universities in Scotland, Germany and elsewhere.

Matriculation examinations

In many education systems, "matriculation" refers to the secondary-school leaving examination that qualifies a student for university admission. Examples include the matriculation examinations of South Africa, where successful completion yields the National Senior Certificate; the Finnish ylioppilastutkinto; and the German Abitur. In South Asian usage, "matric" commonly denotes the class 10 board examination.
