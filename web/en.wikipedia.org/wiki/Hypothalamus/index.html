Hypothalamus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The hypothalamus is a small region at the base of the Brain, below the thalamus, that links the nervous system to the endocrine system through the pituitary gland. Despite being only about the size of an almond, it regulates body temperature, hunger, thirst, sleep–wake cycles, and many autonomic and emotional responses — much of the body's homeostasis.

Functions

Hypothalamic neurons monitor blood temperature, osmolality, glucose and hormone levels. Through releasing and inhibiting hormones sent to the anterior pituitary, it controls growth, thyroid and adrenal function, and reproduction. It also produces the hormones oxytocin and vasopressin (ADH), released from the posterior pituitary, and contains the suprachiasmatic nucleus, the master circadian clock.

Clinical significance

Damage from tumours, surgery or inflammation can cause disorders of temperature control, appetite and obesity, diabetes insipidus (failure of ADH), sleep disturbance and hormonal deficiencies. The hypothalamus is also a target of research into appetite regulation and eating disorders.
