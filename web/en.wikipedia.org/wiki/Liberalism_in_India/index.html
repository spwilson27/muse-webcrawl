Liberalism in India

Liberalism in India is represented by various political parties, movements, and intellectual traditions that emphasise individual rights, the rule of law, limited government, and free markets. Its roots lie in nineteenth-century reform movements that engaged with British liberal thought.

History

Figures such as Raja Ram Mohan Roy advocated social reform and a free press in the 1820s–30s, and the early Indian National Congress (founded 1885) was dominated by moderate liberals such as Dadabhai Naoroji and Gopal Krishna Gokhale, who sought constitutional reform within the British Empire. After independence in 1947, the Indian Constitution embedded liberal principles — fundamental rights, universal suffrage, and an independent judiciary — though economic policy under the early Congress governments was strongly statist.

Parties and economic liberalisation

The Swatantra Party (1959–1974), led by C. Rajagopalachari, championed free enterprise against the "permit raj". The economic reforms of 1991 under P. V. Narasimha Rao and finance minister Manmohan Singh liberalised trade and industry. Later initiatives such as the Swatantra Bharat Party and think tanks like the Centre for Civil Society have promoted liberal ideas, though no avowedly liberal party has achieved major electoral success.
