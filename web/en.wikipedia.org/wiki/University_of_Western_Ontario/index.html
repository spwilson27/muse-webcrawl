The University of Western Ontario (UWO), branded as Western University since 2012, is a public research university in London, Ontario, Canada. Founded in 1878, it is one of Canada's oldest universities and a member of the U15 group of Canadian research universities.

History

The university was founded by Bishop Isaac Hellmuth and the Anglican Diocese of Huron as the Western University of London, Ontario. It was incorporated by an act of the Ontario legislature in 1878 and became non-denominational in 1908. Its first president was the Reverend Alfred J. Cooper, and its early faculties included arts, divinity, law and medicine.

Campus

Western's main campus covers roughly 455 hectares along the Thames River in London, Ontario. The campus is known for its Collegiate Gothic architecture, including University College Tower, a local landmark. The university operates several affiliated university colleges: Brescia, Huron and King's University College.

Academics

Western has twelve faculties, including the Ivey Business School, the Schulich School of Medicine & Dentistry, the Faculty of Law, and the Don Wright Faculty of Music. The university enrolls more than 40,000 students and is consistently ranked among the top research universities in Canada.

Athletics

Western's varsity teams are known as the Mustangs. The football team, one of the most successful in Canadian university sport, plays at Western Alumni Stadium and has won multiple Vanier Cup championships.
