Alcove

An alcove is a small recessed section of a room or an arched opening, typically used for a bed, seating, shelving, or the display of a statue or other object. The word derives from the Spanish alcoba, itself from the Arabic al-qubba, meaning "the vault" or "the dome".

Architecture

In architecture, an alcove is a recess in a wall or a partially enclosed space branching from a larger room. Alcoves have been used since antiquity for sleeping — in medieval and early modern Europe, alcove beds enclosed by curtains were common in parts of France, the Netherlands and Scandinavia, conserving warmth in cold rooms. In Japanese traditional architecture, the tokonoma is a raised alcove in a reception room in which art objects, scrolls and flower arrangements are displayed for guests.

Alcoves also appear in garden design as small recesses in walls or hedges, often containing a seat or statue. In geology, the term describes arched, steep-walled recesses in rock, such as those formed by spring sapping in canyon walls of the Colorado Plateau, which were sometimes used by Ancestral Pueblo peoples for shelter and storage.

Uses

  • Sleeping alcoves — space-saving beds recessed into walls, revived in modern studio apartments;
  • Reading nooks and window seats;
  • Display alcoves for sculpture, shrines or artwork;
  • Architectural niches in churches and mosques, related to the apse and the mihrab.
