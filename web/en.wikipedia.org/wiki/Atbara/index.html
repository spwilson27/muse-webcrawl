Atbara

Atbara (Arabic: عطبرة, ʿAṭbara) is a city in River Nile State in north-eastern Sudan, located at the confluence of the Nile and the Atbara River, about 320 kilometres (200 mi) north of Khartoum.

History

Atbara grew as a railway junction town: it stands where the railway from Khartoum to Wadi Halfa meets the line to Port Sudan on the Red Sea, and its workshops made it a centre of Sudan's railway industry and labour movement. In 1898, near the town, Kitchener's Anglo-Egyptian army defeated Mahdist forces at the Battle of Atbara during the reconquest of Sudan.

Geography and economy

The city lies on the east bank of the Nile just below the mouth of the seasonal Atbara River. It is a major rail hub, with extensive workshops, and historically housed the headquarters of Sudan Railways. Cement production and agriculture in the surrounding irrigated land are also important.

Transport

Atbara station connects Khartoum with Port Sudan and Wadi Halfa; the town is sometimes called the "railway city" of Sudan.
