Drought tolerance

Drought tolerance is the ability by which a plant maintains its biomass production during arid or drought conditions. Some plants are naturally adapted to dry conditions, surviving with protection mechanisms such as desiccation tolerance, detoxification, or repair of xylem embolism. Others, such as many crop plants, are not naturally drought tolerant but have been bred or engineered for improved performance under water-limited conditions.

Mechanisms

Plant responses to water deficit are commonly grouped into three strategies:

  • Drought escape — completing the life cycle quickly, before drought develops; many desert ephemerals germinate, flower, and set seed within weeks of rain.
  • Drought avoidance — maintaining high tissue water potential despite dry soil, through deep or extensive root systems, reduced leaf area, waxy cuticles, stomatal closure, and water-storing tissues (succulence).
  • Drought tolerance proper — continuing to function at low tissue water potential, via osmotic adjustment (accumulating solutes such as proline and sugars), protective proteins such as LEA (late embryogenesis abundant) proteins and dehydrins, antioxidant systems, and membrane stabilisation.

Physiology and signalling

The hormone abscisic acid (ABA) is the central signal: it accumulates in drying roots and leaves and triggers stomatal closure and the expression of stress-responsive genes. Transcription factor families including DREB, NAC, and AREB/ABF regulate large suites of drought-response genes. Crassulacean acid metabolism (CAM) plants — such as cacti and agaves — open their stomata at night to fix CO2, dramatically reducing water loss.

Agricultural significance

Drought is the single largest cause of crop yield loss worldwide. Breeding programmes and biotechnology efforts aim to improve drought tolerance in staples such as maize, wheat, rice, and sorghum; the first commercial transgenic drought-tolerant maize (carrying the bacterial cspB gene) was released in the United States in 2013. Conventional selection for traits such as stay-green, root depth, and canopy temperature depression remains central, since field drought tolerance is a complex, multi-gene trait.
