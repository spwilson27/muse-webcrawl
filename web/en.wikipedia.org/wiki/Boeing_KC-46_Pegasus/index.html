Boeing KC-46 Pegasus

The Boeing KC-46 Pegasus is an American military aerial refueling and strategic military transport aircraft developed by Boeing from its 767 jet airliner. In February 2011, the tanker was selected by the United States Air Force (USAF) as the winner in the KC-X tanker competition to replace older Boeing KC-135 Stratotankers. The first operational KC-46 was delivered to McConnell Air Force Base in January 2019. The Air Force intends to procure 179 Pegasus aircraft by 2027, though deliveries have continued beyond that under further lots.

Development

The KC-46 derives from Boeing's KC-767, derived in turn from the 767-200ER airliner. The KC-X competition was long and controversial: an earlier lease deal collapsed amid scandal, a 2008 award to Northrop Grumman/EADS was overturned on protest, and Boeing won the re-run competition in 2011 with a fixed-price development contract. The design combines the 767-200ER fuselage, 767-400ER wing and flight deck displays, a fly-by-wire refueling boom derived from the KC-10's, and a Remote Vision System (RVS) of cameras instead of a direct-view boom operator station. Fuselages are built in Everett, Washington, at the Boeing Everett Factory.

Program issues

The program suffered cost overruns (borne largely by Boeing under the fixed-price contract, with charges exceeding $7 billion) and technical deficiencies, most notably the RVS, whose cameras distorted depth perception in certain lighting, requiring a redesigned "RVS 2.0" to be retrofitted, and stiffness issues with the refueling boom that limited refueling of lighter receiver aircraft such as the A-10. Deliveries began in 2019 despite open Category 1 deficiencies, with fixes negotiated for later years.

Design and capability

The Pegasus carries up to 96 tonnes (212,299 lb) of fuel, all of it transferable, and can refuel using both a centerline flying boom (1,200 US gal/min) and probe-and-drogue systems via a centerline drogue and wing aerial refueling pods. The main deck accommodates up to 114 troops, 18 military 463L pallets, or aeromedical evacuation of 58 patients. It has defensive systems including infrared countermeasures and is nuclear, biological, and chemical hardened for the crew.

Operators

The United States Air Force is the primary operator, stationing KC-46s at McConnell, Pease, Seymour Johnson, Altus, and other bases. Japan selected the KC-46 (as the KC-767's successor) and took delivery of its first aircraft in 2021; Israel has also ordered four aircraft.

Specifications

Crew: 3 basic (pilot, co-pilot, boom operator); up to 15 with additional crew. Length: 50.5 m (165 ft 6 in); wingspan: 47.5 m (156 ft 1 in); height: 15.9 m (52 ft 1 in). Powerplant: 2 × Pratt & Whitney PW4062 turbofans, 62,100 lbf (282 kN) each. Maximum speed: 914 km/h (Mach 0.86). Range: 11,830 km (7,350 mi). Fuel capacity: 96,297 kg (212,299 lb).
