The penalty area or 18-yard box (also known less formally as the penalty box or simply the box) is an area of an association football pitch. It is rectangular and extends 16.5 metres (18 yards) to each side of the goal and 16.5 metres in front of it. Within the penalty area is the penalty spot, 11 metres (12 yards) in front of the centre of the goal. An arc of 9.15 metres (10 yards) from the penalty spot lies outside the area, marking the minimum distance players must keep at a penalty kick.

Functions

The penalty area has several functions under Law 1 and the other Laws of the Game:

  • A direct free kick foul committed by a defending player inside their own penalty area results in a penalty kick rather than a free kick.
  • The goalkeeper may handle the ball inside their own penalty area (subject to restrictions such as the back-pass rule).
  • Goal kicks and defensive free kicks taken from inside the area are not in play until the ball leaves the area or is touched by another player; opponents must leave the area before the kick is taken.
  • Penalty kicks and kick-offs in penalty shoot-outs are taken from the penalty spot.

History

Penalty areas were introduced in 1902, replacing an earlier rule under which a penalty kick could be awarded for fouls within 12 yards of the goal line across the whole width of the pitch. The D-shaped arc was added in 1937 to keep players the required distance from the penalty spot.

The goal area

Inside the penalty area lies the smaller goal area (the "six-yard box"), extending 5.5 metres to each side of the goal and 5.5 metres in front of it, from within which goal kicks may be taken.
