Thornton Affair

The Thornton Affair, also known as the Thornton Skirmish, was a military clash on April 25, 1846, between United States and Mexican forces on the disputed territory north of the Rio Grande. A Mexican cavalry force ambushed a U.S. dragoon patrol under Captain Seth B. Thornton, killing and capturing most of it. The incident provided President James K. Polk with his justification for asking Congress to declare war on Mexico, beginning the Mexican–American War.

Background

After the U.S. annexation of Texas in 1845, Mexico refused to recognise Texan independence and claimed the Nueces River as the boundary, while the United States claimed the Rio Grande. In early 1846 Polk ordered General Zachary Taylor to advance to the Rio Grande, where Taylor built Fort Texas opposite Matamoros. Mexican general Mariano Arista crossed the river with the Army of the North intending to cut Taylor's supply line to Point Isabel.

The skirmish

On April 24–25, 1846, Captain Seth Thornton led two companies of the 2nd U.S. Dragoons — about 63 men — to investigate reports of Mexican crossings. At the Rancho de Carricitos, roughly 1,600 Mexican cavalry under General Anastasio Torrejón surrounded the dragoons. In the ensuing fight, 11 Americans were killed, 6 wounded, and the rest, including Thornton, were captured.

Consequences

Taylor reported that "hostilities may now be considered as commenced". Polk, who had already decided on war, told Congress on May 11 that Mexico had "shed American blood upon the American soil", and Congress declared war on May 13, 1846. The claim that the clash occurred on American soil was disputed at the time — most famously by Congressman Abraham Lincoln's "Spot Resolutions" — because the territory between the Nueces and the Rio Grande was claimed by both nations.
