Iguanodon

Iguanodon is a genus of herbivorous ornithopod dinosaur that lived in the Early Cretaceous period, roughly 126 to 122 million years ago, in what is now Europe. One of the first dinosaurs to be formally described, it was named by Gideon Mantell in 1825, and it formed part of the original trio of genera on which Richard Owen based the group Dinosauria in 1842.

Description

Iguanodon was a large, bulky herbivore reaching about 9 to 11 metres in length and weighing around 3 to 4 tonnes. It could move both bipedally and quadrupedally. Its most distinctive feature is the large, conical thumb spike, once famously mistaken by Mantell for a horn on its nose. Its beak-like mouth and cheek teeth were suited to cropping and grinding vegetation.

Discovery

Fossil teeth found in Sussex, England, were recognised by Mantell as belonging to a giant herbivorous reptile resembling an iguana, hence the name "iguana-tooth". In 1878 miners at Bernissart, Belgium, uncovered dozens of nearly complete skeletons, allowing the first accurate reconstruction of a dinosaur's posture; the resulting mounts became world famous.

Classification

Iguanodon belongs to the Ornithischia, within the iguanodontian ornithopods, a group that later gave rise to the hadrosaurids (duck-billed dinosaurs).
