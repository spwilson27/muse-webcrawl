The Øresund (Danish: Øresund; Swedish: Öresund) is a strait between the Danish island of Zealand and the Swedish region of Scania. Together with the Great Belt and Little Belt, it forms one of the Danish straits connecting the Baltic Sea to the Kattegat and the Atlantic Ocean. It is one of the busiest shipping lanes in the world.

Geography

The strait is 118 km long and varies in width from 4 km at its narrowest — between Helsingør in Denmark and Helsingborg in Sweden — to 28 km. The island of Saltholm and the artificial Peberholm lie in its southern part, near Copenhagen and Malmö.

History

Control of the strait was strategically vital for centuries. Denmark levied the Sound Dues on foreign ships passing through from 1429 until 1857, a major source of royal revenue collected at Kronborg Castle in Helsingør. The strait also marked the historic border between Denmark and Sweden after Scania was ceded to Sweden by the Treaty of Roskilde in 1658.

The Øresund Bridge

The Øresund Bridge, opened on 1 July 2000, carries a motorway and railway between Copenhagen and Malmö across the strait via Peberholm and the Drogden Tunnel. It created a fixed link integrating the two metropolitan areas.

The Øresund Region

The transnational Øresund Region — Greater Copenhagen and eastern Denmark together with Scania — has a population of about four million and is marketed as a single hub for life sciences, technology, and higher education, with many residents commuting daily across the border.
