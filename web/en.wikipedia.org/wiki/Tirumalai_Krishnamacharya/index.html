Tirumalai Krishnamacharya

Tirumalai Krishnamacharya (18 November 1888 – 28 February 1989) was an Indian yoga teacher, ayurvedic healer and scholar, often called "the father of modern yoga". He is credited with reviving hatha yoga and shaping its modern, posture-based form through his teaching at the Mysore Palace in the 1930s–40s.

Life

Born in Muchukundapura in present-day Karnataka, Krishnamacharya studied Sanskrit, logic and the Vedas, and by his own account spent seven years in Tibet learning yoga from a master named Ramamohana Brahmachari near Lake Manasarovar. Sponsored by the Maharaja of Mysore, he ran a yoga school at the Jaganmohan Palace from 1933, teaching an athletic style combining asana, pranayama and movement (vinyasa).

Students and legacy

His students included B. K. S. Iyengar, K. Pattabhi Jois, Indra Devi, and his son T. K. V. Desikachar — founders respectively of Iyengar yoga, Ashtanga vinyasa yoga, the Western celebrity-yoga movement, and the individualised Viniyoga approach. His maxim "teach what is inside you, not as it applies to you, but as it applies to the person in front of you" summarised his adaptive method.
