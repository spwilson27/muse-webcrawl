Fortification

A fortification (from Latin fortis "strong" and facere "to make") is a military construction designed for the defence of territories in warfare, and is used also to establish rule in a region during peacetime. The term is derived from Latin fortis ("strong") and facere ("to make").

Fortifications are military constructions and buildings designed for defence in warfare. Humans have constructed defensive works for many thousands of years, in a variety of increasingly complex designs. The term is derived from the Latin fortis ("strong") and facere ("to make"). From very early history to modern times, defensive walls have often been necessary for cities to survive in an ever-changing world of invasion and conquest. Some settlements in the Indus Valley Civilization were also the first small cities to be fortified. In ancient Greece, large stone walls had been built in Mycenaean Greece, such as the ancient site of Mycenae (famous for the huge stone blocks of its "cyclopean" walls).

History

The earliest fortifications protected settlements with ditches, earthen ramparts and wooden palisades, as at neolithic and bronze-age hillforts. Ancient Near Eastern and Mediterranean cities raised massive mudbrick and stone walls; medieval Europe developed the castle and the concentric walled town. The spread of gunpowder artillery in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries made high thin medieval walls obsolete and produced the low, thick, angular star fort (trace italienne), with bastions arranged so every wall face was covered by flanking fire, pioneered in Italy and perfected by engineers such as Vauban in France.

In the nineteenth century, rifled artillery and explosive shells forced fortifications underground and into dispersed ring fortresses of detached forts; in the twentieth, fixed lines like the Maginot Line were outmanoeuvred by mobile warfare, while concrete bunkers, trenches and, later, hardened missile silos continued the defensive tradition. Modern fortification focuses on protection against blast, precision weapons and improvised threats, from base perimeter defences to hardened command facilities.

Types

  • City walls and citadels — permanent urban defences.
  • Castles and fortresses — strongpoints controlling territory or routes.
  • Star forts and bastion systems — gunpowder-era artillery fortresses.
  • Coastal fortifications — batteries and forts guarding harbours.
  • Field fortifications — trenches, redoubts and obstacles built by armies in the field.
  • Defensive lines — extended systems such as Hadrian's Wall, the Great Wall of China, the Maginot and Siegfried lines and the Atlantic Wall.
