Kailua-Kona, Hawaii

Kailua-Kona is an unincorporated community and census-designated place (CDP) in Hawaii County, Hawaii, United States, on the western coast of the island of Hawaii. It is the largest settlement in West Hawaii and the centre of the Kona coffee-growing region, with a population of about 20,000 at the 2020 census. The town lies on the leeward slopes of Hualālai volcano, in the district of Kona, and is commonly called simply "Kona" locally.

History

Kailua was the residence of King Kamehameha I in the final years of his life (1812–1819), at Kamakahonu, now the site of Kailua Pier; his successor Kamehameha II broke the kapu system there in 1819. In 1820 American missionaries arrived, and Mokuaikaua Church — Hawaii's oldest Christian church, constructed 1835–1837 from lava rock and coral lime — still stands on Aliʻi Drive. Huliheʻe Palace, built in 1838, later served as a vacation residence of Hawaiian royalty. Through the 19th and 20th centuries the surrounding uplands became famous for Kona coffee, first planted in the 1820s.

Tourism and events

Today Kailua-Kona is a resort town with hotels and restaurants along Aliʻi Drive, a cruise-ship tender port at Kailua Pier, and deep-sea fishing for Pacific blue marlin. It is internationally known as the start and finish of the Ironman World Championship triathlon, held each October since 1981, and hosts the annual Kona Coffee Cultural Festival. Kona International Airport lies about 7 miles north of town. In 2006 a magnitude 6.7 earthquake off the coast damaged buildings in the area, including the historic Huliheʻe Palace.
