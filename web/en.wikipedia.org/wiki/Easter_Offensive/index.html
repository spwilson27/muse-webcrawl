Easter Offensive

The Easter Offensive, known in Vietnam as Chiến dịch Xuân–Hè 1972, was a major military campaign conducted by the People's Army of Vietnam (North Vietnam) against the Army of the Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam) and the United States during the Vietnam War. Launched on 30 March 1972, it was the largest conventional invasion of the war, involving some 200,000 North Vietnamese troops.

Background

By 1972 the United States was withdrawing its ground forces under the policy of Vietnamization, leaving South Vietnamese forces increasingly responsible for their own defence. Hanoi sought to win a decisive victory, seize territory before any negotiated settlement, and influence the Paris peace talks.

The campaign

The offensive struck on three fronts. In the north, North Vietnamese divisions crossed the Demilitarized Zone and captured Quảng Trị. In the Central Highlands, forces advanced toward Kontum. In the south, troops attacked across the Cambodian border toward An Lộc, threatening Saigon. South Vietnamese forces, heavily supported by American air power — including B-52 strikes and, from May, the Operation Linebacker bombing campaign and the mining of Haiphong harbour — eventually halted the advance.

Outcome

An Lộc and Kontum held, and South Vietnamese troops recaptured Quảng Trị in September. The offensive ended in stalemate in October 1972 with very heavy casualties on both sides. North Vietnam retained some territory in the south, which became a factor in the Paris Peace Accords of January 1973.
