Mandeville is a city in St. Tammany Parish, Louisiana, United States, situated on the north shore of Lake Pontchartrain. It was founded in 1834 by Bernard de Marigny de Mandeville and developed as a resort town for New Orleanians escaping the summer heat.

History

Marigny, a wealthy Creole developer who had earlier laid out the Faubourg Marigny in New Orleans, established Mandeville as a planned community. Steamboats carried visitors across the lake, and the town became known for its oak-lined lakeshore and healthful breezes. The Lake Pontchartrain Causeway, completed in the 1950s and 1960s, connected Mandeville directly to the New Orleans metropolitan area and drove suburban growth.

Geography

Mandeville lies at the northern end of the Causeway, one of the longest bridges over water in the world. Its lakefront features a seawall, harbour and the trailhead of the Tammany Trace rail trail.
