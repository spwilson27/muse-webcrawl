Maithili (मैथिली) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken in the Mithila region of northern India and southern Nepal, encompassing northern Bihar and adjoining districts of Jharkhand, and the eastern Terai of Nepal. It is one of the 22 languages listed in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution of India, a status granted in 2003, and is the second most widely spoken language of Nepal. Maithili has a literary tradition reaching back to the fourteenth century, crowned by the poet Vidyapati, whose devotional songs remain central to the region's culture.

History and literature

Maithili developed from the Magadhi Prakrit and Apabhramsha of eastern India. The earliest Maithili literature is associated with Jyotirishvara's Varna Ratnakara (fourteenth century), the oldest prose work in a modern north Indian language. Vidyapati (c. 1352–1448) established Maithili as a literary language with his love and devotional lyrics, which influenced Bengali Vaishnavism and poetry across eastern India. For centuries Maithili was written in its own script, Tirhuta (also called Mithilakshar), as well as in Kaithi; today Devanagari is dominant, though Tirhuta has seen revival efforts and was added to Unicode in 2014.

Classification and dialects

George Grierson classified Maithili as a distinct language of the Bihari group of Eastern Indo-Aryan, though Indian census practice long counted it as a dialect of Hindi, a classification speakers' movements successfully challenged. Major varieties include the standard central dialect of Darbhanga and Madhubani, western and southern varieties influenced by neighbouring Bhojpuri and Magahi, and the Thethi variety of the Saharsa region.

Grammar and features

Maithili is notable among Indo-Aryan languages for its highly elaborate verb agreement system, which can encode not only the subject but also the object and honorific status of participants. It has a rich system of honorifics in pronouns and verbs, extensive use of classifiers in some varieties, and postpositions marking case relations. Vocabulary draws on Sanskrit tatsama words along with Perso-Arabic and local sources.

Status and movements

Recognition movements in the twentieth century won Maithili a place in university curricula, Sahitya Akademi recognition (1965), and finally Eighth Schedule status in 2003. In Nepal, Maithili is protected as a national language and widely used in media in the Madhesh region. Despite its large speaker base, Maithili faces pressure from Hindi in education and administration, and advocates continue to press for its fuller use in schooling and government in Bihar.
