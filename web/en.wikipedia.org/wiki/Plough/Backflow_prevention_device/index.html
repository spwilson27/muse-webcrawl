Backflow prevention device

A reduced-pressure principle backflow preventer

A backflow prevention device is a plumbing fitting designed to prevent water from flowing backwards into a public drinking-water supply — a reversal called backflow — thereby protecting potable water from contamination. Backflow can occur through back-siphonage, when supply pressure falls, or back-pressure, when downstream pressure exceeds supply pressure, and it can draw fertilisers, chemicals or sewage into drinking water.

Types

Devices range from simple to sophisticated. An air gap — a vertical separation between a supply outlet and a flood-level rim — is the simplest and most reliable method. Mechanical devices include single and double check valves, hose-bibb vacuum breakers, pressure vacuum breakers, and the reduced-pressure principle assembly (RPZ), which uses two check valves with a monitored relief zone between them and offers the highest level of mechanical protection. Building and plumbing codes generally require higher-hazard connections, such as irrigation systems, boilers and commercial premises, to be fitted with testable assemblies.

Testing and regulation

Because check valves can foul or fail, mechanical backflow preventers must be tested periodically — commonly annually — by certified testers, and water utilities run cross-connection control programmes to identify and police unprotected connections.
