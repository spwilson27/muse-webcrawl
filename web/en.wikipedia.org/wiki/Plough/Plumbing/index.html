Plumbing

This article is filed under the path Plough/Plumbing in this mirror; it presents the standard article on plumbing — the systems of pipes, drains, fittings, and fixtures installed in buildings for the distribution of potable water and the removal of waterborne wastes.

Plumbing is any system that conveys fluids for a wide range of applications. Plumbing uses pipes, valves, plumbing fixtures, tanks, and other apparatuses to convey fluids. Heating and cooling (HVAC), waste removal, and potable water delivery are among the most common uses for plumbing, but it is not limited to these applications. The word derives from the Latin for lead, plumbum, as the first effective pipes used in the Roman era were lead pipes.

Modern plumbing combines copper supply lines with plastic drainage and vent piping.

History

Plumbing originated during ancient civilisations, as they developed public baths and needed to provide potable water and wastewater removal for larger numbers of people. The Indus Valley Civilisation had sophisticated drainage by around 2700 BCE; Mesopotamian clay sewer pipes date to about 4000 BCE. The Romans developed extensive systems of aqueducts, lead piping, and public latrines and baths. After the fall of Rome, water and sanitation infrastructure stagnated for centuries in much of Europe.

Modern plumbing developed in the nineteenth century, when cities began building separated sewer systems to remove waste rather than dumping it into streets and rivers, and when cast-iron and galvanised-steel supply pipes, flush toilets, and traps became standard in urban housing. The separation of sewage from drinking water, together with water treatment, produced dramatic reductions in cholera and typhoid.

Systems

Water supply

A building's supply system delivers pressurised potable water from a municipal main or a private well to fixtures such as taps, showers, water heaters, and appliances. Materials include copper, cross-linked polyethylene (PEX), chlorinated polyvinyl chloride (CPVC), and galvanised steel in older installations.

Drainage, waste, and vent (DWV)

The drainage system removes wastewater by gravity to a sewer or septic system. Traps retain a plug of water that blocks sewer gases; vent pipes, open to the roof, equalise pressure so traps are not siphoned dry. PVC and ABS plastics dominate modern residential drainage in North America, with cast iron still used where sound damping matters.

Fixtures and regulation

Plumbing fixtures — water closets, lavatories, bathtubs, showers, sinks, and bidets — are devices connected to the system that deliver or drain water. Because plumbing failures and cross-connections can contaminate drinking water and spread disease, the trade is heavily regulated: most jurisdictions license plumbers and enforce plumbing codes, such as the Uniform Plumbing Code and the International Plumbing Code in the United States, that specify pipe sizing, venting, backflow prevention, and fixture clearances.
