Pipe (fluid conveyance)

Steel pipes ready for installation

A pipe is a tubular section or hollow cylinder, usually but not necessarily of circular cross-section, used mainly to convey fluids — liquids and gases — or slurries and powders. Pipes are fundamental to plumbing, water supply, oil and gas transport, chemical processing and heating systems.

Materials and manufacture

Pipes are made from metals (carbon and stainless steel, copper, cast and ductile iron), plastics (PVC, CPVC, polyethylene, PEX), concrete, clay and occasionally glass or lead (now prohibited for drinking water in most countries). Steel pipe is produced either seamless, by piercing and rolling a solid billet, or welded from plate or strip. Pipe size is specified by nominal bore and wall-thickness schedules (in North America) or by nominal diameter DN (in Europe), with pressure ratings set by standards such as those of ASME, ASTM and ISO.

Fittings and joining

Sections are joined by welding, threading, flanging, soldering, solvent cementing or mechanical couplings, and connected to elbows, tees, reducers and valves that control and direct flow. In building services, pipework is distinguished from tubing, which is generally smaller, flexible and specified by exact outside diameter.
