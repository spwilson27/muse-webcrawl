Plumber

A plumber installing pipework

A plumber is a tradesperson who installs, maintains and repairs systems for drinking water, drainage, sewage, heating and gas in homes, commercial buildings and industrial facilities. The word derives from the Latin plumbum, meaning lead, reflecting the lead pipes and sheet lead long worked by the trade; the ancient Romans employed lead extensively in aqueducts and baths, and a Roman plumber was a plumbarius.

Work

Plumbers install and repair pipes, fittings, water heaters, boilers, sanitary fixtures such as baths, sinks and toilets, and appliances including dishwashers and washing machines. They read building plans, cut and join pipe in copper, steel and plastics, pressure-test installations, trace leaks and clear blockages. Much of the work is governed by plumbing codes intended to protect public health by keeping drinking water separate from waste — for example through traps, vents and backflow prevention devices in the drain-waste-vent system.

Training and licensing

In most jurisdictions plumbers qualify through apprenticeships combining workplace training with technical college study, progressing from apprentice to journeyman and, with experience and examination, master plumber. Licensing requirements vary by country and region; gas work in particular usually requires separate certification.
