Drain-waste-vent system

A typical DWV arrangement in a house

A drain-waste-vent system (DWV) is the part of a building's plumbing that removes sewage and wastewater and vents sewer gases safely to the outside. Unlike the pressurised supply system, a DWV system works by gravity, so its pipes are larger in diameter and must be laid at a consistent gentle slope to carry solids along with water.

Components

Wastewater leaves each fixture through a trap — a U-shaped bend that retains a plug of water sealing the room against sewer gas — and flows into branch drains, soil and waste stacks, and finally the building drain and sewer. The vent portion of the system, a network of pipes rising through the roof, admits air so that draining water does not siphon traps dry, equalises pressure, and allows gases to escape above the building. Individual fixtures may be wet-vented, sharing a pipe that serves both drainage and venting functions, and air admittance valves are permitted in some jurisdictions as a substitute for through-roof vents.

Design requirements

Plumbing codes specify pipe sizes by fixture-unit load, maximum trap-arm lengths and slopes (commonly a quarter inch per foot for small pipes), and distances from fixtures to vents. Materials include PVC, ABS and cast iron, chosen for corrosion resistance and noise.
