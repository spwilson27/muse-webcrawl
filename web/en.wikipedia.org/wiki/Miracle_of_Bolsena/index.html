Miracle of Bolsena

Raphael's fresco The Mass at Bolsena (1512), Vatican

The Miracle of Bolsena is a Eucharistic miracle said to have occurred in 1263 at Bolsena, Italy. According to tradition, a Bohemian priest named Peter of Prague, who doubted the doctrine of transubstantiation, was celebrating Mass at the church of Santa Cristina when the consecrated host began to bleed, staining the corporal (altar cloth) with blood.

Aftermath

The blood-stained corporal was carried in procession to Pope Urban IV at Orvieto, where it is still preserved in Orvieto Cathedral. The event is traditionally linked to the pope's institution of the Feast of Corpus Christi in 1264, with the office composed by Thomas Aquinas. The miracle was later depicted by Raphael in the fresco The Mass at Bolsena in the Vatican's Apostolic Palace.
