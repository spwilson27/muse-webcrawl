International development

International development or global development is a broad concept concerning the level of development on an international scale. It is the basis for international classifications such as developed country, developing country and least developed country. The field encompasses the policies, programmes and institutions—foreign aid, multilateral banks, United Nations agencies, NGOs and bilateral donors—that seek to improve economic, social and political conditions in lower-income countries.

Development projects range from infrastructure to health, education and water supply.

History

The modern development era is often dated to US President Harry Truman's 1949 inaugural address ("Point Four"), which framed assistance to "underdeveloped areas" as a Cold War and humanitarian priority. Decolonization, the Bretton Woods institutions (World Bank, IMF), and Marshall Plan precedents shaped the aid architecture of the 1950s–60s. Development economics emerged as a subdiscipline around modernization theory, dependency theory, import substitution, and later market-oriented "Washington Consensus" reforms and structural adjustment programmes of the 1980s–90s.

Goals and measurement

The UN Millennium Development Goals (2000–2015) set targets on poverty, education, health and the environment; they were succeeded by the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (2015–2030), which apply to all countries. Development is measured not only by GDP per capita but by indicators such as the Human Development Index, life expectancy, literacy, multidimensional poverty, and inequality.

Actors

  • Multilateral — World Bank Group, regional development banks, UNDP, UNICEF, WFP, IMF.
  • Bilateral — agencies such as USAID (historically), DFID/FCDO (UK), JICA (Japan), GIZ (Germany), AFD (France).
  • Non-governmental — Oxfam, CARE, BRAC, Médecins Sans Frontières and thousands of smaller organizations; also philanthropic foundations such as the Gates Foundation.
  • New donors and finance — China (Belt and Road Initiative), Gulf states, remittances, impact investing and blended finance.

Debates

Development practice is contested: economists such as William Easterly and Dambisa Moyo criticize aid effectiveness and aid dependency; Jeffrey Sachs and others defend large-scale aid; Amartya Sen reframed development as the expansion of human capabilities and freedoms. Paris Declaration (2005) and Busan principles emphasize aid effectiveness, country ownership and results.
