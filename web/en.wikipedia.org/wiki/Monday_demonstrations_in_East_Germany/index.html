Monday demonstrations in East Germany

Monday demonstrations
DateSeptember 1989 – March 1990 (protests continued in some cities)
LocationLeipzig and other cities, East Germany
CauseOpposition to the SED regime; demands for free elections, freedom of travel and the press
OutcomeFall of the Berlin Wall; path to German reunification

The Monday demonstrations (German: Montagsdemonstrationen) were a series of peaceful political protests against the government of the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) that took place every Monday evening in the autumn of 1989. Beginning in Leipzig after prayers for peace at the St. Nicholas Church, the demonstrations grew from a few thousand participants to several hundred thousand and spread across the country, becoming a decisive force in the Peaceful Revolution that brought down the communist regime.

Demonstrators in Leipzig in the autumn of 1989, marching under the slogan "Wir sind das Volk" — "We are the people."

Origins

Prayers for peace had been held at Leipzig's Nikolaikirche since 1982. In September 1989, as thousands of East Germans fled via Hungary and Czechoslovakia and pro-democracy groups formed, attendees of the Monday prayers began marching through the city centre. The first large demonstration on 4 September 1989 drew about a thousand people and was met with arrests.

Growth and the turning point of 9 October

Despite intimidation, the marches swelled weekly: roughly 20,000 marched on 2 October. For 9 October the regime massed armed police, soldiers, and Stasi units in Leipzig, and hospitals stocked blood supplies; violence like that used at Tiananmen Square earlier that year was widely feared. An appeal for non-violence issued by religious and cultural figures, including conductor Kurt Masur, was broadcast, and some 70,000 demonstrators marched peacefully. Security forces did not intervene. The evening is widely regarded as the point at which the regime lost the initiative.

Aftermath

Demonstrations grew still larger — over 300,000 in Leipzig on 23 October, and around half a million at East Berlin's Alexanderplatz on 4 November. Erich Honecker resigned on 18 October; on 9 November the Berlin Wall fell. The slogan shifted from "Wir sind das Volk" ("We are the people") to "Wir sind ein Volk" ("We are one people"), reflecting the turn toward reunification, which was achieved on 3 October 1990.

Legacy

The Monday demonstrations are commemorated as a landmark of non-violent protest. The term "Monday demonstration" has since been adopted by various protest movements in Germany.
