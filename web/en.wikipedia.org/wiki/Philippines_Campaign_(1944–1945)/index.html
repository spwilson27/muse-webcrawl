Philippines campaign (1944–1945)

Date20 October 1944 – 15 August 1945
LocationPhilippine Islands
ResultAllied victory; liberation of the Philippines

The Philippines campaign (1944–1945), the Liberation of the Philippines, codenamed Operation Musketeer I, II, and III, was the American, Mexican, Australian, and Filipino campaign to defeat and expel the Imperial Japanese forces occupying the Philippines during the closing stages of World War II. The Japanese Army had overrun all of the Philippines during the first half of 1942; the liberation began with amphibious landings on the eastern island of Leyte on 20 October 1944.

Background

General Douglas MacArthur, who had escaped the Philippines in 1942 with the promise "I shall return", championed the liberation against proposals to bypass the islands for Formosa. United States forces, under MacArthur's Southwest Pacific Area command and working with the Philippine resistance and the Commonwealth government of President Sergio Osmeña, prepared an invasion supported by the largest naval force yet assembled.

Course of the campaign

The Leyte landings provoked the Battle of Leyte Gulf (23–26 October 1944), generally considered the largest naval battle of the war, in which the Imperial Japanese Navy was effectively destroyed as a fighting force and the first organized kamikaze attacks were flown. Fierce fighting on Leyte, Mindoro, and then Luzon followed: U.S. forces landed in Lingayen Gulf on 9 January 1945, liberated Manila in February–March 1945 in a battle that devastated the city and killed around 100,000 Filipino civilians, and freed prisoners from camps including Santo Tomas and Los Baños. Operations continued across the central and southern islands, and in the mountains of northern Luzon, until Japan's surrender in August 1945.

Aftermath

The campaign cost heavy casualties on all sides: roughly 14,000 Americans killed, far larger Japanese losses — many to starvation and disease — and enormous Filipino civilian suffering. The Philippines regained its Commonwealth government, and full independence followed on 4 July 1946. General Tomoyuki Yamashita, the Japanese commander, was tried and executed for war crimes.
