Breeders' Cup Classic

Breeders' Cup Classic
Inaugurated: 1984
Distance: 1¼ miles (10 furlongs) on dirt
Grade: I
Purse: US$7 million (in recent years)
Qualification: 3-year-olds and up
Record winner: Tiznow, only two-time winner (2000, 2001)

The Breeders' Cup Classic is a thoroughbred horse race run annually as the feature event of the Breeders' Cup World Championships, held at a different North American track each autumn. It is a Grade I weight-for-age stakes contested over 1¼ miles on dirt for horses aged three and older, and it traditionally plays a decisive role in voting for American Horse of the Year.

History

The first running took place at Hollywood Park in 1984, when 31–1 outsider Wild Again won a dramatic three-way finish. Memorable renewals include the 1988 duel between Alysheba and Ferdinand, the 2009 victory of the mare Zenyatta — the first female winner — and the 2016 showdown in which Arrogate defeated California Chrome. In 2015, Triple Crown winner American Pharoah completed the first "Grand Slam" of American racing by adding the Classic to the Kentucky Derby, Preakness, and Belmont Stakes. Flightline's 2022 romp at Keeneland is among the most acclaimed performances in the race's history.

Qualification

Horses can earn automatic berths through the "Win and You're In" Breeders' Cup Challenge series, with remaining places filled by a selection panel based on form. The field is limited to 14 starters.
