Lexical semantics

Lexical semantics (also lexicosemantics) is a subfield of linguistic semantics concerned with the meanings of words and the relations that hold between them. It studies how and what the words of a language denote, how word meanings are structured in the mental lexicon, and how new meanings arise.

Sense relations

  • Synonymy — sameness or near-sameness of meaning (e.g. begin/commence).
  • Antonymy — oppositeness of meaning (hot/cold).
  • Hyponymy and hypernymy — inclusion relations (dog is a hyponym of animal).
  • Meronymy — part–whole relations (wheel is a meronym of car).
  • Polysemy and homonymy — one word having multiple related, or unrelated, meanings.

Theories

Approaches include componential analysis, which decomposes word meanings into semantic features; prototype theory; semantic field theory; and frame semantics, associated with Charles Fillmore. Computational lexical semantics works with resources such as WordNet, a large database grouping words into synonym sets linked by sense relations, widely used in natural language processing.
