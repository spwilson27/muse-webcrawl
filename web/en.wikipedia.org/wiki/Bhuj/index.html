Bhuj

Bhuj is a city and a municipality in Kutch district in the Indian state of Gujarat. It is the administrative headquarters of the Kutch district and is historically significant as the capital of the princely state of Kutch. The city lies in a desert-like region and is known for its palaces, temples, and handicrafts.

History

Bhuj was founded in 1510 by Rao Hamirji and was made the capital of Kutch in 1549 by Rao Khengarji I. The city grew around Bhujia Hill, on which stands the Bhujia Fort. Under the Jadeja rulers, Bhuj became a centre of trade and craft, famed for its silverwork, embroidery, and bandhani textiles.

On 26 January 2001, Bhuj was devastated by a powerful earthquake measuring 7.7 on the moment magnitude scale. Thousands of people were killed and much of the old city was destroyed. Bhuj has since been largely rebuilt, with new town planning and earthquake-resistant construction.

Places of interest

Notable sites include the Prag Mahal and Aina Mahal palaces, the Swaminarayan Temple, Hamirsar Lake, and the Kutch Museum, the oldest museum in Gujarat. Nearby is the hill fort of Bhujia, associated with the legend of the goddess Bhujia Mata.

Economy and culture

Bhuj is a hub for the handicrafts of the Kutch region, including Ajrakh block printing, Rogan painting, mirror-work embroidery, and leather craft. The city serves as a gateway for tourists visiting the Great Rann of Kutch and the Rann Utsav festival.
