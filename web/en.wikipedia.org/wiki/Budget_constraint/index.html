Budget constraint

In economics, a budget constraint represents all the combinations of goods and services that a consumer may purchase given current prices within their given income. Consumer theory uses the concepts of a budget constraint and a preference map as tools to examine the parameters of consumer choices. For two goods x and y with prices Px and Py and income m, the constraint is:

Px·x + Py·y ≤ m

The budget line

Graphically, the budget set's boundary is a straight line whose intercepts give the maximum amount of each good purchasable if all income is spent on it. The slope, the negative ratio of prices, is the opportunity cost of good x in terms of good y. An increase in income shifts the line outward in parallel; a change in one price rotates it about the other intercept.

Consumer optimum

A rational consumer maximises utility where the budget line is tangent to the highest attainable indifference curve — the point where the marginal rate of substitution equals the price ratio. Changes in prices generate the income and substitution effects decomposed by the Slutsky equation, and repeated price changes trace out the consumer's demand curve.

Extensions

The concept extends to intertemporal choice (saving and borrowing across periods), labour supply (the trade-off between consumption and leisure), rationing and quantity constraints, in-kind transfers, and kinked budget sets created by taxes and benefit programmes. Governments face an intertemporal budget constraint of their own, linking deficits, debt and future taxation.
