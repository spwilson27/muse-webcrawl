Bioterrorism
DefinitionDeliberate release of biological agents to cause illness or death
AgentsViruses, bacteria, fungi, toxins
Key treatyBiological Weapons Convention (1972)

Bioterrorism is terrorism involving the intentional release or dissemination of biological agents — including bacteria, viruses, fungi, or toxins — to cause illness, death, or fear in people, animals, or plants. It is a form of biological warfare carried out by non-state actors.

History

Notable incidents include the 1984 Rajneeshee attack in Oregon, in which Salmonella bacteria were used to contaminate salad bars, sickening 751 people; the Japanese cult Aum Shinrikyo's attempts to disperse anthrax and botulinum toxin in the early 1990s; and the 2001 anthrax letters in the United States, in which spores mailed to media outlets and Senate offices killed five people and infected seventeen others.

Preparedness

Public health authorities classify potential agents by risk; the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's Category A agents include anthrax, botulism, plague, smallpox, tularemia, and viral hemorrhagic fevers. Preparedness measures include disease surveillance, stockpiles of vaccines and antibiotics (such as the Strategic National Stockpile), and laboratory response networks. The Biological Weapons Convention of 1972 prohibits the development and stockpiling of biological weapons by signatory states.
