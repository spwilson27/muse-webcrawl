Dolby Digital

Dolby Digital is a family of audio compression technologies developed by Dolby Laboratories. Originally named Dolby Stereo Digital until 1995, it is known as ATSC A/52 since 2011 and commonly referred to as AC-3. Dolby Laboratories, founded by Ray Dolby in 1965, is an American company specialising in audio noise reduction, audio encoding and compression, and spatial audio technologies.

History

Dolby Digital made its theatrical debut with the film Batman Returns in 1992. It became the standard audio format for DVD-Video, HDTV broadcasts in North America, and many digital television systems worldwide.

Technology

The AC-3 codec uses perceptual coding based on the modified discrete cosine transform (MDCT) to compress multichannel audio into bitrates typically between 96 and 640 kbit/s, supporting up to six discrete channels (5.1).

Versions

Successors and extensions include Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC-3), Dolby TrueHD (lossless), and Dolby Atmos, which adds height channels and object-based mixing.
