Dolby Digital

Dolby Digital is a family of audio compression technologies developed by Dolby Laboratories. Originally named Dolby AC-3 after its third-generation audio coding algorithm, Dolby Digital provides multichannel surround sound for cinema, television, DVDs, Blu-ray discs, and streaming services.

Technology

AC-3 is a lossy perceptual audio codec supporting from mono up to 5.1 channels — left, centre, right, left surround, right surround, and a low-frequency effects (LFE) channel — at bit rates typically between 32 and 640 kbit/s. It uses a modified discrete cosine transform and psychoacoustic bit allocation to discard inaudible information. Dolby Digital debuted in cinemas with the film Batman Returns in 1992 and was adopted as the audio standard for DVD-Video, ATSC digital television in North America, and LaserDisc releases.

Variants

Later members of the family include Dolby Digital Plus (E-AC-3), which supports more channels and higher bit rates and is used by streaming services, sometimes carrying Dolby Atmos object-based audio; Dolby AC-4; and Dolby TrueHD, a lossless format used on Blu-ray discs.
