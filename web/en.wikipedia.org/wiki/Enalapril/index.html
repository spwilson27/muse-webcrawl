Enalapril

Enalapril, sold under the brand name Vasotec among others, is an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor medication used to treat high blood pressure, diabetic kidney disease, and heart failure. For heart failure, it is generally used with a diuretic, such as furosemide. It is given by mouth or by injection into a vein. Onset of effects are typically within an hour when taken by mouth and last for up to a day.

Medical uses

Enalapril is used to treat hypertension, symptomatic heart failure, and asymptomatic left ventricular dysfunction. ACE inhibitors such as enalapril have been shown to reduce mortality in heart failure and to slow the progression of diabetic nephropathy.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, dizziness, feeling tired, and cough. Serious side effects include angioedema, low blood pressure, and kidney problems. Use during pregnancy is not recommended, as ACE inhibitors can harm the fetus, particularly in the second and third trimesters.

Pharmacology

Enalapril is a prodrug: it is hydrolysed in the liver to its active metabolite, enalaprilat. Enalaprilat inhibits the angiotensin-converting enzyme, preventing the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II, a potent vasoconstrictor. The result is vasodilation and reduced secretion of aldosterone.

History

Enalapril was patented in 1978 and came into medical use in 1984. It was developed by researchers at Merck, building on the earlier ACE inhibitor captopril. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is available as a generic medication.
