Product portfolio

Portfolio analysis tools such as the BCG matrix classify products by market share and growth.

A product portfolio is the collection of all products and services offered by a company. Portfolio management in this sense involves analysing the performance of the products in the portfolio and deciding how to allocate resources among them — which to invest in, which to maintain, which to harvest and which to discontinue — in order to meet the company's overall objectives.

Portfolio analysis

The best-known tool is the growth-share matrix, developed by the Boston Consulting Group in the 1970s, which classifies business units or products by relative market share and market growth rate into four categories:

  • Stars — high share in a high-growth market; require investment but generate substantial revenue.
  • Cash cows — high share in a low-growth market; generate cash that can fund other products.
  • Question marks (problem children) — low share in a high-growth market; require a decision to invest heavily or exit.
  • Dogs — low share in a low-growth market; candidates for divestment or repositioning.

Multi-factor alternatives, such as the GE–McKinsey matrix, rate industry attractiveness and competitive strength using weighted sets of criteria instead of single measures.

Balancing a portfolio

A balanced portfolio typically combines products at different stages of the product life cycle, spreading risk so that cash generated by mature products funds the development and growth of newer ones. Portfolio decisions also consider cannibalisation between a firm's own products, synergies in production and distribution, and the strategic fit of each product line with the company's brand and capabilities.
