Ardennes (horse)

An Ardennais draught stallion

The Ardennes or Ardennais is one of the oldest breeds of draught horse, originating in the Ardennes area of Belgium, Luxembourg and France. It is a heavy horse, with a compact, muscular body, thick legs and a calm, docile temperament. Ardennes horses have been used for centuries as war horses, agricultural work horses and, more recently, in forestry and meat production.

History

The Ardennes breed is claimed to descend from horses bred in the region in Roman times; Julius Caesar praised the hardy, rustic horses of the area in his account of the Gallic Wars. The breed was influenced over the centuries by heavier Belgian draught blood and, in some strains, by lighter Oriental crosses introduced in the nineteenth century. During the First World War the region's horse population was devastated, and breeding was rebuilt afterwards around the heavy draught type.

Three related but distinct stud books are maintained: the Belgian Ardennes, the French Trait Ardennais, and the Luxembourg Ardennais. The North American Ardennes Horse Society registers the breed in the United States.

Characteristics

Ardennes horses are among the heaviest of draught breeds. They typically stand between about 15.2 and 16.2 hands (roughly 157–168 cm) at the withers and weigh 700–1,000 kg or more. The head is somewhat heavy with a straight or slightly convex profile; the neck is short and thick; the body is short, broad and deep, with powerful hindquarters. Legs are short and strong with abundant feathering at the fetlocks. Common colours are bay, roan, chestnut, grey and palomino, with black also permitted in some registries.

The breed is noted for its exceptionally quiet temperament, hardiness, and ability to work in hilly terrain and poor forage conditions where other draught breeds struggle.

Uses

Modern Ardennes horses are used for agricultural and forestry work, particularly for logging in areas where mechanised equipment is impractical or environmentally damaging. In Belgium and France the breed is also raised for horse meat. Purebred and crossbred Ardennes are shown at draught horse exhibitions and used for promotional and ceremonial work.
