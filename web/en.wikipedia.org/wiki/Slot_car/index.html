Slot car

A slot car or slotcar is a powered miniature automobile or other vehicle that is guided by a groove or slot in the track on which it runs. A pin or blade extends from the bottom of the car into the slot. Though some slot cars are used to model highway traffic, the great majority are used in the competitive hobby of slot car racing or slot racing.

How it works

The car's guide flag sits in the track's slot and picks up electrical power from metal rails (or braid) flanking the slot. A hand-held controller — essentially a variable resistor or electronic throttle — varies the voltage to the lane, and so the car's speed. The skill of racing is to drive as fast as possible without the car "de-slotting" (leaving the slot) in corners.

Scales and history

Common scales are 1:24, 1:32 and HO (roughly 1:64–1:87). Commercial slot car systems appeared in the 1950s; the British company Scalextric, launched in 1957, became synonymous with the home hobby. Slot racing boomed in the United States in the 1960s, with large commercial raceways, before declining in the 1970s; it persists as a club and home hobby, and modern digital systems allow several cars to share a lane and change lanes at special track pieces.

Manufacturers

Notable makers include Scalextric, Carrera, Ninco, Fly and Slot.it, producing detailed replicas of racing and road cars.
