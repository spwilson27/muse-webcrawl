Calcium signaling

Calcium signalling is the use of calcium ions (Ca²⁺) as an intracellular second messenger to control a vast range of cellular processes, including muscle contraction, neurotransmitter release, hormone secretion, fertilisation, gene expression, cell division, and apoptosis. Few signalling molecules are as universal or as versatile.

The calcium gradient

Cells maintain resting cytosolic Ca²⁺ at about 100 nanomolar, some ten thousand times lower than outside the cell. This steep gradient is maintained by pumps and exchangers — the SERCA pump that fills the endoplasmic reticulum, the plasma membrane Ca²⁺ ATPase (PMCA), and the sodium–calcium exchanger — and it means that opening a channel produces a large, rapid, and energetically cheap signal.

Release and entry

Signals arise when Ca²⁺ enters the cytosol through channels in the plasma membrane (e.g. voltage-gated calcium channels in excitable cells, or store-operated channels gated by STIM and Orai proteins) or is released from internal stores via two receptor-channels of the endoplasmic reticulum: the IP3 receptor, opened by inositol trisphosphate produced after GPCR or receptor-tyrosine-kinase activation, and the ryanodine receptor, central to excitation–contraction coupling in muscle. Calcium itself stimulates further release ("calcium-induced calcium release"), generating waves and oscillations whose frequency and amplitude encode information.

Decoders

Cytosolic Ca²⁺ is interpreted by calcium-binding proteins. Calmodulin, the archetype, changes shape on binding Ca²⁺ and activates targets including CaMK kinases and the phosphatase calcineurin. Troponin C triggers contraction in striated muscle; synaptotagmin senses the Ca²⁺ microdomain at nerve terminals to fuse synaptic vesicles. Mitochondria take up Ca²⁺ to couple workload to ATP production, though overload contributes to cell death.

Termination and pathology

Signals end when pumps restore low cytosolic Ca²⁺ and channels close. Defects in calcium signalling underlie diseases from malignant hyperthermia (ryanodine receptor mutations) and Timothy syndrome (Cav1.2) to immune deficiency (Orai1), and deranged calcium handling contributes to heart failure and neurodegeneration.
