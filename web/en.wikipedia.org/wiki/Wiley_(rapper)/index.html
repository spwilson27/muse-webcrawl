Richard Kylea Cowie Jr. (born 19 January 1979), better known by his stage name Wiley, is a British grime MC, rapper, and record producer from Bow, East London. He is widely regarded as a pioneer — often called the "Godfather of Grime" — of the grime genre, having developed its signature cold, angular electronic sound through his "eskibeat" productions in the early 2000s.

Career

Wiley emerged from the UK garage scene and the pirate radio circuit, performing with the Pay As U Go Cartel before founding the Roll Deep crew. His early tracks, built around icy synth stabs and sparse, rapid-fire beats, defined grime's sound; his 2004 debut album Treddin' on Thin Ice is considered a genre landmark. He mentored a generation of MCs, including Dizzee Rascal. Commercial success followed with singles such as "Wearing My Rolex" (2008) and the UK number one "Heatwave" (2012). His 2017 album Godfather debuted at number nine on the UK Albums Chart and was acclaimed as a return to form.

Recognition and controversies

Wiley was appointed MBE in the 2018 New Year Honours for services to music. In 2020 he was dropped by his management and record labels and banned from major social media platforms after posting antisemitic comments; the MBE was later forfeited. He had earlier been involved in a long-running public feud with rival MCs, characteristic of grime's clash culture, and his 2021 activities included an arrest warrant after failing to appear in court on assault and burglary charges.
