A global navigation satellite system (GNSS) is a satellite constellation that provides positioning, navigation and timing (PNT) services on a global or regional basis. A GNSS receiver determines its position by measuring the time taken for signals to arrive from at least four satellites, each of which broadcasts its precise location and the time according to onboard atomic clocks.

Systems

Four systems offer global coverage: the United States' GPS (Global Positioning System), the first and most widely used, operational since the 1990s; Russia's GLONASS; the European Union's Galileo, designed for civilian control; and China's BeiDou, completed in 2020. Regional systems include Japan's QZSS and India's NavIC. Augmentation systems such as WAAS and EGNOS improve accuracy and integrity for aviation.

Applications

GNSS underpins transport navigation, surveying, precision agriculture, telecommunications network timing, financial timestamps and emergency services. Modern smartphones typically receive signals from several constellations at once, improving accuracy in cities and under tree cover. Vulnerabilities to jamming and spoofing are an increasing concern for critical infrastructure.
