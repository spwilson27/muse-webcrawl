Kennedy Center Honors

The Kennedy Center Honors are annual honours given to individuals in the performing arts for their lifetime contributions to American culture. They have been presented by the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts in Washington, D.C. since 1978.

Selection and ceremony

Honorees are chosen for contributions across dance, music, theatre, opera, film and television. The December ceremony, traditionally attended by the President of the United States, features tribute performances and is broadcast on television. Recipients wear a distinctive rainbow-ribboned medallion.

History

The Honors were created by George Stevens Jr., who produced the gala for decades. Early honorees included Marian Anderson, Fred Astaire, Richard Rodgers and Arthur Rubinstein. Five or six artists are typically honoured each year.
