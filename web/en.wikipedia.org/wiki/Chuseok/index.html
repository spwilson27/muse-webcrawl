Chuseok

Chuseok (Korean: 추석), also known as Hangawi, is a major mid-autumn harvest festival and a three-day holiday in South and North Korea, celebrated on the 15th day of the 8th month of the lunar calendar, on the full moon.

Chuseok
Official name: Chuseok (추석)
Observed by: Koreans
Type: Cultural, harvest
Date: 15th day of 8th lunar month
Related to: Mid-Autumn Festival, Tsukimi

Origins

Chuseok is traced to agrarian traditions giving thanks for a good harvest and honouring ancestors. One folk account links it to a weaving contest in the Silla kingdom.

Customs

Families travel to hometowns, perform ancestral rites called charye, visit and clean ancestral graves (seongmyo and beolcho), and share food. Traditional foods include songpyeon — half-moon rice cakes steamed over pine needles — japchae, galbi and seasonal fruit. Games and dances include ganggangsullae, tug-of-war and ssireum wrestling. Many wear hanbok.

Modern celebration

The holiday causes one of Korea's largest annual migrations. Gift sets of food and household goods are exchanged, and businesses close for the holiday period. It is comparable to the Chinese Mid-Autumn Festival.
