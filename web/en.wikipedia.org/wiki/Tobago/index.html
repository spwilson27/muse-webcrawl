Tobago is an island and ward within the Republic of Trinidad and Tobago. It is located 35 kilometres (22 mi) northeast of the larger island of Trinidad and about 160 kilometres (99 mi) off the northeastern coast of Venezuela. The island's capital is Scarborough. Tobago is considerably smaller and less populous than Trinidad, and its economy is based largely on tourism, fishing, and government services.

Geography

Tobago is roughly 42 kilometres (26 mi) long and 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) wide, with an area of about 300 square kilometres (120 sq mi). A mountainous spine, the Main Ridge, runs along the northeastern two-thirds of the island and is covered by rainforest; the Main Ridge Forest Reserve, established in 1776, is among the oldest protected rainforests in the Western Hemisphere. The southwestern part of the island is flatter, formed of coralline limestone. Tobago is surrounded by coral reefs, most famously Buccoo Reef, and by the calm waters of the Caribbean Sea to the northwest and the Atlantic Ocean to the southeast.

History

Before European contact, Tobago was inhabited by Indigenous peoples, including the Kalina (Caribs). Sighted by Christopher Columbus in 1498, the island was contested for centuries by European powers. The Dutch, French, English, Spanish, and even settlers from the Duchy of Courland (in modern-day Latvia) established colonies or forts there, and Tobago changed hands more than thirty times — more than almost any other Caribbean island. Sugar, cotton, and indigo plantations worked by enslaved Africans dominated the colonial economy.

The island was definitively ceded to Britain by France under the Treaty of Paris in 1814 (following an earlier cession in 1763). In 1889 Tobago was politically joined with Trinidad as a single British colony. Trinidad and Tobago became independent in 1962, and Tobago gained a measure of internal self-government with the creation of the Tobago House of Assembly in 1980.

Economy and tourism

Unlike Trinidad, whose economy rests on oil and natural gas, Tobago's economy depends on tourism, drawn by its beaches, diving, and ecotourism, including birdwatching in the forest reserve and the leatherback turtle nesting beaches. Villages such as Castara, Charlotteville, and Speyside are popular with visitors, and Crown Point, near the Arthur Napoleon Robinson International Airport, is the centre of the tourist trade.

Culture

Tobago's population is predominantly of African descent, and its culture is known for the Tobago Heritage Festival, traditional foods such as crab and dumpling, and musical forms including calypso and steelpan. The island is often said, though disputed, to have inspired Daniel Defoe's Robinson Crusoe.
