Moundville Archaeological Site

Location: Hale County, Alabama, United States
Area: 185 acres (75 ha)
Culture: Mississippian culture
Occupied: c. AD 1000–1450
Designation: National Historic Landmark

The Moundville Archaeological Site, also known as the Moundville Archaeological Park, is a Mississippian culture archaeological site on the Black Warrior River in Hale County, near the modern city of Tuscaloosa, Alabama. At its height in the 13th century AD, Moundville was one of the largest pre-Columbian settlements north of Mexico — a walled ceremonial and political centre of about 185 acres containing 29 platform mounds arranged around a central plaza. It is regarded, along with Cahokia, as one of the two most important sites of the Mississippian world, and is famous for the elaborate engraved stone, copper, and ceramic art recovered from its elite burials.

History of the settlement

The site was first settled around AD 1000 by Mississippian farmers growing maize on the river floodplain. Between about 1150 and 1250 it was transformed into a fortified paramount chiefdom capital: a wooden palisade with bastions enclosed the mound-and-plaza core, and perhaps 1,000 people lived within the walls, with several thousand more in surrounding farmsteads. After about 1300, Moundville declined as a residence centre but continued as a ceremonial and mortuary site for regional elites until roughly 1450, when it was largely abandoned.

Archaeology

The site was excavated extensively in the 1930s by the Alabama Museum of Natural History under David DeJarnette, with Civilian Conservation Corps labour, and earlier by Clarence Bloomfield Moore (1905–06), who recovered richly furnished burials. Some 3,000 burials have been documented. Status-linked grave goods — copper axes and headdresses, stone discs, engraved gorgets, and effigy pottery — illuminate the Southeastern Ceremonial Complex iconography of the Mississippian world. Analysis of the burials has been central to anthropological models of ranked, chiefdom-level society.

The park today

The University of Alabama manages the site as Moundville Archaeological Park, an outdoor museum with the Jones Archaeological Museum (opened 1939, renovated 2010), a reconstructed village, nature trails, and the annual Moundville Native American Festival. It was declared a National Historic Landmark in 1964 and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
