The Ford Model T is an automobile produced by the Ford Motor Company from 1 October 1908 to 26 May 1927. It is generally regarded as the first mass-produced affordable automobile, bringing motor car ownership within reach of ordinary middle-class families and putting America — and much of the world — on wheels.

Design and production

Designed by Henry Ford's team including Childe Harold Wills and Hungarian engineers Joseph Galamb and Eugene Farkas, the Model T had a front-mounted 2.9-litre four-cylinder engine producing 20 horsepower, a two-speed planetary transmission, and a simple, rugged chassis suited to poor roads. Ford's introduction of the moving assembly line at Highland Park in 1913–14 cut chassis assembly time from over 12 hours to about 93 minutes, allowing the price to fall from $850 in 1908 to under $300 by the 1920s. Henry Ford famously remarked that customers could have "any color so long as it is black", since black paint dried fastest.

Impact

By 1921 the Model T accounted for roughly half the cars in the world; more than 15 million were built, a production record for a single model that stood until the Volkswagen Beetle surpassed it in 1972. The car's success transformed manufacturing, labour (Ford's $5 day of 1914), and society. It was succeeded by the Ford Model A in 1927.
