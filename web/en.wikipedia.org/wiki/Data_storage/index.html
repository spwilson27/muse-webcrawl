Data storage

Storage media have evolved from punched cards and magnetic tape to flash memory.

Data storage is the recording (storing) of information (data) in a storage medium. Handwriting, phonographic recording, magnetic tape, and optical discs are all examples of storage media; biological molecules such as RNA and DNA are considered by some as data storage. Recording is accomplished by virtually any form of energy. Electronic data storage requires electrical power to store and retrieve data.

Hierarchy of storage

Computer storage is conventionally divided into a hierarchy by speed and cost. Primary storage (memory) — registers, cache and RAM — is fastest, volatile and directly accessible to the CPU. Secondary storage, such as hard disk drives and solid-state drives, is non-volatile and much cheaper per bit but slower. Tertiary and offline storage, such as magnetic tape libraries and optical media, hold archival data that is accessed infrequently.

Media and technologies

Historically, data has been stored on punched cards and paper tape, magnetic drums, tapes and disks, optical discs (CD, DVD, Blu-ray), and semiconductor flash memory. The capacity of storage devices has grown exponentially for decades, while cost per bit has fallen, enabling modern data centres storing exabytes of information and distributed systems such as cloud storage, which present pooled, redundant storage as a service over networks.

Characteristics

Storage technologies are compared by capacity, access time, throughput, durability, volatility, and cost per unit of information. Many systems combine media — for example, tiering "hot" data on flash and "cold" data on tape — to balance performance against cost.
