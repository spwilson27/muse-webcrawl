Ankylosis

Ankylosis (from Greek ἀγκύλωσις, "crookedness") is the stiffness or fixation of a joint, caused by abnormal adhesion and rigidity of the bones forming the joint. It may result from injury, infection, or inflammatory disease, and may be complete—with bony fusion across the joint (bony ankylosis)—or partial, where fibrous tissue restricts movement (fibrous ankylosis). The term also describes the surgical fusion of a joint (arthrodesis) and, in dentistry, the fusion of a tooth root to the surrounding alveolar bone.

Causes

Inflammatory arthritides are a major cause: ankylosing spondylitis characteristically produces progressive fusion of the sacroiliac joints and spine, giving the "bamboo spine" appearance, and advanced rheumatoid arthritis can ankylose small joints. Other causes include septic arthritis, tuberculosis of joints, trauma with prolonged immobilisation, burns, and heterotopic ossification. Ankylosis of the temporomandibular joint, often following childhood trauma or infection, can severely limit mouth opening.

Treatment

Management depends on the joint and the degree of disability. Physiotherapy may preserve remaining movement in fibrous ankylosis; established bony fusion may be treated by osteotomy to reposition the limb, or by joint replacement (arthroplasty) in suitable joints such as the hip and knee. Deliberate surgical ankylosis (arthrodesis) remains an option for painful, unstable joints where replacement is unsuitable, for example in the ankle, wrist, and spine.
