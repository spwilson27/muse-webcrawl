Lambda

Lambda (uppercase Λ, lowercase λ) is the eleventh letter of the Greek alphabet, representing the voiced alveolar lateral approximant /l/. In the Greek numeral system it has a value of 30.

The letter is widely used as a symbol in science and mathematics: in physics for wavelength, in mathematics and computer science for eigenvalues and for the lambda calculus developed by Alonzo Church, in statistics and logic, and in cosmology for the cosmological constant (capital Λ). The lowercase λ is also the symbol of the radioactive decay constant and of thermal conductivity in some conventions.

Lambda was adopted into the Latin alphabet as the letter L. It has also served as a symbol for various movements and organisations.
