John Forbes Nash Jr.

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Chalkboard portrait style illustration of a mathematician amid game theory payoff matrices]

John Forbes Nash Jr. (1928–2015) was an American mathematician whose work in game theory, differential geometry and partial differential equations reshaped several fields. At 21, his Princeton dissertation introduced the Nash equilibrium, the foundational solution concept of non-cooperative game theory.

Nash shared the 1994 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for this work. His career was interrupted for decades by schizophrenia, from which he partially recovered; his life was portrayed in Sylvia Nasar's biography A Beautiful Mind (1998) and its 2001 film adaptation. He and his wife Alicia died in a car crash in 2015, days after he received the Abel Prize (with Louis Nirenberg) for work on nonlinear partial differential equations.

Mathematical breadth

Beyond game theory, Nash proved deep embedding theorems showing any Riemannian manifold can be realised in Euclidean space, and made foundational contributions to the regularity theory of elliptic and parabolic equations — results analysts rank alongside or above his famous equilibrium.

The Nash equilibrium's ubiquity — economics, evolutionary biology, computer science — made him that rare mathematician whose name is an everyday term in other disciplines.
