The TOP500 project ranks and details the 500 most powerful non-distributed computer systems in the world. Started in 1993 by Hans Meuer, Jack Dongarra, Erich Strohmaier and Horst Simon, the list is updated twice a year, in June (at the ISC High Performance conference) and November (at the Supercomputing conference SC).

Methodology

Systems are ranked by their performance on the LINPACK benchmark—specifically the Rmax value, the maximal sustained floating-point performance in double precision on a dense linear-system problem—expressed in FLOPS. Machines are submitted voluntarily, so the list reflects reported systems rather than a complete census (some large systems, including classified and certain Chinese machines after the mid-2010s, have not been submitted).

History

The list has tracked the progression from gigaflops vector machines to teraflops clusters, petaflops systems (IBM Roadrunner, 2008) and exascale machines beginning with Frontier (2022), followed by Aurora and El Capitan at U.S. national laboratories. Companion rankings include the Green500 (energy efficiency), HPCG (real-world application performance) and Graph500 (data-intensive computing). Trends in the list document the shift toward heterogeneous CPU+GPU architectures and the geographic balance of high-performance computing, mainly among the United States, China, Japan and Europe.
