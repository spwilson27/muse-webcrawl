Chipotle Mexican Grill, Inc. (often simply Chipotle) is an American chain of fast-casual restaurants specialising in burritos, tacos, burrito bowls, quesadillas and salads made to order in front of the customer. Founded by chef Steve Ells in Denver, Colorado, in 1993, the company pioneered the fast-casual segment and grew to more than 3,400 locations, mostly in the United States, with additional restaurants in Canada, the United Kingdom, France and Germany.

History

Ells opened the first Chipotle near the University of Denver using an $85,000 loan from his father, calculating that the store needed to sell 107 burritos a day to break even; it soon sold over 1,000. McDonald's became a major investor in 1998 and helped fund rapid expansion, before divesting in 2006 when Chipotle went public in one of the most successful restaurant IPOs to that date. The company has promoted a "Food With Integrity" philosophy centred on responsibly sourced ingredients.

Food safety crises

In late 2015 and 2016, outbreaks of E. coli and norovirus linked to Chipotle restaurants sickened hundreds of customers across several states, sharply cutting sales and the share price. Chipotle overhauled its food-safety procedures and in 2020 agreed to pay a $25 million criminal fine — the largest ever in a US food-safety case — to resolve charges related to the outbreaks.
