Curtiss Aeroplane and Motor Company


The Curtiss JN-4 "Jenny", the standard American trainer of WWI

The Curtiss Aeroplane and Motor Company (1907–1929) was an American aircraft manufacturer founded by aviation pioneer Glenn Hammond Curtiss. Headquartered in Buffalo, New York, with an important plant in Hammondsport, it became the largest aircraft manufacturer in the world during World War I and a pioneer of naval aviation. In 1929 it merged with Wright Aeronautical to form the Curtiss-Wright Corporation.

History

Curtiss rose to fame winning the 1909 Gordon Bennett speed race at Reims and making the first long-distance flights in America. His company built the first practical seaplanes and flying boats — Curtiss is often called the "father of naval aviation" for his work with the U.S. Navy from 1911, including the NC-4, the first aircraft to cross the Atlantic (1919). During World War I, Curtiss produced thousands of aircraft, above all the JN-4 "Jenny" trainer, of which more than 6,000 were built, and the H-12/H-16 flying boats. After the war, surplus Jennys seeded American barnstorming and airmail.

Later products

In the 1920s Curtiss built successful racing aircraft, the R-series Navy racers, and led early pursuit and observation types for the Army Air Corps, along with engines such as the D-12. Although the company itself disappeared into Curtiss-Wright in 1929, the Curtiss name continued on landmark Second World War designs produced by the successor firm, notably the P-40 Warhawk fighter, the C-46 Commando transport, and the SB2C Helldiver dive bomber.
