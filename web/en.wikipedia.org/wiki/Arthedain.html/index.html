Arthedain

Eriador, in whose northwest lay the realm of Arthedain

Arthedain is a fictional realm in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, one of the three successor kingdoms of Arnor in Middle-earth. It preserved the senior line of the kings of Arnor after the realm's division in the Third Age.

History

When Arnor split in T.A. 861, Amlaith, eldest son of the last king Eärendur, took Arthedain, the northwestern portion, with its capital at Fornost. Unlike its sister realms Cardolan and Rhudaur, Arthedain kept the true line of Isildur and the heirlooms of the North-kingdom, including the palantíri of Amon Sûl and Annúminas.

Fall

Arthedain fought a long losing war against the Witch-king's realm of Angmar. In T.A. 1974 its last king, Arvedui, was driven out and drowned in the Icebay of Forochel; Gondor's forces arrived too late to save the kingdom, though Angmar itself was destroyed the following year. Arvedui's son Aranarth became the first Chieftain of the Dúnedain, whose line led eventually to Aragorn.
