Constellation diagram

[Hero image placeholder: 16-QAM constellation diagram with points on a grid]

A constellation diagram is a representation of a signal modulated by a digital modulation scheme such as quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) or phase-shift keying (PSK). It displays the signal as a two-dimensional xy-plane scatter diagram in the complex plane at symbol sampling instants. In a manner similar to that of an eye pattern, the diagram is used to visualize how the signal is corrupted by channel effects such as noise, distortion and interference.

Interpretation

The horizontal axis represents the in-phase (I) component and the vertical axis the quadrature (Q) component of the signal. Each point in the diagram corresponds to one symbol the modulator can transmit. For a clean signal, all measured points fall exactly on the ideal symbol locations. Distortion reveals itself as spreading clouds around the ideal points: random noise spreads points in all directions, gain imbalance squeezes the constellation along one axis, phase noise rotates it, and non-linear distortion bends it.

The distance between ideal points determines the scheme's tolerance to noise: higher-order modulations such as 64-QAM pack more points into the same amplitude range and are consequently more error-prone, requiring a higher signal-to-noise ratio.

Uses

Constellation diagrams are a standard diagnostic tool in digital communications, cable television, digital radio (DAB), Wi-Fi and cellular systems based on OFDM modulation. Error vector magnitude (EVM) is computed from the displacement of measured points from their ideal positions.
