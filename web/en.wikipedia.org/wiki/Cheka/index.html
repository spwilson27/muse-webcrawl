The Cheka (Russian: ЧК) was the first Soviet secret police organisation, established by decree of Vladimir Lenin on 20 December 1917 under the leadership of Felix Dzerzhinsky. Its official name was the All-Russian Extraordinary Commission for Combating Counter-Revolution and Sabotage.

Activities

During the Russian Civil War, the Cheka carried out the Red Terror: mass arrests, hostage-taking, and executions of alleged counter-revolutionaries, class enemies, and deserters, with death toll estimates varying widely by source. It operated through provincial commissions, its own tribunals, troops, and concentration camps, and its methods — extrajudicial punishment and collective responsibility — became a template for later Soviet security organs.

Legacy

In 1922 the Cheka was reorganised as the GPU, forerunner of the OGPU, NKVD, and eventually the KGB. Soviet and Russian security officers long called themselves "Chekists", and Dzerzhinsky's statue outside the Lubyanka became an emblem of the institution. 20 December is still marked as Security Organs Day in Russia.
