Lizard Lighthouse

LocationLizard Point, Cornwall, England
First lit1752 (current towers)
OperatorTrinity House
Automated1998

The Lizard Lighthouse is a lighthouse at Lizard Point in Cornwall, England, marking the most southerly point of mainland Great Britain and guiding ships entering the western approaches to the English Channel. It is operated by Trinity House.

History

A private light was first established on the Lizard in 1619 by Sir John Killigrew, but it was extinguished after a few years because of difficulties collecting dues. Trinity House took over the station and in 1752 built the present arrangement of two towers joined by cottages, with coal-fired lights. The station was later modernised with oil and then electric lamps, and only the eastern tower remains in use.

Characteristics

The active white tower exhibits a flashing light visible for many miles. The Lizard's powerful fog signal is well known locally. The lighthouse was automated in 1998, and the former keepers' cottages are now used as holiday accommodation and a heritage centre.

Significance

The waters around the Lizard are notoriously hazardous, with many recorded wrecks, and the light has been a key landfall mark for shipping entering the Channel from the Atlantic.
