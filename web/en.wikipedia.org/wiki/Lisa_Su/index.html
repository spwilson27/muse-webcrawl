Lisa Tzwu-Fang Su (born 7 November 1969) is a Taiwanese-born American electrical engineer and business executive who has served as chief executive officer of Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) since October 2014, and as its chair since 2022. She is widely credited with engineering one of the most notable turnarounds in semiconductor history, taking AMD from the brink of irrelevance to a central position in high-performance computing and artificial-intelligence hardware.

Early life and career

Su was born in Tainan, Taiwan, and immigrated to the United States at about age three. She studied electrical engineering at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, earning her bachelor's, master's, and doctorate there; her PhD research concerned silicon-on-insulator technology. She worked at Texas Instruments, spent thirteen years at IBM — where she led the team that used copper interconnects in place of aluminium in chip manufacture — and later held executive roles at Freescale Semiconductor before joining AMD in 2012.

At AMD

As CEO, Su refocused AMD on high-performance computing, gaming, and data centres. The Zen microarchitecture, launched in 2017 in Ryzen processors and Epyc server chips, restored AMD's competitiveness against Intel; follow-on products, including chiplet designs and the Instinct line of AI accelerators, expanded its share of the data-centre market. AMD acquired Xilinx in 2022 in a deal valued at about $49 billion. Su has topped lists of the highest-paid and most influential technology executives, and in 2024 she was named Time's CEO of the year. She is a distant relative — a first cousin once removed — of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang.
