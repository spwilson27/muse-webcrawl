La Scala (Italian: Teatro alla Scala) is a historic opera house in Milan, Italy, and one of the most famous and prestigious opera houses in the world. It was inaugurated on 3 August 1778 with Antonio Salieri's L'Europa riconosciuta, and stands on the site of the church of Santa Maria alla Scala, from which it takes its name.

History

The theatre replaced the fire-destroyed Teatro Regio Ducale under Austrian Habsburg rule, financed by the city's box-holders. La Scala premiered many of the great operas: works by Rossini, Donizetti, Bellini (Norma, 1831) and above all Verdi, whose Nabucco (1842) made his name and whose Otello (1887) and Falstaff (1893) crowned his career. The house also premiered Puccini's Madama Butterfly (1904, in its unsuccessful first version) and Turandot (1926). Under music director Arturo Toscanini in the 1920s the orchestra and chorus rose to world standing; later principal conductors included Claudio Abbado, Riccardo Muti and Daniel Barenboim.

The building

Piermarini's neoclassical auditorium, with its tiers of red-and-gold boxes, was repeatedly modernised; the stage is among the largest in Italy. A major renovation by Mario Botta (2002–2004) added a new backstage tower and restored the historic shell. The traditional season opens on 7 December, the feast of Saint Ambrose, patron of Milan. The La Scala Theatre Museum (Museo Teatrale alla Scala) adjoins the house.
