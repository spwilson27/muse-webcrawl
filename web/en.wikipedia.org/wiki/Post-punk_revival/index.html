Post-punk revival

Also called: Garage rock revival, new wave revival
Era: late 1990s–2000s
Key artists: The Strokes, Interpol, Franz Ferdinand, Arctic Monkeys, Yeah Yeah Yeahs

The post-punk revival, also known as the garage rock revival or new wave revival, is a movement in alternative and indie rock of the late 1990s and 2000s in which bands drew on the sound and style of 1970s–80s post-punk and new wave acts such as Joy Division, Gang of Four, Wire and Television, combined with garage rock energy.

History

The movement broke through commercially in 2001 with the New York band The Strokes, whose debut album Is This It was a critical and popular landmark, and Interpol's Turn On the Bright Lights (2002). British bands including Franz Ferdinand, whose 2004 debut won the Mercury Prize, Bloc Party and Editors followed, as did the Arctic Monkeys, whose 2006 debut became the fastest-selling debut album in UK chart history. Other notable acts include Yeah Yeah Yeahs, LCD Soundsystem, The Rapture, The Killers and The White Stripes (more often classed with garage rock revival).

Influence

The revival restored guitar-based indie rock to commercial prominence after the Britpop and nu-metal eras and reshaped 2000s fashion and music journalism. A further wave of post-punk-influenced bands — including IDLES, Fontaines D.C., Shame and Black Country, New Road — emerged in the late 2010s and is sometimes described as a second revival.
