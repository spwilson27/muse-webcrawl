Lophotrochozoa

KingdomAnimalia
SuperphylumProtostomia / Spiralia
Named byHalanych et al., 1995
Major phylaMollusca, Annelida, Brachiopoda, Bryozoa, Platyhelminthes, Nemertea, Phoronida, Rotifera and others

Lophotrochozoa is a major clade of protostome animals within the Spiralia. It was proposed in 1995 by Halanych and colleagues on the basis of molecular (18S ribosomal RNA) evidence uniting the lophophorate groups — animals bearing a lophophore — with the trochozoans, animals whose larvae are of the trochophore type. Together with the Ecdysozoa (moulting animals such as arthropods and nematodes), it forms one of the two great divisions of the Protostomia.

Members

The clade includes the two largest animal phyla after the arthropods: the Mollusca (snails, bivalves, cephalopods and others) and the Annelida (segmented worms), as well as Platyhelminthes (flatworms), Nemertea (ribbon worms), Brachiopoda, Bryozoa, Phoronida, Rotifera and several smaller groups. Relationships among these phyla remain an active research problem, and alternative groupings such as "Platyzoa" have been proposed and partly abandoned as phylogenomic datasets have grown.

Characteristics

Members share spiral cleavage in early development (as part of Spiralia). Molluscs and annelids typically develop through a trochophore larva with bands of cilia, while brachiopods, bryozoans and phoronids share the ciliated tentacular feeding structure called the lophophore. Lophotrochozoans display enormous diversity of body plans, from microscopic rotifers to the giant squid, and include the most intelligent invertebrates, the cephalopods.
