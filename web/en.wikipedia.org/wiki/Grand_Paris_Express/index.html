The Grand Paris Express is a group of new rapid transit lines under construction in the Île-de-France region of France. The project comprises four new lines of the Paris Métro network (lines 15, 16, 17, and 18) and extensions of the existing Line 14, totalling roughly 200 kilometres of automated metro, mostly underground, with 68 new stations — one of the largest transport infrastructure projects in Europe.

History

The project was announced by President Nicolas Sarkozy in 2009 as the "Grand Paris" initiative and merged with the regional Arc Express orbital proposal. It is managed by the Société du Grand Paris (renamed Société des grands projets in 2023). The extended Line 14 — the backbone of the network — opened to Orly Airport in June 2024, in time for the Paris Olympic Games; the orbital Line 15 and lines 16–18 are scheduled to open in phases through the early 2030s.

Lines

  • Line 15: a 75 km orbital line encircling Paris through the inner suburbs, the longest of the project.
  • Line 16: serving the eastern suburbs from Noisy–Champs to Saint-Denis Pleyel.
  • Line 17: serving Charles de Gaulle Airport and Le Bourget.
  • Line 18: serving Orly Airport, Massy, Saclay, and Versailles.

Aims

The network is designed to connect suburbs directly without passing through central Paris, support new housing and economic clusters, and carry up to two million passengers daily when complete.
