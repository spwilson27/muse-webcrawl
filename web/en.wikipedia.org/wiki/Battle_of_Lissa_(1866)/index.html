Battle of Lissa (1866)

Date20 July 1866
LocationAdriatic Sea, near the island of Lissa (now Vis, Croatia)
ResultAustrian victory
BelligerentsAustrian Empire vs. Kingdom of Italy
CommandersWilhelm von Tegetthoff vs. Count Carlo Pellion di Persano

The Battle of Lissa, fought on 20 July 1866 between the Austrian and Italian fleets in the Third Italian War of Independence, was the first major naval battle between ironclad warships and the largest naval engagement of the 19th century after Trafalgar. A numerically and materially inferior Austrian fleet under Rear Admiral Wilhelm von Tegetthoff defeated the Italian fleet under Admiral Persano.

Background

Italy, allied with Prussia against Austria, sought to seize Austria's Adriatic territories. Persano was ordered to take the fortified island of Lissa, a key Austrian naval station. After days of indecisive bombardment of the island's forts, Persano's fleet was caught mid-redeployment when Tegetthoff's fleet arrived from Pola on the morning of 20 July.

The battle

Tegetthoff advanced in three wedge-shaped divisions and drove into the Italian line, intending to ram and fight at close range where his ships' weaker guns mattered less. A gap opened in the Italian line when Persano transferred his flag between ships, confusing his captains. In the ensuing melee the Austrian flagship Erzherzog Ferdinand Max rammed and sank the Italian ironclad Re d'Italia; the Italian gunboat Palestro later blew up after burning for hours.

Aftermath

Italy lost two ironclads and about 620 men; Austria lost none of its ships and about 40 killed. Persano was tried and disgraced. Although Austria won at Lissa and at Custoza on land, Prussia's victory at Königgrätz decided the war, and Italy gained Venetia by the Treaty of Vienna. The battle's ramming tactics influenced naval design for decades, encouraging the fitting of ram bows to capital ships.
