Perkūnas was the common Baltic god of thunder and the most important deity of the Baltic pantheon, worshipped by Lithuanians, Prussians, and related peoples. In Latvian mythology his counterpart is Pērkons. He is associated with the sky, rain, thunder, lightning, storms, and oak trees.

Mythology

Perkūnas rides through the sky in a chariot drawn by goats, striking devils and evil spirits with his axe or hammer, which returns to his hand like a boomerang. In many folk tales he pursues the devil Velnias, who hides from his lightning. Sacred oak groves, where everlasting fires were kept, were dedicated to him.

Legacy

Baltic paganism survived in Lithuania longer than anywhere else in Europe, and Perkūnas remained a living figure of folklore into the modern era. His name is related to the Proto-Indo-European thunder god reconstructed as *Perkʷūnos, and he remains a potent symbol in Lithuanian national culture.
