Died: January 13, 1958 (aged 77), Beverly Hills, California

Jesse L. Lasky

Jesse Louis Lasky (September 13, 1880 – January 13, 1958) was an American pioneer motion picture producer who was a key founder of what would become Paramount Pictures.

Early life

Born in San Francisco, Lasky worked as a newspaper reporter and then as a vaudeville impresario, presenting musical acts including his sister Blanche's ragtime piano act, before turning to film production.

Film career

In 1913 Lasky co-founded the Jesse L. Lasky Feature Play Company with Samuel Goldwyn and Cecil B. DeMille. Its first production, DeMille's The Squaw Man (1914), shot in a rented barn in Hollywood, was the first feature film made in Hollywood. In 1916 the company merged with Adolph Zukor's Famous Players Film Company to form Famous Players–Lasky, which became Paramount Pictures. Lasky served as its first vice president in charge of production, overseeing stars such as Mary Pickford, Gloria Swanson, and Rudolph Valentino, until the studio's financial troubles forced him out in 1932. He later produced independently, including Sergeant York (1941).

Legacy

Lasky has a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, and the former Lasky-DeMille Barn is now the Hollywood Heritage Museum. His son, Jesse L. Lasky Jr., became a noted screenwriter.
