Mowgli is a fictional character and the protagonist of Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book (1894) and The Second Jungle Book (1895). He is a "man-cub" — an Indian boy raised by wolves in the jungle of the Seeonee hills — whose name, Kipling said, means "frog" in the language of the jungle, a name given by his wolf mother Raksha for his hairless skin.

In Kipling's stories

Separated from his parents during a tiger attack, the infant wanders into a wolf den and is adopted by Raksha and Father Wolf; at Council Rock the pack accepts him, with the black panther Bagheera buying his life with a bull and the bear Baloo speaking for him. Baloo teaches him the Law of the Jungle and the Master Words of the birds and snakes; Bagheera teaches him to fear fire — the "Red Flower" — the one weapon by which he can face the lame tiger Shere Khan, who claims the boy as his prey. The stories follow Mowgli's abduction by the Bandar-log (monkey people) and rescue by Kaa the python, his sojourn in a human village, his killing of Shere Khan, and finally his departure from the jungle to humankind as a young man in "The Spring Running".

Adaptations

Mowgli has been portrayed on screen many times, notably in the 1942 Zoltán Korda film with Sabu, Disney's animated The Jungle Book (1967), Disney's 2016 live-action remake with Neel Sethi, and Andy Serkis's Mowgli: Legend of the Jungle (2018). Kipling's "Mowgli" stories also supplied much of the imagery — Akela, Baloo, the Law, "the good hunting of the pack" — adopted by Baden-Powell's Cub Scouts. Kipling actually wrote the character's first appearance as an adult forest ranger in the 1893 story "In the Rukh", before composing the childhood tales.
