Arnulf of Carinthia (c. 850 – 8 December 899) was the duke of Carinthia who overthrew his uncle, Emperor Charles the Fat, became the Carolingian king of East Francia from 887, and was crowned emperor in Rome in 896. He was the illegitimate son of Carloman of Bavaria.

Rise to power

Arnulf grew up in Carinthia, where his father made him margrave of the Carantanian march. In November 887, dissatisfied with the incapacitated Charles the Fat, the East Frankish magnates deposed the emperor at an assembly at Tribur and elected Arnulf king. Charles died weeks later. Unlike his predecessor, Arnulf did not attempt to reunite the whole Carolingian empire and recognised Odo of Paris in West Francia.

Reign

Arnulf campaigned successfully against the Vikings, crushing a large raiding army at the Battle of Leuven in September 891 — a victory that ended major Viking incursions into East Francia. In the east he allied with the Moravian ruler Svatopluk and later intervened in Italy at the invitation of Pope Formosus, whose coronation of Arnulf as emperor on 22 February 896 marked the high point of his reign. On his return from Rome, Arnulf suffered a stroke — some chroniclers alleged poisoning — and his health never recovered.

Death and succession

Arnulf died at Regensburg on 8 December 899. He was succeeded in East Francia by his six-year-old legitimate son, Louis the Child, the last Carolingian king of East Francia. Another son, Zwentibold, whom Arnulf had made king of Lotharingia, was killed in 900. Arnulf is not to be confused with the later Arnulf, Duke of Bavaria (reigned 907–937).
