The Chief of Staff of the Army (CSA) is the most senior uniformed officer of the United States Army and a member of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. The chief is the principal military adviser to the Secretary of the Army and is responsible for the organization, training, and equipping of the Army, while operational command of forces runs from the President and Secretary of Defense to the combatant commanders.

History

The office was created in 1903 under the Militia Act reforms of Secretary of War Elihu Root, replacing the post of Commanding General of the Army. The first chief was Samuel B. M. Young. During the Second World War, Chief of Staff George C. Marshall presided over the Army's enormous expansion.

Appointment and duties

The chief is nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate, and serves a four-year term, normally with the rank of general. The CSA presides over the Army Staff at the Pentagon, sets readiness and modernization priorities, and advises the President and Congress on Army matters. The chief is assisted by a Vice Chief of Staff and works alongside the civilian Secretary of the Army, who holds statutory authority over the department.
