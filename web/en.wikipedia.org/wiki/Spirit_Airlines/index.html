Spirit Airlines, Inc. is an American ultra-low-cost carrier headquartered in Dania Beach, Florida, near Fort Lauderdale. It operates scheduled services across the United States, the Caribbean, and Latin America, and is known for its bright yellow aircraft livery and its unbundled "Bare Fare" pricing model, under which passengers pay separately for extras such as checked and carry-on baggage, seat selection, and onboard refreshments.

History

The company was founded in 1983 as Charter One Airlines, a Detroit-based charter operator. It began scheduled passenger service in 1992 after rebranding as Spirit Airlines. During the 2000s Spirit repositioned itself as an ultra-low-cost carrier, expanding rapidly from its Fort Lauderdale focus city into markets across the Americas.

In 2022, Spirit agreed to a merger with JetBlue Airways after terminating an earlier deal with Frontier Airlines; a federal judge blocked the JetBlue merger in January 2024 on antitrust grounds. Facing financial pressure, Spirit filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in November 2024 and emerged in 2025 after restructuring its debt.

Business model

Spirit's model emphasises high aircraft utilisation, a single-family fleet of Airbus A320-series aircraft, and ancillary revenue. Its low base fares are supplemented by fees for optional services, a strategy that has made it one of the lowest unit-cost airlines in the United States while also attracting persistent criticism over customer service.

Fleet

Spirit operates an all-Airbus fleet consisting of the A319, A320, A321, and their neo variants, making it one of the largest A320neo-family operators in North America.
