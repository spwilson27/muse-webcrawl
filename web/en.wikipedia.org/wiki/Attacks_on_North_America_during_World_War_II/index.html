Attacks on North America during World War II

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Attacks on North America during World War II were a series of Axis military actions against continental North America — the United States, Canada, Mexico, and their coastal waters — between 1941 and 1945. Although limited in scale and effect, they included submarine campaigns in American waters, shellings of coastal installations, air raids on the Aleutian Islands, and Japanese balloon bombs carried across the Pacific.

German U-boat campaign

Following Germany's declaration of war on the United States in December 1941, U-boats launched Operation Drumbeat (Paukenschlag) against shipping along the U.S. East Coast in January 1942, sinking many merchant ships and tankers in poorly defended waters. U-boats also operated in the Gulf of Mexico, the Caribbean, and Canadian waters, including the Gulf of St. Lawrence, and landed agents by submarine in the United States (Operation Pastorius, 1942) and Canada.

Japanese attacks

Japanese submarines shelled the Ellwood oil field near Santa Barbara, California (February 1942), Fort Stevens, Oregon (June 1942), and the Estevan Point lighthouse in British Columbia. In June 1942 Japanese forces bombed Dutch Harbor, Alaska, and occupied the Aleutian islands of Attu and Kiska — the only occupation of incorporated U.S. territory — until U.S. and Canadian forces retook them in 1943. In September 1942 a submarine-launched floatplane flown by Nobuo Fujita dropped incendiary bombs in Oregon forests, the only enemy air bombing of the contiguous United States. From late 1944 Japan launched thousands of Fu-Go balloon bombs; one killed six people near Bly, Oregon, in May 1945, the only combat deaths in the continental United States caused by enemy action in the war.

Atlantic Canada and the Caribbean

Attacks in Canadian waters included the Battle of the St. Lawrence, sinkings off Newfoundland, and U-boat operations near Halifax. German submarines also attacked shipping and shore installations in the Caribbean, including the bombardment of Aruba in 1942.

Effect

None of the attacks materially damaged the Allied war effort, but they spurred coastal blackouts, convoying, civil defense measures, and — in the climate of fear on the U.S. West Coast — contributed to the context of the internment of Japanese Americans.
