Sichuan pepper

Sichuan pepper (Chinese: 花椒; pinyin: huājiāo, "flower pepper") is a spice produced from the husks of the berries of several species of the genus Zanthoxylum, including Zanthoxylum simulans and Zanthoxylum bungeanum, in the rue family Rutaceae. Despite its name, it is not closely related to black pepper or to chilli peppers.

Place of originChina (Sichuan)
Main ingredientsDried husks of Zanthoxylum berries
Scoville scaleNot applicable (tingling, not hot)

Flavour

Sichuan pepper has a distinctive aroma with citrus notes and produces a characteristic tingling, numbing sensation in the mouth, known in Chinese as má (麻). The sensation is caused by compounds called sanshools (notably hydroxy-alpha-sanshool), which act on touch receptors rather than taste buds. Combined with the heat (là, 辣) of chilli peppers, it creates the defining málà ("numbing and hot") flavour profile of Sichuan cuisine, found in dishes such as mapo tofu, dan dan noodles, and Sichuan hot pot.

Use

The seed husks are used whole or ground, often after light toasting to release their aroma. The bitter black seeds are usually removed. Sichuan pepper is also an ingredient of Chinese five-spice powder, and related Zanthoxylum spices are used elsewhere in Asia — Japanese sanshō, Korean sancho, and the timur of Nepal and Bhutan.

History and regulation

Sichuan pepper has been used in China for millennia and was one of the principal pungent spices before chillies arrived from the Americas in the Ming dynasty era. Between 1968 and 2005 the United States banned its import because the berries could carry citrus canker disease; the ban was lifted for heat-treated pepper that cannot transmit the bacterium.
