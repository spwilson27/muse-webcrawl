Counter-electromotive force

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Counter-electromotive force (counter EMF, CEMF), also known as back electromotive force (back EMF), is the electromotive force that opposes the change in current which induced it. It is a consequence of electromagnetic induction and Lenz's law.

In motors

In an electric motor, the rotating armature cuts through the magnetic field and generates a voltage that opposes the applied supply voltage. The net voltage driving current is the supply voltage minus the back EMF, which grows with rotational speed. This explains why a motor draws its largest current at start-up, when back EMF is zero, and why current falls as the motor speeds up; it also provides inherent speed regulation under load.

In inductors

In any coil or inductor, a changing current induces a counter EMF proportional to the inductance and the rate of change of current. This opposing voltage is responsible for inductive voltage spikes when circuits are switched off, and underlies the operation of transformers and ignition coils.
