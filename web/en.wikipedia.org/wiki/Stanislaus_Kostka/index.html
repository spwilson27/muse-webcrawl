Stanislaus Kostka

Saint Stanislaus Kostka (28 October 1550 – 15 August 1568) was a Polish novice of the Society of Jesus (Jesuits). He is venerated in the Catholic Church as the patron saint of youth, students, and Poland, and is remembered for his intense devotion during a very short life.

Early life

Stanislaus was born at Rostkowo, Poland, into a noble family; his father was a senator and castellan of Zakroczym. He was educated at home and then, from 1564, at the Jesuit college in Vienna together with his elder brother Paul. The brothers lodged in the house of a Lutheran senator, where Stanislaus endured harsh treatment for his devout practices, including long hours of prayer and fasting.

Flight to Rome

Determined to enter the Jesuits despite his family's opposition, Stanislaus fled Vienna in 1567. According to tradition, he walked hundreds of miles, first to Dillingen in Germany, where Peter Canisius tested and encouraged him, and then on to Rome. There Francis Borgia admitted him to the novitiate of Saint Andrew at the Quirinal on 28 October 1567, his seventeenth birthday.

Death and canonization

Stanislaus fell ill in the summer heat of Rome and died on 15 August 1568, the feast of the Assumption, only ten months after entering the novitiate. His reputation for holiness spread quickly; he was beatified in 1605 and canonized by Pope Benedict XIII on 31 December 1726. His feast day is 13 November (15 August in some calendars), and his relics rest in the church of Sant'Andrea al Quirinale in Rome.
