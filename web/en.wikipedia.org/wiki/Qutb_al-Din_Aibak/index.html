Qutb al-Din Aibak (1150–1210) was a Turkic slave-general of the Ghurid ruler Muhammad of Ghor who, after his master's death in 1206, became the first sultan of the Delhi Sultanate and the founder of the Mamluk (Slave) dynasty in India.

Early life and career

Born in Turkestan, Aibak was sold into slavery as a child and eventually purchased by Muhammad of Ghor, under whom he rose through ability to high command. He led Ghurid forces in northern India, winning important victories and consolidating Muslim rule over Delhi and the Gangetic plain after the battles of Tarain (1191–1192), in which the Rajput ruler Prithviraj Chauhan was defeated.

Sultan of Delhi

After Muhammad of Ghor was assassinated in 1206, leaving no heir, Aibak asserted his independence at Lahore and was recognised as sultan. His brief reign was spent securing his territories against rivals such as Taj al-Din Yildiz of Ghazni and Nasir al-Din Qabacha of Sindh.

Death and legacy

Aibak died in 1210 in Lahore after a fall from his horse while playing chaugan (a form of polo). He was succeeded briefly by Aram Shah and then by his slave and son-in-law Iltutmish, who firmly established the sultanate. Aibak is credited with commissioning the Qutb Minar in Delhi — the tallest brick minaret in the world — and the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, built from the remains of demolished temples. His generosity earned him the epithet "Lakh Baksh", giver of lakhs.
