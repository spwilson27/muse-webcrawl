Lighter (barge)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A lighter is a type of flat-bottomed barge used to transfer goods and passengers to and from moored ships. Lighters were traditionally unpowered — moved and steered with "sweeps" (long oars) or, later, towed by tugs — although some are self-propelled. The term comes from the act of "lightening" a ship: by unloading part of its cargo into lighters, a vessel reduces its draught so that it can enter a shallow harbour or berth.

Usage

Lighterage was once the universal method of loading and discharging ships in ports lacking deep-water quays: cargo was carried between ship and shore in lighters, and the men who crewed them were known as lightermen — one of the historic trades of the Port of London, organised through the Company of Watermen and Lightermen. The practice remains important in ports with shallow approaches, in inland waterways, and in specialised operations such as lightering, where very large crude carriers transfer part of their oil cargo to smaller tankers at sea so they can enter port.

Lighter aboard ship

The concept was systematised in the twentieth century in the LASH (Lighter Aboard Ship) system, in which standardised barges are loaded at river ports, towed to a mother ship, and hoisted aboard for the ocean passage, reversing the process at the destination. Related systems include Seabee and BACAT.
