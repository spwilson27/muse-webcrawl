Semigroup

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Semigroup — an algebraic structure with an associative binary operation.

In mathematics, a semigroup is an algebraic structure consisting of a set together with an associative binary operation. The word was coined by the French mathematician Jean-Armand de Séguier in the early twentieth century. A semigroup generalises a group by omitting the requirement of an identity element and of inverse elements, and generalises a monoid by omitting only the identity requirement.

Definition

A semigroup is a set S with a binary operation · : S × S → S satisfying the associative law: (x · y) · z = x · (y · z) for all x, y, z in S. Associativity means parentheses may be dropped, so products such as x₁x₂…xₙ are unambiguous. If the operation is also commutative, the structure is a commutative (or abelian) semigroup.

Examples

The positive integers under addition form a semigroup (indeed, removing 0 from the additive monoid of natural numbers destroys the identity). Every monoid and every group is a semigroup. Other standard examples include strings under concatenation without the empty string, matrices under multiplication, and functions from a set to itself under composition.

Structure theory

The systematic study of semigroups began in the 1920s–1940s with work by Anton Sushkevich and David Rees. Because no inverses are assumed, semigroup theory uses tools quite different from group theory: Green's relations classify elements by the principal ideals they generate, and the Rees theorem describes completely simple semigroups. Semigroups arise naturally in automata theory — the transition structure of a finite automaton forms a semigroup — and in the analysis of partial differential equations via one-parameter operator semigroups.
