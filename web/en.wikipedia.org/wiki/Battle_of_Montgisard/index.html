The Battle of Montgisard was fought between the Kingdom of Jerusalem and the Ayyubid Sultanate on 25 November 1177 during the Crusades. The 16-year-old King Baldwin IV, seriously afflicted by leprosy, led an outnumbered Christian force against the army of Saladin, which had invaded the kingdom from Egypt. The Muslim army was routed and fled in disarray, with Saladin himself escaping only because he rode a racing camel.

Background

In 1177 Saladin planned a major invasion of the Kingdom of Jerusalem from Egypt. Learning of the plan, Baldwin IV marched out of Jerusalem with a much smaller force to relieve Ascalon, where he was temporarily contained by a detachment of Saladin's army. Believing the Christians neutralised, Saladin marched north with the bulk of his army, allowing his troops to disperse and loot the countryside around Ramla, Lydda and Arsuf.

The battle

Baldwin slipped out of Ascalon and, joined by the Knights Templar under Odo of St Amand, intercepted Saladin near Montgisard, close to Ramla. The chronicler William of Tyre recorded about 375 knights in Baldwin's force, along with several thousand infantry. The sudden charge of the Frankish heavy cavalry struck the Ayyubid army while it was strung out and unprepared. The fighting was fierce, and the True Cross was carried before the Christian army. The Ayyubid force collapsed, suffering very heavy casualties.

Aftermath

Saladin escaped to Egypt with perhaps a tenth of his army, according to some accounts. The victory secured the kingdom for several years and elevated Baldwin's reputation. A truce followed in 1180. Saladin returned a decade later and, after the Battle of Hattin in 1187, conquered most of the kingdom, including Jerusalem itself.
