Qualitative research

Qualitative research is a genre of research in the social sciences that investigates social and cultural phenomena through non-numerical data — words, images, observations and artefacts — rather than statistics. It seeks to understand how people interpret their experiences and the meanings they attach to them, typically in natural settings and from relatively small, purposively chosen samples.

Methods

Common approaches include:

  • Ethnography — prolonged participant observation of a community or organisation;
  • Interviews — in-depth, semi-structured or unstructured conversations;
  • Focus groups — facilitated group discussions;
  • Grounded theory — building theory inductively from systematically coded data;
  • Case study and phenomenology — intensive study of a bounded case or of lived experience;
  • Discourse and narrative analysis — study of language and story.

Data are analysed by transcription, coding and thematic analysis, with quality assessed through credibility, transferability, dependability and confirmability rather than statistical validity.

Comparison with quantitative research

Where quantitative research tests hypotheses with measurable variables, qualitative research is exploratory and interpretive, asking "how" and "why" questions. The two are frequently combined in mixed-methods designs. Qualitative inquiry has roots in anthropology and sociology — in the fieldwork of Malinowski and the Chicago School — and is now standard across health research, education, marketing and design.
