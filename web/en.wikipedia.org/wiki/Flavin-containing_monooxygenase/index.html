Flavin-containing monooxygenase

Flavin-containing monooxygenase
ClassEnzymes (oxidoreductases)
CofactorsFAD, NADPH
Human genesFMO1–FMO5

The flavin-containing monooxygenases (FMO) are a family of enzymes that catalyse the oxygenation of a wide variety of nucleophilic nitrogen-, sulfur- and phosphorus-containing compounds, including many drugs and xenobiotics. They use the flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD) prosthetic group and NADPH to insert one atom of molecular oxygen into the substrate.

Function

FMOs are microsomal enzymes found mainly in the liver, kidney and lung. Together with the cytochrome P450 system, they form the major phase I oxidative drug-metabolising machinery. A characteristic reaction is the conversion of trimethylamine to odourless trimethylamine N-oxide.

Clinical significance

Loss-of-function mutations in the human FMO3 gene cause trimethylaminuria ("fish odour syndrome"), in which the body cannot properly oxidise trimethylamine, leading to a strong body odour. FMO variants also contribute to individual differences in drug metabolism and response.
