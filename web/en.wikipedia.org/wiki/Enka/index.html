Enka

Enka (演歌) is a genre of Japanese popular song that emerged in the post-war period and is often compared to the American country music or the blues in its themes and emotional directness. Enka lyrics typically deal with love, loss, longing for home, endurance and the hardships of working-class life, and are delivered with a distinctive vibrato-heavy vocal technique called kobushi.

History

The word enka originally referred to political "speech songs" (enzetsu no uta) performed by activists in the Meiji era. In its modern sense the genre crystallised in the 1950s and 1960s, blending Japanese pentatonic melodies with Western orchestral arrangements. Singers such as Hibari Misora became national icons, and enka dominated Japanese popular music through the 1970s.

Style and performance

Enka songs commonly use the yonanuki pentatonic scale, giving them a recognisably traditional Japanese colour, and are arranged for strings, brass and rhythm section. Performers traditionally wear kimono (for women) or formal suits (for men) and stage act the song's emotion. Although its commercial popularity among younger listeners has declined in favour of J-pop, enka retains a loyal older audience and a guaranteed presence on the New Year's Eve television programme Kōhaku Uta Gassen.
