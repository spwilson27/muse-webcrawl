Sindhi people

RegionsSindh, Pakistan; India; worldwide diaspora
Populationca. 36–40 million
LanguagesSindhi
ReligionsIslam (majority in Pakistan); Hinduism (majority among Indian Sindhis)

The Sindhi people are an Indo-Aryan ethnolinguistic group native to the Sindh region of Pakistan, in the lower valley and delta of the Indus River, speaking the Sindhi language. Sindhis are one of the principal ethnic groups of Pakistan, and a large diaspora lives in India and around the world.

History

Sindh is among the oldest continuously inhabited regions of South Asia, home to the Indus Valley Civilisation cities such as Mohenjo-daro. Over the centuries the region absorbed waves of Iranian, Greek, Arab, Central Asian and Baloch settlement. Sindh became the first region of the subcontinent to come under sustained Muslim rule after the Umayyad general Muhammad bin Qasim's conquest of 711–712 CE, earning it the honorific Bab-ul-Islam ("Gateway of Islam"). Local dynasties such as the Soomras, Sammas, Kalhoras and Talpurs ruled before the British annexed Sindh in 1843.

Partition and diaspora

The Partition of India in 1947 transformed Sindhi society. Most Sindhi Hindus, who formed a largely urban, mercantile population, migrated to India, leaving behind property and ancestral towns; in their place, Muslim refugees (Muhajirs) from India settled in Sindh's cities, especially Karachi and Hyderabad. Indian Sindhis, without a linguistic state of their own, rebuilt prosperous communities in cities such as Mumbai, Ulhasnagar, Ahmedabad, Delhi and Jaipur, and Sindhi merchants established a notable global trading diaspora in places such as Hong Kong, Dubai, the Canary Islands and West Africa.

Culture

Sindhi culture is distinguished by its Sufi heritage — the shrines and poetry of Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai, Lal Shahbaz Qalandar and Sachal Sarmast draw devotees of all faiths — and by crafts such as ajrak block-printed cloth, Sindhi topi caps, ralli quilts and blue pottery. Sindhi Cultural Day (Ekta Day), celebrated in December, sees widespread display of ajrak and topi. Folk music and dance, Indus-river cuisine built on rice, fish and lotus root, and reverence for the river (honoured as Jhoole Lal by Sindhi Hindus) are other hallmarks.
