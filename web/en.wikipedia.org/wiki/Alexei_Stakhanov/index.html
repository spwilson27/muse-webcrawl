Alexei Grigoryevich Stakhanov (Russian: Алексей Григорьевич Стаханов; 3 January 1906 – 5 November 1977) was a Soviet coal miner whose record-breaking shift in 1935 made him a national hero and gave his name to the Stakhanovite movement, a campaign to raise labour productivity across the USSR.

The record shift

On the night of 30–31 August 1935, at the Central Irmino mine in the Donbas, Stakhanov was reported to have hewn 102 tonnes of coal in under six hours — fourteen times his quota — using a pneumatic pick and a reorganised division of labour with assisting timbermen. The feat, publicised by Pravda, was promoted by Stalin's regime as a model for workers everywhere; in September Stakhanov raised the record still further.

Stakhanovism

"Stakhanovites" who overfulfilled norms received higher pay, housing and honours, and the movement spread to factories and farms. Historians note that records were carefully staged and that the movement also intensified pressure on ordinary workers and managers. Stakhanov himself was promoted to administrative posts, awarded the Order of Lenin and the title Hero of Socialist Labour, and the city of Kadievka was renamed Stakhanov in his honour (1978–2016). He died in 1977, having spent his later years in relative obscurity in Torez.
