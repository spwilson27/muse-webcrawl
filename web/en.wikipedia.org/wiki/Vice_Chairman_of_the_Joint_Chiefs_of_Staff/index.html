Vice Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff

Reports toSecretary of Defense; Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff
AppointerPresident of the United States, with Senate advice and consent
Term length2 years (renewable once)
Constituting instrumentGoldwater–Nichols Act of 1986
First holderRobert T. Herres

The Vice Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff (VJCS) is, by U.S. law, the second highest-ranking military officer in the United States Armed Forces, ranking just below the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. The Vice Chairman outranks all respective four-star heads of the service branches, which grants them the authority to command them.

The position was created by the Goldwater–Nichols Department of Defense Reorganization Act of 1986. The first Vice Chairman was General Robert T. Herres of the U.S. Air Force, who took office in February 1987.

Duties

The Vice Chairman acts as the deputy and principal assistant to the Chairman in all matters, presides over meetings of the Joint Chiefs in the Chairman's absence, and serves as vice chairman of the Defense Acquisition Board, overseeing the Joint Requirements Oversight Council (JROC), which validates warfighting requirements. The Vice Chairman also plays a central role in the nuclear command and control process and in advising the President and Secretary of Defense.

Appointment

The Vice Chairman is nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate, serving a single two-year term that may be renewed once. By statute, the Vice Chairman may not be from the same armed force branch as the Chairman.
