Lady Gaga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Lady Gaga performing at the piano in a stripped-back Joanne-era show, in a pink hat, mid-ballad]

Stefani Joanne Angelina Germanotta (born 28 March 1986), known professionally as Lady Gaga, is an American singer, songwriter and actress, a defining pop figure of the twenty-first century. Emerging from New York's Lower East Side club scene with The Fame (2008) and its singles 'Just Dance' and 'Poker Face', she built stardom on theatrical reinvention, avant-garde fashion (the 2010 meat dress among popular culture's most famous garments) and devotion to her fans, the 'Little Monsters'.

Her discography runs from dance-pop (Born This Way, 2011) through jazz standards with Tony Bennett (Cheek to Cheek, 2014; Love for Sale, 2021) to the country-tinged Joanne (2016) and the house revival of Chromatica (2020). As an actress she won acclaim in American Horror Story and the film A Star Is Born (2018), whose song 'Shallow' won the Academy Award. Song titles cited only.

Pop auteur

A child pianist who studied method acting and briefly attended NYU's Tisch School, Germanotta wrote for other artists (Britney Spears, the Pussycat Dolls) before Akon signed her to his label. Her Haus of Gaga creative team, the relentless touring (the Monster Ball was among the highest-grossing debut tours) and her advocacy — the Born This Way Foundation (2012) for youth mental health, prominent LGBTQ+ allyship — built a brand of inclusion as ideology.

Second acts

The Bennett albums made her a credible jazz singer to sceptics; A Star Is Born made her a film star; her 2017 Super Bowl halftime show and the Las Vegas residencies (Enigma and the jazz-and-piano show) showed both her pop and standard-bearing selves. In 2024 she released the standards album Harlequin alongside the film Joker: Folie à Deux, in which she played Harley Quinn, and returned to dance-pop with Mayhem (2025) and its lead single 'Abracadabra'.
