Girard's theorem

In mathematics, Girard's theorem (also called the spherical excess formula) states that the area of a spherical triangle is proportional to its spherical excess — the amount by which the sum of its angles exceeds π radians (180 degrees).

Statement

For a spherical triangle on a sphere of radius R with interior angles A, B, and C, the area is Δ = R² (A + B + C − π). Thus the area depends only on the angles, unlike in Euclidean geometry where the angles do not determine the area.

History

The theorem is named for the French mathematician Albert Girard, who published the result in 1629 in his work on spherical trigonometry, though related results were explored by Thomas Harriot and others around the same era.

Applications

The formula is fundamental in spherical geometry, geodesy, and cartography, and it generalizes to spherical polygons, whose area equals R² times the angular excess computed from the sum of their interior angles.
