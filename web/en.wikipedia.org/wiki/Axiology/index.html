Axiology

Axiology (from Greek axia, "value, worth", and -logia, "study of") is the philosophical study of value. It includes questions about the nature and classification of values and about what things have value and to what degree. Axiology encompasses the two traditional branches of ethics and aesthetics, which study moral and artistic value respectively, but is also applied to questions about economic, religious and other kinds of value.

History and themes

The term was introduced in the early 20th century, drawing on the work of the German philosopher Eduard von Hartmann and the French philosopher Paul Lapie. Value theory asks whether values are objective or subjective, whether some things are intrinsically valuable while others are valuable only instrumentally, and whether values can be ranked or compared. Max Scheler and Nicolai Hartmann developed formal axiology, seeking objective hierarchies of values, while other philosophers treated value judgements as expressions of emotion or preference.
