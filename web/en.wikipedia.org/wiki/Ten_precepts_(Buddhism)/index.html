Ten precepts (Buddhism)

The ten precepts (Pali: dasasikkhāpada; Sanskrit: daśaśikṣāpada) are the training rules taken by novice monks (sāmaṇera) and novice nuns (sāmaṇerī) in Buddhism. They extend the five precepts observed by devout lay followers, adding rules of renunciation appropriate to those who have gone forth into monastic life. Fully ordained monks and nuns observe many more rules — 227 for monks in the Theravāda tradition — but the ten precepts form the moral foundation of the novice's training.

The precepts

The novice undertakes to abstain from:

  1. Killing living beings
  2. Taking what is not given (stealing)
  3. All sexual activity (complete celibacy, extending the lay precept against sexual misconduct)
  4. False speech
  5. Intoxicants that cloud the mind
  6. Eating after midday
  7. Dancing, singing, music, and watching entertainments
  8. Wearing garlands, perfumes, cosmetics, and personal ornaments
  9. Using high and luxurious beds and seats
  10. Accepting gold and silver (money)

Observance

The precepts are formally received from a preceptor during the novice ordination (pabbajjā, "going forth") ceremony, at which the candidate's head is shaved, robes are presented, and the ten precepts are recited in Pali and repeated by the novice. Violation of the first four precepts, or certain other grave faults, can result in expulsion from the order.

Beyond novices, the ten precepts are also undertaken temporarily by lay practitioners: in some traditions, devout laypeople observe the eight precepts on uposatha observance days, a set closely related to the ten (with the seventh precept split and the tenth, on money, often omitted because laypeople cannot practically keep it). In East Asian Buddhism, the ten precepts (Chinese: 十戒) likewise constitute the novice's code, within the framework of the Chinese Vinaya lineages.
