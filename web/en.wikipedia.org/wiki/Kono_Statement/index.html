Kono Statement

Kono Statement
Date4 August 1993
Issued byYōhei Kōno, Chief Cabinet Secretary of Japan
Subject"Comfort women" of the Imperial Japanese military

The Kono Statement is a statement issued on 4 August 1993 by Japan's Chief Cabinet Secretary Yōhei Kōno, following a government study into the "comfort women" system of the Imperial Japanese Army during the Second World War. It remains Japan's most prominent official acknowledgment and apology concerning the issue.

Content

The statement acknowledged that comfort stations were established at the request of the military, that the Japanese Army was involved directly or indirectly in their establishment, management, and the transfer of comfort women, that in many cases women were recruited "against their own will, through coaxing, coercion, etc.", and that they lived "under a coercive atmosphere". It extended the government's "sincere apologies and remorse" to all who suffered "immeasurable pain and incurable psychological wounds".

Aftermath

The statement formed the basis of Japan's official position and of the Asian Women's Fund (1995–2007), which distributed compensation and medical support funded partly by private donations. It has been periodically contested by Japanese nationalists, who dispute the extent of direct coercion; a 2014 government review examined how the statement had been drafted but the Abe administration affirmed it would not be revised. The issue continues to affect Japan–South Korea relations, including disputes over the 2015 bilateral agreement intended to resolve the matter "finally and irreversibly".
