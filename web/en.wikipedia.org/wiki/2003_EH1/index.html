2003 EH1 is a small near-Earth asteroid of the Amor group, discovered by the Lowell Observatory Near-Earth-Object Search (LONEOS) in 2003. It is best known as the probable parent body of the Quadrantid meteor shower, one of the most intense annual meteor showers.

Orbit and the Quadrantids

The object follows a highly inclined, eccentric orbit with a period of about five and a half years, resembling that of a Jupiter-family comet rather than a typical asteroid. Shortly after its discovery, astronomer Peter Jenniskens showed that its orbit matched the debris stream responsible for the Quadrantids, which peak sharply in early January.

Extinct comet hypothesis

2003 EH1 is widely regarded as a dormant or extinct comet nucleus — the burnt-out core of comet C/1490 Y1, recorded by East Asian astronomers in 1490–91, whose debris also fed the Quadrantid stream. Modelling suggests the stream is young, only a few hundred years old, and that the parent body may have shed its meteoroids in a breakup or outburst around that time. Together with asteroid (196256) 2003 EH1's inert appearance, it is a key example of the blurred boundary between asteroids and comets.
