Ethiopian calendar

The Ethiopian calendar (Amharic: የኢትዮጵያ ዘመን አቆጣጠር), also called the Ethiopic or Ge'ez calendar, is the principal calendar used in Ethiopia and a liturgical calendar of the Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Churches. It is a solar calendar derived from the Egyptian calendar and closely related to the Coptic calendar, with a leap day added every four years without exception.

Structure

The year consists of twelve months of thirty days each, followed by a thirteenth month of five days (six in a leap year), called Pagumēn. The months are Mäskäräm, Ṭəqəmt, Ḫədar, Taḫśaś, Ṭərr, Yäkatit, Mägabit, Miyazya, Gənboṭ, Säne, Ḥamle, Nähase, and Pagumēn. The new year, Enkutatash, falls on September 11 in the Gregorian calendar (September 12 in the year before a Gregorian leap year) and marks the beginning of Mäskäräm.

Epoch

The calendar's epoch — its year 1 — corresponds to AD 8 in the proleptic Julian system, because the Ethiopian church computed the date of the Annunciation differently from the Roman church. As a result, the Ethiopian year number is roughly seven to eight years behind the Gregorian year: the Ethiopian year 2017, for example, began on September 11, 2024.

Use

Ethiopia never adopted the Gregorian calendar for civil purposes, so the Ethiopian calendar governs official dates, school years, and holidays in the country; Ethiopia famously celebrated the year 2000 in September 2007 by the Gregorian reckoning. The church also maintains a distinct system for computing Easter and movable feasts. In a related traditional usage, the Ethiopian time system begins the day at dawn, so that what is 6:00 a.m. by international convention is 12:00 in Ethiopian time.
