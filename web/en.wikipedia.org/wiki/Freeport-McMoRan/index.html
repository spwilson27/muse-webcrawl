Freeport-McMoRan Inc., often called simply Freeport, is an American mining company based in Phoenix, Arizona. It is one of the world's largest producers of copper and a major producer of gold and molybdenum. The company operates large, long-lived mines in North America, South America and Indonesia, including the Grasberg mine in Papua, Indonesia, one of the world's largest copper and gold deposits, and the Morenci mine in Arizona, the largest copper-producing mine in North America.

History

The company's roots go back to the Freeport Sulphur Company, founded in 1912, and to McMoRan Oil & Gas, founded in 1969. Freeport-McMoRan Copper & Gold was formed in 1981 as a subsidiary and later spun off. In 2007, Freeport acquired Phelps Dodge for approximately $26 billion, making it the world's largest publicly traded copper producer at the time. Between 2013 and 2016 the company owned oil and natural gas assets before divesting them to refocus on mining.

Operations

In North America, Freeport operates mines including Morenci, Bagdad, Safford, Sierrita and Miami in Arizona, as well as operations in Colorado and New Mexico. In South America it operates the Cerro Verde mine in Peru and El Abra in Chile. In Indonesia, PT Freeport Indonesia operates the Grasberg minerals district; in 2018, following a divestment agreement, the Indonesian state mining holding company Inalum acquired a majority interest while Freeport remained the operator.

Criticism

The Grasberg operation has been criticised by environmental and human rights groups over riverine tailings disposal, the mine's environmental footprint in a biodiverse region, and relations with indigenous Papuan communities and the Indonesian state. The company states that its practices meet applicable standards and that the operation provides substantial economic benefits.
