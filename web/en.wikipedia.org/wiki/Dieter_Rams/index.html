Dieter Rams

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dieter Rams (born 20 May 1932) is a German industrial designer closely associated with the consumer products company Braun and the Functionalist school of industrial design. His unobtrusive approach and his guiding principle of "less, but better" — embodied in his famous ten principles of good design — have exerted a lasting influence on modern product design, notably on the work of Apple designer Jonathan Ive.

Early life and education

Rams was born in Wiesbaden, Germany. Influenced by his grandfather, a carpenter, he studied architecture and interior decoration at the Werkkunstschule Wiesbaden (later part of the RheinMain University of Applied Sciences), graduating in 1953. After a period working for the Frankfurt architect Otto Apel, he joined the electrical products manufacturer Braun in 1955.

Career at Braun

At Braun, Rams initially worked as an architect and interior designer, but quickly gravitated towards product design, working within a team that included Hans Gugelot and colleagues from the Ulm School of Design (HfG Ulm). In 1961 he became head of design at Braun, a position he held until 1995, and in 1968 he also became design director of the furniture company Vitsoe, for whom he designed the celebrated 606 Universal Shelving System.

Among his best-known Braun products are the SK 4 record player (1956, nicknamed "Snow White's coffin", designed with Gugelot), the T 1000 world receiver (1963), the RT 20 table radio, the ET 66 calculator (1987, with Dietrich Lubs), and a series of electric shavers and household appliances. Rams's designs stripped products down to their essential forms, using neutral colours, clear typography, and carefully considered controls.

Ten principles of good design

In the late 1970s Rams formulated his famous ten principles. Good design, he wrote:

  1. is innovative
  2. makes a product useful
  3. is aesthetic
  4. makes a product understandable
  5. is unobtrusive
  6. is honest
  7. is long-lasting
  8. is thorough down to the last detail
  9. is environmentally friendly
  10. is as little design as possible

Legacy

Rams retired from Braun in 1997 but continued to speak and write about design ethics and sustainability, warning against the throwaway culture of consumer electronics. His work is held in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art in New York. The documentary film Rams (2018), directed by Gary Hustwit, chronicled his life and philosophy. Apple's Jonathan Ive has acknowledged Rams as a direct inspiration, and many Apple products echo the geometry and restraint of Rams's Braun designs.
