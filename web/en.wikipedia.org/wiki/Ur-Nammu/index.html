Ur-Nammu (or Ur-Namma, Ur-Engur; Sumerian: 𒌨𒀭𒇉, "servant of Nanna") was a Sumerian king who founded the Third Dynasty of Ur and the Neo-Sumerian Empire, reigning roughly 2112–2095 BCE according to the middle chronology. He unified southern Mesopotamia after the collapse of Gutian domination and is remembered above all for promulgating the Code of Ur-Nammu, the oldest surviving law code in the world, and for building the great ziggurats of Ur and other cities.

Reign

Ur-Nammu began as governor (ensi) of Ur under Utu-hengal of Uruk, who had expelled the Gutians. After Utu-hengal's death he took the royal title himself, subdued rival cities including Lagash — whose ruler Nam-mahani was killed — and extended his authority over Sumer and Akkad and into Elam. He reorganized the administration, standardized weights and measures, dredged canals, restored temples and trade, and declared himself "king of Sumer and Akkad."

Code of Ur-Nammu

The Code of Ur-Nammu, written in Sumerian on clay tablets, predates the Code of Hammurabi by some three centuries. Its prologue credits the king with establishing equity, removing corrupt officials and protecting widows and orphans. The surviving casuistic laws ("If a man...") notably prescribe monetary fines for many bodily injuries rather than the lex talionis ("eye for an eye") of Hammurabi's later code, though capital punishment applies to murder, robbery and certain sexual offenses.

Building works and death

Ur-Nammu built or rebuilt temples across his realm, most famously the Ziggurat of Ur dedicated to the moon god Nanna, with further ziggurats at Eridu, Uruk and Nippur. His monumental Stele of Ur-Nammu depicts the king in the presence of Nanna and Ningal. According to the Sumerian composition The Death of Ur-Nammu, he died in battle — probably against the Gutians — and descended to the underworld, where he presented gifts to its gods. He was succeeded by his son Shulgi, under whom the empire reached its height.
