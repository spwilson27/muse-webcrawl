Delaware General Corporation Law

The Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL), Title 8 of the Delaware Code, is the statute governing corporate law in the U.S. state of Delaware. More than half of all publicly traded companies in the United States, including roughly two-thirds of the Fortune 500, are incorporated in Delaware.

Overview

The DGCL is known for its flexibility, allowing corporations great freedom in structuring their internal governance. Disputes are heard by the Delaware Court of Chancery, a specialized court of equity whose judges develop an extensive body of case law, giving businesses unusual predictability.

Criticism

Critics describe Delaware's dominance as a "race to the bottom" that favors management at shareholders' expense, while defenders argue it represents a "race to the top" producing efficient, well-understood rules. Franchise taxes and incorporation fees supply a significant share of Delaware's state revenue.
