Pali (Pāli) is a Middle Indo-Aryan liturgical language native to the Indian subcontinent. It is widely studied because it is the language of the Buddhist Pali Canon (the Tipiṭaka) of Theravada Buddhism, and it remains the sacred language of that tradition in Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia.

Origins and history

Pali belongs to the Prakrit group of Middle Indo-Aryan languages. Its exact geographic origin has long been debated: Theravada tradition associates it with Magadha, while modern scholarship generally links it to a western or midland dialect that was shaped and standardized as a literary language. The word pāli originally referred to the texts themselves — a "line" or "series" of canonical scripture — as distinct from the commentaries (aṭṭhakathā), and only later came to denote the language.

According to Theravada tradition, the Buddha's teachings were memorized and transmitted orally before being committed to writing in Sri Lanka in the first century BCE at Aluvihāra. The fifth-century commentator Buddhaghosa translated and systematized earlier Sinhala commentaries into Pali, consolidating its position as the language of Theravada scholarship.

Phonology and grammar

Pali has a simpler phonological system than Sanskrit: the vocalic liquids ṛ and ḷ disappear, diphthongs are reduced, and consonant clusters are frequently assimilated (e.g., Sanskrit dharma becomes Pali dhamma). Grammatically it retains eight nominal cases and a rich verbal system, though some Sanskrit forms are simplified. Nouns are written without a dedicated script; Pali has been recorded in many regional alphabets.

Literature

Beyond the Canon itself — the Vinaya Piṭaka, Sutta Piṭaka, and Abhidhamma Piṭaka — Pali literature includes chronicles such as the Sri Lankan Mahāvaṃsa, grammars like Kaccāyana's, and Buddhaghosa's great commentary-cum-manual, the Visuddhimagga ("Path of Purification"). Pali remained a productive scholarly language well into the modern period and has experienced a revival in Buddhist studies worldwide.
