Blow molding

Stretch blow molding of PET bottles

Blow molding (or blow moulding) is a specific manufacturing process by which hollow plastic parts are formed and can be joined together. It is also used for forming glass bottles and other hollow objects. In general, there are three main types of blow molding: extrusion blow molding, injection blow molding, and injection stretch blow molding.

Process

The blow molding process begins with conventional extrusion of a tube of molten resin, called a parison, or, in the injection variants, with an injection-molded preform. The mold closes around the hot plastic, and compressed air is blown in, inflating the material against the cold mold walls where it cools and solidifies. Once the plastic has cooled and hardened, the mold opens and the part is ejected.

Types

Extrusion blow molding is the simplest and most common type, used for products such as milk jugs, detergent bottles, and fuel tanks. Injection blow molding gives better control of neck finishes and is used for smaller containers such as pharmaceutical bottles. Injection stretch blow molding stretches the preform both axially and radially, orienting the polymer chains and greatly improving strength and clarity; it is the standard process for PET soft-drink bottles.

History

The process derives from glassblowing. The first plastic blow molding machines appeared in the 1930s and 1940s, and the development of high-density polyethylene in the 1950s made blow-molded household containers economical, transforming packaging.
