Dutch Formosa

Dutch Formosa was the period of Dutch colonial rule over parts of Taiwan from 1624 to 1662, administered by the Dutch East India Company (VOC). Centred on Fort Zeelandia at Tayouan (modern Anping, Tainan) in the southwest, and later Fort Provintia, the colony exploited Taiwan's position between China and Japan, trading in deerskins, sugar, rattan and Chinese silk and porcelain.

History

After being driven from the Penghu Islands by the Ming, the VOC built Fort Zeelandia in 1624. The company taxed and licensed deer hunting, encouraged Han Chinese immigration to farm sugarcane and rice, conducted missionary work among the Siraya and other Indigenous peoples—creating a romanization of the Siraya language used in "Sinkan manuscripts"—and suppressed resistance, notably in the 1635 punitive campaign against Mattau. A smaller Spanish colony in the north (1626–1642) was expelled by the Dutch.

In 1661 the Ming loyalist Koxinga (Zheng Chenggong), seeking a base against the Qing, besieged Fort Zeelandia. After nine months, Governor Frederik Coyett surrendered in February 1662, ending 38 years of Dutch rule; Koxinga founded the Kingdom of Tungning.

Legacy

Dutch rule left written records of Taiwan's Indigenous languages, reshaped agriculture, and remains symbolized by the surviving walls of Fort Zeelandia and Chihkan Tower (Fort Provintia) in Tainan.
