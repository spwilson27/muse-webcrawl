Chen Kaige (born 12 August 1952) is a Chinese film director and a leading figure of the Fifth Generation of Chinese filmmakers. He is best known internationally for Farewell My Concubine (1993), which won the Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival — still the only Chinese-language film to do so — and was nominated for two Academy Awards.

Early life

Born in Beijing to film director Chen Huai'ai, Chen's schooling was interrupted by the Cultural Revolution, during which, like many urban youths, he was sent down to work in the countryside in Yunnan and later served in the People's Liberation Army. In 1978 he entered the newly reopened Beijing Film Academy, graduating in the celebrated class of 1982 alongside Zhang Yimou and Tian Zhuangzhuang.

Career

His debut feature, Yellow Earth (1984), photographed by Zhang Yimou, is credited with launching the Fifth Generation movement with its bold compositions of the loess landscapes of Shaanxi and its elliptical, critical view of tradition. Subsequent works include The Big Parade (1986), King of the Children (1987), and Life on a String (1991). Farewell My Concubine, an epic spanning fifty years of Chinese history through the lives of two Peking opera performers, brought him worldwide acclaim. Later films include Temptress Moon (1996), The Emperor and the Assassin (1998), Together (2002), the martial-arts epic The Promise (2005), Forever Enthralled (2008), Caught in the Web (2012), Monk Comes Down the Mountain (2015), and Legend of the Demon Cat (2017). He also co-directed the record-breaking patriotic anthology The Battle at Lake Changjin (2021).
