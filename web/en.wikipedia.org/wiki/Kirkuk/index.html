Kirkuk (Arabic: كركوك; Kurdish: کەرکووک, Kerkûk; Turkish: Kerkük) is a city in northern Iraq, the capital of Kirkuk Governorate, situated about 238 kilometres (148 mi) north of Baghdad. Built on the site of the ancient Hurrian and Assyrian city of Arrapha, it stands beside some of Iraq's richest oil fields and is one of the country's most ethnically mixed and politically contested cities, claimed both by the federal government in Baghdad and by the Kurdistan Region.

History

The Kirkuk Citadel stands on a mound occupied since the third millennium BCE; the city is identified with Arrapha, capital of a Hurrian kingdom absorbed into the Neo-Assyrian Empire, near which the Battle of Gaugamela (331 BCE) is sometimes located. Through the Islamic and Ottoman periods Kirkuk was a regional fortress town and caravan centre on the route between Baghdad and Mosul, with a mixed population of Kurds, Arabs, Turkmen, and Christians.

Oil transformed the city. The Turkish Petroleum Company struck oil at Baba Gurgur in 1927 — the gusher that confirmed one of the world's largest fields — and pipelines from Kirkuk to Haifa and Tripoli (1934) and later to Ceyhan in Turkey made the city the centre of northern Iraqi oil production under the Iraq Petroleum Company. Under Saddam Hussein's Ba'athist regime, an "Arabisation" campaign expelled tens of thousands of Kurds and Turkmen and settled Arabs in their place, while the Anfal campaign devastated the surrounding Kurdish countryside.

After the 2003 invasion, Kurdish peshmerga and political parties took a leading role in the city; Article 140 of the Iraqi constitution promised a referendum on Kirkuk's status that was never held. Peshmerga forces secured Kirkuk in June 2014 as the Iraqi army collapsed before the Islamic State, which Kurdish and Iraqi forces later pushed back from the province. Following the Kurdish independence referendum of September 2017, federal Iraqi forces and allied militias retook the city in October 2017, and it remains under federal control amid continuing disputes over its governance, security, and oil exports.

Oil

The Kirkuk oil field, discovered in 1927 and operated today by the state North Oil Company, is one of the oldest producing supergiant fields; its output and the export pipeline to Ceyhan have repeatedly been disrupted by sabotage, war, and the Baghdad–Erbil dispute.
