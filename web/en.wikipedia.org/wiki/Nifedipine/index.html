Nifedipine

ClassDihydropyridine calcium channel blocker
UsesHypertension, angina, preterm labour
Trade namesAdalat, Procardia, others
RoutesBy mouth

Nifedipine, sold under the brand name Adalat among others, is a calcium channel blocker medication used to manage angina, high blood pressure, Raynaud's phenomenon, and premature labour. It was patented in 1967 and approved for medical use in 1981, and appears on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Nifedipine relaxes blood vessels by blocking L-type calcium channels in vascular smooth muscle, reducing blood pressure and cardiac workload. Extended-release formulations are used for chronic hypertension and angina; it is also widely used off-label as a tocolytic to delay preterm labour.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, flushing, ankle swelling, dizziness and palpitations. Rapid-acting capsules can cause abrupt blood pressure falls and are no longer recommended for hypertensive emergencies. It should not be combined with grapefruit juice, which raises its blood levels.
