Hertzsprung–Russell diagram

The Hertzsprung–Russell diagram (abbreviated as H–R diagram, HR diagram or HRD) is a scatter plot of stars showing the relationship between the stars' absolute magnitudes or luminosities versus their stellar classifications or effective temperatures. The diagram was created independently in 1911 by Ejnar Hertzsprung and by Henry Norris Russell in 1913, and represented a major step towards an understanding of stellar evolution.

Forms of the diagram

There are several forms of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram, and the nomenclature is not well defined. The original diagram displayed by Russell in 1913 plotted spectral type on the horizontal axis and absolute magnitude on the vertical axis. Modern observational versions plot effective temperature (decreasing to the right) against luminosity, while theoretical versions used by astronomers plot luminosity against surface temperature computed from stellar models.

Features

Most stars lie along the diagonal band of the main sequence, where stars spend most of their lives fusing hydrogen. Above it lie the red giants and supergiants, stars of high luminosity but low surface temperature; below-left lie the faint, hot white dwarfs. The position of a star on the diagram traces its evolution over its lifetime, making the HR diagram one of the most fundamental tools in astrophysics.
