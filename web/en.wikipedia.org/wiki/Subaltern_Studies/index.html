Subaltern Studies

The Subaltern Studies Group, or Subaltern Studies Collective, is a group of South Asian scholars interested in the postcolonial and post-imperial societies of South Asia, and more broadly the world. Founded in 1982 under the leadership of historian Ranajit Guha, the group sought to write a new history of India "from below", taking its name and inspiration from the Italian Marxist Antonio Gramsci's concept of the subaltern — social groups subordinated by class, caste, gender, age, or office.

Aims and method

In the manifesto of the first volume, Subaltern Studies I (1982), Guha criticised both colonialist and nationalist historiography of India as elitist, arguing that they credited the making of the nation to elites — colonial rulers or the nationalist bourgeoisie — and ignored the autonomous domain of "the politics of the people": peasants, workers, tribals, and other subordinated groups. The collective's work drew on peasant insurgency records, colonial archives read "against the grain", and later on literary theory, poststructuralism, and postcolonial criticism.

Development and influence

Contributors included Shahid Amin, David Arnold, Partha Chatterjee, Dipesh Chakrabarty, and Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak, whose 1988 essay "Can the Subaltern Speak?" became one of the most influential texts of postcolonial theory. The project influenced Latin American subaltern studies and the broader field of postcolonial studies, and its later volumes broadened beyond peasant history to questions of community, nation, gender, and historiography itself. Sumit Sarkar and others criticised the project's later turn away from materialist social history towards discourse analysis.
