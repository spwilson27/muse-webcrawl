XERF-AM

XERF-AM (1570 kHz) is a radio station in Ciudad Acuña, Coahuila, Mexico. It is historically famous as the successor to XER, the powerful "border blaster" station founded by the American quack doctor John R. Brinkley in the 1930s, which broadcast from Villa Acuña across the Rio Grande into the United States.

History

Brinkley's original station XERA was shut down by the Mexican government in 1939 under pressure from the United States. After an interim period, the station returned as XERF in 1947 under new ownership, operating with very high power — reportedly up to 250,000 watts — making it one of the most powerful AM stations in North America.

In the 1950s and 1960s XERF became famous in the United States for its night-time broadcasts of rhythm and blues, country music, and mail-order advertising, receivable across much of North America after dark. The disc jockey Wolfman Jack began his career broadcasting on XERF in the early 1960s before moving to XERB. The station's signal and programming style later inspired portrayals in popular culture, including in the film American Graffiti.

Later years

The station was nationalized by the Mexican government in 1969 and eventually came under the operation of the Instituto Mexicano de la Radio (IMER) in 1983, broadcasting public and cultural programming as La Poderosa. Its power was reduced substantially from its border-blaster heyday.
