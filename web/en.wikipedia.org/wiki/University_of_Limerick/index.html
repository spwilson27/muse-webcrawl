University of Limerick

The University of Limerick (UL; Irish: Ollscoil Luimnigh) is a public research university in Limerick, Ireland. Founded in 1972 as the National Institute for Higher Education, Limerick, it became a university in 1989 in accordance with the University of Limerick Act, the first Irish university established since independence in 1922. Its campus, set in parkland along the River Shannon in the National Technological Park at Castletroy, is known for its cooperative education programme, which places students in paid work experience.

History

The institute admitted its first students in 1972, modelled partly on innovative European and American institutions. Under founding president Edward Walsh, it pioneered cooperative education in Ireland and introduced modular, trimester-based teaching. It gained university status in 1989 alongside Dublin City University. In the twenty-first century UL expanded its medical school (Ireland's first graduate-entry medical programme), its Kemmy Business School, and research institutes such as the Bernal Institute for materials science.

Campus

The campus spans both banks of the River Shannon, linked by the Living Bridge, one of the longest footbridges in Europe. Facilities include the University Concert Hall, the Glucksman Library, and extensive sports grounds; UL styles itself "Ireland's Sporting Campus" and hosts the University Arena. The university maintains one of the largest collections of student accommodation in Ireland in its on-campus villages.

Academics

UL is organized into four faculties: the Kemmy Business School; Education and Health Sciences; Science and Engineering; and Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences (including the Irish World Academy of Music and Dance). Its cooperative education programme, in which students spend about eight months in paid employment, is one of the largest in the European Union.
