The River Clyde (Scottish Gaelic: Abhainn Chluaidh, pronounced [ˈavɪɲ ˈxl̪ˠuəj]) is the third-longest river in Scotland and the ninth-longest in the United Kingdom. It flows from the Lowther Hills in South Lanarkshire through Glasgow to the Firth of Clyde on the west coast. The river was central to the development of Glasgow and to shipbuilding on Clydeside, which produced many of the world's most famous ocean liners.

Course

The Clyde rises near Daer Reservoir in the Lowther Hills and flows north-east through Lanarkshire, passing Lanark and the Falls of Clyde — a series of waterfalls including Corra Linn, now partly harnessed for hydroelectric power and protected as a scenic reserve. At Hamilton and Motherwell it turns west, entering Glasgow from the south-east. It passes through the city centre, where it is crossed by many bridges, then flows through the former shipbuilding districts of Govan, Partick, Clydebank and Renfrew before widening into its estuary below Dumbarton and reaching the Firth of Clyde at Greenock and Helensburgh.

History

The upper Clyde was once too shallow for ocean-going ships. From the 18th century, engineers progressively deepened and narrowed the channel — notably following the work of John Golborne and John Rennie — turning Glasgow into a major port. In the 19th and early 20th centuries Clydeside yards at Govan, Scotstoun, Clydebank and Port Glasgow built a large share of the world's tonnage, including the Cunard liners Lusitania and the three Queens. Shipbuilding contracted sharply after the Second World War, leaving a reduced industry centred on naval construction at Govan and Scotstoun.

Wildlife and regeneration

Once heavily polluted, the Clyde now supports returning salmon and sea trout, and its estuary is an important habitat for wading birds. Regeneration of the Glasgow waterfront has brought the Riverside Museum, the Glasgow Science Centre and the SSE Hydro arena to the banks of the river, and occasional visiting tall ships and the paddle steamer Waverley, the world's last seagoing paddle steamer, which sails from the Clyde.

Tributaries

Principal tributaries include the Kelvin, the White Cart Water, the Black Cart Water, the Leven and the Avon Water.
