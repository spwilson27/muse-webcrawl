Epipolar geometry

Epipolar geometry
FieldComputer vision, photogrammetry
Key objectsEpipole, epipolar line, epipolar plane
Algebraic formsEssential matrix, fundamental matrix
ApplicationsStereo vision, 3D reconstruction, structure from motion

Epipolar geometry is the intrinsic projective geometry between two views of a scene. It is independent of scene structure and depends only on the cameras' internal parameters and relative pose. Epipolar geometry is used in computer vision and photogrammetry to find correspondences between points in stereo images, because it constrains the search for a matching point to a single line.

Concepts

Given a 3D point seen by two cameras, the point, the two camera centres, and the two image points define an epipolar plane. Its intersections with the two image planes are the epipolar lines: a point appearing in one image must lie on the corresponding epipolar line in the other image. All epipolar lines in an image pass through the epipole, the image of the other camera's centre — which may lie outside the visible image when the cameras face similar directions.

Matrices

The geometry is encoded algebraically by the essential matrix for calibrated cameras and by the fundamental matrix for uncalibrated cameras, which can be estimated from point correspondences (for example with the eight-point algorithm) and decomposed to recover relative camera motion. Rectifying a stereo pair so that epipolar lines become horizontal scanlines is a standard preprocessing step in stereo matching and 3D reconstruction pipelines.
