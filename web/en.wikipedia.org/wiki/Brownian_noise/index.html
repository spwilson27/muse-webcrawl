Brownian noise

Brownian noise, also called brown noise or red noise, is a type of noise signal produced by integrating white noise; mathematically it corresponds to a random walk, or Brownian motion. Its name comes from Robert Brown, the botanist who described Brownian motion, not from the colour brown.

Spectrum

Brownian noise has a power spectral density inversely proportional to the square of frequency (1/f²), so its energy decreases by about 6 decibels per octave as frequency rises. Compared with white noise and pink noise, it sounds much deeper and softer—often compared to a waterfall, heavy rain, or distant thunder—because most of its energy is concentrated at low frequencies.

Generation and uses

It can be generated by summing successive random values so that each sample is the previous sample plus a random step, usually with leak or damping to prevent unbounded drift. Brownian noise is used in sound design, sleep and masking applications, ocean acoustics modelling, and in describing natural processes that behave like random walks.
