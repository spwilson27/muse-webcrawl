The green peafowl (Pavo muticus) is a species of peafowl native to the tropical forests of Southeast Asia, including Myanmar, Thailand, Vietnam, Cambodia, Laos, Malaysia and the Indonesian island of Java. It is one of the largest flying birds, and unlike the better-known Indian peafowl, the sexes are quite similar in appearance, both bearing metallic green plumage on the neck and a tall, fan-like crest.

Description

Adult males measure up to about 3 metres including the train of elongated upper-tail coverts, which is displayed in courtship like that of the Indian peafowl. The neck and breast feathers are scale-like and iridescent green and gold in both sexes; females are slightly duller and lack the long train. The species has a loud, far-carrying call and strong legs armed with spurs; it is more wary and less tolerant of human proximity than its Indian relative.

Distribution and habitat

Green peafowl inhabit a range of forest types, from lowland rainforest to dry deciduous woodland and bamboo thickets, usually near water. They were formerly widespread, occurring also in northeastern India, Bangladesh and southern China, but have disappeared from much of that range.

Conservation

The species is listed as Endangered by the IUCN. Populations have declined sharply owing to hunting for meat, feathers and the pet trade, collection of chicks, and the fragmentation and conversion of forest habitat. It survives in protected areas such as Huai Kha Khaeng in Thailand, Baluran and Ujung Kulon in Java, and Yok Don in Vietnam, and conservation programmes include captive breeding and reintroduction efforts. The green peafowl is the national bird of Myanmar and appears in the heraldry and art of the region.
