Margaret Bondfield

Margaret Grace Bondfield (17 March 1873 – 16 June 1953) was a British Labour politician, trade unionist, and women's rights activist. She was the first woman cabinet minister in British history, serving as Minister of Labour in Ramsay MacDonald's second government (1929–1931).

Born in Chard, Somerset, Bondfield left school at thirteen and worked as a draper's assistant, an experience that led her into the shop workers' union (NAUSAWC). She became a full-time union organizer, helped found the National Union of General and Municipal Workers, and served on the TUC General Council, which she chaired in 1923—another first for a woman.

Political career

After several unsuccessful attempts, Bondfield was elected MP for Northampton in 1923, lost the seat in 1924, and was returned for Wallsend in 1926. As a junior minister in 1924 she was among the first women to hold ministerial office in Britain. As Minister of Labour from 1929 she faced mass unemployment during the Great Depression; her administration of unemployment insurance benefits drew criticism from the Labour left, and she remained with MacDonald briefly into the 1931 crisis before losing her seat in that year's election. She never returned to Parliament but remained active in public life, publishing her autobiography, A Life's Work, in 1948.
