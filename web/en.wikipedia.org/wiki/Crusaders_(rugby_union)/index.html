Crusaders (rugby union)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Crusaders play in red and black and are based in Christchurch, New Zealand.

The Crusaders (known as the Canterbury Crusaders until 2009) are a New Zealand professional rugby union team based in Christchurch. They compete in Super Rugby Pacific and represent the upper South Island, drawing players from the Canterbury, Tasman, and West Coast unions and the wider Crusaders region. The Crusaders are the most successful team in Super Rugby history, having won more championships than any other side.

History

The Crusaders were founded in 1996 as one of five New Zealand franchises for the inaugural Super 12 season. After a poor first season, in which they finished last, the team won its first title in 1998, defeating the Blues in the final at Eden Park, and repeated as champions in 1999 and 2000 under coach Wayne Smith, completing a three-peat.

Under coach Robbie Deans, the Crusaders dominated the 2000s, winning further titles in 2002 — when they went through the season undefeated — 2005, 2006, and 2008. After a nine-year drought, coach Scott Robertson led the team to three consecutive Super Rugby titles from 2017 to 2019, and further championships in the COVID-affected competitions of 2020 and 2021 (Super Rugby Aotearoa) and in Super Rugby Pacific in 2022 and 2023. Rob Penney succeeded Robertson in 2024.

Home ground and identity

The Crusaders' traditional home was Lancaster Park in Christchurch, which was severely damaged in the 2011 Christchurch earthquake. The team subsequently played at a temporary stadium in Addington before moving to the redeveloped Apollo Projects Stadium (Christchurch Stadium). Their colours are red and black, matching those of the Canterbury provincial team, and their emblem and themed entertainment traditionally draw on imagery of medieval knights.

Notable players

The Crusaders have produced many All Blacks, including Richie McCaw, Dan Carter, Kieran Read, Sam Whitelock, Andrew Mehrtens, Justin Marshall, and Scott Barrett. Dan Carter finished his career as the leading points scorer in international rugby, and McCaw captained the All Blacks to two Rugby World Cup victories.

Honours

  • Super Rugby champions: 1998, 1999, 2000, 2002, 2005, 2006, 2008, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2022, 2023
  • Super Rugby Aotearoa champions: 2020, 2021
