Curriculum vitae

[Image: An example curriculum vitae document laid out on a desk]

A curriculum vitae (CV) — Latin for "course of life" — is a document that summarises a person's education, work history, skills, and achievements, used principally when applying for jobs, academic positions, grants, and fellowships.

CV and résumé

In most of the English-speaking world outside North America, "CV" denotes the standard job application document, similar to an American résumé: a concise summary of one or two pages. In the United States and Canada, by contrast, a CV is a longer, comprehensive record used mainly in academia, medicine, and research, covering publications, presentations, teaching, and professional memberships in full, and may run to many pages. The European Union promotes a standard format through the Europass CV.

Contents

A typical CV includes contact details; a personal profile or objective; education and qualifications; employment history in reverse chronological order; skills; and, for academic CVs, publications, research funding, and supervision experience. Conventions vary by country: photographs and dates of birth, once common in many European CVs, are generally omitted in the UK and US because of anti-discrimination law.

History

Leonardo da Vinci's 1482 letter to Ludovico Sforza, listing his skills and accomplishments, is often cited as an early precursor of the résumé. Printed CVs became standard in the twentieth century as formal recruitment expanded, and are now usually submitted electronically and screened by applicant tracking systems.
