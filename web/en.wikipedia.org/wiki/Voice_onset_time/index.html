Voice onset time

Voice onset time (VOT), in phonetics, is a feature of the production of stop consonants. It is defined as the length of time that passes between the release of a stop consonant and the onset of vocal fold vibration (voicing) in the following vowel or sonorant.

Categories

Three principal VOT patterns distinguish stop types across languages: voicing lead (negative VOT, voicing begins well before release, as in fully voiced /b d ɡ/ of French or Spanish), short lag (VOT of roughly 0–30 ms, typical of unaspirated voiceless stops and of English word-initial /b d ɡ/), and long lag (VOT above ~50 ms, found in aspirated stops such as English /pʰ tʰ kʰ/).

Research

VOT was systematically studied by Lisker and Abramson (1964), whose cross-language work showed that languages divide the VOT continuum differently to implement voicing contrasts. Infants perceive VOT boundaries categorically, and VOT measurement is used clinically in assessing speech disorders and in speech technology for consonant recognition.
