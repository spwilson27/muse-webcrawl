Solitaire

Solitaire is any tabletop game which one can play by oneself, usually with cards, but also with dominoes, tiles, pegs, or stones. In North America the term commonly refers to card games for one player, known in British English as patience. The most famous variant is Klondike, which became globally popular through its inclusion in Microsoft Windows beginning in 1990.

History

Card solitaire probably originated in northern Europe in the late eighteenth century, with early references in German and Scandinavian game literature. The French term "patience" reflects the game's association with quiet perseverance. Napoleon was popularly said to have played patience during his exile on St Helena, and variants such as Napoleon's Grave are named for him. The game spread widely in the nineteenth century through collections such as Lady Cadogan's Illustrated Games of Patience (1874).

Types of card solitaire

Hundreds of variants exist. Common families include Klondike, in which cards are built up in foundations by suit from ace to king while the tableau is built down in alternating colours; FreeCell, in which nearly all deals are solvable and all cards begin face-up; Spider, played with two decks, in which complete same-suit sequences are removed; Pyramid, in which pairs of cards totalling thirteen are discarded; and Yukon, Canfield, and Golf. Games are classified by whether the layout is fully visible (open), partially hidden, and by the mechanism of building and moving cards.

Computer solitaire

Microsoft Solitaire, a Klondike implementation included with Windows 3.0 in 1990, was intended partly to teach users how to drag and drop with a mouse; it became one of the most played computer games in history and was inducted into the World Video Game Hall of Fame in 2019. Computer versions allow analysis of win rates: for Klondike with a three-card draw, the proportion of winnable deals remains an open question, though estimates place it around 80 percent or higher.

Other forms

Peg solitaire, played with pegs on a board with holes, dates to the court of Louis XIV in the seventeenth century. Mahjong solitaire, a tile-matching game popularised by a 1986 computer version, is unrelated to the four-player game of mahjong. The word "solitaire" is also used for a single gemstone set alone, and for various solitary birds.
