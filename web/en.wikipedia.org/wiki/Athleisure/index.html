Athleisure is a hybrid style of athletic clothing typically worn as everyday wear. The word is a portmanteau of "athletic" and "leisure". Athleisure outfits can include leggings, yoga pants, tights, sneakers, hoodies and jackets that look like athletic wear but are characterized by their design in a way that characterizes them as fashionable, comfortable clothing.

History

The rise of athleisure is generally attributed to improvements in textile technology — synthetic fibres such as spandex and elastane that offered stretch, moisture-wicking and odour resistance — combined with a broader cultural shift toward health and fitness as a lifestyle. Brands such as Lululemon Athletica, founded in 1998, popularised yoga-inspired clothing worn outside the studio.

By the mid-2010s, market analysts were describing athleisure as one of the most significant trends in the apparel industry, taking market share from traditional denim jeans. In 2016 the term was added to the Merriam-Webster dictionary.

Market

The global athleisure market has been valued in the hundreds of billions of US dollars. The trend accelerated during the COVID-19 pandemic, when remote work increased demand for comfortable clothing. Major sportswear companies including Nike and Adidas, fashion houses, and celebrity-led brands all entered the category.

Criticism

Commentators have debated whether athleisure represents a lasting change in dress norms or a passing fad, and some workplaces and schools have introduced dress codes restricting items such as leggings. Others have criticised the environmental impact of synthetic performance fabrics.
