Wildlife of Madagascar

The ring-tailed lemur, among Madagascar's most famous endemic animals.

The wildlife of Madagascar is among the most distinctive on Earth. Because the island separated from the African mainland more than 80 million years ago, its plants and animals evolved in isolation: roughly 90 per cent of its species are found nowhere else. Madagascar is classified as a biodiversity hotspot.

Fauna

  • Lemurs — more than 100 species of primates, from the tiny mouse lemurs to the indri, found only on Madagascar.
  • Carnivorans — the fossa and other endemic euplerids.
  • Chameleons — around half of the world's chameleon species, including the tiny Brookesia leaf chameleons.
  • Tenrecs, rodents and bats — diverse endemic radiations; the island also hosts abundant frogs, geckos and the celebrated comet moth.
  • Birds — about 280 species, nearly half endemic, including vangas, ground rollers and the extinct elephant birds.

Flora

Madagascar has an estimated 11,000–14,000 plant species, the great majority endemic. Highlights include six species of baobab (of eight worldwide), the traveller's palm, and thousands of orchids.

Conservation

Deforestation, slash-and-burn agriculture (tavy), and hunting threaten much of this wildlife. A network of national parks and reserves protects key habitats, but many species remain endangered.
