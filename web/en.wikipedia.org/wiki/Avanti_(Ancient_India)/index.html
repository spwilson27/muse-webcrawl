Avanti (Sanskrit: अवन्ती) was an ancient Indian Mahajanapada (great kingdom), roughly corresponding to the present-day Malwa region of Madhya Pradesh. It was one of the sixteen great kingdoms (Solasa Mahajanapadas) of the 6th century BCE, divided into northern and southern parts by the Vindhya mountains, with capitals at Ujjayini (Ujjain) and Mahishmati respectively.

History

Avanti became a major power under King Pradyota Mahasena, a contemporary of Gautama Buddha and of Bimbisara and Ajatashatru of Magadha. Pradyota waged wars with his neighbours, including the kingdom of Vatsa, and maintained a formidable army; Buddhist and Jain texts remember him as a powerful but ruthless monarch.

The kingdom lay on important trade routes linking northern India with the Deccan and the western ports, which brought it wealth and made Ujjain an enduring commercial and religious centre. Avanti was eventually absorbed into the expanding Magadhan empire under the Nanda dynasty, and later formed part of the Mauryan Empire, whose western viceroy governed from Ujjain. The Avanti region is also remembered as the homeland of the dramatist-poet Kālidāsa's beloved city Ujjayini and is associated with the early history of Buddhism and Jainism.
