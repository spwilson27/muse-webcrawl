The Southern United States (often simply the South, and historically Dixie) is a geographic and cultural region of the United States. The United States Census Bureau defines it as sixteen states — Alabama, Arkansas, Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maryland, Mississippi, North Carolina, Oklahoma, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Virginia and West Virginia — together with the District of Columbia, divided into the South Atlantic, East South Central and West South Central divisions. Cultural and historical definitions of "the South" vary and often centre on the eleven states that seceded to form the Confederate States of America.

History

Colonised by England from 1607, the region's plantation economy rested on enslaved African labour producing tobacco, rice, indigo and later cotton. The South's defence of slavery led to secession and the American Civil War (1861–65), followed by Reconstruction, and then a century of Jim Crow racial segregation. The civil rights movement of the 1950s and 1960s dismantled legal segregation. Since the 1970s the Sun Belt South has grown rapidly through domestic migration, air conditioning, and diversified industries.

Culture

Southern culture is noted for its dialects of American English, its cuisines (barbecue, fried chicken, grits, soul food, Cajun and Creole cooking, Tex-Mex), and its musical heritage — the birthplace of blues, jazz, country, bluegrass, rock and roll, gospel and much of American popular music. The region forms the core of the "Bible Belt", with high rates of evangelical Protestant affiliation. College football, NASCAR and a strong martial tradition also figure prominently in regional identity.

Economy and demography

Once overwhelmingly rural and agricultural, the South now hosts major metropolitan areas — Atlanta, Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston, Charlotte, Nashville, Austin and Miami among them — and leads the nation in population growth. Its demographics combine the historic Black Belt African American population, long-established white communities, Indigenous nations (especially in Oklahoma), and fast-growing Hispanic populations in Texas and Florida.
