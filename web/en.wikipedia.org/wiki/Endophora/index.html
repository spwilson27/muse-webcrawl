Endophora

Endophora is a linguistic and semiotic term referring to expressions whose interpretation depends on material within the same text or discourse, in contrast to exophora, whose referents lie outside the text. The term was introduced by the linguist M. A. K. Halliday and Ruqaiya Hasan in their work on cohesion in English.

Types

Endophoric reference is traditionally divided into two main kinds:

  • Anaphora – reference to something mentioned earlier in the text, as when a pronoun refers back to a previously introduced noun phrase ("Mary arrived. She sat down.").
  • Cataphora – reference forward to something mentioned later in the text ("When he arrived, John sat down.").

Some analyses include a third subtype, sometimes called self-reference or esphora, in which an expression refers to another part of the same sentence.

Function

Endophoric expressions are a principal means of textual cohesion: they bind a discourse together by linking later expressions to earlier or later ones, allowing speakers and writers to avoid repetition and guiding listeners and readers in tracking participants and topics. The study of endophora is central to discourse analysis, text linguistics and the semantics of pronouns and definite descriptions.
