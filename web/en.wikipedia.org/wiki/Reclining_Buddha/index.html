Reclining Buddha

A reclining Buddha is an image representing the Buddha lying on his right side, head resting on his hand or a cushion, depicting the Buddha during his last illness, about to enter parinirvana. The posture is known in Thai tradition as pang sai yat.

Symbolism

Although the scene shows the Buddha's earthly death at Kushinagar, the serene expression conveys that death is not the end, since the Buddha attained enlightenment and escaped the cycle of rebirth. Images often show disciples mourning at his feet, and the soles of the feet may bear auspicious marks, as on the famous statue at Wat Pho in Bangkok.

Notable examples

Famous reclining Buddhas include the 46-metre gilded statue at Wat Pho, Thailand; the 14-metre statue carved in the caves at Gal Vihara, Sri Lanka; and the colossal reclining Buddha at Kushinagar, India.
