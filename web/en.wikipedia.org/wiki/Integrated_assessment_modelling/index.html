Integrated assessment modelling

Integrated assessment modelling (IAM) combines knowledge from multiple disciplines, typically economics, energy systems, land use and climate science, into a single framework to evaluate policy options, most prominently for climate change mitigation.

Applications

Integrated assessment models produce emissions scenarios and pathways used by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, estimate the social cost of carbon, and explore the costs and feasibility of limiting warming to targets such as 1.5 °C or 2 °C above pre-industrial levels. Well-known models include DICE, PAGE, IMAGE, MESSAGE and REMIND.

Criticism

Critics argue that the models embed contestable assumptions about discount rates, damage functions and technological change, and that their complexity can obscure value judgements behind technical outputs.
