Kaggle is an online community of data scientists and machine learning practitioners, owned by Google. It is best known for hosting machine learning competitions in which companies and organisations post a dataset and a problem, and participants compete to build the best predictive model, usually for prize money.

History

Kaggle was founded in Melbourne, Australia, in 2010 by economist Anthony Goldbloom, with Ben Hamner as founding chief technology officer, and moved its headquarters to San Francisco in 2011. Its early competitions — such as a contest to predict hospital admissions — drew attention from the data-mining community, and high-profile contests for organisations such as NASA, Allstate, and the Heritage Provider Network (a $3 million prize, then the largest in crowdsourced data science) followed. In March 2017 Google announced that it was acquiring Kaggle, which became part of Google Cloud; the platform continued to operate under its own brand.

Platform features

Beyond competitions, Kaggle provides free computing resources — browser-based Jupyter notebooks ("Kernels") with CPU and GPU/TPU access — a large repository of public datasets, tutorials and micro-courses ("Learn"), discussion forums, and public code sharing. Participants earn points and rankings in separate tiers (Novice, Contributor, Expert, Master, Grandmaster) across competitions, datasets, notebooks, and discussions; "Grandmaster" status is held by only a small number of the platform's most accomplished users.

Competitions and impact

Kaggle competitions are scored on a public leaderboard during the contest and a held-out private leaderboard at its end. Notable competitions include the 2012 Merck molecular activity challenge, which helped popularise deep learning in drug discovery, the 2015 Diabetic Retinopathy detection contest, and numerous featured competitions in computer vision, natural language processing, and forecasting. Winning solutions are often published and have influenced standard practice — for example, in gradient boosting, where tools such as XGBoost and LightGBM gained prominence through Kaggle use. Critics note that competition-winning models are sometimes over-engineered for the leaderboard metric and may transfer imperfectly to production settings.
