Yule

Yule (also called Jul, jól or joulu) is a winter festival historically observed by the Germanic peoples. It later underwent Christian reformulation and became part of Christmas traditions, and is now also celebrated as a midwinter sabbat in modern paganism, especially Wicca and Heathenry.

Yule
Type: Germanic winter festival
Season: Midwinter / winter solstice
Related to: Christmas, Saturnalia
Customs: Yule log, feasting, wassailing

History

Old Norse sources describe jól as a multi-day midwinter feast with sacrifices (blót), ritual drinking, oath-swearing, and honouring of gods such as Odin — whose byname Jólnir links him to the season — and the dead during the Wild Hunt. King Haakon I of Norway rescheduled Yule to coincide with Christmas in the 10th century.

Customs

Traditions associated with Yule include the Yule log, Yule goat, Yule boar (ham), carolling/wassailing, and gift-giving. Many Christmas customs in northern Europe descend in part from these celebrations.

Modern observance

Modern pagans celebrate Yule at the winter solstice as one of eight sabbats of the Wheel of the Year, marking the rebirth of the sun.
