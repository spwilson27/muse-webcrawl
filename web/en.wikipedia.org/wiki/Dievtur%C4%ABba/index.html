Dievturība

Dievturība is a Latvian neopagan religious movement that seeks to revive the pre-Christian religion of the Latvian people, drawing on folklore, folk songs (dainas), and traditional customs. Its adherents are called Dievturi.

History

The movement was founded in the 1920s, principally by the writer and artist Ernests Brastiņš, in the newly independent Latvian state. Suppressed under Soviet occupation, it survived among the Latvian diaspora and revived after Latvia regained independence in 1991; it has since gained official recognition among Latvia's traditional religions.

Beliefs and practice

Dievturība centres on Dievs, the sky god, alongside goddesses such as Māra and Laima, and on a tripartite cosmos reflected in Latvian ornament and song. Seasonal festivals — including Jāņi (midsummer) and solstice and equinox celebrations — structure its ritual year.
