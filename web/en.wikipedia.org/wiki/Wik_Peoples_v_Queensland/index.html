Wik Peoples v Queensland

Wik Peoples v Queensland (1996) 187 CLR 1, commonly called the Wik decision, is a decision of the High Court of Australia delivered on 23 December 1996. The Court held, by a 4–3 majority, that the grant of a pastoral lease under Queensland law did not necessarily extinguish native title — pastoral leases and native title rights could coexist, with native title yielding to the extent of any inconsistency.

Background

The Wik and Thayorre peoples claimed native title over lands on Cape York Peninsula covered by two pastoral leases (the Holroyd and Mitchellton leases) and sought declarations after the Native Title Act 1993 (Cth), passed following Mabo v Queensland (No 2) (1992), recognised native title at common law. The question was whether pastoral leases — a peculiarly Australian tenure granting grazing rights but limited exclusivity — conferred exclusive possession that necessarily extinguished native title.

Decision and aftermath

The majority (Toohey, Gaudron, Gummow and Kirby JJ) held that the statutory pastoral leases in question did not grant exclusive possession, so native title was not automatically extinguished; where rights conflicted, the leaseholder's rights prevailed. The decision provoked intense political controversy: pastoralists and miners feared uncertainty, Deputy Prime Minister Tim Fischer warned of claims in suburban backyards, and the Howard government responded with its "Wik amendments" — the Native Title Amendment Act 1998, based on a ten-point plan — which wound back parts of the decision and validated many grants. Wik remains a landmark in Australian native title law.
