Solar gain

Solar gain (also known as solar heat gain or passive solar gain) is the increase in thermal energy of a space, object or structure as it absorbs incident solar radiation. The amount of solar gain a space experiences is a function of the total incident solar irradiance and of the ability of any intervening material to transmit or resist the radiation.

Windows and glazing

Objects struck by sunlight absorb its visible and short-wave infrared components, increase in temperature, and then re-radiate heat at longer infrared wavelengths. Where materials such as glass are transparent to short-wave radiation but opaque to long-wave radiation, solar energy becomes trapped — the greenhouse effect. The solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC) quantifies the fraction of incident solar radiation admitted through a window, both transmitted and absorbed-and-released inward.

Design

In cool climates, passive solar design deliberately maximizes solar gain through equator-facing glazing, thermal mass, and insulation, while in hot climates shading, low-SHGC glazing, and overhangs minimize it to avoid overheating. Building energy codes commonly specify maximum SHGC values by climate zone.

Thermal mass

Mass materials such as concrete floors struck by direct sun store the gained heat and release it after sunset, flattening diurnal temperature swings — a principle used in Trombe walls and direct-gain passive houses.
