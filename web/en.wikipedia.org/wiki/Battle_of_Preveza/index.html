The Battle of Preveza was a naval battle that took place on 28 September 1538 near Preveza in the Ionian Sea off western Greece, between the Ottoman fleet under Hayreddin Barbarossa and a Christian Holy League fleet assembled by Pope Paul III. The Ottoman victory established Turkish naval supremacy in the Mediterranean for a generation.

Background

In 1537 Barbarossa, the Ottoman Kapudan Pasha, had ravaged the Venetian possessions in the Aegean and Ionian seas. In response the Pope organised a Holy League of Venice, Spain, the Papal States, Genoa, Malta and other powers. The allied fleet — on paper larger than the Ottoman, with over 300 vessels including big sailing galleons — assembled at Corfu under the nominal command of the Genoese admiral Andrea Doria in Habsburg service.

The battle

The allies found Barbarossa's roughly 140 galleys and galliots drawn up off Preveza. Failing wind left the great allied sailing ships becalmed and separated, while quarrels and mutual suspicion divided the allied commanders. Barbarossa exploited his central position to attack the allied wings in turn; the Venetian and papal squadrons fought hard but were overwhelmed, and Doria's galleys never fully engaged. By nightfall the League had lost dozens of ships sunk or captured and several thousand men, while Ottoman losses were comparatively modest.

Aftermath

The defeat broke the Holy League, and in 1540 Venice made a separate peace ceding its remaining Aegean possessions and paying an indemnity. Preveza confirmed Ottoman dominance of the eastern Mediterranean, which endured until the Battle of Lepanto in 1571.
