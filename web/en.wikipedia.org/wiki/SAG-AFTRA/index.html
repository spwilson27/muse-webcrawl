SAG-AFTRA (officially the Screen Actors Guild – American Federation of Television and Radio Artists) is an American labour union representing approximately 160,000 film and television actors, journalists, recording artists, dancers, disc jockeys, news writers and editors, program hosts, puppeteers, stunt performers, voiceover artists and other media professionals worldwide.

History

The union was formed on 30 March 2012 by the merger of the Screen Actors Guild (founded 1933) and the American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (founded 1937 as AFRA). Members of both unions voted to merge in order to strengthen their collective bargaining position against the major studios, particularly over residuals in new media.

2023 strike

On 14 July 2023 SAG-AFTRA went on strike against the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers — its first film and television strike since 1980 — joining the Writers Guild of America, which had struck in May. The disputes centred on streaming residuals, pay, and protections against the use of artificial intelligence to replicate performers' likenesses. The strike lasted 118 days, ending with a tentative agreement on 8 November 2023 that members ratified the following month.

Functions

SAG-AFTRA negotiates collective bargaining agreements establishing minimum pay (scale), working conditions, residual payments and health and pension contributions. Its contracts include the Codified Basic Agreement for film and television, the Commercials Contract, and interactive media (video game) agreements. The union also organises the Screen Actors Guild Awards, founded in 1995.
