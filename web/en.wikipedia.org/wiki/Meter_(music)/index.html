Meter (music)

In music, meter (American spelling; metre in British usage) is the recurring pattern of stresses or accents that provide the pulse or beat of music. It is specified by a time signature at the beginning of a score or passage and is constructed from beats grouped into regular measures (bars). Meter organises rhythm into hierarchical cycles of strong and weak beats.

Classification

Meters are classified along two dimensions:

  • By number of beats per measure: duple (2), triple (3), quadruple (4), and higher or mixed groupings.
  • By subdivision of the beat: simple meters divide each beat into two parts (e.g. 2/4, 3/4, 4/4), while compound meters divide it into three (e.g. 6/8, 9/8, 12/8).

Meters that do not divide evenly or that use irregular groupings are called complex, asymmetric, or odd meters — for example 5/4 (as in "Take Five") or the additive rhythms of Balkan folk music. Some 20th-century music abandons recurring meter altogether (ametric or free rhythm), and some traditions, such as Indian classical music, organise time through elaborate tala cycles rather than measures.

Meter and perception

Listeners infer meter from patterns of accent — dynamic, durational, and harmonic — and entrain to it, tapping or moving along with the beat. Syncopation and hemiola derive their effect precisely from playing against an established meter. Hypermeter extends the concept to the grouping of measures into larger regular units resembling measures at a slower level.
