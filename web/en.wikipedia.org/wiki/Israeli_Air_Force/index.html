The Israeli Air Force (IAF; Hebrew: זרוע האוויר והחלל, Zroa HaAvir VeHaHalal, "Air and Space Arm") is the air and space force of the Israel Defense Forces. Founded on 28 May 1948, shortly after Israel's declaration of independence, it is widely considered one of the most capable air forces in the world.

History

The IAF grew from the Sherut Avir (air service) of the Haganah, flying a miscellany of civilian aircraft and war-surplus machines — including Czech-built Avia S-199 fighters — during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. It expanded rapidly in the 1950s with French-built Mystère and Mirage jets, achieving a celebrated success at the opening of the Six-Day War in 1967, when Operation Focus destroyed most of the Egyptian Air Force on the ground. In the 1973 Yom Kippur War it suffered heavy losses to surface-to-air missiles before adapting its tactics.

Notable operations include the 1981 strike on Iraq's Osirak reactor (Operation Opera), the 1985 raid on the PLO headquarters in Tunis (Operation Wooden Leg), and extensive air and missile-defence operations in subsequent conflicts. The IAF operates the Arrow and David's Sling missile-defence systems jointly with the Israel Missile Defense Organization.

Aircraft

The force's combat fleet centres on American aircraft: F-15 and F-16 variants and the F-35I "Adir" stealth fighter, which the IAF was the first to declare operationally used in combat in 2018. It also flies C-130 and C-130J transports, AH-64 Apache helicopters, and Israeli-built UAVs. Pilot selection and the three-year flight course are famously rigorous, with very small graduating classes.
