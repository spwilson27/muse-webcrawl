Felix Salten (born Siegmund Salzmann; 6 September 1869 – 8 October 1945) was an Austrian author and literary critic, best known as the author of Bambi, a Life in the Woods (1923), the novel that inspired Walt Disney's animated film Bambi (1942).

Life

Salzmann was born in Pest, Hungary, into a Jewish family and moved with them to Vienna as an infant. He began writing at a young age, adopting the pen name Felix Salten, and became a leading feuilletonist and theatre critic associated with the "Young Vienna" circle around Arthur Schnitzler and Hugo von Hofmannsthal. He wrote novels, plays, essays, travel books and libretti, and was a founder of literary cabaret in Vienna.

An early supporter of Theodor Herzl, Salten visited Palestine in 1924 and wrote about the journey. After the Anschluss of 1938 he emigrated to Switzerland, where he died in Zürich in 1945. His books were banned in Nazi Germany; the hunting rights he held, and his love of nature, informed the world of Bambi.

Bambi

Bambi, a Life in the Woods (Bambi. Eine Lebensgeschichte aus dem Walde) appeared in 1923 and was translated into English in 1928 by Whittaker Chambers. A sequel, Bambi's Children, followed in 1939. Salten sold the film rights for a nominal sum, and Disney's adaptation softened the novel's darker, more realistic treatment of hunting and death. Salten also wrote other animal stories, including The Hound of Florence and the squirrel tale Perri, which Disney also adapted.
