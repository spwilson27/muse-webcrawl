Puttanesca (Italian: [puttaˈneska]; in Italian sugo alla puttanesca) is a pasta dish invented in Naples in the mid-20th century and made from tomatoes, black olives, capers, anchovy, garlic, and dried red chilli peppers. The sauce alone is called sugo alla puttanesca in Italian.

Etymology and legends

The name derives from the Italian word puttana, meaning "prostitute." Several colourful legends surround its origin: that it was a quick dish prepared by Neapolitan brothel cooks between clients; that its pungent aroma lured customers; or, more prosaically, that it was improvised one night by Sandro Petti, co-owner of the restaurant Rancio Fellone on Ischia, who in 1950 cooked a dish from whatever remained in the pantry—tomatoes, olives and capers—for late-arriving friends, reportedly calling it una puttanata qualsiasi ("any whore's-style thing").

Preparation

Garlic and anchovies are fried in olive oil with dried chilli; tomatoes are added and cooked down with black olives (often Gaeta olives) and capers. The sauce is typically served with spaghetti, though it also pairs with penne, bucatini, or linguine. The Lazio variant omits anchovies and includes garlic more prominently; the Neapolitan version traditionally excludes garlic. The dish is prized for its intense, salty, briny flavour and its convenience, being made almost entirely from long-keeping pantry staples.
