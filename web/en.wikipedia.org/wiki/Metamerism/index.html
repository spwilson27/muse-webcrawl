Metamerism

Two metameric samples matching under daylight but differing under incandescent light

Metamerism is a phenomenon in color perception in which two samples that have different spectral power distributions appear to match in color under a given illuminant and observer. Such samples are called metamers, and the match is a metameric match. Because the human visual system reduces the spectrum of light entering the eye to three signals (from the L, M, and S cone cells), physically different spectra can produce identical cone responses.

Types and causes

Illuminant metamerism occurs when two samples match under one light source but not another. Observer metamerism arises from differences in color vision between observers, and geometric metamerism occurs when samples match at one viewing angle but not another, as with pearlescent or interference pigments. Metamerism is quantified by a metamerism index and is a practical concern in printing, textiles, paints, and dentistry, where a repair or reproduction must match under varied lighting.

Related concepts

Metamerism must be distinguished from metamerism in chemistry (an obsolete term related to isomerism) and from metamerism in biology, where it refers to body segmentation. In colorimetry, metameric matches underlie color reproduction technologies: displays and printers reproduce colors not by matching spectra but by producing metameric stimuli that the eye perceives as equal.
