Kalakshetra, now formally known as the Kalakshetra Foundation, is an arts and cultural academy located in Thiruvanmiyur, Chennai, in the Indian state of Tamil Nadu. It was founded in 1936 by the dancer and theosophist Rukmini Devi Arundale and her husband George Arundale. The institution is devoted to the preservation and teaching of Indian classical arts, above all the dance form Bharatanatyam and Gandharva Veda music, in the traditional gurukulam spirit of learning close to nature and one's teacher.

History

Rukmini Devi, who had been trained in ballet before turning to the temple dance tradition then known as sadir, gave celebrated performances in the early 1930s and resolved to create a centre where the art could be taught with rigour and dignity. Kalakshetra began on the grounds of the Theosophical Society at Adyar and later moved to its own roughly 100-acre campus beside the sea at Thiruvanmiyur. In 1993 the Government of India declared Kalakshetra an institution of national importance by an Act of Parliament, and it became an autonomous body under the Ministry of Culture.

Teaching and activities

The foundation runs a college of fine arts offering diplomas in Bharatanatyam, Carnatic music, and visual arts, along with schools, a craft education and research centre known for its Kalamkari and weaving units, and a publishing division. Its annual dance-drama productions, choreographed in Rukmini Devi's distinctive style, are regarded as landmarks of twentieth-century Indian theatre.
