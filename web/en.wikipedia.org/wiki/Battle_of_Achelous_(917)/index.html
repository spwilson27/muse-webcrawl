Battle of Achelous (917)

The Battle of Achelous (Bulgarian: Битка при Ахелой), also known as the Battle of Anchialus, fought on 20 August 917 on the Achelous River near the Bulgarian Black Sea coast, was one of the most disastrous defeats in Byzantine history. Tsar Simeon I of Bulgaria annihilated a large Byzantine army, securing Bulgarian dominance in the Balkans.

LocationAchelous River, near Pomorie, Bulgaria
ResultBulgarian victory
BelligerentsFirst Bulgarian Empire vs Byzantine Empire

Background and battle

After Simeon assumed the imperial title and married his ambitions to taking Constantinople, the Byzantine regency for Constantine VII launched a grand expedition under Leo Phokas, coordinated with a Pecheneg crossing of the Danube that failed when the Byzantine fleet's admiral Romanos Lekapenos quarrelled with the land command. Simeon, keeping a reserve hidden behind hills, let the Byzantines gain initial success, then counterattacked. A rumour that Phokas had fallen panicked the Byzantine ranks; the rout became a massacre, with chroniclers claiming bones littered the field decades later.

Aftermath

Bulgarian forces followed up with victory at Katasyrtai and marched on Constantinople, but Simeon never took the city before his death in 927. The battle marked the zenith of the First Bulgarian Empire and led, under his son Peter I, to Byzantine recognition of the Bulgarian imperial title and patriarchate. See also Simeon I.
