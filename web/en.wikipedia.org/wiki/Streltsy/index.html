Streltsy

The Streltsy (Russian: стрельцы, "shooters"; singular strelets) were the units of Russian guardsmen armed with firearms and bardiches, established by Ivan the Terrible in the mid-16th century and forming Russia's first standing army. They served as infantry, Moscow garrison troops, palace guards, and at times as police and fire brigades.

Streltsy with muskets rested on their bardiches.

History

Ivan IV created the first streltsy units around 1550, organising about 3,000 picked troops in Moscow. The corps expanded greatly during the 17th century, reaching over 50,000 men, many settled in special suburbs (slobodas) with families, hereditary service, tax privileges, and the right to trade and practise crafts — which increasingly distracted them from military readiness.

Uprisings and abolition

The streltsy became a politically powerful and conservative force, intervening in succession disputes. Their major uprisings were the Moscow uprising of 1682 (the Khovanshchina), which made them kingmakers during Peter the Great's childhood, and the Streltsy uprising of 1698, launched while Peter was abroad. Peter crushed the 1698 revolt with mass executions and torture, disbanded the Moscow regiments, and progressively abolished the corps as he built a Western-style regular army; provincial streltsy units lingered into the early 18th century.

The scene of the executions inspired Ilya Repin's famous painting The Morning of the Streltsy Execution and Mussorgsky's opera Khovanshchina.
