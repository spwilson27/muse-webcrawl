International Workers' Day
Also calledMay Day; Labour Day
Date1 May
Observed byWorkers, trade unions, socialist and labour movements worldwide
SignificanceCommemoration of the labour movement and the Haymarket affair

International Workers' Day, also known as Labour Day in some countries and often referred to as May Day, is a celebration of labourers and the working classes promoted by the international labour movement and held every year on 1 May, or the first Monday in May. The day commemorates the Haymarket affair in Chicago of 1886 and the struggle for the eight-hour working day.

Origins

In 1889 the founding congress of the Second International in Paris called for international demonstrations on 1 May 1890 to demand the eight-hour day, choosing the date in honour of the American workers' movement, whose general strike for the eight-hour day began on 1 May 1886 and culminated in the Haymarket affair of 4 May 1886, in which a bomb was thrown at police in Chicago and several anarchist labour organisers were later executed.

Observance

May Day is a public holiday in many countries, marked by marches, rallies, and demonstrations organised by trade unions and left-wing parties. In the Soviet Union and other socialist states it became a major official holiday with large parades in Moscow's Red Square. In the United States and Canada, Labour Day is instead observed on the first Monday of September, a distinction dating to the 1890s, and 1 May is not a federal holiday.

Relationship to May Day traditions

The date coincides with older European spring festivals of May Day, celebrated with maypoles, flowers, and dancing long before the labour holiday. In some countries the two traditions have merged, with labour marches sharing the day with spring festivities.
