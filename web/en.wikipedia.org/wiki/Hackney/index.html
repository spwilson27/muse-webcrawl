Hackney

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mare Street, in the historic centre of Hackney.

Hackney is a district in East London, England, forming the core of the modern London Borough of Hackney. It lies about 4 miles (6.4 km) northeast of Charing Cross and is bounded by the River Lea to the east. Once a rural retreat of villages and market gardens known for its wealthy residents, Hackney became a densely populated, working-class industrial suburb in the Victorian era, and in the twenty-first century has undergone rapid gentrification.

History

The name is first recorded around 1198 as Hakeneia, probably from Old English for "Haka's island" — raised ground in the marshes of the Lea. In Tudor times Hackney was fashionable: courtiers and merchants built large houses there, and the first recorded English use of "Hackney" for hire horses — kept in the pastures around the village — gave rise to the words hackney carriage and ultimately the adjective "hackneyed".

Railways in the nineteenth century turned Hackney into an industrial suburb of furniture-making, clothing, and engineering, with dense terraced housing. Bombing in the Second World War and post-war council housing reshaped the area. From the 1990s, artists and young professionals moved in, and Hackney — especially around London Fields, Broadway Market, and Hackney Wick — became associated with London's creative industries and nightlife, alongside continuing poverty in parts of the borough.

Geography and landmarks

Districts within Hackney include Hackney Central, Dalston, Stoke Newington, Shoreditch, Hoxton, Homerton, and Hackney Wick. Landmarks include Sutton House (a Tudor house maintained by the National Trust), St Augustine's Tower, the Hackney Empire theatre, Victoria Park on its eastern edge, and the marshes and Olympic Park to the east in the Lea valley. The 2012 Summer Olympics were centred just across the Lea in Stratford, with some venues and the media centre in Hackney Wick.

Transport

Hackney is served by London Overground stations including Hackney Central, Hackney Downs, Dalston Junction, and Homerton; the borough famously has no London Underground station in its centre, a frequently debated gap in the Tube network.
