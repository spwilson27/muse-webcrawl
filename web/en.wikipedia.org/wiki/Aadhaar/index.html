Aadhaar (Hindi for "foundation" or "support") is a 12-digit unique identity number issued by the Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI) to residents of India, based on their biometric and demographic data. It is the world's largest biometric ID system, with well over a billion enrolments, and serves as a proof of identity and address anywhere in India.

History

The UIDAI was established in 2009 under the Planning Commission, with Infosys co-founder Nandan Nilekani as its first chairperson. The first Aadhaar number was issued on 29 September 2010 to a resident of Tembhli village, Maharashtra. The Aadhaar (Targeted Delivery of Financial and Other Subsidies, Benefits and Services) Act was passed in 2016, giving the programme a statutory basis.

Uses

Aadhaar underpins India's direct benefit transfer system for welfare payments, the Jan Dhan–Aadhaar–Mobile ("JAM") trinity, and the India Stack of digital public infrastructure, including the e-KYC services used by banks and telecoms and the DigiLocker document platform. It is linked to ration cards, bank accounts, mobile numbers and the PAN tax identifier.

Controversies

Critics have raised concerns about privacy, surveillance, exclusion from welfare when biometric authentication fails, and data security. In the landmark Puttaswamy judgment (2017) the Supreme Court of India recognised privacy as a fundamental right; in 2018 a five-judge bench upheld Aadhaar's constitutionality for welfare and tax purposes while striking down mandatory linking to bank accounts and mobile phones and barring private companies from demanding it.
