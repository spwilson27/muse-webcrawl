Cahiers de doléances

EnglishLists (notebooks) of grievances
Date1789
ContextEstates-General of 1789, French Revolution

The Cahiers de doléances (French for 'notebooks of grievances') were the lists of complaints drawn up in early 1789 by the three Estates of France — clergy, nobility, and commoners — to be presented to the Estates-General convened by King Louis XVI at Versailles.

Tens of thousands of parish and guild cahiers were composed in local assemblies, then summarised into general cahiers for each bailliage; together they form an extraordinary record of French grievances and hopes on the eve of the Revolution.

Contents

Common demands included regular meetings of the Estates-General, voting by head rather than by order, equality of taxation, abolition of feudal dues and venal offices, freedom of the press, and reform of courts and the militia. Many parish cahiers also complained about taxes, tithes, hunting rights, and seigneurial monopolies on mills and ovens.

Drafting

Elections and drafting took place in winter and spring 1789 amid economic crisis. Model cahiers circulated by reformers influenced some texts, but most reflect local drafting and debate, including a few cahiers written by women, such as the Pétition des femmes du Tiers-État.

Legacy

Historians mine the cahiers for the political culture of 1789; their mixture of loyalty to the king and sweeping reform demands illustrates both the depth of discontent and the initially moderate character of the Revolution.
