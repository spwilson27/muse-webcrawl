Title 47 CFR Part 15

Part 15 of Title 47 of the United States Code of Federal Regulations, issued by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), regulates unlicensed radio-frequency devices. It permits low-power transmitters and incidental radiators to operate without an individual licence, provided they meet strict emission limits. Technologies governed by Part 15 include Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cordless phones, garage-door openers and microwave ovens.

Operating conditions

Section 15.5 states the two famous conditions printed on the labels of countless consumer devices:

  1. The device may not cause harmful interference.
  2. The device must accept any interference received, including interference that may cause undesired operation.

Intentional radiators must generally be authorized (certified) before marketing, and users have no regulatory protection from interference by licensed services in the same bands. The industrial, scientific and medical (ISM) bands around 900 MHz, 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz host most Part 15 consumer equipment.

International parallels

Similar unlicensed regimes exist internationally, such as the European Union's rules for short-range devices and Industry Canada's licence-exempt standards, though power limits and channels differ by jurisdiction.
