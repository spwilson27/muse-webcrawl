Durban

Durban (Zulu: eThekwini, meaning "at the bay/harbour") is the third most populous city in South Africa and the largest city in the province of KwaZulu-Natal. It forms the seat of the eThekwini Metropolitan Municipality and is famous for the busiest port in South Africa, its warm subtropical climate and extensive beaches along the Indian Ocean.

History

Evidence of San hunter-gatherers and later Nguni-speaking farmers long predates European settlement. Vasco da Gama sighted the coast on Christmas Day 1497, naming it Natal. British traders founded Port Natal in 1824; in 1835 the settlement was renamed in honour of Sir Benjamin D'Urban, governor of the Cape Colony. Under British rule, indentured Indian labourers arrived from 1860 to work the sugar-cane fields, founding one of the largest Indian communities outside India—Mahatma Gandhi began his activism while practising law in Durban and nearby Pietermaritzburg.

Climate and attractions

Durban has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild winters, making it a year-round holiday destination. Landmarks include the Golden Mile beachfront, Moses Mabhida Stadium (built for the 2010 FIFA World Cup), uShaka Marine World, and the Victoria Street Market. The July Handicap, South Africa's premier horse race, is held at Greyville Racecourse.

Economy

The Port of Durban handles the majority of South Africa's container traffic. Tourism, manufacturing (including Toyota's assembly plant), sugar and the petrochemical industry are major employers.
