Bill Gates

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Bill Gates, portrait photograph, smiling, wearing glasses]

William Henry Gates III (born 1955) is an American businessman and philanthropist best known for co-founding Microsoft with Paul Allen in 1975 and leading it to dominate personal computer software with MS-DOS and Windows.

Born in Seattle, Gates programmed from his teens at Lakeside School, left Harvard in 1975 to start Microsoft ('Micro-Soft'), and licensed — famously not selling outright — the operating system that IBM's PC adopted in 1981. He served as CEO until 2000 and remained chairman until 2014.

Microsoft era

Gates's strategy of licensing software per-PC while retaining rights to sell to all manufacturers made Microsoft the industry's centre of gravity. The 1990s bundled-browser strategy led to the US antitrust trial (1998–2001), in which Microsoft was found to have maintained an illegal monopoly; a breakup order was overturned on appeal and a settlement imposed.

He was the world's richest person for many years (first topping Forbes's list in the 1990s) and remains among the wealthiest despite giving away tens of billions.

Philanthropy

With his then-wife Melinda he founded the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation in 2000, the world's largest private charitable foundation, focused on global health (malaria, vaccines, polio eradication), poverty, and education. The couple divorced in 2021; in 2024 Melinda departed the foundation, which was renamed the Gates Foundation. Gates co-founded The Giving Pledge (2010) with Warren Buffett, urging billionaires to give away most of their wealth.
