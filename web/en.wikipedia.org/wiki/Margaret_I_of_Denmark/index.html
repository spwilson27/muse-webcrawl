Margaret I (Danish: Margrete Valdemarsdatter; c. March 1353 – 28 October 1412) was the youngest daughter of King Valdemar IV of Denmark and, through her political skill, became the effective ruler of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden. She is best known as the founder of the Kalmar Union, which united the three Scandinavian kingdoms under a single monarch for more than a century. Although she was formally queen consort of Norway through her marriage to Haakon VI, she ruled the Scandinavian kingdoms in her own right as regent and was recognised as sovereign by the councils of the three realms.

Early life and marriage

Margaret was born at Søborg Castle in Zealand, the youngest child of Valdemar IV. In 1363, at about the age of ten, she was married to Haakon VI, King of Norway and co-king of Sweden, a union intended to strengthen ties between the Scandinavian kingdoms. She was educated at the Norwegian court, where she was tutored by Märta Ulfsdotter, daughter of Saint Bridget of Sweden. Her only son, Olaf, was born in 1370.

Regency of Denmark and Norway

On the death of her father in 1375, Margaret secured the election of her five-year-old son Olaf as King of Denmark, with herself as regent. When Haakon VI died in 1380, Olaf also inherited Norway, and Margaret ruled both kingdoms as regent. She worked to recover territories lost to the Hanseatic League and the German princes, redeeming pledged crown lands and strengthening royal authority. When Olaf died suddenly in 1387, Margaret was recognised by the Danish council as regent "in full power" and the following year was accepted as ruler of Norway as well.

Sweden and the Kalmar Union

Dissatisfied Swedish nobles rose against the unpopular German-born King Albert of Mecklenburg and offered the Swedish crown to Margaret. Her forces defeated and captured Albert at the Battle of Åsle in 1389, making her ruler of Sweden too. In 1397 she convened a meeting at Kalmar in Sweden, where her great-nephew Eric of Pomerania was crowned king of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden, formalising the Kalmar Union. Margaret nevertheless remained the effective ruler of all three kingdoms until her death.

Death and legacy

Margaret died suddenly on 28 October 1412 aboard her ship in Flensburg harbour, possibly of plague, while campaigning in Schleswig. She was buried first at Sorø Abbey and later transferred to Roskilde Cathedral. She is remembered as one of the most capable rulers of late medieval Europe, praised for her diplomacy, fiscal prudence, and the recovery of crown authority in Scandinavia.
