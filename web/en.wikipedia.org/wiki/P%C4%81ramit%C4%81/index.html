Pāramitā

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pāramitā (Sanskrit: पारमिता; Pāli: pāramī) is a Sanskrit term often translated as "perfection" and used in Buddhism to describe the virtues cultivated by a bodhisattva on the path to enlightenment. In Theravada tradition, ten pāramī are listed; in Mahayana, the six perfections are central, sometimes expanded to ten.

The six perfections

The Mahayana list comprises: dāna (generosity), śīla (ethics/morality), kṣānti (patience), vīrya (energy/effort), dhyāna (meditative concentration) and prajñā (wisdom). Later lists add upāya (skilful means), praṇidhāna (aspiration/vow), bala (spiritual power) and jñāna (knowledge).

Theravada pāramī

The Theravada ten are generosity, morality, renunciation, wisdom, energy, patience, truthfulness, determination, loving-kindness and equanimity. The Buddhavaṃsa and Jātaka tales describe the Buddha perfecting these virtues over many past lives.
