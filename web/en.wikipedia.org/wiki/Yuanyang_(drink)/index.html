Yuanyang (drink)

Yuanyang (Chinese: 鴛鴦; Cantonese Yale: yūn yēung), also called coffee with tea, is a popular beverage in Hong Kong made by mixing coffee with Hong Kong-style milk tea (itself black tea with evaporated or condensed milk). It is served hot or cold in the city's cha chaan teng diners and dai pai dong food stalls.

Name and origin

The name refers to the Mandarin duck (yuanyang), a traditional symbol of conjugal love in Chinese culture, because the male and female birds look strikingly different from each other — just as coffee and tea are an unlikely pair that are nonetheless combined in one cup. The drink is often said to have been created at a dai pai dong in Central, Hong Kong, in the 1950s. A common recipe uses roughly three parts milk tea to seven parts coffee, sweetened to taste.

Variations

A children's version without coffee or caffeine, sometimes called "yuanyang" for kids, mixes Horlicks and Ovaltine, two malted milk drinks popular in Hong Kong. Yuanyang-flavoured products, from ice cream to baked goods, are also sold, and the drink features on cafe menus across the Chinese diaspora and in Malaysia and Singapore, where similar coffee-tea mixtures are known as kopi cham.
