Cultural Property (Japan)

Himeji Castle, designated a National Treasure and a World Heritage Site.

A Cultural Property (Japanese: 文化財, bunkazai) is an item of particular historical, artistic or academic value to Japan, officially designated and protected under the Law for the Protection of Cultural Properties, administered by the Agency for Cultural Affairs.

Categories

Tangible Cultural Properties include buildings, paintings, sculpture, crafts, calligraphy, classical books, historical documents and archaeological materials. Exceptional examples are designated Important Cultural Properties, and the most precious among them are elevated to the status of National Treasures — including Himeji Castle, the shrines and temples of Nikkō, and masterpieces held in museums such as the Tokyo National Museum.

The system also covers intangible heritage (traditional performing arts and craft techniques, whose master practitioners are popularly called Living National Treasures), folk cultural properties, monuments (historic sites, places of scenic beauty and natural monuments), cultural landscapes, groups of traditional buildings, and buried cultural properties.

History

Japan's protection of antiquities began in the Meiji era with laws of 1871, 1897 and 1919. The modern system dates from the 1950 Law for the Protection of Cultural Properties, enacted after a 1949 fire damaged the celebrated wall paintings of Hōryū-ji. Thousands of items have since been designated at national, prefectural and municipal levels.
