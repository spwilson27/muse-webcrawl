Sergei Lvovich Sobolev (Russian: Серге́й Льво́вич Со́болев; 6 October 1908 – 3 January 1989) was a Soviet mathematician working in mathematical analysis and partial differential equations. He is one of the founders of the theory of distributions and introduced the function spaces that now bear his name, the Sobolev spaces, which are fundamental to modern analysis and the finite element method.

Life and career

Sobolev was born in Saint Petersburg and studied at Leningrad State University, graduating in 1929 under Nikolai Günther. He worked at the Steklov Institute of Mathematics in Leningrad and later in Moscow, and participated in the Soviet atomic programme at the Institute of Atomic Energy under Igor Kurchatov, applying mathematical methods to physics and computation.

In 1957 he founded the Institute of Mathematics in Novosibirsk as part of the new Siberian Branch of the Academy of Sciences at Akademgorodok, serving as its director until 1983 and helping to build Novosibirsk into a major scientific centre. He was a full member of the USSR Academy of Sciences and received numerous honours, including Hero of Socialist Labor and the Stalin and State Prizes.

Mathematical work

In the mid-1930s Sobolev introduced the notion of generalized functions—weak solutions and generalized derivatives—independently of and slightly before Laurent Schwartz's theory of distributions. His celebrated embedding theorems describe when a Sobolev space of functions with generalized derivatives in Lp embeds into spaces of continuous functions, results that remain foundational in the theory of partial differential equations. The Sobolev conjugate and the critical exponent arising in these theorems are named for him.

Sobolev also contributed to mathematical physics, elasticity theory, and numerical mathematics, including the theory of cubature formulas.
