Digital distraction

Digital distraction is the diverting of attention away from a primary task by digital devices and media — smartphones, notifications, social media, email and the web. It is studied in psychology, education and human–computer interaction for its effects on learning, work performance and safety.

Mechanisms

Notifications and variable-reward design capture attention exogenously; task-switching imposes cognitive costs, and even the mere presence of a phone has been reported to reduce available cognitive capacity. Students who multitask with media while studying generally retain less and score lower.

Contexts

Digital distraction is a factor in distracted driving, classroom disengagement and reduced workplace productivity. Heavy media multitasking correlates with poorer performance on some attention tasks, though causality is debated.

Mitigation

Strategies include notification management, focus modes and app limits built into operating systems, time-blocking, device-free environments, and "digital wellbeing" or digital-detox practices.
