The Seattle metropolitan area is a metropolitan region in the U.S. state of Washington surrounding the city of Seattle on Puget Sound. The U.S. Office of Management and Budget defines the Seattle–Tacoma–Bellevue Metropolitan Statistical Area as comprising King, Snohomish, and Pierce counties, with a population of about 4.0 million in the 2020 census, making it the 15th-largest metropolitan area in the United States. The broader combined statistical area includes the Bremerton, Olympia, and Mount Vernon areas and exceeds 4.9 million people.

Geography

The region stretches along the eastern shore of Puget Sound between the Olympic Mountains to the west and the Cascade Range to the east, centered on Elliott Bay. Principal cities besides Seattle include Tacoma to the south and Bellevue and Redmond on the Eastside across Lake Washington, as well as Everett to the north.

Economy

The metropolitan economy is anchored by aerospace (Boeing), technology (Microsoft, Amazon, and other software and cloud firms), maritime trade through the ports of Seattle and Tacoma, biotechnology, and coffee retailing. Major employers include Boeing, Microsoft, Amazon, the University of Washington, and Joint Base Lewis–McChord near Tacoma.

Transportation

Interstates 5, 90, and 405 cross the region; Seattle–Tacoma International Airport serves as the main commercial airport. Sound Transit operates Link light rail, Sounder commuter rail, and express buses, while Washington State Ferries connects the region to the Olympic Peninsula and islands.
