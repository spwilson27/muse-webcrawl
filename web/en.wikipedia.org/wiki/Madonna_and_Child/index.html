Madonna and Child

The Madonna and Child is one of the most common subjects in Christian art, depicting the Virgin Mary with the infant Jesus Christ. Images of Mary holding or enthroned with her child appear from early Christian art onward, and the subject dominated religious painting in the Byzantine world, the medieval West, and the Renaissance.

Iconography

Byzantine icon types included the Hodegetria ("She who shows the Way"), in which Mary gestures toward the child, and the Eleusa, or Virgin of Tenderness, in which mother and child press their cheeks together. In the Western Middle Ages the enthroned Madonna, often flanked by saints and angels, was the standard altarpiece format. Renaissance artists such as Giovanni Bellini, Raphael, and Leonardo da Vinci developed more naturalistic, intimate treatments, sometimes placing the figures in a landscape or domestic interior.

Variations

Related subjects include the Madonna and Child with saints, the nursing Madonna (Madonna del Latte), the Madonna of Humility seated low on a cushion, and the Madonna surrounded by angels. The theme remained central in Catholic art through the Baroque era and continues in modern religious art.
