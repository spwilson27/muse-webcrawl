Jules Mazarin (born Giulio Raimondo Mazzarino; 14 July 1602 – 9 March 1661), styled Cardinal Mazarin, was an Italian-born Catholic cardinal, diplomat and statesman who served as chief minister to the kings of France from 1642 until his death. He succeeded his mentor, Cardinal Richelieu, and governed France during the minority of Louis XIV, alongside the regent Anne of Austria. Mazarin concluded the Thirty Years' War on terms favourable to France and broke the power of the Spanish Habsburgs, laying the groundwork for French predominance in Europe.

Early life and diplomacy

Born in Pescina in the Abruzzi to a family in the service of the Colonna, Mazarin was educated by the Jesuits in Rome and studied law. He entered papal diplomacy and distinguished himself in negotiations over the War of the Mantuan Succession, drawing the attention of Richelieu. Entering French service, he served as papal nuncio in Paris (1634–1636), became a naturalised Frenchman in 1639 and was made a cardinal in 1641 — though he was never ordained a priest.

Chief minister

On Richelieu's death in December 1642, and Louis XIII's death five months later, Mazarin became chief minister to the regent Anne of Austria. He brought the Thirty Years' War to a close with the Peace of Westphalia (1648), which secured French gains including much of Alsace, and continued the war against Spain until the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659), which won Roussillon and Artois and arranged the marriage of Louis XIV to Maria Theresa of Spain.

The Fronde

Mazarin's fiscal expedients and foreign birth made him the target of the civil wars known as the Fronde (1648–1653), when parlementary and princely factions challenged royal authority. Twice driven into exile, he manoeuvred patiently abroad and returned triumphant; the young Louis XIV never forgot the lesson of the Fronde. Mazarin amassed one of the largest private fortunes of the century, built the Palais Mazarin in Paris, and assembled a celebrated library — the Bibliothèque Mazarine, opened to the public in 1643, is considered France's first public library.

Death and legacy

Mazarin died at Vincennes on 9 March 1661. His death marked the beginning of Louis XIV's personal rule. By his will he founded the Collège des Quatre-Nations in Paris, now home to the Institut de France and the Bibliothèque Mazarine.
