Lemon v. Kurtzman

Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602 (1971)
Court: U.S. Supreme Court
Decided: June 28, 1971
Chief Justice: Warren Burger

Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602 (1971), was a landmark United States Supreme Court case concerning the Establishment Clause of the First Amendment. The Court struck down Pennsylvania and Rhode Island statutes that provided state aid to religious schools, and in doing so articulated the three-pronged Lemon test used for decades to evaluate government action touching religion.

Background

Pennsylvania's Nonpublic Elementary and Secondary Education Act of 1968 reimbursed religious schools for teachers' salaries, textbooks and instructional materials in secular subjects; Rhode Island's Salary Supplement Act paid a supplement of up to 15 percent to teachers of secular subjects in non-public schools. Taxpayer Alton Lemon and others challenged the Pennsylvania scheme.

The Lemon test

Chief Justice Warren Burger's opinion held that a statute must satisfy three criteria to pass Establishment Clause scrutiny: (1) it must have a secular legislative purpose; (2) its principal or primary effect must be one that neither advances nor inhibits religion; and (3) it must not foster an excessive government entanglement with religion. The Court found both schemes created excessive entanglement through the surveillance and administrative relationships needed to police the secular/religious boundary.

Legacy

The Lemon test became the dominant framework in Establishment Clause jurisprudence, applied — and frequently criticised — in cases concerning school prayer, public displays and funding. Its authority waned after Zelman v. Simmons-Harris and Town of Greece v. Galloway, and in Kennedy v. Bremerton School District (2022) the Court stated that it had long ago abandoned Lemon in favour of an analysis based on historical practices and understandings.
