Antichain

In mathematics, in the area of order theory, an antichain is a subset of a partially ordered set such that any two distinct elements in the subset are incomparable. The size of the largest antichain in a partially ordered set is known as its width; by Dilworth's theorem, the width equals the minimum number of chains needed to partition the set.

Examples and results

In the Boolean lattice of subsets of an n-element set, ordered by inclusion, the family of all subsets of size ⌊n/2⌋ forms an antichain. Sperner's theorem states that this is a largest antichain in that lattice. Antichains also arise in the study of partially ordered sets through Mirsky's theorem, which relates the minimum number of antichains needed to partition a set to the length of its longest chain.
