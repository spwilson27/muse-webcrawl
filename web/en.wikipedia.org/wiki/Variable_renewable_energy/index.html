Variable renewable energy

Variable renewable energy (VRE) or intermittent renewable energy sources are renewable energy sources that are not dispatchable due to their fluctuating nature, such as wind power and solar power, as opposed to controllably dispatchable sources such as hydropower, geothermal, or biomass.

The variability of wind and solar output occurs over timescales from seconds to seasons. Integrating high shares of VRE into electricity grids requires flexibility from energy storage, demand response, interconnection between regions, and dispatchable generation. Although once considered a major barrier, studies and operating experience show grids can accommodate large VRE shares with appropriate planning and market design.

Terminology and impacts

The term "intermittent" is sometimes criticised because output, while variable, is partly predictable using weather forecasting. System impacts include the need for operating reserves, possible curtailment when generation exceeds demand, and changes to the economics of conventional plants.
