Marvin the Martian is an extraterrestrial character from Warner Bros.' Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies cartoons. He frequently appears as a villain in cartoons, and was an antagonist of Bugs Bunny and Daffy Duck (as Duck Dodgers). He wears a Roman-style helmet and skirt — a visual reference to Mars, the Roman god of war — and speaks in a soft, nasal voice, famously deadpanning lines such as "Isn't that lovely?" and lamenting the absence of an "earth-shattering kaboom".

Marvin the Martian, commander of the Mars expeditionary force.

History

The character was created by director Chuck Jones and debuted in Haredevil Hare (1948), in which he attempted to destroy the Earth because it blocked his view of Venus. He was not given the name "Marvin" until much later; on screen he was referred to as Commander X-2, among other designations. Marvin appeared in only five classic theatrical shorts — including The Hasty Hare (1952), Duck Dodgers in the 24½th Century (1953), Hare-Way to the Stars (1958) and Mad as a Mars Hare (1963) — yet became one of the most recognisable Looney Tunes characters, partly through merchandising. He is commonly accompanied by his dog, K-9, and armed with his Illudium Q-36 Explosive Space Modulator.

Personality

Unlike the loud, blustering villains of other cartoons, Marvin is soft-spoken, polite and unflappable; his anger is quiet and childlike ("I'm not angry, I'm just terribly hurt"). His plans for destroying Earth are thwarted in every appearance, typically by Bugs Bunny.

In popular culture

Marvin's image has flown in space: NASA featured him on the Spirit rover's mission patch, and he appeared on the insignia of the Mars exploration programme. He has appeared in films including Space Jam (1996), where he refereed the basketball game, and in numerous television series, video games and advertisements.
