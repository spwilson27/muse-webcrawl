Artificial intelligence

[Image: a glowing neural-network visualisation — nodes and connections forming a brain-like web over a dark background]

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the capability of computational systems to perform tasks typically associated with human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception and language understanding. It is a field of research in Computer science and engineering that builds and studies intelligent machines and the software that enables them to perceive their environment and use learning to achieve goals.

Founded as an academic discipline in 1956 at the Dartmouth Workshop, AI has passed through cycles of optimism, funding booms and 'AI winters'. In the 2010s and 2020s, advances in machine learning — especially deep learning with large neural networks trained on vast data — transformed the field, producing systems such as large language models and image generators with wide public impact.

Approaches

Modern AI is dominated by machine learning: supervised learning from labelled examples, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning, in which an agent learns by trial and reward. Deep learning uses multi-layered artificial neural networks and underlies breakthroughs in computer vision, speech recognition, machine translation, protein-structure prediction (AlphaFold) and generative AI.

Historically, symbolic or 'good old-fashioned' AI represented knowledge with explicit rules and logic, powering expert systems in the 1980s; statistical methods gradually displaced it from the 1990s onward.

Applications and debate

AI is used in search engines, recommendation systems, virtual assistants, medical diagnosis, autonomous vehicles, finance, and scientific research. Its rapid progress has prompted debate about employment, bias, misinformation, safety and the long-term risks of increasingly capable systems, and led to new regulation such as the European Union's AI Act (2024).

Landmark moments include IBM Deep Blue's chess victory over Garry Kasparov (1997), DeepMind's AlphaGo defeating Lee Sedol (2016), and the public release of ChatGPT in November 2022, which brought generative AI to mass attention.
