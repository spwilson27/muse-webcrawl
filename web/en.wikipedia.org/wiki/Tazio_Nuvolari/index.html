Tazio Nuvolari

Tazio Giorgio Nuvolari (16 November 1892 – 11 August 1953) was an Italian racing driver. He first raced motorcycles and then concentrated on sports cars and Grand Prix motor racing. He won 24 Grands Prix, including the 1932 European Championship, and victories at the Mille Miglia, the Targa Florio, the 24 Hours of Le Mans, and the RAC Tourist Trophy.

Career

Nuvolari was born in Castel d'Ario, near Mantua. He began racing motorcycles in 1920 and earned his automobile racing license in 1924. He founded his own team, Scuderia Nuvolari, before joining Alfa Romeo's works effort under Enzo Ferrari. His most famous victory is considered to be the 1935 German Grand Prix at the Nürburgring, where he defeated the dominant German Mercedes-Benz and Auto Union teams in an outdated Alfa Romeo P3.

Driving style and legacy

Nuvolari was renowned for his spectacular four-wheel-drift cornering technique and his indomitable determination, often racing successfully while ill or injured. Ferdinand Porsche called him "the greatest driver of the past, the present, and the future." He died in Mantua in 1953. His hometown maintains the Museo Tazio Nuvolari in his honor.
