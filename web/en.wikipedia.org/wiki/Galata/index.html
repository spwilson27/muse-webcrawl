Galata is a quarter of the Beyoğlu district of Istanbul, Turkey, located at the northern shore of the Golden Horn, the inlet which separates it from the historic peninsula of old Istanbul. The Galata Bridge crosses the Golden Horn into Eminönü. Galata was a colony of the Republic of Genoa between 1273 and 1453 and is dominated by the Galata Tower, built by the Genoese in 1348 and the district's most famous landmark.

History

The area was known in Byzantine times as Sykai and Pera ("the other side"). After the Fourth Crusade and the restoration of Byzantine rule, Emperor Michael VIII granted Galata to the Genoese, who fortified their trading colony and built its walls and the Christea Turris (Tower of Christ), today's Galata Tower. Galata surrendered to Sultan Mehmed II in 1453 without a siege and retained trading privileges; in the Ottoman centuries it became the cosmopolitan quarter of European merchants, bankers and embassies, home to the Bankalar Caddesi financial district and to large Greek, Armenian, Jewish and Levantine communities.

Today

Modern Galata is a lively neighbourhood of cafés, music shops, design studios and historic passages around the tower, connected to İstiklal Avenue by the Tünel, one of the world's oldest underground railways (opened 1875).
