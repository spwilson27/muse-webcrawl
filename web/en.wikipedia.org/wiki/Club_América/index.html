Club de Fútbol América S.A. de C.V., commonly known as Club América or simply América, is a Mexican professional football club based in Mexico City. Founded on 12 October 1916, the club competes in Liga MX, the top flight of Mexican football, and has played its home matches at the Estadio Azteca since 1966.

América is widely recognised as one of the most successful and popular clubs in Mexican football. The club has won a record sixteen league titles, along with seven Copa México titles and seven Campeón de Campeones trophies. Internationally, América has won the CONCACAF Champions Cup/Champions League seven times, a record for a Mexican club.

History

The club was founded on 12 October 1916 by a group of students led by Rafael Garza Gutiérrez and Germán Núñez Cortina. The name "América" was chosen because the founding date coincided with the anniversary of Christopher Columbus's arrival in the Americas. In 1959, the club was purchased by Emilio Azcárraga Milmo, owner of the Telesistema Mexicano (later Televisa) media company, an acquisition that transformed América into one of the wealthiest clubs in the country and earned it the nickname "Millonetas" (the millionaires).

América enjoyed a golden era in the 1980s, winning five league championships during the decade. The club's fierce rivalry with Guadalajara, known as El Súper Clásico, is regarded as the biggest derby in Mexican football. Other major rivalries include the Clásico Joven against Cruz Azul and the Clásico Capitalino against Pumas UNAM.

Colours and crest

The club's traditional colours are yellow and blue, from which derive the nicknames "Cremas" (creams) and "Azulcremas" (blue-creams). The crest features a map of the Americas inside a circle with the club's initials "CA", and the eagle mascot gives the team its best-known nickname, "Las Águilas" (The Eagles).
