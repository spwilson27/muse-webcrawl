Repentance

Repentance is the activity of reviewing one's actions and feeling contrition or regret for past wrongs, accompanied by a commitment to change. It is a central concept in many religions, in which it typically involves acknowledging sin, confessing it, feeling remorse, and turning toward a different way of life; the related secular concept underlies apology and restorative justice.

In Judaism

Teshuvah ("return") is the process of repenting in Judaism, involving recognition of the wrong, remorse, confession, restitution to those harmed, and resolve not to repeat the act. Maimonides codified the laws of repentance in the Mishneh Torah, and the High Holy Days — Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur — are devoted to it.

In Christianity

The New Testament Greek term metanoia denotes a change of mind and heart. Repentance is a precondition of forgiveness in most Christian traditions; in Roman Catholicism it is expressed through the sacrament of Penance (confession, contrition, satisfaction and absolution), while Protestant traditions emphasise inward conversion and faith. Public calls to repentance figure in the ministries of John the Baptist and Jesus.

In Islam and other traditions

In Islam, tawba is sincere repentance to God, requiring cessation of the sin, remorse, and resolve not to return to it, with restitution where others were wronged. Concepts of repentance and atonement also appear in Buddhism, Hinduism and other traditions, often tied to confession and moral renewal.
