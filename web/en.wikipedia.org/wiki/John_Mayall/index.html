John Brumwell Mayall (29 November 1933 – 22 July 2024) was an English blues and rock musician, songwriter and bandleader. As founder of John Mayall & the Bluesbreakers, he was a central figure of the British blues boom of the 1960s, and his band served as a finishing school for many of the most famous musicians in rock: its alumni include Eric Clapton, Peter Green, Mick Fleetwood, John McVie and Mick Taylor. For his lifelong services to music Mayall was dubbed the "godfather of British blues" and continued performing into his late eighties.

Early life

Mayall was born in Macclesfield, Cheshire, the son of a semi-professional jazz guitarist and singer whose record collection introduced him to American blues and boogie-woogie pianists such as Albert Ammons and Meade Lux Lewis. He taught himself piano, harmonica and guitar, attended Manchester Junior School of Art, and served in Korea with the Royal Engineers. Working as a graphic designer, he played music part-time until, encouraged by Alexis Korner, he moved to London in 1963 and turned professional at nearly thirty.

The Bluesbreakers

Mayall's Bluesbreakers became the proving ground of British blues-rock. The landmark album Blues Breakers with Eric Clapton (1966) — the "Beano album" — helped create the model of the guitar hero and the fat Les Paul-through-Marshall tone that defined rock guitar. Subsequent line-ups produced A Hard Road (1967) with Peter Green and Crusade (1967) with the teenage Mick Taylor. Green, McVie and Fleetwood left to form Fleetwood Mac; Taylor joined the Rolling Stones.

After Bare Wires (1968) Mayall disbanded the electric line-up for a quieter, drummer-less acoustic format on Blues from Laurel Canyon (1968), written after moving to California. In 1969 The Turning Point, recorded live in New York, gave him his biggest American hit, "Room to Move."

Later career

Settling permanently in Los Angeles, Mayall recorded prolifically for decades, reviving the Bluesbreakers name in 1982 with guitarists such as Coco Montoya and Walter Trout and later Buddy Whittington. Albums continued into the 2020s, including The Sun Is Shining Down (2022). He retired from touring in 2021 after more than half a century on the road. Appointed OBE in 2005, he was selected for the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame's Musical Influence Award in 2024 and died at home in California on 22 July 2024, aged 90.
