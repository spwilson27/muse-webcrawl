Indiana Territory
Existed 1800–1816; capital at Vincennes, later Corydon.

The Indiana Territory was an organised incorporated territory of the United States that existed from 4 July 1800 to 11 December 1816, when the southeastern portion joined the Union as the state of Indiana. Its governor was appointed by the President of the United States. The territory originally encompassed much of the modern Midwest, but was progressively reduced as Michigan Territory (1805) and Illinois Territory (1809) were split off.

History

Harrison, the territory's dominant figure, negotiated a series of treaties with Native American nations acquiring millions of acres, fought at the Battle of Tippecanoe in 1811, and resigned to serve in the War of 1812; he later became the ninth President of the United States. Posey presided over the transition to statehood, although he lost the election to become the new state's first governor to Jonathan Jennings.

Jonathan Jennings served as the first governor of the State of Indiana from 1816.
