Militia

A militia is generally an army or some other fighting organisation of non-professional or part-time soldiers; citizens of a country, or subjects of a state, who may perform military service during a time of need, as opposed to a professional force of regular, full-time military personnel.

History

Militias descend from ancient levies of able-bodied men obliged to defend their communities. In England the fyrd and later the trained bands filled this role; in Switzerland, cantonal militias became the basis of the national army. Colonial American militias were central to the American Revolutionary War, and the United States Constitution and Second Amendment both refer to the militia.

Modern usage

Today the term covers state defence forces and national guards, official reserve forces, and also private paramilitary groups, some of which are described in media and law as "militia movements". Switzerland and several other states retain conscription-based militia systems.
