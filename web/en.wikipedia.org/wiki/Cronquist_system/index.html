Cronquist system

Cronquist system
AuthorArthur Cronquist (1919–1992)
Key worksThe Evolution and Classification of Flowering Plants (1968; 2nd ed. 1988); An Integrated System of Classification of Flowering Plants (1981)
ScopeFlowering plants (angiosperms)
ClassesMagnoliopsida (dicots), Liliopsida (monocots)

The Cronquist system is a taxonomic classification system of flowering plants developed by the American botanist Arthur Cronquist in a series of monographs and textbooks. For several decades it was the most widely used system of angiosperm classification in North America, and it remains influential in arrangement of herbaria and floras.

Structure

In its best-known version (1981), the system divides the flowering plants into two classes:

  • Magnoliopsida — the dicotyledons, divided into six subclasses (Magnoliidae, Hamamelidae, Caryophyllidae, Dilleniidae, Rosidae, Asteridae) comprising 64 orders and 321 families.
  • Liliopsida — the monocotyledons, divided into five subclasses (Alismatidae, Arecidae, Commelinidae, Zingiberidae, Liliidae) comprising 19 orders and 65 families.

Cronquist treated the Magnoliidae — with Magnoliales first — as the most primitive dicots, and based his groupings on the total evidence of morphology, anatomy, chemistry and other characters available before DNA sequencing.

Displacement by APG

From 1998 the phylogenetic APG system, built on molecular data, progressively replaced Cronquist's system in research and teaching: several Cronquist subclasses were shown not to be monophyletic, and the dicots themselves were split into eudicots and basal lineages. The Cronquist system nonetheless shaped the organisation of many botanical collections and remains a reference point in the history of plant taxonomy.
