Takayasu's arteritis

Takayasu's arteritis is a rare form of large-vessel vasculitis causing inflammation of the aorta and its major branches. It most commonly affects young women, particularly in Asia.

Signs and symptoms

Early symptoms include fever, fatigue, and weight loss. As arteries narrow, patients may develop absent pulses, blood-pressure differences between the arms, limb claudication, and complications such as aneurysms and stroke. It is named after Japanese ophthalmologist Mikito Takayasu, who described related eye findings in 1908. Treatment involves corticosteroids and other immunosuppressants.
