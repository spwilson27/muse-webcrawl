Taekwondo

Taekwondo (Korean: 태권도, also spelled tae kwon do or taekwon-do) is a Korean martial art and Olympic sport involving punching and kicking techniques, with an emphasis on head-height kicks, spinning kicks, and fast kicking techniques. The name is composed of three Korean words: tae (태, "foot" or "leg"), kwon (권, "fist" or "fight"), and do (도, "way" or "discipline").

Taekwondo is characterized by its emphasis on head-height kicks, spinning kicks, and fast kicking techniques. In fact, World Taekwondo sparring competitions award additional points for strikes that incorporate spinning kicks, kicks to the head, or both. Since it was adopted by the Republic of Korea Armed Forces in the 1950s, taekwondo has become widely practiced, with an estimated 200 million practitioners worldwide by the early 21st century.

History

Taekwondo began in the 1940s and 1950s, when Korean martial artists with experience in Japanese karate and the older Korean traditions of taekkyon and subak opened schools called kwans in Seoul after the Japanese occupation ended. In 1955 the kwans were pressured to unify under the name "taekwondo", officially adopted on 11 April 1955. General Choi Hong-hi is credited with coining the name and developing the International Taekwon-do Federation (ITF) style.

In 1973 the Korean government recognized the World Taekwondo Federation (now World Taekwondo, WT), headquartered at the Kukkiwon in Seoul, as the sports governing body. Taekwondo was a demonstration sport at the 1988 Seoul Olympics and became a full medal sport at the Sydney 2000 Games.

Techniques

Training includes sparring (gyeorugi), breaking (gyeokpa), forms (poomsae in WT, tul in ITF), self-defense, and basic techniques such as blocks, kicks, punches, and strikes. Ranks are marked by belts, progressing from white through coloured belts to the black belt degrees (dan).
