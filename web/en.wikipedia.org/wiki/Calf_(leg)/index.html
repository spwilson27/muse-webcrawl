The calf (Latin: sura) is the back portion of the lower leg in human anatomy. The muscles within the calf correspond to the posterior compartment of the leg. The two largest muscles within this compartment are known together as the calf muscle and attach to the heel via the Achilles tendon. Several other, smaller muscles attach to the knee, the ankle and via long tendons to the toes.

Structure

The calf is composed of the muscles of the posterior compartment of the leg. The gastrocnemius is the most superficial and prominent, with medial and lateral heads arising from the femur; beneath it lies the broad, flat soleus, arising from the tibia and fibula. Together they are sometimes called the triceps surae and converge on the Achilles tendon (tendo calcaneus), which inserts on the calcaneus. The small plantaris tendon runs between them.

Clinical significance

Calf pain and cramps are common. Strains of the gastrocnemius ("tennis leg"), Achilles tendinitis and rupture, and deep vein thrombosis are important causes of calf pain and swelling; chronic exertional compartment syndrome affects some athletes. Calf circumference is also used as a marker of muscle mass in assessing frailty and malnutrition.
