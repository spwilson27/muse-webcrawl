Charles E. Bosworth

Clifford Edmund Bosworth (29 December 1928 – 28 February 2015), who published as C. E. Bosworth, was an English historian and orientalist, specialising in Arabic and Iranian studies. He was one of the foremost modern scholars of the history of the Islamic world, and served for many years as editor of the Encyclopaedia of Islam.

Life and career

Bosworth was born in Sheffield and educated at St John's College, Cambridge, where he studied history before turning to oriental studies. He taught at the University of St Andrews and later at the University of Manchester, where he held the chair of Arabic studies. He was a visiting professor at several institutions, including the University of California, Los Angeles, and Princeton University.

He was elected a Fellow of the British Academy in 1981. His scholarship ranged across the medieval Islamic East, with particular attention to the Iranian world, Afghanistan and Central Asia.

Works

Bosworth's best-known monograph is The Ghaznavids: Their Empire in Afghanistan and Eastern Iran 994–1040 (1963), followed by The Later Ghaznavids: Splendour and Decay (1977). He also wrote The New Islamic Dynasties (1996), a standard reference on the ruling houses of the Islamic world, and numerous articles and translations. As editor-in-chief of the second edition of the Encyclopaedia of Islam, he oversaw one of the largest reference projects in Islamic studies.
