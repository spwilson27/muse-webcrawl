Tlatoani (Classical Nahuatl: tlahtoāni, plural tlahtohqueh) is the Nahuatl term for the ruler of an altepetl, a pre-Hispanic state or city-state in the Valley of Mexico and central Mexico. It is formed from the verb tlahtoa, "to speak", and literally means "one who speaks" — the speaker for the community.

Role

The tlatoani combined political, military, and religious authority. Rulers were chosen from within the royal dynasty by a council of nobles rather than by strict primogeniture. The ruler of Tenochtitlan, head of the Aztec Triple Alliance, was styled huehuetlatoani (usually rendered huey tlatoani, "great speaker" or "great ruler"), and is often translated as "emperor" in English sources.

List of rulers of Tenochtitlan

Notable tlatoque of Tenochtitlan included Acamapichtli (the first), Itzcoatl, Moctezuma I, Axayacatl, Tizoc, Ahuitzotl, Moctezuma II (ruling at the time of the Spanish arrival in 1519), Cuitlahuac, and Cuauhtemoc, the last independent tlatoani, executed by Hernán Cortés in 1525.
