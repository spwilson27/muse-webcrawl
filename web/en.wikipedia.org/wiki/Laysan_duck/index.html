Laysan duck

The Laysan duck (Anas laysanensis), also known as the Laysan teal, is a dabbling duck endemic to the Hawaiian Islands. One of the world's rarest waterfowl, it is critically endangered and was once reduced to a handful of individuals on Laysan Island in the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands.

Description

The Laysan duck is a small, dark-brown duck with a distinctive white eye-ring; males have a slightly iridescent green-purple head and a dark green bill. Both sexes are mottled brown. It is largely nocturnal and flighted but reluctant to fly, feeding mainly on brine flies and other invertebrates around Laysan's hypersaline lagoon.

Decline and recovery

Historically found across the Hawaiian archipelago, the species was devastated by introduced rabbits, which destroyed vegetation on Laysan, and by hunting and habitat loss. By 1911 only about a dozen birds remained. Rabbit eradication in the 1920s allowed a slow recovery, and in 2004–2005 ducks were translocated to Midway Atoll to establish a second population as insurance against catastrophe. The population now numbers in the hundreds but remains critically endangered.
