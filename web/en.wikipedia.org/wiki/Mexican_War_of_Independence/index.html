Mexican War of Independence
Date16 September 1810 – 27 September 1821
LocationNew Spain (Mexico)

The Mexican War of Independence (Spanish: Guerra de Independencia de México) was an armed conflict and political process that resulted in Mexico's independence from the Spanish Empire. It began on 16 September 1810 with the Grito de Dolores, the priest Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla's call to arms in the town of Dolores, and ended on 27 September 1821 when the Army of the Three Guarantees entered Mexico City.

Hidalgo and his followers; the Virgin of Guadalupe became the banner of the insurgency.

Course of the war

Hidalgo's largely indigenous and mestizo army swept through the Bajío, taking Guanajuato (storming the Alhóndiga de Granaditas) and winning at Monte de las Cruces, but failed to take Mexico City; Hidalgo was captured and executed in Chihuahua in 1811. Leadership passed to José María Morelos, a more disciplined commander who controlled much of southern Mexico, convened the Congress of Chilpancingo (1813), and promulgated the Constitution of Apatzingán (1814) before his capture and execution in 1815. Thereafter the insurgency survived mainly as guerrilla warfare under leaders such as Vicente Guerrero and Guadalupe Victoria.

Consummation

In 1820, a liberal revolt in Spain unsettled conservative royalists in New Spain. The royalist colonel Agustín de Iturbide negotiated with Guerrero and proclaimed the Plan of Iguala (February 1821), promising independence, Catholicism, and equality ("the Three Guarantees"). The last Spanish chief of government, Juan O'Donojú, signed the Treaty of Córdoba on 24 August 1821, and the Army of the Three Guarantees entered Mexico City on 27 September 1821. Iturbide was crowned Emperor Agustín I of the First Mexican Empire in 1822, though the empire collapsed within a year, leading to the federal republic of 1824.

Legacy

Mexico celebrates 16 September as Independence Day; the president re-enacts the Grito de Dolores from the National Palace balcony each year. Hidalgo is honoured as the Father of the Nation.
