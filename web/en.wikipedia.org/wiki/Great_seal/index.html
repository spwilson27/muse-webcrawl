Great seal

A great seal is a seal used by a head of state, or someone authorised to act in their name, to authorise official instruments of government, in the name of the monarch or the republic on whose authority they are issued. The phrase may refer to both the physical die used to make an impression and to the impression itself.

History

Great seals originated in medieval Europe, where rulers used large double-sided seals to authenticate documents. The practice descends from the seals of the Roman and Byzantine empires and from the personal seals used by early medieval kings. By the high Middle Ages, most European monarchies possessed a great seal, typically administered by a chancellor or a dedicated keeper, and often distinct from the smaller privy seal used for personal correspondence.

Design

Traditional great seals are double-sided, bearing an obverse and a reverse. A common pattern shows the sovereign enthroned on one side and mounted on horseback, or displaying the arms of state, on the other. The seal matrix is usually made of silver or another durable metal, and the impression is made in wax, traditionally beeswax or a wax-resin mixture, attached to the document by cords or a parchment tag. Modern state seals are frequently single-sided and may be embossed on paper rather than cast in wax.

Examples

  • The Great Seal of the United Kingdom, used to signify the sovereign's approval of important state documents, is administered by the Lord Chancellor.
  • The Great Seal of the United States, adopted in 1782, bears the bald eagle on its obverse and the unfinished pyramid with the Eye of Providence on its reverse; it is kept by the Secretary of State.
  • The Great Seal of France dates in its present form to the Second Republic of 1848 and depicts Liberty seated.
  • The Great Seal of Canada is used on formal documents such as proclamations and commissions and is kept by the Registrar General.

Keepers

The office of keeper of the great seal is one of the oldest in many governments. In England the role is held by the Lord High Chancellor of Great Britain; in Scotland a separate Keeper of the Great Seal of Scotland exists, an office held by the First Minister. In the United States, the Secretary of State is the custodian of the federal seal and affixes it to instruments as directed by the President.
