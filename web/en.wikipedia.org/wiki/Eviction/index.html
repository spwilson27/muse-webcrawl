Eviction

Eviction
Also known asUnlawful detainer, ejectment (in some contexts)
Area of lawProperty law, landlord–tenant law
Common groundsNon-payment of rent, lease violation, end of lease term

Eviction is the removal of a tenant from rental property by the landlord. In some jurisdictions it may also involve the removal of persons from premises that were foreclosed by a mortgagee. Depending on the laws of the jurisdiction, eviction may also be known as unlawful detainer, summary possession, summary dispossess, summary process, forcible detainer, ejectment, and repossession, among other terms.

Process

In most jurisdictions, a landlord must follow a formal legal process rather than resorting to self-help measures such as changing locks or shutting off utilities. The process typically involves serving the tenant with a notice to pay or vacate, filing an action in court, a hearing at which the tenant may raise defences, and, if the landlord prevails, enforcement of a possession order by a sheriff or bailiff.

Grounds and defences

Common grounds include non-payment of rent, breach of lease terms, property damage, illegal activity, and expiry of the tenancy. Tenants may defend on grounds such as the landlord's failure to maintain habitable conditions, retaliation, or discrimination. Many jurisdictions impose additional protections, including notice periods, just-cause requirements and relocation assistance.

Effects

Research, notably by sociologist Matthew Desmond in Evicted (2016), links eviction to job loss, homelessness, poor health outcomes and adverse effects on children. Eviction records can make it substantially harder for tenants to secure future housing. During the COVID-19 pandemic, governments in many countries imposed temporary eviction moratoria.
