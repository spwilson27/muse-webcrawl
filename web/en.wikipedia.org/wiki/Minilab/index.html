Minilab

A minilab is a small photographic developing and printing system, as opposed to large central laboratories, designed for on-site, rapid-turnaround processing — classically the "one-hour photo." Minilabs combine a film processor and a photographic printer/paper processor in a compact footprint suitable for retail premises.

History

Minilabs emerged in the late 1970s and spread rapidly in the 1980s with systems from manufacturers such as Noritsu, Fujifilm (the Frontier series), Kodak, Agfa and Konica. Early machines were analog: negatives were printed optically onto photographic paper, which then passed through chemical baths using the RA-4 process for color prints (and C-41 for film development). Their speed and convenience transformed consumer photography, making one-hour processing a standard retail service in drugstores, supermarkets and dedicated photo shops.

Digital minilabs

From the late 1990s, digital minilabs replaced optical printing with scanning and laser or LED exposure of the paper, and later added inkjet and dye-sublimation printing. Digital systems could print from digital camera files as well as film, enlarge and correct images automatically, and produce a wider range of products.

Decline

The shift to camera phones and online sharing sharply reduced consumer film and print volumes in the 2000s and 2010s, leading to the removal of most retail minilabs. Surviving machines are concentrated in professional and specialist labs, and a niche market persists for refurbished equipment.
