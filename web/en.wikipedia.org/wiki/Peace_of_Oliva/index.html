The Peace of Oliva (Polish: Pokój Oliwski; German: Vertrag von Oliva) was one of the peace treaties that ended the Second Northern War (1655–1660). It was signed on 3 May 1660 at the Cistercian abbey of Oliwa, near Gdańsk, between the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, Sweden, Brandenburg-Prussia, and the Holy Roman Emperor.

Terms

Under the treaty, the Polish king John II Casimir Vasa formally renounced his claims to the Swedish throne, which Polish Vasa kings had maintained since Sigismund III was deposed in Sweden in 1599. Poland formally ceded Swedish Livonia — northern Livonia with Riga, which Sweden had held since the 1620s — to Sweden. In return Sweden recognised the full sovereignty of the Duchy of Prussia under Frederick William, Elector of Brandenburg, confirming the settlement reached in the treaties of Wehlau and Bromberg (1657): the Hohenzollerns no longer owed homage to the Polish crown for Prussia. Most occupied territories were restored to their pre-war status, and the Emperor withdrew his forces from the Commonwealth.

Significance

Together with the Treaty of Copenhagen (1660) between Sweden and Denmark-Norway, the Peace of Oliva closed the Swedish "Deluge" era and fixed the Baltic balance of power for a generation. It is especially significant for confirming Prussia's emancipation from Polish suzerainty — a precondition for the later rise of the Kingdom of Prussia — and for settling the Polish-Swedish dynastic dispute that had driven wars between the two states since 1600.
