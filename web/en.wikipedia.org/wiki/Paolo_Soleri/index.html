Paolo Soleri

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Paolo Soleri (21 June 1919 – 9 April 2013) was an Italian architect and designer based in Arizona, United States. He established the educational Cosanti Foundation and Arcosanti, an experimental town in the high desert of Arizona, as a prototype of his concept of "arcology" — the fusion of architecture and ecology.

Biography

Soleri was born in Turin, Italy, and received his doctorate in architecture from the Polytechnic University of Turin in 1946. In 1947 he travelled to the United States and worked for a year and a half as a fellow at Frank Lloyd Wright's Taliesin West in Arizona and Taliesin in Wisconsin. A bridge he designed in Italy, built in 1949, brought him international recognition.

In 1956 he settled in Scottsdale, Arizona, with his wife Colly, where he founded the Cosanti Foundation and began producing the wind bells that helped finance his work. In 1970, construction began on Arcosanti, an experimental "urban laboratory" about 70 miles north of Phoenix intended to demonstrate arcology: dense, three-dimensional, pedestrian-oriented urban structures that minimize resource use and dependence on automobiles.

Arcology

Soleri coined the term arcology (architecture + ecology) and set out his ideas in the 1969 book Arcology: The City in the Image of Man. He argued that urban sprawl wastes land and energy, and proposed compact mega-structures housing thousands of people in a small footprint surrounded by preserved natural landscape. His elaborate drawings of futuristic arcologies influenced science fiction and visionary architecture.

Legacy

Arcosanti remains under construction and hosts workshops, festivals, and tourists. The Soleri Bridge and Plaza in Scottsdale, completed in 2010, incorporates his designs. Soleri received the Golden Lion at the Venice Biennale of Architecture in 2000 for lifetime achievement. He died in 2013 at Cosanti.
