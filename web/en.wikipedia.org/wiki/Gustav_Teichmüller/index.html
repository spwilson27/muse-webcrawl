Gustav Teichmüller

Gustav Teichmüller (1832–1888)
Born: Braunschweig, German Confederation
Occupation: Philosopher
Known for: Coining "ontology" studies; perspectivism; influence on Nietzsche

Gustav Teichmüller (19 November 1832 – 22 May 1888) was a German philosopher who developed an early form of perspectivism and whose writings on Greek philosophy and metaphysics influenced, among others, Friedrich Nietzsche. He spent much of his career at the University of Dorpat (now Tartu, Estonia).

Life and career

Teichmüller studied philosophy at Berlin and Tübingen under Friedrich Adolf Trendelenburg. After teaching at Göttingen and Basel — where he was a colleague during Nietzsche's professorship — he became professor of philosophy at Dorpat in 1871, remaining there until his death. In Basel he lectured on the pre-Socratics, Aristotle, and the history of concepts.

Philosophy

Teichmüller's mature system, set out in Die wirkliche und die scheinbare Welt ("The Real and the Apparent World", 1882), distinguished the immediately given world of self-conscious experience from the constructed "apparent" world of external objects. He criticised both materialism and Kantian transcendentalism, and elaborated a doctrine of perspectives and of being grounded in the self. His Studien zur Geschichte der Begriffe (1874–79) and Literarische Fehden im vierten Jahrhundert vor Christo traced the history of fundamental concepts such as being and substance in Greek thought. He is credited with pioneering the study of the term "ontology" and its history, and with an influential analysis of Aristotle.

Influence

Nietzsche read and annotated Teichmüller's works during his Basel years, and scholars have traced echoes of Teichmüller's perspectivism and critique of the concept of being in Nietzsche's later philosophy. In Russia and the Baltic, Teichmüller's Dorpat school shaped figures such as Evgeny Bobrov and the young Karl Ernst von Baer's intellectual circle's successors.
