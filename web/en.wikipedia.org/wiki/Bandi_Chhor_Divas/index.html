Bandi Chhor Divas

Bandi Chhor Divas ("Day of Liberation of Prisoners") is a Sikh festival commemorating the release of the sixth Sikh Guru, Guru Hargobind, from imprisonment at Gwalior Fort in 1619, together with the 52 Hindu princes (rajas) whose freedom he secured. The festival falls on the same day as the Hindu festival of Diwali and is celebrated with particular splendour at the Golden Temple in Amritsar.

The story

According to Sikh tradition, Guru Hargobind was detained by the Mughal emperor Jahangir at Gwalior Fort, where 52 princes were also held. When Jahangir, advised by the saint Mian Mir and impressed by the Guru's character, ordered his release, the Guru refused to leave unless the princes were freed with him. The emperor ruled that as many princes as could hold onto the Guru's cloak could leave; the Guru had a special cloak made with 52 tassels (or panels), so that every prince could grasp one, and all 52 walked free with him. He thus earned the title Bandi Chhor — "liberator of prisoners."

Celebration

On his return to Amritsar, the city and the Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple) were illuminated with lamps to welcome him, and the tradition of lighting the temple continues: on Bandi Chhor Divas the Golden Temple complex is spectacularly lit and fireworks are held over the sarovar. Sikhs illuminate homes and gurdwaras, exchange sweets, and attend kirtan. The festival is one of the most important in the Sikh calendar, standing in the Nanakshahi calendar alongside Vaisakhi and Hola Mohalla in significance for many congregations.
