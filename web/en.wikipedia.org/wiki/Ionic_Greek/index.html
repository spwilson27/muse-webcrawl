Ionic Greek was a subdialect of the Attic–Ionic or East Greek dialect group of Ancient Greek. It was spoken in Ionia, the central west coast of Anatolia, as well as on the Cyclades, the northern Aegean islands, Euboea, and in Ionic colonies around the Mediterranean and Black Sea. Closely related to Attic, Ionic was the dialect of early Greek prose and science.

Characteristics

The hallmark of Ionic is the shift of long ā to ē throughout (e.g. mētēr for Attic mātēr, "mother"), a change it shares with Attic only in part. Ionic also shows contraction of vowels less often than Attic, loss of the digamma early, and distinctive forms such as kou for Attic pou. The Ionic alphabet, developed in Ionia, was adopted at Athens in 403/2 BC and became the standard Greek alphabet.

Literature

Although the Homeric epics are written in an artificial poetic language drawing on both Ionic and Aeolic forms, their base is largely Ionic. Ionic was the language of Herodotus's Histories, of the Hippocratic medical corpus, and of early Greek philosophy and science in cities such as Miletus — home of Thales, Anaximander and Anaximenes. In the Hellenistic period Ionic was largely absorbed into the Attic-based Koine.
