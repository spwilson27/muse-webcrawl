LTE (telecommunication)

In telecommunications, LTE (Long-Term Evolution) is a standard for wireless broadband communication for mobile devices and data terminals, based on the GSM/EDGE and UMTS/HSPA standards. It increases capacity and speed using a different radio interface together with core network improvements. LTE is the upgrade path for carriers with both GSM/UMTS networks and CDMA2000 networks.

Overview

The standard is developed by the 3GPP (3rd Generation Partnership Project) and is specified in its Release 8 document series, with enhancements described in Release 9 and beyond. The first publicly available LTE service was launched by TeliaSonera in Oslo and Stockholm on 14 December 2009. LTE is commonly marketed as 4G LTE, though the original Release 8 specification technically fell short of the ITU's IMT-Advanced requirements for "true 4G"; the later LTE-Advanced (Release 10) meets them.

Technology

LTE uses orthogonal frequency-division multiple access (OFDMA) for the downlink and single-carrier FDMA (SC-FDMA) for the uplink, together with multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) antenna techniques. It supports scalable channel bandwidths from 1.4 to 20 MHz and both frequency-division duplex (FDD) and time-division duplex (TDD) modes. Peak rates in Release 8 reach 300 Mbit/s downlink and 75 Mbit/s uplink under ideal conditions, while LTE-Advanced with carrier aggregation raises theoretical peaks into the gigabit range.

The core network, the Evolved Packet Core (EPC), is an all-IP architecture that handles voice as packet data (VoLTE) rather than through legacy circuit switching. Since the rollout of 5G networks beginning in 2019, LTE has increasingly served as the anchor layer for non-standalone 5G deployments and remains the most widely used mobile network technology worldwide.
