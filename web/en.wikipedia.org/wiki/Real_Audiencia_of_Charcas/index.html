The Real Audiencia of Charcas (Spanish: Real Audiencia de Charcas) was a Spanish audiencia with a territory in the Viceroyalty of Peru and, later, the Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata. It was seated in the city of La Plata, today Sucre, Bolivia. Established in 1559, it was the highest tribunal of the Spanish Crown in the region known as Upper Peru (Alto Perú), and it exercised both judicial and governmental functions.

Establishment

The Audiencia of Charcas was created by a royal decree of King Philip II on 18 September 1559, and it was formally installed in 1561. Its creation formed part of the Spanish Crown's efforts to consolidate administration in the southern Andes, a region enriched by the silver mines of Potosí, discovered in 1545. The audiencia's first president was Pedro Ramírez de Quiñones.

Like other American audiencias, Charcas was presided over by a president and composed of oidores (judges), a fiscal (crown attorney), and subordinate officials. It served as an appeals court for cases arising within its district and also advised the viceroy on matters of government.

Territory

The jurisdiction of the Audiencia of Charcas was vast. At its greatest extent it covered Upper Peru and, at various times, the governorships of Tucumán, Paraguay, and the Río de la Plata, including the territory of present-day Argentina, Paraguay, and parts of Chile and Brazil. Its boundaries shifted repeatedly during the colonial period. In 1661 an audiencia was briefly established at Buenos Aires, but it was suppressed in 1671; a new Audiencia of Buenos Aires was created in 1783 as part of the Bourbon Reforms.

In 1776 the Audiencia of Charcas was transferred from the Viceroyalty of Peru to the newly created Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata, though it retained close ties to Lima. The intendancy system was introduced into its territory in the 1780s, curtailing some of its governmental functions.

Role in independence

The city of Chuquisaca (La Plata) was an early center of the Spanish American independence movement. The Chuquisaca Revolution of 25 May 1809, in which the audiencia's authority was challenged, is often considered the first cry of independence in Spanish South America. The audiencia effectively ceased to function as the wars of independence engulfed Upper Peru, and it was dissolved following the creation of the Republic of Bolivia in 1825.
