Mood (psychology)

In psychology, a mood is an affective state. In contrast to emotions or feelings, moods are less specific, less intense, and less likely to be provoked or triggered by a particular stimulus or event. Moods are typically described as either positive or negative, and can last for hours or days.

Mood and emotion

Emotions are usually directed at something specific — a person becomes angry at someone — whereas a mood is more diffuse and may colour a person's judgements and behaviour in general. Moods also differ from temperament and personality traits, which are longer-lasting dispositions.

Factors affecting mood

Sleep deprivation, nutrition, exercise, weather, hormonal changes, and social interaction can all influence mood. Chronic disturbances of mood are central to mood disorders such as major depressive disorder and bipolar disorder, in which moods become pathologically persistent or extreme.

Measurement

Psychologists commonly measure mood with self-report instruments such as the Positive and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS), which scores positive affect and negative affect as largely independent dimensions.
