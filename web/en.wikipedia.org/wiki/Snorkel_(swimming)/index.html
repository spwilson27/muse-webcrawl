Snorkel (swimming)

A snorkel is a device used for breathing air from above the surface of the water when the wearer's head is face downwards in the water with the mouth and nose submerged. It is typically a J- or L-shaped tube about 30 cm long with a mouthpiece at the lower end.

Snorkels are standard equipment for snorkelling and are also used by scuba divers for surface swimming, by spearfishers, and in competitive finswimming, where front-mounted snorkels are used. The tube allows breathing without lifting the head, but the swimmer must periodically clear water from it by a sharp exhalation ("blast clearing").

Design

Modern snorkels are made of plastic and rubber, often with a purge valve at the lowest point, a splash guard or dry-top valve at the upper end, and a flexible corrugated section near the mouthpiece. Total internal volume is a safety consideration: an excessively long or wide tube increases dead space and rebreathing of exhaled carbon dioxide, which is why standards limit snorkel dimensions and why snorkels are not recommended for children under certain sizes.

History

Breathing tubes of reed were described in antiquity — Aristotle described divers using an instrument like an elephant's trunk. Modern rubber snorkels were patented in the 1930s and became popular with the post-war growth of recreational diving.
