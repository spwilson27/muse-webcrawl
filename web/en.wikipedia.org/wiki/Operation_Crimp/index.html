Operation Crimp (8–14 January 1966), also known as the Battle of the Ho Bo Woods, was a joint United States and Australian military operation during the Vietnam War, carried out north-west of Saigon in the Ho Bo Woods, Binh Duong Province. It was the largest Allied operation mounted in South Vietnam up to that point, involving more than 8,000 troops, and is notable for the discovery of the extensive Viet Cong tunnel network centred on Cu Chi.

Background

U.S. intelligence believed the Ho Bo Woods concealed the headquarters directing Viet Cong operations against Saigon. The plan employed a "hammer and anvil": American forces of the 1st Infantry Division and 173rd Airborne Brigade would drive through the woods while the 1st Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment — attached to the 173rd — established blocking positions. Operation Crimp marked the first large-scale offensive use of Australian troops in the war and 1 RAR's last major operation before Australia's deployment expanded to a task force at Nui Dat.

The operation

From 8 January, troops encountered unexpectedly heavy resistance from well-entrenched Viet Cong of the D800 Battalion. Australian soldiers attacking positions near their landing zone uncovered entrances to a vast tunnel complex. Rather than sealing and demolishing entrances without exploration, soldiers — following the initiative associated with engineer Captain Sandy MacGregor and others — began entering the tunnels to search them, recovering documents, weapons, and equipment; this work foreshadowed the later "tunnel rats". The tunnels, part of what became famous as the Cu Chi tunnels, contained hospitals, storage, workshops, and the headquarters complex the operation sought, though much of the enemy force withdrew through them. Allied casualties included 8 Australians killed and 29 wounded, with American losses of about 14 killed; Viet Cong losses were estimated at over 120 killed.

Aftermath

Although the enemy headquarters was displaced and a large quantity of documents and materiel captured, the tunnel system could not be destroyed in the time available and the area was later reoccupied by the Viet Cong, remaining a stronghold until the end of the war. The operation was followed later in January by the larger Operation Crimp II and Operation Buckskin in the same region.
