Space Invaders
DeveloperTaito
DesignerTomohiro Nishikado
Release1978 (Japan); 1978 (North America, via Midway)
GenreFixed shooter

Space Invaders is a 1978 shoot-'em-up arcade video game designed by Tomohiro Nishikado and released by Taito in Japan and by Midway in North America. The player controls a laser cannon at the bottom of the screen, moving horizontally and firing upward at five rows of descending aliens, while sheltering behind destructible green bunkers. It was one of the first shooting games and a foundational title of the golden age of arcade video games.

Gameplay and design

The aliens advance side to side and step downward at the screen's edge, accelerating as their numbers fall — an effect that arose accidentally because the hardware rendered the formation faster with fewer sprites, which Nishikado retained as a natural difficulty curve. A mystery-score "mothership" periodically crosses the top of the screen. The game ends if the invaders reach the bottom; high-score competition became a defining feature, as Space Invaders popularised the persistent high-score table.

Impact

The game was a phenomenon in Japan — popular legend holds that it caused a shortage of 100-yen coins — and a blockbuster in North America. Licensed by Atari for the Atari 2600 in 1980, it became the first "killer app" for a home console, quadrupling the system's sales. Its pixelated crab-like invader became an enduring icon of video game culture, and the game helped establish high-score competition, continuing difficulty and the shoot-'em-up genre, earning it recognition as one of the most influential video games ever made.
