Pragmatic Sanction of 1713

The Pragmatic Sanction of 1713 was an edict issued by the Habsburg emperor Charles VI on 19 April 1713. It declared the Habsburg hereditary lands indivisible and provided that, failing male heirs, they could pass to a female successor — in the event, Charles's daughter Maria Theresa.

Background

Under the Mutual Pact of Succession of 1703, the daughters of Charles's elder brother Joseph I had precedence over Charles's own future daughters. The 1713 Sanction reversed this, putting Charles's offspring first. Charles spent much of his reign securing acceptance of the Sanction from the diets of his lands and from foreign powers, often at considerable diplomatic cost, including concessions in the Ostend Company affair.

Provisions

The Sanction established a single order of succession for all the Habsburg territories — the Austrian hereditary lands, Bohemia, Hungary, Croatia, and the Austrian Netherlands and Italian possessions — binding them into an indivisible union.

Consequences

Despite the guarantees, when Charles died in 1740 several signatories repudiated their commitments. Prussia seized Silesia and Bavaria advanced rival claims, triggering the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748). Maria Theresa nevertheless retained nearly all her inheritance, and the Pragmatic Sanction remained a fundamental law of the Habsburg monarchy until 1918.
