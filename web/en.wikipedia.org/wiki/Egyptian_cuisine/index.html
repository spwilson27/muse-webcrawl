Egyptian cuisine

Staples: Bread (eish baladi), rice, fava beans, lentils, vegetables

Signature dishes: Kushari, ful medames, ta'ameya (falafel), molokhiya, mahshi, fatta

Influences: Ancient Egyptian, Levantine, Ottoman, Mediterranean

Egyptian cuisine makes heavy use of legumes, vegetables and fruits grown in the fertile Nile valley and delta. Because Egyptian cooking has evolved around a largely agricultural population, bread — eish baladi, a pocketed flatbread — accompanies nearly every meal and is used to scoop up dips and stews. Many dishes centre on beans and lentils, making the cuisine unusually rich in vegetarian staples, alongside grilled and stewed meats.

History

The cuisine descends in part from that of ancient Egypt — bread, beer, beans and onions were staples of the pharaonic diet — and absorbed influences from successive rulers and trading partners, including Persians, Greeks, Romans, Arabs, Ottomans and the French and British. The result is recognisably related to other eastern Mediterranean and Levantine cuisines while retaining distinctive dishes of its own.

Typical dishes

Kushari, a mixture of rice, lentils and macaroni topped with spiced tomato sauce, chickpeas and fried onions, is often called Egypt's national dish. Ful medames — slow-cooked fava beans with oil, lemon and cumin — is a breakfast staple eaten with ta'ameya, the Egyptian falafel made from fava beans rather than chickpeas. Molokhiya is a garlicky green soup made from jute leaves, served over rice with chicken or rabbit. Stuffed vegetables (mahshi), pigeon stuffed with seasoned rice or freekeh, and fatta — layers of bread, rice and meat in a garlicky tomato or yoghurt sauce — are festive favourites.

Cheese, bread and desserts

Egypt produces ancient and modern cheeses including domiati, a soft white brined cheese, and mish, a fermented cheese matured in spiced whey. Desserts include om ali (a bread pudding with milk, nuts and raisins), basbousa, kunafa and rice pudding. Tea (shai) and thick Turkish-style coffee are the principal drinks; hibiscus tea (karkadeh) is served hot or cold.
