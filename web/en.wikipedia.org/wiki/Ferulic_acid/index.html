Ferulic acid

IUPAC name(E)-3-(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)prop-2-enoic acid
Chemical formulaC10H10O4
Molar mass194.18 g/mol
AppearanceCrystalline powder
Melting point168–172 °C (dec.)
CAS number1135-24-6

Ferulic acid is a hydroxycinnamic acid, an organic compound with the formula (CH3O)C6H3(OH)CH=CHCO2H. The name is derived from the genus Ferula, referring to the giant fennel (Ferula communis). Classified as a phenolic phytochemical, ferulic acid is an amber colored solid. Esters of ferulic acid are found in plant cell walls, covalently bonded to lignin and other biopolymers.

Occurrence in nature

As a building block of lignocelluloses, such as pectin and lignin, ferulic acid is ubiquitous in the plant kingdom. It is plentiful in the cell walls of commelinid plants such as rice, wheat, oats, and pineapple. Especially high concentrations occur in the bran of grains, in popcorn, and in bamboo shoots. Ferulic acid is a metabolite of chlorogenic acid and is produced from caffeic acid by the enzyme caffeate O-methyltransferase.

Metabolism

In plants, ferulic acid is a precursor in the manufacture of other aromatic compounds and cross-links polysaccharides and lignin, helping to rigidify cell walls. In animals that ingest it, ferulic acid is readily absorbed and excreted in the urine as glucuronide conjugates. In vitro studies have indicated it has antioxidant activity, which has led to interest in its potential health benefits, although evidence for effects in humans remains limited.

Applications

Ferulic acid is used in cosmetics and skin-care products as an antioxidant and stabilizer, often combined with vitamins C and E. In the food industry it serves as a precursor for the biotechnological production of vanillin. It is also used as a cross-linking agent and as a matrix in mass spectrometry.
