Dedekind eta function

Dedekind eta function
Notationη(τ)
DomainUpper half-plane
TypeModular form of weight 1/2
Named forRichard Dedekind

In mathematics, the Dedekind eta function, named after Richard Dedekind, is a modular form of weight 1/2 defined on the upper half-plane of complex numbers. Writing q = e2πiτ, it is defined by the infinite product η(τ) = eπiτ/12 ∏n≥1 (1 − e2πinτ), equivalently η(τ) = q1/24 ∏n≥1 (1 − qn).

Properties

  • The eta function is holomorphic on the upper half-plane and never zero there, but cannot be continuously extended to the real axis.
  • Its 24th power is the modular discriminant Δ(τ), a cusp form of weight 12: Δ(τ) = (2π)12 η(τ)24.
  • Under the modular group it transforms with a multiplier system involving 24th roots of unity — the source of many appearances of the number 24 in mathematics and physics.
  • By Euler's pentagonal number theorem, the product ∏(1 − qn) equals a sparse series over generalised pentagonal numbers.

Applications

The eta function appears in the theory of partitions (via the generating function of the partition numbers), in Jacobi's triple product identity, in the transformation law of the Dedekind sums of number theory, and in string theory, where the partition function of a free boson involves η(τ), contributing to the critical dimension 26 of bosonic string theory.
