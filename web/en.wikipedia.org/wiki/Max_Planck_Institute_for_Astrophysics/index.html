The Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics (MPA, German: Max-Planck-Institut für Astrophysik) is a research institute located in Garching, near Munich, Germany. It is part of the Max Planck Society and is devoted to theoretical and observational astrophysics and cosmology.

History

The institute grew out of the department of astrophysics founded at the Max Planck Institute for Physics in Munich by Werner Heisenberg and led by Ludwig Biermann, a pioneer of plasma physics and the theory of solar wind and comets. It became an independent institute in 1958. From 1991 the MPA was hosted on the Garching research campus alongside the Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics and the European Southern Observatory headquarters.

Research

MPA researchers work on stellar structure and evolution, supernovae, compact objects, galaxy formation and evolution, large-scale structure of the universe, cosmic microwave background, and computational astrophysics. The institute is especially known for large cosmological simulations, such as the Millennium Simulation and IllustrisTNG, carried out with international partners, which model the formation of galaxies in a dark-matter-dominated universe.
