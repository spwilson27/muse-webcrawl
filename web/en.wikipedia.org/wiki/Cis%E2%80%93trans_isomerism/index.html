Cis–trans isomerism

Cis–trans isomerism, also known as geometric isomerism, describes certain arrangements of atoms within molecules. The prefixes cis and trans are from Latin: cis means "this side of" and trans means "the other side of" or "across".

Organic chemistry

When substituents are oriented in the same direction, the diastereomer is referred to as cis, whereas when the substituents are oriented in opposing directions, the diastereomer is referred to as trans. A classic example is cis-2-butene and trans-2-butene, which differ in melting and boiling points because rotation about the carbon–carbon double bond is restricted.

E–Z notation

For more complex molecules, the E–Z system based on Cahn–Ingold–Prelog priority rules is preferred: E (entgegen, opposite) and Z (zusammen, together).

Biological significance

Geometric isomerism matters in vision, where the cis–trans isomerisation of retinal in rhodopsin triggers the visual signal cascade.
