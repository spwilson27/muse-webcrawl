Turfgrass

Turfgrass refers to the narrow-leaved grass species grown to form dense, uniform, mown ground cover — turf or sod — for lawns, sports fields, golf courses, parks and other managed landscapes. Turfgrass management is a major horticultural industry and field of study (turf science), concerned with selecting species and cultivars that tolerate close mowing, wear, shade, drought and local climate, and with their fertilisation, irrigation, pest control and cultivation.

Species

Turfgrasses are grouped into cool-season and warm-season types. Common cool-season grasses, suited to temperate climates, include Kentucky bluegrass (Poa pratensis), perennial ryegrass (Lolium perenne), tall and fine fescues (Festuca spp.), and creeping bentgrass (Agrostis stolonifera), the traditional surface for golf putting greens. Common warm-season grasses, suited to tropical and subtropical regions, include bermudagrass (Cynodon dactylon), zoysiagrass (Zoysia spp.), St. Augustine grass (Stenotaphrum secundatum) and buffalograss. Most turf is established from seed or laid as harvested sod; elite sports surfaces increasingly use hybrid systems combining natural grass with synthetic fibres.

Management and environment

A managed turf system involves mowing at species-appropriate heights, aeration to relieve soil compaction, topdressing, overseeding, and irrigation, together with control of weeds, diseases such as dollar spot and brown patch, and insect pests. Turf provides valued benefits — playability, erosion control, cooling and recreation space — but lawns are also criticised for high water, fertiliser and pesticide use and for emissions from mowing, prompting movements toward reduced-input cultivars, clover mixes and lawn alternatives in dry regions.
