Progressive Field

Location: Cleveland, Ohio, United States
Opened: April 4, 1994
Capacity: about 34,788
Tenants: Cleveland Guardians (MLB)

Progressive Field is a baseball stadium located in downtown Cleveland, Ohio, United States, and the home of the Cleveland Guardians of Major League Baseball. It opened in 1994 as Jacobs Field, named for team owners Richard and David Jacobs, and was often nicknamed "The Jake". In 2008 the naming rights were purchased by the Progressive Corporation, an insurance company based in the Cleveland area.

History

The ballpark was built as part of the Gateway Sports and Entertainment Complex alongside the arena now known as Rocket Arena. It replaced Cleveland Stadium. The stadium hosted the 1997 and 2019 MLB All-Star Games and was the site of World Series games in 1995, 1997, and 2016. Between 1995 and 2001 the team set a then-record sellout streak of 455 consecutive games.

Features

The park is known for its view of the Cleveland skyline, its large scoreboard, and distinctive vertical light towers reminiscent of toothbrushes. Renovations in the 2010s reduced capacity and added social gathering spaces and new concessions.
