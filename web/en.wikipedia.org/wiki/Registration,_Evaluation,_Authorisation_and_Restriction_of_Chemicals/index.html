Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals

Short name: REACH
Jurisdiction: European Union
Entered into force: 1 June 2007
Agency: European Chemicals Agency (ECHA), Helsinki

Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals (REACH) is a regulation of the European Union, adopted to improve the protection of human health and the environment from risks posed by chemicals while enhancing the competitiveness of the EU chemicals industry. It entered into force on 1 June 2007 and is administered by the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) in Helsinki.

How it works

REACH places the burden of proof on industry: companies must register chemical substances manufactured or imported in quantities of one tonne or more per year with ECHA, submitting data on their properties and hazards — "no data, no market". ECHA and member states evaluate registrations and substances of concern. Substances of very high concern may require authorisation for specific uses, and the manufacture or use of the most dangerous substances can be restricted or banned across the EU.

Scope and impact

REACH applies to substances on their own, in mixtures and, in some cases, in articles such as consumer goods. It is one of the most comprehensive chemical regulations in the world and has influenced legislation elsewhere, including post-Brexit UK REACH in Great Britain.
