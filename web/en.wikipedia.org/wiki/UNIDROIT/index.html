UNIDROIT

UNIDROIT — the International Institute for the Unification of Private Law — is an intergovernmental organisation headquartered in Rome, Italy, whose purpose is to study needs and methods for modernising, harmonising and coordinating private and in particular commercial law among states.

Founded1926 (as League of Nations institute)
HeadquartersVilla Aldobrandini, Rome
Members65 member states

Instruments

UNIDROIT is best known for the UNIDROIT Principles of International Commercial Contracts (first issued 1994, revised 2016), widely used soft law in arbitration, and for conventions including the 2001 Cape Town Convention on mobile equipment with its Aircraft Protocol, the 1995 Convention on Stolen or Illegally Exported Cultural Objects, and work on secured transactions, leasing, factoring, and digital assets.

Structure

Re-established by multilateral agreement in 1940 after the League of Nations dissolved, it is governed by a Governing Council and a General Assembly of member states, with a Secretariat in Rome.
