French nobility

The French nobility (noblesse française) was a privileged social class in France from the Middle Ages until its abolition in the course of the French Revolution. Under the Ancien Régime, nobles enjoyed hereditary privileges including exemption from many taxes, the right to bear arms and to hold certain offices, and a distinct place in the ceremonial order of the kingdom. The nobility, together with the clergy, formed the privileged orders of the realm, distinct from the Third Estate.

Origins and categories

The French nobility developed out of the feudal aristocracy of the early medieval period. A fundamental distinction existed between the noblesse d'épée ("nobility of the sword"), the old military aristocracy claiming descent from medieval knights, and the noblesse de robe ("nobility of the robe"), families ennobled through judicial and administrative office, particularly service in the parlements. Other routes to nobility included royal letters of ennoblement, municipal office in certain cities (noblesse de cloche), and the purchase of ennobling offices.

By the eighteenth century the nobility numbered perhaps 110,000 to 120,000 people — roughly half of one percent of the French population — though estimates vary. Great aristocratic families at court coexisted with a much larger stratum of provincial hobereaux, country gentlemen of modest means.

Privileges and obligations

Nobles were exempt from the taille, the principal direct tax, in most of France, and enjoyed seigneurial rights over peasants on their estates, honorific precedence, and exclusive access to certain careers and cathedral chapters. In return, nobility carried obligations of military service — the so-called "blood tax" — and nobles were forbidden on pain of dérogeance (loss of status) from engaging in most forms of manual labour and retail trade, though wholesale commerce, mining, metallurgy and glassmaking were permitted in various periods.

Revolution and after

The privileges of the nobility were abolished on the night of 4 August 1789 during the French Revolution, and hereditary nobility itself was abolished in June 1790. Many nobles emigrated; others were executed during the Reign of Terror. Napoleon created a new imperial nobility in 1808, and the Restoration monarchy recognised both old and new titles, but legal privilege was never restored. The Second Empire likewise conferred titles. Since 1870 France has been a republic and noble titles have no legal status, though they continue in social use as part of family names, protected in some circumstances by the courts as accessories to the name.
