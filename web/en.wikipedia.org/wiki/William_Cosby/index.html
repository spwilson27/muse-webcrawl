William Cosby

William Cosby (c. 1690–1736), colonial governor of New York and New Jersey.

William Cosby (c. 1690 – 10 March 1736) was a British colonial official who served as governor of the British colonies of New York and New Jersey from 1732 to 1736. His administration is remembered chiefly for the libel trial of printer John Peter Zenger, a landmark in the development of freedom of the press in America.

Governorship

An Irish-born army officer, Cosby had previously governed Minorca and the Leeward Islands. On arriving in New York in 1732 he immediately became embroiled in a dispute with the acting governor, Rip Van Dam, over salary arrears, suing him in a court whose proceedings Cosby manipulated. His high-handed use of power, interference with courts, and rigging of an election alienated the colonial opposition, which founded the New-York Weekly Journal under John Peter Zenger to attack his administration.

The Zenger trial

Cosby had Zenger arrested for seditious libel in 1734. At the trial in 1735, Zenger's lawyer Andrew Hamilton argued that truth should be a defence against libel; the jury acquitted Zenger, delivering a celebrated victory for press freedom that influenced American legal tradition.

Death

Cosby died of tuberculosis in New York on 10 March 1736, still in office.
