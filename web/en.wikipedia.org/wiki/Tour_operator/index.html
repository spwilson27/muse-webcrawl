Tour operator

A tour operator is a business that typically combines and organizes accommodations, meals, sightseeing and transportation components, in order to create a package tour. Most tour operators are also considered travel wholesalers. They most commonly sell packages to travel agencies or directly to the travelling public.

Operations

Tour operators negotiate contracts with hotels, airlines, ground transportation companies, and local guides, often purchasing capacity in bulk at discounted rates. The components are combined into a package — typically a package tour — which is then sold at a single price. Because operators buy in volume, packages can be cheaper than the sum of their parts bought separately.

Types

  • Inbound tour operators receive visitors in their home country and run tours there for overseas clients.
  • Outbound tour operators sell tours to destinations outside their home country.
  • Domestic tour operators operate within a single country.
  • Ground operators (destination management companies) handle arrangements on the ground on behalf of other operators.

History

The origins of the modern tour operator are often traced to Thomas Cook, who organized railway excursions in England in the 1840s and founded Thomas Cook & Son, which became one of the world's largest travel companies before its liquidation in 2019. Other major operators include TUI Group, the world's largest leisure and travel company.
