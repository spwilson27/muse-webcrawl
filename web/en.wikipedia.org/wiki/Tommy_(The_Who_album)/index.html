Tommy is the fourth studio album by the English rock band the Who, first released as a double album on 23 May 1969. The album was mostly composed by guitarist Pete Townshend, and is a rock opera telling the story of Tommy Walker, a "deaf, dumb and blind boy", including his experiences with life and his relationship with his family. It is widely regarded as the first rock opera and one of the landmark albums of rock music.

Story

Tommy, traumatized after witnessing a killing (involving his father, presumed lost in the First World War, and his mother's lover), becomes psychosomatically deaf, dumb, and blind. He endures abuse from his cousin Kevin and uncle Ernie, becomes a champion pinball player ("Pinball Wizard"), and is eventually cured when a mirror is smashed, regaining his senses ("See Me, Feel Me"). He acquires a following as a supposed messiah, establishes a holiday camp, but his followers rebel against his strict rules, and he retreats into inward reflection. Townshend drew on the teachings of Meher Baba and ideas about consciousness and trauma.

Reception and legacy

The album was a major commercial and critical success, reaching No. 2 in the UK and No. 4 in the US, and it transformed the Who into a top-tier live act. It has sold over 20 million copies worldwide. Tommy was adapted as an orchestral version (1972), a film directed by Ken Russell (1975), and a Broadway musical (1993) that won five Tony Awards. The album was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1998 and is preserved in the US National Recording Registry. Tracks such as "Pinball Wizard", "I'm Free", and "See Me, Feel Me" remain staples of classic rock radio.
