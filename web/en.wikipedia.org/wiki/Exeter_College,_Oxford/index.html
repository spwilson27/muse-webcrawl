Exeter College (in full: The Rector and Scholars of Exeter College in the University of Oxford) is one of the constituent colleges of the University of Oxford in England. It is the fourth-oldest college in the university, founded in 1314 by Walter de Stapledon, Bishop of Exeter, who founded it as a school to educate clergy.

History

The college was founded in 1314 by Walter de Stapledon of Devon, Bishop of Exeter and later treasurer under Edward II, originally for poor scholars from the diocese of Exeter. It was initially known as Stapledon Hall. In the 16th century, donations from alumnus Sir William Petre re-founded and enlarged the college under its present name.

The college is located on Turl Street, where it has stood since its foundation. Notable buildings include the Chapel, designed by Sir George Gilbert Scott in the French Gothic style and built in 1856–1859, and the Jackson Quadrangle. In 2017 the college opened Cohen Quad on Walton Street, its third quadrangle and the first major expansion in its history.

Notable alumni

Exeter's alumni include the writers J. R. R. Tolkien, who read English language and literature at the college, Philip Pullman, Martin Amis, and the economist and broadcaster Stephanie Flanders. The college is also associated with William Morris and Edward Burne-Jones, who were undergraduates together in the 1850s.
