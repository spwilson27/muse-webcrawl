The Adult Attachment Interview (AAI) is a semi-structured clinical research interview developed by Carol George, Nancy Kaplan, and Mary Main in 1985 to assess an individual's state of mind with respect to attachment. Unlike self-report questionnaires, the AAI evaluates how a person talks about their early attachment experiences rather than simply what they report, and it classifies speakers primarily on the coherence of their discourse.

Structure

The interview typically lasts about one hour and contains roughly twenty questions. Respondents are asked to describe their early relationships with each parent, to provide five adjectives for each relationship and to support each adjective with specific memories, and to discuss experiences of separation, rejection, loss, and abuse. They are also asked how these experiences affected their adult personality and their current relationship with their own children.

Classification system

Transcripts are coded using scales developed by Main and Ruth Goldwyn. The principal classifications are:

  • Secure-autonomous (F) — the speaker values attachment, describes experiences coherently, and integrates positive and negative aspects.
  • Dismissing (Ds) — the speaker idealizes or derogates attachment figures while offering little supporting memory, or normalizes painful experiences.
  • Preoccupied (E) — the speaker is entangled in past relationships, with long, angry, or passive discourse that drifts from the questions.
  • Unresolved/disorganized (U/d) — the speaker shows lapses in reasoning or discourse when discussing loss or trauma.

An additional "cannot classify" category is used when no single strategy predominates.

Predictive validity

A landmark finding of AAI research is that a parent's classification predicts, with substantial accuracy, the attachment pattern of their infant as measured in the Strange Situation procedure — in some studies even when the interview is administered before the child is born. This intergenerational transmission is one of the most robust findings in attachment research, though the mechanisms ("the transmission gap") remain debated.

Use in research and clinic

The AAI is widely used in developmental, clinical, and forensic research, including studies of parenting, psychopathology, and psychotherapy outcome. Administration and coding require specialized training, and coding reliability is maintained through certified institutes.
