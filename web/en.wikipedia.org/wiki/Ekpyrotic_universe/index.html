Ekpyrotic universe

Ekpyrotic universe

Type: Cosmological model

Proposed: 2001, by Justin Khoury, Burt Ovrut, Paul Steinhardt and Neil Turok

Framework: String theory / M-theory (brane cosmology)

Related: Cyclic model

The ekpyrotic universe, or ekpyrotic scenario, is a cosmological model of the origin and shaping of the universe. The name comes from the Greek ekpyrosis, meaning "conflagration", which in Stoic philosophy referred to the cyclic destruction and rebirth of the cosmos in fire. The model was introduced in 2001 by Justin Khoury, Burt A. Ovrut, Paul J. Steinhardt and Neil Turok as an alternative to cosmic inflation.

The model

In the ekpyrotic picture, the hot early universe arises from a collision between two three-dimensional worlds (branes) moving along a hidden extra dimension in a higher-dimensional space, inspired by heterotic M-theory. The kinetic energy of the collision is converted into the matter and radiation of the hot Big Bang. Before the collision, the universe undergoes an ultra-slow contraction phase — driven by a scalar field rolling down a steep negative potential — which smooths and flattens space and generates an almost scale-invariant spectrum of density perturbations, playing the role that inflation plays in standard cosmology.

Cyclic version

The ekpyrotic scenario was developed into the cyclic model, in which the collision of branes recurs indefinitely: each "bang" is followed by expansion, dark-energy-driven acceleration that empties and flattens the universe, and then an ultra-slow contraction leading to the next collision. In this view the universe has no beginning or end, and the standard hot Big Bang is a repeating event rather than an origin.

Predictions and criticism

The models predict a spectrum of primordial gravitational waves far weaker than most inflationary models expect, a testable difference. Critics have questioned the naturalness of the potentials and the physics of the brane collision and bounce, and the scenario remains a minority alternative within cosmology.
