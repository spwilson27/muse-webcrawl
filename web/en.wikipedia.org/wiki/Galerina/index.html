Galerina

KingdomFungi
PhylumBasidiomycota
OrderAgaricales
FamilyHymenogastraceae
Type speciesGalerina vittiformis

Galerina is a genus of small, brown-spored agaric mushrooms, most of which grow on decaying wood in forests around the world. The genus contains several hundred described species, many of them extremely difficult to tell apart without microscopy.

Toxicity

Several Galerina species — most notoriously Galerina marginata, the "funeral bell" — contain amatoxins, the same deadly poisons found in the death cap (Amanita phalloides). G. marginata can grow in the same habitats and seasons as sought-after edible or psychoactive wood-inhabiting mushrooms, making careful identification essential.

Identification

Galerina species typically have a rusty-brown spore print, small hygrophanous caps, and distinctive microscopic features such as ornamented spores and characteristic cystidia.
