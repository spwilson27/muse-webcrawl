Nosophobia

Nosophobia, also known as disease phobia or illness anxiety in its broader form, is the irrational and excessive fear of contracting a disease or of having a serious medical condition. The term derives from the Greek nosos (disease) and phobos (fear). Unlike hypochondriasis (illness anxiety disorder), in which the person is preoccupied with the belief that they are already ill, nosophobia centres on the fear of developing or catching a specific disease, such as cancer, heart disease or HIV/AIDS.

Symptoms and behaviour

People with nosophobia may avoid hospitals, doctors or sick people for fear of exposure, or conversely may seek repeated medical testing and reassurance. They may monitor their bodies closely for symptoms, avoid media reports about disease, and experience panic symptoms when confronted with reminders of the feared illness. Fear may intensify during epidemics or after a family member is diagnosed.

Treatment

Treatment generally follows approaches used for other specific phobias and health anxiety: cognitive behavioural therapy, exposure-based techniques to reduce avoidance and reassurance-seeking, and, in some cases, medication such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors. "Medical students' disease", a transient form of health anxiety common among medical students studying diseases, is a related phenomenon.
