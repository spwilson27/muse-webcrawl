Shepherd moon

A shepherd moon (also called a herder moon or watcher moon) is a small natural satellite that clears a gap in a planetary ring or keeps particles within a ring confined. The name is an analogy: the moon's gravity "herds" the ring particles much as a sheepdog controls a flock.

Mechanism

Ring particles orbiting inside the moon's orbit move faster and gain angular momentum when they pass it, while particles outside move slower and lose angular momentum. Over time this exchange pushes material away from the moon's orbit, sharpening ring edges and maintaining gaps.

Examples

Prometheus and Pandora shepherd Saturn's F ring; Pan clears the Encke Gap in Saturn's A ring and Daphnis the Keeler Gap. At Uranus, Cordelia and Ophelia shepherd the epsilon ring, and Neptune's Galatea confines the Adams ring arcs.
