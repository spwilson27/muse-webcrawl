J-pop

J-pop (Japanese: ジェイポップ, jeipoppu), natively also known simply as "pops" (ポップス, poppusu), is a musical genre that entered the musical mainstream of Japan in the 1990s. Modern J-pop has its roots in 1960s pop and rock music, such as the Beatles, and in the kayōkyoku popular songs that preceded it. The term was coined by the Japanese media to distinguish Japanese music from foreign music and is now widely used for most Japanese popular music.

History

The modern industry traces its roots to the Group Sounds bands of the 1960s and the singer-songwriters of the 1970s such as Yumi Matsutoya. In the 1980s, "city pop" and idol singers such as Seiko Matsuda dominated, while Southern All Stars and other bands broadened the market. The term "J-pop" was popularised around 1988 by the radio station J-WAVE; in the 1990s the genre came to encompass acts produced by Tetsuya Komuro, the being "merry" dance units, and later the huge-selling groups of Johnny & Associates and the idol collectives AKB48 and its sister groups. Artists such as Hikaru Utada, Ayumi Hamasaki, Arashi, Perfume and Official HIGE DANdism achieved record sales; Utada's First Love (1999) remains the best-selling album in Japanese history.

Industry and culture

Japan has long been the world's largest market for physical music sales, and J-pop is tightly integrated with television dramas, anime tie-ins, advertising and karaoke. Talent agencies train and package idol performers, while anime songs ("anisong") and Vocaloid music form major subgenres with international fanbases. Since the 2010s, streaming and social media have carried acts such as YOASOBI, Kenshi Yonezu and Ado to global audiences.
