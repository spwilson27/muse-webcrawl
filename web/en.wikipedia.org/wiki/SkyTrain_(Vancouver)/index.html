SkyTrain is the medium-capacity rapid transit system serving the Metro Vancouver region of British Columbia, Canada. Operated by TransLink subsidiaries, SkyTrain has 79.6 km (49.5 mi) of track and uses fully automated trains on grade-separated tracks running on underground and elevated guideways, allowing frequent service. It is the longest fully automated driverless transit system in the Americas and among the longest in the world.

Lines

  • Expo Line — from Waterfront station in downtown Vancouver to King George in Surrey and Production Way–University in Burnaby; opened 1985 for Expo 86.
  • Millennium Line — from VCC–Clark to Lafarge Lake–Douglas in Coquitlam via the Evergreen Extension (2016); Broadway Extension to Arbutus under construction.
  • Canada Line — from Waterfront to Richmond–Brighouse and YVR–Airport, opened 2009 ahead of the Winter Olympics.

Technology

The Expo and Millennium lines use Bombardier/Alstom Innovia Metro (ART) linear-induction-motor trains, fully driverless under automatic train control. The Canada Line, built as a separate concession, uses Hyundai Rotem EMUs with conventional motors. Trains are powered by 600 V DC third rail.

History

The system debuted on 11 December 1985, ahead of Expo 86, for which it served as a showcase of Canadian transit technology. Extensions followed: the SkyBridge over the Fraser River (1990), the Millennium Line (2002), the Canada Line (2009), the Evergreen Extension (2016), and further extensions (Broadway Subway and Surrey–Langley) under construction in the 2020s.
