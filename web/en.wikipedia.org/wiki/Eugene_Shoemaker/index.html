Eugene Shoemaker

Eugene Merle Shoemaker (April 28, 1928 – July 18, 1997) was an American geologist. He co-discovered Comet Shoemaker–Levy 9 with his wife Carolyn Shoemaker and David Levy, and was a pioneer of planetary science and astrogeology, founding the field through his work on impact craters.

Eugene Shoemaker
Born: 28 Apr 1928, Los Angeles
Died: 18 Jul 1997, near Alice Springs, Australia
Known for: Comet Shoemaker–Levy 9, impact crater studies, Apollo training

Career

Working for the U.S. Geological Survey, Shoemaker proved that Meteor Crater in Arizona was an impact crater, founded the USGS Astrogeology Research Program, and trained Apollo astronauts in geology. Health reasons kept him from becoming the first geologist on the Moon.

Comet and death

Shoemaker–Levy 9 broke apart and collided with Jupiter in July 1994, providing the first direct observation of an extraterrestrial collision. Shoemaker died in a car crash in Australia while studying craters; some of his ashes were carried to the Moon aboard Lunar Prospector, making him the only person interred on the Moon.
