The Huntress, also known as Helena Wayne, is a fictional superheroine appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. Created by writer Paul Levitz and artist Joe Staton, she debuted in DC Super Stars #17 (1977). Helena Wayne is the daughter of Batman (Bruce Wayne) and Catwoman (Selina Kyle) of Earth-Two, the parallel world home to DC's Golden Age heroes. Trained by her parents, she became a lawyer and, after her mother's death, took up crime-fighting as the Huntress.

Publication history

Helena Wayne headlined backup features in Batman Family and joined the Justice Society of America in the pages of All-Star Comics. She was killed during the 1985 crossover Crisis on Infinite Earths, and the character was written out of continuity when DC streamlined its multiverse. A new, unrelated Huntress, Helena Bertinelli, was introduced in 1989 using the same name and costume motif.

With the restoration of the multiverse and the launch of The New 52 in 2011, Helena Wayne returned as a central character in the series Earth 2 and the team-up book Worlds' Finest, in which she was stranded on the main DC Earth alongside Power Girl (Karen Starr) and operated for a time under the identity of Helena Bertinelli.

Fictional character biography

Helena Wayne grew up the daughter of a retired Bruce Wayne, who had become Gotham City's police commissioner, and a reformed Selina Kyle. After Catwoman was blackmailed into one last crime and killed, Helena donned a costume modeled on both parents' motifs, brought the killer to justice, and continued as the Huntress, balancing a legal career with membership in the Justice Society. In her later appearances she became a close ally — almost a sister — to Power Girl, the Earth-Two Supergirl.

Powers and equipment

Helena has no superhuman powers. She is a superb athlete, detective, and hand-to-hand combatant trained by Batman and Catwoman, and her signature weapon is a compact crossbow, continuing a motif introduced by the original Golden Age Huntress, a villainess named Paula Brooks.
