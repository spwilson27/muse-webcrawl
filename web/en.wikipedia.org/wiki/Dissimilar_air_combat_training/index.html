Dissimilar air combat training

Dissimilar air combat training (DACT) is flight training in which fighter pilots practise air combat manoeuvring against aircraft of a different type from their own, rather than against similar aircraft. This exposes aircrew to the performance characteristics and tactics of potential adversaries.

History

DACT became institutionalised in the United States military after the Vietnam War, when analyses such as the Ault Report criticised the decline in air-to-air training. The U.S. Navy established the TOPGUN school (Navy Fighter Weapons School) in 1969, using lightweight aircraft such as the A-4 Skyhawk and F-5 to simulate Soviet MiG fighters; the U.S. Air Force created its Aggressor squadrons and the Red Flag exercises in the 1970s.

Practice

Aggressor (or adversary) squadrons fly aircraft painted in foreign colour schemes and employ enemy tactics, techniques, and procedures. Today some adversary support is provided by private contractor companies flying retired military jets. DACT remains a core element of advanced fighter training in many air forces.
