Turkish Van
OriginEastern Turkey (Lake Van region)
CoatSemi-longhair, white with coloured head and tail
TemperamentEnergetic, intelligent, fond of water
RecognitionFIFe, GCCF, TICA

The Turkish Van is a breed of domestic cat originating in the Lake Van region of eastern Turkey. It is distinguished by its chalk-white semi-longhaired coat with colour restricted mainly to the head and tail — a pattern known in cat fancy as the "Van pattern" — and by its unusual fondness for water, which has earned it the nickname "the swimming cat".

History

Cats of this type have lived around Lake Van for centuries. The modern breed dates to 1955, when British photographer Laura Lushington was given two kittens in Turkey and brought them back to England, finding them strikingly different from other cats she knew. Breeding from these and additional imports produced the breed recognized by the GCCF in 1969 and by FIFe in 1971. All registered Turkish Vans trace back to these imports, as Turkey restricts export of the cats.

Characteristics

Turkish Vans are large, muscular, and slow to mature, reaching full size at three to five years. The coat is cashmere-like and water-resistant, with no woolly undercoat; it is noticeably thicker in winter. Markings are typically auburn or cream, though other colours occur. Eyes may be amber, blue, or odd-eyed (one of each). The breed is active, intelligent, and often bonds strongly with a single person.

Van pattern

The term "van pattern" is used for any cat whose colouring is mostly white with colour only on the head and tail, a pattern caused by the piebald white-spotting gene at its most extreme expression.
