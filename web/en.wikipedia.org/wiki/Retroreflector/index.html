Retroreflector

A retroreflector (sometimes called a retroflector or cataphote) is a device or surface that reflects radiation (usually light) back to its source with minimum scattering. This works over a wide range of angles of incidence, unlike a planar mirror, which does this only if it is exactly perpendicular to the wave front. The effect is achieved with either a set of three mutually perpendicular mirrors (a corner reflector) or a transparent sphere or micro-beads with a reflective backing.

Types

Corner reflectors consist of three perpendicular reflecting planes meeting at a corner; a ray entering the corner is reflected three times and exits parallel to its incoming direction. Cat's eye reflectors use a spherical lens with a reflective surface at its focal plane. Mass-produced sheeting for road signs and safety clothing embeds millions of tiny glass beads or microprisms in a transparent layer.

Applications

Retroreflectors are used in road signs, pavement markers, bicycle and vehicle reflectors, high-visibility clothing, and surveying prisms. Arrays of corner reflectors left on the Moon by the Apollo missions (and by Soviet Lunokhod rovers) are still used in lunar laser ranging experiments that measure the Earth–Moon distance to millimeter precision. Satellites also carry retroreflectors for satellite laser ranging, and similar devices are used in optical communications and in radar enhancement of small boats.
