Selma to Montgomery marches
DateMarch 1965
LocationSelma to Montgomery, Alabama, USA
OutcomeContributed to Voting Rights Act of 1965

The Selma to Montgomery marches were three protest marches in March 1965 along the 54-mile highway from Selma, Alabama, to the state capital Montgomery, organized to demand African American voting rights as part of the civil rights movement.

Bloody Sunday and Turnaround Tuesday

On 7 March 1965 — "Bloody Sunday" — state troopers violently attacked about 600 marchers led by John Lewis and Hosea Williams on the Edmund Pettus Bridge; televised images shocked the nation. Martin Luther King Jr. led a second, symbolic march on 9 March ("Turnaround Tuesday"). Activists Viola Liuzzo and minister James Reeb were murdered during the campaign.

Third march

Protected by a federal court order and federalized National Guard, some 8,000 marchers set out on 21 March, arriving in Montgomery on 25 March, where about 25,000 gathered at the Alabama State Capitol as King delivered his address. Months later President Lyndon B. Johnson signed the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

Legacy

The route is now the Selma to Montgomery National Historic Trail. See also Seneca Falls Convention for another landmark rights campaign.
