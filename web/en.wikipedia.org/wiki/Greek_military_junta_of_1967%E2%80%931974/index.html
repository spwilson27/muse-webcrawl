The Greek military junta of 1967–1974, commonly known as the Regime of the Colonels (Greek: Καθεστὼς τῶν Συνταγματαρχῶν), was a right-wing military junta that ruled Greece from 1967 to 1974. On 21 April 1967, just weeks before scheduled elections, a group of mid-ranking army officers led by Colonel Georgios Papadopoulos seized power in a coup d'état, suspending civil liberties, banning political parties, and imprisoning, exiling, or torturing thousands of suspected opponents.

Rule and resistance

The junta imposed censorship, ruled under a 1968 constitution approved by a rigged referendum, and abolished the monarchy in 1973 after King Constantine II's failed counter-coup. Resistance ranged from exile politics and clandestine groups to the Athens Polytechnic uprising of November 1973, which was crushed with tanks. Hardliner Dimitrios Ioannidis then overthrew Papadopoulos and installed a more repressive government.

Collapse

In July 1974 the junta sponsored a coup against President Makarios of Cyprus, prompting Turkey's invasion of Cyprus. The regime collapsed within days; Konstantinos Karamanlis returned from exile to head a national unity government, beginning the Metapolitefsi — Greece's transition to democracy, followed by a referendum abolishing the monarchy and trials of the junta leaders in 1975.
