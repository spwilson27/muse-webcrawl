College basketball

College basketball is basketball that is played by teams of student-athletes at universities and colleges. In the United States, the sport is governed principally by the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA), whose Division I men's and women's championship tournaments — "March Madness" — are among the most watched sporting events in the country.

History

The first intercollegiate games were played in the 1890s, shortly after James Naismith invented the game in 1891. The NCAA tournament began in 1939; UCLA under John Wooden won ten titles between 1964 and 1975. Women's college basketball grew rapidly after Title IX (1972), with the NCAA holding its first women's championship in 1982.

Rules differences

College rules differ from the NBA's in shot-clock length (30 seconds), game structure (two 20-minute halves for men; four quarters for women) and three-point distance, though these have gradually converged.
