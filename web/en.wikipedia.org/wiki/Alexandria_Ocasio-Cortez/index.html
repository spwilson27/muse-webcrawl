Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (born 13 October 1989), often known by her initials AOC, is an American politician and activist serving since 2019 as the U.S. representative for New York's 14th congressional district, covering parts of the Bronx and Queens. A member of the Democratic Party and of the Democratic Socialists of America, she is one of the most prominent progressive voices in Congress.

Early life and 2018 election

Born in the Bronx to a Puerto Rican family and raised in Yorktown Heights, Ocasio-Cortez studied international relations and economics at Boston University. She worked as a bartender and waitress in Manhattan and as an organiser for Bernie Sanders's 2016 presidential campaign. In the June 2018 Democratic primary she defeated ten-term incumbent Joe Crowley, the fourth-ranking House Democrat, in one of the biggest upsets of the midterm cycle; she won the general election and, at 29, became the youngest woman ever elected to Congress.

In Congress

Ocasio-Cortez co-sponsored the Green New Deal resolution with Senator Ed Markey in 2019, and advocates Medicare for All, a federal jobs guarantee, abolition of ICE in its current form, and tuition-free public college. She is a member of the informal progressive group known as "the Squad". Her extensive social media following and televised committee questioning have made her a national figure, admired on the left and a frequent target of conservative criticism. She has been re-elected in 2020, 2022 and 2024.
