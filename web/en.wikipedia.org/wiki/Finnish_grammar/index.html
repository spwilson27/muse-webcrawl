Finnish grammar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Finnish nouns inflect in around fifteen cases.

The grammar of Finnish, like that of other Finnic languages, is agglutinative: suffixes are attached to stems to express case, number, possession, and other categories. Finnish has no grammatical gender and no articles, but it has a rich system of noun cases, vowel harmony, and consonant gradation.

Noun cases

Finnish is usually described as having 15 cases, including the nominative, genitive, accusative, partitive, and a set of locative cases (inessive "in", elative "out of", illative "into", adessive "on/at", ablative "from on", allative "onto") as well as the essive, translative, abessive, instructive, and comitative. The partitive case is used for partial objects, mass nouns, and after numerals.

Phonological processes

Vowel harmony requires that suffix vowels be either front (ä, ö, y) or back (a, o, u) depending on the vowels in the stem. Consonant gradation alternates strong and weak grades of the stops p, t, and k (e.g. kukka "flower" : genitive kukan).

Verbs

Finnish verbs conjugate for person and number in present and past tenses, have four infinitives and several participles, and form a distinctive negative verb (ei) that carries person marking while the main verb takes a special connegative form.
