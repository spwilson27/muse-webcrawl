Bilhana

Bilhana was an 11th-century Kashmiri poet in Sanskrit. He is best known as the author of the Caurapañcāśikā ("Fifty Verses of the Love-Thief"), a celebrated lyric poem, and the Vikramāṅkadevacarita, an epic biography of his patron, the Western Chalukya king Vikramaditya VI.

Life

Bilhana was born in Khonamukha (modern Khonmoh) in Kashmir around the mid-11th century. He left Kashmir in search of fame and fortune, travelling through northern and central India before settling at the Chalukya court in Kalyana, where he was appointed court poet (vidyapati) to Vikramaditya VI.

Works

The Caurapañcāśikā recounts, in fifty stanzas, the poet's secret love for a princess (in legend, the daughter of King Madanabhirama of Panchala) and was supposedly composed while awaiting execution for the affair; charmed by the verses, the king pardoned him. The poem became one of the most copied and imitated works of Sanskrit erotic lyric poetry.
