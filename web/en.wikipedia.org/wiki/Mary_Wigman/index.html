Mary Wigman (born Karoline Sophie Marie Wiegmann; 13 November 1886 – 18 September 1973) was a German dancer and choreographer, notable as the pioneer of expressionist dance (Ausdruckstanz), dance therapy, and training dancers without pointe shoes. She is credited for the innovation of eurhythmics upon Rudolf von Laban's notion of choreutics, and brought the expressive, improvisational, often mask-accompanied German modern dance to prominence, influencing generations including Hanya Holm, Kurt Jooss, and, through her students, the beginnings of American modern dance pedagogy.

Life and career

Wigman studied rhythmic gymnastics with Émile Jaques-Dalcroze at Hellerau, then joined Rudolf Laban at Monte Verità in Ascona and Munich, becoming his assistant and developing her own work. Her breakthrough came with solo dances performed in silence or to percussion of her own devising, most famously the Hexentanz ("Witch Dance", first version 1914, definitive 1926), performed crouched, masked, with convulsive gestures — a radical break from ballet's elevation and prettiness.

In 1920 she founded her school in Dresden, which became the centre of Central European modern dance; her company toured Europe and the United States in the early 1930s to great acclaim. Students such as Hanya Holm exported her method to America. Her relationship with the Nazi regime was ambivalent: she choreographed for the 1936 Berlin Olympics festivities, but her school was closed in 1942 and she fell from favour. After the war she reopened a studio in Leipzig, then moved to West Berlin in 1949, where she taught until shortly before her death in 1973.

Style and legacy

Wigman's dance used tension and release, fall and recovery, grounded movement, breath, and improvisation to express elemental states of being; she often danced without music or with gongs and drums, and used masks to depersonalize the figure. She is regarded as one of the founders of modern dance in Europe and of dance-movement therapy, and her Dresden school model spread expressionist dance across the continent.
