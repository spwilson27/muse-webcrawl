Pilsner (also pilsener or simply pils) is a type of pale lager. It takes its name from the Bohemian city of Plzeň (German: Pilsen), where it was first produced in 1842 by Bavarian brewer Josef Groll. The world's first pale lager, Pilsner Urquell, is still produced there today, and modern pilsner is regarded as the ancestor of the pale lagers that now dominate world beer production.

History

The citizens of Plzeň, dissatisfied with the quality of their top-fermented beer — in 1838 they publicly dumped 36 casks of it — founded a new brewery, the Citizens' Brewery, and hired Groll, who brought with him the Bavarian lager method of cold fermentation and storage. Combining Plzeň's exceptionally soft water, pale English-style malt, local Saaz hops and lager yeast, he produced a clear, golden beer unlike the dark, cloudy ales of the time. Its colour and clarity were shown off by the newly affordable glassware of the era, and the style spread rapidly across Europe and the world.

Styles

The Beer Judge Certification Program recognises several pilsner styles. Czech (Bohemian) pilsner is golden, full-bodied and softly hopped with Saaz. German pils is paler, drier and more bitter, with noble-hop aroma. American pilsner, developed by German immigrants, often uses corn or rice adjuncts. "Pilsner" is a protected designation in the Czech Republic, and the European Union recognises České pivo (Czech beer) as a protected geographical indication.
