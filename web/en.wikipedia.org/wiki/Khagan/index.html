Khagan

Khagan or Qaghan (Old Turkic: 𐰴𐰍𐰣; Mongolian: хаан) is a title of imperial rank in Turkic and Mongolic languages, roughly equivalent to "emperor" or "khan of khans". It denoted the supreme ruler of a khaganate (empire) and ranked above lesser khans.

History

The title first appears among the Xianbei and was adopted by the Rouran Khaganate in the 4th–5th centuries CE. It was used by the Göktürk khaganates, the Uyghur Khaganate, and later by the Mongols: Genghis Khan was proclaimed khagan in 1206, and his successors ruled the Mongol Empire and its successor states as khagans. The Chinese emperor was sometimes addressed by steppe peoples as Tian Kehan, "Heavenly Khagan", notably Tang Taizong.

Usage

While khan could be borne by many chiefs, khagan implied sovereignty over other rulers. In the Mongol Empire the title (often rendered "Great Khan") belonged to the supreme ruler, whose election by a kuriltai was required even by regional khans of the Golden Horde, Ilkhanate, Chagatai Khanate and Yuan dynasty.
