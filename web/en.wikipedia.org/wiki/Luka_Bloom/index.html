Luka Bloom (born Barry Moore, 23 May 1955) is an Irish folk singer-songwriter. He is the younger brother of folk singer Christy Moore. He adopted the stage name "Luka Bloom" in the late 1980s — "Luka" after the Suzanne Vega song of the same name, and "Bloom" after Leopold Bloom, the protagonist of James Joyce's Ulysses.

Career

Moore began performing under his birth name, releasing albums such as Treaty Stone (1978). In 1987 he moved to the United States, performing in New York City and Washington, D.C., and reinvented himself as Luka Bloom. His album Riverside (1990) brought him wider attention, and The Acoustic Motorbike (1992) — which included a celebrated folk cover of LL Cool J's "I Need Love" — became his best-known record. Subsequent albums include Turf (1994), Salty Heaven (1998), Innocence (2006), Dreams in America (2010) and Frugalisto (2016).

Style

Bloom is known for a distinctive guitar style blending folk fingerpicking with flamenco-influenced strumming and lush, effects-laden accompaniment, paired with songs about Ireland, love, travel and social justice. He has lived for many years in County Clare, on Ireland's west coast, whose landscape informs much of his later work.
