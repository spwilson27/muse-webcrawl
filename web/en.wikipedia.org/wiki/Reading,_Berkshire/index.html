Reading (pronounced "RED-ing") is a town and unitary authority area in Berkshire, England. Located in the Thames Valley at the confluence of the River Thames and the River Kennet, it lies about 40 miles (64 km) west of London. It is the county town of Berkshire and one of the largest settlements in England without city status.

History

Reading began as a Saxon settlement called Readingas. It was occupied by the Danes in 871 and grew around Reading Abbey, founded by Henry I in 1121, which became one of the wealthiest monasteries in England before its dissolution in 1538. The town later prospered through the cloth trade, brewing, and seed and biscuit manufacturing — famously home to Huntley & Palmers biscuits and Simonds Brewery.

Economy

Today Reading is a major commercial centre in the Thames Valley, sometimes called the UK's "Silicon Valley" for its concentration of technology companies, including Microsoft and Oracle UK headquarters, alongside insurance and financial services. The Oracle shopping centre anchors the retail core.

Transport and culture

Reading station is a major rail interchange on the Great Western Main Line, with Elizabeth line services to London. The town hosts the annual Reading Festival, one of Britain's largest rock music festivals, held since 1971. The University of Reading, founded in 1892, is known for agriculture, meteorology, and the Henley Business School. Reading F.C. plays at the Madejski Stadium (Select Car Leasing Stadium).
