The Argentinazo, also known in Spanish as the Argentinazo or Cacerolazo, was a period of civil unrest and rioting in Argentina in December 2001, during the country's severe economic crisis of 1998–2002. The unrest culminated in the resignation of President Fernando de la Rúa on 20 December 2001.

Cacerolazo protests in Buenos Aires, December 2001

Background

Argentina had maintained a currency board pegging the peso one-to-one to the US dollar since 1991. By the late 1990s the economy had entered a deep recession, unemployment and poverty rose, and the government of Fernando de la Rúa imposed austerity measures. In early December 2001, Economy Minister Domingo Cavallo imposed the corralito, freezing bank deposits and sharply limiting cash withdrawals, which enraged the middle class.

Events

Protests began in mid-December and escalated into riots and looting across the country. On the night of 19 December, thousands of people in Buenos Aires took to the streets banging pots and pans — the cacerolazo — and marched on the Casa Rosada and Congress chanting "¡Que se vayan todos!" ("Out with them all!"). De la Rúa declared a state of siege, which only intensified protests. Police repression in the Plaza de Mayo left several dead; in total 39 people died in unrest across Argentina. De la Rúa resigned on 20 December and famously left the Casa Rosada by helicopter.

Aftermath

Argentina cycled through several interim presidents in the following two weeks before Eduardo Duhalde was appointed in January 2002. The convertibility regime was abandoned, the peso devalued, and Argentina defaulted on its sovereign debt. The Argentinazo became a symbol of popular rejection of political elites and inspired protest repertoires across Latin America.
