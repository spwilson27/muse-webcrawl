The White Mountains of California are a triangular fault-block mountain range facing the Sierra Nevada across the upper Owens Valley in eastern California. They extend for roughly 60 miles (97 km) as a greatly elevated plateau cut by many deep canyons, at the end of which lies White Mountain Peak, at 14,252 feet (4,344 m) the third-highest summit in California.

Geography

The range lies largely in Mono and Inyo counties, with a small northern extension into Nevada. It is separated from the Sierra Nevada by the Owens Valley to the west and from the Inyo Mountains by Westgard Pass to the south. The eastern flank drops steeply toward Fish Lake Valley and Deep Springs Valley.

Flora and fauna

The White Mountains are home to some of the oldest living organisms on Earth. The ancient bristlecone pine (Pinus longaeva) groves in the Schulman Grove include the tree known as Methuselah, more than 4,800 years old. Lower slopes support sagebrush and pinyon-juniper woodland, while the alpine zone hosts cushion plants adapted to the harsh, dry conditions. Wildlife includes bighorn sheep, pronghorn, golden eagles, and the White Mountains wild horse herd.

Research

The White Mountain Research Center, operated by the University of California, maintains research stations at several elevations, including the Barcroft Station at 12,470 feet (3,800 m), supporting studies in high-altitude physiology, astronomy, and alpine ecology. The range's dry air and high elevation make it attractive for astronomical observation.

Recreation

A rough road leads to the Patriarch Grove of bristlecone pines and the trailhead for White Mountain Peak, one of California's most accessible fourteeners. The Ancient Bristlecone Pine Forest is administered by the Inyo National Forest.
