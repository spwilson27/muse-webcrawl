Formed1 April 1998
TypeExecutive agency
Parent departmentHM Treasury
HeadquartersLondon, United Kingdom

The UK Debt Management Office (DMO) is an executive agency of HM Treasury responsible for carrying out the UK government's debt management policy of minimising the cost of financing the government's borrowing over the long term, while taking account of risk, and for managing the government's cash flows. It was established on 1 April 1998, taking over responsibility for gilt issuance from the Bank of England following the Bank's operational independence for monetary policy in 1997.

Functions

The DMO's main activities are:

  • Issuing gilts (UK government bonds), including conventional, index-linked, and green gilts, through auctions and syndications;
  • Issuing Treasury bills and managing the government's short-term cash position;
  • Managing the National Loans Fund's lending operations, including loans to local authorities through the Public Works Loan Board function it assumed in 2020;
  • Administering certain public-sector funds and the Commissioners for the Reduction of the National Debt.

Gilts

The DMO announces an annual financing remit set by the Chancellor of the Exchequer, detailing the planned volume and structure of gilt sales. It operates through Gilt-edged Market Makers (GEMMs) and publishes auction calendars, results, and data on the outstanding gilt portfolio, which amounts to more than £2 trillion in nominal value.
