Sadegh Sharafkandi

Sadegh Sharafkandi (Kurdish: Sadiq Şerefkendî; 11 January 1938 – 17 September 1992) was an Iranian Kurdish political leader and the secretary-general of the Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan (PDKI). He was assassinated in Berlin in 1992 in the incident known as the Mykonos restaurant assassinations.

Early life and education

Sharafkandi was born in the village of Taragha near Bukan in West Azerbaijan province, Iran. He studied chemistry, earning a degree in Tehran before travelling to France, where he obtained a doctorate in analytical chemistry from the University of Paris in 1976. During his years in Paris he became active in Kurdish student organisations and joined the PDKI.

Political career

After the Iranian Revolution of 1979, Sharafkandi returned to Iranian Kurdistan and rose through the party's ranks, serving in its political bureau. When the party's leader Abdul Rahman Ghassemlou was assassinated in Vienna in July 1989 during talks with Iranian envoys, Sharafkandi succeeded him as secretary-general and continued the party's opposition to the Islamic Republic from exile.

Assassination

On 17 September 1992, Sharafkandi and three colleagues were shot dead by gunmen at the Mykonos Greek restaurant in Berlin, where they had gathered after attending the congress of the Socialist International. A German court, in its 1997 verdict, concluded that the killings had been ordered at the highest levels of the Iranian leadership — a landmark ruling that caused a diplomatic crisis between Iran and the European Union.
