Royal Australian Regiment

The Royal Australian Regiment (RAR) is the parent administrative regiment for the regular infantry battalions of the Australian Army. Raised in 1948 from battalions that had served with the British Commonwealth Occupation Force in Japan, it has provided the core of Australia's regular infantry ever since, with battalions serving in Korea, Malaya, Borneo, Vietnam, Somalia, East Timor, Iraq and Afghanistan. Its motto is "Duty First", and its regimental march is "The Royal Australian Regiment".

History

Following the Second World War, Australia committed an infantry force to the occupation of Japan. The three battalions of the 34th Brigade — the 65th, 66th and 67th Battalions — were redesignated in November 1948 as the 1st, 2nd and 3rd Battalions of the newly created Australian Regiment, which received the "Royal" prefix in March 1949. The 3rd Battalion (3 RAR) was the first Australian unit committed to the Korean War, fighting at Kapyong in April 1951, where it helped earn a United States Presidential Unit Citation.

The regiment expanded greatly during the 1960s, reaching nine battalions for the Vietnam War, in which RAR battalions fought at battles including Long Tan (6 RAR, 1966) and Coral–Balmoral (1 and 3 RAR, 1968). Post-Vietnam reductions and amalgamations reduced the regiment, and since the 2010s it has comprised seven battalions: 1 RAR (motorised, Townsville), 2 RAR (amphibious, Townsville), 3 RAR (mechanised, Townsville), 5 RAR and 7 RAR (mechanised, Darwin and Adelaide), 6 RAR (motorised, Brisbane) and 8/9 RAR (motorised, Brisbane). Battalions rotate through readiness cycles under Plan Beersheba and its successors, and the regiment contributes the rifle companies to Australian battle groups deployed overseas.

Traditions

Each battalion has its own colour patches, mascots and traditions within a common regimental identity; the regiment's birthday, 23 November, is celebrated across all battalions. The Colours of the battalions carry battle honours from Korea, Vietnam and later campaigns.
