Antinutrient

Antinutrients
ExamplesPhytates, lectins, tannins, oxalates
Found inGrains, legumes, seeds, leafy vegetables

Antinutrients are natural or synthetic compounds in food that interfere with the absorption of nutrients. They are found in many plant foods, where they often serve as defences against pests and herbivores.

Examples

Phytic acid (phytate) in grains and legumes binds minerals such as iron, zinc and calcium. Oxalates in spinach can reduce calcium absorption. Lectins in raw legumes can damage the gut lining, and protease inhibitors in soy interfere with protein digestion. Tannins bind proteins and iron.

Reduction

Soaking, sprouting, fermenting and cooking substantially reduce most antinutrients, which is why traditional food preparation often employs these methods. In balanced diets, antinutrients are generally not a concern and some, such as phytate, may have antioxidant benefits.
