Bram Moolenaar (1961 – 3 August 2023) was a Dutch software engineer best known as the creator and long-time lead developer of Vim, a popular text editor used by programmers worldwide. He was a member of the Dutch Unix user group NLUUG and worked for much of his career as a software engineer, including at Google in Zurich from 2006, where he worked on Google Calendar.

Vim

Moolenaar began work on Vim ("Vi IMproved") in 1988, initially for the Amiga computer, basing it on the Stevie editor; he released it publicly in 1991 as "Vi IMitation" and later renamed it. Vim grew into one of the most widely used text editors, ported to virtually every operating system. Moolenaar remained its benevolent dictator for life (BDFL) for over three decades, coordinating development through the Vim developer community and mailing list, and was a prominent advocate of charityware: Vim encouraged users to donate to ICCF Holland, a charity supporting children in Uganda, where Moolenaar volunteered.

Other work

Moolenaar also created A-A-P, a software build tool, developed the Zimbu programming language, and was active in the Dutch free and open-source software community. He received an award from NLUUG in recognition of his work on Vim.

Death

Moolenaar died on 3 August 2023 at the age of 62, following a rapidly progressing illness. His death was announced by his family on the Vim community forum; development of Vim continued under maintainers from the community and the Vim Dev team.
