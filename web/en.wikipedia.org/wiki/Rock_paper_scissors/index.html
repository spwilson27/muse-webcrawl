Rock paper scissors

Rock paper scissors (also known by other orderings, such as scissors paper rock, or roshambo) is a hand game originating in China, where it dates to the Han dynasty. It was imported to Japan, where as janken it spread widely, and reached the West in the early 20th century.

Rules

Two players simultaneously form one of three shapes with a hand: rock (a fist), paper (a flat hand), or scissors (two extended fingers). Rock crushes scissors, scissors cuts paper, and paper covers rock. Identical shapes tie and the throw is repeated. The game has a cyclic, non-transitive dominance structure and is widely used as a fair random selection method.

Strategy

In game theory the only optimal strategy is to choose uniformly at random. Human players show exploitable patterns, and computer programs and world championship competitors exploit tendencies such as beginners favouring rock. Variants add extra gestures, such as lizard and Spock.
