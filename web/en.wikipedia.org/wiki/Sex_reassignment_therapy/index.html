Sex reassignment therapy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Gender-affirming care encompasses psychological, hormonal, and surgical interventions.

Sex reassignment therapy (SRT), also known as gender-affirming therapy, is the medical aspect of gender transition — that is, modifying the sex characteristics of a person to better match their gender identity. It can consist of hormone replacement therapy (HRT) to modify secondary sex characteristics, sex reassignment surgery to alter primary sex characteristics, and other procedures such as permanent hair removal for trans women.

In appropriately evaluated cases of severe gender dysphoria, sex reassignment therapy is often the best when standards of care are followed. Many medical organizations have adopted standards of care, including the World Professional Association for Transgender Health (WPATH).

Terminology

Medical treatment of gender dysphoria may be referred to by many terms, including sex reassignment therapy, gender-affirming therapy, and transsexual medicine.

History

The physician Magnus Hirschfeld coined the term transsexualism in 1923. By 1949, hormonal and surgical treatments were available; the work of Harry Benjamin in the United States in the 1950s and 1960s established the field, and Benjamin's The Transsexual Phenomenon (1966) argued for medical treatment. Standards of care were first published by the Harry Benjamin International Gender Dysphoria Association in 1979.

Standards of care

The Standards of Care for the Health of Transsexual, Transgender, and Gender Nonconforming People published by WPATH outline assessment and treatment guidelines. They recommend psychotherapy or assessment before referral for hormones or surgery, though requirements differ by intervention and by version.

Psychological treatment

Psychological treatments aim to help people adapt to their gender identity and alleviate distress; attempts to change a person's gender identity (conversion therapy) are considered unethical by most major medical associations.

Hormone therapy

Hormone replacement therapy involves administration of sex hormones and hormone suppressants to alter secondary sex characteristics, such as body hair, skin texture, fat distribution, and muscle mass.

Surgery

Sex reassignment surgery refers to surgical procedures that alter the body, including vaginoplasty, phalloplasty, metoidioplasty, mastectomy, and facial feminization surgery.
