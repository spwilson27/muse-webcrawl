PrimeGrid

PrimeGrid is a volunteer computing project that searches for very large prime numbers using the BOINC platform. The project, based at the website primegrid.com, was founded in 2005 (originally under the name PrimeGrid, emerging from the earlier "Message@home" effort) and is one of the most successful distributed prime-search projects, having discovered many of the largest known primes in several families.

Overview

PrimeGrid runs a collection of subprojects, each targeting a different form of prime. Participants donate idle CPU (and later GPU) time; the server distributes small work units, such as primality tests of individual candidate numbers using LLR (Lucas–Lehmer–Riesel) software, or sieving tasks that eliminate composite candidates in bulk before testing.

Subprojects and discoveries

Notable subprojects have included searches for twin primes, Sophie Germain primes, Proth primes, Cullen and Woodall primes, and primes of the form k·2n − 1 and k·2n + 1. PrimeGrid organised the effort that found several record Proth and Cullen primes, and its generalised Fermat prime search has found some of the largest known non-Mersenne primes. In 2010, a PrimeGrid participant found a prime factor in the search related to the Sierpiński problem, and the project's "Sierpiński/Riesel Base 5" and related efforts address similar covering-set problems.

Technology

Most primality testing uses the LLR program by Jean Penné, later supplemented by GPU-based applications. Sieving applications such as tpsieve and gcwsieve prepare ranges by removing candidates with small factors, greatly reducing the number of expensive primality tests required.
