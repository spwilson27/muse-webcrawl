Tensor

In mathematics, a tensor is an algebraic object that describes a multilinear relationship between sets of algebraic objects related to a vector space. Tensors may map between different objects such as vectors, scalars, and even other tensors. There are many types of tensor, including scalars and vectors (which are the simplest tensors), dual vectors, multilinear maps between vector spaces, and even operations such as the dot product.

Tensors are defined independently of any basis, although they are often referred to by their components in a basis related to a particular coordinate system; those components form an array, which can be thought of as a high-dimensional matrix. Just as a vector in an n-dimensional space is represented by a one-dimensional array of n components, a tensor of a given type can be represented by an array whose dimension equals the sum of its covariant and contravariant ranks.

Transformation law

The defining property of a tensor is how its components transform under a change of basis: each index transforms either like a vector (contravariant, written as a superscript) or like a dual vector (covariant, written as a subscript). A tensor with m contravariant and n covariant indices is said to be of type (or rank) (m, n). Scalars are tensors of rank (0, 0), vectors of rank (1, 0), and linear maps can be expressed as tensors of rank (1, 1).

Applications

Tensors are fundamental throughout physics and engineering. The stress and strain of continuum mechanics are described by second-order tensors; the field equations of general relativity are written in terms of the Einstein tensor and the metric tensor; and tensor calculus, developed by Ricci-Curbastro and Levi-Civita around 1900, provides the coordinate-free machinery for differential geometry. In computer science, multi-dimensional arrays of data are routinely called tensors, notably in machine-learning frameworks.
