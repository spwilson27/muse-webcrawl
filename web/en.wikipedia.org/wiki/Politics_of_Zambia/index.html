Politics of Zambia

The politics of Zambia take place in a framework of a presidential representative democratic republic, whereby the president is both head of state and head of government, in a multi-party system. Legislative power is vested in the National Assembly.

History

Zambia gained independence from Britain in 1964 under Kenneth Kaunda (UNIP). It was a one-party state from 1972 to 1991, when Frederick Chiluba's MMD won multi-party elections. Later presidents include Levy Mwanawasa, Rupiah Banda, Michael Sata, Edgar Lungu and Hakainde Hichilema (elected 2021, UPND).

Branches

Executive: president elected for five years. Legislature: 166-seat National Assembly. Judiciary headed by the Constitutional Court and Supreme Court.
