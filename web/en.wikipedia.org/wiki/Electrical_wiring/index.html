Electrical wiring

Electrical wiring is an electrical installation of cabling and associated devices such as switches, distribution boards, sockets and light fittings in a structure. Wiring is subject to safety standards for design and installation, which vary by country and are embodied in regulations such as the National Electrical Code in the United States and BS 7671 in the United Kingdom.

Materials and methods

Conductors are almost always copper (occasionally aluminium for large feeders), insulated with PVC or other polymers. Cables may be run in conduit, trunking, or as sheathed cable assemblies within walls and ceilings. Colour codes identify live (hot), neutral and earth (ground) conductors; these codes differ between regions and eras.

Safety

Circuits are protected by fuses or circuit breakers sized to the cable, and residual-current devices protect against shock. Poor wiring — undersized conductors, loose connections, damaged insulation — is a major cause of electrical fires, so installations typically require inspection and periodic testing.

History

Early installations of the 1880s used knob-and-tube wiring; armoured cable, conduit systems and modern thermoplastic cables followed. Aluminium branch wiring installed in some North American homes in the 1960s–70s later proved a fire risk at terminations.
