Wedderburn's little theorem

In mathematics, Wedderburn's little theorem states that every finite domain is a field — equivalently, every finite division ring is commutative. It is named after Joseph Wedderburn, who gave the first essentially correct proof in 1905.

Statement and significance

The theorem shows that finiteness alone forces commutativity of multiplication in a division ring, a strong restriction with no counterpart for infinite division rings (the quaternions being the classic infinite example that remains noncommutative yet is a division ring, though infinite; finite noncommutative examples cannot exist at all).

Proofs

Several proofs exist, including Wedderburn's original, proofs using counting of conjugacy classes in the multiplicative group, group-theoretic arguments by Witt, and topological proofs. A standard proof uses the class equation on the multiplicative group of the division ring and properties of cyclotomic polynomials to derive a contradiction unless the ring equals its centre.
