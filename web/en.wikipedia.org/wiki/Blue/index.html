Blue

Blue is one of the three primary colours in the RYB colour model and, along with red and green, one of the additive primary colours of light. The colour of the clear daytime sky and the deep sea is caused by the preferential scattering of blue light, an effect known as Rayleigh scattering.

History

Blue pigments were prized in the ancient world: ultramarine was made from lapis lazuli mined in Afghanistan, Egyptian blue was among the first synthetic pigments, and indigo and woad provided blue dyes. Because of its cost and associations, blue became linked with authority, the Virgin Mary in Christian art, and later with police and military uniforms.

Associations

Surveys in Western countries consistently find blue the most popular colour. It is associated with calm, stability and melancholy—hence "the blues"—and is the most common colour of corporate logos and flags.
