Fetal alcohol spectrum disorder

Fetal alcohol spectrum disorders (FASDs) are a group of conditions that can occur in a person who was exposed to alcohol before birth. Effects can include an abnormal appearance (such as a smooth philtrum and thin upper lip), short height, low body weight, small head size, poor coordination, behavioural problems, learning difficulties, and problems with hearing and vision.

Types

The term covers several diagnoses. Fetal alcohol syndrome (FAS) is the most severe form, characterised by growth deficiency, characteristic facial features and central nervous system damage. Other categories include partial fetal alcohol syndrome (pFAS), alcohol-related neurodevelopmental disorder (ARND) and alcohol-related birth defects (ARBD). The older term "fetal alcohol effects" (FAE) has fallen out of use.

Cause and prevention

FASDs are caused by alcohol consumption during pregnancy; alcohol crosses the placenta and can disrupt fetal development. The disorders are entirely preventable if alcohol is avoided during pregnancy. Health authorities such as the U.S. Surgeon General and the CDC recommend abstaining from alcohol when pregnant or planning a pregnancy.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is based on documented prenatal alcohol exposure, facial features, growth and neurodevelopmental assessment. There is no cure, but early diagnosis, supportive environments, educational interventions and medical care can improve outcomes. Estimates of prevalence vary; some studies suggest FASDs may affect around 1–5% of school-age children in the United States.
