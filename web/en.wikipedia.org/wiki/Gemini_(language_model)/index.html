Gemini (language model)

[Image placeholder: Gemini branding]

Gemini is a family of multimodal large language models developed by Google DeepMind, serving as the successor to LaMDA and PaLM 2. Comprising Gemini Ultra, Gemini Pro, Gemini Flash, and Gemini Nano, it was announced on December 6, 2023, and positioned to compete with OpenAI's GPT-4. Gemini models are natively multimodal, trained from the start on text, images, audio, video, and code rather than having modalities bolted on afterwards.

The models were co-developed by teams across Google, including Google DeepMind and Google Research. Gemini was described by Google CEO Sundar Pichai as the company's "largest and most capable AI model". At launch, Gemini Ultra reportedly exceeded GPT-4 on several benchmarks, including the MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding) test, where it scored above the threshold associated with human experts.

Development

Gemini grew out of Google's earlier language-model efforts: BERT, LaMDA, and PaLM. Following the public success of ChatGPT in late 2022, Google accelerated work on a next-generation foundation model under DeepMind, led by Demis Hassabis. The project combined the PaLM lineage with DeepMind's multimodal research, including work related to Flamingo and AlphaGo-style reinforcement learning techniques.

Models

Gemini Ultra is the largest and most capable variant, intended for highly complex tasks. Gemini Pro is a mid-tier model designed to scale across a wide range of tasks and powered the Bard chatbot (later rebranded as Gemini) at launch. Gemini Nano is an efficient on-device model designed to run on smartphones such as the Pixel 8 Pro. Gemini Flash, introduced in 2024, is a lightweight, low-latency variant optimised for high-volume tasks. Later generations — Gemini 1.5, 2.0, and 2.5 — added long-context windows of up to one million tokens (and experimentally two million), "thinking" reasoning modes, and agentic capabilities.

Integration

Gemini was integrated across Google's product line: the Gemini chatbot and mobile app, Google Search's AI Overviews, Gmail, Google Docs and other Workspace tools, Android (as a replacement for Google Assistant in many contexts), and Google Cloud's Vertex AI platform. The API is offered through Google AI Studio and Vertex AI.

Reception

The launch drew scrutiny when a demonstration video was found to have been edited to make interactions appear more seamless than they were in real time, prompting debate about AI marketing practices. Subsequent releases, particularly Gemini 1.5 Pro with its long-context performance and the Gemini 2.5 "thinking" models, were more warmly received by developers and researchers.
