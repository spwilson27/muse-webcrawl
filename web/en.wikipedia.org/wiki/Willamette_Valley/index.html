Willamette Valley

Location: Northwestern Oregon, USA

Principal river: Willamette River

Major cities: Portland, Salem, Eugene

The Willamette Valley is a 150-mile (240 km) long valley in Oregon, in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States. The Willamette River flows the entire length of the valley and is flanked on the west by the Oregon Coast Range and on the east by the Cascade Range. The valley is roughly 25 to 40 miles wide.

Home to around 70 percent of Oregon's population, the valley contains the state's three largest cities — Portland, Salem (the capital), and Eugene. Its fertile soils, deposited in part by the Missoula Floods at the end of the last ice age, make it Oregon's agricultural heartland, producing grass seed, hazelnuts, berries, hops, and Christmas trees.

Wine country

The Willamette Valley AVA is one of the United States' premier cool-climate wine regions, particularly renowned for Pinot noir. With hundreds of wineries and vineyard-designated sub-AVAs such as Dundee Hills and Yamhill-Carlton, wine is a major economic and tourist draw.

History

The valley was the destination of emigrants on the Oregon Trail in the 1840s and was inhabited for millennia before by Kalapuya and other Indigenous peoples. Oregon's progressive land-use planning laws of the 1970s established urban growth boundaries that continue to protect valley farmland from sprawl.
