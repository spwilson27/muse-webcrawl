The Battles of Fort Fisher were two assaults in December 1864 and January 1865 on Fort Fisher, the powerful Confederate earthwork fortress guarding the Cape Fear River approaches to Wilmington, North Carolina — the Confederacy's last major port open to blockade-runners. The fort's capture in January 1865 closed the port and hastened the end of the American Civil War.

The fortress

Built largely of sand and earth, which absorbed shellfire, Fort Fisher stretched nearly two miles along the peninsula between the Atlantic and the Cape Fear River, mounting more than 40 heavy guns. Its commander for much of the war, Colonel William Lamb, and district commander Major General W. H. C. Whiting made it the strongest coastal fortification in the South, nicknamed the "Gibraltar of the South."

First battle (December 1864)

A joint expedition under Major General Benjamin Butler and Rear Admiral David Dixon Porter opened with the detonation of the powder boat USS Louisiana, which failed entirely. After days of bombardment and a partial landing, Butler withdrew against orders, and the attempt was abandoned.

Second battle (January 1865)

Grant replaced Butler with Major General Alfred Terry. After the greatest naval bombardment of the war to that date, some 8,000 Union troops assaulted on 15 January 1865: a diversionary attack by sailors and marines against the sea face drew defenders away while Terry's men stormed the river face and fought traverse by traverse through the fort until nightfall, when the garrison surrendered. Union casualties were about 1,340; the Confederates lost some 500 killed and wounded and about 2,000 captured.

Aftermath

Wilmington fell the following month. With its port closed, the Confederacy's lifeline to the outside world was cut, and Lee's army at Petersburg lost its last supply route; the war ended three months later.
