The Web of Science (WoS) is a subscription-based platform providing access to multiple databases of scholarly literature and citation data, maintained by Clarivate. It is descended from the citation indexes created by Eugene Garfield's Institute for Scientific Information (ISI) and is widely used for literature searching, bibliometric analysis, and research evaluation.

History

Garfield founded ISI in 1960, and the Science Citation Index (SCI) was launched in 1964, followed by the Social Sciences Citation Index (1973) and the Arts & Humanities Citation Index (1978). The indexes were revolutionary because they indexed the references cited by papers, letting users trace how ideas developed and who cited whom. The service moved online as the Web of Science in the 1990s. ISI passed to Thomson Scientific, then Thomson Reuters, and in 2016 the intellectual-property and science business was spun out as Clarivate.

Contents and features

The Web of Science Core Collection indexes selected journals, books, and conference proceedings across the sciences, social sciences, arts, and humanities, with cited references included for each record. Journals are admitted through an editorial selection process. Derived tools include Journal Citation Reports, which publishes the journal impact factor, and analytic products such as InCites. Users can search by topic, author, institution, and funding, follow citation links forward and backward, and generate citation reports with metrics such as the h-index.

Use and criticism

Universities and funders use Web of Science data for tenure review, benchmarking, and funding decisions. Critics note its bias toward English-language and Western journals and the limitations of citation metrics as proxies for quality; competing services include Scopus, Google Scholar, Dimensions, and OpenAlex.
