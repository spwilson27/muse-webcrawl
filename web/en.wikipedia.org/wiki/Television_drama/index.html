Television drama

A television drama (or TV drama) is a program genre in television content that is scripted and (normally) fictional, as distinct from reality television, news, or documentary programming. Television dramas include a wide range of forms: serialised dramas and soap operas, self-contained anthology episodes, miniseries, legal and medical dramas, crime procedurals, and historical costume dramas.

History

Television drama developed from radio drama and stage traditions. In the 1950s, live anthology series such as Playhouse 90 and Studio One defined the "Golden Age of Television" in the United States. Filmed episodic series soon dominated, and by the 1980s serialised primetime dramas such as Hill Street Blues and St. Elsewhere introduced ensemble casts and continuing storylines.

From the late 1990s, cable series such as The Sopranos (1999), The Wire, and Breaking Bad were credited with ushering in a new era of ambitious "prestige television", and the growth of streaming platforms in the 2010s made long-form, binge-released drama a global industry. Drama formats differ by country: British costume drama, Korean dramas (K-dramas), Latin American telenovelas, and Japanese dorama each have strong domestic and export audiences.

Formats

  • Procedural drama: cases resolved within a single episode (e.g., police, legal, and medical procedurals).
  • Serialised drama: story arcs continuing across episodes and seasons.
  • Soap opera: open-ended serial drama, often broadcast daily.
  • Miniseries / limited series: a story told over a fixed, small number of episodes.
  • Anthology series: new stories and characters in each episode or season.
