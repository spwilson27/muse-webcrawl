Zildjian

The Avedis Zildjian Company, simply known as Zildjian, is a musical instrument manufacturer and the largest cymbal manufacturer in the world. It was founded in Constantinople (present-day Istanbul, Turkey) in 1623 by Avedis Zildjian, an Armenian alchemist who discovered an alloy of copper, tin and silver that could be made into cymbals with distinctive musical qualities.

History

The company remained in the Ottoman Empire for three centuries, supplying cymbals to the Sultan's military bands and later to European orchestras. In 1929, Avedis Zildjian III moved the company to the United States, establishing it in Massachusetts. The name "Zildjian" derives from the Turkish zil (cymbal), ci (maker), and the Armenian suffix ian (son of) — "son of the cymbal maker".

Zildjian cymbals became the standard for jazz drummers in the 20th century and remain widely used across genres. The company, headquartered in Norwell, Massachusetts, is among the oldest continuously operating manufacturing companies in the world and is still family-owned; it also produces drumsticks and acquired the Vic Firth company in 2010.
