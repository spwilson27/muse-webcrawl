Bhumibol Adulyadej (5 December 1927 – 13 October 2016), titled Rama IX, was King of Thailand from 1946 until his death in 2016. His reign of 70 years and 126 days was the longest of any Thai monarch and, at its end, the longest current reign of any head of state in the world.

Early life and accession

Bhumibol was born in Cambridge, Massachusetts, where his father, Prince Mahidol Adulyadej, was studying medicine at Harvard. He was educated in Switzerland and succeeded to the throne at the age of 18 after the mysterious death of his elder brother, King Ananda Mahidol (Rama VIII), in 1946. He married Sirikit Kitiyakara in 1950, shortly before his coronation.

Reign

Reigning through decades of coups, military governments, and democratization, Bhumibol developed a role as a national unifier standing above politics, intervening at moments of crisis — most famously during the uprising of October 1973 and the violence of May 1992. He promoted thousands of royal development projects in irrigation, agriculture, and rural development, and articulated the philosophy of the "sufficiency economy". He was also an accomplished jazz musician and saxophonist, a painter, and the inventor of several patented rainmaking and aerator devices.

Death and legacy

Bhumibol died on 13 October 2016 at Siriraj Hospital, Bangkok. A year of official mourning followed, ending with a royal cremation in October 2017. He was succeeded by his son, Vajiralongkorn. In Thailand he is remembered with deep reverence as a father figure of the nation; the monarchy is protected by strict lèse-majesté laws.
