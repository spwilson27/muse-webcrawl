Shulu Ping

The Shulu Ping (Chinese: 書盧平), more commonly known in English scholarship in connection with the Five Elders of Suiyang / Mount Lu gatherings tradition, refers to a cultural and literary theme in classical Chinese poetry and painting. Articles under this title in reference works typically treat the subject briefly: a named figure and gathering associated with literati culture, around which later poets composed matching verses and artists painted handscrolls.

Background

From the Tang dynasty onward, Chinese literati commemorated gatherings of elderly scholars and retired officials in verse and painting. Such assemblies — of which the Orchid Pavilion Gathering (353 CE) and the Five Elders at Mount Lu are the canonical models — celebrated longevity, friendship, and reclusion from court politics. The paintings commonly show white-haired scholars in a landscape setting, attended by servants, with scrolls, qin, and wine; poems inscribed on the scroll record the participants and the occasion.

Legacy

Gathering paintings and their accompanying poems circulated for centuries, were copied in later dynasties, and are preserved in museum collections in China, Taiwan, Japan, and the West. The theme influenced counterpart traditions in Korea and Japan, where "society of elders" (kiro) paintings were commissioned to honour retired scholars.
