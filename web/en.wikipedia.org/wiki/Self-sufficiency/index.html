Self-sufficiency

Self-sufficiency (also called self-containment) is the state of not requiring any aid, support, or interaction for survival; it is a type of personal or collective autonomy. At the level of an economy it corresponds to autarky; at the level of an individual or household it typically involves producing one's own food, energy, and shelter, as in subsistence farming, homesteading, and the back-to-the-land movement.

Household self-sufficiency

Popularised in the 1970s by writers such as John Seymour, whose Complete Book of Self-Sufficiency (1976) became a standard handbook, household self-sufficiency usually combines a vegetable garden, fruit trees, small livestock, food preservation, and increasingly renewable energy such as rooftop solar power and rainwater harvesting. Modern homesteaders and the off-grid movement pursue similar goals with contemporary technology.

National self-sufficiency

States may pursue self-sufficiency in food, energy, or strategic materials to reduce vulnerability to trade disruption. Economists generally note the trade-off with the gains from specialisation and comparative advantage; complete autarky is rare, though food self-sufficiency ratios are tracked by governments such as Japan's.
