The Senate (Dutch: Senaat, French: Sénat, German: Senat) is one of the two chambers of the bicameral Federal Parliament of Belgium, the other being the Chamber of Representatives. It meets in the Palace of the Nation in Brussels.

Composition

Since the sixth Belgian state reform, implemented at the 2014 elections, the Senate is no longer directly elected. It consists of 60 members: 50 senators are appointed by the parliaments of the federated entities (communities and regions) from among their members or electors, and 10 are co-opted by the other senators. The children of the monarch who are of age may also sit as senators by right.

Powers

The 1993 and 2014 reforms greatly reduced the Senate's powers. It retains a role in constitutional revision, institutional matters, and legislation concerning the structure of the state, largely on an equal footing with the Chamber for those subjects; in most ordinary legislation it now has only a limited, reflective role, acting as a chamber of reflection and a meeting place between the communities and regions.

History

The Senate was created in 1831 on the model of an aristocratic upper house, with eligibility originally tied to high tax payments. It was progressively democratized, being directly elected on the same basis as the Chamber from 1921 until the 2014 reform transformed it into an assembly of the federated entities.
