WarnerMedia

Warner Media, LLC (traded as WarnerMedia) was an American multinational mass media and entertainment conglomerate owned by AT&T. Headquartered at 30 Hudson Yards in New York City, it was originally formed as Time Warner in 1990 by the merger of Time Inc. and Warner Communications. AT&T acquired it in 2018 and renamed it WarnerMedia; in April 2022 it was spun off and merged with Discovery, Inc. to form Warner Bros. Discovery.

History

Time Warner was for years the world's largest media company, combining publishing (Time Inc.), film (Warner Bros.), television (HBO, Turner Broadcasting) and, from 2000, AOL — whose merger with Time Warner became a symbol of the dot-com bubble. After spinning off AOL, Time Cable and Time Inc., the slimmed company agreed in 2016 to be bought by AT&T for about $85 billion; the deal closed in June 2018 after the Justice Department failed to block it in court.

Operations

Under AT&T, WarnerMedia was organised around Warner Bros. (film, TV and games), HBO and the streaming service HBO Max (launched May 2020), and WarnerMedia News & Sports, including CNN, TNT and TBS. Its "day-and-date" decision to release Warner Bros.' entire 2021 film slate simultaneously on HBO Max and in theatres was controversial with filmmakers.

Merger with Discovery

In May 2021 AT&T announced it would spin off WarnerMedia and merge it with Discovery, Inc., unwinding its entertainment bet. The transaction closed on 8 April 2022, creating Warner Bros. Discovery under CEO David Zaslav, and the WarnerMedia name ceased to exist.
