Pratt & Whitney R-2800 Double Wasp

The Pratt & Whitney R-2800 Double Wasp is an American twin-row, 18-cylinder, air-cooled radial aircraft engine with a displacement of 2,800 cubic inches (46 litres). First run in 1939, it is part of the long-lived Wasp family and is widely considered one of the finest piston engines ever built. More than 125,000 were produced between 1939 and 1960.

Design

The R-2800 arranged its 18 cylinders in two rows of nine around a forged crankcase. Depending on variant, it used single-stage or two-stage supercharging, and later models employed water injection to permit higher boost pressures. Power ratings grew from about 2,000 hp in early versions to 2,800 hp in late variants fitted to the F4U Corsair's successor models and other types. Advanced features for its era included sodium-cooled exhaust valves and a master-and-articulated connecting rod arrangement typical of radial practice.

Service

During the Second World War the Double Wasp powered many of the most important American combat aircraft, including the Republic P-47 Thunderbolt, the Grumman F6F Hellcat, the Vought F4U Corsair, and the twin-engined Martin B-26 Marauder. After the war it powered airliners including the Douglas DC-6 and the Convair CV-240 family. Its reputation for ruggedness and reliability kept examples in service in cargo and firefighting aircraft for decades. See also radial engine.
