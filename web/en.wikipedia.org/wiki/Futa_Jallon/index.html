Futa Jallon

Futa Jallon (French: Fouta Djallon) is a highland region in the centre of Guinea, West Africa. The region is roughly elliptical, covers an area of about 81,000 km², and consists mainly of grasslands at an average altitude of about 900 metres. The highest point is Mount Loura (1,573 m). Several major West African rivers — the Niger, Senegal, and Gambia — have their sources in these highlands, which are sometimes called the "water tower" of West Africa.

The region is predominantly inhabited by the Fulani people. From the 18th century it was the centre of the Imamate of Futa Jallon, a Fulani Islamic state founded after a jihad, with its capital at Timbo, until French colonization in the late 19th century.
