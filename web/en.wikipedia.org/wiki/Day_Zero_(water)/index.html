Day Zero (water)

Day Zero is a term used in water management for the projected date on which a city's municipal water supply would run out or be shut off to most taps, forcing residents to collect rationed water from distribution points. The term gained worldwide prominence during the Cape Town water crisis of 2017–2018.

Cape Town

After several years of severe drought, the City of Cape Town, South Africa, announced in early 2018 that if dam levels continued to fall, "Day Zero" would arrive when the combined storage of the major dams supplying the city dropped to about 13.5 percent. At that point, municipal water supply to most homes and businesses would be cut, and residents would be limited to 25 litres per person per day collected from around 200 collection points.

Aggressive demand reduction — including strict restrictions, punitive tariffs, pressure management, and a major public campaign — cut the city's water use by more than half compared with pre-drought levels. Combined with winter rains, this pushed Day Zero back repeatedly until the crisis was declared over in 2018 without the taps ever running dry.

Other cities

The concept has since been applied to other cities facing acute water scarcity, including Chennai in India (which experienced its own "Day Zero" in 2019), São Paulo, and several cities in the southwestern United States, and is used more generally in discussions of climate change, urban water planning, and demand management.
