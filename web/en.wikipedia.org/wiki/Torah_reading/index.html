Torah reading

Torah reading (Hebrew: קריאת התורה, Keri'at haTorah) is a Jewish religious tradition that involves the public reading of a set of passages from a handwritten parchment Torah scroll. The term often refers to the entire ceremony of removing the scroll from the ark, chanting the portion, and returning it.

The weekly portion

The Torah is divided into 54 weekly portions (parashot), read in sequence so the entire scroll is completed over the course of a year, ending and restarting on Simchat Torah. Readings take place on Shabbat mornings, Monday and Thursday mornings, festivals, and fast days.

Aliyot and procedure

Congregants are called up (aliyah) to recite blessings before and after sections of the reading; seven are called on Shabbat. The text is chanted according to a system of musical notation called trope (ta'amei ha-mikra), and a gabbai follows with a printed text to correct any error, since the scroll itself contains no vowels or punctuation.

Haftarah

The Torah reading is followed by the haftarah, a thematically linked passage from the Prophets.
