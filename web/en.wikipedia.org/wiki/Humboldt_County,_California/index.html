Humboldt County is a county located in the U.S. state of California, on the far North Coast about 270 miles north of San Francisco. As of the 2020 census the population was about 136,000. Its county seat and largest city is Eureka.

Geography

The county is heavily forested and mountainous, containing much of the remaining old-growth coast redwood forest, protected in Redwood National and State Parks and Humboldt Redwoods State Park. Humboldt Bay, the second-largest natural bay in California, fronts the county's central coast. Major rivers include the Eel, Klamath, Mad and Trinity.

History and economy

Indigenous peoples including the Wiyot, Yurok, Karuk, Chilula, Mattole and Hupa have lived in the region for thousands of years. The county was created in 1853 and named for Humboldt Bay, itself named for Alexander von Humboldt. Timber dominated the economy for over a century; today important sectors include tourism, education (California State Polytechnic University, Humboldt, in Arcata and College of the Redwoods), healthcare, dairy and flower farming, and a historically prominent — now legalised and declining — cannabis industry associated with the Emerald Triangle.
