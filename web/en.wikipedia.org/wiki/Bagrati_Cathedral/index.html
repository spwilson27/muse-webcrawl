The Cathedral of the Dormition, better known as Bagrati Cathedral, is an 11th-century cathedral in Kutaisi, in the Imereti region of Georgia. A masterpiece of medieval Georgian architecture, it stands on Ukimerioni Hill overlooking the city and the Rioni River. The cathedral was built in the early years of the 11th century, during the reign of King Bagrat III, after whom it is named, and was consecrated in 1003 according to an inscription on its northern wall.

History

Bagrati Cathedral symbolised the unification of the Georgian kingdom, having been erected shortly after Bagrat III united the Georgian lands. The spacious cruciform church, crowned by a dome on a tall drum, was richly decorated with carved stone ornament, mosaics and frescoes. In 1692 the cathedral was devastated in an explosion set off by Ottoman troops occupying the fortress of Kutaisi; the dome and roof collapsed, and the building remained a ruin for more than three centuries.

Reconstruction and controversy

Restoration work began in the 1950s and continued intermittently. In the 2000s a full reconstruction was undertaken, rebuilding the dome and adding a controversial modern glass-and-steel insertion and a lift tower on the north side. In 1994 the cathedral, together with the nearby Gelati Monastery, had been inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. Because the reconstruction was judged to have compromised the site's authenticity and integrity, UNESCO placed it on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 2010, and in 2017 the cathedral was removed from the World Heritage List altogether, while Gelati Monastery retained its status.

Architecture

The cathedral is a cross-in-square church about 35 metres high in its original form. Its façades were adorned with blind arcading and elaborate carved window frames characteristic of Georgian architecture of the period. Fragments of the original medieval decoration, including floor mosaics, survive within the reconstructed interior.
