Chaturyuga

The four yugas of a chaturyuga, in the ratio 4:3:2:1

A chaturyuga (Sanskrit: catur-yuga, "four ages"), also called a mahayuga ("great age"), is the basic cycle of time in Hindu cosmology, consisting of four successive world ages or yugas: Satya Yuga, Treta Yuga, Dvapara Yuga and Kali Yuga. One complete chaturyuga lasts 4,320,000 human years, after which the cycle repeats.

The four yugas

The four ages decline in length and in virtue in the ratio 4:3:2:1, measured in divine years of 360 human years each:

  • Satya Yuga (Krita Yuga) — 4,800 divine years (1,728,000 human years). An age of truth and perfection in which dharma stands on four legs.
  • Treta Yuga — 3,600 divine years (1,296,000 human years). Dharma stands on three legs; virtue declines by a quarter.
  • Dvapara Yuga — 2,400 divine years (864,000 human years). Dharma stands on two legs.
  • Kali Yuga — 1,200 divine years (432,000 human years). The present age, in which dharma stands on one leg and strife predominates.

Each yuga is usually reckoned with dawn and twilight periods (sandhya and sandhyamsa) amounting to a tenth of its length at beginning and end, included in the figures above.

Larger cycles

One thousand chaturyugas make one kalpa, a single day of the creator god Brahma, lasting 4.32 billion years and followed by a night of equal length in which the universe rests in dissolution (pralaya). A manvantara comprises 71 chaturyugas (plus a transitional period), presided over by a Manu; fourteen manvantaras make up a kalpa.

The present age

According to traditional calculations, the current Kali Yuga began in 3102 BCE, conventionally associated with the departure of Krishna and the era following the Mahabharata war. Texts such as the Surya Siddhanta and the Puranas describe the yuga system, and the Kali Yuga is said to end when Kalki, the final avatar of Vishnu, restores dharma and begins a new Satya Yuga.
