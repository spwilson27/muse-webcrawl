Hahn–Banach theorem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The geometric Hahn–Banach theorem: a convex set and an outside point can be separated by a hyperplane.

The Hahn–Banach theorem is a central result of functional analysis. It says that a bounded linear functional defined on a subspace of a vector space can be extended to the whole space without increasing its bound, provided the functional is dominated by a sublinear function. The theorem is named after Hans Hahn and Stefan Banach, who proved it independently in the late 1920s (Hahn 1927; Banach 1929), generalising earlier work by Eduard Helly and Frigyes Riesz.

Statement

Let V be a real (or complex) vector space and p : V → ℝ a sublinear function (subadditive and positively homogeneous). If f is a linear functional on a subspace W ⊆ V with f(x) ≤ p(x) for all x ∈ W, then f extends to a linear functional F on all of V with F(x) ≤ p(x) for all x ∈ V. Taking p(x) = ‖f‖‖x‖ on a normed space gives the extension of continuous linear functionals with preservation of norm — hence every normed space has a rich dual space.

Geometric form

Equivalent versions separate disjoint convex sets by hyperplanes: two nonempty disjoint convex sets, one of them open, can be separated by a closed hyperplane; with compactness assumptions strict separation is possible. From this follow core machinery of analysis: the existence of supporting hyperplanes, separation of a point from a closed convex set, and the fact that a closed convex set is the intersection of the closed half-spaces containing it.

Consequences and applications

  • The dual space of any nonzero normed space is nonzero; for each vector x there is a functional of norm 1 attaining ‖x‖.
  • Existence of Banach limits, invariant means, and finitely additive measures.
  • Fundamental theorems of duality in optimization, including the minimax theorem of game theory (via separation) and Lagrange/Kuhn–Tucker conditions.
  • Existence of weak solutions in PDE theory and of equilibrium prices in mathematical economics.

Foundations

The standard proof uses Zorn's lemma (or transfinite recursion) and is therefore non-constructive: the theorem follows from the axiom of choice, indeed from the weaker ultrafilter/Boolean prime ideal theorem, and is not provable in ZF alone.
