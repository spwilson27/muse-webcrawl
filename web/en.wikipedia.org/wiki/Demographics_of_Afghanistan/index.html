Demographics of Afghanistan

The demographics of Afghanistan concern the population of Afghanistan, estimated at roughly 40 million people in the early 2020s. Because Afghanistan has never completed a full modern census (the last attempt was in 1979), all population figures are estimates based on surveys and partial counts.

Ethnic groups

Afghanistan is ethnically and linguistically diverse. Pashtuns are generally estimated to be the largest group (around 40%), followed by Tajiks (about 25–30%), Hazaras (around 10–20%), and Uzbeks (around 6–10%), with smaller Turkmen, Baloch, Aimak, Nuristani, Pamiri and other communities. Exact proportions are politically sensitive and disputed.

Languages

Dari (Afghan Persian) and Pashto are the official languages; Dari functions as the lingua franca of government and business. Uzbek and Turkmen have regional official status in the north, and around 30 other languages are spoken.

Population structure

Afghanistan has one of the youngest populations in the world, with a median age of around 18–19 years and historically high fertility, though rates have been falling. Life expectancy is in the low-to-mid 60s. Literacy, especially female literacy, remains among the lowest in Asia. The overwhelming majority — over 99% — of Afghans are Muslim, mostly Sunni Hanafi with a Shia minority concentrated among Hazaras. Kabul is by far the largest city; roughly a quarter of the population is urban.
