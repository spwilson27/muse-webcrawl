Kefauver Committee

The televised hearings made organised crime a national issue in the United States.

The Kefauver Committee, formally the United States Senate Special Committee to Investigate Crime in Interstate Commerce, was a committee of the U.S. Senate active in 1950–51. Chaired by Senator Estes Kefauver of Tennessee, it investigated organised crime, gambling and corruption across the United States, holding hearings in fourteen cities.

Hearings

The committee heard from hundreds of witnesses, including underworld figures such as Frank Costello, whose televised testimony — famously showing only his nervously twitching hands — riveted viewers. The hearings were among the first congressional proceedings broadcast live on television, drawing an enormous audience and making Kefauver a national political figure.

Findings and impact

The committee reported that a nationwide crime syndicate, the Mafia, operated in the United States and had corrupted local officials and police. It recommended new federal measures against gambling and racketeering, strengthened tax enforcement against criminals, and contributed to later legislation and to public awareness of organised crime, even though some conclusions were later disputed.
