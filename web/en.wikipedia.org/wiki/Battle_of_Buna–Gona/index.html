Battle of Buna–Gona

The Battle of Buna–Gona was fought from 16 November 1942 to 22 January 1943, during the New Guinea campaign of the Second World War. Australian and United States forces attacked the Japanese beachheads at Buna, Sanananda and Gona on the north coast of Papua, where Imperial Japanese troops had landed in July 1942 for their overland advance on Port Moresby via the Kokoda Track.

Allied infantry in the Buna area, late 1942.

Course of the battle

The Allies seriously underestimated the strength and fortifications of the Japanese positions — elaborate networks of coconut-log bunkers in swampy terrain — and the first attacks by inexperienced American troops of the 32nd Infantry Division and Australian battalions of the 7th Division were costly failures. Disease, supply difficulties and appalling conditions compounded the problems. General Douglas MacArthur, pressing for results, relieved the American commander, and under Lieutenant General Robert Eichelberger and with reinforcements, artillery, tanks and improved logistics, the Allies ground forward. Gona fell on 9 December 1942, Buna on 2 January 1943, and the Sanananda position was cleared by 22 January.

Costs

The battle was among the most costly of the Pacific War relative to forces engaged. Allied casualties exceeded 2,800 killed and many more wounded or sick; of roughly 9,000–10,000 Japanese defenders and labourers, nearly all died, many of starvation and disease. The victory secured the Papuan beachheads and provided airfields for subsequent operations along New Guinea's north coast.
