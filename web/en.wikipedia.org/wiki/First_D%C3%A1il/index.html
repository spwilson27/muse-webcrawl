First Dáil

First Dáil Term: 21 January 1919 – 10 May 1921
Location: Mansion House and UCD Earlsfort Terrace, Dublin
Ceann Comhairle: Cathal Brugha (first), then Seán T. O'Kelly
President of Dáil Éireann: Éamon de Valera (from April 1919)
Parties: Sinn Féin (majority)
Context: Irish War of Independence

The First Dáil was Dáil Éireann as it convened between 1919 and 1921. It was the revolutionary parliament of the self-proclaimed Irish Republic, formed by Sinn Féin members elected to the British House of Commons in the 1918 general election who refused to take their seats at Westminster.

First meeting

The Dáil first met on 21 January 1919 in the Mansion House, Dublin. Only 27 of the 73 Sinn Féin members elected were present, many others being in prison. Conducting business in Irish, it adopted a provisional constitution, issued a Declaration of Independence ratifying the 1916 Proclamation, approved a Democratic Programme on social policy, and addressed a message to the free nations of the world seeking recognition.

Government and suppression

In April 1919 Éamon de Valera became President of Dáil Éireann, heading a ministry that established parallel courts, a national loan, and local government structures. The British authorities declared the Dáil illegal in September 1919, driving it underground, where it continued to meet intermittently during the War of Independence until the Truce of 1921.

Legacy

The First Dáil is regarded as the founding legislature of the modern Irish state; every subsequent Dáil is numbered from it, and the centenary of its first sitting was officially commemorated in 2019.
