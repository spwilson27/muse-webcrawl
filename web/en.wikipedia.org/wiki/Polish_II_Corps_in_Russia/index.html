The Polish II Corps in Russia (Polish: II Korpus Polski w Rosji) was a Polish military formation created in Russia in December 1917 during the First World War. It was formed from Polish soldiers serving in the Imperial Russian Army after the February Revolution and the collapse of tsarist authority, as Polish political and military organisations sought to create national armed forces that could support the rebirth of an independent Polish state.

History

The corps was organised in Ukraine, drawing together Polish units that had been formed within the Russian army, including elements in Bessarabia and the Romanian front. At its height it numbered roughly 8,000 men. In March 1918 it united with Polish forces under General Józef Haller, who had crossed from the Austro-Hungarian side with the Polish II Brigade of the Polish Legions after the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk and the Battle of Rarańcza. Haller assumed command of the combined force.

Battle of Kaniów

Following the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk between the Central Powers and Bolshevik Russia, German forces demanded that the Polish corps disarm. When Haller's troops refused and attempted to break through toward territory held by the Allies, they were attacked by German units near Kaniów (Kaniv) on 11 May 1918. After fierce fighting, and having exhausted their ammunition, the Poles were forced to surrender. Many soldiers were interned in prisoner-of-war camps, although a number escaped and later joined Polish formations in the east, including the Polish 4th Rifle Division in Kuban and units forming in Murmansk and Siberia.

Legacy

The Battle of Kaniów became an important symbol in Polish military tradition, commemorated as an act of resistance against German attempts to subjugate Polish forces. Veterans of the II Corps later served in the reborn Polish Army during the Polish–Soviet War.
