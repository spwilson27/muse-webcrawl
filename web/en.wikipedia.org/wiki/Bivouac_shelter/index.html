Bivouac shelter

TypeImprovised or portable shelter
UsesMountaineering, military, emergency
FormsBivvy bag, tarp shelter, snow cave

A bivouac shelter is any kind of improvised camp site or shelter, usually of a temporary nature, used especially by soldiers or mountaineers. The word comes from the French bivouac, originally describing a night watch by an army.

Mountaineering

In mountaineering, a bivouac may be a planned lightweight overnight stop, or an unplanned emergency shelter when climbers are benighted, caught by weather, or unable to descend. Modern equipment includes the bivvy bag — a waterproof, breathable cover for a sleeping bag — as well as lightweight tarps and bothy bags. Snow caves and dug shelters can be built in winter conditions and can be warmer than a tent because snow is an effective insulator.

Military use

Armies use the term for temporary encampments without permanent shelter, where troops sleep under tarps, ponchos, or simple shelters ("bashas") while on exercise or operations.

Technique

Good bivouac practice emphasizes insulation from the ground, protection from wind and precipitation, and conserving body heat; an unexpected bivouac is far more survivable with a small amount of carried emergency equipment.
