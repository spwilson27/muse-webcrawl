Triangular lattice

The triangular lattice is one of the five two-dimensional Bravais lattice types. It consists of points arranged at the vertices of a tiling of the plane by equilateral triangles; each point has six nearest neighbours at equal distances, giving the lattice sixfold (hexagonal) symmetry. Its symmetry group is the wallpaper group p6m.

Properties

The triangular lattice is the arrangement of points obtained, for example, from the densest circle packing in the plane: placing equal circles so that each touches six others gives circle centres on a triangular lattice, achieving the maximum packing density of π/(2√3) ≈ 0.907. It is the dual of the honeycomb (hexagonal) lattice: the Voronoi cells of the triangular lattice are regular hexagons, and the triangular lattice is its own Delaunay triangulation.

In crystallography the triangular lattice underlies close-packed layers of atoms in metals and the basal planes of hexagonal crystals. In statistical physics it hosts classic models such as the triangular-lattice Ising model, where antiferromagnetic interactions are geometrically frustrated because the three spins on each triangular plaquette cannot all be antiparallel.

Related lattices

The kagome lattice is derived from the triangular lattice by removing one quarter of its points. The hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice, with a two-point basis, is the graph of graphene's carbon atoms.
