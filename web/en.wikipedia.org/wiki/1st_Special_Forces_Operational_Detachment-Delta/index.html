The 1st Special Forces Operational Detachment–Delta (1st SFOD-D), widely known as Delta Force, is a United States Army special operations unit under the operational control of the Joint Special Operations Command. It specialises in counter-terrorism, hostage rescue, high-value target capture and other complex special missions, and is considered a Tier One special mission unit alongside the Naval Special Warfare Development Group (DEVGRU).

History

Delta was founded in 1977 by Colonel Charles Beckwith, who had served an exchange tour with the British 22 SAS Regiment and long argued that the US Army needed an equivalent direct-action unit. Activated on 19 November 1977 at Fort Bragg, the unit's first major operation was Operation Eagle Claw, the failed April 1980 attempt to rescue American hostages in Tehran, whose disastrous end at Desert One spurred the creation of JSOC and a wholesale reform of US special operations.

Notable operations

Delta operators took part in the invasions of Grenada (1983) and Panama (1989), where they rescued prisoner Kurt Muse, and in the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu, where two Delta snipers, Gary Gordon and Randy Shughart, were posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor. After the September 11 attacks, Delta squadrons spent two decades in Iraq and Afghanistan on manhunting missions against al-Qaeda and Islamic State leadership, including the 2019 raid in Syria in which Islamic State leader Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi was killed.

Selection and organisation

Recruitment is drawn mainly from the Army, notably the 75th Ranger Regiment and Special Forces groups. Selection is notoriously gruelling, emphasising land navigation and psychological screening, followed by a six-month Operator Training Course. The unit is organised into operational squadrons (A, B, C and D) with supporting elements, though its exact size is classified and it is believed to number around 1,000 personnel.
