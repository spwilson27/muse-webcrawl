Bursitis

Bursitis is the inflammation of one or more bursae — the small fluid-filled sacs of synovial fluid that cushion the areas where skin, muscles, tendons, and ligaments pass over bones. Symptoms typically include pain, swelling, tenderness, and stiffness near the affected joint, and pain that worsens with movement or pressure.

Causes

The most common cause is repetitive motion or prolonged pressure on a bursa, as in kneeling, leaning on the elbows, or throwing. Other causes include acute injury, infection (septic bursitis, most often of the elbow or kneecap), and systemic inflammatory conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis and gout. Well-known forms include prepatellar bursitis ("housemaid's knee"), olecranon bursitis ("student's elbow"), trochanteric bursitis at the hip, and subacromial bursitis at the shoulder.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on the history and examination findings of localised swelling and tenderness over a bursa. Ultrasound or MRI may be used when the diagnosis is uncertain. If infection is suspected, fluid may be aspirated from the bursa and analysed.

Treatment

Most cases resolve with rest, avoidance of the provoking activity, ice, and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). Physiotherapy can help restore movement and strength. Corticosteroid injections may be used for persistent cases. Septic bursitis requires antibiotics and sometimes surgical drainage or removal of the bursa (bursectomy).
