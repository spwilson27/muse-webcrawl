SNOLAB

LocationVale Creighton Mine, Sudbury, Ontario, Canada
Depth~2,070 m (6,800 ft) underground
Established2009 (expanded facility)
FieldNeutrino physics, dark matter, astroparticle physics

SNOLAB is a Canadian underground science laboratory specializing in neutrino and dark matter physics. It is located about 2 kilometres underground within Vale's Creighton nickel mine near Sudbury, Ontario. The great depth shields experiments from cosmic rays, making SNOLAB one of the deepest and cleanest underground research facilities in the world.

History

SNOLAB grew out of the Sudbury Neutrino Observatory (SNO), an experiment built in a cavity at Creighton Mine to detect solar neutrinos. SNO famously demonstrated that neutrinos change flavour (oscillate) on their way from the Sun, proving they have mass; its director Arthur B. McDonald shared the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics. The expanded SNOLAB facility — a much larger complex of clean-room laboratory halls excavated around the original SNO cavity — was completed around 2009–2011.

Experiments

SNOLAB hosts or has hosted a programme of experiments including:

  • SNO+ — the successor to SNO, using liquid scintillator to study neutrinos, including the search for neutrinoless double beta decay.
  • DEAP-3600 — a dark matter detector using liquid argon.
  • PICO — bubble-chamber dark matter searches.
  • HALO — a supernova neutrino detector forming part of the worldwide SNEWS early warning network.
  • SuperCDMS — a cryogenic dark matter experiment.

Facility

All personnel and equipment descend more than two kilometres in the mine cage and pass through a clean-entry process, showering and changing into lint-free clothing, because the laboratory is maintained as a class-2000 clean room. The rock overburden reduces the cosmic-ray muon flux by a factor of about 50 million compared with the surface.
