Battle of Buxar
Date22–23 October 1764
LocationBuxar, Bihar, India
CombatantsBritish East India Company vs alliance of Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula of Awadh and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II
CommandersMajor Hector Munro
ResultDecisive East India Company victory

The Battle of Buxar was fought on 22–23 October 1764 between the forces of the British East India Company, led by Hector Munro, and the combined armies of Mir Qasim (the former Nawab of Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daula, the Nawab of Awadh, and the Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II. The battle was fought at Buxar, a town on the banks of the Ganges in present-day Bihar.

Outcome and significance

Though the allied army greatly outnumbered Munro's roughly 7,000–8,600 men, the Company's disciplined infantry and artillery won a decisive victory. Together with the earlier Battle of Plassey (1757), Buxar confirmed British dominance in northern India. By the Treaty of Allahabad (1765), Shah Alam II granted the Company the diwani — the right to collect revenues — of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa, the fiscal foundation of British rule in India.
