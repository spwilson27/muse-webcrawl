Peredur


Title character of: Peredur son of Efrawg
Tradition: Welsh Arthurian romance

Peredur is a hero of Welsh tradition and the protagonist of Peredur son of Efrawg, one of the Three Welsh Romances preserved in the White Book of Rhydderch and the Red Book of Hergest. The character is the Welsh counterpart of Percival, hero of Chrétien de Troyes' Perceval, the Story of the Grail, and a historical prince named Peredur appears in early Welsh genealogies as a sixth-century ruler of the northern British kingdom of Elmet.

The romance

In the romance, Peredur is raised in seclusion by his mother after his father and brothers are killed, and grows up ignorant of knighthood. Encountering Arthur's knights, he journeys to court, is mocked and prophesied over by the jester and the Black Maiden, and sets out to prove himself. He is trained by his uncles—one of them a lame king whose castle features a mysterious procession of a bleeding lance and a salver bearing a severed head—falls in love with Angharad Golden-Hand, and undergoes a long series of adventures culminating in vengeance upon the Nine Witches of Gloucester, whose sorcery caused his family's suffering.

Relationship to Percival

Scholars debate whether the Welsh tale derives from Chrétien's unfinished Perceval, from shared sources, or developed partly independently: Peredur lacks the Grail quest proper, replacing the Grail procession with the severed head and framing the hero's goal as family vengeance rather than spiritual failure.
