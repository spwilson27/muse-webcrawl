King Camp Gillette (January 5, 1855 – July 9, 1932) was an American businessman and inventor who invented a popular safety razor for use with disposable blades, and founded the Gillette Safety Razor Company. He is credited with creating the "razors and blades" business model, in which the razor handle is sold cheaply to create a market for the recurring purchase of blades.

Early life

Gillette was born in Fond du Lac, Wisconsin, and raised in Chicago, Illinois, where his family survived the Great Chicago Fire of 1871. He worked as a travelling salesman for the Crown Cork and Seal Company, whose founder, William Painter, advised him to invent something that, like the bottle cap, would be used a few times and thrown away.

Invention

In 1895, while shaving with a dull straight razor, Gillette conceived the idea of a razor using a thin, cheap, disposable two-edged blade clamped in a holder. Working with machinist William Emery Nickerson, he overcame the technical problem of producing blades from sheet steel. The Gillette Safety Razor Company was founded in 1901, patents were granted in 1904, and sales took off: the company sold 51 razor sets and 168 blades in 1903, then 90,000 razors and 12 million blades in 1904.

The company supplied razors to the United States Army during the First World War, and Gillette's portrait and signature were printed on blade wrappers, making his face one of the most recognised in advertising.

Utopian writings

Gillette was also a utopian socialist thinker. In The Human Drift (1894) he proposed that the population of North America should live in a single vast city powered by Niagara Falls. His later books, World Corporation (1910) and The People's Corporation (1924), advocated a single corporation owned by the public. He once offered former president Theodore Roosevelt a million dollars to head such a corporation.

Later life and death

Gillette lost much of his fortune in the stock market crash of 1929 and fought costly patent litigation, dying nearly penniless in Los Angeles in 1932. The company he founded survives today as the Gillette brand of Procter & Gamble.
