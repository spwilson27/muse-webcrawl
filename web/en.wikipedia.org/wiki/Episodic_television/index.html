Episodic television

Episodic television is a format of television series in which each episode presents a self-contained story with a beginning, middle, and end, and the status quo of the characters and setting is largely restored by the episode's conclusion. Episodes can therefore be broadcast or watched in almost any order without loss of comprehension.

Characteristics

Episodic series dominate genres such as the sitcom, the police procedural, the medical drama, and the anthology-like adventure show. Classic examples include Star Trek, Law & Order, and most traditional situation comedies. The format suits network scheduling and syndication, since viewers can join at any point and episodes can be rerun freely.

Contrast with serialised television

The opposite approach is serialised television, in which plot arcs continue across many episodes or whole seasons, as in soap operas and much prestige drama of the streaming era. Many modern series are hybrids, combining a "case of the week" with slowly developing serial arcs and character development — sometimes called "procedural with serial elements". Writers sometimes describe purely episodic shows as having a "reset button" pressed at the end of each instalment.
