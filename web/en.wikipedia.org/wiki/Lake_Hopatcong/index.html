Lake Hopatcong is the largest freshwater lake in the U.S. state of New Jersey, covering about 2,560 acres in Morris and Sussex counties, roughly 40 miles west of New York City. It is the headwater of the Musconetcong River.

History

The modern lake was created in stages: two smaller natural ponds were dammed in the 1830s to supply water to the Morris Canal, raising the water level and joining the ponds into a single lake; a larger dam at Hopatcong State Park in the early twentieth century completed its present form. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, Lake Hopatcong was one of the great resorts of the eastern United States, with grand hotels, dance halls, and the Bertrand Island Amusement Park, accessible by train from the New York metropolitan area. Bertrand Island Park closed in 1983, and the lake is now ringed chiefly by year-round and vacation homes.

Recreation

The lake is a major centre for boating, sailing, and fishing (including largemouth bass, muskellunge, and walleye), administered in part through Hopatcong State Park at its southwestern end. Management issues include aquatic weed growth, harmful algal blooms — which closed the lake to swimming in 2019 — and periodic drawdowns for dam and dock maintenance.
