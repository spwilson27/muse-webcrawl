Molar solubility

Molar solubility is the solubility of a substance expressed as the amount of solute, in moles, that dissolves per litre of solution to form a saturated solution. It is commonly used for sparingly soluble ionic compounds in water, whose solubility is small enough that the dissolved amount can be related directly to the compound's solubility product constant, Ksp.

Relation to Ksp

For a salt AxBy dissolving as AxBy(s) ⇌ xA(aq) + yB(aq), if the molar solubility is s mol/L, the ion concentrations at equilibrium are xs and ys, so Ksp = (xs)x(ys)y. For a 1:1 salt such as silver chloride, this reduces to Ksp = s². Solving for s allows solubility to be computed from tabulated Ksp values, provided effects such as hydrolysis, complex formation, and the common-ion effect are accounted for.

Factors affecting molar solubility

The presence of a common ion suppresses dissolution, while acidic solutions increase the solubility of salts whose anions are basic, and complexing agents can greatly increase it. Temperature also changes solubility, in the direction predicted by Le Chatelier's principle.
