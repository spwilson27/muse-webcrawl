Soul Train

Soul Train
Created by: Don Cornelius
First aired: 2 October 1971 (national syndication)
Final episode: 2006
Genre: Music variety / dance show
Signature features: Soul Train Line, Scramble Board, "the hippest trip in America"

Soul Train was an American musical variety television program that aired in syndication from 1971 to 2006, created and hosted for most of its run by Don Cornelius. Celebrating Black music and culture, it featured performances by R&B, soul, funk, disco and hip hop artists and is among the longest-running first-run syndicated shows in US television history.

Format

Each episode combined lip-synced or live performances with studio dancers who showcased the latest dances and fashions. Recurring features included the Soul Train Line — two lines of dancers facing each other while couples danced down the middle — the Scramble Board word puzzle, and Cornelius's closing blessing of "love, peace and soul!". The animated train opening and theme "TSOP (The Sound of Philadelphia)" by MFSB became iconic.

Impact

Soul Train gave national television exposure to artists from James Brown and Aretha Franklin to Prince and hip hop pioneers, at a time when mainstream music shows rarely featured Black performers so prominently. The Soul Train Music Awards (from 1987) and the Lady of Soul Awards extended the brand, and the show's influence persists in dance and music television worldwide.
