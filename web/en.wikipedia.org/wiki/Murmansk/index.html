Murmansk

Murmansk (Russian: Мурманск) is a port city and the administrative centre of Murmansk Oblast, Russia, located in the far northwest of the country on the Kola Bay, an inlet of the Barents Sea. With a population of roughly 270,000, it is the largest city entirely north of the Arctic Circle.

History

The city was founded in 1916 as Romanov-on-Murman, Russia's last city founded under the empire, to serve as an ice-free port connected to the interior by the Murmansk railway during the First World War; it was renamed Murmansk after the October Revolution. During the Second World War it was the destination of the perilous Arctic convoys carrying Lend-Lease supplies from Britain and the United States, and it endured heavy bombing without being captured.

Economy and climate

Warmed by the North Atlantic Drift, Murmansk's port stays ice-free all year despite its latitude, supporting shipping, fishing and the Northern Fleet, whose headquarters is at nearby Severomorsk. The city is home port to Russia's nuclear icebreaker fleet. Its subarctic climate features a polar night lasting about 40 days in winter and a corresponding period of midnight sun in summer. The Alyosha Monument, a 35-metre statue of a Soviet soldier, overlooks the harbour as a memorial to the city's wartime defenders.
