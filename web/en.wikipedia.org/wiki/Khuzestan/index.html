Khuzestan

Country: Iran
Capital: Ahvaz
Area: 64,055 km²
Population: about 4.7 million (2016 census)
Ethnic groups: Persians, Arabs, Lurs, Qashqai and others

Khuzestan province (Persian: استان خوزستان) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, located in the southwest of the country, bordering Iraq to the west and the Persian Gulf to the south. Its capital is Ahvaz. Historically part of ancient Elam, Khuzestan is one of the oldest inhabited regions of Iran and the centre of the country's oil industry, producing the majority of Iran's crude oil and natural gas.

History

The plain of Khuzestan was home to the Elamite civilisation, whose capital Susa was among the great cities of the ancient Near East; the ziggurat of Chogha Zanbil remains from the 13th century BC. The region later formed part of the Achaemenid, Parthian and Sasanian empires. The Arabic name al-Ahwaz and the medieval prosperity of the sugar-producing plain reflect its early Islamic history. The modern name Khuzestan is generally derived from the Khuzis, an ancient people of the region.

Oil was discovered at Masjed Soleyman in 1908 — the first commercial oil strike in the Middle East — and the great refinery at Abadan, once among the world's largest, anchored the province's 20th-century economy. Khuzestan was the main battlefield of the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), suffering extensive destruction, especially in Khorramshahr and Abadan.

Geography and climate

The province consists of a hot alluvial plain watered by the Karun, Karkheh, Dez and Jarahi rivers — the Karun being Iran's largest and only navigable river — rising to the Zagros foothills in the northeast. Summers are among the hottest on Earth; Ahvaz recorded one of the highest reliably measured temperatures in Asia, about 54 °C (129 °F) in June 2017. The Hawizeh and Shadegan marshes in the south are important wetlands.

People

Khuzestan is ethnically diverse, with Persian speakers, a large Iranian Arab population concentrated in the south and west, Lurs in the north and east, and smaller communities. Arab separatist sentiment and grievances over water, pollution and oil revenues have periodically caused tension with the central government, and the province has faced severe water and dust-storm crises in recent years.
