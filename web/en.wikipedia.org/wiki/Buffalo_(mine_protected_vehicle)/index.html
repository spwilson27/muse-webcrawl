The Buffalo is a mine-protected, mine-resistant, ambush-protected (MRAP) vehicle designed for route clearance. Built by Force Protection Industries, it is based on the South African Casspir mine-protected vehicle and is designed to protect its crew from land mines and improvised explosive devices (IEDs) while clearing routes.

Design

The Buffalo has a distinctive V-shaped hull that deflects blast energy away from the vehicle, a high ground clearance, and a heavily armoured capsule for the crew. Its most recognisable feature is a long articulated arm fitted to the front, carrying a camera and a claw or rake, which allows the crew to investigate suspected devices and dig up mines from a standoff distance without leaving the vehicle.

Operational history

The Buffalo entered service with the United States Army and Marine Corps in the early 2000s and was used extensively in Iraq and Afghanistan for route clearance, where IEDs were the leading cause of casualties. It was also adopted by Canada, France, Italy and the United Kingdom. The vehicle's survivability became a central argument for the MRAP programme, which fielded thousands of mine-protected vehicles to replace unarmoured trucks.

Variants

Variants include the Buffalo MPCV, the Buffalo A2 with a heavier chassis, and versions fitted with additional sensors and jamming equipment for electronic countermeasures.
