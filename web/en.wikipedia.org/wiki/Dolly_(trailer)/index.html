Dolly (trailer)

A dolly is an unpowered trailer designed for connection to a tractor unit, truck or prime mover vehicle with strong traction. In trucking, a converter dolly is a one- or two-axle unit equipped with a fifth wheel coupling, used to convert a semi-trailer — which normally must rest on a tractor's fifth wheel — into a full trailer that can be towed behind another trailer or a rigid truck. This allows combinations such as B-doubles and road trains to be assembled.

Types

Converter dollies are described by their coupling arrangement: an A-dolly has a single drawbar with a centred coupling, while a B-dolly has two drawbars and connects to the trailer ahead in a way that resists jackknifing. In aviation, dollies are low wheeled platforms used to move cargo, baggage and aircraft components around airports. In the film industry the word also refers to the camera dolly, and in general use to any small wheeled platform or hand truck for moving loads.

Regulation

Because dolly combinations affect vehicle stability and braking, their use is regulated in many jurisdictions; in parts of North America, twin-trailer combinations using converter dollies ("doubles") are common on interstate highways, while some countries restrict their use or require permits for longer combination vehicles.
