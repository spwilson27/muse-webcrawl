The Committee on the Rights of the Child (CRC) is the body of 18 independent experts that monitors implementation of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child by its states parties, as well as its Optional Protocols on children in armed conflict and on the sale of children, child prostitution and child pornography.

Work

States parties report to the Committee within two years of ratifying the Convention and every five years thereafter; after a dialogue with the state delegation, the Committee issues concluding observations. Children, children's organisations, and national human rights institutions regularly contribute information, and the Committee holds days of general discussion with children's participation. Under the third Optional Protocol, in force since 2014, children (or their representatives) may bring individual communications alleging violations by states parties to that Protocol, and the Committee may conduct inquiries into grave or systematic violations.

General Comments

The Committee interprets the Convention through General Comments, notably No. 5 (2003) on general measures of implementation, No. 12 (2009) on the right of the child to be heard, No. 14 (2013) on the child's best interests, No. 24 (2019) on children's rights in the child justice system, and No. 26 (2023) on children's rights and the environment, with a special focus on climate change.
