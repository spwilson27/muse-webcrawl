S-500 missile system

S-500 Prometey
Type: Surface-to-air / anti-ballistic missile system
Place of origin: Russia
Designer: Almaz-Antey
In service: 2021–present

The S-500 Prometey (Russian: С-500 Прометей, "Prometheus"), also known as the 55R6M Triumfator-M, is a Russian surface-to-air missile and anti-ballistic missile system developed by the Almaz-Antey Air Defence Concern. It is intended to supplement and eventually partially replace the S-400 Triumf and the A-135 anti-ballistic missile system, engaging ballistic missiles, hypersonic weapons, and aircraft at very long ranges and at altitudes extending into near space.

Development

Development of the S-500 began in the 2000s, with design work proceeding through the 2010s. Flight testing of its long-range interceptor missiles was reported from 2018 onward; in one widely reported test in 2018, Russian sources claimed the system engaged a target at a range of about 482 km. The first regiment of the system entered service around Moscow in 2021, according to Russian officials.

Characteristics

Russian sources state that the S-500 can engage up to ten ballistic targets simultaneously at ranges of up to roughly 600 km for ballistic missile defence and about 400 km for air defence, and can intercept targets travelling at hypersonic speeds and at altitudes of up to 180–200 km, including low-orbit satellites. The system is reported to use several missile types, including the 77N6-N and 77N6-N1 hit-to-kill interceptors for exo-atmospheric interception and long-range missiles derived from the S-400's 40N6 family. Its radar complex includes phased-array engagement and acquisition radars mounted on wheeled transporter vehicles, giving the system strategic mobility.

Analysts note that many of the system's claimed capabilities derive from manufacturer and state-media statements and have not been independently verified.
