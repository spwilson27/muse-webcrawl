Za'atar

Za'atar (Arabic: زَعْتَر, romanized: zaʿtar) is a culinary herb or family of herbs of the genus Origanum, and also the name of a spice mixture that includes the herb along with toasted sesame seeds, dried sumac, and often salt and other spices. As a family of related Middle Eastern herbs, it contains plants from the genera Origanum (oregano), Calamintha, Thymus (thyme), and Satureja (savory). The name za'atar alone most properly applies to Origanum syriacum, considered in scholarship to be the hyssop of the Hebrew Bible.

Spice mixture

The prepared mixture varies by region and family recipe. It is typically made from dried za'atar herb (or thyme and oregano substitutes), sumac, toasted sesame seeds, and salt; variations add marjoram, cumin, coriander, fennel seed, or other ingredients. In the Levant, za'atar is traditionally eaten at breakfast by dipping bread first in olive oil and then in the spice mix, and it is also used as a topping for manakish (flatbread baked with za'atar and oil), as well as a seasoning for labneh, hummus, meats, and vegetables.

History and culture

Za'atar has been used in the Levant since ancient times and carries strong cultural significance in Palestinian and broader Arab cuisine, symbolising connection to the land; the herb is gathered wild in the hills of the region. Wild gathering of the plant has been restricted at times for conservation reasons, since Origanum syriacum became threatened by overharvesting, and cultivated varieties are now common.
