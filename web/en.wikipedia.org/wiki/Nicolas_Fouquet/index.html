Nicolas Fouquet, marquis de Belle-Île, vicomte de Melun et Vaux (27 January 1615 – 23 March 1680) was Superintendent of Finances in France from 1653 until his arrest in 1661, after which he was imprisoned for life.

Nicolas Fouquet

Nicolas Fouquet (1615–1680) was a French statesman who rose to become Superintendent of Finances under the young Louis XIV, amassing great wealth and building the celebrated château of Vaux-le-Vicomte. His spectacular fall — arrest, trial and lifelong imprisonment — is one of the famous episodes of the reign.

Rise

Born in Paris into a wealthy family of magistrates, Fouquet bought offices and rose through the parlements, becoming procureur général of the Parlement of Paris. During the Fronde he remained loyal to Cardinal Mazarin, who made him Superintendent of Finances in 1653 jointly with Abel Servien and, after Servien's death, alone. Fouquet restored royal credit largely through his own network and fortune, while enriching himself and cultivating poets, artists and men of letters, including La Fontaine, Molière and Madame de Sévigné.

Vaux-le-Vicomte and fall

At Vaux-le-Vicomte Fouquet employed the architect Louis Le Vau, the painter Charles Le Brun and the gardener André Le Nôtre to create the first great classical French château and garden. On 17 August 1661 he entertained Louis XIV there with legendary magnificence — a display widely believed to have sealed his fate. Jean-Baptiste Colbert, his rival, denounced his accounts; Fouquet was arrested at Nantes by d'Artagnan on 5 September 1661 and tried for embezzlement. In 1664 the court sentenced him to banishment, but Louis commuted the sentence to life imprisonment, and Fouquet died in the fortress of Pignerol in 1680. The same team that built Vaux went on to create Versailles.

Man in the Iron Mask

Fouquet has sometimes been proposed, without convincing evidence, as the true identity of the Man in the Iron Mask, who was imprisoned at Pignerol during his lifetime.
