Image registration

Image registration is the process of transforming different sets of data into one coordinate system. Data may be multiple photographs, data from different sensors, times, depths, or viewpoints. It is used in computer vision, medical imaging, military automatic target recognition, and compiling and analyzing data from satellites. Registration is necessary in order to be able to compare or integrate the data obtained from different measurements.

Transformation models

Registration algorithms estimate a spatial transformation mapping points in one image to corresponding points in another. Simple models include translation, rotation and scaling (rigid registration), affine transformations, and projective transformations. Flexible or deformable (non-rigid) registration uses models such as thin-plate splines, B-splines or dense displacement fields to account for local deformations, as in aligning medical scans of soft tissue.

Methods

Intensity-based methods optimise a similarity metric computed from pixel or voxel intensities — such as sum of squared differences, cross-correlation or mutual information — over the transformation parameters. Mutual information, introduced for registration in the 1990s, is especially valuable for multimodal registration, e.g. aligning MRI with CT or PET scans.

Feature-based methods instead detect corresponding structures — points, lines, contours or surfaces — in each image and compute the transformation that aligns them, using robust estimators such as RANSAC to reject outliers.

Applications

Applications include medical image fusion and image-guided surgery, remote sensing and map updating, panorama stitching in photography, motion estimation in video, and quality control in manufacturing. Registration quality is typically assessed by target registration error on landmarks not used to compute the transform.
