Mary Blair

Mary Blair
Born: Mary Browne Robinson, October 21, 1911, McAlester, Oklahoma, U.S.
Died: July 26, 1978 (aged 66), Soquel, California, U.S.
Occupation: Artist, animator, concept artist
Employer: Walt Disney Company (1940–1953)

Mary Blair (born Mary Browne Robinson; October 21, 1911 – July 26, 1978) was an American artist, animator, and designer who created the concept art and color styling for many Walt Disney films and, after leaving the studio, for Disneyland attractions. Her bold, modern use of color influenced the look of mid-century Disney animation.

Early life and career

Raised in Texas and California, Blair studied at the Chouinard Art Institute in Los Angeles, where she was part of a generation of women artists who later shaped Disney's art. She joined the Walt Disney Company in 1940, working on concept art for Three Caballeros and Saludos Amigos after joining Walt Disney's 1941 South American research tour, which had a lasting influence on her palette.

Disney films

Blair served as concept artist and color stylist on Cinderella (1950), Alice in Wonderland (1951), and Peter Pan (1953). Her graphic, flat style was sometimes at odds with the studio's prevailing realism, but her color scripts set the visual tone of those films. After leaving Disney she illustrated children's books and designed attractions for Disneyland, most famously "It's a Small World," whose bright, stylized look is hers.

Legacy

Blair was named a Disney Legend in 1991 and posthumously inducted into the Women in Animation Hall of Fame. Her work is credited with bringing modernist design into popular animation.
