Conservative Judaism

The Jewish Theological Seminary of America in New York, the movement's flagship institution, reorganised by Solomon Schechter in 1902.

Conservative Judaism, known outside North America as Masorti Judaism (Hebrew for "traditional"), is a Jewish religious movement that regards Jewish law (halakha) as both binding and subject to historical development. It occupies a middle position between Orthodox Judaism, which treats halakha as essentially unchanging, and Reform Judaism, which treats it as advisory. It is one of the largest Jewish denominations in the United States, though its share of American Jewry has declined in recent decades.

Origins

The movement's intellectual roots lie in the "positive-historical" school of Zacharias Frankel in Germany, who broke with the early Reform movement over its abandonment of Hebrew and ritual. In America, the Jewish Theological Seminary (JTS) was founded in 1886; under Solomon Schechter, who became its president in 1902, it became the movement's centre. The United Synagogue of America (now USCJ) was founded in 1913 to organise congregations, and the Rabbinical Assembly in 1901 for clergy.

Beliefs and practice

Conservative Judaism endorses the historical-critical study of sacred texts while affirming revelation and the authority of halakha, which is interpreted and adapted by rabbis using traditional legal methods and communal practice. The movement's Committee on Jewish Law and Standards issues rulings, sometimes endorsing multiple valid positions. Over time it adopted positions such as permitting driving to synagogue on Shabbat (1950), counting women in the minyan (1973), ordaining women as rabbis (from 1985), and later officiating at same-sex weddings — changes that distinguish it from Orthodoxy. Typical Conservative congregations combine traditional liturgy (largely in Hebrew) with mixed seating and egalitarian participation. In Israel, the Masorti movement is smaller and operates alongside the Orthodox establishment without official state recognition of its rabbis.

Institutions

Besides JTS, institutions include the Ziegler School of Rabbinic Studies in Los Angeles, the Schechter Institutes and the Seminario Rabínico Latinoamericano, the Ramah summer camps, and the Masorti/Conservative World Council (Masorti Olami).
