Muhal Richard Abrams

Muhal Richard Abrams (1930–2017), pianist, composer, and co-founder of the AACM.

Muhal Richard Abrams (born Richard Lewis Abrams; September 19, 1930 – October 29, 2017) was an American educator, composer, arranger, clarinetist, cellist, and jazz pianist. He was a co-founder of the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM) in Chicago in 1965 and served as its first president, making him a central figure in the development of free jazz and Black experimental music in the United States.

Life and career

Born in Chicago, Illinois, Abrams was largely self-taught, studying scores from the public library while attending DuSable High School and playing in local rhythm-and-blues and jazz bands, including with the tenor saxophonist Gene Ammons. In 1961 he formed the Experimental Band, a rehearsal ensemble that brought together young Chicago musicians interested in new approaches to improvisation and composition; it became the seedbed of the AACM, which Abrams founded in 1965 with pianist Jodie Christian, drummer Steve McCall, and composer Phil Cohran. The AACM ran a free music school, presented concerts, and insisted on "Great Black Music" as a continuum from ancient to the future.

Abrams's debut album, Levels and Degrees of Light (1967, Delmark), announced his spacious, harmonically adventurous piano style. After moving to New York in the late 1970s he recorded widely for labels including Black Saint, Soul Note, and New World, wrote works for orchestra, string quartet, and big band, and collaborated with Anthony Braxton, Roscoe Mitchell, Henry Threadgill, Chico Freeman, and many others — many of them his former students in Chicago. His students and mentees included some of the most influential improvisers of the following generation.

Recognition

Abrams received a Grammy nomination, the Danish Jazzpar Prize (1990), and in 2010 was named an NEA Jazz Master, the highest American honour in jazz. He died in Manhattan on October 29, 2017, aged 87.
