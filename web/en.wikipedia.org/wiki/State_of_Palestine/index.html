The State of Palestine is a state in the Southern Levant that claims the West Bank and the Gaza Strip, territories occupied by Israel since 1967, with East Jerusalem as its capital. It was proclaimed by the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) in Algiers on 15 November 1988. Since 2012 it has held non-member observer state status at the United Nations, and it is recognised as a state by a large majority of UN member states.

History

Following the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, the West Bank was administered by Jordan and Gaza by Egypt. Israel captured both in the Six-Day War of 1967. The 1993–95 Oslo Accords created the Palestinian Authority with limited self-government in parts of the territories. After Hamas won the 2006 legislative elections and took control of Gaza in 2007, Palestinian governance split between the Fatah-led administration in the West Bank and Hamas in Gaza. Repeated wars in Gaza, including the war that began in October 2023, have devastated the territory.

International status

Palestine is a member of the Arab League, the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, UNESCO, and (as an observer) the United Nations General Assembly; it acceded to the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court in 2015. The United States, Israel, and several other states do not recognise it as a state, and final-status issues — borders, settlements, refugees, and Jerusalem — remain unresolved in the Israeli–Palestinian peace process.

Government

The President of the State of Palestine is the head of state; the Prime Minister heads the government of the Palestinian Authority. The Palestine Liberation Organization remains the internationally recognised representative of the Palestinian people.
