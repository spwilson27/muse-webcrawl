Vadodara

Vadodara (formerly Baroda)
State: Gujarat, India
Location: on the Vishwamitri River
Known as: cultural capital of Gujarat

Vadodara, formerly known as Baroda, is a city in the Indian state of Gujarat. It lies on the banks of the Vishwamitri River, southeast of Ahmedabad, and is one of the largest cities in the state. Renowned for its palaces, parks, universities and museums, Vadodara is widely regarded as the cultural capital of Gujarat.

History

The region's history stretches back some two thousand years, with settlements known over time as Ankottaka, Vadapadraka and Vatpadra. In the 18th century the city became the capital of the Gaekwad dynasty of the Maratha Confederacy. Under Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad III (r. 1875–1939), one of India's most progressive rulers, Baroda pioneered compulsory primary education, banking and industrial development, and employed B. R. Ambedkar in state service. The princely state of Baroda acceded to India in 1949.

Landmarks and culture

The Lakshmi Vilas Palace, built in 1890 in the Indo-Saracenic style, remains the residence of the Gaekwad family and is reputedly four times the size of Buckingham Palace. The Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda, founded in 1949, is a leading institution, famous for its Faculty of Fine Arts. The Baroda Museum and Picture Gallery holds notable collections of Indian and European art, and the nearby UNESCO-listed Champaner-Pavagadh Archaeological Park reflects the region's layered history. The city celebrates Navratri with some of Gujarat's most celebrated garba dances.

Economy

Vadodara is a major industrial centre with large-scale petrochemical, pharmaceutical, engineering and glass industries, anchored by Indian Oil Corporation's Gujarat Refinery and numerous public and private enterprises.
