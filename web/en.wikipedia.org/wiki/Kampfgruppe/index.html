Kampfgruppe

In military terminology, especially that of the German armed forces, a Kampfgruppe (plural Kampfgruppen; literally "battle group" or "combat group") is an ad hoc combined-arms formation organised for a specific task, typically built around an infantry or armoured core with attached artillery, armour, engineers and other supporting elements. The formation is usually named after its commander, as with Kampfgruppe Peiper during the Battle of the Bulge.

History

Improvised battle groups were used by the Imperial German Army in the First World War, but the concept became a hallmark of the Wehrmacht and Waffen-SS in the Second World War. German doctrine prized flexibility: units depleted by combat or grouped for a particular mission were reorganised into kampfgruppen ranging in size from a company to the equivalent of a division. Late in the war, as German formations were shattered, kampfgruppen were increasingly improvised remnants.

Related concepts

The idea parallels other armies' ad hoc groupings, such as the American task force and combat command, the British battlegroup, and the modern NATO battlegroup. In East Germany the term also denoted the Kampfgruppen der Arbeiterklasse (Combat Groups of the Working Class), a factory-based paramilitary reserve organisation — a separate usage.
