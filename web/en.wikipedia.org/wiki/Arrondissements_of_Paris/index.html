The arrondissements of Paris are administrative districts of the city of Paris, France. There are twenty, each governed by an elected council and a mayor, subordinate to the Mayor of Paris. Arranged in a clockwise spiral — often compared to a snail shell, the escargot de Paris — beginning on the Right Bank of the Seine, the arrondissements form the last two digits of Parisian postal codes.

History

Paris was first divided into twelve arrondissements in 1795. When the city annexed surrounding communes in 1860 under Baron Haussmann, the number was increased to the present twenty and the spiral numbering fixed. In 2020 the local administration of the first four (central) arrondissements was merged into a single sector, Paris Centre, though the four districts retain their numbers and identities.

Governance

Each arrondissement elects a council (conseil d'arrondissement) which elects its mayor. Since 1983 these elections have coincided with municipal elections, and arrondissement councillors also sit on the Council of Paris. Their powers cover local facilities such as nurseries, schools' buildings and green spaces, while city-wide policy rests with the Mayor of Paris. This is a municipal arrondissement, a different concept from France's departmental arrondissements.
