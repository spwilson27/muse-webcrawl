The Pink Panther is an American media franchise focusing on a series of comedy-mystery films featuring the incompetent French detective Inspector Jacques Clouseau. The franchise began with the release of the film The Pink Panther in 1963, directed by Blake Edwards and starring Peter Sellers as Clouseau alongside David Niven, Robert Wagner, Capucine and Claudia Cardinale. The "Pink Panther" of the title is, in the films, an enormously valuable pink diamond; the animated pink panther character created for the first film's opening credits by Friz Freleng's studio became a star in his own right, headlining theatrical cartoons and television series.

The original films

In the 1963 film, jewel thief Sir Charles Lytton ("the Phantom") schemes to steal the Pink Panther diamond from Princess Dala at an Italian ski resort, while the bumbling Inspector Clouseau — unaware that his own wife is the thief's accomplice — investigates. Although planned as a vehicle for Niven, Sellers's Clouseau stole the picture, and the series pivoted to centre on him. Sequels followed: A Shot in the Dark (1964), which introduced Herbert Lom as Clouseau's increasingly deranged superior Dreyfus and Burt Kwouk as his manservant Cato; Inspector Clouseau (1968, with Alan Arkin); The Return of the Pink Panther (1975); The Pink Panther Strikes Again (1976); Revenge of the Pink Panther (1978); and, after Sellers's death in 1980, Trail of the Pink Panther (1982) and Curse of the Pink Panther (1983), followed by Son of the Pink Panther (1993) with Roberto Benigni.

Music and the animated panther

Henry Mancini's slinky jazz theme for the 1963 film, featuring Plas Johnson's tenor saxophone, became one of the most recognisable film themes ever recorded. The animated title sequence led to the Oscar-winning short The Pink Phink (1964) and a long run of theatrical cartoons produced by DePatie–Freleng Enterprises, followed by the NBC television series The Pink Panther Show (1969–1978) and later revivals. The character also became widely known as the mascot of Owens Corning insulation.

Reboot

The series was rebooted in 2006 with The Pink Panther, starring Steve Martin as Clouseau, with Kevin Kline, Beyoncé Knowles and Jean Reno; a sequel followed in 2009. The rebooted films were commercial successes despite mixed critical reviews.
