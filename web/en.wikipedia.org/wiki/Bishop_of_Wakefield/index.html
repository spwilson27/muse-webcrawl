The Bishop of Wakefield is a bishop of the Church of England. The title originated with the Diocese of Wakefield, created in 1888 from the southern part of the Diocese of Ripon to serve the industrial towns of the West Riding of Yorkshire.

History

William Walsham How, previously a suffragan Bishop of Bedford, became the first Bishop of Wakefield in 1888 and was notably involved in social ministry in the industrial parishes. Wakefield Cathedral, the former parish church of All Saints, became the seat of the bishop.

When the Diocese of Wakefield was dissolved in 2014 to form the Diocese of Leeds, the title was preserved for an area bishop covering the Wakefield episcopal area, including Wakefield, Huddersfield, Dewsbury, and Halifax.

Role

The Bishop of Wakefield works alongside the Bishop of Leeds and the other area bishops, with particular responsibility for parishes and clergy in the Wakefield area of West Yorkshire.
