Fried parameter

The Fried parameter, or Fried coherence length, denoted r0, is a measure of the quality of optical transmission through the atmosphere due to random inhomogeneities in its refractive index. Introduced by David L. Fried in the 1960s, it represents the diameter of a circular telescope aperture over which the root-mean-square wavefront aberration becomes about 1 radian; equivalently, it is the aperture size at which atmospheric turbulence begins to dominate over diffraction in limiting image resolution.

Definition

Formally, r0 is defined in terms of the refractive-index structure constant integrated along the line of sight: roughly, r0 = [0.423 k² sec(ζ) ∫ Cn²(z) dz]^(-3/5), where k is the optical wavenumber and ζ the zenith angle. The Fried parameter is derived from Kolmogorov turbulence theory and is closely related to the astronomical concept of seeing: the seeing-limited angular resolution is approximately λ/r0.

Applications

The Fried parameter determines the performance of adaptive optics systems: the deformable mirror must have actuators spaced on the order of r0, and the correction must be updated on the coherence timescale τ0 (also due to Fried). It is measured with instruments such as the Differential Image Motion Monitor (DIMM). At excellent observatory sites r0 can exceed 20–30 cm in the near infrared, while urban or daytime conditions may reduce it to a few centimetres.
