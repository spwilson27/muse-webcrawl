Munich massacre

The Munich massacre was a terrorist attack during the 1972 Summer Olympics in Munich, West Germany, carried out by eight members of the Palestinian militant group Black September. They took eleven members of the Israeli Olympic team hostage, all of whom were killed, along with a West German police officer, during a failed rescue attempt.

Attack

In the early hours of 5 September, attackers scaled the fence of the Olympic Village, entered the Israeli quarters at Connollystraße 31, killed two team members who resisted, and took nine hostage, demanding release of 234 Palestinian prisoners. After negotiations, hostages and captors were flown by helicopter to Fürstenfeldbruck airbase, where a botched ambush by German police ended in the deaths of all nine remaining hostages.

Aftermath

The Games were suspended for a day for a memorial service, then continued — a decision still debated. Three surviving attackers were captured but released weeks later after a Lufthansa hijacking. Israel responded with Operation Wrath of God assassinations. A memorial at the Olympic Park and a museum were inaugurated in 2017; in 2022 Germany apologized and agreed compensation with victims' families.
