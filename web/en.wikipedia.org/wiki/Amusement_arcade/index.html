Amusement arcade

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A video game arcade, the dominant form of the amusement arcade since the late 1970s.

An amusement arcade (also called a penny arcade, video arcade, or game center) is a venue housing coin-operated amusement machines: video games, pinball machines, electro-mechanical games, redemption games, claw cranes, and similar attractions. Arcades flourished in boardwalks, seaside resorts, malls, and dedicated premises throughout the twentieth century.

History

The "penny arcade" of the early 1900s offered mutoscopes, fortune tellers, strength testers, and mechanical games for a penny. Pinball expanded the business from the 1930s. The modern video arcade began with Computer Space (1971) and Pong (1972), then exploded in the golden age of arcade video games (roughly 1978–1983) with Space Invaders, Pac-Man, and Donkey Kong. After the video game crash of 1983, arcades revived in the 1990s on fighting games such as Street Fighter II and Mortal Kombat, racing games, and rhythm games.

Decline and persistence

Home consoles and PC gaming eroded the arcade's advantages in the West from the late 1990s, and most standalone arcades closed. Arcades remain culturally central in Japan, where "game centers" offer prize machines, rhythm games, and medal games, and have found niches elsewhere through barcades, retro arcades, esports venues, and family entertainment centers built around redemption games and prizes.
