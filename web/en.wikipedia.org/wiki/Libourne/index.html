Libourne (French pronunciation: [libuʁn]; Gascon: Liborna) is a commune in the Gironde department in Nouvelle-Aquitaine in southwestern France. It lies at the confluence of the Isle and Dordogne rivers, about 35 kilometres northeast of Bordeaux, and is best known as a centre of the wine trade for the nearby vineyards of Saint-Émilion, Pomerol and Fronsac.

History

Libourne was founded as a bastide town in 1270 by Roger de Leybourne, an English seneschal of Gascony, from whom the town takes its name. Built on the site of the Roman settlement of Fozera, it served as a port for the wine trade of the Dordogne valley during the period when Aquitaine was held by the English crown. The town's grid plan, central square and arcaded houses are characteristic of the bastides of the southwest.

During the Hundred Years' War the town changed hands several times before passing definitively to France after the Battle of Castillon in 1453. Its port remained important for the shipment of wine down the Dordogne to Bordeaux and onward to England and northern Europe.

Geography

The town stands on the left bank of the Dordogne where the river is joined by the Isle. The surrounding countryside is dominated by vineyards, including some of the most celebrated wine estates in the world around Saint-Émilion and Pomerol, a few kilometres to the east.

Economy

Libourne's economy centres on wine: it hosts négociant houses, cooperages, and the administrative offices of appellations of the Libournais. Tourism related to the wine country, and the town's weekly markets, are also significant.

Sights

  • The Place Abel Surchamp, the arcaded central square
  • The Tour du Grand Port, a surviving medieval gate tower on the river
  • The church of Saint-Jean-Baptiste
  • The quay along the Dordogne and the Esplanade de la République
