Borough Market is a wholesale and retail food market in Southwark, London, clustered beneath the railway viaducts approaching London Bridge station. One of the largest and oldest food markets in London — with roots claimed in a market at the foot of London Bridge in the 11th century, and a market on the present site since 1756 — it is now celebrated as a destination for specialty, artisan and street food.

[ Image: Stalls and shoppers beneath the railway arches of Borough Market ]

History

A market serving the south end of London Bridge existed by the 11th century. After complaints of congestion, Parliament closed the bridge market in 1754 and authorised the parish of St Saviour's to establish a new market nearby; it opened on the Rochester Yard site in 1756. The present buildings date largely from the 1850s–1860s, including an Art Deco entrance of 1932 on Borough High Street. Through the 19th and 20th centuries Borough functioned principally as a wholesale fruit and vegetable market, supplied by rail.

Modern market

From the 1990s the market reinvented itself around high-quality retail food: a monthly, then weekly, retail market drew specialist producers — cheese makers, bakers, butchers, fishmongers and importers — and a daily trading and street-food scene developed in the Three Crown Square, Green Market and Market Hall areas. The market is run by a charitable trust of trustees, and its traders operate under strict provenance rules. It has featured in films including Bridget Jones's Diary and Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban.
