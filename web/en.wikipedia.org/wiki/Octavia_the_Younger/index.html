Octavia the Younger (Latin: Octavia Minor; c. 69 BC – 11 BC) was the elder sister of the first Roman emperor, Augustus (known as Octavian during her lifetime), and the fourth wife of the triumvir Mark Antony. She was one of the most prominent women of the late Roman Republic, celebrated for her virtue, loyalty and dignity, and she played an important role as an intermediary between her brother and her husband during the years of the Second Triumvirate.

Life

Octavia was the daughter of Gaius Octavius and Atia, niece of Julius Caesar. Her first marriage, to Gaius Claudius Marcellus, produced three children, including Marcus Claudius Marcellus, later the husband of Augustus's daughter Julia and, for a time, regarded as a possible heir. Widowed in 40 BC, Octavia was married to Mark Antony to seal the Pact of Brundisium between Antony and Octavian. She had two daughters with Antony, Antonia the Elder and Antonia the Younger (grandmother and mother respectively of emperors).

Octavia repeatedly worked to preserve the peace, notably at the Pact of Tarentum in 37 BC. Antony's liaison with Cleopatra of Egypt, however, undermined the marriage; in 35 BC Octavia attempted to bring him troops and supplies in Athens but was ordered home, and Antony divorced her in 32 BC. After Antony's defeat and death she raised his children by Cleopatra and Fulvia alongside her own. She never remarried and was famed for her mourning for her son Marcellus, who died in 23 BC; Virgil's recitation of the passage on Marcellus in Book VI of the Aeneid was said to have made her faint.

Legacy

Augustus named the Portico of Octavia (Porticus Octaviae) in Rome in her honour. On her death in 11 BC she received a public funeral, and Augustus delivered a eulogy. Ancient authors including Plutarch and Seneca held her up as a model of Roman womanly virtue.
