Pau Claris i Casademunt (1 January 1586 – 27 February 1641) was a Catalan lawyer, cleric and politician who served as the 94th President of the Generalitat de Catalunya from 22 July 1638 until his death. He is best known for leading the Catalan revolt against the Spanish monarchy at the start of the Reapers' War (Guerra dels Segadors) and for proclaiming the Catalan Republic in January 1641.

Early life and career

Claris was born in Barcelona into a family of lawyers and studied law at the University of Barcelona, taking a doctorate in civil and canon law. He entered the church, becoming a canon of the cathedral of La Seu d'Urgell, and built a legal career defending Catalan institutions and privileges against the encroachments of the Spanish crown. As a member of the Braç Eclesiàstic (ecclesiastical estate) in the Catalan Corts, he emerged as a leading defender of the Catalan constitutions.

Presidency and the Reapers' War

Elected president of the Diputació del General in 1638, Claris confronted the crisis caused by the Franco-Spanish War, in which the Count-Duke of Olivares quartered Castilian troops in Catalonia and demanded Catalan men and money for the war effort. Abuses by billeted soldiers provoked the Corpus de Sang riots in Barcelona on 7 June 1640, in which the Spanish viceroy was killed and the Reapers' War began. Claris directed the defence of the principality, sought French support, and, when Castilian forces advanced on Barcelona, radicalised the revolt: on 16–17 January 1641 he proclaimed the Catalan Republic under French protection, and within days the Generalitat accepted Louis XIII of France as Count of Barcelona.

Death and legacy

Claris died suddenly on 27 February 1641, shortly after the Catalan-French victory at the Battle of Montjuïc; contemporaries suspected poisoning, though modern historians consider a natural illness likely. He is remembered in Catalonia as a national hero and a symbol of resistance to centralising absolutism. A prominent street in Barcelona's Eixample district bears his name, and a monument by Rafael Atché stands on the Passeig de Lluís Companys.
