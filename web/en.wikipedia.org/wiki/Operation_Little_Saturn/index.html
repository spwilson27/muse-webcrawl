Operation Little Saturn

Date: 16–30 December 1942

Location: Middle Don region, Soviet Union

Part of: Battle of Stalingrad, Eastern Front, Second World War

Belligerents: Soviet Union vs. Italy, Germany, Romania

Result: Soviet victory

Operation Little Saturn (Russian: операция «Малый Сатурн») was a Red Army offensive on the Eastern Front in the Second World War, conducted from 16 to 30 December 1942. It was a scaled-down version of the planned Operation Saturn, mounted after Operation Uranus had encircled the German 6th Army at Stalingrad.

Background

Operation Saturn had envisioned a deep drive to Rostov to cut off all German forces in the Caucasus. When Field Marshal Erich von Manstein launched Operation Winter Storm to relieve Stalingrad, the Soviet high command (Stavka) redirected the forces into the more limited Little Saturn, aimed at destroying the Italian 8th Army on the middle Don and threatening the airfields supplying the Stalingrad pocket.

The offensive

The Southwestern and Voronezh Fronts attacked on 16 December in bitter cold and fog. Soviet forces broke through the Italian 8th Army's positions along the Don, and mobile corps raced south. In the Tatsinskaya Raid, General Vasily Badanov's 24th Tank Corps advanced over 150 miles to attack the airfield at Tatsinskaya on 24 December, destroying dozens of German transport aircraft used to supply Stalingrad before being forced to withdraw. The threat to the relief operation's rear compelled Manstein to abandon the drive on Stalingrad on 23 December — the relief attempt that culminated in the fighting at Verkhnekumsky and the Aksay River line.

Outcome

The Italian 8th Army was effectively destroyed, with losses estimated at some 84,830 men out of 235,000, and the Axis front on the Don collapsed. Little Saturn secured the northern flank of the Stalingrad encirclement and set the stage for the Soviet offensives of January–February 1943 that drove Axis forces back toward the Donets.
