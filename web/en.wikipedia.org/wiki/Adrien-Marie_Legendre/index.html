Adrien-Marie Legendre

Adrien-Marie Legendre (18 September 1752 – 9 January 1833) was a French mathematician who made numerous contributions to mathematics, including number theory, elliptic integrals, celestial mechanics and statistics. Well-known concepts bearing his name include the Legendre polynomials and Legendre transformation. He published the method of least squares in 1805, in a priority dispute with Carl Friedrich Gauss, who had used it earlier but published later.

Work

Legendre's Essai sur la théorie des nombres (1798) was the first systematic treatise on number theory; in it he stated the law of quadratic reciprocity (later proved by Gauss) and introduced the Legendre symbol. His work on elliptic integrals, developed over four decades in the Exercices de calcul intégral and the Traité des fonctions elliptiques, classified the integrals into three kinds and laid groundwork later transformed by Abel and Jacobi. In 1805 he published the method of least squares for fitting curves to observations, applying it to the shapes of comets; his Éléments de géométrie (1794) was a hugely successful reformulation of Euclid, used in schools for a century. He also contributed to the determination of the metre, serving on the commission for the meridian survey. His name is one of the 72 inscribed on the Eiffel Tower.
