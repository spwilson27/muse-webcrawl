The Oculus Quest is a standalone virtual reality headset developed by Oculus, a division of Facebook (now Meta Platforms). Released on 21 May 2019, it was the first widely successful all-in-one VR headset: all processing is done on the device itself, using a Qualcomm Snapdragon 835 processor, and it requires no PC, console or external sensors.

Hardware and tracking

The Quest uses "Oculus Insight", an inside-out tracking system in which four wide-angle cameras on the headset track the room and the two Oculus Touch controllers, providing six degrees of freedom (6DoF) for both head and hands. Its OLED display has a resolution of 1440 × 1600 per eye at a 72 Hz refresh rate, and software-based interpupillary distance adjustment is supplemented by a physical slider. Storage options were 64 GB and 128 GB, priced at launch at US$399 and US$499 respectively.

Software

The headset runs an Android-based operating system and games and apps from the Oculus Store; quality standards for Quest titles were stricter than for earlier Oculus platforms. Updates added hand tracking and "Oculus Link", allowing the Quest to function as a PC headset over USB. Popular titles included Beat Saber, Superhot VR, Vader Immortal and Moss.

Reception and legacy

Reviews praised the Quest's freedom from wires and ease of use, calling it a landmark product for consumer VR. It was succeeded in October 2020 by the Quest 2, after which the original Quest was discontinued; the Oculus brand itself was later retired in favour of Meta Quest.
