Shusha (Azerbaijani: Şuşa; Armenian: Շուշի, Shushi) is a city in the Nagorno-Karabakh region of Azerbaijan, situated on a high plateau at about 1,400–1,800 metres in the Karabakh mountains. It is one of the most culturally significant cities of the Caucasus, long a centre of Azerbaijani music and poetry and of Armenian religious life, and a focal point of the conflict over Nagorno-Karabakh.

History

The fortress town was founded in the 1750s by Panah Ali Khan, founder of the Karabakh Khanate, as Panahabad, and became the khanate's capital. Under the Russian Empire it grew into a major commercial and cultural city of the Caucasus, famous for its carpet weaving, silk, and musicians and poets such as the singer-composer Uzeyir Hajibeyov's circle and the poet Khurshidbanu Natavan. The Armenian-majority eastern quarters were destroyed in the 1920 Shusha massacre during the Armenian–Azerbaijani war, and the city's Armenian population was killed or expelled.

Recent history

Armenian forces captured Shusha in May 1992 during the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, a turning point that secured the corridor between Armenia and Nagorno-Karabakh; the Azerbaijani population fled. The city (as Shushi) was administered by the unrecognised Republic of Artsakh until Azerbaijani forces retook it on 8 November 2020 during the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War, days before the ceasefire that ended the fighting. Azerbaijan has since undertaken reconstruction, declared Shusha its cultural capital, and revived festivals such as the Khari Bulbul music festival; the remaining Armenian population departed in September 2023 when Azerbaijan took full control of the region. Landmarks include the Shusha fortress walls, the Ghazanchetsots (Holy Saviour) Cathedral, the Yukhari Govhar Agha Mosque, and the Jidir Duzu plain.

Culture

Shusha is regarded as the cradle of Azerbaijani mugham music and the home of the celebrated Khari Bulbul flower, and it has produced many noted composers, singers, and tar players, as well as Armenian writers, clergy, and military figures. The city was named a cultural capital of the Turkic world for 2023 by TURKSOY.
