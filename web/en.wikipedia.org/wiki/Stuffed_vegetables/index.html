Stuffed vegetables

Stuffed vegetables: peppers, courgettes and tomatoes filled with rice and herbs

Stuffed vegetables are dishes in which hollowed-out vegetables are filled with a savoury stuffing and then cooked, by baking, braising or steaming. The technique is found across the Mediterranean, the Middle East, the Balkans, the Caucasus and South Asia, wherever firm-fleshed vegetables such as peppers, tomatoes, courgettes (zucchini), aubergines (eggplants), onions and vine or cabbage leaves are available.

Fillings and varieties

The archetypal filling combines rice or bulgur with minced meat, onions, herbs and spices, though meatless versions — in Turkish called zeytinyağlı ("with olive oil") and served cold — use rice, currants, pine nuts and herbs. Well-known examples include:

  • Dolma — the broad family of stuffed dishes of the former Ottoman world, including stuffed vine leaves (sarma).
  • Gemista — Greek stuffed tomatoes and peppers with rice.
  • Mahshi — Levantine and Egyptian stuffed courgettes, aubergines and peppers.
  • Stuffed peppers — bell peppers filled with seasoned meat and rice, found from Hungary (töltött paprika) to the United States.
  • Chiles rellenos — Mexican poblano chillies stuffed with cheese or picadillo, battered and fried.

Preparation

Vegetables are cored or scooped, blanched if tough, filled loosely to allow the grain to expand, and cooked in a flavoured liquid — often tomato-based — until tender. Leaf-wrapped versions are rolled tightly and arranged in layers in the pot, sometimes weighted with a plate.
