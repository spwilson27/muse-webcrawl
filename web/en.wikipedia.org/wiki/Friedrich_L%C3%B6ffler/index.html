Friedrich Löffler

Born24 June 1852, Frankfurt (Oder)
Died9 April 1915, Berlin
FieldsBacteriology, virology

Friedrich August Johannes Loeffler (24 June 1852 – 9 April 1915) was a German bacteriologist, a student of Robert Koch, and a pioneer of virology. He is best known for discovering the causative organism of diphtheria and, together with Paul Frosch, for demonstrating in 1898 that foot-and-mouth disease is caused by a filterable agent — the first virus of an animal to be identified.

Career

Loeffler studied medicine at Würzburg and Berlin and joined Koch's Imperial Health Office in 1879. In 1884 he isolated the diphtheria bacillus (Corynebacterium diphtheriae), still sometimes called the Klebs–Loeffler bacterium, confirming Edwin Klebs's earlier observation. He developed Loeffler's methylene blue stain and coagulated serum culture media still associated with his name. In 1888 he became professor of hygiene at the University of Greifswald, where he founded an institute for infectious diseases.

Foot-and-mouth disease and virology

Loeffler and Frosch showed that lymph from animals with foot-and-mouth disease remained infectious after passing through filters that held back bacteria, concluding that the agent was a soluble toxin or an extremely small replicating particle — founding work in the science of viruses. In 1910 he founded a research institute on the island of Riems near Greifswald devoted to the disease; the Friedrich-Loeffler-Institut, Germany's federal research institute for animal health, bears his name and remains based there.
