Franz Xaver Gruber

Franz Xaver Gruber (25 November 1787 – 7 June 1863) was an Austrian primary school teacher, church organist and composer, best known for composing the music of the Christmas carol "Silent Night" (Stille Nacht) to words by Joseph Mohr.

Born: 25 November 1787, Hochburg-Ach, Austria
Died: 7 June 1863, Hallein, Austria
Occupation: Teacher, organist, composer
Known for: Music of "Silent Night" (1818)

Life

Gruber was born in the village of Hochburg-Ach in Upper Austria, the son of a linen weaver. He worked as a weaver until the age of 18 before training as a schoolteacher. In 1807 he became teacher, cantor and organist at Arnsdorf and also served as organist and choirmaster at the church of St Nicholas in the neighbouring town of Oberndorf bei Salzburg, where he met the young priest Joseph Mohr.

"Silent Night"

On Christmas Eve 1818, Mohr brought Gruber a poem he had written two years earlier and asked him to compose a melody and guitar accompaniment for two solo voices and choir. The carol was performed that night at the midnight mass in Oberndorf — famously, according to legend, because the church organ was broken, though this detail is disputed. Gruber later wrote an account of the event (1854) and produced several arrangements of the melody. The song spread through travelling folk singers and became one of the world's most famous carols, translated into hundreds of languages and declared an intangible cultural heritage by UNESCO in 2011.

Later life

In 1835 Gruber moved to Hallein, where he served as choirmaster, singer and organist at the parish church and composed numerous other church works, including masses and songs, most of which remain in local use in Austria. He died there in 1863. The Gruber–Mohr memorial and the Silent Night Chapel in Oberndorf commemorate the carol's creation.
