Microblogging

Microblogging
Type: Online broadcast medium
Notable services: X (Twitter), Tumblr, Weibo, Threads
Characteristic: Short, frequent posts

Microblogging is a broadcast medium in the form of blogging in which users publish short posts — originally restricted to a small number of characters — frequently and in real time. Microblog posts typically consist of brief text updates, often accompanied by images, links, hashtags or short video clips, and are distributed to followers or subscribers.

History

The practice emerged in the mid-2000s. Twitter, launched in 2006 with a 140-character limit inherited from SMS text messaging, popularised the format worldwide; Tumblr (2007) popularised "tumblelogging" of mixed media. China's Sina Weibo, launched in 2009, became the dominant microblogging service there. Other services include Plurk, Mastodon, Bluesky and Threads.

Use and influence

Microblogging has been used for breaking news, political campaigning, protest organisation, customer service, and real-time commentary during live events. Hashtags and trending-topic lists became central mechanisms for aggregating conversation. Critics associate the format with information overload, brevity-driven oversimplification, harassment and the spread of misinformation, while advocates emphasise its democratisation of publishing — anyone with a phone can address a global audience instantly.
