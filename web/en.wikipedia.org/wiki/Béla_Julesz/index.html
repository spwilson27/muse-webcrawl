Béla Julesz

Béla Julesz (19 February 1928 – 31 December 2003) was a Hungarian-born American visual neuroscientist and experimental psychologist in the fields of visual and auditory perception. He is best known for inventing the random-dot stereogram in 1959, which demonstrated that binocular depth perception can arise from disparity alone, without monocular cues, and for his influential theory of textons in preattentive vision.

Biography

Julesz received his PhD in electrical engineering from the Hungarian Academy of Sciences in 1956 and emigrated to the United States after the Hungarian Revolution, joining Bell Telephone Laboratories in 1956. There he worked for more than three decades on visual perception. In 1989 he moved to Rutgers University, where he founded the Laboratory of Vision Research and became State of New Jersey Professor of Psychology.

Research

The random-dot stereogram showed that the brain computes depth from matching patterns between the two eyes even when each eye alone sees only noise—evidence for what Julesz called the "cyclopean eye". His book Foundations of Cyclopean Perception (1971) became a classic. In the 1980s he proposed that preattentive texture discrimination depends on primitive features he called textons.

Honours

Julesz was elected to the National Academy of Sciences and received a MacArthur "genius" Fellowship in 1983.
