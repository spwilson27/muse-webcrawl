Così fan tutte

Così fan tutte, ossia La scuola degli amanti ("Thus do they all, or The School for Lovers"), K. 588, is an Italian-language opera buffa in two acts by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, with a libretto by Lorenzo Da Ponte. It premiered at the Burgtheater in Vienna on 26 January 1790. It is the third of the Mozart–Da Ponte operas, after The Marriage of Figaro and Don Giovanni.

Synopsis

The cynical Don Alfonso wagers with two young officers, Ferrando and Guglielmo, that their fiancées — the sisters Dorabella and Fiordiligi — are not as faithful as they believe. Disguised as exotic strangers, the men court each other's lovers; aided by the maid Despina, the women eventually yield, before the deception is revealed and the couples are (ambiguously) reunited.

Reception

The opera's subject was long considered immoral or trivial, and it was rarely performed in the 19th century, often with altered libretti. In the 20th century it was restored to the core repertory and is now regarded as one of Mozart's finest ensemble works, praised for music of beauty and irony — including the aria "Soave sia il vento" and Fiordiligi's "Come scoglio."
