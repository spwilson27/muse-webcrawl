Stephen Daldry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stephen David Daldry (born 2 May 1960) is an English director and producer of theatre and film. He has been nominated for the Academy Award for Best Director for each of his first three feature films — Billy Elliot (2000), The Hours (2002), and The Reader (2008) — a record matched by few directors. His stage work includes the original productions of An Inspector Calls and the musical Billy Elliot, and he directed episodes of the Netflix series The Crown.

Early life and theatre career

Daldry was born in Dorset, England, and grew up in Taunton, Somerset. He studied English at the University of Sheffield and trained at the East 15 Acting School. He began directing at the Crucible Theatre, Sheffield, worked at the Gate Theatre in London, and became artistic director of the Royal Court Theatre in 1992. His radical 1992 revival of J. B. Priestley's An Inspector Calls for the National Theatre transferred to the West End and Broadway, becoming one of the most celebrated British productions of the decade.

Film career

His feature debut, Billy Elliot (2000), about a young boy from a mining family who aspires to dance ballet during the 1984–85 miners' strike, was a major commercial and critical success. The Hours (2002) interwove the lives of three women connected by Virginia Woolf's Mrs Dalloway and won Nicole Kidman the Academy Award for Best Actress. The Reader (2008), adapted from Bernhard Schlink's novel, won Kate Winslet the Academy Award for Best Actress. Later films include Extremely Loud & Incredibly Close (2011), nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture, and Trash (2014).

Stage and television

Daldry directed the stage musical adaptation of Billy Elliot (music by Elton John), which won ten Tony Awards including Best Musical and Best Director. He directed the first two episodes of The Crown (2016) and served as an executive producer on the series. He also directed Matthew Lopez's play The Inheritance (2018), which won the Tony Award for Best Play, with Daldry winning Best Director of a Play. He was appointed a Commander of the Order of the British Empire (CBE) in 2004.
