Tivoli, Lazio

Tivoli (ancient Tibur) is a town and comune in Lazio, Italy, about 30 kilometres east-north-east of Rome, on the falls of the Aniene river where it issues from the Sabine hills. Its population is around 55,000.

History

Tibur was an ancient Sabine-Latin city, conquered by Rome in the 4th century BC, famed as a cool summer retreat for wealthy Romans. Emperor Hadrian built his vast villa nearby, and the poet Horace had an estate in the area.

Sights

Tivoli hosts two UNESCO World Heritage Sites: Hadrian's Villa (Villa Adriana), the huge 2nd-century imperial palace complex, and the Villa d'Este, a Renaissance palace famous for its terraced gardens and hundreds of fountains, built for Cardinal Ippolito II d'Este. The older Villa Gregoriana park, with its great waterfall, and the ancient Temple of the Sibyl complete the town's celebrated ensemble.
