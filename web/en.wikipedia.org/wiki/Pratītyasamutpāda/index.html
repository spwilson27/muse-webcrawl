EnglishDependent origination, dependent arising, interdependent co-arising

Pratītyasamutpāda (Sanskrit: प्रतीत्यसमुत्पाद; Pali: paṭiccasamuppāda), commonly translated as dependent origination or dependent arising, is a key doctrine in Buddhism shared by all schools. It states that all dharmas ("things" or "phenomena") arise in dependence upon other dharmas: "if this exists, that exists; if this ceases to exist, that also ceases to exist". Nothing exists independently or by its own essence, and no phenomenon is caused by a single independent cause.

The general principle

The underlying principle is one of conditionality: phenomena arise when a complex of conditions is present and cease when those conditions cease. In the early texts this is applied above all to dukkha (suffering, unsatisfactoriness): suffering arises dependent on conditions — principally ignorance and craving — and can therefore be ended by removing those conditions, which is the logic underlying the Four Noble Truths and the Noble Eightfold Path.

The twelve nidānas

The best-known formulation is the chain of twelve nidānas (links), presented in both a forward order explaining the arising of suffering and a reverse order explaining its cessation:

  1. Ignorance (avidyā)
  2. Volitional formations (saṃskāra)
  3. Consciousness (vijñāna)
  4. Mind-and-body (nāmarūpa)
  5. The six sense bases (ṣaḍāyatana)
  6. Contact (sparśa)
  7. Feeling (vedanā)
  8. Craving (tṛṣṇā)
  9. Clinging (upādāna)
  10. Becoming (bhava)
  11. Birth (jāti)
  12. Ageing and death (jarāmaraṇa), with sorrow and suffering

Theravāda commentaries typically interpret the twelve links as spanning three lifetimes; other readings, including many modern ones, treat them as describing processes within a single lifetime or a single moment of experience. The chain is often depicted pictorially around the rim of the bhavacakra (wheel of life).

In Mahāyāna thought

In Mahāyāna Buddhism, Nāgārjuna's Mūlamadhyamakakārikā equates dependent origination with emptiness (śūnyatā): because things arise dependently, they are empty of inherent existence (svabhāva), and this dependent designation is itself the middle way between eternalism and nihilism. In East Asian Buddhism, especially Huayan, dependent origination was elaborated into the vision of the mutual interpenetration of all phenomena, symbolised by Indra's net.
