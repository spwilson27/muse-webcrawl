The Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E) is an American investor-owned utility with publicly traded stock. The company is headquartered in San Francisco, California. It provides natural gas and electricity to 5.2 million households in the northern two-thirds of California, from Bakersfield and northern Santa Barbara County, almost to the Oregon and Nevada state lines.

Overseen by the California Public Utilities Commission, PG&E is the leading subsidiary of the holding company PG&E Corporation, which has a market capitalization of $34.9 billion as of March 2025. PG&E was established on October 10, 1905, from the merger of various predecessor companies.

History

The company traces its origins to the San Francisco Gas Company, founded in 1852. Through a series of mergers and acquisitions of hydroelectric and gas companies across Northern California, PG&E grew into one of the largest utilities in the United States during the early twentieth century, building dams, hydroelectric plants, and an extensive transmission network.

Bankruptcies and controversies

In 2001, amid the California electricity crisis, PG&E filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection, emerging in 2004. In January 2019, the company filed for bankruptcy a second time, facing tens of billions of dollars in potential liabilities from wildfires linked to its equipment, including the 2018 Camp Fire, the deadliest wildfire in California history. PG&E emerged from bankruptcy in July 2020 after agreeing to pay $25.5 billion to settle wildfire claims. In 2020, the company pleaded guilty to 84 counts of involuntary manslaughter in connection with the Camp Fire.

The utility has also been associated with the Hinkley groundwater contamination case, settled in 1996, which was dramatized in the film Erin Brockovich (2000), and with the 2010 San Bruno pipeline explosion, which killed eight people.

Service territory

PG&E's service area covers roughly 70,000 square miles (180,000 km2) of Northern and Central California, serving approximately 16 million people. Its generation portfolio includes the Diablo Canyon Power Plant, the state's last operating nuclear power plant, as well as hydroelectric facilities, natural gas plants, and growing solar and battery storage resources.
