Afrofuturism

Afrofuturism
Term coined: 1993, Mark Dery
Media: Literature, music, film, visual art

Afrofuturism is a cultural aesthetic and philosophy of science that explores the intersection of the African diaspora with science and technology, addressing themes of alienation, reclamation, and imagined futures through a Black cultural lens. Cultural critic Mark Dery coined the term in 1993.

Themes and history

Precursors include W. E. B. Du Bois, Sun Ra's cosmic jazz philosophy, authors Ralph Ellison and Octavia Butler, musician George Clinton's Parliament-Funkadelic, and Lee "Scratch" Perry. Later exponents include Janelle Monáe, N. K. Jemisin, Nnedi Okorafor, and the film Black Panther (2018), which brought Afrofuturist aesthetics to global audiences.

Characteristics

Works often combine science fiction, African mythology and history, magical realism, and non-Western cosmology, reimagining Black history — including slavery reframed through alien abduction — and projecting liberated futures.
