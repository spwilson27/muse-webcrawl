Music of Wales

Wales is often called the "land of song", and its musical identity is famously expressed through choral singing, the harp, and a flourishing folk and popular music culture in both Welsh and English.

Traditional music

The triple harp (telyn deires) is Wales's emblematic instrument, played by harpists since the medieval courts of the Welsh princes. Folk traditions include cerdd dant (improvised vocal counterpoint over harp), plygain carol singing at Christmas, ballads, and dance music for fiddle. The Eisteddfod festival movement, including the National Eisteddfod and the Llangollen International Musical Eisteddfod, sustains competitive choral and folk performance.

Choral and classical music

Male voice choirs, born in the industrial valleys of South Wales—such as the Treorchy and Morriston Orpheus choirs—became world famous. Hymn singing (cymanfa ganu) is a strong congregational tradition. Classical figures include composers William Mathias and Grace Williams, opera singer Bryn Terfel, and harpist Catrin Finch.

Popular music

Welsh popular stars include Tom Jones, Shirley Bassey, Bonnie Tyler, and the bands Manic Street Preachers, Stereophonics, Catatonia, Super Furry Animals, and Goldie Lookin Chain, as well as singer Charlotte Church and mezzo-soprano Katherine Jenkins. A Welsh-language music scene thrives, celebrated since 2013 by Welsh Language Music Day (Dydd Miwsig Cymru).
