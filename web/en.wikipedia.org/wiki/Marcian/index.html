Marcian

Reign: 25 August 450 – 27 January 457
Born: c. 392, Thrace or Illyria
Died: 27 January 457, Constantinople
Predecessor: Theodosius II
Successor: Leo I

Marcian (Latin: Flavius Marcianus; c. 392 – 27 January 457) was Eastern Roman emperor from 450 to 457. A soldier and senator of humble origin, he came to the throne through marriage to the Augusta Pulcheria, sister of the childless Theodosius II.

Reign

Marcian reversed his predecessor's policy of paying tribute to Attila the Hun, refusing further payments; Attila died before campaigning against the East. Domestically he reformed finances, curbed corruption and the sale of offices, and left a full treasury. His reign also saw tension with the Roman general Aëtius and the Western Empire, which he aided little during the Vandal sack of Rome in 455.

Council of Chalcedon

Marcian and Pulcheria convened the Council of Chalcedon (451), which defined Christ as one person in two natures, divine and human. The Chalcedonian Definition became the doctrinal standard of the Eastern Orthodox, Catholic, and most Protestant churches, while its rejection by the Oriental Orthodox churches produced a lasting schism. Marcian was later venerated as a saint in the Orthodox Church.
