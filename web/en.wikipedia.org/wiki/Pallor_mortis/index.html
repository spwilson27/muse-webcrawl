Pallor mortis

The first stage of post-mortem change

Pallor mortis (Latin: pallor "paleness", mortis "of death") is the first of the traditional stages of death, an early post-mortem paling of the skin that occurs within 15–25 minutes of death. It is one of the signs used, cautiously, in forensic pathology when estimating the post-mortem interval.

Cause

When the heart stops, capillary circulation ceases and gravity drains blood from the smaller vessels of the skin, which consequently loses its pinkish tone and appears pale — most noticeably in light-skinned individuals. The effect is rapid but of limited forensic value, because it occurs in nearly all bodies and its timing varies with the circumstances of death. Pallor can be difficult to assess in cases of anaemia or, conversely, congestion.

Sequence of post-mortem changes

Pallor mortis is followed by algor mortis (cooling of the body), rigor mortis (stiffening of the muscles) and livor mortis (dependent lividity as blood pools under gravity), before decomposition proper begins. Together these classic stages help investigators construct a rough timeline, though all are affected by temperature, body size and cause of death.
