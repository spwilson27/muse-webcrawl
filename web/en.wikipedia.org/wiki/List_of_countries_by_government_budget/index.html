By share of GDP

European welfare states have the largest governments relative to GDP: general government expenditure exceeds 50% of GDP in France, Finland, Belgium, Italy and Greece, while the United States spends roughly 38–43% of GDP across all levels of government. At the other extreme, some low-income and resource states spend under 15% of GDP.

Surpluses and deficits

Most listed states run structural deficits; persistent surpluses are characteristic of Norway (oil-funded), Singapore, and a few Gulf states in high-oil-price years.
