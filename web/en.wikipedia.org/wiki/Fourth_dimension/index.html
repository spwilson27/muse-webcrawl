Fourth dimension

The fourth dimension is the dimension beyond the three familiar spatial dimensions of length, width, and height. In mathematics, four-dimensional space generalizes ordinary geometry to objects such as the tesseract (4-cube) and the 3-sphere; in physics, the fourth dimension is usually taken to be time, making the universe a four-dimensional spacetime.

Mathematics

Four-dimensional Euclidean space ℝ⁴ extends coordinates (x, y, z) with a fourth axis w. The hypercube, or tesseract, has 16 vertices, 32 edges, 24 square faces, and 8 cubic cells. Remarkably, four-dimensional space is exceptional: it admits exotic smooth structures (exotic ℝ⁴) not present in any other dimension, and rotations occur in two independent planes simultaneously. Charles Howard Hinton popularized the term "tesseract" and wrote extensively on visualizing the fourth dimension in the 1880s.

Physics

Minkowski spacetime treats time as a fourth dimension with a Lorentzian metric, the setting of special and general relativity. Some theories, including Kaluza–Klein theory and string theory, posit additional compactified spatial dimensions beyond four.

In culture

Edwin Abbott's Flatland (1884) used dimensional analogy to satirize Victorian society; H. G. Wells's The Time Machine (1895) popularized time as the fourth dimension. The idea influenced Cubism and works such as Dalí's Crucifixion (Corpus Hypercubus).
