Flevoland is the twelfth and youngest province of the Netherlands, established on 1 January 1986, when the southern and eastern polders of the former Zuiderzee were merged into one province. Almost all of the land in Flevoland was reclaimed from the sea in the 20th century as part of the Zuiderzee Works, and most of it lies below sea level. The capital is Lelystad, named after Cornelis Lely, the engineer who designed the reclamation scheme; the largest city is Almere, a planned city that has grown into one of the Netherlands' biggest urban areas.

Geography

The province consists of two main parts: the Noordoostpolder, drained in 1942 and attached to the mainland, and the Flevopolder — an artificial island formed by Eastern Flevoland (drained 1957) and Southern Flevoland (drained 1968), enclosed by the Markermeer, IJsselmeer and the bordering lakes. The former island of Urk, once in the Zuiderzee, is now a fishing port town landlocked within the Noordoostpolder. Much of the province is intensively farmed, producing potatoes, cereals and tulips; the Oostvaardersplassen nature reserve, a vast wetland that emerged on planned industrial land, is internationally known for its rewilded herds of large herbivores.

History

The province takes its name from Lacus Flevo, the lake mentioned by Roman authors that once occupied the area before the medieval expansion of the Zuiderzee. Land reclamation followed the Zuiderzee Act of 1918 and the completion of the Afsluitdijk in 1932. Flevoland's planned towns, straight canals and wind-turbine-lined fields make it a distinctive monument to Dutch hydraulic engineering.
