Divergent (novel)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Divergent is a 2011 dystopian novel by American author Veronica Roth. It is the first book in the Divergent trilogy, followed by Insurgent (2012) and Allegiant (2013). Set in a post-apocalyptic Chicago, the novel depicts a society divided into five factions, each dedicated to cultivating a particular virtue. It was published by Katherine Tegen Books and became a bestseller, frequently compared with The Hunger Games.

Plot

Society is split into five factions: Abnegation (the selfless), Amity (the peaceful), Candor (the honest), Dauntless (the brave), and Erudite (the intelligent). At sixteen, every citizen takes an aptitude test and then publicly chooses a faction, often leaving their birth faction behind. Beatrice "Tris" Prior, raised in Abnegation, learns from her test that she is Divergent – equally suited to several factions – and is warned to keep this secret, since Divergents are considered dangerous to the social order. She chooses Dauntless and endures its brutal initiation under instructors including the intimidating Eric and the reserved Tobias "Four" Eaton, with whom she falls in love. Tris discovers that the Erudite, led by Jeanine Matthews, plan to use a mind-control simulation serum to force the Dauntless to destroy Abnegation; because she is Divergent, the serum does not control her, and she fights to stop the attack.

Reception and adaptation

The novel received generally positive reviews for its pacing and themes and won favourite-book honours at the Goodreads Choice Awards. A film adaptation, Divergent, directed by Neil Burger and starring Shailene Woodley as Tris and Theo James as Four, was released in 2014 and launched a film series. Roth later published Four: A Divergent Collection (2014), retelling parts of the story from Tobias's perspective.
