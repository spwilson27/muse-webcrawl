Saint Nicholas Day, also called the Feast of Saint Nicholas, is the feast day of Saint Nicholas of Myra, observed on 6 December in Western Christian countries and many European traditions, and on 19 December in Eastern Orthodox churches that follow the Julian calendar. The day is especially associated with children, who receive small gifts, sweets, and fruit in honour of the saint famed for his secret generosity.

Traditions by region

Netherlands and Belgium

In the Netherlands, Sinterklaas is among the year's most important festivals. Saint Nicholas is said to arrive by steamboat from Spain in mid-November, and on the evening of 5 December (Pakjesavond, "gift evening") he delivers presents, accompanied by his helpers, the Zwarte Pieten. Children leave a shoe by the fireplace with a carrot for the saint's horse and find it filled with treats such as pepernoten, chocolate letters, and marzipan. Belgium celebrates on the morning of 6 December.

Germany, Austria, and Central Europe

In Germany, children put a boot (Nikolausstiefel) outside the door on the night of 5 December; obedient children find it filled with sweets and small presents, while in parts of Germany, Austria, and neighbouring countries the saint is accompanied by a frightening companion — Krampus, Knecht Ruprecht, or Pelznickel — who threatens punishment for the naughty. Saint Nicholas visits schools and kindergartens, reading children's deeds from a golden book.

Other observances

The feast is kept in Luxembourg (Kleeschen), French Flanders and Lorraine, Croatia (Sveti Nikola), Slovenia, Hungary (Mikulás), Poland, and Czech lands, where St. Nicholas is often accompanied by an angel and a devil. In Bari, Italy, which holds the saint's relics, the feast is a major local celebration. In the United States, communities of Dutch and German descent maintain the custom in some areas, though it is largely eclipsed by Christmas and the figure of Santa Claus, who evolved in part from Dutch Sinterklaas.
