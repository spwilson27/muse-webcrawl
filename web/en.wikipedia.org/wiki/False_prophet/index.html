False prophet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In religion, a false prophet is a person who falsely claims the gift of prophecy or divine inspiration, or to speak for God, or who makes false prophecies. A true prophet, by contrast, is regarded within a faith tradition as having genuinely received a divine message. The label is often applied polemically: one tradition's prophet may be another's false prophet.

In the Hebrew Bible

The Book of Deuteronomy prescribes tests for prophecy: a prophet whose prediction does not come to pass has spoken presumptuously and, under the law, was liable to death. The prophet Jeremiah contended with Hananiah, who prophesied that Babylonian captivity would end within two years; Jeremiah denounced him as a false prophet, and the narrative reports that Hananiah died the same year.

In the New Testament

Jesus warns in the Sermon on the Mount to "beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing but inwardly are ravenous wolves", adding that they are known "by their fruits". The New Testament also associates false prophecy with end-times deception and with figures such as the "false prophet" of the Book of Revelation, who serves the Beast.

In Islam

Islamic tradition holds Muhammad to be the final prophet ("Seal of the Prophets"), and several figures who claimed prophecy after him — such as Musaylima, who led a rival movement in Arabia during the Ridda wars — are regarded as false prophets. Self-proclaimed prophets remain a sensitive subject across Muslim communities.

Later usage

The term is applied broadly to charismatic religious leaders whose predictions failed, to founders of movements rejected by mainstream traditions, and metaphorically to anyone whose confident predictions prove wrong.
