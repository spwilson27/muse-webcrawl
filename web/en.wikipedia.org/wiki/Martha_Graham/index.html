Martha Graham

Martha Graham
Born: May 11, 1894, Allegheny City, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Died: April 1, 1991 (aged 96), New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Dancer, choreographer
Known for: Founder of the Graham technique of modern dance

Martha Graham (May 11, 1894 – April 1, 1991) was an American modern dancer and choreographer widely regarded as one of the most influential artists of the 20th century. Her Graham technique — built on the principles of contraction and release — reshaped American dance and is still taught worldwide.

Early life and training

Graham was born in Allegheny City (now part of Pittsburgh), Pennsylvania, and moved with her family to Santa Barbara, California, as a teenager. After seeing Ruth St. Denis perform, she enrolled at the Denishawn school in Los Angeles in 1916, where she studied under Ted Shawn.

Career

In 1926 Graham founded the Martha Graham Center of Contemporary Dance in New York and began creating works that rejected the decorative style of ballet in favour of stark, emotionally intense movement drawn from myth, American history, and the human psyche. Her 180 works include Appalachian Spring (1944, with music by Aaron Copland), Cave of the Heart (1946), Errand into the Maze (1947), and Clytemnestra (1958). She collaborated with composers, sculptor Isamu Noguchi, and fashion designer Halston, and trained generations of dancers including Merce Cunningham, Paul Taylor, and Twyla Tharp. She performed into her mid-seventies and choreographed until the end of her life.

Honours

Graham received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1976 and was named "Dancer of the Century" by Time magazine in 1998.
