Nursing assistant

A nursing assistant, also called a nursing aide, healthcare assistant, patient care assistant or (in the United States) a certified nursing assistant (CNA), is a healthcare worker who provides basic care to patients in hospitals, nursing homes, care homes and other settings, under the supervision of registered nurses or licensed practical nurses.

Duties

Nursing assistants typically help patients with activities of daily living that they cannot manage alone, including:

  • Bathing, dressing, grooming and toileting
  • Feeding patients and monitoring food and fluid intake
  • Moving, turning and transferring patients, and helping them walk
  • Taking and recording vital signs such as temperature, pulse, blood pressure and respiration
  • Making beds, keeping rooms tidy and answering call signals
  • Observing and reporting changes in a patient's condition to nursing staff

Training and certification

Training requirements vary by country and state. In the United States, nursing assistants generally complete a state-approved training programme — often a few weeks to a few months of classroom and clinical instruction — and pass a competency examination to become certified and listed on a state registry; many work in long-term care facilities. In the United Kingdom, healthcare assistants usually train on the job and may work toward qualifications such as the Care Certificate. The role is physically and emotionally demanding, is often relatively low-paid, and provides an entry route into healthcare careers including nursing.
