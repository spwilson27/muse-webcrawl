Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer (TV special)

Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer Genre: Christmas television special
Based on: the song by Johnny Marks
Written by: Romeo Muller
Directed by: Larry Roemer
Narrated by: Burl Ives
Original release: 6 December 1964 (NBC)

Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer is a 1964 American Christmas stop-motion animated television special produced by Videocraft International, Ltd. (later known as Rankin/Bass Productions). First broadcast on NBC on 6 December 1964, it has aired every Christmas season since, making it the longest continuously running Christmas special in the United States. The special was filmed in Rankin/Bass's trademark "Animagic" stop-motion puppet process in Japan.

Plot

Narrated and sung by Sam the Snowman (voiced by Burl Ives), the story expands the famous song. Rudolph, a reindeer born with a glowing red nose, is shunned by the other reindeer and flees Christmas Town with Hermey, an elf who wants to be a dentist, and prospector Yukon Cornelius. The trio reach the Island of Misfit Toys, whose residents—a train with square wheels, a polka-dot elephant, a cowboy who rides an ostrich—long for a Santa who will find them children who want them. Rudolph eventually returns home, confronts the Abominable Snow Monster, and, when a storm threatens to cancel Christmas Eve deliveries, Santa asks Rudolph to guide his sleigh with his nose—redeeming all the misfits.

Music and legacy

Johnny Marks, who wrote the original 1949 hit song recorded by Gene Autry, composed the score, including "A Holly Jolly Christmas", "Silver and Gold" and "The Most Wonderful Day of the Year". Burl Ives's recordings from the special became perennial hits. The special anchors the Rankin/Bass catalogue of holiday classics and its puppet figures, character designs and songs remain embedded in American Christmas culture.
