Crossover (fiction)

TypeNarrative device
DefinitionPlacing characters, settings, or elements from two or more otherwise separate fictional works in a single story

A crossover is the placement of two or more otherwise discrete fictional characters, settings, or universes into the context of a single story. Crossovers may be officially authorised productions — common in comics, film franchises, and shared television universes — or unauthorised works of fan fiction.

History

Ancient and folk literature mixed heroes from different cycles, and 19th-century authors occasionally had characters recur across novels. Universal Pictures' monster films of the 1940s (Frankenstein Meets the Wolf Man, 1943) were early commercial film crossovers. Comics publishers systematised the device: DC and Marvel built shared universes in which heroes guest-starred in each other's titles, culminating in company-wide crossover events such as Crisis on Infinite Earths (1985) and Secret Wars (1984).

Types

  • Shared universe crossovers: works formally set in one continuity (e.g. the Marvel Cinematic Universe, assembled in The Avengers, 2012).
  • Versus crossovers: rival characters meet, often in conflict (Alien vs. Predator, Freddy vs. Jason).
  • Intercompany crossovers: characters owned by different publishers collaborate (JLA/Avengers, 2003–04).
  • Spin-off crossovers: a character moves between a parent series and its spin-off, common in television franchises such as Law & Order or the Chicago shows.
  • Amalgamation: characters are fused into composites, as in Amalgam Comics (1996).

Purposes and criticism

Crossovers reward loyal audiences, consolidate fan bases, and generate marketing events, but are criticised for prioritising franchise management over storytelling and for raising barriers to new readers or viewers through accumulated continuity.
