The BMJ is one of the world's oldest and most influential general medical journals.

The BMJ is a weekly peer-reviewed medical journal published in the United Kingdom by BMJ, a wholly owned subsidiary of the British Medical Association (BMA). Founded in 1840, it is among the world's oldest general medical journals and, with The Lancet, The New England Journal of Medicine and JAMA, one of the most widely read and cited.

History

The journal began on 3 October 1840 as the Provincial Medical and Surgical Journal, the organ of the Provincial Medical and Surgical Association, forerunner of the BMA. It became the British Medical Journal in 1857 and was shortened to BMJ in 1988, adopting the title The BMJ in 2014. Milestones in its history include publishing early work on antisepsis and epidemiology, and its digital arm: bmj.com was among the first major journal websites (1995), and the journal has made all research articles open access since 2008, supported by article-processing charges, while print subscriptions continue for BMA members and institutions.

Content and editorial stance

The BMJ publishes original research, clinical reviews, editorials, news, analysis, and the popular "Minerva" picture column, as well as light-hearted but methodologically real studies in its famous Christmas issue. Under editors including Richard Smith and Fiona Godlee (editor-in-chief 2005–2021, succeeded by Kamran Abbasi), the journal has campaigned on issues such as medical error and patient safety, conflicts of interest in medicine, overdiagnosis, climate change and health, and open data in clinical trials, at times taking an outspoken position on politics and health policy. Its headline-making investigations and endorsements — from tobacco control to criticising research misconduct — have made it one of the most visible voices in global medicine. The journal's impact factor is typically in the range of 90–105, ranking among the top general medical titles.
