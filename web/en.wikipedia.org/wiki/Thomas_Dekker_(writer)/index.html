Thomas Dekker (writer)

Thomas Dekker (c. 1572 – 25 August 1632) was an English Elizabethan dramatist and pamphleteer, a versatile and prolific writer whose career spanned more than thirty years. He is best known for the city comedy The Shoemaker's Holiday (1599) and for prose pamphlets such as The Wonderful Year (1603), which vividly describes the London plague.

Life

Almost nothing is known of Dekker's early life. He first appears in Philip Henslowe's diary in 1598 as a dramatist writing for the Admiral's Men, and he contributed to dozens of plays, many now lost, frequently in collaboration with other writers including Thomas Middleton, John Webster, and Ben Jonson. His satirical feud with Jonson in the "War of the Theatres" produced Jonson's Poetaster and Dekker's reply Satiromastix (1601).

Works

Dekker's most enduring play, The Shoemaker's Holiday (1599), is a festive city comedy celebrating London artisans. Other plays include Old Fortunatus (1599), Patient Grissel (1603, with Henry Chettle and William Haughton), and The Honest Whore (1604–1605, with Middleton).

His pamphlets offer unmatched social detail of Jacobean London: The Wonderful Year (1603) on the plague, The Bellman of London (1608) on crime, and The Gull's Hornbook (1609), a satirical guide to fashionable behaviour. Dekker was imprisoned for debt in the King's Bench Prison from about 1612 to 1619, an experience reflected in later writing.

Legacy

Though his plays were long neglected, Dekker's warm, humane comedies and documentary prose have been widely praised since the nineteenth century for their picture of ordinary London life.
