Manzil

Manzil (Arabic: منزل, plural manāzil) is one of seven approximately equal parts into which the Quran is divided for the purpose of completing a full recitation (khatm) within one week. Each manzil is read on one day of the week.

Divisions

The seven manazil are: (1) Al-Fatiha (1) to An-Nisa (4); (2) Al-Ma'ida (5) to At-Tawba (9); (3) Yunus (10) to An-Nahl (16); (4) Al-Isra (17) to Al-Furqan (25); (5) Ash-Shu'ara (26) to Ya-Sin (36); (6) As-Saffat (37) to Al-Hujurat (49); (7) Qaf (50) to An-Nas (114). A mnemonic for the starting points is Fami Bishawq ("my mouth in longing").

Other divisions

The manzil is one of several traditional schemes for portioning the Quran, alongside the division into thirty juz' for monthly recitation, the hizb and ruku systems. The term manzil also carries the general Arabic meaning of a house, station, or stopping place, and in Sufi literature denotes a spiritual station on the path to God.
