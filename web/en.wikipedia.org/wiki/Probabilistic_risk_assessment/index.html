Probabilistic risk assessment

Probabilistic risk assessment (PRA), also called probabilistic safety assessment (PSA), is a systematic and comprehensive methodology to evaluate risks associated with a complex engineered technological entity such as a nuclear power plant or an aircraft. It answers three questions: what can go wrong, how likely is it, and what are the consequences?

Methods

PRA combines event trees, which model sequences following an initiating event, with fault trees, which decompose a system failure into combinations of component failures and human errors. Component reliability data and uncertainty distributions are propagated through the models, often by Monte Carlo simulation. Analyses are conventionally organised in levels: Level 1 estimates core damage frequency, Level 2 the frequency and magnitude of radioactive releases (source terms), and Level 3 the off-site consequences to the public and environment.

History

The landmark 1975 Reactor Safety Study (WASH-1400, the Rasmussen Report) introduced PRA to nuclear regulation. Modern regulators, including the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission, use risk-informed decision-making that combines PRA insights with deterministic defence-in-depth requirements. PRA is also applied in aerospace (NASA adopted it after the Challenger and Columbia accidents), chemical processing, dams, and space missions.
