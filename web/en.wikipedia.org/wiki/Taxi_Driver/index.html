Taxi Driver is a 1976 American psychological drama film directed by Martin Scorsese and written by Paul Schrader. Robert De Niro stars as Travis Bickle, a mentally unstable Vietnam War veteran who works as a night-shift taxi driver in New York City and descends into violence while fixating on rescuing a child prostitute, Iris, played by Jodie Foster.

Plot

Travis, an insomniac ex-Marine, drives fares through the city's sleaziest districts and grows disgusted by the crime and corruption around him. Rejected by Betsy, a campaign worker he briefly courts, he buys guns, plots to assassinate a presidential candidate, and finally turns his violence on the pimp Sport (Harvey Keitel) and his associates in a bloody shootout that frees Iris — after which newspapers hail him as a hero.

Reception and legacy

The film won the Palme d'Or at the 1976 Cannes Film Festival and was nominated for four Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Actor. Bernard Herrmann's jazz score, completed hours before his death, is widely acclaimed, and De Niro's improvised "You talkin' to me?" mirror scene became one of the most quoted moments in cinema. Taxi Driver is routinely ranked among the greatest films ever made and was selected for the US National Film Registry in 1994. It gained further notoriety when John Hinckley Jr., obsessed with Foster, cited it as inspiration for his 1981 attempt to assassinate President Ronald Reagan.
