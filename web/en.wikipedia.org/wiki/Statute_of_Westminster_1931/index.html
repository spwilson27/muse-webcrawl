The Statute of Westminster 1931 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that sets the basis for the relationship between the Dominions (now Commonwealth realms) and the Crown. It gave legislative equality and independence to the self-governing Dominions of the British Empire — Canada, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, the Irish Free State and Newfoundland — greatly increasing their sovereignty and marking a key step in their development into fully independent states.

Background

The Balfour Declaration of 1926, adopted at the Imperial Conference that year, declared the Dominions "autonomous communities within the British Empire, equal in status, in no way subordinate one to another in any aspect of their domestic or external affairs, though united by a common allegiance to the Crown". Imperial conferences in 1929 and 1930 agreed that this declaration should be given legal form, removing the power of the British Parliament to legislate for a Dominion except at its request and consent.

Provisions

  • The Colonial Laws Validity Act 1865 no longer applied to Dominion laws, so Dominion parliaments could enact legislation repugnant to British law (section 2).
  • No Act of the UK Parliament would extend to a Dominion unless the Dominion had expressly requested and consented to it (section 4).
  • Dominion parliaments gained full power to legislate extraterritorially.
  • Sections 7–9 preserved certain constitutional arrangements for Canada, Australia and New Zealand at their request — for Canada, because its federal and provincial governments could not agree on an amending formula.

Adoption and later history

The statute applied immediately to Canada, South Africa and the Irish Free State. Australia adopted it in 1942 (retroactive to 1939), New Zealand in 1947. Newfoundland never adopted it and reverted to direct rule in 1934 before joining Canada in 1949. Residual links were later severed by measures such as the Canada Act 1982, the Australia Act 1986 and New Zealand's Constitution Act 1986. The statute's preamble, concerning changes to the succession to the throne, remains part of the constitutional framework of the Commonwealth realms and was invoked in the Perth Agreement (2011) process that produced the Succession to the Crown Act 2013.
