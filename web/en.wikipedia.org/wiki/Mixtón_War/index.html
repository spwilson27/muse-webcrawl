Mixtón War

The Mixtón War (1540–1542) was a major indigenous rebellion against Spanish colonial rule in the region of Nueva Galicia in western New Spain (present-day Jalisco, Zacatecas, and Nayarit). It is named for the hill of Mixtón, a stronghold of the Caxcan people.

Background

The harsh campaigns of the conquistador Nuño de Guzmán in the 1530s, along with abuses under the encomienda system, provoked deep resentment among the Caxcan and other peoples of the region. In 1540, indigenous forces began attacking Spanish settlements and besieging Guadalajara.

Course of the war

The rebels, led by leaders including the Caxcan captain Francisco Tenamaztle, fortified hilltop positions (peñoles) and inflicted serious defeats on Spanish forces; the conquistador Pedro de Alvarado died in 1541 from injuries sustained in a retreat during the campaign. The Viceroy of New Spain, Antonio de Mendoza, took personal command, bringing large forces including many indigenous allies, and by 1542 the rebellion was suppressed.

Aftermath

The war prompted debate in Spain over the treatment of indigenous peoples; Tenamaztle himself later traveled to Spain to plead the Caxcan cause before the Council of the Indies, with support from Bartolomé de las Casas.
