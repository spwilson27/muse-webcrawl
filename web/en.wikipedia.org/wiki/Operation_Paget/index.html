Operation Paget was the British Metropolitan Police investigation into allegations that Diana, Princess of Wales, and Dodi Fayed were murdered in a conspiracy, rather than killed in a car crash in Paris on 31 August 1997. The inquiry was established in 2004 at the request of the Royal Coroner and was led by Sir John Stevens, former Commissioner of the Metropolitan Police.

Background

Following the deaths, the French investigation concluded that the crash in the Pont de l'Alma tunnel was caused principally by the driver's intoxication and excessive speed. Dodi Fayed's father, Mohamed Al-Fayed, persistently alleged that the couple had been murdered by British security services at the direction of the Royal Family, claims that gained wide publicity. Because rumours of conspiracy remained prevalent, the coroner asked the Metropolitan Police to investigate them ahead of the British inquests.

Findings

The Stevens Report, published on 14 December 2006 and running to over 800 pages, examined 175 conspiracy claims and rejected them. The inquiry concluded that Diana and Fayed died because their driver, Henri Paul, was drunk and driving too fast, that none of the occupants was wearing a seat belt, and that there was no evidence of a murder conspiracy, of an engagement, or of the involvement of the Duke of Edinburgh or the intelligence services. The 2007–08 inquest subsequently returned a verdict of unlawful killing through negligent driving by Paul and the pursuing paparazzi vehicles.
