Qieyun

The Qieyun (Chinese: 切韻, "Spelling Rhymes") is a Chinese rime dictionary published in 601 CE during the Sui dynasty, compiled by the scholar Lù Fǎyán (陸法言). It is the oldest surviving Chinese rime dictionary and the foundational text for the study of Middle Chinese phonology.

Contents

The dictionary organises 12,158 characters under 193 rhymes, grouped by the four tones of Middle Chinese (level, rising, departing and entering). Within each rhyme, characters sharing the same pronunciation are grouped into homophone sets, each given a fanqie spelling — two characters indicating the initial and final of the syllable — together with a brief gloss. Lu Fayan's preface recounts an evening discussion among scholars some twenty years earlier in which the correct standards of pronunciation were debated, and states that the work synthesised earlier rime books and distinguished refined southern and northern standards.

Influence

The Qieyun became the authoritative standard for literary pronunciation and was repeatedly expanded. Its Tang-dynasty revisions culminated in the Guangyun (1008) of the Song dynasty, which survives complete. The original Qieyun itself was long thought lost until fragments were recovered from the Dunhuang manuscripts in the twentieth century, along with manuscripts of its Tang recensions. Because it records a systematic diasystem of distinctions rather than any single dialect, the Qieyun system is the basis on which scholars reconstruct Middle Chinese.
