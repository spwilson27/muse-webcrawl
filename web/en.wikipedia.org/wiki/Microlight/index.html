Microlight

A flex-wing microlight (trike) in flight

A microlight, known as an ultralight in the United States and Canada, is a small, lightweight aircraft designed to carry one or two people, used mainly for recreation. Definitions vary by country but typically specify a maximum take-off weight — 450 kg (992 lb) for a two-seat landplane in much of Europe, with an allowance for safety equipment such as a ballistic parachute. Microlights evolved from powered hang gliders in the late 1970s.

Types

There are two main categories. Weight-shift control aircraft ("trikes") consist of a powered undercarriage unit suspended beneath a Rogallo-style flex wing, steered by shifting the pilot's weight via a control bar. Three-axis control microlights resemble small conventional aeroplanes, with ailerons, elevator and rudder; modern examples are built of composites, cruise at over 100 mph, and can have ranges of several hundred miles. Powered parachutes and gyroplanes are regulated separately in many jurisdictions but are often grouped with microlights in common usage.

Regulation

Microlighting is generally subject to lighter regulation than general aviation, which keeps operating costs low but imposes limits such as day-only visual flight and restrictions on flying over congested areas. In the United Kingdom the sport is overseen by the British Microlight Aircraft Association (BMAA) under Civil Aviation Authority rules; the UK subcategory was redefined in 2021 to allow aircraft up to 600 kg. Safety standards and pilot licensing (such as the UK NPPL with microlight rating) have steadily converged with those for light aircraft.
