Psychedelia

Psychedelia refers to the subculture, art and music associated with psychedelic drugs and the psychedelic experience, which reached its height in the mid-to-late 1960s counterculture. The word "psychedelic" was coined in 1956 by the psychiatrist Humphry Osmond in correspondence with Aldous Huxley, from Greek psyche (soul, mind) and delos (manifest) — "mind-manifesting".

Art and design

Psychedelic art is characterised by kaleidoscopic and fractal patterns, intensely saturated contrasting colours, morphing lettering and collage, drawing on Art Nouveau, surrealism and optical art. In San Francisco, poster artists such as Wes Wilson, Victor Moscoso and Rick Griffin created the swirling, barely legible posters for concerts at the Fillmore and Avalon Ballroom; in London, designers like Hapshash and the Coloured Coat produced silk-screened posters and album covers.

Music

Psychedelic rock — by the Byrds, Jefferson Airplane, Pink Floyd, the Grateful Dead and the Jimi Hendrix Experience, among many others — used feedback, distortion, electronic effects, Indian instruments and studio experimentation to evoke altered states. The movement crested in 1967 with the "Summer of Love" and albums such as the Beatles' Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band.

Legacy

Psychedelia influenced fashion, advertising, film and later electronic dance music and rave graphics. A scientific revival of psychedelic research in the 21st century, studying psilocybin and related substances for depression and other conditions, has renewed public interest in the era's culture.
