The César Award for Best Actor (French: César du meilleur acteur) is one of the César Awards, presented annually by the Académie des Arts et Techniques du Cinéma to honour the best performance by an actor in a leading role in a French film of the preceding year. The Césars, first held in 1976, are the national film awards of France and are often described as the French equivalent of the Academy Awards.

History

The first recipient was Philippe Noiret, honoured at the inaugural ceremony in 1976. Michel Serrault is the most decorated actor in the category, with three wins — for La Cage aux Folles (1978), Garde à vue (1981) and Nelly and Mr. Arnaud (1995). Gérard Depardieu, the most-nominated performer in the category's history, won twice, for The Last Metro (1980) and Cyrano de Bergerac (1990), the latter performance also earning him the Best Actor prize at Cannes and an Academy Award nomination. Other notable winners include Jean Gabin-era veteran performers, Daniel Auteuil, Jacques Dutronc, Mathieu Kassovitz, Vincent Lindon (for The Measure of a Man), Omar Sy (for The Intouchables, 2011) — the first Black actor to win the award — and Tahar Rahim (for A Prophet).

Selection

Nominees are chosen by the members of the Académie from eligible French-language productions, and the winner is determined by a second round of voting. The corresponding award for actresses is the César Award for Best Actress; supporting performances are honoured in separate categories, and since the 2020s the Academy has also awarded revelation (newcomer) prizes for the most promising actor and actress.
