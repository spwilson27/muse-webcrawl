A magnetometer is a device that measures a magnetic field or magnetic dipole moment. Different types of magnetometers measure the direction, strength, or relative change of a magnetic field at a particular location. The Earth's magnetic field varies from about 25 to 65 microteslas depending on location, and instruments range from simple compasses to superconducting quantum interference devices (SQUIDs) sensitive to femtotesla fields.

History

The first absolute magnetometer was built by Carl Friedrich Gauss in 1833, allowing the intensity of the Earth's field to be measured in mechanical units for the first time. Systematic magnetic observatories were established worldwide in the 19th century, and portable instruments for geological surveying followed in the 20th century.

Types

Scalar magnetometers measure total field magnitude; examples include proton precession and Overhauser instruments and optically pumped (caesium vapour) magnetometers. Vector magnetometers measure field components along one or more axes; the fluxgate magnetometer, widely used in spacecraft and compasses, is the classic example. Hall effect sensors serve in consumer electronics, while SQUIDs achieve the highest sensitivities for laboratory and medical use, such as magnetoencephalography. Modern smartphones contain miniature magnetoresistive magnetometers that function as digital compasses.

Uses

Magnetometers are used in geophysical surveys to locate ore bodies, oil-bearing structures, and buried archaeology; in military anti-submarine warfare (magnetic anomaly detectors); in space missions to map planetary fields; in medicine; and in everyday devices for navigation and orientation.
