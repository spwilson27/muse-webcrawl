Shibuya (渋谷) is a special ward (Shibuya City, Shibuya-ku) in Tokyo, Japan. As a major commercial and entertainment centre, it houses two of the busiest railway stations in the world, Shibuya Station and Shinjuku Station (the latter largely in neighbouring Shinjuku ward). The neighbourhood around Shibuya Station is famed for the Shibuya Crossing — often called the Shibuya Scramble — where as many as roughly 3,000 pedestrians cross at each green light, making it one of the most recognisable urban scenes on Earth and a global symbol of Tokyo.

Landmarks

  • Hachikō statue: a bronze statue outside Shibuya Station honouring the Akita dog Hachikō, who waited daily for his deceased owner for nearly a decade in the 1920s–30s; it is Tokyo's most famous meeting point.
  • Shibuya 109: a landmark department store and trendsetter for young women's fashion (gyaru culture).
  • Shibuya Sky: an open-air observation deck atop the Shibuya Scramble Square tower (opened 2019).
  • Center-gai, Spain-zaka and Dogenzaka: dense streets of shops, restaurants, clubs and love hotels.
  • Yoyogi Park and Meiji Shrine lie at the ward's northern edge, near Harajuku, the other great centre of Tokyo street fashion.
  • NHK Broadcasting Center and the Yoyogi National Gymnasium, built for the 1964 Olympics, are also in the ward.

History and redevelopment

Shibuya grew as a castle town and then as a railway suburb after the opening of Shibuya Station in 1885. It became a ward of Tokyo City in 1932 and a special ward in 1947. From the 1970s, department-store rivalry between the Tokyu and Seibu groups turned it into Tokyo's leading youth-fashion district. A vast station-area redevelopment from the 2010s added towers such as Shibuya Hikarie, Shibuya Stream and Shibuya Scramble Square, with further phases continuing into the late 2020s.

Culture

Shibuya's scramble crossing and neon canyons appear in countless films, advertisements and games — including Lost in Translation, The Fast and the Furious: Tokyo Drift, and the game The World Ends with You. Street Halloween celebrations at the crossing grew so large in the 2010s that the ward introduced restrictions and an alcohol ban in the late 2010s–2020s. The ward office has also been notable in Japan for issuing partnership certificates to same-sex couples, the first municipality to do so (2015).
