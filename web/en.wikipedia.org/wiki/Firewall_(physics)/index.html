Firewall (physics)

Firewall (physics) Proposed: 2012, by Ahmed Almheiri, Donald Marolf, Joseph Polchinski, and James Sully (AMPS)
Context: black hole information paradox, complementarity, AdS/CFT correspondence
Also known as: AMPS firewall paradox

In theoretical physics, a firewall is a hypothetical phenomenon where an observer falling into an old black hole encounters high-energy quanta at or near the event horizon. Proposed in 2012 by Ahmed Almheiri, Donald Marolf, Joseph Polchinski, and James Sully (collectively "AMPS"), the firewall argument sharpened the black hole information paradox by challenging the assumption that an infalling observer experiences nothing unusual at the horizon.

The argument

AMPS combined three widely accepted ideas: that black hole evaporation is unitary (information is preserved, as suggested by the AdS/CFT correspondence), that Hawking radiation is produced by quantum field theory in curved spacetime, and that the equivalence principle implies a smooth horizon for a freely falling observer. They argued that monogamy of entanglement makes all three untenable together: radiation that purifies the outgoing Hawking quanta cannot also be entangled with interior modes as a smooth horizon requires, so the horizon of an old black hole should instead be a violent "firewall" of high-energy particles.

Responses

Proposed resolutions include modifying black hole complementarity, ER=EPR (the conjectured equivalence of entanglement and wormholes), state-dependent interior operators, and computational-complexity arguments about decoding Hawking radiation. The firewall debate has strongly influenced research on quantum gravity, holography, and the nature of spacetime.
