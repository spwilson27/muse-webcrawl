Generation IV reactor

Concept designs of advanced reactor systems under development.

Generation IV reactors are a set of theoretical nuclear reactor designs currently being researched and developed under the Generation IV International Forum (GIF), an international consortium founded in 2001. They are intended as successors to the Generation II and III water-cooled reactors that make up nearly all of the world's operating fleet, with goals of improved sustainability, safety, reliability, economics, and resistance to nuclear proliferation.

Selected systems

From an initial evaluation of about 130 concepts, the GIF selected six reactor technologies for further development:

  • Gas-cooled fast reactor (GFR) – helium-cooled, fast-neutron spectrum.
  • Lead-cooled fast reactor (LFR) – liquid lead or lead-bismuth coolant.
  • Molten salt reactor (MSR) – fuel dissolved in circulating molten fluoride salts.
  • Sodium-cooled fast reactor (SFR) – liquid sodium coolant; the most mature of the six, with operating prototypes in Russia and past experience elsewhere.
  • Supercritical-water-cooled reactor (SCWR) – water above its thermodynamic critical point as coolant.
  • Very-high-temperature reactor (VHTR) – graphite-moderated, helium-cooled, capable of process-heat and hydrogen production; China's HTR-PM is a related demonstration.

Design goals

Most Generation IV systems operate at higher temperatures than current plants, enabling higher thermal efficiency and industrial heat applications. Fast-spectrum designs can breed fuel and burn long-lived actinides, closing the fuel cycle and reducing the longevity of radioactive waste. Safety designs emphasise passive and inherent features that remove decay heat without operator action or external power.

Status

Deployment is expected mostly from the 2030s onward. Russia's BN-800 sodium fast reactor and China's HTR-PM pebble-bed reactor are operating demonstrations closely related to Generation IV concepts, while demonstration molten-salt, lead-cooled, and small modular variants are under construction or licensing in several countries.
