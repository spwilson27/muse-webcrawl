Muslin

Muslin is a cotton fabric of plain weave, ranging from delicate, sheer gauzes to coarse sheeting. The word is commonly believed to derive from Mosul, the city in Iraq through which the cloth was traded to Europe, though the finest muslins originated in Bengal, particularly around Dhaka, in present-day Bangladesh.

Fine muslin cloth, prized for its lightness and softness.

History

Cotton muslin was woven in Bengal for centuries; Dhaka's hand-spun muslins, woven from the fine phuti karpas cotton, were legendary for their fineness — cloth so sheer it was poetically described as "woven air" (baft hawa). Muslins were exported to the Roman world, the Middle East, and, from the 17th century, to Europe by the East India Companies, where they fetched high prices for dresses, turbans, and furnishings.

During the Mughal era, imperial workshops sustained the craft; under British colonial rule, high tariffs and competition from machine-made Lancashire cloth devastated the Dhaka industry, and by the mid-19th century the finest varieties had nearly vanished, along with the specialised spinning knowledge behind them. Recent revival efforts in Bangladesh have worked to restore the traditional fibre and techniques, and Dhaka muslin's weaving tradition was inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2020 as part of the traditional art of Jamdani weaving.

Uses

Muslin is used for summer clothing, dresses (notably the high-waisted white gowns of the late 18th and early 19th centuries), curtains, baby blankets and swaddles, theatre backdrops, and dressmaking toiles (test garments). In cooking, muslin cloth is used for straining and wrapping — for example, in making cheese (cheesecloth) and bouquet garni. Coarser grades serve in bookbinding, upholstery, and as a base for embroidery and quilting.

Types

Grades range from gauze and voile-like sheer muslin, through book muslin (stiffened, for interlinings), to heavy sheeting muslin. Jamdani is a figured muslin in which pattern is woven in by hand with discontinuous weft threads.
