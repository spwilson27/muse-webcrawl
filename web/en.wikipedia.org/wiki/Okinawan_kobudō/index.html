Okinawan kobudō

Okinawan kobudō (沖縄古武道) is the traditional weapon systems of the Ryukyu Islands of Japan, developed alongside karate. The term kobudō means "old martial way" and refers to the practice of weapons such as the bō, sai, tonfa, nunchaku, and kama.

History

The weapons arts of Okinawa developed over centuries, influenced by trade and cultural exchange with China and Southeast Asia. Weapons restrictions imposed under the Ryukyu Kingdom and later under the Satsuma Domain are often credited with encouraging the adaptation of farming and fishing implements — the tonfa (millstone handle), kama (sickle), and nunchaku (horse bridle or flail) — as self-defence tools.

Weapons

The principal weapons of Okinawan kobudō include:

  • Bō — a six-foot staff
  • Sai — a short metal truncheon with two prongs
  • Tonfa — a handled baton held along the forearm
  • Nunchaku — two short sticks joined by cord or chain
  • Kama — a farming sickle, often used in pairs
  • Tekkō, tinbe-rochin (shield and spear), and eku (oar)

Training centres on kata (forms), basic techniques, and partner drills. Notable 20th-century masters include Taira Shinken, who catalogued and preserved many kobudō kata.
