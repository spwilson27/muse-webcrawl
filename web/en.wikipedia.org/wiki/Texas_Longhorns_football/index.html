Texas Longhorns football

The Texas Longhorns football program represents the University of Texas at Austin in American college football. Founded in 1893, the Longhorns compete in NCAA Division I FBS and joined the Southeastern Conference (SEC) in 2024 after a long tenure in the Big 12 Conference.

History

Texas rose to national prominence under coach Darrell Royal (1957–1976), whose teams won three national championships and popularised the wishbone offense from 1968. The 1969 team beat Arkansas in the "Game of the Century" and then Notre Dame in the Cotton Bowl. Under Mack Brown, Texas won the 2005 national championship by defeating USC 41–38 in the 2006 Rose Bowl, led by quarterback Vince Young — widely regarded as one of the greatest college games ever played.

Rivalries

Principal rivalries include the Red River Rivalry with Oklahoma, played annually at the Cotton Bowl in Dallas during the State Fair of Texas, and contests with Texas A&M, renewed when Texas joined the SEC.

Traditions

The team's live mascot is Bevo, a longhorn steer; its hand sign is "Hook 'em Horns"; the school song is "The Eyes of Texas".
