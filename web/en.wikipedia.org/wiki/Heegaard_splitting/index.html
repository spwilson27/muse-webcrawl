Heegaard splitting

In the mathematical field of geometric topology, a Heegaard splitting is a decomposition of a compact oriented 3-manifold that results from dividing it into two handlebodies.

More precisely, a Heegaard splitting of genus g of a closed oriented 3-manifold M is a decomposition of M into two genus-g handlebodies that share a common boundary surface, called the Heegaard surface. Every closed orientable 3-manifold admits a Heegaard splitting, a result that goes back to work of Poul Heegaard in 1898.

History

Heegaard splittings were introduced by Poul Heegaard in his 1898 dissertation. Wolfgang Haken later developed the theory, including the notion of Haken manifolds. The concept has been central to 3-manifold topology, providing a bridge between combinatorial descriptions of manifolds and their geometric properties.

Properties

The minimal genus over all Heegaard splittings of a manifold is called its Heegaard genus. Two splittings of the same manifold are related by stabilisation, the operation of adding a trivial handle; the Reidemeister–Singer theorem states that any two Heegaard splittings of a given manifold have a common stabilisation. Splittings may be reducible, weakly reducible or strongly irreducible, distinctions introduced by Andrew Casson and Cameron Gordon which have been important in the study of manifolds containing incompressible surfaces.

Applications

Heegaard diagrams, which record the attaching curves of a splitting on the Heegaard surface, give a finite presentation of the fundamental group of the manifold. Heegaard splittings also underlie Heegaard Floer homology, developed by Peter Ozsváth and Zoltán Szabó, and provide a setting for the theory of tunnel number and for algorithms that recognise the 3-sphere and the unknot.
