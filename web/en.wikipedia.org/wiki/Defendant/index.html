Defendant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A defendant is a person or entity accused of a crime in criminal prosecution, or against whom relief or recovery is sought in a civil lawsuit (where in equity and some jurisdictions the term "respondent" is used). The opposing party is the prosecutor (criminal) or the plaintiff/claimant (civil).

Rights

Criminal defendants hold the system's core procedural protections: the presumption of innocence, the right to be informed of charges, to counsel (provided if indigent in serious cases), to a speedy and public trial by an impartial tribunal, to confront and cross-examine witnesses, to compel favourable witnesses, to remain silent without adverse inference in most systems, and not to be tried twice for the same offence (double jeopardy). Civil defendants are entitled to notice, an opportunity to be heard, and proportionate process, with judgment enforced only after due adjudication or default. Corporate defendants complicate doctrine — criminal liability of companies, attribution of officers' acts, and sanctions calibrated to organisations are active fields — as are juvenile defendants, whose treatment international law requires to be distinct.
