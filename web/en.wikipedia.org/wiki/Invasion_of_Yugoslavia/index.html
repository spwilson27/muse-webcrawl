Invasion of Yugoslavia


The Axis invasion routes into Yugoslavia, April 1941.

The invasion of Yugoslavia, also known as the April War or Operation 25, was a German-led attack on the Kingdom of Yugoslavia by the Axis powers beginning on 6 April 1941 during World War II. The invasion ended with the unconditional surrender of the Royal Yugoslav Army on 17 April 1941, after a campaign of only eleven days, and was followed by the occupation and partition of Yugoslavia.

Background

Under pressure, Yugoslavia signed the Tripartite Pact with the Axis on 25 March 1941. Two days later a British-supported coup d'état led by air force general Dušan Simović deposed the regent, Prince Paul, and repudiated the pact in practice. Infuriated, Adolf Hitler ordered the destruction of Yugoslavia as a state, postponing the invasion of the Soviet Union to coordinate the Balkan campaign with the simultaneous invasion of Greece.

The campaign

The Luftwaffe opened the attack with a devastating bombing of Belgrade, codenamed Operation Retribution, which killed thousands of civilians. German armoured columns advanced from Bulgaria, Romania, Hungary and Austria, while Italian and Hungarian forces joined the assault. The Yugoslav army, mobilised late, hampered by ethnic divisions and obsolete equipment, collapsed rapidly; King Peter II and the government fled abroad.

Aftermath

Yugoslavia was dismembered: Germany, Italy, Hungary and Bulgaria annexed territory, the puppet Independent State of Croatia was created under the Ustaše, and Serbia was placed under German military administration. Occupation provoked a major resistance struggle by Partisans and Chetniks that tied down Axis forces for the rest of the war.
