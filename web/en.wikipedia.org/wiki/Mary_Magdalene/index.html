Mary Magdalene

Mary Magdalene (sometimes called Mary of Magdala) was a woman who, according to the four canonical gospels, travelled with Jesus as one of his followers and was a witness to his crucifixion and resurrection. She is mentioned by name more often than most of the apostles and is, in the Gospel of John, the first person to see the resurrected Christ.

In the Gospels

Luke 8 introduces Mary, "called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out", among the women who supported Jesus's ministry from their own means. Her epithet likely denotes her hometown, Magdala, a town on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee. All four gospels place her at the crucifixion; the synoptics make her a witness of the burial, and Matthew, Mark, and John present her at the empty tomb. In John 20, Jesus commissions her to announce the resurrection to the disciples, earning her the later title "Apostle to the Apostles".

Later traditions and misconceptions

In the Latin West, Pope Gregory the Great's homily of 591 conflated Mary Magdalene with Mary of Bethany and the unnamed "sinful woman" of Luke 7, fostering the enduring image of her as a repentant prostitute — an identification Eastern Christianity never adopted and the Roman Catholic Church quietly abandoned in its 1969 calendar revision. Medieval legend took her to Provence, where the basilica at Saint-Maximin-la-Sainte-Baume claims her relics. Gnostic texts such as the Gospel of Mary depict her as a visionary leader.

Veneration

Mary Magdalene is a saint in the Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, and Lutheran traditions; her feast day is 22 July. In 2016 Pope Francis raised her memorial to the rank of a feast, underscoring her title Apostolorum Apostola.
