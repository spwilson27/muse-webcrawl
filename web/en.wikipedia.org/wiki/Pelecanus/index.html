Pelecanus


Kingdom: Animalia
Order: Pelecaniformes
Family: Pelecanidae
Genus: Pelecanus Linnaeus, 1758
Type species: Pelecanus onocrotalus (great white pelican)

Pelecanus is a genus of large water birds comprising the pelicans, the only genus in the family Pelecanidae. Pelicans are characterised by a long beak and a large throat pouch used to catch prey and drain water before swallowing. There are eight living species, distributed nearly worldwide in warm waters, absent only from the interior of South America, polar regions, and the open ocean.

Description

Pelicans are among the largest flying birds: the Dalmatian pelican can reach a wingspan over 3 metres and weigh up to about 15 kg. Plumage is predominantly white in several species, while others, such as the brown pelican, are darker. All have webbing between all four toes (totipalmate feet) and breed colonially on coasts and inland waters.

Behaviour

Most species feed mainly on fish, often cooperatively, herding shoals into shallow water. The brown and Peruvian pelicans are plunge-divers, while others scoop fish at the surface. Young are fed regurgitated food. Pelicans feature in the mythology and heraldry of many cultures; the "pelican in her piety" — wounding her breast to feed her young — became a Christian symbol of self-sacrifice.

Species

  • Great white pelican (P. onocrotalus)
  • Pink-backed pelican (P. rufescens)
  • Dalmatian pelican (P. crispus)
  • Spot-billed pelican (P. philippensis)
  • Australian pelican (P. conspicillatus)
  • American white pelican (P. erythrorhynchos)
  • Brown pelican (P. occidentalis)
  • Peruvian pelican (P. thagus)

Conservation

The Dalmatian and spot-billed pelicans have been of conservation concern because of wetland loss, disturbance of colonies, and persecution; the brown pelican recovered strongly after pesticide (DDT) bans in North America and was removed from the U.S. endangered species list in 2009.
