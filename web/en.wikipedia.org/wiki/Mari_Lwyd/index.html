Mari Lwyd

A Mari Lwyd at a Welsh festival

The Mari Lwyd (pronounced [ˈmaːri ˈlʊi̯d]; "Grey Mare" in Welsh) is a wassailing folk custom found in South Wales. The tradition entails the use of a hobby horse made from a horse's skull mounted on a pole and carried by an individual hidden under a white sheet, accompanied by a party who go from house to house at Christmas and New Year, engaging the householders in a sung contest of rhymes called pwnco.

The custom

The Mari's party arrives at a house and sings verses asking for entry; those inside reply in verse, inventing excuses to refuse. The exchange continues until one side runs out of rhymes. If the Mari party wins, they are admitted, and the Mari — its jaw snapped by the carrier — chases the household amid general merriment, while the party is given food and drink. The skull is often decorated with ribbons, glass eyes and a bridle, and the carrier is joined by stock characters in some variants.

History and revival

The custom is first reliably recorded around 1800 in Glamorgan and spread across South Wales in the 19th century, though it is often claimed to be much older, with suggested links to pre-Christian horse veneration and other British hobby-horse traditions such as the Padstow 'Obby 'Oss. Disapproved of by chapels for its rowdiness and drinking, it declined sharply in the early 20th century. Since the later 20th century it has been revived as a celebrated emblem of Welsh folk culture, with notable celebrations at Llangynwyd and Chepstow, and Mari Lwyd parties now appear at festivals across Wales.
