Value chain

A value chain is the set of activities that a firm performs in order to deliver a valuable product or service to the market. The concept was introduced by Michael E. Porter in his 1985 book Competitive Advantage: Creating and Sustaining Superior Performance. By analysing each activity, a firm can identify sources of competitive advantage — either lower cost or differentiation — and the linkages between activities that affect performance.

Porter's generic value chain, showing primary and support activities.

Primary activities

  • Inbound logistics — receiving, storing and distributing inputs.
  • Operations — transforming inputs into the final product or service.
  • Outbound logistics — collecting, storing and distributing the product to buyers.
  • Marketing and sales — inducing and enabling buyers to purchase.
  • Service — enhancing or maintaining the value of the product after sale.

Support activities

  • Procurement — purchasing inputs used in the firm's value chain.
  • Technology development — research, process development and know-how.
  • Human resource management — recruiting, hiring, training and compensation.
  • Firm infrastructure — management, planning, finance, accounting and legal functions.

The difference between the total value created and the cost of performing the activities is the margin. Later extensions of the concept include global value chains in development economics and value chain analysis for industries and sustainability assessment.
