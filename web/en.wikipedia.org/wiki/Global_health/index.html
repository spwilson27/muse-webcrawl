Global health

Global health is the health of the populations in the worldwide context; it has been defined as "the area of study, research, and practice that places a priority on improving health and achieving equity in health for all people worldwide". Problems that transcend national borders or have a global political and economic impact are often emphasized. Thus, global health is about improving health and reducing disparities worldwide, while emphasizing transnational health issues, determinants, and solutions.

Global health can be measured through various indicators, such as life expectancy, infant and child mortality, the prevalence of diseases, and access to clean water, sanitation, and medical care. The discipline combines public health with international health, drawing on epidemiology, medicine, economics, and the social sciences.

History

International health cooperation dates to the nineteenth-century International Sanitary Conferences and the founding of the World Health Organization in 1948. The term "global health" gained prominence from the 1990s, in part reflecting the health effects of globalization, the HIV/AIDS pandemic, and the adoption of the Millennium Development Goals in 2000 and the Sustainable Development Goals in 2015, the latter including a dedicated goal for good health and well-being.

Major issues

Key global health concerns include communicable diseases such as HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria, and COVID-19; the growing burden of non-communicable diseases; maternal and child health; antimicrobial resistance; and health emergencies and pandemic preparedness. Major actors include the WHO, national development agencies, and philanthropic organizations such as the Gates Foundation and partnerships like Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance.
