John Chrysostom

John Chrysostom (Greek: Ἰωάννης ὁ Χρυσόστομος; c. 347 – 14 September 407) was an important Early Church Father who served as Archbishop of Constantinople. He is known for his preaching and public speaking, his denunciation of the abuse of authority by ecclesiastical and political leaders, his Divine Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom, and his ascetic sensibilities. The epithet Chrysostom means "golden-mouthed" in Greek and denotes his celebrated eloquence.

He is honoured as a saint in the Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Catholic, Anglican and Lutheran churches, as well as in some others, and is counted among the Three Holy Hierarchs in Eastern Christianity.

Early life and Antioch

Born in Antioch to a military father and a Christian mother, Anthusa, John was educated by the famous rhetorician Libanius. After a period of extreme asceticism in the Syrian mountains that damaged his health, he returned to Antioch, was ordained, and from 386 preached a celebrated series of sermons, including the homilies On the Statues delivered during the Riot of the Statues in 387.

Archbishop of Constantinople

In 398 John was made Archbishop of Constantinople against his will. He reformed clerical discipline, preached against luxury and corruption, and championed the poor, founding hospitals. His bluntness alienated the empress Eudoxia and rival bishops, above all Theophilus of Alexandria. At the Synod of the Oak (403) he was deposed; popular riots and an earthquake prompted his recall, but further confrontation led to his exile in 404 to Cucusus in Armenia and then towards Pityus on the Black Sea. He died at Comana in Pontus in 407, reportedly with the words "Glory to God for all things."

Works and legacy

Chrysostom's surviving corpus — hundreds of homilies on Genesis, the Psalms, Matthew, John, Acts and the Pauline epistles, plus treatises such as On the Priesthood — is the largest of any Greek Father. His eucharistic liturgy remains the normal Sunday liturgy of the Byzantine Churches. His homilies against Judaizers have been criticised in modern times for their hostile rhetoric toward Jews. His relics were returned to Constantinople in 438 and now rest in the Church of St. George in Istanbul.
