Herald Square is a major commercial intersection and neighbourhood in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, formed by the crossing of Broadway, Sixth Avenue (Avenue of the Americas), and 34th Street. It is named after the New York Herald, whose headquarters building stood there from 1893, and is famous as the home of Macy's flagship store.

Description

Strictly, Herald Square is the northern of two plazas created by the diagonal crossing; the southern section is Greeley Square, named for the newspaper editor Horace Greeley, whose statue stands there. In Herald Square itself is the James Gordon Bennett Memorial, a clock and sculptural group honouring the Herald's founder, featuring the goddess Minerva and two bell-ringers nicknamed Stuff and Guff.

History and retail

The area was farmland crossed by Bloomingdale Road until development followed the theatres and shops of the "Great White Way" northward in the late 19th century. Macy's opened its enormous flagship — for decades billed as the world's largest store — on the square in 1902, and rival Gimbels stood nearby until 1986. The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, founded in 1924, traditionally ends at the store on Herald Square. The square is served by the 34th Street–Herald Square subway station, one of the busiest in the New York system, and by PATH trains to New Jersey. The song "Give My Regards to Broadway" (1904) immortalised the spot: "Remember me to Herald Square."
