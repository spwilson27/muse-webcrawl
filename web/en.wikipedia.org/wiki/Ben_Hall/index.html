Ben Hall

Ben Hall (9 May 1837 – 5 May 1865) was an Australian bushranger active in New South Wales during the 1860s. One of the best-known outlaws of the colonial period, Hall led a gang responsible for hundreds of robberies, yet he cultivated a reputation for avoiding bloodshed: remarkably, no killing was ever attributed to him personally.

Early life

Hall was born at Maitland to ex-convict parents and worked as a stockman and small landholder near Forbes. After his marriage broke down and following arrests he blamed on police harassment — including charges of which he was acquitted — he turned to bushranging, joining briefly with Frank Gardiner's gang before forming his own.

Bushranging career

Between 1863 and 1865 Hall's gang — including John Gilbert and John Dunn — robbed mail coaches, homesteads, hotels and entire townships such as Canowindra, which they occupied for three days, entertaining the residents while the police were locked up. The colonial government passed the Felons Apprehension Act, effectively outlawing Hall and his companions.

Death and legacy

Betrayed by an informer, Hall was ambushed at dawn on 5 May 1865 at Billabong Creek near Forbes and shot dead by police. He remains a folk-hero figure in Australian culture, celebrated in ballads, books and films for his audacity and his gang's flamboyant, relatively non-violent style.
