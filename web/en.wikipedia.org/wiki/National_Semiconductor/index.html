National Semiconductor

National Semiconductor was an American semiconductor manufacturer specializing in analog devices and subsystems, based in Santa Clara, California. Founded in 1959 in Danbury, Connecticut, by engineers from Sperry Rand, the company moved to Silicon Valley in 1967 and became a major producer of analog integrated circuits, operational amplifiers, voltage regulators, and power management products.

History

Under chief executive Charles Sporck, who joined in 1967, National grew rapidly by emphasizing low-cost manufacturing and broad product catalogues of commodity chips. It produced digital logic families, memory devices, and the NS32000 series of microprocessors, and later acquired Fairchild Semiconductor (in 1987, subsequently spun out again in 1997) and, through acquisition, the Cyrix line of x86 processors, which it later sold. In the 2000s the company refocused on high-performance analog products under CEO Brian Halla.

Acquisition

On 23 September 2011, Texas Instruments acquired National Semiconductor for approximately $6.5 billion, and National's products were folded into TI's analog business, greatly expanding TI's position as the world's largest analog semiconductor maker. Well-known National product lines included the LM series of regulators and amplifiers, such as the LM741 operational amplifier and LM7805 voltage regulator, which remain industry staples.
