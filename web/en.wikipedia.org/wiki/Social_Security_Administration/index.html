Social Security Administration

The United States Social Security Administration (SSA) is an independent agency of the U.S. federal government that administers Social Security, a social insurance program consisting of retirement, disability and survivors benefits.

History

Created by the Social Security Act of 1935, signed by President Franklin D. Roosevelt, the agency began as the Social Security Board, later part of the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare and then HHS, before becoming independent again in 1995. It issues Social Security numbers, maintains earnings records for over 180 million workers, and pays monthly benefits to tens of millions of retirees, disabled workers and survivors.

Operations

SSA runs a network of field offices, teleservice centres, hearing offices for disability appeals, and the ssa.gov website. Disability determinations are made largely by state agencies under federal standards. SSI, a needs-based program funded from general revenues, has been administered by SSA since 1974.
