Duke of Argyll

Duke of Argyll is a title created in the peerage of Scotland in 1701, and in the peerage of the United Kingdom in 1892. It is held by the Campbell family, whose head is chief of Clan Campbell. The family seat is Inveraray Castle beside Loch Fyne in Argyll.

History

Archibald Campbell, 9th Earl of Argyll's son Archibald was restored to his father's honours and created the first Duke of Argyll in 1701 for his support of William III. The dukedom carried subsidiary titles including Marquess of Kintyre and Lorne and Earl of Campbell and Cowall. The 8th Duke was created Duke of Argyll in the Peerage of the United Kingdom in 1892, giving the holder an automatic seat in the House of Lords.

Notable holders

John Campbell, 2nd Duke of Argyll (1678–1743), was a leading soldier of the War of the Spanish Succession and an architect of the Union of 1707. Archibald Campbell, 3rd Duke, effectively controlled Scottish patronage for decades. George Campbell, 8th Duke, was a Liberal cabinet minister and father-in-law of Princess Louise, daughter of Queen Victoria.
