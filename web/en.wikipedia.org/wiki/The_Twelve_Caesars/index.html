The Twelve Caesars

AuthorSuetonius
Publishedc. AD 121
Original titleDe vita Caesarum
SubjectBiographies of Julius Caesar and the first eleven emperors

The Twelve Caesars (Latin: De vita Caesarum, "On the Life of the Caesars") is a set of twelve biographies by the Roman historian Suetonius, published around AD 121 while he served as secretary to the emperor Hadrian. It covers Julius Caesar and the emperors from Augustus to Domitian.

Method and content

Rather than narrating each reign chronologically, Suetonius organises his material by category — ancestry, public deeds, appearance, habits, scandals, omens and death — a scheme that makes the work a treasure-house of anecdote, gossip and documentary quotation, including the personal letters of Augustus. Some of its most famous material is disreputable: Tiberius's retirement on Capri, Caligula's alleged plan to make his horse a consul, Nero's performances and deathbed cry qualis artifex pereo ("what an artist dies in me").

Influence

The work set the pattern for imperial biography — Einhard modelled his Life of Charlemagne on it — and, through Robert Graves's 1957 translation and its television adaptation in I, Claudius, shaped the popular image of the early emperors. Its accuracy varies: Suetonius had archival access for the early lives but relied on hostile senatorial tradition and rumour for much else. (Not to be confused with Alexander Blok's poem The Twelve.)
