The Israel Land Administration (ILA; Hebrew: מינהל מקרקעי ישראל, Minhal Mekarke'i Yisra'el) was the Israeli government authority responsible for managing land in Israel that was in state ownership. Established in 1960 under the Israel Lands Law, it administered about 93% of the country's land area on behalf of the state, the Development Authority and the Jewish National Fund (JNF/KKL).

Functions

The ILA allocated state land for housing, agriculture, industry and nature reserves, generally through long-term leases rather than sale of freehold, in line with the principle that "Israel lands" — state, Development Authority and JNF land — should not be sold. Its policy was set by the Israel Land Council, a body including government ministers and JNF representatives.

Reorganisation

A 2009 reform of Israeli land law began a programme of privatising some state land and, in 2013, the Administration was reorganised as the Israel Land Authority (Reshut Mekarke'i Yisra'el), which continues its functions.

Criticism

The ILA and the land regime it administered were criticised by Israeli Arab citizens' organisations and human-rights groups over the role of the JNF in land allocation; the issue reached the Supreme Court in the Kaadan case (2000), which ruled that the state could not discriminate in allocating state land.
