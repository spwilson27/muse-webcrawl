Caucasian War

The Caucasian War (1817–1864) was the Russian Empire's invasion and annexation of the North Caucasus, fought against the indigenous peoples of Chechnya, Dagestan, Circassia, and other regions. It was one of the longest wars of the 19th century.

Course

General Aleksey Yermolov began systematic conquest in 1817, building fort lines such as Grozny. In the northeast, the Caucasian Imamate under Imam Shamil resisted for 25 years using guerrilla warfare in the mountains and forests. In the northwest, Circassian tribes resisted until 1864, after which hundreds of thousands of Circassians were expelled or fled to the Ottoman Empire — remembered as the Circassian genocide.

Legacy

The war populated Russian literature through Pushkin, Lermontov, and Tolstoy, who served in the Caucasus. It completed Russia's hold on the mountain barrier between the Black and Caspian seas.
