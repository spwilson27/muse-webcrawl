Ben Lomond (Scottish Gaelic: Beinn Laomainn, 'Beacon Mountain') is a 974-metre (3,196 ft) mountain in the Scottish Highlands. Situated on the eastern shore of Loch Lomond, it is the most southerly of the Munros.

Geography

Ben Lomond lies within Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park and is owned by the National Trust for Scotland. Its distinctive conical summit is visible from much of the central lowlands of Scotland and gives the loch its famous backdrop, celebrated in the song "The Bonnie Banks o' Loch Lomond".

Ascent

The most popular route, the Tourist Path, begins at Rowardennan and climbs in a long ridge to the summit; a rougher alternative follows the Ptarmigan ridge. The mountain is one of Scotland's most climbed hills, with tens of thousands of ascents each year.

Memorial Park

The area around Ben Lomond forms part of Scotland's first national memorial park, dedicated to those who died in the world wars.
