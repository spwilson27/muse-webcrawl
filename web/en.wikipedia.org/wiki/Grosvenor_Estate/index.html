Grosvenor Estate

The Grosvenor Estate is the portfolio of property and landholdings owned by Grosvenor, the international property group controlled by the family of the Duke of Westminster. The estate is most famous for its central London holdings, which cover much of Mayfair and Belgravia, two of the most valuable residential districts in the world.

History

The London estate originated in the marriage in 1677 of Sir Thomas Grosvenor, 3rd Baronet, to Mary Davies, heiress to the manor of Ebury west of the City of London. The land, much of it then marshy ground along the Thames, began to be developed in the early eighteenth century, when Grosvenor Square was laid out in Mayfair. A second great phase of development in the nineteenth century created Belgravia, centred on Belgrave Square and Eaton Square, largely under the direction of master builder Thomas Cubitt.

Holdings

The estate manages hundreds of acres in Mayfair and Belgravia, comprising residential, office and retail property. Through Grosvenor's wider business, the family also owns property across North America, Asia and continental Europe, as well as the rural estates centred on Eaton Hall in Cheshire and Abbeystead in Lancashire.

Management

The estate is held in a series of trusts for the benefit of the Grosvenor family. Since 2016 the head of the family has been Hugh Grosvenor, 7th Duke of Westminster. The group has adopted long-term stewardship principles, including commitments to environmental sustainability across its portfolio.
