Hypochlorous acid

Hypochlorous acid
Formula: HOCl
Molar mass: 52.46 g/mol
Appearance: Colorless aqueous solution
pKa: 7.53
Related compounds: Hypochlorite, chlorine, chlorous acid

Hypochlorous acid (chemical formula HOCl, also written HClO) is a weak acid that forms when chlorine dissolves in water, partially dissociating into the hypochlorite ion (OCl−). It is a powerful oxidizer and the principal active disinfecting species in chlorine-based sanitizers such as bleach solutions. Salts and esters of hypochlorous acid are called hypochlorites.

Chemistry

Chlorine gas hydrolyzes in water: Cl2 + H2O ⇌ HOCl + HCl. The equilibrium between HOCl and OCl− is pH-dependent; at physiological pH (~7.4) roughly half is present as the neutral acid, which crosses microbial cell membranes far more readily than the charged hypochlorite ion, making HOCl a much faster-acting disinfectant. Hypochlorous acid cannot be isolated in pure form because it decomposes and disproportionates, and is handled only in aqueous solution.

Biological role

HOCl is produced naturally by the human immune system. Activated neutrophils generate it in their phagosomes via the enzyme myeloperoxidase, which catalyzes the reaction of hydrogen peroxide with chloride ions. This microbicidal system helps kill engulfed bacteria and fungi, though excess production contributes to tissue damage in inflammatory diseases.

Uses

Hypochlorous acid is used for water treatment, surface disinfection in food processing and healthcare, wound care formulations, and sanitizing produce. Electrolyzed water systems generate dilute HOCl on site by passing current through salt water. Dakin's solution, a dilute sodium hypochlorite preparation, has been used in wound irrigation since the First World War.
