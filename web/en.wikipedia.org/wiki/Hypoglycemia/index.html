Hypoglycemia

Hypoglycemia, also spelled hypoglycaemia or hypoglycæmia, sometimes called low blood sugar, is a fall in blood sugar to levels below normal, typically below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L). Whipple's triad is used to properly identify hypoglycemic episodes: symptoms consistent with hypoglycemia, a documented low plasma glucose concentration, and relief of symptoms after raising the glucose level.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms may include clumsiness, trouble talking, confusion, loss of consciousness, seizures, and death. A feeling of hunger, sweating, shakiness, and weakness may also be present. Symptoms typically come on quickly.

Causes and treatment

The most common cause of hypoglycemia is medications used to treat diabetes such as insulin, sulfonylureas, and biguanides. Treatment is generally with a source of glucose, such as glucose tablets or foods containing sugar; severe cases may require intravenous glucose or glucagon.
