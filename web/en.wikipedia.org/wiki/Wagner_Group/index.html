The Wagner Group (Russian: Группа Вагнера), officially PMC Wagner, is a Russian state-funded private military company founded around 2014 by Yevgeny Prigozhin and led militarily in its early years by Dmitry Utkin, whose call sign "Wagner" gave the group its name. Long operating with plausible deniability for the Russian state, Wagner became a major combat force in Russia's war against Ukraine and the principal instrument of Russian military influence in parts of Africa and the Middle East.

History

Wagner first appeared during the 2014 annexation of Crimea and the war in Donbas, where its contractors supported pro-Russian separatists. It later fought in Syria in support of the Assad government — including a February 2018 clash with US forces at Khasham — and deployed to Libya, Mozambique, Venezuela, the Central African Republic, and Mali, frequently in exchange for concessions in gold, diamonds, oil, and other resources.

Ukraine and the 2023 mutiny

In the full-scale invasion of Ukraine launched in 2022, Wagner recruited tens of thousands of convicts from Russian prisons and led the assault on Bakhmut, captured in May 2023 after months of attritional fighting at very heavy cost. Prigozhin's public feud with the Russian Ministry of Defence culminated in a short-lived mutiny on 23–24 June 2023, when Wagner forces seized Rostov-on-Don and marched toward Moscow before standing down under a deal brokered by Alexander Lukashenko. On 23 August 2023 Prigozhin and Utkin died in a plane crash north of Moscow.

Aftermath

After the mutiny, Wagner's Ukraine operations were wound down and its fighters offered contracts with the regular military. Its African operations were progressively brought under direct Ministry of Defence control through the "Africa Corps", while Wagner's remnants continued operating in Belarus and some African states. Wagner personnel have been accused by the UN, journalists, and Western governments of war crimes and massacres in Ukraine, Syria, Mali, and the Central African Republic; the United States designated Wagner a transnational criminal organization, and the United Kingdom proscribed it as a terrorist organisation in 2023.
