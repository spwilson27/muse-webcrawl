Gangsta rap


Gangsta rap emerged from Los Angeles in the mid-to-late 1980s.

Gangsta rap or gangster rap, initially called reality rap, is a subgenre of hip hop music that emerged in the mid-to-late 1980s as a distinct style in the United States. Gangsta rap's pioneers were Schoolly D of Philadelphia in 1985, Ice-T of Los Angeles in 1986, and especially the group N.W.A of Compton, California, whose 1988 album Straight Outta Compton brought the genre to national attention. The genre is characterized by lyrics that reflect the violent lifestyles of inner-city America, and it has been widely controversial for its explicit content, alleged glamorization of violence and misogyny.

History

Early milestones include Ice-T's "6 in the Mornin'" (1986) and N.W.A's raw depictions of life in Compton. In the early 1990s the genre dominated hip hop commercially through West Coast artists such as Dr. Dre (The Chronic, 1992), Snoop Dogg and 2Pac, while East Coast acts including The Notorious B.I.G. brought their own variant. The 1990s East Coast–West Coast rivalry culminated in the murders of 2Pac (1996) and The Notorious B.I.G. (1997).

Controversy

Gangsta rap drew criticism from politicians, parents' groups and law enforcement; the FBI sent a letter of protest to N.W.A's label over "Fuck tha Police", and campaigns by figures such as C. Delores Tucker sought to restrict its sale. Defenders argued the genre documented the realities of poverty, policing and street violence rather than endorsing them.

Legacy

The genre shaped mainstream hip hop's sound, fashion and commercial scale, and its influence persists in modern trap and drill music. By the 2000s, gangsta rap's archetypes had been absorbed into popular culture worldwide.
