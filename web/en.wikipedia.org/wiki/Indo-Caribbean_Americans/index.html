Indo-Caribbean Americans

Indo-Caribbean Americans are Americans of Indo-Caribbean origin — descendants of Indian indentured labourers brought to the Caribbean in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, who or whose descendants later migrated to the United States. They originate mainly from Guyana, Trinidad and Tobago, Suriname, and Jamaica.

History

Between 1838 and 1917, Britain, the Netherlands, and France transported hundreds of thousands of Indians as indentured labourers to sugar plantations in the Caribbean. Their descendants form large minorities or pluralities in Guyana, Trinidad and Tobago, and Suriname. Significant migration to the United States followed the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 and political and economic instability in Guyana in the 1970s and 1980s.

Communities and culture

The largest concentration is in New York City, particularly in Richmond Hill, Queens — nicknamed "Little Guyana" — with further communities in Florida, New Jersey, and the Washington, D.C. area. Indo-Caribbean Americans maintain cultural traditions including Diwali and Phagwah (Holi) celebrations, chutney music, and Caribbean-inflected Hindu, Muslim, and Christian religious life.
