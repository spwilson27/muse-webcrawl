Laplace's equation

Laplace's equation is a second-order partial differential equation named after Pierre-Simon Laplace, who first studied its properties. It is written as

∇²f = 0, i.e. Δf = 0

where Δ is the Laplace operator and f is a twice-differentiable real-valued function. Solutions of Laplace's equation are called harmonic functions and are of central importance in many branches of physics and mathematics.

Forms in different coordinate systems

In three-dimensional Cartesian coordinates the equation becomes ∂²f/∂x² + ∂²f/∂y² + ∂²f/∂z² = 0. It also has standard forms in cylindrical and spherical coordinates, chosen according to the symmetry of the problem.

Properties

Harmonic functions satisfy the mean value property: the value at any point equals the average of values over any sphere centred at that point. Consequently they obey a maximum principle — a non-constant harmonic function cannot attain a maximum or minimum in the interior of its domain. On a bounded domain with prescribed boundary values (the Dirichlet problem), the solution is unique.

Applications

Laplace's equation describes the electric potential in charge-free regions, gravitational potential in empty space, steady-state temperature distributions, and the velocity potential of incompressible, irrotational fluid flow. It is closely related to Poisson's equation, which includes a source term on the right-hand side.
