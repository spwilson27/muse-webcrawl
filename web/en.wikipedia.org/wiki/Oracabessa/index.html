Oracabessa

Oracabessa
Country: Jamaica
Parish: Saint Mary
Named for: Spanish Oro Cabeza, "golden head"

Oracabessa is a small town in Saint Mary Parish, Jamaica, on the island's north coast, about 18 kilometres east of Ocho Rios. Its name is thought to derive from the Spanish Oro Cabeza, "golden head", referring to the appearance of the headland at sunset.

History

The area was a centre of the banana trade in the early twentieth century, and its wharf shipped fruit for the United Fruit Company and independent growers. In 1946 the writer Ian Fleming bought land there and built the villa Goldeneye, where he wrote all fourteen of his James Bond novels and stories. Noël Coward also lived nearby at his house Firefly, now a museum, which had earlier been associated with the pirate Henry Morgan's lookout.

GoldenEye and tourism

Since the 1990s the Goldeneye estate has been developed by Island Records founder Chris Blackwell into a resort, alongside the adjacent James Bond Beach. The town remains a working fishing village with a farmers' market, and its fish sanctuary — the Oracabessa Bay Fish Sanctuary, established in 2010 — has been credited with restoring coral and fish stocks and with reintroducing sea turtles nesting on local beaches.
