Organizational learning

Organizational learning is the process by which an organisation improves its actions through better knowledge and understanding. It involves creating, retaining and transferring knowledge within the organisation so that experience — successes and failures alike — is translated into routines, practices and culture that persist beyond individual employees.

Key ideas

Chris Argyris and Donald Schön distinguished single-loop learning, which corrects errors within existing assumptions, from double-loop learning, which questions the assumptions themselves. Peter Senge popularised the "learning organisation" in The Fifth Discipline (1990), emphasising systems thinking and shared vision.

Mechanisms

Knowledge may be stored in documents and databases, embedded in standard procedures, or held as tacit know-how. After-action reviews, mentoring, communities of practice and knowledge-management systems are common tools for spreading and preserving what an organisation learns.
