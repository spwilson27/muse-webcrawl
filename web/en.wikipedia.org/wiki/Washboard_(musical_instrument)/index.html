Washboard (musical instrument)

The washboard is an American folk musical instrument adapted from the laundry tool of the same name. It is used as a percussion instrument, played by scraping, tapping, or striking its ridged metal surface, and is a staple of jug bands, skiffle, zydeco, and Cajun music.

Playing technique

Players typically wear metal thimbles or attach metal finger picks and scrape them across the corrugations to produce rhythmic patterns. The instrument is often worn over the shoulders or held in the lap. Skilled players such as Washboard Sam (Robert Brown) in blues, and players in zydeco bands, have expanded the technique to include complex syncopation and additional attachments such as bells, woodblocks, and cowbells mounted on the board.

History

The washboard entered music-making in the American South in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when household objects were commonly repurposed as instruments by musicians who could not afford manufactured ones. It was central to jug bands of the 1920s and to the British skiffle craze of the 1950s. In Louisiana zydeco, a vest-like variant called the frottoir (French for "scrub board") is worn on the chest, famously played by musicians in bands led by Clifton Chenier.

Construction

Musical washboards are usually made of a wooden frame holding a corrugated sheet of galvanised steel, stainless steel, brass, or aluminium. Purpose-built instruments may have multiple ridged sections of different metals to produce different tones.
