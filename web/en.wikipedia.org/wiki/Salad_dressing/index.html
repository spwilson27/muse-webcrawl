Salad dressing

A salad dressing is a sauce served with salads, typically based on oil and an acid such as vinegar or lemon juice, often with herbs, spices, mustard, egg yolk or cream. Dressings range from simple vinaigrettes to rich emulsified sauces such as mayonnaise-based ranch and Caesar dressing.

Types

Vinaigrette is an emulsion of oil and vinegar (classically 3:1), flavoured with mustard, herbs and seasoning. Creamy dressings include ranch (buttermilk, herbs, garlic), blue cheese, Thousand Island (mayonnaise, ketchup, relish), French dressing (sweet, tomato-based in the US), Italian dressing, and Caesar (egg yolk, anchovy, garlic, lemon, Parmesan). Cooked dressings using a starch-thickened base were common before commercial mayonnaise.

History

Dressed salads date to antiquity — Babylonians and Egyptians used oil and vinegar — and the French refined vinaigrettes and mayonnaise in the 18th and 19th centuries. Commercial bottled dressings appeared in the US in the early 20th century; brands such as Hellmann's, Wish-Bone and Hidden Valley Ranch made dressings a supermarket staple.

Nutrition

Dressings can add significant fat, sodium and sugar to salads; low-fat and yogurt-based versions are marketed as lighter alternatives, though some compensate with added sugar.
