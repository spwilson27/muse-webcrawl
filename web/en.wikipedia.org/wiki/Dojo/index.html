Dojo

A dojo (Japanese: 道場, dōjō, "place of the Way") is a hall or place for immersive learning or meditation, traditionally in Japanese martial arts, where students train under a teacher (sensei). The term, borrowed from Buddhism — where it denoted a place of meditation, rendering the Sanskrit bodhimaṇḍa, the seat of enlightenment — came to be applied to training halls for arts such as judo, karate, aikido and kendo, and has spread worldwide with those arts.

Layout and etiquette

A traditional dojo is arranged around the kamiza or shōmen, the seat of honour at the front, often bearing calligraphy, a portrait of the art's founder, or a small shrine. Students line up by rank, bow on entering and leaving, and bow to the front and to partners before practice. The floor may be tatami or sprung wood matting; shoes are never worn on the mat. Etiquette — cleanliness, punctuality, care of equipment and juniors — is treated as part of the training itself, and students commonly clean the dojo after practice (sōji).

Beyond martial arts

The word has been extended, in Japan and elsewhere, to training places for other disciplines: Zen meditation halls, shogi and go academies, and, in modern usage, programming "coding dojos" where developers practise exercises together. The head of a dojo is often called kanchō or dojo-cho, and the resident teacher's authority and etiquette conventions structure daily practice.
