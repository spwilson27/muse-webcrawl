Turkish Cypriots

Turkish Cypriots (Turkish: Kıbrıs Türkleri) are the ethnic Turkish inhabitants of Cyprus, largely descended from settlers and soldiers who arrived after the Ottoman conquest of the island in 1571, together with converted locals. They speak Cypriot Turkish and are predominantly Sunni Muslims.

History

Under British rule (1878–1960), Turkish Cypriots were a minority of about 18 percent, living in villages and quarters across the island. Intercommunal violence in 1963–64, after the collapse of the power-sharing republic, drove many into enclaves. Following a Greek junta-backed coup in 1974, Turkey intervened militarily; the island was partitioned, with Turkish Cypriots concentrated in the north. In 1983 the north unilaterally declared independence as the "Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus," recognised only by Turkey.

Politics and demography

Turkish Cypriots became citizens of the European Union when Cyprus joined in 2004, although EU law is suspended in the north. In a 2004 referendum, Turkish Cypriots voted in favour of the UN Annan Plan for reunification, which Greek Cypriots rejected. Population figures are politically contested, not least because of immigration from mainland Turkey since 1974; native Turkish Cypriots are often estimated at around 150,000–200,000. Large diaspora communities exist in the United Kingdom, Turkey, and Australia.
