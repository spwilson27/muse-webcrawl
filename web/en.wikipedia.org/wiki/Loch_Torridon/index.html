Loch Torridon
LocationWester Ross, Highland, Scotland
TypeSea loch
Length15 mi (24 km)

Loch Torridon (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Thoirbheartan) is a sea loch on the west coast of Scotland, north of the Applecross peninsula, in Wester Ross. The loch was created by glacial processes and sits in a landscape dominated by the ancient Torridonian sandstone mountains, among the oldest rock formations in Britain.

Geography

The loch runs some 15 miles (24 km) from its mouth on the Minch to the village of Torridon at its head. It is divided into two main basins, Upper Loch Torridon and the outer Loch Torridon, separated by a narrowing near the village of Shieldaig. The River Torridon and the River Balgy flow into the head of the loch.

The mountains rising around the loch include Liathach, Beinn Eighe and Beinn Alligin on the northern side — classic ridge walks and climbs formed from Torridonian sandstone capped with white quartzite. Beinn Eighe National Nature Reserve, established in 1951, was the first national nature reserve in Britain and protects remnants of ancient Caledonian pinewood.

Settlements

Communities around the loch include Torridon, Shieldaig, Inveralligin, Diabaig and Kenmore. The area is sparsely populated and is popular with hillwalkers, climbers and tourists touring the North Coast 500 route. Fishing and fish farming are traditional local industries, and the loch supports a small inshore fishery.
