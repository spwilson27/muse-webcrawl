Complete blood count

A complete blood count (CBC), also known as a full blood count (FBC), is a set of medical laboratory tests that provide information about the cells in a person's blood. The CBC indicates the counts of white blood cells, red blood cells and platelets, the concentration of haemoglobin, and the haematocrit, among other parameters.

Complete blood count
Test ofBlood cells
MeasuresWBC, RBC, platelets, haemoglobin, haematocrit, indices

Parameters

  • White blood cell count and differential (neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, basophils)
  • Red blood cell count, haemoglobin, haematocrit
  • Red cell indices: MCV, MCH, MCHC, RDW
  • Platelet count and mean platelet volume

Uses

The CBC is one of the most commonly performed laboratory tests. It helps detect anaemia, infection, inflammation, bleeding disorders and blood cancers such as leukaemia, and monitors overall health and response to treatment. Modern automated haematology analysers count and classify cells by impedance and light scattering; abnormal results may prompt review of a peripheral blood smear.
