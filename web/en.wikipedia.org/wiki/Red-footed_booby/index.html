Red-footed booby

Scientific name: Sula sula
Family: Sulidae
Order: Suliformes
Conservation status: Least Concern

The red-footed booby (Sula sula) is a large seabird of the booby family, Sulidae. Adults always have red feet, but the colour of the plumage varies: most birds are white with black flight feathers, while others are brown, and some have mixed morphs. It is the smallest of the boobies, at about 70 cm (28 in) in length with a wingspan of up to 1.5 m (5 ft).

Distribution and habitat

The species breeds on tropical islands and coasts of the Atlantic, Pacific and Indian Oceans, including the Galápagos Islands, Hawaii, the Caribbean, and Christmas Island. Unusually among boobies, it nests in trees and shrubs, gripping branches with its strongly webbed feet.

Behaviour

Red-footed boobies are powerful fliers and plunge-divers, feeding mainly on fish and squid caught far out at sea, often in association with schools of predatory fish or dolphins that drive prey to the surface. They may forage more than 100 km from the colony. The female lays a single chalky blue egg in a stick nest, and both parents share incubation, which lasts about 44–46 days; the chick fledges after around 100 days and remains dependent on its parents for several months.

Conservation

The red-footed booby is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN, with a large global population, though local colonies are vulnerable to introduced predators and disturbance.
