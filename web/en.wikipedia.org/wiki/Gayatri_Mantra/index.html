Gayatri Mantra

SourceRigveda 3.62.10
DeitySavitr (solar deity)
SeerVishvamitra
MetreGayatri (24 syllables)
LanguageVedic Sanskrit

The Gayatri Mantra is a highly revered mantra from the Rigveda (3.62.10), dedicated to the solar deity Savitr. Attributed to the sage Vishvamitra, it is one of the most widely recited and studied of all Hindu mantras, a key prayer in daily worship and in the sacred thread (upanayana) ceremony.

Text and translation

Om bhur bhuvah svah
tat savitur varenyam
bhargo devasya dhimahi
dhiyo yo nah prachodayat

The verse, preceded in recitation by the sacred syllable Om and the three vyahritis (bhur, bhuvah, svah — the earthly, atmospheric and heavenly worlds), may be rendered: "We meditate upon the excellent glory of the divine Vivifier; may he impel our understandings." The core verse comprises 24 syllables arranged in three lines of eight, the Gayatri metre.

Significance

The Gayatri is regarded as the mother of the Vedas and the essence of the Upanishads. Traditionally recited at dawn, noon and dusk (the sandhya prayers) by twice-born Hindus after initiation, it symbolises the awakening of the intellect by divine light. The Brihadaranyaka and Chandogya Upanishads discuss its meaning, and the Devi Gayatri is personified as a goddess with five faces. In modern times, figures such as Swami Vivekananda and Mahatma Gandhi popularised its universal, non-sectarian interpretation as a prayer for enlightenment for all.
