The Tokyo Metropolitan Government (東京都庁, Tōkyōto-chō) is the government of Tokyo Metropolis, one of Japan's 47 prefectures. It is headed by a publicly elected governor and a unicameral Metropolitan Assembly of 127 members, elected every four years. Because Tokyo is both a metropolis and the national capital, its government exercises an unusually wide range of functions, including fire protection (the Tokyo Fire Department is the largest urban fire department in the world) and a metropolitan police force.

Structure

Tokyo Metropolis comprises the 23 special wards of central Tokyo, the Tama area's cities, towns and villages to the west, and the Izu and Ogasawara island chains stretching far into the Pacific. The metropolitan government provides prefecture-level services while the special wards operate much like independent cities.

Headquarters

The government relocated from Yurakucho to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building in Nishi-Shinjuku in 1991. The twin-towered building, designed by architect Kenzo Tange and nicknamed "Tax Tower", contains free public observation decks offering views across the Kanto plain to Mount Fuji.

Finances

With an annual budget comparable to that of a mid-sized country and a metropolitan population of about 14 million (over 37 million in the greater metropolitan area), Tokyo's government is one of the largest sub-national administrations in the world.
