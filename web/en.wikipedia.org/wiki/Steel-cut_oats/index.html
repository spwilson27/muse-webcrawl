Steel-cut oats

Steel-cut oats (US), also called pinhead oats, coarse oatmeal or Irish oatmeal, are groats (the inner kernel with the inedible hull removed) of whole oats which have been chopped into two or three pinhead-sized pieces by steel blades. They are less processed than rolled oats, which are steamed and flattened.

Characteristics

Steel-cut oats have a dense, chewy texture and a nutty flavour. Because they are minimally processed, they take longer to cook — typically 20 to 40 minutes simmered in water or milk — though they can be prepared as overnight oats or in slow cookers. They have a lower glycemic index than instant or rolled oats because their coarse structure is digested more slowly.

Nutrition

Like other oat products, steel-cut oats are a source of soluble fibre, notably beta-glucan, along with B vitamins, iron, magnesium and plant protein. They are used primarily as a hot breakfast porridge and in baking and savoury dishes.
