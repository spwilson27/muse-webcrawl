Makarios III

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Makarios III (Greek: Μακάριος Γ΄; born Michael Christodoulou Mouskos; 13 August 1913 – 3 August 1977) was a Greek Cypriot clergyman and politician who served as Archbishop of the Church of Cyprus from 1950 and as the first President of the Republic of Cyprus from 1960 until his death in 1977.

Role in independence

Makarios became the political leader of the Greek Cypriot campaign for enosis (union with Greece). After the EOKA armed struggle began in 1955, the British deported him to the Seychelles in 1956. He accepted the London–Zürich agreements of 1959, which created an independent Cypriot republic instead, and became its first president in 1960, with a constitution balancing Greek and Turkish Cypriot communities.

Presidency and the 1974 crisis

Intercommunal violence erupted in 1963–64 and Makarios survived several assassination attempts. In July 1974 the Greek military junta sponsored a coup against him by the Cypriot National Guard; Makarios escaped and the coup became the pretext for the Turkish invasion of Cyprus, which partitioned the island. He returned in December 1974 and remained president until his death from a heart attack in 1977. Makarios was also a founding figure of the Non-Aligned Movement, positioning Cyprus between East and West.
