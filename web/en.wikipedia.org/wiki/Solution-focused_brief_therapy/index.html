Solution-focused brief therapy


Solution-focused brief therapy (SFBT) is a goal-directed, future-focused form of psychotherapy.

Solution-focused brief therapy (SFBT) is a short-term, goal-directed approach to psychotherapy that focuses on constructing solutions rather than analysing problems. It was developed in the 1980s by Steve de Shazer, Insoo Kim Berg, and colleagues at the Brief Family Therapy Center in Milwaukee.

Techniques

Signature techniques include the miracle question ("Suppose a miracle happened overnight and your problem was solved — what would be different?"), scaling questions that rate progress from 0 to 10, and searching for exceptions — times when the problem is absent or less severe — to identify client strengths and resources.

Applications

SFBT is used in mental health, schools, social work, and coaching, typically in a small number of sessions. Research reviews report small-to-moderate positive effects for a range of difficulties, though the evidence base is debated.
