Selhurst Park

Selhurst Park is a football stadium in Selhurst, in the London Borough of Croydon, England. It is the home ground of Premier League club Crystal Palace, who have played there since the stadium opened in 1924. Its current capacity is about 25,500.

The Holmesdale Road Stand, opened in 1994, dominates one end of the ground.

History

Crystal Palace bought the site of a former brickfield from the London, Brighton and South Coast Railway in 1922. The stadium was designed by the noted football-ground architect Archibald Leitch and opened in August 1924 with a match against Sheffield Wednesday. The ground's record attendance was 51,482, set in 1979 for a match against Burnley that clinched the Second Division championship.

From 1985 to 1991 Selhurst Park was shared with Charlton Athletic — the first groundshare between Football League clubs in England in the modern era — and from 1991 to 2003 Wimbledon also played there after leaving Plough Lane. AFC Wimbledon were later among visiting sides. A Sainsbury's supermarket occupies part of the Whitehorse Lane end of the site.

Stands and redevelopment

The ground comprises the Main Stand (the surviving Leitch structure), the two-tier Holmesdale Road Stand (opened 1994), the Arthur Wait Stand, and the Whitehorse Lane Stand. Crystal Palace has long pursued plans for a substantially rebuilt Main Stand to raise capacity to about 34,000; planning consent for the scheme was granted and updated over successive applications.
