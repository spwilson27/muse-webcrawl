Reseller

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A reseller is a company or individual (merchant) that purchases goods or services with the intention of selling them rather than consuming or using them. Resellers form a link in the distribution chain between manufacturers or wholesalers and end customers. Unlike a distributor, a reseller typically sells directly to the public or to end-user businesses.

Types

Resellers range from ordinary retailers to specialised intermediaries. A value-added reseller (VAR) enhances a manufacturer's product — typically software or hardware — with its own services, integration, customisation or support, and sells the combined offering as a turnkey solution. In information technology, reseller hosting and domain name reselling allow small firms to sell web services provided by larger hosts. Ticket resellers buy event tickets for resale, a practice regulated in many jurisdictions, while "grey market" resellers trade genuine goods outside authorised channels.

Economics

Resellers profit from the margin between their purchase price and resale price. Manufacturers use reseller networks to reach markets without building their own sales channels, setting terms through reseller agreements and, where lawful, minimum advertised price policies. Resale price maintenance is restricted or prohibited in many countries on competition grounds.
