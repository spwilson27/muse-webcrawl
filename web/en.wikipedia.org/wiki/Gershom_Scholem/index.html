Gershom Scholem (5 December 1897 – 21 February 1982) was a German-born Israeli philosopher and historian, widely regarded as the founder of the modern academic study of Kabbalah, Jewish mysticism.

Life

Born Gerhard Scholem in Berlin to an assimilated Jewish family, he rebelled toward Zionism and the serious study of Judaism, learning Hebrew and Talmud largely on his own. He studied mathematics and philosophy at Munich, writing his doctorate on the Bahir, an early kabbalistic text. In 1923 he emigrated to Mandatory Palestine, became head of the Hebrew division of the National Library, and from 1925 taught at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, where he became the first professor of Jewish mysticism in 1933. His lifelong correspondence and friendship with Walter Benjamin became famous after the latter's death.

Scholarship

Scholem's Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism (1941, based on lectures of 1938) mapped the history of Kabbalah from Merkabah mysticism through medieval Spain, the Zohar, Lurianic Kabbalah and Hasidism, overturning the rationalist dismissal of mysticism in earlier Jewish scholarship. His magnum opus, Sabbatai Ṣevi: The Mystical Messiah (1957; English 1973), studied the 17th-century messianic movement around Sabbatai Zevi and its consequences. He argued that mystical and mythic forces — including "counter-history" currents — were central motors of Jewish history.
