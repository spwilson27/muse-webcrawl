Galwan Valley clash

The Galwan Valley clash was a violent confrontation between Indian Army and Chinese People's Liberation Army (PLA) troops on the night of 15–16 June 2020 in the Galwan Valley of Ladakh, along the disputed Line of Actual Control (LAC) between India and China. It was the deadliest clash between the two countries in over four decades.

Background

From May 2020, Indian and Chinese forces were locked in standoffs at several points along the LAC in eastern Ladakh, triggered by Chinese objections to Indian road construction — including an airfield-supporting road to Daulat Beg Oldi — and by broader disagreements over the undemarcated border. Commanders agreed on 6 June 2020 to a phased disengagement, and the two sides began pulling back in the Galwan Valley, near Patrol Point 14.

The clash

On the evening of 15 June, an Indian patrol led by Colonel B. Santosh Babu, commanding officer of 16 Bihar Regiment, went to verify a Chinese observation post that India said had not been removed as agreed. A brawl erupted and escalated into hours of hand-to-hand fighting in steep, high-altitude terrain (around 4,300 m) in darkness. Because firearms were barred near the LAC by bilateral agreements, troops fought with fists, stones, and improvised weapons including clubs wrapped in barbed wire or studded with nails. Many soldiers fell into the fast-flowing, near-freezing Galwan River.

Casualties

India confirmed 20 soldiers killed, including Colonel Babu. China initially gave no figures; in February 2021 it officially acknowledged four PLA deaths, while outside estimates of Chinese casualties varied widely. Dozens more on both sides were injured, and each side briefly detained some of the other's soldiers.

Aftermath

The clash shattered years of relative calm on the border. Both countries rushed reinforcements, armour and artillery to Ladakh, banned dozens of Chinese apps in India's case, and entered a prolonged military and diplomatic standoff. Phased disengagement at Galwan, Pangong Tso, Gogra and Hot Springs followed over 2020–2022, with buffer zones created at friction points; full normalisation of the border relationship remained under negotiation for years afterwards.
