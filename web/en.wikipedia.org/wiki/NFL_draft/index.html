NFL draft

The NFL draft, officially the NFL Annual Player Selection Meeting, is an annual event in which the teams of the National Football League (NFL) select newly eligible players, chiefly from college football. First held in 1936 at the suggestion of future commissioner Bert Bell to promote competitive balance, it has grown into one of the most popular sporting events in the United States.

Format

The draft consists of seven rounds. In each round, teams generally pick in reverse order of the previous season's standings, with the Super Bowl champion selecting last. Teams may trade picks, and additional compensatory picks are awarded to clubs that lose significant free agents. Each team is allotted a time limit per selection — ten minutes in the first round, less in later rounds. Players selected are colloquially identified by round and overall pick number; the last pick is humorously honoured as "Mr. Irrelevant".

Eligibility and scouting

Players must be at least three years removed from high school. Evaluation is intense: the NFL Scouting Combine tests athletes physically and medically, while film study, interviews and pro days feed a year-round scouting industry. Quarterbacks dominate the top of the draft; first overall picks have included Peyton Manning, Andrew Luck, Joe Burrow and Caleb Williams.

Cultural significance

Televised in prime time since 2010 and rotated among host cities since 2015, the draft draws enormous crowds and television audiences. It is famous for its surprises, dramatic trades, celebrated steals (such as Tom Brady, a sixth-round pick in 2000) and notorious busts.
