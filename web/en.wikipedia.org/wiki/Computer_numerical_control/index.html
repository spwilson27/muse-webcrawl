Computer numerical control

Computer numerical control (CNC) is the automated control of machine tools and 3D printers by means of a computer. A CNC machine processes a piece of material (metal, plastic, wood, ceramic, foam, or composite) to meet specifications by following coded programmed instructions, typically G-code.

Operation

The program, often generated by CAD/CAM software, controls spindle speed, feed rate, tool changes, and coordinated motion along multiple axes. Operators load material and tools, set offsets, and monitor machining.

History

Numerical control began in the late 1940s and 1950s with punched-tape systems developed for the US Air Force; with cheaper computers, direct computer control spread through industry from the 1970s onward.

Applications

CNC is used in milling machines, lathes, routers, grinders, laser and plasma cutters, and additive manufacturing, enabling precise, repeatable mass production.
