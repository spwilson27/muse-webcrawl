Khwarezmian (also spelled Chorasmian) is an extinct Eastern Iranian language once spoken in Khwarezm, the oasis region around the lower reaches of the Amu Darya (Oxus) river, south of the Aral Sea, in what is now Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan. It was the language of the Khwarezmian state whose dynasty was overthrown by the Mongol invasion of 1221.

Attestation

The language is attested from about the second century BC in inscriptions on coins, silver vessels and burial ossuaries written in a script derived from Imperial Aramaic, of which the excavations at Toprak-Kala yielded notable examples. After the Arab conquest the language continued to be written in a modified Arabic script. The richest sources are Islamic-era: al-Biruni, himself a native of Khwarezm, recorded Khwarezmian month names, astronomical terms and folk traditions, and the thirteenth-century legal work Qunyat al-Munya contains Khwarezmian sentences glossed in Arabic, which preserve much of its vocabulary and grammar.

Decline

Khwarezmian survived the Mongol destruction of the region but was gradually displaced by Turkic languages and Persian; it appears to have died out by about the fourteenth century. The modern Turkic-speaking Khorezm region preserves no direct descendant, though some scholars have proposed links between Khwarezmian and features of local dialects.
