Alaric I (c. 370 – 410) was king of the Visigoths from 395 until his death. He is best known for the sack of Rome in August 410 — the first time in almost 800 years that the city had fallen to a foreign enemy — an event that resonated across the Roman world as a symbol of the empire's decline.

Career

Alaric served with Gothic foederati in Roman armies, fighting at the Battle of the Frigidus (394). After the death of Theodosius I, his Goths elected him king; seeking land and recognition within the empire, Alaric alternately fought for and against the Eastern and Western courts, campaigning in Greece and Italy. Three times he besieged Rome (408–410), extracting ransoms before finally sacking the city on 24 August 410 after negotiations with the emperor Honorius collapsed.

Death and burial

After the sack, Alaric marched south intending to cross to Sicily and Africa, but storms destroyed his fleet. He died at Consentia later in 410. According to Jordanes, his men diverted the river Busento, buried Alaric with treasure in its bed, restored the river's course, and killed the labourers to keep the grave's location secret. He was succeeded by his brother-in-law Ataulf.
