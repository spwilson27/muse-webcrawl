1908 Summer Olympics

The opening ceremony at White City Stadium, London, 1908.

The 1908 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the IV Olympiad, were held in London, United Kingdom, from 27 April to 31 October 1908. Originally awarded to Rome, the Games were moved to London after the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 1906 diverted Italian funds to disaster relief. They were the fourth Summer Olympic Games and, at over six months, the longest in Olympic history.

Venues and organisation

The centrepiece was the purpose-built White City Stadium in Shepherd's Bush, which seated some 68,000 spectators and housed a running track, cycling track, and swimming pool in a single arena — a first for the Olympics. The Games were staged alongside the Franco-British Exhibition. A total of 2,008 athletes from 22 nations competed in 110 events across 22 sports, including the first appearance of field hockey and figure skating (then part of the Summer Games).

Notable events

The marathon became famous for two reasons. First, its distance was fixed at 26 miles from Windsor Castle to White City, plus 385 yards inside the stadium so the finish would be in front of the royal box — the origin of the modern standard marathon distance of 26 miles 385 yards (42.195 km), formally standardised in 1921. Second, the Italian runner Dorando Pietri collapsed repeatedly within sight of the finish, was helped across the line by officials, and was disqualified, with the gold medal awarded to the American Johnny Hayes; Queen Alexandra presented Pietri with a special cup. The Games also saw controversies over judging, dominated by host-nation officials, which prompted the standardisation of international judging in later Games.

Medal table

Great Britain topped the medal table by a wide margin with 56 gold medals and 146 medals in total, ahead of the United States (23 golds) and Sweden. The 1908 Games also introduced the tradition, suggested by the Bishop of Pennsylvania's sermon, reflected in Baron de Coubertin's celebrated words that the important thing in the Games is "not to win, but to take part."
