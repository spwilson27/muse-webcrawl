Gila Cliff Dwellings National Monument

LocationCatron County, New Mexico, United States
EstablishedNovember 16, 1907
Governing bodyNational Park Service

Gila Cliff Dwellings National Monument is a United States national monument in the Gila Wilderness of southwestern New Mexico. It protects cliff dwellings built by the Mogollon culture in natural caves in the cliffs above the Gila River.

History

The dwellings were constructed between roughly 1275 and 1300 CE, when Mogollon families built rooms within five large cliff alcoves. The people appear to have left the area by about 1300. President Theodore Roosevelt proclaimed the site a national monument in 1907, making it one of the early monuments created under the Antiquities Act.

Description

The monument contains around forty rooms built of stone, mortar, and timber within the alcoves of Cliff Dweller Canyon. A one-mile loop trail leads visitors through the largest cluster of dwellings. The surrounding Gila Wilderness, designated in 1924, was the first congressionally designated wilderness area in the world.

Access

The monument is reached by a winding mountain road from Silver City, New Mexico. A small visitor center and museum exhibit Mogollon pottery and other artifacts recovered from the area.
