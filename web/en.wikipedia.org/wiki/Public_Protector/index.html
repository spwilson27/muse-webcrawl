Public Protector

The Public Protector is an independent constitutional institution in South Africa, established under Chapter 9 of the Constitution of 1996 to strengthen constitutional democracy. The office investigates improper conduct in state affairs and public administration, reports on it, and takes appropriate remedial action.

History and mandate

The office succeeded the earlier Ombudsman under the interim constitution. The Public Protector is appointed by the President on the recommendation of the National Assembly for a non-renewable seven-year term. Holders have included Selby Baqwa, Lawrence Mushwana, Thuli Madonsela, Busisiwe Mkhwebane and Kholeka Gcaleka.

High-profile investigations have covered state capture, presidential benefits and procurement irregularities. The Constitutional Court confirmed in 2016 that the Public Protector's remedial action is binding unless set aside by a court.
