Charter

A charter is the grant of authority or rights, stating that the granter formally recognises the prerogative of the recipient to exercise the rights specified. It is implicit that the granter retains superiority (or sovereignty), and that the recipient admits a limited (or inferior) status within the relationship, and it is within that sense that charters were historically granted, and that sense is retained in modern usage of the term.

A sealed medieval charter

Historical use

The word derives from the Latin charta, meaning paper or document. In medieval Europe, charters were the standard legal instrument by which kings and lords granted land, privileges, markets, fairs or liberties, authenticated with a seal. Famous examples include Magna Carta (1215), in which King John of England conceded rights to his barons, and the charters granted to towns and boroughs that established their rights of self-government. From the late Middle Ages, monarchs issued royal charters incorporating bodies such as universities, guilds, learned societies and trading companies — including the colonial charters under which much of British North America was settled.

Modern uses

The term is now applied to a wide range of founding or authorising documents. A municipal charter is the basic law of a city; a corporate charter (articles of incorporation) creates a company; universities and charities may be founded by charter. International organisations are established by charters, most famously the Charter of the United Nations (1945). The word is also used for documents proclaiming rights, such as the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms (1982), and in commerce for the hire of ships or aircraft under a charterparty or charter agreement.
