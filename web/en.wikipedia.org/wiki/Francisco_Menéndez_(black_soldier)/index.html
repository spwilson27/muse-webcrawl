Francisco Menéndez (black soldier)

Bornc. early 1700s, Gambia region, West Africa
Diedafter 1763
Known forLeader of the free black militia and settlement of Fort Mose, Spanish Florida

Francisco Menéndez (born in the Gambia region of West Africa, died after 1763) was a free Black militiaman and community leader in Spanish Florida. Captured and enslaved by the English in South Carolina, he escaped to St. Augustine, where he won his freedom, commanded the Black militia of the free settlement of Fort Mose, and became one of the most prominent Africans in colonial North America whose life is documented in the historical record.

From enslavement to freedom

Menéndez was born in the Gambia River region and was of Mandingo origin. Transported to South Carolina as an enslaved person, he escaped south to Spanish Florida, where Spanish policy offered sanctuary and freedom to fugitives from English territory who converted to Catholicism. In St. Augustine he was baptized, later purchased and freed, and emerged as a leader of the free Black community, noted for his literacy and his petitions to the Spanish Crown asserting the rights of the fugitives.

Fort Mose

In 1738 Governor Manuel de Montiano formally freed the fugitives and established the town of Gracia Real de Santa Teresa de Mose — Fort Mose — two miles north of St. Augustine, the first legally sanctioned free Black settlement in what is now the United States. Menéndez was appointed captain of its militia. In 1740, during the War of Jenkins' Ear, British forces under James Oglethorpe captured the fort; Menéndez and his men fought alongside Spanish troops in the bloody retaking of Mose, a decisive action in the defense of St. Augustine. Captured by English privateers in 1741 and sold again into slavery in the Bahamas, he escaped or ransomed himself and returned to Florida by 1752, rebuilding Mose after its re-establishment.

Later life

When Spain ceded Florida to Britain in 1763, Menéndez evacuated with the rest of the free Black community to Cuba, where he helped found the town of San Agustín de la Nueva Florida. His documented petitions, military service, and leadership have made him a central figure in scholarship on Black resistance and maroon communities in the Atlantic world.
