Freedom of panorama

Freedom of panorama is a provision in the copyright laws of various jurisdictions that permits taking photographs and video footage and creating other images — such as paintings — of buildings and sometimes sculptures and other art works which are permanently located in a public place, without infringing on any copyright that may otherwise subsist in the works, and the publishing of such images.

Principles

Ordinarily, copyright gives the creator of a work the exclusive right to make derivative works. Buildings, as works of architecture, and public sculptures are usually protected. Freedom of panorama is an exception: it allows anyone to photograph such works in public spaces and publish the images, usually for any purpose, including commercial use. The term derives from the German Panoramafreiheit; German law has contained such a provision since the 19th century.

Variation by country

The scope varies widely. The United Kingdom permits photographs of buildings, sculptures, models and works of artistic craftsmanship in public places. Germany allows images of works permanently on public ways, streets or squares, taken from public ground. France long had no general freedom of panorama, introducing a limited non-commercial exception in 2016. The United States permits photographs of buildings visible from public places but gives no equivalent right for sculptures and other artworks. The absence of a uniform rule in the European Union has generated debate, especially regarding uploads to online platforms and Wikimedia Commons, which relies on freedom of panorama to host many images of modern buildings.
