The Aeronomy of Ice in the Mesosphere (AIM) is a NASA satellite mission launched in 2007 to study polar mesospheric clouds — also called noctilucent clouds — which form ice particles at about 83 kilometres altitude, making them the highest clouds in Earth's atmosphere. The mission is led by Hampton University, the first Small Explorer mission managed by a historically Black university.

Objectives

AIM was designed to determine why polar mesospheric clouds form and how they vary, and whether their reported brightening and expansion toward lower latitudes are connected to climate change in the upper atmosphere. The clouds form over the summer pole when mesospheric temperatures fall to the coldest found anywhere on Earth, and their ice particles nucleate on meteoric smoke particles from ablated meteors.

Instruments

  • CIPS (Cloud Imaging and Particle Size) — a panoramic ultraviolet imager that maps cloud morphology and particle sizes.
  • SOFIE (Solar Occultation for Ice Experiment) — measures temperature, water vapour, methane, ozone, nitric oxide, and cloud extinction by solar occultation.
  • CDE (Cosmic Dust Experiment) — counts interplanetary dust particles entering the atmosphere.

Mission

AIM was launched on 25 April 2007 aboard a Pegasus XL rocket dropped from an aircraft over the Pacific Ocean, into a near-polar orbit at about 600 km altitude. Originally planned for two years, the mission was repeatedly extended and returned the first global-scale, multi-season maps of noctilucent clouds, revealing their sensitivity to water vapour, temperature, and solar activity.
