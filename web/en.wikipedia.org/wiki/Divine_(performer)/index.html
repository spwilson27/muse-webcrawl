Divine (performer)

Divine (born Harris Glenn Milstead; 19 October 1945 – 7 March 1988) was an American actor, singer, and drag queen. Closely associated with the filmmaker John Waters, Divine was a character actor who became a cult figure of underground cinema, starring in Waters films including Pink Flamingos (1972), Female Trouble (1974), and Hairspray (1988). People magazine described him as the "Drag Queen of the Century".

Early life

Milstead was born in Baltimore, Maryland, and grew up in its suburbs. Bullied at school for his weight and perceived effeminacy, he worked as a hairdresser before joining the circle of young filmmakers around John Waters, who gave him the name "Divine" and the billing "the most beautiful woman in the world".

Career

With Waters's Dreamland ensemble, Divine specialised in outrageous, larger-than-life roles, achieving notoriety for the closing scene of Pink Flamingos and acclaim for playing both mother and father in Hairspray. Beyond Waters, he appeared in films such as Lust in the Dust (1985) and Trouble in Mind (1985), performed in theatre, and in the 1980s enjoyed a parallel career as a disco and Hi-NRG recording artist with hits including "You Think You're a Man" and "Walk Like a Man". Divine died of heart failure in 1988, shortly after Hairspray's release, just as he was transitioning to mainstream male television roles.

Legacy

Divine is regarded as a pioneering figure in drag and queer culture; the animated villain Ursula in Disney's The Little Mermaid (1989) was modelled on his appearance.
