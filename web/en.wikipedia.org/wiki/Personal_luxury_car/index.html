Personal luxury car

The personal luxury car is a North American car classification describing somewhat sporty, sophisticated mass-market coupés that emphasised comfort, styling, and high levels of equipment over performance and handling. Typically two-door models with plush interiors, long hoods, and V8 engines, they were marketed as personal cars for affluent buyers rather than as family transport.

History

The concept is generally traced to the 1955 Ford Thunderbird, which Ford marketed as a "personal car" rather than a sports car. The segment flourished from the 1960s through the 1980s with models such as the Buick Riviera, Oldsmobile Toronado (the first mass-produced American front-wheel-drive car), Cadillac Eldorado, Lincoln Continental Mark series, Pontiac Grand Prix, and Chrysler Cordoba — the latter famous for offering "soft Corinthian leather". Sales declined in the 1990s as buyers shifted to luxury sedans, SUVs, and imported coupés, and the traditional personal luxury segment largely disappeared.
