Timor-Leste

Timor-Leste (also known as East Timor), officially the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste, is a country in Southeast Asia. It comprises the eastern half of the island of Timor, the exclave of Oecusse on the island's north-western coast, and the outer islands of Atauro and Jaco. Its capital and largest city is Dili. The country covers about 14,950 square kilometres and has a population of roughly 1.3 million.

History

Timor was colonised by Portugal in the 16th century and remained Portuguese Timor until 1975. After the Carnation Revolution, the territory declared independence on 28 November 1975, but nine days later Indonesia invaded and occupied it, annexing it as its 27th province. The occupation was marked by a violent conflict in which a large portion of the population died. In a 1999 UN-sponsored referendum, the East Timorese voted overwhelmingly for independence; after a period of UN administration, Timor-Leste became independent on 20 May 2002, the first new sovereign state of the 21st century.

Geography

The terrain is mountainous, rising to Tatamailau (Ramelau) at 2,963 metres. The climate is tropical with distinct wet and dry seasons. The country lies within the Coral Triangle, giving its waters exceptionally rich marine biodiversity.

Politics and economy

Timor-Leste is a semi-presidential republic. The official languages are Tetum and Portuguese; Indonesian and English are working languages. The economy depends heavily on offshore oil and gas revenues held in the Petroleum Fund, along with coffee exports and agriculture. Timor-Leste applied to join ASEAN and has pursued full membership, alongside membership of the Community of Portuguese Language Countries.

Demographics

The population is overwhelmingly Roman Catholic, a legacy of Portuguese rule and a distinctive identity marker during the Indonesian occupation. Ethnic groups include the Austronesian and Papuan peoples of the island, with Tetum the most widely spoken language.
