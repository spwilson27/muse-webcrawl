Trial balloon

A trial balloon is information deliberately released, often anonymously or through an indirect channel, in order to gauge the public, political, or market reaction to an idea before it is formally proposed. If the reaction is favourable, the person or organisation behind the leak can proceed with the proposal; if the reaction is hostile, the idea can be dropped and its authorship plausibly denied.

The term derives from the practice of releasing a small pilot balloon to determine wind conditions before a manned balloon ascent. In politics and journalism the metaphor entered common usage in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and the practice is sometimes described by the related phrase "running it up the flagpole to see who salutes."

Use in politics and media

Governments and political parties use trial balloons to test proposed legislation, budgets, appointments, and policy reversals. A minister may allow a proposal to be reported as "under consideration" so that lobby groups, opposition parties, and the press reveal their positions. Trial balloons are also used in business, where rumoured mergers, price changes, or product discontinuations are floated to shareholders and customers in advance.

Journalists may cooperate, knowingly or not, in floating trial balloons; a story attributed to "senior officials" or "sources familiar with the matter" is a common vehicle. Analysts caution that reactions to a trial balloon may themselves be strategic, since interested parties can manufacture outrage or enthusiasm to influence the eventual decision.

Related concepts

Trial balloons are related to kite-flying in British political usage, to focus-group testing of messages, and to the practice of "softening up" public opinion before an unpopular announcement. Unlike a formal consultation, a trial balloon creates no commitment and no official record of a proposal.
