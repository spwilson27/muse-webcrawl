The 36th (Ulster) Division was an infantry division of the British Army in the First World War, formed from the Ulster Volunteer Force, the unionist paramilitary organisation raised in 1912 to resist Irish Home Rule. It became famous for its assault on the first day of the Battle of the Somme.

Formation

When war broke out in 1914, the Ulster Volunteer Force offered itself to the War Office en masse. The division trained in Northern Ireland and at Seaford in Sussex before landing in France in October 1915, part of Kitchener's New Armies.

Battle of the Somme

On 1 July 1916 the division attacked towards the Schwaben Redoubt near Thiepval. It was one of the few formations to reach its objectives on that disastrous day, advancing further than any other British division, but unsupported on its flanks it was forced back with over 5,000 casualties in two days. Four members won the Victoria Cross. The sacrifice of the 36th became central to Ulster unionist memory, often linked symbolically with the anniversary of the Battle of the Boyne.

Later service

The division fought at Messines in 1917 (alongside the 16th (Irish) Division), at Third Ypres and Cambrai, suffered heavily in the German spring offensive of 1918, and took part in the final advance to victory. It was disbanded in 1919.
