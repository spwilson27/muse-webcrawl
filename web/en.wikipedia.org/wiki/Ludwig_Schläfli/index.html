Ludwig Schläfli (1814–1895) was a Swiss mathematician, specialising in geometry and complex analysis, who was one of the key developers of the concept of higher-dimensional spaces. The Schläfli symbol used to describe regular polytopes is named after him.

Life and work

Schläfli studied theology and then mathematics at the University of Bern, where he spent nearly his entire career, becoming professor in 1848. Working in relative isolation and hampered by ill health, he nevertheless produced fundamental work: his Theorie der vielfachen Kontinuität (written 1850–52, published posthumously in 1901) systematically developed the geometry of n-dimensional Euclidean space, defining higher-dimensional polytopes and their notation {p, q, r, ...} — the Schläfli symbol, in which {3,3} is the tetrahedron and {4,3} the cube.

Contributions

Schläfli also derived the integral formula for the volume of n-dimensional spherical geometry, worked on elliptic modular functions, and gave criteria for when spherical or hyperbolic tilings exist. He translated works of his contemporary Jakob Steiner and corresponded with leading European mathematicians. His higher-dimensional geometry, little read in his lifetime, later became foundational for the study of polytopes and manifold topology.
