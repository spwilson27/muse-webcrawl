Trope (literature)

Trope (literature)
Common tropes recur across storytelling traditions
DefinitionA recurring narrative device, motif or figure of speech
Related conceptsCliché, motif, archetype, figure of speech

A literary trope is the use of figurative language — via word, phrase or image — for artistic effect, such as using a figure of speech. The word trope has also come to be used for a common or overused theme or device: a cliché, though "trope" itself is neutral and not inherently pejorative. In modern popular usage, a trope is any recurring convention, motif or storytelling device in works of fiction.

History of the term

The term derives from the Greek tropos, "a turn, a direction, a way". In classical rhetoric, tropes were figures of speech that change the usual meaning of words — including metaphor, metonymy, synecdoche and irony — as classified by writers such as Quintilian.

Modern usage

In contemporary criticism and fan culture, "trope" denotes recognisable narrative building blocks: character types (the mentor, the femme fatale), plot devices (the quest, the love triangle, the deus ex machina) and settings or situations. Reference sites such as TV Tropes popularised this catalogue sense of the word. Scholars note that tropes are not inherently bad; they become clichés when used without variation or self-awareness.
