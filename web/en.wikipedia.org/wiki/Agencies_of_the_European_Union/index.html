Agencies of the European Union

The agencies of the European Union are specialised bodies of the European Union and Euratom set up to carry out particular technical, scientific, or managerial tasks. Distinct from the EU's institutions, they are separate legal entities, and most were created from the 1990s onward as the EU's regulatory workload grew.

Types

Decentralised agencies include the European Medicines Agency (Amsterdam), the European Food Safety Authority (Parma), the European Chemicals Agency (Helsinki), the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (Cologne), the border agency Frontex (Warsaw), Europol and Eurojust (The Hague), and the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (Stockholm). Executive agencies, by contrast, are set up by the European Commission for fixed terms to manage EU funding programmes.

Locations and governance

Agency seats are distributed among member states by political agreement — the relocation of the Medicines Agency from London to Amsterdam after Brexit was a prominent example. Agencies are run by directors under management boards on which member states and the Commission sit, and are financed from the EU budget and fees.
