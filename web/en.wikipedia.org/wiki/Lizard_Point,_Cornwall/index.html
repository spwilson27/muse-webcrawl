Lizard Point, Cornwall

Lizard Point in Cornwall is the most southerly point of mainland Great Britain. It lies at the tip of the Lizard Peninsula, south of the village of Lizard and the town of Helston, and marks the northern entrance to the English Channel's shipping lanes. (Land's End, farther west, is the most westerly point of mainland England.)

Geography and geology

The headland is formed of serpentinite, part of the Lizard Complex — an ophiolite, a slice of ancient oceanic crust thrust onto land during the Devonian. The polished green and red serpentine rock was once popular for ornamental carving. The cliffs support rare plants including Cornish heath, and the surrounding waters are protected for their marine wildlife; basking sharks and grey seals are seen offshore, and the chough has been reintroduced to the cliffs.

Lighthouse and maritime history

The Lizard Lighthouse, built in 1752 (with earlier warning beacons and a 1619 tower by Sir John Killigrew), warns ships away from the dangerous reefs that extend south of the point. The area has seen many wrecks, including the Royal Navy frigate HMS Primrose (1809) and the German barque Bay of Panama in the great blizzard of 1891. The Lizard lifeboat station at Kilcobben Cove serves the surrounding coast. The point was also the site of a Lloyd's signal station that reported passing shipping.
