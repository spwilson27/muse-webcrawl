Conflict is an English anarcho-punk band originally based in Eltham in South London. Formed in 1981, the band's ferocious live shows and uncompromising lyrics about anarchism, animal rights, the anti-war movement and opposition to the state and capitalism made them one of the leading acts of the British anarcho-punk movement, second in prominence only to Crass in the genre's early years.

History

Conflict's debut release was the EP The House That Man Built (1982) on Crass's label, Corpus Christi Records. Their first album, It's Time to See Who's Who (1983), drew the attention of the British music press, with the Sounds and NME papers covering the band's militant stance. In 1983 the band played a central role in the "Stop the City" demonstrations against the City of London financial district, events widely regarded as precursors of the later anti-globalisation movement.

In 1984 the band established its own label, Mortarhate Records, which released records by numerous anarcho-punk acts, and organised large benefit concerts, including the celebrated 1986 "Gathering of the Tribes" show at Brixton Academy. The albums Increase the Pressure (1984), The Ungovernable Force (1986) — often cited as their defining work — and The Final Conflict (1988) combined aggressive hardcore punk with increasingly accomplished production. Vocalist Colin Jerwood remained the band's driving force through repeated line-up changes and hiatuses; Conflict have continued to record and tour intermittently into the 21st century, headlining punk festivals such as Rebellion.

Ideology and influence

Conflict promoted direct action in support of animal liberation, aligning with the Animal Liberation Front and hunt saboteurs, and their song "This Is the A.L.F." became an anthem of the movement. Their confrontational style and DIY organisational model influenced generations of punk and hardcore bands, and The Ungovernable Force is regarded as a landmark of UK punk.
