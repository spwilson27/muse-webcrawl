Central simple algebra

In ring theory and related areas of algebra, a central simple algebra (CSA) over a field K is a finite-dimensional associative K-algebra A that is simple (has no non-trivial two-sided ideals) and whose centre is exactly K.

Examples

The matrix algebra Mn(K) is a central simple algebra over K. More generally, every finite-dimensional division algebra whose centre is K is a CSA, and quaternion algebras and cyclic algebras provide important non-split examples.

Structure theory

By the Artin–Wedderburn theorem, every CSA over K is isomorphic to Mn(D) for a unique division algebra D over K. Two CSAs are Brauer-equivalent if their division parts are isomorphic; the equivalence classes form the Brauer group Br(K) under tensor product. A CSA is called split when D = K, i.e. when it is a matrix algebra.

Properties

By the Skolem–Noether theorem, every automorphism of a CSA is inner. The dimension of a CSA over its centre is always a perfect square; its square root is the degree of the algebra, and the index is the degree of the associated division algebra.
