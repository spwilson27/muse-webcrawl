Crofting

Crofting (Scottish Gaelic: croitearachd) is a form of land tenure and small-scale food production particular to the Scottish Highlands and islands, involving a small agricultural holding — a croft — usually rented from a landlord and often sharing common grazing land with a township of other crofts. There are roughly 21,000 crofts, concentrated in the historic crofting counties of Argyll, Caithness, Inverness-shire, Ross and Cromarty, Sutherland, Orkney and Shetland.

History

The crofting system emerged in the late 18th and 19th centuries as landlords reorganised Highland estates during the Clearances, confining tenants to small plots of poorer land while better land was turned over to sheep farms. Crofters had insecure tenure and faced rack-renting and eviction until agitation — including the Battle of the Braes (1882) — led to the Napier Commission and the Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886, which granted security of tenure, fair rents and compensation for improvements. Later legislation created the Crofters Commission and, in the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003, a right for crofting communities to buy their land.

The croft

A croft is typically a few hectares of inbye land with a share in common grazing. Crofters traditionally combined small-scale livestock rearing (sheep and cattle), potatoes and oats with other work such as fishing, weaving (Harris tweed), quarrying or employment in tourism and public services. Modern crofting law, consolidated in the Crofters (Scotland) Act 1993 and amended by the Crofting Reform (Scotland) Act 2010, imposes duties to reside on or near the croft and to cultivate it or put it to another purposeful use. The Crofting Commission regulates the system today.

Culture

Crofting is closely associated with Gaelic language and culture and with the distinctive dispersed township settlement pattern of the Highlands and Islands. It remains politically and culturally significant in Scotland, with debate over housing pressure, absentee crofters and the future of common grazings.
