Youth hostel

A youth hostel is a form of inexpensive, sociable accommodation where guests can rent a bed, usually a bunk bed in a dormitory, with shared bathrooms, kitchens and communal areas. Hostels offer private rooms as well, but the dormitory arrangement keeps prices low and encourages travellers to meet. They form part of the wider tradition of the hostel, though the name no longer implies an age limit in most places.

History

The youth hostel movement began in Germany in the years before the First World War. Schoolteacher Richard Schirrmann, seeking suitable overnight stops for school groups on walking trips, opened one of the first permanent hostels in Altena Castle in 1912. The idea spread internationally; Hostelling International (founded 1932 as the International Youth Hostel Federation) coordinated national associations, and the Youth Hostels Association of England and Wales was founded in 1930.

Modern hostelling

Today hostels range from rural former schools and converted castles serving walkers and school groups to large city hostels aimed at backpackers. Guests traditionally helped with small chores, and many rural hostels still encourage self-catering. In recent decades, independent and boutique hostels with private rooms and bars have blurred the line between hostels and budget hotels.
