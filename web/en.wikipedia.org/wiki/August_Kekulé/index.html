August Kekulé

August Kekulé

Friedrich August Kekulé (1829–1896), later von Kekulé, was a German chemist who founded the theory of chemical structure. In 1858 he (and independently Archibald Couper) showed carbon is tetravalent and can link to itself in chains — the basis of structural formulas.

In 1865 Kekulé proposed the ring structure of benzene, solving the puzzle of C₆H₆. At a 1890 celebration he recounted that the idea came in a reverie of a snake seizing its own tail — the ouroboros — an anecdote historians treat with affectionate caution.

[Image: Portrait of Kekulé and his benzene ring diagram]

Career

Kekulé studied at Giessen under Liebig, worked in Paris, London and Heidelberg, and held chairs at Ghent (1858) and Bonn (1867). His textbook and his structural theory trained a generation of organic chemists, including van 't Hoff.

Legacy

Structure theory — molecules as definite architectures of atoms — turned organic chemistry from a catalogue of substances into a predictive science and fed directly into the German dye and pharmaceutical industries.
