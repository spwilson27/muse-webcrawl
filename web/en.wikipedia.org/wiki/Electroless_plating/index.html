Electroless plating

Electroless plating, also known as chemical or auto-catalytic plating, is a method of depositing a metal coating onto a substrate by a controlled chemical reduction, without the use of an external electric current. Unlike electroplating, the driving force is a chemical reducing agent in the bath, and deposition proceeds uniformly over the whole exposed surface.

Process

The best-known process is electroless nickel plating, in which nickel ions are reduced by sodium hypophosphite, co-depositing phosphorus to form a nickel–phosphorus alloy. The reaction is catalytic: once started on a suitably prepared surface it continues as long as the part remains in the bath. Because throwing power is effectively perfect, coatings are of even thickness even on complex shapes, sharp edges and internal surfaces.

Properties and uses

Electroless nickel coatings are valued for corrosion resistance, hardness (which can be increased further by heat treatment) and wear resistance. They are applied to valves, pumps, automotive and aerospace components, and to metallise plastics — for example in the manufacture of printed circuit boards and in electromagnetic-interference shielding. Electroless copper and gold processes are also important in electronics fabrication.
