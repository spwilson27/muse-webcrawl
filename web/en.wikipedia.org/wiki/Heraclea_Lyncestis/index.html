Heraclea Lyncestis

Heraclea Lyncestis (Ancient Greek: Ἡράκλεια Λυγκηστίς) was an ancient Macedonian city in the region of Lyncestis, near the modern city of Bitola in North Macedonia. Founded by Philip II of Macedon in the mid-4th century BC, it was named for the hero Heracles, mythical ancestor of the Macedonian royal house.

History

The city stood on the Via Egnatia, the great Roman road across the Balkans, and prospered under Rome as a trading and administrative centre. It became an early Christian episcopal see, and several large basilicas were built in the 5th and 6th centuries AD. Heraclea was sacked during Slavic incursions around the end of the 6th century and abandoned.

Archaeology

Excavations have uncovered a Roman theatre built under Hadrian and Antoninus Pius, baths, a portico, and the Small and Large Basilicas, celebrated for their richly patterned floor mosaics depicting animals, birds, and the Tree of Life — among the finest examples of early Byzantine mosaic art in the Balkans.
