Deliberative polling

Deliberative polling is a form of public consultation developed by political scientist James Fishkin of Stanford University. It combines random sampling with structured deliberation: a representative sample of citizens is surveyed, then brought together to discuss an issue with experts and policymakers over one or more days, and surveyed again afterwards. The final results are presented as the conclusions the public would reach if it had the opportunity to become informed and reflect on the issue.

Method

A deliberative poll begins with a baseline survey of a random sample. Participants receive balanced briefing materials, take part in small-group discussions led by trained moderators, and question competing experts in plenary sessions. A second questionnaire, identical to the first, measures how opinions change. The process is designed to model "what the public would think, had it a better opportunity to consider the question at issue."

Applications

Deliberative polls have been conducted in more than twenty countries on topics including energy policy, constitutional reform, health care and European integration. Notable examples include the Texas utility polls of the 1990s, which influenced investment in renewable energy, and "America in One Room" in 2019. The related concept inspired citizens' assemblies in several countries.
