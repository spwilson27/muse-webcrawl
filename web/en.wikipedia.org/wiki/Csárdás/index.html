Csárdás

The csárdás is a traditional Hungarian folk dance and the musical form associated with it. The name derives from csárda, an old Hungarian term for a roadside inn or tavern. Originating in the early 19th century from the verbunkos recruiting dances, the csárdás became the Hungarian national dance and a staple of Romani band repertoire.

Form

A csárdás characteristically alternates two contrasting sections: a slow, stately opening called the lassú, in 4/4 time, followed by a fast, exuberant friss in 2/4, often accelerating through repeated strains to a whirling climax. Danced by couples, it features proud postures, heel-clicking steps and improvised variations. Ferenc (Franz) Liszt drew on the csárdás in several of his Hungarian Rhapsodies.

Famous examples

The best-known concert csárdás is the Csárdás (1904) for violin and piano by the Italian composer Vittorio Monti, a virtuoso showpiece built on Hungarian folk themes. The dance also features in ballets such as Coppélia and Swan Lake, and in operettas by Emmerich Kálmán, notably Die Csárdásfürstin ("The Csárdás Princess", 1915).
