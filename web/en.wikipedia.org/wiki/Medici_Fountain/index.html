Medici Fountain

The Medici Fountain (Fontaine Médicis) is a monumental fountain in the Jardin du Luxembourg in Paris. It was built around 1630 by Marie de' Medici, widow of King Henry IV and regent of France, as the nymphaeum of the Luxembourg Palace she had constructed.

History

Designed in the Italian grotto style, probably by Tommaso Francini, the fountain originally stood against the palace garden wall. In the 1860s, during Baron Haussmann's renovation of Paris, it was moved and rebuilt about thirty metres, and the sculptor Auguste Ottin added the group of Polyphemus surprising Acis and Galatea that crowns it today.

Description

A long rectangular basin, shaded by plane trees and flanked by statues of a faun and a huntress, leads to the grotto facade with its central niche and giant figures of the rivers Rhône and Seine. It is one of the most photographed corners of the garden.
