Kazimierz Michałowski

Kazimierz Michałowski (14 December 1901 – 1 January 1981) was a Polish archaeologist and Egyptologist, widely regarded as the founder of the Polish school of Mediterranean archaeology and of Nubiology, the study of ancient Nubia. A professor at the University of Warsaw, he directed excavations across Egypt, Sudan, Syria and Cyprus, and founded the Polish Centre of Mediterranean Archaeology in Cairo.

Career

Michałowski studied classical archaeology in Lwów and abroad. In 1936 he launched the Polish-French excavations at Edfu in Egypt, and he went on to direct work at Tell Atrib, Deir el-Bahari (where his team reconstructed the temple of Hatshepsut), Alexandria and Dongola. He served as deputy director of the National Museum in Warsaw and built up its Faras Gallery.

Nubian campaign and Faras

His greatest fame came from the UNESCO salvage campaign in Nubia ahead of the Aswan High Dam: from 1961 Polish excavations at Faras in Sudanese Nubia uncovered a cathedral decorated with over 120 extraordinary early medieval Christian frescoes. Half the paintings, including the famous image of Saint Anna, came to the National Museum in Warsaw — still one of its signature collections — while the rest are in Khartoum. The rescue of the Faras paintings, cut from the walls under his supervision, is considered a landmark of conservation.

Legacy

The University of Warsaw's Polish Centre of Mediterranean Archaeology now bears his name. Michałowski's popular books did much to spread interest in ancient Egypt and the Mediterranean in Poland.
