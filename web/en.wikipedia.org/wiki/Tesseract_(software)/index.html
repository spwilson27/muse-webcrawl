Tesseract is a free and open-source optical character recognition (OCR) engine. It converts images of typed, handwritten, or printed text into machine-encoded text, and supports more than 100 languages, including right-to-left scripts and Chinese, Japanese, and Korean.

History

Tesseract was originally developed at Hewlett-Packard Laboratories between 1985 and 1994, with further work for the Windows port in the mid-1990s. It was a competitive entrant in the 1995 UNLV OCR accuracy tests but was then shelved. HP released it as open source in 2005, and from 2006 to 2018 development was sponsored by Google, which used it in projects such as Google Books. Since then it has been maintained by the open-source community.

Operation

Tesseract processes images in stages: adaptive thresholding converts the input to a binary image, connected components are outlined and grouped into lines and words, and character classification proceeds in two passes. From version 4.0, released in 2018, the engine added a neural-network recogniser based on long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, substantially improving accuracy on difficult text while retaining the legacy engine as an option. Output formats include plain text, hOCR, PDF, TSV, and ALTO XML.

Usage

The engine is used through a command-line interface, e.g. tesseract input.tif output, or through bindings and front ends such as OCRmyPDF and various graphical interfaces. Its trained data files (.traineddata) can be customised or retrained for specialised fonts and languages.
