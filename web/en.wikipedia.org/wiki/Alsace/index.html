Alsace

Alsace is a cultural and historical region in northeastern France, on the west bank of the Rhine bordering Germany and Switzerland. Since 2021 it forms the European Collectivity of Alsace within the Grand Est region. Its capital and largest city is Strasbourg, seat of the European Parliament.

Geography

Alsace stretches along the Rhine plain between the Vosges mountains to the west and the Rhine to the east. The Alsace wine route winds through vineyard villages such as Riquewihr, Eguisheim and Kaysersberg, famed for white wines including Riesling and Gewürztraminer.

History

Long part of the Holy Roman Empire and German-speaking, Alsace was gradually annexed by France in the 17th century. Germany annexed it after the Franco-Prussian War (1871) as Alsace-Lorraine; it returned to France in 1918, was annexed de facto by Nazi Germany in 1940, and restored in 1945. This contested past left a bilingual heritage, distinctive local law (droit local), and a concordat regime retained from the German period.

Culture

Symbols include the stork, half-timbered houses and dishes such as tarte flambée (flammekueche), choucroute and baeckeoffe. Strasbourg Christmas market, dating to 1570, is among Europe's oldest.
