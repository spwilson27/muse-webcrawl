Zellweger syndrome

Zellweger syndrome is a rare congenital disorder characterized by the reduction or absence of functional peroxisomes in the cells of an individual. It is the most severe of the Zellweger spectrum disorders, a group of peroxisome biogenesis disorders that also includes neonatal adrenoleukodystrophy and infantile Refsum disease.

Signs and symptoms

Infants with Zellweger syndrome typically present with severe hypotonia (poor muscle tone), a distinctive high forehead and other craniofacial features, seizures, feeding difficulties, and liver and kidney dysfunction. Eye abnormalities and hearing loss are common. The condition is caused by mutations in any of several PEX genes required for normal peroxisome assembly, and is inherited in an autosomal recessive manner.

Prognosis and treatment

There is no cure; treatment is supportive and symptomatic. The prognosis is poor, and most infants with the classic severe form do not survive beyond the first year of life. The disorder is named after Hans Zellweger, the Swiss-American pediatrician who described its features in the 1960s.
