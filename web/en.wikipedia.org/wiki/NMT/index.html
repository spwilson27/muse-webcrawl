NMT (Nordic Mobile Telephone, in Finnish Nordiskt MobilTelefonsystem) was an analogue automatic mobile phone system specified by the telecommunications administrations of the Nordic countries (Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland and Iceland). It was launched in 1981 and was the first fully automatic cellular telephone network in the world, as well as the first to offer international roaming.

History

Design work on a common Nordic mobile telephone system began in the 1970s, when the Nordic telecom administrations agreed that a shared standard would allow subscribers to roam between the Scandinavian countries. The system entered service in Saudi Arabia and Spain in mid-1981, followed by Sweden in October 1981 and the other Nordic countries shortly afterwards. NMT grew rapidly through the 1980s and was adopted, in whole or in part, by many countries in Europe, the Middle East and Asia.

Technology

The original system, NMT-450, operated in the 450 MHz band with 25 kHz channel spacing. A higher-capacity variant, NMT-900, was introduced in 1986 using the 900 MHz band. Because the system was analogue, speech was transmitted as frequency-modulated audio without encryption, and calls could be intercepted with ordinary radio scanners. NMT supported voice calls only; data services were not part of the original specification.

Decline

During the 1990s the digital GSM standard displaced NMT in most markets. Nordic operators shut down their NMT-900 networks around the turn of the millennium and the NMT-450 networks in the following years, although a few 450 MHz networks continued operating in rural areas into the 2010s because of the band's long range.
