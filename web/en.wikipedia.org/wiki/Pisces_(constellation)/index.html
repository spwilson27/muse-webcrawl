Pisces is a constellation of the zodiac. Its vast bulk — and in fact its faintness — covers most of its region. It lies between Aquarius to the southwest and Aries to the east in the northern celestial hemisphere. Large by area (the 14th largest constellation), Pisces has no star brighter than fourth magnitude, and its stars are best sought under dark skies in autumn in the Northern Hemisphere.

Features

The constellation's distinctive form consists of two small fish, one swimming west below Pegasus (the "Western Fish"), the other north (the "Northern Fish"), joined by a long cord knotted at the star Alrescha (Alpha Piscium, from the Arabic for "the cord"), a close binary star. The vernal equinox — the point where the Sun crosses the celestial equator into the northern sky — currently lies in Pisces, just south of the Western Fish; it marked the start of the astrological year in the period when the equinox was in Aries.

Notable objects

  • M74 — a grand-design spiral galaxy seen nearly face-on; among the faintest of the Messier objects.
  • Van Maanen's Star — one of the nearest known white dwarfs, about 14 light-years away.
  • NGC 520 — a pair of colliding spiral galaxies.
  • Fomalhaut C (LP 876-10) — a distant companion in the Fomalhaut system.

Mythology

In Greek mythology, Pisces represents Aphrodite and Eros, who jumped into the Euphrates and transformed into fish — or were carried away by fish — to escape the monster Typhon; Venus tied their tails together with a cord so they would not be separated. The constellation's origins, however, reach back to Babylonian astronomy, where the region's stars were associated with fish and the god Ea.
