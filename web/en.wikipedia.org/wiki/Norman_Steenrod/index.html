Norman Steenrod

Norman Earl Steenrod (22 April 1910 – 14 October 1971) was an American mathematician best known for his foundational work in algebraic topology. He spent most of his career at Princeton University and shaped the modern form of the subject through his research, students and textbooks.

Contributions

With Samuel Eilenberg, Steenrod laid down the Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms characterising homology and cohomology theories (published in Foundations of Algebraic Topology, 1952). His The Topology of Fibre Bundles (1951) became the standard reference on fibre bundles. He introduced the Steenrod operations — cohomology operations central to computations in algebraic topology — and, with J. H. C. Whitehead, developed the convenient category of compactly generated spaces. He also helped standardise mathematical exposition and terminology, co-authoring How to Write Mathematics.
