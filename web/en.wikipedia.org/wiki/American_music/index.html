American music

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A collage-style scene: a jazz trumpeter, a country fiddler and a rock guitarist on a shared stage]
A collage-style scene: a jazz trumpeter, a country fiddler and a rock guitarist on a shared stage

American music — the music of the United States — is defined by the fusion of European, African, Latin American and Indigenous traditions into original forms that came to dominate world popular music: Jazz, Blues, Country music, Rock and roll, Soul music, Funk, hip hop and the American musical. Its classical tradition (Ives, Copland, Gershwin) and its songbook standards stand alongside these vernacular revolutions in global influence.

Roots and vernaculars

Colonial psalmody, African American spirituals and work songs, Anglo-Celtic ballads and fiddle traditions, minstrelsy (with its racist legacy), brass bands and ragtime converged in the South and its cities. From New Orleans came jazz (Buddy Bolden, Louis Armstrong), from the Mississippi Delta the blues (Robert Johnson), from Appalachia and the honky-tonks country music (the Carter Family and Jimmie Rodgers, recorded 1927), and gospel from the Black church — the four wells of American popular music.

The popular century

Radio, records and Tin Pan Alley industrialised song; swing, bebop and cool jazz succeeded one another; rhythm and blues plus country produced rock and roll in the 1950s (Chuck Berry, Elvis Presley), which then spun off soul, Motown, funk, folk-rock, metal, punk and disco. In the Bronx in the 1970s, hip hop — DJing, MCing, breakdance, graffiti — began the form that has dominated global pop in the twenty-first century. The Broadway musical and Hollywood film song created the 'Great American Songbook' in parallel.

Classical and contemporary

American concert music — Charles Ives, Aaron Copland, George Gershwin, Leonard Bernstein, Philip Glass's minimalism — built institutions (orchestras, conservatories) while absorbing vernacular idioms. Today the US remains the world's largest recorded-music market, its genres fully globalised and its regional scenes (Nashville, Nashville's rivals, Atlanta trap, Chicago drill) feeding a streaming monoculture that still renews itself from local experiment.
