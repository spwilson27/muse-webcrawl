City Circle

City Circle

Location: Sydney, New South Wales, Australia
Type: Underground railway loop
Stations: Town Hall, Wynyard, Circular Quay, St James, Museum, Central

The City Circle is a mostly-underground railway line located in the Sydney central business district and Haymarket, in New South Wales, Australia, that forms the core of the Sydney Trains network. It is used by all lines except the Olympic Park line, the Cumberland Line and the Carlingford line. The line is owned by Transport Asset Holding Entity and operated by Sydney Trains. It opened piecemeal between 1926 and 1956.

History

The City Circle was conceived by engineer John Bradfield as part of his plan for Sydney's railways. The first stations, Town Hall and Wynyard, opened in 1932 together with the Sydney Harbour Bridge lines, while St James and Museum had opened in 1926 as part of the initial underground city railway. The loop was not completed until 1956, when Circular Quay station opened, connecting Wynyard and St James. The delayed completion was due to funding shortages during the Great Depression and the Second World War.

Design and operation

The loop runs in a roughly circular path around the CBD. Two additional tracks at St James and two unused tunnels at Circular Quay and Wynyard survive from earlier schemes. Trains run around the loop in both directions: “inner” (clockwise) and “outer” services, with most suburban lines passing through the loop and continuing to other parts of the network. Circular Quay station is elevated above the quay with views of Sydney Harbour, while the other stations are underground.
