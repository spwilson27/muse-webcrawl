Rolleiflex

Rolleiflex is a long-running series of medium format twin-lens reflex (TLR) cameras made by the German company Franke & Heidecke, later Rollei, beginning in 1929 at Braunschweig.

The classic Rolleiflex uses 120 roll film producing 6×6 cm images, with a viewing lens above a taking lens (often a Zeiss Tessar or Planar, or Schneider Xenotar), a waist-level focusing hood with ground glass, and a crank film advance coupled to shutter cocking. The 2.8F and 3.5F models of the 1960s are especially prized.

Used by photographers such as Richard Avedon, Diane Arbus and Vivian Maier, the quiet, discreet TLR became an icon of 20th-century photojournalism and portraiture. Production of film models continued in small numbers into the 21st century under successor companies.
