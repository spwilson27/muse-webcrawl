Pizza Hut is an American multinational pizza restaurant chain and international franchise founded in 1958 in Wichita, Kansas by brothers Dan and Frank Carney. The chain, headquartered in Plano, Texas, operates nearly 19,000 restaurants worldwide. It is a subsidiary of Yum! Brands, Inc., one of the world's largest restaurant companies.

The original Pizza Hut building in Wichita was built in a style that led to the chain's name: with only room for a short sign, one of the founders' wives suggested "Pizza Hut" because the building resembled a hut. The chain's restaurants became famous for their distinctive red roofs, an architectural trademark designed by architect Richard D. Burke in 1963.

History

The Carney brothers borrowed $600 from their mother to open the first restaurant near Wichita State University. The business franchised in 1959, and by the 1960s Pizza Hut was expanding rapidly across the United States. In 1977, the chain was acquired by PepsiCo, which spun off its restaurant division as Tricon Global Restaurants in 1997 — later renamed Yum! Brands. Pizza Hut was long the world's largest pizza chain before being overtaken by Domino's in sales in 2018.

Products

Pizza Hut is known for its pan pizza, introduced nationally in 1980, as well as the stuffed crust pizza (1995), in which the outer edge of the crust is wrapped around mozzarella cheese. The menu also includes pasta dishes, chicken wings (through WingStreet in some markets), breadsticks, salads and desserts. The chain has experimented with concepts such as Pizza Hut Express locations in airports, stadiums and department stores.

Marketing

The chain's long-running "Book It!" programme, launched in 1984, rewards schoolchildren with a free personal pan pizza for meeting reading goals. Pizza Hut has also been notable for promotional stunts, including delivering a pizza to the International Space Station in 2001 and placing its logo on a Russian Proton rocket in 1999.
