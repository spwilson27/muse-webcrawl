Fender Stratocaster

The Fender Stratocaster, colloquially known as the Strat, is a model of electric guitar designed in 1954 by Leo Fender, Bill Carson, George Fullerton, and Freddie Tavares for the Fender Musical Instruments Corporation. It has been manufactured continuously since 1954 and is one of the most iconic and imitated guitar designs in history.

Design

The Stratocaster features a contoured solid body, three single-coil pickups with a five-way selector switch (originally three-way), a synchronized tremolo bridge, and a bolt-on maple neck. Its double-cutaway shape gives easy access to the upper frets.

Legacy

Stratocasters have been played by countless influential guitarists, including Buddy Holly, Jimi Hendrix, Eric Clapton, David Gilmour, Stevie Ray Vaughan, and John Mayer. Along with the Telecaster, Gibson Les Paul, and Gibson SG, it ranks among the best-selling and most enduring electric guitar models.
