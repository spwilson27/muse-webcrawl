College rock

Era: 1980s
Stylistic origins: Post-punk, new wave, indie rock
Key acts: R.E.M., The Smiths, Pixies, Sonic Youth

College rock is the term used principally in the United States to describe alternative rock music played on student-run university and college radio stations in the 1980s, and the bands associated with that scene. The stations' playlists were often built by students who favoured music outside the commercial mainstream.

Characteristics

College rock drew on post-punk and new wave, jangly guitar pop, and emerging indie rock. It was less a single sound than a distribution and audience ecosystem: college radio, fanzines, independent labels and campus touring circuits, tracked in publications such as the CMJ New Music Report.

Notable artists

R.E.M. from Athens, Georgia became the genre's emblematic success, alongside acts such as The Replacements, Hüsker Dü, Pixies, Sonic Youth, 10,000 Maniacs, The Smiths (in the UK context), and later They Might Be Giants and Camper Van Beethoven.

Legacy

In the late 1980s and early 1990s the audience and industry around college rock merged into the broader commercial category of alternative rock, especially after the breakthrough of grunge in 1991.
