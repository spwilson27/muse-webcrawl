Brenda Hampton

Born: August 19, 1951, Atlanta, Georgia, U.S.

Occupation: Television producer, writer

Years active: 1983–present

Brenda Hampton (born August 19, 1951) is an American television producer and writer. She is best known as the creator of the long-running family drama series 7th Heaven (1996–2007) and the ABC Family drama The Secret Life of the American Teenager (2008–2013).

Career

Hampton began her television career in the 1980s, writing for programs including Sister Kate and working as a writer and producer on series such as Blossom. In 1994 she worked with producer Aaron Spelling, an association that led to the creation of 7th Heaven.

7th Heaven, about a minister, his wife, and their seven children, premiered on The WB in 1996 and ran for eleven seasons, becoming the longest-running series in The WB's history and one of the longest-running family dramas on American television. Hampton served as its creator and executive producer throughout its run.

In 2008 her next series, The Secret Life of the American Teenager, premiered on ABC Family, following a teenager dealing with pregnancy. It ran for five seasons and launched the career of Shailene Woodley. Hampton continued to develop family-oriented programming thereafter, focusing on wholesome, family-centred drama.

Style and themes

Hampton's work is known for dealing with social issues — including teen pregnancy, substance abuse, and family conflict — within a morally grounded family-drama framework, a style that made her shows staples of family-oriented network and cable programming.
