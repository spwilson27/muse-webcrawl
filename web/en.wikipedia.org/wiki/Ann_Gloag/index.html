Ann Gloag

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dame Ann Heron Gloag DBE (born 10 December 1942) is a Scottish businesswoman and philanthropist. She co-founded the Stagecoach Group transport company in 1980 with her brother Brian Souter and (then husband) Robin Gloag, and became one of Scotland's richest women. Since stepping back from the business, she has devoted much of her time and fortune to charitable work in Africa and Scotland.

Business career

Gloag trained as a nurse before entering business. In 1980, using a small inheritance, she, her brother Brian Souter, and her husband Robin Gloag bought two second-hand buses and began running express coach services from Perth — the beginning of Stagecoach. The deregulation of British bus services in the 1980s allowed rapid expansion, and Stagecoach grew into an international transport group operating buses and trains in the United Kingdom and, for a period, abroad. Gloag held executive and board roles in the company for many years and retained a significant shareholding.

Philanthropy

Gloag founded the Gloag Foundation to channel her charitable giving. Her projects have included support for Mercy Ships, which operates hospital ships providing free surgery in Africa; funding for women's and children's health, including work against fistula injuries in childbirth; and support for Freedom From Fistula and Kenya Children's Homes. In Scotland she has supported hospices and educational causes. She owns Beaufort Castle near Inverness and purchased land at Kinfauns Castle in Perthshire, where disputes over public access to her estate attracted public attention.

Honours

Gloag was appointed OBE in 2004 for services to business and was made a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire (DBE) in the 2019 New Year Honours for services to business and philanthropy.
