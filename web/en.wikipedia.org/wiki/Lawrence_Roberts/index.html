Lawrence Roberts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Lawrence Roberts
BornLawrence Gilman Roberts, 21 December 1937, Westport, Connecticut
Died26 December 2018 (aged 81)
Known forProgram manager of the ARPANET; founder of Telenet
[Image: Photograph of Lawrence Roberts in the 1960s beside early networking equipment]

Lawrence Gilman Roberts (21 December 1937 – 26 December 2018) was an American engineer who was the program manager and principal architect of the ARPANET, the precursor to the Internet. Recruited to ARPA in 1966 by Robert Taylor, Roberts designed the network's architecture — adopting packet switching (building on the work of Paul Baran and Donald Davies) and the subnet of Interface Message Processors built by BBN — and managed its deployment to the first nodes in 1969.

Roberts had earlier connected computers across a continent in the 1965 Lincoln Laboratory experiment with Thomas Marill, one of the first demonstrations of computer networking. After ARPA he founded Telenet (1973), the first commercial packet-switching service, which brought networking to businesses and seeded the commercial data-network industry.

With Bob Kahn and Vint Cerf he is routinely counted among the founders of the Internet, receiving the Draper Prize and the Prince of Asturias Award; his role — the manager-architect who turned an idea into a funded, built system — is the organisational counterpart to the more famous protocol designers.
