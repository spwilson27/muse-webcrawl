Fazlur Rahman Khan

Fazlur Rahman Khan (3 April 1929 – 27 March 1982) was a Bangladeshi-American structural engineer and architect, known as the "Einstein of structural engineering" and the "greatest structural engineer of the 20th century" for his revolutionary work in high-rise building systems.

Fazlur Rahman Khan

Born: 3 April 1929, Dhaka, Bengal Presidency, British India
Died: 27 March 1982, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia
Nationality: Bangladeshi, American
Occupation: Structural engineer, architect
Notable works: Willis Tower, John Hancock Center

Early life and education

Khan was born in Dhaka, in present-day Bangladesh. He studied civil engineering at Bengal Engineering College and Ahsanullah Engineering College (now Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology), then earned a master's and PhD in structural engineering at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign on a Fulbright scholarship.

Career

He joined Skidmore, Owings & Merrill in Chicago in 1955. Working with architect Bruce Graham, he designed the John Hancock Center (1969) using the X-braced trussed tube, and the Sears Tower — now Willis Tower (1973), then the world's tallest building — using the bundled tube system.

Tube structural systems

Khan revolutionized tall building design by treating the building as a hollow tube resisting lateral loads. Variants included the framed tube, trussed tube, bundled tube, and tube-in-tube systems, dramatically reducing steel use and enabling supertall buildings. He also pioneered the shear wall frame and outrigger systems and was an early advocate of computer-aided design.

Other projects

His works include One Shell Plaza in Houston, the Hajj Terminal at King Abdulaziz International Airport in Jeddah — a vast tent-like roof — and the United States Air Force Academy Cadet Chapel collaboratively.

Legacy

Khan died of a heart attack in Jeddah in 1982, aged 52. Google honored him with a Doodle in 2017. The Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat awards the Fazlur R. Khan Lifetime Achievement Medal in his name.
