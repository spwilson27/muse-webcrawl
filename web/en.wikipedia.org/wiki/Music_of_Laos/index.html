Music of Laos

The music of Laos is dominated by the traditions of the ethnic Lao, shared with the Isan region of northeastern Thailand. Its signature instrument is the khene, a free-reed bamboo mouth organ, and its most important vocal genre is mor lam.

Khene

The khene is constructed from bamboo pipes of different lengths fitted with free reeds and played by inhaling and exhaling to produce continuous sound. It accompanies singing, dancing, and courtship, and its music, inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2017, is considered emblematic of Lao identity. Mythology credits its invention to a widow who imitated the sounds of birds.

Mor lam

Mor lam is a form of sung storytelling and repartee performed by a singer (mor lam) accompanied by a khene player (mor khene). Traditional lam draws on Buddhist Jataka tales and Lao epics such as the Sang Sinxay, with rapid, witty delivery in regional dialects. Electrified modern versions, closely related to Thai luk thung and mor lam sing, use pop instrumentation and elaborate stage shows and are popular at festivals and temple fairs.

Classical and other music

Lao classical court music, once performed at the royal court of Luang Prabang, uses ensembles related to the Thai mahori and piphat, with xylophones, gongs, and oboes. The country's many ethnic minorities, including the Hmong and Khmu, maintain their own instruments and vocal traditions. Since 1975 the Lao People's Revolutionary Party has promoted official folk troupes, while contemporary Lao pop artists record in Vientiane and for the Lao diaspora abroad.
