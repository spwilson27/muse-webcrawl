The Deaflympics, formerly known as the World Games for the Deaf and the International Silent Games, are a periodic series of multi-sport events sanctioned by the International Olympic Committee at which deaf athletes compete at an elite level. They are organised by the International Committee of Sports for the Deaf (ICSD, founded 1924 as the Comité International des Sports des Sourds).

History

The first games, held in Paris in 1924 with 148 athletes from nine European nations, were the first ever international multi-sport event for athletes with a disability—predating the Paralympic Games by decades. Winter Games were added in 1949 at Seefeld, Austria. The event was known as the World Games for the Deaf until 2001, when the name Deaflympics was formally adopted with IOC approval.

Features

To qualify, athletes must have a hearing loss of at least 55 decibels in their "better ear"; hearing aids and cochlear implants are not permitted in competition, placing athletes on an equal footing. Because competitors cannot hear starting guns or whistles, events use visual signals such as flashing lights and flags. Referees likewise use flags and gestures rather than whistles.

Deaf athletes do not compete in the Paralympics; the Deaflympics is a separate movement rooted in Deaf culture, with its own community and traditions, and ICSD maintains its independence while being recognised by the IOC.
