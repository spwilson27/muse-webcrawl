Cassini–Huygens

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cassini–Huygens
OperatorNASA / ESA / ASI
Launch15 October 1997
Saturn arrival1 July 2004
End15 September 2017 (Grand Finale)
Cassini at Saturn (artist's concept).

Cassini–Huygens was a joint NASA–ESA–ASI mission to Saturn: the Cassini orbiter, the first spacecraft to orbit the ringed planet, and the European Huygens probe, which landed on the moon Titan in January 2005 — still the most distant landing ever made. Over 13 years at Saturn, Cassini transformed understanding of the system.

Cassini found geysers of water ice erupting from the south pole of Enceladus, fed by a global salty ocean beneath its crust; lakes and rivers of liquid methane on Titan; a hexagonal jet stream at Saturn's north pole; and the rings' intricate dynamics, including moons sculpting gaps and waves. Huygens parachuted through Titan's orange haze and photographed rounded pebbles in what looked like a dry riverbed.

Grand Finale

With fuel running out, Cassini was steered through 22 daring dives between the planet and its rings in 2017, then burned up in Saturn's atmosphere on 15 September 2017 — destroyed deliberately so it could never contaminate the potentially habitable moons Enceladus and Titan. The mission's archive remains the reference dataset for Saturn science, and its ocean-world discoveries shaped the agenda of successor missions like Europa Clipper and the planned Dragonfly rotorcraft to Titan.
