Psycho is a 1959 horror novel by American writer Robert Bloch. It tells the story of Norman Bates, the shy manager of an isolated motel, who is dominated by the personality of his dead mother and commits murder while wearing her clothes. The novel was inspired by the crimes of Wisconsin murderer and grave robber Ed Gein, who was arrested in 1957 in Bloch's home state; Bloch has said he began the book before knowing the details of Gein's case and was startled by the parallels. It is best known as the basis for Alfred Hitchcock's 1960 film Psycho.

Plot

Mary Crane, a young office worker in Phoenix, steals $40,000 from her employer's client and flees by car toward her fiancé Sam Loomis in Fairvale, California. Caught in a storm, she stops at the nearly deserted Bates Motel, run by the awkward Norman Bates, who lives with his invalid mother in the house behind the motel. After an uncomfortable supper during which "Mother" berates Norman, Mary decides to return the money — but that night a figure in women's clothing stabs her to death in the shower. Norman, discovering the body, sinks the evidence, including Mary's car, in a swamp. Mary's sister Lila and Sam, aided by private detective Milton Arbogast, trace Mary to the motel; Arbogast is murdered on the motel-house stairs. Lila and Sam's search of the house reveals that Mrs Bates is a mummified corpse propped in the cellar, and that Norman — his personality split between "Norman", "Norma" and "Norman-the-child" — is the killer. He is committed to an institution, wholly possessed by the Mother personality.

Development and themes

Bloch wrote the novel quickly after the Gein revelations supplied a real-world counterpart to his invented killer; themes include the monstrous mother, voyeurism, split identity and the banality of small-town evil. The twist structure — killing the apparent protagonist a third of the way through — was transferred to Hitchcock's film, which Bloch's novel supplied almost scene for scene, adapted by Joseph Stefano.

Sequels and legacy

Bloch wrote two sequels, Psycho II (1982), a satire in which Bates escapes and heads for Hollywood, and Psycho: Sanitarium (2016) was written by Chet Williamson as a midquel; Bloch's own last Bates novel was Psycho House (1990). The novel remains a landmark of American horror fiction and the foundation of the Psycho franchise, including the television series Bates Motel (2013–2017).
