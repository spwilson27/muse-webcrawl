Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad is the largest city in the Indian state of Gujarat and the administrative centre of Ahmedabad district. Located on the banks of the Sabarmati River, it was founded in 1411 by Sultan Ahmed Shah I as the capital of the Gujarat Sultanate. The historic city of Ahmedabad was inscribed as India's first UNESCO World Heritage City in 2017.

History

The old city, enclosed by walls with twelve gates, contains pols — self-contained housing clusters — and monuments such as the Jama Masjid (1424), the Sidi Saiyyed mosque famous for its stone lattice (jali) "Tree of Life" window, and the Bhadra Fort. Ahmedabad became a great textile centre — called the "Manchester of India" — in the 19th and 20th centuries. Mahatma Gandhi made the city his base from 1915, founding the Sabarmati Ashram, from which he launched the Salt March (Dandi March) in 1930.

Economy and culture

Today the economy rests on textiles, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, and a growing services sector; the neighbouring capital Gandhinagar lies 23 km north. The city hosts the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad, designed by Louis Kahn, and CEPT University. Festivals include Uttarayan (kite festival) and Navratri, and the old city's street food is celebrated. Ahmedabad was a candidate host city for events surrounding the 2036 Olympic bid discussions in India.
