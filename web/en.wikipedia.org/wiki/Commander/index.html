Commander

Commander (commonly abbreviated as Cdr) is a common naval officer rank as well as a job title in many armies. Commander is also used as a rank or title in other formal organizations, including several police forces. In several countries, the rank of commander is used in the air force as well.

The rank is above a lieutenant commander (or army major) and below a captain. In NATO terms it carries the code OF-4, equivalent to a lieutenant colonel in land forces.

History

The term originated in the 17th and 18th centuries as "master and commander", the officer in command of a small warship such as a sloop-of-war, who was too junior for a post-captain yet in sole charge of his vessel. In the Royal Navy the rank was formalized in 1794 as "commander". The United States Navy used the title "master commandant" before adopting "commander" in 1838.

Usage by country

United Kingdom

In the Royal Navy, a commander ranks above lieutenant-commander and below captain, wearing three gold bands on the sleeve. Commanders typically command frigates or destroyers, or serve in senior shore appointments. The British Army instead uses "commander" as an appointment — for instance, a brigade commander — rather than a rank.

United States

A commander in the U.S. Navy or Coast Guard (pay grade O-5) wears silver oak leaves and may command a frigate, destroyer, or submarine, an aviation squadron, or a shore installation.

Other uses

"Commander" appears as the head of orders of chivalry and fraternal orders (a Knight Commander), in police forces (a rank in London's Metropolitan Police and the Los Angeles Police Department), and in titles such as commander-in-chief.
