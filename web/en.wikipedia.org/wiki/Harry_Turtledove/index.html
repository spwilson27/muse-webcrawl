Harry Norman Turtledove (born 14 June 1949) is an American author, best known for his work in the genres of alternate history, historical fiction, and science fiction. He has been dubbed "The Master of Alternate History". Among his best-known works are the Southern Victory series, in which the Confederacy wins the American Civil War, and the Worldwar series, in which reptilian aliens invade Earth during the Second World War.

Biography

Turtledove was born in Los Angeles and grew up in Gardena, California. He studied at UCLA, earning a PhD in Byzantine history in 1977, and published a translation of the Chronicle of Theophanes. After several years in technical writing and an editorial job, he turned to fiction full-time. His first novels appeared under the pseudonym "Eric G. Iverson" because his editor thought readers might not believe "Turtledove" was real.

Works

His breakthrough alternate-history novel was The Guns of the South (1992), in which time-travelling Afrikaners supply the Confederacy with AK-47 rifles. How Few Remain (1997) launched the eleven-volume Southern Victory (Timeline-191) sequence. Other major series include Worldwar/Colonization, the Videssos cycle of Byzantine-flavoured fantasy, the War Between the Provinces, and standalone works such as Ruled Britannia and In the Presence of Mine Enemies. His novella "Down in the Bottomlands" won the Hugo Award in 1994, and his short story "Must and Shall" won the Sidewise Award for Alternate History.
