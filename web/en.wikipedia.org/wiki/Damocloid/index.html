Damocloid

Type: Minor planet group
Named for: 5335 Damocles
Orbits: Highly eccentric, often inclined or retrograde
Probable origin: Oort cloud / Halley-type comets

Damocloids are a class of minor planets — asteroids in comet-like orbits that show no coma or tail — named after their prototype, 5335 Damocles. They are thought to be inactive, dormant or extinct nuclei of Halley-type and long-period comets that have lost their volatile material.

Characteristics

Damocloids have large, highly eccentric orbits with Tisserand parameters with respect to Jupiter below 2, resembling those of periodic comets. Many travel on strongly inclined paths, and some are retrograde, orbiting the Sun in the opposite direction to the planets — evidence of an origin in the spherical Oort cloud rather than the flat Kuiper belt.

Examples

A few hundred candidates are known. Notable examples include 5335 Damocles, which crosses from inside the orbit of Mars out to beyond Uranus, and 20461 Dioretsa, one of the first asteroids discovered on a retrograde orbit. Studies suggest damocloids are spectrally red, resembling comet nuclei.
