Ted Stevens Anchorage International Airport (IATA: ANC, ICAO: PANC, FAA LID: ANC) is a major airport in the U.S. state of Alaska, located 5 miles (8 km) southwest of downtown Anchorage. It is named for U.S. Senator Ted Stevens, a longtime Alaska politician who championed aviation in the state.

Cargo hub

Anchorage is one of the world's busiest cargo airports by landed weight, consistently ranking among the top five globally. Its position roughly midway between East Asia and the contiguous United States makes it an ideal technical stop for trans-Pacific freighter flights, which can trade fuel for payload. Major cargo carriers including FedEx Express, UPS Airlines, and Atlas Air operate extensive operations at the airport.

History

The airport was built in 1951 and originally named Anchorage International Airport. During the Cold War it served as a refueling stop for Western airlines flying polar routes to Asia, which could not overfly the Soviet Union. In 2000 the airport was renamed in honor of Senator Ted Stevens.

Facilities

The airport has a South Terminal (domestic) and a North Terminal (international and charter). It is connected to the Alaska Railroad via the Airport Rail station, and a people mover was planned but the rail spur primarily serves cruise passenger trains.
