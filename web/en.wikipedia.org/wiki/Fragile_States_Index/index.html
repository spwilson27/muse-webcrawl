Fragile States Index

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Fragile States Index (formerly the Failed States Index) is an annual report that ranks 179 sovereign states and territories according to their vulnerability to conflict or collapse. It is produced by the Fund for Peace, a United States non-profit research and educational organisation, and has been published in association with Foreign Policy magazine since 2005. The index was renamed from "Failed States" to "Fragile States" in 2014 to reflect the continuum of stability the ranking measures.

Methodology

The rankings are based on twelve political, social, and economic indicators of state vulnerability, each scored from 0 (most stable) to 10 (highest risk), giving a maximum total score of 120. The indicators are grouped into four clusters:

  • Cohesion: security apparatus, factionalised elites, group grievance.
  • Economic: economic decline, uneven economic development, human flight and brain drain.
  • Political: state legitimacy, public services, human rights and rule of law.
  • Social: demographic pressures, refugees and internally displaced persons, external intervention.

Scores are derived through the Fund for Peace's CAST (Conflict Assessment System Tool) framework, which combines content analysis of millions of documents, quantitative data, and qualitative expert review. Countries are then banded into alert categories — from "Very High Alert" through "Warning" and "Stable" to "Sustainable".

Results and criticism

States affected by civil war and institutional collapse — in recent editions including Somalia, Yemen, Syria, Sudan, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo — consistently occupy the highest-fragility positions, while Nordic countries such as Finland, Norway, and Denmark rank as the most sustainable. The index is widely cited by governments, aid agencies, and researchers, but has been criticised for methodological opacity in its content analysis, for the imprecision of the "failed state" concept, and for conflating very different kinds of vulnerability into a single rank ordering.
