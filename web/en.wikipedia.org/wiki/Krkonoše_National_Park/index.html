Krkonoše National Park

Krkonoše National Park (Czech: Krkonošský národní park, often shortened to KRNAP) is a national park in the Liberec and Hradec Králové regions of the Czech Republic. It lies in the Krkonoše Mountains (the Giant Mountains), the highest range in the country, along the border with Poland, where it adjoins Poland's Karkonosze National Park. Together the two parks form a UNESCO biosphere reserve.

Description

The Czech park was established in 1963 and covers about 363 km², plus a protective buffer zone. Its highest point is Sněžka (1,603 m), the highest mountain in the Czech Republic, whose peak is shared with Poland. The park protects a unique mosaic of ecosystems: subalpine meadows, glacial cirques, peat bogs, mountain spruce forests and relict arctic-alpine tundra on the highest plateaus — a legacy of the Ice Age, when the Krkonoše stood at the boundary between the Scandinavian ice sheet and Alpine glaciation. The Elbe river rises in the park, at the Elbe Meadow (Labská louka) near Sněžka.

Notable flora includes endemic species such as the Krkonoše rowan, and wildlife includes red deer, chamois (introduced), and a recovering lynx population. The mountains are among the most visited natural areas in Central Europe, with resorts such as Špindlerův Mlýn and Pec pod Sněžkou, extensive hiking trails and winter skiing; managing heavy visitor pressure while protecting fragile tundra is the park's central challenge.
