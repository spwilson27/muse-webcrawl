Australian Age of Dinosaurs

The Australian Age of Dinosaurs (AAOD) is a natural history museum and fossil preparation laboratory near Winton in outback Queensland, Australia. Founded by grazier David Elliott after the discovery of giant sauropod fossils on his property, it houses the world's largest collection of Australian dinosaur fossils and operates the largest fossil preparation laboratory in the Southern Hemisphere, staffed largely by trained volunteers and citizen scientists.

History

In 1999, David Elliott found a giant sauropod femur while mustering sheep on Belmont Station near Winton; further discoveries, including the specimens nicknamed "Matilda" (the sauropod Diamantinasaurus matildae) and "Banjo" (the theropod Australovenator wintonensis), were excavated with the Queensland Museum and formally described in 2009. The Australian Age of Dinosaurs organisation was formed to continue excavation, preparation and display of fossils from the Cretaceous Winton Formation.

Facilities

The museum complex, set on a mesa known locally as "The Jump-Up", includes a collection building with a theatre, fossil preparation laboratory tours in which visitors watch preparators at work and can train for multi-day "Prep-a-Dino" experiences, and the outdoor Dinosaur Canyon with life-sized bronze dinosaur reconstructions along a boardwalk. The site also hosts the March of the Titanosaurs exhibition, displaying a celebrated trackway of sauropod footprints originally excavated near Winton.

Research

Fossils prepared at the laboratory have contributed to descriptions of new Australian dinosaurs, including the sauropods Savannasaurus and Diamantinasaurus. The museum works with universities and the Queensland Museum and runs annual public digs that have become a model for citizen-science palaeontology and outback tourism.
