King–Byng affair

The King–Byng affair was a Canadian constitutional crisis in 1926, arising from the refusal of the governor general, Lord Byng of Vimy, to accept the advice of Prime Minister William Lyon Mackenzie King to dissolve Parliament and call an election.

Events

Facing a censure motion in a minority Parliament, King asked for a dissolution; Byng refused, an almost unprecedented exercise of reserve power, and invited opposition leader Arthur Meighen to form a government. Meighen's government fell within days, and King won the subsequent election campaigning on the constitutional issue. The affair reshaped the role of the governor general and contributed to the Balfour Declaration of 1926 on dominion autonomy.
