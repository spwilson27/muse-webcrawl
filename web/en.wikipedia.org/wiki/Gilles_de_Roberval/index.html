Gilles de Roberval

Gilles Personne de Roberval (August 10, 1602 – October 27, 1675), French mathematician, was born at Roberval near Beauvais, France. He was a founder member of the French Academy of Sciences and one of the most important French mathematicians of the 17th century.

Born: 10 August 1602, Roberval, France
Died: 27 October 1675, Paris
Known for: Roberval balance, method of indivisibles, trochoid

Career

Roberval arrived in Paris in 1628 and joined the circle of Marin Mersenne. In 1634 he won the Ramus chair of mathematics at the Collège de France, a post awarded by competition every three years that he retained for the rest of his life — partly, it is said, by keeping his best methods secret so that he could continue to win. This secrecy contributed to a priority dispute with Evangelista Torricelli over the cycloid, which Roberval called the "trochoid".

Contributions

Roberval developed a powerful method of indivisibles for finding areas and volumes, computed the area under the cycloid, and drew tangents to curves by treating a curve as the resultant of component motions — an early form of kinematic geometry. He designed the Roberval balance, a linkage balance still widely used in scales, whose enclosed parallelogram mechanism keeps the pans horizontal. He also worked on optics, the vacuum, and the design of scientific instruments, and corresponded with Fermat, Pascal, and Descartes.
