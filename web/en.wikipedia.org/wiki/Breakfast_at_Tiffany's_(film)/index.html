Breakfast at Tiffany's (film)

Breakfast at Tiffany's is a 1961 American romantic comedy film directed by Blake Edwards, written by George Axelrod, and loosely based on the 1958 novella by Truman Capote. It stars Audrey Hepburn as Holly Golightly, a naïve, eccentric café society girl who falls in love with a struggling writer played by George Peppard. The film was released by Paramount Pictures on 5 October 1961 and became one of Hepburn's most iconic roles.

Plot

Holly Golightly, a young Texas runaway reinvented as a Manhattan socialite, lives off wealthy men and dreams of marrying rich while window-shopping at Tiffany & Co. to calm her anxieties — "the mean reds". She befriends her new neighbour, Paul Varjak, a writer kept by a wealthy older woman. As Holly's past (including a husband from her home town) intrudes and her plans unravel, she and Paul fall in love; the film closes with their embrace in the rain after Holly rescues her nameless cat.

Music

Henry Mancini composed the score, and Johnny Mercer wrote the lyrics for "Moon River", which Hepburn sings in the film accompanying herself on guitar. The song won the Academy Award for Best Original Song, and the score won Best Original Score. "Moon River" became a standard recorded by hundreds of artists.

Reception and legacy

The film was nominated for five Academy Awards and won two. Hepburn's portrayal — in the famous black Givenchy dress of the opening scene — became a defining image of 20th-century cinema and fashion. The film has also been criticised for Mickey Rooney's portrayal of Holly's Japanese neighbour, Mr. Yunioshi, widely regarded as a racist caricature; Edwards later expressed regret over the casting. In 2012 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
