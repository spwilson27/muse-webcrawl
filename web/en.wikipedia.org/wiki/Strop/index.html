Strop

A strop is a flexible strip of leather, canvas, or other material used to straighten and polish the blade of a straight razor, a knife, or a woodworking tool such as a chisel. Stropping re-aligns the fine edge of the blade and removes minute imperfections; unlike honing, it does not significantly remove metal or re-form the bevel.

Types

The most common form is the hanging strop, a strip of leather attached at one end to a fixed point and held taut by a handle at the other, historically associated with barbers and straight razors. Paddle strops mount the leather on a flat wooden paddle and are popular for knives and carving tools. Strops may be charged with abrasive pastes, such as chromium(III) oxide (green compound) or diamond emulsions, to give a gentle polishing action.

Technique

The blade is drawn along the strop spine-first, away from the cutting edge, in alternating passes on each side. Drawing edge-first would cut the leather. For a razor, stropping is performed before (and sometimes after) each shave to restore the edge between honings.

Other meanings

In nautical usage, a strop is a ring or band of rope used to fasten or secure objects, such as the rope grommet attaching a block to a spar or stay.
