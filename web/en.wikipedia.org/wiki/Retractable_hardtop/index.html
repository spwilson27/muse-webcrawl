Retractable hardtop

TypeConvertible automobile roof
Also calledCoupé convertible, coupé cabriolet
Early examplePeugeot 402 Éclipse (1935)

A folding hardtop in motion

A retractable hardtop — also known as a coupé convertible or coupé cabriolet — is a car roof that folds and stows automatically in the boot, giving the security and refinement of a fixed metal coupé roof with the open-air option of a convertible.

History

The concept dates to the Peugeot 402 Éclipse of 1935 and the Ford Fairlane 500 Skyliner of the 1950s. It was popularised in modern form by the Mercedes-Benz SLK of 1996, after which many manufacturers offered folding hardtops. The mechanisms are heavy and complex, reduce luggage space when lowered, and from the 2010s many makers returned to lighter fabric roofs.

Design

Roofs are usually made of steel or aluminium in two or three panels, driven by electric and hydraulic actuators. Engineering challenges include sealing against weather, preserving boot space, and meeting rollover safety standards.
