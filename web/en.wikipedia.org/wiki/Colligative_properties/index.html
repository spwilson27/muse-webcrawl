Colligative properties

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Colligative properties
Depend onNumber of solute particles, not their identity
ExamplesVapour pressure lowering, boiling point elevation, freezing point depression, osmotic pressure

In chemistry, colligative properties are properties of solutions that depend on the ratio of the number of solute particles to the number of solvent particles in a solution, and not on the nature of the chemical species present. The word colligative comes from the Latin colligatus, "bound together", reflecting that the properties arise from the collective effect of dissolved particles.

The four properties

For dilute solutions of non-volatile solutes, the classic colligative properties are: relative lowering of vapour pressure (described by Raoult's law); boiling point elevation, ΔTb = Kb·m; freezing point depression, ΔTf = Kf·m; and osmotic pressure, Π = cRT. Here m is molality and c is molar concentration. For electrolytes, the van 't Hoff factor i corrects for the number of particles produced per formula unit dissolved.

Applications

Freezing point depression explains why salt melts ice on roads and why antifreeze protects engines. Colligative measurements were historically important for determining the molar masses of unknown compounds, and osmotic pressure remains central to understanding biological fluids, dialysis and reverse osmosis desalination.
