Supreme Soviet of the Soviet Union

The Supreme Soviet of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics was, under the 1936 and 1977 constitutions, the highest body of state authority and nominally the legislative branch of the Soviet Union. It was elected by universal suffrage, though for most of its existence elections offered only a single approved slate of candidates dominated by the Communist Party.

Structure

The Supreme Soviet was bicameral, consisting of the Soviet of the Union, elected in proportion to population, and the Soviet of Nationalities, representing the union republics, autonomous republics, and other national-territorial units with equal quotas. Its standing body between sessions was the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet, whose chairman acted as titular head of state of the USSR.

History

First convened in 1938, the Supreme Soviet generally ratified decisions made elsewhere, meeting only briefly each year. Under Gorbachev's reforms, the partially free elections of 1989 created the Congress of People's Deputies, which elected a reconstituted, genuinely working Supreme Soviet. The institution disappeared with the dissolution of the Soviet Union in December 1991.
