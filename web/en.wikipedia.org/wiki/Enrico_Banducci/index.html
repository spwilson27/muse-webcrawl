Enrico Banducci

Enrico Banducci (born Harry Charles Banducci; February 17, 1922 – October 9, 2007) was an American nightclub owner and impresario, best known as the founder of the hungry i nightclub in San Francisco, a seminal venue for stand-up comedy and folk music in the 1950s and 1960s. Banducci gave early breaks to performers including Lenny Bruce, Mort Sahl, Bill Cosby, Jonathan Winters, Dick Gregory, Phyllis Diller, the Kingston Trio, Barbra Streisand and Woody Allen.

Early life

Banducci was born in Bakersfield, California, to Italian immigrant parents. He studied violin seriously as a young man and played professionally, but gave up a musical career and moved into the restaurant and club business in San Francisco, working in the city's North Beach nightlife scene.

The hungry i

In 1950 Banducci took over a small Columbus Avenue basement club whose name — the "hungry i" — had been given by a previous occupant (theories about it include "the hungry id" and "i" for "intellectual"). Beginning with folk singers, the club developed a distinctive brick-walled cellar atmosphere and a booking policy that mixed stand-up comedians, satirists, jazz and folk musicians. Mort Sahl's success there from 1953 established the venue as the West Coast centre of the new "sick" and topical stand-up. The Kingston Trio recorded live albums at the club, and it became a required stop on the national nightclub circuit.

Banducci was famous for his loyalty to performers and generous terms, though the club's fortunes waned as comedy moved to television and the North Beach scene shifted to topless entertainment in the mid-1960s. He lost the original club, briefly ran a successor venue nearby, and the hungry i name lived on in a later incarnation as a strip club on Broadway.

Later life and death

Banducci remained in San Francisco, becoming a beloved local character and the subject of a one-man stage show, Enrico Banducci: The Man Behind the hungry i. He died in San Francisco on October 9, 2007, aged 85. The hungry i's role in launching modern stand-up and in the folk revival has been chronicled in documentary films and histories of American comedy.
