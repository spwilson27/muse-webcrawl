Race to the Top (R2T) was a US$4.35 billion United States Department of Education competitive grant program created under the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009. Unveiled by President Barack Obama and Secretary of Education Arne Duncan on July 24, 2009, it rewarded states that adopted education reform policies aligned with federal priorities.

Criteria

States were scored on adopting common standards (such as the Common Core), building data systems to measure student growth, improving teacher effectiveness and evaluation, turning around low-performing schools, and expanding charter schools. Points were also awarded for STEM education.

Rounds and winners

Phase 1 winners (announced March 2010) were Delaware and Tennessee. Phase 2 (August 2010) awarded grants to the District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Maryland, Massachusetts, New York, North Carolina, Ohio and Rhode Island. A third round funded additional states at smaller levels.

Criticism

Critics raised concerns about standardized testing emphasis, the influence of private foundations, teacher evaluation tied to test scores, and whether the competition disadvantaged poorer states.
