PC bang

A PC bang (Korean: PC방, literally "PC room") is a type of LAN gaming center in South Korea, where patrons pay an hourly fee to play multiplayer computer games, most commonly on a local network or online. PC bangs became a central part of South Korean youth culture and esports during the late 1990s and 2000s, and the model has spread to other countries with large gaming communities.

History

The first PC bangs appeared in South Korea in the mid-1990s, initially as internet cafés. Their popularity exploded after the release of StarCraft in 1998 and amid the 1997 Asian financial crisis, which left many young Koreans unemployed and seeking inexpensive entertainment. Rapid nationwide broadband deployment also made high-speed connections common in PC bangs before they were widespread in homes.

By the early 2000s there were an estimated 25,000 PC bangs in South Korea. They became social gathering places and de facto training grounds for professional gamers, contributing to the country's dominance in competitive gaming.

Services and culture

Patrons typically pay by the hour, with rates historically around 1,000 won per hour. Most PC bangs offer high-end gaming PCs, large monitors, and ergonomic chairs. Customers can order food and drinks—often instant noodles (ramyeon), snacks, and beverages—delivered directly to their seats, and many venues operate 24 hours a day.

Popular titles have included StarCraft, League of Legends, Overwatch, and PUBG: Battlegrounds. Publishers such as Riot Games and Blizzard Entertainment have offered in-game benefits to players connecting from registered PC bangs.

Social issues

PC bangs have occasionally been associated with gaming addiction concerns and with incidents of players gaming for extremely long sessions. The South Korean government debated regulations such as the "shutdown law," which restricted late-night online gaming for minors until its repeal in 2021.

Outside South Korea

Similar LAN centers exist in China (where they are called wangba), Taiwan, and elsewhere in Asia, as well as in diaspora communities in North America and Europe.
