Asuka-kyō

Asuka-kyō (飛鳥京) was the capital city of Japan during the Asuka period (538–710 AD), located in what is now the village of Asuka in Nara Prefecture. Rather than a single fixed palace, the capital consisted of a succession of imperial palaces built by successive rulers in the Asuka valley.

History

Asuka became the political centre of the Yamato court in the late 6th century. It was the site of Japan's earliest Buddhist temples, including Asuka-dera (Hōkō-ji), founded in 596, and witnessed the Taika Reforms of 645, which reshaped the Japanese state along Chinese lines. In 694 the capital moved to Fujiwara-kyō, and in 710 to Heijō-kyō (Nara).

Archaeology

Excavations have revealed palace foundations, the Ishibutai Kofun tomb, and carved stone monuments such as the Sakafune-ishi, whose purpose remains debated.
