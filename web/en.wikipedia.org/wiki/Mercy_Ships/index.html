Mercy Ships is an international charity based in Lindale, Texas, that operates the largest non-governmental hospital ships in the world. Founded in 1978 by Don Stephens, the organisation provides free surgical and medical care, training for local healthcare workers, and community development projects, primarily in sub-Saharan Africa.

History

Don Stephens founded Mercy Ships in 1978, inspired in part by the international hospital ship SS Hope. The organisation's first vessel was the retired ocean liner Victoria, purchased and refitted as the MV Anastasis, which served from 1978 until 2007. Subsequent vessels included the MV Caribbean Mercy, the MV Island Mercy, and the MV Africa Mercy, a converted rail ferry that entered service in 2007. In 2022 the purpose-built MV Global Mercy, the world's largest civilian hospital ship, joined the fleet.

Operations

Mercy Ships vessels typically spend around ten months docked in a host nation's port, providing free surgeries such as cleft lip and palate repair, cataract removal, tumour removal, orthopaedic procedures, and obstetric fistula repair. The organisation also runs dental and eye clinics on shore, trains local medical professionals, and supports infrastructure projects such as renovating local hospital facilities.

Volunteers from more than 60 nations crew the ships, including surgeons, nurses, engineers, teachers, and cooks. Crew members pay monthly fees and raise their own support, allowing donated funds to go primarily toward services.
