Château Laurier

Fairmont Château Laurier
Location1 Rideau Street, Ottawa, Ontario, Canada
Opened1 June 1912
StyleChâteauesque
OperatorFairmont Hotels and Resorts

The Fairmont Château Laurier is a grand railway hotel in downtown Ottawa, Ontario, beside the Rideau Canal locks and a short walk from Parliament Hill. Built in the French Gothic Revival Châteauesque style with Indiana limestone and conical copper turrets, it has long been known as the "third chamber of Parliament" for the politicians who live, dine, and deal within it.

History

The hotel was commissioned by the Grand Trunk Railway under president Charles Melville Hays, who died in the sinking of the Titanic in April 1912, weeks before the hotel opened; the gala opening was cancelled and it quietly began operations on 1 June 1912. It is named for Prime Minister Sir Wilfrid Laurier. The building was designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1980.

Features

The hotel has about 429 guest rooms, the Wilfrid's restaurant, and historic interiors including the Reading Room and Adam Room. Its proposed modernist addition in 2019 provoked national controversy over heritage protection before a revised design was approved.
