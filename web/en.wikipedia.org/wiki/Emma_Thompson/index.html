Emma Thompson

Dame Emma Thompson (born 15 April 1959) is a British actress and screenwriter. Regarded as one of the finest actresses of her generation, she has received numerous accolades including two Academy Awards, three BAFTA Awards, two Golden Globe Awards, and a Primetime Emmy Award. She is the only person to have won Academy Awards for both acting and writing.

Early life

Thompson was born in Paddington, London, to the actor Eric Thompson, known for narrating The Magic Roundabout, and the actress Phyllida Law. She was educated at Camden School for Girls and studied English at Newnham College, Cambridge, where she joined the Footlights and became its first female president.

Career

After early television success in the sketch series Alfresco and the sitcom of her own series Thompson, she won a BAFTA for the dramas Tutti Frutti and Fortunes of War (1987). Her film career took off with her performances for Kenneth Branagh, then her husband, in Henry V (1989) and Much Ado About Nothing (1993). She won the Academy Award for Best Actress for Howards End (1992), and as screenwriter won the Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay for Sense and Sensibility (1995), in which she also starred.

Later roles include Sybill Trelawney in the Harry Potter films, Nanny McPhee in the films she also wrote, Karen in Love Actually (2003), P. L. Travers in Saving Mr. Banks (2013), and Mrs Potts in the live-action Beauty and the Beast (2017). She wrote and starred in Last Christmas (2019) and played the Baroness in Cruella (2021).

Personal life and activism

Thompson was married to Kenneth Branagh from 1989 to 1995 and has been married to the actor Greg Wise since 2003. She is known for her environmental activism, including work with Greenpeace and support for climate campaigns, and for charitable work with the Helen Bamber Foundation. She was appointed a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 2018.
