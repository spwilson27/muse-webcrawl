Metric tensor (general relativity)

Spacetime curvature described by the metric tensor

In general relativity, the metric tensor (or simply the metric) is the fundamental geometric object that describes the geometry of spacetime. It is a symmetric rank-2 tensor field, usually written gμν, which defines the infinitesimal spacetime interval ds² = gμν dxμ dxν between nearby events. From the metric one can compute proper time, distances, volumes, angles, geodesics, and the curvature of spacetime.

Role in the theory

The metric is the dynamical field of general relativity: it is determined by the distribution of matter and energy through the Einstein field equations, and in turn it governs the motion of matter, because free-falling bodies follow geodesics of the metric. In the weak-field, slow-motion limit, the time-time component of the metric reduces to the Newtonian gravitational potential. The metric has Lorentzian signature, conventionally (−, +, +, +) or (+, −, −, −), distinguishing timelike, spacelike, and null intervals and defining the light-cone structure of spacetime.

Examples

The simplest metric is the flat Minkowski metric of special relativity. The Schwarzschild metric describes spacetime around a spherical, non-rotating mass and gave the classical tests of general relativity—light bending, perihelion precession of Mercury, gravitational redshift—and predicts black holes. The Kerr metric describes rotating masses, and the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker metric describes the expanding universe of cosmology.
