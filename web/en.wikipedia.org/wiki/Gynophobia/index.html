Gynophobia

Gynophobia or gynephobia is an irrational fear of, or aversion to, women. The term comes from the Greek γυνή (gynē, "woman") and φόβος (phobos, "fear"). It is classified as a specific phobia and is related to, but distinct from, misogyny, which is hatred of or contempt for women. Gynophobia should not be confused with gynophobia in the obsolete medical sense, nor with androphobia, the fear of men.

Symptoms and causes

As with other specific phobias, gynophobia can cause intense anxiety or panic in the presence of women, avoidance behaviour, and physiological symptoms such as rapid heartbeat, sweating, and trembling. Proposed causes include traumatic experiences, cultural and religious upbringing, learned associations, and, in older psychoanalytic literature, unresolved childhood conflicts. Historically the term was used loosely and sometimes pejoratively, and it appeared in discussions of celibate religious traditions and of certain historical figures alleged to have avoided women.

Treatment

Treatment generally follows the approaches used for other phobias: cognitive behavioural therapy, gradual exposure (desensitisation), and, where necessary, medication for anxiety symptoms. The condition is rare in clinical practice compared with common phobias, and the term is now used more often in social and cultural commentary than in psychiatric diagnosis.
