Dubstep is a genre of electronic dance music that emerged in South London in the late 1990s and early 2000s. It is generally characterised by sparse, syncopated drum and percussion patterns with bass lines that contain prominent sub-bass frequencies, and a characteristic half-time rhythm at around 140 beats per minute.

Characteristics

Dubstep rhythms are usually syncopated and often shuffled or incorporating tuplets, with drums typically in half time. The genre is known for its emphasis on bass, especially the "wobble" bass produced by modulating a low-frequency oscillator on a filter cutoff. Early dubstep was dark, minimal and spacious, drawing on dub production techniques such as reverb, echo and bass weight; later North American variants ("brostep") emphasised aggressive mid-range bass growls and heavy drops.

History

The sound developed from UK garage and 2-step around the Big Apple Records shop in Croydon and the Forward>> club night, with producers such as El-B, Horsepower Productions, Skream, Benga and Digital Mystikz shaping the style. Kode9's Hyperdub label and Burial's acclaimed albums (notably Untrue, 2007) brought critical attention. Around 2010–2011, artists such as Skrillex carried a harder version of the sound to mainstream success in the United States, and dubstep elements entered pop, metal and film scoring.
