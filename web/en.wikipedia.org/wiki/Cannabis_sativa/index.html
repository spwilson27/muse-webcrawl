Cannabis sativa is an annual herbaceous flowering plant. The species was first classified by Carl Linnaeus in 1753. The plant's leaves are palmately compound with serrated leaflets. It is believed to have originated in a region spanning Central and South Asia, and has been cultivated throughout recorded history for fibre (hemp), seed oil, food, medicine, and as a recreational drug.

Description

C. sativa plants tend to be tall with narrow leaflets and a longer flowering cycle than Cannabis indica. The plants are usually dioecious, with separate male and female individuals. Female flowers are borne in racemes and produce resin rich in cannabinoids, including THC and cannabidiol (CBD).

Uses

Low-THC varieties grown for industrial purposes are known as hemp and used for textiles, paper, biofuel, and construction materials such as hempcrete. Drug-type varieties are used medicinally and recreationally; medical applications with the strongest support include certain seizure disorders and chemotherapy-induced nausea.

Chemistry

More than 500 compounds have been identified in cannabis, including over 100 cannabinoids. The principal psychoactive constituent is Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC); others include CBD, cannabinol (CBN), and numerous terpenes that give strains their distinctive aromas.
