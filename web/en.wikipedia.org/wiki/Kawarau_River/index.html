Kawarau River

Kawarau River
Location: Otago, New Zealand
Length: about 60 km
Source: Lake Wakatipu (Frankton Arm)
Mouth: Clutha River / Mata-Au at Cromwell

The Kawarau River is a river in the Otago region of New Zealand's South Island. It drains Lake Wakatipu from the Frankton Arm near Queenstown and flows east for about 60 km through the steep Kawarau Gorge, past the Gibbston Valley, to join the Clutha River / Mata-Au near Cromwell, where the two rivers now meet in Lake Dunstan.

History

The river takes its name from a Māori chief, and its valley was a travel route between the west coast pounamu sources and the east. In the 1860s the Kawarau was part of the Central Otago gold rush; the miners' workings around the gorge and the remains of settlements such as those near the Roaring Meg are still visible.

Recreation

The Kawarau is famous as the site of the world's first commercial bungy jumping operation, established by A. J. Hackett and Henry van Asch at the historic Kawarau Gorge Suspension Bridge in 1988. The river is also used for jet boating, white-water rafting and sledging, and the surrounding Gibbston area is a noted wine sub-region, especially for pinot noir.
