Taiichi Ohno (February 29, 1912 – May 28, 1990) was a Japanese industrial engineer and businessman. He is considered to be the father of the Toyota Production System, which inspired the Lean Manufacturing movement in the United States. He devised the seven wastes (or muda in Japanese) as part of this system. He wrote several books about the system, including Toyota Production System: Beyond Large-Scale Production.

Early life and career

Ohno was born in Dalian, China, in 1912 and joined the Toyoda Spinning and Weaving Corporation in 1932, after graduating from Nagoya Technical High School. In 1943 he was transferred to the Toyota Motor Company, where he worked as a shop supervisor in the engine manufacturing shop and gradually rose through the ranks to become an executive. After the Second World War he worked on machine-shop improvements, experimenting from 1948 onward with ideas that would become the Toyota Production System.

Main ideas

The Toyota Production System

Ohno is credited, along with Shigeo Shingo, Eiji Toyoda and others, with developing the Toyota Production System (TPS) between 1948 and 1975. Influenced by the writings of Henry Ford and by observing American supermarkets, Ohno argued that mass production was flawed by overproduction, inventory and defects. TPS rests on two pillars: jidoka (automation with a human touch — stopping production when a defect occurs) and just-in-time production, in which parts are produced and delivered only when needed.

Kanban

Ohno's idea of kanban — a visual scheduling system using cards to signal when more parts were needed — was inspired by American supermarkets, where customers take only what they need and shelves are restocked from the back. He first implemented kanban on a small scale in the engine machine shop in the early 1950s and extended it company-wide by the early 1960s.

The seven wastes

Ohno identified seven categories of waste (muda): overproduction, waiting, transportation, overprocessing, inventory, motion, and defects. Eliminating waste — rather than simply raising output — was the driving goal of his system. He was also known for the "Five Whys" technique for root-cause analysis and for the practice of drawing a chalk circle on the factory floor, in which a manager was made to stand and observe a process until its problems became apparent (the "Ohno circle").

Legacy

Ohno became an executive vice president of Toyota in 1975 and retired in 1978. His ideas deeply influenced manufacturing worldwide, especially through the Lean Manufacturing movement that emerged in the West in the 1980s and 1990s. His 1978 book was translated into English in 1988 as Toyota Production System: Beyond Large-Scale Production.
