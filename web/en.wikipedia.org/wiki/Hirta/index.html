Hirta

Hirta
Location: St Kilda, Outer Hebrides, Scotland
Area: 670 ha (1,700 acres)
Highest point: Conachair, 430 m
Evacuated: 29 August 1930

Hirta (Scottish Gaelic: Hiort) is the largest island of the St Kilda archipelago, the most remote part of the British Isles, lying some 40 miles (64 km) west of the Outer Hebrides. It is the only island of the group to have been permanently settled in modern times, and its abandoned village is among Scotland's most evocative historic sites.

History

St Kilda was inhabited for at least two thousand years. The islanders of Hirta lived by a communal subsistence economy based on seabirds — fulmars, gannets and puffins harvested from the cliffs — together with Soay sheep, some cattle and crops grown in the fertile ground behind Village Bay. Rents were paid in kind to the MacLeod lairds. The population peaked at about 180 in the late seventeenth century but declined through emigration, disease (including a devastating smallpox outbreak in 1727) and the pressures of isolation.

Evacuation and after

In 1930, after years of hardship and the death of a young woman from appendicitis, the remaining 36 islanders petitioned for evacuation and were taken to the mainland aboard HMS Harebell on 29 August 1930. The island passed to the National Trust for Scotland in 1957 and is now a dual UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognised for both its culture and its natural importance, with huge seabird colonies, the unique St Kilda wren and field mouse, and a primitive flock of Soay sheep. A small Ministry of Defence presence serving the Hebrides radar range, together with Trust wardens and researchers, maintains a seasonal population, and work parties restore the village cottages, church and the island's hundreds of stone storage huts (cleitean).
