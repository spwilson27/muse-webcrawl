The American Thoracic Society (ATS) is a nonprofit, nonsectarian organization of physicians, researchers, and other health professionals devoted to respiratory, critical care, and sleep medicine. Founded in 1905 as the American Sanatorium Association — a group concerned chiefly with the treatment of tuberculosis — it adopted its present name in 1960 as the field broadened beyond tuberculosis care. Its roughly 16,000 members work in more than 100 countries.

Publications

The ATS publishes three peer-reviewed journals: the American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine (the "Blue Journal"), the American Journal of Respiratory Cell and Molecular Biology (the "Red Journal"), and the Annals of the American Thoracic Society. Together with bodies such as the European Respiratory Society and the Infectious Diseases Society of America, the ATS issues influential clinical practice guidelines and official statements on diseases including asthma, COPD, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, and community-acquired pneumonia.

International Conference

The ATS International Conference, held annually, is one of the largest gatherings of respiratory clinicians and scientists in the world, drawing thousands of abstracts and attendees. The society also funds research through the ATS Research Program and engages in advocacy on clean air, tobacco control, and research funding.
