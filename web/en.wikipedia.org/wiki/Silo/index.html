Silo

A silo (from Ancient Greek σιρός, siros, "pit for holding grain") is a structure for storing bulk materials. Silos are used in agriculture to store fermented feed known as silage, as well as grain. Silos are commonly used for bulk storage of grain, coal, cement, carbon black, woodchips, food products, and sawdust. Three types of silos are in widespread use today: tower silos, bunker silos, and bag silos.

Types

Tower silo

Storage silos are cylindrical structures, typically 10 to 90 feet (3 to 27 m) in diameter and 30 to 275 feet (10 to 84 m) in height. They are built of concrete staves, steel panels, or poured concrete. Tower silos containing silage are usually unloaded from the top by mechanical unloaders.

Bunker silo

Bunker silos are trenches, usually made of concrete walls, that are filled and packed with tractors and loaders. The filled trench is covered with a plastic tarp to make it airtight.

Bag silo

Bag silos are heavy plastic tubes, typically 8 to 12 feet (2.4 to 3.6 m) in diameter and of variable length, in which silage or grain is sealed and compressed by a dedicated machine.

Silo safety

Silos present serious hazards: engulfment by grain, falls, and explosions of combustible dust. Fermenting silage produces nitrogen oxides (silo gas), which cause the potentially fatal "silo filler's disease" if inhaled.

Other uses

The term is also used figuratively — a missile silo houses an intercontinental ballistic missile, and in computing, an "information silo" is an isolated system unable to exchange data with others.
