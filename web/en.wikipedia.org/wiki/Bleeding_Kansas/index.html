Bleeding Kansas

[Image placeholder: Bleeding Kansas]

Bleeding Kansas, also known as Bloody Kansas or the Border War, was a series of violent civil confrontations in the Kansas Territory and western Missouri between roughly 1854 and 1859. The conflict pitted pro-slavery "Border Ruffians" against anti-slavery "Free-Staters" over whether Kansas would enter the Union as a free or slave state, and it became a dress rehearsal for the American Civil War.

Background

The Kansas–Nebraska Act of 1854 repealed the Missouri Compromise and decreed that settlers in the new territories would decide the slavery question by popular sovereignty. Pro- and anti-slavery settlers rushed into Kansas, and fraudulent elections, rival territorial governments at Lecompton and Topeka, and disputed constitutions followed.

Violence

Key episodes included the sacking of Lawrence by pro-slavery forces in May 1856, John Brown's retaliatory Pottawatomie massacre days later, the Battle of Black Jack, and the Marais des Cygnes massacre of 1858. Guerrilla warfare between Kansas Jayhawkers and Missouri Bushwhackers continued along the border. Estimates of deaths directly attributable to the conflict range from about 56 to 200.

Legacy

The violence in Kansas, widely reported in the national press, helped destroy the Whig Party, spur the creation of the Republican Party, and convince many Americans that compromise over slavery was failing. Kansas entered the Union as a free state on 29 January 1861.
