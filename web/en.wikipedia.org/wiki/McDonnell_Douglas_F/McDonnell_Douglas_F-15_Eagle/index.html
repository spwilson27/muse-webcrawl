The McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle is an American twin-engine, all-weather tactical fighter aircraft designed by McDonnell Douglas (now part of Boeing). Following reviews of proposals, the United States Air Force (USAF) selected McDonnell Douglas's design in 1969 to meet the service's need for a dedicated air superiority fighter. The Eagle first flew in July 1972 and entered service in 1976.

Design

The F-15 was designed under the motto "not a pound for air-to-ground" — a pure air-superiority fighter with a large wing, low wing loading, a thrust-to-weight ratio greater than one, and a powerful APG-63 pulse-Doppler radar able to look down and shoot down low-flying targets. It is armed with an internal M61 Vulcan cannon and AIM-7 Sparrow, AIM-9 Sidewinder and later AIM-120 AMRAAM missiles. The two-seat F-15E Strike Eagle derivative, first flown in 1986, added a deep-strike role.

Operational history

The F-15 has achieved over 100 air-to-air victories with no losses in air combat, in service with the USAF, the Israeli Air Force (in the 1982 Lebanon War and beyond), and the Royal Saudi Air Force in the 1991 Gulf War. Upgraded variants continue in production, including the F-15EX Eagle II for the USAF.
