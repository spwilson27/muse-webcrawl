The McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle is an American twin-engine, all-weather tactical fighter aircraft designed for air superiority. Following reviews of air combat over Vietnam, the U.S. Air Force selected McDonnell Douglas's design in 1969, and the F-15 first flew in 1972. It is among the most successful modern fighters, with over 100 victories and no losses in air-to-air combat.

Design

The F-15 was designed around the energy–maneuverability theory of Major John Boyd, with a large wing, low wing loading, and a thrust-to-weight ratio greater than one, allowing it to accelerate while climbing vertically. It is powered by two Pratt & Whitney F100 turbofan engines. Armament includes a 20 mm M61 Vulcan cannon and AIM-7 Sparrow, AIM-9 Sidewinder, and AIM-120 AMRAAM air-to-air missiles carried on fuselage and wing stations.

Operational history

The single-seat F-15A and two-seat F-15B entered service in 1976, followed by the improved F-15C/D with internal fuel and conformal fuel tank provisions. Israeli F-15s scored the type's first combat victories in 1979. In the 1991 Gulf War, USAF F-15Cs shot down 34 Iraqi aircraft. The two-seat F-15E Strike Eagle, a dual-role strike derivative, entered service in 1989.

Variants and successors

Japan license-produced the F-15J. The F-15E led to the F-15S, F-15K, F-15SG, F-15SA, and F-15QA variants. Boeing's latest version, the F-15EX Eagle II, was ordered by the USAF beginning in 2020, keeping the Eagle in production more than half a century after its first flight.
