The Boeing F/A-18E and F/A-18F Super Hornet are a series of American supersonic twin-engine, carrier-capable, multirole fighter aircraft derived from the McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet. The single-seat F/A-18E and tandem-seat F/A-18F are larger and more advanced derivatives; the Super Hornet's design lineage traces back through the Hornet to the Northrop YF-17 light fighter prototype.

Design and development

The Super Hornet was ordered by the US Navy in 1992 to replace the Grumman F-14 Tomcat and supplement the original Hornet. It is roughly 20% larger than the F/A-18C/D, with a lengthened fuselage, enlarged wings and leading-edge extensions, and two additional weapons stations. It is powered by two General Electric F414-GE-400 turbofans producing 22,000 lbf (98 kN) of thrust each with afterburner. Enlarged rectangular intakes and structural radar-signature-reduction measures distinguish it from its predecessor.

Operational history

The Super Hornet entered service with the US Navy in 1999 and flew its first combat missions in 2002 during Operation Southern Watch and Operation Iraqi Freedom. It serves as the backbone of the Navy's carrier air wings, performing air superiority, strike, close air support, reconnaissance and aerial refuelling ("buddy store") missions. The electronic-warfare variant, the EA-18G Growler, replaced the EA-6B Prowler. Australia operates 24 F/A-18Fs, and Kuwait ordered 28 aircraft. Block III upgrades add a reduced-signature conformal fuel tank option, an advanced cockpit system and network upgrades, with service life extended to 10,000 flight hours.
