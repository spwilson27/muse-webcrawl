McDonnell Douglas Corporation was a major American aerospace manufacturing corporation and defense contractor, formed by the merger of McDonnell Aircraft and the Douglas Aircraft Company in 1967. It produced a number of well-known commercial and military aircraft, including the DC-10 airliner and the F-15 Eagle fighter. McDonnell Douglas merged with Boeing in 1997.

History

James Smith McDonnell founded McDonnell Aircraft Corporation in 1939 in St. Louis, Missouri. The company became a major supplier of fighter aircraft to the U.S. Navy and Air Force, producing the FH-1 Phantom, F2H Banshee, F3H Demon, F-101 Voodoo, and the famous F-4 Phantom II.

Donald Wills Douglas founded the Douglas Aircraft Company in 1921 in Santa Monica, California. Douglas created the DC series of commercial airliners, including the revolutionary DC-3, and military transports such as the C-47 Skytrain.

By the mid-1960s Douglas was in financial difficulty, burdened by the costs of the DC-8 and DC-9 programs and wartime production. The two companies merged on April 28, 1967, forming McDonnell Douglas, with James McDonnell as chairman and David S. Lewis as president.

Products

Commercial airliners included the DC-10 wide-body trijet, the MD-80 series of narrow-body jets, the MD-11, and the MD-90. Military aircraft included the F-15 Eagle, F/A-18 Hornet (with Northrop), AV-8B Harrier II, C-17 Globemaster III airlifter, KC-10 Extender tanker, and the AH-64 Apache helicopter (through the acquisition of Hughes Helicopters in 1984). The company also built the Skylab space station and parts of the Delta rocket family.

Merger with Boeing

After the Cold War, consolidation swept the defense industry. McDonnell Douglas struggled with commercial competition from Airbus and Boeing and lost the Joint Strike Fighter competition in 1996. On December 15, 1996, Boeing announced it would acquire McDonnell Douglas in a $13 billion stock swap; the merger was completed on August 1, 1997, ending McDonnell Douglas as an independent company. Its St. Louis facilities remain a major Boeing defense site, continuing production of the F-15 and F/A-18 families.
