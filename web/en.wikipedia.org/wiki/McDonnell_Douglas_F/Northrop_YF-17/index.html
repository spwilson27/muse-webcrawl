The Northrop YF-17 (nicknamed "Cobra") was a prototype lightweight fighter aircraft designed by Northrop Aviation for the United States Air Force's Lightweight Fighter (LWF) technology evaluation program. The LWF was initiated because many in the fighter community believed that aircraft like the F-15 Eagle had become too large and expensive.

Design and competition

The YF-17 was a twin-engine design derived from Northrop's P-530 Cobra project, featuring prominent leading-edge extensions (LERX) that generated vortex lift at high angles of attack, giving it exceptional manoeuvrability. Two prototypes flew in 1974 against the General Dynamics YF-16. In January 1975 the Air Force selected the YF-16 — which became the F-16 Fighting Falcon — for production.

Development into the Hornet

The story did not end there. The US Navy, required to choose a derivative of the LWF winner for its new carrier fighter, instead selected the YF-17 design, which Northrop developed with McDonnell Douglas into the enlarged, twin-engine F/A-18 Hornet — first flown in 1978 and the direct ancestor of the Super Hornet. The two YF-17 prototypes survive in museums.
