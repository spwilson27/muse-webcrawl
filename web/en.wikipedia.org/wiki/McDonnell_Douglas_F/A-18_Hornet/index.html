The McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet is an all-weather supersonic, twin-engine, carrier-capable, multirole combat aircraft designed as both a fighter and attack aircraft (hence the F/A designation). Designed by McDonnell Douglas and Northrop, it was derived from the Northrop YF-17 lightweight fighter that lost the Air Force competition to the F-16.

Design and service

The Hornet was the US Navy's first strike fighter able to perform both air-to-air and air-to-ground missions interchangeably, replacing the F-4 Phantom II and A-7 Corsair II. It features leading-edge extensions for high angle-of-attack performance, twin General Electric F404 engines and a digital fly-by-wire system. It entered fleet service in 1983 and first saw combat in 1986 against Libya (Operation El Dorado Canyon), then extensively in the 1991 Gulf War, the Balkans, Afghanistan and Iraq. C/D models progressively replaced the original A/B models with improved radar, night-attack capability and weapons.

Operators and successors

The Hornet has been flown by Canada, Australia, Spain, Switzerland, Finland, Kuwait and Malaysia, and was the mount of the US Navy's Blue Angels flight demonstration team from 1986 to 2020. The larger, extensively redesigned F/A-18E/F Super Hornet entered service in 1999 and remains the US Navy's principal carrier strike fighter; the legacy Hornet left US Navy service in 2019 but continued with the Marine Corps and export operators into the 2020s.
