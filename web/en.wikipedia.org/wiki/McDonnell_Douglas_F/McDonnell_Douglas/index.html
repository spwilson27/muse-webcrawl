McDonnell Douglas Corporation was a major American aerospace manufacturing corporation and defence contractor, formed by the merger of McDonnell Aircraft and the Douglas Aircraft Company in 1967. Between then and its own merger with Boeing in 1997, it produced a number of well-known commercial and military aircraft, such as the DC-10 and the MD-80 airliners, as well as the F-15 Eagle and F/A-18 Hornet multirole fighters.

History

James Smith McDonnell founded his aircraft company in 1939 in St. Louis; Donald Douglas founded his in 1920 in Santa Monica, California, producing the famous DC-3 and DC-8. Financial difficulties at Douglas led to the 1967 merger. The combined company built the DC-10 wide-body trijet, the MD-80/MD-90 series, and the MD-11, and was a leading military contractor with the F-4 Phantom II (inherited from McDonnell), F-15 Eagle, F/A-18 Hornet, AV-8B Harrier II, AH-64 Apache helicopter and Tomahawk cruise missile. It also built the Skylab space station and components of the Space Shuttle and Delta rockets.

Merger with Boeing

Weakening commercial sales against Boeing and Airbus led McDonnell Douglas to agree to a merger in December 1996; the $13 billion stock swap was completed on 1 August 1997, creating the world's largest aerospace company at the time. The McDonnell Douglas name disappeared, though its St. Louis operations continue as a major Boeing defence site.
