Erich Priebke

Erich Priebke
Born: 29 July 1913, Hennigsdorf, Germany
Died: 11 October 2013 (aged 100), Rome, Italy
Allegiance: Nazi Germany
Service: Schutzstaffel (SS); Gestapo
Rank: Hauptsturmführer
Known for: Ardeatine massacre; war crimes conviction

Erich Priebke (29 July 1913 – 11 October 2013) was a German mid-level SS commander in the Gestapo who participated in the Ardeatine massacre in Rome on 24 March 1944, in which 335 Italian civilians and political prisoners were murdered in reprisal for a partisan attack. After the war he fled to Argentina, where he lived for nearly fifty years before his exposure in 1994 led to extradition to Italy, trial, and a life sentence, which he served under house arrest in Rome until his death at age 100.

Early life and wartime service

Priebke joined the Nazi Party and the SS in the 1930s and served in the Gestapo. Posted to Rome, he worked in the SD/Gestapo headquarters on Via Tasso under Herbert Kappler. On 23 March 1944, Italian partisans killed 33 German military policemen in the Via Rasella attack; Hitler ordered ten Italians shot for each German. Priebke helped compile the lists of victims and took part in the executions at the Ardeatine Caves the next day, during which 335 people — including 75 Jews — were shot.

Escape to Argentina

After the war Priebke escaped from a British POW camp and, in 1948, reached Argentina along one of the "ratlines" aided by Bishop Alois Hudal. He settled in Bariloche, where he ran a delicatessen and was a figure in the local German-Argentine community, living under his own name.

Exposure, extradition and trial

In 1994 the American television journalist Sam Donaldson confronted Priebke in Bariloche for ABC News; Priebke admitted his role on camera. Argentina arrested him and, after legal proceedings, extradited him to Italy in 1995. A first trial in 1996 ended in controversy when the court ruled the crimes had partly lapsed, provoking public outrage and an appeal; a retrial in 1997–98 convicted Priebke and Karl Hass, imposing life imprisonment, with Priebke's sentence served as house arrest because of his age. He never apologised and maintained he had only followed orders. His death in 2013 sparked disputes over burial; after protests in Italy and Argentina, he was buried secretly in Italy.
