Overview

The Aswan Dam is two dams at Aswan in southern Egypt: the older Aswan Low Dam (completed by the British in 1902 and twice heightened) and the Aswan High Dam (Sadd el-Ali), completed in 1970. The High Dam — an embankment dam 111 m high and nearly 4 km long — ended the Nile's annual flood cycle, created Lake Nasser (one of the world's largest artificial lakes), supplies irrigation water for perennial cropping and generates a significant share of Egypt's electricity (2.1 GW installed).

History and effects

After the US and UK withdrew financing in 1956, Egypt's President Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal — precipitating the Suez Crisis — and the USSR financed and helped build the High Dam. Its costs included the relocation of over 100,000 Nubians, the loss of Nile silt to the delta fields (offset by fertiliser), delta erosion, and the rescue of Nubian monuments — Abu Simbel and Philae were cut apart and moved in UNESCO's famous campaign (1960s).
