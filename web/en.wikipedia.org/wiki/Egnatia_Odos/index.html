Egnatia Odos

Egnatia Odos or Egnatia Motorway (Greek: Εγνατία Οδός) is the Greek part of the European route E90. It is a motorway in northern Greece that runs from the western port of Igoumenitsa to the eastern Greek–Turkish border at Kipoi. It is 670 km (416 mi) long.

Route and construction

The motorway crosses the mountainous regions of Epirus, Macedonia, and Thrace, passing near Ioannina, Grevena, Kozani, Veria, Thessaloniki, Kavala, Xanthi, Komotini, and Alexandroupolis. Its construction involved some of the most difficult terrain of any European road project, requiring dozens of tunnels — including the Driskos tunnel — and hundreds of bridges and viaducts across the Pindos mountains and the valleys of northern Greece.

History

The modern motorway broadly follows the route of the ancient Roman Via Egnatia, which connected Dyrrachium on the Adriatic with Byzantium (Constantinople). Construction of the modern road began in the 1990s, was managed by the state company Egnatia Odos S.A., and was largely completed in 2009 with co-financing from the European Union. It forms part of the Trans-European Transport Networks and greatly reduced travel times across northern Greece, linking the Ionian and Aegean coasts.
