Hòa Hảo

Hòa Hảo
Type: Buddhist religious movement
Founded: 1939, Hòa Hảo village, An Giang, Vietnam
Founder: Huỳnh Phú Sổ
Followers: Concentrated in the Mekong Delta

Hòa Hảo is a Vietnamese religious movement founded in 1939 by Huỳnh Phú Sổ in the village of Hòa Hảo in An Giang Province. It is a reformed form of Buddhism emphasising simple, home-based practice without elaborate temples or clergy, rooted in the earlier Bửu Sơn Kỳ Hương tradition.

Teachings

Hòa Hảo teaching stresses ancestor veneration, gratitude to the nation and the Three Jewels, plain altars with a brown cloth, and ethical living. It rejects costly rituals, spirit mediums, and superstition, making it popular among Mekong Delta peasants.

History

The movement grew rapidly and became a political-military force in southern Vietnam during the 1940s–1950s; Huỳnh Phú Sổ disappeared in 1947 amid conflict with the Việt Minh. Hòa Hảo communities played a notable role in the First Indochina War and the Vietnam War era. Today it is officially recognised in Vietnam, with millions of adherents claimed.
