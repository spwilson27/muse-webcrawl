Hip hop music

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A DJ's hands on two turntables at a Bronx-style block party, in a still evoking hip hop's founding scenes of the late 1970s]

Hip hop music (also called rap music) is a music genre and cultural movement developed in the South Bronx, New York City, in the 1970s, credited principally to DJ Kool Herc, whose 1973 parties at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue pioneered the breakbeat DJ technique. As defined by Afrika Bambaataa, hip hop culture comprises MCing (rapping), DJing, breakdancing and graffiti art — to which later formulations add knowledge as a fifth element.

From block parties hip hop became, by the streaming era, the most-consumed genre in the United States and a global commercial and cultural empire: the Sugarhill Gang's 'Rapper's Delight' (1979), Grandmaster Flash's 'The Message' (1982), Run-DMC's rock crossover, Public Enemy and the Native Tongues, gangsta rap (N.W.A, Tupac, the Notorious B.I.G.), the East–West rivalry of the 1990s, and the dominance of Jay-Z, Eminem, Kanye West, Drake and Kendrick Lamar — whose 2018 Pulitzer Prize was a first for a popular musician — mark its standard history. Song titles cited here only.

Elements and evolution

The DJ (Herc, Grandmaster Flash's quick-mix theory, Grand Wizard Theodore's scratching) built loops from two copies of the same record; the MC evolved from host to lead artist; sampling — from rare-groove archaeology to legal battleground after 1991's Grand Upright ruling — gave the music its collage method. Regional sounds multiplied: Miami bass, Houston's chopped and screwed, Chicago drill and its London descendant UK drill, Atlanta trap, whose hi-hats and 808s dominate world pop.

Cultural reach

Hip hop remade fashion, language, advertising and politics; its golden jubilee in 2023 was marked by museum exhibitions and stadium retrospectives. It is at once the last folk form of the American city and the flagship product of the global music industry — a contradiction its best artists have always rapped about.
