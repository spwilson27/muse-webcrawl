The Severe style is a period in ancient Greek sculpture, roughly from 490/480 to 450 BC, marking the transition between Archaic and Classical sculpture. The Severe style replaced the elaborate smiles and patterned detail of Archaic art with a simpler, more naturalistic and restrained treatment of form and expression.

Characteristics

Sculpture in the Severe style abandoned the "Archaic smile" in favour of sober, pensive expressions; hair was rendered in simpler masses, and drapery in heavier, more plausible folds. Bronze became a favoured medium for freestanding statues, permitting freer poses. Famous examples include the Kritios Boy (c. 480 BC), among the earliest figures shown in true contrapposto; the Artemision Bronze of Zeus (or Poseidon); the Charioteer of Delphi; and the pedimental sculptures of the Temple of Zeus at Olympia, attributed to the so-called Olympia Master.

Context

The style coincided with the Persian Wars and the early decades of Athenian democracy, and its sober naturalism is often read as an expression of the civic ideals of the young Classical city-state.
