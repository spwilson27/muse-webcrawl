Petrophysics

Petrophysics (from the Greek petra, "rock", and physis, "nature") is the study of the physical and chemical properties of rocks and their interactions with fluids, particularly in the context of hydrocarbon exploration and production. Petrophysicists characterise subsurface reservoirs to determine how much oil and gas is present and how readily it can be produced.

Key properties

The fundamental reservoir properties are porosity (the fraction of rock volume that is pore space), permeability (the ease with which fluids flow through the connected pores, measured in darcies or millidarcies), and fluid saturation (the fraction of pore space occupied by water, oil, or gas). Other studied properties include lithology and mineralogy, pore geometry and capillary pressure, grain size and sorting, and the electrical, acoustic, and nuclear responses of the rock.

Methods

Data come from core samples analysed in the laboratory and from well logging — measurements made downhole with gamma ray, resistivity, density, neutron, and sonic tools — interpreted using relationships such as Archie's law relating resistivity to water saturation. Petrophysical evaluation is the foundation of reserve estimation, well completion design, and reservoir modelling, and also applies to groundwater, geothermal energy, and carbon storage.
