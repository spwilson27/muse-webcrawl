Francesco I Sforza (23 July 1401 – 8 March 1466) was an Italian condottiere and the founder of the Sforza dynasty in Milan. One of the most successful mercenary captains of the Italian Renaissance, he rose from common birth to become Duke of Milan, ruling from 1450 until his death and bringing stability to the duchy after the collapse of the Ambrosian Republic. Machiavelli cited him as an example of a prince who rose by virtù and ability.

Early life and career

Francesco was the illegitimate-or-acknowledged son of Muzio Attendolo, nicknamed "Sforza" ("the Forcer"), founder of a renowned company of mercenaries. Raised in the camp, he inherited his father's company after Muzio drowned in 1424 and served, in turn, Milan, Florence, Naples and the Papal States during the Wars in Lombardy, accumulating lands in the March of Ancona and a reputation for loyalty unusual among condottieri.

Marriage and the conquest of Milan

In 1441 he married Bianca Maria Visconti, the only (illegitimate) daughter of Filippo Maria Visconti, Duke of Milan, receiving Cremona and Pontremoli as dowry. When Filippo Maria died in 1447 without a male heir, Milan proclaimed the Golden Ambrosian Republic. Hired as its captain-general, Sforza instead conquered the cities of the duchy one by one and starved Milan itself into surrender, entering the city on 25 March 1450 and being acclaimed duke. His claim rested on his marriage to Bianca Maria.

Rule

As duke, Francesco rebuilt Milan's finances, administration and defences, reconstructing the Castello Sforzesco and founding the Ospedale Maggiore. In foreign policy he was a chief architect of the Peace of Lodi (1454) and the Italic League, which balanced Milan, Florence and Naples against Venice and the Papacy and gave Italy two generations of relative peace. His alliance with Cosimo de' Medici's Florence was the axis of the settlement. He died in 1466 and was succeeded by his son Galeazzo Maria Sforza.
