Sōhei

Sōhei (僧兵, "monk soldiers") were Buddhist warrior monks of both medieval and feudal Japan. At certain points in history they held considerable power, obliging the imperial and military governments to collaborate.

History

The rise of the sōhei began in the late Heian period, when great temples such as Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei and Mii-dera armed their monks to defend temple lands and to intimidate the imperial court. The monks famously carried a portable shrine (mikoshi) into the capital to press their demands.

Weapons and appearance

Sōhei are traditionally depicted wearing robes over armour, with a white cowl over the head, and wielding the naginata, a polearm with a curved blade. The most famous sōhei is perhaps Saitō Musashibō Benkei, the companion of Minamoto no Yoshitsune.

Decline

The power of the warrior monks was broken in the late 16th century: Oda Nobunaga destroyed Enryaku-ji in 1571, and Toyotomi Hideyoshi's sword hunt disarmed religious institutions across Japan.
