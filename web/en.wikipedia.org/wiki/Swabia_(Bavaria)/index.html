Swabia (German: Schwaben) is one of the seven administrative regions (Regierungsbezirke) of the Free State of Bavaria, Germany. Lying in the southwest of the state, it comprises the Bavarian part of the historic region of Swabia, whose larger share lies in neighbouring Baden-Württemberg. Its capital and largest city is Augsburg, Germany's third-oldest city, founded as a Roman camp in 15 BC.

Geography

The region stretches from the Ries crater basin in the north, past the Lech and Danube plains, to the foothills of the Alps and Lake Constance (Bodensee) in the south, including the Allgäu sub-region famous for its alpine scenery, dairy farming and winter sports. Administrative districts include the cities of Augsburg, Kempten and Memmingen and rural districts such as Oberallgäu, Ostallgäu, Günzburg, Neu-Ulm and Lindau.

Culture and identity

Bavarian Swabians speak Swabian (Schwäbisch), an Alemannic dialect distinct from the Bavarian dialects of the rest of the state, and regional identity remains strong: locals are "Swabians in Bavaria". Historically the area was a patchwork of imperial free cities, ecclesiastical states and the margraviate of Burgau before most of it fell to Bavaria in the mediatisation of 1803–1806. The region is known for Swabian cuisine such as Spätzle, for Fugger-era Augsburg's Renaissance heritage, and for Neuschwanstein Castle, which stands in its Ostallgäu district near Füssen and draws millions of visitors.
