Algorithmic transparency

FieldTechnology ethics; public policy
Related conceptsExplainable AI; algorithmic accountability; algorithmic bias
ApplicationsCredit scoring, hiring, criminal justice, content ranking

Algorithmic transparency is the principle that the factors influencing the decisions made by algorithms, and the processes by which those algorithms operate, should be visible and understandable to the people affected by them, to regulators, and to the public.

Rationale

As automated decision systems are used in lending, employment, welfare, policing, and online platforms, opaque "black box" models can conceal errors, embed bias, and make it impossible for individuals to contest decisions. Transparency is proposed as a remedy through measures such as disclosure of decision logic, data documentation, impact assessments, auditability, and explanations of individual outcomes.

Debates

Discussions of transparency involve tensions with trade secrets, privacy, security, and the gaming of disclosed rules, as well as the technical difficulty of explaining complex machine learning models. Transparency is often discussed together with accountability, fairness, and the right to an explanation in data protection law.
