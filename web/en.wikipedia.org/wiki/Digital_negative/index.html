Digital negative

Digital Negative (DNG) is Adobe's open, lossless raw image format, introduced in 2004 as a standardised alternative to the many proprietary raw formats produced by digital cameras. The name also refers, more generally, to a digital master file treated as the equivalent of a photographic film negative.

Features

DNG is based on the TIFF/EP standard and can embed metadata, previews, and camera profiles in a single file. Adobe provides a free DNG Converter for translating proprietary raw files, and some camera makers (including Leica, Ricoh, and Pentax) have adopted DNG as a native capture format.

Reception

Supporters argue a public, documented format protects long-term access to raw photographs, as a film negative does for analogue images. Some major camera manufacturers have continued to prefer their own formats, limiting DNG's universality.
