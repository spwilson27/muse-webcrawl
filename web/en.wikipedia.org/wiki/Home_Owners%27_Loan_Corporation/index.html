Home Owners' Loan Corporation

The Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) was a United States government-sponsored corporation created in 1933 under President Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal to refinance home mortgages in default and prevent foreclosures during the Great Depression.

Operations

Between 1933 and 1936 the HOLC refinanced roughly one million mortgages — about a fifth of all mortgaged urban homes in the United States — exchanging long-term, low-interest loans for short-term high-interest ones that homeowners could not service. It is generally credited with stabilising the housing market and saving many families from foreclosure, and it ultimately turned a small profit for the government before being liquidated in 1951.

Redlining

The HOLC is also notorious for its "residential security maps", which graded neighbourhoods from A ("Best") to D ("Hazardous"). Historians and economists have argued that these maps institutionalised and spread the practice known as redlining, in which credit was withheld from neighbourhoods — disproportionately those with Black and immigrant residents — shaping patterns of segregation and disinvestment for decades.
