James A. Champy (born June 8, 1942) is an American business consultant, author, and organizational theorist. He is best known as the co-author, with Michael Hammer, of Reengineering the Corporation: A Manifesto for Business Revolution (1993), one of the best-selling management books of the 1990s.

Career

Champy graduated from Boston College in 1963 and earned a law degree from Boston College Law School in 1968. In 1975 he co-founded CSC Index, the consulting firm that pioneered business process reengineering, and he served as its chairman and chief executive. He later headed the consulting practice of Perot Systems and served as chairman emeritus of that practice.

Reengineering

In Reengineering the Corporation, Champy and Hammer argued that companies should radically redesign their core processes — rather than automate existing ones — to achieve dramatic improvements in cost, quality, service, and speed. The book sold millions of copies and made "reengineering" a defining management concept of the decade, though the approach was also criticized for being associated with mass layoffs.

Later work

Champy's subsequent books include Reengineering Management (1995), The Arc of Ambition (2000, with Nitin Nohria), X-Engineering the Corporation (2002), Outsmart! (2008), and Delivering Results (2010), which extended his thinking to growth strategies, digital-era process design, and sustained performance.
