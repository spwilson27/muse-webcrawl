Bobby Dodd

Robert Lee "Bobby" Dodd (November 11, 1908 – June 21, 1988) was an American college football player and coach. He served as the head football coach at Georgia Tech from 1945 to 1966, compiling a career record of 165–64–8. Dodd also served as Georgia Tech's athletic director from 1950 to 1976.

Playing career

As a player at the University of Tennessee under Robert Neyland, Dodd played quarterback, tailback and punter, earning All-American honours in 1930.

Coaching career

Dodd's Georgia Tech teams won the 1951 Orange Bowl, 1952 Sugar Bowl and a share of the 1952 national championship, part of a 31-game unbeaten streak. He was known for his emphasis on player welfare, limited-contact practices and strong graduation rates. Georgia Tech's stadium was renamed Bobby Dodd Stadium in his honour in 1988, and the Bobby Dodd Coach of the Year Award is presented annually.

Legacy

Dodd was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame as a player in 1959 and as a coach in 1993, one of only a few people inducted in both capacities.
