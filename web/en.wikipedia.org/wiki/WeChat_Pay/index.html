WeChat Pay (Chinese: 微信支付) is a mobile payment and digital wallet service operated by Tencent and integrated into the WeChat messaging application. Launched in 2013, it allows users to make payments, transfer money, pay bills, and receive funds using a bank card linked to their WeChat account. Together with Alipay, it dominates China's mobile payments market, where QR-code payments have largely displaced cash in urban areas.

History

WeChat Pay's growth was catalysed by the digital "red envelope" feature launched for the 2014 Lunar New Year, which let users send small cash gifts to friends and groups; the promotion, amplified by a televised Spring Festival Gala partnership in 2015, linked hundreds of millions of bank cards to the platform in a short period. The service is operated through Tenpay, Tencent's licensed payment arm.

Functionality

Payments can be made by scanning a merchant's QR code, presenting a code for the merchant to scan, through in-app mini-programs, or via official accounts. WeChat Pay supports peer-to-peer transfers, utility and transport payments, and in-store payment across millions of merchants. Since 2019, WeChat Pay has expanded acceptance abroad so Chinese tourists can pay overseas merchants in renminbi, and it has gradually opened to foreign-issued cards for use within China.

Regulation

WeChat Pay operates under licences from the People's Bank of China. Since 2018, third-party payment platforms in China have been required to route transactions through the centralised NetsUnion clearing platform and hold customer reserves at the central bank. In 2021–2023, Chinese "bar code payment" rules and platform antitrust measures sought to increase interoperability between WeChat Pay and rival systems.
