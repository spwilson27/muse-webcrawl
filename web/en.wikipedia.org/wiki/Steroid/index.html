Steroid

[Image: The steroid nucleus: three six-membered rings and one five-membered ring (gonane)]

Overview

A steroid is an organic compound with four fused rings: three cyclohexane rings and one cyclopentane ring (the gonane or cyclopenta[a]phenanthrene nucleus). Steroids vary by the functional groups attached to this core and by the oxidation state of the rings. Hundreds occur in plants, animals and fungi.

Classes and roles

Biologically essential steroids include cholesterol (the membrane component and biosynthetic precursor), the steroid hormones — glucocorticoids (cortisol), mineralocorticoids (aldosterone), and the sex steroids (androgens such as testosterone, oestrogens, progesterone) — bile acids and vitamin D derivatives. "Steroids" in medicine usually means corticosteroid drugs (anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive); in sport the word refers to anabolic-androgenic steroids, whose misuse is banned. Plant sterols and cardiac glycosides (e.g. digoxin) are further classes.
