Music of Sweden

The music of Sweden shares roots with the neighbouring Nordic countries and ranges from a rich folk tradition to one of the world's most successful popular music industries. Sweden is one of the largest exporters of music relative to its population, producing internationally successful acts such as ABBA, Roxette, Ace of Base, Robyn, Avicii and Zara Larsson, and songwriters and producers such as Max Martin, who has written or co-written more than twenty Billboard Hot 100 number-one singles.

A nyckelharpa, the traditional Swedish keyed fiddle.

Folk music

Swedish folk music centres on dance tunes — the polska, hambo, schottische and waltz — played chiefly on the fiddle. The nyckelharpa (keyed fiddle) is the instrument most closely associated with Sweden, especially the Uppland tradition. Fiddlers (spelmän) gather at spelmansstämmor (fiddlers' meetings), the largest being at Bingsjö and Delsbo. Vocal traditions include kulning, the high-pitched herding calls of women in the mountain pastures, and medieval ballads. Carl Michael Bellman, the 18th-century troubadour of Stockholm tavern life, remains a central figure of Swedish song.

Classical music

Sweden's classical composers include Franz Berwald, Wilhelm Stenhammar, Hugo Alfvén, Kurt Atterberg and Allan Pettersson. The country supports major orchestras such as the Royal Stockholm Philharmonic and the Gothenburg Symphony, and opera houses in Stockholm, Gothenburg and Malmö. Noted performers include the sopranos Jenny Lind, Birgit Nilsson and Nina Stemme.

Popular music

Sweden's modern pop success began with ABBA, winners of the 1974 Eurovision Song Contest with "Waterloo", who became one of the best-selling acts in music history. The national Eurovision selection, Melodifestivalen, is one of Sweden's most-watched television programmes, and Sweden is among the most successful Eurovision countries, with wins including ABBA, Herreys, Carola, Charlotte Nilsson, Loreen (twice) and Måns Zelmerlöw. Roxette and Ace of Base achieved global success in the late 1980s and 1990s.

Behind the performers, Stockholm developed a songwriter–producer industry: Denniz Pop's Cheiron Studios launched the careers of Max Martin and others who shaped global pop from the late 1990s onward, working with Britney Spears, Backstreet Boys, Katy Perry, Taylor Swift and The Weeknd. Sweden is also internationally prominent in heavy metal (In Flames, Opeth, Ghost, Meshuggah, Europe, Yngwie Malmsteen), electronic dance music (Avicii, Swedish House Mafia, Eric Prydz, Alesso), indie pop (The Cardigans, Lykke Li, First Aid Kit) and hip hop. The streaming service Spotify, founded in Stockholm in 2006, further embeds Sweden in the global music economy.
