Pickwick Landing Dam is a hydroelectric dam on the Tennessee River in Hardin County, in the southwestern part of the U.S. state of Tennessee. Named for a 19th-century riverboat landing and the Dickens Reading Club that met there (the source of the name "Pickwick"), the dam was built in the 1930s by the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) as part of its programme to improve navigation, provide flood control, and generate electricity on the Tennessee River. The dam impounds Pickwick Lake, which extends into Alabama and Mississippi.


Pickwick Landing Dam, showing the powerhouse, spillway and lock.

Background and construction

The Tennessee River's "Muscle Shoals" rapids in Alabama had long obstructed navigation. Pickwick Landing was identified in early 20th-century river surveys as a strategic dam site below the shoals. Construction began in 1934 and the dam was completed in 1938; its navigation lock opened in 1937. The original lock, 110 by 600 feet, lifts and lowers barges about 63 feet between Pickwick Lake and Kentucky Lake downstream. A much larger second lock — 110 by 1,000 feet, among the largest in the TVA system — was completed in 1984 to handle modern barge tows.

Design and capacity

The dam is about 113 feet high and more than 7,700 feet long, combining a concrete gravity section (powerhouse and spillway) with long earth embankments. The powerhouse originally housed six generating units, with a combined capacity of roughly 229 megawatts after later upgrades. The spillway is equipped with radial gates for flood discharge. Pickwick Lake covers about 43,100 acres with some 490 miles of shoreline, and is a popular recreation area; Pickwick Landing State Park occupies land below the dam on the Tennessee side.
