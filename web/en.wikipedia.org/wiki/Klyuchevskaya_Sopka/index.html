Klyuchevskaya Sopka

Klyuchevskaya Sopka
Elevation: about 4,750 m (15,584 ft)
Location: Kamchatka Peninsula, Russia
Type: Stratovolcano

Klyuchevskaya Sopka is the highest active volcano on the Kamchatka Peninsula in the Russian Far East and the highest active volcano in Eurasia. It is a large, symmetrical stratovolcano in the Klyuchevskaya group of volcanoes, near the east coast of Kamchatka.

Geology and eruptions

The volcano formed roughly 6,000 years ago. Its frequent eruptions, recorded since the late 17th century, are typically explosive, with lava flows, ash plumes and lahars. It stands close to the neighbouring volcanoes Bezymianny and Kamen. Eruptions in the 21st century have repeatedly disrupted air traffic across the North Pacific.

Climbing

The mountain is a popular but dangerous objective for mountaineers. In 1752... ascents require crossing active lava fields and exposure to eruptive activity, and several climbers have died from volcanic gases and eruptions.
