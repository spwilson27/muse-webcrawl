Jamuna Bridge

The Jamuna Bridge, also known as the Bangabandhu Bridge, is a combined road and rail bridge over the Jamuna River in Bangladesh. Opened in June 1998, it connects Bhuapur on the east bank with Sirajganj on the west, linking the eastern and western halves of the country for the first time by a fixed crossing.

Construction

At 4.8 kilometres long, the bridge was the longest in Bangladesh until the Padma Bridge opened in 2022. It is a pre-stressed concrete box-girder bridge built on steel piles driven deep into the riverbed to cope with the braided, shifting channel and severe monsoon flows. Construction, led by Hyundai Engineering & Construction, began in 1994 at a cost of about US$950 million, financed in part by the World Bank, the Asian Development Bank and Japan.

Role

The bridge carries a four-lane dual carriageway, a dual-gauge railway, an electricity interconnector, a natural gas pipeline and telecommunication cables. It transformed trade and travel between Dhaka and north-western Bangladesh, cutting journey times that previously depended on ferries.
