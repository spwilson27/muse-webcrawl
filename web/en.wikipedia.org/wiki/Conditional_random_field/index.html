Conditional random field

Conditional random fields (CRFs) are a class of statistical modeling methods often applied in pattern recognition and machine learning, where they are used for structured prediction. Whereas a classifier predicts a label for a single sample without regard to neighbouring samples, a CRF can take context into account; for example, the linear chain CRF popular in natural language processing predicts sequences of labels for sequences of input samples.

A linear-chain CRF models a sequence of labels conditioned on an observed sequence.

History and motivation

CRFs were introduced by John Lafferty, Andrew McCallum and Fernando Pereira in 2001. They were designed to overcome limitations of hidden Markov models (which impose strong independence assumptions on observations) and of maximum entropy Markov models, which suffer from the label bias problem. A CRF defines a conditional probability distribution over label sequences given an observation sequence, using feature functions and a global normalisation, and is a type of discriminative undirected probabilistic graphical model.

Training and inference

Parameters are typically trained by maximum likelihood with regularisation, using gradient-based methods such as L-BFGS; the required marginals are computed by the forward–backward algorithm. The most probable label sequence is found with the Viterbi algorithm. Applications have included part-of-speech tagging, named entity recognition, shallow parsing, gene finding and image segmentation.
