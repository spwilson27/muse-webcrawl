Crassulaceae


A stonecrop rosette

The Crassulaceae (from Latin crassus, "thick"), also known as the stonecrop family or the orpine family, is a diverse family of dicotyledon flowering plants characterised by succulent leaves and a specialised form of photosynthesis known as Crassulacean acid metabolism (CAM). The family includes about 1,400 species in roughly 34–35 genera, distributed worldwide but most diverse in arid regions of southern Africa, Mexico, and the Northern Hemisphere.

Description

Members are mostly herbaceous perennials, rarely shrubs or small trees, with fleshy leaves that store water. Flowers are typically actinomorphic with parts in fours or fives, and the fruit is usually a cluster of dry follicles. Many species reproduce readily by vegetative propagation from detached leaves.

Genera and uses

Well-known genera include Crassula (including the jade plant, Crassula ovata), Sedum (stonecrops), Echeveria, Sempervivum (houseleeks), Kalanchoe, and Aeonium. Many are grown as ornamentals and houseplants; stonecrops are widely used in green roofs, while some Kalanchoe and Rhodiola species have medicinal uses.
