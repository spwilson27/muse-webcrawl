Plein air

Plein air painting (from the French en plein air, "in the open air") is the practice of painting outside, rather than in a studio, in order to capture the natural light, colour and atmosphere of a landscape or scene directly from observation. The term is most closely associated with the Barbizon school and the Impressionists, who made outdoor painting central to modern art in the nineteenth century.

MediumOil, watercolour, pastel
Key figuresClaude Monet, Pierre-Auguste Renoir, John Constable, Camille Pissarro
Period19th century – present

History

Although artists had sketched outdoors for centuries, painting finished works outside became practical only in the nineteenth century. The English landscape painters John Constable and Richard Parkes Bonington made oil sketches in the open air in the early 1800s. In France, the Barbizon school painters, including Théodore Rousseau and Charles-François Daubigny, worked directly from nature in the Forest of Fontainebleau from the 1830s onward.

Two inventions greatly aided the practice: the collapsible metal paint tube, patented by John Goffe Rand in 1841, which kept oil paint portable and fresh, and the portable box easel (or "French easel"), which combined a tripod, drawer and canvas holder in one folding unit.

Impressionism

The Impressionists made plein air painting a defining principle. Claude Monet, Renoir, Pissarro and Alfred Sisley painted outdoors to record transient effects of sunlight and weather, applying broken touches of unmixed colour. Monet's series paintings — of haystacks, poplars and Rouen Cathedral — were painted outdoors in campaigns lasting weeks, with canvases swapped as the light changed.

Regional schools

The practice spread internationally: to the Hudson River School and American Impressionists in the United States, the Heidelberg School in Australia, the Group of Seven in Canada, and the Skagen Painters in Denmark. In California, an entire school of plein air landscape painting flourished in the early twentieth century.

Contemporary practice

Plein air painting remains popular today. "Plein air festivals" and paint-outs — competitive events in which artists produce works outdoors within a set time — are held in many countries, and organisations such as Plein Air Painters of America promote the tradition.
