General classification

The general classification (or the GC) in road bicycle racing is the category of competition that ranks riders by their accumulated time over all stages of a stage race. The rider with the lowest cumulative time is the leader of the general classification, and is typically considered the overall winner of the race. The GC is the most prestigious classification in events such as the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia, and Vuelta a España, where leaders are distinguished by special jerseys — the yellow jersey (maillot jaune), pink jersey (maglia rosa), and red jersey (maillot rojo) respectively.

The yellow jersey denotes the Tour de France GC leader

Calculation

A rider's GC time is the sum of finishing times across all completed stages, adjusted by any time bonuses (usually awarded to the first finishers of a stage or at intermediate sprints) and time penalties. When riders finish in a group, they normally receive the same time as the first rider of that group, which prevents dangerous bunch sprints among GC contenders. In team time trials, the team's time (often that of the fifth rider across the line) is credited to each member, with variations by race rules.

GC riders and tactics

Riders who specialize in the general classification, known as GC contenders, typically excel at both climbing and individual time trials. Teams organize their rosters around protecting a GC leader: domestiques fetch supplies, shelter the leader from wind, and chase down rival attacks. Grand Tours are usually decided in high mountain stages and time trials, where the largest time gaps open.

History

The Tour de France introduced the yellow jersey in 1919 so spectators could easily identify the overall leader. Other races adopted distinct colors: the Giro's pink jersey (introduced 1931) matches the pink paper of La Gazzetta dello Sport, while the Vuelta's leadership jersey has changed colors several times, settling on red in 2010.
