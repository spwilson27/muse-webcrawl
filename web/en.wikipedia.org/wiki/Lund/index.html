Lund

Lund is a city in Scania (Skåne), southern Sweden, with a population of roughly 90,000 in the city and about 130,000 in the municipality. Founded around 990, it is one of Sweden's oldest cities and is dominated by Lund Cathedral and Lund University, founded in 1666, one of Scandinavia's largest institutions for education and research.

History

Lund was founded under Danish rule, traditionally attributed to Canute the Great, and became the seat of an archbishopric in 1103, making it the ecclesiastical capital of all Scandinavia. Lund Cathedral, consecrated in 1145, contains a famous astronomical clock and crypt. The city was a major Danish religious and commercial centre in the Middle Ages.

After the Treaty of Roskilde in 1658, Scania passed from Denmark to Sweden. The Battle of Lund in 1676, one of the bloodiest battles in Scandinavian history, was fought nearby during the Scanian War. The university was founded in 1666 partly to integrate the newly Swedish province.

Modern city

Today Lund is a centre for research and high technology, home to Ideon Science Park, companies such as Tetra Pak (founded in Lund), and major research facilities including the MAX IV synchrotron laboratory and the European Spallation Source. Students form a large share of the population, giving the medieval city centre a lively character.
