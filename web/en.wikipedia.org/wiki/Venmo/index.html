Venmo

Venmo is an American mobile payment service founded in 2009 and owned by PayPal since 2013. It allows users in the United States to send and receive money using a smartphone app, linking bank accounts, debit cards or a stored balance.

History

Venmo was founded by Andrew Kortina and Iqram Magdon-Ismail, who conceived it after forgetting a wallet during a trip. It was acquired by Braintree in 2012 for about $26.2 million, and Braintree in turn was acquired by PayPal in 2013. The service grew through its social feed, in which payments are accompanied by messages and emoji and, by default in its early years, publicly visible transaction descriptions — a feature that drew both popularity and privacy criticism.

Service

Standard transfers to a bank account take one to three business days and are free; instant transfers carry a percentage fee. Venmo expanded into debit and credit cards, merchant payments, business profiles and cryptocurrency purchases. It is available only to users with US bank accounts and phone numbers, and payments between friends are intended to be free of fees when funded by bank account or debit card.
