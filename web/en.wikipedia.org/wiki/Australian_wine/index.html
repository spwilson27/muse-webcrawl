Australian wine is wine produced in Australia, one of the world's largest wine exporters. The Australian wine industry developed after vines were planted by the First Fleet at Sydney Cove in 1788, and expanded greatly in the 19th and 20th centuries, particularly in South Australia, Victoria and New South Wales.

History

Gregory Blaxland sent Australian wine to London in 1822, where it won a silver medal. James Busby, often called the father of Australian viticulture, imported hundreds of vine cuttings from Spain and France in 1832, establishing material that seeded the Hunter Valley. German Silesian settlers founded the Barossa Valley industry in the 1840s. In 1955 Max Schubert of Penfolds released the first vintage of Grange Hermitage, which became Australia's most celebrated wine.

Regions and styles

Australia is known for powerful Shiraz from warm regions such as the Barossa Valley and McLaren Vale; dry Riesling from the Clare and Eden Valleys; aged Semillon from the Hunter Valley; and Cabernet Sauvignon blends from Margaret River and Coonawarra, the latter famed for its terra rossa soil. Sparkling wine is made in Tasmania and the Yarra Valley, while Rutherglen produces distinctive fortified Muscat and Topaque wines.

Industry

Production is regulated through the Geographical Indications system administered by Wine Australia. Major companies include Treasury Wine Estates (owner of Penfolds) and Accolade Wines. Exports to the United Kingdom, United States and China have at times made Australia the world's fourth- or fifth-largest wine exporter by value.
