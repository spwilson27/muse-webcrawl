Metropolitan Police

The Metropolitan Police Service (MPS), commonly known as the Metropolitan Police or simply the Met, is the territorial police force responsible for law enforcement and crime prevention within Greater London, excluding the square mile of the City of London (policed by the City of London Police). It is the largest police force in the United Kingdom, with over 30,000 officers, and also carries national responsibilities such as counter-terrorism coordination and protection of the Royal Family and senior government figures.

History

The Metropolitan Police was founded in 1829 by Home Secretary Robert Peel — hence the nicknames "bobbies" and "peelers" for police constables — under the Metropolitan Police Act 1829. It was the first modern, professional police force, organised on the principle of "policing by consent" and headquartered first at 4 Whitehall Place and later at New Scotland Yard. The Criminal Investigation Department (CID) was created in 1878.

Organisation and governance

The force is headed by the Commissioner of Police of the Metropolis, appointed by the Crown on the advice of the Home Secretary, and is overseen by the Mayor's Office for Policing and Crime (MOPAC). Specialist commands cover counter-terrorism, protection, roads and transport policing, and major investigations. The Met's jurisdiction, the Metropolitan Police District, covers London's 32 boroughs.
