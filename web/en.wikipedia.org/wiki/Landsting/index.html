TypeElected county-level assembly (until 2019)

Landsting is the Swedish term for the elected assemblies that governed Sweden's counties (län) in matters of regional self-government, above all public healthcare. Created in 1862, the county councils — in Swedish landsting — were responsible for hospitals and primary care, regional public transport, and certain cultural and educational functions, financed mainly by a proportional county income tax.

Under reforms completed on 1 January 2020, all landsting were renamed regions (regioner), reflecting their broadened responsibility for regional development; the elected assemblies are now called regionfullmäktige. Sweden has 21 regions, including the island of Gotland, where the municipality also performs the regional role.

History

The landsting system dates from the local government reforms of 1862, which separated county-level self-government from the state county administration headed by the governor. Through the twentieth century the landsting progressively took over hospitals and healthcare from the state and municipalities, becoming overwhelmingly healthcare providers — about 90 per cent of their budgets.

Elections

Members of the county assemblies were elected every four years, on the same day as national and municipal elections, by proportional representation. Assemblies ranged from a few dozen members in small counties to over 149 in Stockholm.
