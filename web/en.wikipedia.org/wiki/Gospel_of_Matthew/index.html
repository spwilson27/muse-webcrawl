Gospel of Matthew

The Gospel of Matthew is the first book of the New Testament and one of the three Synoptic Gospels. Written around AD 80–90, it presents Jesus as the promised Messiah and a new Moses, emphasising fulfilment of Hebrew prophecy.

Gospel of Matthew
Chapters: 28
Trad. author: Matthew the Apostle
Key text: Sermon on the Mount (chs. 5–7)

Content

Matthew includes a genealogy from Abraham, the visit of the Magi, the Sermon on the Mount (with the Beatitudes and Lord's Prayer), five great discourses, and the Great Commission ("Go therefore and make disciples of all nations"). It contains parables unique to it, such as the Wise and Foolish Virgins.

Authorship and structure

Traditionally attributed to the apostle Matthew, most scholars hold it was written anonymously in Greek, drawing on Mark and Q, for a Jewish-Christian community, probably in Syria. See Gospel and Gospel of Luke.
