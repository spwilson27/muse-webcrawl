Marie Byrd Land

Marie Byrd Land (MBL) is an unclaimed region of Antarctica. With an area of about 1,610,000 km² (620,000 sq mi), it is by far the largest unclaimed territory on Earth. It is located in West Antarctica, east of the Ross Ice Shelf and the Ross Dependency, stretching from about 158°W to 103°24′W. The region was named by Richard E. Byrd in honour of his wife, Marie, during his first Antarctic expedition in 1929.

Geography

Marie Byrd Land is divided into five coastal areas, from west to east: the Saunders Coast, Ruppert Coast, Hobbs Coast, Bakutis Coast, and Walgreen Coast. Its coastline runs along the Amundsen Sea and the eastern Ross Sea. Inland, the region includes the Ford Ranges, the Flood Range, the Executive Committee Range, and Mount Sidley, the largest dormant volcano in Antarctica at 4,181 m (13,717 ft).

Much of Marie Byrd Land is covered by the West Antarctic Ice Sheet. The Bentley Subglacial Trench, the lowest point on the (bedrock) surface of the Earth, reaching about 2,540 m below sea level, lies within or near the region's interior.

Exploration

The territory was explored from the air during Byrd's first expedition (1928–1930). Further exploration was carried out by later Byrd expeditions and by the United States Antarctic Service Expedition (1939–1941), which operated the Little America III and East Base stations. Systematic mapping was completed using aerial photography and satellite imagery in the following decades. The Byrd Station, established inland in 1957 for the International Geophysical Year, was a major research outpost.

Status

Unlike most of Antarctica, Marie Byrd Land (between 90°W and 150°W, west of the Chilean Antarctic claim and east of New Zealand's Ross Dependency) has never been claimed by any sovereign state, making it the largest terra nullius on the planet. Under the Antarctic Treaty System this absence of a claim has little practical consequence, as sovereignty claims in Antarctica are held in abeyance. The region is occasionally discussed in speculative proposals for micronations, such as the former "Grand Duchy of Westarctica".
