The Platinum Jubilee of Elizabeth II was the international celebration in 2022 marking the 70th anniversary of the accession of Queen Elizabeth II on 6 February 1952. It was the first time any British monarch had celebrated a platinum jubilee, and Elizabeth was the first British sovereign to reign for seventy years. Celebrations were held throughout the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth, centred on an extended bank-holiday weekend in the UK from 2 to 5 June 2022.

Background

Elizabeth II acceded to the throne on the death of her father, George VI, on 6 February 1952. She had previously marked Silver (1977), Golden (2002), and Diamond (2012) jubilees, and became Britain's longest-reigning monarch on 9 September 2015, passing Queen Victoria's record. The Platinum Jubilee was announced with a programme of events spanning 2022, including the Queen's Green Canopy tree-planting initiative and a national baking competition that produced the Platinum Pudding, a lemon Swiss roll and amaretti trifle.

The June weekend

The central weekend began on Thursday 2 June with Trooping the Colour, a parade of more than 1,400 soldiers, and the lighting of Platinum Jubilee beacons across the UK and Commonwealth capitals. On Friday 3 June a Service of Thanksgiving was held at St Paul's Cathedral. On Saturday 4 June the Derby at Epsom was followed in the evening by the Platinum Party at the Palace, a concert outside Buckingham Palace headlined by Queen with Adam Lambert, Diana Ross, and others, watched by a crowd of tens of thousands on the Mall. On Sunday 5 June, millions attended street parties and the Big Jubilee Lunch, and the celebrations closed with the Platinum Jubilee Pageant, a carnival procession through London retracing the seven decades of the reign.

The Queen, aged 96, attended only parts of the programme owing to mobility problems, but appeared on the Buckingham Palace balcony for the opening flypast and again, to wide public emotion, to close the pageant.

Commonwealth and legacy

Events were held across the Commonwealth realms and territories, and the Queen issued an Accession Day message renewing her 1947 pledge of lifelong service and expressing her wish that Camilla, Duchess of Cornwall, would be known as Queen Consort when Charles became king. The jubilee proved to be the last great national celebration of the reign: Elizabeth II died at Balmoral on 8 September 2022, three months after the June weekend, and was succeeded by Charles III.
