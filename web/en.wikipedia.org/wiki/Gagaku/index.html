Gagaku

Gagaku (雅楽, literally "elegant music") is a type of Japanese classical music historically used for imperial court and religious music and dance in Japan from the 5th century. The Gagaku orchestra and dances are performed at the Imperial Palace in Tokyo and at major Shinto shrines and Buddhist temples. In 2009, UNESCO inscribed Gagaku on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

History

Gagaku arrived in Japan from China and Korea between the 5th and 8th centuries, bringing continental repertoires: tōgaku of Chinese (Tang dynasty) origin and komagaku of Korean origin, together with the native court songs and dances kuniburi no utamai and ceremonial Shinto music (mikagura). The imperial music bureau (Gagakuryō) was established under the Taihō Code of 701, and under the patronage of Emperor Shōmu the repertoire took its classical form. Gagaku is regarded as among the oldest continuously performed orchestral traditions in the world.

Instruments and dance

The ensemble combines wind instruments — the piercing hichiriki (double-reed), ryūteki and komabue flutes, and the mouth organ shō, whose sustained chords are said to imitate the cry of the phoenix — with strings (biwa lute and wagon and koto zithers) and percussion including the kakko, taiko and shōko gong. The slow, stately accompanying dance, bugaku, is performed in elaborate brocade costumes and masks, and is divided into "dances of the left" (from China) and "dances of the right" (from Korea).
