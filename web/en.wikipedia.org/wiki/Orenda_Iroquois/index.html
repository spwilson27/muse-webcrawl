The Iroquois was among the most powerful jet engines of its era.

The Orenda PS.13 Iroquois was an advanced turbojet engine designed and built by Orenda Engines, a division of Avro Canada, in the 1950s. It was developed to power the Avro Canada CF-105 Arrow supersonic interceptor, and was one of the most powerful jet engines in the world at the time of its cancellation.

Development

Design work began in the mid-1950s, aiming at a lightweight, high-thrust engine with a target of roughly 30,000 pounds of thrust class performance for production versions. The Iroquois used extensive titanium and advanced alloys to reduce weight, and a relatively simple two-spool layout. It first ran in December 1957 (some sources say 1958 in testing) and flight testing began in 1958, mounted in a pod beneath a borrowed Boeing B-47 Stratojet operated from Canadair's facilities — the only B-47 ever flown by Canada. In testing, the engine exceeded its design goals and ran reliably.

Cancellation

On 20 February 1959 — known in Canadian aviation as "Black Friday" — the government of John Diefenbaker cancelled the Arrow program, and the Iroquois with it. Completed Arrows and engines were ordered destroyed; the surviving Iroquois examples were scrapped, though some components and one engine survived in museum collections. The episode remains a touchstone in debates about Canadian industrial and defense policy.
