MV Le Joola

MV Le Joola was a Senegalese government-owned roll-on/roll-off ferry that capsized off the coast of The Gambia in the Atlantic Ocean on 26 September 2002, resulting in the deaths of at least 1,863 passengers and crew. With an estimated death toll ranking in the top three, it is among the worst non-wartime maritime disasters in history — sometimes called "Africa's Titanic".

Background

The ship was built in Germany in 1990 and named after the Diola (Joola) people of Senegal's Casamance region. She operated the route between Dakar, the Senegalese capital, and Ziguinchor in Casamance, a service of great importance because the region is separated from the north of Senegal by The Gambia. The ferry was designed to carry somewhat over 500 passengers.

Disaster

On the night of 26 September 2002, Le Joola sailed from Ziguinchor bound for Dakar carrying far more people than she was designed for — possibly close to 2,000, many travelling without tickets and many students returning to school after the summer break. Around 11 p.m. the ferry ran into a storm off the Gambian coast and capsized within minutes, trapping hundreds below decks. Only 64 people survived.

Aftermath

Rescue efforts were widely criticised as slow, and the wreck was never salvaged, remaining the resting place of most victims. The Senegalese government acknowledged negligence, dismissed ministers and paid compensation to families, but a French judicial investigation later stalled. Memorials were held in Dakar and Ziguinchor, and the disaster led to tighter safety rules on the route, which is now served by replacement ferries.
