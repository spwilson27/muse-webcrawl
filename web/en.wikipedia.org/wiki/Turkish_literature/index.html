Turkish literature

Turkish literature comprises the oral and written works in the Turkish language, from the earliest Turkic inscriptions through the literature of the Ottoman Empire to the modern Republic of Turkey.

Early and Ottoman literature

The earliest Turkic texts are the 8th-century Orkhon inscriptions. After the adoption of Islam, Turkish literature absorbed Persian and Arabic forms: the Divan poetry of the Ottoman court, flourishing from the 14th to the 19th century with poets such as Fuzûlî and Bâkî, stood alongside folk traditions including the tales of Nasreddin Hodja, the Book of Dede Korkut, and the poetry of Yunus Emre.

Modern literature

The Tanzimat reforms of the 19th century introduced the novel, theatre and journalism; Namık Kemal and İbrahim Şinasi were pioneers. In the republican era, the poet Nâzım Hikmet transformed Turkish verse, Yaşar Kemal's Memed, My Hawk won international fame, and Orhan Pamuk received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2006. Other notable modern authors include Ahmet Hamdi Tanpınar, Oğuz Atay, Latife Tekin and Elif Shafak.
