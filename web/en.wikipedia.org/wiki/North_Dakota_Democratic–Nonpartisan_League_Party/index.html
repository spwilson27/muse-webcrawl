Overview

The North Dakota Democratic–Nonpartisan League Party (D-NPL) is the North Dakota affiliate of the United States Democratic Party. It was formed in 1956 by the merger of the state Democratic Party with the Nonpartisan League (NPL), a progressive farmers' movement founded in 1915. It is one of only two state Democratic parties with a distinct name, the other being Minnesota's Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party.

History

The Nonpartisan League, founded by former Socialist organizer A. C. Townley, swept North Dakota politics in 1916–1918 on a platform of state ownership of mills, elevators, and a state bank to protect farmers from out-of-state corporate interests. It created the Bank of North Dakota and the state-owned North Dakota Mill and Elevator, both of which still operate.

After decades in which the NPL functioned as a faction within the Republican Party, its liberal wing merged with the Democrats in 1956, creating the modern D-NPL.

Notable figures

Senators Quentin Burdick and Byron Dorgan, Representative Earl Pomeroy, and Governor George Sinner were among the party's prominent officeholders. The party's influence declined after 2010 as North Dakota politics shifted strongly Republican.
