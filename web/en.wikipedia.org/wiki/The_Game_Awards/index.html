The Game Awards is an annual awards ceremony honouring achievements in the video game industry. Established in 2014 by Canadian games journalist Geoff Keighley, who produces and hosts the show, it succeeded the Spike Video Game Awards, which Keighley had also hosted.

Format

Held each December and streamed live worldwide, the ceremony combines award presentations — most prominently Game of the Year — with musical performances and premiere trailers and announcements of upcoming games, which have become a major part of its appeal. Winners are determined by a blended vote of an international jury of media outlets (weighted most heavily) and public fan voting.

History and viewership

The first ceremony took place in Las Vegas in 2014. The show has grown into one of the most-watched entertainment livestreams in the world, reporting audiences of over 100 million livestreams for recent editions. Game of the Year winners have included The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild, God of War, Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice, The Last of Us Part II, It Takes Two, Elden Ring, and Baldur's Gate 3.
