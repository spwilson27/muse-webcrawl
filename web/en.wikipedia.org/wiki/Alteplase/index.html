Alteplase, sold under the brand name Activase among others, is a thrombolytic medication used to dissolve blood clots in acute ischemic stroke, acute myocardial infarction (heart attack), massive pulmonary embolism, and occluded intravenous catheters. It is a recombinant form of tissue plasminogen activator (tPA), the enzyme naturally produced by endothelial cells.

Mechanism of action

Alteplase binds to fibrin in a clot and converts entrapped plasminogen to plasmin; plasmin then degrades the fibrin matrix, dissolving the thrombus. Alteplase is manufactured by recombinant DNA technology in Chinese hamster ovary cells and is identical in amino-acid sequence to human tPA. It has a short plasma half-life of only a few minutes, requiring continuous infusion for most indications.

Medical uses

For acute ischemic stroke, alteplase given within 3 hours of symptom onset (and in selected patients up to 4.5 hours) improves functional outcomes, following the landmark 1995 NINDS trial; the principal risk is symptomatic intracerebral haemorrhage, occurring in roughly 6% of treated patients. In myocardial infarction it is used when timely primary angioplasty is unavailable. In smaller doses it is instilled into blocked central venous catheters to restore patency (Cathflo Activase).

Adverse effects and contraindications

Bleeding is the dominant adverse effect; alteplase is contraindicated in patients with active internal bleeding, recent intracranial surgery or haemorrhage, uncontrolled hypertension, or high risk of bleeding. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
