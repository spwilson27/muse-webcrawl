Edwards Air Force Base (EDW) is a United States Air Force installation in the Mojave Desert of California, centred on the vast flat expanse of Rogers Dry Lake. It is the Air Force's principal flight test centre, the home of the Air Force Test Pilot School, and the site of many of aviation's most famous experimental flights, including the first supersonic flight. It also hosts the NASA Neil A. Armstrong Flight Research Center.

History

The flat lakebed at Muroc (later Rogers Dry Lake) was first used by the Army Air Corps in 1933 as a bombing and gunnery range. During the Second World War the secret Bell XP-59 Airacomet, America's first jet aircraft, was tested there. On 14 October 1947, Captain Chuck Yeager flew the rocket-powered Bell X-1 from Muroc past Mach 1, breaking the sound barrier in level flight for the first time. The base was renamed in 1949 for Captain Glen Edwards, killed in the crash of the Northrop YB-49 flying wing.

Through the 1950s and 1960s Edwards hosted the X-plane programme, including the North American X-15, which reached Mach 6.7 and the edge of space. Neil Armstrong was among the test pilots who flew there, and the base later served as the primary alternate landing site for the Space Shuttle; the first shuttle mission, STS-1, landed on Rogers Dry Lake in 1981.

Current role

Today the 412th Test Wing conducts developmental test and evaluation of aircraft and systems — recent programmes have included the F-35 Lightning II, B-21 Raider, and KC-46 Pegasus — while the co-located Air Force Research Laboratory rocket propulsion site develops missile and space propulsion. The base remains synonymous with experimental flight test, celebrated in Tom Wolfe's book The Right Stuff.
