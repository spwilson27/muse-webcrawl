The Sara languages are a group of related Central Sudanic languages spoken by the Sara people in southern Chad and the northern Central African Republic. They form part of the Bongo–Bagirmi branch of the Central Sudanic family within the proposed Nilo-Saharan phylum.

Varieties

The group includes a cluster of closely related varieties, among them Sara Ngam, Sara Mbai (Mango), Sara Madjingay, Gor, Bediondo, and Kaba. Linguists debate whether these constitute a single language with dialects or several distinct languages; mutual intelligibility varies across the continuum. The Ngambay variety is one of the most widely spoken languages of Chad and serves as a lingua franca in the south of the country.

Features

Sara languages are tonal, with tone playing a grammatical and lexical role, and generally have subject–verb–object word order. They are written in the Latin script, and literacy materials, translations, and radio broadcasts exist in several varieties.
