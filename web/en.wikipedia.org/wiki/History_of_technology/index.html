History of technology

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The history of technology is the history of the invention and development of tools and techniques, from stone tools in prehistory to electronics and computing. Technology has shaped economies, warfare, transport and daily life, and its history is often organised around revolutions: the agricultural, industrial and information revolutions.

Prehistory to the ancient world

Early humans made stone, then bronze, then iron tools. Ancient civilisations developed the {W('Wheel')}, writing, irrigation, metallurgy, ships and monumental construction. Greek and Roman engineers advanced gears, mills, concrete and aqueducts, while Chinese inventors produced paper, printing, gunpowder and the compass.

Industrial era

The {W('Steam_engine')} and mechanised textile production drove the {W('Industrial_Revolution')} in 18th–19th century Britain, followed by railways, steel, electricity, the internal combustion engine, chemicals and mass production in a 'second industrial revolution'.

20th century onwards

Electronics, the {W('Transistor')}, computers and the internet drove the information age; aviation, antibiotics, nuclear power and spaceflight also reshaped the century. Historians debate whether technology drives social change or is shaped by it.
