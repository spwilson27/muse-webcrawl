Reproductive health

Reproductive health refers to the condition of male and female reproductive systems during all life stages. It implies that people are able to have a satisfying and safe sex life, the capability to reproduce, and the freedom to decide if, when, and how often to do so. The term is most prominently defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) and was central to the 1994 International Conference on Population and Development (ICPD) in Cairo.

Components

Reproductive health services typically include contraception and family planning, maternal and newborn care, prevention and treatment of sexually transmitted infections including HIV, prevention of unsafe abortion and post-abortion care, and screening for reproductive cancers such as cervical cancer.

Reproductive rights

Reproductive rights encompass the legal and human rights relating to reproduction and sexual health, including the right to decide the number and spacing of children, access to accurate information, and the highest attainable standard of sexual and reproductive health. Access to reproductive health care varies widely between and within countries, and remains a major global public health concern, particularly in relation to maternal mortality.
