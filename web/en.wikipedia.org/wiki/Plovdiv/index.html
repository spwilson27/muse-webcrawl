Country: Bulgaria
Province: Plovdiv Province
Population: approx. 321,000 (city)
Elevation: 164 m

Plovdiv (Bulgarian: Пловдив) is the second-largest city in Bulgaria, after the capital Sofia. It stands on the banks of the Maritsa River in the historical region of Thrace, and is the administrative centre of Plovdiv Province.

Plovdiv is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Europe, with a history spanning more than 6,000 years. It has been known under many names, including Philippopolis, after Philip II of Macedon, and Trimontium in Roman times. The city is built around seven hills — the syenite hills of Plovdiv.

History

Neolithic settlements date to 6000 BCE. Thracians fortified the site as Eumolpias; Philip II conquered it in 342 BCE. Under Rome it became a major city on the Via Militaris. It later passed through Byzantine, Bulgarian and Ottoman hands, becoming a centre of the Bulgarian National Revival in the 19th century. After the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, Plovdiv was capital of autonomous Eastern Rumelia until its unification with Bulgaria in 1885.

Culture

The Ancient Theatre of Philippopolis, the Roman Stadium, and the Old Town's Revival-era houses are major landmarks. Plovdiv was European Capital of Culture in 2019.
