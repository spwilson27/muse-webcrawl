Constitution of the Portuguese Republic

The Constitution of the Portuguese Republic (Constituição da República Portuguesa) is the fundamental law of Portugal, adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 2 April 1976 and in force from 25 April 1976, the second anniversary of the Carnation Revolution that ended the authoritarian Estado Novo regime. It is the sixth Portuguese constitution, following those of 1822, 1826, 1838, 1911 and 1933.

History

The constitution emerged from the revolutionary period of 1974–75. Elected in April 1975 — the first free elections in half a century — the Constituent Assembly drafted a text combining strong guarantees of civil, political and social rights with a programmatic commitment to a transition toward socialism, reflected in extensive nationalizations carried out during the revolution. The Council of the Revolution, a military body, held constitutional oversight powers until the first revision.

Content

The constitution has a preamble and 296 articles organized in four parts: fundamental principles; rights and duties (civil, political, economic, social and cultural); the organization of political power; and guarantees of constitutional review and revision. It establishes a semi-presidential system: a directly elected President with significant reserve powers, a government responsible to the unicameral Assembly of the Republic, an independent judiciary, and a Constitutional Court that reviews laws before and after promulgation. It also provides for the autonomous regions of the Azores and Madeira and a strong system of local government.

Revisions

Seven constitutional revisions have reshaped the original text. The 1982 revision abolished the Council of the Revolution and created the Constitutional Court; the 1989 revision removed much of the socialist economic programme and permitted reprivatization; later revisions (1992, 1997, 2001, 2004, 2005) adapted the constitution to European integration, including the Maastricht Treaty, and refined the powers of the autonomous regions and rules on referendums and extradition.
