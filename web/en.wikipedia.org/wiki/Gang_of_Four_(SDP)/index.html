Gang of Four (SDP)

The Gang of Four was a group of four senior British Labour Party politicians — Roy Jenkins, David Owen, Bill Rodgers, and Shirley Williams — who broke away from Labour in 1981 to found the Social Democratic Party (SDP). The name, coined by the press, alluded to the Gang of Four in post-Mao China.

Background

During the late 1970s the Labour Party moved leftward, adopting policies of unilateral nuclear disarmament, withdrawal from the European Economic Community, and greater trade-union influence over party decisions. Moderate pro-European members became increasingly alienated, particularly after the 1980 party conference and the changes to the leadership electoral college agreed at Wembley in January 1981.

The Limehouse Declaration

On 25 January 1981, the four issued the Limehouse Declaration from David Owen's home in Limehouse, London, announcing a Council for Social Democracy and their intention to break with Labour. The SDP was formally launched on 26 March 1981, and 28 Labour MPs eventually defected to it. In alliance with the Liberal Party, the SDP–Liberal Alliance briefly led opinion polls and won notable by-elections, including Shirley Williams at Crosby and Roy Jenkins at Glasgow Hillhead.

Aftermath

After disappointing results in the 1987 general election, most of the SDP merged with the Liberals in 1988 to form the Liberal Democrats. David Owen led a small continuing SDP until 1990. Jenkins became Chancellor of Oxford University; Williams remained a leading Liberal Democrat peer; Owen sat as a crossbencher.
