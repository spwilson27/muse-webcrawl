Mardonius (Old Persian: Marduniya; Greek: Μαρδόνιος, died 479 BC) was a leading Persian military commander during the Greco-Persian Wars. The son of Gobryas, he was a nephew and son-in-law of King Darius I, and one of the most prominent advocates of the invasion of Greece under Xerxes I. He was killed at the Battle of Plataea, the decisive land battle of the war.

Early career

According to Herodotus, Mardonius was sent in 492 BC to complete the subjugation of Thrace and Macedon and to punish Athens and Eretria for supporting the Ionian Revolt. His fleet was wrecked in a storm off Mount Athos, and he was wounded in a night attack by a Thracian tribe, leading to his recall. He nevertheless remained influential at court and, Herodotus says, encouraged Xerxes to undertake the great invasion of Greece in 480 BC, hoping to become satrap of Greece himself.

Second invasion of Greece

During Xerxes' invasion Mardonius commanded one of the army's divisions. After the Persian defeat at the naval Battle of Salamis in 480 BC, Mardonius persuaded Xerxes to leave him in Greece with a picked force of some 300,000 men (Herodotus's figure) to complete the conquest, while the king withdrew to Asia. Mardonius wintered in Thessaly and attempted to win Athens over by negotiation, using Alexander I of Macedon as an intermediary; the Athenians refused. He occupied and burned the evacuated city of Athens a second time.

Plataea and death

In 479 BC Mardonius withdrew into Boeotia and met the allied Greek army under the Spartan regent Pausanias at the Battle of Plataea. After days of manoeuvring, the battle was joined; Mardonius, conspicuous on a white horse at the head of his guard, was killed — Herodotus credits the Spartan Aeimnestus with striking him down with a stone (or, in Plutarch's account, Arimnestus). With his death the Persian army collapsed, and the Greek victory at Plataea ended the invasion. His body was recovered and honoured by the Persians; his son's later gift of a golden goblet is recorded in anecdotes about the spoils.
