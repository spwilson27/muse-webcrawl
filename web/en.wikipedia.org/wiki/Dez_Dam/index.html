The Dez Dam (Persian: سد دز) is a large hydroelectric arch dam on the Dez River in Khuzestan province, southwestern Iran, north of the city of Dezful. Completed in 1963, it was Iran's first major multipurpose dam project and one of the largest dams in the world at the time of its construction.

Construction and purpose

Construction began in 1959 under a consortium led by Italian firms, as the centrepiece of Iranian development plans for Khuzestan. The double-curvature concrete arch dam stands about 203 metres high and creates a reservoir of roughly 3.3 billion cubic metres. Its underground powerhouse has an installed capacity of 520 MW from eight generating units.

The dam supplies irrigation water to the sugarcane and agricultural plains of northern Khuzestan, provides flood control on the Dez River, regulates flow for downstream navigation and water supply, and generates electricity for the national grid.
