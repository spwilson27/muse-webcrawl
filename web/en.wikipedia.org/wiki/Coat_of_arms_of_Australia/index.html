Coat of arms of Australia

The coat of arms of Australia, officially called the Commonwealth Coat of Arms, is the formal symbol of the Commonwealth of Australia. The current version was granted by King George V on 19 September 1912, replacing an earlier arms granted by Edward VII in 1908. It appears on Australian government documents, buildings, coinage (notably the 50-cent coin), and the covers of Australian passports.

Description

The shield at the centre bears the badges of the six Australian states: New South Wales (the cross of St George with lion and stars), Victoria (the Southern Cross with an imperial crown), Queensland (a Maltese cross with a crown), South Australia (the piping shrike), Western Australia (a black swan), and Tasmania (a red lion). The shield is bordered by an ermine border representing the federation of the states.

Above the shield is the seven-pointed Commonwealth Star (Federation Star) in gold and blue, resting on a blue and gold wreath; six points represent the states and the seventh the territories and any future states. The shield is supported by two native animals, a red kangaroo on the left and an emu on the right — popularly said to have been chosen because they can only move forward, symbolising progress. Behind and below them is a background of golden wattle, Australia's floral emblem, and a scroll bearing the name "Australia".

History

The first coat of arms was granted on 7 May 1908 by King Edward VII; it featured a blue and white shield with the Southern Cross, supported by a kangaroo and emu standing on a grassy mound. The 1912 redesign, prompted by the desire to incorporate the individual state badges, produced the design still in use. States and territories also have their own coats of arms, granted separately.

Use

The Commonwealth Coat of Arms is used by the Australian Government and its agencies to identify Commonwealth property and authority. Its use by private individuals and organisations is restricted under law and convention. It appears on the Australian passport, on government buildings and embassies, and in the chamber of the House of Representatives.
