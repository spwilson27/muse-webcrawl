Gene Deitch

Eugene Merril Deitch
Born: August 8, 1924, Chicago, Illinois, U.S.
Died: April 16, 2020 (aged 95), Prague, Czech Republic
Occupation: Animator, illustrator, film director
Known for: Munro, Tom Terrific, Tom and Jerry and Popeye cartoons made in Prague

Eugene Merril Deitch (August 8, 1924 – April 16, 2020) was an American illustrator, animator, comics artist, and film director. Based in Prague from 1959 until his death, Deitch was known for creating animated cartoons such as Munro, Tom Terrific, and Nudnik, as well as his work on the Popeye and Tom and Jerry series.

Early life and career

Deitch was born in Chicago and moved with his family to California in 1929. After graduating from Los Angeles High School, he began working for North American Aviation, drawing aircraft blueprints. His career in animation began at the UPA studio, where he became known for his work on theatrical and television cartoons. At Terrytoons he created characters including Tom Terrific, whose adventures appeared on the Captain Kangaroo television show.

Life in Prague

In 1959, Deitch travelled to Prague, Czechoslovakia, on a ten-day business trip to supervise production for Rembrandt Films. There he met his future wife, Zdenka Najmanová, and decided to remain. Working from the Barrandov Studios, he directed thirteen Tom and Jerry shorts for MGM in 1961–1962 and a series of Popeye the Sailor television cartoons. His short film Munro (1960), about a small boy accidentally drafted into the army, won the Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film.

Later work

Deitch directed the animated feature Alice of Wonderland in Paris (1966) and was the first director attached to a proposed adaptation of The Hobbit in the 1960s, for which he produced a short film to preserve the rights. He also made popular recordings adaptations and animated versions of children's picture books for Weston Woods Studios. Deitch wrote a memoir, For the Love of Prague, about his life in Czechoslovakia and the Czech Republic.
