Eutrophication

Eutrophication is the process by which an entire body of water, or parts of it, becomes progressively enriched with minerals and nutrients, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus. It has been defined as "increased production of organic matter" in the water.

Mechanism and effects

Nutrient enrichment — from fertiliser runoff, sewage and detergents — stimulates algal blooms. When algae die, bacterial decomposition consumes dissolved oxygen, creating hypoxic "dead zones" such as those in the Gulf of Mexico and Baltic Sea, killing fish and benthic animals and reducing biodiversity and water quality. Some blooms are toxic.

Causes and control

Point sources (sewage outfalls) and diffuse sources (agricultural runoff) both contribute. Controls include phosphate bans in detergents, wastewater treatment with nutrient removal, buffer strips and improved fertiliser management. Lakes can recover slowly because phosphorus stored in sediments recycles internally.
