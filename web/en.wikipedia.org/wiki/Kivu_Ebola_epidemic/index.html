Kivu Ebola epidemic

Kivu Ebola epidemic Location: North Kivu, South Kivu and Ituri, DR Congo
Period: 1 August 2018 – 25 June 2020
Cases: about 3,470
Deaths: about 2,280
Status: Declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (July 2019 – June 2020)

The Kivu Ebola epidemic was an outbreak of Ebola virus disease in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo between August 2018 and June 2020. With roughly 3,470 cases and 2,280 deaths, it was the second-largest Ebola outbreak ever recorded, surpassed only by the West African epidemic of 2013–2016. Unlike earlier outbreaks, it unfolded in an active conflict zone, where armed groups repeatedly attacked response teams and treatment centres.

Course of the outbreak

The outbreak was declared on 1 August 2018 in North Kivu province, days after a separate outbreak in Équateur Province had ended. It spread through the cities of Beni and Butembo and into Ituri and South Kivu, and spilled into Uganda in June 2019. In July 2019 the World Health Organization declared it a Public Health Emergency of International Concern after a case reached the major city of Goma.

Response

The response deployed ring vaccination with the rVSV-ZEBOV vaccine on an unprecedented scale — over 300,000 people were vaccinated — along with the first use of licensed Ebola vaccines and experimental therapeutics, including monoclonal antibodies later shown to greatly improve survival in trials conducted during the outbreak. Community mistrust, misinformation and violence against health workers hampered control; several staff were killed.

The last patient was declared recovered and the outbreak was officially declared over on 25 June 2020, though a small flare-up linked to a survivor occurred in 2021.
