George Walker (soldier)

George Walker VC (1826 – 4 September 1879) was a Scottish recipient of the Victoria Cross, the highest and most prestigious award for gallantry in the face of the enemy that can be awarded to British and Commonwealth forces. He was a sergeant in the 53rd Regiment of Foot, British Army, during the Indian Mutiny.

Victoria Cross action

Walker was about 32 years old and a sergeant in the 53rd Regiment of Foot (later the King's Shropshire Light Infantry) during the Indian Mutiny, when he performed the act of gallantry for which he was awarded the Victoria Cross. During the operations at Lucknow in 1857–58, he distinguished himself by his bravery in action against the mutineers, rallying men under heavy fire and rescuing wounded comrades. His award was gazetted in 1859.

Later life

Walker later achieved the rank of colour sergeant. His Victoria Cross is displayed at the Soldiers of Shropshire Museum at Shrewsbury Castle, Shropshire, England, alongside the medals of other regimental recipients.
