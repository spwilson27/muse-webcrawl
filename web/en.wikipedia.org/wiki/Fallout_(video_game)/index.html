Fallout: A Post Nuclear Role Playing Game is a 1997 role-playing video game developed and published by Interplay Productions. Set in a post-apocalyptic Southern California in the year 2161, decades after a devastating nuclear war, the player assumes the role of the Vault Dweller, who is sent from the underground Vault 13 to find a replacement water chip and save their community.

Gameplay

Fallout uses an isometric view and turn-based combat with action points. Character creation is based on the S.P.E.C.I.A.L. system — Strength, Perception, Endurance, Charisma, Intelligence, Agility and Luck — supplemented by skills, traits and perks. The game is noted for its open-ended quests, moral choices and multiple solutions, allowing players to resolve conflicts through combat, stealth or dialogue.

Development

The game was created by Tim Cain, Leonard Boyarsky and Jason Anderson, and began development using the GURPS tabletop system before licensing disagreements led the team to design S.P.E.C.I.A.L. instead. Its retro-futuristic art direction, dark humor and the mascot Vault Boy became hallmarks of the series.

Reception and legacy

Fallout was a critical success and is widely regarded as one of the greatest video games of all time and a defining title of the computer role-playing genre. It spawned a sequel, Fallout 2 (1998), and a long-running franchise later continued by Bethesda Game Studios with Fallout 3 (2008) and subsequent entries.
