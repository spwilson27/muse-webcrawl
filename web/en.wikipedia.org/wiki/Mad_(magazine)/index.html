Mad (magazine)

Mad (stylized as MAD) is an American humor magazine founded in 1952 by editor Harvey Kurtzman and publisher William Gaines. Launched as a comic book series before becoming a magazine in 1955, it is widely known for its satire of American culture, politics, entertainment, and public figures. Its mascot is Alfred E. Neuman, a freckle-faced boy with a gap-toothed grin whose slogan is "What, me worry?"

History

Mad began in 1952 as a comic book published by EC Comics. When the comics industry came under pressure from the Comics Code Authority in the mid-1950s, Kurtzman converted Mad into a magazine format, which placed it outside the Code's jurisdiction. Under editor Al Feldstein, who led the magazine for nearly three decades, Mad reached a peak circulation of more than two million copies per issue in the early 1970s.

Features

Recurring features have included movie and television parodies, the "Mad Fold-In" created by Al Jaffee, the wordless pantomime comic "Spy vs. Spy" created by Antonio Prohías, Sergio Aragonés's marginal cartoons, and Dave Berg's "The Lighter Side of..." strips. The magazine's contributors are collectively referred to as "The Usual Gang of Idiots."

Influence

Mad is credited with influencing generations of satirists and comedians and with helping define American satirical humor in the second half of the twentieth century. In 2019 the magazine largely ceased publishing new material in its newsstand editions, continuing with reprint-heavy issues sold mainly through comic shops and subscriptions.
