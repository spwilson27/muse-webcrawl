Red Sorghum (Chinese: 红高粱) is a 1987 Chinese film directed by Zhang Yimou in his directorial debut, based on the novel Red Sorghum Clan by Mo Yan, who later won the Nobel Prize in Literature. Starring Gong Li in her film debut, it tells the story of a young woman in a rural sorghum-wine distillery in Shandong province during the Second Sino-Japanese War. The film won the Golden Bear at the 1988 Berlin International Film Festival and brought international attention to China's "Fifth Generation" filmmakers.

Plot

An unnamed narrator recounts the story of his grandmother, Jiu'er, who as a young woman is sent by her poor father to marry the elderly, leprous owner of a sorghum winery. Her sedan chair is attacked by a bandit on the way; one of her bearers saves her. After the winery owner is murdered, Jiu'er takes over the distillery and falls in love with that bearer (the narrator's grandfather), with whom she has a son. When Japanese troops invade and commit atrocities against the villagers, Jiu'er organises her workers to resist; she is killed, and the film ends with an ambush on the Japanese convoy amid the burning red sorghum fields, under a solar eclipse.

Style and reception

The film is noted for its saturated colour cinematography by Gu Changwei — the vivid reds of the sorghum, wine and sun — and for its earthy, unsentimental depiction of peasant vitality, sexuality and resistance. It was the first mainland Chinese film to win a top prize at a major Western festival and is frequently ranked among the greatest Chinese films.

The novel

Mo Yan's Red Sorghum Clan (1987), composed of five novellas telling a multi-generational family saga in Gaomi township, is considered one of the landmarks of contemporary Chinese literature. A television adaptation aired in 2014.
