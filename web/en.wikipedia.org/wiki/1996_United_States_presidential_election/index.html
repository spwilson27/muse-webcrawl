The 1996 United States presidential election was the 53rd quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 5, 1996. Incumbent Democratic President Bill Clinton and Vice President Al Gore defeated the Republican ticket of Senate Majority Leader Bob Dole of Kansas and former Secretary of Housing and Urban Development Jack Kemp of New York. Businessman Ross Perot ran again as the candidate of the Reform Party, winning about 8 percent of the popular vote but no electoral votes.

Background

Clinton had recovered politically from the Republican victory in the 1994 midterm elections, helped by a strengthening economy, a budget confrontation with Congress in 1995–96 that many voters blamed on Republicans, and his positioning as a centrist "New Democrat." Dole, a World War II veteran and longtime senator, resigned his Senate seat during the campaign to present himself as a full-time candidate and "a man of his word."

Campaign

The campaign was comparatively low-key. Clinton emphasized the growing economy, the decline of the federal budget deficit, and protecting programs such as Medicare and education. Dole proposed a 15 percent across-the-board income tax cut and criticized Clinton's character and ethics, but struggled to close the gap in opinion polls. Three televised debates were held in October 1996; they produced no decisive shift.

Results

Clinton won 379 electoral votes to Dole's 159, carrying 31 states and the District of Columbia. His popular-vote share of 49.2 percent made him the first Democrat since Franklin D. Roosevelt to win two consecutive presidential elections outright, though no candidate won a majority of the popular vote. Perot's 8.4 percent was a sharp decline from his 18.9 percent in 1992. Democrats also gained seats in the House but Republicans retained control of both houses of Congress.

Aftermath

Clinton's second term saw continued economic growth, the first federal budget surpluses in decades, and, in 1998, his impeachment by the House of Representatives and acquittal by the Senate.
