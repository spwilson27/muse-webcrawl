Khoja Ahmed Yasawi (Kazakh: Қожа Ахмет Ясауи; 1093–1166) was a Turkic Sufi poet and mystic, founder of the Yasawiyya order, who played a foundational role in spreading Islam among the Turkic peoples of Central Asia. He is revered as a saint across Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and Turkey, and the city of Turkestan (ancient Yasi), where he spent his later life, became a major pilgrimage centre.

Life

Born in Sayram, Yasawi is said to have studied in Bukhara under the Sufi master Yusuf Hamadani, succeeding him as a teacher before returning to Yasi. Tradition holds that at the age of 63 — the age at which the Prophet Muhammad died — he withdrew to an underground cell, where he composed mystical poetry and taught disciples for the rest of his life.

Works and influence

Yasawi composed his hikmets (wisdom poems) in a Middle Turkic vernacular rather than Arabic or Persian, making Sufi teaching accessible to nomadic Turks; the collection attributed to him, the Divan-i Hikmet, is among the earliest monuments of Turkic Islamic literature. His order influenced later Central Asian Sufism, including the Naqshbandi tradition, and his teachings reached Anatolia through dervishes such as Haji Bektash Veli's predecessors.

Mausoleum

Two centuries after his death, Timur commissioned a monumental mausoleum over Yasawi's grave at Turkestan (1389–1405). Though unfinished at Timur's death, it remains the largest surviving Timurid building in Central Asia and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, housing a huge bronze cauldron (the tay qazan) cast in 1399.
