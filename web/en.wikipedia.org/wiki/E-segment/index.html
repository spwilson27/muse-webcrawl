E-segment

The E-segment is the 5th category of the European segments for passenger cars, synonymous with "executive cars". E-segment cars are roughly 4.8 to 5.0 metres (16–16.5 ft) long and sit above the mid-size D-segment and below the full-size luxury F-segment.

Characteristics

E-segment cars emphasise comfort, refinement, and rear-seat space, and are typically offered as four-door sedans/saloons and station wagons/estates, with premium materials and six-cylinder or equivalent electrified powertrains. The archetypal models are the German trio: the Mercedes-Benz E-Class, BMW 5 Series, and Audi A6, which dominate the segment worldwide. Other entrants have included the Lexus GS and ES, Genesis G80, Volvo S90, Jaguar XF, and historically the Cadillac STS/CTS and various Japanese executive sedans.

Market position

In the United States, E-segment cars roughly correspond to the mid-size luxury car class. Sales volumes in Europe have declined as buyers migrate to E-segment SUVs (classified J-segment), such as the Mercedes-Benz GLE and BMW X5, but the segment remains important as the core "business car" market and a technology showcase for brands below flagship level.

Electric models

Battery-electric E-segment cars include the Mercedes-Benz EQE, BMW i5, and Audi A6 e-tron, alongside the Tesla Model S (often classed between E and F segments).
