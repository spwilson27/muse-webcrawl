Hertzsprung–Russell diagram

The Hertzsprung–Russell diagram (abbreviated H–R diagram or HRD) is a scatter plot of stars showing the relationship between their absolute magnitude or luminosity and their stellar classification or effective temperature. Created independently around 1910 by the astronomers Ejnar Hertzsprung and Henry Norris Russell, it is one of the most important tools in the study of stellar evolution.

Description

In the diagram, luminosity increases upward and temperature increases to the left (hot, blue stars on the left; cool, red stars on the right). Most stars fall along a diagonal band called the main sequence, where stars spend most of their lives fusing hydrogen into helium. Above the main sequence lie the red giants and supergiants; below it, in the lower-left, lie the faint, hot white dwarfs.

Interpretation

Because a star's position on the diagram depends on its mass and age, the H–R diagram reveals how stars evolve. Young star clusters show a main sequence terminating at a "turnoff point" whose position gives the cluster's age. A star like the Sun will leave the main sequence, expand into a red giant, shed its outer layers, and end as a white dwarf.

Forms

Variants include the observational colour–magnitude diagram, which plots apparent magnitude against colour index, and versions used for star clusters and galaxies drawing on data from surveys such as the Gaia mission, which have plotted millions of stars.
