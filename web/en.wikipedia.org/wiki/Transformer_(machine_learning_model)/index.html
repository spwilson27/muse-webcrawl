Transformer (machine learning model)

A transformer is a deep learning architecture developed by researchers at Google and based on the multi-head attention mechanism, proposed in the 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need". Transformers dispense with recurrence and convolutions entirely, relying on attention to model relationships between elements of a sequence regardless of their distance.

Architecture

The original transformer consists of an encoder stack and a decoder stack. Each layer contains a multi-head self-attention sublayer and a position-wise feed-forward network, with residual connections and layer normalisation. Because the model has no recurrence, positional encodings are added to the input embeddings to convey word order. Attention is computed with scaled dot-product attention, combining queries, keys and values.

Applications

Transformers became the foundation of modern large language models, including BERT and the GPT family, and have been adapted to computer vision (Vision Transformer), speech recognition, protein structure prediction and multimodal tasks. Their ability to be trained in parallel on large datasets made them highly scalable compared with recurrent networks such as LSTMs.

History

After the 2017 paper, transformers rapidly displaced recurrent architectures in natural language processing. Key milestones include BERT (2018), GPT-2 (2019), GPT-3 (2020) and instruction-tuned conversational models in the 2020s.
