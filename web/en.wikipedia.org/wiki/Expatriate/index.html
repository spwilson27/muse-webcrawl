Expatriate

An expatriate (often shortened to expat) is a person who resides outside their native country. The word comes from the Latin ex ("out of") and patria ("native country, fatherland"). In common usage, the term is often used for professionals sent abroad by their employers, or for people who have chosen to live in another country, as opposed to "immigrant", though the distinction is largely one of social connotation rather than law.

Expatriates form distinct communities in many global cities.

Corporate expatriation

Multinational companies commonly assign employees to foreign subsidiaries for periods of one to five years. Such assignments are expensive — packages may include housing, schooling, tax equalisation and home leave — and human-resource research focuses on selection, cross-cultural training and the high rate of early return ("expatriate failure"). A growing share of expatriates are "self-initiated", moving abroad on local contracts or as freelancers and digital nomads rather than on company packages.

Expatriate communities

Large expatriate populations are found in global financial centres and in the Gulf states, where expatriates can outnumber citizens. Expatriates often cluster in particular districts and sustain international schools, clubs, places of worship and media in their home languages. Children raised abroad are sometimes described as "third culture kids".

Legal and tax issues

Expatriates remain subject to their host country's immigration and tax law and may retain obligations at home; the United States, notably, taxes its citizens on worldwide income regardless of residence, and a small number of Americans renounce citizenship each year partly over compliance burdens such as FATCA reporting.
