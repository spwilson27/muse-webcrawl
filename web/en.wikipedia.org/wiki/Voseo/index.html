Voseo

In Spanish grammar, voseo (Spanish pronunciation: [boˈse.o]) is the use of vos as a second-person singular pronoun instead of tú, together with its associated verbal morphology, along with the object pronoun te and possessives tu/tuyo. It is a characteristic feature of the Spanish spoken in Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay and parts of Central America (Guatemala, Nicaragua, El Salvador, Honduras, Costa Rica), as well as in regions of Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Venezuela and southern Mexico (Chiapas).

History

Vos was originally the formal or plural second-person pronoun of medieval Spanish, from Latin vōs. In early modern Spain the forms tú and vuestra merced (later usted) displaced vos in polite address, and vos fell out of use in the Peninsula as a rustic or disrespectful form. In the Americas, however, the pronoun survived, particularly in areas that were peripheral to colonial administration and less influenced by later peninsular norms.

Verbal forms

Voseo verb forms derive from the old second-person plural. In the present indicative of the most widespread (Rioplatense) type, -ar verbs end in -ás (vos hablás), -er verbs in -és (vos comés), and -ir verbs in -ís (vos vivís), with stress on the final syllable and no diphthongisation of stem vowels: vos tenés rather than tú tienes. The imperative is formed from the infinitive with final stress (hablá, comé, mirá), and the negative imperative uses forms such as no hables.

Social usage

In the Río de la Plata countries voseo is the standard informal address at all social levels and is used in advertising, media and much writing. In parts of Central America its use varies by country, class and context, often coexisting with tú and usted in a three-level system of familiarity and respect.
